# Temporal Discordance in Multi-Organ Recovery Predicts ICU Mortality

## Hypothesis to Test

Patients exhibiting discordant temporal recovery patterns across multiple organ systems (e.g., respiratory recovery preceding kidney recovery, or kidney recovery preceding respiratory recovery) have distinct mortality risks compared to patients with synchronous recovery or those whose recovery stalls in one system.

## Study Population and Main Data

- **Population**: ICU patients in MIMIC-IV with documented organ support and serial measurements
- **Inclusion criteria**:
  - ICU stay duration ≥ 48 hours
  - At least one organ support intervention (vasopressors, mechanical ventilation, or renal replacement therapy)
  - Serial vital signs (≥ 3 measurements) within 72-hour windows
  - Serial organ function measurements (creatinine, urine output, SOFA components)
- **Exclusion criteria**:
  - ICU stay < 48 hours (insufficient recovery window)
  - Missing ≥ 50% of expected serial measurements
  - Non-critical care admissions (ED, general wards)

## Suggested Approach

1. **Define recovery windows**: For each organ system, define a standardized recovery trajectory based on:
   - Respiratory: FiO2 requirements, PEEP levels, ventilator days
   - Renal: Serum creatinine trajectory, urine output rate
   - Circulatory: Vasopressor doses, MAP requirements

2. **Characterize temporal patterns**: For each patient, compute:
   - Time to recovery for each organ (from admission to meeting recovery criteria)
   - Discordance score: |time_respiratory - time_renal| / average_time
   - Pattern classification: synchronous, respiratory-first, renal-first, discordant

3. **Outcomes**:
   - Primary: ICU mortality (dod) within 90 days
   - Secondary: ICU readmission, hospital readmission, length of stay

4. **Analysis**:
   - Cox proportional hazards models with time-varying discordance scores
   - Adjust for baseline severity (APACHE II, SOFA at admission)
   - Compare discordant patterns vs. synchronous vs. stalled recovery

## Scientific Significance

1. **Temporal dimension**: Moves beyond snapshot assessments to characterize recovery trajectories
2. **Multi-organ perspective**: Captures interdependencies between organ systems that single-organ analysis misses
3. **Clinical actionability**: Identifies patients at high risk based on recovery patterns, not just admission severity
4. **Methodological advance**: Demonstrates value of time-series analysis in ICU prognosis

## Main Limitations

1. **Measurement selection**: Vital signs and organ support are selectively measured; may miss important discordance
2. **Recovery criteria**: Standardized recovery thresholds need validation across institutions
3. **Confounding**: Treatment decisions may be influenced by observed recovery patterns (reverse causation)
4. **Missing data**: Gaps in serial measurements may bias temporal pattern estimation
