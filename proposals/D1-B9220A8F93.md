# Proposal: renal safety of laparoscopic versus open minor hepatectomy for HCC

## Decision, unresolved question, and hypothesis

The consequential decision is whether an adult with resectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who is already judged suitable for a minor hepatectomy should undergo a laparoscopic or open operation.

**Strongest claim already supported by inspected evidence.** A 2023 systematic review/meta-analysis of nine randomized trials across mixed liver-resection indications reported less blood loss, shorter stay, and fewer aggregate complications with laparoscopic resection (complication RR 0.57, 95% CI 0.43–0.76), but it was not an HCC-specific kidney-safety study (Mithany et al., DOI 10.7759/cureus.47274; frozen full-text source `[source checksum]`). A 2025 retrospective major-hepatectomy cohort found AKI in 55/169 (32.5%) and associated it with morbidity and one-year mortality, but did not resolve the approach effect in minor HCC resection (Mingaud et al., DOI 10.3390/jcm14155452; frozen full-text source `[source checksum]`). KDIGO defines one AKI criterion as serum creatinine at least 1.5 times baseline within seven days (KDIGO Section 2, DOI 10.1038/kisup.2011.32; frozen full-text source `[source checksum]`).

**Unresolved claim to test.** Among adults with explicitly documented HCC undergoing a first minor hepatectomy after a 365-day liver-directed-treatment washout, laparoscopic rather than open resection reduces seven-day postoperative relative creatinine injury by at least 1 absolute percentage point, without increasing the pathologist-adjudicated positive-margin rate by more than 2 percentage points.

This is a joint safety/oncologic-safeguard hypothesis. It is falsifiable, but the HCC dataset alone can establish only an adjusted institutional association. A causal treatment recommendation requires adequate measured-confounder balance plus external or randomized confirmation.

## Target trial emulation

### Population and time zero

Use one index operation per patient:

- Calendar window: `2020-01-01 00:00:00 <= t0 < 2025-01-01 00:00:00`.
- Age at encounter at least 18.
- Same-encounter HCC diagnosis must match `hepatocellular carcinoma|primary liver cancer|HEPATOCELLULAR|(^|[^A-Z])HCC([^A-Z]|$)`; exclude same-encounter `intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma|cholangiocellular carcinoma|metastatic liver cancer|liver metastasis`.
- A qualifying minor-resection procedure name must match `(partial hepatectomy|partial liver resection|partial resection of the liver|liver tumor resection|special hepatic segment resection|hepatic segment resection)`. Set `t0` to the earliest non-null qualifying procedure `start time`.
- Exclude encounters with any procedure name matching major/transplant `(hemihepatectomy|extended.*liver.*resection|total hepatectomy|liver transplantation|complex HCC resection)`, same-encounter TACE/embolization/thermal ablation `(tace|hepatic artery.*embolization|transarterial chemoembolization|liver.*(radiofrequency|microwave|cryogenic).*ablation)`, or explicit `conversion to open surgery`.
- Require no resection, TACE/embolization, or liver ablation with a non-null procedure time in `[t0-365 days, t0)`.
- If several encounters remain, retain the earliest `t0` per `Patient Master Index`.

These rules produced 8,214 patients in the feasibility pass: 4,407 open and 3,807 laparoscopic. Both comparators exist in each year (2020 counts 119/333; 2021 828/571; 2022 796/715; 2023 1,362/1,062; 2024 1,302/1,126, open/laparoscopic).

### Exposure

Classify the encounter as laparoscopic if any qualifying procedure row contains `laparoscopy`; otherwise classify it as open. All rows sharing `patient master index + visit number` must be considered to avoid treating duplicate generic and approach-specific procedure rows as separate operations. Explicit conversion is excluded; a blinded review of `operative course` must estimate residual conversion misclassification before interpretation.

### Primary outcome

Define **seven-day relative postoperative creatinine injury** as:

[
Y = I\{\max(SCr\;(t0,t0+7d]) / SCr_{baseline} \ge 1.5\}.
]

- `SCr_baseline` is the last positive numeric result strictly before `t0` in `[t0-7 days,t0)`.
- Postoperative SCr is the maximum positive numeric result strictly after `t0` through `t0+7 days`.
- Eligible analyte labels are exactly `Creatinine` and `Creatinine (urgent)`; preserve the original label and perform a sensitivity analysis requiring baseline and postoperative labels to match.
- The ratio is intentionally unit-invariant because the source lacks a lab-unit column. Call this a **KDIGO-compatible creatinine criterion**, not adjudicated AKI: urine output is unavailable, the absolute 0.3 mg/dL criterion cannot safely be applied, and etiology cannot be assigned.

Refined feasibility: baseline/postoperative paired creatinine was present in 4,083/4,407 open patients (92.6%) and 3,616/3,807 laparoscopic patients (95.0%). Median regular-assay baseline values were 74 and 75, respectively. The feasibility-only unadjusted outcome was 148/4,083 (3.62%) open versus 51/3,616 (1.41%) laparoscopic; this is not the study conclusion.

### Secondary outcomes and oncologic safeguard

- Continuous `log(postoperative maximum / baseline)`.
- Postoperative length of stay: `Discharge Time - t0`; report medians and restricted mean days through day 30. This is administrative recovery, not survival.
- Positive resection margin, adjudicated from same-encounter pathology text. Pathology has no timestamp, so it may only be linked by the exact index encounter. At least two blinded clinical reviewers must create a reference set and resolve disagreements; an automated classifier may be used only if both PPV and NPV are at least 0.90 on a held-out adjudicated set. Feasibility found a pathology row in 4,403/4,407 open and 3,802/3,807 laparoscopic encounters, with a `resection margin` mention in 4,376 and 3,778. Naive lexical positive/negative rules overlapped extensively and are invalid.
- Exploratory same-institution 30-day reoperation/readmission may be reported, explicitly not as complete capture of all care.

## Covariates, leakage control, and analysis

Estimate the overlap-population effect with propensity-score overlap weights. The exposure model may use only information timestamped before `t0`:

- age, sex, calendar month/year, department;
- closest preoperative creatinine (7 days), and albumin, total bilirubin, INR, and platelet count (30 days);
- prior-year encounter count and diagnoses for CKD, diabetes, hypertension, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, and ascites, with diagnosis encounters anchored through encounter admission time;
- preoperative ACEi/ARB, diuretic and other nephrotoxic medication starts;
- treatment history before the 365-day washout;
- validated extraction from preoperative examination reports of lesion number, maximum size, segment/location, and macrovascular invasion.

Do not adjust for pathology, postoperative labs, postoperative medications, discharge, or any narrative whose preoperative creation time is unknown. Missing baseline covariates receive explicit missing indicators plus multiple imputation as a sensitivity analysis; outcomes are never imputed as if observed.

Report propensity distributions, effective sample size, and weighted standardized mean differences. Continue to an etiologic estimate only if every specified confounder has absolute SMD below 0.10 and each arm retains effective sample size at least 50% of its raw size. Estimate weighted risks, risk difference `RD=P(Y|lap)-P(Y|open)`, risk ratio, and 95% confidence intervals using robust sandwich variance; bootstrap the entire pipeline as sensitivity analysis. Model calendar year flexibly and report year-specific estimates and interaction. Use inverse-probability-of-observation weighting for paired-creatinine availability and compare with complete-case and same-assay analyses.

The margin analysis is gated after renal superiority and uses a one-sided 95% upper confidence bound for `RD_margin` with a +2-point noninferiority margin.

## Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive:** renal point estimate `RD <= -0.01`, two-sided 95% upper bound below 0, all balance/ascertainment tests pass, and the one-sided 95% upper bound for adjudicated positive-margin `RD_margin < +0.02`.
- **Hypothesis falsified without evidence of harm:** the 95% lower bound for renal RD is greater than `-0.01` (rules out the prespecified clinically meaningful benefit), or the margin safeguard is convincingly violated.
- **Adverse:** the renal 95% lower bound is above 0, or the margin point estimate is at least +0.02 with its lower bound above 0.
- **Inconclusive:** the interval includes both a 1-point benefit and no effect; margin adjudication is unavailable; positivity/balance thresholds fail; paired-creatinine ascertainment differs by more than 5 points after weighting; or conclusions change materially in same-assay or complete-monitoring sensitivity analyses.

Negative-control test: define a preoperative pseudo-injury using maximum creatinine in `[t0-7d,t0)` relative to the last value in `[t0-30d,t0-8d)`. The future surgical approach cannot cause it. An adjusted absolute approach association greater than 1 point with a 95% interval excluding 0 falsifies the causal interpretation. Also compare postoperative sampling count/day coverage; restrict a sensitivity cohort to patients measured on postoperative days 1–2 and again on days 3–7.

## What the experiment can and cannot establish

Computationally checkable claims are cohort counts, exact timing, joins, missingness, exposure rules, creatinine ratios, weighted balance, effect estimates, uncertainty, sensitivity analyses, and whether the prespecified thresholds are met.

Clinical adjudication is required for operative conversion, whether a generic procedure label truly denotes comparable minor resection, tumor size/location extraction, margin status, and whether repeat care was planned. Surgeon identity, blood loss, ischemia duration, urine output, death, care outside the institution, and raw imaging are unavailable. Therefore even a supportive result would justify a kidney-safety signal and a prospective multicenter/randomized study—not prove that laparoscopy causes less AKI or should be selected for every eligible patient.
