# Donor-aware protective-versus-pathogenic target selectivity

Seed: D2-T2. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Does an intervention-associated transcript pattern preferentially suppress a pathogenic fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state, such that a target ranked from an averaged signal is misleading? This matters because macrophage and fibroblast programs can have opposing roles in fibrotic or inflammatory disease: an intervention-associated change in one compartment may reflect a protective response, a pathogenic program, or simply differential cell composition and stimulation. Resolving the question would change whether a target is pursued as context-specific, requires a cell-type-resolved intervention, or is deprioritized.

## Strongest currently supported claim

The strongest current evidence is descriptive: the configured datasets contain macrophage/fibroblast-related conditions and intervention-associated transcript changes. The datasets are available as prepared counts and design metadata. The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that these datasets contain the necessary conditions and transcript changes, not evidence that a specific transcriptomic signature is protective or pathogenic.

## Unresolved claim

Whether the proposed selectivity is an intervention-specific signal or a consequence of donor composition/stimulation, and whether it reflects direct target action or expression association.

## Falsifiable test

A donor-aware linear mixed model analysis of the four configured sources, with donor random effects and condition fixed effects, testing whether the intervention-associated pattern separates protective and pathogenic states. Include a negative-control gene set and a stimulation control to model composition and stimulation artifacts.

## Alternatives

A simpler averaged-signal analysis that ignores donor and cell-type context, which would lose the ability to distinguish context-specific selectivity from composition/stimulation artifacts.
