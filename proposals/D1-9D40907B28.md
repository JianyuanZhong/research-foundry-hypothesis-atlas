# Does source-concordant renal documentation improve a transportable six-hour ICU risk review?

## Scientific deliverable and advance

The future solver must newly construct an auditable eICU six-hour landmark cohort and 30-minute event ledger, then estimate whether a renal process observed after a qualifying recorded creatinine rise adds transportable information for *recorded ICU-unit death by 48 hours*. Completion requires:

1. a frozen cohort-flow and source/value/coverage audit;
2. out-of-fold development and hospital-held-out predictions from an adjustment-only model and a renal-process-augmented model at a prespecified six-hour post-rise review;
3. the paired incremental change in 48-hour recorded unit-death cumulative-incidence risk, calibration and decision-curve net benefit at thresholds 5%, 10% and 20%;
4. competing recorded unit death, alive unit exit and unknown-status censoring estimates;
5. the same comparison in an ordered multi-state model, with hospital-cluster uncertainty, transport and falsification results; and
6. a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive and linked to computed outputs.

Discovery fitted no model and claims no clinical result.

The substantive advance over the parent is decision relevance. A hospital-dependent confirmation/support record is only clinically useful for review if it improves prediction and calibration at a time when a clinician could act, and if that improvement survives hospitals never used for fitting. A positive result would motivate prospective work on renal surveillance/escalation review; it would not establish that the record is biological AKI, that treatment changes mortality, or that an intervention is beneficial. A null result would be useful evidence against using this recorded process as a portable review signal even if it remains associated in-sample.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported by inspected material is data availability and joinability, not an association. The frozen eICU snapshot is `[source checksum]`. Its catalog and schemas expose ICU-relative MAP, creatinine results and revision offsets, infusion rows, renal-support documentation fields, respiratory charting, severity, disposition and hospital identifiers. The bounded schema audit verified these columns. No source establishes administration, treatment intent, true AKI, dialysis initiation, clinical adjudication or causal effect.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adult ICU-unit stays with a first qualifying recorded creatinine rise and no prior strict renal-support record, does the process of distinct repeat-creatinine confirmation and/or newly documented renal-support evidence during the next six hours add clinically useful, hospital-transportable information about recorded unit death in the following 48 hours beyond measured severity, MAP history, creatinine/testing opportunity and other pre-rise information?

Primary hypothesis: at the six-hour review landmark, adding the source-decomposed renal process will reduce held-out-hospital Brier score and improve calibration, and will yield a positive net-benefit improvement at at least one prespecified threshold, with a hospital-cluster 95% interval excluding zero. The hypothesis is falsified by no improvement, deterioration, unstable direction across held-out hospitals, or improvement that disappears when testing opportunity and competing exits are modelled. The practical margin is a prespecified absolute 2 percentage-point reduction in Brier score or 0.02 net benefit at one threshold; this is a decision-scale criterion, not a claim of clinical effectiveness.

## Population and temporal design

The unit is `patientunitstayid`; repeated ICU stays remain separate. Include adults (`patient.age` >=18, parsing `> 89` as 90 with an age-censored flag), with at least two valid raw `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` observations in minutes 0--360 restricted to 20--200 mmHg, a valid mg/dL baseline creatinine, a first raw periodic MAP <65 in [0,360], no qualifying baseline-plus-0.3 mg/dL rise through minute 360, and `patient.unitdischargeoffset > 360`.

The index event r is the first valid `lab` creatinine result at least 0.3 mg/dL above the earliest qualifying baseline in [0,60]. The post-rise risk review is r+360 minutes; retain only stays still observable at that time. The outcome window is (r+360, min(r+2880, unitdischargeoffset)), using ICU-unit observability, never hospital-discharge follow-up. At a unit boundary, `unitdischargestatus=Expired` is recorded unit death, `Alive` is alive unit exit, and NULL is unknown-status censoring; NULL is not recoded as alive. A location-based status sensitivity uses `unitdischargelocation` only when predeclared.

No result, revision, process row or outcome after the applicable boundary may enter a feature. A dynamic sensitivity recomputes features in 30-minute intervals using only history available at each interval, and a secondary 12-hour review landmark tests whether the result is an artifact of the six-hour choice.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The transparent baseline is a regularized cause-specific pooled-logistic competing-risk model on 30-minute intervals. It uses only pre-rise adjustment variables: age/demographics, admission source/unit type, hospital context, MAP minimum/burden/coverage/recovery, baseline creatinine and first-rise delta, pre-rise testing intensity, severity fields, and documented pressor timing. It estimates recorded unit death, alive exit and unknown-status censoring separately. The primary augmented model adds only the six-hour renal process vector. Models are fit with the same cohort, intervals, development hospitals, split and bootstrap units.

The substantive alternative is an ordered piecewise-exponential multi-state model with states for first rise, test opportunity, confirmation, source-specific support, concordance, recorded unit death, alive exit and unknown-status censoring. It models transition-specific hospital effects and separates information carried by test opportunity, confirmation conditional on testing, support-before-confirmation, support-after-confirmation and competing exit. This reveals whether an apparent score improvement is mostly surveillance intensity or a later recorded process; a scalar logistic baseline loses that pathway and timing information. A learned sequence model is deferred unless both prespecified models show reproducible shape-specific held-out miscalibration and a learned model can estimate the same decision estimand. This is a scientific deferral, not a ban on neural methods.

## Split, uncertainty, transport and resources

Hash `hospitalid` deterministically into 80% development and 20% held-out hospitals before cohort summaries, imputation, feature selection or fitting; keep connected `uniquepid` hospital components together where needed. No patient or hospital crosses the split. Fit and tune only in development hospitals. Apply frozen models to held-out hospitals without recalibration. Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates, plus a `uniquepid` sensitivity, and report calibration intercept/slope, Brier/log loss, AUROC/AUPRC as secondary, reliability, observed-versus-predicted CIFs and threshold-specific decision curves.

The future solver is a tabular, interval-level CPU-first study: approximately 4--16 CPUs, 16--64 GiB RAM and a measured-but-not-yet-verified runtime of minutes to a few hours after source parsing; 500 bootstrap refits may require the upper end of the solver envelope. A GPU is not required for the transparent or multi-state fits. If a learned sequence sensitivity is justified, request one allocated A100 and benchmark it on a bounded subset; do not infer GPU absence from ordinary shell behavior. These are planning estimates, not discovery results.

## Interpretation and falsification

Supportive results require the augmented model to meet the 2-point Brier or 0.02 net-benefit margin, improve calibration rather than only discrimination, retain direction on held-out hospitals, and show agreement in the matched multi-state analysis. The interpretation remains that recorded renal-process information can improve transportable prediction of recorded unit death.

Adverse results are no incremental improvement, worsening calibration/net benefit, reversal across hospitals, or attenuation after pre-rise testing and documentation processes are included. This would argue against a portable renal-process risk-review signal.

Inconclusive results include sparse support events, fewer than 10 held-out hospitals, failed overlap, unstable treatment dictionaries, inadequate confirmation, wide cluster intervals, missing severity timing, or disagreement between models/clock sensitivities. They justify another data source, expert adjudication or prospective validation, not a positive or negative clinical claim.

Computationally checkable claims include exact cohort construction, leakage control, source-specific event counts, fitted predictions, uncertainty, calibration, transport and falsification outputs. Clinical adjudication is required to decide whether a record represents true AKI, dialysis initiation, treatment receipt, preventability, clinician action or biological benefit. Those claims require chart review, urine output/renal reserve and treatment-intent evidence, and an external/prospective study.
