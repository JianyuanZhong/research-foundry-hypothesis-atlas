# Discordant recovery after acute kidney injury: does persistent oliguria at creatinine-based reversal predict in-hospital death and RRT beyond the creatinine-only recovery classification?

Developed from expert seed `[starting question]` (parent `[prior hypothesis]`).
Dataset: MIMIC-IV v3.1 (frozen archive, see `work/data-verification.md`).
Status: **proposal with verified data bindings; no cohort analysis has been run.**

## 1. Scientific question and why it matters

Among ICU patients who develop KDIGO AKI and whose serum creatinine (SCr) has returned
to below the KDIGO reversal threshold by 72 h from AKI onset (creatinine-based
reversal), does **persistent oliguria** (urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h over the 24 h before
the 72 h assessment) independently predict **in-hospital death** and **renal
replacement therapy (RRT)** beyond what the creatinine-only recovery classification
predicts?

Creatinine is a lagging, muscle-mass-dependent marker. [K1] showed that in
mechanically ventilated ICU patients, creatinine-based eGFR overestimates measured
(iohexol) GFR by roughly two-fold at ICU discharge after prolonged critical illness,
and that each ICU day was associated with ~2% loss of muscle area — i.e., the
creatinine signal degrades exactly when "recovery" is being judged. [K2] established
that AKI reversal *patterns* (early sustained, late, relapsing, no reversal) carry
very different 1-year survival and that relapse after reversal is common (37%) and
associated with ~5-fold death risk — so how recovery is phenotyped materially changes
risk estimates. Yet reversal in [K2] was defined by KDIGO criteria that already
include urine output; the specific question of whether **urine output discordant with
a creatinine-only "reversal"** carries independent prognostic information has not been
tested in a large EHR cohort.

Clinical consequence: a patient whose SCr has "normalized" but who remains oliguric
may have persistent true renal dysfunction that creatinine-based criteria miss —
affecting RRT planning, fluid management, monitoring intensity, and discharge
decisions. If the discordant group is not at higher risk, the creatinine-only
reversal label is adequate and urine-output tracking at this time point adds no
prognostic value.

**Hypothesis (falsifiable):** among patients with creatinine-based KDIGO reversal by
72 h, those with persistent oliguria at the 72 h mark have higher in-hospital death
and RRT rates than concordant (euvolemic) reversed patients, after adjustment for
baseline and illness-severity covariates.

**Leading rival explanations** (must be considered, not assumed away):
1. *Severity confounding*: oliguria at 72 h simply reflects a sicker patient
   (shock, vasopressors, hemodynamic instability) that also drives death/RRT.
   Mitigation: adjust for severity proxy (SOFA components / vasopressor use), AKI
   stage at onset, and time-to-onset; report unadjusted vs adjusted HRs.
2. *Measurement artifact*: low recorded UO may reflect catheter problems,
   documentation gaps, or true anuria from obstruction rather than renal failure.
   Mitigation: require ≥6 h of documented urine entries in the window (else exclude
   and report count); sensitivity with Foley-only item set.
3. *Reverse causation / treatment*: RRT decisions are made before the 72 h point in
   some patients; RRT *after* t0 is the outcome, and pre-t0 RRT is an exclusion.

## 2. Population and temporal boundaries

- **Unit of analysis**: adult ICU stay (`icustays`), age ≥18 at admission
  (`patients.anchor_age` or computed from `admissions.admittime` minus
  `patients.dob`/anchor fields; use `patients` join on subject_id).
- **Inclusion**:
  1. ≥2 creatinine measurements (labevents itemid ∈ {220615, 229761}, valueuom
     mg/dL, valuenum > 0) in the first 14 days of the ICU stay.
  2. Baseline SCr = first creatinine in the stay (sensitivity: pre-ICU SCr from the
     same admission if it exists in labevents before `icustays.intime`).
  3. AKI onset = first charttime meeting KDIGO stage ≥1 (SCr ≥1.5× baseline OR
     SCr ≥ baseline + 0.3 mg/dL), with onset between ICU hour 0 and 72.
  4. Index time t0 = AKI onset + 72 h; require t0 < `icustays.outtime` − 24 h
     (full 24 h urine window before t0 and ≥24 h outcome window after t0).
  5. No RRT before t0 (procedureevents itemid ∈ {225802, 225441, 225805} with
     starttime < t0) — pre-t0 RRT is an exclusion.
- **Exclusions**: ICU stay < 96 h total; creatinine at t0 missing (use last
  measurement within 12 h before t0, else exclude and report count).

## 3. Exposure groups at t0

Creatinine-based reversal at t0: SCr(t0) < 1.5× baseline AND SCr(t0) < baseline + 0.3.
Urine output rate = Σ outputevents.value (mL) for itemids {226559 Foley, 226560 Void,
226561 Condom Cath, 226567 Straight Cath} with charttime in [t0−24 h, t0], divided by
(weight in kg) × 24 h. Weight = last `inputevents.patientweight` (or
`procedureevents.patientweight`) before t0; default 70 kg. Require ≥6 h of documented
urine entries (count of distinct charttimes with value > 0, or ≥6 entries) in the
window; otherwise exclude from the discordance comparison and report the count.

| group | definition at t0 |
|---|---|
| G1 concordant-reversed | reversed AND UO rate ≥ 0.5 mL/kg/h |
| G2 discordant-reversed | reversed AND UO rate < 0.5 mL/kg/h |
| G3 not-reversed | SCr(t0) ≥ 1.5× baseline OR ≥ baseline + 0.3 |

Primary contrast: G2 vs G1 (G3 as reference context).

## 4. Outcomes (from t0)

- **Primary**: (a) in-hospital death — `admissions.deathtime` not null (or
  `hospital_expire_flag = 1`), with time from t0 to deathtime; censored at
  `admissions.dischtime` if alive. (b) RRT initiation after t0 — first starttime of
  procedureevents itemid ∈ {225802, 225441, 225805} after t0; censored at
  `icustays.outtime` (or discharge) if none.
- **Secondary**: ICU death (deathtime ≤ outtime), AKI recurrence (SCr re-meets
  stage ≥1 after t0), RRT within 7 days.
- Death is a competing consideration for RRT: report RRT with death censored
  separately from a competing-risk (Fine-Gray) sensitivity.

## 5. Covariates at t0 (measured before t0 only)

age, sex (`patients.gender`), AKI stage at onset, time from ICU admission to AKI
onset, SCr at t0 and baseline, SCr slope from onset to t0, SOFA proxy at t0
(creatinine, platelets from labevents, MAP from chartevents vasopressor/arterial
pressure items, vasopressor use from inputevents), mechanical ventilation status
(procedureevents 225792/227194), comorbidity ICD flags optional (diagnoses_icd).

## 6. Analysis plan

**Baseline (simple) model**: adjusted Cox proportional hazards for death and for RRT
(death-censored), G2 vs G1 with G3 as second reference, adjusted for the covariates
above. Report unadjusted and adjusted HRs with 95% CIs; test PH assumption.

**Learned/mechanistic alternative**: time-to-event model using the full 0→t0
trajectory rather than the single t0 classification: Cox (or random survival forest
on the same CPU budget) with trajectory features — SCr slope over [onset, t0],
nadir-to-t0 SCr ratio, time to nadir, cumulative UO over [onset, t0], fraction of
hours with UO < 0.5 mL/kg/h, plus the same covariates. This answers a distinct
question: **how much prognostic information does the 72 h trajectory carry over the
single t0 label?** Compare C-index and calibration (calibration slope/intercept,
Brier score) between the two models on the held-out validation split. The trajectory
model can reveal whether "discordance" is actually a continuum (e.g., UO slope
matters more than the 24 h threshold) or whether the binary label is nearly
sufficient — information the baseline Cox on the binary exposure structurally cannot
show.

**Split**: time-based by `admissions.admittime` year — train on earlier years,
validate on later years (no shuffling; avoids temporal leakage). Report metrics on
both splits.

**Falsification criteria (prespecified)**:
- **Not supported**: adjusted HR for death (G2 vs G1) < 1.15 with upper 95% CI < 1.3,
  AND adjusted HR for RRT < 1.2 with upper 95% CI < 1.4 → the discordance label adds
  no clinically meaningful prognostic signal.
- **Supported**: 95% CI excludes 1.0 in the adverse direction for at least one primary
  outcome, with adjusted HR ≥ 1.3 (death) or ≥ 1.5 (RRT) as the meaningful-effect
  threshold.
- **Inconclusive**: CIs cross 1.0 with width such that the meaningful-effect
  threshold cannot be excluded → report event counts and required sample size; do
  not claim either direction.

**Interpretation map**:
- Supportive: urine-output discordance identifies a higher-risk subgroup among
  creatinine-"reversed" patients → justifies retaining UO in recovery criteria and
  closer monitoring/RRT planning for this group.
- Adverse (null): creatinine-only reversal is adequate for risk stratification at
  72 h; UO tracking at this time point adds no independent prognostic value —
  itself a useful negative result for recovery-criteria design.
- Inconclusive: insufficient events/power; do not change practice.

**Sensitivity analyses** (each reported, none can rescue a falsified primary result):
weight assumptions (60/80 kg fixed); Foley-only urine set; AKI stage ≥2 definition;
48 h and 96 h index times; competing-risk model for RRT; pre-ICU baseline SCr.

## 9. Limitations and what this study cannot establish

- Retrospective EHR: no causal claim that oliguria *causes* death; the adjusted
  association may still reflect residual severity confounding (vasopressor dosing
  granularity, undocumented shock).
- UO documentation is selective; the ≥6 h documentation requirement introduces
  selection (report excluded counts).
- Single-center dataset (MIMIC-IV, Beth Israel); external validation would require
  another dataset.
- The study cannot establish *why* discordant patients fare worse (tubular
  recovery lag vs hemodynamic vs measurement artifact); it only quantifies the
  prognostic gap.
- Clinical adjudication of RRT indication and true kidney function is not
  available in the data; claims about "true renal dysfunction" remain
  mechanistic interpretation, not measured fact.
