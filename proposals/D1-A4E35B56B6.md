# Episode 49 v3: align the hour-48 loop question to a recent recorded pressor-to-stability transition

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`

## Frozen decision, defect, and substantive advance

This child preserves the parent's clinically consequential question: among fluid-accumulated adults still invasively ventilated at ICU hour 48, is starting or restarting an ICU-recorded furosemide/bumetanide delivery during the next 12 hours associated with a clinically meaningful improvement in the bounded recorded 48-hour airway-event-free extubation proxy, without crossing renal or terminal-harm margins?

It repairs one population defect. The parent calls its cohort post-shock but accepts any valid pressor interval from ICU admission through `t0-6h`. At the fixed hour-48 landmark, a brief infusion near admission qualifies even after more than a day without recorded pressor delivery. That mixes a recent transition from resuscitation with remote or transient early exposure and changes the population to which the policy association would apply.

A treatment-blind discovery audit confirms that this is material before outcomes are considered. Of 7,663 frozen broad hour-48 ventilated opportunities, 1,607 passed the parent's prior-pressor plus six-hour-stability rule; 843/1,607 (52.5%) had their last valid recorded delivery more than 24 hours before t0, including 381 more than 36 hours before t0. Among those 843 remote histories, 136 had no more than one cumulative pressor hour and 415 no more than six. Median recency was 24.88 hours. The repaired primary rule retained 764 broad opportunities before the MAP, fluid, urine, potassium, washout, ECMO and RRT gates. No loop assignment or outcome was read and no treatment effect was estimated.

The repair requires valid pressor delivery in `(t0-24h,t0-6h]` and preserves the existing six-hour no-valid-or-ambiguous-pressor stability window. It therefore targets a *recent-pressor/stable recorded population*. “Post-shock” remains shorthand, not an adjudicated diagnosis. The 24-hour cutoff is a phase-alignment convention, not a validated biological threshold; complete 12-hour, 36-hour and legacy remote-inclusive population sensitivities must test dependence on it.

## Evidence-supported claim, unresolved claim, and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported now is narrow: exact MIMIC streams can identify recent recorded pressor delivery, six subsequent recorded pressor-free hours, loop starts, airway proxies, multisource renal events and bounded observation; and the parent's unrestricted prior-pressor rule includes a large remote-history group. This does not prove shock, congestion, perfusion adequacy, actual drug receipt, or outcome validity.

The unresolved falsifiable claim remains:

> In the prespecified recent-pressor/stable, multisource-no-active-RRT measured-overlap discovery population, assignment-compatible A12 follow-up increases the lower bound of five-day recorded 48-hour airway-event-free extubation by more than 0.05 versus B12, while one-sided upper limits remain below +0.03 for recorded creatinine rise and creatinine-or-death, +0.05 for multisource RRT and RRT-or-death, and +0.03 for recorded terminal/hospice/acute-transfer harm.

Fluid accumulation is associated with poor critical-care outcomes, but the optimal timing, amount and duration of de-escalation remain uncertain and individualized. Bircher et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13154636; frozen full XML `[source checksum]`) explicitly describe that uncertainty. Kuriyama et al. 2026 (PMC13476641; `[source checksum]`) found clinically heterogeneous trials and mostly low/very-low certainty, not evidence for this timing policy. Orieux et al. 2026 (PMC13218125; `[source checksum]`) emphasize preserving perfusion and call hemodynamic-guided decongestion feasibility evidence, not a validated therapeutic rule. Hansford et al. 2026 (PMC12927355; `[source checksum]`) support aligning eligibility, assignment and follow-up. The advance is to align the population with the intended recent transition before asking the same bounded policy question, not to claim that 24 hours defines shock recovery.
