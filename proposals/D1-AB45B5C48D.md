# Relative acute glycemic stress at ICU entry: does chronic glycemia change the meaning of acute glucose?

## Status and scientific deliverable

This is an independent MIMIC-IV branch derived from the assessed repairable expert seed candidate `[prior hypothesis]` (`[starting question]`). The seed is an untested idea. No fitted result, prevalence estimate, clinical effect, or reproduction claim is made here.

The future Harbor solver must newly construct a leakage-controlled first-ICU-stay cohort, fit a competing-risk prognostic analysis and its matched alternatives, and report either a gate-passing result or a complete prespecified failure audit. Completion requires:

1. an all-candidate cohort flow and HbA1c-availability selection audit;
2. a row-level exposure/coverage manifest showing exact chart-time and store-time eligibility;
3. acute glucose, HbA1c-derived chronic glycemia, and relative-stress variables generated before the fixed landmark;
4. competing-risk outcome labels with auditable first-transition timing;
5. held-out estimates, calibration/Brier scores, relative-glycemia contrasts, uncertainty, and falsification outputs for the transparent and learned alternatives; and
6. a conclusion that distinguishes the observed HbA1c-selected prognostic association from treatment benefit, mechanism, or an insulin policy.

The actual scientific deliverable is not a glucose calculator or an insulin-treatment recommendation. It is a new estimate of whether chronic glycemic context adds clinically relevant prognostic information to acute glucose at a fixed early ICU landmark, and whether any added information is temporal physiology rather than testing or treatment documentation.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence supports only a narrower claim: acute glucose relative to an HbA1c-derived background is clinically motivated, and a current 2026 MIMIC-IV v3.1 study reports that early 72-hour relative-glycemic trajectories were associated with 28-day mortality in an HbA1c-selected ASCVD cohort, with related external validation. That study is not evidence for this proposal's cohort, assay-specific timing, competing rescue endpoint, or model comparison. The expert seed and that paper do not establish that a given acute glucose level has different short-term meaning after accounting for chronic glycemia in an unselected ICU population, nor that this information improves a defensible clinical decision.

The falsifiable unresolved claim is:

> Among adults in their first ICU stay who have a valid, available-by-landmark HbA1c and repeated early blood-glucose measurements, does a larger acute glucose elevation relative to the HbA1c-derived chronic background predict a higher 48-hour risk of death or newly recorded organ-support rescue, after accounting for the absolute acute glucose, HbA1c, admission context, severity proxies, and glucose-measurement intensity?

This matters because the same 250 mg/dL glucose can represent a different deviation from prior glycemia in a person with HbA1c 5.5% versus 10%. If relative stress adds calibrated information beyond absolute glucose and chronic glycemia, it could improve early risk stratification and selection of patients for prospective metabolic monitoring studies. If it does not, use of a relative metric may add complexity without changing interpretation. Either result is clinically informative. Neither result identifies a safe insulin target.

The substantive advance over the available evidence is deliberately bounded: a pre-landmark, assay-explicit, all-first-ICU design with a 48-hour competing rescue/death endpoint, selection-process controls, and a same-split chronological learned alternative. It is an adaptation, not a reproduction of the 72-hour trajectory study, its ASCVD cohort, its NWICU validation, its group-based trajectory model, or its exploratory classifier.

## Population, index, and temporal boundaries

Use only MIMIC-IV core 3.1, with one index stay per subject: the ICU stay with the earliest `icu/icustays.intime`; if tied, lowest `stay_id`. Require age >=18 using `hosp/patients.anchor_age` and join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `subject_id,hadm_id`, and to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`. Use `anchor_year_group` as a coarse calendar stratum, never `anchor_year` as a date-like predictor and never `dod`.

Set t0 = `icustays.intime` and tL = t0 + 24 hours. Retain a stay only if the patient is alive and the index ICU stay is ongoing at tL (`outtime > tL`), the admission and ICU keys are valid, and the patient has the required exposure measurements. The observation window is [t0,tL), and all primary features must have both clinical `charttime` and recording `storetime` <= tL. A chart-time-only sensitivity uses charttime <= tL but must be labeled as potentially unavailable at the landmark. No post-tL value may be carried backward.

The primary HbA1c cohort requires at least one finite HbA1c in `hosp/labevents` with `itemid in (50852,51631)`, `valueuom` canonical percent, `charttime` in [`admissions.admittime`,tL), and both charttime/storetime <=tL. Use the earliest eligible measurement; tie by `labevent_id`. Do not use HbA1c values after tL to rescue missing early availability. Record all HbA1c measurements, assay item, timing, unit failures, and selection reasons. HbA1c availability is a selection criterion and an estimand limitation, not a harmless missing-value operation.

The primary acute-glucose exposure uses `hosp/labevents` itemid 50931 (dictionary label Glucose, fluid Blood, category Chemistry), finite `valuenum), and canonical mg/dL `valueuom`. Require at least two distinct `charttime` values in [t0,tL), and define acute glucose as the median and maximum of the valid measurements. The planned assay sensitivity adds itemids 50809 (Glucose/Blood/Blood Gas), 52569 (Glucose/Blood/Chemistry), and 52027 (Glucose, Whole Blood/Blood/Blood Gas), preserving item-specific indicators and requiring canonical mg/dL. If the primary 50931 branch lacks support, the solver must report primary non-estimability rather than silently promote the sensitivity branch. Same-time duplicates collapse by median within item and then across items only in the sensitivity branch; distinct-time counts and assay presence remain features.

Define the primary relative-stress index using the observed acute-glucose median:
`eAG = 28.7 * HbA1c_percent - 46.7` mg/dL and `SHR = acute_glucose_median / eAG`, only when eAG is positive and finite. Also report `HGI = acute_glucose_median - eAG` mg/dL and a ratio-free standardized residual from the training partition. These formulas define a measurement contrast; they do not establish a biological or causal quantity. Pre-specify clinically plausible value bounds, count and clip exclusions, and never winsorize after viewing outcomes. The primary continuous estimand is the adjusted association of SHR/HGI with the 48-hour competing-risk CIF at fixed acute glucose and HbA1c. A secondary descriptive contrast compares prespecified training-locked relative-stress quartiles, with the joint exposure distribution and support reported.

## Outcomes and competing risks

Begin outcome follow-up at tL and end at tE = tL + 48 hours, or at the earliest verifiable loss of in-hospital follow-up. Use the following mutually exclusive first transitions:

- D (death): `hosp/admissions.deathtime` in [tL,tE]. If deathtime is absent, `hospital_expire_flag=1` may support death only when the recorded timing permits inclusion; never infer survival from a missing flag.
- R (new organ-support rescue): the first valid `icu/procedureevents.starttime` in [tL,tE] with itemid 224385 (Intubation), 225792 (Invasive Ventilation), 225794 (Non-invasive Ventilation), 225441 (Hemodialysis), 225802 (Dialysis - CRRT), 225803 (Dialysis - CVVHD), 225805 (Peritoneal Dialysis), 225809 (Dialysis - CVVHDF), or 225955 (Dialysis - SCUF), or the start of a new valid vasoactive interval in `icu/inputevents` using itemids 221289, 221662, 221749, 221906, 222315, 229617, 229630, 229631, or 229632. Join these ICU tables on `stay_id`; retain `subject_id,hadm_id` as consistency checks. A support event counts only when its clinical `starttime` is >=tL and its item dictionary link/category is verified. Existing support crossing tL is not a new rescue. Do not pool amount/rate into a pseudo-dose.
- S (alive ICU exit): `icu/icustays.outtime` in [tL,tE], with no earlier D or R, and with an explicit non-death status from `admissions.deathtime`/ `hospital_expire_flag` rules. S is an administrative competing transition, not a claim of recovery.
- N: none of D, R, or S by tE with adequate follow-up.
- U: unresolved timing, missing death ascertainment, invalid joins, or incomplete follow-up that prevents classifying N. U is not a favorable outcome.

Use deterministic tie order D > R > S and preserve the raw event times. Report D and R separately as well as their composite. The primary outcome is the 48-hour cumulative incidence of D or R, with D/R/S handled as competing causes; N is event-free follow-up. Secondary horizons are 24 hours and 7 days only if the same ascertainment rules remain valid. Hospital discharge fields are endpoint/eligibility fields and may not be predictors.

The outcome is deliberately operational. New procedure/input documentation is not equivalent to clinical deterioration, and an ICU stay may have incomplete rescue capture. Bed capacity, clinician intent, treatment limitation, physiologic indication, nutrition, and whether rescue was appropriate or preventable are unavailable or incompletely measured.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative

Freeze a deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 split before fitting, keeping all admissions from a subject in one partition. The HbA1c selection, assay hierarchy, feature construction and any threshold/regularization choice are outcome-independent. A failed support gate is reported; it cannot be repaired by test-driven cohort changes.

B0 is a transparent cause-specific discrete-time complementary-log-log competing-risk model for D, R and S. It uses the admission/demographic/ICU context domain, acute absolute glucose, HbA1c, acute glucose masks and counts, and a small pre-specified set of organ and support-process summaries. It is the appropriate baseline because it asks whether relative glycemic stress adds information beyond the two quantities from which it is constructed and beyond ordinary context. B0 must be reported with and without pre-tL insulin-process summaries; the latter are not a treatment-effect adjustment.

Brel is the primary transparent scientific alternative: B0 plus continuous SHR and HGI, with restricted cubic splines for acute glucose and HbA1c, a pre-specified acute-glucose-by-HbA1c interaction, assay indicators, and glucose-intensity summaries. The core comparison is Brel minus B0 on held-out competing-risk CIF, Brier score, calibration and the adjusted relative-stress contrast. A relative metric that adds no information beyond acute glucose and HbA1c is not a substantive advance.

J1 is a small masked chronological GRU with six 4-hour bins over [t0,tL), static HbA1c/context features, raw acute glucose observations represented with masks and assay indicators, organ-state summaries, and support/measurement-process features. Its three-cause D/R/S hazard head predicts the same labels on the same subjects and split. Hidden size 32 or 64, dropout, early stopping and five seeds are selected only on training/validation. J1 can reveal persistence, reversal, within-window variability and nonlinear time-local effects that B0/Brel's fixed summaries discard. It is not a mechanistic model and cannot rescue a failed relative-glycemia claim. A J1 gain must attenuate under chronological-bin permutation while preserving masks/counts; otherwise it is evidence of process or leakage.

The alternative was selected because this question has a real temporal uncertainty: a single median may lose early peak, persistence and reversal information. A larger transformer was deferred because the configured question has modest fixed-window tabular structure and no verified need for scale; a latent mixture/trajectory model was deferred because HbA1c selection and irregular measurement could make latent classes primarily reflect observation intensity. A treatment-effect model was rejected for this branch: the available tables do not establish insulin intent, nutrition, counterfactual outcomes, or adequate time-varying confounding control. Revisit that branch only if an expert-reviewed treatment strategy, positivity, and outcome/utility definition become available. GPU use is not required for B0/Brel; J1 is expected to fit CPU-first at small size. If repeated fits threaten the future envelope, an allocated GPU may be used with tensors on `cuda:0`; an unallocated shell cannot establish GPU absence.

## Estimands, uncertainty, and evaluation

The primary scientific estimand is the held-out standardized difference in 48-hour CIF(D+R) between observed relative-stress contrasts implied by the Brel model, conditional on the same acute-glucose/context domain, plus the incremental held-out performance of Brel over B0. Report separate CIF(D), CIF(R), and CIF(S), cause-specific hazard ratios or risk differences with clear scale labels, calibration intercept/slope, multiclass and composite Brier scores, and observed-versus-predicted calibration by HbA1c availability/timing and relative-stress strata. Also report the association of continuous SHR/HGI without dichotomization.

The standardized relative-stress contrast must be defined before test scoring: for each test subject, evaluate the fitted risk over the prespecified training-supported HGI/SHR interquartile contrast while holding acute glucose, covariates and observation features fixed, and average only over subjects with common support. Because this is a model-based observed-data contrast, not a physically randomized intervention, label it noncausal. If common support fails, report the contrast as not estimable rather than extrapolate.

Use 1,000 subject-level bootstrap replicates where the solver envelope permits, refitting transparent models within each bootstrap if computationally feasible; otherwise state the exact number and whether intervals condition on fixed test predictions. Bootstrap relative-stress contrasts, Brel-B0 CIF/Brier/calibration differences, J1-Brel differences, D/R/S capture, and HbA1c-selection and timing sensitivity summaries. Preserve the fact that bootstrap uncertainty does not correct unmeasured selection or ascertainment bias.

A 5%, 10% and 20% alert-capture analysis may be reported descriptively for future monitoring research, ranking by CIF(D+R), but it is not the primary decision estimand and must not be called net benefit, utility, or an insulin policy. No treatment or utility data are available to assign costs to false alerts, missed rescue, hypoglycemia, staff workload, or patient preferences. Any clinical workflow recommendation requires a prospective utility/impact study.

## Support gates, falsification, and interpretation

Before fitting, report the all-candidate flow from first ICU stay through adult/live/ongoing-at-tL, glucose availability, HbA1c availability, joint exposure validity, and endpoint follow-up. Freeze a support gate requiring, in the full cohort and each prespecified HGI quartile/sex/age/first-careunit stratum where claimed: at least 40 subjects, at least 10 D+R events, at least 8 held-out subjects, and at least 3 held-out D+R events. Report the number of eligible subjects, D, R, S, N and U. If the gate fails, the corresponding claim is not estimable; do not merge cells or alter cutoffs after outcomes.

Required audits include:

- archive/source SHA, member headers, catalog schema hashes, dictionary labels, links, units and item presence;
- join cardinality, duplicate handling, first-index selection, t0/tL/tE, first-transition ordering, and subject-level split integrity;
- both charttime-only and chart-plus-storetime feature availability;
- HbA1c measurement timing, assay mix, anemia/transfusion proxies if available, missingness, and comparison of HbA1c-observed versus HbA1c-unobserved ICU candidates;
- primary 50931 versus the prespecified union of blood-glucose assays and ICU charted-glucose sensitivity;
- measurement intensity, same-time duplicates, invalid values, negative/nonpositive eAG, unit failures and clipping;
- B0, Brel, process-only Bmask, and a glucose/HbA1c-permuted control preserving missingness/counts;
- within-subject permutation of glucose order preserving values, masks and counts, plus J1 chronology shuffling;
- complete D/R/S label permutation within partition;
- removal of terminal six-hour values and alternative 6-hour versus 24-hour landmarks;
- insulin-present/minutes sensitivity using input intervals, with no rate/amount pooling and no interpretation as treatment effect;
- endpoint components separately, 24-hour versus 48-hour versus 7-day horizons, and explicit U bounds;
- stability by age, gender, anchor-year-group, first-careunit, HbA1c timing, glucose assay and measurement-intensity strata where support permits.

Supportive evidence requires a passed endpoint-independent support gate; a positive, uncertainty-qualified relative-stress association at fixed acute glucose; incremental held-out Brel performance/calibration that is not trivial or unstable; separation from Bmask and glucose/HbA1c permutation; consistency in D/R decomposition; and attenuation of J1's incremental chronology signal after order shuffling. This supports a bounded claim that relative acute glycemia is an associated risk phenotype and may improve risk stratification in this HbA1c-observed MIMIC population.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed relative-stress contrast with adequate support, no Brel improvement over B0, equivalent performance from Bmask or measurement intensity, disappearance with available-by-cutoff timing, or persistence of J1 gains after chronology permutation. This challenges the claim that chronic context adds useful early information; it does not prove that relative glycemia is biologically meaningless or that glucose treatment has no effect.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse HbA1c-observed support, failed common support, too few D/R events, large timing/assay discordance, high U, calibration instability, broad intervals spanning both clinically relevant and null contrasts, or inability to distinguish process from physiology. Inconclusive data cannot be converted into a favorable finding by changing the HbA1c window, glucose assay, landmark or alert workload.

Computationally checkable claims are source provenance, schema/dictionary bindings, join and split integrity, temporal eligibility, exposure calculations, endpoint labels, model fitting, predictions, CIFs, calibration, uncertainty and permutation results. Clinical adjudication is required to determine whether a support event represented deterioration, whether a rescue was appropriate or preventable, whether treatment limitation influenced events, whether insulin or nutrition caused the observed pattern, and whether monitoring or treatment would improve outcomes. External validation in another ICU, prospective measurement of HbA1c/acute glucose, adjudicated physiology, and a randomized or carefully designed causal treatment/utility study are required for transportability, mechanism, insulin recommendations, patient benefit, alert harms, or net clinical utility.
