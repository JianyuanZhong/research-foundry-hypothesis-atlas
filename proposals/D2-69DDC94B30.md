# D2-T1: Intervention-associated transcript response in macrophage-fibroblast context

## Question
Does the intervention-associated transcript response in synovial fibroblasts depend on the presence and phenotype of co-cultured macrophages?

## Hypothesis
The intervention-associated transcript response in synovial fibroblasts is context-dependent: it differs significantly when fibroblasts are co-cultured with macrophages versus when they are cultured alone, and the magnitude and direction of the response vary by macrophage phenotype (e.g., MerTK/CD206-positive vs. negative, LPS-stimulated vs. unstimulated).

## Scientific Value
Understanding whether intervention responses are context-dependent is critical for therapeutic target prioritization. If a drug's transcript-level effect on fibroblasts depends on the presence and phenotype of macrophages, then:
1. Single-cell-type drug screening may miss context-dependent effects.
2. Therapeutic targets identified in isolated cell cultures may not generalize to the in vivo microenvironment.
3. Combination strategies targeting both macrophages and fibroblasts may be necessary for effective intervention.

## Evidence
- GSE95588 contains co-culture experiments with macrophage-fibroblast pairs (m_r1, mf_r1, mt_r1, mtf_r1, m_r2, mf_r2, mt_r2, mtf_r2) and drug-treated conditions (P1_MTF_Dex, P1_MTF_Dic, etc.)
- E-MTAB-8316 contains monocyte samples from rheumatoid arthritis patients with UNC106238 treatment
- E-MTAB-8873 contains FLS samples co-cultured with MerTK/CD206-positive and negative macrophages, with and without MerTK inhibitor
- E-MTAB-8322 contains macrophage samples from healthy controls and RA patients
- [K1] Wei & Xu (2026) describe the rheumatoid synovium as a niche of failed resolution, with imprinted pathological fibroblasts and a macrophage compartment stripped of its pro-resolving, efferocytic subsets working against resolution through reciprocal, largely feed-forward signaling.
- [K2] Bao et al. (2026) show that reduced IGFBP5 expression is associated with pathogenic fibroblast states in RA, and IGFBP5 overexpression in vitro attenuates aggressive features of RA synovial fibroblasts and alters macrophage phenotypic and cytokine profiles in coculture.
- [K3] Wang et al. (2026) demonstrate that BCL9 drives the macrophage M2 program through the MerTK-ERK-SPP1 axis, and pharmacological inhibition of BCL9 effectively attenuates pulmonary fibrosis by reprogramming macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk.

## Unresolved Claim
Whether the intervention-associated transcript response is significantly different in the presence of macrophages versus in isolation, and whether this difference is consistent across donors and macrophage phenotypes.

## Falsifiable Test
Compare the transcript response to intervention (e.g., UNC106238, MerTK inhibitor) in fibroblasts co-cultured with macrophages versus fibroblasts cultured alone. If the response is context-independent, the effect sizes should be similar across contexts. A significant interaction between context and intervention would support the hypothesis.

## Method Alternatives
1. Baseline: Differential expression analysis with DESeq2/edgeR, comparing intervention vs. control within each context
2. Learned: Machine learning model (e.g., random forest, gradient boosting) to predict intervention response from baseline expression, with context as a feature
3. Mechanistic: Pathway analysis to identify specific signaling pathways that mediate context-dependent responses
