# Asynchronous organ recovery after circulatory stabilization in MIMIC-IV

## Scientific question and clinical importance

After vasopressor support is stopped or substantially reduced, do patients whose renal or respiratory proxies remain impaired have a higher risk of subsequent ICU deterioration or death than patients whose proxies have recovered?

This is clinically consequential because a single SOFA assessment or a binary "improved/not improved" label can miss delayed organ failure. A patient may appear hemodynamically stabilized while creatinine, urine output, or oxygenation remains abnormal. If delayed recovery is prognostic, clinicians would have a concrete reason to monitor the recovering organ more intensively after circulatory support is withdrawn, rather than treating vasopressor-free status as full recovery.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that longitudinal laboratory and support patterns can distinguish ICU patients with different prognoses. The expert seed proposes that recovery after circulatory stabilization is asynchronous across organs and that delayed or incomplete recovery predicts later deterioration. That is the unresolved claim.

The seed does not establish that MIMIC-IV contains a validated organ-function label, that vasopressor cessation is a clinically meaningful stabilization point, or that the proposed recovery phenotype has prognostic value. It also does not establish a causal effect of organ recovery on deterioration.

The closest inspected work, Foy et al. [K1], studied longitudinal WBC and platelet dynamics in hospitalized inflammatory cohorts and found that a generic recovery trajectory was associated with adverse outcomes in sepsis and other cohorts. That work supports the broader idea that dynamic recovery patterns can be prognostic, but it does not test organ-specific recovery after circulatory stabilization in an ICU population. Zhuo et al. [K2] used MIMIC-IV to study combined serum sodium and urine output trajectories in sepsis and found different trajectory subtypes associated with 30-day mortality. That work supports the feasibility of longitudinal MIMIC-IV trajectories, but it does not define circulatory stabilization or asynchronous renal/respiratory recovery. Du et al. [K3] reviewed the heterogeneity of septic shock and argued that a single SOFA assessment or binary improved/not-improved label can miss important prognostic information. That work supports the clinical motivation for the study, but it does not test a specific recovery phenotype or define circulatory stabilization.

The experiment tests whether a prespecified, leakage-free renal/respiratory recovery phenotype after vasopressor stabilization predicts subsequent ICU deterioration or death. It does not test whether recovery causes deterioration.

## Prespecified recovery phenotype and temporal design

### Cohort

Include adult ICU stays with a diagnosis of infection or shock, an ICU stay of at least 48 hours, and at least one vasopressor exposure during the first 24 hours of ICU. Exclude stays with less than 24 hours of follow-up after the index time, and exclude patients with missing creatinine or urine output measurements in the required windows.

### Index time

The index time is the first time at which vasopressor support is stopped or reduced to a prespecified low rate and remains stopped for at least 6 hours. This is a circulatory stabilization proxy, not a validated clinical endpoint. If a patient never meets this criterion, they are excluded from the primary analysis.

### Recovery windows

Use a 24-hour pre-index window and a 24-hour post-index window. All recovery measurements must occur before the index time or within the post-index window, but the outcome window begins after the post-index window to avoid leakage.

### Renal recovery proxy

Define renal recovery as a creatinine value within the laboratory reference range and urine output at least 0.5 mL/kg/h over the post-index window. A patient is renal-recovered if both criteria are met. A patient is renal-unrecovered if either criterion is not met.

### Respiratory recovery proxy

Define respiratory recovery as oxygen flow at or below 4 L/min or FiO2 at or below 0.40, with no mechanical ventilation, over the post-index window. A patient is respiratory-recovered if these criteria are met. A patient is respiratory-unrecovered if either criterion is not met.

### Asynchronous recovery phenotype

The primary phenotype is a three-category variable:

- Both recovered: renal and respiratory proxies recovered.
- Renal-only recovered: renal proxy recovered but respiratory proxy did not.
- Respiratory-only recovered: respiratory proxy recovered but renal proxy did not.
- Neither recovered: neither proxy recovered.

The primary comparison is neither recovered versus both recovered, with renal-only and respiratory-only as secondary comparisons.

### Outcome

The primary outcome is ICU deterioration within 72 hours after the post-index window, defined as new vasopressor initiation, new mechanical ventilation, or death. The secondary outcome is ICU mortality.

### Temporal boundaries

No outcome information is used to define the recovery phenotype. All recovery measurements occur before the outcome window. The index time is defined using only information available at that time. Follow-up begins when the phenotype can be determined, as required by the seed.

## Method comparison

### Simple baseline

The baseline is a prespecified logistic regression model with the following inputs:

- Age, sex, race
- ICU length of stay before index time
- Vasopressor exposure duration before index time
- Creatinine value at index time
- Urine output in the 24 hours before index time
- Oxygen flow/FiO2 at index time
- Mechanical ventilation status at index time
- Recovery phenotype (both, renal-only, respiratory-only, neither)

The fitting target is the 72-hour deterioration outcome. The split is a 70% training / 30% validation split, stratified by outcome. The outcome evaluation is AUC, calibration slope, and calibration intercept. The baseline comparison is a model without the recovery phenotype, using only the other covariates.

### Learned alternative

The learned alternative is a gradient-boosted tree model with the same inputs as the baseline, plus the recovery phenotype and a small set of time-varying features computed from the pre-index window. The fitting target is the same 72-hour deterioration outcome. The split is the same 70% training / 30% validation split, stratified by outcome. The outcome evaluation is AUC, calibration slope, and calibration intercept. The baseline comparison is the logistic regression model.

The learned alternative could reveal nonlinear interactions between recovery phenotype and baseline severity that the logistic regression model cannot capture. It could also reveal whether the recovery phenotype adds prognostic value beyond the baseline covariates.

### Resource and time budget

The baseline logistic regression can be fit on CPU in under 10 minutes for a cohort of 10,000 patients. The gradient-boosted tree model can be fit on CPU in under 30 minutes for the same cohort. No GPU is required.

### Selection and deferral reason

The logistic regression is selected as the primary analysis because it is interpretable and directly tests the prespecified recovery phenotype. The gradient-boosted tree is a secondary analysis to test whether nonlinear interactions add prognostic value. If the logistic regression does not show a significant association between the recovery phenotype and the outcome, the gradient-boosted tree is not used to rescue a null result.

## Falsification criteria and interpretation

### Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if:

1. The recovery phenotype is not associated with the 72-hour deterioration outcome in the primary analysis.
2. The association is driven by a single covariate (e.g., creatinine value) rather than the recovery phenotype.
3. The association is not robust to a sensitivity analysis that changes the index time definition or the recovery window.

### Supportive results

Supportive results would be a significant association between the recovery phenotype and the 72-hour deterioration outcome, with the neither-recovered group having the highest risk. This would support the idea that delayed or incomplete organ recovery after circulatory stabilization is prognostic.

### Adverse results

Adverse results would be no association between the recovery phenotype and the outcome, or an association in the opposite direction. This would suggest that the recovery phenotype is not prognostic, or that the index time definition is not clinically meaningful.

### Inconclusive results

Inconclusive results would be a weak association with wide confidence intervals, or an association that is not robust to sensitivity analyses. This would suggest that the study is underpowered or that the recovery phenotype is not well-defined.

## Alternatives not chosen

1. **Causal inference methods**: The study is prognostic, not causal. Causal inference methods are not chosen because the study does not have a randomized treatment assignment, and the recovery phenotype is not a treatment.
2. **Machine learning without a baseline**: A machine learning model without a baseline would not test whether the recovery phenotype adds prognostic value. The baseline is required to make the comparison meaningful.
3. **Different index time**: A different index time (e.g., ICU admission) would not test the specific question about recovery after circulatory stabilization. The index time is defined by vasopressor cessation, which is the most clinically relevant stabilization point.

## Actual scientific deliverable

The actual scientific deliverable is a fitted logistic regression model and a gradient-boosted tree model, with the following outputs:

1. A cohort table with the number of patients in each recovery phenotype category.
2. A logistic regression model with coefficients, confidence intervals, and p-values for each covariate.
3. A gradient-boosted tree model with feature importance and AUC.
4. A calibration plot for both models.
5. A sensitivity analysis table with the results of changing the index time definition and the recovery window.

These outputs establish the completion of the study. The study does not establish a causal effect of organ recovery on deterioration.
