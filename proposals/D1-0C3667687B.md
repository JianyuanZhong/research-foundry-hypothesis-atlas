# Research Proposal: Asynchronous Organ Recovery Predicts ICU Deterioration

## Unresolved Question

**Current Knowledge Gap**: Clinical practice in septic shock emphasizes maintaining hemodynamic stability (blood pressure targets) and oxygenation as primary goals. However, a critical unresolved question remains:

> **Do patients with persistent organ dysfunction (kidney or respiratory) after circulatory stabilization have different outcomes compared to those with concurrent multi-organ recovery, and does this "asynchronous recovery" pattern predict subsequent deterioration?**

**The Gap**: Existing research focuses on:
- Minimum blood pressure as a static threshold metric [K1]
- Total duration of hypotension below threshold [K2]
- Cumulative time below threshold [K3]

These approaches miss a critical temporal dimension: **the timing and pattern of organ-specific recovery relative to circulatory stabilization**. Two patients may have identical BP recovery, but one may recover respiratory function while the kidney remains impaired, while the other recovers both simultaneously. This "asynchrony" may be a critical determinant of subsequent outcomes.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Risk Stratification Beyond Hemodynamics**: Current risk models focus on hemodynamic targets. An "asynchronous recovery" metric could:
   - Identify patients at high risk despite achieving BP targets
   - Predict deterioration in patients with adequate minimum BP but poor organ-specific recovery
   - Inform timing of ICU discharge decisions

2. **Guiding De-escalation Strategies**: Understanding which organs recover first could:
   - Help clinicians prioritize which organ support to wean first
   - Reduce unnecessary ICU stays for patients with complete recovery
   - Prevent premature discharge of patients with residual organ dysfunction

3. **Understanding Recovery Dynamics**: The pattern of recovery (synchronous vs. asynchronous) may reflect:
   - Underlying disease severity
   - Response to specific treatments
   - Natural history of the illness

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Existing work on hypotension-AKI relationships focuses on:
- Cumulative exposure to hypotension
- Duration of hypotension below thresholds
- Rate of BP recovery

This proposal advances by:
- Shifting focus from **hypotension exposure** to **organ-specific recovery patterns**
- Testing whether **recovery asynchrony** (not just recovery rate) predicts outcomes
- Incorporating **multiple organ systems** (kidney + respiratory) to capture the full picture of critical illness recovery

## Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis**: After circulatory stabilization (defined as sustained SBP ≥ 100 mmHg for ≥30 minutes), patients with **asynchronous organ recovery** (persistent kidney dysfunction OR persistent respiratory dysfunction) have higher rates of subsequent deterioration (requiring escalation of organ support or increased vasopressor burden) compared to patients with synchronous multi-organ recovery.

**Secondary Hypothesis**: The risk of subsequent deterioration increases with the **duration of persistent dysfunction** in each organ system, with kidney dysfunction being more strongly associated than respiratory dysfunction.

## Methodological Design

**Baseline Model**:
- Logistic regression predicting subsequent deterioration (escalation of organ support or death within 48 hours)
- Predictors: Minimum SBP, duration of hypotension, total vasopressor dose
- This model captures cumulative exposure but not recovery patterns

**Advanced Model**:
- Logistic regression or Cox proportional hazards predicting subsequent deterioration
- Predictors:
  - Time to circulatory stabilization
  - Duration of kidney dysfunction post-stabilization
  - Duration of respiratory dysfunction post-stabilization
  - Asynchrony score (difference in recovery times between organs)
  - Interaction terms between organ dysfunction duration and other risk factors
- This model captures recovery patterns and asynchrony

**Key Comparison**:
- The advanced model should outperform the baseline in terms of:
  - Discrimination (AUROC)
  - Calibration (Brier score)
  - Net benefit at clinically relevant thresholds

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. There is no significant association between asynchronous organ recovery and subsequent deterioration (p > 0.05)
2. The baseline model (using only hypotension metrics) performs as well as or better than the advanced model (p > 0.05 for difference in AUROC)
3. The asynchrony score shows no predictive value after adjusting for confounders

**Supportive Evidence**:
- Significant association between asynchronous organ recovery and subsequent deterioration (p < 0.01)
- Advanced model outperforms baseline in discrimination and calibration
- Kidney dysfunction duration is more strongly associated than respiratory dysfunction

**Inconclusive Results**:
- Weak or moderate association that may require larger sample size or external validation
- Mixed results across different hospital cohorts

## Required Clinical Adjudication

The following elements require clinical adjudication beyond computational analysis:
1. **Definition of "subsequent deterioration"**: The exact clinical criteria for deterioration (e.g., need for escalation of organ support, death, length of stay extension) should be defined by clinical experts.
2. **Cut-off values for organ dysfunction**: Thresholds for kidney dysfunction (creatinine, urine output) and respiratory dysfunction (SpO2, FiO2) should be validated by clinicians.
3. **Circulatory stabilization criteria**: The definition of "sustained SBP ≥ 100 mmHg for ≥30 minutes" should be validated as clinically appropriate.
