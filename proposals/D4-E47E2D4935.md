## Decision and unresolved question

The decision analogue is a risk-triage review after a participant has a current biomarker panel: does an earlier panel change the estimated near-term risk enough to alter a defensible triage classification, beyond the current panel and the interval between assessments?

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In adults with valid paired biomarker panels and a semantically verified future recorded-event endpoint, adding the prior panel to current biomarkers, age, sex, calendar year and assessment interval improves held-out calibration and triage utility, especially among participants in the current-only model’s middle-risk band. The improvement must persist after repeat-selection and representation controls.

This is a prognostic information hypothesis. It is not a claim that prior testing causes better outcomes, that biomarkers mediate disease, that an optimal repeat interval exists, or that a clinical action should be taken.

The strongest evidence already available is narrower: the snapshot contains repeated-position columns for 16 numeric biomarker fields, assessment-position fields, and paired code/date arrays. That establishes computability of a bounded audit, not biomarker identity, specimen timing, disease onset, complete follow-up, or clinical benefit. The unresolved claim is whether prior values add decision-relevant information once current values and repeat spacing are separated. That is the substantive advance over a generic “change predicts disease” analysis: it distinguishes prior-value information from selection/spacing information and tests whether the gain is useful in the part of the risk distribution where triage uncertainty is greatest.

## Population and temporal boundary

The population is adults with valid age/sex, numeric and dictionary-valid values for all 16 selected fields at both positions 0 and 1, and valid assessment values (T0=`53-0.0`, T1=`53-1.0`). Restrict to (0.25 < T1-T0 <= 5) years only after an external/approved dictionary establishes that field 53 is an assessment date. The primary estimand is conditional on this paired-panel cohort; it is not transported to people without a repeat panel.

Exclude a participant from the primary future endpoint only for a verified qualifying target record on or before T1; never label the remainder disease-free. Follow-up is strictly after T1 and ends at T1+5 years. A same-day death takes precedence only if the death field semantics pass. Use same-position code/date pairs: do not sort codes and dates independently.

The primary outcome is a three-category recorded outcome: earliest qualifying target record after T1 and by T1+5, death before or at that boundary, or no qualifying record. A target requires a prespecified code mapping and at least two distinct verified clinical domains among the intended metabolic/renal/cardiovascular domains. If code semantics, code-date pairing, or timing cannot be verified, the solver must not invent labels. It must report a reproducible fail-closed audit. “No qualifying record” is neither disease-free nor complete follow-up.

If individual observation ends and censoring semantics are verified, a separately labelled sensitivity may estimate five-year competing-risk cumulative incidence. It is never the primary mode merely because it is more familiar. Without that evidence, report recorded categories only and avoid incidence, onset, survival, or complete-follow-up language.

## Models and estimands

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold split, published as an `eid`-hash manifest and shared by every model, selection model and uncertainty calculation. All imputation, scaling, penalty/tuning and repeat-propensity fitting are training-fold-only.

- **B0, transparent baseline:** current position-1 biomarker panel + age + sex + current assessment year. No prior panel and no interval.
- **I1, interpretable model:** B0 + (L=T1-T0), followed by B0 + L + the eight position-0 biomarker values. Use a regularized additive multinomial probability model with named coefficients and predeclared missingness handling. The nested B0 versus B0+L contrast isolates interval/spacing information; the I1 versus B0+L contrast is the primary prior-history contrast.
- **I1-change sensitivity:** current panel + prior panel expressed as eight raw differences (x_1-x_0) + L. Do not annualize unless specimen timing and units justify it.
- **N2, substantive nonlinear alternative:** gradient-boosted multinomial probabilities using exactly the I1 inputs, participants, outcome, folds and held-out metrics, with only prespecified current-by-prior discordance, current-by-L, prior-by-L and threshold terms. It is intended to reveal clinically meaningful thresholds or discordance patterns that additively constrained I1 cannot represent, not to win a leaderboard.

For each held-out participant retain `eid`, fold, T0, T1, L, outcome, B0/B0+L/I1/I1-change/N2 predictions, and the B0-derived current-risk band. Define lower/middle/upper bands by training-fold B0 predicted-risk 20th and 80th percentiles; never define bands using I1 or outcome.

The primary estimand is the paired held-out Brier contrast:
`Delta_history = BS(I1)-BS(B0+L)`,
overall and within the B0 middle-risk band. A secondary decomposition reports `BS(B0+L)-BS(B0)`, so any apparent gain cannot be attributed to prior values if it is actually spacing information. Report calibration intercept/slope and plots, category counts, discrimination as secondary, and paired participant bootstrap intervals with folds preserved.

Decision relevance is a predeclared secondary, not a treatment claim. At a threshold grid supplied before outcome inspection (for example 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10%, 15%), report threshold-crossing counts from B0+L to I1, directional crossing counts, observed event fraction in crossed groups, and standard decision-curve net benefit with uncertainty. These values are exploratory unless a clinician supplies a target-risk threshold and an action/utility matrix. A prediction improvement alone cannot justify a repeat-testing recommendation.

## Selection audit and falsification

Among all population-anchor participants, create the flow to T0-complete and then observed valid T1. Fit a fold-local repeat-observation model using only pre-T1 information: T0 panel, age, sex, T0 calendar year and any additionally dictionary-verified covariates. Report repeat rates, standardized differences, overlap/positivity, and an inverse-probability-of-repeat weighted sensitivity. This is a transport/generalizability sensitivity, not removal of unmeasured selection or a causal effect of assessment.

Required falsification controls are:

- permute outcome labels within fold and repeat-selection strata; performance must collapse toward null;
- permute prior panels or change vectors within current-risk band, interval regime and missingness/selection strata while preserving current values;
- deliberately swap code/date positions and break code/date pairing; the integrity audit must detect this and the substantive result must not be accepted;
- run a pre-T1 negative-control endpoint and a post-boundary exclusion/leakage scan over every exposure field;
- compare raw prior, raw change and annualized-change representations;
- compare complete-case results with prespecified fold-local missingness handling;
- report interval-regime, selection-weight, threshold-grid and fold-stability sensitivity;
- retain a prediction for every eligible participant and audit duplicate `eid` and cross-fold membership.

Supportive evidence requires all semantic gates to pass, at least 100 qualifying target events overall, adequate cells (at least 200 participants and 30 events for any claimed subgroup), no integrity failure, a negative primary upper confidence bound for `Delta_history`, and a consistent middle-band direction under selection weighting and representation sensitivity. Stable N2 discordance/threshold structure may strengthen interpretation but is not required.

Adverse evidence is no improvement or worsening of I1 over B0+L, a gain explained by B0+L rather than prior values, reversal across interval regimes, loss under selection weighting or integrity controls, or worse calibration despite a small score gain. Inconclusive evidence includes failed semantic/timing gates, fewer than 100 events, sparse or nonpositive cells, unstable folds, incompatible code/date definitions, or materially different conclusions under missingness/endpoint sensitivities. A fail-closed audit is a scientifically useful result about data insufficiency, not support for the hypothesis.

## Alternatives, selection and resources

The simple nested model is selected as the primary scientific comparison because it makes the estimand auditable: current status, then interval, then prior history. The interpretable additive model reveals direction and calibration, while N2 can expose nonlinear thresholds and current/prior discordance that the additive model loses. N2 is deferred from primary inference if tuning is unstable, calibration is worse, or its gain is small and uncalibrated.

A mechanistic organ-score alternative is not scientifically useful here: local field labels, units, assay quality, specimen dates and verified organ assignments are absent, so it would manufacture biology. A Delphi-like transformer is deferred because the export is not a validated dated disease sequence; a more complex sequence model would not answer the interval-versus-prior question. A genetics interaction is deferred because no verified PRS/sequence input is available. Revisit these branches only with the missing dependency and a new estimand.
