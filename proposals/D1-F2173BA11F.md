# Branch A provisional HCC proposal

## Scientific opening and claim boundary

The clinically consequential question is whether the trajectory of postoperative liver function and synthetic reserve after hepatectomy contains information about subsequent HCC recurrence that is not captured by baseline tumor burden and the immediate postoperative state. The leading explanation is ordered recovery failure: persistent or worsening synthetic impairment after resection may mark a vulnerable remnant and/or ongoing tumor biology. A materially different rival is surveillance/selection artifact: postoperative labs and imaging are more often obtained for symptomatic or high-risk patients, so trajectory may proxy care intensity. A third rival is residual tumor severity: preoperative stage, size, vascular invasion, and pathology may explain both trajectory and recurrence.

The available evidence currently supports only that the HCC snapshot contains longitudinal encounter dates, procedures, diagnoses, labs, examination reports, medications, and pathology text linked by patient/encounter identifiers. It does not establish the hypothesis. The unresolved claim is:

Among patients with HCC who undergo hepatic resection and have at least one postoperative surveillance encounter, a prespecified postoperative liver-function trajectory (especially persistent/worsening synthetic reserve) predicts radiology- or diagnosis-documented HCC recurrence within 2 years beyond baseline tumor/pathology and immediate postoperative status.

This is an association/risk-stratification claim, not a causal or mechanistic claim.

## Population and temporal design

Index population: HCC patients with a hepatic resection procedure, an HCC diagnosis in the linked encounter or a prespecified adjudication rule, and at least one postoperative encounter. Select the first qualifying resection per patient. Require at least one postoperative lab in days 0–30 for the primary analysis; retain complete-case and missingness sensitivity analyses.

Index time is resection 开始时间. Primary prediction window: postoperative days 0–30, secondary days 31–90. Follow-up for recurrence: days 0–730, primary horizon 2 years. Recurrence is a composite endpoint defined before fitting: new HCC recurrence diagnosis in diagnoses or recurrence-compatible examination report in examinations after index date, with manual adjudication for ambiguous reports. It is not a medication or encounter count.

Primary estimand: 2-year risk difference and risk ratio for recurrence between worsening/persistent impairment and recovering trajectories, adjusted for baseline age, sex, resection type, preoperative tumor/pathology features, preoperative labs, and immediate postoperative state. Freeze trajectory classification before outcome inspection. Secondary estimand: incremental predictive value over baseline, using time-dependent patient-level cross-validation and calibration.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative

Simple baseline: prespecified landmark logistic/Cox model using only baseline and immediate postoperative information: age, sex, resection type, preoperative HCC stage/pathology indicators, preoperative and day 0–7 labs, and immediate postoperative complications if documented. It tests whether the usual clinical snapshot is adequate and is auditable.

Substantive alternative: a time-varying trajectory model on the same patients and endpoint. For each prespecified assay, fit a robust longitudinal representation over days 0–90 (low-dimensional functional/latent trajectory or carefully regularized sequence model), then combine slope, nadir, persistence, and uncertainty with baseline covariates. The target is the same 2-year recurrence endpoint. The alternative can reveal direction and persistence of recovery, distinct recovery patterns, and whether sparse-measurement uncertainty changes risk; the baseline loses ordering, persistence, and patient-specific trajectory shape.

The alternative is scientifically useful only if it identifies a reproducible, interpretable trajectory pattern and improves held-out discrimination/calibration or decision-curve performance beyond baseline. A neural sequence model is optional; a low-dimensional functional model is preferable if lab density is sparse. Future solver: patient-level splits, no outcome leakage, nested tuning, bootstrap/repeated cross-validation uncertainty, and calibration plots. CPU is sufficient for cohort construction and baseline; one GPU is optional for a bounded sequence model after a measured feasibility probe. Discovery diagnostics are separate from the future solver envelope.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: trajectory group/features remain associated with recurrence after baseline adjustment, are directionally consistent in held-out patients, improve calibration/discrimination, and are not confined to patients with more surveillance. This supports risk stratification and prospective validation, not causality or mechanism.

Adverse: no reproducible incremental signal, or signal disappears after accounting for surveillance intensity, baseline stage, and immediate postoperative state. This argues against trajectory as a useful discriminator and redirects attention to baseline severity or measurement practice.

Inconclusive: too few resection patients/events, missing units/assay coverage, ambiguous recurrence labels, or wide uncertainty. This does not refute the hypothesis and requires better adjudication, more complete longitudinal data, or a larger cohort.

Stronger claims require validated recurrence adjudication, reliable tumor size/vascular invasion/pathology details, treatment intent/response, imaging modality/timing, and external validation. Clinical experts must adjudicate ambiguous resection and recurrence labels. No treatment recommendation follows from this observational experiment.
