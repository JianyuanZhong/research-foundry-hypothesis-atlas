# Does age modify the decision value of a prior UKB biomarker panel?

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`  
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`  
Catalog: `[internal dataset path]`, [source checksum]  
Status: frozen research proposal; no model has been fitted and no clinical conclusion is claimed.

## Question, evidence boundary and advance

After a person has a valid current biomarker panel and an earlier paired panel, does the incremental predictive information in the earlier panel differ by verified age at recruitment? This addresses a consequential triage question: should the same “retain the prior panel” interpretation be applied across ages, or is historical information useful only in a clinically identifiable vulnerability stratum?

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Conditional on a valid paired panel, current panel, sex, calendar year and verified assessment interval, the prior panel improves prospective prediction of a later qualifying recorded event more in adults aged 65 or older than in adults younger than 55, with the difference visible in calibration or predeclared triage summaries and stable under selection and representation controls.

The primary heterogeneity estimand is

`H_age = [BS(I1) - BS(B0+L)]_{age >= 65}
         - [BS(I1) - BS(B0+L)]_{age < 55}`.

The Brier score is for the parent’s three-category outcome. A negative `H_age` means a larger history-related reduction in prediction error among older participants. The 55–64 stratum is an independently reported middle stratum, not a post hoc bridge. The parent’s pooled `Delta_history = BS(I1)-BS(B0+L)` remains a secondary reference so that an interaction cannot be presented as an overall improvement.

This is a prognostic information and effect-modification claim. It does not estimate the causal effect of ordering a repeat panel, an age-specific treatment effect, an optimal repeat interval, a biological mechanism, disease onset, or a clinical policy. The strongest available evidence supports only that field `21022-0.0` has locally verified age-in-years metadata and that the export contains repeated-position panel, assessment, and paired outcome fields. It does not establish biomarker identities, units, specimen dates, disease status, complete follow-up, or clinical benefit. The substantive advance over the mature parent is a new decision-relevant estimand: whether the parent’s history-information claim is transportable across a catalog-verified age dimension, rather than assuming one pooled contrast applies equally to all participants.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use the parent’s population anchor and paired-panel definition without changing its selection:

1. Start with unique `eid` in `population`, requiring a parseable age in years and valid sex under the approved field metadata. Retain age as a continuous year value and predeclare strata `<55`, `55–64`, and `>=65`. Do not silently exclude the observed ages 37–39 or >70; they remain in the continuous and pooled estimands and are reported in the appropriate tail stratum.
2. Require numeric, dictionary-valid values for all eight selected panel fields at both positions 0 and 1. Field IDs and positions are kept literal; no field is called creatinine, cystatin C, glucose, lipids, or another analyte without a field-level dictionary.
3. Require `53-0.0` and `53-1.0` and use them as T0 and T1 only after the parent’s assessment/date semantic gate passes. The instance suffix is not itself elapsed time. Require `0.25 < T1-T0 <= 5` years after valid date parsing.
4. Use only participants with a valid current assessment landmark T1. The primary estimand is conditional on being observed with a valid paired panel; it is not transported to people without a repeat panel.
5. Exclude from the primary future endpoint only participants with a verified qualifying target record on or before T1. Do not call everyone else disease-free.
6. Follow strictly after T1 and through T1+5 years. The parent’s three-category endpoint is: earliest qualifying recorded target after T1 and by the horizon; death before or at the horizon; or no qualifying recorded event. A target requires the parent’s predeclared mapping and at least two distinct verified clinical domains. Same-position code/date pairing is mandatory; codes and dates must never be sorted independently.
7. If death and target occur on the same verified date, apply the parent’s predeclared death precedence. If individual observation ends and censoring semantics are verified, a separately labelled competing-risk sensitivity may be added. Without that evidence, retain recorded categories and do not use incidence, survival, or complete-follow-up language.

The solver must fail closed if the age metadata hash/unit, sex coding, assessment timing, panel validity, outcome code/date semantics, or endpoint completeness prerequisite fails. A fail-closed result must identify the exact failed gate and counts available before it.

## Models and age-specific estimands

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold split based on a published `eid)-hash manifest. Every model, selection audit, imputation, tuning operation, bootstrap and permutation uses the same participant membership. All preprocessing and tuning is training-fold-only.

### Transparent nested baseline

- **B0:** current position-1 panel, age `21022-0.0`, sex `31-0.0`, and current assessment calendar year. This is the current-only baseline.
- **B0+L:** B0 plus `L=T1-T0). The interval is added only after timing semantics pass. This isolates repeat spacing from prior-value information.
- **I1:** B0+L plus the eight position-0 panel fields, fit as a regularized additive multinomial probability model with named coefficients and training-fold-only imputation/scaling.
- **A1 age-interaction sensitivity:** I1 plus centered age in years per decade interacted with each prespecified prior-minus-current change. Use a regularized additive multinomial model with shrinkage. A1 is the interpretable pooled test of age modification; it must not create post hoc age cutoffs or choose interactions from held-out outcomes.

The primary age-specific contrast uses I1 versus B0+L within each fixed age stratum. Define `Delta_history(a)=BS(I1|a)-BS(B0+L|a)`. Estimate `H_age` as the older-minus-younger difference above. Report the middle stratum, overall pooled contrast, and a continuous age-by-history interaction sensitivity. The age strata are fixed before outcome inspection; if a stratum has fewer than 200 eligible participants or 30 qualifying events, do not make a subgroup claim and report it as sparse.

For each held-out participant retain `eid`, fold, age, age stratum, sex, T0, T1, L, endpoint category, B0/B0+L/I1/A1 probabilities, and the B0-derived current-risk band. Define lower/middle/upper current-risk bands by training-fold B0 predicted-risk 20th/80th percentiles, not by I1, age stratum outcome rates, or full-data outcomes. Report stratum-specific calibration intercept/slope and plots, multiclass Brier contrasts, and discrimination only as secondary. Use paired participant bootstrap intervals with folds preserved. The primary uncertainty interval for `H_age` must preserve within-participant pairing and the same model predictions; a stratified bootstrap may be reported only as a labelled sensitivity.

### Substantive learned alternative

**M2 age-adaptive nonlinear model** is a gradient-boosted multinomial probability model trained on exactly the I1 input columns, participants, outcome, and folds. It may learn nonlinear age-by-history, age-by-current-level, and age-by-interval structure. It must use a prespecified small tuning grid and training-fold-only early stopping or calibration. It is evaluated with exactly the same held-out age-specific Brier, calibration, current-risk-band threshold crossing and decision-curve summaries as I1/A1.

M2 answers a substantive uncertainty that A1 may miss: whether history information turns on at a nonlinear age or current/prior discordance boundary rather than changing smoothly per decade. It is not selected because it is more complex or because GPUs exist. M2 cannot replace the transparent age-specific estimand. If M2 is unstable, poorly calibrated, materially reverses age-stratum conclusions, or improves only uncalibrated discrimination, the A1/I1 result is retained and M2 is reported as adverse or deferred.

A simple baseline loses nonlinear age-by-history thresholds but exposes the estimand, direction, calibration and age interaction. M2 can reveal clinically relevant discordance regions that an additive model averages away. A mechanistic organ-score model is not scientifically useful in this snapshot: field identities, units, specimen dates, assay quality and organ assignments are not locally verified. A transformer-like disease sequence is deferred because the catalog declares no temporal columns and the paired positions are not a validated dated disease history. The exact alternative rationale and resource record are in `work/ukb-age-modifier-alternatives.md`.

## Decision relevance

Decision summaries are secondary and are not treatment claims. Before inspecting outcomes, predeclare a risk-threshold grid such as 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10% and 15%, and retain the same grid in every age stratum. Report:

- counts moving across each threshold from B0+L to I1, direction of movement, and the held-out event fraction in each crossed group;
- net benefit curves for B0+L and I1, with paired uncertainty, within each age stratum and the B0 middle-risk band;
- calibration intercept/slope and observed-to-predicted summaries by age stratum;
- `H_age` and its uncertainty, not only an overall score gain.

These are decision-analytic summaries of recorded outcomes. A clinician-specified action threshold and utility matrix are required before translating them into a recommendation about review or repeat testing.

## Selection audit and falsification

Among the full population anchor, create a flow through valid age/sex, T0-complete, observed-valid T1, and final paired-panel cohort. Fit the repeat-observation model fold-locally using only pre-T1 information: T0 panel values, age, sex, T0 calendar year, and any additional covariate whose dictionary semantics are separately verified. Report repeat rates, standardized differences, overlap/positivity, and an inverse-probability-of-repeat weighted sensitivity. This addresses selection/generalizability, not unmeasured confounding or a causal testing effect. Report repeat-selection rates by age stratum and whether the age heterogeneity estimate changes under weighting.

Required falsification and integrity controls are:

- permute endpoint labels within fold and repeat-selection strata; both pooled history contrasts and `H_age` should collapse toward null;
- permute prior panels or change vectors within current-risk band, age stratum, interval regime and missingness/selection strata while preserving current values;
- deliberately swap code/date positions and break code/date pairing; the integrity audit must detect the corruption and no substantive result is accepted;
- run a pre-T1 negative-control endpoint and a post-boundary exposure/leakage scan over every input field;
- compare raw prior, raw change, and annualized-change representations only where timing and units justify them;
- compare complete-case and prespecified fold-local missingness handling;
- report interval-regime, selection-weight, threshold-grid, age-stratum, continuous-age and fold-stability sensitivities;
- retain one prediction per eligible `eid`, audit duplicate `eid), overlap disagreement and cross-fold membership;
- test whether any apparent `H_age` is explained by different base event rates or calibration alone by reporting age-standardized calibration and the paired Brier decomposition.

The age modifier is not supported by a significant interaction alone. Supportive evidence requires all semantic/timing gates to pass, at least 100 qualifying target events overall, at least 200 participants and 30 events in each age stratum used for a claim, no integrity failure, a negative upper confidence bound for `Delta_history` in the claimed stratum, and a directionally consistent `H_age` under weighting, representation and fold sensitivities. For M2, stable age-dependent calibration/threshold or discordance structure is stronger support than a small uncalibrated score change.

Adverse evidence is no improvement in either age stratum, improvement confined to B0+L spacing rather than prior values, a reversal under selection weighting or integrity controls, no meaningful age heterogeneity, worse calibration despite a score gain, or a learned age pattern that fails held-out stability. This would support using one pooled current/history interpretation or focusing on assessment timing rather than age tailoring.

Inconclusive evidence includes a failed age metadata/coding gate, failed assessment or code/date timing gate, fewer than 100 target events, sparse age strata, nonpositive repeat-selection overlap, unstable folds, endpoint ambiguity, or materially different age conclusions under missingness and endpoint sensitivities. Fail closed; do not convert an inconclusive result into evidence against the hypothesis.

## Resources and interpretation limits

The proposed solver is CPU-first tabular work. The inherited planning envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM and 28,800 seconds. This should cover selected-column parsing, joins, five-fold fits, paired bootstrap, selection weighting, permutations and the small M2 tuning grid, but the estimate is unverified until measured. A GPU is neither required nor prohibited; an allocated benchmark would be needed before using one for M2. No proposer or solver weight training is authorized.

Computationally checkable claims are source/schema/hash integrity, row and join audits, age-stratum counts, paired-panel selection, model fitting, held-out predictions, Brier/calibration contrasts, `H_age`, threshold arithmetic, bootstrap intervals, selection-weight sensitivity and permutation behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for biomarker identity and units, specimen-level timing, the meaning of health outcome codes, disease onset, complete ascertainment, validated action thresholds/utilities, causal effects of testing, treatment benefit, optimal scheduling, mechanism, external transportability and prospective policy.

A supportive result would justify clinical adjudication of the endpoint and a prospective study of age-tailored monitoring or review. An adverse result would argue against age tailoring for this prognostic information task, while preserving the possibility that current levels or interval information matter. An inconclusive result identifies the missing evidence. None is a clinical recommendation or proof of benefit.
