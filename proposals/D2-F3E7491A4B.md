# Evolution: MerTK-inhibitor program selectivity in E-MTAB-8873

## 1. Scientific question and primary estimand

**Unresolved question.** In the three matched MerTK/CD206-positive RA synovial coculture units, does pre-treating macrophages with a MerTK inhibitor reduce the harmful fibroblast inflammatory/destructive program more than it reduces the fibroblast matrix/TGF-beta repair program or the macrophage repair-linked identity program, and is any apparent harmful-program reduction distinguishable from a global panel-wide suppression effect?

This remains a **perturbation-response selectivity** question. It does not test MerTK target causality, fibroblast-intrinsic mechanism, patient benefit, or clinical safety.

### Primary program-score estimand

For each matched unit \(i\) and feature \(f\), define the paired log2 change

\[
d_{if}=\log_2(y^{I}_{if}+1)-\log_2(y^{U}_{if}+1),
\]

where \(I\) is the MerTK-inhibitor sample and \(U\) is the matched untreated sample.

Let \(H\) be the 10 harmful fibroblast inflammatory/destructive features, \(R\) the 9 fibroblast matrix/TGF-beta repair features, and \(M\) the 9 macrophage repair-linked identity features. Define the raw program scores

\[
h_i=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{f\in H} d_{if},\quad
r_i=\frac{1}{|R|}\sum_{f\in R} d_{if},\quad
m_i=\frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{f\in M} d_{if}.
\]

Let \(B\) denote the 417 panel features outside these 28 program features. The **panel-wide background shift** is

\[
g_i=\operatorname{median}_{f\in B} d_{if},
\]

and the **panel-wide perturbation magnitude** is

\[
q_i=\operatorname{median}_{f\in B}|d_{if}|.
\]

The primary estimand is the median panel-normalized harmful-program change across the three matched units:

\[
\theta_H=\operatorname{median}_{i\in\{R1,R2,R5\}}\left(h_i-g_i\right).
\]

Interpretation:

- \(\theta_H<0\): the harmful program falls more than the typical non-program panel feature.
- \(\theta_H\approx 0\): the harmful program moves with the panel-wide background, so selectivity is not established.
- \(\theta_H>0\): the harmful program is not reduced relative to background.

The same normalization defines \(\theta_R=\operatorname{median}(r_i-g_i)\) and \(\theta_M=\operatorname{median}(m_i-g_i)\), but \(\theta_H\) is the single primary estimand. The repair and macrophage scores are prespecified co-primary interpretive outcomes, not independent confirmatory tests.

This normalization is the **global-suppression control**. If MerTK inhibition broadly suppresses expression, the background median \(g_i\) becomes negative and \(h_i-g_i\) approaches zero; a harmful-program reduction then cannot be mistaken for selectivity. If the inhibitor selectively reduces the harmful program while leaving the background and protective programs comparatively intact, \(h_i-g_i\) becomes negative while \(r_i-g_i\) and \(m_i-g_i\) remain near zero or positive. The magnitude \(q_i\) is reported as the typical scale of panel perturbation and is used in the inconclusive rule below.

## 4. Baseline analysis

1. Read the prepared expression matrix and design JSON.
2. For each sample, retain raw molecular counts and join by `sample_id`.
3. For each of the three matched MerTK/CD206-positive units, compute \(d_{if}\) for all 445 features.
4. Compute \(h_i\), \(r_i\), \(m_i\), \(g_i\), and \(q_i\).
5. Compute the primary normalized harmful-program contrast \(h_i-g_i\) for each unit and their median \(\theta_H\).
6. Obtain an exact restricted sign-flip null distribution for \(\theta_H\) by flipping the signs of the three unit-level contrasts. There are \(2^3=8\) assignments.
7. Report:
   - the three unit-level \(h_i-g_i\) values;
   - median \(\theta_H\);
   - exact two-sided sign-flip p-value;
   - exact sign-flip interval, using the most conservative 87.5% interval because only eight permutations are possible;
   - unit-level \(r_i-g_i\) and \(m_i-g_i\);
   - \(g_i\) and \(q_i\);
   - per-gene \(d_{if}\) for all 28 program features, labeled exploratory.
8. Repeat the same calculation for the single Mneg unit as an exploratory specificity check.
9. Sensitivity analyses:
   - use all 445 features rather than the 417 background features for \(g_i\) and \(q_i\);
   - use a rank-based program score (median of feature ranks within each unit);
   - repeat without zero-heavy `MMP13`;
   - leave-one-unit-out analysis to identify whether a single unit drives the result.

The exact sign-flip test is conditional on the matched run and donor pairs and does not treat the six samples or three donors as independent ordinary t-test observations. Because donor `SA266A` crosses cell type across R2 and R5, the test remains a paired within-unit contrast rather than a donor-cluster inference.

## 5. Run/donor-aware uncertainty

### Primary uncertainty

Use the exact sign-flip distribution over the three paired unit contrasts. This is the smallest-sample valid inference available and preserves the matched design. With three units, the minimum possible two-sided p-value is 0.25; therefore no conventional 0.05 significance claim is possible. The exact interval should be reported as an 87.5% interval, not misrepresented as a 95% interval.

### Donor/run-aware sensitivity

Fit a small hierarchical Bayesian sensitivity model to the three unit-level contrasts:

\[
c_i = h_i-g_i,\quad
c_i \sim \mathrm{Normal}(\mu + \alpha_{\mathrm{run}(i)} + \beta_{\mathrm{FLS}(i)} + \gamma_{\mathrm{mac}(i)},\sigma^2).
\]

Use weakly informative priors on \(\mu\), the run, FLS-donor, and macrophage-donor effects, and on \(\sigma\). Because the same donor `SA266A` appears as a macrophage donor in R2 and an FLS donor in R5, include donor identity as a shared random effect rather than assuming three independent donor clusters. Report the posterior median and 89% credible interval for \(\mu\), and label this model as a sensitivity analysis only.

This model is not a confirmatory test. With three observations, it cannot reliably separate run, FLS-donor, macrophage-donor, and residual variance. Its purpose is to show whether the direction and magnitude of the primary paired contrast are stable or disappear under run/donor-aware partial pooling.

## 6. Simple baseline versus learned/mechanistic alternative

### Simple baseline

- **Inputs:** the 33-sample by 445-feature prepared expression matrix and the matched sample pairs listed above.
- **Target:** estimate \(\theta_H=\operatorname{median}(h_i-g_i)\) and the corresponding repair and macrophage contrasts.
- **Split/resampling:** no training/test split; exact sign-flip over the three matched units.
- **Uncertainty:** exact 87.5% sign-flip interval and two-sided p-value.
- **Outcome evaluation:** direction and magnitude of \(\theta_H\), plus whether \(\theta_R\) and \(\theta_M\) remain non-negative.
- **CPU budget:** under 1 minute on 1 CPU core and less than 1 GB RAM.
- **Selection reason:** with three matched units, this is the most defensible primary analysis because it makes no unstable feature-selection or hyperparameter assumptions and directly tests the prespecified program-level question.

### Learned/mechanistic alternative

A more substantive alternative is a **hierarchical program-response model** that jointly estimates harmful, repair, and macrophage program shifts while accounting for run and donor structure.

- **Inputs:** the same 445-feature matrix and matched pairs.
- **Fitting target:** jointly model the three normalized program contrasts \(h_i-g_i\), \(r_i-g_i\), and \(m_i-g_i\) as correlated outcomes, with run and donor random effects and a program-level covariance matrix.
- **Split/resampling:** no held-out split is possible with three units; use Bayesian posterior simulation or parametric bootstrap over runs/donors as a sensitivity analysis.
- **Uncertainty:** report posterior medians and 89% credible intervals for program contrasts and their differences, especially \((h_i-g_i)-(m_i-g_i)\) and \((h_i-g_i)-(r_i-g_i)\).
- **Outcome evaluation:** whether the harmful-program reduction exceeds the repair and macrophage reductions after background normalization, and whether program-level covariance suggests coordinated versus isolated effects.
- **CPU budget:** under 5 minutes on 1–2 CPU cores and 2 GB RAM.
- **Selection/deferral reason:** this alternative could reveal whether the three programs move together or separately, which the simple median score cannot distinguish. However, with only three units it cannot support stable variance estimation or honest held-out validation. It is therefore retained as a secondary mechanistic sensitivity analysis, not the primary confirmatory test.

A supervised neural model or sparse feature-selection model is deferred because three matched units cannot support stable training, tuning, or honest held-out evaluation. GPU use is unnecessary.

## 7. Negative-state exploratory check

Compute the same normalized program contrasts for the single R1 MerTK/CD206-negative pair.

Interpretation:

- If the harmful-program reduction is similar or larger in the negative state, the effect is not specific to the MerTK/CD206-positive context.
- If the reduction is absent or reversed in the negative state, that is consistent with positive-state specificity but cannot establish it from one unit.
- If the negative-state result is discordant with the positive-state result, it should be reported as exploratory and not used to rescue an otherwise inconclusive primary result.

## 8. Decision rules

### Supportive

A supportive result requires all of the following:

1. \(\theta_H<0\), and all three unit-level \(h_i-g_i\) values are negative.
2. The exact sign-flip p-value is reported, recognizing that 0.25 is the minimum possible with three units.
3. The median repair and macrophage contrasts are not similarly negative; preferably \(\theta_R\ge 0\) and \(\theta_M\ge 0\), or at least both are materially less negative than \(\theta_H\).
4. The median absolute background perturbation \(q_i\) does not dominate the harmful-program effect; specifically, \(|\theta_H|\) should exceed the median \(q_i\) or be comparable to it.
5. Sensitivity analyses do not reverse the direction of \(\theta_H\).

A supportive result supports only a bounded claim: **in these three matched ex vivo RA cocultures, MerTK-inhibitor pretreatment appears to reduce the harmful fibroblast program relative to panel-wide background more than it reduces repair-linked programs.**

### Adverse

An adverse result occurs if any of the following hold:

1. \(\theta_H\ge 0\), indicating no harmful-program reduction relative to background.
2. \(\theta_H<0\) but \(\theta_R\) and \(\theta_M\) are comparably or more negative, indicating broad suppression of both harmful and repair-linked programs.
3. The background median \(g_i\) is strongly negative across units and the harmful program does not fall beyond that background shift.
4. The harmful-program reduction is driven entirely by one unit and reverses under leave-one-unit-out or hierarchical sensitivity analysis.

An adverse result challenges the selectivity hypothesis in this assay and would argue against pursuing this specific MerTK-blockade timing/context without a more selective or better-timed intervention.

### Inconclusive

An inconclusive result occurs if:

1. Unit-level \(h_i-g_i\) values are discordant in sign.
2. The magnitude of \(\theta_H\) is smaller than the typical background perturbation \(q_i\).
3. The exact sign-flip interval is wide and crosses zero.
4. Sensitivity analyses using all 445 features, rank-based scores, or removal of `MMP13` reverse the direction.
5. The hierarchical run/donor-aware model yields a credible interval that spans zero or reverses direction.
6. The single negative-state check is strongly discordant with the positive-state result.

An inconclusive result should be reported as uncertainty, not as evidence of no effect or therapeutic failure.

## 9. Claims requiring clinical adjudication or another study

The following are not computable from E-MTAB-8873 and require additional evidence:

- MerTK target causality, because the inhibitor may have off-target effects.
- Fibroblast-intrinsic mechanism, because the assay is pseudobulk coculture.
- Patient-level heterogeneity or clinical benefit.
- Safety or therapeutic efficacy.
- Generalization beyond these three female RA synovial coculture units.
- Cell-type-specific transcript origin.

Stronger conclusions would require:

- genetic MerTK perturbation or rescue;
- vehicle-controlled replicate experiments;
- cell-type-resolved transcriptomics or spatial profiling;
- additional independent donor-matched units;
- prospective expert clinical and wet-lab adjudication.
