# Nighttime ICU Discharge and Residual Instability: A Falsifiable eICU Experiment

## 1. Scientific Opening and Unresolved Question

ICU discharge timing is a consequential clinical decision with direct implications for patient safety. Patients discharged during nighttime hours (22:00–06:00) may face reduced staffing, fewer available specialists, and less supervisory oversight compared to daytime discharges. The unresolved question is whether nighttime discharge is associated with excess risk of adverse outcomes (ICU readmission, hospital mortality) and whether this excess risk is concentrated in patients with residual instability at the time of discharge.

**Leading claim:** Nighttime discharge is associated with higher 48-hour ICU readmission rates, and this association is stronger among patients with residual instability (ongoing vasopressor requirement, abnormal vital signs, or incomplete recovery of organ function) at discharge.

**Strongest rival:** The association is driven by confounding by indication — sicker patients are more likely to be discharged at night because they deteriorate later in the day, and the apparent excess risk is explained by baseline severity rather than discharge timing per se.

**What answering would change:** If nighttime discharge with residual instability is confirmed as a risk factor for readmission, this would support development of targeted discharge readiness criteria that account for time-of-day, inform staffing decisions (e.g., requiring senior physician sign-off for nighttime discharges of unstable patients), and identify a modifiable process factor that could be addressed through operational changes. If the association is explained by confounding by indication, the focus would shift to ensuring that discharge readiness criteria adequately capture severity regardless of timing.

## 4. Experiment Design

### Population
eICU ICU stays with:
- Complete vital sign data in the 24 hours prior to ICU discharge
- ICU length of stay ≥ 24 hours (to exclude very brief stays)
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Exclusion: ICU discharge to hospice or death in ICU (for readmission analysis)

### Temporal Boundaries
- **Pre-discharge window:** 24 hours before ICU discharge (for residual instability assessment)
- **Outcome window:** 48 hours post-discharge (for ICU readmission) and 7 days (for hospital mortality)
- **Nighttime definition:** Discharge time between 22:00–06:00 (local time, derived from icudischargetime offset)
- **Daytime definition:** Discharge time between 06:00–22:00

### Variables
**Exposure:**
- Discharge time: nighttime (22:00–06:00) vs. daytime (06:00–22:00)

**Residual instability (pre-discharge window):**
- Vasopressor requirement: any vasopressor infusion in the 24 hours before discharge
- Vital sign abnormalities: HR > 120 or < 50, SBP < 90 or > 180, SpO2 < 92%, temp > 38.5°C in the 24 hours before discharge
- Lab abnormalities: WBC < 4 or > 12, platelets < 100, creatinine > 1.5, lactate > 2 in the 24 hours before discharge

**Covariates:**
- Age, sex, race (patient table)
- APACHE score (apachePatientResult)
- Primary diagnosis (diagnosis table, ICD-9/10)
- ICU length of stay (patient table)
- Hospital type (hospital table)

**Outcome:**
- Primary: 48-hour ICU readmission (binary)
- Secondary: 7-day hospital mortality (binary)

### Analysis
1. **Descriptive:** Compare nighttime vs. daytime discharge rates, residual instability prevalence, and outcomes
2. **Regression:** Logistic regression with time-of-day as exposure, adjusted for severity (APACHE, diagnosis, age, sex, ICU LOS)
3. **Interaction:** Test whether residual instability modifies the effect of nighttime discharge (interaction term: nighttime × residual instability)
4. **Sensitivity:**
   - E-value for unmeasured confounding
   - Restricted to patients with complete data
   - Propensity score matching on baseline severity
   - Subgroup analysis by hospital type

### Falsification Criteria
- **Null hypothesis supported:** If nighttime discharge shows no excess risk after adjustment for severity and residual instability, the hypothesis is not supported
- **Confounding by indication:** If the association is driven entirely by baseline severity (APACHE, diagnosis), the residual instability mechanism is not confirmed
- **Negative control:** Test whether nighttime discharge predicts outcomes in patients with NO residual instability (expected null). If a significant association persists in the stable subgroup, this suggests confounding by indication rather than a residual instability mechanism
- **Boundary condition:** If the association is present only in specific diagnosis groups (e.g., sepsis, cardiac surgery), the generalizability is limited

## 5. Method Comparison

### Simple Baseline: Logistic Regression
- **Model:** Logistic regression with pre-specified covariates (age, sex, APACHE, diagnosis, vasopressor use, vital sign summary statistics)
- **Strengths:** Interpretable coefficients, easy to validate, computationally cheap
- **Limitations:** Assumes linear relationships, cannot capture complex interactions between time-of-day and residual instability patterns
- **Resource estimate:** CPU-based, ~1 hour for cohort extraction + model fitting on ~200K ICU stays

### Learned Alternative: Gradient-Boosted Trees (XGBoost)
- **Model:** XGBoost with the same features plus temporal features (vital sign trends, lab trajectories over the 24-hour pre-discharge window)
- **Strengths:** Can capture non-linear interactions, feature importance to identify which aspects of residual instability matter most, handles missing data natively
- **Limitations:** Less interpretable, requires careful hyperparameter tuning, risk of overfitting
- **Resource estimate:** CPU-based, ~2-4 hours for cohort extraction + model fitting + hyperparameter tuning

### What the Alternative Could Reveal
The XGBoost model could reveal non-linear interactions between time-of-day and residual instability patterns that a linear model would miss. For example, the effect of nighttime discharge might be non-linear in vasopressor dose (e.g., only patients on high-dose vasopressors are at risk). Feature importance analysis could identify which aspects of residual instability (vasopressor, vital signs, labs) matter most for the nighttime discharge effect.

### Evaluation
- **Metrics:** AUC, calibration (Brier score), clinical decision curves
- **Split:** 70% training, 15% validation, 15% test (stratified by nighttime/daytime)
- **Comparison:** Both models evaluated on the same held-out test set

### Selection/Deferral Reason
The logistic regression is the primary analysis for hypothesis testing (interpretable, falsifiable). The XGBoost model is a secondary analysis to explore whether non-linear patterns exist. If the logistic regression shows a clear interaction effect, the XGBoost model can confirm and refine the finding. If the logistic regression shows no effect, the XGBoost model can be used to check whether a non-linear effect was missed.

## 6. What Would and Would Not Be Established

**Supportive results would establish:**
- An association between nighttime discharge and excess risk of ICU readmission
- That this association is stronger in patients with residual instability
- A potential target for operational intervention (discharge timing)

**Supportive results would NOT establish:**
- Causality (observational study, residual confounding possible)
- That changing discharge timing would improve outcomes (would require RCT)
- The mechanism by which nighttime discharge increases risk (staffing, oversight, patient factors)

**Adverse results would establish:**
- That nighttime discharge is not associated with excess risk after adjustment
- That residual instability does not modify the effect of discharge timing
- That the current practice of nighttime discharge is not a safety concern (in this population)

**Inconclusive results would establish:**
- That the available data cannot distinguish between the leading claim and the rival
- That further study (e.g., RCT, external validation) is needed

## 7. Limitations and Unavailable Evidence

- **No calendar dates:** eICU removes calendar dates, so nighttime is defined by offset from ICU admission, not absolute time. This is a limitation but does not prevent the analysis.
- **No staffing data:** eICU does not include nurse staffing or physician availability data, so the mechanism (reduced oversight) cannot be directly tested.
- **No post-discharge data:** eICU does not include data after hospital discharge, so 30-day readmission and long-term outcomes cannot be assessed.
- **Single dataset:** External validation would require access to other ICU datasets (e.g., MIMIC-IV).
- **Clinical adjudication:** The definition of "residual instability" is based on structured data and may not capture all clinically relevant instability (e.g., delirium, functional status).
