# Discordant AKI recovery: creatinine vs urine output and fluid status

## Question

Among ICU patients with KDIGO stage 2-3 acute kidney injury, does creatinine recovery without concurrent recovery in urine output or fluid balance identify a discordant-recovery phenotype with worse subsequent outcomes than concordant recovery?

## Why it matters

Creatinine is the standard marker for AKI recovery, but it can be affected by muscle mass, fluid shifts, and measurement timing. Urine output and fluid balance provide complementary information about renal perfusion and function. If discordant recovery (creatinine improves but urine output/fluid status does not) carries higher mortality or longer ICU stay, clinicians should not rely on creatinine alone to declare recovery. This could change how recovery is assessed and when supportive care is escalated.

## Evidence base

[K1] Haines et al. (2023) demonstrated that eGFR creatinine systematically overestimated kidney function after prolonged critical illness, with a posterior mean difference of 33 ml/min/1.73m² at ICU discharge. Cystatin C better estimated true kidney function because it was unaffected by muscle loss. This supports the biological plausibility that creatinine recovery may not reflect true renal recovery.

[K2] MIMIC-IV (Johnson et al., 2023) provides data for over 65,000 ICU patients at BIDMC, including creatinine, urine output, and fluid balance measurements. The dataset is deidentified and suitable for retrospective analysis.

[K3] KDIGO (2012) defines AKI stages based on creatinine and urine output criteria. Stage 2-3 AKI is the entry criterion for this analysis.

- Follow-up: 28-day mortality, ICU length of stay

## Competing explanations

1. True residual renal impairment: discordant recovery reflects ongoing kidney damage
2. Measurement artifact: differential measurement frequency or timing
3. Fluid management: aggressive diuresis or fluid restriction confounds the signal
4. Baseline severity: sicker patients have both AKI and poor recovery

## Falsification

- If discordant recovery is not associated with worse outcomes after adjustment for baseline severity and measurement practices, the hypothesis is adverse
- If the association is driven by differential measurement frequency, it is an artifact
- If the association disappears in a sensitivity analysis with complete-case urine output, it is inconclusive

## Method comparison

- Baseline: logistic regression with prespecified covariates (age, sex, baseline creatinine, AKI stage, fluid balance, measurement frequency)
- Learned alternative: gradient-boosted trees with the same covariates, evaluated by calibration and discrimination
- The learned model can reveal nonlinear interactions between creatinine trajectory and fluid status that the baseline misses
- Selection: use the learned model if it improves calibration without overfitting; otherwise retain the baseline
