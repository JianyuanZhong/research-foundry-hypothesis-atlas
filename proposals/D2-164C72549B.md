## Question and target

Does the intervention-associated transcript response in macrophage–fibroblast coculture depend on macrophage state, such that the direction or magnitude of response differs by context and is not explained by donor or composition alone?

Named target/pathway: the intervention-associated transcript response, interpreted as a pharmacologic signal rather than a proven direct target. The experiment tests whether the response is context-dependent and whether it can be used to prioritize a therapeutic target.

Clinical importance: if the response is context-dependent, it may reflect a true pharmacologic effect that can be used to prioritize targets; if it is an artifact of donor or composition, it should not be used for target prioritization. Resolving this changes whether the response is a reliable signal.

## Estimand and analysis

Within-donor paired contrast of intervention versus vehicle, stratified by macrophage state, with donor-aware modeling. Pre-specified direction/magnitude thresholds: a consistent context-specific contrast (e.g., opposite direction or >2-fold difference in effect size between macrophage states) would be supportive; a null or reversed contrast after donor adjustment would be adverse; a contrast that is present but not reproducible across donors would be inconclusive.

## Competing explanations

- Donor-specific baseline differences.
- Macrophage composition shifts.
- Assay batch effects.
- Indirect effects of medication on fibroblast activation rather than direct target action.

## Outcomes

- Supportive: a consistent context-specific contrast across donors, after donor adjustment, with pre-specified direction/magnitude thresholds.
- Adverse: a null or reversed contrast after donor adjustment.
- Inconclusive: a contrast that is present but not reproducible across donors, or a contrast that is confounded by composition.
