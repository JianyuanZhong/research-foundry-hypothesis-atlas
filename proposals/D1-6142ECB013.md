# Interval-specific nodule handoff and observation-aware same-system surveillance

## Question, evidence, and unresolved claim

Incidental pulmonary-nodule follow-up fails at a communication boundary: an index radiology recommendation may not be represented in the discharge plan, and an observed same-system follow-up may be absent even when outside care occurred. A retrospective CTPA cohort (Kwan et al., PMCID PMC6625441; full text read from the frozen public source) found 65 patients with explicit radiology suggestions, 35 (53.8%) without repeat imaging within the recommended window, and discharge-summary mention was associated with timely imaging. The FIND program report (Zaki-Metias et al., PMCID PMC10287591; full text read) found follow-up completion 34/67 (50.7%) after versus 16/52 (30.8%) before a structured tracking program, while explicitly describing outside-system care and inability to contact patients as reasons that an integrated record cannot classify non-follow-up. A 2024 review/interview study (Digby et al., PMCID PMC10944736; full text read) identified variable reporting, suboptimal communication, and variable guideline adherence. These sources support a care-transition problem and feasibility of tracking; they do not establish that discharge documentation causes surveillance or improves cancer outcomes.

The testable MIMIC claim is narrower: among adults with an expert-adjudicated, incidental, unconditional recommendation for chest CT at 3, 6, or 12 months, (1) a patient-specific interval-bearing discharge plan is more likely when the radiology recommendation is explicit and structurally surfaced, and (2) among patients with evidence of continued local observability, that propagated plan is associated with recommendation-timed same-system chest CT. The primary estimand is an adjusted association, not an intervention effect. The study will never call an unobserved patient nonadherent.

## Population and frozen timing rules

Unit is the first eligible recommendation admission per subject. Adults are age >=18 by `anchor_age + year(admittime)-anchor_year`, with anchor age 91 represented as 90+ rather than an exact value. Link a radiology report to an admission when `(subject_id,hadm_id)` matches and `admittime - 12 hours <= charttime <= dischtime`; the 12-hour ED allowance is fixed before analysis and a zero-hour sensitivity is required. The report must be a CT/CTA chest by `radiology_detail` `field_name`/`field_value` where available, with text fallback, and must contain an incidental pulmonary/lung nodule and an unconditional interval-specific chest-CT recommendation at exactly 3, 6, or 12 months. Exclude lung-screening examinations, recommendations conditional on risk, recommendations that no follow-up is needed, known/suspected lung cancer staging or surveillance, active hospice/palliative discharge, and patients who die before exposure ascertainment. “New/indeterminate” and incidental status require blinded dual clinical/radiology adjudication using only report text and radiology history available through the index `storetime`; no future note may define eligibility. A prior report establishing guideline-complete stability excludes the index; absence of two years of prior local reports is retained as an explicit missing-history indicator, with a >=2-year-history sensitivity analysis.

For each eligible index report, use the final discharge summary with `storetime <= dischtime + 7 days`; define exposure only from text available at that `storetime`. The follow-up clock begins at `max(dischtime, summary.storetime)`. A +24-hour storage sensitivity analysis is prespecified. Missing discharge summary is not “omission”; it is excluded from the primary propagation comparison and tabulated.

Exposure has three blinded-adjudicated levels: (A) closed-loop propagation, an affirmed patient-specific nodule plus chest CT action plus interval/date compatible with the radiology recommendation; (B) partial propagation, a patient-specific nodule/CT plan without a compatible interval; (C) omission, no patient-specific actionable nodule plan. Negation, history, family history, hypothetical text, and copied radiology text without an active plan do not count. The primary contrast is A versus C; B is descriptive and an ordinal secondary analysis. Separately, classify whether the index recommendation is under a dedicated `RECOMMENDATION(S)` heading or embedded in impression/body; this is an upstream propagation question, not a clinical-outcome intervention.

## Outcomes and observation state

The primary observed outcome is a subsequent same-system chest CT/CTA report whose `charttime` is after exposure and within ±42 days of the recommendation due date, where due date is calculated as index `charttime` plus 3, 6, or 12 calendar months (calendar-month addition, not an approximate 30-day substitution). A report is nodule-focused only when blinded adjudication finds nodule surveillance/comparison/impression language; this is a secondary outcome. Other outcomes are any chest CT by due+42 days, time to first chest CT, early imaging before the lower due-date tolerance, and descriptive escalation/stability on the first follow-up report.

Observation is not synonymous with follow-up. For each person and due window, define `observed_through_window` if any later MIMIC radiology report or later admission is recorded after due+90 days, or if death is recorded before the end of the window. The primary surveillance analysis reports (i) the observed-through-window cohort, (ii) an unknown/right-censored group with neither marker, and (iii) all-eligible inverse-probability-of-observation weighted sensitivity. The unknown group is never assigned outcome=0. Report exposure/outcome overlap explicitly: counts and proportions in each handoff arm crossed with observed versus unknown and with timely CT. A death before due-window end is a competing event, not a failed surveillance event. `dod` and in-hospital death are incomplete mortality evidence and are labeled accordingly.

## Analysis and falsification

First report adjudicated counts, annotation sensitivity/specificity/PPV with uncertainty, arm sizes, due intervals, observation markers, and missing-history patterns. Estimate unadjusted risks with exact/Wilson intervals. The adjusted association is a standardized risk difference and risk ratio for observed timely CT, using overlap-weighted logistic regression and a doubly robust outcome model only if overlap and event-count gates pass. Covariates must be known no later than summary storage: age category, sex, race/language/insurance, admission route/type, length of stay, ICU stay, transfers, discharge location, final service, interval, index CT type, nodule size/type/multiplicity/newness, prior local admission/CT counts, COPD/emphysema, smoking-code availability, malignancy history excluding active lung cancer, and severity/comorbidity. ICD features come from `diagnoses_icd` joined to `d_icd_diagnoses`; no future report or outcome enters the propensity model. Report standardized mean differences, effective sample size, propensity distributions, 0.05/0.95 trimming, and exact interval/CT-type/service-stratified estimates when overlap is poor. With <30 adjudicated subjects per primary arm, <15 timely events, severe non-overlap, or primary annotation PPV <0.90, stop at descriptive/stratified estimates and call the association inconclusive.

The heading analysis estimates recommendation-heading-to-closed-loop propagation, adjusted only for pre-report factors and report explicitness. It is a mechanism analysis and cannot be called mediation. Negative controls are mandatory: (1) chest CT before the index recommendation or before exposure, which cannot be caused by handoff; (2) non-chest imaging in the timely window, testing generic system engagement; and (3) an unrelated stable benign discharge finding without an action, testing whether documentation merely marks high-utilization patients. A positive prior-imaging control or similarly large generic-imaging association indicates residual utilization confounding, leakage, or extraction error. Robustness varies ±42 to ±90 days, uses CTPA-only and exact interval subsets, excludes all cancer codes, requires post-window local observation, uses zero versus 12-hour admission linkage, and excludes CTs occurring only during later acute admissions.

Supportive evidence requires a positive, clinically meaningful adjusted association with uncertainty excluding zero, acceptable annotation and overlap, consistency in the nodule-focused endpoint, no positive temporal control, and materially weaker generic-imaging control. It supports prioritizing a prospective tracking or stepped-wedge evaluation, not causal benefit, malignancy detection, guideline appropriateness, or survival improvement. An adverse result is a nonpositive association with adequate precision or increased premature/cascade imaging without better timely surveillance; this argues that discharge text alone is insufficient. Inconclusive results include wide intervals spanning ±10 percentage points, arm/event scarcity, poor overlap, discordant observation restrictions, or control associations. Any apparent benefit that disappears after restricting to demonstrably observed patients is interpreted as observation/engagement bias, not proof of failure.

Required expert review includes true nodule versus atelectasis/infection/granuloma, incidental versus cancer work-up, unconditional versus conditional recommendation, prior stability, interval compatibility, patient-specific versus copied discharge text, and nodule-focused versus symptom-driven follow-up. MIMIC lacks pixels, complete outside imaging, orders/referrals, patient notification, appointments, outpatient visits, patient preference, reliable pack-years, pathology/cancer stage, and complete post-discharge mortality. Automatic verification can check source hashes, joins, time ordering, frozen filters, overlap, model calculations, confidence intervals, and conclusion-to-output linkage; it cannot establish clinical appropriateness, outside-care absence, causality, or patient benefit.
