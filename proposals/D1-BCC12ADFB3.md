# Working proposal: separating early tumor-marker persistence from postoperative liver injury after HCC resection

Status: data-bound, reference-complete proposal; no outcome model has been fitted. The source audit and feasibility counts below are observed. All effect estimates and model performance are planned.

## Scientific question and deliverable

At a fixed day-60 landmark after first curative-intent HCC resection, do biomarker trajectories contain a persistent tumor-associated component—distinct from the transient liver-injury response—that identifies patients with documented residual or early recurrent disease during days 61–365?

The scientific deliverable is a newly fitted comparison of:

1. a simple, clinically legible day-60 landmark survival model; and
2. a constrained two-process longitudinal state-space/joint survival model fitted to irregular biomarker measurements from day -90 through day +60.

Completion requires: a frozen cohort flow; assay-label and missingness audit; fitted baseline and alternative on identical patient splits; held-out discrimination, calibration and uncertainty; learned injury and tumor-state trajectories with identifiability diagnostics; surveillance-adjusted sensitivity analyses; and conclusions explicitly limited to the computed outcome definition. Readiness or successful code execution is not completion.

## What is supported, what remains unresolved, and why it matters

The strongest supported claim is that postoperative AFP response is prognostic, not diagnostic. In 549 selected patients with preoperative AFP >400 ng/mL, incomplete AFP response at 4–6 weeks was associated with much higher recurrence and worse recurrence-free survival [K1]. A separate international cohort found that adding 6- and 12-month AFP to a time-varying Cox model improved C-index relative to a static preoperative model [K2]. Persistent AFP can nevertheless occur after apparently curative resection without detected recurrence; the inspected case report explicitly describes that counterexample and nonmalignant liver conditions that can elevate AFP [K3].

The unresolved claim is whether the *shape and cross-marker coherence* of the first 60 postoperative days separates two explanations:

- Leading explanation: after resection, AFP/PIVKA-II persistence or renewed rise that decouples from recovering liver-injury markers reflects occult residual or early recurrent HCC.
- Strongest rival: the same apparent “non-clearance” is generated by perioperative hepatocyte injury/regeneration, baseline cirrhosis or steatosis, assay drift, treatment selection, or more intensive testing/imaging of sicker or marker-positive patients.

The explanations differ observably. The tumor explanation predicts persistent or rising total AFP and/or PIVKA-II after ALT/AST, bilirubin and INR recover, followed by documented recurrence despite comparable surveillance opportunity. The injury explanation predicts synchronous biomarker and liver-injury excursions that resolve together, with little incremental association once injury severity and observation intensity are represented. Neither pattern alone proves mechanism: association after adjustment cannot establish occult tumor biology.

This matters because a trustworthy day-60 signal could justify earlier cross-sectional imaging or specialist review, whereas treating an injury-driven AFP excursion as cancer could cause unnecessary imaging, anxiety or intervention. The proposed advance over [K1] and [K2] is not merely a more complex predictor: it tests an earlier window, uses co-timed injury and complementary tumor markers, and explicitly models surveillance opportunity. It also directly tests the specificity problem bounded by [K3].

## Hypothesis

Among adults with definitive HCC undergoing a first curative-intent liver resection, no documented recurrence or HCC-directed retreatment through day 60, and adequate pre/postoperative measurements, the day-60 latent tumor-marker state will:

1. have a stronger association with documented recurrence-management events during days 61–365 than the latent injury state;
2. improve held-out calibration and prediction over a landmark model using the latest AFP/fold-change and peak injury markers; and
3. retain direction and useful magnitude in pathology-supported, no-prior-treatment, and surveillance-observed analyses.

This hypothesis is falsified if the injury state predicts the outcome at least as strongly, the tumor state is unstable or non-identifiable, or the alternative has no clinically meaningful held-out improvement after surveillance correction.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Primary cohort:

- age at least 18 years;
- earliest encounter with a definitive `Diagnosis Name` containing `hepatocellular carcinoma` or `primary liver cancer`, excluding uncertainty terms, plus a same-encounter curative-intent liver-resection label;
- resection labels require liver plus resection terms and exclude transplant, donor surgery, biopsy/puncture, cyst surgery, and explicitly recurrent repeat resection;
- primary analysis excludes HCC-directed TACE/embolization, ablation, systemic treatment, transplant or prior liver resection in days -180 to -1; an all-resection adjusted cohort is secondary;
- pathology text supporting HCC is required for the primary clinical cohort and is a sensitivity restriction if it reduces event support below the feasibility gate;
- no explicit recurrence, residual disease, metastasis, or HCC-directed retreatment from index through day 60;
- at least one numeric exact-label total AFP in days -90 to -1, at least one in days 0–14, at least one in days 15–60, and at least one acute liver-injury panel in days 0–14;
- follow-up opportunity after day 60, defined by an encounter, examination or procedure dated at least day 61.

Index is surgery start. The exposure window is day -90 through day +60. Prediction occurs once, at day 60. Outcome follow-up is day 61 through day 365, administrative end, or last documented encounter, whichever occurs first. Events before or on day 60 exclude the patient rather than leak into predictors.

## Variables

Baseline covariates available before or at surgery: age, sex, calendar year, prior encounter/testing intensity, procedure approach/extent from `surgery`, cirrhosis and hepatitis text indicators, and pathology features including margin, vascular invasion, satellite lesions, capsule and cirrhosis. Text features are lexical flags and must be reported as unvalidated proxies; they are not clinical staging.

Longitudinal values are log-transformed after marker-specific censor-string parsing and normalized to each participant’s preoperative median. Tumor-marker inputs are total AFP; PIVKA-II and AFP-L3 are optional secondary channels, never required for cohort membership. Injury inputs are ALT, AST, total bilirubin, albumin, INR and platelet count. Measurement times, missingness masks and test counts are inputs because ordering intensity is informative.

Treatment-selection variables include prior TACE/embolization, ablation, resection, transplant and systemic-therapy flags. Surveillance-intensity variables include pre-landmark encounter count, lab count, imaging count, calendar year and site/service proxies available from encounter department and procedure source.

## Outcomes and adjudication

Fully computable primary outcome: first **documented recurrence-management event** during days 61–365, defined as any of:

1. an affirmative liver/abdominal CT or MRI report containing HCC-context recurrence, residual, new lesion, progression or metastasis language after a negation-aware rule;
2. a diagnosis explicitly naming postoperative HCC recurrence; or
3. a new HCC-directed TACE/embolization, ablation or repeat liver resection after the landmark.

Components are reported separately. Treatment-only events are not equated with biological recurrence; they may represent adjuvant practice or selection. Patients without a post-landmark observation are censored, never labeled event-free.

Clinical target outcome: expert-adjudicated residual or recurrent HCC from imaging, pathology and clinical documents, blinded to modeled risk scores. The automated experiment must generate a review queue and a stratified sample of rule-positive and rule-negative reports, but clinical adjudication expertise is not available in the configured data/runtime. Therefore the solver and verifier may establish only results for the documented proxy unless adjudication is independently supplied.

Surveillance artifact is addressed in three prespecified analyses:

- observed-care cohort, with event components explicit;
- surveillance-observed subset requiring cross-sectional liver imaging during follow-up;
- inverse-probability-of-observation weighting based only on information available by day 60, with weight truncation at the 1st/99th percentiles.

Negative-control outcomes are non-HCC procedures and unrelated imaging findings. A biomarker state that predicts generic healthcare contact as strongly as HCC-directed events favors surveillance bias.

## Methods on the same scientific question

### Simple landmark baseline

Fit elastic-net pooled logistic regression for event by day 365 and a cause-specific Cox model from day 60. Inputs are age, sex, calendar year, pathology flags, prior treatment, baseline AFP rank, latest day-15–60 AFP/preoperative AFP log ratio, acute maximum ALT and bilirubin ratios, latest INR/albumin, and pre-landmark measurement counts. Missingness indicators are explicit. Hyperparameters are chosen only in validation data.

This model is the clinically interpretable baseline: it preserves a few landmarks but loses ordering, irregular intervals, transient-versus-persistent shape and cross-marker covariance.

### Scientifically substantive alternative

Fit a constrained continuous-time two-state longitudinal model jointly with post-landmark event risk:

- latent injury state (I_i(t)), initialized by surgery and constrained toward recovery, loads strongly on ALT/AST/bilirubin/INR and inversely on albumin/platelets;
- latent tumor-marker state (T_i(t)), allowed to clear, plateau or rise, loads on AFP and optional PIVKA-II/AFP-L3;
- AFP is permitted to load on both states, which is the key competing-explanation test;
- assay-specific observation models handle irregular timing, censor strings and missing channels;
- day-60 event hazard uses (T_i(60)), (I_i(60)) and the same baseline covariates as the landmark model.

Fit by variational inference in PyTorch with constrained parameters, three random seeds and posterior predictive checks. Compare with an unconstrained two-factor model to ensure conclusions are not created solely by sign constraints. The alternative is selected because it can estimate whether AFP persistence is shared with injury or independent of it; a generic sequence transformer was deferred because it would add flexible prediction without directly identifying the competing temporal processes. A simpler multivariate functional principal-component model is retained as a diagnostic fallback if the state-space model is non-identifiable.

## Split, evaluation and uncertainty

Use the catalog’s participant hash and only discovery buckets 0–79: buckets 0–59 train, 60–69 validation, 70–79 locked internal test. All encounters for one `patient master index` remain in one split. Buckets 80–99 are reserved and not used in discovery. Report a secondary temporal-generalization slice for surgeries in the latest two complete calendar years represented in the locked test set.

Primary evaluation:

- component event counts and cohort flow by split;
- time-dependent AUC/C-index, integrated Brier score through day 365, calibration intercept/slope and calibration plots;
- AUPRC because event prevalence may be low;
- bootstrap 95% confidence intervals clustered by participant, including paired differences between methods;
- surveillance-weighted and surveillance-observed results;
- decision-curve net benefit for expedited-imaging thresholds of 5%, 10% and 20%, explicitly exploratory because the outcome is a proxy;
- posterior/seed stability of (T(60)), (I(60)), state loadings and their hazard coefficients.

No feature selection, cutoff tuning or outcome-rule revision is allowed after looking at locked test outcomes. Exploratory lexical refinements are done in train/validation and frozen before test.

## Result interpretation and falsification

Supportive result: in the locked test set, the tumor state is associated with the documented event more strongly than the injury state; the alternative improves calibration and has a paired C-index gain of at least 0.03 or integrated-Brier improvement of at least 0.01 with a 95% interval excluding no improvement; and direction persists in pathology-supported, no-prior-treatment, surveillance-observed and weighted analyses. This would support earlier imaging prioritization research, not prove occult tumor or treatment benefit.

Adverse result: AFP variation loads mainly on injury, (I(60)) is at least as prognostic as (T(60)), state separation is unstable, or the alternative’s upper confidence bound excludes a 0.02 C-index gain while calibration is no better. This falsifies the proposed incremental discrimination and favors transient injury/measurement as the practical explanation. The direction should be revised toward measurement validation rather than rescued with a larger model.

Inconclusive result: fewer than 150 adjudicable events, fewer than 500 fully observed landmark patients, poor assay comparability, surveillance weights with effective sample size below 50% of nominal, wide paired intervals spanning meaningful benefit and harm, or state non-identifiability. This neither supports nor refutes occult disease. The next study would require prospective standardized AFP/PIVKA assays and scheduled imaging.

The smallest decision-changing check is the frozen cohort/outcome audit before model fitting. Continue only if the exact-label trajectory cohort has at least 500 patients and 150 credible post-landmark events, the recurrence rule has estimated PPV at least 0.85 in blinded review, and no split has fewer than 30 events. Revise to a measurement-validation study if PPV is lower; defer if adjudication is unavailable; abandon the latent-state direction if tumor/injury states cannot be recovered in simulation and posterior predictive checks.

## Feasibility evidence and budget

Observed bounded scans, not study results:

- 105,044 encounters from 43,815 patients, spanning 2010–2026.
- Refined rules found 25,281 earliest definitive-HCC resections, 17,685 with same-encounter pathology support and 20,354 in discovery buckets 0–79.
- In the -90/+60-day extraction there were 76,298 exact total-AFP rows (74,875 numeric), 59,867 PIVKA-II rows (59,485 numeric), and 33,862 AFP-L3 rows (27,671 numeric).
- A looser exact-AFP/injury trajectory screen yielded 8,958 discovery patients and 6,880 after excluding treatment before the landmark; 3,311 had a recovery-window AFP. These counts are not the final cohort.
- The conservative strict probe found only 244 landmark patients with recorded cross-sectional imaging during days 61–365, 129 with affirmative imaging phrases, 672 with post-landmark HCC-directed treatment, and 693 with either proxy component. A separate broader probe found 1,016 complete-core patients and 316 recurrence-term cases. Definitions differ and may not be combined.
- Full source scans took 135–170 seconds on 4 CPUs/16 GiB, establishing preprocessing feasibility.

Planned solver budget: 8 CPUs and 32–64 GiB for extraction and baseline fitting (about 30–60 minutes); one allocated A100 GPU, 8 CPUs and 64 GiB for three state-space fits and checks (estimated 2–4 hours, unmeasured); total under the 8-hour planning envelope. CPU fitting remains acceptable if the bounded cohort is small. GPU use is a throughput choice, not scientific merit.

## Claims the verifier can and cannot check

Computationally checkable: exact source hashes and columns; cohort membership; no patient leakage; time windows; event-component extraction; assay parsing; split isolation; fitted-output existence; metrics and bootstrap intervals; whether conclusions match supportive, adverse or inconclusive criteria; and whether every reported conclusion links to a computed table/figure.

Not automatically checkable: whether a narrative report truly establishes recurrent HCC, whether surgery was clinically curative, whether treatment was adjuvant versus recurrence-directed, biological identity of latent states, causal benefit of expedited imaging, or generalizability outside this institution. These require hepatobiliary/radiology/pathology review, missing assay-unit metadata, prospective standardization or another study.

## Alternatives retained and revisit conditions

- Generic transformer/GRU over all EHR events: deferred because it does not directly test tumor-versus-injury explanations. Revisit only if the mechanistic model is identifiable but leaves important nonlinear residual sequence information and the cohort/event count supports a fair comparison.
- Single 4–6-week AFP cutoff: retained as a literature-aligned sensitivity analysis, not primary, because local units are absent and absolute cutoffs cannot be verified.
- Curative ablation/transplant cohorts: deferred to avoid mixing distinct injury kinetics and selection. Revisit as external boundary tests after the resection model is frozen.
- Pure imaging-adjudicated recurrence: clinically preferable but currently limited by sparse recorded surveillance and unavailable expert adjudication. It becomes primary only with a sufficiently sized, blinded adjudicated set.
