# Donor-aware macrophage-to-FLS communication in E-MTAB-8873

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

In rheumatoid arthritis synovium, reciprocal macrophage–fibroblast-like synoviocyte (FLS) signaling has been proposed to sustain inflammation and failed resolution [K1][K3]. A clinically consequential unresolved question is whether exposure to macrophages induces a reproducible, condition-specific transcriptional response in FLS that can be distinguished from FLS-only and macrophage-negative controls. Resolving this would determine whether the available paired coculture data support a testable stromal–myeloid mechanism, or whether apparent effects are better explained by donor, cell composition, batch, or assay artifacts.

This is a hypothesis, not a computed result. The closest inspected experimental work supports the value of preserving macrophage–FLS interactions in RA synovial models [K2], but does not establish this targeted assay’s response or direct ligand-receptor causality.

## Falsifiable experiment

Population: all 33 E-MTAB-8873 FLS/coculture condition records, restricted to records with a verified sample-to-design join and a defensible contributing-donor representation. Do not silently drop records or infer missing donor identity.

Primary contrasts:
1. macrophage-positive versus FLS-only, within the same FLS donor context where available;
2. macrophage-positive versus macrophage-negative, with the same donor/condition structure;
3. mixed macrophage conditions as a secondary composition check, not as an independent validation.

For each feature, estimate the condition contrast in log-expression space after adding a small count offset. Use a donor-aware paired/stratified contrast or a contributing-donor random-effects model. The primary uncertainty unit is the contributing donor/context, not the cell. Use a donor/condition permutation null that preserves the observed donor and condition structure. Pre-LPS and inhibitor conditions are mechanistic context only; they are not independent causal replicates.

Prespecify a direction and effect-size threshold before inspecting results (for example, a log2 fold-change threshold of 0.25 and a donor-level false-discovery or permutation threshold of 0.05, subject to verification that the design supports the estimand). Require consistency across donors and separation from FLS-only and macrophage-negative controls. Report feature-level effects, uncertainty intervals, permutation p-values, and the number of donors contributing to each contrast.

Supportive result: a reproducible condition-specific signature with donor-aware uncertainty, separation from both controls, and no comparable signal in the negative-control contrast. Adverse result: no separation, reversal, or failure to survive donor/condition permutations. Inconclusive result: unresolved donor mapping, insufficient independent donor contexts, low detectability, or uncertainty too large to distinguish communication from composition/technical artifacts.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Baseline: a simple donor-aware paired/stratified contrast per feature with donor-level uncertainty and permutation inference. It directly tests the prespecified condition effect and is transparent about which donors contribute.

Substantive alternative: a hierarchical/regularized model across the 445 measured features, retaining donor and condition effects while borrowing information across features. It could reveal coordinated pathway-level structure that a single-feature contrast loses, but it cannot establish direct ligand-receptor action or causality. The alternative is deferred unless the baseline establishes metadata integrity and adequate feature-level signal. Both methods use the same inputs, contrasts, split (training/fitting on the available design with donor-stratified permutation), outcome, and uncertainty evaluation.

The actual scientific deliverable is a fitted donor-aware contrast table and a prespecified condition-specific signature test, including effect estimates, uncertainty, permutation results, negative-control behavior, and an explicit supportive/adverse/inconclusive interpretation. No full study result is claimed here.

## Competing explanations and limits

The strongest rival explanations are: (i) FLS donor identity or age/sex differences; (ii) macrophage composition or ratio; (iii) batch/technical effects; (iv) treatment (LPS or MerTK inhibitor) rather than macrophage exposure; and (v) targeted-panel measurement or cell-count differences. The design addresses these with paired donor contexts, FLS-only and macrophage-negative controls, condition-preserving permutations, and explicit treatment/context labels, but cannot eliminate all residual confounding.

Expression association cannot establish direct target action, ligand-receptor causality, clinical transition, or treatment benefit. Clinical phenotype, treatment history, age completeness, and independent external validation are not assumed available. Causal interpretation requires perturbation or independent experimental evidence. The three demonstration directions were considered: the D3-M2 communication question is pursued because its paired controls are available; D3-M1 composition/within-cell regulation is deferred because the targeted FLS assay does not resolve whole-tissue composition; D3-M3 persistence/remission is deferred because these cross-sectional condition states do not establish longitudinal transitions.
