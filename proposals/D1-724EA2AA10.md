# Persistent hyperlactatemia under improving versus worsening measured perfusion

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly produce a source-audited prognostic study, not a reproduction and not a fitted result in this proposal. Completion requires:

1. an all-eligible first-ICU-exit flow and a coverage flow for repeated lactate and paired perfusion proxies;
2. a row-level provenance manifest proving the clinical and recording times, item dictionaries, units, interval clipping and joins;
3. a prespecified persistent-lactate/measured-perfusion phenotype with an explicit indeterminate category;
4. matched held-out predictions from a transparent latest-state baseline, the inherited generic-history baseline, a process-only control, and a learned joint-trajectory representation;
5. competing-risk cumulative incidence, calibration, Brier scores, effect uncertainty and measurement/selection sensitivity;
6. negative-control, permutation, timing, modality and endpoint falsifications; and
7. a conclusion ledger that separates prognostic association from microcirculatory diagnosis, fluid response, treatment effect, discharge appropriateness, utility and transportability.

No cohort count, model coefficient, performance result or clinical conclusion is claimed here.

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It retains the parent’s lactate/perfusion question, exact MIMIC-IV source bindings, process-only comparison, competing transitions and live-exit selection audit. It repairs the primary estimand by adding a one-hour pre-exit availability boundary and by making the perfusion rule, recording-time requirements and missing-output handling deterministic. The mature live-exit branch remains a stronger foundational selection study; this branch should supersede it only if the phenotype is sufficiently covered and adds stable, physiology-specific information beyond generic late-history and observation process.

## Unresolved question and evidence boundary

The expert seed proposes that persistently high lactate may have different meanings when perfusion is improving versus worsening, and suggests a later fluid-response analysis. The source dictionaries establish that candidate lactate, MAP, vasoactive and Foley records exist. They do not establish repeated-measurement coverage, that these proxies measure microcirculatory function, or that fluids were indicated or effective.

The strongest claim supported before fitting is therefore only:

> MIMIC-IV contains structured, dated candidate measurements from which a repeated-lactate and measured-perfusion trajectory can be constructed, subject to unit, timing and coverage verification.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among first eligible live ICU exits with persistent hyperlactatemia and adequate paired measured-perfusion coverage, is the 48-hour risk of a first ICU rescue or timed in-hospital death lower when the measured perfusion-proxy trajectory is improving than when it is worsening, after adjustment for the parent’s generic late-history representation and observation process?

The primary hypothesis predicts a lower standardized 48-hour R/D cumulative incidence for persistent-lactate/improving-measured-perfusion than persistent-lactate/worsening-measured-perfusion, and an incremental held-out calibrated prediction gain for the phenotype model over the matched generic-history model. A null or reversed contrast, no incremental gain, a gain reproduced by process-only variables, or instability under timing/coverage/modality controls is adverse or inconclusive, not confirmation.

This is an observed-exit prognostic estimand. It does not estimate the effect of giving fluid, the effect of withholding fluid, whether lactate was caused by hypoperfusion, whether perfusion was biologically adequate, whether an ICU discharge was premature, or what would happen under a different discharge policy.

## Population, temporal boundaries and outcomes

Use one index per hospital admission: the first `icu/icustays` record with valid `intime < outtime` and an observed live ICU exit. An exit is provisionally live if `outtime < admissions.deathtime` or `deathtime` is missing and `hospital_expire_flag=0`; cases with death at or before `outtime`, or with `hospital_expire_flag=1` and missing `deathtime`, are retained in the flow but excluded from the timed primary estimand or placed in the prespecified unknown group. Do not replace a non-eligible first ICU stay with a later stay. Join the index stay to its admission by `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and report all exclusions.

Set t0=`outtime` and the availability landmark tL=t0-1 hour. Require tL to be at least 12 hours after `intime`. The primary feature window is [t0-13h,t0-1h], represented by twelve one-hour bins; the early phenotype half is [t0-13h,t0-7h] and the late half is [t0-7h,t0-1h]. Use half-open intervals except include a row exactly at the final tL boundary once. No primary feature may have clinical time, recording time, interval start, or derived coverage after tL. The one-hour buffer is fixed before inspecting outcomes.

The phenotype-eligible population requires at least one valid lactate value in each half and valid paired perfusion-domain summaries in each half. The solver must report the all-eligible flow, each domain’s coverage, the joint phenotype-eligible flow, phenotype prevalence, and indeterminate fraction. It must not treat a missing lactate, MAP or Foley observation as normal.

Define first mutually exclusive transitions in (t0,t0+48h]:

- R: a later ICU stay with a different `stay_id`, same `hadm_id`, and `intime > t0) and `intime <= t0+48h`;
- D: `admissions.deathtime` in the interval;
- S: alive `admissions.dischtime` in the interval, before any R or D, treated as an observation-boundary competing transition rather than a safe outcome;
- N: no R, D or S while the admission is ascertainable through 48 hours;
- U: `hospital_expire_flag=1` with missing `deathtime`, or another explicitly documented untimed-death case.

Assign the earliest event time; use D before R only for an exact timestamp tie, and document any unresolved ties. Primary analyses estimate 48-hour R/D composite cumulative incidence with S competing and U excluded from timed-event estimation. Report R, D and S separately, N as event-free continuation, a complete-ascertainment subset, adverse and non-adverse U bounds, and a 7-day sensitivity. A result confined to S or N is not evidence for adverse residual perfusion physiology.

## Phenotype definition

All item IDs and units must be verified from the dictionary before extraction. Lactate is `hosp/labevents.itemid=50813`, joined to `hosp/d_labitems`. For the primary analysis, retain numeric `valuenum` only when `valueuom` is a documented compatible lactate unit (for example, mmol/L after case/spacing normalization); do not silently infer or convert an empty or incompatible unit. Require the same normalized unit rule in both halves. Define persistent hyperlactatemia as early and late lactate medians both >=2.0 in that unit. Freeze >=4.0 as a high-lactate sensitivity. Report unit strata and rejected/non-numeric records.

Measured perfusion is explicitly a proxy composite, not microcirculatory adjudication. In each half:

- MAP is the median numeric value from arterial MAP 225312, arterial blood-pressure mean 220052, or non-invasive blood-pressure mean 220181 in `icu/chartevents`, joined by `stay_id`, with `charttime<=tL), dictionary `linksto=chartevents`, and compatible mmHg units. Record the modality used. The primary rule uses the prespecified source hierarchy 225312, then 220052, then 220181 within each half; sensitivity analyses use arterial-only and non-invasive-only observations rather than hiding modality changes.
- Vasoactive support is a source-defined interval process from `icu/inputevents`: for each half, calculate the union duration in minutes of valid intervals for any prespecified agent, clipped to the half. Do not add doses across agents or compare mg with units. Also record any-support, agent count and raw interval count. An absent qualifying interval is a source-defined no-support state, and its measurement-process limitation is tested by the process controls.
- Urine output is the sum of non-negative numeric Foley values from `icu/outputevents.itemid=226559`, divided by the fixed six-hour half duration. Use `charttime), retain zero as a measured zero, and retain no-row as missing; do not treat missing output as zero. The primary coverage rule requires at least one valid Foley record in each half. Output-presence and an observed-output-only sensitivity are required.

For each domain, score direction relative to early-to-late change: improving is MAP change >=+5 mmHg, vasoactive union-duration change <=0 minutes, and urine-rate change >=0; worsening is MAP change <=-5 mmHg, union-duration change >0 minutes, and urine-rate change <0; values between thresholds are neutral. The joint measured-perfusion score is the sum of the three domain scores (+1, 0, -1). Define improving measured perfusion as score >=+2 and worsening measured perfusion as score <=-2. All other persistent-lactate cases are discordant/indeterminate and remain in flow and a three-level sensitivity; they are never forced into either primary contrast. This majority rule is fixed before outcome inspection. Leave-one-domain-out, continuous-change, modality-specific and alternative MAP threshold sensitivities test whether one proxy drives the finding.

The primary contrast is persistent lactate + improving measured perfusion versus persistent lactate + worsening measured perfusion. Secondary contrasts compare persistent versus non-persistent lactate within each measured-perfusion category, with the same coverage rules. Early/late liver and illness context may include ALT 50861, AST 50878, total bilirubin 50885, creatinine 50912, bicarbonate 50882, hemoglobin 51222, platelets 51265 and WBC 51300 from `hosp/labevents), using numeric values, valid clinical/recording times and separate measurement indicators. They are adjustment variables and possible alternative lactate explanations, not proof of etiology.

## Models, targets and fair comparison

Freeze a deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split on the accessible participants before inspecting held-out outcomes; no subject crosses partitions. The primary Bdisc-versus-B1 comparison is on the same phenotype-eligible population. Fit clipping, imputation, scaling, category definitions, feature selection, calibration, any class weights and M2 component labels using training/validation only. Do not use `dod`, discharge notes, discharge location, future transfers, post-tL rows or endpoint fields in a prediction matrix.

- B0, the transparent baseline, is a penalized one-hour discrete-time cause-specific hazard model for R, D and S. It uses admission/demographic/ICU context, terminal-bin physiology and explicit masks/process variables. It establishes what the latest measured state can do.
- B1, the inherited generic-history comparator, uses the same context and terminal features plus order-blind summaries across the twelve one-hour bins: medians/means, robust spread, abnormal-bin fractions, valid-bin and measurement counts, time-since-last observation, lab-testing indicators, Foley charting and vasoactive presence/duration. It tests generic late history without giving it a physiologic interpretation.
- Bmask is B1’s process-only control: retain observation masks, counts, testing indicators, Foley charting and vasoactive presence/duration but remove physiologic values. If Bmask matches Bdisc or B1, a physiology-specific reading fails.
- Bdisc is the selected primary alternative. It adds the prespecified phenotype, raw early/late lactate and MAP/output/pressor changes, modality indicators, and liver-marker values/masks to B1. Its primary scientific comparison is the adjusted standardized 48-hour R/D CIF and held-out Brier/calibration difference versus B1; this is a prognostic association, not a causal effect.
- M2 is a learned joint-trajectory alternative on the same eligible input vector. Robust-scale the continuous early-to-late changes using training statistics, fit a two-component diagonal-covariance Gaussian mixture in training phenotype-eligible cases, and pass out-of-sample posterior component probabilities plus the continuous values to the same cause-specific hazard model. A component may be described as improving/worsening only when its training means satisfy the frozen directional rules; otherwise report an unlabeled latent trajectory representation. M2 can reveal coupled nonlinear structure lost by the hand-defined majority rule, but a learned gain cannot validate the clinical label.

The primary support criterion is predeclared: the improving-minus-worsening adjusted R/D CIF contrast must have the predicted direction with a subject-bootstrap 95% interval excluding zero, and Bdisc must improve held-out 48-hour R/D Brier by at least 0.005 versus B1 with its paired interval excluding zero, without a clinically material calibration deterioration. The 0.005 threshold is a prespecified practical prediction margin, not a claim that smaller differences have no scientific value. M2 is corroborative only; it must report component stability, posterior calibration and the same held-out metrics.

Report cause-specific hazards, 12/24/48-hour R/D CIF, R/D/R/D-component/S calibration intercept and slope, transition-aware Brier scores, AUROC/AUPRC, paired test-set differences, adjusted standardized phenotype contrasts and subject-level 95% intervals. Also report positive/negative predictive values only for predeclared review thresholds if the solver adds them; they are not utility or intervention results.

## Falsification and interpretation

The solver must run these checks with fixed seeds and retain outputs:

1. **Provenance/timing:** verify archive member, header, dictionary label/linksto/unit, join cardinality, unique index logic, clinical and recording times, interval validity/clipping, and no post-tL or prohibited field in features.
2. **Physiology versus process:** compare Bdisc with B1 and Bmask, and a process-only phenotype encoding. A comparable process-only gain defeats a physiology-specific interpretation.
3. **Direction construction:** swap early and late halves within participant while preserving counts/masks. The directional contrast and M2 interpretation should attenuate; persistence indicates a construction or leakage defect.
4. **Outcome falsification:** permute first-transition labels within partition and refit. Any retained predictive increment indicates leakage or evaluation failure.
5. **Selection falsification:** assess q overlap and repeat metrics by q strata; use a negative-control six-hour live-exit process model. A much stronger phenotype signal for exit-process prediction than R/D supports selection/process explanation.
6. **Endpoint decomposition:** repeat R and D separately with S explicit, and repeat U bounds and complete ascertainment. A gain limited to S or administrative continuation is not support for perfusion biology.
7. **Measurement robustness:** repeat lactate cutpoints 2/4, valid-bin thresholds 4/6/8, arterial-only and non-invasive-only MAP, continuous changes, leave-one-domain-out, output-observed-only, care-unit and anchor-year strata. Never select a held-out cutoff.
8. **Learned-model discipline:** fit M2 and all preprocessing within training; document component-label stability across training resamples. If the component directions do not meet the rule, do not attach improving/worsening language.
9. **Coverage and uncertainty:** low phenotype prevalence, high indeterminate/U fractions, incompatible units, low q overlap/effective sample size, sparse events or wide intervals are inconclusive rather than negative.

Supportive results require adequate coverage/event support, the predicted improving-versus-worsening association, the Bdisc incremental margin over B1, Bdisc exceeding Bmask, and stability across ascertainment, modality, care-unit, q-overlap and endpoint controls. Adverse results include reversal, no increment, process-only replication, label-permutation resistance, strong measurement dependence, poor calibration or instability. Inconclusive results do not resolve the question.

Computationally checkable claims are source/member/schema/item provenance, row timing, joins, cohort flow, phenotype and indeterminate counts, split integrity, feature whitelist, model fit specification, held-out predictions, CIF/Brier/calibration, bootstrap uncertainty, q overlap and falsification outputs. Clinical adjudication or additional data are required for true microcirculatory dysfunction, lactate etiology, clinician intent, appropriateness of discharge, fluid responsiveness, treatment indication, preventability, ward monitoring, intervention effect, net benefit and transportability. A future causal fluid study would need treatment indication and timing, dose/composition, fluid responsiveness, time-varying confounders, and outcomes under alternatives; this study cannot provide them.

## Alternatives not selected and revisit conditions

The mature live-exit branch remains the comparator because its primary contribution is selection-aware, real-time prognostic validity at observed ICU exits. This child is worth retaining only as a distinct scientific branch: it asks whether a particular physiologic discordance explains heterogeneity within a repeated-lactate subgroup. It should be revisited or superseded by the mature branch if the phenotype is sparse, mostly indeterminate, dominated by modality/process, or fails the preregistered increment.

A chronological GRU or transformer is not the primary alternative here because the parent already tests chronology against order-blind history and the new uncertainty is cross-domain perfusion/lactate meaning. Revisit a longer sequence model only if M2 or Bdisc shows robust cross-domain information, the event count supports the extra parameters, and a new question about longer order dependence is stated. A fluid-treatment causal model is deferred until treatment intent, valid exposure definition, responsiveness and confounding control are available. No method is rejected because of neural networks or GPUs; complexity is deferred because it does not answer the current uncertainty.
