### 3.4 Decision, justified by those numbers

**The EXPANDED population (index susceptibility panel + the admission has an ICU stay + in hospital
at the anchor + ICU `intime` ≤ anchor + broad-spectrum agent active) is the PRIMARY analysis
population** (n = 6,970 at `T_info`; 1,531 exposed). ICU-at-anchor (n = 4,403) becomes a
prespecified **restriction analysis** with "in ICU at the anchor" as a reported effect modifier, not
a second-choice fallback.

Justification, entirely from the measured numbers above:

1. The ICU-at-anchor population cannot support **any** defensible equivalence margin on the key
   contrast: its conservative half-width is 5.11 pp (ρ = 0) to 6.46 pp (ρ = 0, DE = 1.6). A ±5 pp
   margin is *mechanically* satisfiable there only when the estimate is imprecise — exactly the
   defect R1 names.
2. The expanded population's conservative half-width on the key contrast is 3.63 pp (ρ = 0) /
   4.59 pp (ρ = 0, DE = 1.6) — still wider than the 2.5 pp margin I consider scientifically
   defensible (§5). So **even the expanded population cannot establish equivalence**. I say that
   explicitly rather than lowering the margin to fit the data. The study's deliverable on U2 is
   therefore re-specified as a **bound**, not an equivalence test.
3. The expanded population is also the *more* clinically relevant one for this question: the
   susceptibility result arrives a median 69.8 h after collection, by which time many patients have
   left the ICU. Conditioning on being in the ICU at `T_info` conditions on a post-collection
   survival/severity state and shrinks n by 37%, with a measured 30-day mortality of 25–26% vs
   19–21% in the expanded set — i.e. the ICU-at-anchor restriction is itself a selection on severity
   at the anchor.

Trade-off recorded honestly: the expanded set includes ward patients, for whom the time-varying
physiology covariates in `icu/chartevents` do not exist. Their nuisance models must rely on
`hosp/labevents` + baseline covariates + an "in ICU at anchor" indicator; that is a real loss of
confounder resolution bought with precision, and the restriction analysis exists to detect it. If
the ICU-at-anchor and expanded `RD_info` differ by more than the ICU-at-anchor half-width, the
covariate-resolution loss is material and is reported as such.

21.3% in the expanded population. The gate as written would **not pass**.

Two explanations, which the repaired design must separate rather than choose by taste:

* **(a) Order-record churn.** A "strict drop in regimen MAX spectrum sustained 24 h" also fires when a
  broad agent's order simply expires or is re-written. 14,737/154,933 cohort order rows had a missing
  or non-positive `stoptime` **[OBSERVED]**, and `hosp/prescriptions` nominal stop times are
  administrative. Churn is anchor-independent, which would explain a high narrowing prevalence at
  anchors where no information exists.
* **(b) Real empiric narrowing.** Clinicians genuinely narrow on clinical grounds (defervescence,
  Gram stain, source control) before susceptibilities, which is the very selection channel the
  negative control is meant to quantify.

Consequences, prespecified now:

1. The gate is **replaced** by a *churn-discounted* gate: the exposure must be re-measured with (i)
   `hosp/emar` administration events (feasible: 99.49% dictionary coverage **[OBSERVED]**) requiring
   ≥1 administered dose of the narrower regimen and zero administrations of the dropped broad agent
   in the 24 h hold window, and (ii) `hosp/poe` discontinuation chains
   (`discontinue_of_poe_id`/`discontinued_by_poe_id`) as explicit evidence that a broad agent was
   *stopped by an order action* rather than lapsing. The gate passes only if, under the
   administration-confirmed definition, narrowing prevalence at `T_info` exceeds that at
   `T_pre_strict` by ≥ 5 percentage points. **[UNRESOLVED]**: that re-measurement was not run in this
   attempt; the eMAR string coverage needed for it was measured, the exposure re-derivation was not.
2. If the churn-discounted gate also fails, the honest conclusion is that **MIMIC-IV order records
   cannot distinguish deliberate de-escalation from order churn at a 48 h resolution**, and the
   negative-control contrast is reported as uninterpretable rather than as a selection estimate.
   That outcome is a legitimate, publishable negative measurement result about the dataset, and it is
   the main new risk this child exposes.

### 4.4 The `X` (stop-all) arm is an end-of-life channel, measured

**[OBSERVED]** 30-day mortality in the stop-all arm at `T_info` is **53.15%** (ICU-at-anchor;
508 episodes) and 29.53% (expanded; 1,131), versus 22.8%/26.0% and 19.1%/21.2% for N/C. Stopping all
antibacterials within 48 h of the susceptibility result is strongly associated with death, consistent
with comfort-measures transitions. This confirms the parent's decision to analyse X separately, and it
quantifies the end-of-life channel the parent could only flag descriptively. Any definition of
"de-escalation" that merges X into N would manufacture harm.

## 5. Falsification criteria, re-set (the core of the R1 repair)

The parent's rule was: adverse if the paired CI for `RD_info − RD_pre` ⊂ ±5 pp. Measured half-widths
(§3.3) are 2.6–6.5 pp depending on population and assumptions, so that rule is satisfiable by
imprecision. It is withdrawn. The replacement has three parts.

### 5.1 A margin that is defensible, stated with its attainability

The margin must be tied to the effect the literature claims, not to what the data can detect. The
largest published de-escalation mortality RD in this dataset family is −4.91 pp (95% CI −7.27 to
−2.23) for antipseudomonal de-escalation at day 4 **[K1]**. If the information-response component is
to explain that association, it must be of comparable size. I therefore set

> **Δ = 2.5 pp** (≈ half of the **[K1]** point estimate) as the margin of scientific indifference for
> `RD_info − RD_pre`.

**Attainability, measured in advance:** the conservative crude half-width on that contrast is 3.63 pp
(ρ = 0) in the primary population and 4.59 pp with DE = 1.6 **[ASSUMPTION]**. Under ρ = 0.5 it is
2.59 pp. **So Δ = 2.5 pp is at the edge of attainability and probably not attainable.** This is stated
*before* the estimate, and the decision rule is built so that non-attainment produces an
interpretable bound rather than a conclusion.

### 5.2 The decision rule that cannot fire by default

Let `D = RD_info − RD_pre_strict` with a subject-level paired bootstrap CI (same bootstrap draws
across anchors), half-width `h`.

* **Information-response effect supported.** `D < 0`, CI excludes 0, **and** the same ordering holds
  for `T_pre −24/−48/−72 h` without being monotone in lead time, **and** the churn-discounted
  exposure gate (§4.3) passed. Meaning: part of the de-escalation association is attributable to
  acting on susceptibility information. It would still not establish that de-escalation *causes*
  benefit.
* **Selection-only explanation supported (adverse for the literature).** Requires **both**
  `CI(D) ⊂ ±2.5 pp` **and** `h ≤ 2.5 pp`. If `h > 2.5 pp` this conclusion is **not available**,
  whatever the point estimate. This is the specific repair: an imprecise null can no longer be read
  as "it is selection".
* **Bounded, the expected outcome.** If `h > 2.5 pp` (which the measured numbers say is likely), the
  deliverable is the *upper bound* `|D| ≤ |D̂| + h` on the information-response component, reported
  with its ρ and DE diagnostics, plus the statement: *this study cannot adjudicate an
  information-response component smaller than h pp; a multicentre dataset or a randomized
  reassessment-timing trial is required.* This is a number the study produces in every scenario and
  it is not satisfiable by being underpowered — a wider CI makes the bound *weaker*, not the
  conclusion stronger.
* **Reverse-adverse.** `D > 0` with CI excluding 0: the pre-information anchor shows a *more*
  protective association than the real anchor, which invalidates the negative control as a lower
  bound (plausible if empiric narrowing is reserved for the clearly improving). Must be reported as
  negative-control failure, never re-read as harm from information-responsive de-escalation.
* **Void.** The churn-discounted gate fails (§4.3) → the contrast is not interpreted at all.

### 5.3 Deliverable (a) — the anchor-measurement result, which exists regardless

Reported: `RD_conv_cx`, `RD_conv_rx`, `RD_info` with paired bootstrap CIs for the differences, plus
the already-measured exposure-misclassification facts. **[OBSERVED]** in this branch: the
conventional anchor `T_conv_cx` = specimen `charttime` + 72 h precedes `T_info` — i.e. falls before
the susceptibility result existed in the record — for **2,733/6,227 (43.9%)** ICU-at-anchor index
episodes and **6,990/13,997 (49.9%)** expanded index episodes. Cohort collection→storage lag:
ICU-at-anchor p10 45.4 / median 69.2 / p90 120.8 h; expanded p10 46.0 / median 72.0 / p90 141.7 h.
Decision rule for (a) unchanged from the parent: anchor matters if
`|RD_conv_cx − RD_info| ≥ 3 pp` with a paired CI excluding 0, or if exposure discordance ≥ 10%.
**[UNRESOLVED]** the episode-level exposure cross-tabulation between anchors (parent reported 15.8%
discordance; I did not recompute it — my probe measured prevalence at each anchor separately, not the
2×2 table).

### 5.4 Prespecified sensitivity analyses that bear on the decision rule

1. Specimen-list widening (add `SWAB`, `CATHETER TIP-IV`, `FOOT CULTURE`, `URINE,KIDNEY`,
   `ASPIRATE`): raises n and therefore shrinks `h`; reported as a separate population with its own
   `h`, never pooled post hoc with the primary.
2. Grace window 24 h vs 48 h; hold 12 h vs 24 h.
3. Exposure definitions E2 (own-panel "S" agent retained, anti-pseudomonal/anti-MRSA dropped) and E3
   (MIMIC-IV-internal antibiogram-weighted spectrum, OSAR-style per **[R-OSAR]**) — both
   **[UNRESOLVED]** in this attempt.
4. **Outcome-ascertainment set, now measured rather than caveated [OBSERVED, Lead].** The
   prespecified **primary** outcome is *all-cause death within 30 days of the anchor using
   `hosp/patients.dod` augmented by `hospital_expire_flag`*; the **co-reported** outcome is
   *in-hospital death by day 30 with discharge alive counted as survival*. They are not
   interchangeable: over all 85,242 ICU-stay admissions, 9,475 (11.1%) die in hospital and all carry
   a `dod`, while of the 75,767 discharged alive only 31.3% have any `dod` and 4,370 (**5.77%**) die
   within 30 days of `dischtime` — an ≈5.8 pp difference in events, larger than Δ = 2.5 pp and of the
   same order as **[K1]**'s entire −4.91 pp effect. 95.3% of alive discharges occur within 30 days of
   `admittime`, so a 30-day window from an ICU anchor almost always extends past discharge. Both
   outcomes are reported for every anchor; the decision rule in §5.2 is evaluated on the primary one
   only, and a disagreement in sign or margin status between the two is itself a reportable result.
5. **Informative censoring through hospice, prespecified [OBSERVED, Lead].** 2,100 ICU-stay
   admissions are discharged to HOSPICE (vs 9,449 DIED, 22,252 HOME HEALTH CARE, 21,286 HOME, 14,702
   SNF). A hospice/comfort-measures discharge is close to a death in intent and is plausibly more
   frequent in the non-narrowed arm, so: (i) the proportion discharged to hospice is reported by arm
   as a descriptive flag at every anchor, and (ii) a sensitivity analysis treats hospice discharge as
   a death at `dischtime`. Post-discharge `dod` completeness (31.3%) is also not plausibly random
   with respect to discharge destination, which is itself downstream of the same recovery state the
   negative control is designed to measure — so a null primary result could in principle be produced
   by differential ascertainment, and that alternative explanation is checked by the
   in-hospital-only co-reported outcome, where ascertainment is complete by construction.
   Limit: `dod` is a date, not a datetime, and these percentages are population-level, not
   cohort-specific; they must be recomputed inside the analytic cohort.
6. ICU-at-anchor restriction as the covariate-resolution check (§3.4).

## 7. Method comparison (kept from the parent, re-justified against *my* sample sizes)

Full detail in `method-comparison.md`. Summary and what changed:

* **Baseline (primary):** clone-censor-weight with pooled-logistic censoring hazards and weighted
  Kaplan–Meier; 5-fold cross-fitted nuisances; 500 subject-level bootstrap replicates reused across
  anchors for paired CIs. Clone-block table ≈ 6,970 × 2 arms × 8 six-hour blocks ≈ 111,520 rows for
  the primary population (vs ≈ 60,560 in the parent's smaller cohort).
* **Substantive alternative (co-primary for the key contrast):** cross-fitted LTMLE with a small
  SuperLearner library {GBT, RF, splined logistic, intercept} and explicit trajectory features
  (6/12/24 h slopes and AUC of norepinephrine-equivalent dose, lactate, temperature, WBC, P/F,
  creatinine; consecutive-hours-of-improvement counter). Scientific reason unchanged and now better
  supported: `RD_pre` is only a valid selection measure if "who gets narrowed" is well specified; if
  the real propensity is trajectory-driven, a last-value linear model underestimates `|RD_pre|` and
  manufactures an apparent information effect. My measured crude pattern (RD ≈ 0 at `T_pre_strict`,
  −2 pp at `T_info`) is exactly the pattern that could be produced that way, so this comparison is now
  load-bearing rather than decorative.
* **Deferred: sequence model (GRU/temporal transformer propensity, 1 A100, ~2 h).** The parent deferred
  it at 3,785 episodes / ~660 events. My primary population has **6,970 episodes / 1,531 narrowing
  events** — roughly 2.3× the events. That is still small for a recurrent propensity model whose
  output must be *calibrated* for IPW, and ~45% of the primary population are not in the ICU at the
  anchor so they have no hourly chartevents stream at all, which is the input such a model needs.
  Deferral therefore stands, with the revisit condition made quantitative: revisit if (i) the
  specimen-widened population exceeds ~12,000 episodes with >2,500 events **and** the analysis is
  restricted to the ICU-at-anchor subset where hourly physiology exists, **or** (ii) the
  LTMLE-vs-baseline `RD_pre_strict` gap exceeds 3 pp, i.e. flexibility demonstrably matters.
  No GPU is requested in this branch; per `skills/phai-hardware/SKILL.md` the deployment has A100s
  available through allocated jobs, so this is a scientific judgement about events-per-parameter and
  input availability, not a hardware-availability claim.
* **Deferred: turnaround time as an instrument.** Unchanged: turnaround correlates with specimen type
  and organism, which independently predict outcome, so exclusion fails. Recorded, not silently
  dropped.

## 8. What supportive, adverse and inconclusive results mean; what is checkable

**Computationally checkable in MIMIC-IV 3.1:** every number in §1, §3, §4 (all already computed);
the five RDs and their paired contrasts; the churn-discounted exposure re-derivation from `hosp/emar`
and `hosp/poe`; weight/positivity diagnostics; organ-support-free days; new-positive-culture
incidence; baseline-vs-LTMLE comparison; the three exposure definitions.

**Supportive** (information-response effect): changes what "de-escalation" studies are measuring —
the day-3/day-4 convention mixes informed with uninformed narrowing, and the informed part carries the
association. Implication, deliberately narrowed after audit threat T1: the *estimand* in this literature should
be defined on result availability rather than on a fixed day, i.e. this is a measurement-validity
correction for future de-escalation studies. It is **not** a claim that accelerating turnaround
improves outcomes — **[K2]** randomised that and found no benefit — and it is not a recommendation to
change reassessment timing in practice. **Adverse** (selection-only, only if precision permits): the observational de-escalation
"benefit" should not be used to justify the practice, which is coherent with the randomized evidence
**[K3]** (116 patients; noninferiority on ICU stay *not* demonstrated, superinfection 27% vs 11%,
mortality similar) and with **[K2]** (a randomised 14-hour acceleration of susceptibility-result
arrival produced no DOOR or mortality benefit). **Inconclusive** (the likely outcome): a quantified upper bound on the
information-response component plus the anchor-misclassification result, which stands on its own.

**Requires clinical adjudication, not resolvable here:** whether a given narrowing was
microbiologically appropriate; infection vs colonisation for urine/respiratory isolates; recurrence
vs new infection vs contamination; adequacy of source control (ICD procedure codes are date-only);
and whether a stop-all episode (53.2% 30-day mortality **[OBSERVED]**) was a comfort-measures
transition — MIMIC-IV has no reliable goals-of-care timestamp.

**Requires other data or another study:** when the result was actually read or telephoned (no field
exists; the parent measured 0 of 321,328 commented susceptibility rows mentioning "PRELIM"
**[REPORTED BY PARENT, NOT RE-VERIFIED HERE]**); whether de-escalation *causes* benefit
(randomization); external validity beyond one US academic centre; resistance-ecology effects.

## 9. Claim discussion against the three inspected key works (audited slate)

**[K1] Zhang *et al.* 2026** (Front. Med.; `doi:10.3389/fmed.2026.1924203`, PMID 42724069) is the
closest prior work and the direct bound on novelty: same dataset and version (MIMIC-IV v3.1), same
clinical decision, target-trial emulation, 5,762 patients, antipseudomonal de-escalation 30-day
mortality RD **−4.91 pp (95% CI −7.27 to −2.23)**. Inspected text (abstract, and in the parent branch
also the publisher HTML Methods "Study design and data sources" / "Eligibility criteria") shows its
time zero is **72 h after antibiotic initiation**, with "culture information available before time
zero" used as an *eligibility* criterion — a requirement that culture data exist, not an anchor
placed at a measured per-patient result-storage time. It also names the rival explanation this child
is built to measure ("de-escalation may mark early recovery that is incompletely captured in
structured data") and leaves it unmeasured. **Contribution relative to [K1]:** the anchor is the
measured `storetime`, and the named rival becomes an estimated quantity through a *measured-feasible*
pre-information anchor (5,223 eligible episodes, 1,112 narrowing events **[OBSERVED]**).
**What [K1] establishes and this child does not dispute:** at a day-4 anchor, de-escalation is not
associated with higher mortality. **Limitation of [K1] as evidence here:** the favourable estimate is
labelled exploratory by its own authors and the early-recovery explanation is acknowledged, not
tested; per audit threat T2, this proposal claims credit for *quantifying* that explanation, not for
identifying it.

**[K2] Banerjee *et al.* 2026 — the FAST randomized clinical trial** (JAMA;
`doi:10.1001/jama.2026.5487`, PMID 41999287) is the reference that most threatens this design, which
is why it occupies a key slot. FAST randomised rapid phenotypic antimicrobial susceptibility testing
versus standard testing in Gram-negative bacteraemia, achieved a genuine **≈14-hour** shift in time
to antibiotic escalation/de-escalation, and found **no** superiority on the primary desirability of
outcome ranking (DOOR **48.8%**, 95% CI 45.3–52.4) and no difference in 30-day mortality or the other
secondary outcomes. Two consequences are absorbed above: (i) the timing-benefit framing is withdrawn
(§0.5 item 2) — randomised evidence already says that making susceptibility information arrive
earlier does not by itself improve patient outcomes; (ii) the novelty claim is narrowed, because FAST
*does* measure de-escalation timing from an information-arrival anchor. **What [K2] does not
establish, and what leaves this study a question to answer:** FAST randomises *when the information
arrives*, leaving the clinician's decision free. It does not estimate the effect of *the decision
itself at a common informational state* ("de-escalate now vs continue, given the result is in"),
which is the estimand here and which exists in randomised form only in **[K3]** at n=116. FAST
therefore cannot explain why observational de-escalation cohorts report a ≈5 pp mortality benefit;
this child measures one named candidate explanation for that discrepancy. **Limitation:** abstract
only inspected (Europe PMC core record attached); DOOR construction, the turnaround-time
distribution and subgroup results were not inspected, and FAST covers Gram-negative bacteraemia only,
a narrower population than this cohort.

**[K3] Leone *et al.* 2014** (Intensive Care Med 40:1399–1408; `doi:10.1007/s00134-014-3411-8`,
PMID 25091790) is the only randomized test of the de-escalation *decision* and bounds how much the
observational literature can claim: 116 patients, ICU stay 9 (IQR 5–22) vs 8 (IQR 4–15) days, mean
difference 3.4 days (95% CI −1.7 to 8.5) against a 2-day noninferiority margin — noninferiority
**not demonstrated** — superinfection **27% vs 11%** (p = 0.03), more antibiotic days after
de-escalation, mortality similar. It is the direct source of the observational-versus-randomised
discrepancy this child is about, and the precedent for the §5 structure: a prespecified margin plus
an honest statement that the data did not reach it. **Limitation:** abstract only inspected;
underpowered for mortality; single-country, pre-2014 practice; says nothing about anchor timing.

**Novelty statement, audited and bounded.** Information-arrival anchors are **established practice**
for *measuring how fast clinicians act* — in rapid-diagnostics randomized trials (**[K2]**;
Banerjee 2015 rmPCR; RAPIDS-GN 2021, which randomised at Gram-stain positivity and reports
standard-of-care AST availability at mean 44.9 ± 12.1 h) and in at least one observational EHR cohort
(Heo & Kim 2026, which reports the result-reporting and sampling anchors side by side and quantifies
a −3.53-day gap). The parent's broader claim is therefore withdrawn. What survives, stated precisely
over the works actually inspected in this episode and its parent:

> No inspected work uses a measured per-patient susceptibility-result-availability timestamp as the
> **time zero for exposure assignment** in a causal de-escalate-versus-continue contrast with a
> patient outcome; the works using an information-arrival anchor use it to measure clinician speed
> as an *outcome*, while the works estimating the effect of de-escalating assign exposure either at
> randomisation (**[K2]**, **[K3]**, RAPIDS-GN) or at a fixed clock time (**[K1]**, the DIANA family,
> and the 48–72 h time-out convention documented by Sava 2026). Additionally, no inspected work
> reports the per-patient collection-to-result-availability lag distribution in a public EHR, nor
> quantifies how often that lag places the conventional day-3 anchor before the result existed, and
> no inspected work measures the healthy-responder explanation with a pre-information pseudo-anchor.

The scope statement in §1 is correspondingly restricted to the **de-escalation-effectiveness**
literature (target-trial emulations and de-escalation-versus-continuation cohorts) and explicitly
exempts the rapid-diagnostics trial literature. Two further audit corrections are adopted:
**the measured lag is not an anomaly** — MIMIC-IV's collection-to-`storetime` interval is in the same
range as RAPIDS-GN's standard-of-care AST availability, so the opening must not present it as a data
oddity; the point is that routine microbiology turnaround is long enough that a day-3 anchor
systematically precedes the result. And **`storetime` is an upper-bound proxy for clinician
awareness**, a batch validation/storage event, not a verified notification time; "never under 24 h"
is a property of MIMIC-IV 3.1 `storetime` semantics, which is why §8 lists clinician awareness as
requiring other data. **This is not an exhaustive search:** 19 indexed queries in roughly two hours,
no hand-searching of reference lists, no non-English or grey literature, and three of the four
bounding works were inspectable only as abstracts (`litAudit/audit.md` §6 records the full limits).
If a further search finds a work assigning *exposure* at a measured per-patient result-availability
time with an outcome contrast, the measurement-novelty claim weakens to "first application to the
de-escalation mortality estimand in MIMIC-IV", while deliverables (a) and (b) are unaffected.

**Main remaining objections to this child.** (1) The narrowing prevalence at the pre-information
anchor (21.3%) is nearly as high as at `T_info` (22.0%), so the exposure may be dominated by order
churn; if the §4.3 gate fails, deliverable (b) collapses to a dataset-measurement negative result.
(2) Even the primary population probably cannot reach the 2.5 pp margin, so the headline causal claim
will most likely be a bound, not an adjudication — by design, but it limits the clinical payoff.
(3) The expanded population buys precision with reduced time-varying confounder resolution for ward
patients. (4) The cascade is ~1/3 smaller than the grandparent's because of the specimen list; the
specimen-widening sensitivity is prespecified but unmeasured. (5) After **[K2]**, the clinical payoff
of even a clean supportive result is a correction to how this literature should be *estimated*, not a
new treatment or timing recommendation; readers who want an actionable timing intervention will find
this proposal less attractive, and that is the honest position rather than a weaker one.
(6) The strongest single-point objection remains §4.3: if order-record churn, not deliberate empiric
narrowing, explains the 21.3%-vs-22.0% prevalence near-equality, the negative control measures record
artefact rather than clinician behaviour, and deliverable (b) collapses to a dataset-measurement
negative result. That is computable on the bound data (`hosp/emar`, `hosp/poe`) and is the next
repair.
