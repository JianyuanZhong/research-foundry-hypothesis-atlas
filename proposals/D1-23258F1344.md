# Episode 21 successor: persistent lactate at an eight-hour pressure-recovery landmark

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and substantive advance

When an ICU patient has received an early norepinephrine record and monitored mean arterial pressure (MAP) is stably at least 65 mm Hg near an eight-hour reassessment, does failure of lactate to fall still mark a clinically material mortality gradient, or is it mainly a correlate of starting severity, specimen timing, and hospital testing practice?

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis:** in the EICU target population defined below, lactate nonclearance (<20%) is associated with at least a **+10 percentage-point** higher measurement-process-overlap-standardized probability of hospital death than clearance of at least 20%, among patients whose recorded MAP is recovered during the final hour before the eight-hour landmark.

This is a prognostic discordance hypothesis, not an effect of lactate-guided care, fluids, vasopressors, MAP targeting, or lactate normalization. The +10-point absolute margin is a prespecified decision-relevance anchor for whether this phenotype merits prospective mandatory reassessment; it is not a validated minimum clinically important difference or a biological cutoff.

The episode-20 parent measured “recovery” only during T0+60 through T0+120 while allowing the follow-up lactate as late as T0+480. It therefore could include patients whose pressure subsequently deteriorated and called that recovery sustained. Requiring both early and late recovery is temporally attractive but the outcome-blind audit leaves only 127 patients in seven overlap hospitals, below the inherited eight-hospital gate. This successor makes the clinically relevant correction without mining outcomes: pressure recovery is assessed in the final hour before the decision landmark. That population has 216 patients in ten exposure-overlap hospitals. Follow-up specimen time is added to the primary standardization because EICU cannot support a tightly synchronized 5–7-hour sample at multicenter scale.

## What existing evidence supports—and what remains unresolved

Two inspected current full-text sources support only a bounded rationale.

Diab et al. (Frontiers in Medicine 2025; DOI 10.3389/fmed.2025.1679297; frozen full HTML source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) studied 574 adults with sepsis or septic shock at one emergency department. Clearance >10% between first and second lactates had a borderline adjusted association with in-hospital death (OR 0.66, 95% CI 0.42–1.04). It did not isolate patients with MAP recovered at a fixed post-vasopressor landmark or test multicenter transport.

Tóth et al. (Annals of Intensive Care 2026; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100106; frozen full-text XML source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) synthesized eight randomized trials with 2,394 shock patients. Tissue-perfusion-guided therapy did not significantly reduce 30-day mortality (RR 0.96, 95% CI 0.83–1.10) or 90-day mortality (RR 0.94, 0.82–1.07), although it reduced early fluid volume. This supports uncertainty about intervention benefit and the inadequacy of assuming that a prognostic marker dictates treatment.

The strongest EICU evidence currently supports **computational feasibility only**. Complete-source, no-sampling reconstruction of snapshot `[source checksum]` found 13,973 adult earliest-person qualifying norepinephrine stays surviving the landmark; 612 had valid serial lactate and baseline vital context; 403 also met final-hour MAP recovery across 69 hospitals. The outcome-blind five-per-group hospital gate retained 216 people in ten hospitals: 106 clearance and 110 nonclearance. There were 39 and 62 hospital deaths, respectively, but those crude rates are noninferential because starting physiology, measurement time, and hospital selection differ.

The outcome-blind model audit found that ridge strength C=10 was the least weakly regularized member of the ordered grid 0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1,000,000 that met the fixed balance criterion: group ESS 96.93 and 97.95, propensity range 0.152–0.890, and maximum absolute weighted standardized mean difference 0.073. Outcomes were not used to choose the population or C. Neither these diagnostics nor the crude mortality counts support the unresolved +10-point hypothesis.

## Population and temporal boundaries

All offsets are integer minutes relative to ICU admission. Read every row in the configured source files; do not sample.

1. Adults have numeric `age>=18`; released `age='> 89'` is mapped to 90.
2. For each ICU stay, define T0 as the minimum `infusionDrug.infusionoffset` from 0 through 1440 inclusive whose case-folded `drugname` contains `norepinephrine` or `levophed`. Dose/rate fields do not define eligibility.
3. Among all qualifying stays for one `uniquepid`, retain the stay with smallest T0, breaking ties by smallest `patientunitstayid`. This is the earliest qualifying recorded stay, not necessarily the first ICU stay.
4. Set the landmark L=T0+480. Require `unitdischargeoffset>=L`, `hospitaldischargeoffset>=L`, and exact `hospitaldischargestatus` Alive or Expired. Thus the binary hospital outcome is after L, although exact death time remains unavailable.
5. Baseline lactate B is the latest collection in [T0−360,T0] with case-folded trimmed `labname='lactate'`, numeric `labresult` 0.2–30, and `labresultrevisedoffset<=T0+60`. Require B>=2. At the selected collection time, retain rows at the greatest eligible revision offset; if they contain more than one distinct valid value, exclude the patient. Identical duplicates collapse to the smallest `labid`. The source audit found `labmeasurenamesystem='mmol/L'` for released lactate rows; any reconstructed row with a different or missing system unit is excluded and reported.
6. Follow-up lactate F is selected among valid lactates collected in [T0+120,T0+480] and revised by L. At each collection offset, retain the greatest eligible revision and exclude that offset if distinct valid values conflict. Choose the clean offset nearest T0+360, then the earlier offset, then smallest `labid`. Record elapsed time E=follow-up offset−T0 and include E in the primary model. All exposure information is available by L.
7. Collapse duplicate `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset` rows to the median valid `systemicmean` (20–180) and median valid `heartrate` (20–250) separately. Final-hour pressure recovery requires at least six unique offsets in [T0+420,T0+480] with valid MAP and at least 80% of those offset medians >=65. Record final-hour median MAP and the fraction >=65.
8. Baseline vital context requires at least three unique offsets in [T0−60,T0) at which both MAP and heart rate are valid. Their medians are covariates.
9. Before opening outcomes, retain hospitals with at least five clearance and five nonclearance patients under all prior rules. If fewer than eight hospitals, fewer than 50 people, or fewer than 20 hospital deaths in either exposure group result on independent reconstruction, the experiment is inconclusive; do not weaken a rule.

The target is therefore an eight-hour survivor population with early recorded norepinephrine, clinician-selected serial lactates, baseline monitor context, final-hour recorded pressure recovery, and hospital-level trajectory overlap. It is not all shock, all sepsis, all vasopressor use, or all patients whose perfusion recovered.

## Exposure, outcome, estimand, and baselines

Define relative clearance C=(B−F)/B. Exposure X=1 is C<0.20; comparator X=0 is C>=0.20. A lactate rise is nonclearance. The primary outcome D is exact `hospitaldischargestatus='Expired'`.

The primary estimand is:

Delta = weighted P(D=1 | X=1) − weighted P(D=1 | X=0)

in the measured-overlap target induced by the fixed exposure model. It is a measurement-process- and physiology-standardized prognostic association, not a causal effect.

Fit scikit-learn `LogisticRegression(C=10, penalty='l2', solver='lbfgs', max_iter=10000)` with X as outcome and:

- age; sex indicators including missing;
- B, median baseline MAP, and median baseline heart rate;
- T0 and E;
- final-hour median MAP and fraction of valid final-hour MAP offsets >=65;
- fixed indicators for the ten retained hospitals.

Winsorize continuous terms at pooled 1st/99th percentiles, then standardize with pooled means and population SDs. Assign weight 1−e to X=1 and e to X=0, where e is fitted P(X=1), and normalize weights to mean one within each group. No mortality value enters population definition, hospital selection, preprocessing, or the exposure model.

Required comparators are (a) crude risks/RD in the same 216-person target; (b) hospital-only overlap weights; (c) a parent-compatible model omitting E and final-hour MAP terms; and (d) a ridge logistic mortality model using continuous clearance clipped to −100% through +100% with the same context terms. The exact episode-20 early-window population is reconstructed as a separate temporal-definition sensitivity. No comparator or sensitivity can rescue the primary classification.

## Uncertainty, diagnostics, and falsification

Run 5,000 hierarchical bootstrap replicates with seed 20260921.

For each replicate, sample the ten hospitals with replacement and assign a unique hospital-copy ID. Within each copied hospital and exposure group, sample `uniquepid` clusters with replacement to that original hospital-group count and assign person-copy IDs. Recompute winsorization/standardization, encode site by hospital-copy ID, refit the fixed C=10 exposure model, and re-estimate group risks, Delta, and risk ratio. Do not collapse repeated copies back to the original hospital or person. A replicate that loses an outcome class, does not converge, or gives a nonfinite estimand fails; no substitute model is allowed. Report percentile 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles and the failure fraction.

Report source-stage reconciliation; group counts/deaths; site counts; crude and weighted risks, RD and risk ratio; propensity range; group ESS; maximum group-normalized person-weight fraction; maximum hospital weight fraction; weighted SMD for every model column; all bootstrap estimates; and leave-one-hospital-out RDs.

Primary gates are:

- both group ESS >=40;
- no propensity outside 0.02–0.98;
- maximum person-weight fraction within either group <=5%;
- maximum absolute weighted SMD <=0.10;
- maximum hospital weight fraction <=35%;
- bootstrap failure <=5%;
- at least eight of ten leave-one-hospital-out fits finite.

Classification is deterministic, with gate failure taking precedence:

- **Supportive:** every gate passes, the lower 95% bound for Delta is >=+0.10, and at least 80% of finite leave-one-hospital-out point estimates are positive. This supports only a large noncausal prognostic discordance in the selected phenotype.
- **Adverse to materiality:** every gate passes and the upper 95% bound is <+0.10. Report any smaller positive, null, or negative estimate; do not translate this into “no association.”
- **Opposite association:** every gate passes and the upper bound is <0. This is adverse to the hypothesis but does not mean nonclearance is protective.
- **Inconclusive:** the interval contains +0.10, any gate fails, or computation is undefined.

## Prespecified sensitivity hierarchy

These are descriptive unless a separate interval is explicitly reported, and none changes the primary class:

1. follow-up lactate restricted to T0+240 through T0+480; the no-outcome audit yields 136 people in seven overlap hospitals;
2. tight T0+300 through T0+420 sampling; the audit yields only 50 people in three overlap hospitals and is expected to be feasibility-limited;
3. both early [T0+60,T0+120] and final-hour pressure recovery; 127 people in seven overlap hospitals;
4. early-only parent recovery using the finalized conflict rules; 192 people in ten hospitals;
5. clearance cutoffs 10% and 30%;
6. final-hour recovery defined as median MAP>=65;
7. exclusion of B>10;
8. globally earliest ICU stay per person;
9. all 403 complete final-hour-recovery patients with ridge hospital indicators but no five-per-group site gate;
10. exclusion of a latest pre-landmark `carePlanGeneral` exact `cplgroup='Care Limitation'` value other than `Full therapy`; never use `activeupondischarge`.

## Interpretation and stronger claims

A supportive result would show that, among this selected recorded phenotype, normal-range MAP near the landmark does not erase a large mortality gradient associated with persistent lactate. It would justify an independently collected study of a mandatory multimodal reassessment trigger with synchronized vasopressor administration/dose, fluids, urine output, capillary refill, mental status, organ support, liver function, beta-agonist exposure, and specimen type. It would not support giving fluid, increasing vasopressor, adding inotropy, targeting lactate normalization, or any treatment benefit.

An adverse result would argue against advancing this exact EICU phenotype as a major escalation trigger at the +10-point threshold, while preserving any smaller association. An opposite result would reject the direction but should first prompt clinical review for serial-testing selection, treatment limitation, liver dysfunction, and unmeasured treatment.

An inconclusive result means the snapshot cannot distinguish a material from smaller gradient. It must not trigger cutoff, time-window, diagnosis, or subgroup mining. A larger independent cohort is required.

Treatment-effect claims require randomization or a credible longitudinal target trial with administered treatment timestamps, doses, dynamic confounders, and expert protocol review. Clinical utility requires prospective impact evaluation. Sepsis generalization requires infection and organ-dysfunction adjudication. Tissue-hypoperfusion or “hemodynamic incoherence” mechanisms require perfusion measurements absent here.

## Missing evidence, adjudication, and verifier contract

EICU cannot establish that the first matching infusion row is true norepinephrine initiation or administration; distinguish transfer on ongoing drug; reliably harmonize dose/rate units; determine treatment target or fluid administration; adjudicate arterial versus venous lactate; determine why/when a specimen was ordered; measure capillary refill, mottling, mental status, tissue perfusion, cardiac output, liver clearance, seizures, or beta-agonist contributions; establish exact death time/cause; or fully ascertain treatment limitations. Serial-lactate inclusion, landmark survival, and MAP/lactate documentation create a selected target. Recorded MAP is a monitor-derived summary, not organ-perfusion truth.

The automatic verifier can check source hashes/headers, source read-only behavior, complete scanning, drug matching, T0 and one-person ordering, landmark inequalities, lab collection/revision/conflict logic, unit handling, follow-up tie rules, final-hour MAP and baseline-vital windows, outcome-blind hospital gate, exact reconciliation, model matrix/C, weights, bootstrap copy IDs/seed/count, diagnostics, intervals, sensitivities, and conclusion classification.

It cannot verify drug administration, specimen type, monitor validity, tissue perfusion, clinical rationale, causal exchangeability, sepsis, treatment benefit, external transport, or clinical utility. Those require clinical adjudication, unavailable measurements, expert review, prospective data, or another study.

Verifier fixtures must include: a supportive interval with all gates and site direction passing; +6 points with CI +2 to +9 as adverse to +10 but evidence for a smaller association; −3 points with CI −12 to +6 as adverse to +10 with direction unresolved; −12 points with CI −20 to −4 as opposite but not protective; +12 points with CI −3 to +24 as inconclusive; and any gate failure as inconclusive. Correct computation paired with claims about fluids, vasopressors, treatment benefit/harm, tissue hypoperfusion, sepsis-wide generalization, or deployment must fail verification.
