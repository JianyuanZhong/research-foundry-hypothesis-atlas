# Nighttime ICU discharge and residual instability: does pre-discharge instability explain the nighttime excess? (eICU)

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (expert seed [internal campaign], "Nighttime ICU discharge").
Dataset: eICU-CRD 2.0 snapshot [source checksum].
Status: WORKING DRAFT v2 (episode 2). Probe results marked [measured]; design sections prespecified but not yet executed.

## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

Discharging patients from the ICU to lower-acuity units at night is common and has been
associated with worse outcomes (Goldfrad & Rowan, Lancet 2000: crude OR 1.46,
case-mix adjusted 1.33 for hospital mortality; effect attenuated to 1.17, 95% CI
0.92-1.49 after adding "premature discharge"). A propensity-matched single-center
study (Correia et al., PLoS One 2018) found no association with in-hospital
mortality (OR 1.17, 95% CI 0.79-1.73). The evidence is therefore **conflicting and
inconsistent**, and no large multi-center study has asked the mechanistic question:
is the nighttime excess concentrated among patients who are still physiologically
unstable at discharge (residual instability), i.e., is "nighttime" a proxy for
premature/forced discharge of sicker patients?

A discovery probe [measured this episode] in eICU reproduces the crude signal:
among alive discharges to lower-acuity locations, death in a subsequent ICU stay
occurred in 799/12,374 (6.46%) nighttime vs 912/20,701 (4.41%) daytime discharges
(crude OR 1.498) — a crude nighttime excess of the same magnitude as Goldfrad &
Rowan, in a US multi-center cohort. This makes the concentration question
empirically live in this dataset.

Clinically this matters because if the excess risk is concentrated in a measurable
pre-discharge instability state, hospitals can (a) flag at-risk nighttime discharges
for step-down monitoring, (b) use the instability state rather than clock time as the
trigger for discharge-timing decisions, and (c) target staffing/monitoring resources
to the subgroup that actually carries the risk. If instead the excess is diffuse and
not explained by measurable instability, clock-time-based rules are not the right
lever and organizational factors (bed pressure, staffing) dominate — which eICU
cannot measure (no bed-capacity or staffing data), and the finding would point to
other data sources.

Leading claim: the nighttime-discharge excess in early post-discharge adverse
outcomes is concentrated among patients with residual instability in the 24 h
before discharge (vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, new/worsening organ
dysfunction). Strongest rival: the association is an artifact of (a) illness
severity at admission (sicker patients stay longer and are more likely to be
discharged late), (b) hospital practice (some hospitals discharge more at night
AND have worse outcomes), or (c) selection in defining "discharged alive." The
discriminating comparison is the within-hospital night-vs-day outcome difference
among patients with similar pre-discharge instability states, tested for
consistency across hospitals.

## 3. Population and temporal boundaries (prespecified)

Inclusion:
1. unitvisitnumber = 1.
2. unitdischargestatus = 'Alive'.
3. unitdischargelocation in {Floor, Step-Down Unit (SDU), Acute Care/Floor,
   Telemetry, Other ICU, Other Hospital, Other External, Other} (transfer to a
   lower-acuity or external location; excludes Home, Skilled Nursing Facility,
   Death).
4. unitdischargeoffset >= 1440 min (ICU stay >= 24 h, so a 24 h pre-discharge
   window exists).
5. Age 18+ (patient.age).
6. unitdischargetime24 parseable to hour 0-23.
7. A subsequent ICU stay exists for the same patienthealthsystemstayid (required
   for the primary outcome; stays without a subsequent stay are excluded from the
   primary analysis and reported in the funnel).

Exposure:
- night_discharge = 1 if hour(unitdischargetime24) in {22,23,0,1,2,3,4,5,6}
  (22:00-06:59, matching Goldfrad & Rowan's "out of office hours"); sensitivity:
  00:00-04:59 ("early hours").

Residual-instability state (measured in the 24 h BEFORE ICU discharge, i.e.,
offset in [unitdischargeoffset-1440, unitdischargeoffset)):
- vasopressor_24h: any infusionDrug row with drugname containing
  'norepinephrine' (primary) or any of 'vasopressin', 'epinephrine', 'dopamine'
  (sensitivity) in the window.
- ventilated_24h: any respiratoryCare row with airwaytype in {Oral ETT, Nasal ETT,
  Tracheostomy, Double-Lumen Tube, Cricothyrotomy} with respcarestatusoffset in the
  window, or ventstartoffset < unitdischargeoffset <= ventendoffset.
- new_aki_24h: creatinine (labname='creatinine') in the window >= 1.5 x the last
  creatinine drawn >24 h before discharge (if a prior value exists; else 0 and
  flagged as missing-baseline).
- instability_score = count of {vasopressor_24h, ventilated_24h, new_aki_24h}
  (0-3). Primary stratification: instability_score >= 1 vs 0.

Covariates:
- age, gender (patient).
- apache score (apachePatientResult.apachescore, fallback acutephysiology score).
- icu_los_days = unitdischargeoffset/1440.
- admissionweight (if present).
- hospitalid (random effect).

Outcome (post-discharge):
- PRIMARY: death in a subsequent ICU stay of the same patienthealthsystemstayid
  (unitdischargestatus='Expired' in the next unit stay). This is a computable
  proxy for early post-discharge deterioration/death; it captures deaths that
  return to ICU, NOT all hospital deaths.
- SECONDARY: (a) short-interval readmission proxy — next-stay discharge offset
  within 4,320 min of the prior discharge (0 < gap <= 4320); (b) discharge to
  Other ICU (deterioration proxy).
- ADJUDICATION NOTE: eICU does not provide a clean post-ICU hospital death flag
  or inter-stay timestamps (unitadmitoffset empty). The primary outcome therefore
  undercounts post-discharge deaths (only those returning to ICU are captured) and
  the interpretation must not claim hospital-mortality causality. The short-interval
  proxy is a coarse readmission measure (offsets are relative to each stay's own
  start, so the gap is approximate). These limitations are stated explicitly and
  bound the claims.

## 4. Analysis plan

1. Baseline GLMM (mixed-effects logistic, hospital random intercepts):
   outcome ~ night_discharge + instability_score + apache + age + icu_los_days +
   (1|hospital). Primary estimand: interaction night_discharge:instability_score
   (does the night effect differ by instability state?).
2. Stratified analysis: within instability_score=0 and >=1 strata, night-vs-day
   ORs with 95% CI, hospital random effects. The direct test of the seed
   hypothesis: "excess risk concentrated among unstable patients" = significant
   night effect in the >=1 stratum, null/attenuated in the 0 stratum.
3. Hospital-level consistency: distribution of hospital-level night-effect
   estimates; fraction of hospitals with positive night OR in the unstable
   stratum.
4. Learned alternative (same scientific question): gradient-boosted trees
   (sklearn HistGradientBoosting, CPU) predicting the primary outcome with the
   same covariates; compare feature importances of night_discharge vs
   instability markers; partial dependence of night_discharge by
   instability_score to reveal whether the night effect is concentrated in
   high-instability regions (non-linear/threshold structure the GLMM cannot show).
5. Evaluation: pre-specified hospital-level holdout (leave 2 hospitals out, chosen
   by size, fixed before fitting). Report holdout AUC, calibration slope, and the
   stratified night effect.

## 5. Falsification and interpretation

- Supportive: significant positive night effect in the instability>=1 stratum
  (OR >= 1.2, p < 0.05) with a materially smaller or null effect in the
  instability=0 stratum (OR < 1.1), consistent in >= 80% of hospitals, and the
  GBM partial dependence shows the night effect concentrated in high-instability
  regions. Interpretation: nighttime discharge risk is concentrated in a
  measurable pre-discharge instability state; supports flagging unstable
  nighttime discharges for enhanced step-down monitoring.
- Adverse (hypothesis refuted): night effect null in both strata (OR 0.9-1.1,
  p > 0.05 in both) across both model families and holdout. Interpretation: no
  measurable nighttime excess in this population/outcome; clock-time rules not
  supported; organizational factors (unmeasured) may dominate.
- Inconclusive: night effect present in both strata similarly (diffuse, not
  concentrated), or significant in only one model family, or outcome events too
  sparse (< 300 deaths in the unstable-night cell). Interpretation: cannot
  separate concentration from diffuse effect; requires hospital-mortality data
  (e.g., MIMIC-IV, which has discharge disposition and death flags) or
  bed-capacity/staffing data to test the organizational mechanism.

What each result would/would not establish: a supportive result establishes
association and cross-hospital stability of a concentrated night effect, not
causality (discharge timing is confounded by bed pressure and clinical
judgment; no randomization). It would NOT establish that delaying nighttime
discharges improves outcomes (that requires a trial or quasi-experimental
design). An adverse result argues against clock-time-based discharge rules in
this population. Neither result establishes the organizational mechanism
(bed pressure), which eICU cannot measure.

## 6. Method comparison (baseline vs learned alternative)

Baseline: hospital-level GLMM with the night x instability interaction.
Strengths: interpretable ORs, hospital random effects, direct test of the
concentration hypothesis. Limitations: assumes linear log-odds, cannot reveal
threshold/non-linear structure in the instability markers.
Learned alternative: GBM with the same covariates. Strengths: captures
non-linearities and interactions automatically; partial dependence shows WHERE
the night effect lives (which instability region). Limitations: less directly
interpretable; hospital clustering handled by fitting on pooled data with
hospital as a feature (or repeated fitting); no built-in random effects.
The two test the same scientific question (is the night effect concentrated in
instability?) with complementary sensitivity: GLMM for the average interaction,
GBM for the shape. GPU not required; tabular fits are minutes-scale on 16 CPU.

## 9. Limitations and honest gaps

- eICU lacks a clean post-ICU hospital mortality flag and inter-stay timestamps
  (unitadmitoffset empty); primary outcome is death-in-subsequent-ICU-stay
  (computable, undercounts post-discharge deaths), with short-interval
  readmission and Other-ICU discharge as secondary proxies. The original
  hospital-mortality question is only partially answerable.
- No bed-capacity or staffing data; organizational mechanism (bed pressure)
  cannot be tested.
- "Residual instability" is measured from routine labs/infusions/ventilation,
  not a validated discharge-readiness score; the instability_score is a
  prespecified composite, not a clinical tool.
- Night definition (22:00-06:59) follows Goldfrad & Rowan; sensitivity to
  00:00-04:59.
- Abstract-only inspection for K1/K2; full texts not read.
- Death-in-subsequent-stay may be influenced by hospital readmission practices
  (some hospitals readmit more readily), a form of practice confounding analogous
  to the discharge-timing confounding.
