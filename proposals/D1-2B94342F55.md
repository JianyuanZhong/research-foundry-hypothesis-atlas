# eICU lab-order process: within-site severity signal or between-site workflow signal?

**Status:** proposed, not executed. This is a substantive, bounded evolution of `[prior hypothesis]`; no cohort statistics or model results are asserted. The locked population, time origin, 24-hour adult first-stay landmark, and later in-hospital death outcome are retained. This proposal is strictly prognostic/descriptive and does not estimate a causal effect of testing.

## Scientific opening, evidence, and contribution

The prior design compares physiology-only with physiology-plus-lab-process prediction and tests site transport. That can reveal fragility, but by itself cannot distinguish site workflow from latent severity: poorer performance at a new hospital may reflect case mix, coding, prevalence, label capture, or measurement practice. The unresolved question is more specific: **conditional on observed physiology at the 24-hour landmark, is the remaining predictive contribution of lab testing intensity primarily a within-hospital patient-level signal, or is it concentrated in between-hospital differences in testing practice?**

The leading explanation is that testing frequency/timing partly indexes a hospital's workflow, laboratory capture, or threshold for ordering tests. The strongest rival is severity/clinician concern: sicker patients receive more tests, and observed physiology (including APACHE-derived summaries) incompletely captures the clinician's information and evolving state. A third rival is purely technical site variation (analyte naming, data completeness, outcome coding). These accounts make partially different predictions: after adjustment for landmark physiology, a persistent within-hospital association is more consistent with patient-level severity/concern than a purely between-hospital workflow proxy; a process association concentrated in between-hospital means, with little within-hospital residual increment, is more compatible with site workflow/capture. Neither pattern identifies mechanism, and site-level case mix remains confounded with workflow.

The data resource is a multicenter ICU database with linked vitals, laboratory measurements, and illness-severity variables [K1]. Existing eICU benchmark work already compares clinical, baseline, and deep-learning models for mortality and other endpoints [K3], so another generic classifier leaderboard would add little. Most directly, a recent cross-database ICU study separates creatinine measurement-process features from clinical creatinine trajectories and reports that process signal varied between MIMIC-IV and eICU and added little value after the clinical trajectory block [K2]. That is close, important prior evidence—not evidence that all-lab ordering predicts death or that a workflow explanation is established. The proposed extension tests one fixed 24-hour landmark and all-lab process features against direct observed severity, and decomposes within- versus between-hospital information. K2 was available only as an inspected Europe PMC abstract in this branch; its AKI outcome, creatinine focus, and repeated windows differ materially from this question.

**Strongest existing claim:** EHR measurement-process features can show site-dependent predictive signal in the closely related ICU creatinine/AKI task [K2]; eICU supports multicenter structured ICU analysis [K1], and multicenter prediction benchmarks already exist [K3]. **Untested claim:** in the proposed adult 24-hour landmark cohort, lab-order process information for subsequent in-hospital death remains incrementally predictive within hospitals after measured severity, or is concentrated between hospitals. **Clinical importance:** a site-concentrated proxy is a warning against interpreting process-only risk or transporting it without recalibration; a stable within-hospital increment may help prognosis but still cannot establish appropriateness of testing or justify changing it.

## Target population, landmark, exposure features, outcome

- **Population:** one first ICU unit stay per person (`patient.unitvisitnumber = 1`, further deduplicated/checked with `patient.uniquepid`); adult at ICU admission using documented age values. eICU top-codes age above 89; include such records as adults only under a documented, prespecified age rule. Exclude unknown age rather than infer it. Require known hospital discharge status and offset. Flow counts and exclusions will be reported.
- **Landmark risk set:** alive and still in ICU at 1,440 minutes after ICU admission: `unitdischargeoffset > 1440` and no evidence of in-hospital death by the landmark. The primary cohort's exact death-before-landmark logic requires verification of `hospitaldischargestatus`, `hospitaldischargeoffset`, `unitdischargestatus`, and `unitdischargeoffset` semantics. Any contradictory/ambiguous status-offset records are excluded in the primary analysis and tabulated. This is conditional prediction among 24-hour ICU survivors, not all ICU admissions.
- **Time origin/window:** eICU offsets are minutes relative to ICU admission; use only data timestamps `0 <= event_offset <= 1440`. Process and clinical-state values are constructed strictly from records available by hour 24. No later charting, ICU/hospital discharge information beyond outcome ascertainment, or outcome-derived APACHE fields may enter predictors.
- **Outcome:** subsequent in-hospital death by hospital discharge, from `patient.hospitaldischargestatus`, conditional on being alive at hour 24. `hospitaldischargeoffset` establishes the discharge event is after the landmark / validates label chronology; it is not a predictor. Keep a separate explicit exclusion list for unknown/ambiguous disposition. This is not fixed-horizon mortality after discharge.
- **Process block:** from `lab`, per-stay count of result records, count of distinct test labels and mapped test groups, repeat count, proportion of repeated tests, first/last result offsets, inter-result spacing summaries, and counts by predeclared bins (0–6, >6–12, >12–24 hours). Use `labresultoffset`, not `labresultrevisedoffset`; result records with offsets outside the window are excluded. Preserve all labname/system/interface strings for auditable site-specific mapping. Process variables must be specified before fitting; no label-driven feature selection.
- **Measured clinical-state block:** summarize first-day measured vitals from `vitalPeriodic` and `vitalAperiodic` by prespecified clinically plausible summaries (minimum, maximum, mean, last available value and missingness for heart rate, respiration, temperature, SpO2, systolic/diastolic/mean pressure; unit-aware validation required). Include numeric lab results only for analytes with a prespecified common mapping and unit/measurement-system review; report unmapped, conflicting, and site-specific representations, and do not treat absence as a normal value. `apacheApsVar` provides first-day score inputs such as neurologic components, WBC, temperature, respiratory rate, sodium, heart rate, mean BP, pH, hematocrit, creatinine, albumin, PaO2, PaCO2, BUN, glucose, bilirubin, FiO2, ventilation and dialysis indicators. It is a potentially useful severity anchor, not an unquestioned 24-hour landmark snapshot: APACHE timing/window and completeness must be verified by clinical/data-dictionary review before primary use. Until verified, report a primary model using directly timestamped features only and an explicitly secondary APACHE-severity sensitivity analysis. Exclude `apachePatientResult.predictedhospitalmortality`, `actualhospitalmortality`, `actualicumortality`, and all other post-outcome/label-like result columns from predictors.
- Include available admission-context covariates from patient (gender, age under the rule, ethnicity, unit type, admission source) with explicit unknown categories; avoid hospital identity as an input to the prediction model except for the hierarchical decomposition / held-out-site design.

## Two complementary analyses on the same question

### Prespecified simple baseline

Use regularized or unpenalized logistic regression (choose via training-fold-only nested validation; keep class weighting off for primary calibrated probability estimation) with the measured clinical-state block and context covariates as baseline, then a nested addition of patient-level lab process features. Hospital fixed intercepts are included in the within-hospital analysis; a separate group split holds out entire hospitals and freezes all fitted preprocessing and coefficients. The primary contrast is paired patient-level Brier score for physiology+process minus physiology-only on identical evaluation patients; negative difference denotes better process-augmented Brier score. Report calibration-in-the-large/slope, AUROC and AUPRC as secondary. Do not use hospital ID as a deployable predictor in held-out-hospital scoring.

### Substantive alternative: within/between hierarchical logistic decomposition

Fit a multilevel logistic model to the same eligible stays and same clinical-state variables. For each continuous process feature, decompose the patient feature into (a) deviation from the training-set hospital mean (within-site component) and (b) hospital mean (between-site component); categorical/heterogeneous test breadth gets prespecified interpretable counts/rates. Model outcome with hospital random intercept and partially pooled random slopes for the within-site process component, plus prespecified restricted-spline terms for physiologic severity. Fit and evaluate by grouped hospital folds; calculate group means from training hospitals only for prediction, with no test-outcome or test-feature leakage. Compare its posterior/cluster-robust interval estimates of within- versus between-site process coefficients, hospital slope heterogeneity, and held-out hospital Brier/calibration to the logistic baseline.

The alternative could show whether the overall process–mortality association is primarily within-site or between-site and how much process effect varies by hospital, whereas a conventional pooled logistic feature coefficient collapses these components and can be dominated by site composition. It does not convert that contrast into a causal decomposition: unobserved case mix, clinician judgment, capture, and outcome ascertainment can remain hospital-correlated. Use weakly regularizing priors or prespecified penalization; report convergence/diagnostics, partial pooling, uncertainty, and sensitivity to site size. If too few hospitals or too little event support to estimate varying effects, declare the alternative infeasible rather than substitute a neural net or silently simplify the question.

## Splits, uncertainty, and falsification

- **Main evaluation:** grouped 5-fold hospital split, fixed before outcome inspection, with at least one sufficiently supported held-out fold per hospital assignment; person-level `uniquepid` grouping is enforced across folds even if an individual has multiple admissions. If cross-hospital repeat person IDs make independent hospital assignment impossible, exclude/assign the entire person to one fold and report affected records. An internal sensitivity uses patient-grouped random folds stratified by hospital; it is not a transport test.
- **Within-hospital check:** within each adequately sized hospital, patient-grouped train/test partition (or cross-fit) and compare nested prediction blocks; aggregate effects with equal-hospital and patient weights, both reported. This makes the unit of uncertainty explicit and avoids a few large hospitals determining the result.
- **Primary summaries:** paired Brier increment for adding process features, both overall and separately for within-site validation and held-out hospitals; difference of increments between these regimes. Also estimate within/between process coefficients and their intervals from the hierarchical alternative. Bootstrap hospitals (resampling hospitals and then patients within hospital) for interval estimates; report number of hospitals, events, and bootstrap support. If site count/support is insufficient, use a descriptive site-stratified plot and state imprecision; do not present false precision.
- **Falsification / robustness:** label permutation within hospital should collapse predictive signal toward the within-hospital prevalence baseline; a process-only model's performance must not be called severity-adjusted evidence. Compare directly timestamped severity-only, severity+process, and APACHE-augmented severity+process arms. Repeat after dropping hospitals with poor common analyte mapping and after restricting to agreed common analytes. Test hospital-level process average versus within-site deviation separately. Verify no feature timestamp >1,440; audit duplicates, person leakage and label-offset contradictions. Include calibration and prevalence by held-out hospital, not discrimination alone.
- **Supportive for workflow/capture interpretation (not proof):** little/no clinically meaningful within-hospital Brier gain and within-site process coefficient near null after observed physiology, while between-hospital process means or mapping/capture explain marked heterogeneity and pooled/random patient splits overstate gain. This supports caution about process proxy transport, not that workflow caused mortality.
- **Supportive for latent severity/concern rival (not proof):** within-site process features retain robust incremental prediction after direct physiology/APACHE adjustment, with consistent direction across hospitals. This indicates residual patient-level prognostic information; residual unmeasured severity, concern, care trajectory, or selection remain explanations.
- **Adverse to useful process signal:** confidence interval excludes a prespecified practically relevant improvement in Brier in both within-site and held-out hospital evaluation, with adequate event/site support. This would argue against continuing this feature block for this endpoint, not refute informative testing processes in other outcomes/cohorts.
- **Inconclusive:** broad intervals, unstable site slopes, inadequate common-support, missingness or contradictory outcome fields, APACHE timing uncertainty, or discordant bootstrap/sensitivity analyses. Continue only after resolving the binding issue; do not interpret a nonsignificant result as absence of signal.

A practical threshold for meaningful Brier improvement must be set before seeing outcomes, justified with a clinical/statistical reviewer and expected event prevalence; until then report effect sizes and intervals without post hoc threshold claims. AUROC increases alone do not establish clinical utility.

## Clinical adjudication and evidence unavailable

Needed before main execution: (1) clinical reviewer confirmation of age coding and adult rule for age `>89`; (2) authoritative mapping of eICU hospital and ICU status codes / offsets to alive-at-landmark and subsequent death, including disposition edge cases; (3) verification of APACHE-Acute Physiology Score variable collection window relative to the 24-hour landmark and its missingness/derivation; (4) clinical/laboratory review of `labname`, `labmeasurenamesystem`, `labmeasurenameinterface`, units, repeats, duplicate/result timing, and cross-hospital common analytes; (5) independent review of whether lab-result event time adequately proxies order time, since only result-offset is available here; and (6) review of adequately supported site folds and an a priori practical Brier threshold.

The available tables do not provide explicit clinician intent, order indication, contemporaneous narrative reasoning, true order timestamp, raw waveforms, complete external hospital identity, or experimental variation in testing policy. Outcome conditioning on alive/continued ICU stay at hour 24 creates survivor/length-of-stay selection. Hospital fixed/random effects and APACHE adjustment cannot remove unmeasured severity, treatment pathways, selection, coding, measurement error, or lab capture differences. Thus no causal claim about the effect of ordering, testing appropriateness, mechanism, or an intervention is identifiable here; those would require prospective adjudication, an independent external cohort and/or a justified quasi-experiment.

## Deliverable, resource plan, and selection decision

**Scientific deliverable:** a new fitted pair of nested mortality-prediction models plus a within/between hierarchical logistic analysis, out-of-hospital/site prediction files, paired Brier/calibration reports with hospital-bootstrap intervals, coefficients/posterior intervals and site heterogeneity, and a predeclared outcome/falsification report linking every conclusion to computed outputs. This requires model fitting; no fitting is done in discovery. Positive, adverse, and inconclusive interpretations above are part of the deliverable.

**Compute plan:** future solver may use CPU only (e.g., 8 CPUs, 32 GiB RAM, up to 2 hours for staged reading, feature aggregation, five grouped folds, baseline plus multilevel fits and 1,000 site bootstrap replicates); staged checkpointing and bounded bootstrap batches. eICU `lab.csv.gz` is ~517 MB compressed and vital monitor CSVs are large; use column projection/chunked reads or a temporary local columnar derivative in the workspace, with sources read-only and full four-dataset access retained. These are planning estimates, not measured runtime. No GPU is indicated for logistic/hierarchical modeling; revisiting a GPU alternative would require evidence that a substantive nonlinear representation answers this same severity/workflow distinction better, not just a small predictive gain.

**Selection:** choose the hierarchical within/between decomposition as the scientific alternative to the simple logistic baseline because it directly estimates the competing within-site versus between-site patterns and heterogeneous site slopes. Defer GRU/sequence learning: lab-result sequences might encode evolving severity/timing, but sequence predictive gain alone does not separate workflow from severity, and the records are irregular results rather than validated order sequences. Revisit only if timestamped, mapped sequences yield a clear held-out within-site versus between-site interpretive target and enough events/sites. No neural model is rejected categorically.

## Other demonstration families (brief disposition)

- **Delphi:** learned longitudinal disease-history ordering is not the appropriate first test here; this is a single ICU landmark/process decomposition, not longitudinal disease-natural-history modeling. eICU has structured offsets, so a future temporal sequence adaptation is possible, but would not resolve the primary rival by itself. The original UK Biobank trajectories and external Danish validation are not available in this dataset.
- **ALADYNOULLI:** do not reproduce the genetic/GP latent-disease model; genetic inputs and its disease-state target are not the available eICU question. An EHR-only latent trajectory model could be a new adaptation, but would obscure rather than sharpen the site-versus-severity decomposition without clear additional observable predictions. Revisit only with a specific latent-state estimand and a demonstrated separation from measured severity.
- **Oncoformer:** multimodal oncology EHR/CXR task is unrelated; there are no image payloads in eICU. The seed's missing image dependency precludes full multimodal reproduction, not every lab-only adaptation. A lab-only transformer is deferred here because the hierarchical comparison is more directly falsifiable for this explanatory question.
