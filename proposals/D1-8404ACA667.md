# Persistent residual instability at ICU discharge: a falsifiable MIMIC-IV study

Status: proposed Lead design; parent adaptation of expert seed 10
Parent candidate: [prior hypothesis]
Dataset snapshot: MIMIC-IV core 3.1, snapshot [source checksum]

## Scientific question and hypothesis

When an adult is being transferred out of the ICU, does persistent oxygen requirement or physiologic volatility during the immediately preceding 24 hours identify a clinically meaningful subgroup at increased risk of ICU readmission or death soon after transfer, beyond ordinary demographic and stay-level severity summaries?

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among adult ICU stays (anchor_age >= 18, with MIMIC's >89 grouping retained) discharged alive to a non-ICU location before the end of the same hospitalization, higher prespecified residual-instability burden in the 24 hours ending at ICU outtime is associated with higher risk of ICU readmission or in-hospital death within 48 hours after outtime. A secondary hypothesis is that the order and persistence of measurements—rather than only their fixed summaries—improves calibrated prediction of the same endpoint.

This is an association/prognosis study. It cannot establish that delaying discharge, increasing monitoring, or changing oxygen treatment would prevent the endpoint.

## What is already supported, and what remains unresolved

MIMIC-IV documentation supports that ICU stays have stay_id, intime and outtime; ICU event tables link measurements by stay_id/itemid; non-consecutive ICU stays are retained and can represent either planned procedural transfers or unanticipated readmissions. The local catalog verifies the relevant structured tables and fields below. The demonstration material supports temporally honest longitudinal modeling: the Delphi paper models dated sequences with causal masking and held-out temporal evaluation, while the methods note explicitly treats that as an adaptation pattern, not evidence that the MIMIC question is solved.

A current Europe PMC search (frozen source IDs [source checksum] and [source checksum]) returned abstracts of recent generic ICU-discharge prediction studies, including MIMIC work. Only abstracts were inspected through the search response; no full paper or supplement is claimed as read. Thus the unresolved claim is not “machine learning can predict readmission.” It is whether a transparent residual-instability phenotype, and specifically its within-window persistence/recovery trajectory, adds clinically coherent information beyond fixed summaries in this configured MIMIC snapshot.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use the first qualifying ICU discharge per hospitalization as the primary index, with a sensitivity analysis allowing all qualifying discharges clustered by subject. Include an index icu/icustays row with nonmissing outtime, where outtime is before hosp/admissions.dischtime, and where deathtime is missing or after outtime. Require that the index outtime precedes any later ICU intime; later same-hospitalization ICU stays are retained as candidate outcomes, while same-hospitalization non-consecutive transitions and their transfer context are separately flagged for review.

Index time is t0 = icustays.outtime. Features use only charted observations with t0 - 24 hours <= charttime < t0 from the same stay_id. No post-outtime measurement, discharge note, radiology report, or future outcome feature may enter the model. Primary follow-up is [t0, t0+48h], truncated at hospital discharge if earlier.

The main analysis uses the catalog’s participant-level discovery partition (hash namespace ehr-hypothesis-discovery-v1, dataset mimic, subject_id, buckets 0-79); the reserved 80-99 partition is not available and is not an external validation set. A forward sensitivity split uses patients.anchor_year_group only as a grouped era label; deidentified calendar dates cannot be aligned across subjects.

## Outcomes

Primary: composite of (a) a subsequent icu/icustays.intime for the same subject_id, hadm_id after t0 and within 48 hours, or (b) hosp/admissions.deathtime in [t0,t0+48h]. Report components separately.

Secondary: ICU readmission by 7 days, death by 7 days or before admissions.dischtime, and a competing-event table distinguishing readmission, death without readmission, and discharge alive without either. A readmission is an administrative proxy and may include planned procedure-related transfer because MIMIC documentation states this cannot be perfectly differentiated.

## Exposure/phenotype construction

Create hourly bins on [t0-24h,t0). For each item, retain numeric values after removing nonnumeric values and flagging impossible values according to item units; do not silently winsorize. For each hour compute the last value and an observed indicator. Primary instability features are:

1. Oxygen burden: median and maximum FiO2 (223835), median and maximum O2 Flow (223834), and fraction of observed hours with mechanical ventilation (226260). Do not combine FiO2 and flow into one physical unit; standardize each on the training partition.
2. Physiologic volatility: within-window SD and median absolute successive change for SpO2 (220277), HR (220045), RR (220210), and mean BP using NBPm (220181) or ABPm (220052), with source indicator and coverage.
3. Persistence/recovery: number and length of consecutive hourly bins with high oxygen burden or abnormal/volatile vital signs, plus the last-hour level and robust slope. Thresholds must be fixed before outcome fitting: use clinical thresholds documented in the analysis script or training-partition quantiles, and report both. No feature is allowed to use t0 or later.

Missingness is part of the observation process, not proof of stability. Report coverage and fit a sensitivity model with coverage/documentation-count features. Do not interpret a high charting intensity coefficient as physiology.

## Analysis plan

First report cohort flow, item support, hourly coverage, outcome counts, and overlap/planned-transfer flags. The solver must preserve the actual inclusion/filtering counts.

Primary baseline: logistic regression for the 48-hour composite with prespecified fixed summaries and missingness indicators; use restricted cubic splines for continuous oxygen burden/LOS only if knots are fixed from training. Report odds ratios per SD and calibration curve, Brier score, AUROC and AUPRC with bootstrap CIs. Cluster bootstrap by subject if multiple index stays are used.

Substantive alternative: fit the 3-4-state latent-state model on training stays only using the six measurement channels (FiO2, O2 flow, SpO2, HR, RR, mean BP), hourly missingness indicators, and a monotone time index. Select state labels by emission profile in the training set, not by outcome. Use posterior state probabilities and transition probabilities at t0 as prespecified features in a penalized logistic outcome model. Compare it with the baseline on the untouched participant-held-out test partition and the forward era sensitivity split. Decompose any gain by an ablation removing transition/order features while retaining marginal summaries. Evaluate calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC, and bootstrap uncertainty. A result is scientifically useful only if the state pattern is interpretable and directionally stable, not merely if AUROC increases.

## Falsification and robustness

The primary hypothesis is falsified if, in the held-out data, the prespecified oxygen-burden and volatility gradients do not show monotone risk (or their 95% CIs remain compatible with no association), and the predeclared trajectory model does not improve calibration or discrimination beyond baseline; an inverse, well-supported relationship also falsifies the stated direction. An inconclusive result is low event support or wide intervals that cannot distinguish clinically relevant effects from no effect.

Predeclare:
- repeat with 6-hour and 24-hour windows and a 7-day endpoint;
- use FiO2/flow separately, and NBP versus ABP separately;
- exclude stays with same-hospitalization ICU transitions plausibly related to a planned procedure, where an explicit proxy exists, and report all-stay results;
- stratify by first_careunit and mechanically ventilated versus non-ventilated patients;
- compare a physiologic-only model with a charting-intensity-only model;
- permute outcome labels within the participant-held-out evaluation as a pipeline negative control; performance must return to chance and calibration must not appear spuriously improved;
- lock all feature thresholds, state fitting, preprocessing and model selection to training data.

Supportive evidence is a calibrated, reproducible positive gradient across windows/care units plus an interpretable trajectory advantage over fixed summaries. Adverse evidence is null/inverse gradients or unstable results across reasonable measurement sources. Inconclusive evidence is sparse outcomes, poor item coverage, or incompatible transitions; it should trigger a data-quality report, not a clinical recommendation.

## Clinical meaning and limits

If supportive, the result justifies prospective evaluation of an ICU-discharge monitoring-risk phenotype and possibly a locally validated decision-support tool. It does not prove that increased monitoring, delayed transfer, or altered oxygen therapy improves outcomes. If adverse, it argues against treating residual oxygen/vital instability as a generalizable discharge warning signal in this setting. If inconclusive, the next requirement is expert adjudication of planned versus unplanned ICU returns and of clinically meaningful discharge readiness, or a prospective/multicenter study.

Unavailable or essential evidence includes clinician adjudication of whether a return was planned, actual ward monitoring intensity, code-status/treatment-limitation context, bedside work of breathing, oxygen-device semantics beyond the charted item values, and external validation. CXR images and raw waveforms are not present; radiology text is available but excluded. Deidentified dates are shifted per subject, so cross-subject calendar-time claims are invalid. These gaps prevent claims about preventability, mechanism, or transportability.

## Scientific deliverable

Completion requires newly fitting the prespecified baseline and latent-state trajectory models, producing a versioned cohort-flow table, feature-support report, held-out predictions with uncertainty, calibration/discrimination comparison, state-emission/transition report, sensitivity/falsification results, and an evidence-bounded conclusion tied to those outputs. Readiness or code execution alone is not completion.

## Alternatives not chosen and revisit criteria

- A large GRU/transformer sequence model is deferred, not prohibited: revisit only if the latent-state model has adequate hourly support, the baseline-versus-state comparison reveals a real unresolved ordering question, and the solver budget permits a predeclared neural sensitivity analysis. A neural model must use the same split/outcome and add interpretability/uncertainty analysis.
- A treatment-effect emulation of ICU discharge timing is not chosen because discharge is a clinician-selected intervention with unmeasured goals-of-care and planned-procedure confounding; expert adjudication and richer treatment data would be required.
- A text/radiology multimodal model is not chosen because CXR images are unavailable and note timing/clinical semantics are not reliable enough for this primary question.
- Seeds 03, 04, 07, and 09 should be revisited if measured GFR, microcirculation, source-control/adjudication, or ischemia labels become available.
