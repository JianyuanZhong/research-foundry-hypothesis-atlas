# Does laboratory surveillance mean something different around documented support transitions?

Status: Episode 47 substantive child of assessed-valid [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic and documentation-process experiment. It is not a causal treatment study, a test-ordering intervention, a physiology study, a hospital-quality ranking, or evidence that changing support or laboratory surveillance improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported before a new fit is limited to data provenance and availability: the frozen eICU snapshot contains patient-linked laboratory result/revision offsets, recorded vital trajectories, structured treatment and respiratory-care event fields, care-plan EOL offsets, recorded ICU disposition and hospital identifiers. A bounded read-only audit found broad recorded support coverage in the correctly ordered first-stay cohort. No new outcome model was fitted in this episode.

The parent tested whether revision-safe laboratory observation history adds information after recorded vitals and documented organ-support activity. A clinically consequential interpretation gap remains: the same increase in laboratory observation may have two very different meanings.

1. It may be an early-warning process that retains information about later recorded death in patients without a recent documented support event, after measured vital state and care context are included.
2. It may be a workflow marker concentrated around a recorded ventilation/vasopressor/other support event, where laboratory review and support documentation co-occur and the apparent signal is not independent patient information.

The unresolved claim is therefore:

> At ICU-relative minute 360, does the revision-safe laboratory-process increment for later recorded ICU death differ by an outcome-blind, pre-landmark documented-support context, and does any context-specific increment survive final-bin masking, support-timing permutation, endpoint-specificity checks, and hospital-held-out evaluation?

This matters because a future silent review system would have different clinical implications depending on the answer. A signal limited to recorded support-event documentation should be interpreted as a care-process cue and not as an independent biochemical warning. A signal that persists among patients without a recent documented support event, after recorded physiology and care context, could justify a narrowly targeted prospective silent-review study. Neither result identifies who should receive a test, proves occult injury, or supports changing treatment.

The substantive advance over the parent is a preregistered mechanism/subgroup test. The parent asks whether laboratory-process information survives support adjustment on average. This child asks where the residual information lives, using a support context defined before the final 60 minutes so that a positive result cannot be explained solely by near-landmark co-documentation. It does not add cosmetic model complexity or interpret a recorded support row as delivered therapy.

## Evidence boundary and hypotheses

The eICU documentation states that the database is a multi-center relational archive of recorded vital signs, laboratory measurements, care-plan documentation, diagnoses and treatment information, and that different interfaces can make measurements absent even when care occurred. That documentation and the local catalog do not establish specimen collection/order time, delivered therapy, continuous support, complete goals-of-care status, or physiologic truth.

The primary estimand is a predictive/documentation-process estimand, not a causal estimand.

For context group g, define Delta_g as the held-out, macro-hospital mean reduction in integrated competing-risk Brier score from adding laboratory-process channels to the same vital-plus-documented-support model, evaluated only in group g:

Delta_g = Brier(B_vS; g) - Brier(B_vSL; g).

Positive Delta_g means the laboratory-process extension predicts the recorded outcome better in that group. The primary context contrast is:

I = Delta_transition - Delta_no_transition,

where transition is the prespecified recent-event group below and no_transition combines the two non-transition documentation groups only for the primary contrast if each component passes its own support gate. Report the three group estimates separately; pooling cannot conceal a sparse or adverse component.

Primary hypothesis H_context is that laboratory-process information is not homogeneous across recorded support context: the transition-group increment differs from the no-transition increment. The clinically interpretable directional alternatives are:

- Independent-review pattern: a positive Delta_no_transition remains after support adjustment and final-bin masking, and is not explained by lab-link or bin-order permutation. This supports residual patient-linked information outside recent support-event workflow.
- Transition-workflow pattern: Delta_transition is the only positive or materially larger increment, and it attenuates when the last 60 minutes or support timing is masked/permuted. This supports a workflow interpretation, not a biological or treatment interpretation.
- Broad residual pattern: both groups have positive, uncertainty-compatible increments with a stable interaction contrast. This supports context-dependent recorded-data information but not a single clinical policy.
- Null: I is compatible with zero and all group increments are compatible with no meaningful improvement.

The null for the primary test is no context interaction after the parent channels and fit-only preprocessing. A high overall model discrimination without a context interaction is not support for H_context.

## Exact context phenotype

Construct context from outcome-blind recorded support information through minute 300 only. Support categories and eligibility are frozen from the development fit portion; all categories and matching strings are saved, including unmatched text.

A qualifying documented support activity is an in-window literal treatment match in treatmentstring for:

- invasive mechanical ventilation: mechanical ventilation
- non-invasive ventilation: non-invasive ventilation
- CPAP/PEEP: CPAP/PEEP therapy
- oxygen therapy: oxygen therapy
- vasopressors: vasopressors
- inotropic agent: inotropic agent
- renal-support activity: dialysis, ultrafiltration or C V V H

For treatment, use numeric treatmentoffset in [0,300] for the context and [0,360] for the full predictor representation. Do not use activeupondischarge as a time-varying state.

A respiratory documented event uses numeric respcarestatusoffset, ventstartoffset, ventendoffset, priorventstartoffset or priorventendoffset. A transition event is only the parent's conservative event definition: a respiratory ventstartoffset or ventendoffset in the specified interval, an airway-type change ordered by currenthistoryseqnum, or a change in occupied treatment-category bins. A respiratory row, airway label or treatment string is not treated as proof of continuous support, initiation, escalation, weaning, cessation, dosage, or administration.

Define three mutually exclusive groups using bins [0,60], (60,120], (120,180], (180,240], (240,300]:

- G0: no qualifying documented support activity or conservative transition by minute 300.
- G1: qualifying documented support activity by minute 300 but no conservative transition in (240,300].
- G2: a conservative documented transition in (240,300], with or without other prior support activity.

G0 means no qualifying documentation in these tables, not no physiologic support. G1 means recorded activity without a recent recorded transition, not stable organ function. G2 means a documented event pattern, not a true clinical transition. These labels are assigned without outcomes. The final predictor bin (300,360] is not used to define G2, which makes a final-60-minute mask possible.

Primary context inference is unsupported unless each G0/G1/G2 group has at least 500 eligible episodes, 20 hospitals, 200 recorded deaths and 20 alive discharges in the relevant held-out aggregation, and the treatment/respiratory overlap and codebook gates pass. If G0, G1 or G2 fails, report its count and omit the unsupported interaction rather than merge it silently. A secondary two-group contrast (G2 versus G0+G1) is allowed only if both components were independently audited and the pooled interpretation is explicitly labeled secondary.

## Models and analysis

All models use identical eligible rows, first-stay selection, outcome windows, folds, missingness handling, fit-only scaling, lab codebook, vital fields, EOL channels, support codebook and support representation.

Transparent models:

- B_v: admission context, vital state/observation burden, recorded vital trajectory, landmark laboratory state and EOL channels; no support channels and no laboratory-process timing.
- B_vS: B_v plus outcome-blind recorded support activity and conservative transition channels from treatment and respiratoryCare. InfusionDrug is sensitivity only.
- B_vSL: B_vS plus revision-safe laboratory-process channels and the parent laboratory-process-by-recorded-trajectory interaction.
- B_vSL-G: B_vSL plus main effects for G0/G1/G2 and prespecified interactions between every laboratory-process block and G2/G1 indicators. The interaction is fit as a pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazard model, not chosen by subgroup p-values.
- B_resultS and B_resultS-G: paired result-time clock sensitivity on the identical lab rows.
- Support-only, lab-only and vital-process-only matched controls quantify whether an apparent group contrast is generic observation intensity.

The primary subgroup quantities are out-of-fold Delta_G0, Delta_G1 and Delta_G2 from B_vS versus B_vSL, the interaction I, and the corresponding death calibration differences. The fitted interaction model supplies coefficient and standardized hazard contrasts, but predictive subgroup increments and uncertainty, not coefficient significance alone, determine interpretation. For fixed-review diagnostics, report capture, PPV, false-review burden and number needed to review at death-CIF thresholds 0.01, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10 within supported groups. These are label-relative diagnostics, not clinical utility or net benefit.

Each transparent model is fit on 12 cause-specific intervals with regularization, imputation, standardization, calibration and thresholds learned only in the development fit portion.

Scientifically substantive learned alternative:

- T_vSL is a one-layer six-bin GRU with separate death and discharge hazard heads and no hospital identifier. It receives exactly the same admission, vital, EOL, support, laboratory state, laboratory process, trajectory and missingness channels as B_vSL. It does not receive an additional group label; G is deterministically derived from the same support channels and is used only for preregistered subgroup evaluation.
- A parameter-matched non-temporal MLP receives the same flattened patient-level channels and is mandatory.
- The GRU tests whether persistence, within-window ordering, and cross-domain timing of a laboratory observation relative to a support event carry information lost by additive summaries. The MLP tests nonlinear summary capacity without temporal order. A within-patient six-bin order shuffle is mandatory.
- A temporal claim requires a stable held-out subgroup increment or context contrast for the GRU versus MLP that weakens under six-bin order shuffle. A GRU win without that pattern is not scientific support. The elastic-net remains the primary interpretable estimate if the learned branch is unstable, nontransportable or order-insensitive.

## Splits, uncertainty and transportability

Use five outer hospital-held-out folds, approximately 20% of hospitals each. Keep uniquepid groups together. Within each development fold, use a 75/25 hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Learn support category eligibility, transition codebooks, lab key codebook, trajectory field support, scaling, imputation, regularization, calibration, GRU stopping and thresholds only in the fit portion. No outcome-derived context, analyte selection or hospital predictor is allowed.

Primary uncertainty is 95% intervals from at least 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions; if unstable, use a prespecified hierarchical hospital/fold model and report why. Bootstrap Delta_G0, Delta_G1, Delta_G2 and I jointly, preserving the group labels and competing outcomes within sampled hospitals. Report fold-level and hospital-level group sizes, deaths, discharges, censoring, missingness, overlap, category support and regime composition. Macro-hospital estimates are primary; patient-weighted estimates are secondary.

Transportability is assessed by leave-one-surveillance-regime-out diagnostics using hospital numbedscategory, teachingstatus and region, and by per-hospital context increments. A one-hospital/interface effect is not transport support. No external validity beyond these 208 hospitals is claimed.

## Falsification and stress tests

1. Reassign laboratory-process histories within hospital and baseline-risk strata, preserving process distributions while breaking patient linkage. A context interaction that remains materially unchanged is not patient-specific evidence.
2. Permute the six laboratory-bin labels within patient, preserving total lab count, key composition, revisions and missingness. A temporal interpretation that survives is falsified.
3. Reassign recorded support histories within hospital and baseline-risk strata. Recompute G from the reassigned histories. A positive context contrast that persists after breaking support-patient linkage is a process artifact.
4. Permute support event timing/bins within patient while preserving total support-category burden. If G2 versus G0/G1 is unchanged, the claim is about burden, not transition timing.
5. Mask (300,360] laboratory, support and vital bins and refit. Because G is defined through 300, this directly tests whether the result is driven by the final predictor bin. Also repeat with early bins masked as a timing sensitivity.
6. Compare death-CIF and alive-discharge increments. A comparable or larger discharge increment indicates length-of-stay/discharge workflow rather than death-specific information.
7. Compare B_vS-B_v with B_vSL-B_vS, and with a matched vital-process-only extension. A support-only or generic observation increment as large as the laboratory increment weakens a lab-specific interpretation.
8. Run respiratoryCare-only, treatment-only and infusion corroboration analyses. Dependence on one ambiguous text category or disagreement between sources is adverse or inconclusive.
9. Repeat on common lab key/interface subsets, revision-known-only rows, and result-time versus conservative availability clocks on identical rows. Do not pool uncertain units.
10. Repeat across hospital surveillance regimes and leave one supported regime out. A contrast confined to one interface or site fails transport support.
11. Run GRU order shuffle and MLP controls. A temporal claim requires loss under order shuffle and stability across held-out hospitals.
12. Audit every row for no post-360 predictors, no activeupondischarge state inference, no invalid revision use, no hospital ID predictor, no target-fold preprocessing, no outcome-derived support eligibility and no Apache fields.

## Interpretation gates

Supportive context evidence requires:

- all cohort, row-overlap, codebook, category and group support gates pass;
- a positive, uncertainty-compatible Delta in at least one prespecified group and an uncertainty-compatible nonzero context contrast I, with group-specific estimates reported;
- the purported independent-review pattern is stable after final-bin masking, revision/interface/common-panel checks, patient-link permutation attenuation and leave-one-regime-out evaluation; or, if the transition-workflow pattern is supported, the transition concentration and its masking/permutation attenuation are internally consistent;
- the alive-discharge increment is materially smaller for a death-specific interpretation;
- the learned branch, if used to claim temporal information, beats the matched MLP with uncertainty and loses its advantage under order shuffle.

Adverse evidence includes unchanged performance after laboratory or support-link permutation; a null or reversed context contrast; an increment explained by final-bin masking; a comparable discharge increment; support-only or vital-process-only replacement; reliance on post-landmark revisions; concentration in one hospital/interface; an ambiguous treatment string; or a GRU advantage that survives order shuffle. These findings reject the proposed interpretation even if aggregate discrimination is high.

Inconclusive evidence includes a failed group event/hospital gate, inadequate overlap, sparse or ineligible support categories, intervals spanning zero and the prespecified meaningful effect, unstable hospital folds, unresolved lab interface/unit compatibility, excessive revision missingness, disagreement between support sources, nonconvergence, or a learned model that cannot be reproduced within the envelope. Inconclusive subgroup evidence does not erase a valid parent global estimate and must not be silently converted into a smaller population or a simpler claim.

It cannot establish clinical adjudication, causal benefit, clinical utility, or external validity. Stronger conclusions require timestamped orders/collections, unit-aware assays, administration-quality support records with start/stop/dose, raw waveforms, expert-adjudicated support/recovery states, an external health system, and a prospective silent-mode impact or randomized study. A negative result is evidence about this recorded-data estimand, not proof that a biological warning or treatment effect is absent.
