# Asynchronous Organ Recovery and Subsequent Deterioration in Critical Illness

## Hypothesis

After circulatory stabilization in critically ill patients, **the temporal trajectory of organ function recovery**—specifically the time to recovery of kidney and respiratory function—predicts subsequent clinical deterioration and mortality, independent of baseline severity and single-timepoint SOFA scores.

## Unresolved Question

Current critical care practice relies heavily on **single-timepoint severity assessments** (e.g., SOFA scores at admission or at stabilization) to guide prognosis and treatment decisions. However, these static measures may miss critical information about the **dynamic recovery process**.

The unresolved question is: **Does the pattern of organ recovery (rapid vs. delayed vs. discordant) carry prognostic information that single-timepoint assessments miss?**

## Clinical Importance

1. **Prognostic refinement**: Identifying patients at high risk despite appearing stable could trigger earlier escalation of monitoring and interventions.
2. **Treatment timing**: Understanding which organ recovery patterns predict deterioration could inform when to escalate or de-escalate organ support.
3. **Resource allocation**: Patients with discordant or delayed recovery patterns may require more intensive monitoring and resources.
4. **Clinical decision support**: Dynamic recovery metrics could augment static risk scores in clinical decision support systems.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

| Existing Approach | Limitation | Our Advance |
|------------------|------------|-------------|
| Single-timepoint SOFA scores | Misses temporal dynamics of recovery | Captures recovery trajectory and timing |
| Binary organ support (on/off) | Ignores when recovery occurs | Models time-to-recovery for each organ |
| Aggregate organ dysfunction scores | Cannot distinguish discordant patterns | Tests kidney vs. respiratory recovery trajectories separately |
| Admission severity alone | Cannot predict post-stabilization deterioration | Captures recovery phase deterioration risk |

## Data Bindings

### Primary Dataset: MIMIC-IV (mimic dataset)

#### Tables Used:
1. **`icu/icustays`** - ICU admission and discharge times, care units
   - Columns: `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `stay_id`, `first_careunit`, `intime`, `outtime`, `los`
   - Purpose: Define ICU stays and identify stabilization timepoints

2. **`icu/chartevents`** - ICU vital signs and measurements
   - Columns: `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `stay_id`, `caregiver_id`, `charttime`, `itemid`, `value`, `valuenum`, `valueuom`, `warning`
   - Purpose: Extract time-series of organ function measurements

3. **`icu/d_items`** - ICU item definitions
   - Columns: `itemid`, `label`, `abbreviation`, `category`, `unitname`
   - Purpose: Map chartevent items to clinical variables (e.g., creatinine, urine output, SpO2)

4. **`icu/inputevents`** - Fluid and medication inputs
   - Columns: `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `stay_id`, `starttime`, `endtime`, `itemid`, `amount`, `rate`, etc.
   - Purpose: Track fluid balance and vasoactive support

5. **`icu/outputevents`** - Urine output and other outputs
   - Columns: `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `stay_id`, `itemid`, `value`, `valueuom`
   - Purpose: Measure urine output for kidney function assessment

6. **`hosp/pharmacy`** - Pharmacy medications
   - Columns: `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `starttime`, `stoptime`, `medication`, etc.
   - Purpose: Track vasoactive support (vasopressors, vasodilators)

7. **`hosp/admissions`** - Admission and discharge information
   - Columns: `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `admittime`, `dischtime`, `deathtime`, `hospital_expire_flag`
   - Purpose: Define study population and outcomes

8. **`icu/ingredientevents`** - Detailed medication administration
   - Columns: `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `stay_id`, `starttime`, `endtime`, `itemid`, `amount`, `rate`, etc.
   - Purpose: Detailed tracking of vasoactive support initiation and cessation

### Key Variables to Extract:

1. **Circulatory Stabilization Timepoint**:
   - Defined as the time when vasoactive support (vasopressors) is discontinued or reduced below a threshold
   - Or when MAP (mean arterial pressure) remains stable for a defined period

2. **Kidney Function Recovery**:
   - Time to creatinine normalization (returns to upper limit of normal)
   - Time to urine output recovery (≥0.5 mL/kg/hr for 6 hours)
   - Duration of renal replacement therapy (RRT)

3. **Respiratory Function Recovery**:
   - Time to SpO2 ≥92% on room air or minimal O2
   - Time to discontinuation of mechanical ventilation
   - Duration of vasopressor support

4. **Outcomes**:
   - 30-day mortality
   - ICU mortality
   - Hospital mortality
   - Re-escalation of organ support within 7 days
   - Duration of ICU stay

## Scientific Approach

### Baseline Approach (Traditional)
- Use single-timepoint SOFA scores at admission or stabilization
- Compare outcomes between high vs. low SOFA groups
- Standard Cox proportional hazards or logistic regression

### Learned/Mechanical Alternative
- **Time-to-recovery modeling**: Use survival analysis to model time to each organ function recovery endpoint
- **Recovery trajectory classification**: Use machine learning to classify patients into recovery patterns (rapid, delayed, discordant)
- **Dynamic risk prediction**: Build models that incorporate both baseline severity and recovery trajectory features
- **Feature importance**: Use SHAP values or similar to identify which recovery metrics contribute most to prediction

### Comparison
The learned approach should:
1. Achieve comparable or better discrimination (AUC) than baseline models
2. Provide interpretable recovery metrics that correlate with outcomes
3. Identify patient subgroups with discordant recovery patterns that have poor outcomes despite stable SOFA scores

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **falsified** if:
1. Recovery trajectory metrics do not independently predict outcomes after adjusting for baseline severity
2. Single-timepoint SOFA scores perform as well as or better than recovery trajectory metrics
3. No specific recovery patterns (rapid vs. delayed) show consistent prognostic value
4. Recovery trajectories do not predict deterioration after stabilization

The hypothesis will be **supported** if:
1. Time-to-recovery metrics significantly improve prediction over baseline models
2. Specific recovery patterns (e.g., delayed kidney recovery) consistently predict poor outcomes
3. Recovery trajectories identify high-risk patients missed by static assessments
4. The added predictive value is clinically meaningful (e.g., reclassification of ≥10% of patients)

## Study Population

- **Inclusion**: Patients ≥18 years old admitted to ICU with a primary diagnosis of infection or shock
- **Exclusion**: Patients who die within 24 hours of admission (no opportunity for recovery), patients without ICU admission, patients without post-stabilization follow-up data
- **Temporal boundaries**: MIMIC-IV 2016-2019 admissions (to ensure data quality and consistent practices)

## Analysis Plan

1. **Define stabilization timepoint**: Use vasoactive support cessation as the anchor
2. **Define recovery endpoints**:
   - Kidney: creatinine < upper normal limit, urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr for 6 hours
   - Respiratory: SpO2 ≥92% on room air, ventilator weaning
3. **Model recovery trajectories**: Use Cox proportional hazards or parametric survival models
4. **Compare prediction performance**: AUC, calibration, reclassification metrics vs. baseline SOFA models
5. **Identify high-risk patterns**: Patients with delayed or discordant recovery despite stable SOFA

## Required Clinical Adjudication

The following require clinical review before finalizing:

2. **Recovery endpoint thresholds** - The thresholds for kidney and respiratory recovery need clinical validation.
3. **Confounding by indication** - Patients whose organs recover may differ systematically from those whose organs don't recover in ways not captured by the data.
4. **Causal inference** - The analysis will be observational; we can establish associations but not causation.
