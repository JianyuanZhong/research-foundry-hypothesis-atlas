# Persistent versus resolving recovery-context mismatch at first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must newly construct and fit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 study of whether the persistence of a recovery-context mismatch through the final three hours before first ICU outtime distinguishes a clinically different early post-ICU pathway from a mismatch that resolves before the landmark. This is a successor to candidate-71: it preserves the parent’s exact cohort, 12-hour evidence ledger, competing-event definitions, transfer-topology bounds, observation safeguards and respiratory/hemodynamic decomposition, while resolving one remaining clinical ambiguity.

Completion requires a locked subject-level test-set package:

1. run_manifest.json: catalog, source, snapshot and schema hashes; exact archive members; verified item labels; cohort, window, persistence, tie, q and topology rules; software versions and output hashes.
2. cohort_audit.parquet: one row per subject’s earliest valid ICU stay, with join status, temporal-integrity checks, eligibility, window coverage, source-channel coverage, persistence-stratum eligibility and every exclusion.
3. hourly_features.parquet: twelve left-closed one-hour bins in [outtime-12h,outtime), with chart-time physiology, interval support overlap, procedure corroboration, observation masks, source-channel indicators and no post-outtime predictors.
4. persistence_ledger.parquet: for each domain and hour, support evidence, observed non-positive versus uninformative absence, q recoding, valid physiology measurements, instability flags, early/late-three-hour status and mutually exclusive recovery trajectory class.
5. transfer_outcome_audit.parquet: every later ICU candidate through the 48-hour/discharge boundary, every candidate bridge row, careunit topology, event ordering and lower/upper-bound labels.
6. bound_cif.parquet: 6-, 24- and 48-hour CIFs for documented-bridge ICU return (L), any later ICU return (U), in-hospital death before return, and alive discharge before return; standardized trajectory contrasts, L/U compatible intervals, bound widths and sensitivity-grid results.
7. Locked patient-level test predictions from a transparent competing-hazard baseline and an observation-aware factorized two-domain hidden semi-Markov alternative, using identical inputs, splits, outcome definitions and sensitivity scenarios. Include calibration, IPCW Brier scores, subject-bootstrap uncertainty, overlap/positivity/ESS and split stability.
8. falsification_outputs.parquet and interpretation_map.parquet: each reported claim linked to a computed estimate, horizon, event count, uncertainty interval, L/U width, aggregate-burden balance, sensitivity result and evidence limitation.

The scientific deliverable is the estimated persistence-versus-resolution contrast and its uncertainty, not a prediction leaderboard. No fitted association, model result or clinical conclusion is claimed here.

## Supported evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence is the parent’s bounded feasibility/topology audit: among 52,591 eligible adult first outtimes, 2,661 had a later ICU return within 48 hours, 2,060 had a qualifying documented non-ICU bridge and 601 did not; 294 returns had multiple bridge candidates. These counts establish that the proposed cohort and imperfectly observed return topology are computationally substantial. They do not establish an exposure association, that a return was unplanned or preventable, or that any support was intentionally withdrawn.

The parent also establishes a defensible representation: recorded support, charted physiology, observation intensity and transfer topology can be kept distinct. Its unresolved claim is whether a final-six-hour support/physiology mismatch is associated with a return-dominant versus death-dominant pathway. The remaining clinically important ambiguity is temporal:

> Among adults with a parent-defined recovery-context mismatch at first ICU discharge, does mismatch that remains observable in both the early and late three-hour segments immediately before outtime have a larger early ICU-return-compatible and/or death CIF than mismatch that is observed early but resolves before the final three hours, after accounting for aggregate burden and observation process?

The directional domain hypothesis is prespecified but noncausal: persistent respiratory mismatch is expected to be more return-dominant, while persistent hemodynamic mismatch is expected to have a relatively larger death component. The primary confirmatory contrast is persistent versus resolving mismatch within each domain; respiratory-versus-hemodynamic differences are secondary and require adequate support. This is a hypothesis about the prognostic meaning of a recorded trajectory, not an intervention effect of stopping support.

The question matters because a transient abnormality that resolves before transfer has a different discharge-review implication from instability still present at the boundary. A positive result would sharpen retrospective identification of cases warranting review and motivate prospective monitoring/adjudication; it would not authorize an automatic discharge rule.

A hypothesis is falsified if the persistent-versus-resolving compatible contrast is null or reversed after standardization to the same aggregate burden and process covariates, if it is unstable across q/coverage/topology frontiers, or if the contrast is reproduced by time-order permutation that removes persistence while preserving the same hourly values. A result positive only for any return (U) but not the documented-bridge lower endpoint (L) does not support a documented post-ICU escalation interpretation.

The landmark is icustays.outtime. Resolve a one-minute death/return tie in favor of death, a discharge tie in favor of the non-discharge event, and repeat key outputs with 0- and 5-minute tolerances.

## Recovery trajectory and persistence definitions

Use the parent’s exact 12 one-hour bins and evidence rules. Divide them into early segment E = [outtime-6h,outtime-3h), three bins; late segment F = [outtime-3h,outtime), three bins; and preceding half P = [outtime-12h,outtime-6h).

Respiratory-positive support evidence is valid icu/chartevents O2 flow >2 L/min (item 223834), FiO2 >0.21 (223835), PEEP >0 (220339), or a corroborating intubation/extubation/NIV procedure event. Procedures corroborate a transition only and are never treated as continuous ventilation. Vasoactive-positive evidence is valid overlap in icu/inputevents for norepinephrine 221906, epinephrine 221289/229617, dopamine 221662, phenylephrine 221749/229630/229631/229632, or vasopressin 222315.

For each domain and relevant segment, classify positive evidence, observed non-positive evidence, uninformative absence, contradiction or invalid evidence. Require at least two valid respiratory and two valid MAP observation bins in each parent half (P and [outtime-6h,outtime)) for the primary complete-observation population. For persistence classification, additionally require at least one usable relevant physiology measurement in each of the three E bins and each of the three F bins; otherwise retain the case in the audit but classify it as coverage-inadequate, not resolved. Recode only uninformative absence under q in {0,.10,.25,.50,.75,1.00}; never recode observed non-positive values.

A domain is recently withdrawn when it is positive in P and has no positive evidence in [outtime-6h,outtime), subject to q and adequate evidence. Durable recorded-off requires no positive evidence in both P and [outtime-6h,outtime) with adequate evidence in both. The support ledger stores domain-specific status and channel masks rather than collapsing missingness to off.

For each hourly bin, respiratory instability is present when SpO2 <90% (item 220277) or respiratory rate >=30/min (220210); hemodynamic instability is present when MAP <65 mmHg using arterial MAP 220052 or non-invasive MAP 220181. If multiple measurements occur, preserve raw values and define the bin flag using the prespecified valid-value rule; do not count missing as stable. Report valid denominators, measurement counts, source channel, and maximum/mean burden as secondary summaries.

Within each domain, require parent mismatch eligibility: domain support is recently withdrawn or durable-off and corresponding instability burden is above the parent’s locked threshold, defined as more than one abnormal bin among the six bins in [outtime-6h,outtime). The parent’s any-abnormal-bin and more-than-two-abnormal-bin thresholds remain sensitivities.

New primary trajectory labels:

1. persistent_mismatch: parent mismatch eligibility, with at least one abnormal relevant bin in E and at least one in F;
2. resolving_mismatch: parent mismatch eligibility, at least one abnormal relevant bin in E, and zero abnormal relevant bins in F;
3. late_onset_mismatch: zero abnormal relevant bins in E and at least one in F; retain separately and never fold into resolving;
4. persistent_multisystem: both respiratory and hemodynamic domains meet persistent_mismatch;
5. other/unclassifiable: active support, discordant or uninformative support, contradictory/invalid evidence, insufficient bin coverage or no parent mismatch.

For mutually exclusive primary reporting, assign a hierarchy: persistent multisystem first; then persistent respiratory-only; persistent hemodynamic-only; resolving respiratory-only; resolving hemodynamic-only; late-onset; and other/unclassifiable. Do not use the hierarchy to erase the domain-specific ledger. Primary pairwise estimands are within-domain persistent versus resolving mismatch, and a pooled contrast only if the two domains are prespecifiedly standardized to the same domain mix. The parent’s concordant-recovery comparison remains a secondary external reference, not a replacement for the new within-mismatch question.

The primary persistence definition is based on presence in both three-hour segments rather than a point value at outtime. Sensitivity definitions are abnormality in at least two bins per segment, burden >1/3 in each segment, a 6-hour rolling persistence rule, and a continuous interaction between early burden, late burden and domain. Every definition reports coverage and cases excluded for inadequate observation.

## Estimands and competing outcomes

For trajectory group g and horizon h in {6,24,48} hours, estimate standardized first-event CIFs:

- L_g(h): later ICU return preceded by a qualifying documented non-ICU bridge;
- U_g(h): any later ICU return;
- D_g(h): in-hospital death before return;
- A_g(h): alive hospital discharge before return.

These are observed/proxy trajectory associations, not causal estimands. Standardize persistent and resolving groups to the common eligible covariate/observation distribution using the predeclared full model; show raw and standardized results. Covariates include parent phenotype components, early and late aggregate physiology burdens, support-channel status, observation masks/row counts, transfer-density process measures, admission context, age, gender, anchor-year group and ICU LOS, with only prespecified support-by-mismatch and early-by-late burden interactions.

For any two groups A and B, report the nonparametrically compatible interval for an unobserved transition-compatible ICU escalation contrast:
[ L_A(h) - U_B(h), U_A(h) - L_B(h) ].
Do not report a midpoint as truth. Report interval width, whether it crosses zero, and separate U, L, D and A contrasts. Bootstrap the bound operation inside each subject-level bootstrap replicate. Use 95% percentile or BCa intervals as predeclared by the solver, but report which choice was used; do not substitute a p-value for an uncertainty package. Preserve results under q, observation coverage, threshold, tie and bridge-topology scenarios.

The primary directional gate is that persistent versus resolving mismatch should have a positive compatible interval for ICU-return-compatible escalation or, if the interval crosses zero, the study must not call persistence supported. A domain-specific return-dominant pattern additionally requires a larger standardized return contrast than death contrast with uncertainty and overlap adequate for that comparison. Death-dominant is a descriptive competing-event pattern and never evidence of treatment limitation.

## Transparent baseline

Fit pooled one-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards for L return, U return, death and alive discharge, plus a separate aggregate-return model. Use the same 12 hourly inputs, trajectory labels, raw/summary physiology, support ledger, observation masks, source-channel indicators, admission context, demographics, ICU LOS and prespecified interactions. The transparent baseline may use segment-level counts and indicators (early burden, late burden, support status and persistence label), but no post-landmark features or unregistered feature search.

Use nested models:

- physiology/support and early/late burden only;
- observation/mask/process only;
- full prespecified model;
- full model with persistence label versus full model with aggregate six-hour burden only.

Standardize locked test predictions to the common primary population and calculate group CIFs, absolute contrasts, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier score, event counts, overlap, positivity and ESS at 6/24/48 hours. Use subject bootstrap and fixed split seeds 17, 29 and 43. The baseline is the primary estimand engine because it directly estimates observable competing-event CIFs and preserves L/U partial identification. It is intentionally limited: segment summaries do not represent all within-hour ordering, duration or cross-domain transition structure.

## Scientifically informative alternative

Fit an observation-aware factorized two-domain HSMM on the identical 12 hourly bins and exact source ledger. Respiratory and hemodynamic latent states have explicit duration and persistence/withdrawal transitions; emissions use only the same physiology, support and procedure-corroboration variables; a separate observation model uses masks, row counts and source-channel availability. The post-landmark component has ICU, documented non-ICU and unknown-location topology states, with observable hazards aligned to L, U, death and discharge.

Fit state-transition, duration and regularization parameters on train/validation only; lock test predictions and the q/coverage/topology grid before test evaluation. Evaluate observable outcomes with exactly the same CIF, calibration, IPCW Brier, bootstrap, overlap, ESS and split-stability criteria as the baseline. Estimate persistent-state probabilities and duration distributions only as model-based sensitivity outputs. A latent transition-compatible hazard must be clipped/rejected if outside [L,U]; a latent state is never called ground truth.

The alternative can reveal whether persistent versus resolving mismatch corresponds to a stable duration-dependent physiological/support state or merely two separated chart observations; whether respiratory and hemodynamic mismatch durations differ; and whether observation intensity explains the apparent resolution/persistence contrast. The baseline loses this information by reducing the hourly path to predeclared segment summaries. Agreement on observable L/U/death/discharge contrasts is evidence of representation robustness; disagreement is an answer about model dependence, not permission to select the favorable method. No fitted result is claimed.

## Falsification, sensitivity and interpretation gates

Pre-register:

- source/schema/archive/hash and timestamp audits; no predictor after outtime; no subject leakage;
- preservation of all 12 bins and explicit missingness; no missing-as-stable rule;
- within-window bin-order permutation separately for physiology and support, preserving the same values and aggregate burden where possible;
- a time-reversal/order-destruction control that removes persistence while retaining six-hour burden;
- storetime substitution/negative control, which may diagnose delayed documentation but cannot carry clinical signal;
- physiology/support-only, observation/process-only and full-model contrasts;
- removal of procedure corroboration and one-channel-at-a-time analyses;
- q grid, segment coverage thresholds, instability thresholds, 6-hour versus 12-hour window, 3-hour versus 2-hour/rolling persistence rules;
- 0/5-minute event ties, bridge duration 0/15/30/60 minutes, careunit-only versus interval bridges, discharge exclusion and ICU-unit matching;
- transfer-careunit permutation, +6-hour transfer-time shift and pre-landmark pseudo-return topology control;
- discharge as censoring versus competing event;
- first-careunit, admission-type, anchor-year, ICU-LOS and transfer-density strata;
- event counts, positivity, ESS, L/U widths, calibration and split stability for every primary phenotype/horizon;
- HSMM duration stability, latent-state entropy and envelope compliance.

Adverse/falsifying evidence includes a null or reversed persistent-versus-resolving compatible interval; a signal that vanishes after balancing aggregate burden and observation process; persistence after order destruction; dependence on one chart channel, threshold or bridge rule; only a U signal with null L; non-positivity; large L/U intervals; or latent estimates outside the observable envelope.

Supportive evidence requires adequate events and overlap, a predeclared directional compatible interval with a subject-bootstrap 95% interval excluding zero, stability across q/coverage/topology frontiers, a coherent domain-specific competing-event pattern, and baseline/HSMM agreement on observable outcomes with no positive permutation control. It remains a retrospective association and discharge-review hypothesis generator, not a validated bedside rule.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse resolving/persistent strata, wide intervals or L/U widths, high latent entropy, unstable domain mix, poor overlap, method disagreement without a predeclared resolution, or insufficient final-three-hour coverage. A positive result with wide compatible intervals is not supportive merely because a point estimate is positive.

Interpretation map:

- Positive L and U stable across topology: supports an association between persistent recorded mismatch and aggregate/bridge-observed early return, but not unplannedness, preventability, intent or causality.
- Positive U with null L: supports at most an aggregate return association; reject the stronger documented-escalation interpretation.
- Positive D: indicates a competing death association; it does not establish treatment limitation, delayed rescue, discharge error or inappropriate care.
- Loss of the contrast after order destruction, with adequate event support: supports persistence/order as informative; failure to lose it means aggregate burden or process may explain it.
- Method disagreement or latent entropy: report as model-dependent/inconclusive rather than choosing the favorable output.

## Clinical evidence limits

The trajectory is a proxy for recorded treatment/documentation and physiology. It does not prove intentional support withdrawal, true absence of support, discharge readiness, monitoring adequacy, deterioration, reason for return, planned versus unplanned transfer, preventability or a causal effect of discharge. MIMIC lacks validated ICU-discharge adjudication, treatment-limitation/goals-of-care labels, ward/intermediate-care monitoring, oxygen-device detail, bedside deterioration labels, raw waveforms, MIMIC-CXR images and external validation. Discharge notes and detail rows are used only to quantify documentation availability; local text processing is not a validated detector for these concepts. Timestamp shifts preserve within-subject intervals but invalidate cross-subject calendar comparisons.

A stronger conclusion requires expert adjudication of support and discharge intent/readiness, complete ward and transfer monitoring, reason/plannedness review and external validation; a causal discharge-policy claim requires another design. A positive retrospective result can justify prospective review of persistence at transfer, not clinical action by itself.
