# Hypothesis: Discordant Multi-Organ Functional Reserve Predicts Worse Outcomes After Acute Illness

## Scientific Question

**Primary hypothesis:** Among patients with acute illness (infection, shock, or other critical conditions), those exhibiting *discordant* multi-organ functional reserve—defined as heterogeneous depletion across organ systems (e.g., depleted muscle strength with preserved kidney function, or depleted cardiac function with preserved muscle strength)—experience worse outcomes than those with uniform reserve depletion (all organs depleted) or uniform preservation (all organs preserved).

**Secondary hypothesis:** The pattern of discordance (which organs are depleted vs. preserved) predicts specific types of adverse outcomes (e.g., cardiac-depleted discordance predicts cardiovascular complications; muscle-depleted discordance predicts infection complications).

## Clinical Importance

1. **Risk stratification**: Current prognostic tools rely heavily on chronological age and conventional severity scores (SOFA, APACHE II) that may not capture heterogeneous reserve depletion. This hypothesis tests whether discordant reserve patterns provide additional risk information.

2. **Precision intervention**: If specific discordant patterns predict specific complications, interventions could be targeted to the depleted organ systems.

3. **Beyond frailty scores**: Existing frailty indices aggregate multiple measures into a single score, losing information about which systems are depleted. This hypothesis tests whether the *pattern* of depletion matters, not just the overall burden.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

**Closest prior work:**
- Frailty indices and single-organ reserve measures have been studied separately but not in combination
- Biological aging clocks (epigenetic, proteomic) predict outcomes but don't distinguish between organ-specific reserve
- Organ-specific reserve measures (e.g., grip strength for muscle, eGFR for kidney) haven't been tested for their *discordant* patterns

**Novelty:**
- This is the first systematic test of whether *discordant* (heterogeneous) reserve depletion predicts worse outcomes
- Tests the mechanistic question of whether having "one good organ" provides a buffer against deterioration
- Extends beyond chronological age to test whether the *pattern* of reserve matters, not just the average

## Main Variables

### Exposure: Discordant Multi-Organ Reserve Pattern

**Reserve domains measured:**
1. **Muscle reserve:** Grip strength (`26410-0.0` in UKB), lean mass percentage
2. **Cardiac reserve:** Ejection fraction, BNP, heart rate variability
3. **Renal reserve:** eGFR (creatinine-based), cystatin C-based eGFR
4. **Metabolic reserve:** BMI, albumin, inflammatory markers (CRP, WBC)

**Discordance metric:**
- For each patient, calculate the "discordance score" as the coefficient of variation (CV) of the standardized reserve scores across domains
- Low discordance (CV < 0.1): all domains similarly depleted or all preserved
- High discordance (CV > 0.3): heterogeneous pattern (some domains depleted, others preserved)

**Discordance categories:**
1. **Uniform depletion:** All domains below threshold (e.g., grip strength <50% predicted, eGFR <60, EF <50%, BMI <18.5)
2. **Uniform preservation:** All domains above threshold (e.g., grip strength >120% predicted, eGFR >90, EF >70%, BMI >25)
3. **Cardiac-muscle discordance:** Cardiac depleted (EF <50%) with muscle preserved (grip >100% predicted), or vice versa
4. **Renal-muscle discordance:** Renal depleted (eGFR <60) with muscle preserved, or vice versa
5. **Mixed discordance:** Heterogeneous pattern across ≥2 non-adjacent domains

**Primary outcome:**

**Secondary outcomes:**

## Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis

**Model:** Cox proportional hazards regression with time-varying covariates

**Hypothesis test:**
- Compare hazard ratios for all-cause mortality across discordance categories
- Test interaction: Is the hazard ratio for high discordance vs. low discordance different across specific discordance patterns?

**Expected findings (if hypothesis supported):**
- High discordance (heterogeneous depletion) has higher hazard ratio than uniform preservation
- Specific discordance patterns have higher hazard ratios than uniform depletion
- Discordance effect persists after adjusting for chronological age and comorbidities

### Secondary Analyses

**1. Organ-specific discordance:**
- Test whether cardiac-muscle discordance predicts cardiovascular outcomes
- Test whether renal-muscle discordance predicts infection outcomes

**2. Temporal dynamics:**
- Analyze whether discordance develops over time (initial vs. sustained)
- Test whether early discordance predicts later deterioration

**3. Mechanistic exploration:**
- Test whether discordance mediates the relationship between frailty and outcomes
- Test whether specific discordance patterns are associated with specific biomarkers of inflammation or organ stress

### Baseline Comparison

**Simple baseline:**
- Logistic regression comparing discordant vs. non-d discordant patients on outcomes
- Check for confounding

**Advanced baseline:**
- Propensity score matching on discordance pattern
- Inverse probability weighting

## Falsification Criteria

**Hypothesis will be falsified if:**
1. Discordance pattern does not predict outcomes after adjusting for chronological age and comorbidities
2. Uniform depletion and uniform preservation have similar hazard ratios (i.e., the pattern doesn't matter, only the average burden)
3. Discordance effect is entirely explained by unmeasured confounding (e.g., socioeconomic status, healthcare access)

**Hypothesis will be supported if:**
1. High discordance has significantly higher hazard ratio than uniform preservation
2. Specific discordance patterns have distinct hazard ratios for specific outcomes
3. Discordance effect persists after comprehensive adjustment

**Hypothesis will be inconclusive if:**
1. Discordance effect is borderline significant (p > 0.05)
2. Discordance effect is only present in subgroups (e.g., only in elderly, only in certain comorbidity profiles)
3. Discordance effect is attenuated after comprehensive adjustment

## Method Comparison

### Baseline Approach
**Method:** Logistic regression comparing discordant vs. non-d discordant patients on outcomes

**What it tests:** Simple association between discordance and outcomes

**What it misses:**
- Temporal dynamics of discordance
- Specific patterns vs. overall burden
- Mechanistic insights (which organs matter most)

### Substantive Alternative: Mechanistic Model
**Method:** Structural equation modeling or causal mediation analysis

**What it tests:** Whether discordance operates through specific pathways (e.g., inflammation, immune dysfunction)

**What it reveals:**
- Mechanistic pathways from discordance to outcomes
- Potential mediators (e.g., inflammation, immune dysfunction)
- Intervention targets (which pathways to modulate)

## Clinical Adjudication Required

**Missing evidence:**
1. **Causal inference:** Observational study cannot establish causality. RCTs of interventions targeting specific discordance patterns would be needed.

2. **Measurement validation:** Need to validate reserve measurements in critical illness populations (UKB is community-dwelling, MIMIC is ICU-admitted).

3. **Discordance thresholds:** Optimal cut-offs for "depletion" vs. "preservation" in each domain need clinical validation.

4. **Intervention testing:** Whether interventions targeting depleted organ systems improve outcomes requires RCT evidence.
