# UKB metabolic change after established disease: selection-process repair

## Episode-12 deliverable and substantive change

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent has a strong matched route/death comparison and a clinically meaningful one-domain-to-second-domain question. It nevertheless describes a repeat-panel selection model as pre-panel while including whether a participant was alive and free of a second domain before panel 1. Those are post-panel states and can leak the very future process being diagnosed into a purported measurement-selection model.

This child preserves the parent’s primary conditional estimand and repairs that selection description. The primary analysis is explicitly conditional on being alive, still in the same single coded domain, and completely observed at panel 1. It separately audits (1) survival/no-second-domain to panel 1 and (2) panel-1 measurement availability among those eligible at panel 1. A selection-weighted sensitivity models panel observation using only panel-0 information among the panel-1-eligible population; it is not described as transport to the full UKB cohort. No post-panel state is an input to an exposure, selection, or outcome model.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

Available evidence supports the narrower claim that baseline metabolic indices are associated with later cardiometabolic disease and that multistate models can describe disease progression. A current UKB study found associations of baseline atherogenic indices with incidence/progression of cardiometabolic multimorbidity and used multistate models, but its abstract describes participants free of baseline type 2 diabetes, coronary heart disease and stroke. It does not establish whether dated within-person change after an existing cardiometabolic domain adds prognostic information, or whether that information differs by the route to the second domain. The local expert seed UKB-09 raises the same unresolved change-beyond-current-level question and is explicitly untested.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UKB participants aged 40–69 with exactly one coded cardiometabolic domain (diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or chronic kidney disease) already present at biomarker panel 0 and still the same single domain at panel 1, annualized worsening in HbA1c, triglycerides and/or HDL predicts the first transition to a second domain after a prespecified one-year lag, beyond panel-1 levels, existing-domain identity, disease duration and prespecified covariates. The incremental association may differ by supported transition route, but should not be reproduced for acute appendicitis.

This is a prognostic/internal-prediction hypothesis. It is not a causal effect of changing a biomarker, proof that monitoring improves outcomes, a treatment recommendation, or a chart-adjudicated disease-onset claim.

## Phenotype, time zero and population

Before fitting, freeze a codebook with normalized case/whitespace and explicit prefixes:

- diabetes: ICD-10 E10–E14; ICD-9 250;
- cardiovascular disease: ICD-10 I20–I25, I50, I60–I69; ICD-9 410–414, 428, 430–438;
- chronic kidney disease: ICD-10 N18/N19; ICD-9 585/586;
- negative control: acute appendicitis, ICD-10 K35; ICD-9 540.

Scan every diagnosis/date suffix in both coding systems. Pair a code only with the date at the identical suffix. A code-only value, date-only value, invalid date, blank/nonfinite value, or unrecognized code is audit-only and cannot create an event. A domain’s first event is the minimum valid paired date across all positions. These are first recorded administrative codes, not confirmed clinical onset. Parse death separately; when a disease and death share a date, death wins in the primary convention and the reverse tie is a sensitivity.

Let `d0=53-0.0` and `d1=53-1.0`, cross-checked against the corresponding assessment dates after ISO normalization. Include age 40–69 inclusive, valid sex, `d1>d0`, a 365–3,650-day panel gap, and finite HbA1c/TG/HDL at both main instances. Require main and assessment date agreement. At d0, exactly one domain must be present with its first paired code date on or before d0; the participant must be alive and have no second domain by d0. At d1, require the same single domain, no second domain, no death, and a complete valid panel. The primary exposure cohort therefore cannot have a disease transition or death during the measurement interval.

Disease duration is `(d1 - first-domain-date)/365.25` and is included in both models. Define the one-year lag landmark `o=d1+365 days`. Exclude participants with a second-domain event or death in (d1,o]; the primary risk set is exactly those alive with one domain at o. Follow from strictly after o to the first second-domain event, death, the fixed administrative boundary of the frozen outcome snapshot, or five years. Death is absorbing and competing. A 0-day-start analysis and 2-/5-year lags are labeled sensitivities; they do not silently replace the primary estimand. A same-day event/death tie uses death first primarily and the reverse rule secondarily.

For marker j, calculate annualized change `g_j=(x_j1-x_j0)/((d1-d0)/365.25)`; reverse HDL so positive means worsening. Standardize using fit-partition means/SDs only. Creatinine/cystatin C are available at instances 0/1 and are a prespecified secondary renal extension, not a substitute for the primary three-marker question. No instances 2/3 are invented.

## Selection-process audit and estimands

Define the panel-0 eligible process `R0) using only information known by d0: age, sex, panel-0 markers, baseline domain history through d0, panel-0 covariates and alive/no-second-domain status at d0.

Then report two separate processes:

1. `C1=1`: alive and still without a second domain at d1, among R0. Model this landmark survival/state-retention process with panel-0 predictors only for diagnostics. It is not used as a feature in either prognostic model.
2. `M1=1`: complete valid panel-1 date and core markers with main/assessment date agreement, among C1=1. Model measurement availability with panel-0 predictors only. The primary estimand remains conditional on M1=1 and the one-domain risk set at o. If positivity is adequate, stabilized inverse-probability-of-measurement weights, truncated at the 1st/99th percentile, are a sensitivity targeting the C1 population with measured panel-0 covariates. They do not transport to participants who died, acquired a second domain, lacked panel 1 for unmeasured reasons, or the full UKB population.

Report flow and positivity by age, sex, baseline domain, panel-0 risk strata and panel-gap strata. Never include `C1), d1 outcomes, or any post-panel state as a predictor of M1. Latest covariates no later than d1 are BMI, averaged BP, smoking, center, age, sex, existing-domain identity, panel gap and explicit missingness indicators; fit-partition medians/modes handle missing covariates. This repair makes survival selection and observation selection visible rather than conflating them.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

Both models use exactly the same primary cohort, route/death state space, origin, endpoint, censoring, preprocessing, weights, participant split and locked evaluation.

M0 is the interpretable current-level baseline: cause-specific Cox hazards for each supported existing-domain → absent-domain route and for death. Inputs are panel-1 HbA1c, TG, HDL, existing-domain identity, disease duration, age, sex, latest BMI/BP/smoking/center, panel gap and missingness indicators. Use fit-frozen restricted cubic splines and explicit route-support/separation checks. Numerical integration/Aalen–Johansen combines route/death hazards into five-year competing-risk cumulative incidence for any second domain.

M1 is the substantive route-aware temporal alternative: the identical route/death hazards, population and evaluation, adding the three dated changes to the second-domain route hazards. Prespecified change-by-existing-domain interactions are used only with adequate fit-partition support; fully route-specific change coefficients are secondary and are not reported as reliable when sparse. Dated changes do not enter the primary death hazard. Thus the primary M1–M0 contrast tests incremental information in the earlier dated panel after current levels and established-domain duration, not a different endpoint architecture or hidden death model.

This route-specific transition structure is a mechanistic alternative because it distinguishes which existing→new-domain pathway carries the signal; it can reveal route heterogeneity that an any-second-domain current-level Cox model loses. A compact two-panel recurrent/temporal-attention model was also specified before selection with exactly the same two panel values/dates, covariates, target, split, metrics and bootstrap uncertainty. It is deferred because two observed assay panels provide little sequence structure and its capacity could absorb repeat-testing indication or regression-to-the-mean artifacts. A latent measurement-error model is likewise deferred: no local replicate assay or verified assay coefficient-of-variation field identifies its variance. Revisit either alternative only after three verified dated assay panels, assay-error validation, or a bounded preregistered diagnostic demonstrates stable locked value beyond M1. This is a scientific dependency decision, not a neural-network or GPU ban.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following, subject to adequate route/event support: a worsening-direction joint change association after current levels, duration and gap; locked-test M1 improvement in Brier, calibration or net benefit with a paired 95% interval favoring improvement; no material calibration harm by sex, age or existing domain; persistence across lag, gap, missingness and panel-observation weighting sensitivities; and no comparable appendicitis association. Route heterogeneity is supportive only when its route-support rule is met and the relevant interval excludes zero. Within-state, sex-stratified and gap-bin change permutations must not reproduce the signal.

Adverse evidence is a null/reverse joint change association, loss after duration adjustment, no locked improvement or worsened calibration/net benefit, disappearance under participant-level splitting/lag/selection weighting, a comparable appendicitis association, or a permutation signal. This falsifies the incremental prognostic claim in this selected cohort, not biological harmlessness or causality.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few complete two-panel participants or second-domain events, poor measurement positivity, code/date disagreement, unsupported routes, separation, snapshot truncation, severe subgroup calibration failure, or intervals spanning meaningful benefit and no benefit. A threshold result without a specified clinical action is inconclusive for policy value.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/headers, one-to-one joins, field rules, code/date pairing, first coded dates, cohort flow, selection models, duration/gap distributions, split integrity, fits, predictions, uncertainty, calibration, Brier, decision curves, permutations and sensitivity analyses. Confirmed disease onset, treatment effects, repeat-testing indication, medication/adherence effects, clinical benefit, transport to non-repeat-tested people and external validity require clinical adjudication, additional evidence or another study.
