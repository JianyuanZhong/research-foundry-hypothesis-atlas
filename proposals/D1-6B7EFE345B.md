# Provisional hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery after current severity is accounted for

Status: provisional and untested. This repairs expert seed `[starting question]`; it is not a completed analysis or selected candidate.

## Proposed relationship

Among critically ill adults with an AKI episode who survive to a prespecified landmark, a creatinine-improving but urine-output- or fluid-status-nondiscordant recovery pattern will be associated with worse subsequent in-hospital mortality and/or persistent AKI than a concordant recovery pattern, after adjustment for current severity and treatment context. The clinically important claim is conditional: discordance may add prognostic information beyond creatinine level and trajectory at the landmark, but it is not established.

## Observed versus untested

The seed observes that creatinine can fall while urine output or fluid status does not improve. It does not establish prevalence, independence from current severity, or outcome association. Creatinine and urine output measure different dimensions, and creatinine can be affected by body composition, age, and treatment. A cited measurement study suggests creatinine and cystatin C can disagree during critical illness, but it does not establish this phenotype or outcome association.

## Smallest discriminating MIMIC analysis

Use MIMIC-IV ICU stays with an identifiable AKI episode and a fixed landmark after AKI onset (for example 48 hours), requiring survival and continued observation to the landmark. Define creatinine recovery from serial `hosp/labevents` creatinine values. Define urine-output recovery from `icu/outputevents`, and fluid-status context from `icu/inputevents` and `icu/ingredientevents`; use `icu/procedureevents` to identify renal replacement therapy and `icu/d_items`/`icu/chartevents` to resolve item labels and bedside measurements. Link `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` and `hosp/patients` for age, sex, admission type, discharge/death timing, and hospital outcomes. Restrict the primary analysis to stays with sufficient observation and explicit urine measurement; a secondary analysis will quantify how measurement availability changes the result.

The primary comparison is discordant versus concordant recovery at the landmark, with prespecified creatinine and urine-output definitions. Adjust for current creatinine, its slope, prior severity, vasopressor/diuretic and RRT context, and observation/measurement intensity. The smallest baseline is a landmark logistic or Cox model using current severity and creatinine trajectory. The alternative is a trajectory-based model using the pre-landmark sequence of creatinine, urine output, and fluid balance (for example a prespecified functional/segmented trajectory or a compact recurrent model), evaluated on the same held-out outcome. The alternative is scientifically useful only if it reveals whether ordering and co-movement add information beyond endpoint summaries; complexity alone is not justification.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support requires a reproducible association or incremental discrimination/calibration improvement in a held-out split, with direction and uncertainty reported, and sensitivity analyses showing the result is not explained solely by missing urine measurements, diuretic/RRT exposure, or current severity. Adverse evidence is no incremental signal after adjustment, or reversal under a prespecified observation-intensity analysis. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse discordant groups, unstable estimates, or inability to distinguish treatment from physiology; it motivates a narrower measurement-validation study rather than a causal claim.
