# Early Post-TACE Hepatic Deterioration as a Prognostic Marker in HCC

## Question
In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who undergo transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does a composite measure of early hepatic deterioration during the first 7 days after the procedure predict subsequent hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days, after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and post-procedure observation intensity?

## Clinical importance
TACE is a common locoregional treatment for HCC, but a subset of patients experience early hepatic deterioration that may signal a need for escalation to systemic therapy or closer surveillance. Identifying these patients could inform treatment-selection decisions and resource allocation. The unresolved question is whether the early laboratory trajectory itself carries prognostic information beyond baseline liver function, or whether it merely reflects more intensive post-procedure monitoring.

## Evidence and closest prior work
- [K1] Decraecker et al. (2026) review the limitations of Child-Pugh scoring and the potential value of dynamic liver function monitoring and ALBI grade in HCC patients.
- [K2] HCC TACE prognosis literature: existing studies often rely on static baseline liver function scores and do not capture the early post-procedure laboratory trajectory.
- [K3] ALBI dynamic monitoring: the ALBI grade (albumin, bilirubin) is a validated liver function score in HCC, but its dynamic application in the early post-TACE window is not well established.

## Design
- Population: HCC patients who undergo TACE, with complete early laboratory data (bilirubin, albumin, INR) during the first 7 days.
- Exposure: early hepatic deterioration composite (bilirubin, albumin, INR; ALT/AST excluded due to zero early rows).
- Outcome: hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days.
- Adjustment: baseline liver function, tumor burden, post-procedure observation intensity.
- Analysis: baseline logistic regression vs. time-aware survival model.
- Falsification: if the early composite does not improve prediction beyond baseline after adjustment for observation intensity, the hypothesis is falsified.
- Sensitivity: 3-day, 5-day, 10-day windows.
- Decision-curve analysis for clinically actionable treatment escalation.

## Data bindings
- HCC source files: exact procedure names, laboratory names, tables, columns, join keys, time fields.
- Sample size: 20,064 TACE patients, ~16,000 with complete early labs, 817 outcome events.

## Prognostic vs. causal distinction
This study estimates a prognostic association, not a causal treatment effect. The early hepatic deterioration composite is a prognostic marker, not a treatment.

## Sensitivity analysis
The 7-day window is tested with 3-day, 5-day, and 10-day alternatives to assess robustness.

## Decision-curve analysis
Clinically actionable treatment escalation is evaluated using decision-curve analysis to determine whether the early composite improves net benefit over baseline.
