# Plasma protein–disease association after removing a data-driven shared systemic axis: does any protein carry disease-specific signal?

Episode 9. Parent: [prior hypothesis] (episode 8, valid, mu 31.90).

## Unresolved question and clinical importance
Plasma-proteomic screens repeatedly nominate a single circulating protein (GDF15, FABP1, CHI3L1,
IL-6/CRP family) as a **preclinical, disease-specific** biomarker of one diagnosis [K2]. It is
unresolved whether such a protein–diagnosis association marks a disease-specific process (claim A)
or mainly a **shared systemic morbidity/inflammation/aging axis** that is elevated across many
diagnoses and tracks cumulative disease burden (claim B) [K3]. The distinction decides whether a
protein is a plausible disease-specific early-detection target or only a generic risk/severity
correlate — decisive for biomarker programmes. [K1] establishes that the UKB-PPP Olink platform
detects very many protein–disease associations, so the open question is not "is there an
association" but **how much of it is disease-specific rather than shared-axis**.

## Leading claim vs strongest rival
- Claim A (disease-specific): the case–control difference in protein p for disease d largely survives
  removal of a leave-one-protein-out shared axis and a flexible age model.
- Claim B (shared axis): most of the p–d association is explained by the shared cross-disease axis
  and/or flexible age, leaving little disease-specific residual.
Decisive observation: the **per-(protein,disease) attenuation fraction** A(p,d) after these
adjustments, evaluated against paired positive/negative control fixtures — not the raw association.

## Evolved design (episode 9 additions, the substantive delta vs parent)
1. **Case-count–grounded disease-inclusion rule (pre-specified).** Disease groups are ICD-10
   3-character blocks defined *before* analysis. A group enters primary analysis only if it has
   `n_cases ≥ 200` among the 53,014 (power floor) and `n_cases ≥ 50` and `n_controls ≥ 500` for
   reporting. Group definitions and counts are pinned by the bounded diagnostic `work/gen9/diag_cases_out.json`
   (launched this episode; if it does not return within the attempt, the rule stands and the solver
   must recompute the counts as a required output).
2. **Added negative-control outcomes** to attack the artifact rival: (i) a low-morbidity
   "reference" group defined by ABSENCE of all pre-specified disease blocks, and (ii) an
   external-cause ICD-10 block (V01–Y98). A shared systemic axis is expected to attenuate
   *disease* contrasts but should not create a disease-specific protein signal for external causes.
3. **Added negative-control proteins** (proteins not expected to track systemic morbidity) so that
   the shared axis S is not built only from the tested proteins.
4. **Power/identifiability precondition.** Before any attenuation is interpreted, the solver must
   report: variance of S, per-group case/control counts, and the observed vs permutation-null
   distribution of A(p,d). Permutation null = shuffle disease labels; if observed A is inside the
   permutation null, the estimator is uninformative and results are **inconclusive**, not adverse.
5. **Covariate-overlap (positivity) check** for age and sex across cases/controls per group.

## Estimand and analysis (executed by the solver, not this episode)
For each protein p and disease group d:
1. Baseline contrast Δ_base(p,d): standardized mean difference / logistic β of p between cases and
   controls, adjusted for age (`n_21003_0_0`) + sex (`n_31_0_0`); proteins rank-inverse-normal
   transformed (UKB-PPP standard).
2. Shared-axis score S_{-p}: first PC of the 2,922 remaining proteins (p held out), fit on the
   analysis sample only (no leakage).
3. Adjusted contrast Δ_adj(p,d): baseline + linear S_{-p}.
4. Flexible-age rival Δ_ageflex(p,d): baseline + natural spline in age (4 df).
5. **Attenuation fraction A(p,d) = (|Δ_base| − |Δ_adj|) / |Δ_base|**, with bootstrap CIs.
Required solver outputs: a table of Δ_base, Δ_adj, Δ_ageflex, A(p,d) with CIs for every (p,d) cell
above a pre-set baseline-signal threshold; the positive/negative control fixture results; the
permutation-null distribution; and the group counts/positivity report.

## Baselines and the substantive alternative (method exploration)
- **Simple baseline**: age+sex-adjusted per-protein disease contrast (1) — interpretable, no latent factors.
- **Rival mechanistic/statistical alternative**: leave-one-protein-out shared-axis adjustment (2–3) —
  tests whether a single latent cross-disease direction absorbs the signal.
- **Learned alternative (same question)**: elastic-net/ridge cross-disease risk score trained on the
  *other* 2,922 proteins with participant-level cross-validation; ask whether p adds incremental
  out-of-sample discrimination beyond that learned multi-protein factor. This reveals what the
  single-PC baseline loses: whether the shared structure is one linear direction or a
  multi-dimensional predictor, and whether p retains disease-specific information under a stronger
  learner. Fitting target: ΔA = A_PC − A_learned for the same (p,d) cells; split: 5-fold CV on
  participants, axis and outcome refit within each training fold; evaluation: calibration and
  out-of-sample log-loss/Brier vs the PC baseline; resource budget: CPU tabular, ~1–3 h
  (unverified estimate), no GPU required. Selection reason to use the learner: it is the only
  branch that can falsify single-axis sufficiency; it is deferred to the solver because it does not
  change the identified estimand for the baseline comparison.

## Falsification criteria (pre-specified)
- **Supportive of claim A (disease-specific)**: a prespecified set of proteins (e.g. GDF15, FABP1,
  CHI3L1) with A(p,d) bootstrap CI excluding 0.5 (i.e. <50% of the contrast is shared-axis) for
  their target disease, *and* the negative-control outcomes show low/ null disease-specific signal.
- **Supportive of claim B (shared axis)**: the same canonical proteins have A(p,d) CIs consistent
  with ≥0.5 attenuation, and the shared axis alone reproduces most case–control separation.
- **Adverse / estimator failure**: observed A indistinguishable from the permutation null (noise),
  or the positive control (case labels built from S) fails to attenuate near-completely.
- **Inconclusive**: insufficient cases (<200), failed positivity, or permutation-null overlap.

## What each outcome would / would not establish
- A supportive (specific) result would establish that at least some protein–disease associations
  are not reducible to one cross-disease linear axis in this cross-sectional sample. It would **not**
  establish causation, incidence, or that the protein precedes disease onset.
- A supportive (shared-axis) result would bound the disease-specific reading of the canonical
  biomarkers. It would **not** prove the shared axis is biological rather than a severity/assay
  correlate, because temporal ordering is unavailable.

## Required additional data / clinical adjudication
Temporal ordering (an Olink-namespace draw date or a documented crosswalk to the `eid`-namespace
tables), a documented ICD-10 block list with clinician adjudication of prevalent vs incident states,
and out-of-sample replication cohort(s). These are explicitly out of scope here.
