TITLE
From elapsed days to observed readiness: a risk-set study of when to resect HCC after TACE

UNRESOLVED DECISION, HYPOTHESIS, AND ADVANCE
The clinical decision is not whether day 60 has passed; it is whether an HCC patient who remains a resection candidate after TACE should undergo hepatectomy now or continue short reassessment. The prespecified hypothesis is that, at weekly post-TACE decision landmarks from day 21 through day 84, resection within the next 7 days while the most recent bilirubin/INR/albumin panel shows unresolved or worsening hepatic physiology is associated with worse day-5-to-7 postoperative biochemical hepatic recovery than resection after documented stabilization/recovery. Among patients already meeting the laboratory readiness rule, the secondary strategy contrast is resection within 7 days versus deferral for 8–21 days; the hypothesis there is non-inferiority of prompt surgery for biochemical recovery, because once physiology has recovered an arbitrary additional wait may not improve safety and may delay tumor control.

This child replaces the parent's arbitrary >28–60 versus >60–90 split with repeated risk-set landmarks, measures rather than assumes physiologic readiness, models continuous time since TACE, and includes people still unresected at each landmark rather than defining eligibility by eventual surgery. It remains an observational association study, not evidence that a readiness rule or delay is causally beneficial.

WHY IMPORTANT / WHAT EVIDENCE ALREADY SUPPORTS
TACE may injure non-tumor liver, whereas unnecessary delay may permit progression or loss of resectability. Na et al. studied 435 patients with huge HCC, only 33 receiving preoperative TACE, and reported more morbidity, pleural effusion, and Clavien-Dindo III–IV complications without overall- or recurrence-free-survival improvement; the full text also describes nonrandom clinical selection and tumor features strongly related to prognosis (PMID 41000227; PMCID PMC12457797; DOI 10.4174/astr.2025.109.3.194; frozen full text [source checksum] inspected). This supports concern about perioperative morbidity and confounding, not an optimal interval or a validated readiness criterion.

Strongest claim presently supported by the Harbor evidence: the snapshot contains dated HCC/TACE/resection records and substantial longitudinal bilirubin, INR, and albumin coverage, making a dynamic readiness association computable. In a complete feasibility scan, 737 selected first-resection patients had a last-TACE interval >14–120 days; 568 had both pre-TACE and preoperative bilirubin/INR/albumin triplets, 583 had serial measurements of all three markers, and 567 had postoperative day-5-to-7 bilirubin plus INR. A deliberately broad non-eventual-resection screen found 19,112 HCC patients with a TACE record, 2,873 with a same-calendar-day bilirubin/INR/albumin panel at a first day-14-to-90 unresected assessment, and 597, 46, 96, and 2,134 respectively with resection in 0–7, 8–21, >21 days, or no later recorded resection. These are feasibility counts, not treatment-effect results, and the broad screen lacks imaging confirmation of resectability.

Untested claim: observed laboratory recovery/stability identifies a clinically meaningful post-TACE decision state and modifies the association between prompt resection and postoperative hepatic recovery. A causal recommendation requires prospective validation or a stronger multicenter target trial with treatment intent, resectability, complete follow-up, and surgical physiology.

TARGET POPULATION, TIME ZERO, AND REPEATED LANDMARKS
Unit of analysis is a patient-landmark risk set; inference uses one or more eligible episodes per patient with patient-clustered uncertainty.

Include adults >=18 with explicit HCC, an adjudicated TACE episode, and at least one institutional post-TACE assessment. Create weekly landmarks L=21, 28, 35, ..., 84 exact days after TACE. At L require: alive as far as the institutional record can show; no recorded resection, transplant, repeat TACE, systemic HCC therapy, or definitive competing local therapy before L; no report available by L documenting extrahepatic progression or unresectability; and a bilirubin/INR/albumin panel whose three latest values were each measured within L-7 to L. The gold-standard primary cohort further requires a hepatobiliary surgeon/radiologist, blinded to outcomes, to determine from records available by L that resection was still a clinically plausible option. Patients are not required ever to undergo resection.

Exclude emergency embolization for ruptured HCC unless separately adjudicated, transplant/donor cases, impossible chronology, outside TACE with unknown date, and values failing laboratory plausibility/unit review. A repeat TACE or competing treatment ends that episode; a later TACE may create a new episode if independently eligible. To avoid multiplying highly correlated choices, the primary analysis randomly selects one eligible landmark per episode after fixing a reproducible seed; a secondary pooled risk-set analysis uses all eligible landmarks and patient/episode-clustered standard errors.

PHYSIOLOGIC READINESS STATE, DEFINED BEFORE TREATMENT ASSIGNMENT
Reference values B0, I0 and A0 are the latest valid total bilirubin, INR and albumin in [-7 days,0) before the index TACE. At each landmark use the latest marker values in [L-7,L]. Estimate marker slopes using all valid post-TACE measurements through L over the preceding 14 days, requiring >=2 distinct calendar days for a slope; where a slope is unavailable, readiness is “not classifiable,” not “unready.”

The primary continuous readiness burden is a prespecified standardized score oriented toward worse reserve: log(BL/B0) + (IL-I0)/0.10 - log(AL/A0), each component winsorized at the 1st/99th percentile and then z-standardized within calendar era. The score, its three components, and time since TACE enter the model continuously; no 60-day cut is used.

For clinically interpretable strata, “recovered/stable” requires all of: BL <=1.10*B0, IL <=I0+0.10, AL >=0.90*A0; and, when a marker has serial values, no adverse 14-day trend exceeding +5% bilirubin, +0.05 INR, or -5% albumin. “Unresolved/worsening” requires at least one threshold failure; “not classifiable” is reported separately. These thresholds are hypotheses to validate, not established safety cutoffs. Sensitivities use 5%/15% recovery tolerances, absolute ALBI after unit validation, separate routine versus 急 assay names, and flexible joint marker trajectories. A prior overly strict requirement for two complete triplet days yielded only 9 classifiable readiness events among 737 selected patients; therefore the definitive design uses marker-specific slopes plus current complete panels and does not claim that strict rule is feasible.

STRATEGIES AND ESTIMANDS
At each eligible landmark assign:
A. prompt resection: qualifying liver resection starts >0 through <=7 days after L;
B. active deferral: no resection in days 0–7, with resection in days 8–21;
C. no recorded resection through day 21, retained to describe loss of resectability/competing treatment and selection, but not pooled with B as if equivalent.
Use clone-censor-weighting as a sensitivity for the 0–7 versus 8–21 strategies; censor clones at deviation, repeat TACE, systemic/local competing treatment, or last institutional contact. Because death and outside treatment are incomplete, call this a risk-set emulation rather than a fully identified target trial.

Primary estimand: among adjudicated resection-plausible landmark patients with an operation within 21 days and postoperative outcome observed, the standardized adjusted mean difference in log(maximum day-5-to-7 bilirubin / landmark bilirubin) for prompt versus deferred resection, including a prompt-by-continuous-readiness interaction. The principal scientific test is whether worse readiness burden predicts worse recovery and whether the prompt-versus-deferred contrast is more adverse at worse readiness. This is an association among patients resected within 21 days; inverse-probability-of-resection and outcome-observation weights address measured selection, while C and all unresected risk-set members expose rather than hide the conditioning mechanism.

Key secondary estimand: in the recovered/stable stratum, test whether prompt resection is non-inferior to deferral, margin 0.10 on the log bilirubin-ratio scale (ratio margin exp[0.10]=1.105), with INR concordance required. This is clinically actionable but cannot by itself establish oncologic benefit.

OUTCOMES
Primary: log(maximum total bilirubin during postoperative days 5–7 / landmark bilirubin). Require positive numeric values.
Key secondary: maximum day-5-to-7 INR minus landmark INR; day-5-to-7 albumin relative change; joint bilirubin and INR worsening; trajectories over postoperative days 1–7; length of stay from 入院时间/出院时间; and risk of remaining unresected or receiving competing therapy by landmark+21 among the full risk set. The latter is a selection/feasibility outcome, not tumor progression unless adjudicated.
Exploratory clinician-adjudicated outcomes in the index resection encounter: ISGLS PHLF and grade, encephalopathy, new/worsened ascites, biliary complication, infection/sepsis, ICU escalation, reoperation, and in-hospital death. The parent's full scan found only two cases meeting bilirubin >50 plus INR >1.5 at days 5–7, so no sparse binary biochemical proxy is primary.

BASELINES, CASE MIX, AND ANALYSIS
Report risk-set counts, readiness-marker coverage, strategy counts, missingness, distributions and standardized differences at every landmark. Baselines are: (1) the parent's crude 60-day cut reproduced only as historical context; (2) continuous restricted cubic spline of exact TACE-to-operation time; (3) current lab levels only; (4) current levels plus marker-specific slopes and pre-TACE anchors; (5) weighted risk-set model with tumor/procedure case mix.

At each landmark model prompt versus deferred/no recorded resection using only information available by L: age, sex, calendar year, department, exact days since TACE with spline, B0/I0/A0, current bilirubin/INR/albumin, marker-specific slopes and measurement intensity, creatinine, platelets, ALT, hemoglobin, number of prior TACE episodes, prior HCC treatment, cirrhosis/portal hypertension/ascites/viral hepatitis/diabetes/renal disease, and adjudicated latest imaging tumor diameter, multiplicity, viable disease/response, macrovascular invasion, rupture, extrahepatic disease, cirrhosis and ascites. For postoperative outcome models additionally adjust for resection extent and approach extracted from 手术, but report a model without these potentially treatment-mediated surgical choices. Use stabilized inverse-probability weights with truncation at 1st/99th percentiles, doubly robust weighted regression, patient-clustered bootstrap CIs, positivity diagnostics, effective sample sizes, and calendar-era/site-service fixed effects if identifiable. Multiple imputation is allowed for baseline covariates, never for readiness state or primary outcome.

Imaging extraction must be validated against two blinded clinical reviewers in >=200 reports stratified by era, timing and strategy. If PPV or sensitivity is <0.90 for tumor size/multiplicity/vascular invasion/progression, use adjudicated labels or declare the timing contrast residually confounded. Report models restricted to patients with a contrast CT/MRI available before L and stratified by major versus minor resection.

NEGATIVE CONTROLS AND BIAS/FALSIFICATION CHECKS
1. Surgical case-mix negative-control outcome: postoperative day-1-to-3 hemoglobin nadir relative to preoperative hemoglobin, adjusted for operation type. A standardized prompt/defer or readiness association >=0.20 or p<0.01 indicates residual operative complexity/bleeding confounding and downgrades hepatic interpretation.
2. Pre-treatment pseudo-outcome: apply the same outcome construction to bilirubin changes entirely before the index TACE (-14 to -8 versus -7 to <0). An association between future strategy and this prior change after adjustment reveals selection or assay-era bias.
3. Non-hepatic readiness negative-control exposure: pre-landmark hemoglobin slope. If it predicts the primary hepatic recovery as strongly as the hepatic readiness score after baseline hemoglobin and operation adjustment, generic surveillance/illness intensity rather than hepatic recovery may explain the result.
4. Assessment-density control: include counts of labs, encounters and imaging in the prior 14 days; repeat within patients with protocol-like complete panels. A finding restricted to intensely monitored patients is inconclusive.
5. Landmark and grace-period robustness: repeat landmarks 14–70 and 28–98 days, and grace periods 5 versus 10 days. No isolated cutpoint may define support.
6. Assay/calendar checks: compare routine and 急 names, specimens, years and value distributions; rerun in 2018–2025 and routine-only assays.
7. Conditioning audit: compare all eligible unresected risk-set members with those resected by 21 days; report weighted resection/outcome observation probabilities. If unresected members lack outcome by design, do not infer safety for them.
8. Positivity stop: call the strategy contrast inconclusive if either arm has effective sample size <100, fewer than 50 outcome-observed operations, maximum stabilized weight >20 after truncation, or >10% of observations outside common support.

PRESPECIFIED INTERPRETATION AND FALSIFICATION
Supportive for readiness: each 1-SD worse readiness burden is associated with >=15% higher adjusted geometric mean postoperative bilirubin ratio (ratio >=1.15, 95% CI excludes 1.00), INR worsens concordantly, the association is coherent across marker components/landmarks, imaging adjustment does not materially erase it, and no negative control fails. A significantly adverse prompt-by-readiness interaction, with prompt resection ratio >=1.15 in unresolved/worsening patients, would support prospective testing of a readiness-guided postponement protocol; it would not prove delay prevents PHLF.

Supportive for prompt surgery after recovery: in recovered/stable patients, the upper 95% CI for prompt/deferred bilirubin ratio is <1.105, INR excludes a clinically adverse difference >0.10, and controls/positivity pass. This supports that an extra arbitrary wait is not associated with better biochemical recovery in this selected population, not that prompt surgery improves cancer survival.

Adverse/opposite: worse readiness burden is associated with ratio <=0.87 with CI excluding 1, or prompt surgery among unresolved patients has better bilirubin and INR recovery, would refute the directional hypothesis and suggest that the laboratory state is a poor readiness proxy or that selection dominates. If deferral is materially safer even after recovered/stable status, recovery thresholds are insufficient.

Falsified/bias-dominated: any prespecified negative-control failure, incoherent bilirubin/INR directions, disappearance after validated tumor/operation adjustment, an isolated landmark/grace-period effect, major assay-era discontinuity, or severe positivity failure prevents the proposed readiness interpretation. The rule itself is falsified as clinically useful if readiness adds <0.02 adjusted R-squared and the CI excludes a 10% change in bilirubin ratio across its interquartile range.

Inconclusive: CIs include both no association and the supportive/adverse thresholds; fewer than 100 effective patients per strategy or 50 outcomes per arm; imaging validation fails; readiness is unclassifiable for >30%; outcome observation differs by >15 percentage points after weighting; outside-care/death loss cannot be bounded; or results depend on the strict complete-triplet trajectory rule already shown to be sparse. A precise CI within 0.87–1.15 supports no clinically material biochemical association in the observed resection-plausible risk sets only.

ADJUDICATION, MISSING EVIDENCE, AND CHECKABLE CLAIMS
A hepatobiliary surgeon and interventional radiologist must validate TACE/resection lexicons, distinguish planned preoperative TACE from rescue/emergency embolization, establish at each landmark whether resection was genuinely plausible, adjudicate reasons for delay and competing therapies, and classify resection extent. Two blinded reviewers with disagreement resolution must validate imaging variables and adjudicate ISGLS PHLF/major complications. The automatic experiment can verify file hashes, row filters, composite joins, chronology, landmark eligibility, lab calculations, weights, models, uncertainty, negative controls and whether reported claims match outputs. It cannot establish resectability, treatment intent, true clinical readiness, causal benefit, ISGLS grade or a safety recommendation.

Unavailable or incomplete evidence includes verified lab units/reference ranges, raw imaging and future-liver-remnant volumetry, ICG clearance, Child-Pugh/ALBI as standardized fields, ECOG/performance status, anesthesia flowsheets, blood loss and transfusion units, portal pressure, complete outside TACE/treatment, exact death follow-up, reasons for nonresection, and long-term tumor progression/survival. Stronger clinical claims require these data, expert review, external validation, and preferably prospective randomization at a validated readiness landmark.
