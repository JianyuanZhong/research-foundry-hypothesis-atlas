## Unresolved clinical question and advance

When an adult leaves an ICU, is the ordered pattern of physiologic recovery during the preceding 24 hours informative beyond the latest state and the fact that clinicians measured the patient—and does that information remain useful when evaluated in a different ICU care-unit context? This matters because ward surveillance and ICU-bed review need to distinguish durable stability from a superficially acceptable last value. A signal that disappears across care units would be a workflow-local association and should not be treated as a transportable discharge-risk phenotype.

The strongest claim supported before fitting is limited: repeated structured measurements, residual oxygen/hemodynamic abnormalities, and care-process transitions are recorded in this snapshot and are clinically plausible correlates of near-term risk. The imported seed and the inspected demonstrations do not establish an effect size, plannedness or intent of ICU return, causal effects of discharge/monitoring, or the benefit of an intervention. The cancer demonstration's main article and full STAR Methods were unavailable; only its supplement was inspected.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: in the full defined adult ICU-outtime cohort, prespecified final-day recovery/persistence/oscillation/support-transition features (B1) improve locked 48-hour cause-specific estimation of first subsequent recorded ICU admission, with no material death-calibration harm, beyond a strong last-state-plus-process baseline (B0) and a value-free process null (P0). A transport hypothesis is tested secondarily: the B1 increment over B0 remains directionally consistent and calibration-acceptable in leave-one-last-careunit-out evaluation after careunit/service/transfer identity fields are removed from all models. This is within-hospital contextual transport, not external validation.

## Models and transport test

P0: pooled-logistic discrete-time competing hazards using demographics/admission context, ICU duration, pre-landmark careunit/service/transfer identity, and value-free observation-process features. B0: P0 plus last valid physiologic/support state and its recency/count/mask/gap variables. B1: B0 plus the fixed recovery/persistence/oscillation/abnormal-burden/support-transition block. B2: one-layer 32-unit GRU-D over the same four ordered bins plus baseline/process covariates, two hazard heads, dropout 0.1, maximum 40 epochs, validation early stopping. All preprocessing is training-only; use five fixed subject-hash 70/15/15 splits.

The primary contrasts are B1-B0 and B1-P0 on locked test predictions for 48-hour ICU-admission CIF and death calibration. The learned alternative is only a secondary B2-B1 contrast. Report 48-hour/seven-day cause-specific Brier and integrated Brier, CIF calibration intercept/slope, AUROC/AUPRC and five-seed variability, with 2,000 subject-level paired bootstrap replicates when support allows. Report thresholds 1%, 2%, 5%, 10% for observed-risk reclassification/net benefit under a declared hypothetical utility; do not interpret it as a discharge or monitoring effect.

Transport stress test: derive eligible last-careunit groups from icustays. For each care-unit holdout with adequate event support, remove careunit/service/transfer identity features from P0/B0/B1/B2, train on all other units with patient-level separation, and evaluate the held-out unit. Map all generic numeric process features identically; no held-out category is encoded. This is not external validation and not a hospital effect. If the holdout leaves too few events, report the unit as descriptive only.

## Falsification and interpretation

Audit one row per index hospitalization, split integrity, zero post-landmark predictor timestamps, invalid-value/unit counts, interval clipping, unresolved timing, event ordering, observation-density flow and careunit-holdout eligibility. Required perturbations are last-bin-only; P0; within-density-stratum value permutation retaining masks/process; within-patient bin-order permutation; removal of masks/delta-times; removal of careunit/transfer/service channels; label permutation within event-rate strata; the six-observed-bin parent sensitivity; composite versus competing-event estimands; and post-landmark destination only as a descriptive audit.

Supportive evidence requires a B1 increment over B0 and P0 with uncertainty compatible with nonzero improvement, no material death-calibration harm, survival across observation-density strata, removal by value/order permutation, and directionally consistent calibration and increment in eligible held-out care units. If B2 improves B1, its gain must also disappear after order/value perturbation and not worsen calibration. A result confined to P0, or a B1 gain that vanishes after process adjustment or in held-out care units, supports workflow/observability dependence rather than a transportable recovery phenotype. A gain that is unstable across density, careunit, threshold or competing-event definitions is limited association. Inconclusive results include inadequate event support, unresolved timing, contradictory clocks, or wide uncertainty; these trigger no clinical conclusion.

Computationally checkable claims are cohort construction, feature timing, split integrity, event definitions, model fits, comparative metrics, calibration, reclassification, transport contrasts and permutation behavior. Clinical claims require expert adjudication of device validity, plannedness/intent, goals of care, true physiologic recovery, appropriate monitoring harms/benefits, and prospective external/impact validation. The data lack raw waveforms, imaging, complete bedside workflow, rapid-response data and a cross-hospital validation cohort. No claim of causality, benefit from delaying discharge, value of a monitoring intervention, or generalization beyond this deidentified single-site snapshot may be made.
