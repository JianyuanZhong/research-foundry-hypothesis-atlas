# Conservative transfer-topology ascertainment of early failure after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly fit and evaluate a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study. It will test whether the parent's recorded-support recovery contrast is associated with a reproducible, conservatively ascertained post-ICU escalation rather than an artifact of mixing ICU transfers, ward intervals, and missing topology.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest available evidence supports data feasibility only. The snapshot contains linked ICU stays, hospital transfers, charted physiology, support-related ICU observations, hospital death/discharge times and the required identifiers/times. The source/header/schema and d_items checks establish availability and labels; they do not establish a support-withdrawal association, clinical deterioration, discharge appropriateness, or causal benefit.

The parent candidate established a valuable observation-identifiability distinction: positive recorded support, observed non-positive values, and no qualifying row are not interchangeable, and a locked q-sensitivity analysis is needed for uninformative missingness. It also refined any later ICU stay into a transfer-confirmed versus immediate/unconfirmed outcome. A remaining unresolved measurement problem is that comparing a transfer row only with the later ICU's first/last careunit can misclassify a bridge when an admission has multiple ICU units or incomplete transfer intervals. A more conservative, admission-wide ICU-unit topology is therefore a substantive outcome-ascertainment repair.

Primary hypothesis:

> At the first ICU outtime, among adults with adequate prespecified respiratory and MAP observation coverage, recent recorded respiratory and/or vasoactive withdrawal is associated with a higher standardized 48-hour cumulative incidence of a conservatively documented non-ICU-to-ICU transition than durable recorded-off evidence, after observation/process adjustment and across the locked missing-support q scenarios.

This is a prognostic, record-based hypothesis. It does not test whether stopping support causes return or whether ICU discharge should be delayed. The primary endpoint is a documented non-ICU bridge that is not compatible with any ICU-unit interval in the same admission; topology-ambiguous and immediate returns are retained as separate competing outcomes. The exact aggregate any-later-ICU-return estimand remains a secondary comparator.

The directional claim is falsified if the prespecified 48-hour confirmed-return contrast is non-positive, reverses, loses its uncertainty-supported direction under the locked q range or topology sensitivities, or is not distinguishable from the ambiguous-return process. A 2-percentage-point absolute-risk difference is a prespecified interpretability gate, not a validated bedside threshold.

## Clinical importance and advance

ICU discharge balances bed capacity against rapid escalation risk. A later ICU stay is consequential but heterogeneous: it may follow an observed ward interval, represent an immediate/procedural return, or reflect incomplete transfer documentation. If a recorded recent withdrawal pattern predicts only aggregate or topology-ambiguous returns, it should not be described as evidence of ward deterioration. If it predicts the conservative documented-bridge endpoint across missing-support assumptions, the result is a more reproducible retrospective signal for prioritizing future surveillance research.

The advance is outcome validity and interpretability. The study builds an admission-wide ICU-unit map, refuses to call a bridge confirmed when its careunit overlaps any ICU interval, retains ambiguous returns instead of discarding them, and reports both conservative and aggregate estimands. The result can distinguish a signal that survives stricter outcome ascertainment from one driven by immediate or poorly documented returns. It still cannot establish planned/unplanned intent, preventability, treatment limitation, clinical readiness, or surveillance benefit.

Supportive evidence would be a positive confirmed-return contrast with adequate events/positivity, calibrated held-out predictions, stable bootstrap intervals and direction across q, endpoint tolerances, ICU-unit mapping and timing falsifications; agreement between baseline and HSMM is supportive of reproducibility, not proof of clinical truth. Adverse evidence includes null/reversed contrast, collapse after q or admission-wide topology correction, signal confined to ambiguous/aggregate return, persistence after transfer-careunit permutation, or dependence on storetime/last-value coding. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse confirmed events, high topology ambiguity, poor overlap, unstable split estimates, wide intervals, high latent-state entropy or contradictory bridge rules.

## Conservative topology rule

For each earliest later ICU stay with `later.intime > outtime` and before the 48-hour/discharge bound, inspect every valid `hosp/transfers` row for the same `subject_id,hadm_id` with non-null `careunit` and `intime < outtime`. Build the union of all ICU intervals from `icu/icustays` in that admission, using each interval's `intime,outtime` and linked careunit fields. A transfer row is a candidate bridge only if its clipped interval lies between index `outtime-5 min` and later ICU `intime+5 min`, has at least 30 minutes duration (primary), and its careunit does not match any ICU careunit/interval compatible with the admission-wide map. Record all candidates, overlap minutes, eventtype, missing/invalid times and ambiguity.

Classify the earliest later ICU event as conservative confirmed only if exactly one best bridge (greatest clipped duration; ties earliest transfer outtime then smallest `transfer_id`) survives the primary rule and no ICU-unit ambiguity remains. If any plausible bridge overlaps an ICU interval, or ICU topology is incomplete/contradictory, classify it ambiguous/immediate rather than confirmed. No qualifying row never proves no ward stay. Sensitivities vary endpoint tolerance 0/5 minutes, minimum bridge duration 0/15/30/60 minutes, careunit-only versus interval-overlap logic, exclusion of `eventtype='discharge'`, and conservative versus parent later-stay-only ICU map. A careunit permutation within admission, preserving ICU times/durations, is a topology negative control.

## Exposure ledger and analysis

For each half-window and hour, classify respiratory and vasoactive evidence as positive, observed non-positive, no qualifying row/interval, contradictory or invalid. Respiratory positive means a valid chart value with O2 flow >2 L/min, FiO2 >0.21, PEEP >0, or corroborating intubation/NIV interval. Vasoactive positive means valid overlap for a listed inputevent. Procedures corroborate transitions, not continuous support. Keep arterial/NIBP MAP separate and convert Fahrenheit audibly.

Require at least two respiratory and two MAP observation bins per half. Durable recorded-off means no positive respiratory/vasoactive evidence in either half; recent respiratory withdrawal means positive preceding-half and none final-half; recent vasoactive cessation is analogous; preserve active, uninformative, discordant and inadequate states. No-positive evidence is not continuous absence.

Use observation fractions, gaps, row/source counts, interval counts, contradictions, pre-index transfer process counts, first/last careunit, ICU LOS, admission context, age, gender and anchor-year group. Recode only uninformative no-row hours under locked q in `{0,.10,.25,.50,.75,1.00}`, with shared, domain/half-specific and higher-vasoactive-q scenarios. Report label changes and effective sample size; this is sensitivity, not identification.

The transparent primary baseline fits pooled one-hour cause-specific hazards through 48 hours for the four topology events and the parent three-event aggregate. Nested recorded-physiology/support, masks-only, process/context-only and full models expose documentation dependence. Standardize held-out CIFs to the common eligible population; a locked coverage-eligibility inverse-odds diagnostic may target the broad cohort but is not causal or external transportability.

The substantive alternative fits a two-domain factorized hidden semi-Markov model over twelve bins with duration distributions, constrained persistence/withdrawal transitions, physiology/support emissions and an explicit observation model for masks, row counts and source channels. It predicts the same topology-refined and aggregate hazards using identical features and patient splits. It can reveal whether ordered duration/discordant recovery is reproducible information lost by fixed summaries and whether that information differs by confirmed versus ambiguous return. Report states only with low enough posterior entropy, stable occupancy/meaning across splits and calibrated held-out performance; otherwise the transparent baseline is the scientific result and HSMM is inconclusive.

A GRU/TCN is deferred because added sequence capacity would not directly resolve topology ambiguity or missing-support assumptions. ## Falsification, uncertainty and interpretation limits

Use patient bootstrap 95% intervals, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier, event counts, overlap/positivity and effective sample size. Test hourly-order shuffles preserving masks/support totals; mask-only/support-only/physiology-only/full ablations; charttime-to-storetime negative timing; last-value coding; no procedure corroboration; separate arterial/NIBP and FiO2/O2/PEEP channels; q, threshold, window, coverage, tie and topology variants; admission-wide versus later-stay-only ICU map; transfer-careunit permutation; pseudo-landmark six hours earlier with a new leakage-free outcome window; aggregate versus confirmed versus ambiguous returns; discharge-as-censoring sensitivity; and first-careunit/admission-type/anchor-year/transfer-density strata. Audit all invalid intervals, duplicates and post-outtime leakage.

Supportive results require a positive prespecified confirmed-return contrast with uncertainty excluding zero, adequate overlap/events, stable direction across q and reasonable topology rules, calibration, and no negative-control dependence. Adverse results include reversal/null after conservative topology or q adjustment, confinement to ambiguous/aggregate return, dependence on storetime or one bridge rule, topology-permutation persistence, non-positivity or unidentifiable HSMM states. Inconclusive results include sparse confirmed events, high ambiguity, wide/unstable intervals, poor calibration/transport overlap, contradictory timing rules or high state entropy.

Computationally checkable claims are cohort joins, temporal integrity, recorded observations/support intervals, topology classification, competing-risk estimates, calibration, uncertainty, model comparison and sensitivity consistency. The data cannot establish unrecorded support, clinical deterioration, bedside readiness, treatment limitation, goals of care, planned/unplanned intent, preventability, ward surveillance adequacy, causal benefit, clinical utility or transportability. Those require clinician adjudication, richer ward/rapid-response data, validated transfer semantics, external validation and prospective or causal study.
