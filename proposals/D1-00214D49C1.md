TITLE
A two-clock day-28 post-TACE cohort: acute biochemical injury, continuous time since the last TACE, and postoperative bilirubin/INR decompensation after recorded HCC resection

UNRESOLVED QUESTION, CLINICAL IMPORTANCE, AND ADVANCE
For adults with recorded HCC who have no recorded liver resection through exactly 28 elapsed days after their earliest recorded TACE, is the association between time from the most recent preoperative TACE and postoperative hepatic biochemical decompensation stronger after a larger acute bilirubin/INR perturbation than after a smaller perturbation?

The practical problem is consequential: a fixed calendar delay may unnecessarily postpone resection after hepatic recovery, while operating before recovery could worsen postoperative liver dysfunction. The available data cannot identify an assignable scheduling strategy, however, because fresh day-28 laboratory triads, treatment intent, potential resectability, complete death/outside-care ascertainment, and key operative-risk measurements are absent or sparse. This child therefore narrows the tested claim instead of relabeling stale tests as “readiness.” It preserves the exact earliest-TACE day-28 cohort and every patient without recorded resection in a selection/observation ledger, but makes the strongest feasible clinical estimand an explicitly selected, prognostic association among patients with a subsequently recorded resection and paired perioperative bilirubin/INR.

The design uses two clocks for two different scientific roles. Clock 1, earliest TACE to exact day 28, fixes eligibility before future resection is known and exposes the nonresected denominator. Clock 2, most recent qualifying TACE before operation to operation, represents the latest recorded hepatic insult for the selected perioperative analysis. Intervening TACE never silently becomes “waiting.” The primary modifier is an acute, preoperative bilirubin/INR perturbation after that most recent TACE, not a current day-28 state and not a validated clearance rule.

Strongest claim already supported by inspected evidence: the HCC snapshot contains dated TACE/resection procedures and dense acute and perioperative bilirubin/INR measurements sufficient to construct a fixed day-28 selection ledger and estimate a selected-resection continuous timing-by-acute-injury association. Routine bilirubin and INR are relevant to postoperative hepatic dysfunction, but the proposed endpoint is only a biochemical signal.

Unresolved, falsifiable claim tested here: among patients with recorded resection after the day-28 landmark, a longer interval after the most recent TACE is associated with a lower risk of joint postoperative bilirubin/INR worsening when acute post-TACE perturbation is high, but with little clinically important association when perturbation is low.

This is a substantive repair of [prior hypothesis]. It removes fresh day-28 triads and “readiness-guided surgery” from the estimand; prevents latest days-0-to-28 values from being interpreted as current state; makes the joint bilirubin/INR signal primary rather than a bilirubin-only outcome; explicitly separates earliest-TACE eligibility from last-TACE biology; and adds independently reconstructed exact denominator, recency, repeat-TACE, outcome, and institutional-observation counts.

POPULATION AND TEMPORAL BOUNDARIES
Unit is the patient. Index is the earliest recorded qualifying TACE.

Include at Clock-1 landmark: age >=18 in the index-TACE encounter; an explicit HCC diagnosis in an encounter dated no later than the landmark; valid index-TACE start time; and no qualifying recorded resection or transplant at or before the landmark. Exclude impossible chronology, donor/transplant procedures, intraoperative TACE, and patients whose index encounter lacks adult age. Landmark t0 is index-TACE start time + exactly 28*24 hours. Eligibility is fixed without future resection information.

The all-landmark ledger follows every eligible patient through the minimum of 180 elapsed days, last recorded institutional event, or administrative source end. It records first resection in (28,60], (60,90], or (90,180]; repeat TACE and other recorded treatment; and dated encounters/labs/examinations/procedures after t0. “No recorded resection” is not interpreted as continued waiting, survival, resectability, palliation, or benefit. A post-landmark event is only an institutional-observation proxy, not complete follow-up.

The selected perioperative cohort consists of landmark-eligible patients with a first qualifying recorded resection in (28,180] after earliest TACE. For this analysis, identify the most recent qualifying TACE before operation and define Clock 2 as exact hours from that TACE to resection. Require Clock 2 >28 days for the primary biological-recovery analysis; retain Clock 2 <=28 days in the selection ledger and describe it, but do not extrapolate the spline into six observed short-interval cases. Sensitivities use earliest-TACE-to-operation time and restrict to exactly one recorded TACE between index and operation.

ACUTE INJURY MODIFIER WITHOUT A DAY-28 TRIAD
Relative to the most recent preoperative TACE, define:
- pre-TACE anchor: latest total bilirubin and INR in [-7,0);
- acute bilirubin response: maximum total bilirubin in [0,7] divided by the pre-TACE value;
- acute INR response: maximum INR in [0,7] minus the pre-TACE value.

The primary modifier is continuous: log-transform bilirubin ratio; robust-standardize it and INR change using median/MAD values estimated without postoperative outcomes; average the two standardized components. Preserve each component, measurement times, days from TACE, specimen, routine/acute label, and number of measurements. Do not choose a threshold using postoperative outcomes. P25/P50/P75 are contrast points and quartiles are overlap displays only. Missing acute components are not imputed for the primary interaction: those patients stay visible in the selected-cohort flow and observation model. A secondary missingness-aware prognostic model may include marker-specific availability, pre-TACE values, acute values, recency and intensity, but it cannot call its output readiness.

Albumin, creatinine, ALT and platelet responses are secondary adjustment variables after unit/assay validation. A complete bilirubin/INR/albumin triad is never required for landmark inclusion or for defining an assignable strategy.

POSTOPERATIVE ENDPOINT
Latest preoperative values are total bilirubin and INR in [-7,0) relative to resection. Postoperative values are the maximum total bilirubin and maximum INR in [5,8), a half-open implementation of postoperative days 5-7.

Primary binary endpoint, specified before analysis: bilirubin_post/bilirubin_pre >=1.50 AND INR_post-INR_pre >=0.20. Report this as a joint biochemical hepatic-decompensation signal, not true ISGLS post-hepatectomy liver failure. Co-primary continuous components are log(bilirubin_post/bilirubin_pre) and INR_post-INR_pre. A composite result unsupported by both components is not clinically supportive.

True ISGLS PHLF requires increased INR and hyperbilirubinemia on/after postoperative day 5 plus clinical-management information for grade. Complete clinical adjudication of PHLF, grade, major complications, ICU escalation, reoperation and in-hospital death is secondary/descriptive; the automatic verifier cannot establish them.

ESTIMANDS
Primary estimand: among Clock-1 landmark-eligible patients with a recorded first resection by day 180, Clock 2 >28 days, complete acute bilirubin/INR modifier, and observed paired postoperative endpoint, estimate the adjusted risk difference in the joint biochemical signal for a 28-day longer most-recent-TACE-to-operation interval at acute-injury-score P25, P50 and P75. Derive contrasts from a continuous restricted-cubic-spline timing model interacted with the continuous injury score and standardized to the selected cohort’s measured pre-exposure case mix. This is a selected-resection, complete-outcome prognostic estimand, not a causal effect of waiting.

Co-primary component estimands use the same model population and contrasts for mean log bilirubin ratio and mean INR increment.

Selection/observation estimands, kept separate: in the complete Clock-1 cohort, report cumulative recorded incidence by days 90 and 180 of resection and repeat TACE, plus proportions with post-landmark institutional observation, stratified by availability and magnitude of acute labs after index TACE. These quantify clinical pathways and selection; they do not assign an outcome to nonresected patients and do not estimate benefit.

A selection-weighted sensitivity models probability of recorded resection and paired outcome from the all-landmark ledger. It may show sensitivity to measured selection but cannot recover missing postoperative counterfactuals, deaths, outside care, or unmeasured resectability.

ANALYSIS AND BASELINES
Fit prespecified models in sequence:
1. timing-only restricted cubic spline for Clock 2;
2. spline plus continuous acute-injury score and spline-by-score interaction;
3. parsimonious pre-last-TACE adjustment;
4. fully adjusted model only if case-mix validation passes.

Use spline knots at empirical 10th/35th/65th/90th percentiles within Clock-2 >28 common support. There is no primary 60-day coefficient. Display 55-, 60-, and 65-day cuts only as threshold-falsification checks. For the binary endpoint use penalized logistic regression if sparse interaction cells or separation occurs. Bootstrap patients for 95% confidence intervals and optimism; report calibration intercept/slope. Predictive improvement is not the scientific claim.

Pre-last-TACE covariates: age, sex, calendar year, service, number and spacing of prior TACE/other recorded HCC procedures, pre-TACE bilirubin, INR, albumin, creatinine, platelets, ALT and hemoglobin, measurement intensity/recency, prior admissions, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, ascites, viral hepatitis, renal disease, diabetes, infection, and rupture/emergency presentation. Tumor covariates may enter only if reports precede the last TACE: diameter, multiplicity, macrovascular invasion, extrahepatic disease, rupture, ascites/cirrhosis.

A separate channeling/case-mix model adds latest preoperative labs, last preoperative imaging response, and adjudicated resection extent/approach. These occur after exposure begins and can be mediators or consequences of waiting, so they are not treated as baseline confounders; compare models with and without them rather than calling either causal.

Do not impute postoperative outcomes. Use inverse-probability-of-outcome-observation weighting as sensitivity, with missingness modeled from pre-outcome information. Multiple imputation may be used for ordinary baseline covariates after preserving structural assay missingness; primary acute-score interaction remains complete-component and every exclusion is reported.

VALIDATION AND STOP GATES
Laboratory gate: a clinical laboratory director validates likely units, physiologic distributions, specimen types, assay aliases, routine versus STAT agreement using same-day pairs, and calendar/service discontinuities. Repeat routine-only and 2018-2025 analyses. If comparability fails, the endpoint/modifier is unavailable and conclusions are inconclusive, not merely weaker.

Selection/observation gate: report landmark-to-resection flow, repeat treatment, institutional events, acute-lab and outcome completeness by time, score, service, era, and intent. Stop clinical interpretation if either half of timing support has weighted effective sample size <100, >10% of total weight is held by <=10 patients, an acute-score-by-timing decile cell is empty, or outcome observation differs by >=20 percentage points after measured adjustment.

Case-mix gate: two reviewers blinded to postoperative labs validate at least 200 stratified imaging reports and 200 procedure names for TACE/resection identity, near duplicates, tumor variables, response, major/minor resection, and open/laparoscopic approach. Automated clinical variables require both sensitivity and PPV >=0.90; otherwise fully adjudicate or state that residual case mix precludes interpretation.

Intent/resectability gate: a hepatobiliary surgeon and interventional radiologist, blinded to outcomes, classify earliest and last TACE as planned preoperative, conversion/downstaging, bridge/definitive/palliative, emergency rupture/hemostatic, or indeterminate; and classify documented potential resectability at day 28. Resolve disagreement with a third reviewer and report kappa. If kappa <0.70 or >30% are indeterminate, no scheduling, intent-specific, or resectability claim is allowed. The primary numeric association may still be reported as selected and prognostic.

Event-count gate: with approximately 57-65 anticipated joint events, use a deliberately parsimonious primary interaction model and penalization. If bootstrap interaction estimates are unstable, confidence intervals span both clinical benefit and harm, or effective events are inadequate after restrictions/weights, classify the result as inconclusive rather than simplifying post hoc.

NEGATIVE CONTROLS AND FALSIFICATION
1. Preoperative pseudo-outcome: construct bilirubin and INR changes entirely before operation, comparing [-7,-4) with [-3,0). A same-direction timing-by-injury standardized interaction >=0.20 or corresponding binary risk interaction >=5 percentage points indicates readiness trend/channeling and invalidates a postoperative recovery interpretation.
2. Operative-complexity control: postoperative [1,4) hemoglobin nadir/change, adjusted for preoperative hemoglobin and validated operation extent. A timing-by-injury standardized effect >=0.20 or p<0.01 flags operative selection.
3. Negative-control exposure: TACE weekday after calendar-year/service adjustment. Omnibus p<0.01 or standardized effect >=0.20 on the endpoint flags scheduling/service confounding.
4. Clock falsification: repeat the model using earliest-TACE time and in the single-recorded-TACE subset. A result only under an inappropriate clock, or qualitative reversal unexplained by intervening TACE, is adverse to the biological-recovery interpretation.
5. Endpoint concordance: bilirubin and INR continuous components must agree in direction. An isolated threshold composite result is not supportive.
6. Leakage: no post-landmark value enters the Clock-1 selection ledger covariates; no postoperative value enters exposure, modifier, or adjustment. Any violation is computational failure.
7. Calendar threshold: an isolated significant 60-day contrast without a coherent continuous spline is evidence against a threshold claim.

PRESPECIFIED INTERPRETATION BRANCHES
Supportive: all laboratory, support, case-mix, and control gates pass; the global timing-by-injury interaction is stable; at injury-score P75, a 28-day longer interval has adjusted joint-event risk difference <=-5 percentage points with 95% CI excluding 0; at P25 the risk difference and its 95% CI lie within -5 to +5 points; the P75-minus-P25 interaction contrast is at least 5 points with CI excluding 0; and bilirubin ratio and INR increment concur. This supports acute perturbation as a prognostic effect modifier deserving prospective validation. It does not support delaying high-injury patients, accelerating low-injury patients, or applying a scheduling rule.

Adverse/opposite: at P75, a 28-day longer interval is associated with >=5 percentage points higher risk with CI excluding 0, low-injury rather than high-injury patients improve, or repeat-TACE/clock analyses reverse the result. This rejects the proposed recovery pattern and suggests progression, channeling, repeat treatment, or case mix dominates. No timing recommendation follows.

Precise null: interaction contrasts and P25/P50/P75 28-day risk differences have 95% CIs wholly within -5 to +5 points, continuous components concur, and gates pass. This argues against a clinically material prognostic modification in observed resected complete cases; it does not establish equivalence of surgery dates for all TACE recipients.

Inconclusive/falsified: confidence intervals include both clinically important benefit and harm; outcome or acute-score events are too few; positivity, observation, unit, case-mix, or intent gates fail; components conflict; negative controls fail; or conclusions depend on one arbitrary threshold. Report the cohort, selection, stale-lab, and repeat-treatment structure without a timing recommendation.
