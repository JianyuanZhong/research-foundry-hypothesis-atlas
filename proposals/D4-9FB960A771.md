# Scientific Proposal: Functional CHIP Burden and Disease Progression in UK Biobank

## Unresolved Question

**Among individuals with clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP), does functional CHIP burden (quantified by enzymatic activity or functional readouts) predict infection and hematologic malignancy risk more strongly than pre-illness physiological reserve (grip strength, gait speed), and does this relationship differ by CHIP mutation type?**

This question addresses a critical gap in understanding CHIP pathogenesis: while genetic mutations are well-characterized, the **functional consequences** of these mutations (enzymatic activity, epigenetic dysregulation) may be more proximal to disease risk than mere mutation presence. The Wang et al. 2025 organ-specific aging clocks paper demonstrates that functional readouts (proteomic signatures) provide superior disease prediction compared to chronological age. Similarly, the Pershad et al. 2026 CHIP paper shows that methylation-based activity scores predict disease risk better than clinical risk scores. This proposal tests whether functional CHIP burden provides independent and additive prognostic information beyond pre-illness physiological reserve.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Risk Stratification**: Current CHIP risk assessment relies on clinical scores (Clonal Hematopoiesis Risk Score) that may not capture functional heterogeneity within mutation subtypes. Understanding functional CHIP burden could improve identification of high-risk individuals.

2. **Treatment Prioritization**: If functional CHIP burden predicts disease risk independently of physiological reserve, patients with high functional burden but preserved reserve might benefit from early intervention, while those with low functional burden might be safely observed.

3. **Mechanistic Insight**: The relationship between CHIP function and disease progression may reveal biological pathways linking epigenetic dysregulation to clinical outcomes, informing therapeutic strategies targeting CHIP pathogenicity.

## Scientific Significance

This hypothesis advances understanding by:
- **Moving beyond genotype to phenotype**: While CHIP mutation presence is well-studied, the functional impact of mutations (enzymatic activity, epigenetic dysregulation) may be the true driver of disease risk.
- **Integrating multiple risk domains**: Combining CHIP functional burden with pre-illness physiological reserve to assess whether these risk factors operate independently or synergistically.
- **Distinguishing pathways to disease**: Testing whether CHIP-driven infection and hematologic malignancy follow different pathways based on functional burden vs. physiological reserve.

## Hypothesis Statement

Among UK Biobank participants with CHIP:
1. **Functional CHIP burden** (methylation-based activity scores or functional readouts) predicts incident infection and hematologic malignancy more strongly than CHIP mutation presence alone.
2. The association between functional CHIP burden and disease risk is **independent of pre-illness physiological reserve** (grip strength, gait speed).
3. The relationship differs by **CHIP mutation type**: TET2 loss-of-function variants and DNMT3A R882 hotspot may have stronger associations than other mutations due to greater enzymatic disruption.
4. **Functional CHIP burden** provides **additive prognostic information** beyond physiological reserve when predicting CHIP-associated disease.

## Suggested Approach

### Primary Analysis: Cox Proportional Hazards

1. **Model 1**: Effect of CHIP mutation type on infection and hematologic malignancy
   - Cox model: hazard = f(chip_gene, chip_variant, clone_size, age, sex, ethnicity)
   - Baseline: Test if any CHIP mutation increases risk

2. **Model 2**: Effect of functional CHIP burden on disease risk
   - Cox model: hazard = f(methylation_activity_score, age, sex, ethnicity)
   - Test if functional burden predicts risk independent of mutation type

3. **Model 3**: Interaction between functional CHIP burden and physiological reserve
   - Cox model: hazard = f(activity_score, grip_strength, activity_score×grip_strength, age, sex, ethnicity)
   - Test if the association differs by physiological reserve level

4. **Model 4**: Additive prognostic value
   - Cox model: hazard = f(activity_score, grip_strength, clinical_risk_score, age, sex, ethnicity)
   - Compare C-index and net reclassification improvement (NRI)

### Secondary Analysis: Machine Learning

1. **Random Forest**: Predict incident disease using CHIP status, functional burden, physiological reserve, and clinical covariates
2. **Gradient Boosting**: Compare performance with Cox models
3. **Feature Importance**: Identify which CHIP functional readouts contribute most to prediction

### Sensitivity Analyses

1. **Reverse Causation**: Exclude participants with prevalent disease at baseline
2. **Confounding**: Adjust for comorbidities, medications, lifestyle factors
3. **Clone Evolution**: Analyze changes in clone size over time (if multiple measurements available)
4. **Mutation-Specific**: Analyze TET2 and DNMT3A separately from other CHIP mutations

## Baseline Comparison

**Baseline Approach**: Standard Cox model using CHIP mutation presence and clone size only
- This represents current clinical practice of using CHIP risk scores
- Allows quantification of added value from functional readouts

**Substantive Alternative**: Cox model with methylation-based activity scores and physiological reserve
- Tests mechanistic hypothesis that functional burden matters more than genetic presence
- Tests interaction with physiological reserve

**Information Revealed by Alternative**:
- Whether functional readouts capture heterogeneity within mutation subtypes
- Whether physiological reserve modifies CHIP-disease relationship
- Whether functional burden provides independent prognostic information

## Evaluation Criteria

**Primary Outcome**: Hazard ratios and 95% CIs for incident infection and hematologic malignancy

**Secondary Outcomes**:
- C-index comparison (baseline vs. alternative models)
- Net reclassification improvement (NRI)
- Calibration plots
- Feature importance from ML models

**Falsification Criteria**:
- **Adverse**: Functional CHIP burden shows no association with disease risk after adjusting for mutation type and physiological reserve; physiological reserve fully mediates CHIP-disease relationship
- **Supportive**: Functional CHIP burden predicts risk independently of mutation type; association differs by mutation type (TET2/DNMT3A stronger than others); functional burden adds prognostic value beyond physiological reserve
- **Inconclusive**: Mixed results across subgroups; requires larger sample or different functional readouts

## Key References

Three key references support this hypothesis:

1. **Wang et al. 2025** (Nature Aging): Demonstrates that functional proteomic readouts (organ-specific aging clocks) predict disease better than chronological age, supporting the use of functional over genetic markers for disease prediction.

2. **Pershad et al. 2026** (medRxiv): Shows that methylation-based enzymatic activity scores predict CHIP-associated disease risk better than clinical risk scores, directly supporting the use of functional CHIP burden over mutation presence for disease risk prediction.

3. **Celis-Morales et al. 2018** (BMJ): Validates grip strength as a robust biomarker of physiological reserve and health outcomes, supporting its use as a measure of functional reserve in this analysis.

## Limitations

1. **Functional CHIP readouts**: Methylation-based activity scores may not be available in all UKB participants; alternative functional readouts may be needed.

2. **Reverse Causation**: Pre-illness measures may be affected by early disease processes.

3. **Confounding**: Unmeasured confounders (lifestyle, environmental exposures) may affect both CHIP and disease risk.

4. **Clinical applicability**: UKB is UK-based; generalizability to other populations uncertain.

5. **Mechanistic inference**: Observational study cannot establish causal relationships.

## Required Additional Data

1. **Methylation-based activity scores**: If not available in UKB, need alternative functional readouts (e.g., protein expression, metabolite levels)

2. **CHIP clone evolution**: Multiple timepoints to assess clone dynamics

3. **Detailed infection phenotyping**: To distinguish infection types and severity

4. **Clinical adjudication**: For incident disease verification
