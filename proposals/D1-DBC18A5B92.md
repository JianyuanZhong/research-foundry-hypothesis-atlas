# Staged validation of the EHR vasopressor-restart endpoint

## Question and substantive advance

The inherited branch cannot support a causal comparison of norepinephrine-first versus vasopressin-first discontinuation. Among 6,040 MAP-eligible repeated landmarks, assigned-strategy positivity failed: 211 NE-first actions (3.49%) and 167 VP-first actions (2.76%) are both below the frozen 5% gate, and the 5,638 no-action landmarks are not controls. The observed first-stop cohort is also underpowered (84 NE-first and 53 VP-first episodes; 49 and 24 determinate failures). These facts, and the null/adverse prognostic enrichment results, remain frozen.

The clinically important unresolved question is narrower and prior to any future stopping-order trial:

> Does an EHR interval that appears to restart the stopped vasopressor within six hours correspond to genuinely resumed pump delivery for hemodynamic support, and is that correspondence sufficiently symmetric between NE-first and VP-first episodes to serve as an outcome?

The strongest currently supported claim is only that the endpoint is computable but restart-dominated. There are 73 raw failures, 59 with a stopped-agent restart, and 36 restart-only. The prior narrow transition-compatible heuristic identified 13 restarts, including six restart-only cases, so removing those six changes failures from 73 to 67; this is a documentation diagnostic, not pump truth. The new audit shows that plausible symmetric transaction-reconstruction rules invalidate 13/59 (22.0%) raw restart indices under a shared-chain plus dose-concordance rule and 16/59 (27.1%) under a shared-chain 60-minute rule. MIMIC therefore contains useful evidence that the restart label is unstable, but it does not contain a pump-native reference standard or reliable clinician intent. That is the substantive advance over repeating the parent’s prospective PPV proposal: the proposal first quantifies how much a defensible retrospective reconstruction can alter the dominant endpoint component and uses that result to determine whether MIMIC can do more than prioritize review.

The experiment is strictly observational and noncausal. It will not estimate a treatment effect, recommend an order of pressor discontinuation, or treat `inputevents` as proof of delivered flow, pump-off time, rescue, or intent.

## Phase 1: executable MIMIC retrospective audit

### Frozen population and time origin

Use the inherited 137-episode cohort exactly, with no resampling, post-result exclusion, or eligibility repair. It consists of adults at their first qualifying non-simultaneous documented first stop in their first ICU stay after at least six hours of concurrent NE and VP, with the comparator active. Time zero (`t0`) is the inherited stopped-agent interval end. Retain the inherited forward-time eligibility rules and raw endpoint unchanged.

The raw outcome window is `(t0, min(t0 + 6 hours, ICU exit, death)]`. The raw composite is positive at the first stopped-agent restart, competing/rescue pressor initiation, confirmed NE escalation, repeated MAP below 60 mmHg, death, or active-pressor ICU exit. One inherited episode is indeterminate. Restart-only status is not redefined as success, and a reconstructed continuation is not interpreted as clinical success or failure.

Frozen negative findings that must be reproduced in any execution report are:

- assigned-strategy positivity: 211/6,040 NE-first and 167/6,040 VP-first, both below 5%;
- 84 NE-first and 53 VP-first observed first-stop episodes, with 49 and 24 determinate failures;
- 73 raw failures, 59 restart-containing failures, and 36 restart-only failures;
- prognostic M1 formal Brier improvement 0.000174 (bootstrap 95% interval -0.000884 to 0.001235), M2 incremental improvement 0.000111, adverse 2020–2022 stress performance, and no score or low-risk subgroup advanced;
- no positive inherited MCS case and 137 MCS-unknown cases, never MCS-negative; and
- no causal interpretation.

### Symmetric instability estimand

For each episode, identify the boundary row ending at `t0` and scan every subsequent positive normalized row for the stopped agent. Apply each rule identically to every adjacent pair before and after the inherited boundary, with no selective deletion:

- R0: bridge only overlap or contiguity, gap <=0 minutes;
- R1: gap <=15 minutes and shared order/link token;
- R2: gap <=60 minutes, shared order/link token, and dose ratio 0.8–1.2;
- R3: gap <=60 minutes and shared order/link token;
- R4: gap <=60 minutes and dose ratio 0.8–1.2 regardless of chain.

If the boundary connects forward, invalidate that original index under that rule; never reinterpret it as a successful cessation. Report, overall and by arm, the number and proportion of inherited indices preserved, invalidated, raw restarts preserved, and raw restarts invalidated. Also report the reconstructed end-time shift and the first post-boundary row’s transaction metadata.

For raw restarts, report boundary and restart `statusdescription`, `storetime - endtime` or `storetime - starttime`, order-category fields, `caregiver_id` continuity, `isopenbag`, `continueinnextdept`, dose concordance, and missingness. These are documentation-instability features only.

For charted physiology, use MAP from `t0 - 2 hours` through `t0 + 12 hours`, excluding nonzero warning and values outside 20–200 mmHg. Define a broad pre-restart signal as any MAP below 65 between `t0` and restart start or a last pre-restart MAP at least 10 mmHg below the two-hour pre-`t0` median. This is chronology evidence and review prioritization, not proof of rescue or intent. Construct a review-priority stratum requiring all of: boundary status `Stopped`, restart gap at least 10 minutes, no shared order/link token, and the broad physiology signal.

### Retrospective results and gates

The executed output found 137/137 usable boundaries and 59/59 raw restarts. Under R1, 1 of 137 indices was invalidated; under R2, 13 of 137 overall and 13 of 59 raw restarts were invalidated; under R3, 16 of 137 and 16 of 59 raw restarts; under R4, 23 of 137 and 23 of 59. R2 preserved 36/46 NE-first raw restarts and 10/13 VP-first raw restarts. R3 preserved 34/46 and 9/13, respectively. All transaction fields inspected were present for all 59 raw restarts, so missingness does not explain the observed instability. Boundary status was `Paused` for 35 and `Stopped` for 24 raw restarts. MAP was available before restart in 49/59 and broad physiology signal occurred in 38/59. The review-priority stratum contains 17 cases overall: 13 NE-first and 4 VP-first.

The operational interpretation is adverse for a MIMIC-only endpoint-truth claim: results vary materially across plausible, symmetric reconstruction rules, and the review-priority stratum is too small and arm-imbalanced to be a balanced validation subset. It is still useful for constructing a prospective/manual-review sampling frame. The MIMIC retrospective phase passes only the computational feasibility gate (complete execution and reproducible provenance), not an endpoint-validity gate.

For any future re-execution before prospective data are available, classify the retrospective stage as:

- **Supportive for a review frame:** complete execution; at least 90% of raw restart indices preserved under R2 and at least 85% under R3; at least 20 review-priority cases overall and at least five per arm; no arm has more than a 15-percentage-point difference in invalidation under R2. This supports targeted review only, never pump validity.
- **Adverse:** any preservation or arm-balance threshold fails, or rule-dependent classification is material. The current audit is adverse: R2 invalidates 22.0% of raw restarts, R3 27.1%, and the review frame has only four VP-first cases.
- **Inconclusive:** source extraction, boundary matching, clock fields, or MAP coverage cannot be reproduced, or an implementation error leaves a material number of boundaries unresolved. Inconclusive means repair the computation, not loosen thresholds.

No retrospective gate can establish pump delivery, clinical support, or intent. Those require Phase 2.

## Phase 2: prospective pump-native and clinician-intent validation

### Population and enrollment

At ICUs able to export synchronized pump data, screen consecutive adults forward in time using the frozen phenotype: concurrent supported NE and VP for at least six hours; NE >0 to 0.20 mcg/kg/min; VP >0 to 0.04 U/min; neither dose increased in the prior 30 minutes; no epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine active in the prior two hours; alive and in ICU; and at least three MAP observations across three of four prior 30-minute bins, including the final bin, with median MAP at least 65 and minimum at least 55 mmHg. Enter an episode when a clinician documents or executes a non-simultaneous first stop while the comparator remains active. Do not use future restart, outcome, or pump export completeness for eligibility.

Enroll all eligible stops, including apparent non-restarts, consecutively. For the primary PPV analysis, target 200 adjudicable EHR-restart candidates in each observed-stop arm. This is a validation target, not a treatment-trial sample size. At a PPV near 0.80, 200 per arm gives approximately 0.056 two-sided 95% normal half-width before finite-sample adjustment; it also permits a one-sided lower-bound test against 0.70 with useful power under a true PPV around 0.80. Before any arm-specific adjudication aggregate is unblinded, a blinded pilot of 30 restart candidates per arm may estimate event prevalence, indeterminate rate, and clustering and revise the target upward; it may not lower the PPV, symmetry, or indeterminate gates. If VP-first accrual is insufficient, report the arm as infeasible rather than pooling arms or treating NE-first as its control. Continue all apparent non-restarts in the same sampling frame to estimate sensitivity, specificity, and NPV.

### Required prospective data

For each episode, retain raw synchronized data and source identifiers for:

1. Pump-native delivery: pump and channel ID, drug-library identity, concentration, programmed and delivered rate, start/stop/pause/resume/bolus/alarm events, bag or syringe ID, line ID, channel association, and export downtime at second-level resolution.
2. Workflow transitions: bag/syringe replacement or empty event, line change, channel or pump replacement, handoff, transfer, concentration change, and medication reconciliation events.
3. EHR transactions: item, start/end/store times, amount/rate and units, weight, order and link IDs, caregiver, order-category fields, open-bag and next-department indicators, status, and medication/order audit trail.
4. Intent: timestamped permanent stop versus temporary pause/titration, reason for restart, rescue intent, and whether restart followed hypotension, perfusion concern, procedure, transport, handoff, or documentation correction. Missing reason is unknown.
5. Physiology and competing rescue: continuous/high-frequency MAP with source and quality flags, all vasoactive channels, death, ICU transfer, lactate and perfusion markers as context. Do not manufacture intent from physiology.
6. MCS truth: device type, insertion/removal, active/standby state, flow/support setting, and source confirmation for IABP, Impella, ECMO, LVAD/RVAD, or other support.
7. Clock quality: synchronization offsets and missing intervals among pump, monitor, EHR, and adjudication systems.

### Reference standards and adjudication

Construct packets around every EHR restart and a prespecified stratified sample of apparent non-restarts, oversampling the MIMIC-derived review-priority features without changing the primary consecutive estimand. Two blinded critical-care/pharmacy reviewers independently inspect pump traces, EHR audit trails, transition metadata, and time-local physiology without seeing arm-level aggregate results. Resolve disagreement by a third reviewer and report agreement, disagreement reasons, and indeterminate rate.

The primary intent-qualified reference-positive event requires a genuine pump-off interval, subsequent delivered stopped agent, and adjudicated hemodynamic-support intent. A separate pump-only reference requires a genuine off interval and subsequent delivered stopped agent but does not require intent. Classify interruptions as uninterrupted delivery split by documentation/bag/channel transition, technical pause, transfer continuation, clock mismatch, no delivered drug, or indeterminate. Define the minimum off-duration and delivered-dose threshold in a blinded technical pilot before arm-specific results are opened, and report sensitivity to 1-, 5-, 10-, 30-, and 60-minute off-duration definitions.

### Estimands and decision gates

Primary estimands are PPV of the raw EHR restart within six hours against each reference standard by observed-stop arm, and the absolute NE-first minus VP-first PPV difference. Report exact or Wilson 95% intervals, the number adjudicated, and all indeterminates. The endpoint-validation claim passes only if:

- each arm has observed PPV at least 0.80 and a reported lower confidence bound, with no arm-specific point estimate below 0.70;
- the absolute PPV difference is below 0.10 and a prespecified 90% equivalence interval lies within (-0.10, 0.10) when the planned target permits it;
- indeterminate adjudication is below 10% per arm;
- findings are stable across the off-duration sensitivity analysis and after excluding documented clock/export failures; and
- error fractions are not materially concentrated in one arm, site, unit, calendar period, pump system, or transition type.

The pump-only and intent-qualified standards must be reported separately. Passing pump-only but failing intent-qualified validation means the EHR restart detects delivery but not rescue intent, so it cannot be used as the full clinical endpoint. Secondary analyses estimate sensitivity, specificity, NPV, and indeterminate rate using all consecutively enrolled stops; summarize error causes and evaluate the retrospective transaction screen only in site- and time-held-out data. MCS can be called negative only within a demonstrated device-observation coverage process; otherwise it remains unknown.

### Stopping rules

Stop or pause the validation before full accrual if any of the following occurs, with the rule assessed by a blinded independent data-quality monitor where possible:

- after at least 50 adjudicated restarts in an arm, its point PPV is below 0.60 or its accumulating lower confidence bound is incompatible with the minimum acceptable 0.70 threshold under the locked sequential rule;
- after at least 50 adjudicated restarts in both arms, the point PPV difference is at least 0.20 or one arm’s indeterminate rate exceeds 20%;
- pump export is selectively absent or delayed for more than 10% overall or more than 5 percentage points differently by arm/site, or clock offset cannot be bounded sufficiently to classify the locked gap thresholds;
- a safety or governance review identifies that data capture is altering medication delivery or that adjudication requires unblinding to treatment outcomes; or
- the VP-first arm cannot accrue at least 25 candidates within the prespecified enrollment period. This is an operational infeasibility stop, not evidence of equivalence.

Do not stop for a favorable PPV point estimate alone. A supportive result requires the final locked gates above. An adverse result is PPV below gate, arm asymmetry, poor sensitivity, or material transition/clock misclassification; it falsifies use of the raw restart component as a sufficiently valid symmetric outcome. An inconclusive result is excessive indeterminacy, missing or unsynchronized pump data, inadequate VP-first accrual, or incompatible site workflows; remedy with better capture or another validation study, never by lowering thresholds or retrospective relabeling.

## What the result can and cannot establish

Supportive results would justify using the validated restart definition in a subsequent prospective study, but would not prove clinical benefit, establish a superior stopping order, establish safety, or justify randomization. Adverse results would require pump-native delivery and adjudicated intent in any future endpoint. Inconclusive results would leave endpoint truth unresolved. None of these phases can determine equipoise, minimum clinically important benefit, or ethical randomization without another clinically reviewed study.

Computationally checkable claims include source binding, cohort counts, time windows, dose normalization, symmetric rule application, invalidation counts, missingness, MAP coverage, PPV/confusion-matrix arithmetic, confidence intervals, and stopping-rule calculations. Clinical adjudication is required for true pump-off time, delivered flow, rescue intent, clinical importance, MCS activity, safety, and whether the endpoint is fit for a future trial. No conclusion from MIMIC alone may be stated as pump truth.

## Public evidence boundary

Public literature can motivate treating vasopressor restart as a clinically relevant but heterogeneous endpoint; for example, the open systematic review by Hamed et al. (DOI `10.1007/s40119-023-00301-0`, Europe PMC source acquired for this episode) reports IV-vasopressor restart as a secondary outcome while finding heterogeneous evidence for liberation strategies. It does not validate MIMIC transaction intervals or establish an order effect. The research-ambition demonstrations were inspected only within their stated availability limits and are methodological examples, not evidence for this endpoint.

## Frozen conclusion

The compact prognostic branch remains closed, assigned positivity remains failed, observed arms remain underpowered, MCS remains unknown, and all interpretation remains noncausal. The executed MIMIC audit is reproducible and useful for exposing endpoint instability, but its material rule dependence and small VP-first review stratum make it adverse for retrospective endpoint validation. The decision-relevant next step is a separately governed prospective study with synchronized pump-native events, workflow transitions, clinician intent, clock quality, true MCS state, and blinded adjudication. Only that study can determine whether a charted restart is a valid rescue endpoint.
