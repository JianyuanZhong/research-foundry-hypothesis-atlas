TITLE
Prompt repeat TACE after the first post-TACE scan shows viable HCC: a 14-day grace-period target-trial emulation of tumor control versus hepatic injury

UNRESOLVED CLINICAL DECISION AND HYPOTHESIS
For a patient with HCC whose first contrast CT/MRI after an initial documented TACE still shows viable intrahepatic tumor, should repeat TACE be delivered promptly or should the team defer repeat TACE while reassessing or switching modality? The unresolved hypothesis is that a strategy of repeat TACE within 14 days of the response scan, compared with no repeat TACE during those 14 days (later locoregional/systemic treatment allowed), is associated with at least a 5-percentage-point lower 180-day risk of radiographically adjudicated progression, without at least a 5-percentage-point increase in 30-day hepatic deterioration.

This is a treatment-strategy association, not a causal benefit claim. The observational EHR cannot establish exchangeability because ECOG status, intended staged-TACE plans, technical selectivity, clinician judgment, and complete outside care are unavailable or incompletely measured.

WHY IT MATTERS / ADVANCE OVER EXISTING KNOWLEDGE
AASLD guidance states that patients treated with TACE should have multiphase CT or contrast MRI at approximately 6 weeks; repeat treatment is provided on demand for continued viable disease, whereas lack of initial response or progression after one to two sessions should prompt consideration of refractoriness and alternative treatment (Singal et al., Hepatology 2023, DOI 10.1097/HEP.0000000000000466; inspected full PMC text at frozen source [source checksum]). However, that guidance does not define how promptly an on-demand second TACE should occur. The ABCR study found that radiologic nonresponse, AFP, BCLC stage, and Child-Pugh worsening predict poor outcomes before a second TACE, but it was a prognostic study rather than a test of response-scan-to-retreatment delay (Adhoute et al., J Hepatol 2015, PMID 25463541, DOI 10.1016/j.jhep.2014.11.014; abstract inspected at frozen source [source checksum]).

The substantive advance is to anchor time zero at the response scan documenting viable disease, explicitly remove same-day scans where treatment ordering is ambiguous, emulate two timing strategies with a grace-period method rather than classify patients using future exposure, and jointly quantify tumor-control and liver-injury signals. The strongest claim currently supported by the literature is that response assessment and selective retreatment matter. The untested claim is whether a short response-scan-to-repeat-TACE interval improves disease control without unacceptable hepatic injury in comparable patients.

POPULATION AND TEMPORAL BOUNDARIES
- Adults age >=18 with confirmed HCC and a first documented qualifying TACE session in the snapshot.
- First post-session liver contrast CT/MRI occurring 28-70 days after that session (primary window centered on the guideline-recommended ~6 weeks; sensitivity 14-90 days).
- The report must be independently adjudicated as definite viable intrahepatic HCC after treatment (mass-like arterial enhancement/washout, explicit viable/residual tumor, or unequivocal progression). Reports that are equivocal, only describe lipiodol deposition, or cannot distinguish HCC from metastasis are excluded from the primary cohort and retained for sensitivity analysis.
- Time zero is the response-scan `开始时间`. Exclude a TACE session on the same calendar day as the scan, because this dataset cannot reliably determine whether imaging preceded or followed intervention. Exclude macrovascular invasion or extrahepatic spread at time zero from the primary cohort because immediate repeat TACE may be inappropriate; analyze them separately.
- Require time zero no later than 2025-07-05, allowing 180 days before the observed administrative end (2026-01-01). Require at least one institutional encounter in the 90 days before initial TACE and at least one source event after time zero; report stricter 180-day prior-activity and 180-day observable-follow-up sensitivity cohorts.
- One episode per patient: first qualifying response scan. Outside treatment and death are not captured, so EHR silence is not assumed to mean event-free survival.

TREATMENT STRATEGIES AND ESTIMAND
At time zero, clone each eligible patient into:
A. Prompt-repeat strategy: initiate a repeat qualifying TACE session on days 1-14 after the response scan.
B. No-prompt-repeat strategy: do not initiate repeat TACE on days 1-14. Ablation, resection, systemic treatment, or repeat TACE after day 14 remain allowed and are described as downstream strategy components; a sensitivity analysis censors at any alternative antitumor treatment during the grace period.

Use clone-censor-weighting: censor a clone when its observed course becomes incompatible with its assigned strategy and weight by estimated adherence probabilities. This avoids immortal-time bias from defining groups by future treatment. Primary estimand is the standardized 180-day risk difference (A minus B) for radiographic progression in the eligible institutional population, with risk ratio as secondary. This remains an observational target-trial estimand conditional on measured covariates, not a proven causal effect.

OUTCOMES
Primary effectiveness outcome: independently adjudicated first radiographic progression by day 180 after time zero, from subsequent contrast CT/MRI reports, defined as unequivocal new intrahepatic lesion, growth/new enhancement of viable treated tumor, new macrovascular invasion, or new extrahepatic metastasis. Two blinded adjudicators review reports in temporal pairs with treatment timing masked; disagreements go to a third hepatobiliary radiologist. Raw images are unavailable, so formal mRECIST cannot be claimed. Report progression-free risk only among patients with outcome-observable follow-up, with inverse-probability-of-observation weighting and bounds treating missing reports as all progressed versus none progressed.

Primary safety outcome: 30-day hepatic deterioration, a composite of (i) exact-label total bilirubin at least 2 times the last value in days -14 to 0, (ii) INR increase >=0.3 from the last value in days -14 to 0, or (iii) newly documented ascites or hepatic encephalopathy in a diagnosis, imaging report, or adjudicated clinical document. Require comparable lab observation windows; report components separately. This definition does not assume unavailable lab units for the ratio and uses INR as unitless, but exact-label/specimen distributions must first be validated.

Secondary outcomes: 90- and 180-day AFP ratio among baseline AFP-secretors with the exact same assay label; time to alternative systemic therapy; number of inpatient encounters; subsequent resection/ablation; and 90-day radiographic disease control. Overall survival is not an outcome because death linkage/disposition is unavailable.

BASELINE CONFOUNDING CONTROLS (measured before time zero only)
Age, sex, calendar year; interval from initial TACE to response scan; number of prior documented TACE sessions and prior resection/ablation; adjudicated largest viable tumor diameter, lesion count, unilobar/bilobar distribution, radiographic response, macrovascular invasion, extrahepatic disease, ascites and cirrhosis; AFP and its pre-scan slope; exact-label albumin, bilirubin, INR, platelet, ALT and creatinine values/slopes; diagnosis-derived CNLC/BCLC information where explicitly recorded; HBV/HCV and antiviral treatment; concurrent systemic anticancer therapy; inpatient versus outpatient context; department; encounter density and imaging/lab intensity during the prior 90 days. Do not infer absent ECOG, Child-Pugh class, or planned staged treatment as normal. Report missingness and use missing indicators plus multiple imputation for partially observed numeric covariates, with complete-case sensitivity analysis.

ANALYSIS
1. Freeze treatment and outcome dictionaries, then validate them against stratified random samples: at least 100 candidate TACE rows, 100 response reports, and 100 progression reports, enriched for negation/equivocal language. Report PPV and sensitivity against dual review; if either is <0.90 for cohort-defining viable disease, use full manual adjudication rather than lexical labels.
2. Describe selection at every step and treatment timing histogram. Assess overlap and standardized mean differences before/after weighting; truncate weights at 1st/99th percentiles and report effective sample size. If either strategy has <100 adjudicated patients, effective sample size <100, or key tumor-burden covariates have absolute SMD >0.10 after weighting, do not present a definitive comparative estimate.
3. Fit weighted pooled-logistic cumulative-incidence models through day 180 with flexible time terms and cluster-robust SEs by patient (one episode, two clones). Use bootstrap CIs including imputation and weighting. Model treatment/adherence and observation using only pre-time-zero or time-updated pre-deviation covariates, as appropriate.
4. Sensitivities: grace periods 7 and 21 days; response-scan windows 28-56 and 14-90 days; repeat-TACE-only versus any liver-directed rescue; exclude concurrent systemic therapy; exact timestamp versus calendar-day session collapse; complete-case and quantitative E-values/bias analysis for an unmeasured confounder; calendar-period stratification; and worst/best-case missing outcome bounds.
5. Negative-control/falsification checks: after weighting, treatment strategy must not predict covariates measured before time zero, including progression language in the pre-index scan and prior-30-day hospitalization. A pseudo-index 30 days before the actual response scan should not show a comparable association. Large residual associations invalidate exchangeability claims rather than being explained away.

PRE-SPECIFIED INTERPRETATION / FALSIFICATION
Supportive: the adjusted 180-day progression risk difference is <= -5 percentage points with a 95% CI excluding 0, while the upper 95% CI for excess 30-day hepatic deterioration is <+5 points; overlap, balance, adjudication, and missing-outcome bounds are acceptable. This supports a measured institutional association consistent with benefit of prompt repeat TACE, not causality or survival benefit.

Adverse: prompt repeat TACE has higher progression (lower 95% CI for RD >0) or excess hepatic deterioration >=+5 points with CI excluding 0, or no effectiveness improvement while hepatic deterioration increases. This argues against routine prompt repetition in this population and may favor reassessment or modality switch, subject to confounding.

Falsified/no comparative claim: negative controls fail, key balance remains >0.10, weight effective sample size is inadequate, treatment/report classification validation is <0.90 without full adjudication, or missing-outcome bounds reverse the conclusion.

Inconclusive: CIs span both a 5-point benefit and a 5-point harm, event counts are low, follow-up imaging is too differential, or results materially change across reasonable scan/grace windows. The correct conclusion is insufficient institutional evidence, not equivalence.

EVIDENCE LIMITS AND REQUIRED ADDITIONAL DATA
Computationally checkable: source joins, timestamps, session collapse, eligibility windows, measured covariates, treatment initiation, lab ratios/INR change, weighting, balance, estimates, uncertainty, and sensitivity analyses. Clinically adjudicated: viable tumor at time zero, progression, tumor burden, ascites/encephalopathy, treatment intent, and whether a same-day or planned multi-session intervention represents one treatment course. Unavailable: raw imaging, complete BCLC/ECOG/Child-Pugh, verified laboratory units, technical TACE details/selectivity, outpatient/outside-hospital care, reasons for delay, complete adverse events, and death linkage. Therefore the experiment cannot establish mRECIST response, overall survival, treatment efficacy, or a universal timing recommendation. A causal timing recommendation would require external validation with complete staging and mortality, expert hepatobiliary/radiology review, and preferably a prospective pragmatic trial or a stronger multi-institutional target-trial emulation.
