# Method comparison for UKB seed 23

## Scientific deliverable and the unresolved claim

The inherited question is whether repeated pre-diagnostic CBC discordance carries cancer-type-specific information: a pattern of haemoglobin falling while platelets rise should be more CRC-specific (occult blood loss/inflammation is one plausible explanation), whereas a broader multi-lineage change should be relatively more associated with hematologic cancer. The available evidence supports only that anaemia and longitudinal CBC abnormalities can be associated with CRC risk; it does not establish that a repeated CBC pattern distinguishes CRC from hematologic cancer, identify occult blood loss or clonal hematopoiesis as the mechanism, or justify clinical referral.

The deliverable is newly fitted, uncertainty-quantified evidence for one prespecified contrast and one bounded learned comparator:

1. An interpretable cause-specific competing-risk analysis estimating the CRC-versus-hematologic difference in association for CBC discordance.
2. A learned discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model fitted on the same CBC observations to test whether nonlinear interactions and temporal risk shape contain information lost by the linear contrast model.
3. Held-out predictions, cumulative-incidence/calibration outputs, coefficient or feature-effect summaries, and falsification results. A small predictive improvement alone is not a scientific conclusion.

The primary estimand is the difference between the CRC and hematologic cause-specific log-hazard coefficients for the pre-specified haemoglobin/platelet discordance contrast, among participants cancer-free at the second CBC and alive/event-free at a two-year delayed entry. The primary follow-up window is from 2 through 7 years after the second CBC assessment; 0–2 years is a prespecified near-diagnostic sensitivity window, and 7–10 years is exploratory if administrative follow-up supports it.

## Common cohort and timing rules

1. Require nonmissing `eid`, `53-0.0`, `53-1.0`, all eight primary CBC values at instances 0 and 1, and `53-1.0 > 53-0.0`. Record the assessment gap; do not silently treat a missing value as zero.
2. Define the landmark (T_1 = 53	ext{-}1.0). Exclude anyone with any nonmissing cancer date `40005-* leq T_1), regardless of cancer type. This is a cancer-free-at-second-CBC cohort, not a claim that all occult cancer has been excluded.
3. Define delayed entry (E=T_1+730) days. For the primary estimand, retain only participants without cancer or death in ((T_1,E]), then follow from (E) through (min(T_1+7	ext{ years}, 2022	ext{-}12	ext{-}31)). The source audit found the latest nonmissing cancer date to be 2022-06-01 and latest death date 2022-12-19; the fixed administrative boundary prevents data-dependent follow-up extension.
4. Define the near-diagnostic sensitivity estimand from T1 to T1 + 730 days, with the same target/competing-event definitions. Effects present only in this window are interpreted as detection or measurement-timing evidence, not long-latency risk.
5. For each participant, the first post-entry event is CRC, hematologic cancer, other cancer, death, or censoring. For a target cause, other event types are competing events in the cumulative-incidence analysis and are censoring events in the cause-specific hazard risk set. The solver must report counts, person-time, event dates, assessment-gap distribution, and missingness before fitting.

CBC preprocessing is frozen before looking at cancer outcomes. Fit sex-stratified or sex-adjusted training-set means and standard deviations for each CBC variable at instance 0; use those training quantities to standardize both instances. Let (d_j=z_{j,1}-z_{j,0}). The pre-specified contrasts are:

- (D_{HP}=d_{platelet}-d_{haemoglobin}), where a larger value means a relative platelet rise and haemoglobin fall.
- (D_{ML}=mean(abs(d_{WBC}), abs(d_{haemoglobin}), abs(d_{RDW}), abs(d_{platelet}))), a fixed summary of multi-lineage instability.

The model also includes the four baseline CBC z-scores and four changes separately, because the contrast is not allowed to hide level or regression-to-the-mean effects. Use age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP, CBC assessment gap, and the corresponding baseline date year as prespecified adjustment variables. No outcome-guided thresholds, post-landmark labs, or field selection is allowed.

## Participant-level temporal split

Sort eligible participants by (T_1), preserving participant rows intact. Use the earliest 70% of landmark dates for development and the latest 30% for the final held-out test. Within the development period, use rolling participant-level folds for feature preprocessing, hyperparameter selection and out-of-fold calibration. No person contributes to both sides, and no cancer date, normalization statistic, imputation value, or tuning choice from the held-out period is used during fitting. If either held-out target event has fewer than 100 events, report the learned model as an imprecise feasibility result rather than replacing the temporal split with a random split.

## Method A: transparent baseline

Fit two cause-specific Cox models, one for CRC and one for hematologic cancer, with the same covariates and (D_{HP}), (D_{ML}), CBC levels and changes. Use penalized partial likelihood only if separation or convergence requires it, with the penalty and convergence status recorded. The primary test is the cause-by-(D_{HP}) interaction, equivalently the difference between the two cause-specific coefficients. Report hazard ratios, the CRC-minus-hematologic coefficient difference, robust 95% confidence intervals, and Schoenfeld/time-varying-effect diagnostics.

As a competing-risk descriptive analysis, estimate 5-year cumulative incidence for each target under Aalen–Johansen with other cancer and death as competing events. A Fine–Gray subdistribution analysis is secondary and must not replace the cause-specific estimand. Compare the pre-specified discordance groups only for illustration; the primary inference remains continuous and prespecified.

This baseline answers the explanatory question directly: is the same measured CBC discordance associated differently with the two cancer types after adjustment? It loses nonlinear thresholds, interactions (for example, a platelet rise that matters only at low haemoglobin), and patient-specific risk trajectories.

## Method B: substantive learned alternative

Fit a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model using the identical eligible participants and the same raw information: the eight CBC levels at instances 0 and 1, their four changes, assessment gap, CRP baseline/change when available, age, sex, BMI, smoking and landmark year. Do not give it cancer dates or any post-landmark variable.

Expand each participant into one row per one-year interval from (E) to (T_1+7), stopping at the first event or censoring. The fitting target is a four-class interval outcome conditional on being event-free at interval start: CRC, hematologic cancer, other cancer/death, or no event. Include interval number as a feature so the model estimates time-varying hazards. Use a fixed, modest HistGradientBoostingClassifier or equivalent gradient-boosted tree implementation, with hyperparameters selected by rolling development folds. Derive target-specific cumulative-incidence predictions from the predicted interval hazards, accounting for the competing event class.

The learned alternative is scientifically substantive because it can reveal whether the baseline’s linear contrast is an inadequate summary: nonlinear CBC ranges, interactions among all four cell lines, and different time profiles for CRC versus hematologic cancer. It is not a mechanism detector. Use permutation/SHAP-style summaries only as descriptive evidence, computed without looking at the held-out outcomes when defining the model.

Compare three nested information sets on the same held-out participants:

- (M_0): age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP, gap and landmark year.
- (M_A): (M_0) plus the pre-specified CBC levels, changes and contrasts in the cause-specific baseline.
- (M_B): (M_0) plus the identical raw CBC levels and changes in the learned model.

The substantive comparison is (M_B) versus (M_A) for target-specific calibration and cumulative-incidence log score/Brier score at 2 and 5 years after delayed entry, with time-dependent AUC or Uno C-index reported secondarily. Require calibration intercept/slope, observed-versus-predicted cumulative incidence, and bootstrap 95% intervals from participant-resampled held-out predictions. A small AUC gain without better calibration or a more informative discordance pattern is not a reason to prefer B.

## Falsification and interpretation

The study is supportive only if the primary held-out or full-development association has the prespecified direction—larger (D_{HP}) relatively more CRC-associated than hematologic cancer—and the CRC-versus-hematologic coefficient difference is not explained solely by the 0–2-year window. A supportive learned result additionally requires stable target-specific calibration and a reproducible nonlinear or interaction pattern across development folds, not merely a small discrimination increase.

Adverse evidence is any of the following: the interaction is directionally reversed with a confidence interval excluding the prespecified direction; the difference disappears after the two-year delayed-entry rule; the same CBC pattern predicts other cancers similarly, eliminating type specificity; or the learned model’s apparent advantage disappears on the temporal holdout with poor calibration. These outcomes revise or abandon the discordance interpretation while leaving open simpler descriptive associations.

Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals from sparse held-out hematologic events, unstable results across the instance-0/1 versus instance-0/2 sensitivity cohorts, or acceptable discrimination with poor calibration. It supports more data or adjudication, not a positive hypothesis claim.

Checks that must be run without favorable-result peeking:

- Report 0–2, 2–7 and available 7–10-year windows separately.
- Repeat with CBC changes adjusted for assessment gap and with winsorization rules fixed before outcome inspection.
- Test the target-specific interaction against other primary cancer codes as a specificity control.
- Permute cancer type within event dates in development as a pipeline negative control; the type interaction should collapse toward zero.
- Compare complete-case participants to the full two-assessment eligibility denominator and report selection; do not impute missing CBC as normal.
- Verify paired date/code alignment and audit duplicate or implausible dates before event derivation.

## Resources and method decision

The discovery audit used short CPU scans: reading the Parquet schema took about 4 seconds and reading the selected CBC/outcome columns took about 6–7 seconds in the ordinary shell; full model fitting and bootstrap uncertainty are not measured. The configured discovery budget is 2 concurrent jobs, 2 GPU slots and 9,000 science seconds. The future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds. This proposal requests CPU execution for both methods: 8–16 CPUs and approximately 32–64 GiB for feature construction and fitting, with 2–4 hours for the complete baseline, gradient-boosted model, rolling development folds and 200–500 participant bootstrap replicates. GPU use is unnecessary for the proposed HistGradientBoosting model; a GPU would be considered only if a solver substitutes a documented model after a bounded feasibility measurement. These are planning estimates, not completed study results.

The baseline is selected as the primary scientific method because its estimand and uncertainty map directly onto the competing explanations and can be audited by clinicians. Method B is retained as a prespecified substantive challenge, not as an automatic winner. A latent Gaussian-process/state model or transformer is deferred: the verified cohort has only two broadly complete CBC assessments (instance 2 has 5,862 complete rows), no dense serial laboratory history for most participants, and no verified genetic requirement for this question. Those methods could be revisited if a later source supplies repeated dates and adequate outcome support; complexity alone is not a reason to use them.

## Evidence and clinical limits

The closest inspected work [K1] reports a retrospective longitudinal CBC signal for CRC metastatic phenotype but explicitly says prospective independent validation is still needed. [K2] establishes that anaemia can contribute to a validated primary-care CRC risk equation, but it is a static, single-disease result and does not establish CBC discordance or hematologic specificity. [K3] supports temporal validation and trajectory-aware modeling as design principles; it does not authorize reproducing its genetic latent model with these data.

The UKB registry fields do not provide pathology adjudication, iron/ferritin or reticulocyte studies, peripheral smear, marrow studies, FIT/colonoscopy findings, cancer stage, symptoms, infection timing, medications, transfusions, or treatment. Therefore no result can distinguish occult bleeding from inflammation, nutrition, clonal hematopoiesis, reverse causation or healthcare-use/measurement selection. A positive association is descriptive/predictive evidence, not a causal or mechanistic claim. Clinical action would require expert review of code definitions and CBC measurement quality, external validation in a clinical population, and a prospective study linked to diagnostic outcomes. An automated verifier can check the data binding, derived outputs, split, uncertainty and whether conclusions match those outputs; it cannot establish the missing clinical mechanism or clinical utility.
