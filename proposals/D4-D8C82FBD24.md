## Scientific opening, strongest evidence, and unresolved claim

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. Large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, this difference was associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes, so a combined equation can lose prognostic information [K2]. Administrative AKI coding can be insensitive and subgroup-dependent relative to creatinine-defined AKI [K3]. These works do not establish that equal-combined-eGFR negative discordance selectively predicts biological AKI, that UKB first-appearance dates are encounters, or that broad diagnosis thresholds are biologically exchangeable with N17.

The unresolved primary hypothesis is: among supported P− and P0 profiles at equal combined eGFR, P− has a larger 10-year contrast for first recorded N17 than for first recorded N18 after measured baseline clinical characteristics and pre-index first-appearance history are balanced.

The leading bounded interpretation is recorded acute-renal selectivity. The strongest rival is stable generalized morbidity/recording activity: if it persists from pre-index history, it should affect broad discovery-date frequency and conditional same-day density as well as renal first appearances. A second rival is clustered coding density, which should concentrate N17 on dates carrying many newly appearing nonrenal groups. Actual contact, admission severity, testing, disease onset, coding intent, and biological AKI are unavailable and remain observationally inseparable.

The substantive advance is a falsifiable comparison of N17 versus N18 combined with a support-aware analysis of measured first-appearance frequency and density. Even a supportive result means only “recorded N17 selectivity beyond measured pre-index first-appearance activity”; it justifies external encounter/laboratory adjudication, not a clinical action.

## Scientifically substantive alternative: support-aware recurrent-date/mark model

The same strongest rival is tested with all available first-appearance history rather than sparse threshold crossings.

1. Fit marker-blind population parameters on the 70% common-risk training split.
2. Use an explicit no-history hurdle and early [index−10y,index−5y) versus late [index−5y,index) date counts, group counts, bundle marks, recency, chapters, and principal confirmation.
3. Fit yearly piecewise-exponential recurrent nonrenal discovery-date intensity with population frailty, and a zero-truncated negative-binomial bundle-size mark conditional on a date with correlated frailty; death is absorbing.
4. Infer participant effects from pre-index history only. If fewer than three pre-index discovery dates exist, integrate over the population posterior and retain a support indicator; do not emit an apparently precise individual factor.
5. Compare fixed-covariate, intensity-only, mark-only, and correlated two-part variants. Fit on training, calibrate/select on validation, evaluate once on untouched test.
6. Estimate profile contrasts in recurrent date frequency and conditional bundle density, plus a pre-index-factor-adjusted renal sensitivity. Never update factors with post-S dates, bundles, renal outcomes, or death. The renal sensitivity is conditional and assumption-dependent, not mediation or a causal direct effect.

This model retains subthreshold timing, recurrent dates, all bundle marks, and their dependence. It loses code composition and depends on frailty/negative-binomial assumptions. Median early and late date counts were zero in both raw profiles; only 3,910/19,818 P− and 1,902/11,982 P0 had dates in both early and late windows. Therefore hurdle/support-stratified calibration and population integration are essential.

Report yearly date-count and zero calibration, bundle mean/variance/quantiles/tail, B30/F15/H10 calibration/Brier where sufficiently supported pooled, N17/N18/death calibration, joint deviance, frailty correlation, early-to-late transport, and bootstrap stability. Require calibration intercept |≤0.10| and slope 0.8–1.2 for sufficiently supported binary endpoints; observed/expected yearly counts 0.8–1.25 in ≥8/10 years; 90th bundle-quantile error ≤1 group or ≤20%; no Brier degradation >0.01 versus transparent prediction; and acceptable calibration in no-history/history-support strata. Failure is diagnostic failure, not renal evidence.

Code-composition NMF remains deferred because it tests a different composition/salience question and could absorb baseline morbidity. Revisit only if the frozen two-part model fails a held-out process target and training/validation NMF repairs that target without >0.01 Brier or >5% deviance deterioration elsewhere. A transformer remains deferred because only first appearances are available and sequence prediction cannot repair profile-cell support. A true-GFR model requires measured GFR or repeated-marker error data.

## Leakage, identifiability, and uncertainty

The count-only audit cannot be used to choose a direction. Population process fitting is marker-blind. Participant effects use pre-index data only; post-S broad processes are outcomes. ## Falsification and result meanings

All interpretations require verified C/field semantics, clinician code review, profile/matching/balance gates, sufficient renal precision, calibrated process fit, bootstrap success, and sensitivity agreement.

Supportive for bounded recorded-N17 selectivity requires lower 95% bounds of `theta17` and `D` >0, matched/principal agreement, and a calibrated process model showing no comparably material P−/P0 difference in recurrent broad date frequency or conditional density. B30/F15/H10 and N17 bundle counts are descriptive corroboration only. This cannot exclude unmeasured contact or renal-code salience.

Adverse to acute-versus-chronic selectivity is `D` with upper 95% bound ≤0 or a precise similar N17/N18 contrast. Both renal theta upper bounds ≤0 are adverse to both profile claims.

Adverse stable-process evidence is a calibrated, material P−/P0 contrast in recurrent broad date frequency commensurate with the renal contrast. Adverse density evidence is a calibrated material contrast in conditional bundle density, with descriptive N17 concentration on dense dates if present. These patterns are compatible with morbidity/capture or clustered coding, not proof of contact/admission.

Inconclusive includes unverified C, failed support/matching/balance, sparse renal intervals spanning material alternatives, failed temporal transport, process miscalibration/instability, marginal/conditional disagreement, death-driven or principal/tie reversal, or <90% bootstrap success. Failed secondary profile-cell gates are known limitations, not evidence for or against the hypothesis.
