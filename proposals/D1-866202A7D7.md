# Does a worsening structured nursing-assessment acuity predict subsequent ICU deterioration and mortality beyond baseline severity and contemporaneous vitals?

A falsifiable, data-bound experiment on eICU-CRD 2.0 (evolved from [prior hypothesis]; table family `nurseAssessment` is unused by any delivered candidate).

## 1. Hypothesis

**H1 (falsifiable):** A rise in a composite nursing-assessment acuity score (ordinal-encoded LOC + Stimulus + Speech + Orientation + Edema) measured at an index time *t* predicts (a) subsequent ICU deterioration (new vasopressor start OR new mechanical ventilation within 24 h after *t*) and (b) ICU mortality, **incrementally beyond** a baseline model of admission APACHE score + contemporaneous vitals (mean arterial pressure, heart rate, SpO2 in the 12 h before *t*).

**H0:** No incremental signal — the nursing-assessment acuity change is redundant with APACHE + contemporaneous vitals (ΔAUC ≈ 0, 95% CI including 0).

**Strongest rival explanations (and the observation that separates them):**
- **R1 Reverse causation / leakage:** the nurse documents acuity *after* the deterioration has already begun (a nurse documenting "obtunded" at the same moment a vasopressor is started). Separated by the 6 h index-time lead: the acuity rise must precede the deterioration event by ≥ 6 h. If the incremental signal disappears when the lead is removed, R1 is the driver.
- **R2 Severity confounding:** sicker patients both have worse nursing assessments and worse outcomes. Separated by adjustment for admission APACHE + contemporaneous vitals; if the acuity-change term is null after adjustment, R2 explains the raw association.
- **R3 Documentation-practice artifact:** more-frequent nursing assessments (or different hospital documentation habits) create spurious "change" signals. Separated by hospital random intercept + a sensitivity analysis conditioning on assessment frequency (assessments per 12 h).

## 2. Scientific opening

**Established (inspected):**
- Structured point-of-care acuity/dependency measures (COMFORTER) improve post-MET in-hospital mortality risk ranking beyond NEWS (AUC 0.84 vs 0.75) [K1].
- Electronic early-warning / track-and-trigger systems rely primarily on vital signs and assessment scores (62.7% of reported clinical indexes), but the evidence is limited by heterogeneous targets and poor reporting of development history [K2].
- First-24-h routine vital signs alone support an interpretable ML framework with AUROC 0.803 for in-hospital mortality in MIMIC-IV (XGBoost, 46,259 survivors / 5,339 deaths) [K3].

**Unresolved question:** Whether a *change* in a structured nursing-assessment acuity composite — a data source that is routinely captured in eICU-CRD but unused by any delivered candidate — adds incremental prognostic value for ICU deterioration and mortality **beyond** admission severity + contemporaneous vitals. The closest inspected works test either (a) a static acuity/dependency score in the ward [K1], (b) the field of early-warning systems [K2], or (c) a vitals-only baseline [K3]; none tests a structured nursing-assessment *change* as an incremental ICU prognostic feature.

**Substantive advance over existing knowledge:** This is the first test, to our inspection, of whether the *trajectory* of a structured nursing neurologic/edema acuity composite carries incremental signal beyond vitals + APACHE for ICU deterioration and mortality. A positive result would justify adding nursing-assessment acuity change to ICU early-warning dashboards; a null result would bound the incremental value of this data source (consistent with the ~0.01 AUC incremental bound implied by [K1]/[K3]) and argue for vitals-only models.

## 3. Data bindings (verified)

Source: `nurseAssessment.csv.gz` (197,999,490 bytes; [source checksum]); schema `datasets/eicu/table-a782000603e9e364.json`.
Columns: `nurseassessid`, `patientunitstayid`, `nurseassessoffset`, `nurseassessentryoffset`, `cellattributepath`, `celllabel`, `cellattribute`, `cellattributevalue`. Identity `patientunitstayid`; temporal `nurseassessoffset`/`nurseassessentryoffset`.

Executed probe ([research job], 78 s): 15,602,498 rows; 13,001 unique stays. Ordinal encodings verified:
- **LOC** (348,071): normal > lethargic > sedated > somnolent > obtunded > comatose.
- **Stimulus** (338,670): spontaneous > sound > pain > unresponsive.
- **Speech** (343,662): clear > ET/Trach > garbled > none > moan/grunt > slurred > aphasic > inappropriate > dysphasic (ET/Trach and "unable to assess" treated as missing for the composite).
- **Orientation** (798,589): count of intact domains among {to person, to place, to time, to situation}; "unable to assess" = missing.
- **Edema** (239,057): no edema < generalized < anasarca.

Composite acuity score = sum of ordinal ranks (LOC 0–5, Stimulus 0–3, Speech 0–8, Orientation 0–4, Edema 0–2), range 0–22; higher = worse. Acuity *change* = score(t) − score(t−12 h) (first two assessments ≥ 6 h apart).

Join keys and outcome tables:
- `patientunitstayid` → `apachePatientResult` (admission APACHE), `vitalPeriodic`/`vitalAperiodic` (systemicmean, heartrate, sao2), `infusionDrug` (vasopressor drugnames: norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine), `respiratoryCharting` (ventstartoffset for new MV), `patient`/`hospital` (hospital id, age, sex).
- Index time *t*: second nursing assessment in the stay with ≥ 6 h separation from the first and ≥ 24 h before ICU discharge.
- Deterioration outcome: new vasopressor start OR new MV within 24 h after *t*.
- Mortality outcome: ICU mortality (disposition).

Analysis cohort (prior ep14 probe, re-verified): 11,238 stays, 16 hospitals, 6.97% mortality, 34.2% deterioration rate, clean vasopressor drugnames, vitals systemicmean/heartrate/sao2 present.

## 4. Analysis plan

**Models (pre-specified 2×2):**
- **M0 (baseline):** logistic regression / HistGradientBoosting on admission APACHE + contemporaneous vitals (MAP, HR, SpO2 in 12 h before *t*).
- **M1 (alternative):** M0 + acuity-change term (and acuity level at *t*).
- Primary: logistic (interpretable coefficient + odds ratio); secondary: HistGradientBoosting (nonlinear interactions).

**Temporal-ordering control (R1):** index-time 6 h lead — acuity change must precede the deterioration event by ≥ 6 h. Sensitivity: remove the lead (same-time documentation) and compare ΔAUC; if the signal collapses, R1 (reverse causation) is the driver.

**Hospital structure (R3):** hospital random intercept (multilevel logistic) + fixed-effects sensitivity. Conditioning on assessment frequency (assessments per 12 h) as a sensitivity analysis.

**Evaluation:** ΔAUC (M1 − M0) with bootstrap 95% CI (1,000 resamples, hospital-stratified); calibration (Brier); discrimination by deterioration and mortality outcomes separately.

**Falsification criteria:**
- **Supportive:** ΔAUC > 0 with 95% CI excluding 0 for at least one outcome (deterioration or mortality), robust to the 6 h lead and hospital random intercept.
- **Adverse:** ΔAUC ≈ 0 (95% CI including 0) after the 6 h lead — the acuity change is redundant with vitals + APACHE; bound the incremental value and prefer vitals-only models.
- **Inconclusive:** ΔAUC > 0 but CI includes 0, or the signal appears only without the 6 h lead (R1) — requires a larger cohort / prospective confirmation.

**Compute:** CPU-only, < 2 h (11,238 stays × ~50 features; logistic + HistGBM + bootstrap). No GPU required.

## 5. Limitations (honest)
- 16-hospital analysis cohort — narrow random-intercept range; multicenter generalization untested.
- ET/Trach and "unable to assess" treated as missing — possible informative missingness.
- Observational, single time period; no causal claim.
- Composite acuity is a proxy for neurologic/edema status; does not capture the full nursing assessment (respiratory, cardiovascular, Braden).
- Deterioration outcome (new vasopressor/MV) is a surrogate, not a hard clinical endpoint.
