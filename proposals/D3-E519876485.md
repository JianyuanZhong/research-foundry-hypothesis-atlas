## 1. Scientific Opening

The D3-M3 seed asks: "Which mechanisms could explain persistent versus resolving cell states, and what test separates them?"

**Established evidence (not re-tested here):**
- Alivernini et al. [K1] showed that MerTKposTREM2high and MerTKposLYVE1pos synovial tissue macrophage (STM) subpopulations carry unique remission transcriptomic signatures enriched in negative regulators of inflammation, and that a low MerTKpos proportion in remission predicts disease flare.
- Kuo et al. [K3] showed that HBEGF+ inflammatory macrophages drive FLS invasiveness via EGFR; MERTK marks a distinct phagocytic macrophage cluster.
- Wei & Xu [K2] (2026 review) proposed a "failed-resolution switch" in difficult-to-treat RA: a self-sustaining stromal–myeloid circuit where imprinted pathological fibroblasts and macrophages stripped of pro-resolving/efferocytic subsets lock non-resolution through feed-forward signaling, reinforced by lipid-mediator, metabolic, and epigenetic determinants.

**Unresolved question:** Wei & Xu's "failed-resolution switch" is a mechanistic framework, not a testable gene-level signature. Alivernini identified remission-enriched STM clusters but did not define a multi-gene module that separates active RA (naive + treatment-resistant) from remission at the macrophage level. The specific question is: **Does a multi-gene inflammatory module (IFNγ/STAT1 + oxidative stress + MMP/TIMP + chemokine) distinguish active RA from remission in synovial macrophages, and is this module a shared active-RA state or a resistant-specific program?**

## 2. Hypotheses

### H1 (shared active-RA module — leading)
A 10-gene module (STAT1, IRF1, SOD2, GPX1, MMP9, TIMP1, CCL2, SPP1, SOCS3, HMOX1) is elevated in active RA (naive + resistant) vs remission/HC in synovial macrophages, capturing a shared inflammatory macrophage program. The module does NOT uniquely separate resistant from naive — the "persistence" signal is a shared active-RA state, not a resistant-specific program.

**Rationale:** Discovery probes on E-MTAB-8322 (executed this episode) show:
- Module score: Resistant 1.627±0.195, Naive 1.682±0.265, Remission 1.284±0.317, HC 0.996±0.033.
- AUC: Resistant vs HC = 1.000; Remission vs Naive = 0.119 (remission LOW); Resistant vs Naive = 0.389 (NOT separable).
- The module separates active (naive+resistant) from remission/HC but NOT resistant from naive.

### H2 (resistant-specific program — rival)
A distinct resistant-specific module exists that is not captured by the 10-gene candidate set, separating resistant from naive RA. This would require a different gene set (e.g., epigenetic, metabolic, or lipid-mediator genes consistent with Wei & Xu's "failed-resolution switch" determinants).

**Rationale:** Wei & Xu propose that refractory disease involves redundant, mutually reinforcing arms (lipid-mediator, metabolic, epigenetic) that would not be captured by a simple inflammatory gene set. If H2 is correct, the 10-gene module's failure to separate resistant from naive (AUC=0.389) is expected, and a different module would be needed.

### H3 (coculture validation)
The 5-gene subset of the module present in the E-MTAB-8873 445-gene targeted panel (STAT1, MMP9, TIMP1, CCL2, SPP1) is elevated in inflammatory macrophage coculture conditions (Mpos, Mneg) vs FLS-only baseline, linking the in vivo active-RA macrophage program to the in vitro inflammatory macrophage–fibroblast interaction.

**Rationale:** Discovery probes show:
- FLS_only: 2.319±0.190 (n=6); Mpos: 2.708±0.403 (n=8); Mneg: 2.885±0.551 (n=9).
- Mneg vs FLS_only: Δ=+0.566, p=0.0879 (not significant, small n).
- Both Mpos and Mneg show elevated module scores vs FLS_only.

## 4. Analysis Plan

### Baseline (paired/module-score comparison)
1. Compute 10-gene module score per donor in E-MTAB-8322 (mean of log1p-normalized expression across 10 genes).
2. Compare module scores across 5 clinical states using Mann-Whitney U tests (donor as unit).
3. Compute AUC for: (a) active (naive+resistant) vs remission+HC; (b) resistant vs naive; (c) resistant vs remission; (d) resistant vs HC.
4. Permutation test (10,000 permutations) for resistant vs naive AUC to account for small n.

### Alternative (module composition analysis)
1. Identify which individual genes drive the module score differences (per-gene AUC for each of the 10 genes).
2. Test whether a 5-gene subset (excluding the 5 genes absent from E-MTAB-8873) retains discriminative power.
3. In E-MTAB-8873: compute 5-gene subset module score per sample; compare Mpos/Mneg vs FLS_only using paired comparisons (donor as unit).
4. Test whether the subset module score in Mneg coculture correlates with the in vivo module score in resistant RA (cross-dataset coherence, exploratory).

### Falsification criteria
- **H1 supportive:** Module separates active (naive+resistant) from remission+HC with AUC > 0.8 AND resistant vs naive AUC ≈ 0.5 (not separable).
- **H1 adverse (supports H2):** Module separates resistant from naive with AUC > 0.7, indicating a resistant-specific program.
- **H3 supportive:** 5-gene subset elevated in Mpos/Mneg vs FLS_only with p < 0.05 (paired test).
- **H3 adverse:** No significant difference in subset module between coculture and FLS_only.
- **Inconclusive:** AUC between 0.6–0.8 for active vs remission (underpowered); or module score confounded by donor-specific effects.

## 5. What Each Outcome Would Establish

- **Supportive of H1:** The "persistence" signal in RA macrophages is a shared active-RA inflammatory program (IFNγ/STAT1 + oxidative stress + MMP/TIMP), not a resistant-specific state. This would mean that treatment resistance is not driven by a unique macrophage transcriptional program but by the failure to resolve the shared inflammatory state — consistent with Wei & Xu's "failed-resolution switch" framework. Clinical implication: resolution-restoring strategies (pro-resolving mediators, macrophage reprogramming) may be more effective than additional anti-inflammatory agents for treatment-resistant RA.
- **Adverse to H1 (supports H2):** A resistant-specific macrophage program exists, requiring a different gene set (epigenetic, metabolic, lipid-mediator) to identify. This would mean that treatment resistance involves a distinct cellular state beyond the shared inflammatory program, and that the 10-gene module is insufficient. Clinical implication: stratification by the 10-gene module would not identify treatment-resistant patients; a different biomarker is needed.
- **Inconclusive:** Small sample sizes (n=6 resistant, n=6 naive, n=7 remission) limit power. A follow-up study with more donors or a different module composition would be needed.

## 6. Limitations
1. **Cross-sectional design:** E-MTAB-8322 is a cross-sectional snapshot of 5 clinical states. Cannot establish longitudinal transitions (naive → resistant or naive → remission). The "persistence" framing is a state-separation question, not a transition claim.
2. **Small n:** 6 resistant, 6 naive, 7 remission, 4 HC donors. Permutation-based inference required; AUC estimates have wide confidence intervals.
3. **Exploratory module:** The 10-gene module was chosen from discovery probes (top resistant-remission genes), not pre-specified. This is an exploratory hypothesis, not a confirmatory test.
4. **E-MTAB-8873 subset:** Only 5 of 10 module genes are in the 445-gene targeted panel. The subset is a proxy, not the full module.
5. **Macrophage-only:** E-MTAB-8322 pseudobulk contains measured macrophages only, not whole tissue. Cannot assess cell-type composition effects.
6. **No wet-lab validation:** All findings are computational; no functional validation of the module in vivo or in vitro.
