# MerTK inhibition in the macrophage–fibroblast circuit: separating benefit from harm in RA synovial cocultures

**Domain:** Therapeutic Target Prioritization (D2) · **Seed:** D2-T2 ([prior hypothesis], evolved) · **Episode:** 1

## 1. Unresolved question and its clinical importance

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) synovitis is sustained by a stromal–myeloid circuit: inflammatory macrophages activate fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS), which produce IL-6, chemokines and matrix metalloproteinases that drive joint destruction, while macrophages with a MerTK/TAM-receptor program (MERTK/MRC1/AXL/CD163) are associated with efferocytosis, inflammatory resolution and drug-free remission. The unresolved question for target prioritization is directional: **does pharmacologic inhibition of macrophage MerTK (UNC106238) in RA FLS–macrophage cocultures reduce the harmful fibroblast inflammatory/matrix program in a macrophage-state-dependent manner, and does the same intervention preserve or erode the protective MERTK+/remission-linked macrophage program?**

Clinically this matters because an intervention that suppresses macrophage-driven FLS activation while dismantling the pro-resolving macrophage state could yield a net-worse outcome (impaired resolution, flare risk), and conversely a profile that reduces the FLS program without eroding the protective macrophage module would prioritize MerTK-dependent signaling as a candidate axis for context-specific targeting. Current knowledge does not answer this: the harmful macrophage→FLS axis (e.g., HBEGF-driven FLS invasiveness) [K1] and the association of MERTK+ macrophage programs with resolving/fibrosis-resolution states [K2] are established in isolation, but their **joint response to the same intervention has not been measured with separated benefit vs adverse readouts**, and remission studies show that anti-rheumatic suppression alone does not reinstate protective myeloid states [K3]. This proposal does NOT rebrand the known NOTCH3, HBEGF or PGDH findings from these datasets; it asks a new intervention-direction question across them.

## 2. Hypothesis (testable claim)

H1: In RA FLS–macrophage cocultures, macrophage pre-treatment with the MerTK inhibitor UNC106238 reduces the FLS inflammatory/matrix program (IL6, IL11, MMP1/2/3/9/12/13, CXCL1/2/3/5/8 module) **more in MerTK/CD206pos-macrophage cocultures than in MerTK/CD206neg cocultures** (state-dependence), while **reducing** the FLS-instructing macrophage module; and the same inhibitor leaves the macrophage pro-resolving module (MERTK, MRC1, AXL, CD163, CD200, ARG1, APOE, C1QA/B) measurably **depleted** in coculture (adverse readout), such that benefit and adverse readouts dissociate rather than co-move.

Rival (R1): UNC106238 effects are macrophage-state-independent (uniform shift), i.e., context does not matter for the intervention response. Rival (R2): observed effects are run/batch artifacts (R1/R2/R5 experimental runs) rather than biology. Rival (R3): apparent FLS-program changes reflect coculture cell-composition shifts rather than per-cell transcriptional change.

## 4. Analysis design

**Readout modules** (fixed a priori):
- Harmful FLS program score = log-normalized mean of IL6, IL11, MMP1, MMP2, MMP3, MMP9, MMP12, MMP13, MMP14, CXCL1, CXCL2, CXCL3, CXCL5, CXCL8 (all measured in the 8873 panel).
- Protective/pro-resolving macrophage module = MERTK, MRC1, AXL, CD163, CD200, ARG1, APOE, C1QA, C1QB; plus an empirically defined **remission macrophage module** from E-MTAB-8322 (pseudobulk differential expression, remission vs active RA donors, MAST/edgeR-style GLM, FDR<0.05), intersected with the 445-gene panel for cross-application to 8873.

**Baseline (simple, primary) analysis:** pseudobulk per-sample module scores → donor-paired, run-stratified linear contrasts in 8873 (Mpos vs FLS_only; Mneg vs FLS_only; within-context effect of UNC106238 vs untreated; interaction UNC106238 × macrophage state), with donor pairs (FLS donor × macrophage donor) modeled as random/blocking factors and batch R1/R2/R5 as covariate; repeated context measurements averaged per donor/condition before inference (domain-guide limit). In 8316: 6 paired donor instances, paired analysis (UNC106238 vs none) on module scores and gene-level FDR, reporting the same harmful-FLS and pro-resolving module deltas, with no 12-independent-donor claim.

**Substantive alternative (learned/mechanistic) analysis on the same question:** a cell-type deconvolved mixed model / non-negative matrix factorization on the 8873 pseudobulk matrix (or deconvolution using marker genes PTPRC/CD14 vs COL1A1/COL3A1) to test whether the intervention's apparent FLS-program effect survives correction for cell-composition shifts (R3), and a regularized (elastic-net) interaction model predicting module scores from context × pretreatment to detect non-additivity the simple contrasts may miss. **What the alternative adds over the baseline:** separates per-cell transcriptional change from composition change — the baseline cannot; without it, a "benefit" signal could be an artifact of fewer macrophages in the well. **Resource estimate:** CPU-only, minutes-scale compute (445×33 matrix + 58k×12 matrix + per-donor 8322 pseudobulks); feasible within the solver CPU envelope; no GPU needed.

**Falsification criteria:**
- *Supportive:* UNC106238 lowers the FLS harmful-program score vs untreated within coculture (paired, run-stratified), with a significant state × inhibitor interaction (direction larger in Mpos), AND the pro-resolving macrophage module changes in the opposite direction (depletion) — establishing dissociated benefit/adverse readouts; 8316 whole-transcriptome direction concordant.
- *Adverse for H1 (state-dependence):* no interaction (uniform or null inhibitor effect across Mpos/Mneg), or inhibitor effect present only after composition correction collapses.
- *Adverse for benefit-without-harm:* FLS program reduced but pro-resolving module also reduced proportionally (co-moving) — i.e., no separability: the intervention profile is non-selective in expression terms.
- *Inconclusive:* high donor/run heterogeneity swamping paired contrasts (insufficient effective n), or panel-driven module scores with all-zero counts.

**Pre-specified caveats:** expression-only evidence — no protein or functional invasion/efferocytosis readout; no causality or target-engagement proof (inhibitor is applied to macrophages pre-coculture; off-target effects of UNC106238 cannot be excluded); in-vitro LPS+coculture ≠ in-vivo synovium; remission module from 8322 is a cross-sectional human comparator, not a treatment effect; remission patients are not a MerTK-inhibitor-treated group.

## 5. Outcomes and what they would mean

- **Supportive:** MerTK-dependent macrophage signaling contributes to the harmful FLS program in a state-dependent way, and inhibiting it carries an explicit adverse-expression signal on pro-resolving macrophage genes — a prioritization decision (which macrophage state to target, and what safety readouts to monitor) becomes concrete and testable in wet-lab/adjudicated follow-up.
- **Adverse (null or uniform):** the harmful FLS program is not MerTK-state-dependent; resources shift to macrophage-state-independent or fibroblast-intrinsic axes; useful negative finding.
- **Inconclusive:** heterogeneity/panel limits dominate; the required next step would be per-cell (not pseudobulk) analysis of the original 8873 cell matrices and larger donor panels.

## 6. Key works

- [K1] Kuo D, et al. HBEGF+ macrophages in rheumatoid arthritis induce fibroblast invasiveness. Sci Transl Med 2019 (PMID 31068444; DOI 10.1126/scitranslmed.aau8587) — establishes the harmful macrophage→FLS axis and is the source of GSE95588; known finding, not rebranded here.
- [K2] Ng MTH, et al. A single cell atlas of frozen shoulder capsule identifies features associated with inflammatory fibrosis resolution. Nat Commun 2024 (PMID 38374174; DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-45341-9) — MERTK+LYVE1+MRC1+ macrophage program associated with spontaneous fibrosis resolution; bounds the "protective macrophage-linked state" concept (non-RA tissue).
- [K3] MacDonald L, et al. Distinct tissue-niche localization and function of synovial tissue myeloid DC subsets in health, and in active and remission Rheumatoid Arthritis. bioRxiv 2024.07.17.600758 (PPR883711) — same RACE/Glasgow grouping; sustained remission resolves inflammatory niches but fails to recover tolerogenic myeloid states; motivates measuring whether interventions erode or spare protective states.
