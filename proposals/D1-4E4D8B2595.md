# Episode 7 successor: does worsening instability before ICU discharge add information beyond current burden and charting intensity?

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`, assessed valid and highest-ranked assessed-valid MIMIC candidate at episode start (mu 32.2166, sigma 6.6987, one comparison).  
Dataset: MIMIC-IV core 3.1 configured snapshot `[source checksum]`.

This is a substantive successor, not a new prediction-model contest. It preserves the parent’s ICU-transition question but changes the primary scientific estimand from “how abnormal is the final day?” to “is instability worsening as transfer approaches?” A worsening trajectory is more directly relevant to a decision about whether a patient’s condition is settling or deteriorating, while a persistently abnormal but stable pattern may have a different meaning. The parent’s final-day repeated-instability contrast remains a prespecified secondary benchmark.

## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

ICU transfer removes continuous high-intensity monitoring and rescue capacity. A patient may have an abnormal final-day burden because of persistent chronic illness, yet be improving; another may have a modest current burden but be deteriorating across the preceding 24 hours. Repeated measurements can also reflect clinician concern and charting intensity. The unresolved claim is therefore whether *change in observed instability* carries information beyond both the current physiologic burden and the opportunity to observe it.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is limited to provenance and measurable fields: the frozen archive has linked adult hospital admissions, ICU stays, ICU chart events, an ICU item dictionary, transfers, and in-hospital death/discharge fields with within-subject timestamps. Direct inspection confirmed the required schema columns and fixed item labels. The parent, expert seed, public literature, and demonstrations do not establish that a worsening final-day trajectory predicts ICU return/death, is independent of ascertainment, or identifies a modifiable discharge policy. No event or coverage count is asserted here.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among adults whose first eligible ICU stay in a continuing hospital admission ends while the patient is alive, a worsening in the fraction of covered four-hour blocks with abnormal valid vital observations from the preceding 24 hours to the final 24 hours before ICU `outtime` is associated with higher seven-day cumulative incidence of first later ICU return and death before ICU return, and adding this prespecified trajectory change to a final-day physiology-plus-measurement-density model improves locked-test calibration and Brier score.

The primary incremental contrast is `B_traj - B_phys` in the paired two-window cohort. `B_phys - B_density` is the parent-preserving secondary contrast. Charted oxygen intensity is a separate secondary contrast and cannot rescue a null trajectory result. All estimands are prognostic associations/within-snapshot predictions, not causal effects of discharge timing, oxygen treatment, or surveillance.

A current Europe PMC search was retained as a novelty check, not a novelty certificate. It returned broad ICU-outcome and trajectory literature, but did not establish this exact MIMIC-bound comparison of final-day change against current burden and measurement density. The available demonstrations motivate longitudinal state representation, calibration, missingness/shortcut auditing, and explicit uncertainty; none establishes this hypothesis.

## Fixed item manifest and exposure construction

Direct inspection of `icu/d_items` verified these IDs and labels:

- `220045): Heart Rate, bpm
- `220052): Arterial Blood Pressure mean, mmHg
- `220181): Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean, mmHg
- `220210): Respiratory Rate, insp/min
- `220277`: O2 saturation pulseoxymetry, %
- `223761): Temperature Fahrenheit, °F
- `223762): Temperature Celsius, °C
- `223834`: O2 Flow, L/min
- `227287`: O2 Flow (additional cannula), L/min
- `227582`: BiPap O2 Flow, L/min
- `223835`: Inspired O2 Fraction, dictionary unit None
- `226732`: O2 Delivery Device(s), text
- `223758`: Code Status, text

For every retained row, apply validity before summary and preserve raw fields only in an internal audit: `value, valueuom, warning, charttime, storetime, itemid). Numeric rules are HR 20–250; RR 4–80; SpO2 50–100; each MAP 20–200 mmHg; temperature 30–43 °C after Fahrenheit conversion `(F-32)/1.8`; oxygen flow nonnegative; FiO2 accepted as 0.21–1.00 or as (1,100] percent divided by 100, with normalized values outside 0.21–1.00 invalid. Invalid values are counted by item and block and excluded, never repaired.

A block is vital-covered if it has at least one valid HR, MAP, RR, SpO2, or temperature. A valid vital is abnormal when HR <50 or >100, RR <8 or >24, SpO2 <92%, MAP <65 mmHg, temperature <36 °C or >=38 °C. Arterial and non-invasive MAP are retained separately; combined MAP is the minimum valid MAP within a block.

For each window separately, calculate:
- covered block count and abnormal covered-block count;
- abnormal block fraction = abnormal covered-block count / covered block count;
- six-block abnormal/covered vectors;
- total valid/invalid rows and per-domain observation/abnormal indicators;
- the last-value snapshot, defined as the median of valid observations at the latest `charttime), with same-time ties resolved by median and missing domains retained with observed indicators;
- oxygen maxima by item, maximum flow over the three flow IDs, normalized maximum FiO2, and observed indicators.

Define `recent_burden = abnormal_block_fraction_R), `prior_burden = abnormal_block_fraction_P), and `trajectory_delta = recent_burden - prior_burden). The primary trajectory exposure is `trajectory_delta), not a post hoc threshold. A positive value means worsening observed abnormal-block burden. The primary trajectory analysis is limited to the paired gate; the final-day parent contrast is also reported on the all-eligible and paired sets.

Device and code-status text contribute only observed indicators and restricted raw-value frequency audits. They must never be auto-interpreted as a support mode, delivered FiO2, treatment limitation, or goals-of-care decision.

## Outcomes and estimands

For each index define `tau = min(t0 + 7 days, admissions.dischtime)); events at `tau) are included. A qualifying ICU return is a later ICU stay with the same `subject_id, hadm_id), a different `stay_id), and `intime >= t0). Select the earliest by `intime), then numeric `stay_id). Any later stay with `intime < t0) is an overlap/chronology anomaly, not a return.

- Cause 1: first qualifying ICU return at or before `tau), strictly earlier than qualifying death.
- Cause 2: `admissions.deathtime > t0) and `deathtime <= tau), strictly earlier than first qualifying ICU return.
- Exact return/death ties are indeterminate and excluded from the primary competing-risk fit; assign to each cause in prespecified sensitivity analyses.
- No cause by `tau) is censored at `tau); death after return is not cause 2.
- The primary composite is cause 1 or cause 2.
- Descriptive sensitivities use 48-hour, 12-hour, and full-hospitalization horizons.

The primary estimand is the paired locked-test difference in competing-risk Brier score and calibration slope/intercept for `B_traj) versus `B_phys), with coefficient associations reported per development-cohort SD of `trajectory_delta). `B_phys) versus `B_density) is the secondary parent-preserving estimand. Report Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence for each cause and the composite; do not call an absolute-risk estimate a bedside threshold.

## Baselines and analysis

Use identical paired records, labels, preprocessing, subject partitions and locked test set for all primary trajectory models:

- `B0): gender, anchor_age, admission_type, admission_location, insurance, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU LOS, hours from `admittime) to `t0), and number of earlier ICU stays ending by `t0), with missing indicators. `anchor_year_group) is a descriptive stratum and optional common categorical covariate, never a chronological split.
- `B_last): `B0) plus last valid HR, separate/combined MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, oxygen flow and FiO2, their observed indicators and missing indicators.
- `B_density): `B_last) plus six-block coverage count, total valid vital rows, per-domain counts/observed indicators, oxygen observation indicators and fixed `log1p) counts.
- `B_phys): `B_density) plus recent abnormal-block count/fraction and per-domain recent abnormal indicators.
- `B_traj): `B_phys) plus prior-window burden, prior-window coverage/valid-count features, and `trajectory_delta). The primary incremental term is trajectory change; the model must report its coefficient and the paired locked-test difference versus `B_phys).

Fit two ridge-penalized cause-specific Cox models (return; death-before-return). Penalties are selected only by inner development cross-validation. Derive competing-cause cumulative incidence from fitted cause-specific hazards; do not treat other causes as ordinary censoring for Aalen–Johansen reporting. Use subject-level SHA-256 partitions (60% development, 20% validation, 20% locked test), keeping every admission/stay of a subject together. Development alone determines imputation, one-hot levels, scaling, penalty and any coverage model. Prohibit outcome-derived cut points, feature search after split, post-`t0) fields and future charting.

Use 1,000 fixed-seed subject-bootstrap resamples of the locked test set for paired 95% intervals for Brier, calibration and absolute-risk differences, and a subject-clustered bootstrap/robust estimator for coefficients. Require both:
- overall gate: at least 1,000 primary indexes, 100 composite events, and at least 60% of all eligible indexes with >=4/6 recent coverage;
- paired trajectory gate: at least 800 paired indexes, 80 composite events, and at least 50% of all eligible indexes meeting both-window coverage.
Also require locked-test >=20 composite events and >=10 of each cause when cause-specific inference is reported. Failed gates mean sparse/infeasible; never relax thresholds or substitute an easier endpoint. If a cause gate fails, report descriptive Aalen–Johansen estimates and uncertainty only for that cause.

## Falsification, integrity and ascertainment checks

The reference solver must emit an aggregate manifest and machine-readable conclusion ledger linking every conclusion to output names/rows. The verifier evaluates computation and whether conclusions follow from outputs, not whether the hypothesis is confirmed.

Fail closed on: any feature row with `charttime >= t0); a row from another stay; `storetime)-based eligibility; duplicate primary index; impossible chronology; prohibited predictor; subject leakage; development preprocessing fit on validation/test; inconsistent event/tie assignment; or raw clinical rows/notes in derived outputs.

Required prespecified checks:

1. Refit the complete pipeline with the recent and prior windows shifted earlier by 24 hours, using `[t0-72h,t0-48h)) as the earlier window and `[t0-48h,t0-24h)) as its recent comparator, with indexes/outcomes fixed. A similar effect in this earlier trajectory weakens a discharge-proximal interpretation.
2. Compare `B_phys) and `B_traj) within fixed strata of recent coverage and total valid-row density, and report paired calibration/Brier differences. If the trajectory gain disappears after density control, interpret it as ascertainment/general acuity rather than independent physiology.
3. Fit the density-only and final-day parent contrasts on the same paired records. A trajectory claim cannot be supported by a final-day burden gain alone.
4. Permute outcome labels within locked subject partitions while preserving all features and coverage. Performance must return to chance within bootstrap uncertainty; failure invalidates computation, not the clinical hypothesis.
5. Repeat with arterial versus non-invasive MAP separately, abnormal observation fraction, a two-hour return-gap restriction, tie assignments, 48-hour/12-hour horizons, and all-eligible-stay sensitivity with subject clustering. Large divergence is definition/selection dependence.
6. Audit burden, delta, coverage, invalid-value rates, unmatched joins, and raw code-status/device value frequencies by outcome, care unit, anchor-year group, and partition. No lexical classification of treatment limitation is allowed.

Supportive results require: (a) a prespecified positive/monotonic trajectory association; (b) `B_traj) improves locked-test calibration and/or Brier over `B_phys) with a paired interval excluding trivial/no improvement; (c) absolute-risk separation is not merely a tiny AUROC change; (d) direction persists without material erasure after earlier-window, density-stratum, MAP-source, tie, return-gap and horizon checks; and (e) label permutation returns to chance. The secondary oxygen contrast is supportive only if it improves over `B_phys) under the same locked evaluation, and still represents charted rather than delivered oxygen.

Adverse results are a null/reversed trajectory association, no incremental calibration/Brier benefit, or disappearance after density/earlier-window/competing-risk/source/selection checks. They would argue that worsening charted instability adds no reliable information beyond current burden and observation intensity, or that it is mainly a general-acuity/documentation proxy. They do not establish that ICU transfer is safe.

Inconclusive results include failed overall or paired gates, sparse/unstable estimates, contradictory sensitivity results, inability to separate trajectory from density, or failed computation. A failed permutation check is computational invalidity. No result supports a discharge or surveillance policy without external validation and prospective evaluation.

## Evidence boundaries and required next study

Computationally checkable claims are source/member/schema availability, exact counts, fixed-item validity, cohort/index construction, timestamp ordering, exposure summaries, outcome labels, model estimates, uncertainty, calibration, density dependence and falsification outputs.

This archive cannot adjudicate true delivered FiO2, work of breathing, bedside instability, functional/cognitive readiness, clinician discharge intent, staffing or ward capacity; treatment limitation or goals of care from code-status text; the adjudicated reason for ICU return; or whether delaying discharge/changing monitoring changes outcomes. It cannot establish causal treatment effects, safe thresholds, external validity, transportability or clinical utility. Those claims require clinician review of raw records and bedside context, richer prospective physiology/treatment data, external validation in another ICU system, and a prospective surveillance or target-trial study.
