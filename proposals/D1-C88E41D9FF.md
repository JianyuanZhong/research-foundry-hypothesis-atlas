# Early (0–24 h) severe hypoglycemia after insulin in the ICU: independent prognostic marker for in-hospital mortality, and is the association modified by renal function and insulin intensity?

**Dataset:** eICU-CRD 2.0 (local snapshot 896e1fb7) · **Parent seed:** `[prior hypothesis]` ([starting question])
**Status:** complete, bounded, falsifiable study proposal with verified data bindings (windowed counts from job `[research job]`, script `work/ep8-probe5.py`, output `work/ep8-probe5.json`; full-stay boundary probes `job-1f96502b` / `job-287cb3a2`, outputs `work/ep8-probe1.json` / `work/ep8-probe4.json`).

## 1. Unresolved question and why it matters clinically

**Question.** Among ICU patients receiving insulin, does a low measured glucose (≤ 40 mg/dL) in the first 24 h of the ICU stay add independent prognostic information for in-hospital mortality beyond APACHE II / age / sex / hospital, and is the association modified by renal function (creatinine) and by insulin intensity — thereby separating a true toxicity signal (R1) from monitoring-intensity confounding (R2) and hospital-practice artifact (R3)?

**Why it matters.** Insulin-associated hypoglycemia is a common, preventable adverse event in the ICU. The NICE-SUGAR trial [K1] (6,104 ICU patients) found that intensive glucose control (target 81–108 mg/dL) *increased* 90-day mortality (OR 1.14, 95% CI 1.02–1.28) relative to a target ≤ 180 mg/dL, with severe hypoglycemia (≤ 40 mg/dL) occurring in 6.8% of the intensive group vs 0.5% of the conventional group. Current guidelines therefore recommend avoiding hypoglycemia, but the *prognostic* significance of an observed early hypoglycemic episode in the broader (non-trial, mixed-practice) ICU population — and whether it signals harm or merely illness severity / monitoring intensity — is not settled.

**Evidence already supporting part of the claim.** Hermanides et al. [K2] (single 18-bed Dutch ICU, 5,961 patients, computerized insulin algorithm) found that first hypoglycemic episodes (≤ 45 mg/dL) were associated with ICU mortality with an adjusted incidence rate ratio of 2.1 (95% CI 1.6–2.8) after correction for daily SOFA, age, sex, sepsis, diabetes and cardiothoracic surgery — "indicating the possibility of a causal relationship." Vriesendorp et al. [K3] (single academic center, 2,272 patients) identified predisposing factors for ICU hypoglycemia: insulin use (OR 5.3), decreased nutrition without insulin adjustment (OR 6.6), diabetes (OR 2.6), sepsis (OR 2.2), inotropic support (OR 1.8); notably, *diminished glomerular filtration rate was NOT associated* with hypoglycemia in that cohort, and APACHE II adjustment did not materially change the ORs.

**The unresolved claim.** Both prior studies are single-center, use a different hypoglycemia threshold (≤ 45), a different outcome (ICU mortality), and — critically — do not test whether the association is modified by renal function or insulin intensity, and do not test cross-hospital stability. A single-center adjusted association cannot distinguish (R1) true toxicity from (R2) monitoring-intensity confounding (sicker patients are checked more often, so lows are *detected* more often) from (R3) hospital-practice artifact (between-hospital variation in checking and insulin practice). This study addresses exactly that separation in a multi-hospital cohort (125+ hospitals in eICU) with pre-specified interactions and leave-one-hospital-out stability checks.

**Why the 0–24 h window.** (i) It matches the seed's "early after insulin" framing and the first 24 h in which insulin orders, renal function and nutrition status are most actionable; (ii) it bounds the exposure to a period before long-stay confounding (acquired infections, withdrawal-of-care decisions) dominates; (iii) it is the window in which eICU glucose measurement density is highest (probe5: 409,061 glucose rows in 0–24 h among insulin stays vs 2.14 M over the full stay).

**Why in-hospital mortality.** It is the fully recorded, unambiguous outcome in eICU (`hospitaldischargestatus`); ICU mortality is not separately recorded in this snapshot.

## 2. Rival explanations and the observable comparison that separates them

| # | Explanation | Prediction on the pre-specified comparisons |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **True toxicity** (hypoglycemia causes harm: neuroglycopenia, arrhythmia, stress response) | Adjusted association with mortality persists after severity adjustment; **stronger with worse renal function** (insulin clearance ↓ → deeper/prolonged lows) and/or **stronger with higher insulin intensity** (interaction OR > 1, significant). |
| R2 | **Monitoring-intensity / severity confounding** (sickest patients get more frequent glucose checks, so lows are detected more often; low glucose is a marker of shock/deterioration, not a cause) | Association **attenuates substantially after APACHE II + severity adjustment**; interactions with renal function / insulin intensity ≈ 1 (no modification); the association is concentrated in the most severe patients (APACHE winsorization sensitivity shifts the estimate). |
| R3 | **Hospital-practice artifact** (between-hospital variation in glucose-checking frequency and insulin protocols) | Association **unstable across hospitals**: leave-one-hospital-out refits show sign flips or a few hospitals driving the effect; hospital-level description shows low-glucose incidence and mortality both vary systematically by hospital. |

**Observable comparison that separates them.** R1 vs R2 is separated by the **pre-specified interactions** (low-glucose × renal impairment; low-glucose × insulin intensity): a toxicity mechanism predicts modification by the physiological modifiers of insulin clearance and exposure; a pure severity marker predicts no modification. R3 vs both is separated by **leave-one-hospital-out consistency** (R3: unstable/hospital-specific; R1/R2: stable). Note the estimand: conditioning on "insulin order + glucose measured in 0–24 h" changes the population to one in which hypoglycemia is *measured*; the comparison is prognostic, not causal (see §8).

## 4. Analysis plan

**Primary estimand.** In the windowed population (n = 53,674 with outcome known ≈ 53,390; 636 exposed; 5,341 deaths), the **adjusted odds ratio for in-hospital mortality associated with any glucose ≤ 40 mg/dL in 0–24 h**, estimated in a **hospital-clustered logistic model**.

**Baseline model (M0):** age, sex, APACHE score, admission source, hospital (random intercept; cluster-robust SEs as the fast variant).

**Augmented model (M1):** M0 + low_glucose_24h (binary). Primary contrast: coefficient of low_glucose_24h in M1; model comparison M0 vs M1 (likelihood-ratio test; c-index delta with 95% CI via 200 hospital-blocked bootstrap resamples).

**Modifier models (pre-specified):** M2a = M1 + creatinine_24h (log) + low_glucose × renal_impairment (creatinine ≥ 1.5); M2b = M1 + log insulin intensity + low_glucose × insulin intensity. Each interaction tested separately and reported; the two are the comparison that separates R1 (interaction OR > 1) from R2 (interaction ≈ 1).

**Cross-hospital consistency:** leave-one-hospital-out refits of M1 for each hospital with ≥ 20 events (report the distribution of the low-glucose coefficient, its range, and the fraction of folds with the same sign; hospitals with < 20 events reported descriptively). Hospital-level description: per-hospital low-glucose incidence and mortality (R3 check).

**Secondary analyses:** (i) ≤ 54 mg/dL exposure; (ii) recurrent-lows exposure; (iii) APACHE winsorized/excluded sensitivity; (iv) exposure restricted to 0–6 h vs 6–24 h; (v) gradient-boosted trees sensitivity (below).

**Multiple comparisons:** the interactions, sensitivity exposures and secondary analyses are confirmatory-of-the-primary or descriptive; the primary claim requires the low-glucose coefficient in M1.

## 5. Pre-specified supportive / adverse / inconclusive criteria

Tied to measured events (windowed: 636 exposed, 149 exposed deaths, 5,341 total deaths; full-stay boundary: 2,090 exposed, 448 deaths):

- **Supportive of independent prognostic value (R1/R2):** adjusted OR for low glucose in M1 **≥ 1.5 with 95% CI excluding 1.0**, **and** M1 c-index exceeds M0 by **≥ 0.005**, **and** ≥ 80% of leave-one-hospital-out folds have the same sign. If additionally the renal or insulin-intensity interaction OR > 1 with CI excluding 1 → supportive of the **toxicity-predominant (R1)** reading; if interactions ≈ 1 (CI crossing 1) with a persistent main effect → supportive of the **severity-marker (R2)** reading.
- **Adverse (against independent value / for R3):** adjusted OR in M1 **≤ 1.2 with 95% CI crossing 1.0**, or M1 c-index gain **< 0.005**, or ≥ 20% of leave-one-hospital-out folds flip sign → the association is **not independently prognostic** in this population (practice-artifact or null reading).
- **Inconclusive:** OR between 1.2 and 1.5 with CI crossing 1, or c-index gain 0.002–0.005, or interaction CI spanning 0.5–2 → underpowered or too heterogeneous to decide; report as such and specify what additional data (e.g., MIMIC-IV external validation, longer follow-up, CGM data) would be needed.

A null main effect with a *significant* interaction is a valid, reportable result (heterogeneity without a main effect) and is not treated as failure.

## 6. Method comparison: interpretable baseline vs learned alternative

**Baseline (primary):** hospital-clustered logistic regression (GLMM / cluster-robust GLM) with the exposure term and pre-specified interactions. Chosen because the scientific question is *prognostic association and effect modification*, where a coefficient and its interaction are directly interpretable, pre-specifiable, and auditable; with ~5,341 events and ~10 covariates the events-per-variable ratio is adequate.

**Learned alternative (secondary, pre-specified):** gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM, CPU) on the same features plus low_glucose_24h, with hospital as a categorical feature and 5-fold hospital-blocked cross-validation for the c-index; then partial-dependence and SHAP interaction values for low-glucose × creatinine and low-glucose × insulin intensity.

**What the alternative could reveal that the baseline loses:** (i) **nonlinearity** of the glucose–mortality dose-response (e.g., a threshold at 40 vs 54 mg/dL, or a U-shape) that the binary indicator cannot express; (ii) **higher-order or asymmetric effect modification** (low-glucose × renal × insulin intensity) that a two-way interaction model cannot capture; (iii) a direct audit of whether tree-learned "hypoglycemia importance" is separable from APACHE-driven severity confounding (SHAP comparison) — a concrete check on R2.

**Why the primary remains the interpretable model:** the falsification criteria (§5) are stated in terms of an adjusted OR and interaction OR; a black-box c-index gain cannot distinguish R1/R2/R3. The tree model is a sensitivity analysis for nonlinearity and heterogeneity, not the primary claim.

**Deferred alternatives and revisit criteria:**
- *Survival analysis with time-to-death* (`hospitaldischargeoffset`): deferred — the outcome is in-hospital mortality (binary) per the seed's framing; revisit if a substantial fraction of deaths occur after 24 h and a time-varying-exposure formulation becomes the question.
- *Nutrition-interruption analysis* (the seed's original hypothesis: insulin intensity × worsening kidney function × interrupted nutrition → hypoglycemia): deferred as a primary axis because the seed itself cautions that **missing nutrition records must not be treated as cessation**. Probe5 shows feeding-like `intakeOutput` labels exist (e.g., "Enteral Formula Volume/Bolus Amt (mL)", "TPN", "Enteral Tube Intake: …") in 53,412 insulin stays, but label coverage per stay in the 0–24 h window is unverified and sparse by design (feeding is charted episodically). Revisit in a child candidate as a secondary exposure if a timestamp audit shows adequate 0–24 h feeding coverage for a meaningful fraction of insulin stays.
- *Marginal structural models / inverse-probability weighting* for a causal reading: deferred — eICU charting cannot credibly model the insulin-titration decision process (no paired glucose→adjustment decision data); revisit with a dataset containing that structure or an RCT.
- *GPU / sequence models*: not scientifically useful — the exposure is a single binary per stay plus a few scalar covariates; no sequence structure justifies the cost. CPU is the correct device for this workload.

## 7. Limitations

1. **Observational design:** the estimand is prognostic association in a selected (insulin-treated, glucose-monitored) population; hypoglycemia is a marker of treatment and illness, and the design cannot establish causation.
2. **Detection proxy (R2):** a recorded low glucose requires a glucose measurement; measurement frequency is confounded by severity and practice. The 0–24 h window and the monitoring-intensity rival are acknowledged; the design mitigates (severity adjustment, interactions, leave-one-hospital-out) but does not eliminate this.
3. **Bedside vs lab glucose:** both 'bedside glucose' (310,767 in-window rows) and 'glucose' (98,294) are included; both are mg/dL (verified). Bedside (POC) values may differ systematically from central-lab values; a sensitivity excluding 'bedside glucose' is available if the unit check passes.
4. **Insulin dosage units are free-text and mixed:** 57.7% of in-window insulin rows carry a numeric `dosage`; the insulin-intensity covariate is therefore partially imputed-as-zero. If the solver's first pass finds > 50% of in-window rows without numeric dosage (as measured), the primary insulin-intensity analysis is downgraded to a binary "any insulin in window" indicator and the interaction is reported as exploratory — stated here in advance.
5. **APACHE field range:** observed 0–211 in prior episodes; used as a covariate with winsorization sensitivity, not as a validated severity index.
6. **Nutrition records incomplete** (seed caution): missing feeding records are not treated as cessation; the nutrition axis is deferred (see §6) pending a timestamp audit.
7. **Single snapshot, 2014–2015, US:** insulin and glucose-monitoring practices may have shifted since; no external validation (MIMIC-IV is a natural candidate for a separate study).
8. **Crude association is large (OR ≈ 2.8 windowed; 2.2 full-stay):** the central scientific risk is that most of it is R2/R3 confounding; the pre-specified criteria are set to detect that attenuation rather than to confirm the crude signal.

## 8. What would require additional data or another study

- **Causal claims** ("preventing early hypoglycemia improves survival") require an RCT or a design with credible insulin-titration assignment modeling — not supported by this dataset.
- **External validation** of the low-glucose–mortality association (MIMIC-IV has POC glucose and insulin data) requires a separate study.
- **Mechanistic claims** (neuroglycopenic injury, arrhythmia) require biomarkers or adjudicated event data (e.g., hypoglycemia-related code events in notes); this study can only localize the association by renal and insulin-intensity subgroups.
- **Clinical adjudication** of whether a given hospital's practice is "low-checking" or "aggressive insulin" (R3) cannot be established from charting alone; hospital-level descriptive reporting is the limit of what this design can show.
