# Early Post-TACE Hepatic Deterioration as a Prognostic Marker in HCC

## Question
In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who undergo transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does a composite measure of early hepatic deterioration during the first 7 days after the procedure predict subsequent hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days, after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and observation intensity?

## Clinical importance
TACE is a common locoregional treatment for HCC, but a subset of patients experience early hepatic deterioration that may signal a need for escalation to systemic therapy or closer surveillance. Identifying these patients could inform treatment-selection decisions and resource allocation.

## Evidence and closest prior work
- [K1] Decraecker et al. (2026) review the limitations of Child-Pugh scoring and the potential value of dynamic liver function monitoring and ALBI grade in HCC patients with moderate liver dysfunction. They do not establish a validated early post-TACE deterioration marker.
- [K2] Yang et al. (2026) develop the AAPS (AFP-ALBI-PIVKA-II-serum albumin) scoring system for HCC patients receiving PD-1 inhibitor plus TACE. Their model is retrospective, small, and focused on tumor marker response at 6-8 weeks rather than early hepatic deterioration.
- [K3] Shmatko et al. (2025) demonstrate that transformer-based models can learn longitudinal disease progression from health records, but their demonstration uses population-scale diagnoses and does not specifically address post-TACE hepatic deterioration.

## Hypothesis
A composite of bilirubin, albumin, INR, and transaminase changes in the first 7 days after TACE is associated with a higher risk of hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days, after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and observation intensity.

## Design
- **Population**: HCC patients with TACE procedures in the HCC dataset, indexed at TACE start time.
- **Exposure**: Composite of bilirubin, albumin, INR, and transaminase changes in the first 7 days post-TACE.
- **Outcomes**: Hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days.
- **Baseline**: Logistic regression with the deterioration composite and covariates.
- **Substantive alternative**: Time-aware survival model (Cox/flexible parametric) to assess temporal consistency and competing risks.
- **Falsification**: No association after adjustment, or association explained by observation intensity.

## Limitations
- Exact TACE procedure names and laboratory names need confirmation from source files.
- Outcome definitions are proxy-based (diagnoses and labs).
- Observational design cannot establish causality.
- Sample size and power need assessment.
- Single-center data may limit generalizability.
