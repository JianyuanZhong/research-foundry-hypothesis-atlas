# Episode 18 — posterior-thigh MFI, falls recall timing, and measured response selection

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank participants with visit-2 MRI and observed posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI), is higher bilateral posterior-thigh MFI associated with a classifiable visit-3 report of any fall in the preceding year, after the same measured baseline adjustment as the valid parent? Does that respondent-conditional association remain materially similar when the visit-3 recall window is nominally separated from visit-2 MRI by a ≥730-day visit gap, rather than the primary ≥366-day gap? Separately, among participants with visit-2 MRI/MFI and complete baseline covariates, how sensitive is the observed complete-case association to measured selection into a timely return and classifiable response under a missing-at-random (MAR) response model?

Falls matter because they can cause injury, fracture, disability, and loss of independence. A stable, precise positive association would justify independent prospective study with dated/adjudicated falls; a precise adverse result would lower the priority of posterior-thigh MFI as a falls-risk phenotype. This analysis does not produce a screening tool, treatment effect, causal effect, or estimate of true fall-event risk.

## Evidence supported versus tested

Three inspected works delimit what is known. The UK Biobank analysis reports broad associations of thigh MFI with mortality and disease, but its abstract does not establish falls [K1]. The closest MRI-thigh overlap associates posterior/anterior muscle-volume ratio with falls, but measures volume distribution rather than MFI and does not resolve the recall timing here [K2]. Roe et al. summarize cross-sectional and longitudinal population evidence of age-related myosteatosis, while noting few studies in diverse populations and unresolved contributions of disease and disuse [K3]. This review bounds the age-versus-disease/disuse interpretation; it does not establish either explanation for this cohort. The closest overlap means novelty is not claimed for “thigh MRI and falls” generally. The substantive advance is a bounded MFI-specific, two-timepoint association with an explicitly tested recall-window boundary and a distinct measured-response-selection sensitivity.

**Hypothesis under test:** within the fixed ≥366-day respondent-conditional cohort, one SD higher visit-2 mean bilateral posterior-thigh MFI has a positive adjusted association with a classifiable visit-3 report of any fall; in the nested ≥730-day cohort, the association remains directionally and materially compatible after the longer separation of the nominal recall window from MRI.

**Strongest rival explanation:** MFI marks pre-existing frailty, illness, inactivity/disuse, prior falls, or body composition, while differential visit attendance, response, and recall/reporting shape the observed association. The ≥730-day restriction may weaken recall-window overlap, but it also selects a different set of returners and cannot distinguish timing effects from cohort composition, survivor/participation selection, or other differences in elapsed time. Measured-response weighting tests only measured selection under MAR among MRI/MFI-observed participants. Adjustment or weighting cannot identify a mechanism or remove unmeasured confounding, selection on MRI attendance, MNAR response, differential recall, or outcome misclassification.

## Interpretation and claims requiring future evidence

Supportive results: a positive, reasonably precise ≥366 estimate, compatible ≥730 restricted estimate, and no material well-supported measured-MAR weighting shift would support a temporally bounded, respondent-conditional association robust to these two measured sensitivities. Next step is independent replication using prospectively dated/adjudicated falls and explicit follow-up ascertainment.

Adverse results: a precise inverse/null result or an adequately supported ≥730 incompatibility with the primary estimate would lower confidence in the association or its recall-boundary robustness. It does not prove MFI is unrelated to actual falls; misclassification, power, selection and construct differences remain.

Inconclusive results: wide intervals, sparse events or failed overlap/weight gates do not refute the hypothesis. Continue only if an independent data source can improve event timing, adjudication or response support; otherwise defer rather than add exploratory subgroups.

Unavailable/essential evidence: exact participant fall dates, falls severity/context and clinical adjudication; MRI attendance/quality beyond available records; validated MFI measurement range/QC thresholds if absent; complete category codebook if not available at execution; and evidence on MNAR/nonresponse. Stronger causal or mechanism claims require a separate longitudinal design with repeated exposures, event-dated outcomes and confounding control, ideally independent replication and expert adjudication. No automated verifier can certify these clinical claims; only computation, estimands, consistency of reports with outputs, and uncertainty calculations are computationally checkable.
