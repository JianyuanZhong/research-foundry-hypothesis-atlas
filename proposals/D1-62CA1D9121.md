# Provisional HCC hypothesis: post-hepatectomy laboratory trajectory and 30-day deterioration

Status: provisional checkpoint for episode 7. This is a proposed relationship and experiment, not a completed analysis or clinical conclusion.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with a recorded liver resection in the HCC snapshot, the early post-operative trajectory of liver-related laboratory abnormalities (initially operationalized as bilirubin, INR, ALT, and albumin when available) will be associated with a clinically important deterioration signal during the subsequent 30 days, beyond baseline disease severity and operative context. The unresolved claim is whether a reproducible trajectory, rather than a single post-operative value, identifies a distinct recovery/deterioration pattern that could inform surveillance or escalation research.

## Evidence status and clinical importance

The HCC dataset README and catalog identify longitudinal procedure, laboratory, encounter, diagnosis, medication, examination, pathology, and clinical-document sources. Prior board records report a large resection population and availability of the listed laboratory fields, but the current checkpoint has not re-verified row counts, headers, units, or timestamp completeness. The proposed association, endpoint validity, causal interpretation, and clinical utility are therefore unproven.

A positive result would motivate prospective validation of an early warning pattern; an adverse result would show that the apparent signal is explained by baseline severity, timing, missingness, or observation practice; an inconclusive result would identify the measurement or adjudication evidence needed before a treatment decision is considered.

## Planned discriminating experiment

Population: adults in the HCC snapshot with a procedure-table record matching a liver-resection label, restricted to records with a usable procedure time and a defined 30-day follow-up window. Time zero is the procedure start time; exclude or sensitivity-analyze records with missing/ambiguous times.

Inputs: procedures table for surgery label/time; labs table for the specified analytes and timestamps; encounters and transfers tables for post-operative care context and deterioration proxies; diagnoses, medications, pathology, examinations, and clinical documents only as prespecified covariates or adjudication aids, never as a substitute for missing primary fields.

Baseline: a pre-specified model using baseline severity, operative context, and the first post-operative laboratory value. Alternative: a low-dimensional trajectory model (for example, slope/curvature or a regularized functional/latent trajectory summary) fitted to the same analytes over a fixed early post-operative window. Compare held-out temporal predictions and calibration, with uncertainty estimates and a negative control based on a pre-procedure or otherwise temporally invalid window.

Outcome: a prespecified 30-day deterioration signal derived from available encounter/transfer and, if feasible, clinically adjudicated documentation. The exact endpoint must be frozen after inspecting the actual fields and must distinguish death, ICU/transfer, readmission, or other deterioration only when those labels are genuinely present.

Falsification: the trajectory model is not supported if it adds no reproducible information beyond baseline plus the first value, if its signal disappears after timing/missingness controls, if it fails temporal holdout, or if the negative-control window performs comparably. A null result is not proof of no biological mechanism.

The three inspected key works and their frozen source receipts are not yet attached at this provisional checkpoint.

## Deliverable and alternatives

The future solver must newly fit the baseline and trajectory models, estimate held-out performance and uncertainty, produce the prespecified endpoint and negative-control analyses, and report whether the trajectory claim is supportive, adverse, or inconclusive. A simple baseline is scientifically necessary; the trajectory alternative is retained because it can reveal ordered recovery information lost by a single value. A mechanistic causal model is deferred because treatment assignment and confounding are not adequately identified in these sources.
