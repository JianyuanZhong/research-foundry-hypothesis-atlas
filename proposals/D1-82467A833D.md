# Discordant recovery after AKI: does creatinine recovery overstate renal recovery?

## Status

Working proposal for MIMIC-IV v3.1 (episode 1). This is a scientific design, not a completed analysis. No patient-level results have been estimated yet.

## Clinical question

Among ICU patients with AKI, does apparent recovery defined by creatinine improvement identify patients whose urine output remains abnormal, and are those patients at higher risk of subsequent renal escalation, ICU death, or in-hospital death?

The clinically consequential unresolved claim is narrower than "creatinine is invalid." The claim is that a creatinine-based recovery definition can misclassify a subgroup whose urine output remains abnormal, and that this subgroup has worse subsequent outcomes than either patients with concordant recovery or patients who have not recovered.

## Why this matters

AKI recovery is often inferred from creatinine because it is repeatedly measured and familiar. However, creatinine generation and clearance can be affected by muscle mass, nutrition, fluid balance, and sampling practices. Urine output is a direct but noisy renal output measure. If creatinine recovery is discordant from urine output, clinicians may make premature decisions about diuresis, renal replacement therapy, escalation, or discharge.

The result would change the next decision by determining whether a simple creatinine-only recovery rule is adequate for risk stratification or whether a multi-measure recovery phenotype is needed. A null result would support the adequacy of creatinine for this particular prognostic question, although it would not establish equivalence to measured GFR.

## Competing explanations

1. **True discordant recovery:** creatinine has returned near baseline while urine output remains abnormal because renal recovery is incomplete or heterogeneous.
2. **Measurement artifact:** creatinine sampling is sparse, affected by dilution/concentration, or influenced by changes in muscle mass and nutrition. Urine output may be under-recorded or affected by catheterization and fluid administration.
3. **Treatment artifact:** fluid administration, diuretics, vasopressors, or renal replacement therapy can change creatinine and urine output without a simple common interpretation.
4. **Severity/selection artifact:** sicker patients receive more frequent labs and monitoring, have more fluid shifts, and are more likely to die or require escalation.

The experiment must distinguish the first explanation from the others as much as the data permit. It cannot establish measured GFR or a causal mechanism.

## Population and index time

Use MIMIC-IV v3.1. The planned population is adult ICU patients with an AKI episode during an ICU stay.

- AKI episode: use KDIGO-style creatinine and urine-output criteria, with a prespecified minimum observation window and a baseline creatinine. If baseline creatinine is unavailable, use a documented prior in-hospital value or a prespecified conservative fallback; report the fallback separately.
- Index time: first time at which creatinine-based recovery is met after AKI onset, or a prespecified fixed post-onset time if recovery is not met. The exact index rule must be fixed before outcome analysis.
- Require sufficient urine-output observations around the index time; define a minimum number of measurements and a maximum gap.
- Exclude patients who die before the index time and patients with an outcome window shorter than the prespecified follow-up unless handled by a prespecified censoring rule.

The first analysis should be descriptive and phenotype-validation: define concordant recovery, discordant recovery, and non-recovery. The prognostic analysis should begin at the index time and use only information available by that time.

## Phenotype definitions

Define the following groups at the index time:

1. **Concordant recovery:** creatinine recovery plus urine output meeting prespecified recovery thresholds.
2. **Discordant recovery:** creatinine recovery but urine output not meeting recovery thresholds.
3. **Non-recovery:** creatinine not recovered.

The exact thresholds must be prespecified and justified. A reasonable starting point is creatinine within a prespecified ratio of baseline and urine output above a prespecified rate over a prespecified window. These thresholds are not yet validated and must be checked against data coverage.

## Analysis

### Baseline analysis

Use a prespecified multivariable model for the primary outcome (e.g., 7-day ICU death or renal escalation) with the following covariates measured before or at the index time:

- Age, sex, race/ethnicity, insurance
- ICU length of stay to index time
- AKI stage
- Creatinine at index time and change from baseline
- Urine output over the prespecified window
- Vasopressor use (from `inputevents` or `chartevents`)
- Mechanical ventilation (from `procedureevents`)
- Sepsis or infection (from `diagnoses_icd` or `microbiologyevents`)

The baseline model is a logistic regression or Cox model, depending on the outcome. The primary comparison is discordant recovery versus concordant recovery, with non-recovery as a reference or secondary comparison.

### Learned alternative

A prespecified gradient-boosted model (e.g., LightGBM) on the same covariates and the same temporal split. The learned model is not a substitute for the baseline; it is a check on whether the discordant phenotype adds information beyond what a flexible model can capture from the same inputs. If the learned model does not improve over the baseline, the discordant phenotype may not be adding information. If it does, the discordant phenotype may be a useful summary of a complex pattern.

The learned model is trained on a prespecified training set and evaluated on a held-out test set. The split is by patient, not by row, to avoid leakage. The primary evaluation metric is the area under the ROC curve and the area under the precision-recall curve, with calibration reported.

### Sensitivity analyses

- Vary the index time and the recovery thresholds.
- Exclude patients with missing urine output.
- Restrict to patients with a prespecified minimum number of measurements.
- Report results by AKI stage and by presence/absence of renal replacement therapy.
- Run a secondary analysis with fluid balance included, if coverage permits.

## Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive:** discordant recovery is associated with higher 7-day ICU death or renal escalation than concordant recovery, after adjustment for the prespecified covariates, and the association is robust to the sensitivity analyses.
- **Adverse:** discordant recovery is not associated with worse outcomes, or the association is explained by the covariates, or the association is driven by a small number of patients with missing data.
- **Inconclusive:** the association is present but the confidence interval is wide, or the association is present in one sensitivity analysis but not another, or the data coverage is too low to support a robust conclusion.

The experiment cannot establish that creatinine recovery is "wrong" or that urine output is "right." It can only show that a creatinine-based recovery definition misclassifies a subgroup with worse outcomes, after adjustment for the available covariates.

## Limitations

- No measured GFR is available in MIMIC-IV. The analysis can only identify suspected discordant recovery.
- Urine output is subject to measurement and recording artifacts.
- The analysis is observational and cannot establish causality.
- The results are limited to the MIMIC-IV population and may not generalize to other settings.

## Method comparison and selection

The baseline is a prespecified multivariable logistic regression or Cox model. The learned alternative is a gradient-boosted model on the same inputs. The learned model can reveal whether the discordant phenotype adds information beyond what a flexible model can capture. If the learned model does not improve over the baseline, the discordant phenotype may not be adding information. If it does, the discordant phenotype may be a useful summary of a complex pattern.

The baseline is selected as the primary analysis because it is interpretable and directly tests the discordant phenotype. The learned model is a secondary check. A neural network or GPU-based model is not required for this question; the data are tabular and the primary analysis is a prespecified comparison.
