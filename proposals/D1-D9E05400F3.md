# All-stay event calibration of the airway-independent >=2 ventilator-setting-family signal against explicit RT Vent Start/Off transitions

## Substantive advance and decision

This child of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]` resolves a different and more direct semantic question than either parent. The parents showed that the airway-independent signal is highly prevalent in their frozen care-plan-related landmark cohort, is dominated by structured setting families and common pairs, and is entangled with site-specific respiratory workflow. Those adverse/inconclusive findings remain valid. The unresolved question was whether the same signal nevertheless changes in the expected direction around an independently recorded structured ventilation transition.

A full-source, care-plan-independent, self-controlled event study now provides strong calibration evidence for that narrower claim. In stays with complete 12-hour pre/post observation, >=2 setting-family documentation rose from 7.9% in the six hours before an RT Vent `Start` to 89.4% in the six hours after, and fell from 52.2% before RT Vent `Off`/`Suspended` to 16.8% after. Stay- and hospital-clustered intervals exclude zero in the expected directions; pseudo-times changed by only -2.4 percentage points; and a 30-minute event washout retained large directional changes. The appropriate conclusion is supportive for **transition sensitivity of a structured ventilation-documentation marker**, not for true extubation, invasive modality, continuous active ventilation, or clinical liberation.

This result complements rather than reverses the parents. A marker may be transition-sensitive and still fail specificity outside explicit-transition sites or episodes. Hospital coverage was only 11-12 hospitals, the prior work found substantial cross-hospital workflow heterogeneity, and the RT transition itself is a structured chart field rather than an adjudicated bedside gold standard. The signal can therefore be used as a calibrated documentation-state measure in explicitly supported settings, but must not be silently upgraded to clinical truth.

## Evidence already supported versus hypothesis tested

### Strongest claim supported before this experiment

In the exact inherited 3,788 landmark rows, two or more numerically plausible ventilator-setting families were present in approximately 92-96% across airway-lookback classes. Immediate identical bundles, a single family, and generic non-respiratory chart volume did not explain most positivity; however common setting pairs and non-target respiratory documentation strongly tracked the signal, transport to unseen hospitals was poor, and stronger value-specific transition, note, or treatment evidence was sparse. Thus the parents supported a recurrent, site-dependent structured respiratory-documentation pattern, not active invasive ventilation.

### Falsifiable hypothesis tested here

Independently of care-plan targets and frozen landmark selection, the airway-independent >=2 setting-family signal is a directional structured state marker around explicit RT Vent transitions: it should increase after RT Vent `Start`, decrease after RT Vent `Off`/`Suspended`, show little discontinuity at event-free pseudo-times, preserve direction after excluding setting records within 30 minutes of the transition, and show the expected direction across contributing hospitals.

### Strongest claim supported after execution

The setting-family signal is strongly transition-sensitive in the eICU hospitals and fully observed episodes that actually record explicit RT Vent Start/Off transitions. It can distinguish before-versus-after structured documentation regimes around these charted transitions. This is calibration to another structured chart field, not validation against clinical ventilation truth.

## Exact sources, schema, records, joins, and temporal fields

The analysis uses the eICU 2.0 snapshot `[source checksum]`, source-catalog [source checksum]. Both sources are ordinary gzip-compressed CSV files, not archive members.

1. Table `respiratoryCharting`, exact source `[internal dataset path]`, [source checksum].
   - Required columns: row key `respchartid`; stay key `patientunitstayid`; event time `respchartoffset`; provenance-only entry time `respchartentryoffset`; `respcharttypecat`; `respchartvaluelabel`; `respchartvalue`.

Positivity is at least two distinct families anywhere in a bin. Exact repeated `(patientunitstayid, respchartoffset, family)` observations are collapsed for family/time counts.

## Population, deduplication, observability, and fixed bins

The source population is every eICU unit stay represented in `patient`; every respiratoryCharting row was screened. The bounded analysis population consists of explicit RT transition clusters satisfying `transition offset >= 720` minutes and `transition offset + 720 <= unitdischargeoffset`, ensuring four complete six-hour bins within the ICU stay. Negative-offset pre-admission time is excluded.

The 4,120 raw Start/Off/Suspended records contained no exact duplicate event tuples. Within each stay, sorted transition records are clustered beginning at the earliest record and including records through +30 minutes. Same-direction records collapse to one event; clusters containing both On and Off directions are conflicts and are excluded from the primary directional analysis but counted. This produced 4,094 clusters across the full source; 1,386 had complete 12-hour pre/post observation, including two conflicting clusters. The primary cohort is 1,384 non-conflicting events in 798 stays: 517 On events in 425 stays across 11 hospitals and 867 Off events in 571 stays across 12 hospitals.

Four prespecified half-open bins are independently rebuilt with no carry-forward:

- far pre: `[T-720,T-360)`
- immediate pre: `[T-360,T)`
- immediate post: `[T,T+360)`
- far post: `[T+360,T+720)`

Component outputs are positive indicator, number of distinct families, family-time records, distinct chart times, and named components. A 30-minute washout removes setting records in `[T-30,T+30]` from both adjacent bins to test whether co-charting at the exact transition drives the result.

A deterministic pseudo-time control uses one uniformly sampled complete-window anchor per event-bearing stay, at least 720 minutes from every eligible transition cluster. Seed 22022 yielded 785 pseudo-times across 12 hospitals. Pseudo-times are a within-source secular/documentation control, not random treatment assignment.

## Analysis and executed results

The primary estimand is the within-event change in >=2-family positivity from immediate pre to immediate post, separately for On and Off. Secondary estimands compare immediate pre with far pre and far post with immediate pre. Descriptive component, record, and timestamp means show whether binary changes represent broader activity changes. Percentile intervals use 3,000 bootstrap replicates, separately clustering complete event rows by stay and by hospital. Equal-hospital means/ranges include hospitals with at least five events of that direction.

### RT Vent Start as positive direction control

Among 517 On events, >=2-family positivity was 9.9%, 7.9%, 89.4%, and 70.2% across the four bins. The immediate post-minus-pre increase was +81.43 percentage points; stay-clustered 95% interval +77.33 to +85.49 and hospital-clustered interval +78.92 to +92.42. Mean family count rose from 0.29 to 2.72, target-family records from 0.37 to 6.71, and distinct target chart times from 0.19 to 2.53. The far-post rate remained 62.28 points above immediate pre (stay interval +57.39 to +67.08; hospital interval +59.51 to +81.82).

There was no material pre-event discontinuity for On: immediate pre minus far pre was -1.93 points, with both stay and hospital intervals crossing zero. After removing all target-setting records within 30 minutes of the transition, pre positivity was 6.4% and post positivity 76.2%, a +69.83-point change. Thus exact-time co-charting contributes but does not explain the direction.

### RT Vent Off/Suspended as negative direction control

Among 867 Off events, positivity was 67.5%, 52.2%, 16.8%, and 20.8%. Immediate post-minus-pre was -35.41 points; stay-clustered 95% interval -40.60 to -30.87 and hospital-clustered interval -51.96 to -31.68. Mean family count fell from 1.67 to 0.52, records from 2.62 to 1.06, and target chart times from 1.14 to 0.41. Far post remained -31.49 points below immediate pre (stay interval -37.30 to -26.18; hospital interval -49.22 to -27.47).

A pre-trend/anticipation signal is present for Off: immediate pre was already 15.22 points below far pre (stay interval -18.51 to -12.10; hospital interval -17.67 to -6.82). This limits an abrupt-interruption interpretation and is compatible with setting de-escalation, documentation tapering, or event timestamp lag before the structured Off record. The 30-minute washout left the post-minus-pre change essentially unchanged at -35.99 points.

### Pseudo-times and hospital heterogeneity

At 785 event-free pseudo-times, positivity was 36.6%, 34.4%, 32.0%, and 31.6%; immediate post-minus-pre was only -2.42 points (stay interval -4.08 to -0.76; hospital interval -3.72 to -0.63). A small secular decline exists, but is far smaller than either transition contrast.

For hospitals with at least five directional events, all ten On hospitals had positive post-minus-pre changes (range +66.67 to +100 points; equal-hospital mean +89.09), and all ten Off hospitals had negative changes (range -72.73 to -11.11; equal-hospital mean -45.42). Magnitudes vary and hospital coverage is sparse, but direction is consistent in this supported subset.

## Prespecified support, adverse, and inconclusive gates

Support for transition sensitivity requires: On post-minus-pre >= +20 points and Off <= -20 points; stay-clustered and hospital-clustered intervals exclude zero in the expected directions; each direction is at least 15 points farther from zero than the pseudo-time change; direction survives the 30-minute washout; and no hospital with at least five events reverses direction. All gates were met.

An adverse result would be a reversed On or Off contrast, a transition contrast no larger than pseudo-times, disappearance after the event washout, or consistent hospital-direction reversal. None occurred.

An inconclusive result would be expected-direction point estimates with either clustered interval crossing zero, fewer than five usable hospitals for a direction, dominant conflicting clusters, or loss of effect after enforcing complete observation. Those gates were not met for the operational documentation hypothesis. Nevertheless, interpretation remains clinically inconclusive because the reference transition field is not independently adjudicated and the represented hospitals are a selected subset.

## Clinical meaning and claim limits

The strongest supported result is that the >=2-family structured setting signal behaves in the expected direction around independently selected structured RT transition records and far more strongly than at event-free pseudo-times. This makes it useful as a **documentation-state marker** for sensitivity analyses where hospital support and transition calibration are explicit.

The experiment does not establish that `Start` is true initiation of invasive ventilation, that `Off`/`Suspended` is extubation, that every setting belongs to an invasive ventilator, or that the signal indicates continuous support between chart times. It does not validate use in hospitals without explicit transitions, resolve NIV versus invasive modality, identify SBTs, establish durable liberation, assess care quality, estimate causality, or show patient benefit. On and Off asymmetry and the Off pre-trend also argue against reading the estimates as biological treatment effects.

True clinical validation requires blinded episode-level adjudication of airway/device presence, ventilator mode, actual device connection, RT/nursing narrative, extubation/reintubation, transfer/discharge boundaries, and preferably ventilator-device logs. Representative sampling across hospitals with and without explicit transition usage is needed to estimate sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, and inter-rater agreement. Interface audit trails are needed to separate event-entry timing from bedside timing.

Automatically checkable claims are source row/value counts, exact joins and offsets, deduplication/conflict accounting, observability, bin membership, target-family mappings/ranges, event and pseudo-time counts, component/activity summaries, bootstrap seeds/replicates/intervals, hospital direction tables, washout outputs, and the exact zero-row frozen mapping.
