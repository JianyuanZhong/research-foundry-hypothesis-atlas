# eICU laboratory-testing signals and hospital transport of ICU mortality prediction

**Status:** proposed study; no final-cohort outcome-model results have been run. The configured source data are read-only. All four configured datasets remain available; this study uses only eICU.

## Question and evidence boundary

Among adults still in ICU at hour 24, does adding first-day laboratory testing frequency/timing to measured physiology improve prediction of later in-hospital death, and is that increment smaller in hospitals withheld from model development?

Prior inspected literature documents both institution-sensitive ICU prediction/generalizability and that laboratory missingness can encode illness course or clinician concern while exposing site-level data-definition problems. These findings motivate, but do not prove, the hypothesis. The leading account is that test-process signals partly encode site/workflow-specific practices and therefore transport less well. The strongest rival is that testing intensity proxies genuine but incompletely measured severity or clinician concern, retaining predictive value at unseen hospitals. Case mix, treatment pathways, assay/name mapping, outcome coding, and selection at the 24-hour landmark are additional alternatives.

The experiment tests predictive increment and transportability, not causality. A positive result supports only a dataset- and task-specific predictive pattern; it does not show that testing causes mortality, that the signal is merely workflow artifact, or that altering testing benefits patients. Similar held-out benefit would weaken the proposed fragility pattern for these sites, not establish universal transportability. Three distinct inspected key works and their source receipts/excerpts are inherited unchanged from the valid parent; see its `key-references.json` and supporting source snapshots.

## Population, estimand and outcomes

Population: first ICU visit, adults with nonmissing/unambiguous adult age, alive and still in ICU at 24 hours, and with later in-hospital outcome ascertainable. Define “adult,” age handling (including EICU's obfuscated age values), and exclusions before fitting; report the flow and excluded/missing counts. This is conditional prediction among landmark survivors still in ICU, not all ICU admissions.

Feature window: ICU offsets 0 through 1440 minutes, inclusive only if the record is available by the landmark; enforce no leakage. Primary outcome: in-hospital death after hour 24, using `hospitaldischargestatus`/`hospitaldischargeoffset` and prespecified label rules. Audit status semantics, follow-up completeness, and consistency across sites before inference; ICU discharge is not the outcome. Patients discharged from ICU before the landmark are excluded by the estimand. Report label exclusions and sensitivity to ambiguous statuses.

Primary comparison: paired out-of-sample change in Brier score and calibration when adding lab-process features to the same physiology-only features. Compare that incremental value under patient-grouped internal validation and whole-hospital holdout validation. The hypothesis predicts an internal benefit with smaller or absent improvement in held-out hospitals. Report hospital-specific support and uncertainty; pooled site results must not obscure sparse sites. Discrimination is secondary, not a substitute for proper scoring/calibration.

## Models and method rationale

**Simple baseline:** prespecified regularized logistic regression on physiology summaries (fixed vital summaries/counts and numeric lab values only after unit/name harmonization), paired with an otherwise identical model adding lab-result process counts/timing. Training-only fitting of imputation, scaling, feature selection and calibration; identical cohort and splits.

**Substantive alternative:** bounded one-layer GRU on the same 24-hour records, in 24 hourly bins. Inputs are the same vital summaries/counts and laboratory test-category counts; masks distinguish unobserved values from normal values. Fit physiology-only and physiology-plus-test-process GRUs with identical target and cohort, binary cross-entropy, early stopping on development-only hospital-grouped validation, hidden width 32–64, at most 30 epochs and three prespecified seeds. Compare its paired process-block increment to logistic regression's on identical held-out sites. It can preserve ordering, recency, test clusters and interactions that aggregation/linear terms discard; it cannot identify why clinicians ordered tests or establish a biological/workflow mechanism. Select it only if category harmonization and held-out event support pass and temporal patterns yield stable held-out information beyond the baseline; defer if support/harmonization is inadequate, and do not rescue with post-hoc feature redefinition or subgrouping. The baseline remains the primary estimand test.

## Splits, uncertainty and decision criteria

Partition by `hospitalid` for development versus held-out evaluation; within development hospitals, patient-grouped internal split. Guard against any `uniquepid` crossing training/evaluation, including repeated stays at different hospitals. Fit every preprocessing step in training folds only. EICU has no calendar dates, so it cannot support train-on-earlier-calendar-time/test-on-later-calendar-time validation; the relevant temporal boundary is only past 0–24-hour input predicting a post-hour-24 outcome. Do not claim temporal drift validation.

Report cohort/site patient and event counts; missingness and harmonization decisions; paired Brier differences with 95% uncertainty intervals for process-feature addition in internal and held-out regimes; calibration-in-the-large and slope/intercept; secondary AUROC/PR-AUC; site-level estimates and uncertainty. Resample held-out hospitals for transport uncertainty and account for repeated patient identity as specified before fitting. Do not infer contrast from “significant in one split, not the other”; estimate the paired difference between increments directly.

Supportive: prespecified internal increment plus materially smaller/absent held-out increment with uncertainty/calibration supporting the contrast. This is consistent with, not proof of, site-sensitive workflow information. Adverse: no internal increment, or comparably useful well-calibrated held-out increment; claim against fragility only if precision excludes a meaningful decrement. Inconclusive: wide intervals, inadequate held-out hospitals/events, unstable small sites, conflicting proper-score/calibration evidence, or unresolved labels/assay mapping. A low-powered null is not falsification. No threshold should be selected after seeing results.
