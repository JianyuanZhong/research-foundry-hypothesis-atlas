# Administration-confirmed susceptibility-triggered antimicrobial de-escalation in ICU patients

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]` (lineage: `[prior hypothesis]` from expert seed `[starting question]`)
Dataset: MIMIC-IV 3.1, snapshot [source checksum].
Source archive: `[internal dataset path]` ([source checksum]); source members are read from `mimic-iv-3.1/...` and never modified.

## Unresolved question

Among adult ICU patients with a single named organism from a qualifying specimen, at least one in-vitro susceptible narrower systemic antibiotic, and broad empiric systemic therapy active at the first available susceptibility report, does administration-confirmed narrowing of broad coverage within 24 hours associate with lower 30-day all-cause mortality than continued administration-confirmed broad coverage, after adjustment for pre-result severity and organism/treatment context?

This is an associational target-trial emulation. It does not claim proof of causality, clinician awareness of the report, adequate source control, or clinical appropriateness of narrowing.

## Clinical importance and advance

Broad empiric therapy is often necessary at sepsis onset, but unnecessary continuation increases resistance, toxicity, and cost. [K1] found no higher mortality after day-4 anti-MRSA or antipseudomonal de-escalation in MIMIC-IV, but used elapsed time as the landmark and treated lower mortality estimates as exploratory. [K2] emphasizes that definitions, timing, eligibility, denominators, and safety evidence remain heterogeneous and low certainty. [K3] shows randomized biomarker-guided de-escalation can shorten therapy without a detectable mortality difference, but is not susceptibility-guided ICU care.

This study advances [K1] by making **the first available susceptibility report** the information-based index, requiring a susceptible narrower target, and classifying actual receipt from eMAR administration events rather than orders alone. It advances [K2] by prespecifying the denominator and administration semantics. It complements [K3] by testing a microbiology-informed ICU strategy with mortality as the primary outcome.

## Target-trial specification

1. **Eligibility:** Adults aged ≥18 at ICU admission; alive and hospitalized through assignment; suspected/confirmed bacterial infection with a qualifying culture; broad systemic anti-MRSA and/or antipseudomonal/resistant Gram-negative therapy active at T0; at least one in-vitro susceptible, narrower systemic target; single named organism. Exclude chronic dialysis, severe immunosuppression, and long-course/specialized infections identifiable from ICD/procedure data (endocarditis, osteomyelitis, CNS infection, necrotizing/prosthetic/burn infection, persistent bacteremia). Report every exclusion in a flow diagram.
2. **T0:** First qualifying susceptibility `storetime`; if absent, `chartdate` at documented day resolution. `storetime` is operational availability, not proof of clinician awareness.
3. **Assignment window:** T0 through T0+24h. De-escalation requires a confirmed systemic administration event showing stop, discontinued, held, or replacement of at least one broad class, without losing all active systemic coverage against the organism, and a susceptible narrower systemic agent administered or already active. Continuation requires a confirmed systemic administration of the broad class through the window. Exclude death, discharge, comfort-only status, or unclassifiable therapy before T0+24h. Non-systemic topical, ophthalmic, inhaled, lock, and desensitization products do not define exposure.
4. **Primary outcome:** All-cause death within 30 days after T0+24h using `patients.dod`; date-boundary ties are conservatively death.
5. **Secondary outcomes:** hospital death; ICU/hospital-free days through 14 and 28 days; death-adjusted antibiotic-free days; creatinine-based KDIGO stage 2–3 AKI within 7 days among patients without chronic dialysis; recurrent/persistent resistant culture within 14 days, interpreted cautiously because follow-up cultures are ordered selectively.
6. **Covariates:** Only pre-T0 variables: demographics, admission type, care unit, calendar period, comorbidities, organism, specimen source, baseline antibiotic classes, culture timing, SOFA components/support intensity, vasopressors, ventilation, renal replacement, temperature, WBC, lactate, creatinine, urine output, and recent/worst/change summaries. No post-assignment outcome or treatment data enter the propensity model.

## Analysis and method comparison

- **Primary baseline:** Overlap-weighted propensity-score target-trial comparison. Fit a logistic propensity model for administration-confirmed de-escalation versus continuation using only pre-T0 covariates, apply overlap weights, and estimate the weighted 30-day absolute risk difference and relative risk with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals clustered by `subject_id`. Report weighted standardized mean differences; require <0.10 for principal covariates or label imbalance.
- **Substantive alternative:** A prespecified dynamic-state model stratifies the same landmark population by pre-T0 organ-support trajectory (improving, stable, worsening) using only data before T0, then estimates overlap-weighted effects within strata and tests an exposure-by-state interaction. This mechanistic alternative can reveal whether the average association reflects recovery state and whether narrowing is concentrated among already-improving patients; the baseline loses that heterogeneity.
- **Measurement sensitivity:** Repeat the primary analysis with an order-only exposure definition from `prescriptions` and with 12-, 36-, and 48-hour windows. Report the proportion and clinical composition of administration-versus-order discordance. Prespecify that >20% discordance is a material measurement failure requiring the administration-confirmed result to be interpreted as the primary exposure and order-based estimates as secondary.
- **Rival explanations:** Estimate negative-control outcomes not plausibly affected by de-escalation (e.g., admission-source fractures), pre-T0-only placebo exposures where possible, and E-value sensitivity analyses for unmeasured severity, source control, and clinician concern. These diagnostics cannot prove causality.

## Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive:** A lower 30-day mortality risk with confidence intervals excluding no effect, acceptable overlap/balance, <20% order-administration discordance, and stable direction across prespecified windows would support the susceptibility-availability, administration-confirmed estimand as a robust association and reduce exposure-misclassification and immortal-time concerns. It would not establish causality or a treatment recommendation.
- **Adverse:** Higher mortality with confidence intervals excluding no effect would argue against this narrow de-escalation strategy in the eligible population and would require examination of infection source, resistant organisms, and appropriateness before any stewardship implication.
- **Inconclusive:** No clear association, inadequate overlap, sparse events, >20% discordance, or sensitivity estimates reversing direction would leave safety and effectiveness unresolved.
- **Claims requiring additional evidence:** Clinician awareness, source control, unmeasured infection severity, and clinical appropriateness require chart adjudication or a prospective trial. External transportability requires another dataset; a causal recommendation requires randomized or carefully adjudicated prospective evidence.
