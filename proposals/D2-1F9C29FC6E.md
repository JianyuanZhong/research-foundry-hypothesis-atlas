# Provisional hypothesis: macrophage–fibroblast context modifies the intervention-associated FLS transcript response

Seed: D2-T1
Status: provisional and evidence-bound; no inferential result has been computed in this episode.

## Proposed relationship

Among rheumatoid-arthritis fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) exposed to the interventions represented in the configured E-MTAB-8316 assay, the direction and magnitude of the intervention-associated transcript response will depend on macrophage/coculture context rather than being a context-independent FLS response. Operationally, this is a reproducible intervention-by-macrophage-context interaction after accounting for donor pairing and repeated context measurements.

This is an intervention-associated expression hypothesis. It is not a claim that a drug acts directly on a target, that the expression change is causal, or that clinical benefit has been observed.

## Clinical importance and advance

Target prioritization is consequential when an intervention appears effective in one inflammatory microenvironment but ineffective or potentially harmful in another. A context-independent response would support a simpler prioritization rule; a reproducible context interaction would motivate microenvironment-specific evaluation and identify which biological state should be measured before testing a candidate intervention. The advance over a descriptive drug-response table is a prespecified interaction test with donor-aware controls, rather than treating every expression change as equivalent.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The configured assay is documented as containing intervention, macrophage/condition, donor-related metadata, and expression measurements. That supports feasibility of a context-stratified expression analysis, not the interaction itself. The unresolved claim is whether the intervention-by-context interaction is reproducible after donor-aware adjustment, rather than being explained by donor composition, batch, or condition assignment. Existing evidence does not establish direct target action, mechanism, or clinical efficacy.

## Alternatives and limitations

The donor-aware additive model is the simple baseline. The interaction model is scientifically substantive because it tests whether the intervention effect changes with microenvironment, not merely whether expression predicts context. A mechanistic perturbation or target-specific validation would be needed to distinguish direct target action from indirect transcriptional response; it is unavailable from this expression dataset alone. Missing clinical outcomes, direct target measurements, independent technical replicates, and a verified assay registry prevent causal or clinical claims. The three demonstration papers are not being reproduced: their EHR/sequence or multimodal methods are not the scientific comparison here, and any adaptation would require a separately justified design.
