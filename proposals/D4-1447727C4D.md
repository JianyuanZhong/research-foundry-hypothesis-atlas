# Creatinine–cystatin C discordance and low muscle reserve as a missed-risk signal for kidney events

## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

Creatinine-based eGFR is convenient but can be biased upward when creatinine generation is low. The inspected critical-illness evidence supports that physiological concern, but it does not show whether discordance between creatinine- and cystatin-C-based estimates identifies future renal events in community-dwelling adults, or whether low muscle reserve marks the subgroup in whom creatinine-only assessment is most misleading.

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among UK Biobank participants without a recorded hospital AKI or CKD diagnosis at baseline, a lower cystatin-C-based eGFR relative to creatinine-based eGFR is associated with a larger hazard of a subsequent dated hospital AKI diagnosis among participants with lower baseline grip strength, after adjustment for creatinine-based eGFR, age, sex and BMI.

The key estimand is the interaction between standardized eGFRcys minus eGFRcr and standardized grip strength in a cause-specific hazard model for first incident AKI. CKD (N18*) is a prespecified secondary outcome, not silently substituted for AKI. A clinically recognizable secondary contrast compares participants with eGFRcr at least 60 but eGFRcys below 60 with participants whose two estimates are concordant, stratified by low versus higher grip.

The strongest available evidence supports only that creatinine and cystatin C can differ for physiologically meaningful reasons, especially with muscle loss, and that UKB contains these assays, grip measurements and dated hospital diagnosis fields. It does not support this prospective interaction, a calibrated clinical risk threshold, or a management recommendation.

## Why it matters and substantive advance

If confirmed, the result would identify a concrete group in whom creatinine-only screening may under-recognize renal vulnerability, informing whether cystatin C testing or closer renal monitoring deserves prospective evaluation. The advance is not another generic risk score: it tests a mechanism-motivated, clinically actionable heterogeneity claim and asks whether the signal persists beyond eGFRcr itself and beyond general frailty proxies.

A supportive result would still be prognostic evidence from selected UKB participants, not proof that ordering cystatin C or changing treatment improves outcomes. A null result would be valuable evidence against this proposed screening rationale.

## Analysis, baseline and alternative

The primary full-cohort model is a cause-specific Cox model for first N17* event with eGFRcr, standardized discordance, standardized grip, discordance-by-grip interaction, age, sex and BMI. Use robust standard errors, prespecified splines as a sensitivity analysis, and report interaction HR/95% CI plus marginal 5-year risks and risk differences. Report a Fine–Gray sensitivity as a different estimand with death as the competing event, not as confirmation.

Use a 60/20/20 participant-level split by `eid` for held-out evaluation; all transformations, standardization, imputation and model tuning are fitted inside training data. Fit the same transparent model and a small discrete-time gradient-boosted survival model on identical inputs and intervals. The learned model targets interval-specific AKI hazard through five years and is evaluated on held-out Brier score, time-dependent AUC and calibration with bootstrap intervals. Its substantive output is the stability of the joint discordance-by-grip risk surface, including whether nonlinear thresholds or regions exist that the baseline interaction loses. Do not select a model because of a small predictive improvement, and do not use the learned surface to claim causal effect modification.

Primary uncertainty includes bootstrap or sandwich intervals, missingness flow, positivity, event counts, calibration and sensitivity to one-sided grip, repeat measures, two-year landmarking and alternative eGFR equation. The two-year landmark analysis repeats the cohort definition while excluding AKI/CKD events within two years after baseline to probe reverse causation and subclinical illness.

Approximate resources are 4 CPUs, 8 GiB RAM and no GPU, with streaming extraction and bounded bootstrap capped at two hours. If event support, code/date semantics, or endpoint censoring cannot be verified, the required output is an explicit inconclusive prerequisite report and neither model may be interpreted clinically.

## Falsification criteria and interpretation

Prespecify the following before outcome fitting:

1. The primary hypothesis is not supported if the interaction estimate is null or opposite in direction with a sufficiently narrow 95% interval excluding a minimally important interaction threshold, or if the apparent signal disappears in the two-year landmark and code/date sensitivity analyses.
2. A comparable discordance-by-grip association for a prespecified nonrenal negative-control outcome (for example, dated K35* appendicitis) weakens renal specificity; report it as a bias diagnostic, not proof of no confounding.
3. A permutation in which discordance is shuffled within sex-age strata should remove the interaction; failure indicates leakage or model/pipeline error.
4. If the interaction is explained by baseline N18 history, missingness, or differential diagnosis ascertainment, the proposed “missed risk” interpretation is falsified.
5. If the learned model produces unstable surfaces across splits or only a trivial metric change without stable heterogeneity, it does not provide substantive alternative information.

Supportive results require adequate paired event support, a reproducible directionally prespecified interaction, uncertainty excluding no meaningful effect, acceptable positivity/calibration, and no comparable negative-control or permutation signal across sensitivity definitions. They support a prognostic hypothesis and justify external validation or clinical adjudication, not a clinical policy.

Adverse results include a null/opposite interaction, no incremental value beyond eGFRcr, or strong dependence on early events or code definitions. These are informative negative findings. Inconclusive results include missing/unverified Showcase coding, insufficient paired dates, too few events, unstable censoring, wide intervals, or failed model diagnostics; they permit no clinical conclusion.

The snapshot lacks measured GFR, outpatient laboratory trajectories, urine output, adjudicated KDIGO AKI stage, inflammation measures sufficient to resolve cystatin-C confounding, detailed muscle mass, treatment, clinician adjudication and an independent cohort. Therefore the experiment cannot establish true filtration accuracy, causal mediation, treatment benefit, clinical utility, or transportability. Those claims require clinical adjudication, gold-standard renal measures, additional longitudinal data, external validation and ultimately a prospective decision-impact study.
