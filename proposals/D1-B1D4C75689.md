## Deterministic missing-Y partial identification

Run for every assay, origin, and future block over all BP-eligible rows, including missing Y. Fit M0/M1 only on pre-origin observed complete triplets and freeze finite predictions `p0_i,p1_i` for every BP row. If any required prediction is nonfinite, stop the block as computationally invalid rather than dropping that row. Let N be the number of BP-eligible rows and n_mis the missing count.

Set `L_train=min(Y_train)` and `U_train=max(Y_train)` over the exact finite training parser before inspecting future Y. If the training range is empty or degenerate, or any observed future Y is outside [L_train,U_train], label the temporal bound unsupported and do not widen it. A full-source range may appear only as a separately labeled descriptive check. If no future Y is observed, m_ref below uses the training observed-Y mean and is marked training-derived; it does not create future Y.

For observed rows define `e0=(Y-p0)^2`, `e1=(Y-p1)^2`, `d=e0-e1`. For a missing row and model j:

```
e_j,min = (clip(p_j,L_train,U_train)-p_j)^2
e_j,max = max((L_train-p_j)^2,(U_train-p_j)^2)
d(y) = (y-p0)^2-(y-p1)^2
     = 2*y*(p1-p0) + p0^2-p1^2
```

For each missing row, contrast extrema are exactly `min(d(L_train),d(U_train))` and `max(d(L_train),d(U_train))`. Add observed terms and divide by fixed N for sharp conditional `Delta_lower`/`Delta_upper` MSE bounds for M0-M1, overall and by arm using each arm's BP denominator. Report missing fractions and all-missing-at-L, all-missing-at-U, and least-favorable endpoint scenarios. A positive Delta means lower M1 squared error. These are deterministic finite-sample identification regions conditional on frozen predictions and observed rows, not confidence intervals.

For model-specific SSE, divide endpoint sums by N to obtain MSE bounds. The conservative, non-primary RMSE-difference enclosure is `[sqrt(MSE0_lower)-sqrt(MSE1_upper), sqrt(MSE0_upper)-sqrt(MSE1_lower)]`; never call it sharp or standardize it by an unidentified test SD. If observed test-Y SD is nonfinite or zero, G and its CI are non-estimable; valid unstandardized bounds may still be reported.

## Required secondary uncertainty contract for the bound endpoints

Whenever the deterministic bounds are estimable, the compiler must also attempt a separately labeled patient-cluster bootstrap of the endpoint function. This is sampling/cluster sensitivity conditional on the observed missingness pattern, frozen predictions, and frozen training range; it is not an uncertainty distribution for the unobserved Y values and is not a confidence interval for the identification region.

1. Define the resampling frame separately for each assay, future block, and arm from the actual BP-eligible rows. The cluster key is `patient master index`; retain every selected event row belonging to a sampled patient, including its observed/missing status, observed Y if present, fixed `p0_i,p1_i`, and original endpoint labels. Do not resample rows independently, deduplicate across arms, or create synthetic events.
2. Within each arm, sample the unique BP-eligible patient keys with replacement, using exactly the original number of unique patients in that arm. Store integer multiplicity `w_gk` for each patient key. This is a stratified bootstrap, not an unconditional arm-prevalence bootstrap. If an arm has fewer than two unique BP-eligible patients, endpoint-bootstrap uncertainty is non-estimable for that arm and must be reported as such.
3. In replicate b, weight every retained event row by its patient's multiplicity. Set `N_{g,b}=sum_i w_i` over BP rows in arm g and `N_b=sum_g N_{g,b}` overall. Thus repeated patients contribute all their selected events and the denominator is the replicate's weighted BP-eligible event count, not the original N and not the number of distinct resampled patients. N_{g,b} must be positive whenever the source arm contains at least one patient.
4. Keep `L_train`, `U_train`, `p0_i`, `p1_i`, training sample, preprocessing, model coefficients, and observed/missing flags fixed exactly as in the point analysis. Never refit the model, recompute the training range, alter the clipping rule, reveal a missing Y, or use a full-source range inside a replicate. For each replicate compute weighted observed d terms and weighted missing-row lower/upper endpoint terms, then divide by N_b (or N_{g,b} for arm-specific bounds). This explicitly defines the bootstrap functional and prevents a compiler from silently changing the target.
5. Use a declared deterministic seed and B=2000 replicates. For each of `Delta_lower` and `Delta_upper`, overall and by arm, report the empirical 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles as a “cluster-bootstrap sensitivity interval for endpoint,” not as a 95% confidence interval for the unidentified Delta. Report B requested, B completed, failures and reasons, unique patient counts, effective observed patient counts per replicate (and their summary), and the exact seed. A resource failure may yield fewer completed replicates only when recorded; it makes the secondary interval incomplete, never a reason to impute or drop rows.
6. Do not combine endpoint-bootstrap percentiles with deterministic endpoints into a narrower interval. Report a two-layer object: the point identification region `[Delta_lower,Delta_upper]` and, separately, endpoint-sensitivity intervals `[Q_.025(lower),Q_.975(lower)]` and `[Q_.025(upper),Q_.975(upper)]`. The same separation applies to model-specific MSE and conservative RMSE enclosures. The deterministic sign rule is unchanged: point `Delta_lower > 0` is robust over the declared training-range completion set; `Delta_lower <= 0 <= Delta_upper` is selection-sensitive; and unsupported/invalid gates override numerical interpretation. Bootstrap endpoint intervals cannot upgrade a crossing point region to supportive evidence.

The primary G uncertainty remains a distinct paired, arm-stratified patient-frequency bootstrap with frozen pre-origin predictions and the original point-estimate `SD_test(Y)` denominator, B=2000, percentile interval, zero/nonfinite denominator stop rules, and at least two observed patient clusters. Do not conflate that bootstrap with the required bound-endpoint sensitivity bootstrap. Neither bootstrap supplies evidence against missing-outcome selection.

## Common-displacement tipping point

Let `m_ref` be the observed future-Y mean when at least one future Y exists; otherwise use the training observed-Y mean and mark it training-derived. Set every missing value to `y_i(delta)=clip(m_ref+delta,L_train,U_train)`. Since each contrast is affine in y, Delta(delta) is continuous piecewise linear, with only shared clipping breakpoints `L_train-m_ref` and `U_train-m_ref`. Evaluate the observed component once, use slope `sum_{missing,unclipped}2(p1_i-p0_i)/N` on each segment, solve linearly, retain roots only in their segment and verify by deterministic endpoint evaluation. Report all roots in `[L_train-m_ref,U_train-m_ref]`, choosing smallest absolute magnitude, ties by smallest delta. If Delta(0)=0 report zero; if no root exists report sign-robust for this common-displacement scenario. This is a sensitivity coordinate, not an imputation or clinical plausibility claim.

## Interpretation and falsification contract

Supportive evidence requires successful reconstruction and clock assertions, outcome-blind and complete-triplet arm gates, an estimable prespecified primary result in an adequately supported block, no material ascertainment warning, and deterministic Delta bounded strictly positive throughout the declared training-range completion set. This supports only selected short-horizon prognostic association in that calendar mixture. A positive deterministic bound is not a confidence statement and does not establish transport.

Adverse evidence is a nonpositive primary contrast in an adequately supported block, repeated M1 degradation, or a bounded Delta sign reversal. Selection-sensitive evidence is favorable observed-case performance with Delta crossing zero, a near-boundary tipping point, a strong observation gradient, or discrepant ascertainment. Inconclusive evidence includes failed gates, unsupported/degenerate range, nonestimable SD or bootstrap, low ESS, influential clusters, nonestimable small cells, nonfinite predictions, incomplete secondary endpoint bootstrap, or dependence on the full-source range. The parent audit may leave 2022 locally supportive and 2023/2024 transport-inconclusive; no new finding is claimed.

The compiler/verifier must test all of the following: duplicate-safe joins and exact denominators; no Y leakage in event selection, support diagnostics, or fitting; missing Y is not negative; future observed Y outside the training range invalidates the temporal bound; affine contrast and piecewise-linear tipping arithmetic; untimed documents never enter the Y window; examination/encounter presence is not completed follow-up; endpoint bootstrap uses patient multiplicities and weighted event denominators; training range and predictions remain fixed in every bound replicate; endpoint sensitivity intervals are not confidence intervals and are not merged with deterministic regions; the primary G bootstrap keeps its original SD denominator and remains separate; failed gates preserve descriptive status; and bounded signs are never upgraded to causality, treatment effect, safety, utility, transport, actionability, or benefit.

Computationally checkable claims include source hashes/headers/schema IDs, duplicate-safe joins, reconstruction counts, clocks, funnels, diagnostics, finite-range checks, endpoint arithmetic, tipping roots, frozen predictions, and both bootstrap bookkeeping contracts. Clinical assay units/reference ranges, severity, imaging response, administration/intent, external-care capture, mortality, hepatic failure, completed follow-up, causal effects, transport, utility, actionability, and patient benefit require adjudication or an independent temporal/external/prospective cohort.
