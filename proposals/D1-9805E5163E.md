# Provisional eICU hypothesis: dynamic laboratory-monitoring reduction during persistent instability

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a continuous ICU stay who are physiologically unstable at a prespecified index time, a reduction in laboratory testing intensity during the immediately preceding 12 hours is associated with higher risk of a clinically defined deterioration in the next 12 hours, beyond contemporaneous measured severity and recent support. The association should be tested against end-of-life care-plan documentation, discharge/transfer context, and documentation/interface artifacts. A residual association in held-out hospitals would support a monitoring-process signal, but would not establish that changing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

## Uncertainties and missing evidence

The key unresolved rival explanations are end-of-life treatment limitation, discharge/transfer and interface interruption, documentation practices, and residual confounding by severity. `carePlanEOL` is sparse and documentation-dependent; missing documentation is not equivalent to no EOL intent. The source-row/header audit, exact literature claim mappings, and full feasibility analysis are still pending. Direct interface-interruption flags, staffing/bed capacity, complete pre-ICU history, and clinical adjudication of deterioration are unavailable or not yet established.

## Falsifiable test

Index hourly times from 6 through `unitdischargeoffset - 12` hours in adult continuous ICU stays with valid vital and laboratory observations in the preceding 24 hours. Define exposure as a reduction in distinct laboratory analytes/episodes in `(t-12,t]` versus `(t-24,t-12]` (primary: zero after positive prior testing, or at least 50% reduction after at least two prior episodes). Define primary outcome as first new or escalated respiratory support in `respiratoryCare` or new/escalated vasopressor/infusion in `infusionDrug` during `(t,t+12]`; treat death, discharge, and transfer as competing events. Compare a prespecified contemporaneous-severity discrete-time logistic baseline with a longitudinal time-varying testing/physiology model. Use hospital-held-out validation, calibration, discrimination, absolute risk differences, clustered uncertainty, and negative controls (within-hospital exposure permutation, post-index exposure, and a non-lab documentation timestamp control). Supportive results require reproducible held-out-hospital association after explicit EOL/discharge handling with null negative controls. Adverse results include attenuation, instability, or negative-control signal. Wide intervals or inability to distinguish interface interruption from intent are inconclusive. None establishes causality or a recommendation to increase testing.
