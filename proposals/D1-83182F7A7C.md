# Testing gaps during instability and acute ICU deterioration: a data-verified transportability experiment

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

When an adult ICU patient is observably unstable, an abrupt reduction in laboratory testing is associated with higher risk of a documented acute deterioration event during the following 24 hours, and the reduction signal is less transportable across hospitals than a model using clinical state alone.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that EHR observation/missingness patterns can encode care processes and that process shifts can impair transportability. The unresolved claim is whether a reproducible, directionally positive instability-by-testing-reduction association exists in this eICU snapshot after adjustment for measured physiology and support. This is observational: it does not claim that reducing tests causes deterioration, that a quiet chart reflects clinician intent, or that a model should trigger care.

This matters because a robust, transportable association could justify prospective study of monitoring adequacy in unstable ICU patients; a null association would discourage universal “quiet chart” warning rules; and a site-specific association would identify a documentation/process shortcut rather than a portable clinical marker. The substantive advance over the imported seed is a prespecified estimand, repaired non-overlapping exposure windows, concrete deterioration labels, hospital-held-out evaluation, and falsification tests.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use `patient.csv.gz` and retain the first ICU stay per person (`unitvisitnumber=1), grouped by `uniquepid`) among adults. Parse numeric age and map values such as `> 89` to 90; report all other nonnumeric ages and do not silently impute them. Require numeric `unitadmitoffset` and `unitdischargeoffset`, at least 36 hours of ICU observation, and a hospital ID.

Create candidate landmarks every 6 hours at ICU-relative offsets 12 through 72 hours, retaining only landmarks with at least 24 hours until unit discharge. Primary analysis samples one eligible landmark per stay with a fixed recorded seed; a secondary discrete-time analysis uses all eligible landmarks with patient-clustered uncertainty. Exclude a landmark if an EOL discussion/save is recorded at or before it in `carePlanEOL.cpleoldiscussionoffset` or `cpleolsaveoffset`; label this as an imperfect proxy. Do not use any record with a timestamp after the landmark as a predictor.

## Exposure and instability

Deduplicate `lab.csv.gz` observations within `patientunitstayid, labname, labresultoffset`, retaining the row with the earliest nonmissing `labresultrevisedoffset` and requiring numeric `labresult`. Use a case-normalized, source-audited core panel of creatinine, BUN, glucose, sodium, potassium, WBC/white blood cell count, platelets and lactate, matching `labname` together with `labmeasurenamesystem` and `labmeasurenameinterface`. Record the exact retained labels and counts; if any analyte has fewer than 1,000 observed stays, drop it before modeling and report it rather than substitute another analyte.

For landmark t, define distinct-analyte counts in non-overlapping windows:

- prior count: observations in [t-18h, t-6h);
- recent count: observations in [t-6h, t);
- require prior count >=4;
- abrupt reduction: recent count <= 50% of prior count AND no core-panel observation in the quiet tail [t-4h,t).

The quiet-tail rule is separate from the recent count and therefore does not contradict it: a recent observation may occur in [t-6h,t-4h). Preserve recent-minus-prior count and longest gap as continuous secondary exposures. Prespecify gap thresholds of 2, 6 and 8 hours as sensitivity analyses.

Define instability using only [t-6h,t]: any valid `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean <65` or `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean <65`, or at least one documented pressor record in `infusionDrug.infusionoffset`. Normalize `drugname` to a versioned list of norepinephrine/noradrenaline, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine and dopamine. Because `infusionDrug` has no end time, the exposure is a documented pressor-record proxy, not proof of continuous infusion. Report the MAP source used, invalid values, pressor label map and source coverage.

## Outcome and estimand

The primary composite deterioration event in (t,t+24h] is the first of:

1. ICU death, defined by `patient.unitdischargestatus` indicating expired and `unitdischargeoffset` in the window;
2. a first normalized pressor record in `infusionDrug` after t when no normalized pressor record occurred in [t-6h,t]; or
3. a first invasive-ventilation start in `respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset` after t when no ventilation interval from `ventstartoffset` through `ventendoffset` crosses t.

Open/ambiguous ventilation intervals must be retained under a documented conservative rule and repeated under an alternate rule. The pressor component is explicitly “new documented pressor record,” not treatment initiation. Secondary outcomes are each component, ICU death alone, and a cause-specific analysis treating alive discharge/transfer before 24 hours as a competing event. Landmarks with discharge before 24 hours are excluded from the primary risk set by design; report later discharge/transfer censoring separately.

The primary estimand is the standardized 24-hour risk difference for abrupt reduction versus no reduction within unstable and stable strata, and their interaction (difference in risk differences), using B1 predictions and hospital-stratified patient bootstrap 95% intervals. The transport estimand is the change in calibration intercept/slope, Brier score and risk-difference reproducibility when process features are removed (B0/A0) and when each hospital with at least 200 eligible landmarks is held out. Do not use hospital ID as a predictor.

## Models and splits

B0 is a regularized pooled-logistic state model with demographics, time since admission, admission-only `apacheApsVar` fields and `apachePatientResult.acutephysiologyscore/apachescore`, recent/last 6-hour summaries and slopes of MAP, heart rate, respiration and SpO2, recent core-lab values/slopes, current recorded ventilation/pressor indicators, and missingness indicators for state values. B1 adds prior/recent testing counts, longest gap, analyte-specific observation indicators and the abrupt-reduction indicator. Exclude all `actual*`, future length-of-stay, future ventilation and post-landmark fields from predictors.

A0/A1 is a small GRU or causal temporal-convolution model over the preceding four 6-hour bins, with exactly the same state/process inputs and explicit missingness masks. Fit only on training patients; use a fixed patient-level validation split within training hospitals for early stopping; evaluate untouched hospitals. No hospital ID predictor is allowed. Primary uncertainty is patient bootstrap stratified by hospital; secondary repeated seeds assess learned-model stability. Report risk-based metrics (standardized risks, risk differences, calibration, Brier and decision curves at thresholds frozen before analysis); AUROC is secondary.

## Falsification and robustness

- Repeat quiet-tail thresholds at 2, 6 and 8 hours and use continuous count-change/longest-gap exposures. A signal appearing only at one arbitrary cutoff is weak.
- Permute lab event times within each stay while preserving lab counts, hospital and stay duration. The reduction interaction and process transport signal should attenuate.
- Test the stable stratum as a negative-control context. A similarly large effect in stable patients suggests a general documentation/discharge artifact.
- Exclude landmarks within 12 hours of discharge/transfer, with EOL proxy records, or with abrupt loss of all source-table coverage; compare with the primary cohort.
- Repeat MAP-source priority and pressor label normalization; report support and missingness by hospital.
- Verify all feature timestamps are <=t, including revised lab timestamps; if `labresultrevisedoffset` is after t, do not use the result at that landmark.
- Before modeling, report eligible stays/landmarks, analyte observed-stay counts, event counts and event timing by hospital. If support is sparse, labels are unstable, or held-out hospitals lack both exposure levels and events, return an inconclusive feasibility result.

## Result interpretation and evidence limits

Supportive means the unstable-stratum interaction is directionally positive with a 95% interval excluding zero, persists across the threshold/timestamp/censoring checks, and the process signal remains calibrated and directionally reproducible in held-out hospitals. This supports an observational monitoring-risk marker and prospective study—not a treatment recommendation, causal effect or quality ranking.

Adverse means the interaction is near zero/negative with adequate precision, disappears under timestamp shuffling or censoring checks, or process features degrade materially in held-out hospitals. This would argue against a universal warning rule and could still show that site-specific workflow patterns are unsafe to transport.

Inconclusive means inadequate analyte/event support, unstable label mapping, no eligible exposure contrast, severe site heterogeneity, or wide intervals that cannot distinguish the above. Do not choose thresholds or claim a clinical effect in that case.

Computationally checkable claims are cohort construction, exact timestamps, feature/label leakage prevention, fitted parameters, standardized risks, intervals, calibration/Brier metrics, hospital holdout results and falsification outputs. Clinical adjudication is needed to decide whether a pressor/ventilation record was clinically meaningful or indicated, whether missing labs reflect interface failure, intentional monitoring or undocumented care, and whether an intervention is safe. Staffing, bed capacity, clinician intent, full treatment-limitation status, pre-ICU trajectory and prospective utility are unavailable; no mechanism or causal claim can be inferred.

## Scientific deliverable

The experiment is complete only after newly fitting B0/B1 and A0/A1 and producing: (i) a machine-readable manifest with snapshot, exact label map, filters, seeds, joins, splits and resource use; (ii) source/event support audits by hospital; (iii) primary interaction and standardized risk-difference estimates with uncertainty; (iv) state-versus-process calibration/Brier and hospital-held-out outputs; (v) secondary component/competing-risk analyses; and (vi) all falsification results. An input audit or reference execution alone does not establish the hypothesis.
