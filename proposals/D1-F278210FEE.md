# Early invasive-MAP burden versus nadir for subsequent creatinine-defined AKI in eICU

## Status and scientific deliverable

This is a computable observational landmark study, not an executed result. The future solver must (1) construct and audit a first-24-hour invasive-MAP cohort, (2) estimate whether duration-normalized hypotension burden contains held-out information about strictly subsequent creatinine-defined AKI beyond MAP nadir, (3) fit a prespecified temporal functional model on the same target to test whether timing/fragmentation information lost by the baseline matters, and (4) report hospital-held-out discrimination, calibration, uncertainty, artifact analyses, and honest interpretations.

Completion requires cohort-flow and missingness tables; exposure and outcome validation summaries; fitted baseline, burden, and temporal models; hospital-held-out predictions; cluster-bootstrap intervals; coefficient/standardized-risk plots; temporal-weight and ablation plots; and machine-readable result/claim links. It does **not** require confirmation of the hypothesis.

## Opening, evidence, and unresolved claim

The strongest existing claim supported by inspected evidence is modest: across heterogeneous ICU observational studies, hypotension is often associated with AKI and stronger exposure often accompanies stronger association, but meta-analysis did not confirm an aggregate AKI association and definitions varied substantially [K1]. In severe sepsis, FINNAKI found that lower time-adjusted MAP in the first ICU day was independently associated with five-day AKI progression, using validated 2/5-minute values aggregated to ten-minute medians [K2]. KDIGO defines AKI using creatinine and urine-output changes and warns that creatinine-only application, uncertain baseline, and incomplete monitoring miss cases and do not identify etiology [K3].

The unresolved claim tested here is:

> Among adult first ICU admissions alive and in ICU at 24 hours, with adequate first-day invasive MAP coverage, an admission creatinine, no creatinine-defined AKI progression during the exposure window, and at least one later creatinine, a greater fraction of observed first-day time with MAP <65 mmHg predicts higher risk of subsequent creatinine-defined AKI even after flexible adjustment for MAP nadir, measured severity, vasopressor recording, MAP measurement density, and hospital transport.

This is a conditional prognostic/descriptive hypothesis. “At the same nadir” means conditional on a flexible nadir term and confirmed by nadir-stratified standardization; it is not literal randomization. The leading explanation is that sustained low perfusion carries information that a transient nadir loses. Rivals are global illness severity, treatment/severity reflected by vasopressor use, selective invasive monitoring, missing-bin behavior, increased creatinine testing, and reverse timing (kidney dysfunction already evolving during exposure). These explanations differ observably: a genuine incremental temporal signal should survive flexible nadir and staged covariate adjustment, transport to held-out hospitals, remain under coverage/landmark/testing sensitivities, and show stable burden/timing patterns; an artifact should attenuate sharply with the corresponding control or fail transport.

Clinical importance: if burden robustly outperforms nadir, monitoring systems and prospective studies should summarize cumulative exposure rather than alarm on the single lowest value. A null with useful precision would argue against elevating a noisy cumulative metric over nadir. Neither result sets a treatment threshold.

## Population and temporal rules

Index one stay per person: first chronological `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitstaytype='admit'`; if malformed ties occur, smallest `patientunitstayid`. Adults ≥18, alive/in ICU at minute 1,440, with at least 202/288 valid invasive-MAP bins, an admission creatinine in [-360,+360] minutes, at least one creatinine in (-360,1440], and at least one in (1440,10080]. Exclude day-1 `dialysis=1` in the phenotype-validity analysis; report main results with and without this exclusion because its timing within day 1 is unavailable. Do not infer chronic dialysis absence from missingness.

Exposure window is ICU minutes [0,1440); landmark is 1,440. Outcome window is (1440,10080], administratively censored at unit discharge. A stay has prevalent/evolving exposure-window AKI and is excluded if any creatinine through the landmark rises ≥0.3 mg/dL from the admission value or reaches ≥1.5 times admission. This cannot exclude community-acquired AKI when admission creatinine is already elevated; state that limitation.

Subsequent creatinine-defined AKI is either (a) a rise ≥0.3 mg/dL from the last valid pre-landmark creatinine by minute 4,320 (48 hours after landmark), or (b) any creatinine through minute 10,080 ≥1.5 times admission creatinine. The executable should also implement the general all-pairs 48-hour rule as a sensitivity. Urine-output AKI, renal-replacement initiation, etiology, and biomarker-confirmed injury are not claimed.

## Exposure construction and artifact rules

Deduplicate same-stay/same-offset rows by median. Discard MAP <20 or >180 mmHg; flag but do not automatically discard physiologically abrupt adjacent changes. Do not interpolate primary values. Define coverage as valid bins/288 and normalize duration to observed bins:

- Nadir: minimum valid `systemicmean`.
- Primary burden: (24 × n(MAP<65)/n(valid)), equivalent observed hours under 65.
- Secondary depth-duration: mean deficit (Σ max(65-MAP,0)/n(valid)), mmHg.
- Temporal descriptors: longest consecutive run (gaps exactly five minutes), episode count, four six-hour burden values, and 24 hourly burdens/masks.

Repeat at thresholds 55, 60, and 70; coverage 50/60/80/90%; excluding isolated one-bin lows; winsorizing at 0.5/99.5%; restricting to median and 90th-percentile gaps of five minutes; and using irregular noninvasive MAP as a separate modality analysis with no carry-forward beyond 60 minutes. A burden result that appears only under one artifact rule is inconclusive.

## Transparent prespecified baseline and estimand

Create a deterministic hospital-held-out 70/15/15 train/validation/test split using SHA-256(`"eicu-map-aki-20260923:"+hospitalid`), with all stays from a hospital in one partition. Before outcomes are modeled, verify each partition has ≥20 hospitals and ≥100 events; if not, rotate a documented salt once and freeze it. No patient or hospital crosses partitions. Model choices and thresholds use train/validation only; test is opened once.

Fit penalized logistic regression:

- B0 (nadir baseline): restricted cubic spline of MAP nadir (knots from training only) plus age, sex, ethnicity, admission source, unit type, admission weight, admission creatinine, creatinine-test counts/timing available by landmark, MAP coverage/gap metrics, and missing indicators; add the non-BP/non-creatinine `apacheApsVar` variables as the prespecified measured-severity block.
- B1 (burden model): B0 plus a restricted cubic spline of primary burden. No automatic feature search.
- B2 (treatment-rival): B1 plus any vasopressor, number of distinct infusion offsets, first/last offset, number of hourly bins with entries, and agent classes. Attenuation from B1 to B2 is reported; B2 is not interpreted as a direct effect because treatment can be mediator, response to severity, or collider.

The primary association estimand is the test-population standardized risk difference and risk ratio comparing each person’s fitted risk at the training-set 75th versus 25th burden percentile within their 5-mmHg nadir stratum, restricted to common support, holding measured covariates fixed. Also report the burden spline and hospital-cluster-robust odds-ratio contrasts. This is an associational estimand.

Evaluate test log loss (primary predictive metric), Brier score, AUROC, AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope, calibration curves, and net benefit over plausible alert probabilities (descriptive only). Compare B1 versus B0 using paired hospital-cluster bootstrap (1,000 resamples) for Δlog loss and all metrics; bootstrap the full preprocessing/refit. Report pooled and equal-hospital-weighted results. A within-hospital fixed-effect sensitivity among hospitals with both outcomes addresses site case mix; it is separate from new-hospital transport.

## Scientifically substantive temporal alternative

Fit a penalized functional logistic model on the same split and outcome. Inputs are the 24 hourly fractions of valid bins below 65, hourly nadirs, hourly coverage masks, and hourly vasopressor-entry masks plus B0 static covariates. Represent the hypotension coefficient over hour with six cubic B-spline basis functions and a second-difference roughness penalty selected on validation log loss; include longest-run length and a prespecified hypotension×vasopressor-entry functional interaction. Call this T1. It is newly fitted to predict subsequent AKI.

B1 knows total burden and nadir but loses order, fragmentation, recovery, and treatment co-occurrence. T1 can reveal whether equal total burden concentrated late, early, or in long runs has different transportable information. Compare T1 with B1 on held-out log loss/calibration and with three falsifying ablations: permute hourly order within stay while retaining total burden/nadir; remove missingness channels; remove vasopressor channels. Stable temporal weights require cluster-bootstrap intervals and consistent sign in validation and test; they are not mechanistic renal-perfusion effects.

A small temporal convolutional network over 288 five-minute MAP/mask bins was considered. It could learn nonlinear episode morphology, but adds less directly interpretable information than T1 for this question and increases overfitting/site-shortcut risk at ~11,000 stays. Revisit only if T1 materially improves held-out log loss, residual diagnostics show unresolved within-hour morphology, and at least 1,500 events remain after final exclusions. Proposed envelope then: one A100, 4 CPUs, 32 GiB, ≤2 hours, five seeds. Selected B0–T1 work is CPU-suitable: 8–16 CPUs, 32–64 GiB, approximately 1–3 hours including 1,000 hospital bootstraps; this estimate is unverified, while the full audit scan measured ~52 seconds on the ordinary environment.

Repeat the outcome analysis with inverse-probability-of-observation weights estimated out-of-fold by hospital, stabilized and truncated at 1st/99th percentiles. Include post-test count only in descriptive sensitivity, never as a baseline predictor of an outcome it helps define.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support requires both: (1) B1’s held-out standardized 75th-versus-25th burden risk difference is >0 with a hospital-bootstrap 95% interval excluding 0 after B0 adjustment, and (2) B1 improves held-out log loss over B0 with its 95% interval excluding 0, without major calibration deterioration. It must retain direction under ≥60/80% coverage, fixed 48-hour ascertainment, and within-hospital analyses. T1 is separately supported only if it improves held-out log loss over B1 and nonconstant temporal structure survives order/missingness/vasopressor ablations.

Adverse evidence is an upper 95% bound ≤1 absolute percentage point for the standardized burden risk difference together with no held-out log-loss gain, or a reversed association stable across hospitals. That would refute a clinically meaningful incremental burden signal under this measurement design, not prove hypotension harmless.

Inconclusive evidence includes intervals spanning both 0 and +1 percentage point, strong hospital heterogeneity, failure under held-out hospitals, effect disappearance under coverage/testing/timing controls, or unstable T1 weights. Revise toward a measurement-validation study if measurement controls dominate; defer if event/common-support counts become inadequate. Do not repeatedly alter thresholds after test inspection.

A supportive result establishes only a transportable adjusted association and incremental prognostic information. It does not show that hypotension caused AKI, that vasopressors caused or prevented injury, that MAP 65 is optimal, or that raising MAP reduces risk. Those claims need richer pre-ICU renal baselines, hourly urine output and RRT timing, fluid/cardiac-output/nephrotoxin information, clinical adjudication of AKI cause, and ultimately a prospective intervention study. An adverse result does not validate nadir as causal. Clinical experts must adjudicate artifact bounds, dialysis/CKD exclusions, vasopressor mapping, and actionability.
