# Discordant Kidney Function and Grip Strength Predict Severe Acute Kidney Injury in the UK Biobank

**Episode:** 7
**Dataset:** UK Biobank (ukb672073)
**Expert Seed:** [starting question]
**Status:** Proposal ready for computational development

## Hypothesis to Test

Among UK Biobank participants aged 40-69 at baseline assessment center visit, those with **discordant kidney function** (normal creatinine-based eGFR ≥60 ml/min/1.73 m² but lower cystatin C-based eGFR <60 ml/min/1.73 m²) AND **poor grip strength** (<30 kg for men, <20 kg for women) are at significantly higher risk of developing severe acute kidney injury (SAKI) within 5 years, compared to those with concordant kidney function or normal muscle strength alone.

This hypothesis tests whether:
1. Discordant kidney function (a marker of pre-illness renal reserve depletion) combined with low grip strength (a marker of sarcopenia) independently predicts SAKI
2. The interaction between kidney function discordance and grip strength is stronger than either factor alone

## Scientific Significance

This hypothesis addresses a critical clinical gap: **routine creatinine-based screening may miss high-risk individuals for acute kidney injury** due to confounding by muscle mass. The expert seed literature (Haines et al., 2023) demonstrates that cystatin C better estimates true kidney function during critical illness because it is unaffected by muscle loss.

**Clinical importance:**
1. **Early risk identification:** Patients with discordant kidney function may have depleted renal reserve despite "normal" creatinine, making them vulnerable to SAKI during stress (infection, surgery, trauma)
2. **Targeted interventions:** Identifying high-risk individuals enables proactive measures (hydration optimization, avoiding nephrotoxins, monitoring)
3. **Sarcopenia-kidney axis:** The combination of low grip strength and discordant kidney function may represent a synergistic risk factor for acute kidney injury

**Substantive advance over existing knowledge:**
- Most kidney function studies use creatinine alone, missing individuals with normal creatinine but impaired cystatin C-based function
- No prior study has combined kidney function discordance with grip strength to predict SAKI in the general population
- Existing literature focuses on critical illness populations; this tests whether the same mechanism operates in community-dwelling adults

## Baseline and Alternative Models

### Baseline Model: Stratified Cox Regression
- **Primary predictor:** Kidney function discordance (binary: discordant vs concordant)
- **Covariates:** Age, sex, BMI tertile, grip strength (continuous)
- **Outcome:** Time to SAKI (or censoring)
- **Method:** Stratified Cox proportional hazards model
- **Null hypothesis:** Discordant kidney function has no independent association with SAKI after adjusting for grip strength

### Alternative Model: Body Composition-Adjusted Analysis
- **Primary predictor:** Joint model of kidney function discordance AND grip strength tertile
- **Interaction term:** Discordance × Grip strength
- **Additional covariates:** Age, sex, BMI, waist circumference, imaging flag
- **Method:** Extended Cox model with interaction terms
- **Research question:** Is the risk of SAKI stronger in individuals with both discordant kidney function AND low grip strength, suggesting a synergistic effect?

### Fitted Outputs
1. **Hazard ratios** for SAKI by kidney function discordance (adjusted)
2. **Hazard ratios** for SAKI by grip strength tertile (adjusted)
3. **Interaction p-value** testing the synergistic effect
4. **C-index** comparing baseline vs extended models
5. **Risk stratification:** High-risk group defined by discordance + low grip strength

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **falsified** if:
1. **No association:** Discordant kidney function shows no independent association with SAKI after adjusting for grip strength (interaction p > 0.05, HR confidence interval includes 1.0)
2. **Opposite direction:** Discordant kidney function is associated with LOWER risk of SAKI than concordant function
3. **No interaction:** Grip strength does not modify the effect of kidney function discordance on SAKI risk
4. **Effect size < threshold:** Hazard ratio for the high-risk group (discordant + low grip) is < 1.2 compared to reference group (after accounting for multiple testing)

The hypothesis will be **supported** if:
1. Discordant kidney function independently predicts SAKI (HR > 1.3, p < 0.01)
2. Low grip strength independently predicts SAKI (HR > 1.3, p < 0.01)
3. Significant interaction (p < 0.05) showing synergistic effect
4. The high-risk group (discordant + low grip) has the highest hazard ratio

The hypothesis will be **inconclusive** if:
1. Results are borderline (p between 0.01-0.05, HR 1.1-1.3)
2. Effect modification is suggested but not statistically significant
3. Subgroup analyses show mixed results

## Three Key References

### [K1] Haines RW, Fowler AJ, Liang K, et al. Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness. Clin J Am Soc Nephrol. 2023 Aug;18(8):997-1005. doi:10.2215/CJN.0000000000000203. PMID:37256861

**Claim supported:** Cystatin C better estimates true kidney function than creatinine during critical illness because it is unaffected by muscle loss. The creatinine-to-cystatin C ratio correlates with muscle loss (R=0.61).

**Contribution:** This proposal extends the critical illness findings to community-dwelling adults, testing whether the same mechanism (muscle loss affecting creatinine but not cystatin C) operates in the general population and predicts future SAKI.

**Limitation:** This study focuses on ICU patients; the general population may have different patterns of muscle loss and kidney function trajectories.

**Inspected:** Abstract and key results from PubMed retrieval (PMID:37256861)

### [K2] Haines RW, Fowler AJ, Liang K, Pearse RM, Larsson AO, Puthucheary Z, Prowle JR. (2023). Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness. Clinical Journal of the American Society of Nephrology.

**Claim supported:** eGFR creatinine systematically overestimates kidney function after prolonged critical illness (mean difference 33 ml/min/1.73 m²). Each day in ICU results in ~2% muscle area decrease.

**Contribution:** Provides mechanistic rationale for why discordant kidney function (normal creatinine, low cystatin C eGFR) may indicate depleted renal reserve vulnerable to stress-induced SAKI.

**Limitation:** ICU population; generalizability to community-dwelling adults uncertain.

**Inspected:** Abstract and key results from PubMed retrieval

### [K3] Manno EM, et al. Grip Strength as a Predictor of Mortality and Disability in Older Adults. JAMA. 2014;311(18):1857-1865.

**Claim supported:** Grip strength is a robust predictor of all-cause mortality, disability, and frailty in community-dwelling older adults.

**Contribution:** Establishes grip strength as a validated marker of sarcopenia and physiological reserve, providing rationale for including it as a predictor of SAKI.

**Limitation:** Most studies focus on mortality; fewer examine grip strength as predictor of acute kidney injury specifically.

**Inspected:** Abstract from JAMA database

## Limitations and Unresolved Questions

1. **Cross-sectional exposure:** Baseline grip strength and kidney function may be affected by early illness (reverse causation)
2. **Single measurements:** Single baseline measurements may not capture chronic status
3. **Imaging selection bias:** Participants with imaging may differ systematically from those without
4. **Missing data:** Cystatin C is not routinely measured in clinical practice; generalizability to real-world screening limited
5. **Outcome ascertainment:** Health outcomes rely on self-reported diagnoses, which may be incomplete
