# Proposal: Association Between Initiation of Systemic Therapy After TACE and 12-Month Survival in Hepatocellular Carcinoma

## 1. Scientific Question

Among HCC patients who have undergone TACE, does initiation of systemic therapy (tyrosine kinase inhibitors or immune checkpoint inhibitors) within 90 days after the last TACE procedure associate with improved 12-month survival, after adjustment for baseline liver function (ALBI grade), tumor burden, and demographic covariates?

## 2. Hypothesis

**Primary hypothesis**: In HCC patients with ≥1 TACE procedure, initiation of systemic therapy (sorafenib, lenvatinib, donafenib, regorafenib, apatinib, sintilimab, camrelizumab, tislelizumab, atezolizumab, pembrolizumab, nivolumab, or ipilimumab) within 90 days after the last TACE is associated with a hazard ratio < 1.0 for 12-month mortality compared to no systemic therapy, after multivariable adjustment.

**Rationale**: The "therapeutic time migration" framework [K1] posits that timing of systemic therapy relative to locoregional therapy may be a dynamic therapeutic biomarker. Real-world evidence from a Vietnamese cohort [K2] showed that TACE + lenvatinib had higher recorded imaging response than TACE alone (73.9% vs 35.3%), but survival was not estimable due to small sample and missing outcomes. The SHARP trial established sorafenib's survival benefit in advanced HCC, but real-world data on the *timing* of systemic therapy initiation after TACE failure—particularly in a large Chinese cohort with modern immunotherapy agents—remains sparse.

## 3. Clinical Importance

- TACE is the standard of care for intermediate-stage (BCLC-B) HCC, but most patients eventually progress.
- The optimal timing for initiating systemic therapy after TACE failure is a critical clinical decision: too early risks unnecessary toxicity, too late risks disease progression beyond the window of systemic therapy benefit.
- Current guidelines (EASL, AASLD, CSCO) recommend systemic therapy after TACE failure but do not specify optimal timing.
- A large real-world cohort (n ≈ 21,000 TACE patients) provides the statistical power to estimate this association with appropriate adjustment.
- Results would inform clinical decision-making about when to escalate from locoregional to systemic therapy.

## 4. Distinction from Parent Candidate

The parent candidate ([prior hypothesis]) examines **post-TACE AFP kinetics** as a dynamic predictor of 12-month HCC recurrence. This candidate examines **treatment escalation timing** (systemic therapy initiation) as a predictor of 12-month survival. The two are complementary:
- Parent: biomarker-driven (AFP trajectory) → recurrence risk
- This: treatment-driven (systemic therapy timing) → survival outcome
- Shared population: HCC patients with TACE
- Different exposure, different outcome, different clinical question

## 6. Population Definition

### Inclusion Criteria
1. HCC diagnosis (诊断名称 contains 肝细胞癌 or 原发性肝癌 or 肝癌)
2. ≥1 TACE procedure (手术 contains TACE-like keywords: 栓塞, 动脉化疗栓塞, TACE)
3. First TACE procedure between 2015-01-01 and 2025-01-01 (to ensure ≥12 months follow-up for most patients)
4. Age ≥ 18 years

### Exclusion Criteria
1. First TACE before 2015-01-01 (insufficient follow-up window)
2. Last encounter before first TACE + 30 days (insufficient observation)
3. Age < 18
4. Missing 患者主索引 or 就诊时间

### Expected Sample Size
- 21,156 patients with TACE; after applying time window and follow-up criteria, expect ~15,000–18,000 patients
- Systemic therapy group: expect ~3,000–5,000 patients (based on drug prevalence)
- No systemic therapy group: expect ~10,000–13,000 patients

## 7. Exposure Definition

**Systemic therapy initiation**: First prescription of any systemic anti-HCC agent (from the list above) with 开始时间 within 90 days after the last TACE procedure 开始时间.

**Classification**:
- **Exposed**: ≥1 systemic therapy prescription starting within [last_TACE_date, last_TACE_date + 90 days]
- **Unexposed**: No systemic therapy prescription in this window

**Sensitivity**: Vary the window to 30, 60, 120, 180 days.

**Drug classification**:
- TKI: sorafenib, lenvatinib, donafenib, regorafenib, apatinib, anlotinib
- ICI: sintilimab, camrelizumab, tislelizumab, atezolizumab, pembrolizumab, nivolumab, ipilimumab
- Anti-VEGF: bevacizumab (often combined with atezolizumab)

## 8. Outcome Definition

**Primary outcome**: 12-month survival (alive at 365 days after first TACE).
- **Event**: Last encounter date < first_TACE_date + 365 days (proxy for death/loss to follow-up)
- **Censoring**: Last encounter date ≥ first_TACE_date + 365 days

**Secondary outcomes**:
- 6-month survival
- 24-month survival
- Time to next TACE (treatment continuation)

**Limitation**: Last encounter as death proxy is imperfect. Patients may be lost to follow-up without dying. This is a known limitation of EHR-based survival studies.

## 10. Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis
- **Model**: Cox proportional hazards regression
- **Exposure**: Systemic therapy initiation (binary)
- **Outcome**: 12-month survival
- **Adjustment**: Age, sex, ALBI grade, AFP category, number of prior TACE, calendar year
- **Output**: Hazard ratio (HR) with 95% CI, p-value
- **Proportional hazards assumption**: Check with Schoenfeld residuals; if violated, use time-dependent coefficients or restricted mean survival time (RMST)

### Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Different exposure windows**: 30, 60, 120, 180 days
2. **Drug class stratification**: TKI vs ICI vs anti-VEGF
3. **ALBI grade stratification**: Separate HRs for ALBI 1, 2, 3
4. **IPTW (Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting)**: Propensity score weighting to balance covariates between exposed and unexposed
5. **E-value calculation**: Quantify unmeasured confounding needed to explain away the observed association
6. **Competing risk**: Treat death from other causes as competing event (if identifiable)
7. **Subgroup by calendar year**: 2015–2019 (TKI era) vs 2020–2025 (ICI era)

### Baseline Comparison
- Unadjusted comparison of 12-month survival between exposed and unexposed groups
- Standardized mean differences (SMD) for covariate balance

## 11. Falsification Criteria

### Supportive Result
- HR < 0.85 with 95% CI not crossing 1.0 (i.e., ≥15% reduction in hazard)
- Consistent direction across sensitivity analyses (≥4/6)
- ALBI-stratified HRs show benefit in ALBI 1–2 but not ALBI 3 (biologically plausible)
- E-value > 2.0 (moderate robustness to unmeasured confounding)

### Adverse Result
- HR > 1.15 with 95% CI not crossing 1.0 (systemic therapy associated with worse survival)
- This would suggest: (a) severe confounding by indication (sicker patients receive systemic therapy), (b) systemic therapy in this population is harmful, or (c) the death proxy is biased (e.g., patients on systemic therapy have more encounters and appear "alive" longer)
- Inconclusive if HR 0.85–1.15 with wide CI crossing 1.0

### Inconclusive Result
- HR 0.90–1.10 with 95% CI crossing 1.0
- Inconsistent direction across sensitivity analyses
- E-value < 1.5 (highly sensitive to unmeasured confounding)
- This would suggest: the association is weak or null, and the data cannot distinguish a small benefit from no effect

### What Would Change the Next Decision
- **Supportive**: Justifies a prospective study or trial of early systemic therapy initiation after TACE
- **Adverse**: Suggests that systemic therapy in this context may be harmful or that the death proxy is severely biased; would need clinical adjudication of death status
- **Inconclusive**: Would suggest that the question requires a different design (e.g., target trial emulation with explicit eligibility and follow-up windows) or a different population (e.g., restricted to ALBI 1–2)

## 12. Limitations

1. **Death proxy**: Last encounter date is an imperfect proxy for death. Patients may be lost to follow-up. This is the most important limitation and could bias the HR toward the null (if lost-to-follow-up patients are more likely to be in the unexposed group) or away from the null (if systemic therapy patients have more encounters).
2. **Confounding by indication**: Patients who receive systemic therapy may be sicker (more advanced disease, more TACE failures) or healthier (better liver function, more aggressive treatment). IPTW and E-value partially address this but cannot eliminate it.
3. **Free-text drug names**: Pattern matching may miss some systemic therapy prescriptions or include non-HCC indications.
4. **No BCLC staging**: Cannot directly stratify by tumor stage.
5. **Single institution**: Results may not generalize to other settings.
6. **No imaging data**: Cannot assess tumor response to TACE or systemic therapy.
7. **No unit column in labs**: ALBI calculation requires assuming standard units (μmol/L for bilirubin, g/L for albumin).
8. **Immortal time bias**: If systemic therapy is defined as "within 90 days after last TACE," patients must survive to the exposure window. This is addressed by defining the risk set from first TACE and treating systemic therapy as a time-varying exposure in sensitivity analyses.

## 13. Method Alternatives

### Baseline (Primary)
- **Cox PH regression** with multivariable adjustment
- **Strengths**: Standard, interpretable, well-understood assumptions
- **Limitations**: Proportional hazards assumption may be violated; cannot capture time-varying effects of systemic therapy

### Alternative 1: Time-Dependent Cox Model
- Treat systemic therapy as a time-varying binary exposure (0 before initiation, 1 after)
- **What it reveals**: Whether the survival benefit of systemic therapy changes over time (e.g., early benefit that attenuates)
- **Cost**: More complex coding; requires careful handling of immortal time bias
- **When to use**: If baseline Cox shows non-proportional hazards

### Alternative 2: Propensity Score Matching
- Match exposed and unexposed patients 1:1 on propensity score
- **What it reveals**: Whether the association persists in a balanced cohort
- **Cost**: Reduces effective sample size; may not find matches for rare covariate combinations
- **When to use**: As a sensitivity analysis to IPTW

### Alternative 3: Target Trial Emulation
- Explicitly define eligibility, treatment strategies, follow-up window, and causal contrast
- **What it reveals**: Whether the association holds under a rigorous causal framework
- **Cost**: More complex design; requires careful specification of all components
- **When to use**: If the primary analysis suggests a clinically meaningful association that needs causal interpretation

### Alternative 4: Machine Learning (Gradient Boosting)
- Train a gradient boosting model (e.g., XGBoost) to predict 12-month survival
- Compare feature importance of systemic therapy vs. covariates
- **What it reveals**: Whether systemic therapy is an independent predictor beyond linear effects; captures non-linear interactions
- **Cost**: Less interpretable; requires hyperparameter tuning; risk of overfitting
- **When to use**: As an exploratory analysis to identify non-linear effects or interactions missed by Cox regression
- **GPU**: Not needed; CPU sufficient for tabular data of this size

### Selection Rationale
- Primary analysis uses Cox PH (baseline) because it is the standard for time-to-event data and provides interpretable HRs
- Time-dependent Cox is the primary alternative if PH assumption is violated
- ML is exploratory only, not for primary inference
- The choice is driven by the clinical question (treatment effect estimation) rather than predictive accuracy
