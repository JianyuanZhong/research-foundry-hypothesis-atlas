# Provisional HCC trajectory-versus-care-process hypothesis

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with an exact HCC diagnosis and a valid admission timestamp, an ordered 7-day laboratory trajectory may contain incremental information about a subsequent non-routine inpatient procedure beyond an admission snapshot. The unresolved interpretation is whether any incremental signal reflects evolving patient state or instead treatment indication, care intensity, and documentation patterns.

## Grounding and strongest supported claim
The parent branch reports a bounded HCC audit with 104,554 exact HCC patient/admission keys and 20,052 timed HCC procedure rows after day 7 and before discharge (6,779 keys). This supports feasibility of a temporal procedure-proxy experiment, not that procedures represent deterioration or that trajectory has clinical value. No model has been fitted and no effect is claimed.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative
The simple baseline is penalized logistic regression using age, sex, department, first available assay values, time-zero treatment/medication indicators, and documentation counts. The alternative is a patient-level ordered sequence/state-space model with the same covariates plus within-assay values, time since admission, specimen type, and missingness. Both use patient-level splits, nested temporal validation, and repeated bootstrap uncertainty. The alternative can reveal persistence, ordering, and cross-marker interactions that the snapshot loses; this is a scientific comparison, not a claim that complexity is preferable.

## Falsification and rival test
The strongest rival is that procedures are scheduled or triggered by treatment pathways and documentation rather than patient deterioration. Therefore the trajectory advantage must be evaluated on the non-routine endpoint, adjusted for process/documentation variables, and compared with (a) a timing-shifted negative control and (b) within-patient temporal-order permutation. A positive result in the negative control, an advantage confined to routine procedures, disappearance after process adjustment, or instability across department/missingness/assay definitions is adverse to the evolving-state interpretation. Sparse events, unstable endpoint dictionary, missing timing, or inability to adjudicate clinical meaning are inconclusive. A supportive result would require a nontrivial held-out advantage with uncertainty excluding a prespecified trivial margin, stability across sensitivities, and no positive negative control; it would still not establish causality or clinical harm.

## Missing evidence and next checks
The source lacks lab units, validated death/ICU fields, and independent clinical adjudication. The procedure endpoint may remain an administrative proxy. Before selection, verify the exact catalog/schema and inspect the procedure-label distribution and timing coverage; freeze the routine dictionary; determine whether a negative-control endpoint is computable; and obtain or explicitly defer clinical adjudication. The three parent references are abstract-only and motivate, but do not establish, this HCC hypothesis.

## Method decision
A snapshot baseline is scientifically necessary because it tests whether temporal information adds anything beyond static state and care process. The time-aware alternative is scientifically useful because ordering and persistence are the substantive unresolved information. A larger neural sequence model is deferred unless event support and runtime justify it; CPU is appropriate for the tabular baseline and bounded sequence fit.
