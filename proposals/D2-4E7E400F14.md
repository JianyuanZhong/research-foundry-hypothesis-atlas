# MerTK cell-context therapeutic-window experiment

**Status:** protocol/design only. No retrospective reanalysis, intervention, or biological result was run in this branch. The prior platform failure is packaging evidence, not scientific evidence.

## Question and decision

Does MERTK-specific inhibition reduce inflammatory macrophage-to-fibroblast signaling in an inflammatory RA context while sparing efferocytosis and repair functions in remission-associated synovial macrophages, or do observed inhibitor-linked expression patterns instead arise from UNC106238 exposure/off-target action, cell injury, carry-over, donor composition, or assay context? A positive result would justify testing a *cell-restricted* MERTK strategy, not broad systemic inhibition or clinical efficacy. A consistent functional harm signal would argue against broad blockade; target-independent effects would de-prioritize UNC106238 as evidence for MERTK.

### Evidence boundary and falsifiable predictions

The strongest relevant published claim inspected is that MerTK-positive synovial tissue macrophage states associate with RA remission and can support stromal repair; Alivernini et al. also report inhibitor-associated molecular observations [K1]. This overlap means the broad transcript association is not itself a new result. Huelse et al. review MERTK roles in tissue homeostasis/repair and immune regulation and pharmacologic targeting [K2]; the work bounds the safety rationale but is not RA functional proof. Richard et al. report that synovial matrix turnover relates to immune-cell spatial patterning during inflammation resolution [K3], showing that matrix function/context cannot be reduced to macrophage expression alone. All three inspected items here were Europe PMC metadata/abstract records only; no full text or supplement was inspected in this branch.

**Leading hypothesis:** after confirmed MERTK target engagement, genetic loss and pharmacological inhibition will move inflammatory macrophages toward lower FLS inflammatory output, but the same target loss in remission-associated MERTK+ STM will reduce efferocytosis and FLS-supported matrix repair. Cell-context-specific treatment therefore has a safety boundary.

**Strongest rival:** UNC106238 changes function through non-MERTK targets, dose-dependent toxicity, or residual compound carried to FLS. In that case its effect will not be reproduced by MERTK knockdown/CRISPRi and will persist after MERTK rescue or show a drug-by-exposure rather than perturbation-by-context pattern. A second ordinary rival is donor/state selection: apparent context differences vanish after within-donor randomized contrasts and donor-level uncertainty is included.

Predictions differ on orthogonal perturbation and rescue: MERTK-specific effects should track MERTK engagement and be reproduced by independent MERTK loss, reversed by guide-resistant wild-type MERTK rescue, and not reversed by kinase-dead rescue. An UNC106238-only phenotype, especially at cytotoxic or unmeasured free concentrations, supports off-target/exposure rather than target causality. Even convergent in-vitro evidence cannot establish in-vivo delivery selectivity or patient benefit.

## Prospective protocol (indispensable new data)

### Stage 0: assay and exposure gate (do not infer a dose from the historical annotation)

Before efficacy experiment, analytically confirm UNC106238 identity, purity, solubility, stability, vehicle, free concentration range, and concentration-time exposure in macrophage medium and after wash. Run a blinded concentration range in macrophages and FLS, with vehicle and positive cytotoxicity controls. Quantify viability, apoptosis, and residual compound in the final macrophage wash/coculture medium (LC-MS/MS). Choose a low/mid dose range from measured exposure producing a prespecified MERTK engagement without >10% viability loss; if no such window exists, stop UNC106238 testing and proceed only with genetic perturbation. The historical reported 250 µM is not presumed physiological or selective.

### Population and biological units

Prospectively recruit 24 independent RA synovial-tissue donors, target 12 active-disease and 12 sustained-remission donors, subject to clinical adjudication and tissue yield; these targets are design assumptions, not source dataset counts or power guarantees. Collect paired peripheral blood monocytes if feasible. Isolate synovial macrophages and sort/characterize the remission-associated STM-enriched fraction prospectively (e.g., viable CD45+CD14+/CD64+ MERTK-high/CD206+; freeze marker gates and phenotype definitions before treatment). From the same blood donor differentiate monocyte-derived macrophages (MDM). Classify disease activity/remission using rheumatologist-adjudicated status, medication history, DAS28/clinical record and sampling date; this essential context information is unavailable retrospectively. Use independently obtained primary RA FLS donors, each macrophage preparation crossed with two FLS donors using a balanced incomplete block; FLS and myeloid donor are distinct biological random factors. Technical wells are nested replicates, not n. Target 12 evaluable donors per context, with an initial blinded variance/pilot gate and power calculation for the donor-by-context interaction; expand before unblinding if the prespecified primary-effect precision is unattainable and resources/ethics permit. Report all screened, failed-yield and excluded donors.

### Randomized perturbation matrix

Within each myeloid donor, randomly allocate aliquots (plate position randomized, analyst blinded) to:
- vehicle; UNC106238 at 3 analytically selected concentrations; unrelated selective MERTK inhibitor/chemotype after in-vitro kinase profiling; and an inactive structural analogue if available;
- non-targeting CRISPRi/siRNA, two independent MERTK-targeting guides/reagents, and guide-resistant MERTK-WT rescue or kinase-dead rescue. Use identical transfection/vector controls. Confirm knockdown and rescue before coculture. No single inhibitor is treated as selective by assertion.
- context controls: unstimulated MDM, LPS-activated MDM (1 ng/mL to match published setup, exact duration prespecified), and sorted STM under basal and defined inflammatory challenge; separate direct FLS-only drug exposure from myeloid-only dosing. Match wash/dilution procedures and include a carryover-spike control in which FLS receive measured final-wash residual drug.

For the core cell-context estimand, dose only myeloid cells; wash thoroughly before pairing with untreated FLS. In parallel, separate FLS-only dosing estimates direct stromal drug effects and a no-cell conditioned-medium control estimates residual compound effects. Allocate each donor's cells to each condition; collect aliquots before and after challenge, never count wells/cells as independent donors.

### Schedule and measurements

Day -2 to -1: deliver CRISPRi/siRNA or rescue vectors; verify knockdown/rescue. Day 0: isolate and phenotype; begin MDM differentiation as required (record protocol lot and day), sort STM; randomize conditions. Day 5 (or validated differentiation endpoint): activate designated MDM with 1 ng/mL LPS; expose UNC106238 1 h before LPS and maintain through prespecified 6 h engagement and 24 h functional readouts. For STM, use the same drug pulse/wash schedule with unstimulated and inflammatory-challenge strata. Record exact elapsed time and measured concentrations. At 0, 1, 6, 24, and 48 h collect target engagement, viability, secreted factors and coculture outcomes; 48 h is upper functional window, not a clinical time point.

**Target engagement / pathway:** total and phospho-MERTK (validated phosphosite assay/flow or immunoblot), receptor occupancy/biochemical engagement if validated, Gas6-dependent activation, downstream pAKT/pERK, surface MERTK, and intracellular/free drug exposure. Confirm MERTK transcript/protein knockdown and WT/kinase-dead rescue. Measure AXL/TYRO3 and a focused off-target kinase/phosphoprotein panel to reveal related TAM receptor activity; use an in-vitro kinase panel to qualify compounds before interpreting phenotype.

**Primary benefit endpoint:** prespecified 24 h FLS inflammatory output composite in macrophage-conditioned or transwell coculture: secreted IL-6, IL-8/CXCL8, CCL2 and MMP3 protein (standardized log-scale mean after fixed assay calibration), with individual components reported. Predefine one primary score and donor-level paired contrast, e.g., equally weighted standardized mean of IL-6, IL-8 and MMP3; do not derive the score from observed treatment effects. Confirm at 48 h.

**Primary harm endpoints:** (1) efferocytosis index: percentage/number of macrophages internalizing labeled apoptotic autologous or standardized apoptotic target cells at fixed 60–90 min, extracellular fluorescence quenched; distinguish binding from internalization and normalize to viable cell count; (2) FLS repair/matrix function: FLS scratch closure and collagen/fibronectin deposition or matrix contraction at 48 h after myeloid conditioned medium/transwell, with FLS viability. These measure different safety domains. Secondary: macrophage viability/apoptosis, cytokines (TNF, IL-1β), lipid mediator profile if assay validated, FLS invasion/migration, targeted transcript markers. Not substitute primary outcomes with post-hoc genes.

### Estimands, analysis and uncertainty

Primary estimands are donor-average within-donor treatment effects and context interactions: (A) inflammatory MDM UNC106238 vs vehicle on the 24 h FLS inflammatory composite; (B) remission-associated STM MERTK loss vs non-targeting control on efferocytosis and repair; (C) context-by-perturbation interaction; and (D) concordance of drug effects with genetic loss and reversal by WT versus kinase-dead rescue. Separate effects by perturbation; do not combine pharmacological and genetic arms into one treatment indicator.

Fit prespecified hierarchical mixed models with fixed context, perturbation/dose, challenge, time and their key interactions; random intercepts/slopes for myeloid donor and crossed FLS donor, with plate/batch blocking terms. Analyze continuous endpoint on prespecified transformed scale. For counts/proportions use binomial/logit model with denominators; assay QC/outlier exclusions decided blind to condition. Show donor-level paired estimates, mean/median contrasts, 95% intervals, all raw donor points, dose response, residuals, variance components, multiplicity-controlled primary endpoint family, and sensitivity to missing-not-at-random donor yield. Bootstrap resampling unit is donor (preserving FLS crossing); technical wells are aggregated or modeled as nested measurement error. Provide smallest effect sizes of interest (SESOI) and equivalence bounds agreed with clinicians/lab before unblinding; report precision/power from pilot without claiming population clinical equivalence.

Off-target discrimination requires convergent causal pattern: comparable phenotype at engaged, non-toxic drug exposures and independent MERTK genetic loss; loss of effect after WT rescue (and failure of kinase-dead rescue); no residual drug effect in FLS carryover-spike; and no corresponding phenotype explained by AXL/TYRO3 inhibition. If the drug and genetics disagree, prioritize genetic/rescue evidence for target attribution and characterize compound pharmacology separately. Rescue itself can alter expression; show expression near physiological range.

## Three-way interpretation and decision gates

- **Supportive:** in inflammatory MDM, confirmed target engagement and genetic loss produce concordant reduction in the prespecified FLS inflammatory composite; WT rescue restores output while kinase-dead rescue does not; in remission-associated STM, MERTK loss reduces efferocytosis and/or repair beyond prespecified harm bounds. This supports a *cell-context-dependent functional tradeoff* and warrants a delivery/selectivity study. It does not prove cell-targeted delivery, in-vivo benefit, clinical efficacy, safety, or that the historical drug signal was on-target.
- **Adverse to therapeutic-window hypothesis:** no meaningful inflammatory benefit despite adequate engagement and precision, or a similar/severe loss of repair/efferocytosis in both contexts, or genetics contradict the drug signal with no WT-rescuable target effect. This weakens/dismisses the proposed MERTK target rationale in this model; drug-only effects instead implicate off-target/exposure or assay artifacts. A precise null is evidence against the proposed effect; an imprecise null is not.
- **Inconclusive:** insufficient target engagement, unacceptable toxicity, poor cell yield, failed rescue, large donor heterogeneity with intervals spanning both benefit and harm bounds, assay irreproducibility, missing clinical context, or unresolved carry-over. Do not promote a broad MERTK intervention; repeat/optimize only the failed gate, or stop if there is no non-toxic engaged exposure.

Continue to delivery development only if target-specific benefit is reproducible across donors and safety signal is context-linked with adequate precision. Revise if benefit depends on stimulation/dose or one donor stratum; require independent donor confirmation before target claim. Stop UNC106238 mechanistic interpretation if compound phenotypes lack genetic/rescue concordance or are explained by cytotoxicity/residual drug. Stop broad target prioritization if functional benefit is absent or resolution functions are substantially harmed. Clinical adjudication of remission, assay gate validation, and eventual in-vivo pharmacokinetics/tissue distribution and human clinical study are outside automatic computation.

## Same-question methods comparison and scientific deliverable

The required deliverable is a new donor-crossed functional dataset and fitted donor-aware estimate of whether an MERTK-specific context-by-perturbation benefit/harm pattern exists, including uncertainty and rescue/target-engagement concordance. No historical reanalysis can deliver this.

**Transparent baseline (selected first):** paired within-donor contrasts of randomized vehicle vs each inhibitor/genetic arm, estimated with mixed-effects regression and donor-cluster bootstrap, separately for primary benefit and harm endpoints. Inputs: exposure, target engagement, perturbation assignment, context/challenge/time, all stated outcomes, donor and FLS-pair IDs, batch. Fitting target: donor-average treatment/context interaction and uncertainty, with adjusted group estimates and small-effect/equivalence intervals. Split: nested development/holdout by myeloid donor, balanced context; reserve 25% donors as locked confirmation (if n=24, six donors, three/context); no cells or wells split across folds. Evaluate held-out calibration/coverage and direction/size of paired contrasts; bootstrap donor-level 95% intervals on full randomized study remains the primary inferential result. Compare condition-specific fixed estimates with donor random effects and check influence. This is interpretable and matches the randomization; it is adequate as the primary analysis.

**Substantive alternative (preplanned secondary, not a replacement):** nonlinear hierarchical exposure–target-engagement–function model, fitting context-specific Emax/EC50 and maximal effects with donor random effects, measured free concentration and pMERTK/occupancy as engagement covariates, genetic perturbation/rescue as mechanistic anchors, and joint benefit/harm endpoints. Predict functional response under observed concentration and engagement trajectories; estimate whether functional changes track MERTK engagement versus drug concentration independent of engagement and quantify donor variation. Inputs are the same prospective data plus measured free exposure, engagement, AXL/TYRO3 phospho readouts and genotype/rescue. Fit only after Stage 0 assay calibration; donor-held-out split identical to baseline, all repeated measurements from a donor remain together. Compare against baseline by held-out log predictive density/MAE and interval coverage, and more importantly by prespecified mechanistic contrast: does exposure-only or target-engagement-linked curve best explain held-out endpoints, and does it predict rescue/genetic effects? Use donor bootstrap/posterior intervals and report identifiability diagnostics. It may distinguish an exposure-toxic curve from target-engagement-mediated, saturable function and quantify context-specific therapeutic window, information a paired contrast loses. It cannot prove mediation if engagement is measured with error or MERTK perturbation affects parallel pathways; genetic rescue is necessary evidence.

Approximate solver budget (planning estimate, not measured): baseline CPU 4 cores, 8–16 GB RAM, <1 h per analysis including bootstrap and mixed models; mechanistic hierarchical fit CPU 8 cores, 16–32 GB RAM, 1–4 h for a few chains/sensitivity runs; GPU not justified for low-dimensional donor-level data. Wet-lab cost/time is unestimated. Discovery budget was not spent fitting either method. Defer the nonlinear alternative if the Stage 0 assay does not reliably quantify engagement/free exposure, donor count is too low to identify dose-response, convergence/calibration is poor, or held-out improvement/interpretive separation is negligible. Do not choose it merely for complexity or a small predictive improvement. Both methods cannot substitute for prospective intervention; retrospective transcript screens do not have these functional or exposure inputs.
