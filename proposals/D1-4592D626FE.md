Title: The observable handoff decision for incidental pulmonary-nodule recommendations: a complete, adjudication-gated MIMIC-IV study of discharge-plan propagation and recommendation-timed same-system CT

Clinical decision, strongest supported claim, and unresolved hypothesis

The clinically actionable decision that MIMIC-IV can observe most directly is not whether a patient “adhered” to surveillance. MIMIC cannot see outside imaging, ordering intent, patient notification, or appointment completion. It can observe a narrower upstream transition: an interval-specific recommendation finalized in an inpatient radiology report before discharge, and whether the final discharge-summary artifact faithfully carries the same patient-specific nodule, chest-CT action, and interval.

Strongest claim supported before this experiment: pulmonary-nodule follow-up is often incomplete, communication failures are one contributor, and multifaceted tracking systems appear more effective than report-only changes, although most nonrandomized intervention evidence has serious or critical risk of bias. Kwan et al. found discharge-summary mention associated with timely imaging in a 65-patient inpatient/ED CTPA cohort (J Hosp Med 2019; PMID 30794133; PMCID PMC6625441). A 2025 systematic review of 31 intervention studies found heterogeneous benefits across tracking, templates, decision support, NLP, process improvement, and patient-facing interventions; it judged tracking systems especially promising but emphasized high risk of bias and the need for rigorous trials (Chest 2025; DOI 10.1016/j.chest.2025.02.031; PMID 40081655; PMCID PMC12264345; full public XML inspected). A 2025 abstract reports that EHR-integrated radiology dictation macros can trigger clinician notification and tracking, but that workflow is not equivalent to a discharge-plan handoff and its observational implementation does not establish causal benefit (J Imaging Inform Med 2025; DOI 10.1007/s10278-024-01357-7; PMID 39806184).

Unresolved primary process hypothesis: among adults with an adjudicated, unconditional 3-, 6-, or 12-month chest-CT recommendation for an incidental pulmonary nodule that was finalized and available before hospital discharge, fewer than 80% have a faithful recommendation propagated into the final discharge summary stored within 24 hours after discharge. The primary estimand is the opportunity-level faithful-propagation proportion, with omission, incomplete/discordant propagation, and explicit alternate resolution reported separately. It is a direct process estimand, not a causal effect.

Unresolved secondary hypothesis: faithful propagation is associated with a higher risk of a recommendation-timed chest CT documented in the same MIMIC health system than omission, after measured case-mix and prior-utilization adjustment. The null standardized risk difference is 0. This endpoint is explicitly “MIMIC-observed recommendation-timed CT,” not all-system completion, guideline adherence, or patient benefit.

The consequential intervention target is chosen by a prespecified bottleneck matrix. A low faithful-propagation proportion supports testing a discharge workflow that creates an interval-specific task before exit. High propagation with low observed surveillance shifts the target downstream to active tracking/outreach. Propagation without a specific surveillance association means documentation alone is unlikely to close the loop. A dedicated radiology RECOMMENDATION(S) section is not a primary intervention target: the complete proxy audit found it too rare for a credible comparison.

Population and adjudication

Unit: first eligible recommendation admission per subject. Include adults age >=18 with a CT in the linked ED/inpatient window and an unconditional, patient-specific recommendation for chest CT at exactly 3, 6, or 12 months after an incidental pulmonary/lung nodule or indeterminate focal nodular pulmonary opacity. Chest CT, CTPA/CTA, and abdominal CT that visualizes lung bases may be index examinations. Exclude scheduled lung-cancer screening, an examination whose stated indication is nodule surveillance, explicit lung-cancer staging/surveillance, recommendation text contingent on unmeasured risk (“if high risk,” “optional”), no-follow-up recommendations, nonpulmonary nodules, death before discharge-summary ascertainment, hospice/comfort-only transition, and a chest CT that already satisfies or supersedes the recommendation before summary ascertainment.

Do not require proof that the nodule is “new” or guideline-eligible. That parent criterion demanded longitudinal stability, pack-years, morphology, and clinical context that MIMIC cannot reliably reconstruct. Instead, the experiment tests fidelity to the radiologist’s explicit recommendation. Record new/changed/stable/unknown and prior same-system imaging, but never infer that the recommendation itself was guideline-appropriate.

Every rule-screened candidate is adjudicated; proxy text labels are never analysis truth. Two radiology-trained reviewers, blinded to discharge text and future imaging, independently decide index eligibility and abstract interval, action, nodule site, size/type/multiplicity when stated, recommendation certainty, indication/incidental status, report section, and whether a pre-discharge addendum changes the recommendation. Consensus or a third reviewer resolves disagreement. Separately, two transition reviewers, blinded to the index report and future outcomes, review the discharge summary and abstract any patient-specific pulmonary-nodule mention, named action, interval/date, responsible clinician/service if stated, and documented alternate resolution. The comparison algorithm then matches the independently abstracted fields. Outcome reviewers see subsequent radiology reports without discharge exposure.

Discharge outcome categories
- Faithful: affirmed patient-specific pulmonary nodule plus chest CT (or unambiguous thoracic CT) and the same 3/6/12-month interval or equivalent calendar due date.
- Incomplete/discordant: nodule mention without action; CT action without timing; or a different interval/action with no explicit clinical resolution.
- Omitted: no patient-specific nodule follow-up content.
- Explicit alternate resolution: documented patient refusal/preference, goals-of-care decision, specialist takeover with a concrete alternative plan, already scheduled outside-system follow-up, or a documented clinical decision superseding CT. This is reported as a distinct disposition, not silently counted as successful propagation or failure. Adjudicators assess documentation, not whether the alternate decision was medically correct.

Copied guideline boilerplate, negated/historical/family mentions, generic “follow up with PCP,” and copied radiology prose lacking a patient-specific plan are not faithful. Primary inference compares faithful, incomplete/discordant, omitted, and alternate-resolution proportions. The prespecified binary faithful-versus-omitted contrast excludes only the separately reported alternate-resolution category; it never retrospectively excludes people based on subsequent imaging.

Temporal ordering and leakage prevention

Recommendation eligibility requires final eligible report/addendum `storetime <= dischtime` and `storetime <= discharge-summary storetime`. The propagation endpoint is measured at final-summary `storetime`. For surveillance analyses, time at risk begins at `max(dischtime, discharge-summary storetime)`; no CT before that time can be attributed to the documented summary. The recommendation due date remains index `charttime + 3/6/12 calendar months`. Future radiology can define outcomes and observation status only, never index eligibility, recommendation interval, discharge exposure, or baseline covariates. A strict sensitivity analysis requires both report and final summary stored by discharge. Report post-discharge summary storage explicitly because `storetime` is not proof that the plan was communicated to the patient before exit.

Outcomes and observation-aware follow-up

Primary process outcome: faithful-propagation proportion by 24 hours, with exact 95% confidence interval; key complementary outcomes are incomplete/discordant, omission, alternate resolution, and final-summary availability by discharge and by 24 hours.

Secondary surveillance outcome: first MIMIC-observed chest CT/CTA with `charttime` after surveillance time zero and within 42 days before through 42 days after the recommendation due date. Report +/-30- and +/-90-day sensitivities. A nodule-focused version requires indication/comparison/impression language showing surveillance of the index nodule and blinded adjudication. An “any post-exposure chest CT by due+42 days” endpoint captures early evaluation but is not called recommendation-timed. Acute-admission CT and nodule-focused surveillance are shown separately.

Absence of a MIMIC CT is never called nonadherence or failed follow-up. For every arm, report a joint three/four-state outcome by due+42: (1) MIMIC-observed timed CT; (2) no timed CT but a continued same-system trace (another MIMIC admission or radiology event through or after the window); (3) no subsequent MIMIC trace, hence outside care/loss of capture unknown; and (4) recorded death before window end. The all-eligible risk of state 1 is the primary secondary estimand because it is exactly observable. Restriction to continued-trace patients is descriptive sensitivity analysis, not the primary analysis, because post-exposure observability can be a collider. Model observation state separately and use inverse-probability-of-observation only as a sensitivity analysis under explicitly stated, unverifiable missing-at-random assumptions.

Negative controls and utilization diagnostics

1. Temporal placebo: chest CT in an 84-day pseudo-window centered at `index charttime - recommendation interval`, before discharge propagation. A comparable faithful-versus-omitted association indicates residual utilization/team-selection confounding or extraction error.
2. Generic-utilization outcome: non-chest radiology in the recommendation-timed window. A propagation association comparable to the chest-CT association suggests general same-system engagement rather than nodule-specific action.
3. Observation outcome: continued same-system trace itself. A large exposure association warns that selection/capture, not surveillance, drives results.
4. Mechanism specificity: nodule-focused timed CT should show at least as strong an association as any chest CT; an association confined to acute, non-nodule CT does not support a handoff mechanism.
5. Repeat with CTPA index studies, lung-base abdominal CT, chest CT, exact by-discharge summaries, no cancer codes, no acute-admission follow-up CT, and >=2 years of prior same-system radiology history. These are sensitivities, not undisclosed cohort pruning.

Estimands and analysis

Primary upstream estimand: `P(faithful propagation by summary storetime | adjudicated eligible recommendation, report available before discharge, final summary available by 24h)`, plus the full multinomial disposition distribution. Test the threshold hypothesis by whether the exact 95% confidence interval lies wholly below 0.80. Also report the unconditional cascade per 100 adjudicated recommendations: report available before discharge -> summary available by discharge/24h -> faithful/incomplete/omitted/alternate. This separates a delayed/missing summary artifact from content omission.

Secondary associational estimand: standardized risk difference and risk ratio for MIMIC-observed recommendation-timed CT under faithful versus omitted documentation among otherwise eligible recommendations. Begin with unadjusted arm risks and interval-specific exact strata. The parsimonious adjustment set is fixed before outcome analysis: recommendation interval; index exam coverage (chest/CTA versus lung bases); nodule size/type/newness as abstracted; prior-year same-system chest CT and admission counts; age category; sex; cancer-history category excluding active staging; COPD/emphysema; ICU exposure; medical versus surgical discharging service; length of stay; discharge destination; and language/insurance as access proxies. These administrative labels are not biological variables, and models are repeated without them.

Use overlap weighting or regression standardization only after diagnostics. Report propensity distributions, weighted standardized mean differences, effective sample sizes, arm/event counts, and bootstrap confidence intervals. Limit outcome-model degrees of freedom to the available event count; collapse to exact stratification on interval, exam coverage, prior utilization, and service if needed. Do not label either approach causal: clinician diligence, communication channels, outpatient relationships, patient preference, and outside-system care remain unmeasured.

Predeclared gates
- Complete adjudication is required; automated candidate-screen sensitivity must be >=0.95 on adjudicated cases and PPV of final faithful/omitted labels must be >=0.90 with uncertainty reported.
- Primary process inference requires >=200 eligible recommendations and no unresolved adjudication disagreements; otherwise proportions are descriptive.
- Adjusted surveillance comparison requires >=50 adjudicated faithful and >=50 omitted subjects, >=15 timed CT events in each arm, overlap-weighted effective sample size >=40 per arm, no extreme structural nonoverlap, and weighted absolute standardized mean differences <=0.10 for key covariates. Failure invokes descriptive exact/stratified results only.
- A surveillance result is not called supportive if the temporal placebo or generic-utilization control has a standardized absolute risk difference at least as large and in the same direction, if the nodule-focused endpoint is weaker while acute CT drives the result, or if exposure-related observability materially explains it.

Complete no-sampling audit and preserved negative findings

A new read-only audit scanned all 2,321,355 `radiology` rows, all candidate-linked discharge rows, all 6,046,121 `radiology_detail` rows, all 331,794 `discharge_detail` rows, the complete admissions and patients archive members, and all longitudinal radiology rows for candidate subjects; no row sample was used. Under a deliberately broad lexical recommendation proxy, 1,291 inpatient-window reports were found, 1,241 had CT metadata, and 1,194 were first candidate subjects. Of these, 1,022 had a discharge summary stored by 7 days, 770 by 24 hours, but only 285 by discharge. After the audit’s leakage, crude cancer/hospice/death, and 24-hour gates, 643 proxy subjects remained: 403 faithful, 188 omitted, and 52 partial/discordant. These are feasibility proxies, not clinical truth; the parent’s narrower screen found 692 first subjects, illustrating screen-definition sensitivity and the need to adjudicate every candidate.

Among the 643 proxy-gated subjects, 394 had the audit’s continued-system trace: 253 faithful, 99 omitted, and 42 partial/discordant. Recommendation-timed same-system CT occurred in 44/253 faithful and 16/99 omitted (17.4% versus 16.2%); any chest CT by window end was nearly identical (77/253, 30.4%, versus 30/99, 30.3%). Thus the complete proxy audit does not show a compelling crude surveillance benefit and this negative finding is preserved. The all-gated crude timed-CT risks were 49/403 (12.2%) versus 17/188 (9.0%), while continued-trace rates differed (62.8% versus 52.7%), demonstrating why post-window restriction can change results and cannot be treated as innocuous “complete follow-up.” Prior-year chest CT also differed (11.7% versus 15.4%), and timely non-chest imaging in the continued-trace subset was 36.4% versus 41.4%; these controls do not mimic a positive handoff effect but show meaningful baseline/observation imbalance.

The report-section branch failed feasibility: an exact full-text heading proxy found only 8 dedicated RECOMMENDATION(S) headings among 1,022 summary-linked candidates and 6 among 643 proxy-gated subjects; only 2/6 were crude faithful propagations. Moreover, `radiology_detail` contains exam metadata and report/addendum links, not report sections. Report location may be abstracted descriptively, but no adjusted heading-versus-embedded comparison will be attempted unless adjudication unexpectedly yields >=50 per arm and adequate overlap. The discharge plan—not report-section formatting—is therefore the consequential MIMIC-observable intervention target.

Interpretation and falsification

Supportive upstream result: the upper 95% confidence bound for faithful propagation is <80%, with a substantial omitted/incomplete fraction and reliable adjudication. Interpretation: a measurable report-to-discharge process gap exists, justifying prospective testing of an auto-populated, interval-specific discharge task finalized before exit. It does not show that the radiology recommendation was medically correct or that propagation benefits patients.

Adverse-to-the-proposed-target result: faithful propagation is >=90% with a lower confidence bound >=80%, or most apparent failures are valid alternate resolutions. Interpretation: discharge text is not the main bottleneck; active tracking, scheduling, patient communication, or cross-system exchange is a more consequential intervention target.

Supportive secondary result: adjusted faithful-versus-omitted risk difference for MIMIC-observed nodule-focused timed CT is >0 with a confidence interval excluding 0, all positivity/event/accuracy gates pass, and negative controls are smaller and nonsupportive. Interpretation: discharge propagation is a promising communication target associated with same-system ascertainment and warrants a randomized or stepped-wedge implementation study.

Adverse secondary result: adequate-precision risk difference is <=0, or faithful propagation is associated mainly with premature/acute CT rather than nodule-focused surveillance. Interpretation: documentation alone may be ineffective or may encourage low-value cascades; test tracking/adjudication rather than more text.

Inconclusive result: confidence interval spans +/-10 percentage points, gates fail, annotation is unreliable, observability differs materially, or negative controls are positive. Interpretation: MIMIC cannot resolve the downstream strategy association. The upstream propagation distribution remains reportable if its own adjudication gate passes.

Claims outside computational verification

An automatic verifier can check source hashes, archive members, joins, version chains, report/summary `storetime` ordering, candidate counts, adjudication-table completeness, due-date arithmetic, exam classification, disposition matching, arm/event/positivity gates, controls, models, and whether conclusions match outputs. It cannot determine true Fleischner eligibility, smoking pack-years, recommendation appropriateness, whether a plan was actually discussed or delivered, outside imaging, ordering/referral intent, patient refusal unless documented, malignancy truth, cancer stage, delayed diagnosis, radiation harm, survival, or patient benefit. Those require expert review, linked outpatient/outside-system data, and a prospective intervention study.
