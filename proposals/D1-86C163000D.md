# Patient-state versus documentation signal in hospital transport of early ICU mortality models

Status: complete child design; no study result is claimed. Child of assessed valid `[prior hypothesis]`.

## Opening and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent established a clinically consequential transportability question: whether laboratory-result presence, timing, and frequency make ICU mortality models less transportable across hospitals. The strongest inspected evidence supports only bounded claims: temporally structured EHRs can predict mortality across centers [K1]; information presence/missingness can predict risk and shifts may threaten transportability [K2, abstract only]; and external mortality models can retain discrimination while local adaptation improves calibration under fragmented data [K3]. None distinguishes patient-state information from hospital/documentation practice.

Leading explanation: testing process is partly patient-state signal. Even after observed values and demographics, sicker patients may be tested more often, at different times, or for a different analyte set. A same-hospital process permutation should therefore remove much of its incremental prediction, and a time shift should attenuate any timing-dependent component.

Strong rival: process features mainly encode hospital workflow/documentation. A same-hospital permutation preserves site-specific analyte availability and recording density while breaking patient linkage; if it retains the held-out process increment or its site degradation, documentation is a credible explanation. Ordinary severity/case-mix and downstream workflow are additional rivals. This design tests descriptive prediction patterns, not causality.

Primary hypothesis: among adult first ICU admissions alive and under observation at 6 hours, the original value-plus-process model (VP-GBM) has larger internal-to-hospital-held-out deterioration than the capacity-matched value-only model (V-GBM), and that contrast is attenuated by a same-hospital process permutation (NC-GBM) or a cyclic within-window time shift (TS-GBM). Support requires the prespecified primary contrast and a coherent control pattern; a small AUROC gain alone is insufficient.

Clinical importance: evidence of workflow dependence would support value-only inputs, harmonized feeds, site validation, or recalibration before deployment. Stable process transport would support auditing rather than assuming testing causes outcomes. No result authorizes more testing, claims clinical benefit, or identifies a mechanism.

## Features

Inside each outer training partition, define analyte as normalized `labname` + `labmeasurenamesystem`, falling back to interface when system is blank. Select the 30 analytes with the largest distinct eligible-patient coverage, subject to >=1% coverage, using training hospitals only; report fewer if fewer qualify. For each analyte, use latest valid value and original offset in [0,360], with no carry-forward. Value inputs are age, gender, ethnicity, admissionweight, and training-fold median-imputed latest values. Process inputs are per-analyte presence, total valid-row count, distinct-offset count, first and last result offsets, plus overall valid-row and distinct-offset counts. Process inputs are proxies for observed recording intensity, not confirmed orders.

## Negative-control and time-shift checks

These are secondary, pre-outcome checks and do not change the primary cohort or estimand.

NC-GBM: within each fitting or scoring partition and hospital, sort stays by SHA-256(`"eicu-process-null-v1\\0"+patientunitstayid`) and cyclically assign each stay the complete original process vector of the next stay in that hospital. Keep original values and outcome; use no outcome in the permutation. This preserves the hospital’s process distribution while breaking patient-to-process linkage. If a one-stay hospital occurs, omit it from NC-only summaries and report it (the primary >=100-stay rule should prevent it).

TS-GBM: for each retained lab row, replace only its process offset by `t_shift=t-180` if `t>=180`, otherwise `t_shift=t+180`. This cyclically preserves [0,360], analyte presence, row count, and distinct-offset count while displacing first/last timing. Keep original patient linkage, lab values, and value-vector latest offset. TS-GBM uses values plus shifted process features. An unchanged TS result suggests intensity/recording density rather than time alignment; attenuation after TS plus attenuation after NC is more consistent with patient-linked, time-sensitive information. These patterns remain observational and cannot prove mechanism.

P-GBM is process-only and tests whether process inputs alone transport. An optional [720,1080] descriptive window is allowed only for stays with that window observed; it cannot alter or rescue the primary experiment.

## Models, split, outcomes, and uncertainty

V-Logit is the transparent elastic-net logistic baseline. V-GBM is fixed histogram gradient boosting on values. VP-GBM is the identical learner and settings with values plus original process features and is the primary learned comparison. P-GBM, NC-GBM, and TS-GBM use the identical GBM with process-only, permuted-process-plus-values, and shifted-process-plus-values inputs. Preprocessing, analyte selection, categories, imputation, class policy, seed, and hyperparameters are learned/frozen within outer training folds. The learned model tests nonlinear value-process interactions; the simple baseline is retained for interpretability. A temporal neural model is deferred because these controls directly address the unresolved interpretation and no evidence yet shows summary timing is inadequate; revisit only after a bounded diagnostic demonstrates that limitation.

Hold out hospitals, not patients. Sort hospital IDs by SHA-256(`"eicu-transport-v1\\0"+hospitalid`) and greedily balance eligible-stay counts into five contiguous folds. Fit on four folds and predict held-out hospitals plus a fixed 20% patient-stratified internal sample from the training hospitals, assigned by SHA-256(`"eicu-internal-v1\\0"+patientunitstayid`). No patient split is external validation.

Report AUROC (primary discrimination), AUPRC, Brier score (primary probability accuracy), calibration intercept/slope and calibration-in-the-large, and decision-curve net benefit at 5%, 10%, 20%, against treat-none/treat-all. The modeled action is senior review/goals-of-care and monitoring review, not treatment. Report per-hospital metrics, event prevalence, fold counts, and effective sample sizes.

For model m, define `I_m,e = AUROC(m,e)-AUROC(V-GBM,e)`; for Brier and absolute calibration-slope error orient signs so positive means worse error. Preserve the parent primary estimand:
`D_AUC=(AUC_VP,internal-AUC_VP,external)-(AUC_V,internal-AUC_V,external)`.
Define `C_NC=I_VP,external-I_NC,external` and `C_TS=I_VP,external-I_TS,external), with internal analogues, direct held-out differences, and analogous calibration/utility contrasts. Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples with replacement, identical indices across paired models, for 95% intervals. Do not use patient bootstrap as the sole uncertainty estimate.

## Interpretation and falsification

Patient-linked support requires the lower 95% CI for `D_AUC` >0, an original VP process increment or prespecified calibration/utility improvement, NC attenuation toward V (`C_NC>0) with CI excluding zero), and TS attenuation for any claimed timing component (`C_TS>0) with CI excluding zero). This supports a descriptive patient-linked interpretation only.

Documentation/workflow support is a positive `D_AUC` with NC retaining a similar held-out process increment or degradation, particularly if V is stable; or an unchanged TS process increment while P-GBM shows the same site sensitivity. This means hospital-preserved recording intensity/workflow is sufficient to explain much of the observed pattern, not that documentation caused error.

The hypothesis is adverse if the upper CI for `D_AUC` is below zero and VP is no worse in held-out calibration or net benefit, with controls not showing a hidden penalty. Results are mixed when controls disagree, and inconclusive when intervals span zero, hospitals/events are inadequate, analyte selection is unstable, or transformations fail technical checks. AUROC alone cannot confirm the claim.

## Limits, alternatives, and compute

The observational design cannot separate case mix, severity, treatment, workflow, interface completeness, discharge selection, and label differences fully. The lab table cannot distinguish not ordered from ordered-but-not-recorded and lacks collection/order/interface audit. Six-hour conditioning can induce selection; `actualicumortality` has no usable timestamp. Clinical adjudication is needed for individual omissions, interface failures, end-of-life decisions, and alert impact. Prospective silent deployment and an impact study are required before use.

CPU is appropriate for gzip scanning, tabular extraction, six model fits across five folds, and cluster bootstrap. Planning estimate (not measured): 16 CPUs, 64–128 GiB RAM, 2–6 hours, within the configured 16-CPU/256-GiB/8-hour solver-planning envelope. A readiness job should measure one fold and feature extraction; no GPU is required. This is a task-specific study estimate, not proposer/solver training.
