# Multi-Organ Recovery Trajectory Hypothesis

## Scientific Question

After initial stabilization in critical illness, patients whose recovery trajectories across multiple organ systems show **asynchronous patterns** have worse outcomes than those with **synchronous recovery**.

This hypothesis extends the fundamental insight from [K1] that WBC-platelet co-regulation defines universal inflammatory recovery trajectories, to a multi-organ framework. While Foy et al. demonstrated that exponential WBC decay followed by delayed linear PLT growth characterizes favorable inflammatory recovery, this proposal tests whether this temporal coordination principle generalizes across multiple organ systems, and whether deviations from synchronous recovery (asynchronous patterns) predict adverse outcomes.

## Clinical Importance

The resolution of this hypothesis has direct clinical implications:

1. **Early Risk Stratification**: Identifying patients with asynchronous organ recovery trajectories could enable earlier intervention before deterioration becomes irreversible.

2. **Precision Risk Models**: Moving beyond aggregate organ scores (SOFA, APACHE) to trajectory-based risk assessment that captures dynamic recovery patterns.

3. **Resource Optimization**: Identifying high-risk patients could optimize ICU resource allocation and monitoring intensity.

4. **Mechanistic Insight**: Understanding whether recovery asynchrony is a universal feature of critical illness or specific to certain populations could inform targeted interventions.

## Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis**: After circulatory stabilization in the ICU, patients exhibiting greater asynchrony in multi-organ recovery trajectories (measured by the Recovery Asynchrony Index, RAI) are more likely to experience in-hospital mortality, independent of traditional severity scores (APACHE II, SOFA).

**Core Prediction**: Higher RAI values (greater temporal discordance between organ system recovery trajectories) predict increased mortality risk after adjusting for baseline severity, demographics, and organ support intensity.

## Study Population and Main Data

**Population**: Adult ICU patients in MIMIC-IV with:
- Admission with infection or shock (ICD-10 codes for sepsis, septic shock, pneumonia, etc.)
- At least 3 daily laboratory measurements for at least 3 SOFA components
- At least 5 daily vital sign measurements
- ICU length of stay ≥ 3 days

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Patients with active major bleeding
- Patients with known pre-existing end-stage organ failure (e.g., dialysis-dependent CKD, cirrhosis with variceal bleeding)
- Pregnancy

**Main Data Tables**:
- ICU_CHARTFLAGS: subject_id, hadm_id, ICU_CHARTFLAGS (ICU admission identification)
- LOSG: subject_id, hadm_id, LOSG (length of stay, discharge status)
- ICUEVENTS: subject_id, hadm_id, ICUEVENTS (ICU events including transfusions)
- ICUVITALS: subject_id, hadm_id, ICUEVENTS (hourly vitals including BP, HR, SpO₂)
- LABVALUES: subject_id, hadm_id, ICUEVENTS (hemoglobin, lactate, pH, creatinine, bilirubin, platelets)
- DIAGNOSES_ICD: subject_id, hadm_id, DIAGNOSES_ICD (admission diagnoses)
- PROCEDURES_ICD: subject_id, hadm_id, PROCEDURES_ICD (procedures to rule out major bleeding)
- PATIENTS: subject_id, AGE, SEX, RACE (demographics)

## Recovery Asynchrony Index (RAI) Definition

The RAI is a weighted Euclidean distance metric that quantifies temporal discordance between organ system recovery trajectories:

RAI = √[Σᵢ wᵢ · (Rᵢ,ₜ - μᵢ,ₜ)² / σᵢ²]

Where:
- Rᵢ,ₜ = normalized recovery trajectory for organ system i at time t
- μᵢ,ₜ = mean trajectory across all patients at time t
- σᵢ² = variance of trajectories at time t
- wᵢ = weight for organ system i (based on clinical importance)

**Organ Systems**:
- Kidneys (creatinine): w = 0.25
- Lungs (PaO₂/FiO₂): w = 0.25
- Heart (MAP): w = 0.25
- Liver (bilirubin): w = 0.2
- Coagulation (platelets): w = 0.05

**Temporal Window**: RAI computed from ICU admission to 72 hours after circulatory stabilization (defined as SOFA score ≤ 2 for at least 24 consecutive hours).

## Primary Outcome

30-day all-cause mortality (from LOSG table: hospital_expire_flag = 1)

## Baseline Approach

**Baseline Model**: Cox proportional hazards regression predicting 30-day mortality:

h(t) = h₀(t) · exp(β₁·RAI + β₂·SOFA + β₃·Age + ... + ε)

**Baseline Assumption**: RAI has similar hazard ratios after adjusting for traditional severity scores (SOFA, APACHE II).

**Baseline Prediction**: RAI adds little to traditional severity scores in predicting mortality.

## Substantive Learned Alternative

**Mechanistic Model**: Hierarchical mixed-effects Cox model with:

log(h(t)) = γ₀ + γ₁·RAI + γ₂·SOFA + γ₃·(RAI × SOFA) + u_icu + εᵢⱼ

**Key Parameters**:
- γ₁: Main effect of RAI on mortality
- γ₂: Main effect of SOFA on mortality
- γ₃: Interaction between RAI and SOFA (tests whether RAI effect is modified by severity)
- u_icu: Random intercept for ICU (accounts for ICU-specific effects)

**What Learned Model Reveals That Baseline Cannot**:
1. **Non-linear RAI effects**: RAI may have non-linear relationship with mortality
2. **ICU-specific effects**: Different ICUs may have different baseline RAI distributions
3. **Interaction heterogeneity**: RAI effects may vary by SOFA strata
4. **Individual-level uncertainty**: Random effects provide personalized risk estimates

## Analysis Plan

### Phase 1: Phenotype Validation
- Compute RAI for all eligible patients
- Validate RAI temporal dynamics (RAI should decrease over time in recovering patients)
- Check distribution of RAI by outcome (mortality vs. survival)
- Verify temporal consistency (RAI measured at different time points)

### Phase 2: Unadjusted Analysis
- Descriptive statistics by RAI quartiles
- Unadjusted RAI-mortality associations
- Distribution of RAI by baseline characteristics

### Phase 3: Adjusted Analysis
- Cox regression with baseline covariates:
  - Age, sex, race
  - Admission SOFA score
  - Admission APACHE II score
  - Admission lactate
  - Admission hemoglobin
  - Renal function (creatinine)
  - Hemodynamic status (vasopressor use)
  - Admission diagnosis categories
  - Treatment intensity (ventilation, dialysis, etc.)

### Phase 4: Interaction Analysis
- Test γ₃ (RAI × SOFA) significance
- Stratified analysis by SOFA quartile
- Subgroup analysis by:
  - Admission diagnosis (sepsis, trauma, cardiac, etc.)
  - Age (<65, ≥65)
  - Comorbidity burden (low, moderate, high)
  - Baseline RAI (low, moderate, high)

### Phase 5: External Validation
- Split-sample validation (70% training, 30% test)
- Temporal validation (2018 vs. 2020)
- If possible, comparison with external dataset

## Falsification Criteria

**Hypothesis Falsified If**:
1. γ₁ = 0 (p > 0.05) after adjustment for multiple testing
2. RAI has similar hazard ratios to SOFA after adjustment
3. Negative control outcomes show RAI effect (suggests measurement error)
4. Negative control exposures show no RAI effect (suggests confounding)

**Hypothesis Supported If**:
1. γ₁ < 0 and significantly different from 0 (p < 0.05)
2. RAI has independent predictive value after adjusting for SOFA
3. Negative controls support causal interpretation
4. Results robust to multiple analytic approaches

**Hypothesis Inconclusive If**:
1. γ₁ non-significant but descriptive analysis shows difference
2. Results vary substantially by analytic method
3. Negative controls inconclusive
4. Sample size insufficient for precise estimation
