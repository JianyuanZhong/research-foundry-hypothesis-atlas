# Residual instability before ICU-to-ward transfer: temporal and workflow transport stress test

Status: proposed substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. No predictor model has been fit and no trajectory hypothesis result is claimed. This child preserves the frozen 49,157-transfer population, `t0`, final-24-hour inputs, strict decision-time availability, raw 48-hour endpoint, endpoint adjudication, pooled subject split, S0/T1 primary estimand, first-transition extension, source bindings, and three inspected references. ## Scientific opening, evidence boundary, and advance

The parent can establish internal held-out prognosis and adjudicated first-transition prioritization, but its pooled random subject split mixes care eras. That design can hide a model that ranks patients by era-specific charting, oxygen documentation, transfer thresholds, or destination workflow while appearing internally stable.

The strongest inspected evidence supports only bounded premises. A longitudinal model predicted seven-day ICU readmission/death in MIMIC-IV and an external hospital, but its authors explicitly identified operational factors, DNR status, and distinct composite components as limitations [K1]. A systematic review found advantages for longitudinal predictors but substantial variation in populations, windows, and outcomes [K2]. EHR measurement timing and frequency encode healthcare process as well as patient state [K3]. MIMIC documentation further establishes that raw shifted dates are not comparable across subjects and that `anchor_year` plus `anchor_year_group` can align a stay to an approximate real-year interval.

The strongest claim currently supported is therefore feasibility: the exact frozen cohort spans informative approximate eras and has adequate event support for locked late-era tests. Neither the audit nor the literature establishes that T1 transports, that its signal is physiologic, or that monitoring changes outcomes.

**Frozen primary hypothesis and estimand.** In the fixed adult first ICU-to-general-ward cohort and strict decision-time information set, T1's prespecified final-24-hour value-trajectory features improve held-out raw 48-hour three-state prediction over S0. The pooled primary estimand remains paired test-set `Brier(S0)-Brier(T1)` on the frozen seed-20260923 60/20/20 subject split.

**New falsifiable transport hypothesis.** When S0 and T1 are fitted only in strictly early-era transfers, selected and calibrated only in a middle era, and then frozen, T1 retains incremental raw 48-hour and adjudicated R+D first-transition information in both 2017–2019 and 2020–2022 approximate-era tests. The gain should not be confined to a destination family, sparse/late charting stratum, valid-live-discharge prediction, or the observation-process stream.

**Leading claim versus rival.** The leading claim is that final-24-hour measured-value history contains durable patient-state information useful across practice drift. The strongest rival is workflow-specific prediction: changes in who is transferred, where they go, when and how often they are measured, oxygen/device documentation, treatment limitations, and opportunities for ICU return generate the apparent gain. A frozen model that retains value-specific gain across later eras and structured workflow strata is compatible with durable patient-state information. A gain that collapses, reverses, or is reproduced by process-only inputs is more compatible with era/workflow specificity. Neither pattern identifies physiology causally.

**Clinical advance.** The result determines whether this task merits external/prospective deployment evaluation or should remain a descriptive internal predictor tied to one historical workflow. It cannot show that delaying transfer, increasing monitoring, or retaining an ICU bed benefits patients.

## Calendar-era surrogate and locked temporal split

Raw shifted years are forbidden for cross-subject era comparison. For each transfer, parse `patients.anchor_year_group` as lower and upper real-year bounds and compute:

`offset_year = year(t0)-patients.anchor_year`

`possible_year_lo = max(2008, group_lo + offset_year)`

`possible_year_hi = min(2022, group_hi + offset_year)`.

The clipping bounds come from the configured MIMIC-IV source period, 2008–2022. Exact year within this interval remains unknown.

Assign a transfer to a temporal role only when its entire possible interval lies in one block:

- development: `possible_year_hi<=2013`;
- validation/model selection: `possible_year_lo>=2014 and possible_year_hi<=2016`;
- locked transport test: `possible_year_lo>=2017 and possible_year_hi<=2019`;
- locked severe-drift stress test: `possible_year_lo>=2020`;
- otherwise: `boundary_ambiguous`.

To prevent subject leakage, among strict-role rows assign each subject to the latest temporal role they enter and use only that subject's rows in that latest role; withhold their earlier strict-role rows from temporal fitting/evaluation. Boundary-ambiguous rows never enter temporal fitting/evaluation. They remain in the frozen pooled primary analysis and are described, not discarded from the scientific population.

The bounded exact-cohort audit found:

- development: 17,215 transfers, 15,008 subjects, 936 composites (752 returns, 201 deaths);
- validation: 8,859 transfers, 8,310 subjects, 436 composites (332 returns, 115 deaths);
- 2017–2019 transport: 6,435 transfers, 6,017 subjects, 341 composites (240 returns, 109 deaths);
- 2020–2022 stress: 5,285 transfers, 4,850 subjects, 288 composites (207 returns, 91 deaths);
- 10,323 boundary-ambiguous transfers and 1,040 earlier strict-role rows withheld for subject isolation.

Component counts overlap when return and death both occur. These are support counts, not model results.

No model is updated, recalibrated, thresholded, or selected after either late-era outcome set is opened. The 2017–2019 test is opened first; the 2020–2022 stress set is opened only after the first test outputs and conclusion status are frozen.

**Observation-process strata:**

- sparse/other: every other transfer;
- high availability lag: transfer-level median nonnegative `storetime-charttime` among selected chart rows exceeds the development-set 75th percentile; standard lag otherwise; negative lags are counted and reported, not silently converted;
- process-risk quintile: quintiles of the early-development O1 prediction, frozen in development. O1 uses static transfer context, masks, counts, recency, store-lag distributions, and caregiver counts but no measured values.

Also report destination family × dense/sparse, transfer day/night (19:00–06:59 is night), source ICU, exact destination, and exact latest `services.curr_service`. Formal cell claims require at least 500 transfers and 50 R+D events in the evaluated late-era union; smaller cells are descriptive and automatically inconclusive. Ward staffing, bed occupancy, monitoring technology, rapid-response availability, and clinician transfer intent are unavailable and cannot be inferred from these proxies.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative at matched specificity

### Simple temporal baseline

Refit the frozen ridge multinomial S0-E and T1-E from scratch using only clean development rows. Fit imputation, scaling, splines, penalties, device dictionaries, and every preprocessing object in development; choose penalty and perform multinomial temperature calibration only in 2014–2016 validation. Do not include era or role as a predictor. Evaluate the unchanged models on both locked late-era sets.

Repeat the parent's M0/M1 six-hour pooled multinomial first-transition models on the same temporal roles, valid-live-discharge rule, endpoint sensitivities, and S0/T1 inputs. This preserves the raw S0/T1 primary estimand while asking whether any transport gain concerns R+D rather than discharge workflow.

This baseline directly measures transport of prespecified interpretable summaries. It loses nonlinear cross-channel ordering and cannot cleanly separate value-history from process-history representations.

### Learned two-stream temporal alternative

Fit the parent's compact two-stream causal TCN L2-E on the identical twelve two-hour bins, raw three-state target, development/validation/late-era roles, static inputs, and calibration protocol:

- value stream: standardized observed and forward-filled values, no masks or gaps;
- process stream: masks, time since last measurement, counts, decision-time store-lag summaries, and caregiver counts, no measured values;
- static stream: exactly S0 context;
- separate two-layer causal convolutions, kernel 3, dilations 1 and 2, 32/64 channels, dropout 0/0.2; concatenate streams before the three-class head;
- Adam learning rate 1e-3 or 3e-4, five fixed seeds, validation-log-loss early stopping, validation-only temperature scaling.

Mandatory frozen heads/ablations are full L2-E, value+static, process+static, and static only. Repeat the within-person pre-endpoint value-bin order permutation with the final bin fixed, and the patient-level value-sequence permutation within destination, S0-E risk decile, and development-frozen density quintile.

L2-E can reveal nonlinear relapse, ordering, and separate value/process contributions that T1-E summaries lose. It is selected only if it is calibrated and improves on T1-E in both locked late-era sets; otherwise the simpler model is preferred. A domain-adversarial or latent-physiology model is deferred: without external sensor, staffing, or policy data, making an era representation hard to decode would not identify physiology and could erase legitimate case-mix information. Revisit it only with an independently observed environment/policy change or external site.

A validation-only recalibration sensitivity separates correctable prevalence/calibration shift from ranking/information loss. Recalibration on either late-era set is prohibited in the primary analysis and, if shown after completion, is explicitly post-deployment adaptation rather than transport.

## Falsification and result meanings

Report the frozen pooled statuses first. Never use a late-era subgroup or learned model to rescue an adverse pooled S0/T1 result.

**Durable incremental trajectory information supported:** `Delta_fixed_e>0` with paired 95% interval above zero in both locked late-era sets; T1-E calibration slope is 0.8–1.2 in both after only middle-era calibration; and neither endpoint-adjudication sensitivity reverses the R+D direction. Clinical materiality is a separate label: it requires point `Delta_fixed_e>=0.002` in both late-era sets and positive lower bounds.

**Physiology-compatible value-information pattern supported, bounded:** durable incremental support above; full L2-E versus process+static has a positive paired interval in both late eras; patient-level value permutation removes at least half of that point gain in both; and no supported destination × density cell has a negative interval. This supports transportable measured-value information beyond recorded observation process. It does not establish biology, mechanism, or treatment benefit.

**Workflow/era-specific prediction dominant:** the frozen pooled analysis is supportive but both late-era upper bounds exclude a 0.002 T1-E gain, or the full model has no positive gain over process+static in either late era, or supported destination/density cells show material sign reversal. This argues against an unqualified deployment claim; it does not prove that physiology is irrelevant.

**Simple summaries adequate:** T1-E transports and L2-E's 95% upper bound excludes an additional 0.002 Brier gain in both late eras, with no extra capture above 2 R+D events per 1,000 at every quota. Prefer T1-E.

**Adverse:** T1-E worsens late-era Brier or R+D IBS with an interval excluding zero, calibration fails materially after frozen middle-era calibration, or endpoint sensitivities reverse the conclusion. This falsifies transport of the fitted predictor, not residual-instability biology.

**Inconclusive:** intervals include benefit and harm/materiality bounds; either late era has fewer than 200 R+D events after required rules; calibration fails without clear discrimination loss; learned seeds are unstable; endpoint sensitivities disagree; or workflow cells lack support. Sparse destination cells remain descriptive. An imprecise null is not falsification.

Support permits only the claim that a historical single-center value-trajectory signal transported across approximate MIMIC practice eras and measured workflow strata. Actual external transportability requires another institution; clinical usefulness requires workflow-specific capacity/cost evaluation; intervention benefit requires a prospective or quasi-experimental study; physiology and plannedness require expert adjudication and additional measurements.
