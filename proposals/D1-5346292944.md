# Hypotension duration, observed renal deterioration, and competing discharge/death: an eICU transportability successor

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

Among adults in a first ICU stay with adequate time-stamped MAP observation and a pre-landmark creatinine, does the duration-weighted burden of MAP below 65 mmHg during the preceding six hours identify a higher 24-hour cumulative incidence of a creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal beyond minimum MAP and pre-window clinical state, and does that association reproduce in hospitals not used for fitting?

The primary hypothesis is that, within prespecified minimum-MAP strata, greater low-MAP minutes has a positive, non-zero standardized risk difference for the first observed renal deterioration signal before 24 hours after accounting for baseline physiology and the observation process. The secondary hypothesis is that a time-ordered trajectory model will reveal whether recovery shape and observation timing contain information beyond the transparent minimum-plus-duration summary; it must reproduce in held-out hospitals to count as scientific evidence.

This is an observational risk-marker study. It does not estimate the causal effect of permitting hypotension, administering a pressor, or targeting MAP 65. Available evidence supports only that eICU contains dense offset-based MAP measurements, creatinine-labelled laboratory rows, ICU discharge/death fields and hospital identifiers. The preceding audit reported 200,859 patient rows, 85,658 adult first-stay candidates meeting the 36-hour screen, 146,671,642 periodic-vital rows with 28,036,994 valid MAP rows, and 39,132,531 laboratory rows. These are feasibility facts, not an effect estimate. The unresolved claim is whether duration adds reproducible information about a subsequent observed renal deterioration signal after separating it from minimum MAP, monitoring intensity, discharge and death.

If robust, this could motivate prospective duration-aware hemodynamic surveillance and kidney-protection research. It cannot justify a MAP threshold or treatment policy. The advance over the parent is an explicit competing-risk and observation-process estimand: the parent’s documented pressor/ventilation composite can be driven by clinician documentation and does not directly represent organ injury. This child uses the closer organ signal while refusing to call it adjudicated AKI.

## Population, landmarks and exposure

Retain the first ICU stay per person: age >=18 (map >89 to 90 and report other nonnumeric ages), unitvisitnumber=1, nonmissing hospital, numeric unitdischargeoffset, and at least 36 hours from unit admission to discharge. If one uniquepid has conflicting first-visit rows, keep the earliest valid admission and report the conflict.

Candidate landmarks are t = 12, 18, ..., 72 hours after the ICU-relative zero. Retain t only when at least six hours precede it, at least 24 hours remain before recorded unit discharge, and EOL offsets do not precede t. Use all eligible landmarks with patient-clustered inference; one fixed-seed eligible landmark per stay is a sensitivity analysis. No row with a clinical/result timestamp after t may enter a predictor.

For valid numeric vitalPeriodic.systemicmean in [-50,300] mmHg, sort by observationoffset. Each observation represents the following min(5, next_offset-current_offset) minutes, with no carry-forward over gaps and no interval past t. In [t-360,t), calculate covered minutes, low_map_minutes for MAP <65, low_map_fraction, minimum MAP, mean/SD, last MAP and coverage. Require >=80% coverage; repeat 70% and 90%. Never infer hypotension through a gap. Repeat MAP thresholds 60 and 70, use aperiodic MAP separately, and report episodes descriptively while using minutes as the primary exposure.

## Renal signal, competing events and estimand

Baseline creatinine is the last numeric retained creatinine with labresultoffset <= t in [t-48h,t] and labresultrevisedoffset <= t. Require baseline >0 and a plausible range fixed after the label audit. The primary future signal is the first future retained creatinine in (t,t+24h] that is >= baseline + 0.3 or >=1.5 times baseline on a demonstrably comparable source scale. A secondary analysis uses the last pre-t baseline. Later values do not create additional events.

The event process is ordered and mutually exclusive:

1. Renal deterioration signal before ICU discharge/death and within 24 hours.
2. ICU death before a renal signal.
3. Alive ICU discharge/transfer before a renal signal.
4. No renal signal by 24 hours while still in ICU and with a future creatinine observation.
5. Censoring at 24 hours, discharge or death when no future creatinine is observed.

Death and discharge are competing events, not negatives. Item 5 is not assigned a normal-creatinine outcome. A separate surveillance analysis reports the probability and timing of any future creatinine observation, distinguishing organ signal from documentation intensity. The primary renal cumulative-incidence analysis passes an observation model based on pre-t covariates and early-window observation history, with inverse-probability weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles. Complete-case and unweighted cause-specific analyses are sensitivity analyses. If the lab audit cannot establish a comparable scale, the renal analysis is infeasible/inconclusive rather than being relabelled AKI.

The estimand is the standardized 24-hour cumulative-incidence risk difference for event 1 comparing >60 versus 0-60 low-MAP minutes within minimum-MAP strata, under the observed eligible-landmark distribution, with event 2 and event 3 as competing risks and observation censoring handled as specified. Cause-specific and subdistribution hazard summaries are secondary. The transport estimand compares calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, standardized risk difference and burden-by-minimum-MAP interaction in untouched hospitals with >=200 eligible landmarks and both exposure/event levels.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The transparent baseline is a regularized multinomial competing-risk pooled-logistic model, M_min, with minimum MAP spline but no low-MAP duration, plus pre-window MAP mean/SD/last value/coverage, heart rate, respiration and SpO2 summaries/slopes, last pre-t creatinine and change, age, sex, ethnicity, time since admission, admission-only Apache variables/scores, current documented ventilation and pressor-record indicators, and missingness indicators. Hospital is a stratification variable, not a feature. M_duration adds low-MAP minutes/fraction and its prespecified interaction with minimum MAP. Both produce CIFs, standardized risks, competing-event probabilities, calibration, Brier score and decision curves at thresholds frozen before fitting. M_min versus M_duration is the primary incremental-information test.

The substantive alternative is a small six-bin temporal convolution or GRU, T_shape, over the same six pre-t hours, using hourly MAP summaries/masks, heart rate, respiration, SpO2, creatinine observation/value history, pressor/ventilation record history, admission covariates, and explicit missingness/observation masks. Fit duration-free T_min and full-trajectory T_shape to the same competing-risk/observation-aware target. It can reveal transient-versus-sustained hypotension, recovery slope, ordering of MAP and creatinine observations, and irregular gaps that minimum plus total duration loses. It is scientifically useful only if its shape findings improve calibration or reproduce a prespecified effect pattern in held-out hospitals; AUROC alone is not clinical value.

Fit on patients from training hospitals, with patient-level train/validation separation, early stopping and frozen preprocessing. Evaluate untouched hospitals; no hospital ID predictor. Use three learned seeds and patient-within-hospital bootstrap intervals. Budget under inputs.json: two CPUs, <=8 GiB; 20-40 minutes extraction, <15 minutes per regularized model and 45-90 minutes for two temporal variants. GPU is deferred because it does not resolve a different scientific uncertainty.

## Transport, uncertainty and leakage controls

Use hospital-held-out evaluation with a recorded seed, without ranking hospitals by outcome. Patients never cross fit/validation/test partitions. Hospital metadata is descriptive/stratifying only. Primary intervals use patient-resampled, hospital-stratified bootstrap; report the number and size of contributing sites. Repeat one-landmark-per-stay and cluster all landmarks by patient.

Every predictor requires timestamp <=t; for labs, revised timestamp must also be <=t. Future creatinine is outcome-only. Exclude landmarks within 12 hours of discharge/transfer in sensitivity analysis, and report death/discharge and future-creatinine observation rates by hospital and exposure group. Do not condition on a future test without reporting the observation model.

## Falsification and interpretation

Gate interpretation on exact retained labels/interfaces, plausible values, comparable units, baseline/future support, both exposure levels within MAP strata, and site support. Repeat MAP source, thresholds, coverage and burden cut points. Permute the order of pre-t MAP observations while preserving values, counts and coverage; duration-shape association should attenuate. Use a pre-landmark placebo creatinine-rise rule in [t-24h,t), using only pre-t values; a similarly strong placebo indicates reverse-time severity or documentation bias. Report future-lab observation as a process outcome, not as a false negative control. Exclude EOL proxies, near-discharge landmarks, abrupt loss of MAP coverage and implausible creatinine jumps. Compare observation-weighted, complete-case and unweighted estimates with weight diagnostics. Add pressor/ventilation status only as sensitivity adjustment. Refit the parent monitoring-gap comparator on this identical cohort/outcome; duration is not superior from AUROC alone.

Supportive results require a positive duration contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero, adequate precision, persistence across source/threshold/coverage and weighting checks, weak placebo association, and either a reproducible duration effect or prespecified recovery-shape pattern in held-out hospitals. This supports an observational renal-risk marker and prospective monitoring research, not a treatment target.

Adverse results are a precise null/negative contrast, disappearance after permutation or observation adjustment, a strong placebo, domination by discharge/death differences, or failure in held-out hospitals; these support minimum MAP, illness severity or documentation selection as the reproducible signal. Inconclusive results include non-comparable units/labels, too few paired values, inadequate MAP coverage, absent exposure contrast, unstable weights, too few supported hospitals or wide intervals. No clinical recommendation follows.

Computationally checkable: snapshot/file hashes, filters/joins, MAP arithmetic, label/value audit, timestamp safety, event classification, fitted parameters, CIFs, standardized risks, uncertainty, calibration/Brier/decision metrics, split integrity, site transport and falsification outputs. Not established: true perfusion, adjudicated KDIGO AKI, urine output, pre-ICU creatinine, clinician intent, true pressor dose/stop time, fluid balance, treatment limitation beyond the EOL proxy, reasons for missing tests, or benefit from acting on a warning. These require adjudication, richer data or a prospective study.
