## Opening, strongest supported claim, and unresolved hypothesis

The strongest evidence-supported claim is modest. ICU hypotension has often been associated with AKI, but definitions are heterogeneous and meta-analysis did not confirm the aggregate AKI association [K1]. In 423 severe-sepsis patients, FINNAKI found lower first-day time-adjusted MAP associated with five-day AKI progression using prospectively validated 2/5-minute measurements aggregated to ten-minute medians [K2]. KDIGO defines AKI using creatinine and urine-output changes while warning that creatinine-only and incompletely monitored applications miss cases and do not establish etiology [K3].

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adult eICU patients represented by one deterministically selected eligible hospital encounter, alive and still in ICU at hour 24, with at least 202/288 valid first-day invasive-MAP bins, an admission creatinine, no creatinine-defined progression through hour 24, and a strictly later creatinine opportunity, greater duration-normalized time with MAP <65 mmHg carries higher held-hospital risk of subsequent creatinine-defined AKI beyond a flexible MAP-nadir model.

This is a conditional prognostic/descriptive hypothesis, not a causal perfusion or treatment effect. “Beyond nadir” means a flexible conditional contrast and nadir-stratified standardization, not randomization at a common nadir.

The leading explanation is that sustained low perfusion contains clinically relevant information lost by a transient nadir. The strongest rivals are illness severity, vasopressor response, selective invasive monitoring, MAP artifact, greater creatinine surveillance among sicker/hypotensive patients, length-of-stay opportunity, and renal deterioration already underway during exposure. The study distinguishes these only to the extent that measured patterns differ: a transportable burden signal should survive fixed-bin construction, day-1 progression exclusion, flexible nadir/severity adjustment, held-hospital testing, and fixed ascertainment windows; an observation or artifact signal should attenuate under the corresponding checks. Association after adjustment cannot identify renal hypoperfusion as mechanism.

Clinical importance is specific: robust incremental burden information would justify cumulative-hypotension summaries in monitoring research rather than relying on a single nadir. A precise adverse result would argue against promoting this noisy derived metric over nadir in this population. Neither result chooses MAP 65 as a treatment target or shows that raising MAP prevents AKI.

## Frozen population, clocks, exposure, and outcome

Start with adults age ≥18 (`> 89` mapped to 90), `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitstaytype='admit'`, and `unitdischargeoffset>1440`. Retain the smallest unit number/ID within `patienthealthsystemstayid`. Because no cross-encounter absolute date exists, select one eligible encounter per `uniquepid` by ascending MD5 of `"eicu-map-aki-index-20260924:"+patienthealthsystemstayid`, then encounter/stay ID. This happens before reading exposure or outcome support. The estimand applies to this reproducibly sampled eligible encounter population, not first-ever ICU admission.

Exposure is `observationoffset>=0 AND <1440`. Map every row to `floor(observationoffset/5)`, producing bins 0–287; within each stay/bin take median `systemicmean` after excluding values <20 or >180 mmHg. Require ≥202 nonempty bins. Do not interpolate. Flag triplet inconsistency (systolic < diastolic or MAP outside diastolic−5 to systolic+5), adjacent-bin change >40 mmHg, source multiplicity, and missingness. Primary nadir is minimum valid bin MAP. Primary burden is `24*n(MAP<65)/n(valid bins)`, equivalent observed hours. Secondary depth-duration is `sum(max(65-MAP,0))/n(valid)`. Prespecified timing features are 24 hourly burden fractions/nadirs/masks, longest exactly adjacent low run, episode count, and four six-hour burdens.

Creatinine uses numeric `lower(trim(labname))='creatinine'`, system unit mg/dL case-insensitively, range 0.1–20, collapsed by median at stay/result offset. `labresultoffset`, not revised offset, is biological time. Admission creatinine is the earliest valid value in `[-360,360]`. A person is excluded for day-1 progression if any value through minute 1,440 is ≥0.3 mg/dL above admission or ≥1.5 times admission. Minute 1,440 is pre-landmark.

The outcome window is strictly `(1440,min(10080,unitdischargeoffset)]`. Subsequent creatinine-defined AKI is (a) maximum creatinine through minute 4,320 at least 0.3 mg/dL above the last valid pre-landmark value, or (b) any value through minute 10,080 at least 1.5 times admission. Implement the general all-pairs 48-hour criterion as a sensitivity. Primary analysis conditions on at least one strictly later creatinine and reports this selection; patients without testing are not non-events. Urine-output AKI, dialysis initiation, injury etiology, and community-acquired AKI are not identified.

## Falsification and interpretation

Burden support requires both:

1. B1's standardized 75th-versus-25th burden risk difference is positive with a hospital-bootstrap 95% interval excluding zero after B0 adjustment; and
2. held-test `ΔLL=LL(B0)-LL(B1)` is positive with its 95% interval excluding zero and no material calibration deterioration.

Direction must persist under 60%/80% coverage, triplet/artifact exclusion, fixed 48-hour ascertainment, observation weighting with adequate positivity, and within-hospital analysis. T1 is separately supported only if it improves held-test log loss over B1 with a 95% interval excluding zero and nonconstant temporal information survives order/missingness/vasopressor ablations.

Adverse burden evidence is an upper 95% bound ≤1 absolute risk point for the standardized contrast together with no held-test log-loss gain, or a stable reverse association across hospitals and observation checks. This argues against clinically meaningful incremental burden information under this design; it does not prove hypotension harmless or nadir causal.

Inconclusive evidence includes intervals spanning both zero and +1 point; inadequate common support; calibration or transport failure; unstable hospital effects; observation-weight failure; material change under fixed-window/artifact rules; or T1 weights that reverse across validation/test. A positive complete-case result that disappears with observation controls is evidence for an ascertainment rival, not support. An imprecise null is not refutation.

A supportive result establishes a transportable adjusted association and incremental prognostic information conditional on the measured population/outcome opportunity. It does not show that hypotension caused AKI, that vasopressors helped or harmed, that 65 mmHg is optimal, or that intervention on burden changes outcomes. Stronger claims require pre-ICU renal baselines, timed urine output/RRT, fluids, cardiac output, nephrotoxins, delivered treatment validation, clinical AKI-cause adjudication, external validation, and ultimately a prospective intervention.
