# Substantive Child Proposal: Temporal Discordance Between Inflammatory and Functional Recovery After Acute Illness

## Scientific Question

**Among hospitalized patients with acute illness, does the degree of discordance between the rate of inflammatory burden decline and the rate of functional reserve recovery during hospitalization predict 30-day mortality better than either trajectory alone, and does this relationship differ by pre-illness physiological reserve?**

## Clinical Importance

1. **Prognostic Refinement**: Current risk scores (SOFA, APACHE II) assess individual organs or static measures. Understanding temporal discordance could improve mortality prediction beyond existing scores.

2. **Treatment Prioritization**: Patients with discordant trajectories (rapid inflammatory decline but slow functional recovery, or vice versa) may warrant intensified monitoring or targeted interventions.

3. **Biological Mechanisms**: The relationship between inflammatory and functional recovery may reveal biological pathways linking immune response to physical performance, informing future therapeutic strategies.

## Scientific Significance

This hypothesis advances understanding by:
- Testing whether the **rate of change** matters, not just baseline levels
- Examining whether inflammatory and functional recovery **diverge** in patients at highest risk
- Investigating whether pre-illness reserve modifies the inflammatory-functional recovery relationship

**Novelty**: No prior study has examined temporal discordance between inflammatory and functional recovery trajectories during acute hospitalization.

## Methodology

### Population
- Participants with acute hospitalization (ICD-10 codes indicating acute illness)
- Must have:
  - Inflammatory markers (columns 630-1001) measured at baseline and at least one follow-up
  - Functional measures (columns 87-3.33, 92-3.30) measured at baseline and at least one follow-up
  - Pre-illness grip strength in additional_exposures
  - 30-day mortality outcome in health_outcomes
- Exclude participants with missing key variables

### Variables

#### Inflammatory Trajectory:
- Baseline: Mean of inflammatory marker columns (630-1001) at instance 0.0
- Follow-up: Mean of inflammatory marker columns at earliest follow-up instance > 0.0
- Rate of decline: (Baseline - Follow-up) / time_interval (days)

#### Functional Trajectory:
- Baseline: Mean of functional measure columns (87-3.33, 92-3.30) at instance 0.0
- Follow-up: Mean of functional measure columns at earliest follow-up instance > 0.0
- Rate of recovery: (Follow-up - Baseline) / time_interval (days)

#### Discordance Metric:
- Absolute difference between inflammatory decline rate and functional recovery rate
- Or: (Inflammatory decline rate) - (Functional recovery rate)

#### Pre-illness Reserve:
- Grip strength from additional_exposures
- Categorize as low (< median) or high (≥ median)

#### Outcome:
- 30-day mortality: 1 if died within 30 days of hospitalization, 0 otherwise

### Statistical Analysis

#### Primary Analysis:
- Cox proportional hazards regression:
  - Hazard ratio for 30-day mortality
  - Covariates: Discordance metric, pre-illness reserve, age, sex, baseline comorbidity burden
  - Compare models:
    1. Baseline inflammatory burden only
    2. Baseline functional reserve only
    3. Discordance metric (primary hypothesis test)
    4. Both baseline measures + discordance metric
    5. Discordance metric + pre-illness reserve (effect modification)

#### Secondary Analysis:
- Gradient boosting classifier for mortality prediction
- Compare AUC and calibration between:
  - Baseline inflammatory only
  - Baseline functional only
  - Discordance metric
  - Combined model

#### Falsification Criteria:
- **Supportive**: Discordance metric significantly improves mortality prediction beyond baseline measures (p < 0.05 for interaction term, higher AUC for combined model)
- **Adverse**: Discordance metric does not add predictive value (p > 0.05, no improvement in AUC)
- **Inconclusive**: Discordance metric shows some association but effect size is small (0.01-0.05 AUC improvement)

## Limitations

1. **UK Biobank limitations**:
   - Instance-based timestamps (not exact dates)
   - ICD-10 to organ system mapping needs verification
   - Grip strength alone may be incomplete frailty measure

2. **Data limitations**:
   - Not all participants have both inflammatory and functional measures at follow-up
   - Selection bias in participants with repeated measurements
   - Reverse causation (illness affecting both inflammation and function)

3. **Generalizability**:
   - UK Biobank participants are generally healthier than typical ICU populations
   - Findings may not generalize to critically ill patients
