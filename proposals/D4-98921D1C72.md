Distinguishing these explanations is a consequential robustness question even if the parent predictive gain is small.

## Robustness panel for attendance and repeat-measurement artifacts

The robustness question is tested only after the primary model is computed and all gates are prespecified. Every comparison uses paired held-out predictions, identical capacity definitions, and the same primary five-fold assignment where the outcome is unchanged.

1. **Attendance diagnostic and selection sensitivity.** Fit a five-fold cross-fitted observation model on `B` for `A` using only baseline `[F, age, eGFRcr_0, eGFRcys_0]` and four baseline missingness indicators that are deterministically zero within `B` but are retained only if the source parser identifies a field-specific missing state before the complete-case restriction. Use the same training-fold spline preprocessing, logistic fit, probability clipping to `[.05,.95]`, normalized inverse-probability weights `w_i=(1/p_i)/mean(1/p_i)` on `R_complete`, weight clipping at the 1st and 99th training-fold percentiles, and effective sample size `ESS=(sum w_i)^2/sum(w_i^2)`. This is a sensitivity analysis only. If the observation model is nonfinite, has any fold with fewer than 20 attended participants or 20 non-attenders, or has ESS below 0.25 times the attended sample size, the weighted branch is `ROBUSTNESS_TECHNICAL_INCONCLUSIVE`, not adverse.

2. **Attendance-artifact contrast.** Evaluate primary M0 and M1 unweighted and with the frozen weights. Within five cross-fitted quintiles of `p_i` formed using training-fold cutpoints, report aggregate paired `DeltaE10` and `DeltaNB2`; do not rerank within quintiles. Define the attendance-gradient contrast as the weighted least-squares slope of fold-independent quintile-specific `DeltaE10` on quintile midpoint, with its 98% bootstrap interval. A gain is attendance-suspect if unweighted gain is positive but weighted gain has a point estimate below zero or its 98% interval crosses zero while the unweighted interval excludes zero, or if the absolute attendance-gradient slope exceeds 2 events per 10 percentage points per unit propensity and its 98% interval excludes zero. These rules are descriptive falsification criteria, not a claim that weighting identifies a target population.

3. **Timing and repeat-schedule artifact contrast.** Decompose global 10% selected sets and paired gains into `[2,4)`, `[4,5)`, and `[5,8]` without reranking inside bands. A schedule artifact is flagged if the primary M1 gain is positive only in one timing band and the other two bands have opposite point estimates with simultaneous intervals excluding zero, or if the direction reverses after the attendance weighting. A stable signal must not require a particular repeat interval. A band with fewer than 20 events is reported as `UNDERPOWERED`, not adverse.

4. **Cross-analyte endpoint symmetry.** Repeat the fixed model comparison for `Ycys` and `Yboth`. Define event-yield contrasts at 10% as `DeltaE10_cr`, `DeltaE10_cys`, and `DeltaE10_both`. Define the predeclared asymmetry `H = DeltaE10_cr - DeltaE10_cys`. A measurement-process concern is flagged if M1 is positive for `Ycys` but not `Ycr` with a simultaneous adverse or null `Ycr` contrast, or if `H` has a 98% interval entirely above 5 events per 10 percentage points, indicating endpoint-specific performance rather than cross-analyte reproducibility. Concordant positive results across `Ycr`, `Ycys`, and `Yboth` are consistent with, but do not prove, a biomarker-specific signal. Because both endpoints reuse related analytes, symmetry is a robustness check, not assay validation.

5. **Repeat-measurement nulls.** In addition to the primary baseline-eGFR-stratified Z permutation, permute Z within baseline eGFRcr deciles and sex, preserving all attendance and outcome fields; reconstruct folds and refit on each permutation. Run a marker-label swap in which the baseline cystatin-derived component is replaced by a within-stratum random permutation while baseline eGFRcr remains fixed. Run a reverse-label analysis, a predictor-null analysis, and a private elapsed-time leakage sentinel. Any replicated positive gain under these nulls falsifies the biomarker-specific interpretation. Null failure is technical inconclusive if the permutation count, folds, or refits are incomplete; it is not silently called adverse biology.

No branch uses repeat cystatin or repeat creatinine as a predictor. The cross-analyte panel changes only the prespecified outcome and baseline anchor as stated, and does not create a fixed-horizon estimand.

## Interpretation gates and falsification criteria

A certified primary result is **supportive of the bounded biomarker hypothesis** only if all of the following hold: source and certificate integrity pass; primary M0/M1 dimensions are exactly 9/10; calibration has no material M1 harm; DeltaNB(2%) is at least `.001` with lower 98% bound above zero; Delta event yield at 10% is at least `5` with lower 98% bound above zero; at least 8 of 10 repeats favor M1 on each axis; the contiguous 5–15% capacity contrasts are positive; weighted and unweighted results do not satisfy the attendance-artifact rule; no schedule-artifact rule is triggered; `Ycr`, `Ycys`, and `Yboth` do not show the endpoint-specific measurement concern; and no predictor-null, reverse-label, or leakage sentinel reproduces the gain. Even then, the conclusion is only reproducible incremental prioritization for a qualifying observed exported repeat.

A **biomarker-artifact adverse robustness result** requires certified reproducible evidence that the primary apparent gain is attendance-suspect, endpoint-specific, schedule-dependent, or null-replicated, with the relevant simultaneous interval and repeat-direction requirement met. This is an adverse result for the biomarker-specific interpretation, not proof that cystatin C is clinically invalid. A primary adverse result requires certified negative incremental evidence with an upper interval below zero and at least 8 of 10 adverse repeats, or certified material calibration harm.

A **scientific inconclusive** result is required when practical floors are not met, intervals cross zero, endpoint symmetry is underpowered, or robustness evidence conflicts. An **operational or technical inconclusive** result is required for certificate failure, source or schema failure, incomplete nulls, failed positivity or ESS, nonfinite computation, malformed parser, or unexplained diagnostic-anchor mismatch. Pre-certification stops are `NO_SCIENTIFIC_RESULT`; they are neither supportive nor adverse.

Supportive evidence cannot establish measured-GFR accuracy, chronicity, CKD, kidney failure, first clinical testing, outcomes among non-attenders, assay validity beyond the supplied certificate, causal testing benefit or harm, clinical threshold appropriateness, patient preferences, cost-effectiveness, treatment impact, or transportability. Those claims require clinical adjudication, assay and specimen metadata, repeated clinical assessments, linked care records, measured GFR, an intervention study, or another external cohort.
