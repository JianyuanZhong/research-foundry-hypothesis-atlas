# Dynamic hepatic tolerance after TACE and clinically focused short-term return in HCC

## Status and hypothesis

This is an episode-2 evolution of [prior hypothesis]. It is an observational, prognostic hypothesis, not a treatment recommendation.

**Hypothesis.** Among adults with HCC who are discharged after a first *observed* TACE episode, an early within-person post-procedure laboratory trajectory (0–72 hours versus the 14 days before TACE) identifies patients at higher risk of a clinically important subsequent inpatient return beyond baseline hepatic reserve, prior utilization, demographics, and recorded disease burden. The primary estimand is the incremental adjusted prognostic association of the trajectory block; it is not the causal effect of TACE or of a laboratory-triggered intervention.

The clinically focused secondary hypothesis is that the trajectory is more strongly associated with a later admission carrying a liver-decompensation diagnosis signal than with an arbitrary admission label. That signal is a computational phenotype requiring clinical adjudication; it is not itself a diagnosis.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported by inspected public evidence is narrower: hepatic reserve is relevant to tolerance and outcomes after locoregional HCC treatment. Sanai et al. (Scientific Reports 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-54466-4; PMCID PMC13457603, frozen source [source checksum]) studied TACE/TARE at four centers, defined hepatic decompensation using ascites, variceal bleeding, or portosystemic encephalopathy, and reported baseline ALBI as a predictor of post-LRT decompensation. That is external evidence about baseline reserve and adjudicated decompensation in another cohort. It does not establish that a routinely recorded early, within-person change after TACE adds information beyond baseline values, predicts short-term return in this source, or supports a discharge intervention.

The unresolved claim is therefore: conditional on baseline values and observed case mix, does the early laboratory trajectory add reproducible short-term risk information, particularly for a later return with a liver-complication diagnosis signal? This matters because discharge timing, post-discharge contact, and escalation for possible post-TACE hepatic injury are consequential clinical decisions. A robust incremental signal would justify a prospective, clinically adjudicated monitoring study; no incremental signal would argue against treating these routinely recorded changes as a stand-alone escalation rule.

The advance over baseline-risk work is a time-anchored, patient-level comparison of baseline-only versus early post-procedure trajectories, with both an observable utilization endpoint and a more clinically specific but adjudication-dependent endpoint. It also directly tests whether the apparent signal survives calendar drift, missingness, assay-specific handling, and the fact that scheduled repeat treatment cannot be distinguished from unplanned readmission.

## Exposure and outcome construction

Read `labs` from `[internal dataset path]`, ordinary file, keyed by (`patient master index`, `encounter number`) and timed by `laboratory time`. Use only rows with the exact assay labels: total bilirubin, albumin, international normalized ratio, and in the expanded set alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, creatinine, platelet count. Parse `quantitative result` conservatively as numeric; retain `qualitative result`, `specimen type`, and unparseable values only for an availability audit. Do not assume every row was collected at procedure time merely because it shares an encounter key.

For each assay, define the baseline median in [TACE start −14 calendar days, TACE start), and the early median in [TACE start, TACE start +72 hours], with the procedure timestamp excluded from baseline. The trajectory is early minus baseline, computed separately by assay. A primary core cohort requires paired total bilirubin, albumin, and INR; an expanded cohort additionally requires the four enzymes/creatinine/platelet values. Use assay-specific fold-training robust standardization only for models. Never pool assays, impose a clinical cutoff, or calculate ALBI: absence of units makes a validated ALBI score unavailable. Expected clinical directions (not imposed in fitting) are increases in bilirubin, INR, ALT, AST, and creatinine and decreases in albumin and platelets.

The primary outcome is any subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days after discharge. The clinically focused secondary phenotype is a subsequent admission whose diagnosis name contains one or more prespecified observed terms for decompensation (for example ascites/peritoneal effusion, hepatic encephalopathy/hepatic coma, esophageal and gastric fundal varices, or gastrointestinal bleeding) on that later encounter. Report each component separately and do not merge “cirrhosis” alone into incident decompensation. This is a diagnosis-coded signal, not an adjudicated hepatic decompensation endpoint. A small blinded expert review sample should assess positive and negative phenotype examples using the relevant `clinical_documents` and `examinations` text, but the automatic experiment must not claim that review occurred unless it is actually performed.

## Baseline comparator and analysis

Fit a prespecified patient-level logistic baseline-only model for each outcome with age, sex, index department, baseline assay medians, number of prior encounters in the preceding 365 days, and prior recorded indicators derived by date-aligned encounter joins for cirrhosis, portal-vein thrombosis, ascites, diabetes, and prior liver surgery. Use the exact diagnosis/procedure strings listed in the catalog/compiled code and publish all counts. These lexical indicators are utilization markers, not validated comorbidity adjudications. Do not use post-index medication/order information in the primary model; medication classes from `medications` ([internal dataset path]; `Medication`, `Start time`, `End time`, `Administration route`, `Medication type`) may appear only in a pre-index sensitivity analysis.

Fit a trajectory model adding the seven assay-specific changes. The primary inferential test is the global likelihood-ratio test for the added trajectory block. Report assay-specific adjusted odds ratios per training-fold IQR with 95% confidence intervals, absolute risk differences at representative covariate patterns, AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, and patient-level bootstrap intervals. Include complete-case and missingness-indicator analyses; inverse-observation-weighting is a sensitivity analysis only. If assay semantics or units fail domain validation, report only direction-free statistical associations and withdraw clinical lab interpretation.

Use patient-level five-fold cross-validation within discovery buckets 0–79, with all preprocessing and tuning inside folds. Add a chronological sensitivity split (index dates 2010–2022 for derivation, 2023–2025-12-02 for testing), maintaining patient separation. A bounded elastic-net logistic model and shallow gradient-boosted model may compare whether the trajectory result is robust to functional form, but model complexity is not a clinical endpoint and in-sample gain is not support. No GPU is required.

Run a density/gap sensitivity analysis (minimum number of observations and maximum gaps in baseline/early windows), an assay-label sensitivity analysis, and a procedure-source sensitivity analysis. Shuffle or reverse the timing of lab measurements within patient as a temporal-leakage falsification; the pre-specified trajectory signal should attenuate if its apparent value is genuinely time-ordered. Check that no follow-up encounter/lab/order row enters the feature windows and that duplicate-key joins do not multiply patients.

## Falsification and interpretation

Call the primary hypothesis supportive only if, for the primary all-cause outcome, the trajectory block has global LR p < 0.05, optimism-corrected out-of-fold Brier improvement over baseline has a 95% bootstrap interval excluding 0, and the direction/calibration conclusion is not reversed in the chronological split. For the clinically focused secondary outcome, require adequate event count stated before modeling and consistent direction; if event counts are sparse, treat that result as inconclusive regardless of p-value. These criteria establish prognostic evidence only.

Call it adverse/falsified if the primary trajectory block shows no incremental evidence and no held-out performance improvement, or if its apparent gain disappears/reverses chronologically, under density/gap handling, or after timing shuffling. A gain limited to in-sample high-capacity models, or a result that is not calibrated, is a model failure. A null all-cause result with a stable decompensation-coded result is not a failure of all-cause prediction; it is a discordant/inconclusive clinical-phenotype result requiring review.

Call the episode inconclusive if the procedure normalization is not reproducible, assay units/semantics cannot be validated, outcome event ascertainment cannot be reconstructed from timestamps, complete-case availability is strongly calendar/department dependent, the secondary phenotype has too few events, or uncertainty cannot distinguish no incremental information from a clinically meaningful gain.

Supportive means a prospective study of clinically adjudicated post-TACE decompensation and monitoring pathways is warranted. Adverse means these routinely recorded trajectories should not be treated as a stand-alone escalation signal. Inconclusive means obtain better temporal, laboratory-unit, outpatient, scheduling, staging, and adjudicated outcome data. None of these results establishes treatment harm/benefit, a safe discharge threshold, recurrence, tumor response, mortality benefit, or clinical truth of the lexical phenotype.

## Evidence unavailable or requiring another study

The HCC snapshot has no validated BCLC stage, tumor number/size, vascular invasion, performance status, Child–Pugh score, procedure dose/territory, reliable scheduling intent, mortality table, or dependable outpatient capture. `clinical_documents` ([internal dataset path]; no temporal column; fields include Chief Complaint, History of Present Illness, Past History, Admission Status, Course of Diagnosis and Treatment, Discharge Status, Discharge Diagnosis, Procedure Details) and `examinations` ([internal dataset path]; `Examination`, `Examination Findings`, `Examination Diagnosis`, `Start Time`) can support blinded expert review, not automatically trusted stage, imaging response, or decompensation labels. `pathology` ([internal dataset path]; no temporal column) is optional histologic support only. The catalog says `vitals` ([internal dataset path]), `transfers` ([internal dataset path]), and `front_page` ([internal dataset path]) contain only the two identifiers and are excluded from physiologic/transfer analyses. These are source limitations, not reasons to invent proxy variables.

It cannot establish whether an admission was unplanned, whether a diagnosis-coded signal is true hepatic decompensation, causal treatment effects, transportability to another hospital, or benefit from changing care.
