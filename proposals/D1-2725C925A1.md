# Does the frozen early albumin/bilirubin direction panel add information about whether the repeat-TACE encounter remains open at 72 hours?

## Clinically consequential successor question

This proposal preserves the complete frozen construction in `[prior hypothesis]`: the HCC snapshot, 319/1,491 recorded pathways, 159/526 independently selected first repeat-TACE events, exact medication ontology and exclusions, five source files, strict laboratory parser and duplicate rule, assay-specific `B/P/H` clocks, and the common same-encounter post-repeat panel nearest +24 hours in inclusive `[12h,36h]`. It does not widen the population, move any laboratory clock, or reinterpret a laboratory direction as a clinical event.

It asks a genuinely different, fixed-clock decision-information question:

> At the time the frozen early panel becomes available, do its two within-assay direction indicators—albumin lower than the contemporaneous pre-repeat value and total bilirubin higher than that value—improve out-of-fold prediction that the recorded repeat-TACE encounter will still be open at `event_time+72h`, beyond pre-event trajectory, measurement-process, workflow-timing, calendar, and demographic information?

Define `D72=1` only when the composite-key-linked `encounters.出院时间` is strictly later than `event_time+72h`; equality is `D72=0`. This endpoint is **recorded event-to-discharge timing**. It is not morbidity, medical necessity, hepatic decompensation, toxicity, a complication, recovery, safety, response, treatment benefit, or a recommendation to discharge or retain a patient. The question is clinically consequential as an information test for fixed-clock disposition planning: if the two direction indicators add reproducible prediction, they warrant external validation as operational forecasting features; if they do not, the simpler non-laboratory-history representation is preferable for this narrowly defined forecasting task. Actual discharge decisions require clinical assessment and data absent here.

This successor is distinct from the exhausted branches. It does not retry the zero-event 30-day adjudication proxy, and it does not depend on a later laboratory panel observed in only 41/131 episodes. The disposition clock is complete in the frozen 131 episodes and starts from the already locked early-panel landmark.

## Evidence supported now versus hypothesis still untested

### Strongest claim supported by the complete-source audit

A no-sampling audit rescanned every row in all five frozen parent sources before examining any predictor–disposition association. It reproduced:

- 319 systemic-record and 1,491 comparator pathways;
- 159 and 526 independently selected repeat-TACE events;
- 28,159,928 laboratory rows, 476,846 exact target-assay rows, 476,820 valid uncensored numeric-time rows, seven duplicate assay-timestamp groups, and zero discordant duplicate groups; and
- the frozen observed-panel analysis cohort of 34 systemic-record and 97 comparator episodes.

All 131 selected event encounters linked one-to-one to an encounter record on (`患者主索引`,`就诊号`), all had valid admission, visit, and discharge timestamps, none had admission or visit after the repeat event, none had discharge before the event, and no selected panel occurred after discharge. The selected panel ranged from +15.458 to +35.935 hours. Event-to-discharge time had median 59.933 hours (IQR 57.408–83.533). At the prespecified operational clocks, 112 encounters remained open at +48h, 65 at +60h, 48 at +72h, and 26 at +96h. For the locked `D72` endpoint, the two classes are therefore 48/131 and 83/131; arm counts are 15/34 systemic-record and 33/97 comparator. Five deterministic arm-and-`D72`-balanced folds each contain 24–27 patients, 9–10 `D72=1`, 15–17 `D72=0`, and 6–7 systemic-record episodes.

The audit tabulated the fixed 48/60/72/96-hour operational candidates without inspecting laboratory directions or any predictor–outcome association. It locks 72 hours because the latest eligible panel is at 36 hours, leaving at least a 36-hour prediction horizon, while retaining both outcome classes in every held-out fold. The threshold was not optimized against predictor performance. The audit support is [source checksum]; its implementation is [source checksum] and directly executes the inherited frozen implementation [source checksum].

These are endpoint availability, chronology, prevalence, and computational-support facts. They do not show that either laboratory direction predicts `D72`.

### Existing public evidence and substantive advance

A 2026 retrospective study of 466 liver-cancer patients undergoing interventional treatment reported associations between nutritional indices incorporating albumin and a prolonged hospital stay defined at that study's 75th percentile (Zhang et al., *Frontiers in Oncology*, DOI `10.3389/fonc.2026.1723228`, PMCID `PMC12900735`; full Europe PMC XML inspected). That study supports the broad importance of forecasting hospital duration after liver-cancer interventions. It does not validate this cohort, repeat-TACE event, within-patient directional features, +72-hour clock, or causal interpretation, and its ≥14-day outcome is not interchangeable with `D72`.

The substantive advance here is a temporally ordered, leakage-safe incremental-information test at an observed early-panel landmark, with complete recorded disposition timing. It asks whether the newly available direction-only panel adds information beyond what was already knowable from the remote/current trajectory and the measurement process. It does not substitute encounter duration for a clinical complication.

### Untested hypothesis

The primary hypothesis is:

> Adding the two frozen early-panel direction indicators reduces pooled paired out-of-fold Brier score for `D72` by at least 0.02 relative to an otherwise identical pre-panel model.

The 0.02 margin is a locked design convention for a potentially meaningful forecasting increment, not a validated clinical-utility or discharge-policy threshold.

## Locked incremental-prediction experiment

The 131-person denominator has 48 `D72=1` and 83 `D72=0`, so use one parsimonious pooled model, no arm-specific fitting, interactions, feature selection, tuning, nonlinear learners, or outcome-driven threshold changes.

Construct the pre-panel variables:

- `BP_alb_down = 1[P_albumin < B_albumin]`;
- `BP_bili_up = 1[P_bilirubin > B_bilirubin]`;
- arm indicator;
- age;
- sex encoded as `male` and `unknown/other`, female reference;
- numeric repeat-event year;
- `TACE2_to_repeat_days`;
- `P_old_lead_hours`, the larger positive `event_time-P_time` across assays;
- selected `panel_hours` after `event_time`;
- shared `H_any`, `logN=log(1+N_H)`, and `logR=H_any*log(1+R_hours/24)`.

Verify equality of albumin/bilirubin `H_any`, `N_H`, and `L_time` among all 131 episodes, as required by the parent. Any disagreement is computationally inconclusive; do not choose one assay's clock. When `H_any=0`, set `logN=logR=0`. No other imputation is allowed.

Fit nested logistic models:

`Q_pre: D72 ~ BP_alb_down + BP_bili_up + arm + age + sex + event_year + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_old_lead_hours + panel_hours + H_any + logN + logR`

`Q_early: Q_pre + E_alb_down + E_bili_up`

Only the two frozen early-panel directions differ between models. Thus the increment cannot be credited merely to remote/current state, whether/how often/how recently the intermediate panel was measured, calendar era, or the selected panel's timing.

### Cross-fitting and estimand

Assign five deterministic arm-and-`D72`-balanced folds. Within each arm x `D72` cell, SHA-256 sort `hcc-repeat-tace-disposition-v1|患者主索引`, then assign rank modulo five. Reproduce held-out fold sizes 27,27,27,26,24 with `D72=1` counts 10,10,10,9,9 and systemic counts 7,7,7,7,6. Require both outcome classes and both arms in every training and held-out fold.

Within each training fold, standardize age, event year, TACE2-to-repeat days, P-old lead, panel hours, logN, and logR using training means and population standard deviations. Do not scale binary variables. If a sex category is absent in training, fix its coefficient to zero in both models for that fold. Fit logistic regression with an unpenalized intercept and fixed L2 slope penalty `lambda=1`, minimizing mean binomial log loss plus `0.5*sum(beta^2)`. Use deterministic Newton or LBFGS optimization, gradient tolerance `1e-8`, maximum 10,000 iterations. No fallback or data-dependent penalty is allowed.

Require convergence, finite coefficients/probabilities, finite positive variance for retained continuous variables, and nonzero held-out prediction variance. A zero-variance continuous feature is centered and fixed to zero in both models and reported; more than one in any fold is model-fragile and inconclusive.

The primary estimand is pooled paired out-of-fold:

`Delta_D72 = Brier(Q_pre) - Brier(Q_early)`.

Positive values favor the early direction block. Report both Brier scores, training-prevalence-only null Brier, log loss, prediction range and variance, paired patient contributions, and descriptive calibration intercept/slope. Report arm-stratified Brier values only descriptively; do not infer pathway effects or interactions.

## Uncertainty and falsification

1. **Full-refit conditional bootstrap.** Use 1,000 replicates, seed `20260913`. Resample patients with replacement within each primary-fold x arm x `D72` cell, preserving cell sizes and original fold assignment. Recompute multiplicity-weighted training means/SDs, refit both models, and calculate multiplicity-weighted pooled `Delta_D72`. Report percentile 95% interval and fraction `Delta_D72<=0`. Fewer than 950 estimable replicates is inconclusive. This uncertainty is conditional on the frozen selected cohort and recorded encounter clocks, not population or utility uncertainty.
2. **Early-panel linkage destruction.** Jointly permute each patient's paired `(early_albumin, early_bilirubin)` values across patients within arm and primary fold 500 times, seed `20260914`, retaining recipient `P/B/H`, panel time, covariates, disposition, and fold. Recompute `E_alb_down` and `E_bili_up`, refit the pipeline, and report the permutation distribution of gains. Support requires observed gain above its 95th percentile. This tests patient-specific linkage, not causality.
3. **Partition stability.** Repeat the point-estimate pipeline using salts ending `-v2` through `-v5`. A favorable result is partition-sensitive if fewer than four of five gains are positive or, when the primary gain is at least 0.02, their median is below 0.02.
4. **Influence.** From primary out-of-fold paired Brier contributions, delete one patient at a time without refitting. Report the range and largest absolute paired contribution. A favorable estimate is influence-sensitive if any deletion changes its sign or shifts it by at least 0.02.
5. **Component attribution.** Fit `Q_pre+E_alb_down` and `Q_pre+E_bili_up` separately as secondary analyses. If the two-feature result is favorable but only one single-feature gain is positive, label it component-attributed and name that assay. Do not imply a coordinated biological mechanism. Report the four joint direction cells descriptively.
6. **Clock sensitivity.** Recalculate only the endpoint at strict +96h without changing the population or predictors. This is descriptive/model-sensitivity only because only 26 events are available. If the primary result is favorable but the +96h gain reverses sign, state that the information is specific to the +72h operational clock. Never replace the primary endpoint post hoc.
7. **Administrative-clock audit.** Report event year, admission-to-event time, panel time, event-to-discharge time, and panel-to-discharge time by `D72`; inspect impossible or heaped timestamps. Any impossible chronology or unexplained composite-key conflict is reconstruction failure. Even clean timestamps cannot prove clinical readiness or medical necessity.

## Ordered interpretation branches

1. **Reconstruction failure / negative feasibility.** Any source/hash/schema mismatch; unexplained failure to reproduce 319/1,491 pathways, 159/526 events, laboratory parser counts, 34/97 frozen episodes, complete one-to-one encounter linkage, 48/83 `D72` classes, shared history clocks, or required fold support; any selected panel after discharge; or any key/clock/window violation. Stop. Do not alter the endpoint or population.
2. **Computationally inconclusive.** Required values are nonfinite; a fold loses a class or arm; optimization/prediction checks fail; more than one continuous variable is zero-variance in a fold; or fewer than 950 bootstrap replicates are estimable. Report feasibility only.
3. **Adverse predictive degradation.** Gates pass and the bootstrap interval lies wholly below zero. Conclude only that adding the two early directions worsened out-of-fold prediction of the recorded +72h open-encounter indicator under the locked model.
4. **Supportive decision-information evidence.** Gates pass; `Delta_D72>=0.02`; bootstrap lower bound is above zero; `Q_early` beats the training-prevalence null; observed gain exceeds the 95th linkage-permutation percentile; and the estimate is neither partition- nor influence-sensitive. Conclude only that the two direction indicators add reproducible prognostic information for whether the recorded event encounter remains open at +72h in this selected observed-panel cohort. Apply component-attributed and clock-specific qualifiers when triggered.
5. **Precise null for the design-sized increment.** The interval is not wholly below zero and its upper bound is below +0.02. Rule out a 0.02 Brier improvement under this locked design and conditional uncertainty; favor the smaller feature representation for this forecast only. Do not recommend reduced laboratory monitoring.
6. **Small/fragile favorable result.** The interval lower bound is above zero but the point is below 0.02, or the point reaches 0.02 but linkage, partition, influence, or null-benchmark gates fail. Report the gain and failed gate without a supportive claim.
7. **Inconclusive precision.** The interval spans both zero and +0.02, or another locked ambiguity remains. State that 131 episodes cannot distinguish no increment from the design-sized gain.

Supportive, adverse, null, and inconclusive outcomes are all informative about whether this feature block should survive into external operational-validation work. None establishes a discharge policy.

## Claim limits and verifier boundary

The HCC snapshot lacks discharge orders, documented medical readiness, reasons for prolonged stay, bed availability, local scheduling protocols, complications adjudication, mortality, outside encounters, TACE technical details, verified medication administration, laboratory units/reference ranges/analyzer harmonization, fluid balance, albumin infusion/transfusion, infection/bleeding context, Child–Pugh/MELD, tumour burden, and radiographic response. `出院时间` may be an administrative timestamp. Conditioning on repeat TACE, required `B/P`, and an observed post panel can create selection/collider bias. The early panel may itself be protocol-timed. Reusing `P` to define a direction can create mathematical dependence, although `P` is identically represented in both nested models.

A verifier can check source hashes and full scans; exact columns, joins, and clocks; frozen cohort/event reconstruction; parser and duplicate handling; the 131-person landmark; encounter chronology; `D72`; direction variables; folds; training-only preprocessing; fixed-penalty fitting; paired `Delta_D72`; bootstrap; linkage permutation; partition, influence, component, and clock diagnostics; and branch precedence. It can check whether the reported conclusion follows from computed outputs.

A verifier cannot establish morbidity, toxicity, decompensation, medical necessity, discharge readiness, safety, causal treatment effects, clinical utility, fairness, transportability, or policy benefit. Those claims require clinical adjudication of why the encounter remained open, unit-validated laboratories, treatment/supportive-care context, expert review, and external or prospective validation.

## Remaining uncertainty

No predictor–`D72` association or model has been inspected. The endpoint is complete and computationally supported, but it may primarily reflect local workflow. The strongest possible result is therefore narrow: incremental prediction of a recorded fixed-clock disposition indicator among 131 selected observed-panel repeat-TACE encounters.
