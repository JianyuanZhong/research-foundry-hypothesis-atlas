## 1. The unresolved claim, the strongest claim the evidence already supports, and what would change clinically

### 1.1 The clinical practice at issue

Androgen-deficiency work-up in men begins with a **total** testosterone assay, and treatment
thresholds are written in total-testosterone units (commonly 8–12 nmol/L bands). But most
circulating testosterone is bound to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG), so total testosterone is
to a large degree a *readout of SHBG*. SHBG is suppressed by adiposity, hepatic steatosis,
hyperinsulinaemia and chronic illness. The consequence is a structural ambiguity in the number
clinicians act on: a low total testosterone may reflect reduced gonadal androgen output, or merely
reduced binding capacity in a metabolically ill man whose *free* hormone is unremarkable.

### 1.2 Strongest claim the existing evidence already supports

- Men with low measured total testosterone have higher all-cause mortality. In the mass-
  spectrometry individual-participant meta-analysis of 9 cohorts (255,830 participant-years),
  baseline testosterone below 7.4 nmol/L predicted higher all-cause mortality and below
  5.3 nmol/L higher CVD mortality [K1].
- **In the same analysis the SHBG association runs the opposite way to the free-hormone
  prediction**: lower SHBG was associated with *lower* all-cause mortality (Q1 vs Q5 median 20.6 vs
  68.3 nmol/L; adjusted HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.77–0.95) and lower CVD mortality (HR 0.81, CI 0.65–1.00);
  the authors conclude "SHBG concentration was positively associated ... with all-cause and CVD
  mortality" [K1]. If risk were driven by free hormone, higher SHBG at fixed total testosterone
  would mean *less* free hormone and therefore *more* risk, not less.
- Genetically higher testosterone reduces type 2 diabetes risk in men (OR 0.86, 95% CI 0.76–0.98)
  while raising it in women (OR 1.37) [K2]. So testosterone is not epiphenomenal in men — there is
  a credible sex-specific causal channel to metabolic disease.
- The measurement substrate is itself contested. The free hormone hypothesis holds that only the
  unbound fraction is biologically active, yet "the assumptions of stoichiometry, binding dynamics,
  and the affinity of the prevailing models of testosterone binding to sex hormone-binding globulin
  and human serum albumin are not supported by published experimental data and are most likely
  inaccurate", and there are "widely recognized problems with the direct measurement as well as the
  computation of free testosterone concentrations" [K3].

### 1.3 The unresolved claim this experiment tests

Put [K1] and [K2] together and a specific contradiction appears that neither resolves. [K2] gives
a causal channel from testosterone to male metabolic disease; [K1] shows that the *binding protein*
behaves in the direction opposite to the free-hormone account. Nobody has tested the question that
decides what clinicians should measure:

> **Does the joint risk surface in (total testosterone, SHBG) space collapse onto a single
> free-hormone axis, or does it require two separate axes — and does the answer differ between the
> metabolic outcome and the androgen-sensitive muscle outcome?**

Two explanations with opposite observable predictions:

- **H_A — androgen-deficiency state.** Low total T is consequential because it indexes low
  bioavailable androgen. Because calculated free testosterone is approximately
  `log fT ≈ log T − c·log SHBG` with `c ∈ (0,1)`, H_A predicts that in a mutually adjusted model
  the coefficients on `log T` and `log SHBG` carry **opposite signs**, and that the two-dimensional
  risk surface is a function of the single index `log T − c·log SHBG` alone.
- **H_B — SHBG/metabolic-illness phenomenon (the measurement-validity rival).** Low total T is
  largely a shadow of low SHBG, and SHBG is a liver/adiposity/insulin-resistance marker in its own
  right. H_B predicts that, at fixed adiposity, `log T` and `log SHBG` carry the **same sign** for
  incident type 2 diabetes (both higher = lower risk), that the total-T coefficient attenuates
  towards the null once SHBG and adiposity are held fixed, and that no single free-hormone index
  can describe the surface.

Define the **primary estimand** as the iso-risk contour slope
`r = β_logSHBG / β_logT` from the mutually adjusted model. H_A requires `r ∈ (−1, 0)`;
H_B predicts `r ≥ 0` for incident type 2 diabetes. The sign of a single number separates them.

Discriminating leverage comes from three places, all available on this island:

1. **Conditional sign at fixed adiposity** — the sign of `r`, per outcome.
2. **Outcome dissociation** — grip strength is the androgen-sensitive phenotype; incident type 2
   diabetes is the metabolic phenotype. H_A predicts the *same* free-hormone axis governs both.
   H_B permits (indeed predicts) that `r` differs in sign between them.
3. **Discordant low-T men** — men with low total T and *low* SHBG versus low total T and *high*
   SHBG. These are the two groups current practice treats most differently once free T is
   calculated, and the preliminary cell counts below show they are not rare.

A preliminary, **unadjusted** [V] cross-tabulation already makes the question non-trivial. In the
analysis cohort (n = 181,590 men), with low total T defined as < 8.6 nmol/L and SHBG split at the
cohort median 37.24 nmol/L:

| cell | n | incident T2D | % | deaths | % | median BMI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| low T, SHBG below median | 24,203 | 2,936 | 12.1 | 2,532 | 10.5 | 29.19 |
| low T, SHBG above median | 4,525 | 448 | 9.9 | 821 | 18.1 | 27.46 |
| not low T, SHBG below median | 66,558 | 4,217 | 6.3 | 4,558 | 6.8 | 27.70 |
| not low T, SHBG above median | 86,304 | 4,183 | 4.8 | 10,550 | 12.2 | 26.16 |

All four numbers in each row are [V]. Two features matter. First, **within low-T men, diabetes risk
is higher in the low-SHBG group** — the direction H_B predicts and the opposite of what the
free-hormone account predicts. Second, **deaths run the other way** — higher in the high-SHBG low-T
group — reproducing the direction reported in [K1] and establishing an outcome dissociation inside a
single cohort. These are crude proportions with no adjustment for age (the high-SHBG group is older)
and are presented only to show the comparison is populated and the contrast is real enough to be
worth estimating properly. The 5x5 quintile grid of total T by SHBG [V] shows the same thing with
monotone gradients on both margins (lowest-T/lowest-SHBG cell 2,140/17,515 = 12.2% incident T2D;
lowest-T/highest-SHBG 82/1,186 = 6.9%; highest-T/lowest-SHBG 53/1,021 = 5.2%).

### 1.4 What the result would change clinically

- **If H_B holds for the metabolic outcome** (`r ≥ 0`, total-T coefficient attenuated to the null at
  fixed SHBG and BMI): the man whose total T is low *because* his SHBG is low — the commonest
  presentation, n = 24,203 [V], median BMI 29.2 [V] — will be reassured by a *normal calculated free
  testosterone* while being the highest-metabolic-risk man in the clinic. The implied rule change is
  concrete: **report SHBG alongside total testosterone and treat the low-T/low-SHBG combination as a
  metabolic flag rather than an androgen-deficiency finding**, and *restrict* free-T estimation to
  the discordant low-T/high-SHBG group rather than applying it as a universal rescue step.
- **If H_A holds** (`r ∈ (−1,0)`, single free-hormone index sufficient, and the same axis governs
  grip): current practice is under-measuring, and universal free-T estimation (or an SHBG-adjusted
  total-T threshold) is justified across the low-T population, not just in discordant men.
- **If the answer differs by outcome** (plausible given the table above): then "low T" is not one
  state, and a single total-testosterone threshold cannot serve both the metabolic and the
  musculoskeletal decision. That conclusion alone would change guideline structure, because current
  thresholds are outcome-agnostic.

None of these follow from [K1] or [K2]. [K1] estimated testosterone and SHBG associations but, from
the inspected abstract, did not test whether the joint surface reduces to a free-hormone index, and
cohort SHBG strata differ in adiposity. [K2] is Mendelian randomisation on genetically predicted
testosterone, which cannot answer what the *clinically measured* total-T assay is indexing in an
individual patient. [K3] establishes that the computation route (Vermeulen-type) rests on
questionable binding assumptions, which is exactly why the empirical contour slope `r` is more
informative than any formula-derived free-T value.

### 2.3 The measurement-time problem (why this is not a nuisance here)

Testosterone has strong diurnal variation and UK Biobank drew blood across the working day without
standardisation. This directly threatens the *total*-T measurement far more than SHBG (which is
diurnally stable), so any failure to control it biases the comparison between the two exposures —
i.e. it biases `r`, the primary estimand. Honest position:

- **Time of blood draw (field `3166`) and fasting time (field `74`) are [NOT-IN-PARQUET].** The
  Assessment_Center parquet derivative has 517 columns and does not contain them; they exist only in
  the 35.8 GB full Assessment_Center CSV. The solver must extract these two columns (plus the others
  listed in section 3.3) by a targeted single-pass column projection over that CSV. This is a
  declared, budgeted step, not an assumption.
- **Assessment month/season** is derivable from `53-0.0` with no extra extraction and will be
  included as a harmonic (sin/cos of day-of-year) term.
- **Assessment centre (field `54`) is [NOT-IN-PARQUET]** and also requires the full-CSV extraction;
  without it, centre-level assay batch and calendar drift cannot be absorbed. If the extraction
  fails, the design falls back to month-of-assessment plus a sensitivity analysis restricted to the
  interquartile range of calendar time, and the limitation is reported rather than hidden.
- **Within-person reliability is measurable here and is a strength of this design.** Among men with
  hormones at both instance 0 and instance 1 (**[V]** n = 6,636, median 4.42 y apart), Pearson
  correlation across visits is **0.655 for total testosterone and 0.845 for SHBG** [V]. Total
  testosterone is markedly the noisier exposure. That asymmetry is not a footnote: classical
  attenuation alone will shrink `β_logT` relative to `β_logSHBG` and could manufacture an H_B-looking
  result. The measurement-error alternative in section 5 exists specifically to handle this, and the
  two reliability coefficients above are its calibration inputs.

### 3.4 Dtype and missingness facts that must not be assumed away

**Every column in every ukb672073 parquet derivative is `string` dtype** — verified for all
exposure, outcome, date and covariate columns actually used [V]. Missing values are SQL nulls, not
sentinel strings. All numeric use therefore goes through an explicit coercion
(`pd.to_numeric(..., errors="coerce")`) and all dates through `pd.to_datetime(..., errors="coerce")`.
Any computed number produced without that coercion is wrong, silently.

Second hazard, found and fixed during this episode's feasibility work: naive `NaT` handling in the
first-diagnosis-date reduction. `NaT` cast through `datetime64[ns] -> float64` becomes a large
negative integer, not `NaN`, so `np.nanmin` silently returns a spurious earliest date for every
participant with any missing slot. The verified implementation uses compact int64 day numbers with
an explicit sentinel and a byte-string code matrix:

```python
code_arr = hro[icd_cols].fillna("").to_numpy(dtype="S8")   # 259 cols x 502k rows, ~414 MB
BIG = np.int64(10**9)
dnum[:, j] = np.where(d.isna().values, BIG, d.values.astype("datetime64[D]").astype("int64"))
valid = dnum < BIG
# per code-set: earliest valid day number, BIG -> NaN before converting back to a date
```

Any solver re-implementation must reproduce this behaviour; the reference is
`work/branchC/feasibility.py` ([source checksum]).

### 4.3 Mortality

`40000-0.0` date of death. **22,017 deaths among core men; 18,461 deaths in the analysis cohort**
[V]; dates 2007-08-17 to 2022-12-19 [V]. Mortality is the outcome on which [K1] reported the
*positive* SHBG association, so it is the natural external anchor: if this cohort does not reproduce
the [K1] SHBG-mortality direction, the whole comparison is suspect and that is a reportable adverse
result about the design, not about the hypothesis.

### 4.4 Supporting / negative-control outcomes (all [V], core men, incident after baseline)

`I21`/`I22` myocardial infarction 7,297; `I50` heart failure 9,071; `S72` hip fracture 1,684;
`K70`/`K74` cirrhosis 1,700; `N18` chronic kidney disease 10,125; any `C*` cancer 33,544;
`C61` prostate cancer 10,833.

Roles: **hip fracture** is a second androgen-sensitive outcome (so H_A predicts it shares grip's
axis); **cirrhosis** is a positive control for the hepatic-SHBG pathway (H_B predicts a strong SHBG
axis there); **prostate cancer** is an androgen-exposure control with an expected positive
testosterone direction per [K2]; **CKD** and **any cancer** function as illness-burden outcomes with
no strong androgen prior. A finding that total T's conditional association is similar across
androgen-sensitive and androgen-irrelevant outcomes would indicate generic illness confounding, not
androgen biology.

### 4.5 Negative-control exposure

**Serum albumin `30600-0.0`** (and, after full-CSV extraction, ALT/ALP) is used as a
negative-control *exposure*: a liver-synthesised protein that falls with adiposity and chronic
illness but has no androgen-receptor mechanism. If total testosterone's mutually adjusted
association with incident T2D is of similar magnitude to albumin's, the parsimonious reading is
shared illness/liver confounding (H_B), not androgen action. This is a cheap, interpretable
falsification lever that requires no extra data.

## 5. Baseline and substantive alternative, at equal specificity, on the same question

Both arms estimate the **same** target: the shape of the log-hazard (or mean-grip) surface over
(log total T, log SHBG) at fixed adiposity, and in particular the contour slope `r` and whether the
surface collapses to one index.

### 5.1 BASELINE (primary) — prespecified joint spline Cox model and the contour-slope estimand

- **Inputs**: analysis cohort n = 181,590 [V]; exposures `logT`, `logSHBG` (standardised);
  covariates age `21022-0.0`, BMI `21001-0.0`, body-fat % `23099-0.0`, smoking `20116-0.0`,
  alcohol `1558-0.0`, ethnicity `21000-0.0`, month-of-assessment harmonics from `53-0.0`, plus (after
  full-CSV extraction) blood-draw time `3166`, fasting time `74`, centre `54`, waist `48`.
- **Models**, in a fixed prespecified order so that the comparison is not a fishing exercise:
  1. `M1`: `logT` alone.
  2. `M2`: `logT + logSHBG` (mutual adjustment) — yields `β_logT`, `β_logSHBG` and
     **`r = β_logSHBG / β_logT`** with a CI by the delta method and by 1,000-replicate bootstrap.
  3. `M3`: `M2` + adiposity (BMI, body fat %, waist).
  4. `M4`: restricted cubic splines (4 knots at the 5/35/65/95th percentiles) on both exposures plus
     their **tensor-product interaction**, giving the full surface.
  5. `M5`: single-index constraint — hazard a smooth function of `logT − c·logSHBG` with `c` free.
     `M4` vs `M5` is a **nested likelihood-ratio test of the free-hormone hypothesis**.
  6. `M6`: cFT as the sole exposure (the current-guideline foil, labelled formula-based).
- **Interaction scale, stated explicitly.** The primary interaction between total T and SHBG is
  assessed on the **multiplicative (log-hazard) scale**, because the Cox model is multiplicative and
  the free-hormone constraint is naturally a linear constraint in log concentrations. A secondary
  **additive** (absolute-risk-difference at 10 years, via the Breslow baseline) assessment is
  reported because clinical thresholds act on absolute risk, and a multiplicative-null/additive-
  present pattern is a substantive result, not a nuisance. Both are prespecified; neither is allowed
  to be selected after seeing results.
- **Strength arm**: linear regression of max grip at instance 0 on the same surface; and, in the
  29,253 men with a repeat [V], linear mixed / change-score model for grip slope with
  time-between-visits as a covariate.
- **Uncertainty and splits**: full-cohort estimation with bootstrap CIs for `r`; plus a prespecified
  **50/50 random split** where the surface shape (`M4`/`M5` comparison) is chosen on half A and the
  contour slope re-estimated on held-out half B. Reporting half-B estimates guards against the
  surface-shape decision being an artefact of flexible fitting.
- **Budget**: CPU only. 16 cpus, 32 GB, ~2–4 h wall clock for all models plus 1,000 bootstraps
  (estimate, **unverified** — not timed in this episode).

### 5.2 SUBSTANTIVE ALTERNATIVE — latent free-hormone measurement-error model with a learned joint surface

What the baseline loses: the baseline treats measured total T and measured SHBG as the exposures.
But the verified within-person reliabilities are **0.655 for total T and 0.845 for SHBG** over a
median 4.42 years (n = 6,636) [V]. Differential attenuation of exactly this size can push
`β_logT` towards zero relative to `β_logSHBG` and *manufacture* an H_B-looking answer from an
H_A-true world. The baseline cannot distinguish "total T carries no information" from "total T is
measured worse". **That is the single most consequential threat to this proposal's inference, and it
is the scientific reason the alternative exists.**

- **Structure**: a latent-variable hazard model. Latent `F_i` (free/bioavailable hormone axis) and
  latent `S_i` (true binding-protein/metabolic axis). Measurement layer: observed `logT_i` and
  `logSHBG_i` are noisy functions of `(F_i, S_i)` constrained by the mass-action relation among
  total T, SHBG and albumin; measurement variances are **identified by the repeat-measure subset**
  (n = 6,636 with both visits; T r = 0.655, SHBG r = 0.845 [V]) and propagated to the full cohort.
  Hazard layer: `log h = g(F, S, adiposity, age)` where `g` is a monotone-constrained neural
  additive / tensor-spline surface. The decisive comparison is the nested restriction `g` depends on
  `F` only (H_A) versus `g` requires both axes (H_B) — the same test as `M4` vs `M5`, but now with
  attenuation corrected.
- **Fitting target**: penalised full likelihood (Cox partial likelihood for T2D and mortality;
  Gaussian likelihood for grip), fitted by EM/variational inference over the latent axes.
- **Splits and evaluation**: 5-fold cross-validation for the penalty and architecture; held-out
  partial-likelihood deviance and time-dependent C-index at 5 and 10 years; 200 bootstrap replicates
  for the CI on the corrected contour slope and on the H_A-vs-H_B likelihood-ratio statistic.
  Calibration of 10-year absolute risk in the discordant low-T cells (n = 24,203 and n = 4,525 [V])
  is reported, because those cells are where the clinical decision changes.
- **Resources and justification for GPU**: the expensive object is 5-fold x 200-bootstrap x (latent
  EM with a flexible surface) on 181,590 x ~25 design columns with ~11.8k and ~18.5k events — i.e.
  thousands of repeated full-likelihood fits with dense matrix operations and risk-set cumulative
  sums. That is a genuine matrix-throughput workload, not a model-name flourish. Request
  **gpus = 1, cpus = 16, memory_mb = 65536, timeout 28,800 s**, well inside the stated envelope
  (cpus <= 16, gpus <= 8, memory <= 256 GB, <= 28,800 s), with checkpointing per fold. The resource
  estimate is **unverified** — no GPU probe was run in this discovery episode, and if the fit does
  not converge inside 28,800 s the correct response is to reduce bootstrap replicates (reporting
  wider CIs) rather than to drop the attenuation correction, which is the scientific point.
- **Simpler fallback at the same scientific target**: regression-calibration / SIMEX using the same
  two reliability coefficients. This is a few CPU-minutes and gives a first-order attenuation
  correction. It is specified as a *mandatory* companion so that the headline conclusion never rests
  solely on the heavier latent model.

### 5.3 Which is primary, and why

**The baseline is primary.** The scientific question is a sign-and-identifiability question about a
two-parameter contrast (`r`, and whether one index suffices). A prespecified, transparent,
reproducible Cox specification answers it directly and is what a guideline committee could audit.
The learned alternative is **confirmatory and threat-specific**: its job is to establish that the
baseline's answer survives correction for the verified 0.655-vs-0.845 reliability asymmetry and does
not depend on an imposed linear functional form. If the two disagree — specifically, if the
attenuation-corrected `r` crosses zero while the uncorrected `r` does not — the honest conclusion is
that this island cannot settle the question with single-measurement hormones, and the proposal says
so in advance (section 6.3) rather than preferring whichever arm is more favourable.

### 5.4 Alternatives considered and rejected, with the evidence that would justify revisiting

- **Mendelian randomisation.** Rejected as the primary design: [K2] has already done sex-specific MR
  for testosterone in UK Biobank using 2,571 genome-wide significant associations in 425,097
  participants, including the male T2D estimate OR 0.86. Repeating it adds nothing, and MR cannot
  answer a measurement-validity question about what a clinical assay indexes in an individual. The
  Genomics table in this island (`table-52347effdd802a74.json`) holds genotyping metadata and
  principal components, not the variant-level dosages an instrument would need. Revisit only if
  variant-level data were added and the question changed from "what does the assay index" to "what
  does lifetime hormone exposure cause".
- **Olink proteomics-based illness-burden adjustment.** Rejected on data-governance grounds, not
  scientific ones: `metadata.json` declares the olink/dta files a separate identifier namespace with
  no authorised participant join to ukb672073. Revisit only if a documented crosswalk is added.
- **Hormone-trajectory latent-class model.** Rejected as infeasible: biochemistry exists at
  instances 0 and 1 only, with n = 6,636 men having both [V]. Two points in a 3.4% subsample cannot
  support trajectory classes. Revisit if instance-2 biochemistry is ever released.

## 6. Falsification criteria

All thresholds are prespecified. `β` are per-SD log-hazard coefficients from `M3` (mutual +
adiposity adjustment); `r = β_logSHBG / β_logT`.

### 6.1 H_B (measurement-validity rival) is supported if, for incident type 2 diabetes

1. `r >= 0` with the bootstrap 95% CI excluding the whole interval (−1, 0); **and**
2. `β_logT` attenuates from `M1` to `M3` by >= 50% in magnitude, with the `M3` per-SD HR within
   [0.95, 1.05] and its 95% CI contained in [0.90, 1.10]; **and**
3. `M4` (two axes) beats `M5` (single free-hormone index) with likelihood-ratio p < 0.001 and
   held-out (half B) partial-likelihood deviance improvement; **and**
4. the attenuation-corrected `r` from section 5.2 retains sign.

Interpretation: the routine total-testosterone assay, as used for metabolic risk reasoning, is
mis-targeted; SHBG should be reported alongside; and calculated free testosterone would misdirect
care in the 24,203-man low-T/low-SHBG group [V].

### 6.2 H_A (androgen-deficiency state) is supported if

1. `r` lies in (−1, 0) with 95% CI excluding 0; **and**
2. `M5` is not rejected against `M4` (LRT p > 0.05, or ΔAIC < 10), with the fitted `c` in (0, 1);
   **and**
3. the **same axis sign pattern** appears for incident T2D, max grip and hip fracture (the three
   outcomes H_A says share a mechanism); **and**
4. `β_logT` in `M3` corresponds to a per-SD HR <= 0.90 or >= 1.10 for T2D (i.e. not merely
   statistically but materially non-null).

Interpretation: low total T is a real androgen-deficiency state; SHBG-aware interpretation (free-T
estimation or an SHBG-conditioned total-T threshold) is justified across the low-T population.

### 6.3 Inconclusive, defined in advance

- `r`'s CI spans both 0 and a value in (−1, 0); or
- `M4` vs `M5` LRT p between 0.001 and 0.05 with no held-out improvement; or
- the uncorrected and attenuation-corrected `r` differ in sign.

Any of these is reported as **"this island cannot settle it with single-measurement immunoassay
hormones"** — a legitimate and informative outcome, not a failure to be rescued by adding subgroups
or switching outcomes. An imprecise null does not refute H_A.

### 6.4 Pre-committed adverse-result handling

- If this cohort fails to reproduce the [K1] direction (higher SHBG -> higher all-cause mortality)
  in `M3`, the design's external anchor has failed and the T2D result is downgraded to exploratory,
  regardless of how clean it looks.
- If the negative-control exposure albumin shows a conditional T2D association of magnitude >= 70%
  of total testosterone's, generic illness/liver confounding is the parsimonious explanation and
  **neither** H_A nor H_B is declared supported.
- Outcomes, code sets, exclusions and thresholds above are frozen before fitting. No outcome
  substitution or subgroup addition is permitted to rescue a claim; any needed change returns to the
  Lead as a child proposal.

### 6.5 What this island cannot deliver, honestly stated

No result here can establish:

- **Measured free or bioavailable testosterone.** There is none in UK Biobank. cFT is a formula
  whose binding assumptions [K3] says are "most likely inaccurate". Resolving H_A vs H_B definitively
  requires equilibrium-dialysis free T or an LC-MS/MS reference assay in the same men.
- **LC-MS reference testosterone.** UK Biobank total T is a platform immunoassay; [K1] deliberately
  restricted to mass-spectrometry cohorts. Immunoassay performance degrades at the low
  concentrations that define the exposure of interest (cohort p1 = 4.81, p5 = 6.70 nmol/L [V]) —
  precisely where H_A and H_B are to be distinguished. This is the deepest limitation of the design,
  and it is non-repairable here.
- **LH or FSH**, so primary and secondary hypogonadism cannot be separated. The LH finding in [K1]
  (LH > 10 IU/L predicting mortality) cannot be examined on this island at all.
- **Longitudinal hormone trajectories** beyond two points in 6,636 men [V].
- **Androgen treatment records**, so prevalent/incident testosterone therapy is unascertainable
  (section 2.1).
- **Clinical adjudication** of symptomatic hypogonadism, and of whether an incident `E11` code
  represents the clinical onset rather than the first hospital contact.
- **Primary-care diagnoses**, so milder/earlier T2D managed entirely in general practice is missed;
  the HbA1c >= 48 exclusion partially mitigates prevalent misclassification but not incident
  under-ascertainment.
- **Causality.** Even a clean two-axis surface is a conditional-association claim about what an assay
  indexes. It would license a *measurement and reporting* change; it would not license a treatment
  claim. An androgen-therapy trial stratified on baseline SHBG is the study that would.

## 7. Alternative explanations taken seriously

**7.1 Reverse causation from occult illness.** Incipient disease lowers both SHBG and total T, and
inflammation lowers SHBG specifically. Handling: (a) landmark sensitivity analyses excluding the
first 2 and 5 years of follow-up — affordable given 11,784 events [V]; (b) adjustment for the
illness-burden index (CRP median 1.28, p90 5.00 mg/L [V]; WBC; albumin; count of prevalent HES
codes); (c) exclusion of men with prevalent cirrhosis, CKD or cancer in sensitivity analysis
(`K70`/`K74` 446 prevalent, `N18` 415, any `C*` 8,197 [V]). Residual confounding by undiagnosed
illness cannot be eliminated and is stated as such.

**7.2 Adiposity as mediator versus confounder — and why adjustment changes the estimand.** Adiposity
sits upstream of SHBG (suppressing hepatic SHBG synthesis), downstream of androgen action (androgens
affect body composition), and upstream of T2D. Verified correlations [V]: T–BMI −0.306,
SHBG–BMI −0.312 — nearly identical, which is itself evidence that adiposity drives both hormone
measures rather than one. Consequences, stated rather than hidden: the BMI-adjusted `β_logT` is a
**direct-effect-flavoured** quantity that deliberately removes any pathway through body composition,
so it is the right estimand for the *measurement* question ("does the assay add information beyond
what the clinician already sees?") and the **wrong** estimand for the *biological* question ("does
androgen deficiency cause diabetes?"). Both the unadjusted and adjusted models are reported with
this interpretation attached, and the difference between them is presented as a finding about
adiposity's role, not as a nuisance. Adjusting for a mediator can also induce collider bias; the
proposal does not claim mechanism from an adjusted coefficient.

**7.3 Assay limitations at low concentrations.** Platform immunoassay total T is least reliable
exactly in the low range that defines the exposure. The verified within-person correlation of 0.655
for total T versus 0.845 for SHBG [V] quantifies the asymmetry in this very cohort. Because
differential attenuation biases `r` in the H_B direction, the measurement-error arm (section 5.2)
and the SIMEX/regression-calibration companion are mandatory, and the attenuation-corrected `r` is
reported alongside the raw one. Additional probe: restrict to men above the assay's lower reporting
limit and re-estimate; and compare `r` estimated in the top versus bottom half of the total-T
distribution, where differential reliability should bite unevenly.

**7.4 Diurnal and seasonal sampling.** Blood-draw time `3166` and fasting time `74` are
**[NOT-IN-PARQUET]** and must be extracted from the 35.8 GB Assessment_Center CSV; month is
derivable from `53-0.0` without extraction. Because diurnal variation hits total T much harder than
SHBG, failing to control draw time biases `r` towards H_B. Sensitivity analyses: restrict to a
narrow draw-time window; stratify by morning versus afternoon; include draw time as a spline. If the
extraction is unavailable, the limitation is reported as potentially decisive rather than minimised.

**7.5 Selection into the repeat-assessment subset.** The grip-change arm has 29,253 men of 181,151
with baseline grip [V], i.e. 16%, and instance-1/instance-2 attendance is strongly non-random
(healthier, nearer the Stockport/imaging centres, surviving). Handling: compare baseline
characteristics of repeat-attenders versus non-attenders; inverse-probability-of-attendance
weighting; and report the grip-change arm as direction-testing rather than effect-size-estimating.
A null grip-change finding in this subset would be weak evidence, and the proposal says so now.

**7.6 Competing risk of death.** 18,461 deaths versus 11,784 incident T2D events in the analysis
cohort [V] — deaths outnumber the primary events. Low-T/high-SHBG men have a strikingly higher crude
death proportion (18.1% vs 10.5% in low-T/low-SHBG [V]), so informative censoring by death could by
itself generate an apparent SHBG–T2D gradient. Handling: Fine–Gray subdistribution hazards for T2D
with death as a competing event, reported alongside cause-specific Cox; and 10-year cumulative
incidence functions in the four discordant cells. If the cause-specific and subdistribution results
disagree in sign, that disagreement is itself reported as the finding.

**7.7 Age as a shared driver.** SHBG rises and total T falls with age, so the (T, SHBG) plane is
partly an age plane. Age `21022-0.0` enters as a spline, and the surface is re-estimated within age
strata (<55, 55–64, >=65) to confirm `r` is not an age artefact.

## Expert cautions from seed UKB-33, preserved explicitly

1. Total testosterone in this cohort is a **platform immunoassay**, not LC-MS/MS, and is least
   trustworthy at the low concentrations that define the exposure of interest.
2. Hormones come from a **single, non-standardised blood draw** subject to diurnal and seasonal
   variation; one measurement is not a person's hormonal status.
3. **Reverse causation from occult illness** is a first-order threat, not a footnote: illness lowers
   SHBG and total T together.
4. **Adiposity is simultaneously confounder and mediator**; adjusting for it changes what is being
   estimated, and this must be stated rather than treated as routine covariate control.
5. There is **no measured free or bioavailable testosterone** on this island; formula-based free T is
   a derived convenience, not a measurement.
6. **Men on exogenous androgens cannot be excluded** without prescription data.
7. Low total T and metabolic illness are **entangled by construction**; any claim of a distinct
   "state" must show separable predictions, not merely a surviving coefficient.
