Question, existing evidence, and unresolved hypothesis

Prior lineage established that the frozen selected-case extraction contains 998 eligible participants with a first recorded malignancy in follow-up: 75 recorded CRC and 923 recorded non-CRC malignancies. It also showed that adding the three CBC slopes to a nested-CV prediction model produced essentially no discrimination increment (AUC change +0.000318) and that a stronger-null label-permutation diagnostic was nonextreme (plus-one p=0.211788). Those results did not test the parent's adjusted residual-moment null.

The executed hypothesis was exactly:

H0: E[(Y-m(C))(S-g(C))] = 0,

where Y distinguishes recorded CRC from another recorded malignancy, C contains age at repeat visit, sex, repeat-visit haemoglobin, MCV, platelet count, and post-entry diagnosis-lag stratum, and S contains the adverse-oriented annual slopes for haemoglobin, MCV, and platelet count. The unresolved clinical question was whether these three trajectories retain a reproducible adjusted linear score association with cancer type among future recorded malignancy cases. This remains a selected-case association target vulnerable to collider selection; it is not conditional independence, incidence prediction, screening performance, or causation.

Frozen computation

C was exactly [age_L, sex, hb1, mcv1, plt1, lag_late]. S was exactly [-(hb1-hb0)/years, -(mcv1-mcv0)/years, +(plt1-plt0)/years]. Diagnosis lag was first malignant date minus E, with lag_late=0 for days 0-365 and 1 for 366-1459.

Twenty five-fold cross-fit repetitions used seeds 20260909 through 20260928 and joint Y-by-lag stratification. Continuous C and each slope target were standardized using training-fold moments only. In every outer training set, m(C) was ridge logistic regression, with intercept and lag_late unpenalized, and g_j(C) was a separate ridge linear model. Inner five-fold tuning used the frozen grid 10^-4 through 10^4. Held-out scores were psi_i=(Y_i-m_hat(C_i)) times (S_i-g_hat(C_i)).

The parent specified 20 repetitions but did not state how to collapse their 20 score vectors to a single test. Before inspecting results, execution completed this implementation detail by averaging each participant's score contribution over repetitions, then estimating covariance and drawing participant-level Gaussian multipliers from the 998 averaged contributions. This retains participants, variables, estimand, and score definition; it treats the prespecified fold schedule as fixed. Every repetition-level result is also retained. This completion should not be read as integrating nuisance/fold uncertainty, and another prespecified aggregation rule would require a new sensitivity study.

The primary test used heteroskedasticity-robust score covariance, a global three-degree-of-freedom Wald statistic, and 20,000 centered fixed-score Gaussian multiplier replicates with a plus-one p-value. Parallel audits were: an ordinary unpenalized comparator-versus-comparator-plus-slopes logistic likelihood-ratio benchmark with 5,000 parametric bootstrap replicates; an unpenalized logistic/linear nuisance sensitivity; 20 joint slope-block permutations within lag-by-sex strata as implementation controls; and an exploratory replacement by the RDW-30070 trajectory.

Computational audit and falsification

All 100 outer folds contained both classes and had zero train/test overlap. All 18,400 primary nuisance fits, 2,400 unpenalized-sensitivity fits, and 4,600 RDW fits converged. All score contributions were finite; cross-fitted m predictions ranged from 0.0508 to 0.1363. The primary covariance was well-conditioned and required no ridge. Repetition-level primary multiplier p-values ranged from 0.616 to 0.854, so no fold repetition suggested a hidden rejection.

The comparator nuisance was weak: cross-fitted log loss ranged from 0.26672 to 0.26766 versus intercept-only 0.26676, and calibration slopes varied materially. This is not evidence for H0. It limits confidence in fine biological interpretation, although finite predictions, overlap, repetition stability, alternative nuisances, and the ordinary benchmark all support the computational conclusion that this analysis did not find a robust adjusted score signal.

In 20 lag-by-sex slope-block implementation-control permutations, one p-value was below 0.05 and the minimum was 0.04730. One of 20 is compatible with nominal chance and does not indicate repeated extreme behavior. These permutations are not a scientific null test. The prior random-label diagnostic p=0.211788 also remains only a stronger-null implementation guard.

An independent aggregate validation job passed: exact cohort hash and counts, fold support and nonoverlap, finite predictions and scores, convergence totals, covariance conditioning, calibration replicate counts, population gate, and claim scope were checked against the serialized result. The observed multiplier statistic and bootstrap use the same fixed averaged score definition by construction. Computational reproducibility is therefore supported for the stated implementation; automatic checks cannot validate case coding, registry completeness, exchangeability, biological mechanism, or clinical relevance.

Strongest supported conclusion

Within this exact selected set of future recorded malignancy cases, this prespecified linear residual-moment analysis found no reproducible adjusted association of the three adverse-oriented CBC slopes with recorded CRC versus recorded non-CRC malignancy: primary multiplier p=0.798, unpenalized nuisance p=0.775, and parametric benchmark p=0.854. The estimates are close to zero relative to their sampling uncertainty and stable over the declared fold repetitions. This is null-compatible/adverse evidence against a readily detectable linear score signal in this selected-case dataset, not proof that the trajectories have no association.

The evidence does not support the stronger directional hypothesis that all three adverse-oriented slopes are positively associated with CRC type. It also cannot quantify a clinically meaningful equivalence margin because none was prespecified and the fold-standardized moment has no direct clinical-unit threshold. Thus a claim of biological absence is inconclusive even though the test result itself is clearly non-supportive.

Supportive results would have required global rejection under multiplier calibration, concordant rejection under unpenalized nuisances and the parametric benchmark, coherent positive direction, and passed audits. The observed result does not meet those criteria. An adverse or null-compatible result means that the proposed adjusted score signal was not detected reproducibly in this selected recorded-case contrast. An inconclusive computational result would have arisen from fold, fit, covariance, calibration, or method instability; those failures were not observed, but clinical interpretation remains constrained by weak nuisance calibration, only 75 CRC cases, absence of a meaningful equivalence margin, and selection on future malignancy.
