# MERTK-state dependence of synovial fibroblast responses

## Question, hypothesis, and importance

**Untested hypothesis:** In the 48-hour ex-vivo rheumatoid arthritis synovial tissue macrophage–fibroblast co-culture, macrophage pretreatment with the MerTK inhibitor UNC1062 attenuates the measured matrix/TGF-response profile induced by MerTK/CD206-positive macrophages in FLS, relative to untreated Mpos co-culture; a shift toward measured inflammatory features is a prespecified secondary prediction. This is a bounded test of MERTK-inhibitor-associated macrophage-state dependence, not a claim that MerTK signaling between a named ligand and FLS receptor is proven.

This matters because MerTK-positive macrophage subsets have been linked with remission-associated, inflammation-resolving profiles and induction of FLS repair responses [K1]. If pharmacologic perturbation of that cell state weakens an FLS matrix/TGF-response program, it is a consequential warning about which synovial macrophage states should be preserved or altered in future therapeutic research. The available data are ex-vivo and extremely limited in independent paired units; no drug, disease-modifying benefit or patient treatment decision is supported.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

Alivernini et al. report MerTK-positive macrophage subsets with remission-associated signatures and FLS repair-response induction [K1]. Kuo et al. established a different RA macrophage-to-FLS pathway, HBEGF–EGFR, that increases FLS invasiveness [K2]. That pathway is not being relabeled as a novel finding here; HBEGF and EGFR are absent from E-MTAB-8873. Zhang et al. identify extensive inflammatory cell-state heterogeneity in RA synovium and associations with treatment response [K3], limiting any generalization from a small ex-vivo assay.

**Strongest supported claim:** prior work establishes that synovial macrophage states can influence fibroblast responses, including repair and invasiveness. **Unresolved claim being tested:** whether UNC1062 pretreatment of the Mpos compartment in this particular assay is associated with attenuation of a measured FLS matrix/TGF-response profile beyond sample-pair variation or nonspecific inflammation. Neither the literature nor the data currently establish such an effect. The substantive advance is a tightly bound, treatment-relevant boundary test of a known cell-state hypothesis, rather than discovery of a new ligand–receptor pathway.

Strongest rivals: differences in contributing donor or experimental run; inconsistent inhibitor-label/dose/washout; compound carryover directly affecting FLS; macrophage toxicity or generic change in activation rather than on-target MERTK action; an inflammatory stimulus response unrelated to the repair/matrix program; and a transcript shift that does not translate to protein or functional tissue effects.

## Falsification and decisions

- **Supportive:** both verified R2/R5 blocks show attenuation of the Mpos-associated fixed matrix/TGF-response set under macrophage UNC1062, not dominated by one feature; secondary inflammatory changes are reported separately, and the exploratory pattern is not merely the available LPS-control pattern. This supports an inhibitor-associated FLS transcript shift in these conditions and merits an independent replication. It does not prove on-target MERTK specificity, direction through a ligand/receptor, matrix function, FLS repair, clinical disease modification or patient benefit.
- **Adverse:** both valid blocks show no attenuation or an opposite effect, or the apparent change is driven by one block/feature and resembles nonspecific LPS response. This down-ranks the predicted transcript-level effect in this assay, not MerTK biology generally.
- **Inconclusive:** donor/sample mapping or inhibitor-label equivalence fails, fewer than two valid blocks remain, block effects disagree materially, or uncertainty/technical provenance is too weak. A wide interval or imprecise null does not refute the hypothesis.

Before interpreting, expert/protocol adjudication must verify actual FLS/STM identities, sorted phenotype, contributor/donor map, raw sample-to-tag mapping, the protocol's 446-vs-445 count discrepancy, technical replicate relation, UNC1062 administration/washout and R1/R2/R5 label equivalence. If results warrant continuation, conduct an independent donor-paired experiment randomizing Mpos macrophage vehicle vs macrophage-restricted genetic or pharmacologic MERTK perturbation/rescue; include inhibitor washout and direct FLS-only inhibitor exposure controls, Mneg and LPS controls, macrophage viability/target engagement, and FLS-only/conditioned-medium/transwell/direct-contact comparisons. Measure secreted GAS6/PROS1 and cytokines, FLS RNA/protein/phosphosignaling, and a functional matrix/invasion/repair outcome over a prespecified time course. Only a named ligand/receptor blockade/rescue with independent donors can establish directional communication; tissue localization plus clinical outcomes would be required for claims about patient disease or treatment choice.

## Method alternatives and precedent disposition

The simple paired baseline is chosen for transparency and the tiny donor-level information available. The alternate multivariate measured-panel model is retained as a scientifically substantive check for coherent, treatment-context-specific FLS response rather than chasing predictive gain. A neural predictor, unconstrained pathway search or detailed mechanistic simulation is deferred because the sample units are too few and key inputs (matched macrophage panel, ligand/protein concentrations, target engagement and functional outcomes) are absent; added complexity cannot resolve the claim. Revisit with those dependencies and independently held-out donor pairs. No new model has been fitted during discovery.

The three clinical demonstration methods are not adapted in this molecular assay: Delphi requires dated longitudinal disease histories; Oncoformer’s multimodal EHR/imaging data and main methods were unavailable in the reference bundle and do not map to the synovial assay; ALADYNOULLI is an EHR/genetic longitudinal model, not applicable here.
