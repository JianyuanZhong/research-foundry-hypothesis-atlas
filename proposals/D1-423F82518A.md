# Historical trajectory beyond the contemporaneous pre-repeat assay state

## Decision question and substantive repair

At the time a patient is being considered for a recorded repeat TACE, does the patient's *pre-repeat longitudinal assay history* add reproducible prediction of the next observed same-assay panel beyond the assay level measured immediately before repeat TACE and the other information available at that decision time? This reverses the parent nesting. The parent tested whether `P` adds to a remote baseline `B`; this version tests whether history adds to `P`, which is the clinically relevant direction if a current assay already summarizes present state.

The primary question is prognostic and computational, not causal: among the selected, observed complete-triplet episodes, does `H` improve out-of-fold prediction of `Y` over a model containing `P`, pathway, and pre-event covariates? A positive result supports reproducible serial-history information about the next *observed laboratory measurement*. It does not establish that history should alter repeat-TACE decisions, that TACE caused a change, or that albumin/bilirubin changes are toxicity, recovery, response, benefit, or safety. Because HCC provides neither laboratory units nor an adjudicated clinical outcome, this is the strongest decision-time question identifiable in the source; actual clinical utility requires a subsequent study with actionable outcomes and decision consequences.

## What is known and what remains untested

The frozen source audit supports availability facts, not an outcome association: the common-TACE2 pathway construction has 319 systemic-record and 1,491 TACE2-only patients; first strict repeat-TACE events on TACE2 calendar days 15–90 are 159/319 and 526/1,491; complete assay triplets are albumin 40/118 and total bilirubin 41/119, with common-timestamp triplets 40/118. The inherited audit also found no additional albumin triplets and only one additional bilirubin triplet per arm from assay-specific rather than common timestamps.

The unresolved claim is narrower: conditional on the frozen pathway, a recorded first repeat event, a complete triplet, the contemporaneous pre-event assay `P`, and prespecified pre-event covariates, a prespecified history `H` improves prediction of the next observed assay level `Y`. The direction and magnitude are not assumed. A pathway contrast is exploratory only. Availability counts must be recomputed before any result is interpreted.

## Reverse-nested history definition

The history must be constructed strictly before `P` and without using the selected P encounter. Define the historical window as `[TACE2 event time, repeat-event time−72 hours)`, using the exact repeat-event clock. This avoids allowing a near-event measurement to be counted both as history and as P, and leaves a prespecified 72-hour separation between history and the current-state measurement. Use only exact assay-label observations for the assay under analysis.

Define a low-dimensional, assay-specific `H`:

1. `B`, the remote pre-TACE2 anchor above.
2. `N_H`, the number of valid uncensored numeric assay results in the historical window, excluding the selected P encounter and duplicate timestamp groups handled by the locked duplicate rule.
3. `S_H`, the ordinary least-squares slope of assay value versus elapsed hours from TACE2, calculated only when at least two valid results occur at distinct timestamps; otherwise it is missing. No slope is calculated using P.
4. `R_H`, the elapsed hours from TACE2 to the last historical assay result, with a missing indicator when no historical result exists.

`H` is intentionally small because only 40/41 systemic complete triplets and 118/119 comparator triplets are available. Within each training fold, center/scale numeric H features, impute missing `S_H` and missing historical values by the training-fold median, and include locked missingness indicators. Do not require historical trajectory completeness as an additional inclusion criterion: otherwise the estimand changes through an outcome-adjacent observation-selection rule. Report the number with no historical result, at least two distinct historical timestamps, and each assay-specific usable-history pattern. If the prespecified history features cannot be computed for a meaningful fraction, report that limitation and do not replace the window or feature definition after outcome inspection.

The historical features are not a change-on-change outcome. `B` and `S_H` are candidate predictors, while `P` remains in every primary model. Do not use `Y−P` or `P−B` as a primary endpoint or predictor. A secondary descriptive plot may show trajectories, but no interpretation of a difference score as vulnerability or procedural perturbation is permitted.

## Primary estimand and nested analysis

For assay `a`, let `C` denote the complete-triplet, first-repeat-event population. The primary estimand is the difference in cross-fitted prediction performance between:

```
M0 (current-state model): Y_a = alpha + beta_P P_a + beta_A A + theta'Z + error
M1 (current-state + history): Y_a = alpha + beta_P P_a + beta_A A + theta'Z + gamma'H_a + error
```

`A=1` is systemic-record and `A=0` is TACE2-only. `Z` is a locked compact set available before `P` and before `Y`: age, sex, TACE1–TACE2 interval, entry year/month, latest pre-index department, prior encounter/procedure counts and procedures, encounter-timed baseline observation/disease proxies, and pre-TACE2 laboratory values/timing for albumin, bilirubin, INR, platelet, creatinine, and sodium with missingness indicators. Remove redundant components deterministically before outcome fitting using the inherited rule (weighted absolute baseline SMD >0.10) or a compact core frozen before outcome inspection. Do not include Y, post-repeat information, post-event medications, or any selection variable derived from assay panels. If `B` is duplicated in Z, retain one deterministic copy in H and document the rule.

Fit each assay separately. The principal performance estimands are out-of-fold change in RMSE and MAE, standardized RMSE (`RMSE/SD(Y)`), cross-fitted R-squared increment, and calibration slope/intercept of M1 versus M0, with 95% uncertainty from a patient-cluster bootstrap that repeats the complete fold assignment (or a prespecified HC3-compatible interval for the coefficient plus bootstrap for performance). Report the partial history coefficients with HC3 intervals, but do not treat a nonzero coefficient as clinical utility. Native-scale performance is reported with the no-unit caveat; standardized performance avoids inventing units or thresholds. No assay pooling is permitted.

Freeze a five-fold cross-fitting split stratified by pathway where feasible. Every fold fits imputation, scaling, covariate reduction, and coefficients on training data only, then evaluates its held-out data. Report fold support separately by arm and assay. If any held-out fold has fewer than five observations in either arm, has zero outcome variance, or if the historical feature matrix is rank deficient in training, mark the relevant comparison unstable/inconclusive rather than changing folds or using a more flexible model. Do not fit machine learning as a substitute for the locked parsimonious linear model.

Use baseline-overlap and repeat-event-plus-observation weighting only as sensitivity analyses. Model repeat-event selection among all 1,810 frozen-arm patients and complete-triplet observation among selected events using pre-TACE2/event-independent information. Present unweighted complete-triplet performance first, then cross-fitted overlap/IP selection-weighted performance, positivity, balance, weight concentration, and arm-specific ESS. Weighting diagnoses differential selection; it does not recover unobserved outcomes or remove collider bias from conditioning on repeat TACE and panel observation.

## Falsification, stability, and non-tautology gates

1. **Current-state dominance gate.** M0 must be the clinically natural reference containing P and Z. A history gain is only credible if it is out-of-fold, not merely an in-sample coefficient or a gain over B alone. Report the difference between the old nesting (`B` versus `B+P`) and the repaired nesting only as a descriptive audit, never as the primary claim.
2. **Pre-event pseudo-current gate.** Where at least two earlier eligible assay observations exist, choose a locked pseudo-current timestamp strictly before the repeat event and construct a pseudo-next observation wholly before the event. Apply the same `P` versus `P+H` nesting with history ending at least 72 hours before the pseudo-current. A comparable history gain before repeat TACE supports generic serial predictability rather than event-specific information; since the primary estimand is not causal, this weakens only any event-linked interpretation, not the narrower prediction result. If support is inadequate, mark not estimable.
3. **Temporal leakage gate.** Verify no H row is in the selected P encounter, occurs at or after P, occurs after the repeat event, or is used through a patient-level duplicate join. Re-run with all same-timestamp duplicates excluded and with concordant-only handling.
4. **Timing stability.** Repeat the same nested comparison under all inherited pre/post selectors and common-timestamp pairing. A gain dependent on one window is unstable.
5. **Selection sensitivity.** Compare unweighted complete cases with cross-fitted selection/overlap weighting. Material changes, failed positivity, or low ESS mean selected-observation dependence, not a robust pathway finding.
6. **Baseline and measurement sensitivity.** Stratify descriptively by P and B quintile/standardized level; report whether the gain is concentrated in extreme or poorly measured states. Use reliability correction only if duplicate laboratory data support it; otherwise state reliability is unavailable.
7. **Influence and optimism.** Report fold-level results, patient-cluster bootstrap optimism, leave-one-out influence, leverage/Cook/DFBETA for history terms, and a prespecified 1st/99th percentile weight truncation sensitivity. A gain driven by a few patients is inconclusive.
8. **Negative-control assay/time check.** Repeat the pipeline with a locked non-target pre-event laboratory only if its exact label and adequate observations are documented before outcome analysis; otherwise do not invent a negative control. No public query or private clinical-record release is allowed.

## Interpretation branches

- **Supportive:** M1 improves standardized out-of-fold RMSE/MAE or R-squared over M0 for an assay, calibration is not degraded, the gain is not influence-dominated, survives locked timing and selection sensitivities, and passes fold/positivity checks. Conclude only that the prespecified pre-repeat history contains reproducible incremental information about the next observed same-assay panel beyond current P and Z in selected, observed repeat-TACE episodes. This is a prerequisite signal for decision-support research, not evidence for changing TACE, a threshold, treatment effect, toxicity, recovery, response, safety, benefit, or mechanism.
- **Adverse:** M1 does not improve, is less stable, loses its gain after current-state adjustment, or is matched by the pseudo-current pre-event gain. Conclude that remote history adds no robust information under this clock, or that any apparent gain is compatible with serial persistence, measurement process, regression to the mean, or selection. Do not revert to the parent claim.
- **Precise null:** If the confidence interval excludes a prespecified small standardized improvement chosen before outcome inspection and all computational gates pass, conclude no detectable history increment of that magnitude in this selected cohort. This is not equivalence, absence of hepatic biology, or proof of no clinical value; no validated unit or minimal important difference exists.
- **Selection-sensitive or unstable:** Material weighting dependence, poor overlap/ESS, fold rank failure, influence concentration, timing reversal, or missing history for most triplets yields an inconclusive/selection-sensitive result, not a positive claim.
- **Inconclusive:** Failure to reproduce 319/1,491 arms, 159/526 events, expected 40/118 and 41/119 triplets, exact clocks/joins, or sufficient pseudo-current support; broad uncertainty; or unavailable unit/clinical adjudication. Preserve the failure and do not widen windows or choose a favorable endpoint.
- **Pathway interaction:** `H×A` is exploratory and should be reported only if the pooled primary analysis is stable. With 40/41 systemic triplets, no arm-specific history claim or treatment-pathway conclusion is supportable from this dataset.

## What this cannot establish

HCC lacks laboratory units, analyzer/reference ranges, reliable inpatient/outpatient status, adjudicated acute illness, transfusion or albumin/fluid balance, imaging/tumor burden or response, performance status, complete Child–Pugh context, repeat-TACE rationale, technical details/complications, verified medication administration/adherence/dose, outside-care capture, and reliable mortality. Diagnoses have no native timestamps and lexical matching is not expert adjudication. Conditioning on a recorded repeat TACE and a measured complete panel is post-baseline selection and may induce collider bias through disease, access, clinician decisions, observation, and survival; the day-14 observation rule is not proof of survival.

An automatic verifier can check source hashes, row counts, cohort/event/triplet counts, exact joins and clocks, history-window exclusion, parser and duplicate handling, fold construction, training-only preprocessing, nested-model predictions, uncertainty, weighting, timing and pseudo-current gates, and whether conclusions match the reported branch. It cannot establish clinical importance, causal effects, treatment administration, safety, or whether a prediction would change a clinician's decision. A consequential decision-utility study requires units and reference ranges, validated clinical endpoints (for example adjudicated hepatic decompensation or clinically meaningful post-TACE outcome), treatment rationale/exposure, and prospective or externally validated evaluation of net benefit.
