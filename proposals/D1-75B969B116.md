# Early post-TACE reserve decline: corroborated decompensation or ascertainment intensity?

## Scientific deliverable and clinical importance

This is a substantive successor to [prior hypothesis]. It preserves the parent’s first high-specificity TACE index, recorded-compensated HCC cohort, six-assay baseline/early laboratory design, and the days 15–90 outcome boundary. It adds a prespecified test of outcome corroboration: does early reserve decline predict an independently stored examination corroboration of a new decompensation diagnosis, or does it mainly predict diagnosis recording when monitoring and measurement are more intense?

The clinical decision is whether an apparently compensated patient’s early post-TACE laboratory deterioration should be treated as a signal meriting prospective intensified assessment. A signal that survives a stricter, cross-table outcome definition would justify clinical adjudication and prospective validation. A signal confined to diagnosis-only records or strongly dependent on measurement opportunity would caution against interpreting it as hepatic decompensation or using it to trigger care.

The new deliverable is not a causal effect of TACE, a treatment recommendation, or a validated clinical score. It is a fitted, uncertainty-quantified comparison of the same prognostic signal across two outcome definitions:

1. the parent outcome, a new qualifying decompensation diagnosis; and
2. a strict corroborated proxy requiring that diagnosis plus a positive, same-encounter examination record in the corresponding decompensation domain.

Completion requires a cohort/flow table, exact vocabulary and assay audit, one row per patient-index, diagnosis-only and corroborated outcome counts, the parent interaction estimate, the corroborated interaction estimate when event support allows, the contrast between the two outcome associations, locked baseline-versus-temporal-model predictions, calibration/Brier/AUROC/AUPRC/decision-curve outputs, patient-bootstrap 95% intervals, and every prespecified falsification and sensitivity result. A source audit or fitted model without the outcome-definition comparison is not completion.

## Evidence boundary and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is that the local HCC snapshot contains encounter-linked procedures and diagnoses, timestamped examinations, and repeated timestamped laboratory records sufficient for an observed-care prognostic analysis. A bounded scan found that the relevant record types are populated, but it does not establish that TACE coding is complete, that diagnosis labels are adjudicated, or that examination text is clinically reliable.

The parent’s unresolved claim is that early worsening in albumin, bilirubin, INR/PT, platelets, creatinine and sodium carries incremental and baseline-reserve-dependent information about a later recorded decompensation diagnosis. The successor’s unresolved claim is narrower and more demanding:

> Among patients in the parent’s recorded-compensated, first high-specificity TACE cohort, the early-worsening × baseline-reserve interaction is positive for a new diagnosis-plus-same-encounter-examination corroboration during days 15–90, and its association is not explained away by pre-outcome measurement opportunity. Conversely, if early worsening predicts diagnosis-only events but not corroborated events, or its association collapses after process controls, the hidden hepatic-decompensation interpretation is not supported by this snapshot.

The interaction and absolute-risk contrast remain prognostic estimands. They do not estimate biological failure, the effect of TACE, monitoring effects, treatment benefit, or causal mediation.

## Outcomes: diagnosis, corroboration and ascertainment

### Parent diagnosis outcome (D)

D is the first new qualifying diagnosis during index+15 through index+90 for ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatic failure or upper gastrointestinal bleeding. Use exact normalized positive concepts and exclude uncertain, historical, hypothetical and clearly negated labels under the parent’s lexical rules. A diagnosis linked to an encounter is an observed record event, not adjudicated decompensation. Report each component and its first event time separately. ### Strict corroborated outcome (C)

C=1 only when D=1 and, for the first qualifying D encounter, the same `(Patient Master Index, Visit Number)` has an examination row with parseable `Start Time` in index+15 through index+90 whose `Examination Findings` or `Examination Diagnosis` contains a positive concept in the same domain:

- ascites: `ascites` or `peritoneal effusion`;
- encephalopathy: `hepatic encephalopathy`;
- gastrointestinal bleeding: `upper gastrointestinal bleeding`, `gastrointestinal bleeding`, `hematemesis` or `melena`; 
- hepatic failure: `hepatic failure` or an exact mapped hepatic-failure term.

The text flag must use the local conservative context parser to reject explicit negation, uncertainty, family-history, hypothetical and historical markers where supported. If domain matching is ambiguous, C=0 and the row is retained in an ambiguity audit. Same-encounter and time-bound linkage are mandatory; an examination elsewhere in the patient’s record cannot corroborate D. Examination text is an unvalidated lexical proxy, not independent clinical adjudication. The primary D analysis is retained regardless of C density.

### Treatment-concordance audit (T)

As a separate secondary descriptive endpoint, report D plus a newly started candidate treatment within [diagnosis time−1 day, diagnosis time+7 days] in `medications`: for ascites, a mapped diuretic/albumin pattern; for encephalopathy, lactulose/rifaximin; for gastrointestinal bleeding, octreotide/somatostatin or another prespecified mapped pattern; for hepatic failure, albumin/vasopressor-related patterns only if clinically reviewed. Use exact drug dictionaries and report each drug separately. Because these medications have multiple indications and exposure may be a response to D, T is not an independent endpoint, is not used to replace C, and is not interpreted as treatment benefit.

For each patient report one mutually exclusive outcome category: no D, D without C, or C. T is an additional flag. The principal corroboration comparison is C versus D; the D-without-C group is the ascertainment-discordant category.

## Estimands and analysis

The primary interaction estimand remains the parent’s continuous interaction. Fit a prespecified regularized logistic model for D:

`logit P(D=1) = alpha + beta1*early_worsening + beta2*baseline_reserve + beta3*(early_worsening x baseline_reserve) + covariates`.

Covariates are age, sex, index department, prior HCC history, baseline six component values, baseline availability/recency, early assay delays/counts, and index-encounter opportunity variables defined before day 15. The primary scientific quantity is beta3 and model-based absolute-risk contrasts for a one-SD early worsening at baseline-reserve quartiles learned in training data. Quartiles are descriptive; do not select a threshold from the test set.

Fit the identical model for C only if the complete-case C event count passes the support rule in the evidence note. If it does not, estimate C incidence, C/D concordance, and exact-binomial or bootstrap uncertainty descriptively without unstable regression. The key corroboration contrast is:

- Delta-interaction = beta3(C) − beta3(D), with a patient bootstrap that refits both models within each resample; and
- the risk ratio/absolute-risk difference comparing predicted risk under early worsening versus no early worsening for C and D at prespecified baseline-reserve quartiles.

Because C is a subset of D, do not treat beta3(C) and beta3(D) as independent estimates. The bootstrap resamples patient-index rows and respects the paired outcome structure. A two-sided 95% interval is reported; no p-value-only decision is used.

As a complementary multinomial ascertainment analysis, model the mutually exclusive categories no D, D without C and C using the same exposure and covariates when counts permit. Report early-worsening and interaction contrasts for D-without-C versus no D and C versus no D. This directly tests whether early worsening is preferentially associated with corroborated events or only with diagnosis recording. It is descriptive of observed records, not a measurement-error correction.

Process/ascertainment analyses are prespecified:

- Add only information available by index+14: early laboratory count, assay availability, assay delays, number of encounters in index−14 through index+14, index discharge status/date and examination count in the early window.
- Repeat the complete-window analysis restricted to patients with a qualifying encounter opportunity in the outcome window, while clearly labeling this as a selection sensitivity because the event can itself generate an encounter.
- Report the distribution of outcome-window encounters, examinations and labs by no D, D-without-C and C, but do not condition the primary model on post-event counts.
- Use an unrelated/non-hepatic new diagnosis category as a negative-control ascertainment stress test only if its vocabulary can be frozen without post hoc selection; early worsening should not show a large specific interaction there if the result is hepatic rather than generic surveillance.
- Compare the strict C definition with a broader examination vocabulary and with the dated medication T flag, without promoting whichever version confirms the hypothesis.

Baseline comparison: the parent’s static regularized logistic regression uses no early labs and predicts D (and C if supported). The substantive learned alternative is patient-level temporal gradient-boosted trees over ordered observations from index−14 through index+14 for the six assays, with assay identity, value, time-from-index, baseline/early flag, missingness, counts, age, sex, prior history and index context. Fit/tune only on a patient-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split, stratified by outcome category; use a later-index holdout if event counts permit, calibrate on validation, and lock test predictions. Cap at 500 trees and 10 configurations. Evaluate both models on the same locked patients and endpoint using Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC, AUPRC, decision-curve net benefit and bootstrap intervals. Feature importance is exploratory only. The learned model can reveal nonlinear thresholds and temporal patterns that the composite score loses, but a gain driven by assay counts or missingness is evidence of measurement-process dependence, not biological corroboration. The interaction estimate comes from the prespecified regression, not from post hoc model explanations.

A latent GP/mixed-effects trajectory model is deferred because this question needs an outcome-definition contrast and it adds missing-data/local-optimum assumptions before event validity is established. A transformer/generative sequence model is deferred because additional sequence capacity does not resolve unvalidated outcome text and could amplify surveillance shortcuts. These are question-specific deferrals, not blanket exclusions of neural networks or GPU use. Revisit either only after adequate repeated trajectories, corroborated events and a scientific need for patient-specific latent slopes are documented.

## Uncertainty, falsification and interpretation

Use patient bootstrap resampling with a fixed seed and at least 1,000 successful replicates where model support permits. Report event counts, missingness, assay mapping uncertainty, outcome-category counts and follow-up opportunity imbalance before estimates. Do not use asymptotic intervals alone for sparse C. Preserve split seed, source/schema hashes, filters, row counts, raw-to-normalized vocabularies, model artifacts, bootstrap outputs and software versions in the workspace.

Supportive result: the early-worsening × baseline-reserve interaction is directionally positive for D and C; the C interval excludes zero or a prespecified clinically negligible interaction margin; C/D concordance is not trivial; the C association is not materially erased by pre-day-15 process controls; and the learned trajectory model does not obtain its signal primarily from measurement-count/missingness features. Locked-test calibration/Brier or decision-curve improvement over the static baseline is supportive only when uncertainty is shown. This supports incremental prediction of a stricter observed-care corroboration proxy and motivates adjudication; it does not prove true hepatic decompensation, causality or utility.

Adverse result: C is null/reverse while D is positive; D-without-C dominates and the association attenuates after process controls; the signal changes materially with lexical vocabulary; or boosting gains are confined to counts, encounter opportunity or missingness. This supports ascertainment intensity or coding as a plausible explanation and refutes the claim that the parent laboratory signal is independently corroborated in this snapshot. It does not prove that biological reserve decline is irrelevant.

Inconclusive result: fewer than the support threshold of complete-case corroborated events, wide paired intervals, unstable text mapping, no reliable complete-window negatives, assay ambiguity, severe process imbalance, failed later-index holdout, or inability to obtain clinically interpretable review. This means the data cannot resolve the question. Do not collapse C and D or claim a negative biological result.
