## Scientific repair and deliverable

The parent question and operational endpoint are unchanged:

> Among patients observable at day 42 after a first locally recorded exact-code TACE and without an exact-code repeat before day 42, does the recorded assay-state channel at day 42 add held-out information beyond matched observation and care-process information for the first later exact-code repeat TACE in ([43,181))?

The remaining ambiguity is temporal. A day-42 assay increment could be a reusable recorded patient-state signal, or it could mark a near-term planned return and the intensity of the workflow that produces a repeat code. A single pooled ([43,181)) loss difference cannot show whether the increment is concentrated in the early part of follow-up. This child adds a prespecified competing-risk/multi-state decomposition of the same endpoint:

- (E), the first strict-code repeat with (43 leq Delta < 91) days, is the early operational repeat state.
- (L), the first strict-code repeat with (91 leq Delta < 181) days and no prior (E), is the later operational repeat state.
- The primary endpoint remains (Y=Emathbin{lor}L), the first strict-code repeat with (43 leq Delta <181). No early repeat is reclassified as a primary event: patients with a repeat at (0<Delta<43) remain excluded from the day-42 landmark risk set and are retained in the early-repeat audit.

“Early workflow” is an interpretation to test, not an observed intent label. A pattern is workflow/lead-time-consistent only when early risk is mainly explained by (P_{

The new scientific deliverable is a frozen, patient-held-out pair of cause-specific hazards/CIFs and channel increments for (E) and (L), alongside the inherited pooled endpoint contrast. - removing ((35,42]) records and truncating process features at day 28;
- within-patient assay timestamp permutations preserving assay/result multisets and process counts;
- numeric assay shuffles within calendar and baseline-history blocks;
- process-mark permutations within calendar and event-count strata;
- capacity-matched process-only models and assay-payload-only models;
- development outcome-label permutations scored against untouched true test labels;
- undated strict-TACE exclusion, alternate valid `obs_end), and exact-code-only versus separately frozen high-specificity alias sensitivity.

Supportive evidence for a reusable recorded-state increment requires a simultaneous lower 95% bound for (D_Y^{state}>0), calibrated B, consistent ordinary and observation-weighted direction, and persistence of (D_L^{state}) (or both timing heads) under the seven-day control. A state-versus-workflow result is specifically adverse to the state interpretation when early improvement is reproduced by timestamp/value permutations, is no larger than the process increment, or disappears under lead-time/process truncation while the late conditional state increment is unsupported. Evidence for workflow/lead-time is still descriptive and does not establish intent. Mixed early-positive/late-negative findings should route to workflow/scheduling linkage; early- and late-positive findings route to imaging/intent adjudication only if calibration and support pass. An imprecise or support-failing contrast is inconclusive, not a refutation.
