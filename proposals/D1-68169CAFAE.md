# Time-weighted hypotension burden and subsequent creatinine-based AKI in eICU

## Hypothesis and clinical importance

Among adults with an early ICU hypotensive episode, does the time-weighted burden of MAP below 65 mmHg predict subsequent creatinine-based AKI beyond the MAP nadir, baseline kidney function, and the density of blood-pressure observations?

This matters because a nadir can miss prolonged moderate hypotension, while a duration signal could identify patients who need closer renal monitoring or prompt review of hemodynamic management. The claim is prognostic, not a causal treatment target. Resolving it would determine whether duration is a reproducible risk descriptor or merely a marker of illness severity, treatment, or documentation practice.

The strongest available evidence supports that hypotension and dynamic MAP patterns are clinically relevant, but does not establish that burden adds stable information beyond nadir in this eICU population. Flick et al. quantified postoperative hypotension duration/severity with hourly MAP monitoring and exploratorily associated it with AKI [K1]. Ao et al. reported associations between dynamic/individualized MAP features and kidney outcomes in older hospitalized patients, while acknowledging confounding and the need for external validation [K2]. Muralidharan et al. reported that an ICU AKI prevention bundle was associated with lower moderate-to-severe AKI and resource use but not overall AKI or mortality, without isolating hypotension duration [K3]. The unresolved claim is whether burden is a reproducible incremental prognostic signal after explicit control for nadir and observation density.

Include adults (`age` parsed as >=18; exclude nonnumeric or `> 89`), first ICU stay per `patienthealthsystemstayid), with a valid MAP in `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` or `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` during the first 24 ICU hours. Require at least one MAP <65 mmHg and at least six valid MAP observations. Exclude unavailable baseline creatinine, documented pre-existing dialysis/renal replacement therapy, and fewer than 24 hours of follow-up. Define the index episode as the first contiguous MAP<65 interval after the first qualifying MAP, with a 30-minute grace period for missing observations; repeat with no grace, 60-minute grace, and thresholds 60/70.

## Exposure, outcome, and analysis

Create a 5-minute grid from the first qualifying MAP through 24 hours. Use last observed MAP without extrapolation beyond 30 minutes. Primary exposure is the fraction of covered time below 65 mmHg; secondary exposure is area below 65 mmHg. Also compute nadir, median, observation count, covered fraction, median gap, and periodic/aperiodic source mix.

Primary outcome is incident creatinine-based AKI through 72 hours after index episode: increase >=0.3 mg/dL from the minimum creatinine in the prior 24 hours or >=1.5 times that baseline, using numeric `labname=creatinine`, `labresult`, `labresultoffset`, and `labmeasurenamesystem=mg/dL`. Stage 2/3 is secondary. This is explicitly not full KDIGO AKI because reliable urine-output adjudication is unavailable.

Baseline covariates are age, sex, ethnicity, hospital, unit type, admission source, baseline creatinine, and pre-index MAP/heart-rate summaries. Treatment descriptors (time to first vasopressor, class, dose/rate, duration) are reported but not adjusted as post-exposure mediators in the primary model. Include hospital fixed effects or hospital-clustered uncertainty.

Simple baseline: multivariable logistic regression for AKI with nadir, baseline creatinine, age, sex, unit type, hospital, and MAP observation density. Compare burden-only, nadir-only, and burden+nadir using prespecified likelihood-ratio tests, adjusted odds ratios, calibration, and hospital-clustered bootstrap intervals.

Substantive alternative: a regularized gradient-boosted tree model on the same 5-minute summaries, with nested hospital-level train/test split and time-ordered split within hospitals. Compare discrimination, calibration, and decision-curve net benefit. It can reveal nonlinear burden–nadir interactions and heterogeneity by baseline kidney function or treatment timing that the linear baseline loses, but cannot identify a mechanism. A discrete-time survival alternative with cumulative burden and time-varying treatment is deferred unless event timing and missingness are adequate.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: burden adds a prespecified incremental association (upper 95% CI for adjusted OR excludes 1 and likelihood-ratio test favors burden+nadir), with stable direction across hospitals, thresholds, grace periods, and observation-density strata, and the learned model improves calibration/decision value without relying on post-index treatment. This supports a prognostic duration signal, not a treatment target.

Adverse: burden adds no information beyond nadir/density, reverses after density adjustment, or is confined to one hospital/source. This favors nadir, severity, or observation-practice explanations and stops the duration claim.

Inconclusive: wide intervals, sparse events, or materially different results under creatinine ascertainment and grace/threshold choices. Report uncertainty; do not call an imprecise null a refutation.

Unavailable evidence includes validated full KDIGO urine-output adjudication, reliable pre-ICU baseline creatinine for every stay, clinician-confirmed AKI, complete nephrotoxin exposure, and causal effect of raising MAP. Clinical adjudication or a prospective/controlled study is required for those stronger claims.

## Demonstration dispositions and alternatives

The three research demonstrations were not reproduced. The Bayesian/natural-history and multimodal cancer demonstrations are not pursued because their central dependencies and clinical questions do not match this eICU hemodynamic question; a lab-only adaptation would not answer the duration-versus-nadir uncertainty. The organ-aging demonstration is also not pursued because its proteomic/aging-clock inputs are not the relevant eICU variables. The selected eICU adaptation is a bounded prognostic study, not a reproduction.

The simple logistic model is retained as the interpretable primary test. The learned alternative is selected because it can expose nonlinear interactions and transportability failures that a minimum-MAP summary loses. The mechanistic time-to-event model is deferred, not rejected: revisit it if the primary outcome has sufficient event timing and missingness. A GPU is not required for the planned tabular fits; future solver planning may use CPU with the configured 16-CPU/262,144-MiB envelope, while any repeated neural sequence model would require a separate bounded GPU feasibility check.

## Required solver deliverable

The solver must construct the cohort and 5-minute exposure grid, estimate the primary adjusted burden association and compare burden+nadir with nadir-only, produce hospital-held-out and time-ordered evaluation of the learned alternative, and report uncertainty, calibration, decision value, sensitivity analyses, and a conclusion tied to computed outputs. It must not report a causal treatment effect or full KDIGO AKI claim.
