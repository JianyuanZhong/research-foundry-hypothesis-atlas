# HCC early trajectory versus care-process artifact (episode 19 repair)

## Unresolved question and clinical importance
Among adults admitted with an exact documented diagnosis of 肝细胞癌 (HCC), does an ordered 7-day within-assay laboratory trajectory add information about a subsequent non-routine inpatient procedure beyond an admission snapshot that represents treatment indication and documentation intensity? The key rival is that apparent trajectory value reflects routine HCC treatment, care intensity, or documentation rather than evolving patient state. Resolving this matters because it determines whether temporal monitoring could inform expert review, while avoiding an unsupported claim that a procedure equals death, ICU admission, or clinical harm.

## Hypothesis and evidence boundary
The hypothesis is predictive, not causal: a time-aware representation of liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker measurements from admission through day 7 will improve held-out prediction of a prespecified non-routine post-window procedure over a snapshot model, with uncertainty excluding a trivial margin; the incremental value should be smaller for routine HCC treatment procedures and absent in a timing-shifted negative control. The strongest supported claim is only that the HCC snapshot contains joinable encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, and procedure records with substantial timed procedure support. No fitted effect is claimed.

## Frozen endpoint and audit
Use endpoint dictionary version `hcc-nonroutine-v1`. Normalize only by trimming whitespace and lower-casing for matching; retain original labels. Eligible procedures have parseable `开始时间`, are strictly after `入院时间 + 7 days`, and are not after `出院时间` when discharge is available. ## Experiment and alternatives
Cohort: exact HCC diagnosis, valid admission time, and one of `肝肿瘤内科, 肝胆肿瘤与肝移植外科, 肝胆肿瘤内科, 肝肿瘤外科`; unrestricted exact-diagnosis cohort is sensitivity. Feature window is admission through admission+7 days and no later than discharge. Preserve specimen type, time since admission, missingness, and parseable raw `定量结果`; do not infer units.

Baseline: penalized logistic regression with age, sex, department, first available assay values, time-zero routine-treatment/medication indicators, and documentation counts. Alternative: patient-level ordered sequence/state-space model with the same covariates and within-assay temporal information. Split by `患者主索引`, use nested time-aware validation and repeated bootstrap uncertainty. Evaluate held-out discrimination, calibration, and incremental performance. The alternative can reveal persistence, ordering, and cross-marker interactions lost by a snapshot; it is not chosen for complexity. A larger neural sequence model is deferred unless event support and runtime justify it.

A timing-shifted negative control shifts procedure labels by a fixed 14-day offset while preserving labels and patient-level counts, or evaluates a pre-window outcome with the same feature window. A within-patient laboratory-order permutation tests whether ordering rather than measurement count matters.

Supportive: nontrivial held-out trajectory advantage for the non-routine endpoint, uncertainty excluding a prespecified trivial margin, stability across department/documentation/missingness/assay sensitivities, and absence in the timing-shifted control. Adverse: no improvement, degradation, advantage confined to routine procedures, disappearance after process adjustment, or positive timing control. Inconclusive: sparse events, unstable dictionary categories, excessive untimed records, imprecise intervals, or inability to adjudicate clinical meaning.

## Evidence and limitations
[K1] Yang et al. (2026) reported that routine admission bilirubin, direct bilirubin, AST, and INR predicted day-7 total bilirubin in a small HBV cirrhosis cohort, supporting a short-term laboratory-response comparator; it does not establish HCC prognosis or causality. [K2] Bui et al. (2026) reported that stage, microvascular invasion, resection margin, and Child-Pugh status predicted postoperative disease-free survival in a retrospective HCC resection cohort, bounding the importance of static factors; it does not test early inpatient trajectories or escalation. [K3] Millarch et al. (2026) reported that a temporal EHR model using ordered vitals, labs, and medications predicted mortality across a nonlinear trauma trajectory and outperformed static severity scores, motivating the sequence alternative; it is not HCC evidence and does not transfer automatically.
