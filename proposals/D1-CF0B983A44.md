# Provisional hypothesis: duration versus nadir of early ICU hypotension for subsequent AKI

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU admission with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. Cumulative duration is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Grounding and unresolved question

The clinical opening is that a single nadir may be transient or artifact-prone, while sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved claim is whether duration and trajectory add clinically meaningful information about subsequent AKI beyond nadir after timing and ascertainment constraints are made explicit. The available eICU data can support a bounded observational comparison if the exact local columns, joins, timestamps, and creatinine/urine semantics are verified.

The strongest claim currently supported is only that this is a plausible, falsifiable question grounded in the existing eICU candidate lineage; no computed effect, causal mechanism, or treatment recommendation is claimed here.

Use the configured eICU source files and catalog bindings, with patient/admission-level joins only through documented keys. The primary exposure is cumulative time with systolic BP <65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, derived from valid BP rows. The primary outcome is a creatinine-defined AKI event in 24–72 hours, anchored to a pre-exposure baseline creatinine when available. Compare a simple nadir-plus-covariates baseline with a time-aware duration model using the same inputs, split, and held-out evaluation. Report uncertainty and hospital-held-out performance.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results would show a reproducible duration association and incremental information beyond nadir in held-out hospitals, with uncertainty compatible with a clinically meaningful effect. Adverse results would show no reproducible incremental signal, a reversal, or dependence on measurement intensity/artifact controls. Inconclusive results would be imprecise estimates or failure to verify the required local fields; they would not refute the hypothesis. A null result would not establish that hypotension duration is irrelevant.

## Alternatives and compute

The simple baseline is a regularized regression or survival model using nadir, baseline severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The substantive alternative is a time-aware model using the ordered BP trajectory and exposure duration on the same target and split. The alternative can reveal whether timing and persistence matter beyond a single nadir, information the baseline loses. CPU is likely adequate for bounded tabular fits; GPU is not required unless a larger sequence model is chosen.
