# Prospective full-verification study of whether an EHR restart captures intent-qualified pump-native rescue

## Decision question, supported evidence, and untested claim

The clinical decision is not which vasopressor should be stopped first. The prerequisite decision is whether a binary “stopped-agent restart within six hours” derived from EHR infusion transactions is sufficiently complete, accurate, and symmetric to be an endpoint in a later discontinuation-order trial.

All inherited negative findings are frozen. Among 6,040 MAP-eligible repeated landmarks, assigned actions were rare: 211 norepinephrine-first (NE-first; 3.49%) and 167 vasopressin-first (VP-first; 2.76%), both below the 5% gate; 5,638 no-action landmarks are not controls. The inherited observed-stop cohort has 84 NE-first and 53 VP-first episodes, with 49 and 24 determinate composite failures. Observed stopping order is not assigned treatment, the arms are not exchangeable, and these counts do not support a direct comparison. Of 73 raw failures among 136 determinate episodes, 59 include a charted stopped-agent restart and 36 are restart-only. Mechanical circulatory support (MCS) is unknown in 137/137 episodes. The compact temporal models had negligible Brier improvement (M1 about 0.000174 over M0; M2 about 0.000111 over M1, below the 0.01 gate) and adverse later transport; no score or subgroup advances.

The complete inherited transaction audit also remains frozen. It used all 137 inherited episodes, with no episode or row sampling after the declared stay/item/time filters, and reproduced all 137 boundaries and 59 raw restarts. Symmetric continuity rules invalidated 0, 1, 13, 16, or 23 original stop indices; the inherited chain-plus-dose rule invalidated 13/137 indices and 13/59 restart indices (10 NE-first, 3 VP-first). All 13 were `Paused` boundaries; nevertheless, neither `Paused` nor `Stopped`, shared order chains, dose concordance, caregiver identity, `storetime`, or MAP chronology establishes pump delivery or clinician intent. The audit supports label instability only, not a false-positive rate.

The unresolved, prospective hypothesis is:

> In each observed first-stop arm, the episode-level EHR restart label has both positive predictive value (PPV) and sensitivity above 0.80 for a prespecified intent-qualified pump-native rescue within six hours, and the between-arm absolute difference is less than 0.10 for each metric.

This child makes sensitivity co-primary with PPV. A PPV-only study could declare success while missing many pump-native rescues that never create a new EHR interval, particularly if omission differs by stopped drug, site, or pump/EHR interface. That would make a later trial endpoint directionally biased even if most recorded restarts were genuine.

## Why episode-level PPV and sensitivity are co-primary

The contemplated endpoint is binary per stop episode: did rescue occur within six hours? Therefore episode-level PPV and sensitivity directly answer future endpoint suitability. Event-level one-to-one matching is secondary because it identifies timestamp, duplicate, and segmentation errors but is not the trial endpoint. The paired difference in EHR-positive and reference-positive incidence is also secondary: equal false-positive and false-negative counts can cancel, yielding a small net discrepancy despite poor endpoint validity. Specificity, negative predictive value, agreement, event timing, and paired prevalence discrepancy are important diagnostics but cannot replace the co-primary PPV/sensitivity gates.

## Locked prospective design

### Sites, run-in, and consecutive population

Recruit at least six adult ICUs using synchronized smart-pump, monitor, and EHR exports. Before validation, each site processes a fresh run-in of 50 consecutive qualifying first-stop episodes. The definitions below, including the five-minute off and rescue thresholds, are locked before run-in; run-in may repair clock mapping or export infrastructure but may not alter labels. No arm-specific accuracy is opened. A site proceeds only if at least 95% of run-in windows have a unique patient-channel-drug linkage, measured cross-system clock offset, and complete pump export from two hours before through six hours after index (or through earlier death/ICU exit), and at least 90% have a contemporaneous intent source. A failed site repairs capture and repeats with 50 new episodes; a second failure ends that site's contribution as feasibility-only. Run-in episodes are excluded from validation.

Validation enrolls every consecutive eligible person, irrespective of whether the EHR later records a restart. There is no case-control, enriched-positive, or two-phase verification sample. Only the first qualifying episode for a person during the study is included.

Eligibility is determined from information available by the initial cessation:

1. age at least 18 years, alive and in an adult ICU;
2. pump-supported concurrent NE and VP delivery for at least six continuous hours immediately before cessation;
3. at the start of the final pre-index 30 minutes, NE greater than 0 and no more than 0.20 mcg/kg/min and VP greater than 0 and no more than 0.04 U/min, with neither drug increased during those 30 minutes;
4. no pump-delivered epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine during the prior two hours;
5. at least three valid MAP observations spanning at least three of the four prior 30-minute bins, including the final bin; median MAP at least 65 mmHg and minimum MAP at least 55 mmHg; and
6. an observed, non-simultaneous first cessation of one study drug while the comparator continues pump-native delivery.

A simultaneous cessation is one in which both drugs cross below the delivery threshold within five minutes; it is excluded and counted. Procedure/transport pauses known before index are excluded and counted. MCS is not inferred from a missing record and is not silently excluded: actual device type/state is captured and reported as a prespecified stratum. Eligibility is prospective and independent of any future EHR restart, rescue, MAP, death, exit, or capture result.

### Independent index and observation window

The reference index `t0` is the first pump-native timestamp at which the first-stopped drug falls below its agent-specific delivery threshold and remains below it for at least five continuous minutes while the comparator remains above threshold. The delivery thresholds are locked at 0.005 mcg/kg/min for NE and 0.005 U/min for VP, using the pump drug library, concentration, patient weight, channel identity, and recorded delivered-rate stream. A programmed rate without a linkable drug/channel/concentration is not accepted as delivery.

The primary window is `(t0, min(t0 + 6 hours, ICU exit, death)]`. Death or ICU exit closes rather than erases the window. An export or linkage gap in the observable portion makes the reference label indeterminate under the rules below; it is not coded negative. Analyses at pump-off thresholds of 1, 10, 30, and 60 minutes, and rescue delivery thresholds of any positive rate and 0.01 in the corresponding agent unit, are prespecified sensitivity analyses and cannot replace the five-minute/0.005 primary definition.

### Index test: EHR restart label

The index test is generated automatically and frozen before reference adjudication. Apply the MIMIC-compatible normalization symmetrically:

- NE: item 221906; retain mcg/kg/min; divide mcg/min by contemporaneous valid weight 30–300 kg; reject normalized values above 5 mcg/kg/min.
- VP: item 222315; divide units/hour by 60; retain units/min; reject values above 0.2 units/min.

An EHR-positive episode has at least one new normalized positive interval for the stopped drug with `starttime` in the primary window and `endtime > starttime`. A row already spanning `t0` does not become a new restart; if pump-native rescue occurs while one EHR interval spans the initial stop and rescue, the EHR episode is negative and can be a false negative. `orderid`, `linkorderid`, `statusdescription`, `storetime`, caregiver, `isopenbag`, and `continueinnextdept` are not used to repair the primary label. The first qualifying EHR start is retained for secondary event matching. Clock comparison uses the prospectively measured mapping but does not move events across the six-hour boundary; boundary-near cases within the measured 99th-percentile clock error are reported separately.

### Target reference event: intent-qualified pump-native rescue

A reference-positive episode requires both components within the primary window:

1. **Pump-native resumption:** after the qualifying off interval, the stopped drug again reaches at least 0.005 mcg/kg/min (NE) or 0.005 U/min (VP) for at least five continuous minutes, with linkable drug, concentration, patient, channel, and delivered-rate records and no unresolved export gap; and
2. **Rescue intent:** two independent critical-care/pharmacy adjudicators determine from timestamped, contemporaneous sources that the resumption was deliberately undertaken to treat or prevent worsening hypotension or impaired perfusion after the cessation. Routine bag/syringe replacement, channel or pump exchange, transfer continuation, order reconciliation, a technical/procedural pause, correction of a mistaken stop, or uninterrupted delivery segmented only in documentation is reference-negative for intent-qualified rescue.

The pump-resumption algorithm is executed before adjudication. Adjudicators receive the pump trace, monitor physiology, cointerventions, workflow events, and contemporaneous intent documentation, but not the derived EHR index-test label, inputevent chain/status diagnostics, aggregate arm accuracy, or each other's label. Drug identity cannot be hidden, but aggregate arm results remain blinded. They classify rescue-positive, pump resumption without rescue intent, uninterrupted/technical or workflow transition, no pump resumption, clock/export failure, or intent-indeterminate. A third adjudicator resolves disagreement without seeing aggregate accuracy. Report raw agreement and Gwet AC1 and kappa. If raw agreement is below 85% or both chance-corrected coefficients are below 0.70 after the first 100 validation episodes, suspend adjudication, clarify the manual without viewing accuracy, and restart primary validation on new episodes; the old episodes remain feasibility data. A second failure makes the study inconclusive.

A secondary pump-only reference removes the intent component. It separates EHR-to-pump technical validity from rescue-intent validity; it cannot substitute for the primary target.

### Required prospective data unavailable in MIMIC

For every enrolled episode collect synchronized, source-identifiable data:

- pump/channel/patient identifiers; drug-library identity; concentration; programmed and delivered rates; start/stop/pause/resume/bolus/alarm events; channel association; second-level timestamps; export gaps and downtime;
- bag/syringe identifiers where available; replacement, empty-container, line/concentration change, channel/pump handoff, procedure/transport pause, transfer, and medication reconciliation;
- the complete EHR administration/transaction audit trail, including the fields used in the retrospective audit;
- timestamped intended cessation, permanent stop versus temporary pause/titration, restart decision, reason, and relationship to hypotension/perfusion concern; unknown intent remains unknown;
- continuous/high-frequency MAP with source and quality flags, all vasoactive channels, fluids/boluses and cointerventions, ICU exit, and death;
- actual MCS device type, insertion/removal, active/standby state, flow/support setting, and device/registry confirmation; and
- mappings and measured offsets among pump, monitor, EHR, and adjudication clocks, including time zone/daylight-saving handling.

MIMIC-IV core lacks pump-native logs, raw waveforms in this configured snapshot, a validated intent field, and a source that can establish MCS absence. Its input transactions and intermittent MAP cannot supply this reference standard.

## Estimands, denominators, and analysis

For arm `a`, let `E=1` denote the episode-level EHR restart label and `R=1` the intent-qualified reference rescue.

Co-primary estimands, separately for NE-first and VP-first, are:

- `PPV_a = P(R=1 | E=1, arm=a)`, denominator all EHR-positive validation episodes in arm `a`;
- `Sensitivity_a = P(E=1 | R=1, arm=a)`, denominator all reference-positive validation episodes in arm `a`;
- `Delta_PPV = PPV_NE - PPV_VP`; and
- `Delta_Sens = Sensitivity_NE - Sensitivity_VP`.

All episodes are submitted to the same reference process. For conservative decision bounds, an unresolved EHR-positive episode contributes no true positive in the PPV numerator but remains in its denominator; an unresolved EHR-negative episode is added as a potential false negative in the sensitivity denominator. These metric-specific worst-case assignments are reported alongside determinate-only estimates. Endpoint suitability is judged on the conservative estimands, preventing capture failure from creating apparent success.

Secondary outputs are the complete determinate confusion matrices; specificity and negative predictive value; pump-only accuracy; indeterminate fractions by EHR label and arm; McNemar paired discordance and `(EHR-positive proportion - reference-positive proportion)` with confidence interval; event counts; and timing error. For event-level analysis, all pump-native resumptions and all new EHR positive intervals in the six-hour window are one-to-one matched to minimize absolute time difference subject to a locked ±10-minute caliper. Unmatched pump events, unmatched EHR events, duplicates, and median/95th-percentile absolute lag are reported. This matching cannot override episode labels.

Prespecified documentation strata are boundary `Paused` versus `Stopped`, shared versus distinct chain, R2-compatible versus incompatible, and presence versus absence of pre-rescue MAP deterioration. They test transport of the frozen audit diagnostics; they do not clean the endpoint or define treatment effects.

### Confidence and multiplicity rules

For each arm and each accuracy metric, success requires the one-sided 95% score lower confidence bound to exceed 0.80. For each arm difference, use two one-sided tests at alpha 0.05; equivalence requires the 90% Miettinen-Newcombe score confidence interval to lie wholly within `(-0.10, 0.10)`. The conclusion is an intersection-union decision: all four arm-specific accuracy gates and both equivalence gates must pass. No multiplicity adjustment is needed to claim the conjunction because failure of any component defeats the global claim; all component estimates and intervals are nevertheless reported. Secondary analyses are descriptive with 95% intervals; Holm adjustment is used only for the four prespecified site-interaction tests (PPV and sensitivity by arm). No early accuracy success or outcome-based futility analysis is allowed.

### Sample size, accrual, and stopping

Accrue until both arms have at least 220 EHR-positive episodes for PPV and at least 220 reference-positive episodes for sensitivity. All consecutive episodes enrolled through the common database-lock date are analyzed, including excess episodes in the faster-accruing arm. With 220 denominator events, at least 186 correct classifications are required for a one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound above 0.80. If true accuracy is 0.90, binomial planning power for this gate is about 99.6%. Under equal arm accuracy of 0.90, a fixed-220-per-arm planning approximation gives about 93.3% power for one metric's 0.10 equivalence test. The latter is a Wald planning approximation only; final inference uses the locked score method. These are component operating characteristics, not a claimed probability that the full conjunctive study succeeds.

Operational accrual is capped at the earliest of 1,000 analyzed episodes in either arm, 2,000 total validation episodes, or 36 months after the first site enters validation. The retrospective EHR-positive proportions (46/84 NE-first and 13/53 VP-first) are planning signals only and do not estimate prospective pump rescue prevalence. If all four metric denominators are not reached by the cap, the result is inconclusive and exact attained precision is reported. Quarterly reports disclose only enrollment, arm counts, metric-denominator counts, capture completeness, and site concentration; labels and accuracy remain blinded. There is no sample-size re-estimation from observed accuracy.

A participating-site claim requires at least six sites, both arms represented at at least four sites, and no site contributing more than 30% of any arm-specific PPV or sensitivity denominator. Site-specific estimates are shown when the metric denominator is at least 20. Prespecified binomial mixed models estimate site variance and leave-one-site-out estimates. Material heterogeneity is defined as either an adjusted site accuracy range greater than 0.15 or an adequately supported site estimate below 0.70. If the pooled conjunctive gates pass but material heterogeneity is present, the result is adverse for a transportable raw-EHR endpoint and at most supportive for the identified site/pump interfaces. If site prediction intervals are too wide to classify this rule, transport is inconclusive rather than homogeneous.

Stop a site's accrual immediately for unresolved patient-channel linkage, clock failure, or pump export failure affecting more than 5% of its latest 50 episodes; resume only after a new passing run-in. Stop the whole study as operationally inconclusive after two adjudication-reliability failures, inability to maintain at least four validation sites, or the administrative cap. These rules do not permit lowering thresholds, changing windows, dropping EHR negatives, or retrospectively selecting a continuity rule.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive for endpoint suitability:** all four conservative arm-specific PPV/sensitivity lower bounds exceed 0.80; both 90% arm-difference intervals lie inside ±0.10; metric denominators and capture/reliability gates are met; no material site/interface heterogeneity is present; and primary conclusions are not reversed by clock-error and off-duration sensitivity analyses. This supports only the intent-qualified restart component under the validated capture systems. It does not validate other composite components, establish safety or efficacy, identify a preferred stopping order, or justify an EHR-only endpoint at unstudied interfaces.

**Adverse:** any completed arm-specific PPV or sensitivity gate fails, either arm difference is non-equivalent, paired errors are materially directional, event matching reveals systematic missed or spurious events, or material site heterogeneity shows that raw EHR behavior is not transportable. A low sensitivity result is decisive even with high PPV: pump-native rescue missed by EHR falsifies endpoint completeness. A low PPV result means recorded restarts often do not represent intent-qualified rescue. Future work must use pump-native/adjudicated rescue or validate a new rule in an independent cohort; this study cannot choose a favorable retrospective reconstruction post hoc.

**Inconclusive:** denominator targets are not reached; confidence intervals cross a required boundary; indeterminacy, capture, linkage, clock, or adjudication gates fail; site concentration or prediction intervals prevent transport assessment; or VP-first accrual remains inadequate. Inconclusive is not evidence of accuracy or symmetry and does not authorize relaxed margins.

Even a supportive diagnostic study does not show that rescue causes harm, that stopping order causes rescue, that either order is preferable, or that randomization is safe. A later intervention study requires expert review, equipoise, protocolized ascertainment, and independent clinical outcomes.
