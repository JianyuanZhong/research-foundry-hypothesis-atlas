# MerTK remission signal: cell state or macrophage-state composition?

**Status: proposal only.** No Harbor computation or reanalysis has been run here. This draft is an evidence-bounded child of `[prior hypothesis]`; it changes the question from a MerTK inhibitor therapeutic-window screen to the upstream ambiguity in the remission-associated expression observation. It does not claim new evidence or clinical efficacy.

## Hypothesis and decision

**Falsifiable hypothesis:** Among synovial tissue macrophages from RA donors, the remission-versus-treatment-naive difference in overall MERTK expression is substantially explained by a higher abundance of a MerTK-high macrophage state, rather than a uniform within-state increase of MERTK across comparable states. A competing cell-intrinsic explanation predicts higher MERTK per cell within donor-reproducible macrophage states, even after standardizing state proportions.

The direction matters because composition-driven association would prioritize state abundance, recruitment/survival, or tissue-niche regulation as the next target-development question; a stable within-state shift would instead justify testing state-specific regulation of MerTK. Either result can help choose the next functional experiment. Neither cross-sectional result demonstrates that MerTK causes remission, that macrophages are protective, or that inhibition has a therapeutic window.

**Strongest claim supported by inspected/available evidence:** The selected parent and the public study associated with E-MTAB-8322 report remission-associated MerTK-positive synovial macrophage populations. The E-MTAB-8322 source metadata directly records disease/response groups and macrophage-enriched single-cell RNA-seq, but does not supply cell-state labels in the inspected SDRF/design. The independent Hanlon et al. study reports loss of macrophage homeostasis preceding clinical RA onset [K1]; it supports the relevance of macrophage population/state balance, not this proposed remission-specific decomposition. These are existing observations, not results computed by this proposal.

**Unresolved claim tested:** Is the MERTK remission contrast primarily composition or within-state expression when donors—not cells—are the inferential units? “Primarily” will be judged from donor-bootstrap uncertainty around composition and within-state counterfactual components, not from a cell-level p-value or a classifier's predictive score.

**Leading explanation and rival:** The proposed leading explanation is a state-composition shift (more cells in a MERTK-high/resolution-associated state); the substantive rival is a cell-intrinsic MERTK increase within matched states. Additional rivals include unequal tissue digestion/freezing/recovery, sequencing depth, age/sex imbalance, donor selection, and state-label instability. Because all groups are cross-sectional, sorted macrophages, and from one published study, these explanations cannot be fully separated from group-specific collection or treatment-selection effects.

## Harbor analysis plan

**Population and time boundary.** Adult human synovial-membrane, sorted macrophage samples from the single E-MTAB-8322 cross-sectional collection. Primary comparison: the 7 RA remission donors versus the 6 RA treatment-naive donors, using one donor-level unit each. Status is the source's recorded contemporaneous response group only; do not interpret it as longitudinal treatment response or predict future remission. Resistant RA (n=6) is a secondary contrast, not pooled with naïve disease. Do not pool healthy/undifferentiated arthritis into the primary control.

**Primary outcomes.** (1) Donor/state pseudobulk normalized MERTK expression and fraction of MERTK-detected cells within each reproducible macrophage state. (2) State proportions among recovered/sorted macrophages per donor. (3) Donor-level remission-minus-naive difference in overall MERTK, decomposed into a composition-only counterfactual (hold state-specific expression at naïve levels, replace proportions) and within-state component (hold proportions at naïve reference, replace state-specific expression). The estimand is descriptive and conditional on recovered cells, not a tissue abundance estimate. Define state clusters without remission labels and exclude MERTK plus the prespecified resolution-score genes from the clustering features to reduce circularity; freeze QC/normalization, clustering, and minimum-cell eligibility before revealing group labels. Cells too few for reliable state estimates stay missing at donor/state, not replaced with zero.

**Baseline (selected).** First audit a donor-level naive-vs-remission test of aggregate pseudobulk MERTK; then perform conventional donor-level state-stratified pseudobulk and proportion analysis. Estimate contrasts with donor permutation (labels permuted only among 13 primary RA donors) and donor bootstrap intervals, reporting raw effects and uncertainty. Report the decomposition for both MERTK mean expression and detection fraction; this simple baseline is transparent and statistically aligned with the small number of donors. Sensitivity analyses: adjust only for measured age/sex as descriptive covariates; leave-one-donor-out; alternate reasonable cluster resolutions; exclude a state if fewer than 3 primary donors per group pass the preregistered cell threshold. No cell-level significance testing as confirmatory evidence.

**Substantive alternative, same question.** Fit an overdispersed hierarchical latent-mixture model to raw cell UMI counts, with donor random effects, latent macrophage states, and group effects allowed separately on state weights and within-state MERTK expression. Fit/simulate target is the donor-indexed cell-by-gene count likelihood, not clinical status prediction. State inference uses the same marker-exclusion rule and predeclared feature set; it must not use MERTK to define the clusters whose within-state MERTK contrast it tests. Compare posterior (or bootstrap) composition and state-specific effect intervals with the baseline; evaluate donor-held-out predictive log likelihood/calibration using leave-one-donor-out folds (all cells from a donor held out together), plus posterior predictive checks for per-donor state proportions and MERTK-detection counts. The model could reveal uncertainty from ambiguous/continuous state membership and estimate a formal joint decomposition, which hard clusters discard. It cannot identify causal mechanisms, and the small number of donors risks prior/model sensitivity; therefore it is a prespecified sensitivity/alternative, not a more authoritative replacement. If the model is unstable or poorly calibrated, report failure and retain the simpler baseline; do not tune to maximize remission separation.

**Outcome uncertainty and falsification.** Report cluster marker profiles, donor-specific cell counts, group contrasts, donor-level bootstrap 95% intervals, permutation p-values as descriptive support only, and the two decomposition components with intervals. Set the meaningful-effect threshold before unblinding using a biological/MERTK assay sensitivity criterion obtained from the reference study/protocol or expert review; if unavailable, do not invent a numeric threshold—report interval compatibility and call a null imprecise where it spans both directions.

- **Support composition hypothesis:** remission has higher abundance of an independently identified MERTK-high state; the composition component explains most of the aggregate MERTK contrast and the within-state effect is small/uncertain around the prespecified equivalence margin. This supports a composition-associated expression explanation in this dataset only; it does not prove altered recruitment, survival, or a protective state.
- **Support cell-intrinsic rival / adverse to composition hypothesis:** remission shows a coherent within-state MERTK increase across donor-reproducible states, while state proportions cannot account for aggregate effect; uncertainty excludes the predeclared small-effect region for composition. This supports within-state expression association only, not MerTK causality or function.
- **Mixed:** both components are nontrivial, discordant across states, or cluster definitions materially change them; revise toward a mixed/continuous-state account and select a perturbation that separates state frequency from per-cell function.
- **Inconclusive:** wide donor-level intervals, very sparse cell-state coverage, low MERTK detection, or unstable state assignments overlap both explanations. The absence of evidence is not falsification. Defer target prioritization and obtain better sampled, independent donor data.
- **Adverse to both simple accounts:** no consistent remission contrast or signs reverse across reasonable state definitions; revise the premise, inspect measurement/selection artifacts, and stop treating this dataset as evidence for a general remission-linked MerTK signal.

A computationally checkable solver deliverable is the derived donor/state table, exact sample-to-donor joins, QC/eligibility flow, frozen state-label procedure, raw effects/intervals/decomposition, sensitivity results, and a conclusion whose direction matches those outputs. Whether macrophage states are biologically protective, whether MerTK is causal, and whether its blockade affects clinical outcomes require expert interpretation and a separate perturbation study; automatic verification cannot establish them.

## Functional next test and stopping rule

The smallest decisive follow-up is a donor-blocked experiment using fresh RA synovial macrophages sorted into phenotype-defined MerTK-high/low strata (or a state-specific assay validated against the computational labels), with orthogonal MERTK loss-of-function and rescue, non-targeting control, and verified target engagement. Measure per-cell efferocytosis/resolution readouts plus inflammatory cytokines and a separately scored fibroblast repair response in matched coculture. Balance donor and initial state proportions; analyze donor as the biological unit. A perturbation effect within each stratum would support direct MerTK-dependent function; an effect only through altered state abundance would favor composition; absent or opposite functional effects would undermine a protective interpretation. Clinical consequence would still require prospective patient outcomes and safety/efficacy data. No such experimental data are currently present in E-MTAB-8322.

Continue to this functional study only if the computational decomposition is stable across donors and label sensitivity, and expert review confirms a feasible state-specific assay. Revise if both components matter or latent labels are unstable. Defer if confidence intervals cannot distinguish mechanisms. Abandon the MerTK-specific direction if the original group contrast fails replication under donor-level analysis or the proposed functional experiment shows no target-dependent effect in adequately controlled conditions.
