# Early post-TACE laboratory trajectories and 90-day non-TACE hospitalization

## Unresolved question and clinical stakes

For patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), decisions around TACE discharge and early follow-up rely mainly on pre-treatment liver reserve and tumor burden. It is unresolved whether **routine laboratory changes recorded during the index TACE admission** identify a distinct short-term deterioration process that predicts subsequent non-TACE hospitalization beyond static pre-TACE risk.

Resolution matters clinically: a reproducible early-trajectory signal could help time discharge, target post-TACE laboratory follow-up, and prioritize patients for closer in-person review. A null result would also be useful: it would argue that early routine labs add little to baseline liver-function and tumor-risk information and that follow-up intensity should not be adjusted for small within-admission changes without additional evidence.

This is explicitly a **prognostic/physiology question, not a treatment-effect claim**. TACE is not randomized by trajectory and residual confounding by tumor burden, severity, ordering practice, care access, and unobserved death can remain.

## Closest inspected work

- **[K1] Kohla et al. 2015** prospectively studied 102 Child-Pugh A patients and defined post-TACE decompensation as a Child-Pugh point increase at one month. Post-TACE INR, bilirubin, ALT, and AST rose while albumin fell, but baseline larger tumor size, higher AFP, and lower albumin were the multivariable predictors. It supports the relevance of post-TACE lab changes but does not establish their short-term predictive increment.
- **[K2] Han et al. 2020** built and validated TACE-specific pre/post models in 4,621 patients. Post-TACE response improved survival prediction, and albumin, bilirubin, AFP, tumor number/size, vascular invasion, and cause were important. This shows post-treatment information can matter, but its outcome is long-term survival and radiologic response rather than early hospitalization.
- **[K3] Farinati et al. 2016** developed and validated the ALBI system from albumin and bilirubin, establishing that a simple static liver-function score is prognostic in HCC. It bounds the required comparison: any trajectory model must beat a strong static liver-function baseline, not merely be significant.

The advance is therefore a **near-term, decision-relevant incremental-information test**, on a larger longitudinal institutional cohort, using only labs available before discharge.

## Data-bound population and temporal design

### Cohort

1. Start with `encounters` from `data_基本信息_2500296761891079109.csv`. One row is one encounter, keyed by `患者主索引` + `就诊号`; use `年龄`, `性别`, `入院时间`, `出院时间`, and `就诊科室`.
2. Identify TACE in `procedures` from `data_手术_8024330590283626027.csv`, joined by `患者主索引` + `就诊号`. Define TACE as a case-insensitive match to `TACE` in `手术`; keep the earliest `开始时间` per patient as the index procedure. An exploratory local count found **4,471 first-TACE patients**.
3. Exclude patients without an index admission or discharge time, and those whose earliest TACE is after the last complete snapshot boundary. Age must be nonmissing and interpreted as age at the encounter; derive no birth dates.
4. Perform complete-case analysis for prespecified labs, but report exclusion as a material selection characteristic; compare included versus excluded patients on age, sex, year, department, and outcome.

### Predictor windows

Use `labs` from `data_检验_609065997844652188.csv`, joined by the same two keys and timestamped by `检验时间`.

- **Pre-TACE window:** earliest available result in the index encounter from admission through 72 hours before index TACE, plus a sensitivity window ending at TACE.
- **Post-TACE window:** all results from index TACE through discharge, with only the latest value before discharge used for the primary change model.
- Tests are exact assay names: `总胆红素`, `白蛋白`, `肌酐`, `国际标准化比值`, `血小板计数`, `丙氨酸氨基转移酶`, `甲胎蛋白`, and `活化部分凝血活酶时间`.
- Cross-assay pooling is not allowed; the lab table has no unit column, so a single exact Chinese assay label defines each predictor. Missing values are retained as missing rather than zero-imputed.

A local feasibility count found 4,190–4,310 patients per key test in index encounters, with approximately 3,800–3,940 having a post-TACE result. Pre and post coverage must be recalculated after exact complete-case filtering.

### Outcome

Primary event is **first non-TACE hospitalization within 90 days after index discharge**, detected in subsequent encounter rows with `入院时间` in `[index 出院时间, index 出院时间 + 90 days)`. Exclude a readmission from the outcome if its `就诊号` has a TACE procedure row, because scheduled TACE is treatment delivery rather than deterioration. Use time from index discharge to first qualifying admission. Patients with no such admission are censored at 90 days; institution-only capture means outpatient death or care elsewhere is unobserved.

Secondary descriptive outcomes are:
- 30-day non-TACE hospitalization;
- 90-day hepatic-complication hospitalization, using prespecified diagnosis terms for ascites, gastrointestinal bleeding, encephalopathy, jaundice, or infection in `diagnoses` from `data_诊断_7504718184492840569.csv`, joined by the two keys.

No clinical-note NLP is used for the primary outcome. Narrative text in `clinical_documents` remains available for later adjudication but the frozen text extraction detector is unvalidated outside cirrhosis and does not support decompensation claims.

### Leakage and temporality

No information after index discharge may enter predictors. Patient-level chronological split prevents leakage across encounters. Because index procedure is the patient's first recorded TACE, prior treatment history at other institutions is unobserved; this is a limitation, not a hidden feature. The model must use only labs available before discharge. If a discharge occurs before a lab result, that result is excluded.

## Primary analysis: baseline versus substantive alternative

Fit both models by patient-level chronological split: train on procedures before 2019-01-01, validate 2019–2021, and test 2022 onward. Random splitting is not used because the question is temporal transport.

### Baseline

A static logistic model or Cox model for 90-day hospitalization with:
- age and sex;
- index year and department;
- pre-TACE log-bilirubin, albumin, creatinine, log-INR, platelet count, log-ALT, and log-AFP;
- prespecified diagnosis flags (cirrhosis, hepatitis B, portal hypertension, ascites, varices) from the index encounter.

This baseline is stronger than ALBI alone and directly represents the leading static explanation.

### Alternative

Add within-admission trajectory features:
- `post - pre` change for each lab;
- relative change, with direction prespecified (worsening: bilirubin, creatinine, INR, ALT, APTT increase; albumin and platelets decrease);
- time from procedure to last post-TACE lab;
- number of repeated post-TACE labs and last-result-to-discharge interval.

Estimate unpenalized or lightly penalized coefficients for transparency, and report bootstrap confidence intervals. The learned comparison should be a **nested model test plus decision-curve/utility analysis**, not just a tiny AUC gain. A gradient-boosted temporal model may be a secondary exploratory analysis, but it is not required because the primary scientific question is whether trajectories add information, not whether a complex learner can maximize prediction.

### Prespecified success and failure criteria

Supportive evidence requires all of:
1. improvement in held-out time-dependent AUC or C-index by at least 0.025 (non-bootstrap CI excluding zero);
2. a likelihood-ratio test significant at two-sided alpha 0.05 after correction for eight trajectory families;
3. calibration slope within 0.8–1.25 and calibration-in-the-large compatible with a clinically plausible risk range;
4. a decision-curve net benefit improvement at clinically relevant risk thresholds (2–10%).

Adverse evidence is a null or negative incremental result after adequate power and complete-case sensitivity. Inconclusive evidence is wide CIs, unstable calibration across time bins, or strong informative missingness. A significant association without calibration/utility improvement is not sufficient.

## Interpretations and required claims

- **Supportive:** early routine trajectory contains information about a near-term deterioration pathway not captured by static admission risk. This supports a prospective validation study and could inform follow-up timing; it does not prove causality or a biological mechanism.
- **Adverse:** baseline liver reserve and clinical care factors dominate, so routine within-admission trajectories do not justify a new risk tool in this setting.
- **Inconclusive:** local data are insufficient, but no evidence supports changing practice.
- A trajectory association could reflect measurement practice: sicker patients may receive more labs and more attention, so lab intensity must be included and checked with missingness sensitivity. It could also reflect longer index stays, treatment intensity, or unmeasured tumor burden. Therefore any supportive result is a **measurement/process-adjacent prognostic finding**, not proof that TACE caused deterioration.
- Stronger clinical claims require external validation, adjudicated unplanned-hospitalization status, mortality/out-of-system records, procedure details such as chemotherapy dose and embolization extent, and imaging/tumor burden.

## Method exploration record

- **Delphi demonstration:** a longitudinal learned sequence model is possible, but the scientific uncertainty here is an explicit increment of early routine trajectories over static risk. A small, transparent trajectory model is the appropriate first test; a full transformer would obscure the incremental claim and is not necessary for feasibility.
- **ALADYNOULLI demonstration:** its longitudinal latent-trajectory idea motivates modeling repeated lab values, but its genetic component and full latent model are unnecessary. A bounded trajectory feature model tests the substantive question directly.
- **Oncoformer demonstration:** missing images prevent a multimodal reproduction; lab-only sequence modeling is a valid bounded adaptation only if it answers a prespecified incremental question.
- **Deferred learned alternative:** if the transparent trajectory model is supportive, a later study can fit a regularized temporal learner with patient-level folds and compare calibration/utility. It is deferred now because model capacity is not the current scientific uncertainty.
- CPU-only implementation is sufficient: the proposed cohort is roughly 4,500 patients and a tabular fit is small. The future solver envelope is 16 CPUs and 28,800 seconds; expected fit time is minutes to a few hours, measured only for initial data scans, not full model training.
