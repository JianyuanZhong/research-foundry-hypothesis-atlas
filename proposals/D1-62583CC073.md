# Residual instability at ICU discharge: a time-specific competing-risk audit

Status: candidate-ready design; no cohort scan, model fit, Harbor execution, or clinical result was run.

Parent/context: [starting question] ([prior hypothesis]) and prior valid design [prior hypothesis]. This branch is an independent audit child, not a revision of the mature AKI branch.

## Decision and scientific opening

ICU discharge is a consequential transition: the patient is alive and leaving intensive monitoring, but the same-hospital risk set remains open for ICU return or death. [K1] found that post-ICU-transfer ward vital/laboratory flags were associated with near-term ICU readmission or death in a small single-center surgical cohort. That supports a short-term prognostic opening, but it does not establish a discharge-time phenotype, independence from the receiving unit, or an actionable effect of monitoring. [K3] links respiratory independence and post-discharge dependency to mortality in a small elderly cohort, but bundles function, destination, support and long-term prognosis. [K2] shows that MIMIC-IV readmission models can compare associative and learned temporal models, while also illustrating that predictive or “causal-fused” outputs do not identify a discharge intervention effect.

The unresolved question is:

> Among adults discharged alive from an index ICU stay, does persistent multi-domain physiologic abnormality in the final 24 hours identify a higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU readmission versus hospital death, after a conservative availability rule, with destination, residual severity and observation opportunity audited as rival explanations?

Primary hypothesis: relative to patients with no persistent core domain abnormality, patients with at least two persistent domains have higher 48-hour first-event incidence of ICU readmission or hospital death, and the component-specific contrasts are not confined to one immediate destination or to dense documentation. This is an observed prognostic association, not a causal effect of discharge delay, monitoring, oxygen therapy, or treatment limitation.

The clinical advance is boundary testing. A positive result would identify a reproducible high-risk transition phenotype suitable for prospective monitoring or discharge-readiness validation. A result restricted to a destination, sparse charting pattern, or hospice-like disposition would redirect the question toward discharge selection and goals of care. A temporal result that distinguishes persistent from newly abnormal or resolving trajectories would add clinically interpretable timing information beyond a risk score.

## Leading explanation and strongest rivals

- Physiologic instability predicts adverse transition: the association persists after pre-discharge severity standardization, in adequately observed patients, across immediate-destination strata, and is stronger for persistent or newly abnormal trajectories than for a matched earlier window.
- Discharge selection/severity: the final-24-hour association disappears after pre-window severity adjustment, is similar in the earlier [t_d-48h,t_d-24h) window, or is present regardless of domain pattern. Conditioning on ICU discharge is part of the estimand and cannot be removed by regression.
- Care-unit destination: the association is concentrated in particular receiving units. Immediate destination is measured after t_d and is therefore a descriptive selection audit, not a pre-discharge confounder or primary adjustment variable.
- Treatment limitation/goals of care: death, hospice/palliative destination, and sparse intervention may reflect undocumented limits. MIMIC fields available here do not provide a validated pre-t_d goals-of-care indicator. No model, discharge note, hospital-expire flag, or destination label will be treated as a substitute.
- Observation opportunity/charting: the phenotype is mainly a dense-monitoring signature. A documentation-density gradient with similar outcome association, or strong attenuation after density stratification, weakens the physiologic interpretation.
- Support meaning: O2 flow/FiO2 can reflect prescribed support or chronic need rather than hypoxemia. Oxygen-support and physiologic abnormality components are reported separately; the item does not establish respiratory failure, device setting, work of breathing, or clinician intent.

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must produce, from computed outputs:

1. A flow from adult admissions to first eligible ICU stay, alive ICU discharge, availability-qualified exposure, and each outcome risk set, including exclusions and unknown bins.
2. A dictionary/provenance audit with exact item labels, linksto, categories, units, numeric/text handling and fail-closed collision rules.
3. Four exposure states (stable, one persistent domain, at least two persistent domains, unknown/insufficient) plus domain-specific components, trajectory classes and observation counts.
4. First-event 48-hour counts and Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidences for ICU readmission, hospital death and alive hospital discharge; readmission/death risk differences with bootstrap intervals.
5. Pre-discharge severity, destination, observation-density and earlier-window audits with sparse-cell rules.
6. A fixed-summary cause-specific baseline and a chronological GRU on exactly the same eligible subjects, with held-out Brier/calibration, trajectory-standardized predictions and uncertainty.
7. Conclusions linked to these tables and figures. No completion claim is valid from readiness or model fit alone.

## Population, landmarks and leakage control

Unit is one hospital admission, using the earliest valid ICU stay for that subject_id/hadm_id pair. Join:

- patients on subject_id; retain anchor_age >= 18 and report gender.
- icustays on subject_id, hadm_id; require intime < outtime and choose minimum intime.
- admissions on subject_id, hadm_id; define t_d = icustays.outtime.
- retain deathtime null or deathtime > t_d. A death exactly at t_d is not an alive ICU discharge.
- retain every immediate destination, including ward, step-down/intermediate, hospice/palliative and unknown.

Primary physiology window is [t_d-24 hours,t_d), divided into four half-open 6-hour bins. A core record is eligible only when clinical charttime < t_d and, when storetime is present, storetime <= t_d. Missing storetime is fail-closed for primary core exposure. This conservative rule makes the primary signal plausibly available by discharge; a charttime-only sensitivity explicitly measures documentation-lag sensitivity. Values at or after t_d never enter predictors. The earlier-window audit is [t_d-48h,t_d-24h), with the same availability rule.

A patient is exposure-known only if at least three of four primary bins contain a valid core observation. A missing bin is unknown, not normal. The all-eligible flow retains insufficient-observation patients as unknown; the primary observed-discharge prognostic estimand is clearly labeled as restricted to exposure-known patients. A sensitivity requires core respiratory/oxygen observation in all four bins. No threshold or inclusion rule is changed after outcome inspection.

Domain flags are fixed before outcome inspection:

- respiratory/oxygen: SpO2 <92%, O2 flow >0 L/min, or FiO2 >0.21;
- hemodynamic: MAP <65 mmHg, HR >100 or <50 bpm;
- ventilatory pattern: RR >24 or <8/min;
- temperature: Celsius <36.0 or >38.3.

A domain is persistent when flagged in at least two of four bins. Exposure states are stable/no persistent domain, exactly one persistent domain, at least two persistent domains, and unknown/insufficient. The primary contrast is at least two versus stable. Report oxygen-support flags separately from SpO2 and report the fixed trajectory classes: stable, persistent (flagged in all observed bins), newly abnormal (only the final two bins), resolving (only the first two bins), and intermittent (two non-adjacent flagged bins), with an unknown class when required. These classes are descriptive and are not a post hoc threshold search.

## Outcomes and competing-risk estimands

Read icu/icustays fields subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, outtime; read hosp/admissions fields subject_id, hadm_id, admittime, dischtime, deathtime and hospital_expire_flag. For [t_d,t_d+48h), define the earliest clinical first event:

- ICU readmission: a later icustays.intime for the same subject_id/hadm_id, with intime >= t_d and < t_d+48h; the index stay is excluded.
- Hospital death: admissions.deathtime >= t_d and < t_d+48h.
- Alive hospital discharge: admissions.dischtime >= t_d and < t_d+48h, only when no earlier readmission or death.
- Event-free: no event and still under the same-hospital risk set at 48 hours.

If readmission and death have the same timestamp, assign death first because readmission requires survival and emit a data-integrity flag. A death after readmission remains a secondary outcome, not a first-event death. hospital_expire_flag is a consistency check, never a replacement for a missing death time.

The primary estimands are standardized 48-hour cumulative incidence and absolute risk difference for first ICU readmission and first hospital death, with alive hospital discharge as a competing event. Report the composite readmission-or-death only as a secondary summary. Use Aalen-Johansen for unadjusted CIFs and cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards in six-hour post-discharge intervals for adjusted standardization. The adjustment set is pre-t_d only; immediate destination is not in the primary adjustment set. A multinomial first-event model is sensitivity only. The estimand is conditional on reaching an observed alive ICU discharge and, for the primary contrast, having adequate pre-discharge observation; it is not a causal discharge policy effect.

Immediate destination is the first hosp/transfers row with subject_id, hadm_id and intime >= t_d; use transfer_id, eventtype, careunit, intime and outtime, and classify unknown when no row is available. Ultimate disposition is separately admissions.discharge_location; it is reported separately and never substituted for immediate destination. Report primary estimates within prespecified destination families, suppressing cells with fewer than 20 events or fewer than 100 patients as inconclusive rather than merging after seeing outcomes.

Count valid core and all non-core chartevents, labevents, inputevents and outputevents by bin. Standardize estimates across quartiles of non-instability documentation density frozen from training data. A similar density gradient, or attenuation after density stratification, is adverse to a physiologic interpretation. Support records are process descriptors, not treatment confounder controls or causal interventions.

The note/discharge source is available as note_id, subject_id, hadm_id, note_type, note_seq, charttime, storetime and text; note/discharge_detail has note_id, subject_id, field_name, field_value and field_ordinal. Notes may be written after t_d and contain future information, so they are excluded from primary predictors and adjustment. hosp/omr has subject_id, chartdate, seq_num, result_name and result_value but is not a verified goals-of-care field. No structured pre-t_d treatment-limitation field was verified. Discharge location, death and sparse treatment are bounds on this missing evidence, not adjudication.

To test discharge selection/timing, repeat the fixed baseline in the earlier [t_d-48h,t_d-24h) window among patients observed in both windows, with static severity and density held constant. If late and early signals are similar, residual severity/selection is favored; if late trajectories are stronger, temporal specificity is supported but causality is still not established.

## Matched baseline versus substantive temporal alternative

The actual comparison is fixed before fitting.

Baseline B is an auditable cause-specific six-hour discrete-time logistic model with interval intercepts and covariates: exposure state, domain counts, window-level median/minimum/maximum/range and flagged-bin counts (no bin order), age, gender, admission type, ICU length/timing, pre-window lab severity, and observation counts/masks. It is fit separately for readmission, death and alive discharge and standardized to 48-hour CIFs. This baseline answers the primary association question transparently and supplies destination/density audits.

Temporal alternative T is a one-layer GRU competing-risk model on the same patients, windows and outcomes. Its 12 chronological two-hour bins contain the eight numeric channels, per-channel missingness masks, elapsed-time/bin index, core and total observation counts, and domain flags; static covariates and pre-window severity enter a static head. The output is three cause-specific hazards (readmission, death, discharge) for eight six-hour post-discharge intervals. Use a deterministic SHA-256 subject_id split 70/15/15, training-only normalization and density cut points, seed set {17,29,43}, hidden size 32, dropout 0, Adam learning rate 1e-3, batch size 128, maximum 50 epochs, and early stop after five validation epochs without improving mean cause-specific Brier score. Report each seed and the prespecified aggregate; no patient may cross a split.

Primary model comparison is held-out cause-specific Brier score and 48-hour calibration, with AUROC/AUPRC secondary. Bootstrap patients within the held-out test split for confidence intervals. Scientific evidence for timing/persistence requires more than a small discrimination gain: report standardized held-out predictions for matched classes stable, persistent, newly abnormal, resolving and intermittent, and compare chronological T with an order-permuted training control using the same bins/masks. If T improves calibration but trajectory contrasts are absent, report predictive utility only. If chronology and order-permuted models are similar, the data do not support an ordering claim. Saliency is descriptive and not a mechanism.

This is a substantive alternative because B loses the temporal arrangement of equally frequent abnormalities, while T can test whether persistence, recency, resolution and missingness change the competing-event risk. The baseline remains primary for auditability. Transformers, causal-fused models and treatment-effect analyses are deferred: the dataset lacks validated treatment limitation, clinician intent, discharge rationale, a well-defined intervention and external validation. Revisit only after independent clinical adjudication or a prospective monitoring study.

Planning estimate, not a result: archive scans and materialization are managed CPU work; B requires about 2–4 CPUs and 8–16 GiB after materialization. T is a small 32-hidden-unit GRU, expected to fit within one allocated A100 or CPU fallback, with at most three seeds and one checkpoint per seed. Resource estimates are planning estimates, not measured runtime, and must be checked against inputs.json solver_planning_limits. No proposer or solver training is authorized in discovery.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results require all of the following: the primary at-least-two-versus-stable standardized risk difference is positive with a 95% interval excluding zero; component estimates are reported rather than hidden by the composite; the direction is not confined to one adequately sized destination or density stratum; the late window is more informative than the prespecified earlier window; and temporal trajectory contrasts show higher risk for persistent/newly abnormal patterns than matched stable/resolving patterns. This establishes a reproducible prognostic association compatible with residual instability only.

Adverse results include a confidence interval wholly at or below zero, signal limited to oxygen support with no corroboration from physiologic domains, disappearance after pre-window severity or density audit, comparable earlier-window signal, a similar process-density gradient, or no held-out temporal distinction. These favor severity, discharge selection, support meaning or observation artifact. A temporal model improvement without a stable exposure contrast is predictive utility only.

Inconclusive results include fewer than 100 patients or 20 events in a required stratum, high unknown exposure fraction, dictionary/unit/availability failure, competing-event inconsistency, or intervals spanning clinically meaningful benefit and harm. Do not alter cutoffs, merge destinations or redefine unknown after outcome inspection. Clinical adjudication is required for treatment limitation, discharge rationale, chronic oxygen, work of breathing, functional status and whether a measurement was actually considered in discharge. External validation or prospective monitoring/intervention is required before a discharge policy or causal claim.

## Three inspected key works

[K1] Rens et al. provides the closest short-term observation: a small surgical ICU-to-ward cohort in which post-transfer vital/laboratory flags were associated with ICU readmission or death within 48 hours. It does not establish a MIMIC discharge-time phenotype or distinguish destination, goals of care and charting opportunity.

[K2] Remoundou et al. demonstrates an associative-versus-learned/causal-fused MIMIC-IV readmission comparison. It bounds the present method choice: predictive performance and feature realignment cannot by themselves establish a causal discharge intervention.

[K3] Gülen et al. reports respiratory independence and post-discharge dependency associations with one-year mortality in 131 very elderly ICU survivors. It supports the importance of separating respiratory support, destination and dependency, but its population, horizon and single-center design do not answer the 48-hour MIMIC question.
