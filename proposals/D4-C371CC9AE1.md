# UKB seed 03: does muscle function and posterior thigh myosteatosis add information beyond thigh muscle quantity for future mobility decline or falls?

Status: delivery-ready proposed design; no hypothesis model has been fitted and no scientific result is claimed. The bounded scans below are availability diagnostics only.

## Scientific opening, evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest inspected evidence supports a descriptive, cross-sectional regularity: MRI-derived thigh distribution patterns can be associated with weak grip and falls beyond a total-muscle-mass measure [K1]. A review supports myosteatosis as a plausible correlate of strength and physical function, but explicitly leaves open whether it is an aging marker, a consequence of disease/disuse, or a measurement/selection artifact [K2]. The GLIS consensus treats muscle mass, muscle strength, and muscle-specific strength as related but distinct components, with physical performance an outcome rather than a component [K3]. None of these establishes that the exact UKB posterior-thigh MFI fields, grip at the MRI assessment, or thigh quantity provide prospective incremental information for later mobility decline or falls.

The unresolved clinical question is:

> Among UKB participants with valid bilateral thigh MRI quantity and posterior-thigh MFI at the same MRI assessment, does adding contemporaneous hand-grip function and posterior-thigh MFI to a quantity-only model improve the absolute-risk estimate for a subsequent decline in usual walking pace or a subsequent fall report over the next 2–5 years, after accounting for age, sex, body size, smoking, ethnicity and baseline pace/falls, and does that incremental signal persist after pain/arthritis/neurologic and attendance-selection stress tests?

This is a prognostic/descriptive estimand, not a causal muscle mechanism, sarcopenia diagnosis, treatment effect, individual clinical prediction rule, or proof that MFI causes falls. A supportive result would justify external, adjudicated validation and measurement work; it would not authorize clinical screening. An adverse result would narrow the clinically useful claim toward function alone, quantity alone, or non-muscle explanations. An inconclusive result would identify a sparse MRI/repeat-assessment limitation, not refute the hypothesis.

The substantive advance over [K1] is prospective temporal ordering and an explicit nested quantity-versus-function/quality estimand using the configured fields. The advance over [K2] is a bounded test of disease/disuse and selection rivals rather than an assumed mechanism. The advance over [K3] is an executable test of whether the conceptual components add information for a patient-relevant future outcome.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Primary population: one row per unique `eid) with parseable `T_M=53-2.0), valid nonmissing bilateral volume and bilateral MFI at instance 2, valid index grip `46-2.0/47-2.0), and valid `924-2.0) and `2296-2.0) after UKB code validation. Do not require a repeat MRI. Report duplicate IDs, invalid dates, impossible instance ordering and each field's missingness before exclusions.

Define quantity `Q` as the sum of the four bilateral thigh fat-free volumes, with a prespecified height-normalized version `Q_h=Q/(height^2)` using `21002-2.0) as a sensitivity. Standardize Q within the development split, never using holdout moments. Define grip `G=max(46-2.0,47-2.0)`; report left/right and sex-stratified versions separately. Define MFI `M=(23355-2.0+23356-2.0)/2) only if both values are valid; higher MFI is not automatically interpreted as causal myosteatosis without the field's unit/coding check. Also report the left-right MFI difference and bilateral mean.

The primary future assessment is the earliest j in {0,1,3} for which `53-j.0) is parseable, strictly after T_M, within `(T_M,T_M+5 calendar years]), and both requested outcome fields are valid. If several qualify, retain the earliest for the primary estimand and use all later observations in a clustered longitudinal sensitivity. Require at least 30 days between dates; report the gap distribution. This handles the fact that assessment instance numbers do not themselves establish chronology.

Primary outcome `Y_walk): a lower usual-walking-pace category at the first eligible post-index assessment than at `924-2.0), after validating the official categorical order. If the UKB coding does not support a defensible order, the primary result is inconclusive and category-specific transition probabilities are reported instead. Secondary `Y_fall): any positive falls-in-last-year category at the first eligible post-index assessment, with a sensitivity restricted to participants with no positive `2296-2.0) report. A recurrent-falls sensitivity uses the coding-defined repeated-fall category only if its meaning is verified. ## Estimands and baseline versus substantive learned alternative

The clinical estimand is the standardized incremental absolute-risk information over the 5-year horizon, with the actual observed covariate distribution in the locked holdout:

- Model Q: Q (or Q_h), age, sex, BMI, smoking, ethnicity, index walking pace, index falls and index calendar year.
- Model Q+F+M: the exact Model-Q inputs plus G and M, with a prespecified G×M interaction in the mechanistic sensitivity. The principal incremental estimand is the difference in standardized 5-year risk between these models for fixed contrasts: (i) low versus high G at the development SDs while holding Q and covariates fixed, and (ii) high versus low M at development SDs while holding Q, G and covariates fixed. Report absolute risk difference, 95% interval, calibration and Brier score, not only a likelihood-ratio p-value.
- Report Q-only, Q+G, Q+M, and Q+G+M separately. This distinguishes function-only from quality-only information and avoids attributing an MFI signal to grip.

For the transparent baseline, fit cause-specific discrete-time logistic models for Y_walk and Y_fall with a single first-event horizon, or ordinal transition regression if the coding supports it. Use restricted complete-case exposure/outcome analysis with explicit denominators, and multiple imputation only for adjustment covariates in development as a sensitivity. Use robust standard errors, prespecified standardization, bootstrap resampling of participants (at least 1,000 replicates if the event count permits), calibration intercept/slope, Brier score and absolute-risk contrasts.

The substantive learned alternative is a CPU generalized additive/discrete-time model using exactly the same participants, index fields, outcome, horizon, split and covariates. It fits smooth nonlinear terms for Q, G and M plus a prespecified tensor interaction Q×M and G×M, with shrinkage and monotonicity constrained only where clinically justified by the coding. It can reveal a threshold or quantity-quality mismatch—e.g., MFI risk concentrated among people with preserved Q—that an additive baseline can miss. It cannot reveal unmeasured pain, disease indication, neuromuscular mechanism or scanner biology. Compare it with the baseline on locked-holdout absolute calibration, Brier score, log score, standardized risk contrasts and bootstrap uncertainty; AUC/C-index is secondary.

Use a participant-level temporal split by `T_M): earliest 70% of index dates for development and latest 30% for a locked holdout. All transformations, code-category decisions, scaling, spline knots, imputation, tuning, thresholds and weights are learned in development only. No `eid) appears in both splits. If the MRI complete-case event count is below 50 for either primary outcome, treat inferential direction as inconclusive; if below 20, do not fit the learned alternative beyond a prespecified convergence/readiness check. Do not replace the split with a random split to gain events.

## Rival explanations and falsification rules

Pain/arthritis/neurologic rivals are clinically essential. In the full same-namespace source, bind `3571-2.0) back pain, `3414-2.0) hip pain, `3773-2.0) knee pain, `2956-2.0) generalized pain, `3404-2.0) neck/shoulder pain, `4067-2.0) facial pain and `3799-2.0) headache, plus `20002-2.k) for k=0..36 self-reported non-cancer illness codes. Use the UKB coding dictionary to create and publish code lists for arthritis/musculoskeletal and neurologic illness; never invent numeric code meanings. Run the primary estimand (a) in no-reported-pain strata, (b) excluding any specified regional pain, and (c) excluding participants with either illness family. Report interaction and standardized risk shifts. A marked attenuation or pain/disease interaction is adverse to a muscle-specific interpretation. If selected clinical fields cannot be parsed or have poor coverage, state that this rival remains unresolved; do not call adjustment a clinical adjudication.

Selection and timing falsifications:

1. Report MRI-complete versus MRI-incomplete and outcome-attending versus outcome-missing denominators by age, sex, BMI, smoking, ethnicity, index pace/falls and attendance dates. Fit a development-only participation/return-attendance model with those same baseline variables and use stabilized inverse-probability weights only as a sensitivity; report positivity, weight tails and weighted/unweighted estimates. Poor positivity is inconclusive, not grounds for favorable trimming.
2. Repeat the estimand for gaps `(0,1]), `(1,3]), and `(3,5]) years, with exact calendar boundaries. A signal confined to the shortest band is compatible with reverse causation or assessment proximity; a signal persisting in later bands is stronger temporal evidence but remains observational.
3. Condition on index pace and index falls in every primary model. Repeat after excluding any index fall report. If the MFI/grip contrast exists only among people already slow or falling, report effect modification rather than a general reserve claim.
4. Compare estimates in the original MRI-complete population with estimates after excluding participants whose MRI or outcome date is implausibly ordered, duplicated or outside the field-defined assessment window.
5. Use the repeat-MRI fields as a measurement-stability sensitivity: require `53-3.0>T_M) and compare direction of Q/M/G change with future outcome only if the repeat subset is supported. This is not a mechanism test.
6. A negative-control implementation check permutes the outcome labels within development while preserving index exposures and split; standardized incremental contrasts should center near zero. This checks leakage and code implementation, not biology.

Missingness is part of the result: give field-level and joint missingness tables, compare complete-case and development-only imputation sensitivity, and never treat missing MRI, grip or outcome as zero or as a favorable outcome. No source can provide scanner acquisition timestamp, adjudicated falls, fall circumstances, rehabilitation, physical-activity dose, medication indication, pain severity over follow-up, clinician-confirmed arthritis/neurologic disease, fracture severity, intervention exposure or external validation. The falls field is a recalled categorical assessment, not a continuous event registry. These limits prevent causal, mechanistic or deployment claims.

## Supportive, adverse and inconclusive interpretations

Supportive evidence requires, at minimum: a nonzero and clinically meaningful holdout absolute-risk contrast for Q+G+M versus Q alone for Y_walk or Y_fall with a 95% interval excluding zero and a prespecified absolute-risk improvement boundary of 2 percentage points; directionally consistent Q+G+M increments across the primary outcome and at least one timing/selection sensitivity; calibration not materially degraded; and the learned model's gain expressed in calibrated absolute risk, not only ranking. The result must not be confined to pain/arthritis/neurologic strata, the earliest time band, or participants who return for repeat testing. This would support external validation and measurement adjudication only.

Adverse evidence is a precise null for the incremental estimand with adequate events, a reversal after conditioning on baseline pace/falls or excluding pain/disease, a signal confined to the near-assessment band, strong weighted/unweighted selection divergence, poor calibration, or a learned-only ranking gain without absolute-risk improvement. These results weaken the claim that function/MFI adds stable clinical information beyond quantity; they do not prove quantity is sufficient or that muscle is irrelevant.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 50 events, a 95% interval spanning both zero and the 2-point boundary, invalid pace ordering, sparse or unparseable clinical-rival fields, poor positivity, nonconvergence, too few repeat observations, or inability to establish that later outcomes occur after T_M. A wide null is not refutation. Automatic computation can check bindings, row counts, dates, missingness, model outputs, calibration, uncertainty and adherence of the conclusion to these rules. Clinical adjudication, measurement validation, causal attribution, transportability and intervention decisions require expert review or another study.

## Alternatives considered and actual scientific deliverable

Before selection, the alternatives were made comparable:

- Transparent nested regression was selected as the primary baseline because it yields auditable standardized absolute-risk contrasts and exposes whether MFI and grip add information beyond Q. Inputs are the exact fields above; target is Y_walk/Y_fall; split is the temporal 70/30 holdout; uncertainty is participant bootstrap plus robust intervals; expected CPU budget is 4–8 cores and 32–64 GiB RAM, with approximately 1–4 hours including selected-column extraction from the full CSV (unverified).
- The same-input GAM/discrete-time alternative was retained because its Q×M and G×M surfaces can reveal nonlinear thresholds or preserved-quantity/poor-quality subgroups that the baseline loses. It is CPU-feasible at this MRI sample size; expected budget is 4–8 cores, 32–64 GiB and under 2 hours after extraction (unverified). It is deferred as the primary decision model if fewer than 20 events or if its surfaces are unstable in development.
- A transformer/sequence model was not chosen: the strict complete index MRI core observed in the diagnostic is only 810, repeat outcome support is sparse, and there is no need for latent sequence assumptions to answer the quantity-versus-function/quality comparison. Revisit only with a larger complete longitudinal MRI cohort and adjudicated outcomes. This is a scientific adequacy decision, not a blanket rejection of neural methods or GPU use.
- A causal mediation or mechanistic model was not chosen because pain, disease activity, scanner acquisition, rehabilitation, activity dose and clinical fall adjudication are unavailable. Adding latent variables would not identify those mechanisms.

The actual deliverable is newly fitted, locked-split Q, Q+G, Q+M, Q+G+M baseline models and the same-input GAM alternative, plus: source/schema and namespace audit; exact participant flow and missingness; validated instance dates and transition tables; quantity/MFI/grip distributions; primary and sensitivity absolute-risk contrasts; calibration/Brier/log outputs; bootstrap intervals; pain/arthritis/neurologic and selection falsification tables; temporal-band results; negative-control permutation; and a conclusion labeled supportive, adverse or inconclusive and tied to those computed files. Readiness or the availability diagnostic is not completion, and this proposal contains no fitted result.
