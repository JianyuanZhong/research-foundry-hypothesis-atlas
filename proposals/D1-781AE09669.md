# Hypotension burden with ascertainment-selection transportability sensitivity

## Question and substantive advance
Among adults with a qualifying first-24-hour hypotensive episode, does the parent’s adequate-creatinine-ascertainment restriction materially select the prognostic contrast between MAP burden+nadir and nadir-only, and can a prespecified inverse-probability-of-ascertainment (IPA) analysis recover a transportable association to the broader eligible cohort without coding missing creatinine as no AKI?

The parent’s strongest supported claim is only that hypotension duration/severity is associated with kidney outcomes in prior work [K1] and [K2], and that ICU prevention-bundle associations do not isolate hypotension duration or establish causality [K3]. The unresolved claim is whether the parent’s adequate-ascertainment result is a reproducible prognostic signal conditional on observation, or an artifact of selecting patients whose creatinine was measured in a prespecified pattern.

## Proposed substantive refinement
Retain the parent’s primary estimand unchanged: adjusted incremental association of time-weighted MAP<65 burden beyond nadir and pre-index observation opportunity among the adequate-ascertainment cohort. Add a prespecified selection/transportability estimand for the broader eligible cohort, defined before outcome inspection.

### Cohort and ascertainment indicator
Use the parent’s exact cohort: adults 18–89, first ICU stay per patienthealthsystemstayid, at least one valid MAP in vitalPeriodic.systemicmean or vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean during the first 24 hours, at least one MAP<65, at least six valid MAP observations, baseline creatinine available, no documented pre-existing dialysis/RRT, and at least 24 hours of follow-up. Index is the first contiguous MAP<65 interval after the first qualifying MAP with the parent’s 30-minute grace; retain no-grace, 60-minute, thresholds 60 and 70 sensitivities.

For every eligible stay, define A=1 for the parent’s adequate ascertainment: at least two post-index numeric creatinine measurements, one in 0–24 h and one in 24–72 h, with no gap >12 h between the first and last qualifying measurement. Define A=0 otherwise. A is an observation-process indicator, not a clinical outcome. The primary outcome remains incident creatinine-based AKI through 72 h after index (>=0.3 mg/dL above baseline or >=1.5 times baseline), observed only when the relevant measurements exist. Never code A=0 as no AKI.

### Pre-index ascertainment model
Fit a prespecified model for P(A=1) using only information available by the index and the MAP observation process, never post-index creatinine values or AKI. Candidate predictors, all defined before index, are:
- patient demographics and admission context: age, gender, ethnicity, hospitalid, unittype, unitadmitsource, unitvisitnumber, unitstaytype;
- pre-index MAP opportunity and physiology: number and timing of valid MAP observations, coverage fraction, median gap, source mix (periodic versus aperiodic), pre-index MAP median/nadir/variability and pre-index heart-rate summaries;
- pre-index laboratory opportunity: count, first/last timing and maximum gap of any numeric lab measurements before index, plus baseline creatinine and its timing; do not use post-index lab counts or values;
- pre-index documentation opportunity: counts/timing of pre-index infusionDrug, medication orders and treatment records, with drugordercancelled retained, and pre-index MAP/vital observation density. These are observation proxies, not treatment effects.

The ascertainment model must be fit separately inside each training hospital and use hospital-held-out predictions for the transportability analysis. Use a regularized logistic model with prespecified missingness indicators and no post-index leakage. If the model is unstable or effective sample size is too small, report IPA as inconclusive rather than clipping or changing the cohort.

### Estimands and analysis
1. Parent primary: nested logistic models in A=1, opportunity-only versus opportunity+nadir versus opportunity+nadir+burden, with hospital-clustered bootstrap uncertainty. This remains the main claim.
2. Selection audit: report A by burden strata, hospital, baseline creatinine, pre-index observation opportunity, and discharge timing; report the fraction eligible with A=1 and the distribution of predicted P(A=1). This directly tests whether adequate ascertainment is concentrated in particular care/observation patterns.
3. IPA sensitivity: among A=1, use stabilized inverse-probability weights w=P(A=1|X)/P(A=1), with X restricted to the pre-index block above. Estimate the same nested contrasts in a weighted logistic analysis targeting the broader eligible cohort. Report unweighted and weighted estimates side by side, weight diagnostics (min/max/quantiles, effective sample size, overlap), and hospital-specific weighted estimates. Do not interpret weights as causal identification; they are a sensitivity to the selection model.
4. Standardization sensitivity: standardize the burden+nadir versus nadir-only contrast to the observed distribution of X in A=1 and, separately, to the broader eligible cohort’s X distribution using the fitted outcome models. Compare with the weighted estimate. This is a model-based transportability check, not a claim of exchangeability.
5. Negative control: repeat the selection model with post-index documentation features removed and with a deliberately nonclinical documentation feature (source mix or gap pattern) as the target. If the burden contrast is explained only by documentation intensity, the primary duration claim is weakened.

All model fitting, preprocessing, regularization and weight estimation must be inside training folds. Hospital-held-out evaluation is primary; within training hospitals use time-ordered splits by ICU admission offset or earliest qualifying MAP offset. Use hospital-clustered bootstrap intervals for both primary and weighted contrasts, and report calibration, Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC and decision-curve net benefit only as secondary summaries.

### Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: the burden+nadir contrast is directionally stable in A=1, weighted and standardized analyses, with uncertainty excluding the null and no material hospital/observation-density heterogeneity. This supports a reproducible prognostic duration signal that is not confined to the selected ascertainment pattern, conditional on the stated model assumptions.

Adverse: the contrast disappears or reverses under IPA/standardization, has poor overlap/effective sample size, is driven by one hospital/source, or the selection model shows A is strongly predictable from pre-index documentation independent of burden. This favors observation-process or selection explanations and stops the broader transportability claim.

Inconclusive: sparse A=1 events, unstable weights, wide intervals, or materially different results across strict/observed-trajectory views. An imprecise null is not decisive refutation.

The analysis cannot establish full KDIGO AKI, clinician-confirmed AKI, causal treatment effects, or transportability beyond eICU hospitals. Missing evidence includes validated urine-output adjudication, reliable pre-ICU baseline creatinine for every stay, complete nephrotoxin exposure, and clinician review.

## Method alternatives
The simple baseline remains the parent’s multivariable logistic regression in A=1; it is interpretable and directly tests the burden coefficient. The substantive learned alternative is a regularized gradient-boosted tree using the same five-minute summaries and feature blocks, with nested hospital-held-out/time-ordered evaluation, compared opportunity-only versus opportunity+nadir versus opportunity+nadir+burden. It can reveal nonlinear burden–nadir interactions and heterogeneity lost by the linear baseline, but a small AUROC gain is not scientific success. A mechanistic discrete-time survival model and neural sequence model remain deferred because the central uncertainty is ascertainment and observation, not a defensible causal time-to-event or waveform estimand.

## Unresolved issues and next step
The key unresolved feasibility issue is whether the pre-index observation features provide enough overlap to estimate P(A=1) without excessive weights; this must be measured by the solver, not assumed.
