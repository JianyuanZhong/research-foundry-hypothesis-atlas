# Live ICU discharge: ascertainable competing risks and observation-robust late instability

## Scientific deliverable and substantive evolution

The future Harbor solver must newly produce a complete, auditable measurement-and-ascertainment analysis:

1. a first eligible live-ICU-discharge cohort and flow table, with every exclusion separated into invalid timing, coverage insufficiency, and outcome-ascertainment unknown;
2. a twelve-bin late-window physiology representation and a separate observation/testing-process representation, each with per-row provenance and clinical-time checks;
3. a prespecified held-out comparison of a latest-state/context baseline with an order-blind twelve-hour-history model using the same source whitelist, patient split, and competing-risk endpoint;
4. a primary competing-risk estimand for first ICU readmission, timed in-hospital death, or alive hospital discharge before 48 hours, plus a clearly labeled fixed-horizon sensitivity;
5. sensitivity estimates for selective measurement coverage and missing timed death using stabilized inverse-probability weights, overlap diagnostics, and adverse/non-adverse unknown-label bounds;
6. a process-only model, chronology shuffles, mask/measurement-intensity falsifications, endpoint sensitivities, and subject-level bootstrap uncertainty;
7. paired held-out predictions and calibration/discrimination outputs for an order-blind aggregate model and a masked chronological GRU, with the GRU retained only as a substantive test of temporal order;
8. a conclusion-to-output audit that distinguishes computationally checkable prognostic findings from claims requiring clinical adjudication, external data, or an intervention study.

This is a substantive successor to assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the live-discharge landmark, exact MIMIC-IV 3.1 bindings, same-input baseline/history comparison, subject-level splitting, and observational limits. The material repair is that the late instability score, the documentation/testing process, and the outcome-ascertainment mechanism are now distinct estimands. The primary competing-risk outcome explicitly includes alive hospital discharge before 48 hours as a terminal competing state. A missing `deathtime` with `hospital_expire_flag=1` is an unknown label, not a stable discharge and not an automatically censored non-event.

The future solver must fit models and produce held-out predictions; no result is claimed by this proposal.

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and evidence boundary

At live ICU outtime, does the preceding 12-hour history contain reproducible physiologic information about the first near-term post-ICU state—ICU readmission, timed in-hospital death, or alive hospital discharge—beyond the terminal recorded state, admission/ICU context, and the intensity of observation and testing? This matters because patients at higher near-term risk could plausibly merit structured handoff review or enhanced post-ICU surveillance. MIMIC-IV does not record a randomized review or monitoring policy, discharge intent, or preventability, so this study can assess prognostic information and ascertainment robustness only. It cannot estimate whether delaying transfer, adding monitoring, or changing treatment improves outcomes.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is limited: the configured snapshot contains timestamped ICU boundaries, structured bedside observations, interval input records, laboratory testing records, and admission death/discharge fields. The MIMIC data and related prediction literature do not establish that a fixed late instability construct is measurement-invariant, that it predicts a competing-risk state beyond terminal state and charting intensity, or that a post-ICU intervention would help.

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among first eligible live ICU discharges, a twelve-hour history model that uses observed physiology and the exact observation-process signature improves held-out prediction of the competing first-event distribution—ICU readmission, timed death, alive hospital discharge before 48 hours, or no event by 48 hours—over a baseline using terminal physiology, context, and the same terminal/process information; the improvement persists in a coverage- and ascertainment-weighted sensitivity and is not reproduced by observation masks alone.

The primary incremental estimand is the paired test-set difference in multiclass Brier score:
`Delta_Brier_history = Brier(B1) - Brier(B0)`,
where lower is favorable. B0 and B1 use the same population, subject split, endpoint, source whitelist, preprocessing discipline, and observation-process variables; B1 adds the prespecified preterminal history. Report in addition the paired Brier difference for the clinically relevant composite risk `R or D`, calibration of its predicted cumulative incidence, and the differences in predicted cumulative incidence for each cause. A negative difference is incremental prognostic information, not net benefit, clinical utility, causal benefit, or evidence that discharge was inappropriate.

A second, mechanistically informative estimand compares B1 with a process-only history model Bmask that receives the same context, terminal observation/process fields, and all preterminal masks/counts/testing indicators but no physiologic values. If B1 outperforms Bmask after ascertainment/coverage sensitivity, the result is more consistent with information in observed physiology; if the difference disappears, documentation intensity or selective testing is a sufficient explanation. This remains an observational diagnostic, not proof that the measurements represent latent physiology.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The source unit is an ICU stay linked to one hospital admission.

1. Read every `icu/icustays` row with non-null `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, outtime`. Reject `outtime <= intime`, `outtime < admittime`, and rows without a matching admission.
2. Inner join `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`; require non-null `admittime` and `dischtime`.
3. Define an eligible live ICU discharge at `t0 = outtime` when `outtime < dischtime` and either `deathtime` is null or `deathtime > outtime`. The primary index is the earliest eligible discharge per `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, ordered by `outtime`. Report an all-eligible secondary analysis clustered by subject and hospitalization; it is not pooled as independent observations.
4. The target cohort is all eligible first discharges. For the primary complete-ascertainment analysis, retain an explicit ascertainable subset, but do not silently redefine the target: publish counts and baseline/process distributions for target-cohort exclusions, and estimate the all-eligible target under the stated missing-at-random sensitivity weights when overlap permits.
5. Define a coverage-qualified subset for the raw instability analysis as at least four of twelve bins having at least two valid core bedside domains. Do not call this subset representative by default. Model the probability of coverage qualification from pre-window context and prior observation intensity, report stabilized inverse-probability weights, effective sample size, weight percentiles, clipping, and positivity diagnostics.
6. Split every primary observation by `subject_id`; no subject occurs in more than one partition. Never align subjects by calendar timestamp because MIMIC timestamps are subject-specifically shifted.

The feature window is exactly `[t0 - 12 hours, t0]`, divided into twelve one-hour bins:
`[t0-12h,t0-11h), ..., [t0-1h,t0]`.
Use `charttime` for charted observations and `starttime/endtime` for interval inputs. `storetime` is never used for ordering, inclusion, missingness, or exposure duration. No row with clinical time after `t0` may enter a feature matrix, even if it was stored earlier.

## Primary ascertainable competing-risk estimand

For each index, order the first post-`t0` state within `(t0, min(t0+48h, dischtime)]`:

- **R: ICU readmission.** A later `icu/icustays` row with the same `subject_id, hadm_id`, a different `stay_id`, and `intime > t0`. Use its first qualifying `intime`.
- **D: timed in-hospital death.** `hosp/admissions.deathtime > t0`, no later than `dischtime`, and `deathtime <= t0+48h`. If death and readmission timestamps tie, assign D first; the composite R-or-D is unchanged.
- **S: alive hospital discharge before 48 hours.** `dischtime < t0+48h`, no qualifying R or D occurred earlier, and the record does not indicate death before or at discharge: `deathtime` is null or `deathtime > dischtime`, with `hospital_expire_flag=0`. This is a terminal competing state because after discharge the same-admission ICU-readmission process is no longer ascertainable in the configured data.
- **N: no R, D, or S by 48 hours.** The patient remains under observation at the horizon with no qualifying event.
- **U: outcome ascertainment unknown.** `hospital_expire_flag=1` with missing `deathtime`, or any other record-level contradiction that prevents deciding whether death occurred inside the horizon. U is retained in the flow and unknown-label analysis; it is excluded from the complete-ascertainment primary metric, never imputed as N or S.

A known ICU readmission or known timed death resolves the first state even if another field is incomplete. A later ICU stay after `dischtime` is not an event in this same-admission estimand. A non-null `deathtime` outside the 48-hour window is not D for the primary horizon. `hospital_expire_flag=0` with no `deathtime` does not imply death absence unless the admission record supplies a valid discharge state.

The primary clinical estimand is the 48-hour cumulative incidence vector `(CIF_R, CIF_D, CIF_S, CIF_N)` for the first state, with the composite near-term adverse incidence `CIF_A = CIF_R + CIF_D`. CIFs are calculated from discrete hourly cause-specific hazards or an equivalent competing-risk estimator, never by treating S as an ordinary negative outcome without reporting it. The prediction target is the corresponding four-state distribution among complete-ascertainment records. Report `CIF_A` at 12, 24, and 48 hours and cause-specific R/D/S components.

The primary estimand is first reported in the complete-ascertainment population with its exact target definition. The all-eligible sensitivity uses stabilized inverse-probability-of-ascertainment weights estimated without post-`t0` features, with a prespecified logistic model using demographics, admission/ICU context, pre-window coverage, measurement counts, and `hospital_expire_flag` availability indicators. It assumes conditional ascertainability given those recorded variables; it does not recover an unrecorded death time. Report overlap and effective sample size. If the weight model has poor overlap, label the all-eligible weighted result inconclusive rather than extrapolating.

For the fixed-horizon sensitivity, report the parent-style binary composite R-or-D within 48 hours with alive discharge administratively censored, but label it secondary and show how many S states were removed. The primary interpretation remains the competing-risk CIF, because discharge before 48 hours changes the opportunity for readmission.

## Measurement construct and exact feature separation

### Physiologic instability

Core bedside domains are HR, MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, FiO2, O2 flow, and PEEP. Use `valuenum` and `valueuom`; retain source item IDs. Reject fixed impossible ranges: HR 20–250 bpm, MAP 20–200 mmHg, RR 2–80/min, SpO2 50–100%, temperature 30–43 C, O2 flow 0–100 L/min, and PEEP 0–40 cmH2O. For FiO2, divide numeric values above 1.5 by 100, then retain 0.21–1.00. Record rejected rows and unit failures.

Within each bin, deduplicate each domain at a clinical timestamp by the median of valid numeric values. For MAP, use the highest-priority available source in this order: arterial `220052`, non-invasive `220181`, ART `225312`; then take the median within that source. A bin is valid for the instability score if at least two of the eight domains are observed. Do not carry values forward and do not impute an unobserved domain as normal.

Fixed abnormal indicators are: HR <50 or >120; MAP <65; RR <8 or >24; SpO2 <92; temperature <36 or >=38.5 C; FiO2 >0.40; O2 flow >=4 L/min; PEEP >5 cmH2O. For valid bin b, define:
`instability_b = abnormal_observed_domains_b / observed_domains_b`.
Define the primary LII as the unweighted mean of `instability_b` across valid bins, with at least four valid bins. Equal bin weighting prevents a densely charted hour from dominating, and the denominator prevents missing domains from being labeled normal.

Required robustness analyses are:

- require at least 4, 6, and 8 valid bins;
- require at least 4 observed core domains per valid bin;
- report complete-case domain-specific versions for the most frequently observed domains;
- compare equal-bin scoring with a prespecified coverage-weighted descriptive score;
- omit the terminal bin in an exploratory preterminal score while retaining terminal-state prediction separately;
- report the distribution of valid bins, observed domains, unit failures, and item-source conflicts by outcome-ascertainment category.

The primary instability construct is therefore not a latent “true readiness” label. It is a reproducible summary of observed abnormality conditional on observed domains.

### Observation and testing process

For every bin, save the eight binary domain masks, observed-domain count, valid-bin flag, and chart-row count. Also save:

- whether any laboratory assay was charted in the bin and counts of the six specified assays;
- whether Foley output was charted in the bin;
- whether a listed vasoactive/inotrope interval overlaps the bin, and the clipped overlap minutes;
- number of distinct clinical timestamps and source-item conflicts;
- prior-window measurement intensity summaries, separated from physiology.

The laboratory process uses `hosp/labevents` `charttime`, `itemid`, `valuenum`, `valueuom`, `priority`, and `comments), joined on `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and restricted to `charttime <= t0`. Use `hosp/d_labitems` to verify IDs. The six specified IDs are lactate `50813`, creatinine `50912`, bicarbonate `50882`, hemoglobin `51222`, platelets `51265`, and WBC `51300`. Values are not silently substituted for missing tests. Lab values can enter a prespecified sensitivity model, but the primary bedside LII does not treat selective lab testing as physiologic absence.

Foley output uses `icu/outputevents` item `226559`, with `charttime <= t0`; missing output is not zero. Vasoactive/inotrope presence uses `icu/inputevents` item IDs `221906, 222315, 221289, 229617, 221749, 229630, 229631, 229632, 221662, 221653, 221986`. Clip intervals defined by `starttime/endtime` to the feature window. Reject missing or reversed interval timing for duration; use a separate clinically timed presence flag only where timing is valid. No dose is used as a comparable physiologic quantity.

Observation-process variables are included in B0, B1, and Bmask in the same source whitelist. A process signal is not relabeled as physiologic instability.

## Decision models and fair baseline/history comparison

The decision target is a prognostic triage signal available at `t0`: predicted competing risks for structured post-ICU review or monitoring. The records do not contain a policy effect, so threshold tables are operating characteristics only.

Define the common context C as: `anchor_age`, `gender`, `anchor_year_group`, `first_careunit`, `last_careunit`, ICU duration at `t0`, `admission_type`, `admission_location`, earlier ICU-stay count in the admission, and pre-window coverage/process summaries. Do not use `discharge_location`, notes, `dod`, or any post-outtime field.

- **B0 latest-state/context baseline:** C; terminal-bin numeric values and masks for the eight core domains; terminal observed-domain count; terminal lab/output/pressor process indicators.
- **B1 order-blind history model:** exactly the B0 inputs plus order-blind summaries over all twelve bins: per-domain mean, median, minimum, maximum, SD, IQR, abnormal-bin fraction, valid-bin count, observation count/missingness, pressor presence, clipped pressor minutes, and testing/output indicators. Exclude bin identity, slopes, reversal counts, sequence position, endpoint fields, and post-outtime data.
- **Bmask process-only history model:** exactly the B0 inputs plus the full history of masks, counts, lab-testing indicators, Foley charting, and vasoactive interval presence/minutes, but no physiologic values. It tests whether B1’s improvement requires observed values rather than the act of charting.
- **GRU chronological alternative:** twelve chronological vectors containing the eight numeric domains, masks, fixed abnormal flags, observed-domain count, and timed process indicators, with identical C concatenated to the final hidden representation. Use a small masked GRU (hidden size 32 or 64, dropout and early stopping selected on validation only) with one multinomial/specified hazard head. It is not a causal model.

All models use the same target, split, source whitelist, subject-level partition, and training/validation-only preprocessing. Fit a penalized multinomial logistic model or discrete-time cause-specific hazard model for B0 and B1; choose the model form before test evaluation and keep it identical across B0/B1. For the GRU, use five fixed seeds and validation selection. Calibrate only on training/validation data.

The primary comparison is B1 versus B0 using paired test predictions. The physiology-specific secondary comparison is B1 versus Bmask. The temporal-order comparison is GRU versus B1. The GRU can reveal persistence, recovery, fluctuation, and coupled order that aggregate summaries lose. It is selected only because that information bears directly on the instability construct; neural complexity or a small AUROC change is not itself a clinical advance. A last-bin-only comparator remains a descriptive diagnostic.

## Split, uncertainty, and weighting

Before fitting, freeze one deterministic approximately stratified 70/15/15 split by subject, stratifying only on available training-time event categories after resolving the split manifest. All preprocessing, scaling, regularization, model selection, calibration maps, coverage/ascertainment weight models, and thresholds use training/validation only. No test outcome selects LII quartiles, features, clipping, or model settings. LII quartiles are computed outcome-free in the training population for modeling and separately reported in the test population without outcome-based cutpoint changes.

For each model report:

- multiclass Brier score for R, D, S, and N;
- Brier score for A=R-or-D and calibration intercept/slope for A;
- CIF calibration at 12, 24, and 48 hours;
- AUROC/AUPRC for A and one-vs-rest cause-specific secondary metrics;
- paired risk differences at prespecified absolute-risk thresholds such as 2%, 5%, and 10%, selected without test outcomes;
- confusion/operating tables only as descriptive threshold characteristics, never as net benefit.

Use 1,000 subject-level bootstrap replicates when within the solver cap; otherwise report the exact number completed and mark execution-limited. Bootstrap the entire test prediction comparison and, for weighted analyses, refit or appropriately recompute the ascertainment/coverage weights within each replicate. Report successful replicate counts, weight ESS, clipping, and 95% intervals. Preserve seeds, split manifest, model states, predictions, and bootstrap indices.

The LII association is secondary and observational. Fit a cause-specific or discrete-time competing-risk model with LII per training IQR and prespecified C/process covariates. Report cause-specific coefficients and standardized 48-hour CIF contrasts `CIF_A(Q75)-CIF_A(Q25)`, `CIF_R(Q75)-CIF_R(Q25)`, and `CIF_D(Q75)-CIF_D(Q25)`. Do not call these treatment effects or discharge-appropriateness contrasts. Repeat with coverage and ascertainment weights; report nonlinearity only as a prespecified spline sensitivity with validation discipline.

## Falsification and diagnostic criteria

All derived rows must contain `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, t0, bin_start, bin_end, source_table, archive_member, itemid, clinical_time, filter_reason`. Export assertions that every feature clinical time is <= `t0`, every input interval was clipped using clinical time, no subject crosses partitions, and prohibited fields do not appear in any feature matrix.

Required falsifications:

1. **Mask/process-only test:** compare Bmask with B0 and B1 with Bmask. If Bmask matches B1, the apparent history signal is plausibly documentation/testing intensity.
2. **Preterminal chronology shuffle:** within test patients, shuffle bins 1–11 while holding terminal bin 12, all values/masks, C, and endpoint fixed. A chronology-dependent GRU advantage should attenuate.
3. **All-bin shuffle:** shuffle all twelve bins as a secondary check and report the changed terminal state.
4. **Coverage perturbation:** repeat with valid-bin thresholds 4, 6, and 8 and with inverse-coverage weighting. A result that changes sign or disappears under modest ascertainment definitions is fragile.
5. **Label ascertainment sensitivity:** compare complete-ascertainment, weighted all-eligible, and unknown-label adverse/non-adverse bounds. Do not pool U into N.
6. **Endpoint sensitivity:** compare the primary R/D/S/N competing-risk target with the fixed-horizon R-or-D analysis and 7-day analogues. Report readmission-first and death-first components.
7. **Terminal-state diagnostic:** compare B0 with a context-only model and a terminal-only model to show whether the history increment is distinct from terminal physiology.
8. **Within-split outcome permutation:** permute labels within training/validation/test partitions under a fixed seed and refit. Discrimination and calibration improvement should collapse to chance; a persistent gain indicates leakage or an evaluation defect.

Supportive history evidence requires Delta_Brier_history < 0 with a 95% interval excluding zero, no important calibration degradation, directionally consistent CIF_A improvement in the coverage and ascertainment sensitivities, and a nontrivial B1-versus-Bmask contrast. Supportive temporal evidence additionally requires GRU improvement over B1 and attenuation after preterminal shuffle. These rules do not establish utility, preventability, or policy benefit.

Adverse evidence includes no history increment, calibration worsening, instability across coverage thresholds, disappearance after ascertainment weighting, Bmask matching B1, a gain confined to care-unit or measurement-intensity strata, shuffle-resistant chronology claims, or a label-permutation failure. These support the narrower conclusion that terminal status or the documentation process—not recoverable temporal physiology—explains the signal.

Inconclusive evidence includes low event counts, high U fraction, poor weight overlap, low effective sample size, inadequate valid-bin coverage, malformed interval timing, unit-validation failures, unstable calibration, contradictory endpoint counts, or discordant sensitivity estimates. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.

## Clinical adjudication, missing evidence, and claim limits

Computationally checkable claims are the source/member/schema/item provenance; joins; row counts; timing and coverage exclusions; U-state flow; interval clipping; split integrity; feature whitelist; model configuration; held-out predictions; CIF/Brier/calibration outputs; weight overlap and ESS; bootstrap intervals; and falsification effects.

Unavailable or non-identifiable claims include clinician discharge intent, ICU-transfer appropriateness, goals of care and treatment limitations, preventability, ward surveillance intensity, bed/staffing pressure, whether charted oxygen equals delivered physiology, why a test was ordered or omitted, whether a missing death time represents a particular clinical course, whether enhanced review/monitoring changes outcomes, net clinical benefit, external validity, and causal treatment effects. These require clinical adjudication, an external cohort with better death/readmission ascertainment, and preferably a prospective or interventional evaluation.

A result that survives all computational sensitivities can support only: “the specified observed-data representation has reproducible prognostic information for this MIMIC-IV competing-risk endpoint, under the stated ascertainment assumptions.” It cannot support: “the patient was discharged too early,” “the instability was causal,” “the event was preventable,” or “monitoring would reduce readmission or death.”

## Alternatives, actual selection, and resource plan

The simple baseline is B0 penalized multinomial logistic/discrete-time hazard regression: terminal physiology plus context and observation process. It is transparent, CPU-feasible, and directly tests whether a live-discharge snapshot is adequate.

The learned alternative is the matched B1 aggregate history model plus the masked chronological GRU. B1 tests whether preterminal history adds information without chronology; the GRU tests whether persistence, recovery, fluctuation, or coupled temporal order adds information that order-blind summaries lose. Both use the same Y=(R,D,S,N) target, subject split, held-out Brier/CIF calibration, AUROC/AUPRC, and subject-bootstrap uncertainty. The process-only Bmask is necessary to determine whether any claimed physiology increment survives charting-process control.

The selected primary method is the aggregate B1 comparison against B0 because it is the most interpretable and CPU-feasible test of the scientific question. The GRU is a prespecified secondary alternative, not a merit bonus. Do not defer it merely because it is neural; defer or downsize it only if actual profiling shows it cannot fit the solver envelope. A causal discharge-policy model is explicitly not chosen because randomized policy, discharge intent, treatment limitation, ward monitoring, and preventability evidence are unavailable.

Future solver planning envelope: up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds. The discovery compute limit is 7,200 seconds and is separate. Cohort aggregation and multinomial/logistic fits are expected CPU-feasible. The bounded 12-step GRU has low memory demand and should be CPU-feasible for one split/five seeds; one allocated A100-SXM4-80GB may accelerate repeated seeds/bootstrap inference if measured profiling requires it. Full ZIP reading and 1,000 weighted bootstrap replicates are unmeasured; the solver must preserve completed counts and label execution-limited intervals rather than silently shrinking the hypothesis. No discovery fit or scientific outcome was performed.
