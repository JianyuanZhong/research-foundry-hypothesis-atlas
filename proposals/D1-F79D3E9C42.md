# When should laboratory surveillance fall after recorded instability?

Status: Episode 47 substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`, adapting the assessed eICU seed `[starting question]`. This is a fixed-landmark, observational recorded-care/process experiment. It is not a trial of laboratory ordering, a causal test of monitoring intensity, a test of treatment efficacy, a physiologic-recovery study, or a hospital-quality ranking.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported by the material inspected is limited. The frozen eICU snapshot contains multi-hospital, ICU-relative structured laboratory, vital-sign, treatment, respiratory-care, care-plan and discharge records. The bundled eICU documentation explicitly warns that interfaces differ by care unit and that absence of an interface can yield no data even when a measurement occurred. The parent establishes a rigorous way to ask whether laboratory-observation history adds recorded-data prognostic information after vital and documented-support adjustment, but it does not establish a clinical result; no fitted result is reused here.

The unresolved clinical-process question is:

> Among adult first ICU stays with reproducible recorded instability and prior laboratory surveillance, does an abrupt fall to no recorded laboratory availability in the final hour before minute 360 identify a death-specific risk signal in the next 24 hours, beyond preceding recorded physiology, support activity, prior laboratory state, and goals-of-care documentation—and is that signal specific to the unstable subgroup rather than a generic discharge or interface artifact?

This is clinically consequential because a surveillance fall after instability has at least two incompatible interpretations. It may be a benign transition, impending discharge, end-of-life documentation, or an interface gap. Alternatively, it may mark a mismatch between recorded monitoring and ongoing instability. Distinguishing these interpretations determines whether a future silent-mode review policy should even be considered. A positive association would not show that more testing improves survival or that a missed biochemical injury occurred. It would identify a reproducible recorded-data signal that merits prospective adjudication. A null result would be valuable evidence against treating this process pattern as a general review cue.

The substantive advance over the parent is a prespecified within-stay change and effect-modification estimand. The parent asks whether a laboratory-process increment remains after support-context adjustment. This successor asks whether the *timing of a surveillance reduction immediately after an instability episode* has a death-specific association, tests it against a stable recorded-state subgroup, and explicitly separates it from care transition, end-of-life, and interface explanations. This is a new clinical process question, not a larger model or another generic transport score.

## Hypothesis and evidence boundary

Primary H1 is a subgroup interaction hypothesis. In the primary instability risk set, the observed contrast between no available laboratory time in (300,360] after at least two available times in (180,300] and continued availability will have a positive, uncertainty-compatible association with the recorded-death cumulative incidence through 1,800 minutes. The contrast will be larger than the analogous contrast in a support-matched, non-unstable subgroup. Adding the final-hour process features will improve hospital-held-out death-specific integrated competing-risk Brier score after adjustment for prior state and care-process variables.

The competing outcome is alive ICU discharge in (360,1800], including transfer/disposition recorded as `unitdischargestatus = Alive`. A comparable or larger process increment for alive discharge is evidence that the pattern mainly captures disposition or length-of-stay workflow. The administrative censoring outcome is no event by minute 1,800.

The nulls are: no incremental signal after preceding state; no instability-specific interaction; persistence after patient/time permutation; equivalent signal from generic documentation loss; or a signal confined to hospitals, interfaces, end-of-life documentation, or the final-bin timing definition. None of these results establishes absence of biological deterioration, because biological state and unrecorded tests are not observed.

The general landmark cohort is all such first stays. The primary dynamic risk set R additionally requires:

- at least two distinct revision-safe laboratory availability times in (180,300], so that a fall is observable rather than merely a no-interface row;

- no `carePlanEOL` save or discussion offset in (300,360] in the primary “care-eligible” analysis. All R stays are retained for a prespecified EOL-inclusive sensitivity.

Define recorded vital instability I=1 when, among distinct `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset` times in (180,300], at least two times separated by >=30 minutes have any of: numeric `systemicmean < 65`, `sao2 < 92`, `heartrate >= 120`, or `respiration >= 24`. I=0 is a support-matched comparator with the same observation-support requirements and no qualifying abnormal observation in that window. Missing fields do not count as normal. The primary analysis is I=1; the I=0 group estimates whether the process contrast is instability-specific. Thresholds and the 30-minute separation are frozen before outcome analysis. A secondary, separately labeled instability phenotype adds a documented support transition in (180,300], but it cannot replace the vital-defined primary state.

For `lab`, retain a row only if numeric `labresultoffset` is in [0,360] and `labresultrevisedoffset` is missing or <=360. Define `availability_offset = max(labresultoffset, labresultrevisedoffset)` when revision is numeric, otherwise `labresultoffset`. The primary exposure uses distinct `availability_offset` times, not raw row counts. The paired result-time sensitivity uses distinct `labresultoffset` times on the identical retained row set. Invalid or post-360 revisions are excluded from primary predictors and counted in a leakage audit.

Define the primary reduced-surveillance exposure D=1 when the patient has >=2 distinct availability times in (180,300] and zero distinct availability times in (300,360]. D=0 requires >=2 distinct availability times in both (180,300] and (300,360]. The baseline number of times, distinct key/interface count, revision-known/missing count and gap/span through minute 300 remain covariates. A secondary continuous change uses last-bin versus prior-window counts and is not allowed to redefine the primary exposure. A no-interface patient is not silently treated as D=1.

The outcome window is (360,1800], in 12 intervals (360,420], (420,480], ..., (1740,1800]. Cause 1 is recorded ICU death when `unitdischargestatus = Expired` and numeric `unitdischargeoffset` is in this window. Cause 2 is alive ICU discharge when `unitdischargestatus = Alive` and the offset is in this window. Missing/other status is reported and excluded by a frozen rule; destination is never used to impute death. Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, and undocumented death are outside the estimand.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative

Method selection follows the scientific comparison. Both branches use exactly the same rows, R/I definitions, six time bins, admission/state covariates, missingness, fit-fold codebooks/scaling, outcomes, and hospital folds.

Interpretable baseline:

- `B_pre`: pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards for death and discharge, with admission context, recorded vital state/trajectory through 300, I, prior laboratory state/process through 300, documented support activity/transitions through 300, and EOL documentation through 300. It excludes all (300,360] laboratory process variables.
- `B_drop`: `B_pre) plus D, last-bin distinct lab availability/result times, key/interface count, revision status, and prespecified I-by-D interaction. The primary scientific increment is `B_drop - B_pre`, evaluated in I=1 and through the I-by-D contrast. `B_drop` is not interpreted as a causal effect of ordering or withholding tests.

Scientifically substantive alternative:

- `T_drop`: a one-layer six-bin GRU with separate death and discharge hazard heads and the same admission, vital, lab-state, lab-process, support, EOL, and missingness channels as `B_drop`. The sequence can represent whether abnormal vitals in (180,300] are followed by loss of lab availability in (300,360], persistence across bins, and cross-domain co-movement that additive baseline summaries may lose. It must not use `hospitalid), post-360 values, APACHE mortality/APS fields, or future-derived variables.
- Fit a parameter-matched non-temporal MLP on the same patient-level channels and a within-patient six-bin order-shuffle control. A learned advantage is scientific evidence for temporal ordering only if it disappears under order shuffle and remains over the matched MLP in held-out hospitals. A GRU winning without that falsification is not supportive.

The baseline is preferred for the primary interpretation because the process exposure and I-by-D interaction are auditable. The GRU is retained because temporal ordering is a substantive uncertainty in this question, not because it is complex. A mechanistic latent monitoring-state model is deferred: verified specimen/order times, laboratory units, raw waveforms, delivered-dose intervals, and expert-adjudicated physiologic state are unavailable. Revisit it only with those dependencies and a prespecified observation/outcome joint model.

Report the 95% interval for the 1,440-minute standardized recorded-death CIF contrast D=1 versus D=0 separately for I=1 and I=0, plus the difference of those contrasts. If a gate fails, report the affected estimand as inconclusive rather than shrink to a more convenient population.

## Falsification and observation-process controls

1. Reassign laboratory process histories within hospital, I, prior lab-intensity and baseline-risk strata, preserving per-patient process distributions while breaking patient linkage. The primary death contrast should attenuate.
2. Permute the two pre-landmark lab-bin labels (180,300] and (300,360] within patient among support-compatible rows, preserving total counts, key composition, revisions and missingness. A signal that survives is not evidence that reduction follows instability.
3. Reassign the instability labels or vital histories within hospital and baseline-risk strata, preserving observation burden. A patient-specific instability-by-drop effect should attenuate.
4. Fit the analogous documented-support drop after I using treatment and respiratory-care event counts. A similarly large effect indicates generic care-documentation or discharge workflow rather than laboratory-specific information.
5. Compare recorded death and alive-discharge CIF increments. A discharge increment as large or larger, especially if concentrated near the landmark, is adverse to a deterioration interpretation.
6. Repeat after excluding any EOL save/discussion in [0,360], then include EOL timing as a covariate; report the difference rather than claiming EOL absence.
7. Mask (300,360] predictors and refit the baseline; then mask earlier bins. If the claimed association is recreated by earlier process alone or depends on an invalid post-landmark field, reject the timing interpretation.
8. Repeat with result-time versus conservative availability-time clocks on identical retained lab rows, revision-known-only rows, and common key/interface panels. No unit conversion or cross-interface pooling is allowed.
9. Repeat respiratory-care-only, treatment-only, and infusion corroboration analyses. Infusion fields cannot establish administration, dose, duration or delivery.
10. Repeat over outcome-free hospital surveillance regimes and leave one supported regime out. A one-hospital/interface effect or a failed overlap gate is not transportable evidence.
11. Vary the recorded-instability threshold in a prespecified sensitivity (each single abnormal field and the two-observation requirement). If the claim reverses under clinically adjacent definitions, classify it as unstable/inconclusive.
12. For `T_drop`, run order-shuffle and parameter-matched MLP controls. Do not interpret a surviving shuffle advantage as temporal biology.
13. Audit every predictor for no post-360 value, no `activeupondischarge` state inference, no invalid revision, no hospital identifier, no target-fold preprocessing, no APACHE fields, and no text-derived diagnosis.

## Interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all support and overlap gates; a positive held-out I=1 death-specific D contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero, an incremental `B_drop-B_pre` improvement, and an instability interaction whose interval excludes zero. To call it clinically material, the lower interval bound for the 1,440-minute death-CIF interaction must exceed a prespecified 1 percentage point; smaller positive effects remain exploratory. The effect must be smaller or null for alive discharge, attenuate under patient-link and time-bin permutations, survive final-bin and clock/interface checks, and be stable in macro-hospital estimates and leave-one-regime-out folds. The learned branch additionally requires order sensitivity over the matched MLP. The bounded conclusion would be: in supported held-out eICU hospitals, a recorded laboratory-surveillance reduction after recorded vital instability is a death-specific prognostic process marker not redundant with preceding recorded state. It would not be “under-testing caused deterioration.”

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversed I=1 contrast, no I-by-D difference, unchanged contrast after patient/time permutation, a similarly large support-only or discharge contrast, concentration in one interface/hospital, dependence on EOL/disposition, or a learned gain that survives order shuffling. High overall discrimination does not rescue these failures.

Inconclusive evidence includes failure of the dynamic risk-set or event gates, sparse D cells, unstable hospital folds, intervals spanning zero or the materiality threshold, nontransportable interface coverage, disagreement between result and availability clocks, revision ambiguity, nonconvergence, or inconsistent instability definitions. A negative/inconclusive result speaks only to this recorded-data estimand and cannot prove that monitoring was adequate or that biological deterioration was absent.
