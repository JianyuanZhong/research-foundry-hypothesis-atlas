## Scientific Question

**Primary Hypothesis**: After circulatory stabilization in critically ill patients, discordant organ recovery patterns (temporal dissociation between recovery trajectories of different organ systems) are independently associated with higher risk of subsequent deterioration and mortality, beyond what is captured by any single organ function measure or aggregate severity scores.

**Unresolved Question**: Can we identify patients at high risk of deterioration based on the temporal discordance between organ-specific recovery trajectories, and does this risk stratification outperform traditional single-organ metrics (e.g., creatinine alone) or aggregate severity scores (e.g., SOFA)?

## Clinical Importance

### 1. Current Limitations in Critical Care Practice
Current critical care practice relies heavily on:
- **Aggregate severity scores** (SOFA, qSOFA) that provide a single composite measure of organ dysfunction
- **Single-organ metrics** (e.g., creatinine for kidney function, PaO2/FiO2 for respiratory function)
- **Static assessments** performed at discrete timepoints (admission, daily rounds)

These approaches have critical limitations:
- **Masking discordance**: A patient may have excellent kidney recovery while respiratory function deteriorates, yet a stable SOFA score would miss this dangerous pattern
- **Delayed detection**: Aggregate scores may remain stable while individual organs are diverging in opposite directions
- **Inappropriate interventions**: Stable aggregate scores may lead clinicians to de-escalate care when one organ system is deteriorating

### 2. Risk of Harm from Ignoring Discordance
Patients with discordant recovery patterns may receive:
- **Inappropriate treatment intensification**: Continuing aggressive ventilation or vasopressors when kidney function has recovered
- **Premature de-escalation**: Discontinuing renal support when respiratory function is still precarious
- **Missed deterioration**: Delayed recognition of impending failure in one organ system

### 3. Treatment Optimization
Identifying patients at risk of deterioration based on discordant recovery could enable:
- **Proactive monitoring**: Enhanced surveillance for patients with discordant patterns
- **Preemptive interventions**: Earlier escalation or de-escalation of support
- **Risk-stratified care**: Targeted resources for highest-risk patients

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

### 1. Beyond Static Severity Assessment
The expert seed mimic-01 ("Asynchronous organ recovery") proposed that "after circulatory stabilization, patients whose kidney or respiratory function remains impaired are more likely to deteriorate again." However, this proposal advances beyond the seed by:

- **Focusing on temporal discordance** rather than persistent impairment: The key insight is not just which organs remain impaired, but whether different organs recover at **different rates** after stabilization
- **Testing against single-organ and aggregate metrics**: Rather than just identifying high-risk patients, we test whether discordance-based prediction **outperforms** existing approaches
- **Temporal specificity**: Defining recovery as the trajectory from stabilization to deterioration, not just a binary state

### 2. Theoretical Framework
This proposal introduces the concept of **"discordant recovery"** as a distinct phenotype:
- **Concordant recovery**: Multiple organ systems improve together
- **Discordant recovery**: Different organs recover at different rates, potentially signaling systemic instability

This reframes the clinical question from "which organs remain impaired" to "which organs are recovering at different rates relative to each other."

### 3. Methodological Innovation
The proposal specifies:
- **Latent trajectory modeling**: Using machine learning to learn individual recovery trajectories, similar to approaches in [K1]
- **Discordance metrics**: Quantifying temporal divergence between organ-specific trajectories
- **External validation**: Testing against outcomes not used in training

### Key Variables for Analysis

#### Perfusion Status (Effect Modifier)
- **Vasopressor use** (from `icu/inputevents` or `icu/chartevents`): Norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine
- **Lactate levels** (from `hosp/labevents`): Serial measurements
- **Norepinephrine dose** (from `icu/inputevents`): mcg/kg/min
- **Perfusion status definition**: Stable (norepinephrine ≤ 0.05 mcg/kg/min AND lactate ≤ 2.0 mmol/L) vs. Hypoperfused (either > thresholds)

#### Organ Function Measures
- **Kidney**: Serum creatinine, urine output, creatinine clearance
- **Respiratory**: PaO2/FiO2, ventilator settings, ventilator-free days
- **Cardiovascular**: MAP, heart rate, cardiac output (if measured)
- **Liver**: Bilirubin, INR, albumin
- **Gastrointestinal**: Lactate clearance, bowel function

#### Outcome Variables
- **Deterioration**: Defined as ≥1 point increase in SOFA score or requiring escalation of organ support
- **Mortality**: In-hospital ICU mortality
- **Length of stay**: ICU length of stay (ILS), hospital length of stay (HLS)
- **Readmission**: 30-day readmission rate

#### Timepoints for Recovery Assessment
- **Baseline**: At ICU admission or at circulatory stabilization
- **Recovery assessment**: At stabilization (post-vasopressor weaning)
- **Follow-up**: Serial assessments throughout ICU stay

## Baseline Approach (What This Proposal Tests Against)

### Baseline: Logistic Regression with Static Features
The baseline model would predict deterioration using:
- Admission SOFA score
- Admission organ function metrics
- Demographics and comorbidities
- Admission vitals

**Limitation**: This baseline cannot capture temporal dynamics or discordance between organ systems.

### Alternative: Learned Longitudinal Trajectory Model
The proposed alternative would:
1. **Learn individual organ recovery trajectories** using a sequence model (e.g., modified GPT-2 or transformer architecture, similar to [K1])
2. **Compute discordance metrics**: Temporal divergence between organ-specific trajectories
3. **Predict deterioration** based on discordance patterns

**Scientific Information Revealed**: The learned model could identify subtle, non-linear patterns of discordance that static metrics miss, potentially revealing:
- **Non-monotonic recovery**: Organs that improve then deteriorate
- **Asynchronous divergence**: Different organs diverging at different timepoints
- **Systemic instability**: Discordance patterns that precede deterioration by hours/days

## Analysis Plan

### Study Population
- **Inclusion**: ICU patients ≥24 hours
- **Exclusion**: <24 hours, transients, non-ICU transfers
- **Cohort**: All patients with ≥1 vasopressor administration (to define stabilization event)

### Temporal Boundaries
- **Index date**: Circulatory stabilization (defined as ≥24 hours off vasopressors with stable vitals)
- **Follow-up**: 0-48 hours post-stabilization (primary outcome window)
- **Censoring**: ICU discharge, death, or end of follow-up

### Primary Analysis
Compare discordance-based prediction of deterioration vs. baseline (static features) using:
- **C-statistic** (discrimination)
- **Calibration** (calibration plot, Brier score)
- **Net reclassification improvement (NRI)**
- **Decision curve analysis**

### Secondary Analyses
- **Subgroup by perfusion status**: Does discordance matter more in hypoperfused vs. stable patients?
- **Subgroup by organ system**: Which organ pairs show strongest discordance?
- **Temporal patterns**: At what timepoint post-stabilization does discordance become predictive?

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. **No association**: Discordant recovery patterns show no association with subsequent deterioration after adjusting for baseline organ function
2. **No improvement**: Discordance-based models do not outperform baseline static-feature models in discrimination or calibration
3. **No clinical utility**: Decision curve analysis shows no net benefit over clinical decision-making using standard practices

### Supportive Evidence (Not Proof)
- Discordance patterns are associated with higher deterioration risk
- Discordance-based models outperform baseline models
- Discordance metrics identify additional high-risk patients

### Inconclusive Results
- Discordance is associated with outcomes but models are not significantly better than baseline
- Discordance patterns vary by organ system or patient subgroup

## Missing Evidence and Clinical Adjudication

### Unavailable Data
- **Ground truth for discordance**: No existing clinical tool measures discordance; this is novel
- **External validation**: No external ICU dataset available for validation
- **Mechanistic data**: No data on why discordance predicts deterioration (biological mechanisms unknown)

### Clinical Adjudication Required
- **Definition of "stabilization"**: Clinical consensus needed on what constitutes circulatory stabilization
- **Discordance threshold**: What magnitude of discordance is clinically meaningful?
- **Actionability**: Would clinicians act on discordance signals? (Requires prospective validation)

**Limitation**: Focuses on disease diagnoses across the lifespan, not acute organ recovery trajectories; uses UK Biobank data, not ICU-specific data; does not address the specific question of discordant organ recovery.

### [K2] Zhuang et al. 2026 - Association of Active Fluid De-escalation Timing with Clinical Outcomes in Patients with Septic Shock
**Claim**: Early fluid de-escalation (within 24 hours of hemodynamic stability) in septic shock patients is associated with improved mortality and reduced organ dysfunction compared to late de-escalation, supporting the importance of timing in organ-support interventions.

**Contribution**: Establishes that the state of the patient at intervention time (hemodynamic stability) modifies treatment effects; provides empirical evidence that optimal timing of organ-support interventions matters for outcomes.

**Limitation**: Focuses on fluid de-escalation as a single intervention, not general discordance between organ systems; uses aggregate fluid balance rather than individual organ recovery trajectories; does not test prediction of deterioration based on temporal patterns.

**Inspected**: Abstract available at pubmed:41593444, [source checksum]

### [K3] Vincent et al. 1998 - Use of the SOFA Score to Assess the Incidence of Organ Dysfunction/Failure in Intensive Care Units
**Claim**: The SOFA score is an effective method to describe organ dysfunction/failure in critically ill patients, but regular repeated scoring is needed to monitor disease development; survivors and nonsurvivors follow different courses in SOFA score trajectories.

**Contribution**: Establishes the baseline metric (SOFA) against which discordance-based prediction is tested; demonstrates that SOFA score trajectories differ between survivors and nonsurvivors, validating temporal assessment of organ dysfunction; highlights the clinical motivation for improving beyond aggregate scores.

**Limitation**: Provides aggregate severity score that masks discordance between organ systems; does not address temporal dynamics or discordance between organs; does not provide methods for predicting deterioration based on discordant patterns; focus is on assessment rather than prediction.
