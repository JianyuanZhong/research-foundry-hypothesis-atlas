## Supported claim, unresolved claim, and substantive advance

The strongest evidence supported before this experiment is narrower. Chen et al. (Kidney Medicine 2024; DOI 10.1016/j.xkme.2024.100796; PMCID PMC10986041; controlling-parent frozen full-text XML inspected) supports internal prediction of creatinine-cystatin eGFR discordance in UK Biobank, not this allocation contrast. Lees et al. (JAMA Network Open 2022; DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.38300; PMCID PMC9597396) was acquired and inspected from full-text Europe PMC XML source_id `[source checksum]`, retrieved-source [source checksum]. It studied UK Biobank participants with urine albumin below 30 mg/g and evaluated cystatin-C-based reclassification for risk stratification over median 11.5 years. It supports the clinical relevance of asking about cystatin information when albuminuria is low; it does not test AG1 versus G1, a nine-year inpatient N18 endpoint, or testing benefit. The parent’s Estrella evidence remains abstract/core-record only; no unavailable full article is claimed as inspected.

The unresolved claim is whether the parent’s allocation contrast has prognosis relevance among observed A1 participants. Total N18 enrichment alone could be driven by people whose UACR already flags kidney risk. Conversely, A1-tagged enrichment would show that the fixed list contains more people with a later recorded kidney-code event despite a low observed urine ratio. That is a substantive complementarity test and could prioritize a prospective study in people who lack an albuminuria trigger.

Even full support establishes neither “nonalbuminuric CKD” nor actionability. A single UKB UACR is variable, the instance does not prove specimen timing, N18 is an inpatient administrative code, and cystatin C was not assigned. Persistent A1 status, true CKD, appropriate clinical action, and benefit require repeated timed tests, measured GFR, adjudication, and another study.

## Interpretation limits and stronger evidence required

Support means only that the frozen AG1 list has a higher absolute yield of participants with observed low snapshot UACR and a later recorded inpatient N18 code. It strengthens the rationale for an adjudicated prospective comparison, but does not prove low-albuminuria CKD, filtration accuracy, persistence, mechanism, management change, cost-effectiveness, fairness, prevention, or benefit.

Adverse evidence weakens the claim that AG1's prognosis enrichment extends to observed A1 participants. Inconclusive evidence leaves it unresolved. Neither state identifies an optimal workflow.

Stronger claims require repeated, clinically timed UACR/eGFR; outpatient and primary-care kidney diagnoses; individual censoring/emigration and kidney-replacement dates; measured GFR; medication and downstream-action data; chart/nephrologist adjudication; costs, turnaround, harms, and equity review; external validation; and a prospective implementation or randomized testing-strategy study with patient-centered outcomes.
