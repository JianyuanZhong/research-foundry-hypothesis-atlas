# Fixed-capacity value of prior biomarker history for pathway-specific review

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`
Catalog: `[internal dataset path]`, [source checksum]
Status: frozen research proposal; no model has been fitted and no clinical conclusion is claimed.

## Scientific question and substantive advance

The incumbent asks whether prior-panel information improves Brier score by pathway. This child makes the clinical decision explicit:

> After a current assessment, when only a fixed number of people can be sent for pathway-specific review, does retaining the earlier biomarker panel change which people are selected and capture more of the subsequently recorded target-domain events than a current-panel-plus-spacing policy?

The primary estimand is the paired, held-out difference in event capture at a prespecified review capacity, separately for cardiovascular (C), renal (R), and metabolic (M) target pathways:
`TCE_d(K)=P(Y_d=1 mid i in TopK(M1b,d))-P(Y_d=1 mid i in TopK(M1a,d))`,
where `K=floor(0.10*n_eligible)` is fixed within each outer-fold evaluation set and `TopK` is ranked using held-out predicted probability for domain `d`. The estimand is conditional on the valid paired-assessment cohort, not a causal effect of ordering a repeat test or of review.

The primary hypothesis is direction-free and falsifiable: prior history changes fixed-capacity review allocation in a clinically material, pathway-specific way. Support requires a prespecified minimum absolute capture difference of 0.01 for at least one pathway, a two-sided 95% interval excluding zero, and no material calibration deterioration; a separate omnibus test of the three `TCE_d` values assesses heterogeneity. The 0.01 margin is a planning criterion for this study, not a validated clinical benefit threshold.

This advances the parent by replacing an abstract score contrast with a constrained triage estimand: whether historical information changes the composition and target-event yield of a finite review list. Brier score, log loss and calibration remain necessary safety/evidence measures. A score gain without useful or safe fixed-capacity reallocation is not supportive.

## Population, landmark and endpoint

Let `T0=53-0.0` and `T1=53-1.0` only after an evidence gate certifies that these are comparable assessment dates. Retain participants with valid age, sex, all 16 selected biomarker cells, parseable `T0<T1`, and `0.25 < L=(T1-T0)/365.2425 <= 5` years. Restrict `T1 <= 2013-12-31` and define the administrative analysis horizon `H=T1+5 years`; this is a study boundary, not proof of complete follow-up. The paired cohort is therefore explicitly a selected repeat-assessment population.

Use all 259 positions i=0,...,258. For each position, pair `41270-0.i` with `41280-0.i`; never shift codes or dates after missingness. Exclude a person from the at-risk population if a valid qualifying C, R or M code/date is on or before T1. This means no qualifying recorded history, not disease-free status.

After T1, classify the first valid date no later than H into C, R, M, death, or no qualifying record. Freeze C=`I00-I99`, R=`N00-N29`, and M=`E08-E13` only if an inspected authoritative codebook certifies the code system, semantics and date meaning. If certification fails, stop before model fitting. Same-day multiple domains are a joint category in the primary multinomial outcome and are reported rather than assigned arbitrarily. Death is the earliest valid post-T1 date in `40000-0.0` or `40000-1.0`, with same-day death taking precedence. The primary recorded-event analysis does not call no-record participants disease-free.

A separately labelled prospective five-year analysis is permitted only if assessment timing, outcome semantics and individual observation-end/complete-ascertainment evidence are certified. Otherwise, all language remains “recorded event within the defined export window.” Biomarker semantics and units must also be certified before any analyte-specific or biological interpretation; negative values cannot be globally recoded.

## Models and decision policy

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold split, `fold = integer(SHA256(UTF-8(decimal(eid)))) mod 5`, published with the fold manifest. All preprocessing, imputation, scaling, regularization, calibration, tuning, and any repeat-selection weighting are fitted within outer training folds.

- **M0 current-only audit:** current instance-1 values, approved age, approved sex and T1 calendar year.
- **M1a transparent baseline:** M0 plus `L), the interval between assessments. This is the incumbent clinical baseline because it controls for the information that a repeat-assessment policy itself reveals.
- **M1b prior-history model:** M1a plus the eight instance-0 values. The primary comparison is M1b versus M1a on identical eligible participants, outcomes and folds.
- **M1c change sensitivity:** M1a plus the eight current-minus-prior differences, only after field semantics permit arithmetic; otherwise it is omitted with a gate record.
- **M2 same-input learned alternative:** gradient-boosted multinomial probabilities using exactly M1b inputs and the same participants, split and outcome. Prespecify current-by-prior discordance, current-by-L and prior-by-L interactions or smooth effects through a training-fold-only fixed grid. M2 can reveal nonlinear thresholds and discordance patterns that the transparent additive model loses, but it cannot replace the primary nested comparison.

For each domain d and model, apply the same held-out risk-ranking rule to select `K=floor(0.10*n_test)` people in each outer-fold test set. The primary outputs are:

1. held-out `TCE_C(K)`, `TCE_R(K)`, and `TCE_M(K)`, with paired participant bootstrap 95% intervals;
2. overlap, additions, removals and event yield of M1a versus M1b review lists, including cross-domain list overlap;
3. multiclass and one-vs-domain Brier score, log loss, calibration intercept/slope and reliability plots;
4. sensitivity, positive predictive value/event yield and false-review proportion at K, plus descriptive results for K=5% and 20%;
5. the omnibus heterogeneity statistic for the three `TCE_d(K)` values and prespecified C-R, C-M and R-M contrasts;
6. M2 interaction/shape summaries only when stable across outer folds and its calibration is not materially worse.

The fixed 10% capacity is a prespecified analytical capacity, not an operational recommendation. No threshold is learned from the outcome or from M1b predictions. A “review” is an analytic label for selection; no clinical action is performed.

## Uncertainty, selection and support

Use 2,000 paired participant bootstrap replicates stratified by outer fold and endpoint category, preserving each participant’s held-out predictions and fixed K definition. Report fold-specific estimates and ranges. If intervals condition on the fitted outer models rather than refitting each bootstrap sample, state that training uncertainty is not included.

The primary supportive rule requires all relevant semantic/timing/ascertainment gates, at least 100 events in each of C, R and M and at least 200 eligible participants per reported pathway-capacity cell, no integrity or leakage failure, and a 95% interval excluding zero for at least one `TCE_d(K)` with absolute estimate at least 0.01. The omnibus pathway contrast must be supported if claimed; calibration intercept shifts must be <=0.05 and slopes within 0.10 of M1a for the augmented model in each adequately supported domain. A stable M2 pattern may explain the result but cannot rescue an unstable or uncalibrated transparent estimate. If C/R/M support thresholds are not met, report the corresponding estimand as inconclusive rather than pooling it.

Repeat-assessment selection is not causal correction. From all participants with a valid T0 panel, estimate repeat observation using only T0 panel, age, sex and T0 calendar year, fit within training folds, and report repeat rate, covariate balance, overlap, ESS and a fold-local inverse-probability-of-repeat sensitivity. Do not use T1 values or post-T1 outcomes. A supported weighting analysis that reverses the primary result is adverse for transport to the T0-eligible population; poor positivity or ESS makes it inconclusive.

## Falsification and integrity

Retain catalog/source/schema hashes, exact headers, row counts, key uniqueness, join coverage, overlap agreement, eligibility flow, code/date pairing failures, codebook version, endpoint flow, folds, preprocessing manifests, predictions, bootstrap seeds and capacity-list ledgers.

Run:

- outcome-category permutation within outer fold and capacity strata;
- prior-panel permutation within current-risk, interval and repeat-selection strata, preserving current values, L, missingness and selection;
- code/date position permutation and a deliberately shifted-position control, which must be rejected by the pairing audit;
- label/date permutation and a negative-control pre-T1 recorded endpoint frozen before outcome inspection;
- complete-case versus fold-local missingness sensitivity;
- raw-prior versus raw-change sensitivity where semantics permit;
- early-through-2012 fit with 2013 no-refit transport if support permits;
- leakage scans covering dates, outcomes, imputation, calibration, weighting and list construction;
- fold stability and calibration checks for all models.

Supportive evidence must survive the appropriate feature permutation and selection sensitivity. Permutation checks computation and leakage, not causality. A result can be adverse even if Brier improves if the fixed-capacity list captures no additional events, worsens calibration, or reverses under supported selection weighting. Inconclusive includes any failed gate, sparse domain events, ambiguous codebook, inadequate ascertainment, poor overlap/ESS, unstable folds, or intervals too wide for the practical margin.

## Alternatives, fit deliverable and resource boundary

The transparent nested M1a/M1b comparison is selected because it isolates prior values from repeat spacing and maps directly to a finite review capacity. The same-input M2 learner is scientifically useful because it can reveal discordance, threshold and interval-dependent structure hidden by additive terms, while its held-out calibration and replicated shape prevent complexity from being treated as evidence. Both alternatives have the same population, target, split and evaluation.

A disease-history transformer is deferred: the local export has sparse field-position arrays but no certified dated disease sequence, so it would not identify whether a learned pattern reflects chronology. A mechanistic organ-reserve model is deferred because analyte identities, units, assay quality, specimen dates and organ mappings are not certified. Imaging/visceral-fat, proteomics, CHIP, genetics/PRS and cancer-stage adaptations are deferred because the catalog provides no verified raw imaging, sequence-variant bundle, validated staging chronology or required modality semantics. These branches are revisitable only when the named dependency is supplied.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted fold-safe M0/M1a/M1b/M1c/M2 models, held-out participant predictions, capacity-specific list ledgers, `TCE_d(K)` estimates with uncertainty, calibration/safety metrics, selection diagnostics, falsification outputs and an interpretation linked to those files. A complete fail-closed gate certificate is an acceptable computational deliverable if semantics or ascertainment cannot be certified; it is not a positive or negative clinical result.

The future solver is CPU-suitable: regularized multinomial and boosted tabular fits, five folds and bootstrap scoring within the configured planning envelope of 16 CPUs, 256 GiB and 8 hours. This is an unverified planning estimate, not a measured runtime. GPU use is unnecessary unless a measured benchmark shows material benefit. Discovery and future solver budgets remain separate.

## Interpretation limits

Computationally checkable claims include source integrity, cohort flow, endpoint implementation conditional on the certified codebook, held-out predictions, fixed-capacity arithmetic, uncertainty, calibration, weighting diagnostics, leakage and permutation behavior. Expert adjudication is required for the clinical meaning of codes, specimen timing, disease onset, severity, complete ascertainment, whether a recorded event merits a particular review, and any action threshold. External validation and a prospective policy study are required before recommending repeat-testing policy, pathway referral, treatment, or clinical deployment.
