# Sleep-Wake Regularity and Metabolic Disease Risk in UK Biobank

## Hypothesis

Among UK Biobank participants with pre-existing metabolic abnormalities (elevated BMI or waist circumference), sleep-wake pattern variability (measured via accelerometer field 3 data) predicts incident diabetes after adjusting for total sleep duration.

## Scientific Rationale

Sleep regularity has been associated with metabolic health in observational studies. However, most research focuses on sleep duration alone. This hypothesis tests whether sleep pattern *variability* (a measure of regularity) provides additional risk information beyond sleep duration, particularly among metabolically vulnerable individuals.

## Clinical Importance

If sleep pattern variability predicts diabetes risk independently of sleep duration:
1. Sleep quality assessment could be added to metabolic disease screening
2. Interventions improving sleep regularity may benefit high-risk individuals
3. Sleep duration and regularity may be distinct targets for prevention

- **Dataset**: UK Biobank (ukb672073 snapshot)

- From online_followup data (ICD-10 codes for diabetes)

## Analysis Plan

### Baseline Model (Reference)
- Cox model: diabetes ~ BMI + age + sex
- Establishes risk in metabolically abnormal individuals

### Primary Model (Hypothesis)
- Cox model: diabetes ~ sleep_variability + BMI + age + sex + sleep_duration
- Tests whether sleep variability adds predictive value beyond BMI and sleep duration

### Alternative: Machine Learning
- Random Forest with: sleep_variability, BMI, age, sex, sleep_duration
- Compare performance with/without variability feature

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. No significant association between sleep variability and diabetes after adjusting for confounders (p > 0.05)
2. Sleep variability explains no additional variance beyond sleep duration and BMI
3. Effect size is negligible (e.g., hazard ratio < 1.15 for high vs. low variability)

## Supportive Results

The hypothesis would be **supported** if:
1. Significant positive association: higher variability predicts higher diabetes risk
2. Independent effect after adjusting for sleep duration and BMI
3. Effect size is clinically meaningful (e.g., HR > 1.2)

## Inconclusive Results

Results would be **inconclusive** if:
1. Weak but significant association (p < 0.05 but HR ~1.1-1.2)
2. Results vary by subgroup (age, sex, BMI category)
3. Possible confounding (medication use, undiagnosed disease)

## Key Limitations

1. **Field interpretation**: Field 3 data format requires careful interpretation; exact meaning of values (334, 386, etc.) is unclear
2. **Short monitoring**: Accelerometer data represents short-term patterns, not long-term habits
3. **Reverse causation**: Metabolic abnormalities may affect sleep patterns
4. **Missing data**: Some participants may lack complete measurements
