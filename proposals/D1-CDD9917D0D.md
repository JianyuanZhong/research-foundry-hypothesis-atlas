# Observation-aware dynamic hepatic reserve after first TACE: episode 3 repair

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

For adults with hepatocellular carcinoma undergoing a first recorded transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does early post-procedure deterioration in routine liver-function measurements add reproducible prognostic information beyond pre-TACE state and history, after separating biological trajectory from whether and when clinicians obtained follow-up tests?

The primary hypothesis remains narrow and falsifiable: among discovery-partition adults with a first qualifying TACE, no coded hepatic decompensation in the prior 180 days, and sufficient observation to define the landmark, a worsening within-person trajectory from days -14:-1 to +1:+7 is associated with a higher probability of a new coded hepatic-decompensation encounter on days +8:+90, and improves held-out proper scoring and calibration over a current-state/history baseline. The episode-3 repair adds a second requirement: the trajectory association and incremental score must remain materially present when the model represents the observation process (assay availability, count and timing), rather than allowing test intensity to masquerade as reserve.

This is prognostic information for a coded endpoint. It is not a claim that TACE causes decompensation, that a lexical diagnosis is adjudicated liver failure, that the model defines a treatment threshold, or that a patient without a later record is event-free.

## Established evidence, unresolved claim, and advance

The strongest evidence available supports only that baseline hepatic reserve and treatment/tumor context are associated with post-TACE outcomes in richer cohorts. The local HCC snapshot has repeated dated laboratory values, TACE procedures, encounters and diagnosis labels, but lacks validated tumor staging, standardized imaging response, reliable vitals, complete death capture and adjudicated decompensation.

The unresolved claim is whether a within-person peri-TACE trajectory provides information beyond current levels and history, and whether that information is biological enough to survive adjustment for the care process that generated the measurements. The advance is an observation-aware landmark comparison: a simple current-state baseline, a latent assay-specific reserve trajectory, and an explicit observation-process sensitivity/joint model evaluated on the same patient-held-out patients. This distinguishes a clinically plausible response signal from “sicker patients were tested more often.”

## Method comparison and deliverable

For each exact assay/window calculate median, observation count, median timing and numeric parse failure. Use training-fold medians and IQRs only.

The simple baseline is a train-fold-fitted penalized logistic regression for the primary coded outcome using baseline assay medians, age, sex, prior encounter count, prior coded liver disease/decompensation, and pre-index treatment-context indicators. Missingness categories or multiple imputation are fitted within train; complete-case sensitivity is required.

The substantive alternative is a Bayesian assay-specific latent reserve model with separate assay scales, measurement error, irregular observation times, a pre-index state and post-index transition/recovery slope. Fit an observation-process component on the patient-day/assay-window record: assay availability, count and timing are predicted from pre-index state/history and latent state, with parameters learned only in training patients. Report posterior latent day-7 change and observation-process summaries. This reveals discordant recovery and uncertainty while testing whether apparent signal survives informative measurement.

Use deterministic patient-level 60/20/20 train/tune/test folds within discovery buckets. Fit standardization, imputation, priors, transitions and observation-process parameters only on train/tune; touch test once. If the joint model is nonidentified or exceeds budget, preserve diagnostics and classify the alternative comparison inconclusive; do not silently substitute a simpler method.

Completion requires newly fitted baseline and joint latent/observation-process models, cohort flow, actual assay/event/follow-up counts, split hashes, fitted parameters, posterior trajectory and observation summaries, held-out predictions, bootstrap intervals, calibration/proper-score/net-benefit comparisons, and falsification outputs.

## Estimands, uncertainty and falsification

Primary association: adjusted odds ratio and patient-bootstrap 95% interval for an IQR increase in latent worsening trajectory for +8:+90 coded decompensation. Primary incremental estimand: paired test-set change in log loss and Brier score for latent/joint versus baseline. Report AUROC/AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope and reliability, and decision-curve net benefit at prespecified reporting thresholds 5%, 10%, 20%; these are not treatment targets. Bootstrap at patient level.

Supportive results require an adverse-direction association with useful precision, proper-score improvement whose uncertainty excludes no improvement, no material calibration harm, and consistent direction after removing observation-count/timing variables; the death-composite sensitivity should not be directionally incompatible without explanation. This supports incremental prognostic information for coded outcomes only.

Adverse results are a precise null or reversal, no proper-score improvement, worse calibration, or a gain that disappears after observation-process adjustment or when test-frequency features are removed. This falsifies the claim in this snapshot, not the biological relevance of hepatic reserve.

Inconclusive results include sparse trajectories/events, inadequate 90-day observation, materially different diagnosis-type strata, nonidentification/instability, or intervals too wide to distinguish meaningful improvement. No recommendation follows.

Negative controls are: baseline-only model; assay count/missingness/timing-only model; within-patient permutation of post-index lab times preserving values; removal of observation-process variables; and a pre-index negative-control outcome window where support permits. A gain reproduced by process-only features, appearing in the pre-index control, or disappearing under valid time permutation indicates leakage or care-process bias.

The verifier can check joins, partitioning, windows, fitted parameters, held-out predictions, uncertainty, scoring and whether claims match outputs. It cannot establish diagnosis adjudication, causal TACE effects, complete death ascertainment or benefit from a monitoring policy; those require expert chart adjudication and an independent cohort.
