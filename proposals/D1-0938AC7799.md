# Audited protocol: does pH-conditioned hidden hyperoxemia generalize across EICU hospitals?

## Question, hypothesis, and clinical advance

Pulse oximetry is the practical bedside oxygen target, but on the flat portion of the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve a saturation of 96–97% may coexist with substantially different arterial oxygen tensions. Acid-base state and inspired oxygen may alter that mapping. The clinically important unresolved question is not whether oxygen causes organ injury, but whether a nominally acceptable SpO2 target reliably excludes arterial hyperoxemia across heterogeneous ICUs, or whether acidemia identifies a clinically meaningful failure of that measurement rule.

**Primary falsifiable measurement hypothesis:** among first-day blood-gas observations from adults with *probable* invasive ventilation, an archived monitor SpO2 of 96–97%, same-panel PaCO2, and a preceding charted FiO2 setting, the hospital-standardized probability of PaO2 >120 mm Hg is at least 10 percentage points higher when pH is <7.35 than when pH is 7.35–7.45, after standardization to the same SpO2, FiO2, PaCO2, and six-hour-block distribution.

This is a marginal measurement contrast, not a causal effect of acidemia. It tests whether pH materially changes the bedside interpretation of SpO2 at a fixed observed setting. Its advance over the available single-center evidence is an audited multi-hospital estimate, explicit separation of physiological association from documentation artifacts, and an honest test of how far the relationship generalizes. A supportive result would justify prospective evaluation of a pH-conditioned oxygen alert or target. It would not justify lowering oxygen, claim tissue hyperoxia, or show benefit.

## Supported claim versus unresolved claim

Delgado et al. (DOI `10.3390/antiox15020235`; complete Europe PMC XML source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum], inspected) supports the strongest current empirical claim: in one tertiary center, 21,406 adults and 717,064 paired measurements showed that SpO2 ≥98% often accompanied PaO2 hyperoxemia, SpO2 96–97% was generally physiological, and pH and FiO2 modified the mapping. Their data used one-minute institutional data and aligned ABGs with median SpO2 and FiO2 in the preceding five minutes.

Diop and Mounier (DOI `10.4103/mgr.MEDGASRES-D-25-00028`; complete Europe PMC XML source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum], inspected) supports the limitation that PaO2 is not blood oxygen content, tissue hyperoxia, oxidative injury, or treatment benefit.

Neither source establishes the EICU 10-point contrast, between-hospital stability, correctness of EICU ventilation/FiO2 semantics, or utility of changing care. Those are untested. The research-ambition availability guide was inspected; none of its demonstration papers is used as substantive evidence here, and the unavailable cancer main article was not treated as read.

## Audited feasibility and the changes it requires

A complete-file, read-only scan of EICU snapshot `[source checksum]` found:

- 120,474 valid first-day PaO2 panel times in selected adult first ICU units; 117,988 had valid pH at the identical stay and offset and 117,807 had no conflicting PaO2/pH duplicate.
- 10,131 same-offset adult panels met the repaired probable-invasive-ventilation proxy. A preceding FiO2 was available for 2,220 within five minutes, 3,197 within ten, 5,957 within thirty, and 8,565 within sixty. Among the 5,957 at thirty minutes, 4,880 had preceding-five-minute SpO2.
- Exact-label `FiO2`, six-hour thinning, and the discharge boundary yield 4,039 observations from 2,918 stays, 2,843 persons, and 68 hospitals. There are 496 observations at SpO2 96–97: 222 acidemic, 215 normal-pH, and 59 other; 495/496 have PaCO2, leaving 436 primary complete cases in the two comparison groups.
- Ten hospitals have at least 100 final observations for internal-external measurement-model validation. Only four have at least 50 SpO2 96–97 observations and at least 10 observations in each primary pH group; those four contribute 299/496 band observations. No hospital has 100 band observations.
- The post-discharge-check crude PaO2>120 prevalence is 12.61% in acidemia and 6.98% at normal pH, a 5.64-point unadjusted difference. This is a feasibility description, not the adjusted repeated-observation test.
- The parent’s literal ventilation interval is infeasible: 60,565 `respiratoryCare` rows have positive `ventstartoffset` and zero `ventendoffset`; only eight fully linked observations survive a strict closed-interval definition. The repaired status-bounded proxy retains 4,880 before thinning.

Thus the pooled 10-point estimand remains computable. The original requirement for five hospitals with at least 100 band observations is impossible and is replaced, before modeling, by a limited four-hospital consistency analysis. Even a supportive result cannot establish broad US-hospital transportability. The same-panel pH model is retained as a measurement model but its prior “decision-curve benefit” gate is removed: at that blood draw PaO2 is already known, so a same-time model is not a deployable detector.

## Population, time boundaries, and deterministic construction

1. From `patient`, select age ≥18; map `> 89` to 90 only for modeling and report that category separately. Exclude unknown/non-numeric ages. Select one ICU unit per `patienthealthsystemstayid` by the smallest numeric `unitvisitnumber`, breaking ties with smallest `patientunitstayid`. The actual source header does **not** contain the catalog-listed `unitadmitoffset`, so it is not used. This selects a first unit within the recorded encounter, not a first lifetime admission. Repeated health-system encounters are retained and linked by `uniquepid`.
2. Candidate index time is `labresultoffset` from 0 through 1440 minutes, additionally not exceeding numeric `unitdischargeoffset`. Require `labname='paO2'`, `labmeasurenamesystem='mm Hg'`, and value 20–760, plus `labname='pH'` at exactly the same `patientunitstayid + labresultoffset`, value 6.80–7.80. Pair same-offset `paCO2`, 10–150 mm Hg, for the primary model.
3. For each analyte key, retain all valid rows long enough to audit duplicates. If duplicate valid values are identical, collapse them to that value. If PaO2 or pH has more than one distinct valid value at that key, exclude the panel; report the conflicting values and revision offsets only as aggregate counts. A sensitivity selects maximal `labresultrevisedoffset`, then maximal `labid`. Same offset is a blood-gas-panel proxy, not proof of one arterial specimen because EICU has no specimen identifier or specimen-source field.
4. Define *probable invasive ventilation* at the index only when both conditions hold:
   - the stay has a `respiratoryCare.airwaytype` exactly in {Oral ETT, Nasal ETT, Tracheostomy, Double-Lumen Tube, Cricothyrotomy}; and
   - either a current record has `ventstartoffset <= index` and [`ventendoffset >= index` with end > start, or `ventendoffset=0` and `respcarestatusoffset >= index`], or a prior record has `priorventstartoffset <= index <= priorventendoffset` with end > start.
   
   This uses a later respiratory-care entry to bound an otherwise open current record; it does not assert continuous airway documentation. Report strict-closed, naïve-zero-as-open, and status-bounded counts separately. The primary population must always be called “probable invasive ventilation.”
5. FiO2 primary linkage uses only exact `respchartvaluelabel='FiO2'`. Strip whitespace and a terminal percent sign from `respchartvalue`; convert numeric values ≤1 to percent and retain 21–100. At each stay/time/label, choose maximal `respchartentryoffset`, then maximal `respchartid`; then choose the most recent `respchartoffset` in [index−30,index]. Retain event lag and entry lag. Values represent charted settings/observations, not verified delivered FiO2. A harmonized sensitivity admits exact labels `FIO2 (%)` and `Set Fraction of Inspired Oxygen (FIO2)`; 5-, 10-, and 60-minute lookbacks are separate sensitivities.
6. SpO2 is the median of valid `vitalPeriodic.sao2` values 70–100 at `observationoffset` in [index−5,index]. Collapse same-offset duplicates by median first. Record count, last-value lag, and within-window range. These are one or two already-aggregated, unvalidated five-minute monitor medians—not raw continuous samples. Sensitivities use the last preceding value and 10-/15-minute windows; no future value is allowed.
7. Among fully paired panels, retain the earliest by `labresultoffset`, then deterministic panel key, in [0,360), [360,720), [720,1080), and [1080,1440], with 1440 assigned to the fourth block. Maximum is four observations per stay. Selection occurs after linkage but never uses PaO2 value or outcome status.
8. Report the full funnel overall and by hospital: PaO2, same-offset pH, unambiguous panel, probable ventilation, FiO2, SpO2, six-hour thinning, discharge boundary, primary pH groups, and PaCO2 completeness. Also report observations/stays/persons, hospital concentration, time lags, and excluded conflicts.

## Variables and outcomes

Primary outcome is observed arterial hyperoxemia, PaO2 >120 mm Hg. Secondary outcomes are PaO2 >150, continuous PaO2, and PaO2 <60 as a safety description. These are arterial blood-gas states only.

Primary contrast is pH <7.35 versus 7.35–7.45 within SpO2 96–97. Alkalemia >7.45 is secondary. Primary standardization variables are exact SpO2 (96 versus 97), charted FiO2 percent, same-panel PaCO2, and six-hour block. Hospital is a hierarchical intercept; `uniquepid` identifies repeated persons. Age, gender, ethnicity, unit type, admission source, admission weight, and hospital characteristics are descriptive and enter a prespecified case-mix sensitivity, not the sparse primary model. Ethnicity is not skin pigmentation and cannot adjudicate pulse-oximeter bias.

## Primary analysis and repeated observations

Fit a Bayesian hierarchical logistic model in the 436 audited complete cases:

`logit P(PaO2>120) = alpha_hospital + beta_A*acidemia + beta_S*I(SpO2=97) + beta_F*(FiO2−50)/10 + beta_C*(PaCO2−40)/10 + block`.

Use a common acidemia slope for the primary pooled estimand, hospital random intercepts `alpha_hospital ~ Normal(alpha, tau)`, Normal(0,1.5) priors for standardized fixed effects, Normal(0,2.5) for the intercept, and half-Normal(0,1) for `tau`. Standardize each observation twice (acidemic and normal pH) while preserving its SpO2, FiO2, PaCO2, block, and hospital, and average the probability difference over the primary population. Report posterior median, 95% credible interval, absolute risks, effective sample size, R-hat, prior-predictive checks, and posterior predictive calibration.

Repeated observations are retained. Uncertainty is checked with a two-stage bootstrap that resamples hospitals, then `uniquepid` within hospital, refitting the same model for 1,000 successful replicates. A one-observation-per-person sensitivity keeps the earliest fully paired index by offset; disagreement >5 points is an inconclusive flag. The case-mix sensitivity adds age (linear per decade), gender, four broad unit-type groups, and admission source with hierarchical shrinkage; no stepwise selection is allowed.

## Hospital consistency and measurement-model baselines

The transportability claim has two explicitly separate parts:

- **Primary-contrast consistency:** the four frozen evaluable hospitals are those with ≥50 SpO2 96–97 observations and ≥10 in each pH group, selected only by audited counts. Fit the same parsimonious model with hospital-by-acidemia partial pooling and report each hospital’s standardized risk difference. “Positive in ≥70%” becomes exactly at least three of four. Report the random-slope heterogeneity and 95% prediction interval. Four sites can support limited consistency, never broad transportability.
- **Measurement-model generalization:** use all final observations with SpO2 90–100 and leave out each of the ten hospitals with ≥100 final observations. Train without hospital identifiers and without specification tuning: B1 SpO2; B2 SpO2+FiO2; B3 SpO2+FiO2+pH+PaCO2, with three-knot restricted splines for continuous terms. B0 is the heuristic SpO2 ≥98%. Report held-out calibration-in-the-large, slope, Brier score, log loss, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV for PaO2>120, overall and in SpO2 96–97. Pool only after showing every site. AUROC/AUPRC are secondary.

B3 is an explanatory measurement benchmark, not a bedside decision rule, because pH and PaO2 come from the same panel. Decision-curve net benefit is not claimed. A clinically deployable rule requires a separate study using a pH measured before a future decision point.

## Sensitivity analyses and falsification gates

Run, without replacing the primary result: PaO2 >150; continuous PaO2 with robust residual checks; final-revision rather than conflict exclusion; FiO2 windows 5/10/60 minutes; harmonized FiO2 labels; require `respchartentryoffset <= index`; last rather than median SpO2; SpO2 windows 10/15 minutes; one observation per stay and per person; first six-hour block only; naïve-zero-as-open ventilation proxy; case-mix adjustment; and exclusion of cardiothoracic/neuro units. The strict closed-interval cohort is reported as a feasibility boundary (audited n=8), not analyzed inferentially.

Before inference, require ≥200 observations in each primary pH group, ≥30 total primary outcomes, ≥20% retention from probable-ventilation gas panels through FiO2+SpO2 linkage, and all four frozen evaluable hospitals to retain ≥10 observations in each pH group. Model convergence must meet R-hat <1.01 and bulk/tail effective sample size ≥400; otherwise the result is inconclusive.

- **Supportive for the full hypothesis:** pooled standardized risk difference median ≥+10 points, 95% credible interval excludes 0, bootstrap interval is directionally concordant, at least three of four evaluable hospitals have positive posterior-median differences, and the heterogeneity prediction interval excludes a clinically important opposite effect of −5 points. B3 must improve mean held-out Brier score over B2 without median calibration slope leaving 0.8–1.2. This supports a pH-conditioned measurement association and limited multi-hospital consistency only.
- **Adverse:** the 95% interval’s upper bound is <+10 points, which falsifies the prespecified minimum even if a smaller positive association remains; an upper bound <0 supports an opposite association. Failure of B3 to improve held-out calibration/Brier performance rejects incremental measurement-model value but does not by itself erase an estimated physiological contrast. A precise 6-point difference is adverse to the 10-point hypothesis, not “no association.”
- **Inconclusive:** the interval contains both +5 and +10 points; a data/convergence gate fails; fewer than four hospitals remain evaluable; the prediction interval spans both ≤−5 and ≥+10; any primary linkage sensitivity moves the risk difference by >5 points or reverses sign; excluding delayed FiO2 entries changes the conclusion; or no result is robust to plausible ventilation semantics. If the pooled contrast is supportive but hospital criteria fail, report “pooled association supported; transportability inconclusive,” not full support.

## Verification boundary and claims requiring another study

The automatic verifier can check source hashes and headers, exact labels and ranges, stay/time joins, duplicate logic, absence of future event times, the discharge and six-hour boundaries, complete-case funnel, hospital gates, model formulas/priors, repeated-person resampling, convergence, held-out predictions, computed risk differences and intervals, and whether the submitted conclusion follows the supportive/adverse/inconclusive rules. Tests must reject a solver that computes correctly but claims oxygen toxicity, tissue hyperoxia, treatment benefit, or broad transportability; they must accept justified supportive, adverse, partial-support, and inconclusive interpretations.

Automatic verification cannot establish that same-offset rows are one arterial specimen, that an open respiratory record means ventilation at the ABG, that an airway persisted, that charted FiO2 was delivered, that pulse-oximeter values were artifact-free, or that pH should change oxygen management. A clinical informatics review of source-system mappings is essential; raw device/waveform data and prospectively synchronized airway, delivered-FiO2, SpO2-quality, and ABG specimen data are needed for external validation. A prospective protocol study—and ultimately a randomized study with hypoxemia, organ injury, and patient-centered outcomes—is required before recommending a lower target or claiming clinical benefit.
