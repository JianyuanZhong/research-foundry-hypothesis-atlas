# Return-specific information in within-assay laboratory trajectories after HCC-coded admission

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly freeze an adult, first-locally-recorded HCC-coded inpatient admission cohort; construct the exhaustive post-discharge Y90 outcome; create locked Q/M/L/T feature manifests; fit matched transparent and time-aware models; evaluate the trajectory increment on a later calendar era and a patient-disjoint random split; and emit prediction, calibration, uncertainty, missingness, timestamp, join, and falsification ledgers.

Completion is this artifact set and an evidence-bounded conclusion, whether the trajectory hypothesis is supported, adverse, or inconclusive. No model has been fit in discovery and no clinical effect or utility is claimed.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

Existing evidence can support the general idea that longitudinal EHR laboratory histories may contain prognostic information, and the supplied HCC snapshot demonstrably contains dated assay rows. It does not establish that a laboratory trend, rather than the fact of measurement or a single latest result, identifies patients who will return to the local system after discharge. The earlier candidate asks whether assay payload transports across calendar eras; it does not isolate the clinically intuitive change-over-time component.

The unresolved question is:

> Among adults with a first locally recorded HCC-coded inpatient admission, does strictly prior within-assay laboratory trajectory information improve calibrated prediction of the next 30-day locally recorded return beyond capture intensity, assay selection/sampling, and the latest assay-specific level—and does that increment persist in a later calendar era?

Primary hypothesis: the trajectory block T (within-assay direction, slope, variability, and recency-weighted change, defined only from valid repeated assay measurements) improves held-out prediction of the three-state post-discharge outcome Y90, especially R30, beyond Q+M+L. The trajectory increment should be larger for R30 than for later observation without R30 (O90), and should remain directionally compatible when models frozen after development are evaluated on 2021–2025 anchors.

This is an information and transport hypothesis, not a claim that a trajectory is biologic deterioration, active HCC, recurrence, treatment indication, or a causal reason for return. A result that predicts only later observation is evidence for a measurement-process explanation, not support for return-specific clinical risk.

## What is supported, what is tested, and the advance

Supported before the experiment: the frozen HCC snapshot and catalog document usable encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, procedure, order, medication, examination, narrative-document, pathology, and identifier tables; labs have assay identity, qualitative and quantitative result fields, specimen type, and native test time; encounters have native admission/discharge times. The snapshot metadata explicitly says laboratory units/reference ranges and result-release times are unavailable. Prior candidate audits identify timing irregularity and sparse genuinely prior history, but no fitted result establishes this hypothesis.

Tested by the experiment: whether repeated same-assay values contain incremental, reproducible, calendar-robust information after accounting for the observation process and the latest measured value. Required data are valid repeated assay rows with strict pre-anchor availability attribution, a valid discharge time, and follow-up encounter rows. Required outputs are locked paired score differences for T versus L and the R30-versus-O90 contrast, with uncertainty and all gates.

The substantive advance is a decomposition of laboratory information into selection/observation, current level, and longitudinal change. It can distinguish a potentially actionable follow-up signal from artifacts in which clinicians order more tests in patients already under closer surveillance, or an isolated latest value carries the entire signal. It is a bounded adaptation, not a reproduction of any supplied demonstration.

## Information sets and feature decomposition

All primary predictors stop strictly before t0. A history row is eligible only if it joins to a non-anchor encounter with parseable native admission and discharge, linked discharge < t0, linked admission < t0, and native event time in [t0-730 days,t0). Labs use 检验时间; procedures 开始时间; orders 开立时间; medications 开始时间; examinations 开始时间. Anchor-linked rows are excluded even if their event timestamp precedes admission, and retained only in a sensitivity branch. Report invalid/missing-time, missing-linked-discharge, overlap, anchor-linked, post-t0, duplicate, unmatched, and boundary counts.

Exclude 姓名, 身份证号, 手机号, 医保/就诊卡号, and 住院号.

- Q (capture/process): age, sex, department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, prior completed-encounter count/duration/recency, source-specific row counts and distinct linked-encounter counts for labs/procedures/orders/medications/examinations, number of source types, invalid/excluded-time counts, left-truncation and no-history indicators. Q has no assay identity or result.
- M (assay selection/sampling): prior 检验 identity, assay-specific counts and distinct encounters, sampling count, span and recency, 标本类型 availability/missingness, and result-missingness indicators. Rare assay vocabulary and thresholds are fit-only and frozen.
- L (latest level): for each assay, the latest valid pre-t0 定量结果 and/or assay-specific 定性结果, plus time since measurement and within-assay missingness. Numeric parsing and assay-specific impossible-value rules are frozen; values from different assays are never pooled because no lab-unit column exists.
- T (trajectory): only for assays with at least two valid observations at distinct 检验时间, compute last-minus-previous change, robust slope over time, absolute change, sign, within-assay dispersion, number of intervals, and recency-weighted change. Standardize within assay using fit-only parameters. A trajectory eligibility/missingness flag is mandatory and is not silently converted to stability. Sensitivity analyses vary minimum separation and use only two most recent observations. No cross-assay scale or reference range is assumed.

The primary nested comparison is Q+M+L versus Q+M+L+T. The parent’s broader P-minus-M contrast (all assay values and trajectories versus menu/process) is a continuity secondary analysis. Process-only Q/M models and assay-identity/value permutation controls are mandatory.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods use identical patients, strict history rows, Q/M/L/T manifests, Y90 labels, partitions, preprocessing eligibility, and locked test cases.

Transparent baseline B fits regularized multinomial logistic models for Y90 and binary logistic models for R30 and conditional O90-versus-U90. It reports Q, Q+M, Q+M+L, and Q+M+L+T nested contrasts with fit-only standardization, frozen rare-category rules, proper scoring, and calibration. It is auditable but treats Y90 as a static label and loses the timing structure of R30 versus O90.

Substantive alternative H fits matched discrete-time complementary-log-log hazards: R30 intervals [0,7), [7,14), [14,30), followed by O90 intervals [30,60), [60,90) conditional on no R30. The same Q/M/L/T blocks enter every transition with interval offsets and prespecified regularization; coherent Y90 probabilities are derived. H tests whether T changes return timing rather than only a pooled label and whether a calendar shift affects return versus later-observation hazards. That transition decomposition is scientific information lost by B, not a complexity bonus.

A learned irregular sequence encoder is deferred, not prohibited. It could consume the same assay-event sequence, times, identities, results, missingness, and specimen type and reveal nonlinear multi-assay ordering that engineered T loses. It is deferred until T is stable, repeated-assay support passes, and H residuals show reproducible nonlinear/order structure; otherwise it adds optimization and calibration variance without resolving observation ambiguity. If revisited, compare it on the same locked Y90 task and partitions. GPU is optional; one allocated A100 using cuda:0 is available, but CPU-first sparse B/H is the planning choice. No neural feasibility claim is made.

## Estimation, uncertainty, and compute

Primary outcomes are paired held-out multiclass log loss and multiclass Brier score for Y90, with R30 and conditional O90 decompositions. Report calibration intercept/slope, reliability by state and era, prevalence, feature support, Q+M+L versus Q+M+L+T deltas, and corresponding H deltas. Report later-minus-earlier interaction and trajectory-specific R30-minus-O90 contrast.

Use at least 1,000 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples, preserving every patient’s rows and linked histories. Report 95% intervals and a clinically negligible score-difference margin selected before locked evaluation. Minimum support gates are required for each era, state, and trajectory-eligible stratum. Threshold/net-benefit summaries at 5%, 10%, and 20% are descriptive only and cannot establish utility.

The future solver envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM, 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds. CPU-first B/H fitting is a planning estimate, not a measured full-study runtime; sparse feature materialization, repeated bootstrap fitting, and memory remain unverified. The discovery audit is a bounded source scan and not a solver fit. No proposer or solver weights are trained.

## Falsification and interpretation

Mandatory falsifications:

1. permute trajectory values within assay and calendar era while preserving identity, times, counts, and missingness;
2. permute assay identities while preserving values, times, and row counts;
3. shuffle complete histories within the patient split while preserving Q;
4. shuffle laboratory times within patient while preserving values and assay identities;
5. permute calendar-era labels for the interaction test;
6. compare future documentation intensity in [td+30,td+90) among non-R30 patients, using it only as a process outcome;
7. verify no post-t0, anchor-linked, future-window, current-anchor, or cross-partition feature;
8. reconcile all Y90 states, hazard-derived probabilities, joins, duplicate counts, and trajectory eligibility.

Supportive evidence requires adequately supported later-era T-minus-L improvement for R30, calibrated predictions, a 95% interval excluding clinically negligible harm, compatible earlier and later effects, attenuation under trajectory permutation, no comparable Q/M-only explanation, and concordant B/H results. The R30 increment should exceed the O90/documentation increment or return-specific interpretation is not supported. This would show stable information in recorded pre-admission laboratory change for local return; it would not establish that acting on a score improves outcomes.

Adverse evidence includes null or harmful later-era T-minus-L, sign reversal beyond the interaction bound, survival after trajectory permutation, assay-identity permutation survival, process-only reproduction, or a trajectory signal confined to O90/documentation intensity. This would show that the observed trajectory representation is not a stable return-specific signal here, not that laboratory biology lacks prognostic value.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse repeated assays, dominant U90, wide interaction intervals, high invalid-time/result-missingness burden, insufficient trajectory support, unstable assay vocabulary/parser, unresolved joins or row inflation, inadequate follow-up, or B/H disagreement without a prespecified resolution. Do not redefine eras or eligibility to obtain support.

## Demonstration and seed disposition

- The natural-history/Delphi demonstration supports considering dated structured-history learning. Its unavailable external Danish cohort prevents reproducing external validation. This design adapts dated-history ideas into an auditable trajectory block and defers a neural encoder until stable residual sequence signal exists.
- The Bayesian/ALADYNOULLI demonstration supports a longitudinal latent alternative, but its original genetic inputs are unavailable. This HCC adaptation uses a same-input discrete-time competing-transition hazard model; a latent encoder is a future branch, not an assumed reproduction.
- The cancer/Oncoformer demonstration has an available supplement, but the main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable, and HCC image files are absent. A lab-only adaptation could be considered later, but it would not resolve the current trajectory-versus-observation estimand and is deferred.
- The expert-seed catalog contains UK Biobank, MIMIC-IV, and eICU cards but no HCC seed. Those imported seeds are not parents for this HCC episode; the four configured datasets remain directly accessible, but cross-dataset transport is not claimed.

This child preserves the parent’s strongest evidence-bound question while making the clinically interpretable trajectory increment itself the primary new estimand.
