## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

A first inpatient N18 code may reflect true kidney-related prognostic enrichment, but it may also be more likely to be recorded in people who have more inpatient contact or general frailty. Grip is biologically and clinically related to vulnerability, so a favorable N18 contrast alone cannot distinguish kidney-specific information from generic ascertainment.

The prespecified question is:

> In the exact frozen O1_hold population and 466-slot nested AFG1-versus-AF1 contrast, does the incremental grip allocation contrast for first recorded inpatient N18 exceed its contrast for a predeclared nonrenal inpatient ascertainment control over the same five-year window?

The primary specificity hypothesis is `Psi = Delta18 - DeltaG > 0`, where Delta18 is the parent’s fixed-denominator AFG1-minus-AF1 N18 yield difference and DeltaG is the same difference for the nonrenal control. The hypothesis is not that grip causes kidney disease, prevents N18, improves survival, or proves CKD. It tests whether the observed allocation enrichment is more kidney-directed than a generic inpatient-contact/frailty signal.

A kidney-specificity-supportive result requires all of the following:

1. The inherited upstream hierarchy is supported.
2. The inherited N18 gates pass and the inherited N18 label is `n18_targeting_supportive`.
3. The negative-control gates pass.
4. The one-sided 95% lower limit for Psi is strictly greater than zero.
5. The negative-control one-sided 95% upper limit for DeltaG is strictly below the inherited one-event margin m. Thus a small unresolved generic contrast below the experiment’s one-event resolution is not presented as a clinically material falsification.

This is an allocation-enrichment specificity test, not a treatment-effect or causal interaction estimand.

## Exact nonrenal ascertainment control

For every authenticated O1_hold participant, inspect the 259 position-paired fields `41270-0.0` through `41270-0.258` and `41280-0.0` through `41280-0.258`. Normalize a code by trimming whitespace and uppercasing. A control-eligible code is nonblank and its first character is one of:

`A B C D E F G H I J K L M S T Z`.

This is a fixed lexical set corresponding to nonrenal ICD-10 chapters and injury/external-cause/contact chapters. It deliberately excludes the entire `N` chapter (genitourinary disease), `R` symptom/sign codes that can include renal manifestations, `O-P-Q` obstetric/perinatal/congenital chapters, and `U` codes whose interpretation is release-dependent. It is not selected from any AFG1 or AF1 result and is frozen before outcome opening.

Let `TG_i` be the earliest strictly parsed date paired to a control-eligible code at the same position. Define:

`G_i = 1{t1_i < TG_i <= t5_i and (Tdeath_i absent or TG_i < Tdeath_i)}`.

A same-day death prevents `G_i=1`, exactly as for N18. N18 and G may both occur for one participant; G is not a competing endpoint and is not added to N18. A code outside the set is not a G event. Blank arrays mean no recorded eligible code in the supplied window, not proof of no nonrenal illness or no hospital contact.

This control is intentionally broad enough to measure general nonrenal inpatient ascertainment and frailty-related contact, while the exclusion of N and R avoids making it a disguised renal endpoint. It is still a diagnosis-record control, not a direct count of admissions: the source has diagnosis codes and paired dates, not a verified encounter table or admission identifier.

Integrity rules for G are prespecified. A nonblank control-eligible code with a blank or unparseable paired date is a G endpoint-integrity failure; it is not a nonevent or imputed date. A code/date must use the same array position. Duplicate `main` and `health_outcomes` fields must agree exactly before health_outcomes is used canonically. ## Estimands and paired analysis

For p in {AFG1, AF1}, with immutable denominator 466:

`YG(p) = 1000 * sum(i in S_p) G_i / 466`

`DeltaG = YG(AFG1) - YG(AF1)`.

The inherited N18 quantities remain:

`Y18(p) = 1000 * sum(i in S_p) E18_i / 466`

`Delta18 = Y18(AFG1) - Y18(AF1)`.

The new primary specificity estimand is:

`Psi = Delta18 - DeltaG`.

Equivalently, Psi is the fixed-denominator difference between the two 31-person directional-swap contrasts. It is computed participant-wise in each paired bootstrap draw, preserving the covariance between N18 and G for the same people. Never divide by survivors, events, complete cases, list events, or swap events. Report both list numerators/yields, the 435-person overlap, both 31-person directional swaps, the symmetric-difference identity, Delta18, DeltaG and Psi.

Use the exact inherited 2,000 bootstrap draws over canonical-eid-sorted O1_hold. Memberships, denominators, fits, rankings, and upstream labels remain fixed in every draw. For each draw calculate N18, G and Psi together. Report point estimates, standard errors, two-sided 95% intervals and inherited one-sided 95% limits. Use the inherited marked percentile fallback only when its required condition occurs. There is one primary specificity estimand; the G endpoint itself is a prespecified falsification diagnostic, not a free multiplicity family.

## Interpretation and falsification

Supportive results would show that, within this frozen internal allocation experiment, the AFG1-versus-AF1 N18 enrichment is larger than the same allocation’s broad nonrenal inpatient enrichment, with uncertainty excluding zero and generic enrichment below the one-event planning resolution. This would justify a prospective study with adjudicated kidney outcomes and an explicitly monitored generic-contact endpoint.

Adverse results would show that the nonrenal contrast is at least materially positive and exceeds the N18 contrast with uncertainty. This would falsify the narrow kidney-specificity claim for this snapshot and allocation; it would not establish equivalence, AF1 superiority, absence of renal disease, or that grip has no clinical value.

Mixed results would mean a kidney-excess contrast exists but generic ascertainment/frailty is also materially enriched. The clinically appropriate conclusion would be that N18 alone is insufficient for kidney-specific deployment decisions.

Inconclusive results include sparse controls, wide Psi intervals, equality at a boundary, malformed or discordant source pairs, or inherited upstream failure. They mean unresolved, not no difference and not negative evidence.

A positive Psi remains vulnerable to incomplete outpatient capture, diagnosis coding practices, differential follow-up, competing death, emigration/censoring, and residual frailty. The control is not a validated negative control in the causal-inference sense: some nonrenal diseases can share biology with kidney disease, and broad diagnosis ascertainment can be affected by genuine illness rather than contact alone.
