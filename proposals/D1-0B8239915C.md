# Residual Physiological Instability Before ICU Discharge Predicts ICU Readmission and In-Hospital Death in MIMIC-IV

## Scientific Question

In ICU patients discharged alive, does residual physiological instability (elevated oxygen requirements OR high vital sign variability) in the 24-48h before ICU discharge independently predict ICU readmission and in-hospital death, after adjusting for illness severity, discharge destination, and mechanical ventilation status?

The key unresolved claim is the **PRE-DISCHARGE WINDOW**: patients who are discharged from the ICU may still have residual physiological derangements that are not captured by a single discharge assessment. The question is whether the trajectory of physiological parameters in the hours before discharge (not just the value at discharge) carries independent prognostic information for post-discharge outcomes.

## Clinical Importance

- ICU readmission affects ~5-10% of ICU patients and is associated with increased mortality, longer hospital stay, and higher costs [K1].
- Post-ICU mortality and readmission are major quality metrics; identifying at-risk patients before discharge could inform monitoring intensity, step-down protocols, and early warning systems [K3].
- Vital sign time series carry prognostic information beyond single timepoint measurements [K2]. However, it is unknown whether simple, interpretable variability metrics (coefficient of variation) in the pre-discharge window specifically predict ICU readmission.
- If residual instability before discharge independently predicts readmission, this identifies a modifiable target: delaying discharge or increasing monitoring intensity for patients with unstable vital signs in the pre-discharge window.

## Strongest Existing Evidence

1. **ICU readmission prediction**: Remoundou et al. (2026) [K1] predicted 30-day ICU readmissions using MIMIC-IV with associative and causal-fused AI models. Length of stay was the primary predictive driver (ATE -0.038). However, this study focused on model architecture comparison, not on identifying specific physiological risk factors in the pre-discharge window.

2. **Vital sign time series and ICU outcomes**: Jandoubi & Akhloufi (2026) [K2] showed that 24-h vital sign time series provide complementary information for ICU deterioration prediction beyond structured clinical variables (AUROC 0.9014 for composite deterioration endpoint). However, this study used a deep learning model for a composite endpoint, not specifically for ICU readmission, and did not test the pre-discharge window.

3. **Post-ICU readmission risk factors**: Farsani et al. (2026) [K3] showed that 90-day sepsis-associated readmission is associated with comorbidity burden, non-home discharge, and abnormal lactate (6.37% readmission rate; 32.3% mortality among readmissions). However, this study was specific to sepsis patients and used administrative data, not ICU-level physiological data.

## Unresolved Claim

The specific claim that **residual physiological instability in the 24-48h before ICU discharge** (not just the value at discharge, not just administrative risk factors) independently predicts ICU readmission and in-hospital death has not been tested in a large, data-resolvable cohort. Existing studies either:
- Use AI models without identifying specific physiological risk factors [K1],
- Test vital sign time series for composite deterioration, not specifically for ICU readmission [K2],
- Or use administrative data without ICU-level physiological measurements [K3].

## Hypothesis

In ICU patients discharged alive, residual physiological instability (FiO2 > 0.4 OR SpO2 < 94% OR coefficient of variation of HR > 0.15 OR coefficient of variation of MAP > 0.20 in the 24h before ICU discharge) is independently associated with ICU readmission within 72h and in-hospital mortality, compared with stable physiology, after adjusting for age, sex, SOFA proxy, mechanical ventilation status at discharge, and discharge destination.

## Study Design

### Population

Adult (age ≥ 18) ICU stays in MIMIC-IV 3.1 with:
- ICU discharge (icustays.outtime not null)
- Alive at ICU discharge (admissions.deathtime null or after icustays.outtime)
- ≥ 24 hours of ICU stay (sufficient time for pre-discharge window)
- ≥ 1 vital sign measurement in the 24h before ICU discharge

### Time Zero (ICU Discharge)

icustays.outtime for each ICU stay.

### Exposure (Pre-Discharge Window)

In the 24-hour window before icustays.outtime:
- **Residual hypoxemia**: FiO2 (223835) > 0.4 OR SpO2 (220277) < 94% at any point in the window
- **Vital sign instability**: coefficient of variation (SD/mean) of HR (220045) > 0.15 OR coefficient of variation of MAP (220052) > 0.20 in the window
- **Combined instability**: either hypoxemia OR instability
- **Stable**: neither hypoxemia nor instability

Patients with < 3 vital sign measurements in the window for the relevant parameter are excluded from that parameter's assessment but retained if they meet the other criterion.

### Primary Outcome

**ICU readmission within 72h of ICU discharge**: a second icustays row for the same hadm_id with intime within 72h of the first stay's outtime. (7,695 hadms with ≥2 icustays identified in probe1.)

### Secondary Outcomes

1. **In-hospital mortality**: admissions.deathtime not null (11,790 patients with deathtime in probe1).
2. **ICU length of stay**: icustays.los (days).
3. **Hospital length of stay**: admissions.dischtime - admissions.admittime.

### Covariates

1. **Demographics**: age (anchor_age in patients), sex (gender in patients).
2. **Illness severity**: SOFA-style proxy computed from labevents (lactate 50813/52442/53154, platelets, bilirubin, creatinine 50912/52546) and chartevents (MAP 220052, FiO2 223835) in the 24h before ICU discharge.
3. **Mechanical ventilation at discharge**: extubation (227194) or intubation (224385) in procedureevents within 24h before ICU discharge.
4. **Discharge destination**: transfers table eventtype and careunit after ICU discharge.
5. **ICU LOS**: icustays.los.

### Analysis Plan

1. **Descriptive**: baseline characteristics by exposure group (instable vs stable), with standardized mean differences.
2. **Primary analysis**: Logistic regression for ICU readmission, instable vs stable, adjusted for age, sex, SOFA proxy, mechanical ventilation status, discharge destination, and ICU LOS. Report odds ratio (OR) with 95% CI.
3. **Secondary analyses**:
   - Logistic regression for in-hospital mortality.
   - Linear regression for ICU LOS and hospital LOS.
   - Cox proportional hazards for time to ICU readmission (censored at death or hospital discharge).
4. **Sensitivity analyses**:
   - Vary the pre-discharge window: 12h, 24h, 48h.
   - Vary the instability thresholds: HR CV > 0.10, 0.15, 0.20; MAP CV > 0.15, 0.20, 0.25.
   - Restrict to patients with ≥ 6 vital sign measurements in the window.
   - Stratify by mechanical ventilation status at discharge.
   - Restrict to patients discharged to floor (not step-down or home).
   - Propensity score matching on baseline covariates.

### Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis is **falsified** if:
1. The adjusted OR for ICU readmission (instable vs stable) is within the prespecified null band (OR 0.8-1.25, 95% CI crossing 1.0), AND
2. The effect is explained by illness severity / discharge destination (i.e., the OR moves toward 1.0 after adding these covariates, with the adjusted OR within the null band).

If the OR is outside the null band (e.g., OR > 1.25 or OR < 0.8), the hypothesis is **supported** (if OR > 1.25, instability predicts readmission) or **refuted in the opposite direction** (if OR < 0.8, instability is associated with lower readmission, which would be unexpected).

If the OR is within the null band but the effect is NOT explained by severity/destination, the result is **inconclusive** — the data do not support a meaningful association, but the study may be underpowered.

## Method Comparison

### Baseline: Fixed Vital Sign Values at Discharge

A simple comparison of vital sign values at a single time point (e.g., the last measurement before ICU discharge) between patients who are readmitted vs not. This is the "naive" approach.

**Limitation**: A single timepoint does not capture the trajectory or variability of vital signs. A patient with a stable HR of 90 at discharge may have had a highly variable HR (60-120) in the preceding hours, indicating underlying instability.

### Alternative: Trajectory/Fluctuation Metrics Over Pre-Discharge Window

Our proposed approach computes the coefficient of variation (SD/mean) of HR and MAP over the 24h before ICU discharge, and checks for residual hypoxemia (FiO2 > 0.4 or SpO2 < 94%) in the window. This captures the trajectory and variability that a single timepoint misses.

**What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses**:
- **Variability**: The CV captures the range of fluctuation, not just the central tendency. A patient with HR ranging 60-120 (CV 0.25) is physiologically different from a patient with stable HR 90 (CV 0.05), even if both have HR 90 at discharge.
- **Residual hypoxemia**: Checking for FiO2 > 0.4 or SpO2 < 94% at any point in the window captures patients who required supplemental oxygen before discharge but may have been weaned to room air at the exact discharge timepoint.
- **Trajectory**: The window-based approach captures the direction and magnitude of change, not just the endpoint.

### Why the Alternative Is Scientifically Substantive

The baseline (fixed-timepoint) approach would likely show an association between abnormal vital signs at discharge and readmission, but this association would be confounded by the fact that patients with abnormal vital signs at discharge are often more ill. The alternative (window-based variability) approach is more likely to isolate the effect of physiological instability, because it captures the dynamic pattern of vital signs rather than a single snapshot.

## Limitations

1. **Single-center data**: MIMIC-IV is a single-center dataset (Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center). Generalizability to other ICUs is uncertain.
2. **ICU readmission definition**: We define ICU readmission as a second icustays row for the same hadm_id. This may include planned re-admissions (e.g., post-surgical) as well as unplanned readmissions.
3. **Vital sign measurement variability**: Vital signs are measured at irregular intervals; the CV may be affected by measurement frequency rather than true physiological variability.
4. **No measured GFR or organ function**: The SOFA proxy is an approximation; true organ function is not measured.
5. **Selection bias**: Patients who are discharged from the ICU are a selected population; those who die in the ICU are excluded.
6. **Discharge destination**: The transfers table may not capture all discharge destinations (e.g., transfer to another hospital).
7. **No EEG or other objective measures**: Physiological instability is measured by vital signs only; there is no objective measure of neurological or organ function.
