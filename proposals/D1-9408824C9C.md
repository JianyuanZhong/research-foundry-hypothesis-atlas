# Provisional HCC trajectory-versus-care-process hypothesis

## Proposed relationship
Among adults admitted with an exact documented HCC diagnosis, an ordered 7-day laboratory trajectory may add held-out predictive information about a subsequent, non-routine inpatient procedure beyond an admission snapshot and measured treatment/documentation intensity. The primary endpoint is a computable procedure-based escalation proxy, not death, ICU admission, decompensation, or clinical harm.

The clinically important unresolved claim is whether temporal laboratory information contains information about later care escalation that is not already encoded by admission severity, routine HCC treatment, observation intensity, or documentation practice. A positive result would justify expert review of temporal monitoring; it would not establish a biological mechanism or treatment effect.

## Strongest supported claim and exact source bindings
The strongest claim currently supported is only that the frozen HCC snapshot contains joinable encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, and procedure records with timed procedure rows sufficient for a bounded endpoint audit. The inherited audit reported 104,554 exact HCC patient/admission keys, 321,207 timed HCC procedure rows, 15,774 untimed HCC procedure rows, and 20,052 HCC procedure rows after day 7 and before discharge (6,779 keys). These are availability/support counts, not evidence that the endpoint is clinically meaningful.

## Falsifiable test
Fit, on the same cohort and patient-level held-out split, (1) a simple admission snapshot model using baseline labs, demographics, admission department, and pre-window treatment/documentation indicators, and (2) a substantive time-aware model using ordered within-assay liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker measurements through day 7. Compare held-out discrimination/calibration and uncertainty for a prespecified post-day-7, pre-discharge non-routine procedure endpoint. Repeat with routine-treatment procedures and a timing-shifted negative control. Support requires incremental value beyond a trivial margin with uncertainty excluding it, smaller value for routine procedures, and no value in the negative control. Adverse evidence is no incremental value or a negative-control signal. Inconclusive evidence is insufficient event/assay support, unstable units, or uncertainty spanning clinically relevant margins.

## Missing evidence and limits
The exact non-routine procedure dictionary, event counts, laboratory units/assay mapping, clinical endpoint adjudication, and all fitted results are unresolved. The procedure endpoint is not validated as deterioration or harm. The three inspected key-reference bundle and final experiment card must be attached before selection.
