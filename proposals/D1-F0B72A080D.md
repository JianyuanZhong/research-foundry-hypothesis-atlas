# Relative laboratory surveillance and transportable ICU risk

Status: Episode-17 successor to [prior hypothesis] and [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic, transportability, and measurement-process study. It is not a causal test-ordering study, hospital-quality ranking, clinical-utility trial, or claim that changing testing improves survival.

## Repair and scientific question

The parents establish a well-supported fixed landmark and recorded-death competing-risk question. The remaining substantive weakness is process-versus-workflow identification. A laboratory count or missingness pattern can be associated with outcome because a patient's condition affects testing, because hospitals have different surveillance intensity, or because both occur. Whole-hospital holdout and a site-descriptor diagnostic expose transport failure, but raw process features can still combine patient signal with hospital-scale workflow. A within-hospital permutation only supplies a null; it does not test whether relative surveillance position survives a shift in absolute testing intensity.

This successor therefore prespecifies two distinct transport estimands:

1. strict zero-shot transport of raw patient-level laboratory process features to hospitals whose outcomes are entirely held out; and
2. an outcome-free, unsupervised site-standardized transport diagnostic in which process features are represented as percentiles relative to predictor-window episodes in the target hospital.

The second analysis is explicitly not zero-shot: it assumes access to an unlabeled stream of target-hospital episodes, but no target outcomes or post-landmark information. It asks whether being unusually observed, recently observed, or sparsely observed relative to local practice is more portable than absolute counts. The primary claim remains the strict zero-shot result.

The hypothesis is:

> Among adult first ICU stays at a fixed six-hour ICU-relative landmark, patient-level laboratory observation timing and intensity add transportable, decision-relevant information beyond laboratory state and minimal availability. The raw process increment should improve held-out-hospital calibration and competing-risk prediction, attenuate under state-risk- and hospital-stratified process permutation, and remain directionally present after outcome-free target-hospital percentile normalization. If the increment disappears under normalization or is matched by hospital descriptors, the stronger interpretation is a workflow fingerprint rather than patient-specific information.

“Decision-relevant” is operationalized as a prespecified hypothetical risk-alert analysis at fixed risk thresholds and alert burden. It is not clinical benefit.

## What is known and what is unresolved

The local outcome-blind audit attached to the assessed eICU parent reports 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU-death events by 1,800 minutes, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows known by the six-hour landmark, and 111,655 episodes with at least one eligible landmark-known laboratory row. These counts establish computational support for a hospital-transport experiment only. They do not show that testing predicts death, that a process feature represents clinician intent, or that an alert benefits a patient.

The expert seed [starting question] proposes that testing-frequency and missingness-dependent models lose performance across hospitals. I inspected and assessed that seed as repairable untested source material. The present proposal supplies the missing local estimand, outcome, leakage controls, and process/workflow decomposition. The two assigned parents were also inspected: the renal/nutrition direction is explicitly deferred because its conservative support audit found only three relevant episodes and no detected low-glucose event; it is not revived here.

The strongest claim available before fitting is therefore: eICU contains enough adult first-stay, hospital, and revision-timed laboratory structure to test a recorded short-horizon ICU-death transport hypothesis. The unresolved claim is the hypothesis above. No fitted result is asserted.

## Population and temporal estimand

Use the frozen eICU snapshot [source checksum].

Include the first adult ICU stay per person:

- patient.age is at least 18; values greater than 89 are recoded to 90;
- patient.unitdischargeoffset is numeric and greater than 360;
- select the smallest valid numeric patient.unitvisitnumber within patient.uniquepid, breaking ties by patientunitstayid;
- retain only the selected stay and only if it reaches the six-hour landmark.

The primary predictor window is ICU-relative [0,360] minutes. No lab result, revision, or derived feature with a relevant time after minute 360 is permitted in the primary feature matrix. There is no invented unitadmitoffset: the local metadata defines offsets as minutes relative to ICU admission.

For a lab row to be eligible as landmark-known numeric information, lab.labresultoffset must be in [0,360], lab.labresult must parse as numeric, and lab.labresultrevisedoffset must be at most 360 or null under a frozen, reported null-revision convention. The result time and revision time are distinct. A result measured in the window but revised after minute 360 is not known at the landmark and cannot be used. Conflicting or unresolved units/interfaces are retained in the audit but excluded from numeric state features unless the pre-outcome codebook resolves them.

The primary outcome is first patient.unitdischargestatus normalized exactly to Expired with patient.unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800]. The primary estimand is the cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death by 1,800 ICU-relative minutes after the landmark. An alive ICU discharge before 1,800 is a competing event, not ordinary independent censoring. A stay still in the ICU at 1,800 without recorded death is administratively censored at the horizon. Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, and latent or unrecorded death are outside this estimand.

For the secondary alert diagnostic, recorded death by 1,800 is the event and alive discharge before 1,800 is treated as a non-event in the hypothetical decision-curve formula. This is a label-relative utility calculation, while the primary Brier and CIF analyses retain the competing event.

## Feature construction

Freeze the codebook and all support decisions before using outcome labels.

State inputs for each retained analyte include the first and last valid numeric value in [0,360], range and slope only when at least two valid measurements exist, prespecified clinical threshold flags, and an analyte-level availability indicator. Include patient demographics/admission context listed above. All numeric transformations, medians, clipping and scaling are fitted within the outer development hospitals.

Minimal availability is kept explicit because a value-only model would conflate unavailable state with a numerical value. Detailed process is a separate block. The process block includes, for each retained analyte, number of eligible result rows, number of distinct result times, first-result time, last-result time, minutes from last result to 360, first-to-last span, inter-result gap summaries, revision-known indicator, and stable labmeasurenamesystem/labmeasurenameinterface indicators. It also includes total eligible lab rows, distinct analyte count, total observation span and the count of analytes with any result. No outcome, post-landmark information, hospitalid, or target-hospital outcome summary enters a primary patient feature.

A result's revision status is an information-availability feature, not a clinical state. The audit must report how many measurements are excluded for late revision and how null revisions are handled. Interface/system indicators are included only when their support exists in both development and held-out hospitals; otherwise they move to the audit rather than being silently imputed.

## Model comparison on identical inputs

All models use the same episode set, outer folds, competing labels and horizon.

B0 is a transparent elastic-net pooled discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly rows from minute 360 through minute 1,800 and two heads: recorded death and alive ICU discharge. It uses demographics/admission context, state value summaries, threshold flags and one analyte availability indicator per retained analyte. It excludes detailed timing, counts, intervals, interface/system indicators, hospitalid and hospital descriptors. Imputation, scaling, regularization and any spline choices are fit within development hospitals.

B1 is the matched transparent raw-process model. It uses exactly the B0 episode-hour rows, target, folds and state/availability inputs, then adds only the process block. The primary incremental estimand is the paired held-out-hospital difference B1 minus B0 in integrated competing-risk Brier score, calibration slope/intercept and alert decision outputs. A process-only model and value-only lower-bound are secondary ablations, not replacements for B0.

B2 is the relative-process model. It uses exactly B1's state and process channels except every continuous process variable is replaced by its empirical percentile within the target hospital, calculated from a prespecified outcome-free 10% calibration subset of eligible predictor-window episodes in that target hospital. The remaining target episodes are scored without using their outcomes, discharge status or any post-landmark field. Hospital-specific calibration subsets are sampled with a fixed seed and never used for reported outcome metrics. Hospitals with fewer than 50 eligible calibration candidates or fewer than 20 evaluation episodes are unsupported for B2. B2 is labeled unsupervised site adaptation and is not pooled into the strict zero-shot primary estimate.

T is a secondary one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins spanning [0,360]. Each bin contains the same retained analyte values, availability masks, counts, recency, revision-known and stable interface/system channels used by B1; no hospital ID is provided. It has death and alive-discharge heads. A next-bin-observation auxiliary head may be fitted only to characterize the measurement process, with its loss prespecified and its output excluded from the death prediction at evaluation. T is informative if ordering, persistence or co-movement improves calibrated held-out-hospital performance beyond B1, not merely if it gains AUROC.

Before selecting a primary method, the transparent B0 and learned T were compared conceptually on the identical inputs: B0 supplies auditability and a direct process-block contrast; T can reveal temporal structure lost by summaries but risks learning interface-specific sequence patterns and is less interpretable. Therefore B0/B1 are primary, T is conditional on support and convergence, and B2 is the targeted workflow-identification diagnostic. The detailed comparison, exact inputs, targets, split, uncertainty and resource estimates are recorded in support-episode17-method-selection.md.

Report each hospital's count, event count, competing discharge count and whether it passes the support gate.

Use hospital-clustered bootstrap or a hierarchical random-effects interval for paired differences, with at least 1,000 resamples if feasible. Do not report a favorable hospital or threshold selected after fitting as the primary result. Calibration-in-the-large and slope are reported per hospital and pooled macro-hospital; integrated Brier uses competing-risk predictions with administrative censoring and alive discharge as a competing event.

## Decision-analytic output

For each held-out episode, use the predicted cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death by minute 1,800. At q in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, alert when predicted risk is at least q. Report alerts per 100 stays, recorded-death capture, PPV, calibration and the hypothetical net benefit per 100 stays:

100 * [TP/N - FP/N * q/(1-q)]

Here TP is a recorded death by 1,800 and FP is an episode without that recorded death; alive ICU discharge is counted as a non-event only for this secondary hypothetical alert calculation. The primary competing-risk metrics are not replaced by this formula.

Define fixed baseline-risk strata using out-of-fold B0 cumulative incidence: low <0.01, intermediate 0.01 to <0.05, and high at least 0.05. A stratum-threshold cell is interpretable only if it has at least 200 held-out stays, 30 recorded deaths in aggregate, and at least 10 stays in at least 80% of contributing hospitals. Otherwise it is unsupported, not evidence against the hypothesis. The primary actionability pattern requires a directionally favorable B1-minus-B0 calibration/Brier result, positive incremental net benefit in at least one supported prespecified cell with a simultaneous interval excluding zero, attenuation under the matched process permutation, and no comparable site-descriptor-only signal. B2 is interpreted separately: persistence after outcome-free percentile normalization strengthens the relative-surveillance interpretation; raw-only improvement supports only a workflow-scale association.

No alert threshold is a treatment recommendation. The data do not measure alert burden harms, clinician response, patient preferences, or treatment effect.

## Falsification, sensitivity and support gates

The following are frozen before outcome evaluation:

1. **State-matched process permutation.** Within each held-out hospital and B0-risk stratum, permute each complete patient-level process vector across episodes while preserving the hospital and stratum distribution; keep values, availability and outcomes fixed. The B1 increment and its decision increment should attenuate toward the B0 null. A persistent increment indicates residual state/site structure, leakage or a flawed null, not patient-specific actionability.
2. **Hospital workflow diagnostic.** Fit a secondary site-descriptor-only model using hospital.numbedscategory, hospital.teachingstatus and hospital.region. It is descriptive and not a deployment competitor. If its held-out performance matches B1, the process result is plausibly a workflow fingerprint. Raw hospitalid is prohibited as a predictor in held-out inference; a patient-random raw-ID model is permitted only as an optimism diagnostic.
3. **Relative-scale diagnostic.** Compare B1 with B2 on the separately labeled unsupervised adaptation cohort. A loss of the effect under B2 is adverse to the relative-surveillance interpretation; persistence is supportive but does not prove transport to a new hospital without unlabeled calibration episodes.
4. **Common-panel and strict-revision sensitivity.** Repeat with only analytes whose name/unit/interface mapping is common across all retained folds, and repeat after excluding all null-revision rows. A conclusion that reverses only under a plausible revision convention is not robust.
5. **Leakage checks.** Prohibit post-360 result/revision fields, apachePatientResult actual outcomes and any outcome-derived target-hospital statistic. A deliberately post-landmark feature run may be used as a pipeline sentinel; it must never be reported as a scientific model.
6. **Process ablation.** Separately remove timing, count/intensity, interface/system and revision sub-blocks. A gain attributable only to interface/site coding is workflow evidence, not patient-level process evidence.
7. **Support gate.** Before fitting, require at least 100 recorded-death events overall, at least 10 event-bearing hospitals in every retained transport fold, at least 20 eligible episodes in every hospital contributing to a primary macro estimate, positive overlap in the state and process distributions, and finite calibration/Brier estimates. If a fold fails, report it as unsupported and do not pool it into a transport claim.

## Interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following: the primary B1-minus-B0 difference is favorable for held-out macro-hospital calibration or competing-risk Brier with uncertainty; at least one supported prespecified alert cell has positive incremental hypothetical net benefit under the simultaneous interval; the state-matched process permutation attenuates the increment; the common-panel and strict-revision sensitivities do not reverse it; and the site-descriptor diagnostic does not account for a comparable gain. If B2 also retains a directionally favorable effect, the narrower conclusion may be that relative surveillance position is portable when an unlabeled target-hospital reference stream is available. Even then, the claim is predictive and observational.

Adverse evidence is a reproducible null or worsening for B1 in held-out hospitals, a calibration harm at relevant thresholds, reversal under strict revision/common-panel handling, persistence under process permutation, or a gain matched by site descriptors. That refutes the proposed patient-specific transport interpretation in this snapshot; it does not prove that laboratory testing is clinically irrelevant.

Inconclusive evidence includes insufficient events or event-bearing hospitals after the frozen codebook, no common support, unsupported B2 target hospitals, unstable temporal convergence, intervals spanning clinically meaningful decision ranges, or fold-specific reversals that cannot be resolved by the prespecified uncertainty analysis. Inconclusive results must report the flow and support diagnostics without selecting the best AUROC or treating readiness as a scientific result.

## Limits and deferred claims

The recorded death label is an eICU discharge status, not adjudicated cause or complete mortality. A lab result is an observation record, not proof of a test order, clinician concern, physiologic truth, or treatment response. The release lacks reliable order intent, full medication administration semantics, patient preferences, alert response, post-discharge mortality, external hospitals, validated narrative text and raw waveforms. The observation-process effect may be confounding by severity, staffing, unit type or unmeasured workflow.

A supportive computation cannot establish that clinicians should test more or less, that an alert improves survival, that the model is clinically safe, or that the process representation will transport beyond this snapshot. Those claims require expert adjudication of clinical state and test intent, external validation in new hospitals, a prespecified intervention/decision target, and a prospective impact or randomized evaluation. A causal test-ordering model is deferred until orders, timing, indication and treatment response are available. The full multimodal demonstrations are not reproduced; the learned temporal model is a bounded eICU adaptation motivated by the inspected method guidance, with no claim that unavailable paper supplements were read.
