# HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B: leakage-safe decision-relevance and heterogeneity envelope

## Successor, frozen schema, and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`, the highest-mu assessed-valid parent assigned for this episode. The parent already supplies a compiler-ready missing-`Y` partial-identification and uncertainty contract. This child preserves that parent exactly where it matters: the adult HCC repeat-TACE population, event construction, assay-specific clocks, annual forward origins, nested prognostic estimand, training-only preprocessing, outcome-blind support rules, deterministic missing-outcome bounds, and strict noncausal boundary are all frozen.

The strongest claim supported by the inherited evidence is narrow. In selected repeat-TACE records with a valid remote baseline assay `B`, a contemporaneous pre-repeat assay `P`, and an observed same-encounter post-event assay `Y`, the locked model comparison can test whether `P` adds short-horizon prognostic information beyond `B` and the pre-event core. The executed audit supports at most a local prognostic association in an adequately supported calendar mixture; severe later `Y` loss, sparse systemic-record arms, and selection diagnostics prevent a claim of stable temporal transport. Nothing in the inherited evidence establishes treatment response, a completed TACE, assay interpretation, patient-important outcome, safety, utility, causal effect, or benefit.

The unresolved clinically consequential question is more specific than whether a pooled RMSE improves:

> **When the incremental prognostic signal is present, is it concentrated in identifiable record-defined baseline strata, and does adding `P` change a prespecified surrogate monitoring-priority category often enough to be useful for clinician review, while remaining robust to missing-`Y` and observation-selection uncertainty?**

The proposed answer is deliberately limited. It creates an auditable *decision-relevance envelope* from records: (1) subgroup-specific held-out prognostic gains and heterogeneity contrasts, (2) assay-specific predicted-value tier crossings caused by adding `P`, and (3) selection and placebo diagnostics. A tier crossing is a record-level trigger for review or future prospective adjudication, not a recommendation to treat, repeat TACE, alter a drug, or discharge a patient. Because the HCC files contain no validated laboratory units/reference ranges, no imaging files, no reliable completed-treatment indicator, no patient-important endpoint, no treatment intent, no utility weights, and no causal treatment comparison, the envelope is a surrogate/operational translation only.

The advance over the parent is therefore not a new clinical outcome or a new causal estimand. It is a pre-specified, leakage-safe way to ask whether a statistically incremental assay predictor is heterogeneous and operationally visible in the available record, without converting a laboratory forecast into a benefit claim. The primary pooled estimand and its interpretation remain unchanged.

## Decision-relevance question and record-defined strata

### A. Baseline strata for heterogeneity

The primary heterogeneity analysis uses strata defined without future `Y` and without post-event care. For each future block, freeze all cutpoints and transformations from the corresponding training data. Use the following, assay-specific and mutually reportable dimensions:

1. **Baseline forecast tier:** quartiles of the training-only M0 predicted `Y` distribution among finite training complete triplets (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4). The tier is a prognostic-risk analogue, not a clinical severity class because assay units/reference ranges are absent.
2. **Baseline assay level tier:** quartiles of training `B`, separately for albumin and total bilirubin. If fewer than four nonempty training strata or a repeated-cutpoint problem prevents distinct categories, mark this dimension non-estimable rather than inventing bins. This is a relative within-snapshot ranking, not a normal-range statement.
3. **P lead-time tier:** pre-event `P_lead_hours` in training-defined tertiles, with the inherited positive-lead rule. This asks whether the incremental value is concentrated when the contemporaneous assay is obtained earlier versus later; it does not assert a monitoring policy.
4. **Record arm:** systemic-record indicator versus comparator, exactly as inherited. It is a data-source/pathway label, not a treatment assignment or causal contrast.
5. **Calendar block:** 2021–2024 origin block, preserving the forward validation design. Calendar-specific results are transport diagnostics, not a time trend claim.

The primary heterogeneity report prespecifies dimensions 1, 4, and 5. Dimensions 2 and 3 are secondary mechanistic/operational descriptions and cannot rescue a failed primary gate. Do not stratify on `P-B`, observed `Y`, post-event recording, downstream medication, or any text-derived clinical interpretation in the primary heterogeneity analysis. A supplementary `P-B` surprise display may be emitted only as an explicitly post-predictor descriptive partition, never as evidence of effect modification or a pre-event action rule.

For every dimension and level, report the outcome-blind funnel and the complete-triplet count. A stratum with fewer than 20 BP-eligible episodes overall, fewer than 10 per inherited arm where both arms are claimed comparable, fewer than 10 observed complete triplets, or fewer than two observed patient clusters is descriptive/non-estimable for inferential comparison. Small-cell results remain visible as counts and are not silently pooled.

### B. Subgroup prognostic gains and heterogeneity contrasts

Using the already frozen held-out predictions `p0_i` and `p1_i`, calculate within each estimable future block, assay, arm, and pre-specified stratum:

* `RMSE0_g`, `RMSE1_g`, `SD_g(Y)`, and `G_g=(RMSE0_g-RMSE1_g)/SD_g(Y)` on observed complete triplets only;
* the paired squared-error contrast `Delta_g = mean((Y-p0)^2 - (Y-p1)^2)` on the same observed rows;
* the descriptive heterogeneity contrast `H_g = G_g - G_all` using the same block's pooled observed complete-triplet `G_all` and the same frozen predictions;
* patient-cluster paired bootstrap intervals for `G_g`, `Delta_g`, and `H_g`, resampling patients within arm and retaining all their events, with predictions and the original point-estimate denominator fixed as in the parent contract.

`G_g` and `H_g` are secondary conditional prognostic summaries. They do not replace pooled `G`, and no interaction p-value is required or interpreted as a causal interaction. Use a declared seed and 2,000 patient-frequency replicates when feasible; record smaller numbers only with a resource-failure reason. A zero/nonfinite stratum SD, no observed outcomes, fewer than two patient clusters, nonfinite prediction, or empty stratum yields `non-estimable`, not zero. Do not use strata discovered from the test outcomes, and do not select the “best” stratum after seeing its estimate.

To prevent a misleading “positive subgroup” story, define a **heterogeneity envelope** as follows. A dimension is `robustly differential` only if (a) at least two pre-specified levels pass support and outcome gates, (b) the level-specific point estimates have opposite signs or their 95% fixed-prediction cluster-bootstrap intervals are separated from the pooled sign in a predeclared direction, and (c) the corresponding missing-`Y` all-BP deterministic bounds do not make the sign pattern wholly selection-dependent. Otherwise label the pattern `compatible with heterogeneity`, `selection-sensitive`, or `inconclusive`; do not assert homogeneity from a nonsignificant contrast.

### C. Surrogate monitoring-priority translation

For each assay and training origin, derive empirical forecast tiers from the finite training observed `Y` distribution at fixed quantiles q10, q25, q50, q75, and q90. These are within-dataset forecast bands only. If the training range is empty/degenerate, future observed `Y` lies outside the training range, or the assay has no finite predictions, translation status is `unsupported` for that block. Never map the tiers to clinical normal/abnormal labels.

For each future BP-eligible row, assign:

* `tier0`: tier of frozen M0 prediction `p0_i` under the training cutpoints;
* `tier1`: tier of frozen M1 prediction `p1_i` under the same cutpoints;
* `crossing`: whether `tier1 != tier0`, and whether the change is upward, downward, or multi-tier;
* `distance`: absolute change in tier index;
* `directional_surprise`: sign of `p1_i-p0_i`, reported as a model movement only.

The primary translation output is a **review-queue proxy**: the count and fraction of BP-eligible rows whose `P`-augmented forecast crosses at least one pre-registered empirical tier boundary, overall and by baseline forecast tier, arm, calendar block, and P-lead tier. The queue is not a recommendation; it identifies records for clinician review or prospective study design. It cannot be called a change in treatment eligibility, liver failure risk, response, toxicity, or benefit.

For observed complete triplets, additionally report calibration-by-tier and the paired absolute/squared error of M0 versus M1 within each `tier0 -> tier1` transition. For missing `Y`, report counts and deterministic bounded contributions only; never impute `Y` or label a crossing correct/incorrect. Use the parent's training-range affine contrast bounds over all BP-eligible rows to calculate, for each translation stratum, whether the sign of the aggregate M0-versus-M1 squared-error contrast is robust, crosses zero, or is unsupported. Tier crossings do not alter these bounds.

A translation is **surrogate-supportive** only when the pooled primary `G` is estimable and favorable, the relevant strata pass support gates, tier-crossing patterns are reproducible in at least one forward block without a material selection warning, and all-BP bounds do not cross zero for the corresponding aggregate contrast. Even then, the conclusion is only that the added assay value changes a record-defined laboratory forecast category in a reproducible selected setting. It does not establish clinical usefulness. A large crossing fraction with no gain, a gain confined to a tiny cell, or a crossing pattern driven by missingness is not supportive.

### D. Optional recorded-care linkage, kept separate from clinical outcome

Reuse the parent outcome-blind ascertainment definitions, without changing any primary model or bound:

* `exam_opportunity`: any examination row with valid `开始时间` in (event time, event time +72h];
* `followup_encounter`: an additional distinct encounter with valid `就诊时间` in that interval;
* `document_presence`: nonempty linked `clinical_documents` row for the selected encounter;
* recorded non-drug-order, medication, or procedure presence when their source-specific time is valid and falls in a declared window.

Use mutually exclusive recorded-process strata with precedence `exam+follow-up`, `exam only`, `follow-up only`, `document-only`, `none`, plus a source-family cross-tab. These markers can test whether tier crossings are observable in pathways where records are more complete, but they are not actions received, completed follow-up, assay review, treatment intent, or patient benefit. Because document rows lack native time, document presence is never put into the `Y` window. Any post-event care marker is excluded from model fitting, forecast tier assignment, `Y` selection, missing-outcome completion, inverse weighting, and causal interpretation.

## Partial identification and selection sensitivity

Run the parent deterministic missing-`Y` layer for every assay, origin, block, arm, and heterogeneity stratum that has finite frozen predictions. Use all BP-eligible rows, including missing `Y`; fit models only on pre-origin observed complete triplets. Define `L_train` and `U_train` from training observed `Y` using the exact inherited parser, and stop the bound if the range is empty, degenerate, or any observed future `Y` is outside it. For each missing row, use the affine contrast

`d_i(y)=2*y*(p1_i-p0_i)+p0_i^2-p1_i^2`

and exact endpoint values at `L_train` and `U_train`; preserve fixed BP denominators. Report all-missing-at-L, all-missing-at-U, and least-favorable endpoints, as deterministic identification regions rather than confidence intervals. Retain the conservative non-primary RMSE enclosure and the piecewise-linear common-displacement tipping coordinate. Do not make a stratum appear robust by replacing its missing values with the observed stratum mean.

The main selection-sensitive table crosses, for each assay/block/arm/stratum:

1. outcome-blind BP-eligibility count;
2. observed-Y count and Wilson interval;
3. pre-Y ascertainment category;
4. observed-versus-missing pre-Y SMDs for B, age, timing, arm, and forecast tier;
5. observed-case `G_g`/`Delta_g`;
6. all-BP lower/upper `Delta_g` and endpoint sign;
7. normalized transport ESS, observation ESS, maximum diagnostic weight, and same-arm nearest-neighbor flags from the parent;
8. tier-crossing counts among all BP rows and among observed rows.

Observation probabilities and transport weights remain diagnostics only. No inverse-probability reweighting, MAR claim, imputation, or external transport claim is permitted. A favorable observed-case transition with a zero-crossing all-BP envelope is `selection-sensitive`, not supportive.

## Falsification and negative controls

All negative controls are diagnostics for implementation, leakage, linkage, or recording artifacts; none is a causal randomization test.

1. **P-only restricted linkage placebo.** Within each training/future partition, permute raw `P` across rows only within inherited arm and broad calendar block, while holding `B`, the compact core, `Y`, row membership, patient folds, event dates, and outcome-observation pattern fixed. Recompute the full nested pipeline, predictions, subgroup gains, and tier crossings. A stable primary or heterogeneity pattern that survives this placebo is adverse to the claimed P linkage. Because raw-P reassignment is not exact conditional randomization, label this a restricted linkage placebo.
2. **No-increment implementation placebo.** Set the M1 prediction equal to the frozen M0 prediction without refitting, and require `G=0`, `Delta=0`, `H_g=0`, and no tier crossings up to numerical tolerance. Any nonzero result indicates an arithmetic, tier, denominator, or output bug. This is a computational control, not a scientific null result.
3. **Calendar-shifted recording negative control.** Recompute only the descriptive recorded-care/tier-crossing linkage using a prespecified shifted window (for example, [event +7 days, event +10 days]) that is outside the `Y` clock. This tests whether apparent translation is merely a general documentation-intensity artifact. It cannot show that care causes observation or benefit. A similar magnitude in the shifted window is a selection-sensitive warning.
4. **Pre-event timing negative control.** Test whether post-event tier-crossing status is associated with a pre-event examination/document/order presence that is defined before event time and cannot be downstream of the forecast. This is a recording-process diagnostic. A strong unexplained association is not evidence for clinical actionability and should trigger selection-sensitive labeling.
5. **Outcome-order and clock controls.** Verify that no `Y` timestamp, `Y` value, document text, post-event order, medication, procedure, or examination enters event selection, stratum construction, training preprocessing, threshold derivation, or prediction. Verify strict boundaries at -72h, event time, +72h, and +7-day placebo edges with synthetic rows. Any violation invalidates the affected block.
6. **Assay separation control.** Albumin and total bilirubin must have independent cutpoints, predictions, missingness bounds, tiers, and outputs. A pooled cutpoint, cross-assay unit conversion, or shared outcome row is a hard failure.
7. **Arm/calendar support control.** Re-run the translation after removing each arm or calendar block descriptively. This is not a redefinition of the primary estimate. If the queue or heterogeneity conclusion exists only in a sparse arm, a single calendar block, or outside training support, label it transport- or selection-sensitive.

## Interpretation precedence and falsifiable branches

Apply this precedence in order; do not let an attractive favorable subgroup override a higher-priority failure.

### Supportive, but only surrogate-level

Call the result `surrogate-supportive` only if reconstruction and clock assertions pass; the pooled primary estimate is estimable; relevant subgroup gates pass; the P-only placebo is adverse to the placebo hypothesis (incremental signal attenuates as expected); the no-increment control is exact; the tier-crossing output is finite and reproducible in an adequately supported future block; the all-BP deterministic envelope for the relevant contrast remains strictly favorable; and there is no material observation/transport warning. The permitted conclusion is: `P` adds selected short-horizon prognostic information and changes a record-defined assay forecast tier in that supported calendar mixture. It is not evidence of treatment response, completed care, safety, utility, actionability, or patient benefit.

### Adverse

Call `adverse` when the primary pooled contrast is nonpositive in an adequately supported block, the added model repeatedly degrades, the P-only placebo is as favorable as or more favorable than the real linkage, the no-increment control fails, assay separation fails, or tier crossings are common without improved forecast accuracy. A negative result is informative: it argues against using this record field as a reproducible short-horizon prognostic/monitoring-priority signal in the tested setting. It still does not establish no clinical effect.

### Heterogeneity-supportive but not policy-supportive

If at least two adequately supported, pre-specified baseline strata show a reproducible, directionally distinct `G_g`/`Delta_g` pattern, the fixed-prediction bootstrap and deterministic all-BP envelopes support the difference, and placebo/clock checks pass, label `heterogeneity-compatible`. This can motivate prospective adjudication of which patients benefit from additional measurement or review. It cannot identify a treatment-effect subgroup, authorize an action threshold, or claim that the tier is clinically meaningful.

### Selection-sensitive

Use `selection-sensitive` if complete-case performance is favorable but any all-BP envelope crosses zero; observed versus missing SMDs/ascertainment gradients are material; the translation is concentrated in observed-care strata; calendar-shifted recording controls are similarly strong; the result changes after excluding a small influential cluster; or subgroup sign depends on a sparse cell, arm, or calendar block. Selection-sensitive is a substantive result, not a failed analysis.

### Unsupported or inconclusive

Use `unsupported` for failed reconstruction, range violation, nonfinite predictions, missing/degenerate training range, invalid clocks, or assay/unit ambiguity. Use `inconclusive` for failed support gates, fewer than two observed patient clusters, nonestimable SD/bootstrap, low transport or observation ESS, sparse systemic arms, no observed Y in a block, or a bounded region too wide to resolve the sign. Do not convert a point estimate into evidence when these gates fail.

## What can and cannot be concluded

Computationally checkable outputs include source hashes and schemas; duplicate-safe joins; inherited denominators and event reconstruction; exact assay clocks; training-only strata and quantiles; finite frozen predictions; subgroup RMSE/Delta/H calculations; fixed-denominator cluster bootstrap bookkeeping; tier crossing counts; deterministic missing-Y endpoints; tipping roots; placebo outcomes; recording-window precedence; and fail-closed status labels.

The records do not provide validated assay units/reference ranges, imaging response, tumor burden, hepatic decompensation, mortality, toxicity, patient-reported outcomes, treatment intent, verified medication/procedure administration, external-care capture, utility weights, clinician adjudication, or a randomized/target-trial treatment contrast. Therefore this experiment cannot establish that a tier crossing is clinically important, that a patient should receive repeat TACE or any medication, that monitoring changes improve outcomes, that one subgroup benefits causally, or that the forecast has safety or cost utility. Those claims require expert review of the original clinical context and a prospective or independently linked cohort with patient-important outcomes, treatment intent/exposure, adverse events, units/reference ranges, and clinically validated decision thresholds.

## Compiler and verifier contract

Emit only de-identified aggregate rows keyed by assay, origin, future block, arm, dimension, stratum, and status. For each row include denominator counts, observed/missing-Y counts, forecast-tier cutpoint provenance, `G_g`, `Delta_g`, `H_g`, fixed-prediction bootstrap replicate/failure counts, all-BP bound endpoints, tier-crossing counts/fractions, observation and transport diagnostics, placebo status, and interpretation precedence. Never emit raw patient keys, raw text, or individual clinical notes.

The compiler must run the parent primary and bound pipeline first, then the secondary frozen-prediction heterogeneity and translation layer. A failed secondary layer must not silently change the primary result. The verifier must test both correct computation with unsupported conclusions and incorrect computation with apparently favorable conclusions. In particular, it must reject: (a) calling empirical assay tiers clinical thresholds; (b) calling a tier crossing benefit or actionability; (c) treating subgroup gain as a causal interaction; (d) treating a document/order/medication/procedure row as completed care or administration; (e) using post-Y fields or Y-derived cutpoints; (f) pooling assay units; (g) calling deterministic bounds confidence intervals; (h) reporting a favorable observed subgroup when the all-BP envelope crosses zero as supportive; and (i) claiming homogeneity from a nonsignificant heterogeneity contrast.

Synthetic verifier fixtures must include: an exact no-increment case; a favorable observed subgroup with a zero-crossing missing-Y envelope; a tier crossing driven only by a post-event field; duplicate composite-key rows; an out-of-range future Y; a sparse arm; tied quantile cutpoints; a nonfinite prediction; a shifted-window recording artifact; a P-only placebo that remains favorable; and a true supported surrogate-level pattern with no patient-benefit label. The reference execution establishes only computational feasibility and does not establish clinical truth.
