# Branch A — Durable vs prodromal vs record-redundant: a horizon-and-trajectory triage of 2,923 plasma proteins against 22 hospital-ascertained diseases in the UKB olink_dta namespace

Dataset island: `population_multiomics` (UK Biobank), identifier namespace **`olink_dta` only**.
Episode 1, Generation worker Branch A. All numbers marked **[V]** were computed by me in this
episode from the frozen parquet derivatives; numbers marked **[U]** are unverified estimates.

---

## 0. Executive summary of what is new here

Proteome-wide UK Biobank studies now report >10^5 protein–disease associations and derive
drug-target and "early risk marker" claims from them [K1][K2]. An observed prospective
association between a plasma protein and a future hospital diagnosis can, however, arise from
three sources with completely different consequences for target triage:

1. **DURABLE/constitutional** — the protein indexes a long-standing state or pathway upstream
   of disease (the reading needed for target triage);
2. **PRODROMAL** — the protein reads out disease biology already present but not yet coded;
3. **RECORD-REDUNDANT** — the protein re-expresses comorbidity/illness burden that is already
   written in the participant's prior hospital-diagnosis trajectory, i.e. information the
   health system already holds for free.

These make different, testable predictions about (a) the *horizon profile* of the effect and
(b) behaviour after conditioning on the *full prior diagnostic trajectory* (not a comorbidity
count). This proposal specifies a single computable experiment that estimates both axes for
~64,000 (protein, disease) pairs and emits a usable triage, with prespecified positive,
negative and synthetic control fixtures and numeric falsification thresholds.

The experiment is executable entirely inside `olink_dta`. I have already measured the
feasibility numbers (Section 2) and three design-critical facts that are **not** in the Lead's
brief and that materially change the design:

* **[V] Date format correction.** First-diagnosis dates in `s_41280_*` are SAS-style
  `DDMMMYYYY` strings (e.g. `11AUG2011`), **not** ISO. Lexicographic comparison against
  `"2011-01-01"` is meaningless and silently assigns every record to "pre-2011". Any
  landmark split must parse `DD`+`MMM`+`YYYY`. `n_31_0_0` is the string label `Male`/`Female`,
  and `n_21003_0_0` is a numeric string; every column in both parquet files is `string`.
* **[V] The proteome matrix is block-structured, not randomly missing.** Of 2,923 protein
  columns, **1,459 have <10% missing and 1,464 have >=10% missing** (missingness histogram is
  bimodal at ~2–5% and ~15–18%). The missing-indicator of one high-missing assay (`A1BG`)
  agrees with the missing-indicators of other high-missing assays in **mean 96.8% (min 93.0%)**
  of participants, i.e. a shared *participant-level* block, not independent limit-of-detection
  censoring. **8,162** participants lack the reference Expansion assay. This is the Olink
  Explore 1536 core vs Explore Expansion design, independently described in [K2]
  ("Explore 1536 and Explore Expansion platform, targeting 2,923 unique proteins by 2,941
  assays"). Consequence: a single "complete-case 2,923-protein" analysis would silently drop
  ~15% of participants and change the population between protein tiers.
* **[V] The Olink subset is strongly, measurably non-random — detectable without leaving the
  namespace.** Comparing pre-2011 first-diagnosis prevalence in the 53,014 Olink participants
  vs the 449,236 `dta` participants without Olink: **SLE (M32) prevalence ratio 80.3**
  (0.607% vs 0.008%; 322 vs 36 people), dementia 3.00, Parkinson 2.76, multiple sclerosis 2.23,
  CKD 2.14, heart failure 1.97, AKI 1.65, sepsis 1.49, COPD 1.44 — against an overall
  pre-landmark code-burden ratio of only **1.12** (mean 4.44 vs 3.97 codes) and essentially
  identical mean age at assessment (56.81 vs 56.50) and sex split. This is the UKB-PPP
  "consortium-selected" subset [K2] made visible from inside `olink_dta`. It must drive a
  prespecified outcome-screening rule, not a footnote.

## 1. Scientific opening, strongest supported claim, and the exact unresolved claim

### 1.1 The opening

[K1] (Deng et al., *Cell* 2025, abstract inspected) analysed essentially the same cohort this
island contains — 53,026 individuals × 2,920 plasma proteins, vs **[V]** 53,014 × 2,923 in
`[UKB data file]` — and reported 168,100 protein–disease associations across 406
prevalent and 660 incident diseases, AUC > 0.80 for 183 diseases, and, via pQTL integration,
"474 causal proteins ... 37 drug-repurposing opportunities and 26 promising targets". [K2]
(Carrasco-Zanini et al., *Nat Med* 2024, abstract + introduction + part of results inspected)
showed that sparse 5–20-protein signatures beat basic clinical information for 67 of 218
diseases (median ΔC-index 0.07) and beat clinical information plus 37 clinical assays for 52.

Both are strong results. Neither, in the material I inspected, separates the three sources
above. [K2] states explicitly that it "excluded prevalent cases (first occurrence before or up
to the baseline assessment visit) or incident cases recorded within the first 6 months of
follow-up". A 6-month blanking period is far shorter than the recognised prodromal interval
for the diseases where proteomics looks most impressive (haematological malignancy, pulmonary
fibrosis, motor neuron disease, dilated cardiomyopathy); and the clinical comparator is a fixed
covariate set, not the patient's recorded diagnosis trajectory. [K1]'s incident analyses, as
described in the abstract, likewise do not report horizon-stratified effect profiles or
trajectory conditioning.

This is not a manufactured anomaly. It is a boundary of an existing explanation, and it is
already visible in the data I measured: **[V]** a trivial feature of the free text of the
medical record — the *count* of distinct 3-character ICD-10 codes first recorded before
2011-01-01 — already stratifies subsequent 2011–2022 hospital incidence in the Olink cohort by
1.6–4.3 fold (group means, unadjusted; group 1 = 0–1 prior codes, group 2 = 2–5, group 3 = >=6):

| outcome | 0–1 prior codes | 2–5 | >=6 | ratio (>=6)/(0–1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| heart failure | 1.96% | 3.00% | 8.39% | 4.3 |
| sepsis | 2.13% | 2.95% | 6.72% | 3.2 |
| dementia | 1.29% | 2.11% | 4.08% | 3.2 |
| lung cancer | 0.78% | 0.88% | 2.06% | 2.6 |
| type 2 diabetes | 5.12% | 6.31% | 9.71% | 1.9 |
| pancreatic cancer | 0.27% | 0.32% | 0.44% | 1.6 |

A protein that merely tracks this burden will look like an "early risk marker" and like a
"shared cross-disease protein" (>650 proteins shared across >=50 diseases in [K1]) without
being a candidate target at all.

### 1.2 The strongest claim current evidence already supports

> Plasma protein levels measured once in middle age carry substantial prospective
> **predictive** information about hospital-ascertained incident disease, for many diseases
> exceeding what basic clinical information and routine clinical assays provide [K1][K2].

I take this as established and will not re-test it.

### 1.3 The exact unresolved claim this experiment tests

> **Claim U.** For the majority of (protein, disease) pairs whose prospective association is
> statistically robust, the association is *not* durable-constitutional: it is concentrated in
> the early follow-up horizon (prodromal readout) and/or it is largely reproduced by a model of
> the participant's prior recorded hospital-diagnosis trajectory (record redundancy). A
> minority class of pairs is durable *and* non-redundant, and that minority — not the full
> association list — is the defensible input to target triage.

Claim U is falsifiable in both directions and both directions are informative. It is a claim
about **predictive provenance**, not about mechanism. Specifically:

* A pair surviving both filters is **not** thereby shown causal. Durability + non-redundancy is
  a *necessary* condition for a target-like reading, not a sufficient one. Establishing
  causality requires cis-pQTL Mendelian randomisation (genotypes are in a **forbidden
  namespace** on this island) or trial evidence.
* Conditioning on the recorded trajectory changes the estimand from "total prospective
  association" to "association within strata of what the health system already knows". If a
  protein acts causally through a pathway that also generates prior admissions, this is
  deliberate over-adjustment. The redundancy axis answers a **decision-relevant** question
  (*does this protein add information a clinician does not already have?*), not a mediation
  question. This distinction is prespecified and will be stated in every conclusion.

### 1.4 Rival explanations for a positive finding of "prodromality" (declared in advance)

The single most serious rival is **unobserved death as informative censoring**. Death
(field 40000) is not in `olink_dta`. A protein that predicts near-term mortality (GDF15, NEFL,
most illness markers) will mechanically show a strong early-window effect and an attenuated
late-window effect, because high-marker participants are removed from the risk set before the
late window without being censored. This produces the *same* horizon signature as prodromality.
Section 5.4 specifies a computable partial discriminator for it. A second rival is
**calendar-time ascertainment shock**: **[V]** the number of first-diagnosis records in the
whole file falls from 541,771 (2019) to 470,562 (2020) and recovers to 556,543 (2021) — the
COVID-19 disruption sits inside the late window.

### 2.3 Temporal boundaries — and exactly what they cost

* **Time zero is not observed.** Blood draw date = assessment date = field 53, absent here.
  It is known only to lie in the UKB recruitment window, 2006-03 to 2010-10.
* **Landmark L = 2011-01-01**, a common calendar origin after recruitment closed, so that every
  participant's sample certainly precedes L.
* **History** = all ICD-10/ICD-9/OPCS-4 codes whose *first* date (41280/41281/41282) is `< L`.
  Because 41280 gives the date of the first occurrence, a code with first date >= L is correctly
  excluded from history: there is no post-landmark leakage through this construction.
* **Administrative censoring C = 2022-10-31**, taken from **[V]** the observed maximum parsed
  41280 date over all 502,250 participants (observed minimum 1992-03-31).
* **Horizon windows** (calendar, common to all participants):
  W1 = [2011-01-01, 2013-01-01) — 2.00 y; W2 = [2013-01-01, 2016-01-01) — 3.00 y;
  W3 = [2016-01-01, 2022-10-31] — 6.83 y.
  (A finer 4-window split 2011–12 / 2013–14 / 2015–17 / 2018–22 has also been costed; counts in
  Section 2.5.)

**What the common landmark costs, stated plainly.**
1. The gap between blood draw and L is **unobserved and varies from ~0.2 to ~4.8 years**. "W1"
   is therefore 2.2–6.8 years post-draw, not 0–2. The sharpest prodromal interval (0–2 y
   post-draw) falls *before* L for most participants and is absorbed into "prevalent" and
   excluded. **Direction of bias: against detecting prodromality.** A positive prodromality
   finding is therefore conservative; a null prodromality finding is *not* evidence of
   durability, and will not be reported as such.
2. Age at L equals age-at-assessment plus the same unobserved 0.2–4.8 y gap, so **age at
   landmark is known only up to ~±2.3 y**, constant within person. This is non-differential
   measurement error in a strong confounder and leaves residual age confounding. Mitigation:
   natural-spline age-at-assessment (5 df) plus sex, and a prespecified sensitivity restricted
   to a ±5-year age band where the residual age gradient is small.
3. Participants whose disease was diagnosed between draw and L are classified prevalent and
   excluded. This is a *selection on a post-draw event* and will deplete the most
   protein-extreme participants; reported as a limitation, magnitude unquantifiable here.

## 3. Baseline vs substantive learned alternative, at equal detail

Both arms answer the *same* question — how much of a protein's prospective association is
durable after the recorded trajectory is accounted for — and differ only in how the recorded
trajectory is represented.

### 3.1 Arm B (BASELINE): horizon-stratified Poisson regression with a hand-built history summary

**Design.** Person-period file: one record per (participant, window) for windows in which the
participant is at risk, with `log(person-time)` offset; person-time truncated at first event.
Fit, for each (protein p, disease d):

```
log λ_w(i) = α_w + β_w · z_p(i) + f(age_i; ns 5df) + sex_i + g_w(H_i) + τ·tier_i
```

with window-specific protein coefficients β_W1, β_W2, β_W3 obtained as `z_p × window`
interactions in a single stacked fit, so that Cov(β_W1, β_W3) is available analytically and
β_W1 − β_W3 has an exact standard error (no bootstrap needed for the primary contrast).

**History summary `H` (three nested versions, prespecified):**
* **B0** — none (age + sex only). Gives the "total association" β^A.
* **B1** — `log(1 + #distinct pre-L ICD-10 3-char codes)`, `log(1 + #distinct pre-L OPCS-4
  3-char codes)`, `log(1 + years since first ever hospital record)`, and indicator of any
  pre-L record.
* **B2** — B1 + 17 prespecified Charlson-style comorbidity indicators built from pre-L
  3-character ICD-10 codes (MI, HF, PVD, CVD, dementia, chronic lung, rheumatic, peptic ulcer,
  liver mild/severe, diabetes ±complications, hemiplegia, renal, any malignancy, metastatic,
  HIV) + 10 indicators for the most frequent pre-L chapters.

**Estimation.** 64,306 pairs × 3 nested history versions. Implemented as batched
Newton–Raphson IRLS in PyTorch over protein blocks of 64, with the non-protein design fixed per
(disease, history version): design tensor (64, N_periods, K) — ≈ 64 × 1.4e5 × 25 float32 ≈
0.9 GB per block, 10 Newton steps. FDR by Benjamini–Hochberg within (disease × window × tier).

**What B loses.** B1/B2 represent the record as *counts and unordered indicators*. They cannot
represent (i) the **order** of prior codes (I21 → I50 vs I48 → I50 carry different prognoses),
(ii) **recency** (an E11 code from 1998 vs 2010), (iii) **interactions** among codes, (iv) the
identity of the ~1,400 codes outside the Charlson list, or (v) procedure codes as severity
markers. Under-representing the record **systematically under-adjusts**, which **inflates
apparent durability** — exactly the direction that would make Claim U look false for the wrong
reason. This is why an adequate history representation is scientifically necessary here and not
decoration.

### 3.2 Arm L (LEARNED ALTERNATIVE): a trajectory transformer trained on the 449,236 non-Olink
participants, supplying a per-person per-disease history-only risk

**Exact inputs (all in `olink_dta`, all strictly pre-L).** Token sequence per participant,
ordered by first-occurrence date: 3-character ICD-10 codes (`s_41270_*`/`s_41280_*`, ~1,577
vocabulary items observed pre-2011), 3-character ICD-9 codes (`s_41271_*`/`s_41281_*`),
3-character OPCS-4 procedure codes (`s_41272_*`/`s_41282_*`), plus static tokens for sex
(`n_31_0_0`) and age-at-assessment band (`n_21003_0_0`, 2-year bins). Each event token carries a
continuous "years before L" feature (bucketed to 1-year bins, capped at 20) added as a learned
temporal embedding; absolute age at event is **not** constructible (no date of birth and no
assessment date in this namespace) and this limitation is encoded by using *calendar distance to
L* rather than age as the sequence clock.

**Architecture and fitting target.** Decoder-only transformer, 8 layers, d_model 256, 8 heads,
context 128 tokens, ~7 M parameters — the Delphi-2M design idea [K3] scaled to this task. Two
heads, trained jointly:
* *Auxiliary head* (representation learning): next-token prediction over the ~2,500-item code
  vocabulary across the whole pre-L sequence (cross-entropy).
* *Target head* (the quantity actually used): from the final hidden state at L, a discrete-time
  multi-label hazard over the three windows W1/W2/W3 for each of the 27 Tier-1+Tier-2 diseases,
  i.e. 81 logits, trained with masked binary cross-entropy that excludes participants prevalent
  for that disease at L. The output used downstream is
  `S_d,w(i) = logit(P(event of d in window w | pre-L trajectory, sex, age band))`.

**Splits and leakage control.**
* **Participant-disjoint by construction**: trained *only* on the 449,236 `dta` participants who
  are **not** in the Olink file, with an internal 90/5/5 train/val/test split. No Olink
  participant contributes a gradient. Applied frozen to all 53,014 Olink participants.
* **No post-landmark information enters the input**: inputs are filtered by parsed first-date
  `< 2011-01-01`; the parse is unit-tested against the `DDMMMYYYY` format.
* Because the Olink cohort is enriched (Section 0), the transferred score may be miscalibrated.
  It enters the outcome model as a **natural spline (4 df) covariate** (`g_w(H) = ns(S_d,w; 4)`),
  whose coefficients are estimated on Olink outcomes exactly as age's are. Both raw-score and
  spline-adjusted results are reported, plus a calibration-in-the-large statistic on Olink.

**Prespecified adequacy criterion (and fallback).** Arm L is used for the primary redundancy
axis **only if**, in the held-out 5% of non-Olink participants, its pooled time-dependent
C-index across the 27 diseases exceeds that of the best baseline history summary (B2, refit on
the same non-Olink data) by **>= 0.02** with a non-overlapping bootstrap 95% CI, **and** the
pooled C-index is **>= 0.65**. If either fails, B2 carries the redundancy axis, Arm L is reported
as a negative methodological result, and the recorded reason to revisit it is: a larger
vocabulary (4-character codes), longer context, or primary-care data (unavailable here).

**Evaluation of both arms.** Per disease and window: time-dependent AUC and C-index with
participant-level bootstrap (500 reps) CIs; calibration slope/intercept and calibration plots in
deciles; continuous NRI and ΔC-index for "protein added on top of history" vs "history alone",
by horizon; and, as the headline comparative object, the distribution across pairs of
`R = 1 − β^C_W3 / β^A_W3` (redundancy) under Arm B2 vs Arm L. A systematic gap
`R_L > R_B2` quantifies exactly how much "durability" a comorbidity count manufactures.

### 3.3 Which arm carries the primary estimand, and why

* **Axis 1 (horizon profile / prodromality) — primary estimator is Arm B.** The estimand
  (β_W1 − β_W3 and β_W1/β_W3 per pair) is identified by window-specific regression with age, sex
  and a history summary; it does not need a learned representation, and a transparent GLM gives
  calibrated inference across 64,306 pairs with an analytic covariance for the contrast. Adding a
  learned covariate here would buy precision at the cost of interpretability of the main contrast.
* **Axis 2 (redundancy) — primary estimator is Arm L, conditional on the adequacy criterion.**
  Here the learned model is not a performance upgrade, it is the *correct operationalisation of
  the rival hypothesis*: the rival says "the protein re-expresses the record", and the record is
  an ordered, timed, high-cardinality object. Using a count to stand for the record would bias
  the test in favour of my own claim's negation being rejected. Arm B2 is retained as the
  prespecified fallback and is reported alongside in every table.
* **Alternative not chosen, and what would justify revisiting it.** A fully joint
  protein+trajectory multimodal transformer (proteins as additional input tokens, trained
  end-to-end on the Olink subset) was considered and *deferred*: with 53,014 participants and
  as few as 103–553 W1 events per disease, an end-to-end model cannot deliver per-pair effect
  sizes with calibrated uncertainty for 64,306 pairs, which is the actual deliverable. It would
  become worth revisiting if the goal shifted from per-pair triage to a single best-performing
  risk model, or if the Olink sample were >150k.

## 5. Falsification criteria, with numeric thresholds

Let **S1** = pairs with FDR q<0.05 for β_W1 under B1, **S3** = pairs with q<0.05 for β_W3 under
B1 (Tier-1 diseases, both protein tiers).

**Gate G (validity of the triage; evaluated first).** All four must hold:
(i) >=5/7 prodromal positives show β_W1 − β_W3 > 0 at q<0.05;
(ii) >=3/5 durable positives have q<0.05 for β_W3 under the primary redundancy adjustment with
|β_W1 − β_W3| 95% CI containing 0 (D5 excluded from the count if it fails — see 4.2);
(iii) <=1/6 negative controls reach q<0.05 in any window;
(iv) permutation-null significant fraction <= 0.07 against nominal 0.05; V1 R>=0.90; V2 <=5%;
V3 >=20/22; V4 passes.
**If G fails, F1–F3 are reported as uninterpretable and the deliverable is a methods-negative.**

**F1 — Is prodromality a major contributor?**
*Supportive of U*: >= 20% of S1 pairs classified PRODROMAL (β_W1 q<0.05 AND
(β_W1−β_W3)/|β_W1| >= 0.5 AND 95% CI of (β_W1−β_W3) excluding 0).
*Adverse*: < 7%. *Inconclusive*: 7–20%.
Interpretation of adverse: once events within ~2 years of the landmark are removed, protein
associations are horizon-stable — but recall that the unobserved 0.2–4.8 y draw-to-landmark gap
already biases toward this result (Section 2.3), so adverse here must be reported as
"prodromality not detectable **beyond** 2.2–6.8 years post-draw", not as "no prodromality".

**F2 — Is record redundancy a major contributor?**
*Supportive of U*: median R over S3 pairs >= 0.25 under the primary (Arm L) adjustment, with a
bootstrap 95% CI lower bound > 0.15.
*Adverse*: median R < 0.10 with bootstrap 95% CI upper bound < 0.15.
*Inconclusive*: between, or Arm L fails its adequacy criterion and only B2 is available (B2 is
a lower bound on R; a B2-only median R >= 0.25 is still supportive, a B2-only median R < 0.10 is
**not** adverse, because B2 under-adjusts).

**F3 — Does a durable, non-redundant class exist and is it a minority?**
*Supportive of U*: the DURABLE class contains >= 500 pairs spanning >= 200 distinct proteins and
>= 10 diseases, **and** constitutes < 50% of S3.
*Adverse to U (and scientifically interesting)*: DURABLE >= 50% of S3 — i.e. most robust
proteomic associations really are horizon-stable and non-redundant, strengthening the standard
target-triage reading.
*Adverse to the method*: DURABLE < 50 pairs while Gate G passes — the triage is too strict to be
useful; report thresholds' sensitivity curve.

**F4 — Does the learned trajectory recover information the baseline loses?**
*Supportive*: median(R_L − R_B2) over S3 pairs >= 0.05 with bootstrap 95% CI excluding 0, and
pooled held-out C-index gain >= 0.02 (the adequacy criterion). *Adverse*: median(R_L − R_B2)
<= 0.01 — a comorbidity count is a sufficient summary of the record for this purpose, which is
itself a useful negative result (it would say a 7M-parameter sequence model is unnecessary for
proteomic triage, and should be published as such).

### 5.4 Prespecified handling of the "unobserved death" rival to F1

Death is not observable here, so informative censoring cannot be eliminated. Three computable
partial checks, all prespecified:
1. **Record-silence proxy.** Define `silent(i) = 1` if participant *i* has >=1 hospital record
   (any ICD-10/ICD-9/OPCS-4 first date) before 2016 and **no** record at all in 2019-01-01…
   2022-10-31. Estimate, for every protein, the association with `silent` (age/sex-adjusted) →
   a per-protein *attrition score* A_p. `silent` mixes death, emigration and good health, so A_p
   is an upper bound on a mortality signal, not a mortality estimate.
2. **Discriminator.** Regress the per-pair prodromality index (β_W1 − β_W3) on A_p across all S1
   pairs. If A_p explains **>= 40%** of the variance in the prodromality index, the F1 result is
   declared **confounded by attrition** and reported as inconclusive for the prodromal rival.
   If A_p explains **<= 15%**, the prodromality signal is judged not primarily attritional.
3. **Alive-in-late-window sensitivity.** Repeat the W3 analysis restricted to participants with
   >=1 hospital contact after 2018-01-01 (demonstrably still in the system). This conditions on
   the future and is itself biased toward the null for healthy participants; it is reported only
   as a bound, with the direction stated.

### 5.5 Conclusions this island cannot support, under any result

* **Causality / target validity.** Requires cis-pQTL MR (genotypes are in `ukb672073`/
  `ukb671626`, forbidden) or trial evidence. The output is a *prioritisation*, never a target claim.
* **True constitutional stability.** Requires repeat proteomics (not available). "Durable" here
  means "horizon-stable over 2.2–11.8 years post-draw", which is a weaker property.
* **Competing-risk-correct hazards and mortality endpoints.** Require the death registry (40000).
* **Cancer incidence.** Requires 40005/40006; here cancers are hospital-coded only, so true
  incidence dates are later and incomplete, and screen-detected cancers never admitted are missed.
* **Whether a code is a new diagnosis or a carried comorbidity.** Requires clinical adjudication
  or primary-care records; the Parkinson's anomaly (Section 2.5) shows this is not hypothetical.
* **Treatment at baseline.** Medication is invisible here, so a protein lowered or raised by
  treatment (PCSK9 by statins, NT-proBNP by diuretics) cannot be interpreted on the level scale.

## 6. Serious alternative explanations, and how each is handled

1. **UKB-PPP consortium selection.** Measured, not assumed: **[V]** SLE prevalence ratio 80.3
   (Olink vs non-Olink), dementia 3.00, Parkinson 2.76, MS 2.23, CKD 2.14, HF 1.97, AKI 1.65,
   sepsis 1.49, COPD 1.44, against a global burden ratio of 1.12. *Handling*: (a) the prevalence
   ratio is computed for every candidate outcome and published as a cohort-selection table;
   (b) outcomes with PR >= 1.5 (PD, CKD, HF, AKI within Tier 1) are flagged "high-selection" and
   carry a mandatory sensitivity analysis excluding participants with any pre-L code from the
   enriched set {M32, G35, G20, F00–F03/G30, N18, I50}; (c) classification concordance between
   main and sensitivity analyses is reported per flagged disease; (d) selection on *prevalent*
   disease largely conditions out of the incident analysis, but selection induces a
   collider-type path whenever the protein and the selecting disease share causes — stated,
   not claimed solved.
2. **Batch / plate / panel effects.** The panel block (Tier C vs Tier X) is the dominant
   technical structure **[V]** and is handled by tiering rather than imputation. Individual plate
   IDs are not in this namespace; residual plate effects are a stated limitation. Partial
   mitigation: proteins are rank-inverse-normal transformed within tier; a tier indicator enters
   every model; all cross-pair summaries are reported separately by tier so that a tier-specific
   artefact cannot masquerade as biology.
3. **Unobserved death / informative censoring.** The principal rival to any prodromality
   finding; handled by the three checks in Section 5.4 and, failing those, by downgrading F1 to
   inconclusive.
4. **Hospital-only ascertainment.** Outcomes are in-patient ICD-10 only: community-managed
   disease (early T2D, mild asthma, most incident Parkinson's) is captured late or not at all,
   and the "incident" date is an *admission* date, not an onset date. This inflates apparent
   prodromality (onset precedes coding) and makes prevalent/incident misclassification
   systematic. Handled by: the Parkinson's flag; reporting results separately for
   admission-driven (sepsis, pneumonia, AKI, hip fracture) vs elective-surgery-driven
   (cataract, gallstones, hernia, OA) vs chronic-coded (T2D, CKD, COPD) outcome classes.
5. **Protein missingness / LOD.** Verified **[V]** to be dominated by the panel block
   (missing-indicator agreement 0.930–0.970 across high-missing assays), with residual per-assay
   missingness of ~1.6–8% in Tier C. Handled by per-protein complete-case within tier with N
   reported; plus a prespecified check that for the classified pairs the missingness fraction is
   uncorrelated with the prodromality index (Spearman |ρ| < 0.10 required, else flagged).
6. **Age-at-sampling uncertainty.** Age at landmark known only to ±~2.3 y (Section 2.3).
   Handled by 5-df spline on age-at-assessment plus the narrow-age-band sensitivity; residual
   age confounding is stated as irreducible on this island.
7. **Treatment at baseline invisible.** No medication data. This is why D5 (PCSK9) is declared a
   weak control in advance and why no level-based conclusion about a drug target is drawn.
8. **Calendar-time ascertainment shock.** **[V]** first-diagnosis record counts 2019: 541,771;
   2020: 470,562; 2021: 556,543. W3 straddles the COVID-19 disruption. Handled by calendar-year
   strata in the W3 Poisson offset and a sensitivity truncating W3 at 2020-01-01.
9. **Multiplicity and winner's curse.** 64,306 primary pairs; BH-FDR within
   (disease × window × tier); classification thresholds are absolute (on β and R), not rank-based;
   the permutation null calibrates the realised false-discovery fraction; a 50/50
   participant-disjoint split-half replication of the final classification is reported
   (classification agreement κ, prespecified reporting, no threshold gate).

## 10. Unresolved objections I could not settle

1. **Prevalent-vs-incident misclassification cannot be resolved here.** The Parkinson's count
   profile **[V]** shows that hospital-only ascertainment assigns carried community diagnoses to
   early windows. Without primary-care records or adjudication, "PRODROMAL" and "already
   diagnosed elsewhere" remain observationally merged for chronic, community-managed diseases.
2. **Unobserved death.** The record-silence proxy (5.4) can bound but not remove it.
3. **The draw-to-landmark gap (0.2–4.8 y, unobserved)** blunts the prodromality axis in a way I
   cannot correct within this namespace; only its *direction* (conservative) is known.
4. **Whether R_L > R_B2 reflects genuine extra record information or transfer miscalibration**
   from the enriched Olink cohort. The spline adjustment absorbs monotone miscalibration but not
   a change in the *ranking* induced by selection. Partially addressable by refitting Arm L on
   a propensity-reweighted non-Olink sample; not costed here.
5. **No external replication cohort** exists inside this island, and the natural one
   (`ukb671626`/`ukb672073`) is namespace-forbidden. Split-half replication inside Olink is the
   only available substitute and shares all selection artefacts.
