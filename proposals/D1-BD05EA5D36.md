TITLE
Day-28 post-TACE landmark: biochemical readiness, continuous waiting time, and early hepatic recovery after first HCC resection

CLINICAL DECISION, ADVANCE, AND FALSIFIABLE HYPOTHESES
For an adult with documented HCC who received TACE, remains unresected 28 days later, and is still being considered for first liver resection, does operating sooner associate with worse postoperative hepatic recovery, and does a measured biochemical-readiness profile identify when elapsed time itself ceases to matter?

This child repairs two central biases in the parent. First, eligibility is fixed at a day-28 landmark among all recorded TACE recipients, before future resection is known; patients who are not subsequently resected remain in the strategy-denominator rather than silently disappearing. Second, the primary timing exposure is continuous, not a 60-day dichotomy. The actionable dynamic strategy is: from day 29 through day 90, resect at the first observed operation date at which the latest available bilirubin, INR and albumin meet a prespecified, unit-validated readiness rule; otherwise continue waiting through day 90. An immediate practical contrast estimates the 60-day risk of resection for patients who are ready versus not ready at day 28. Among those resected by day 90, a secondary, explicitly selected survivor analysis estimates whether each additional 14 days from TACE to operation is associated with postoperative biochemical recovery after controlling for readiness trajectory and tumor/procedure case mix.

Primary hypothesis H1 (decision process): day-28 readiness is associated with a clinically material increase in subsequent resection by day 90 without an excess of recorded day-29-to-90 hepatic decompensation. H2 (perioperative safety): among resected patients, the adjusted day-5-to-7 bilirubin ratio improves smoothly with longer delay only when day-28-to-preoperative liver reserve has not recovered; after readiness and case-mix adjustment, a residual 60-day discontinuity should be absent. These are observational association hypotheses. They do not establish that forcing surgery, waiting, or a laboratory threshold causes benefit.

Clinical importance and advance: fixed waiting periods can postpone oncologically necessary surgery in recovered patients yet expose unrecovered patients to liver failure. The parent showed computability but conditioned on eventual resection and promoted an arbitrary 60-day comparison. This study asks whether a patient-specific readiness state, not a calendar cut, can organize the decision, while explicitly quantifying attrition before resection. Existing evidence supports only that preoperative TACE is nonrandomly selected and may correlate with morbidity (Na et al., 2025, PMID 41000227, PMCID PMC12457797; full text was inspected by the parent). It does not support an optimal interval or validate this readiness rule. The strongest snapshot-supported claim is that dated TACE, resection, serial liver-reserve tests, imaging reports and postoperative tests make the proposed associations computable. The unresolved claim is whether biochemical readiness modifies the continuous timing-recovery association.

ACTUAL BOUNDED FEASIBILITY AND WHAT IT RULED OUT
The parent read all 105,044 encounter, 1,810,646 diagnosis and 338,040 procedure rows and streamed the full lab source. This child independently reread those sources, streamed all 28,159,928 lab data rows and all 1,736,178 examination data rows, and inspected all 490,077 clinical-document data rows. Real source rows confirmed parseable procedure and laboratory timestamps, exact assay names, and duplicated multi-organ examination text under a shared 检查号.

Using the parent's first-resection/last-prior-TACE reconstruction, exact elapsed time, adult restriction, and interval >28 to <=180 days yielded 826 resected patients: 381 at >28-60, 223 at >60-90, and 222 at >90-180. The one-patient difference from the parent's 224 in the middle interval reflects enforcing adult/index encounter linkage in this rerun and must be reconciled before execution, not hidden. In these arms respectively:
- bilirubin/INR/albumin were all observed during post-TACE days 0-7 and all again during preoperative days -7 to <0 in 271/172/166;
- preoperative bilirubin+INR and postoperative day-5-to-7 bilirubin+INR were complete in 253/154/158;
- acute bilirubin+INR, preoperative bilirubin+INR, and postoperative bilirubin+INR were complete in 206/130/136;
- baseline and postoperative-day-1-to-3 hemoglobin for the negative control were present in 341/204/198;
- a deduplicated imaging study existed within 60 days before operation in 381/221/220, and between TACE and operation in 376/221/220.
At a genuine fixed day-28 landmark, however, late outpatient recovery measurements during days 8-28 were present for bilirubin in only 21/12/18 and all bilirubin/INR/albumin in days 14-28 in only 57 of 1,089 resected-with-prior-TACE patients. Thus a dynamic rule requiring a fresh day-28 laboratory draw is infeasible and is not the primary design. The feasible readiness state uses the latest recorded measurement in days 0-28, with recency modeled and a preoperative-update analysis among resected patients. In the three arms, any day-0-to-28 bilirubin was available in 347/204/205 and INR in 313/184/186. Imaging by day 28 was available in only 116/67/79, so post-landmark imaging cannot be required for primary eligibility.

A broad all-TACE landmark screen found 19,112 patients with an earliest qualifying TACE and 12,746 adult patients with no recorded resection through day 28; 239 had resection on days >28-60, 203 on >60-90, 307 on >90-180, and 11,682 had no subsequent recorded resection. These counts differ from the selected resection cohort because the landmark starts at earliest TACE rather than retrospectively choosing last TACE before surgery. This large nonresected denominator repairs conditioning on eventual resection, but absence of resection is not proof of a deliberate waiting strategy or continued institutional follow-up.

Treatment-intent feasibility is limited but explicit: among 826 resected patients, a deliberately broad lexical screen of TACE-encounter documents found planned/preoperative/conversion wording in 47, rupture/emergency wording in 35, and delay/readiness terms in 388; operation-encounter documents yielded 67, 33 and 572. These are screening counts, not validated intent labels. In the all-TACE landmark population, a separate broad screen identified only 322 with any preoperative/planned/downstaging wording and 517 with rupture/emergency wording. Therefore a clinically interpretable primary analysis must be restricted to dual-reviewed "resection-intended or potentially resectable" patients if adjudication yields enough cases; otherwise the all-TACE strategy analysis is descriptive/inconclusive and the strongest feasible analysis remains the selected-resection continuous association.

POPULATION, LANDMARK, STRATEGIES, AND FOLLOW-UP
Unit: patient; main index is earliest recorded qualifying TACE.
Include: age >=18 in index-TACE encounter; explicit HCC diagnosis in index or a prior encounter; valid TACE time; alive/under observable institutional care and no qualifying resection or transplant through exactly 28 elapsed days. Evidence of alive/observable at day 28 is any encounter, lab, examination, procedure or document-linked encounter spanning or after day 28. Exclude prior recorded liver resection before/through day 28, donor/transplant procedures, impossible timestamps, and a TACE name indicating intraoperative delivery. Ruptured/emergency embolization is excluded from the resection-intended primary cohort but retained as a prespecified separate stratum.

Landmark t0 = index TACE 开始时间 + 28*24 hours. Baseline information ends at t0.
Readiness state at t0 uses the most recent available bilirubin, INR and albumin from [TACE, t0], plus their post-TACE peak/nadir, change from latest pre-TACE value in [-7,0), days since measurement, creatinine, platelets and ALT. Missingness and measurement recency are part of the state, not silently complete-cased. The absolute candidate rule, applied only if units are clinically validated, is bilirubin <=1.20 times its pre-TACE value, INR <=1.10 times pre-TACE (and <1.5), and albumin >=0.90 times pre-TACE, with no coded/document-adjudicated new ascites, encephalopathy, infection or renal deterioration through t0. If units/pre-TACE values cannot be validated, use within-assay log changes and define readiness from a prespecified multivariable continuous score; do not call it clinical readiness.

Strategies from t0:
A. "Resection soon": qualifying resection within the next 32 days (through day 60).
B. "Continue waiting": no resection through day 60; allow resection during days >60-90. Report the >90-180 trajectory separately, not as equivalent care.
For a per-protocol dynamic analysis, clone each eligible, adjudicated patient into: D1, resect within a 14-day grace period after first observed readiness while days 29-90; D2, do not resect through day 60, then resect within 14 days of first readiness through day 90. Censor a clone at strategy deviation and use inverse-probability-of-censoring weights. Because readiness measurements are sparse and clinically triggered, this is secondary and is abandoned if fewer than 100 adherent effective patients per strategy. Primary all-landmark estimand is instead the adjusted 60-day cumulative incidence of first resection by day 90 according to day-28 readiness, with death/outside-care limitations explicit.

Follow until earliest day 90 for strategy uptake/decompensation; resected patients additionally through postoperative day 7 and index discharge. Administrative observation is bounded by 2025-12-31 and source encounters; no assertion of survival after last institutional contact.

OUTCOMES AND ESTIMANDS
1. Primary landmark process outcome: first qualifying resection by day 90. Estimate standardized 60-day risk difference and ratio for ready versus not ready at t0, conditional on adjudicated intent and measured baseline case mix. This tests whether the proposed state corresponds to actual operability; it is not a benefit endpoint.
2. Co-primary safety/process outcome: recorded hepatic decompensation during days 29-90 (new/worsened ascites, encephalopathy, bilirubin/INR deterioration, renal deterioration, urgent admission), using structured labs/diagnoses plus blinded document review. Report jointly with resection because a rule that merely selects healthier patients is not validated as beneficial.
3. Perioperative primary among patients actually resected by day 180: log(maximum total bilirubin on postoperative days 5-7 / latest preoperative bilirubin in [-7,0)). Main exposure is continuous exact days from last preoperative TACE to operation, modeled per 14 days with restricted cubic splines at empirical 10th/35th/65th/90th percentiles. There is no primary 60-day coefficient.
4. Key secondary: maximum day-5-to-7 INR minus baseline; joint bilirubin and INR worsening; albumin change; postoperative days 1-7 trajectories; LOS. True ISGLS PHLF and grade, major complications, ICU escalation, reoperation and in-hospital death require blinded clinical adjudication. The previously observed two stringent bilirubin>50 plus INR>1.5 events make that binary proxy exploratory only.

The landmark estimand applies to recorded, adjudicated potentially resectable patients under institutional observation at day 28. The perioperative estimand remains a selected-resection association and is explicitly not transported to everyone receiving TACE.

CONFOUNDING, CASE MIX, AND ANALYSIS
Measured baseline confounders at t0: age, sex, calendar year, department, number/prior timing of TACE and other recorded HCC treatments, TACE source/name, pre-TACE and days-0-to-28 bilirubin/INR/albumin/creatinine/platelets/ALT/hemoglobin levels and trajectories, measurement recency/intensity, prior cirrhosis, portal hypertension, ascites, viral hepatitis, diabetes, renal disease, infection, rupture/emergency presentation, admissions and LOS history. Tumor covariates from reports available by t0 are maximum diameter, multiplicity, macrovascular invasion, extrahepatic disease, rupture, ascites/cirrhosis and response; future reports cannot enter the landmark propensity model. For the resected analysis, use the last preoperative report and adjudicated resection extent (minor/major, anatomic/nonanatomic, open/laparoscopic) as case-mix variables, while labeling operation extent as a mediator/procedure-selection variable in sensitivity analyses rather than pretending it is baseline at day 28.

Unmeasured or unavailable confounding includes BCLC/ECOG, Child-Pugh/ALBI unless units validated, ICG clearance, future-liver-remnant volume, raw images/mRECIST, surgeon/MDT preference, technical TACE selectivity, explicit operative scheduling, response prompting surgery, blood loss/transfusion/anesthesia detail, socioeconomic access, outside TACE/resection/death and reasons for loss to follow-up. These preclude causal recommendations.

Fit outcome regressions plus overlap weighting for day-28 readiness; use flexible missingness/recency indicators and multiple imputation for covariates, never impute postoperative outcomes. Compare with conventional regression and entropy balancing. For continuous timing among resected patients, report unadjusted spline, readiness-adjusted spline, then imaging/resection-case-mix-adjusted spline; bootstrap patients for 95% CIs. Estimate readiness-by-time interaction, but require at least 100 effective observations in each readiness stratum. Report standardized differences, weight distributions, overlap effective sample sizes and outcome missingness by timing/readiness. Selection repair: model probability of observed resection by day 180 among all day-28-eligible, intent-adjudicated patients and present inverse-odds-of-selection weighted perioperative sensitivity; state that it cannot recover outcomes for nonresected patients or remove unmeasured progression/death selection.

Tumor extraction must be validated in >=200 stratified, dual-reviewed reports. If PPV or sensitivity for essential diameter/multiplicity/macrovascular-invasion labels is <0.90, automated labels cannot support the main adjusted clinical interpretation; either fully adjudicate or call residual case-mix confounding inconclusive.

NEGATIVE CONTROLS AND FALSIFICATION
1. Surgical-complexity negative-control outcome: postoperative day-1-to-3 hemoglobin nadir/change, adjusted for baseline hemoglobin and adjudicated operation type. Timing/readiness should not directly reduce operative blood loss. An absolute standardized timing effect >=0.20 or p<0.01 indicates residual surgical selection and invalidates a hepatic-safety interpretation.
2. Preoperative pseudo-outcome: apply the postoperative-ratio construction to bilirubin and INR entirely before operation (-7 to -4 versus -3 to <0). An adjusted geometric-mean ratio outside 0.90-1.10 with CI excluding 1, or same-direction standardized effect >=0.20, indicates readiness-trend confounding and downgrades H2.
3. Negative-control exposure: day of week of TACE should not predict day-5-to-7 hepatic recovery after calendar year/service adjustment. A standardized effect >=0.20 or omnibus p<0.01 flags scheduling/service confounding.
4. Landmark falsification: day-28 readiness must not "predict" any event occurring before day 28 after the features' own timestamps. Any leakage is a computational failure. Repeat landmarks at days 21 and 35; a clinically interpretable continuous pattern should not appear only at day 28.
5. Threshold falsification: report 55-, 60- and 65-day cuts only as displays. An isolated significant 60-day contrast with no coherent spline and no readiness interaction is evidence against the calendar-threshold hypothesis.
6. Assay/calendar checks: routine-only assays; 2018-2025; paired routine/急 distributions; winsorization-free robust models. Material discontinuity or implausible INR/bilirubin values triggers assay adjudication.
7. Positivity/missingness stop rules: call strategy comparison inconclusive if either arm has overlap-weighted ESS <100, >10% of total weight is carried by <=10 patients, readiness lab completeness differs by >=20 percentage points after adjustment, or observation through day 90 cannot be distinguished from loss to institutional care.

PRESPECIFIED INTERPRETATION BRANCHES
Supportive for a readiness-guided prospective protocol: (a) ready-at-day-28 patients have >=10 percentage-point higher standardized resection-by-day-90 probability; (b) no >=5 percentage-point higher recorded day-29-to-90 decompensation risk; (c) among resected patients, delay beyond readiness has a bilirubin geometric-mean ratio per 14 days between 0.90 and 1.10 with CI excluding benefits larger than 10%, whereas patients not yet ready show an improving continuous trend (ratio <=0.90 per 14 days, CI excluding 1); (d) INR agrees; and (e) negative controls, overlap, intent and imaging adjudication pass. This supports testing a readiness-triggered schedule prospectively, not implementing it or claiming waiting/early surgery is causal.

Adverse/opposite: readiness does not predict resection, predicts >=5 percentage-point more decompensation, or longer waiting after apparent readiness is associated with better bilirubin/INR recovery (ratio <=0.90 per 14 days with CI excluding 1). This rejects the proposed rule and suggests it omits clinically important recovery or case mix; do not accelerate surgery.

Precise null: timing spline and readiness interaction CIs lie within 0.90-1.10, INR concurs and controls pass. This argues against a clinically material biochemical timing association in observed resected patients; it does not prove equal tumor control or safety in all TACE recipients.

Inconclusive/falsified: intent cohort too small; day-28 follow-up not verifiable; units fail; ESS/positivity stop rule; tumor extraction invalid; negative control fails; bilirubin and INR conflict; CIs include both benefit and harm thresholds; or selection weights are unstable. Report feasibility and selection mechanisms without a timing recommendation.

TREATMENT INTENT, ADJUDICATION, AND CLAIM LIMITS
Two reviewers (hepatobiliary surgeon and interventional radiologist, blinded to postoperative laboratory outcome) must classify index TACE as planned preoperative/neoadjuvant, conversion/downstaging with possible resection, bridge/definitive/palliative, emergency hemostatic for rupture, or indeterminate; record documentary evidence and whether the patient remained potentially resectable at day 28. Resolve disagreement by a third reviewer and report kappa. They also validate TACE/resection lexicons, day-28 decompensation, tumor burden/response and operation extent. Two blinded clinicians adjudicate ISGLS PHLF/grade and major complications in resection encounters. Sparse true PHLF events mean adjudicated PHLF is descriptive/exploratory, not the modeled primary endpoint.

Computational verification can establish row counts, timestamps, joins, no look-ahead, lab windows, readiness calculations, strategy assignment, spline/weighting implementation, uncertainty and whether reported conclusions match outputs. It cannot establish treatment intent, potential resectability, true PHLF grade, raw-imaging response, complete follow-up, causal safety, or a clinical scheduling recommendation. Those require expert adjudication, missing evidence and a prospective protocol.

WHAT CHANGED FROM THE PARENT AND WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAIN
Changed: fixed eligibility at day 28 before future resection; retained nonresected patients in the denominator; replaced the arbitrary binary primary exposure with continuous timing and a readiness interaction; made a dynamic strategy secondary because real late-day-28 labs are sparse; added exact readiness/imaging/intent counts, selection weighting, operation-case-mix handling, a negative-control exposure, stricter leakage/positivity rules and explicit process/decompensation branches. Preserved: exact source bindings, elapsed-time procedure definitions, paired day-5-to-7 bilirubin/INR outcome, sparse-PHLF caution and observational claim limits. Remaining uncertainties are severe: only a small minority has explicit preoperative intent wording, day-8-to-28 labs are uncommon, follow-up and outside care/death are incomplete, lab units are unverified, and clinician judgment/FLR/ICG/blood loss are absent. Therefore the all-TACE target-trial component may be infeasible after adjudication; if so, the prespecified strongest feasible repair is the continuous, readiness-adjusted association in the 826 observed resected patients, transparently labeled selected and noncausal.
