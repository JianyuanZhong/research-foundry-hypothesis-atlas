# Persistent Deep Sedation After Hemodynamic Stabilization Delays Liberation from Mechanical Ventilation in MIMIC-IV

## Scientific Question

In mechanically ventilated ICU patients who have achieved hemodynamic stabilization, does CONTINUED DEEP SEDATION (RASS ≤ -4) after stabilization independently delay successful liberation from mechanical ventilation compared with lighter sedation (RASS ≥ -1), after adjusting for illness severity and indication for deep sedation?

The key unresolved claim is the **POST-stabilization window**: sedation given before stabilization is expected and often necessary; the question is whether keeping patients deeply sedated AFTER they no longer need it is independently harmful to weaning.

## Clinical Importance

- Mechanical ventilation affects ~33% of ICU admissions; prolonged ventilation is associated with increased VAP, ICU stay, and mortality.
- Deep sedation (RASS ≤ -4) is common in the early ICU course but is often continued beyond the period of hemodynamic instability.
- The Society of Critical Care Medicine recommends minimizing benzodiazepine use due to evidence of prolonged intubation [K2].
- If continued deep sedation after stabilization independently delays weaning, this identifies a modifiable target: structured sedation weaning protocols triggered by hemodynamic stabilization.
- The question is clinically consequential because it distinguishes between sedation that is necessary (for instability) and sedation that is iatrogenic (continued after stabilization).

## Strongest Existing Evidence

1. **Sedation depth and ventilation duration**: Li et al. (2026) [K1] showed in a retrospective cohort that lower deep sedation exposure (RASS ≤ -3) was associated with shorter mechanical ventilation duration (adjusted ratio 0.79, 95% CI 0.67-0.93) and shorter ICU stay. However, this study did not isolate the post-stabilization window; deep sedation exposure was measured over the entire ventilation period, and the study was small (n=102) with limited adjustment.

2. **Sedation interruption protocols**: Schweickert & Kress (2008) [K2] reviewed two categories of sedation protocols — patient-targeted sedation and daily interruption of continuous sedative infusions — both of which have achieved clinical success in decreasing duration of mechanical ventilation and ICU length of stay by minimizing drug accumulation. The key insight is that the goal is "a patient who is awake and can be readily examined," and that daily interruption focuses care providers on "achieving a period of awakening in the earliest phases of critical illness possible." This supports the hypothesis that continued deep sedation beyond the point of need is harmful, but does not quantify the effect of the POST-stabilization window specifically.

3. **Confounding by indication in sedation studies**: Ding et al. (2026) [K3] conducted a time-resolved 24-hour landmark cohort study of RASS-defined examinability after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage. They explicitly note that "the findings remain vulnerable to confounding by indication and do not establish a drug-specific causal effect or downstream clinical benefit." This is the central methodological challenge for our study: patients who remain deeply sedated after stabilization may be doing so for a reason (e.g., neurological injury, refractory agitation, elevated ICP), and the observed association with delayed weaning may reflect the underlying condition rather than the sedation itself.

## Unresolved Claim

The specific claim that **continued deep sedation AFTER hemodynamic stabilization** (not before, not overall) independently delays liberation from mechanical ventilation has not been tested in a large, data-resolvable cohort. Existing studies either:
- Measure deep sedation exposure over the entire ventilation period (not isolating the post-stabilization window) [K1],
- Are protocol-based RCTs that do not allow estimation of the naturalistic effect of continued deep sedation [K2],
- Or are small, single-center studies that explicitly acknowledge confounding by indication without adequately addressing it [K3].

## Hypothesis

In mechanically ventilated ICU patients who have achieved hemodynamic stabilization, continued deep sedation (RASS ≤ -4 for ≥24h after stabilization) is independently associated with delayed liberation from mechanical ventilation (longer time to extubation) and increased in-hospital mortality, compared with lighter sedation (RASS ≥ -1), after adjusting for illness severity, indication for deep sedation (wake-up deferral reasons), and sedative dose.

## Study Design

### Population

Adult (age ≥ 18) ICU stays in MIMIC-IV 3.1 with:
- ≥ 48 hours of mechanical ventilation (Vent Mode itemid 223849 present in chartevents)
- ≥ 1 RASS assessment (itemid 223753)
- ≥ 1 vasopressor input event (to define stabilization)

### Time Zero (Stabilization)

The first time point at which BOTH of the following are met:
1. **Off all vasopressors**: no active vasopressor infusion (norepinephrine 221906, dopamine 221662, epinephrine 221289, vasopressin 222315, phenylephrine 221749 in inputevents) for ≥ 6 hours, OR norepinephrine-equivalent rate < 0.25 mcg/kg/min (using rate and patientweight from inputevents).
2. **MAP ≥ 65 mmHg**: mean arterial pressure (itemid 220052) ≥ 65 mmHg sustained for ≥ 6 hours.

Time zero is the first time point where both conditions are simultaneously satisfied. Patients who never achieve stabilization are excluded from the primary analysis (reported descriptively).

### Exposure (Post-Stabilization)

After time zero, patients are classified based on their sedation status in the 24-hour window following stabilization:
- **DEEP SEDATION**: RASS ≤ -4 (i.e., "Very Sedated" or "Unarousable" in the text value) for ≥ 24 hours after time zero.
- **LIGHTER SEDATION**: RASS ≥ -1 (i.e., "Calm/Cooperative" or "Sedated") for ≥ 24 hours after time zero.
- **INDETERMINATE**: patients who do not meet either criterion (e.g., mixed RASS values, insufficient RASS data) are excluded from the primary analysis but reported descriptively.

RASS text-to-numeric mapping:
- Calm/Cooperative = 0
- Sedated = -1
- Very Sedated = -4 (deep)
- Unarousable = -5 (deep)
- Agitated = +1 to +4
- Very Agitated = +4
- Danger Agitation = +5

### Primary Outcome

**Time to successful liberation from mechanical ventilation**: time from time zero to first extubation (itemid 227194 in procedureevents) after time zero. Patients who are not extubated during the ICU stay are censored at ICU discharge (outtime in icustays).

### Secondary Outcomes

1. **Ventilator-free days** (VFD) in the first 28 days of ICU stay: number of days alive and not mechanically ventilated.
2. **ICU length of stay**: icustays.los (days).
3. **In-hospital mortality**: hospital_expire_flag in admissions, or deathtime before discharge.

### Covariates

1. **Demographics**: age (anchor_age in patients), sex (gender in patients).
2. **Illness severity**:
   - Primary: SOFA-style proxy computed from labevents (lactate 50813/52442/53154, platelets, bilirubin, creatinine 50912/52546) and chartevents (MAP 220052, FiO2 223835).
   - Secondary: APACHE II (itemid 226743) where available (sparse; reported descriptively).
3. **Time-zero physiology**: FiO2 (223835), PEEP (220339), HR (220045) at time zero.
4. **Indication for deep sedation**: Daily Wake Up Deferred (itemid 223780) reasons in the 24-hour window after time zero. Key reasons: "Hemodynamic Instability," "Unstable Vent Status," "Neuromusc Block," "Unstable Airway," "Elevated ICP," "CMO," "OR/Procedure." These are used as adjustment variables and for stratified analysis.
5. **Sedative dose**: total amount of propofol (222168), midazolam (221668), lorazepam (221385), dexmedetomidine (225150/229420) in the 24-hour window after time zero (from inputevents.amount).

### Analysis Plan

1. **Descriptive**: baseline characteristics by exposure group (deep vs lighter), with standardized mean differences.
2. **Primary analysis**: Cox proportional hazards model for time to liberation, deep vs lighter, adjusted for age, sex, SOFA proxy, time-zero FiO2/PEEP, wake-up deferral reasons, and sedative dose. Report hazard ratio (HR) with 95% CI.
3. **Secondary analyses**:
   - Logistic regression for in-hospital mortality.
   - Linear regression for ICU LOS.
   - VFD analysis.
4. **Sensitivity analyses**:
   - Vary deep sedation threshold: RASS ≤ -4 vs RASS ≤ -3.
   - Vary stabilization definition: off vasopressors only (no MAP criterion) vs MAP only (no vasopressor criterion).
   - Restrict to patients with ≥ 48 hours of RASS data after time zero.
   - Stratify by wake-up deferral reason (e.g., "Hemodynamic Instability" vs "Neuromusc Block" vs none).
   - Propensity score matching on baseline covariates.
   - Inverse probability weighting.

### Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis is **falsified** if:
1. The adjusted HR for time to liberation (deep vs lighter, post-stabilization) is within the prespecified null band (HR 0.8-1.25, 95% CI crossing 1.0), AND
2. The effect is explained by wake-up deferral reasons / illness severity (i.e., the HR moves toward 1.0 after adding these covariates, with the adjusted HR within the null band).

If the HR is outside the null band (e.g., HR > 1.25 or HR < 0.8), the hypothesis is **supported** (if HR > 1.25, deep sedation delays liberation) or **refuted in the opposite direction** (if HR < 0.8, deep sedation is associated with faster liberation, which would be unexpected and require investigation).

If the HR is within the null band but the effect is NOT explained by wake-up deferral/severity (i.e., the HR remains within the null band even after adjustment, and the unadjusted HR is also within the null band), the result is **inconclusive** — the data do not support a meaningful association, but the study may be underpowered.

## Method Comparison

### Baseline: Fixed-Timepoint Sedation Status

A simple cross-sectional comparison of RASS at a single fixed time point (e.g., 24 hours after ICU admission) between patients who are ultimately extubated vs not. This is the "naive" approach that many retrospective studies use.

**Limitation**: This approach does not isolate the post-stabilization window. A patient who is deeply sedated at 24 hours may be doing so because they are hemodynamically unstable (expected) or because they are being kept sedated unnecessarily (iatrogenic). The fixed-timepoint approach conflates these two scenarios.

### Alternative: Time-Varying Exposure from Stabilization

Our proposed approach defines time zero as the point of hemodynamic stabilization and measures sedation depth in the 24-hour window AFTER stabilization. This isolates the post-stabilization window, which is the period where deep sedation is most likely to be iatrogenic rather than necessary.

**What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses**:
- The **timing/indication distinction**: by anchoring to stabilization, we separate sedation that was necessary (before stabilization) from sedation that may be unnecessary (after stabilization).
- The **post-stabilization window**: the specific period where the hypothesis is most testable. If deep sedation after stabilization is harmful, the effect should be concentrated in this window.
- **Confounding by indication**: by adjusting for wake-up deferral reasons (which capture the clinical justification for continued sedation), we can test whether the association between deep sedation and delayed weaning persists after accounting for the reasons clinicians give for keeping patients sedated.

### Why the Alternative Is Scientifically Substantive

The baseline (fixed-timepoint) approach would likely show an association between deep sedation and delayed weaning, but this association would be confounded by the fact that patients who are deeply sedated at a fixed time point are often more ill or have more indications for sedation. The alternative (time-varying, post-stabilization) approach is more likely to isolate the causal effect of continued deep sedation, because it conditions on the point at which the patient no longer needs deep sedation for hemodynamic reasons.

## Limitations

1. **Confounding by indication**: Even with adjustment for wake-up deferral reasons, residual confounding is possible. Patients who remain deeply sedated after stabilization may have unmeasured indications (e.g., subtle neurological injury, refractory pain) that are not captured in the structured data.
2. **Single-center data**: MIMIC-IV is a single-center dataset (Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center). Generalizability to other ICUs is uncertain.
3. **RASS measurement variability**: RASS is a subjective assessment that varies by clinician. The text values in MIMIC-IV are not always mapped to a consistent numeric scale.
4. **No measured GFR or organ function**: The SOFA proxy is an approximation; true organ function is not measured.
5. **Selection bias**: Patients who are extubated early may be different from those who are not, independent of sedation depth.
6. **APACHE II sparsity**: APACHE II is only available for a subset of patients; the SOFA proxy is used as the primary severity measure.
7. **No EEG or other objective sedation measures**: Sedation depth is measured by RASS only; there is no objective measure of brain activity.

## Falsifiable Test Summary

- **Hypothesis**: Continued deep sedation (RASS ≤ -4) after hemodynamic stabilization independently delays liberation from mechanical ventilation.
- **Test**: Cox PH model for time to extubation after stabilization, deep vs lighter, adjusted for severity and indication.
- **Falsification**: Adjusted HR within 0.8-1.25 AND effect explained by wake-up deferral/severity.
- **Supportive result**: Adjusted HR > 1.25 (deep sedation delays liberation).
