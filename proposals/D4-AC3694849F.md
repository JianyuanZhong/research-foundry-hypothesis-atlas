# Does posterior-thigh MFI add to muscle distribution for later falls-report association?

## Question and clinical significance

Among UK Biobank `ukb672073` participants with visit-2 body MRI and a later valid visit-3 falls response, is higher visit-2 bilateral posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) associated with any fall reported for the year before visit 3, beyond visceral adiposity, muscle-volume distribution, prior falls, and measured function? The fixed primary hypothesis is a positive conditional association per 1-SD higher posterior-thigh MFI among selected participants with date-ordered visits separated by at least 366 days.

Falls can cause injury, fracture, disability, and loss of independence. An association surviving this prespecified set of measured comparators would justify external validation of MRI muscle-quality phenotyping; a precise near-null would lower its priority. The clinically consequential unresolved point is whether MFI carries respondent-conditional prospective information beyond already-published thigh distribution signals, not whether this observational study proves causality or supports screening/treatment.

## Evidence, hypothesis, and rival

**Strongest claims currently supported:** K1 reports broad UKB thigh-MFI associations with mortality and incident disease [K1]; it does not establish falls-specific prospective association. K2 reports cross-sectional thigh muscle distribution/P:A ratio association with falls [K2], so a broad MRI-muscle/falls novelty claim is not appropriate. K3 explicitly leaves aging versus disease/disuse explanations unresolved and notes regional heterogeneity [K3]. Prior campaign's aggregate-only screen recorded 4,415 complete cases with 707 coded fall responses and 3,708 coded no-fall responses after the date and covariate gates (candidate `[prior hypothesis]`). These counts are a prior feasibility computation, not an association estimate, independent validation, or clinical adjudication. No model outcome estimate is available.

**Unresolved hypothesis:** in the fixed complete-case population below, posterior-thigh MFI has a positive adjusted association with later any-fall self-report, beyond VAT, body composition/distribution, age, sex, activity, grip, pace, smoking, and prior falls. **Strongest rival:** MFI is a marker of age, obesity, inactivity/disuse, illness, pre-existing frailty/prior falls, or selection into imaging and follow-up; shared recall/measurement error or return-visit selection creates or distorts the association. Temporal separation does not prove the MFI change preceded falls, and adjustment does not remove unmeasured confounding. These data can compare the association's shape and concentration across measured frailty strata, but cannot distinguish those rival mechanisms or validate event truth.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support requires a positive adjusted MFI association with uncertainty excluding the prespecified null range, stable direction/shape across baseline and shrinkage-GAM analyses, adequate event support, and no result driven only by sparse interactions or a poorly calibrated split. It supports only a conditional association with a later reported response among selected complete cases and motivates independent prospective validation.

An adverse result is a sufficiently precise near-null or opposite association across the baseline and alternative; it weakens the hypothesis that MFI adds a report-level signal beyond measured muscle distribution and frailty proxies and lowers external-validation priority. It does not prove no effect or equivalence unless a clinically meaningful margin is prespecified and excluded.

An inconclusive result includes wide intervals, sparse outcome strata, unstable GAM/selection weights, failed diagnostics, or unresolved coding semantics. It means the available evidence cannot resolve the association; it is not confirmation or refutation.
