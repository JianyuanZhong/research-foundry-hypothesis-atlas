# UKB17 repair: posterior-thigh MFI and later falls report

## Question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank `ukb672073` participants with body MRI at imaging visit 2 and a valid falls response at visit 3, does higher bilateral posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) at visit 2 have a positive adjusted association with a later report of falls, beyond visceral adiposity, thigh-muscle distribution, and measured baseline frailty/function? Require visits in date order and at least 366 days apart; the response asks about the year before visit 3. Falls can cause injury, disability, and loss of independence. A stable incremental signal would justify prospective validation of muscle-quality phenotyping; a precise adverse/null result would lower its priority. This is not a causal or treatment-effect hypothesis.

**What evidence already supports.** The UKB thigh-MFI work reports broad mortality/incident-disease associations, not this lagged falls-report comparison [K1]. The closest overlap reports cross-sectional falls/function associations for thigh muscle distribution [K2], so a general MRI-thigh/falls novelty claim is not justified. A myosteatosis review leaves aging versus disease/disuse explanations open [K3]. These inspected abstracts bound the question; they do not demonstrate this hypothesis. Prior campaign work reported an aggregate complete-case screen (4,415 participants, 707 coded positive, 3,708 coded negative), but it is inherited feasibility evidence only—not a result of this episode, an association estimate, external validation, or clinical adjudication.

**Hypothesis under test.** Among the fixed complete-case selected returnees below, 1-SD higher visit-2 bilateral posterior-thigh MFI is positively associated with any visit-3 falls response after the prespecified adjustment set. **Strongest rival.** MFI may mark age, adiposity, inactivity/disuse, pre-existing frailty/illness or prior falls; recall/reporting error and selective MRI/follow-up could also create or distort the pattern. Baseline adjustment, measured-frailty interactions, and temporal ordering can test whether the result is robust to measured proxies and timing, but cannot eliminate unmeasured confounding, reverse causation, or selection bias. Neither association nor predictive improvement identifies a mechanism.
