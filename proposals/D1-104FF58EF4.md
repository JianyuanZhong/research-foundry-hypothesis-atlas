# Joint-assay observation audit for frozen forward P-beyond-B repeat-TACE HCC validation

## Targeted successor and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`, not a new estimand. The parent correctly repaired outcome-blind support and assay-specific Y-observation selection, but it still assessed observation separately by assay. That leaves an important ambiguity: a favorable albumin result and a favorable bilirubin result may appear to represent two corroborating outcomes while actually being available on different selected event subsets. Conversely, complete-case loss could reflect a missing panel rather than assay-specific outcome selection. In this HCC source, the distinction is computable and clinically consequential for interpreting whether the two laboratory signals are concordant evidence or merely selected fragments.

The repair adds an event-level **joint-assay observation audit**. After constructing the same outcome-blind B/P risk set for each assay, it inner-joins the two assay risk sets on patient and exact selected repeat-event fields. It reports four mutually exclusive post-event observation patterns among events with pre-Y support for both assays: both assays observed, albumin only, bilirubin only, and neither. This is a diagnostic layer only. It does not replace the assay-specific complete-triplet cohorts, pool laboratory values, impute Y, reweight the RMSE, alter the model, or change the estimand. It distinguishes three interpretations that the parent’s separate fractions cannot fully distinguish: (i) supportive concordance when both assays remain well observed in an adequately supported block; (ii) adverse observation discordance or joint observation collapse, which undermines claims of corroboration even if one assay’s complete cases look favorable; and (iii) inconclusive evidence when joint support or arm support is sparse.

The strongest existing evidence remains narrow: the locked assay-specific forward comparison supports incremental short-horizon prediction only in the adequately supported 2022 calendar block, and the parent’s 2023/2024 audit demonstrates sparse systemic support and severe Y-observation loss. The new unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable: **within outcome-blind-supported selected repeat events, are the albumin and total-bilirubin endpoints jointly observed often enough, and with sufficiently balanced arm composition, to interpret their separate 2022 gains as concordant evidence rather than outcome-specific selection?** No clinical outcome, treatment response, safety, utility, or causal claim is made.

## Joint observation diagnostic

For every selected repeat event, create one row per assay from the inherited risk set, then inner-join albumin and bilirubin rows on (`patient master index`, `arm`, `event_day`, `event_date`). This exact event join is required to keep the unit an event and avoid multiplying records. Before reading Y, define `joint_BP`. Among joint-BP events define:

- `both`: valid post-event Y under both exact assay-specific Y clocks;
- `albumin_only`: albumin Y observed and bilirubin Y not observed;
- `bilirubin_only`: bilirubin Y observed and albumin Y not observed;
- `neither`: neither assay Y observed.

Report total and systemic/comparator counts for training and future blocks, Wilson 95% intervals for the joint observation fraction (`both / joint_BP`), and the four pattern counts. Also report the same joint-BP support gate as a context gate: at least 20 joint-BP events total and at least 10 in each descriptive arm in both pre-origin training and future test. This is stricter than the assay-specific gate and is not an exclusion or redefinition of the assay-specific estimand.

Interpretation thresholds are prespecified and descriptive. Flag joint observation below 80%, any albumin-only or bilirubin-only pattern, any arm with fewer than 10 joint-BP events, and any future block failing the joint support gate. A block can be supportive for concordant observation only if the inherited reconstruction and assay-specific support gates pass, joint-BP support also passes, joint observation is at least 80% without observation-pattern discordance, and the assay-specific RMSE criteria are met. A favorable assay-specific estimate with joint observation failure is not concordant multi-assay evidence. Do not call a single assay-only pattern a biological disagreement; it is missingness/observation discordance.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive branches

**Supportive.** A future block is supportive only if the frozen reconstruction and clocks pass; both assay-specific and joint outcome-blind support gates pass; both assay-specific RMSE gains meet the locked interval/estimand criteria; joint observation is at least 80%; and no material assay-only observation pattern or arm-specific observation collapse is present. Such a result supports concordant incremental short-horizon prediction in that selected calendar mixture only. The executed 2022 block meets the joint support/observation conditions and is therefore compatible with the parent’s cutoff-specific supportive claim, but it does not establish clinical utility, causal benefit, or stability beyond 2022.

**Adverse.** A gated assay-specific interval at or below zero, repeated gated M1 degradation, or a sufficiently supported block with marked arm-specific worsening challenges incremental prediction in the selected observed population. A joint-observation adverse result is a high rate of neither/assay-only observation or a material arm imbalance despite pre-Y support; it defeats the claim of concordant two-assay corroboration even when one complete-triplet RMSE is favorable. It does not prove B alone is sufficient, a biological mechanism, or a treatment effect.

**Inconclusive.** Sparse systemic or total joint-BP support, joint observation below 80%, assay-only observation, influential episodes, calendar extrapolation, denominator/clock mismatch, or missing assay units makes the block descriptive. The executed 2023 and 2024 blocks are inconclusive for transport; 2024 is especially non-informative because only one systemic event has both assay outcomes. Inconclusive does not mean absence of prediction.
