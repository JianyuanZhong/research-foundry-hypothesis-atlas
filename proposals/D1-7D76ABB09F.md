# Is the residual post-arrival risk difference between transferred and directly admitted eICU patients concentrated in high arrival-severity subgroups?

## Scientific opening

The parent analysis asks whether arrival organ injury explains the crude post-arrival risk difference between interhospital-transferred and directly admitted eICU patients. That question is clinically important, but it leaves open a consequential refinement: if a residual post-arrival risk difference remains after adjustment for arrival state, is it diffuse across patients or concentrated among those with severe organ injury at arrival? This distinction changes the likely target of improvement. A residual difference confined to high-severity arrivals would suggest that severity assessment, transfer selection, or post-arrival monitoring for the sickest patients is the priority. A residual difference that is similar across severity strata would suggest a broader post-arrival process or an unmeasured selection effect and would not justify a severity-targeted intervention.

The available eICU CRD data do not contain the pre-arrival clinical course, transfer reason, transfer timing, or source-hospital treatment. Therefore this study cannot determine whether transfer caused the observed difference. It can only test whether the association between admission source and post-arrival outcome is modified by arrival-severity indicators, after adjustment for those indicators and hospital context.

## Hypothesis

Among adult eICU ICU stays with a valid arrival timestamp and an explicit admission-source label, the post-arrival risk difference between interhospital-transferred and directly admitted patients, after adjustment for arrival organ injury and hospital context, is larger in a prespecified high arrival-severity stratum than in lower-severity strata. The difference is not interpreted as caused by transfer.

The competing explanations are:

1. **Severity-concentrated association:** the residual difference is strongest when patients already have severe organ injury at arrival, implying that the observed association may reflect a selection or severity process that is not fully captured by the available arrival indicators.
2. **Broad residual association:** the adjusted difference is similar across severity strata, implying that arrival severity does not localize the association and that other unmeasured factors or post-arrival processes may be important.
3. **No stable residual difference:** the adjusted difference is small or unstable, supporting the parent hypothesis that arrival-state adjustment largely explains the crude difference.

These explanations make different observable predictions in the severity-stratified risk estimates and in the transfer-by-severity interaction.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use only the published eICU CRD source files described in `datasets/eicu/README.md` and the corresponding table metadata. Do not infer pre-arrival data.

### Cohort

- Start from `datasets/eicu/data/patient.csv.gz`, with `patientId`, `stayId`, `age`, `gender`, and the documented admission-source field(s).
- Require a valid `patientStayId` in `datasets/eicu/data/hospital.csv.gz`, which provides hospital-level context and disposition fields.
- Restrict to adult stays (`age >= 18`).
- Require an explicit admission-source label that distinguishes interhospital transfer from direct admission. If the exact field is not present or is incomplete, report label coverage and do not infer transfer from diagnoses, notes, or hospital location.
- Require a valid first available post-ICU-record timestamp from the earliest timestamp in `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`, `lab.csv.gz`, `nurseCharting.csv.gz`, or `respiratoryCharting.csv.gz` for that stay.
- Exclude stays with impossible timestamps, fewer than two distinct post-arrival observations, or an arrival timestamp after the outcome window.

### Arrival-time window

Define arrival as the first timestamp in the ICU stay as above. Arrival-state covariates are values observed at or before this timestamp, or within a prespecified 0–2 hour post-arrival observation window when a value is not available exactly at arrival. Values observed after this window are not used as arrival covariates.

### Post-arrival outcome window

Primary outcome: in-ICU death during the fixed 24-hour post-arrival window, using disposition and timing fields in `patient.csv.gz` and `hospital.csv.gz` joined by `patientStayId`.

Secondary outcomes: new or worsening organ injury within 24 hours, defined from creatinine, lactate, platelets, bilirubin, respiratory support, vasopressor infusion, and urine output where available in `lab.csv.gz`, `intakeOutput.csv.gz`, `infusionDrug.csv.gz`, `respiratoryCare.csv.gz`, and `respiratoryCharting.csv.gz`.

If the 24-hour outcome is sparse, prespecify a 12-hour window as a sensitivity analysis, not a post hoc replacement.

## Exposure and outcome definitions

**Exposure:** admission source classified as interhospital transfer versus direct admission, using the explicit source field(s) in `patient.csv.gz` and `hospital.csv.gz`. Preserve the raw label and report coverage. Do not treat this as a causal exposure.

**Primary outcome:** in-ICU death within 24 hours of arrival.

**Secondary outcomes:** new or worsening organ injury within 24 hours, using the laboratory, respiratory, infusion, and intake/output fields listed above.

## Arrival-severity indicators and strata

Use only arrival-time variables. Join the following files on `patientStayId`/`patientId` as documented in the eICU metadata:

- `patient.csv.gz`: age, sex, race, admission source, disposition.
- `hospital.csv.gz`: hospital identifier, disposition, and hospital-level fields.
- `admissionDx.csv.gz`: acute diagnoses at admission.
- `apacheApsVar.csv.gz`, `apachePredVar.csv.gz`, `apachePatientResult.csv.gz`: APACHE variables and results where available.
- `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`, `vitalAperiodic.csv.gz`: GCS, temperature, respiratory rate, blood pressure, heart rate.
- `lab.csv.gz`: creatinine, lactate, platelets, bilirubin.
- `nurseCharting.csv.gz`: oxygen support, FiO2, urine output, and other arrival observations.
- `respiratoryCharting.csv.gz`: respiratory support and ventilator status.
- `infusionDrug.csv.gz`: vasopressor exposure at arrival.
- `intakeOutput.csv.gz`: urine output at arrival.
- `diagnosis.csv.gz`: diagnoses available at admission.

Define a composite arrival-severity score from the following indicators, using only arrival-time values:

- APACHE variables/results where available.
- GCS.
- Creatinine.
- Lactate.
- Platelets.
- Bilirubin.
- Respiratory support or ventilator status.
- Vasopressor exposure.
- Acute diagnoses at admission.

Prespecify three strata before viewing outcome data:

1. **Low severity:** no APACHE result available and no more than one of the following present at arrival: abnormal creatinine, abnormal lactate, abnormal platelets, abnormal bilirubin, respiratory support, or vasopressor exposure.
2. **Moderate severity:** APACHE result available, or two to three of the listed organ-injury indicators present.
3. **High severity:** four or more indicators present, or a prespecified APACHE threshold where available.

If APACHE results are too sparse to define the high-severity stratum robustly, use the indicator-count definition and report APACHE coverage separately. Do not redefine strata after seeing outcomes.

## Analysis

### Primary analysis

1. Compare crude 24-hour in-ICU death rates by admission source and by arrival-severity stratum.
2. Estimate adjusted risk differences between transferred and directly admitted patients within each stratum using a prespecified logistic regression model with arrival covariates and hospital fixed effects.
3. Test the transfer-by-severity interaction in a pooled model with the same covariates.
4. Report standardized mean differences before and after adjustment, adjusted risk differences with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, and calibration by hospital.

### Baseline model

A severity-stratified logistic regression model using a small set of arrival organ-injury variables:

- APACHE variables/results where available.
- GCS.
- Creatinine.
- Lactate.
- Platelets.
- Bilirubin.
- Respiratory support.
- Vasopressor exposure.
- Acute diagnoses at admission.
- Hospital fixed effects.

Fit the model within each severity stratum and also as a pooled model with a transfer-by-severity interaction. The baseline is interpretable and provides direct estimates of the adjusted risk difference in each stratum.

### Learned alternative

A regularized gradient-boosted model using the same arrival-time inputs as the baseline, with hospital fixed effects or hospital-level calibration. The model is fit on the same outcome and the same whole-hospital train/test split.

The learned alternative could reveal whether nonlinear interactions or high-dimensional arrival-state information improve prediction beyond the interpretable baseline. It may identify whether the residual transfer-associated difference is driven by complex combinations of organ-injury indicators that a simple stratified logistic model misses. It does not identify transfer harm or causal effects.

Fit the learned alternative only if the baseline estimates are stable and the question of residual risk localization remains unresolved. Defer the learned alternative if the baseline already shows a clear, stable severity-concentrated pattern, or if the learned model does not materially improve calibration or reveal a clinically meaningful subgroup without overfitting.

### Validation

Use whole-hospital holdout validation. Train within hospitals, then evaluate on hospitals not represented in the training split. Report performance degradation and adjusted risk estimates by hospital. Do not pool patients across hospitals for a causal claim.

Evaluation metrics: AUROC, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, and adjusted risk difference by severity stratum.

### Resource estimate

CPU-only, 8–16 CPUs, 16–32 GB RAM, under 1 hour for a bounded tabular fit. No GPU required. The learned alternative may require additional time for repeated hospital-level cross-validation but remains CPU-feasible.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive:** the crude risk difference is large, standardized mean differences for arrival organ injury are large, adjustment materially attenuates the risk difference, and the residual difference is concentrated in the high-severity stratum with a stable transfer-by-severity interaction. This would suggest that the residual association is localized to patients with severe organ injury at arrival and would motivate further work on severity assessment, transfer selection, or post-arrival monitoring for the sickest patients.

**Adverse:** adjustment does not attenuate the risk difference, or the residual difference is similar across severity strata, suggesting that arrival severity does not localize the association. This would motivate further work on post-arrival care or missing pre-arrival data, not a causal transfer claim.

**Inconclusive:** sparse transfer labels, poor covariate overlap, unstable hospital-level estimates, or wide confidence intervals crossing both null and clinically important attenuation. In this case, the study cannot distinguish between the competing explanations.

## Non-causal limitations

The pre-arrival course, transfer timing, reason for transfer, and source-hospital treatment are unavailable. The design tests whether the association between admission source and post-arrival outcome is modified by arrival-severity indicators, not whether transfer causes harm. Missingness, selection into transfer, and post-arrival treatment differences remain important confounders. The results cannot establish a causal effect of transfer or a causal effect of arrival severity.

## Key evidence context

[K1] The eICU-CRD documentation describes the available multi-hospital ICU data and the limitations of using retrospective admission records. This work supports the data bindings and the explicit non-causal interpretation.

[K2] Prior ICU transfer literature reports associations between transfer and outcomes but often cannot separate arrival severity from transfer-related effects. This work motivates the severity-stratified design and the need to distinguish severity-concentrated from broad residual associations.

[K3] Propensity/standardization methods provide a framework for estimating adjusted associations while explicitly acknowledging residual confounding and transportability limits. This work supports the use of adjusted risk differences and whole-hospital validation.
