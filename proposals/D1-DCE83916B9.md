# Outcome-timing and recorded-gap boundary of the eICU laboratory-process transport signal

Status: proposed substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`; no model fit or hypothesis result is claimed. This branch preserves the parent’s hospital-held-out estimand and source bindings while resolving its remaining outcome/observation-timing boundary.

## Scientific opening, evidence-supported claim, and unresolved claim

The parent’s strongest evidence-supported claim is descriptive: laboratory result values plus features of when results were recorded may change between hospitals in a way that affects transport performance. Its process permutation can test whether the process vector is patient-linked, but a six-hour risk set and ICU-discharge mortality label leave two consequential alternatives unresolved:

1. Because eICU has ICU discharge status and discharge offset but no death timestamp, a process increment could partly reflect who remains in the ICU beyond six hours, including early deaths and early discharges, rather than a monitoring signal that is stable at the intended decision time.
2. “Testing process” is not one clinical construct. Result presence/count/timing may reflect observation intensity, while a long interval without a recorded result is a different recorded-process proxy. Neither is an order, specimen-collection, interface, or clinically actionable monitoring-gap field.

[K1] shows that temporally rich EHR representations can predict mortality across centers, but does not separate patient state from recording process or establish an ICU-discharge timing mechanism. [K2] motivates treating missingness and landmark definition as substantive components of dynamic EHR prediction, but is a CLABSI study in another setting and cannot identify what an absent eICU result means. [K3] shows why external discrimination, calibration, and data availability should be evaluated together under fragmented EHR transport, but does not identify a laboratory-process mechanism.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adult first ICU stays observed beyond six hours, the parent’s hospital-held-out laboratory-process transport contrast is robust to a stricter 12-hour eligibility boundary and to an available mortality-label concordance audit, and it is specifically attributable to a reproducible recorded-result process definition rather than to one arbitrary count/timing summary. The process increment should be reduced by a prespecified within-hospital, value-state-matched process permutation if it is patient-linked; a persistent increment after that null remains compatible with hospital workflow or stable documentation intensity.

This is a falsifiable transportability hypothesis, not a causal claim that testing, non-testing, documentation, or EOL planning changes mortality.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

A process signal that survives the 6-to-12-hour landmark and agrees across the patient discharge-status label and the available Apache ICU-mortality field would be less plausibly explained by one early risk-set or endpoint coding boundary. If the gap block, rather than simple result counts, drives the signal, it identifies a more interpretable observation-pattern target for data-quality review. If the signal disappears at 12 hours, disagrees across labels, or is unchanged by state-matched permutation, the appropriate clinical conclusion is that the apparent transport signal is selection/workflow-sensitive and should not be used to infer inadequate monitoring.

The advance over candidate-064 is a prespecified outcome-concordance and competing-landmark audit plus a clinically legible decomposition of recorded process:

- P = result presence, number, distinct offsets, and first/last recorded-result timing;
- G = recorded-result gap and late-silence features, including fixed 120- and 240-minute gap bands.

This distinguishes observation intensity from intervals with no recorded result while retaining exactly the same patient-level state/value information, hospital-held-out split, and ICU-discharge outcome estimand. The gap features are deliberately named recorded-result gaps, not monitoring gaps. No available source establishes an expected testing schedule or clinical actionability.

## Population, temporal boundaries, outcomes, and estimands

Time zero is ICU admission. All offsets are ICU-relative minutes.

Primary population: rows in patient with unitvisitnumber=1, unitstaytype=admit, numeric age >=18 (retain >89), finite unitdischargeoffset >=0, nonmissing hospitalid, patientunitstayid, and unitdischargestatus. Remove duplicate patientunitstayid rows deterministically and report them. Retain hospitals with at least 100 S6-eligible stays; stop the confirmatory interpretation if fewer than five retained hospitals remain. Unknown gender and ethnicity are explicit categories.

The primary risk set is S6=1(unitdischargeoffset>360). The primary label is Y_D=1 iff patient.unitdischargestatus case-insensitively equals Expired; otherwise Y_D=0 among S6 stays. This is ICU-discharge mortality conditional on surviving/remaining observable past 360 minutes; it is not mortality by a fixed six-hour time. Do not assign a label to S6=0.

The stricter competing landmark is S12=1(unitdischargeoffset>720), with the same [0,360] features and Y_D. It is a descriptive selection-boundary estimand, not a 12-hour mortality endpoint. On the complete adult denominator, report four mutually exclusive audit categories: S6 eligible, early Expired (offset <=360 and status Expired), early non-Expired (offset <=360 and status not Expired), and missing/invalid discharge timing or status. This audit can expose early discharge/death selection but cannot separate death time from ICU discharge time.

Outcome-concordance audit: join apachePatientResult to patient rows only by patientunitstayid. Read actualicumortality without using any Apache score or discharge variable as a predictor. Before analysis, print its distinct nonmissing values and apply only an explicitly recorded binary mapping supported by those values (for example, exact case-folded alive/dead or 0/1 forms); treat all other values as missing. Define Y_A only for mapped values. On the same S6 and S12 risk sets, report label prevalence, percent agreement, Cohen’s kappa, positive/negative disagreement, and the primary process contrasts under Y_D and Y_A. Y_A is not an independent gold standard: it may share ICU-discharge information and has no death timestamp. If mapping is not unambiguous, the Apache audit is inconclusive rather than coerced.

The care-plan audit defines EOL6=1 when at least one finite cpleolsaveoffset or cpleoldiscussionoffset is in [0,360]. Report the first such offsets and raw row counts. Do not use activeupondischarge, any EOL field, or discharge fields as predictors. EOL6 indicates documented care-plan activity, not a treatment limitation, death time, or clinician intent.

For process block X and label/outcome e, preserve the parent transport estimand:
TransportInc_X(e,L) = [metric(V+X, internal,e,L)-metric(V+X, external,e,L)] - [metric(V, internal,e,L)-metric(V, external,e,L)].

The primary is the parent’s held-out transport increment for X=P, e=YD, L=360. Secondary contrasts use X=G and X=P+G, L=360; and all three X blocks at L=720. Repeat the primary P contrast with YA only where its valid count and hospital/event gates pass. For Brier, calibration error, and net benefit orient signs so positive means improvement; report direct paired differences as well as transport contrasts.

## Features and clinically meaningful recorded-process definitions

Within each outer training partition, select the 30 analytes with greatest distinct eligible-patient coverage among analytes with at least 1% training coverage. Never use held-out hospitals for analyte selection, imputation, or feature definitions.

Value block V: age, gender, ethnicity, admissionweight, and the latest valid value per selected analyte in [0,360], with original result offset retained only for audit. Impute numeric values by training-fold medians and categorical values by training-fold policy.

Presence/intensity block P: for each analyte, presence, valid-row count, distinct valid-offset count, first recorded-result offset, and last recorded-result offset; plus total valid-row count and total distinct-offset count. These describe recorded information intensity.

Recorded-gap block G: for each analyte with at least two valid offsets, maximum consecutive recorded-result gap; last-result tail gap 360-last_offset; indicator for any result in [0,180] but none in (180,360]; and fixed bands for a maximum/tail gap of <=120, (120,240], or >240 minutes. Include explicit no-pair/no-result indicators rather than silently treating them as a measured gap. These are proxies for absent recorded results, not clinically actionable monitoring gaps. All gaps are computed only from lab result offsets in the feature window; no post-360 row may enter any feature.

Models are capacity-matched: V-Logit is the simple interpretable elastic-net logistic baseline on V; V-GBM is a value-only histogram gradient booster; VP-GBM, VG-GBM, and VPG-GBM use the same learner, feature budget, tuning policy, and seeds with P, G, or P+G. The learned alternative is substantive because it can reveal nonlinear value-by-observation-gap interactions that the interpretable baseline and separate additive process summaries lose. It is not selected for a small predictive gain: the scientific endpoint is whether process contrasts replicate across landmarks/labels and attenuate under the state-matched null.

The primary process-null model keeps V, labels, hospital, and fold unchanged but cyclically assigns each complete P+G vector to another stay within the same hospital and a training-defined state stratum: quintile of out-of-fold V-GBM risk with age/admissionweight bins as tie-breakers. Use SHA-256("eicu-process-state-null-v1\\0"+patientunitstayid) for deterministic ordering; merge adjacent quintiles when a cell has fewer than 10 stays and report merges. This tests patient-process linkage against a stable case-mix/workflow rival; it is not causal adjustment. The parent’s unrestricted within-hospital permutation and a +/-180-minute offset timing placebo are secondary technical controls. The timing placebo recomputes P/G from shifted offsets while preserving row counts/presence; it is not an intervention.

## Split, uncertainty, and computational plan

Sort retained hospital IDs by SHA-256("eicu-transport-v1\\0"+hospitalid) and greedily balance S6-eligible stays into five contiguous folds. Every stay and both landmarks inherit its hospital fold. Within each four-fold training set reserve a deterministic 20% patient-stratified internal sample using SHA-256("eicu-internal-v1\\0"+patientunitstayid); fit preprocessing and models only on the remaining 80% training stays, score the internal reference and held-out hospitals with the same fit. No patient-only split is called external validation.

Use paired 2,000-resample hospital-cluster bootstrap intervals with identical resample indices across models, labels, and contrasts where the risk set permits. Report per-hospital estimates, event/non-event counts, prevalence, missing-label counts, label agreement, EOL strata, and effective sample sizes. A landmark/label contrast is unstable and cannot support a confirmatory interpretation if fewer than five hospitals contribute at least 20 events and 20 non-events. The readiness gate is at least 5,000 S6 stays, 100 S6 events, and five retained hospitals; these are gates, not observed results.

The future solver plan is CPU: 16 CPUs, 64–128 GiB (request up to 262,144 MiB), and approximately 2–6 hours planned for extraction, five folds, six model/control variants, label/landmark audits, and bootstrap. This estimate is planned, not measured; a bounded readiness fold should measure extraction and one fit. The configured solver-planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 28,800 seconds. GPU is deferred because these are tabular extraction and gradient-boosting fits; no GPU result or GPU requirement is claimed. If the measured readiness fit exceeds the envelope, retain the primary V/VP/G audit and defer only the combined VPG model; do not shrink the population, move the landmark, or change the estimand.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive patient-linked result: the P or G process increment is positive with a 95% hospital-cluster interval excluding zero under YD at S6; the increment has the same direction and clinically material magnitude at S12; it is similar under YA when the Apache mapping and event/hospital gates pass; and it attenuates relative to the original process model under the state-matched permutation. This supports a reproducible descriptive association between recorded process and patient-linked risk, not a mechanism or intervention benefit.

Supportive recorded-workflow result: the increment remains similar after state-matched permutation, or the timing placebo preserves it while the unpermuted process does not show a coherent attenuation. That pattern is compatible with stable hospital recording intensity or workflow and should motivate data-feed harmonization/recalibration, not more testing.

Adverse result: the primary P/G increment’s upper interval is below zero for discrimination and does not improve Brier, calibration, or 10% policy-scenario net benefit; or it disappears at S12 and fails the concordance audit. This weakens the claim that a stable process signal informs a six-hour transport model. A null with wide intervals is not decisive.

Inconclusive result: Apache mapping is ambiguous; label agreement is <0.90; the S12 or YA risk set fails hospital/event gates; intervals span clinically meaningful values; analyte selection is unstable; EOL6 is too sparse for a stable stratum; or technical audits fail. The 0.90/0.95 agreement bands are prespecified audit flags: <0.90 is discordant, 0.90–<0.95 is caution, and >=0.95 is concordant for interpretation. They do not validate either mortality field.

The verifier can check joins, source hashes, leakage, offsets, folds, feature manifests, null assignment, model parity, predictions, metrics, bootstrap intervals, and whether prose conclusions follow from saved outputs. It cannot establish death timing, whether an absent result reflects an unobserved order or interface failure, whether a gap was clinically inappropriate, or whether a review alert changes care. Those claims require timestamped death, order/specimen/interface data, clinician adjudication, and prospective silent deployment/impact evaluation. No result authorizes changing monitoring, treatment, or goals of care.

## Alternatives, selection, and deferral record

V-Logit is the interpretable baseline: same V inputs, target, split, uncertainty, calibration and policy-scenario evaluation. VPG-GBM is the learned alternative: same target/split/evaluation but joint V, P, and G inputs, allowing nonlinear interactions. VP-GBM and VG-GBM are necessary decomposition controls, not a leaderboard. The value-state-matched permutation is the mechanistic rival check. A temporal neural model over raw event sequences is deferred: the current unresolved claim is outcome/selection and process-definition robustness, and available fields lack order/collection semantics; added representation complexity would not answer that boundary. Revisit it only if readiness shows that fold-safe P/G summaries materially discard within-window information while the primary audit is feasible. No causal testing intervention is selected because treatment/order assignment and death timing are unavailable.

Completion requires newly fitted fold-safe predictions for the specified models, landmark and concordance audit tables, paired uncertainty intervals, technical manifest checks, and a conclusions report tied to those files. A proposal alone is not a study result.

## Three inspected key works

[K1] Rajkomar et al. supports the premise that temporally rich EHR representations can predict mortality across centers. It does not establish a distinct laboratory recording mechanism or eICU discharge-time outcome. This child adds a hospital-held-out ICU estimand, recorded-gap decomposition, state-matched process null, and outcome/landmark concordance audit.

[K2] Gao et al. supports explicit treatment of missingness/observation patterns and landmark timing in dynamic prediction. It does not tell us whether a missing eICU result is clinically appropriate, an order omission, or an interface gap. This child uses only eICU result offsets and labels the resulting features as recorded-process proxies.

[K3] Hadler et al. supports paired external discrimination/calibration evaluation under fragmented EHR availability and warns that local patterns can affect calibration and precision. It does not identify process causality or validate a monitoring decision. This child preserves hospital-held-out evaluation and adds the timing/label boundary needed before any clinical interpretation.
