# Provenance-stratified, all-eligible validation of preoperative M2 triage in HCC

## Clinical decision, established evidence, and unresolved claim

The decision is whether an adult undergoing a first recorded liver resection for HCC should be flagged for multidisciplinary review because high-grade microvascular invasion (M2) may be present. The flag could prioritize review of the operative plan or a later adjuvant/surveillance discussion; it is not a recommendation for wider resection, neoadjuvant therapy, adjuvant therapy, or any treatment.

The strongest claim supported by inspected external evidence is narrower. MVI is a pathology-defined feature and its severity is associated with recurrence and survival: the inspected Yao et al. full text (DOI 10.21037/hbsn-22-411; PMCID PMC10839725) gives the M0/M1/M2 definitions and reports materially worse outcomes for M2 in a 227-person multicenter cohort. A newer inspected study (Li et al., DOI 10.21037/jgo-2025-1-1029; PMCID PMC13188018) explores preoperative PET/CT prediction in only 79 transplant patients and calls for further validation. These studies do not establish that routine reports and laboratories add decision value in this HCC source, nor that a triage flag changes surgery or outcomes.

The remaining clinically important question after the parent design’s repair of grade contamination and selective ascertainment is transportability across the report-generating process:

> Among all eligible resection encounters, does a preoperative report-plus-lab model add clinically useful M2 triage information beyond a timestamp-safe structured baseline and report-availability metadata, and is that incremental value stable across CT versus MRI report strata and calendar drift?

The primary hypothesis is that the extension’s paired net benefit at a fixed 0.30 M2-review risk threshold is positive in the locked 2020–2021 test cohort and is not harmful in either prespecified CT or MRI stratum. The substantive advance is a transportability claim about a clinical workflow artifact: a model that works only in one report template or machine era is not ready to support triage. The hypothesis is allowed to fail, and sparse or unidentified pathology in a stratum produces an inconclusive result rather than being silently dropped.

## Population and temporal boundary

Use only the discovery-accessible HCC records under the catalog’s participant-level partition; do not describe the reserved 20% as external validation. The Harbor program must report both discovery/reserved partition counts and the complete attrition table.

Eligibility is defined before parsing the pathology endpoint:

- age at least 18 in `encounters.age`;
- first qualifying liver-resection encounter for each participant during 2015-01-01 through 2021-12-31, inclusive, selected by earliest `procedures.start time` and then deterministic `encounter number` tie-break;
- a procedure row on the composite encounter key whose normalized `procedures.Surgery` matches a frozen, clinically reviewed liver-resection dictionary (for example, liver segment/lobe/partial hepatectomy terms); the dictionary and every matched raw value must be output;
- HCC compatibility from a same-key `diagnoses.Diagnosis Name` or pathology diagnosis matching a frozen HCC dictionary. Diagnosis is an eligibility signal only, never a predictor.

Do not claim that the source proves “untreated” or “apparently resectable”: it has no reliable treatment-line chronology, staging registry, tumor board time, or image files. If the frozen procedure/HCC dictionaries cannot be clinically reviewed, the Harbor result is computable but clinically ungradable, and no deployment interpretation is allowed.

For each eligible encounter, define the research index time (t_0) as `procedures.start time` for the qualifying operation. The usable pre-index window is:

- examinations with `examinations.Start time < t_0`, same `Patient master index, encounter number`, and a pre-index CT/MRI modality dictionary match;
- labs with `labs.test time < t_0`, same composite key, and an assay-specific dictionary match;
- age and sex from the same `encounters` row.

A strict less-than rule excludes simultaneous/perioperative records. Orders, medications, diagnoses, clinical documents and pathology are not used as predictor values because their timestamps are absent, unreliable, or post-index provenance cannot be established. The exam start time is only an availability proxy; the source has no report-finalization timestamp or actual MDT decision time.

Use 2015–2018 for model development, 2019 for calibration and threshold locking, and a patient-disjoint 2020–2021 locked test. No tuning, vocabulary changes, feature selection or threshold changes may use the locked test. Report 2022–2025 only as a prospective-era data-availability and endpoint-identifiability audit; do not call it a performance validation because the parent audit found a collapse in recoverable selected grades and pathology has no timestamp.

## Endpoint and its uncertainty

The endpoint is pathology-defined M2 on the same composite encounter key, not a diagnosis code, recurrence, or treatment outcome. Join to `pathology` and inspect `Pathology`, `Findings`, and `Examination Diagnosis`. Restrict to a clinically reviewed surgical-specimen context and HCC-compatible pathology. The endpoint parser must be an anchored state machine:

1. locate an assigned MVI/vascular-invasion result in the relevant specimen diagnosis, not a generic explanation or teaching definition;
2. recognize M0/M1/M2 only when the assigned-value syntax is explicit;
3. handle negation, “no findings,” multiple specimens and multiple clauses;
4. reject rows where the grade is only present in a definition, historical reference, explanatory legend, or a conflicting clause;
5. classify all other cases as unknown.

Two independently implemented parsers must agree on the locked test. A blinded clinical pathologist should review a random sample of each parser’s positive, negative, conflicting and unknown strata, and all disagreements if feasible. This review is required to interpret the endpoint; it cannot be fabricated by the automatic verifier. Sampling adequacy, number of blocks, distance from tumor, and slide review are unavailable, so even an explicit M0/M1/M2 result is an imperfect reference.

Set (Y=1) only for an adjudicated/algorithmically concordant explicit M2; set (Y=0) for concordant explicit M0 or M1; set (Y=?) for missing, ungradable, conflicting, non-surgical or unresolved pathology. Keep every eligible patient in the denominator. Do not complete-case the primary analysis.

## Predictors and nested clinical baselines

Construct three frozen, patient-disjoint models:

- (B), the structured baseline: age, sex, pre-index assay-specific last values and clinically meaningful within-assay summaries in the 30 days before (t_0), plus missingness indicators and the number of qualifying lab results;
- (B+M), (B) plus availability/provenance metadata: whether a qualifying CT or MRI report exists before (t_0), modality family, report count, and machine-label availability. Machine labels are metadata, not clinical measurements;
- (E=B+M+T), adding only pre-index text from `examinations.Findings` and `examinations.Diagnosis` for the frozen CT/MRI dictionary.

The primary incremental contrast is (E) versus (B+M), so any value is attributable to report content rather than simply knowing that a report or machine record exists. The secondary contrast is (B+M) versus (B). Use assay-specific standardization or rank/indicator representations because `labs.Quantitative Result` has no unit column. Do not pool differently named assays merely because their names look similar. No clinical-document text, pathology text, procedure name, diagnosis name, post-index examination, or postoperative lab enters a predictor.

Use a prespecified regularized logistic model and a fixed, auditable text representation (for example, character/word n-grams with training-only vocabulary); a simpler penalized generalized linear model is preferred for verification. Calibration is fit only in 2019. The verifier must also run a prevalence-only model and a label-permuted negative-control model. A complex model or a small AUC improvement is not evidence of clinical value.

## Primary estimand and provenance stability

At review threshold (p_t=0.30), define net benefit as
[
NB = TP/n - FP/n\, [p_t/(1-p_t)].
]
The primary estimand is the paired patient-level (Delta NB=NB_E-NB_{B+M}) on all eligible locked-test patients. A review flag means “send for expert review”; it does not imply treatment.

For unknown (Y), compute the exact sharp lower and upper bounds for paired (Delta NB) by assigning each unknown outcome the value that minimizes or maximizes its paired contribution. Bootstrap patients, not rows, for uncertainty; report the 95% interval for both bounds and the proportion/number of unknown endpoints. An apparent complete-case positive result is secondary and cannot override bounds that include clinically harmful values.

The locked test has two prespecified provenance strata, assigned before model fitting from the pre-index exam label: CT-family and MRI-family. The modality dictionary, exact `examinations.Examination` values, and counts are output. A stratum requires at least 100 eligible patients and at least 20 endpoint-known M2 cases to be estimable; otherwise its transportability result is inconclusive. Exact machine labels from `examinations.Machine Model` and report-template labels are examined in a descriptive sensitivity analysis only, with a minimum of 100 patients and no claim for sparse strata.

Supportive evidence requires all of the following in the locked test:

- the pooled 95% lower bound of (Delta NB) is greater than 0.02;
- each estimable CT/MRI stratum has a 95% lower bound no less than -0.02;
- calibration-in-the-large remains within ±0.05 and calibration slope within 0.80–1.25 for (E), with uncertainty reported;
- (E) does not fail either leakage control below.

Adverse evidence is a pooled 95% upper bound below -0.02, or a pooled positive result accompanied by a stratum 95% lower bound below -0.02, or a calibration failure that would make a 0.30 flag unsafe. A model that improves discrimination but fails these decision criteria is adverse for the stated clinical use.

The result is inconclusive if the partial-identification interval crosses both -0.02 and +0.02, if endpoint-known counts or overlap are insufficient, if a stratum is not estimable, if the dual parser disagreement exceeds 5%, or if the locked test cannot be kept independent. No alternate threshold may rescue an adverse or inconclusive primary result; thresholds other than 0.30 are exploratory.

## Falsification and verifier requirements

The Harbor implementation must report:

- a label-permutation test in which any apparent (Delta NB) is compatible with zero;
- a patient-level text shuffle/negative-control analysis;
- a future-information control using labs/exams after (t_0), which is expected to perform better only as a leakage demonstration and must never be used for the main estimate;
- a non-target exam control (for example, pre-index chest/ECG reports) whose incremental value should not exceed the target CT/MRI text after multiplicity-aware uncertainty;
- year-by-year calibration and performance from development/calibration/test, with no retrospective selection of a favorable year;
- overlap, effective sample size, missingness, parser agreement, endpoint-known fraction and exact row counts for every filter;
- sensitivity to excluding versus retaining machine metadata, and to the clinically reviewed subset of explicit pathology.

The verifier must test not only arithmetic: it must reject a conclusion that says “the hypothesis was confirmed” when the computed bound is inconclusive, when only complete cases were analyzed, or when a stratum is harmful. It must also accept a correctly computed adverse or inconclusive result as a scientifically useful outcome. Computation can check joins, clocks, parsing implementation, estimands, intervals, baselines and linkage of prose to outputs. It cannot establish that the M2 parser is clinically correct, that pathology sampling was adequate, that a 0.30 review threshold is acceptable to clinicians, or that review changes treatment, recurrence or survival.

## What this would and would not establish

A supportive result would justify a prospective, clinically reviewed validation of a report-plus-lab M2 review flag with explicit local template monitoring. It would not justify changing resection extent, giving adjuvant therapy, or claiming improved recurrence or survival.

An adverse result would be clinically valuable: it would show that apparent report signal is not reliable enough for a fixed review policy or is not transportable across CT/MRI workflows, and should prevent deployment without redesign.

An inconclusive result would identify the limiting evidence—unknown/ambiguous pathology, inadequate stratum size, parser disagreement, unavailable timestamps, or failed overlap. It would motivate pathology adjudication, standardized sampling, timestamped report/MDT data, raw images, and prospective external validation.

Clinical claims requiring additional evidence include the correctness of pathology grading, the acceptability and workload of the 0.30 threshold, whether experts act on the flag, treatment effect, recurrence, survival, and generalization outside this single institutional snapshot. The experiment tests incremental predictive decision value under explicit missingness; it does not test clinical truth by QA alone.
