# Recorded insulin intensity, observed renal change, and subsequent low glucose in eICU

## Episode 6 successor and clinical decision

This is a substantive new direction from expert seed `[starting question]`, developed using the strongest eICU parents for landmarking, observation-process control, competing-state accounting, and hospital-held-out validation. The safety decision is whether an ICU surveillance rule should flag patients for more frequent glucose checks when a recorded insulin infusion is accompanied by an early worsening creatinine signal, or whether apparent risk is mostly a testing/documentation artifact.

This is an observational prognostic experiment about recorded events. It does not estimate the effect of giving insulin, stopping nutrition, changing an insulin protocol, or targeting a glucose threshold.

## What is supported and what remains unresolved

The available eICU snapshot structurally contains:

- ICU-stay identifiers, age, sex, hospital, admission source and discharge offsets in `patient`;
- timestamped glucose and creatinine rows in `lab`, with numeric values, result text, measurement-system and interface fields;
- timestamped insulin rows in `infusionDrug`, including `drugname`, `drugrate`, `infusionrate`, `drugamount`, and `patientweight`;
- insulin medication-order fields in `medication`; and
- heterogeneous timestamped intake/output cells in `intakeOutput`.

A local header/row audit verified the exact headers and representative insulin, glucose, creatinine, dextrose, tube-feeding-flush and residual labels. A full compressed-file semantic scan was submitted but remained active at episode drafting time; therefore no event or cohort counts are claimed here. The prior successful eICU audit established field presence for the broader patient/lab/infusion/intake-output collection, but prior failed audits due to path/code errors are not evidence.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is therefore only that the snapshot records candidate insulin, glucose and creatinine observations with ICU-relative timestamps. It does not establish administered dose, nutrition delivery or cessation, complete kidney injury ascertainment, or clinical benefit.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among adults with a non-low baseline glucose, a recorded insulin-infusion intensity during ICU minutes 0–360 will be more strongly associated with a first subsequently recorded low glucose during minutes 360–1,440 when the observed creatinine trajectory during minutes 0–360 is worsening, after adjustment for baseline glucose/creatinine, early physiology, illness context, hospital and observation density.

The primary estimand is the interaction between recorded insulin intensity and observed creatinine change in a hospital-held-out prediction/association model for the 6–24-hour recorded low-glucose phenotype. The null is no reproducible interaction and no held-out improvement over the insulin-plus-baseline model. This is not a causal effect-modification claim.

## Population, landmark and variables

Use one `patientunitstayid` as the index unit. Retain `uniquepid` only for person-clustered sensitivity analysis. Include adults with numeric `patient.age >= 18`; report missing-age and the eICU `> 89` age convention explicitly. Require `patient.unitdischargeoffset >= 360` and follow-up opportunity through 1,440 minutes; stays discharged or dying earlier enter a competing/unknown state rather than being coded as normoglycemic.

The landmark is ICU minute 360. Eligibility requires:

1. at least one valid glucose row with `labname` exactly `glucose` or `bedside glucose` in minutes 0–360, with a prespecified accepted mg/dL unit string from `labmeasurenamesystem` or `labmeasurenameinterface`;
2. baseline glucose >=70 mg/dL, so the outcome is not simply persistence of a pre-existing low value;
3. at least one valid creatinine row with exact `labname=creatinine` in minutes 0–360 and the same prespecified unit rule;
4. an insulin exposure row in minutes 0–360 from `infusionDrug` whose `drugname` contains insulin and whose name explicitly reports units/hr (for example, `Insulin (units/hr)`), with finite nonnegative numeric `drugrate` or `infusionrate`; and
5. no use of rows after minute 360 to define eligibility or covariates.

The primary outcome is the first valid glucose value <70 mg/dL in minutes 360–1,440. Severe low glucose <54 mg/dL and a threshold sensitivity at <80 are secondary. A result is a recorded low-glucose phenotype, not adjudicated symptomatic or iatrogenic hypoglycemia.

The primary insulin exposure is the recorded infusion rate, summarized as the median and maximum valid rate, number of insulin rows, and gap-limited time-weighted rate. Between consecutive insulin rows, carry a rate only when the gap is <=60 minutes; otherwise split the interval and report the uncovered fraction. Do not infer continuous administration from a row. The primary contrast is a prespecified clinically interpretable rate increment (for example, per doubling after log1p transformation), with the exact transform and winsorization fixed before outcome analysis. A sensitivity uses any recorded insulin row versus none within the eligible infusion-defined cohort, and another uses the `medication` order record separately.

Observed renal change is the last minus first valid creatinine in minutes 0–360, requiring two measurements at least 60 minutes apart. The primary variable is continuous change in mg/dL; an observed increase of >=0.3 mg/dL is a secondary descriptive stratum. Neither is called KDIGO AKI because the snapshot lacks complete prior renal history and a full 48-hour/seven-day adjudication frame. Baseline creatinine and measurement interval are included separately.

Baseline/context covariates are pre-landmark glucose and creatinine summaries; age, gender, ethnicity, hospital admission source, unit type and unit stay type from `patient`; early noninvasive MAP from `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` with `observationoffset`; early observation counts/gaps for glucose, creatinine, insulin and vital signs; and, if used, only pre-landmark fields from `apacheApsVar`/ `apachePatientResult` with an explicit leakage audit. Apache aggregates are not primary because their first-24-hour construction can overlap the landmark.

Nutrition is not a primary exposure. `intakeOutput` fields `intakeoutputoffset`, `intakeoutputentryoffset`, `cellpath`, `celllabel`, `cellvaluenumeric` and `cellvaluetext` can be audited descriptively for tube-feeding flush, residual, dextrose and other labels. Missing intake/output is not interruption, and a dextrose or flush row is not validated caloric delivery. A nutrition-process sensitivity may report label availability and gaps, but cannot test the expert seed's full “interrupted nutrition” claim.

## Outcomes and competing ascertainment states

Follow-up starts after minute 360 and ends at the earliest of minute 1,440, `patient.unitdischargeoffset`, or ICU death/discharge. Use `unitdischargestatus`, `unitdischargelocation`, and offsets only to define competing discharge/death/transfer states; do not treat discharge as normoglycemia.

Report four states:

1. recorded low glucose;
2. no recorded low glucose with adequate follow-up and at least one qualifying post-landmark glucose;
3. ICU death/discharge before adequate glucose ascertainment; and
4. unknown because post-landmark glucose is absent or timestamp/unit/value rules fail.

The primary analysis uses cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards in 6–12, 12–24 and 24–48-hour bins only where the corresponding window is available, with the 6–24-hour window as the prespecified primary horizon. A competing-risk sensitivity treats death and discharge as separate absorbing outcomes. Unknown outcome ascertainment is not silently classified as negative; an inverse-observation-weighted sensitivity estimates observation probability from pre-landmark density, unit, hospital, admission source and early severity, with positivity and weight truncation reported.

## Baselines, transportability and uncertainty

Fit the same transparent model family in five grouped hospital folds, holding all label maps, unit rules, imputation, transforms and tuning within training hospitals:

- M0: demographics, admission/context, baseline glucose and creatinine, early physiology, and observation-density features;
- M1: M0 plus recorded insulin intensity and insulin observation-process features;
- M2: M1 plus observed creatinine change and the prespecified insulin-intensity × creatinine-change interaction;
- Morder: an exploratory/sensitivity model using `medication` insulin-order fields rather than infusion rows;
- Mnutrition: descriptive only, adding intake/output label-process features without interpreting them as nutrition interruption.

The primary test is M2 versus M1 for the interaction and held-out risk performance. Report interaction estimates with 95% confidence intervals, fold- and hospital-level Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC and precision-recall area, plus absolute risk contrasts at prespecified insulin and creatinine-change values. Use hospital-cluster bootstrap or fold-level intervals and a `uniquepid`-clustered sensitivity. Do not use a random patient split as the main validation.

A feasibility gate precedes interpretation: report cohort flow, insulin-rate completeness, baseline/follow-up glucose counts, two-creatinine availability, unit concordance, hospital overlap, and each ascertainment state. If any gate fails, classify the result inconclusive and do not relax definitions after viewing outcomes.

## Falsification criteria and interpretation

Supportive results require a directionally coherent interaction, a clinically interpretable held-out risk difference, lower held-out Brier score or better calibration for M2 than M1, and stability across multiple hospitals, the <54 threshold, rate summaries and reasonable gap rules. The result supports a transportable marker of recorded low-glucose risk in patients with worsening observed creatinine; it does not support insulin dose harm, renal-causal modification, nutrition interruption, or a monitoring policy without clinical review.

Adverse results include a null or sign-reversing interaction, no held-out improvement, effect confinement to one hospital or high-density cases, instability after density/observation weighting, or disagreement between infusion and order definitions. They weaken the claim that this recorded interaction is a reproducible signal in this snapshot; they do not prove that renal physiology does not affect insulin pharmacology or hypoglycemia risk.

Inconclusive results include too few eligible two-creatinine insulin-exposed stays, nonpositive observation weights, poor unit/label concordance, inability to identify a stable post-landmark glucose opportunity, large infusion/order disagreement, or discharge/death dominating follow-up. The next requirement would be a validated medication-administration record, glucose-device data, nutrition delivery/cessation timestamps, pre-ICU renal reserve, and expert adjudication of symptomatic hypoglycemia—not a post hoc cutoff or treating missingness as normal.

Computationally checkable claims are source integrity, exact joins, label/unit counts, temporal ordering, exposure formulas, cohort flow, state accounting, model predictions, grouped validation, uncertainty and falsification permutations. Clinical claims requiring additional evidence are whether an insulin row represents administration, whether a glucose value was symptomatic or treated, whether renal change is true AKI, whether nutrition stopped, whether the interaction is causal, and whether a surveillance intervention improves outcomes.

Prespecified falsifications are:

- shuffle insulin rates within hospital and broad baseline-glucose/creatinine strata;
- repeat with insulin timing shifted to a pre-landmark pseudo-outcome and with post-landmark values excluded from all features;
- compare infusion-derived exposure with order-derived exposure without pooling their semantics;
- vary the 60-minute gap rule and glucose thresholds (<54, <70, <80);
- test whether the interaction is explained by glucose/creatinine/insulin measurement density, unit mismatch, hospital or discharge timing;
- use a negative-control laboratory outcome selected before analysis that should not be specifically linked to insulin intensity, while retaining the same observation process;
- verify that no M2 feature uses a lab, discharge field or rate row after minute 360.
