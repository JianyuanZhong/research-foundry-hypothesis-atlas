# Inflammation-Functional Discordance Predicts Multi-Organ Decline

## Scientific Question

Among adults with established multimorbidity, does the degree of discordance between baseline inflammatory burden (C-reactive protein, fibrinogen, white blood cell count) and functional reserve (grip strength, gait speed, physical activity) predict subsequent multi-organ decline more strongly than either measure alone?

## Clinical Importance

- **Problem**: Current clinical practice focuses on individual organ systems but rarely addresses the phenomenon of "functional reserve" - the ability to maintain homeostasis despite organ stress.
- **Gap**: While inflammation is known to drive disease progression, the interaction between inflammatory burden and functional reserve remains poorly characterized. Most studies examine either inflammation or function in isolation.
- **Impact**: Identifying patients at highest risk of multi-organ decline could enable earlier intervention strategies targeting both inflammatory pathways and functional preservation.

## Hypothesis Statement

At equivalent levels of inflammatory burden, individuals with lower functional reserve (grip strength, gait speed, or physical activity) experience faster progression to multi-organ decline. Conversely, at equivalent functional reserve, higher inflammatory burden predicts faster progression.

## Study Population

**Population**: UK Biobank participants (ukb672073.csv)
- **Inclusion**: Age 40-75 at baseline, no prior hospitalization for acute organ failure
- **Exclusion**: Missing key outcome variables, non-European ancestry, BMI >40 kg/m²
- **Sample size**: ~350,000 eligible participants (estimated)

## Analysis Plan

### Phase 1: Descriptive
1. Distribution of inflammatory and functional measures
2. Correlation between inflammation and function
3. Prevalence of multi-organ decline by baseline characteristics

### Phase 2: Primary Analysis
1. Cox regression with time-to-multi-organ decline as outcome
2. Main effects: inflammation score, functional score
3. Interaction term: inflammation × function
4. Adjust for: age, sex, ethnicity, BMI, smoking, baseline comorbidities

### Phase 3: Discordance Analysis
1. Calculate expected functional reserve using age/sex/BMI reference curves
2. Compute discordance scores
3. Cox regression with discordance as primary predictor
4. Compare results with baseline models

### Phase 4: Sensitivity Analysis
1. Reverse causation: Exclude events within first 6 months
2. Sensitivity to multi-organ definition (1 vs 2+ organs)
3. Competing risk of death
4. Subgroup analysis by baseline disease burden

## Falsification Criteria

**Hypothesis is supported if**:
- Interaction term is significant (p<0.05): inflammation and function have independent effects
- Discordance score predicts outcome better than either measure alone (ΔAUC > 0.03)
- Discordance association persists after adjusting for baseline comorbidities

**Hypothesis is falsified if**:
- No interaction between inflammation and function (interaction p>0.05)
- Discordance score shows no association with outcome (AUC identical to inflammation alone)
- Discordance association reverses direction or disappears after adjustment

**Hypothesis is inconclusive if**:
- Discordance shows modest association (0.02 < ΔAUC < 0.03)
- Results are sensitive to key assumptions (e.g., multi-organ definition)
- Discordance improves prediction only in subgroups

## Key Limitations

1. **Reverse causation**: Baseline inflammation may be elevated due to subclinical disease
   - Mitigation: Sensitivity analysis excluding first 6 months of follow-up

2. **Measurement error**: Single time-point inflammation/function measures
   - Mitigation: Treat as time-varying exposures where possible

3. **Residual confounding**: Unmeasured lifestyle factors, genetic predisposition
   - Mitigation: Genetic risk scores as additional covariates (if data available)

4. **Multi-organ definition**: Arbitrary threshold of ≥2 organs
   - Mitigation: Sensitivity analysis with 1 and 3+ organs

5. **Functional reserve estimation**: Expected values from reference population
   - Limitation: Reference may not capture individual trajectories

## Scientific Novelty

This hypothesis advances beyond existing expert seeds by:
1. **Integrating multiple modalities**: Combining inflammation biomarkers with functional measures
2. **Testing discordance**: Rather than main effects, testing the *gap* between expected and observed function
3. **Multi-organ outcomes**: Focusing on multi-system decline rather than single-organ outcomes
4. **Mechanistic insight**: The discordance approach tests whether functional reserve acts as a buffer against inflammatory damage
