# Hypothesis: Diuretic Timing and Hypoperfusion Severity Predict 28-Day Mortality in Sepsis

## Scientific Question

**Primary Hypothesis**: In sepsis patients with fluid accumulation, the interaction between diuretic timing and ongoing hypoperfusion severity (vasopressor requirement) predicts 28-day mortality. Specifically, early diuretic initiation in patients with severe hypoperfusion (requiring vasopressors) is associated with worse outcomes compared to early diuretics in patients with stable perfusion.

**Secondary Hypothesis**: The optimal timing window for diuretic initiation depends on the trajectory of fluid accumulation and perfusion status, suggesting a dynamic decision rule rather than a fixed time-to-diuretic threshold.

## Unresolved Question

While existing literature describes:
- Association of diuretics with mortality in sepsis (Wang et al., 2025 [K1])
- Universal recovery trajectories defined by co-regulatory dynamics of white blood cell and platelet populations (Foy et al., 2022 [K2])
- Long-term cardiovascular consequences of severe infection (Sipilä et al., 2023 [K3])

There is limited evidence on:
1. **Interaction between diuretic timing and ongoing hypoperfusion**: Does the benefit of early diuretics diminish or reverse in patients with severe hypoperfusion?
2. **Dynamic decision rules**: Is there a specific trajectory of fluid accumulation and perfusion that predicts the optimal timing window?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Treatment Optimization**: Current guidelines recommend diuretics for fluid overload, but there is uncertainty about when to initiate in patients with ongoing shock. This hypothesis addresses a critical clinical decision point.
2. **Risk Stratification**: Understanding how diuretic timing interacts with hypoperfusion severity could inform risk-adjusted treatment decisions.
3. **Trial Design**: Results could inform the design of future trials on diuretic timing in sepsis with varying degrees of hypoperfusion.

## Study Population

MIMIC-IV ICU patients who:
- Have a diagnosis of sepsis (ICD-10 codes: A40-A47, R65.20, or documented sepsis in chart notes)
- Have fluid accumulation (positive net fluid balance > 2L, or documented fluid overload)
- Have at least one diuretic administration during their ICU stay
- Duration of ICU stay ≥ 72 hours (to allow for meaningful outcome assessment)

## Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis: Cox Proportional Hazards Model

**Outcome**: 28-day all-cause mortality (from ICU admission)

**Exposures**:
1. **Diuretic timing relative to hypoperfusion severity**:
   - Continuous: Time from ICU admission to first diuretic (hours)
   - Categorical: Early (<12h), Intermediate (12-24h), Late (>24h)

2. **Hypoperfusion severity at diuretic initiation**:
   - Continuous: Vasopressor dose (µg/kg/min) at time of diuretic
   - Categorical: No vasopressors, Low (<0.1), Moderate (0.1-0.5), High (≥0.5)

3. **Interaction term**: Diuretic timing × Vasopressor severity

**Covariates**:
- Age, gender, race
- SOFA score components (from lab data)
- Lactate levels
- Initial fluid balance (first 24h)
- APACHE II score (if available)

### Secondary Analysis: Machine Learning Comparison

Compare Cox regression with:
- Random Forest survival model
- Gradient boosting survival model

**Question**: Do machine learning models provide additional predictive power beyond traditional regression?

### Hospital-Level Analysis

Include hospital fixed effects to account for:
- Differences in sepsis management practices
- Variations in fluid management protocols
- Differences in diuretic availability and use

## Method Comparison

### Baseline Method: Time-to-Event Analysis with Covariate Adjustment
- Cox proportional hazards regression
- Adjusts for confounders using multivariable regression
- Tests main effects and interaction terms

### Alternative Method: Machine Learning Survival Models
- Random Forest survival analysis
- Gradient boosting survival models
- Tests whether non-linear relationships or interactions are captured better

**Scientific Question**: Does the interaction between diuretic timing and hypoperfusion severity follow a linear pattern, or are there non-linear thresholds that traditional regression misses?

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. Diuretic timing has no significant association with mortality after adjusting for hypoperfusion severity
2. The interaction between diuretic timing and hypoperfusion is not significant (p > 0.05)
3. Machine learning models do not outperform Cox regression by a meaningful margin (e.g., C-index difference < 0.02)

The hypothesis would be **supported** if:
1. There is a significant interaction between diuretic timing and hypoperfusion severity
2. Early diuretics in severe hypoperfusion are associated with worse outcomes
3. Machine learning models capture additional predictive signal

## Limitations

1. **Confounding by indication**: Patients who receive diuretics may differ systematically from those who don't, even after adjustment
2. **Missing data**: Some fluid balance data may be incomplete
3. **Temporal ambiguity**: Chart events may not capture the exact timing of clinical decisions
4. **Generalizability**: MIMIC-IV data comes from a single academic center; findings may not generalize to community hospitals
