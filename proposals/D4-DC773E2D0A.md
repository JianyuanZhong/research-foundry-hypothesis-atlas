## Decision-relevant unresolved question

The mature UKB branch asks whether retaining an earlier biomarker panel adds prospective recorded-risk information beyond current values, repeat spacing and measured repeat selection, and whether that increment differs by age. Its primary age contrast is anchored to age at recruitment. The clinical decision represented by the study, however, is made when the second panel is reviewed. With intervals up to five years, recruitment-age strata can therefore assign a participant to the wrong decision-age group.

This successor tests a different and consequential validity claim:

> Among participants with a valid paired panel, does the held-out incremental value of prior-panel values beyond current-panel values and verified repeat spacing differ by age at the current repeat assessment, after common-support standardization for baseline risk, spacing, calendar year, sex and repeat-selection propensity? Is that landmark-age result consistent with, or materially different from, the recruitment-age result?

Define the landmark-age proxy only after the age and assessment-date gates pass:

A_T1 = 21022-0.0 + (T1 - T0)/365.2425,

where 21022-0.0 is the verified age-at-recruitment field and T0/T1 are the verified comparable dates in 53-0.0 and 53-1.0. This is an age-at-landmark proxy, not exact date-of-birth age. The primary age contrast is A_T1 >= 65 versus A_T1 < 55; 55-64 is descriptive and a prespecified three-level sensitivity. Recruitment-age strata remain a sensitivity. No observed ages are trimmed to improve balance.

The substantive advance is not a new leaderboard model. It tests whether the age modifier survives alignment to the time at which historical information would be reviewed. A material disagreement between landmark-age and recruitment-age estimates would identify an age-timing/transport failure in the parent interpretation and could change whether age-specific retention of history is considered relevant. A consistent result strengthens only the conditional prognostic-information claim.

## Estimands and selection control

For age group g defined by A_T1, let d_g = E_g,w[BS(I1)-BS(B0+L)] on held-out paired participants. The primary estimand is H_landmark = d_(A_T1>=65) - d_(A_T1<55). Negative d_g means prior values reduce held-out multiclass Brier error; a negative H_landmark means the reduction is larger in the older landmark-age group. Report the 55-64 group, pooled contrast, continuous-age interaction, unweighted H_raw, and the corresponding recruitment-age contrast as prespecified sensitivities.

Use the same outcome-blind selection controls as the parent. Among T0-complete participants, model valid paired observation using only T0 panel values, recruitment age, sex and T0 calendar year; estimate stabilized inverse-probability-of-repeat weights with predeclared 1st/99th percentile truncation. Report overlap, maximum weight and effective sample size by landmark-age group, and fail the transport sensitivity for poor positivity or ESS. This targets the measured T0-complete population under a missing-at-random assumption; it is not a causal testing effect.

For each outer fold, fit age-overlap weights for A_T1>=65 versus A_T1<55 using training-only B0+L predicted target/death risks, L, T0 calendar year, sex and repeat propensity; use overlap weights and normalize within group. Report a three-level sensitivity and covariate-balancing sensitivity on the same variables. Do not use held-out outcomes, I1 predictions or threshold movement to define weights. If overlap is poor, H_landmark is inconclusive rather than extrapolated.

## Models and analysis

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on eid, published with a manifest and no cross-fold duplicate audit. Every imputation, scaling, regularization, calibration, weighting, tuning, bootstrap and permutation is fold-local.

- B0: current position-1 panel, recruitment age 21022-0.0, sex 31-0.0 and T0 calendar year.
- B0+L: B0 plus verified interval L. This remains the current-plus-spacing baseline.
- I1: B0+L plus all eight position-0 panel fields, using a regularized additive multinomial probability model. This is the primary prior-value increment.
- A1: I1 plus centered landmark-age proxy per decade interacted with prespecified prior-minus-current contrasts, with shrinkage. It is an interpretable continuous-age sensitivity; it does not create age cutoffs from held-out outcomes.

Keeping recruitment age in B0 and using A_T1 for stratification preserves the parent’s B0 -> B0+L -> I1 decomposition. A_T1 is deterministically derived from recruitment age and L for age alignment; it is not added to B0 in a way that makes the spacing increment disappear. The primary comparison uses I1 and B0+L predictions on the same held-out participants.

Retain a bounded nonlinear alternative: a small gradient-boosted multinomial probability model with the same current, spacing, prior and age-alignment inputs, and a matched no-prior version. Use a small depth/regularization grid and training-only stopping/calibration. It may reveal nonlinear landmark-age-by-history or current/prior-discordance regions that A1 averages away, but cannot replace H_landmark.

Report in order: H_landmark and its two component d_g values; raw and repeat-selection-weighted contrasts; multiclass log loss; Brier reliability/resolution decomposition; outer-test calibration intercept/slope and observed-versus-predicted target/death summaries; calibration-robust inner-training recalibration; fixed illustrative target-risk thresholds 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10% and 15%; and exploratory decision-curve net benefit. Thresholds are descriptive and not validated action utilities. Use paired participant bootstrap intervals with fold-local weights and report fold instability. Do not make a subgroup claim with fewer than 200 eligible participants or 30 target events in a stratum.

## Falsification and interpretation

Retain source and schema hashes, exact headers, row counts, key uniqueness, joins, overlap agreement, gate counts, timing checks, code/date pairing checks, the deliberately broken pairing test, leakage scan, fold manifest, model predictions and all weight diagnostics. Permute endpoint labels within fold and repeat-selection strata; the history increment and H_landmark should collapse toward null. Permute prior panels within landmark-age, current-risk, interval, missingness and selection strata while preserving current values and spacing. Recompute current-only versus spacing-only versus prior-value nested contrasts. Compare landmark-age and recruitment-age contrasts, continuous-age and fixed-stratum analyses, common-support and repeat-selection weighting, complete-case and fold-local missingness handling, and calibration/resolution rather than discrimination alone.

Supportive evidence requires passed biomarker, outcome and assessment gates; at least 100 target events; minimum age-group support; adequate common-support and repeat-selection overlap/ESS; no integrity failure; and a predeclared non-negligible direction and practical margin (for example absolute Brier difference 0.001 plus a threshold-crossing or calibration criterion) with uncertainty reported. Stronger support requires consistency after calibration, selection weighting, age-definition sensitivity and nonlinear sensitivity. A landmark-age contrast alone is not sufficient.

Adverse evidence is a null or opposite H_landmark, a gain explained by spacing or baseline calibration, disappearance after overlap/selection weighting, or a landmark-age result that materially disagrees with recruitment-age findings. That would weaken a robust age-specific history-retention interpretation while leaving current-value and spacing information open. A nonlinear pattern that fails across held-out folds is also adverse.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed semantic/timing/ascertainment gates, fewer than 100 target events, sparse landmark-age cells, poor overlap or ESS, unstable weighting, endpoint ambiguity, broken code/date pairing, materially different prespecified missingness/endpoint results, or an unverified age-at-landmark proxy. Inconclusive is not evidence against the hypothesis.

## Alternatives and scientific limits

The selected alternative is the age-aligned nested design because it changes the decision-time estimand using already present fields while preserving the parent’s validated decomposition. The exact comparison and selection reasoning are recorded in work/ukb-age-timing-alternatives-v1.md.

A reliability-aware latent persistent-burden model is retained but deferred. It would decompose the two observations into persistent and nonpersistent components and could distinguish stable burden from transient discordance, but two nontechnical observations do not identify assay error separately from biological change and no specimen timing or assay-quality metadata is verified. Revisit it with technical replicates, specimen timing or a clinically specified latent measurement model.

The UKB expert seeds on infection severity, organ aging, kidney-function discordance, fat distribution, sleep, cardiovascular risk after infection, CHIP, cancer reserve, metabolic deterioration and genetic-risk protection were inspected as alternatives. The local catalog does not verify the required proteomic identities, raw imaging, accelerometry, narrative disease staging, sequence variants, PRS, or adequate event chronology for those claims. They remain unselected/repairable imported ideas, not evidence or parents of this successor. The metabolic deterioration seed is conceptually adjacent but does not resolve the decision-age timing issue. No biomarker identity, PRS, disease ascertainment, infection stage or organ mechanism is invented.

The demonstrations and methods summary informed the distinction between a transparent nested baseline and a learned nonlinear alternative.
