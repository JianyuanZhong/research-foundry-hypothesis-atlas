# Episode 8 successor: does transfer-proximal worsening add information beyond current physiology and charting?

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`, the highest-ranked assessed-valid MIMIC candidate at episode start (mu 34.031, sigma 6.168, two comparisons).

This is a substantive estimand repair. The parent asks whether repeated final-day instability adds information beyond a last observation and observation density. This child asks the more clinically interpretable unresolved question of whether the observed state is worsening as transfer approaches. It also repairs an algebraic ambiguity in the prior trajectory child: if recent burden is already in the model, `recent - prior` is not an independent predictor. The primary model therefore adds prior-window burden at fixed recent burden and reports the equivalent worsening contrast as a derived, pre-specified contrast. The parent’s final-day repeated-burden contrast remains a secondary benchmark.

## Unresolved question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

ICU transfer removes continuous high-intensity monitoring and rescue capacity. A patient may have a high final-day burden because it is persistent but stable, or because the burden has recently worsened. Repeated charting is also more common in patients who are ill or whose clinicians are concerned. The unresolved question is whether a transfer-proximal change in observed physiology identifies risk beyond the current final-day state, last values, and the observation process itself.

The strongest claim currently supported is provenance and feasibility only: the frozen MIMIC-IV 3.1 core snapshot contains linked admissions, patients, ICU stays, timestamped ICU chart events, the ICU item dictionary, transfers, and in-hospital death/discharge fields; direct source-header and dictionary inspection confirms the required columns and fixed item labels. The expert seed and demonstration papers motivate longitudinal state representation and ascertainment auditing, but they do not establish this association. No prior result establishes that worsening is independent of charting density, predicts a competing ICU-return/death endpoint in this cohort, or identifies a safe discharge or surveillance policy.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among adults whose first eligible ICU stay in a hospital admission ends alive while that admission continues, patients with a lower prior-24-hour abnormal-vital burden at the same final-24-hour burden (equivalently, greater worsening into the final day) have lower risk of first ICU return or death before ICU return within seven days; adding the prior-window state to a model containing current physiology, last values, and measurement-density features improves locked-test competing-risk calibration and/or Brier score.

The estimand is prognostic association and incremental prediction at ICU `outtime`. It is not a causal effect of discharge timing, oxygen therapy, surveillance, or treatment. A positive result would justify prospective evaluation of trajectory-aware post-ICU monitoring; it would not justify changing discharge policy.

## Population and time-zero construction

Build the cohort and select the index before reading chart exposures or future outcome rows. Parse all times as timezone-naive datetimes and record unmatched keys and impossible chronology.

1. Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`.
2. Candidate index stays require `anchor_age >= 18`, nonmissing `intime`, `outtime`, and `admissions.dischtime`, `outtime < dischtime`, ICU duration at least one hour, and `deathtime` missing or strictly later than `outtime). Do not use chart rows or later outcomes for eligibility.
3. Within each `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, sort candidates by `intime), then numeric `stay_id), and select exactly one earliest candidate. Later eligible stays are possible ICU-return outcomes, not additional primary opportunities. Retain all eligible non-index stays for chronology audit and an all-eligible-stay sensitivity.
4. Set `t0 = index outtime`. Define the recent window R as `[t0 - 24 hours, t0)` and the prior window P as `[t0 - 48 hours, t0 - 24 hours)`, each divided into six consecutive four-hour blocks. Exclude `charttime >= t0), rows before `t0-48h), and rows from another `stay_id`. `storetime` never defines eligibility.
5. The paired trajectory estimand requires at least four of six blocks with valid vital-domain coverage in both R and P. This paired gate is fixed before outcome analysis. All eligible indexes, including those failing it, remain in the aggregate manifest and are compared by outcome, care unit, anchor-year group, and density. The all-eligible parent estimand is retained as a secondary analysis using R only.

For the resource limit in `inputs.json` (`science_seconds=7200`), use a two-stage projection-only implementation: read the small admissions/patients/icustays/transfers members to freeze the index/outcome manifest, then make one sequential pass through `mimic-iv-3.1/icu/chartevents.csv.gz` inside the ZIP, retaining only selected index stays, the fixed item IDs, and rows in `[t0-48h,t0)`. Do not materialize all chartevents or rescan the archive per item. Use bounded memory, declared CPU/memory/time, checkpoints where useful, aggregate/audit/model outputs only, and fail closed if the one-pass projection or output manifest cannot complete.

## Fixed item manifest and deterministic exposure construction

Direct inspection of `icu/d_items.csv.gz` verified:

- `220045` Heart Rate, bpm
- `220052` Arterial Blood Pressure mean, mmHg
- `220181` Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean, mmHg
- `220210` Respiratory Rate, insp/min
- `220277` O2 saturation pulseoxymetry, %
- `223761` Temperature Fahrenheit, °F
- `223762` Temperature Celsius, °C
- `223834` O2 Flow, L/min
- `227287` O2 Flow (additional cannula), L/min
- `227582` BiPap O2 Flow, L/min
- `223835` Inspired O2 Fraction, dictionary unit None
- `226732` O2 Delivery Device(s), text
- `223758` Code Status, text

For retained rows, apply validity before summaries and retain raw `value, valueuom, warning, charttime, storetime, itemid` only in an internal audit. Derived files contain aggregate features and no raw clinical rows or notes.

Use `valuenum) for numeric features. Validity is fixed: HR 20–250; RR 4–80; SpO2 50–100; each MAP 20–200 mmHg; temperature 30–43°C after Fahrenheit conversion `(F-32)/1.8`; oxygen flow nonnegative; FiO2 accepted as 0.21–1.00 or values in (1,100] divided by 100, with normalized values outside 0.21–1.00 invalid. Count invalid values by item and block; exclude them rather than repair them.

A block is vital-covered if it contains at least one valid HR, MAP, RR, SpO2, or temperature. A valid vital is abnormal when HR <50 or >100, RR <8 or >24, SpO2 <92%, MAP <65 mmHg, temperature <36°C or >=38°C. Arterial and non-invasive MAP remain separate; combined MAP is the minimum valid MAP within a block.

For each window calculate covered blocks, abnormal covered blocks, abnormal-block fraction, six-block abnormal/covered vectors, valid/invalid row counts, per-domain counts, last-value snapshots, and missing/observed indicators. A last value is the median of valid observations at the latest `charttime`; ties at a timestamp are resolved by median. Missing domains remain missing. Oxygen features are maximum valid flow across the three flow IDs and maximum normalized FiO2, plus item-specific observed indicators. Device and code-status text contribute only observed indicators and restricted raw-value frequency audits; they are never interpreted as support mode, delivered FiO2, treatment limitation, or goals of care.

Define `recent_burden = abnormal_block_fraction_R` and `prior_burden = abnormal_block_fraction_P`. The clinically interpretable worsening contrast is `recent_burden - prior_burden`. Because `recent_burden) is already in `B_phys`, do not put both this delta and `prior_burden` into the same primary regression as separate free predictors. The primary nonredundant parameterization is `B_traj = B_phys + prior_burden + prior-window coverage/valid-count features`; report the derived risk contrast for a prespecified increase in delta at fixed recent burden. This directly tests whether a stable current abnormal state differs from one that recently worsened. The parent’s R-only burden contrast is secondary.

## Outcomes and estimands

For each index define `tau = min(t0 + 7 days, admissions.dischtime)`; include events at `tau). A qualifying ICU return is a later ICU stay with the same `subject_id, hadm_id`, a different `stay_id`, and `intime >= t0); select the earliest by `intime), then numeric `stay_id`. A stay with `intime < t0` is an overlap/chronology anomaly, not a return.

- Cause 1: first qualifying ICU return at or before `tau`, strictly earlier than qualifying death.
- Cause 2: `admissions.deathtime > t0` and `deathtime <= tau), strictly earlier than the first qualifying ICU return.
- Exact return/death ties are indeterminate and excluded from the primary competing-risk fit; sensitivity analyses assign all ties to return and all to death.
- No cause by `tau` is censored at `tau`; death after return is not cause 2.
- The composite is cause 1 or cause 2. Descriptive sensitivities use 48-hour, 12-hour, and through-discharge horizons.

Report Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence for each cause and the composite. The primary estimand is the paired locked-test difference in seven-day competing-risk Brier score and calibration slope/intercept for `B_traj` versus `B_phys`, plus the development-cohort-SD standardized association of the derived worsening contrast. `B_phys) versus `B_density` and the all-eligible R-only analysis are secondary. Absolute-risk contrasts use development-defined 10th and 90th percentiles only for descriptive scale and are not bedside thresholds.

## Baselines and analysis

Use identical paired records, labels, preprocessing, subject partitions, and locked test set for all primary trajectory models.

- `B0`: gender, anchor_age, admission_type, admission_location, insurance, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU LOS, hours from `admittime` to `t0`, and number of earlier ICU stays ending by `t0`, with missing indicators. `anchor_year_group` is a descriptive stratum and optional common categorical covariate, never a chronological split.
- `B_last`: `B0) plus last valid HR, separate/combined MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, oxygen flow and FiO2, observed/missing indicators.
- `B_density`: `B_last` plus six-block R coverage, total valid vital rows, per-domain counts/observed indicators, oxygen observation indicators, and fixed `log1p` counts.
- `B_phys`: `B_density` plus R abnormal-block count/fraction and per-domain R abnormal indicators.
- `B_traj`: `B_phys` plus P burden and P coverage/valid-count features. No redundant delta term is fitted beside P burden; the derived worsening contrast is reported at fixed R burden.

Fit two ridge-penalized cause-specific Cox models (first return; death before return), selecting penalty only by inner development cross-validation. Derive cumulative incidence from cause-specific hazards; do not treat competing causes as ordinary censoring in Aalen–Johansen reporting. Partition by subject-level SHA-256 into 60% development, 20% validation, and 20% locked test, keeping every admission and stay of a subject together. Development alone determines imputation, one-hot levels, scaling, penalty and any coverage model. Prohibit outcome-derived cut points, post-`t0` fields, future charting, and feature search after the split.

Use 1,000 fixed-seed subject-bootstrap resamples of the locked test set for paired 95% intervals for Brier, calibration and absolute-risk differences, and subject-clustered bootstrap or robust estimates for coefficients. Require the overall gate of at least 1,000 primary indexes, 100 composite events, and at least 60% of all eligible indexes with >=4/6 R coverage. Require the paired trajectory gate of at least 800 paired indexes, 80 composite events, and at least 50% of all eligible indexes meeting both-window coverage. For cause-specific inference require at least 20 locked-test composite events and at least 10 of each cause. Failed gates are reported as sparse/infeasible; thresholds are never relaxed. If a cause gate fails, report descriptive Aalen–Johansen estimates and uncertainty only for that cause.

## Falsification, integrity, and interpretation

The reference solver must emit an aggregate manifest and a machine-readable conclusion ledger linking every conclusion to output names/rows. The verifier checks correct computation and whether conclusions follow from results; it must not reward confirmation of the hypothesis.

Fail closed on any feature with `charttime >= t0`, a row from another stay, `storetime)-based eligibility, duplicate primary index, impossible chronology, prohibited predictor, subject leakage, validation/test preprocessing fit, inconsistent tie assignment, or raw clinical rows/notes in derived outputs.

Required checks:

1. Shift both windows earlier by 24 hours to P'=`[t0-72h,t0-48h)` and R'=`[t0-48h,t0-24h)`, holding indexes and outcomes fixed. A similar or stronger result there weakens a transfer-proximal interpretation.
2. Compare `B_traj) versus `B_phys) within fixed strata of recent coverage and total valid-row density. If the gain disappears, interpret it as ascertainment/general acuity rather than independent physiology.
3. Repeat the trajectory contrast with abnormal valid-observation fraction, arterial and non-invasive MAP separately, two-hour ICU-return gap, tie assignments, 48-hour/12-hour horizons, and all-eligible-stay clustering. Large divergence is definition/selection dependence.
4. Fit the parent’s R-only `B_density` versus `B_phys` contrast on the same paired records. A trajectory claim cannot be supported by a final-day burden gain alone.
5. Permute outcome labels within locked subject partitions while preserving all features and coverage; performance must return to chance within bootstrap uncertainty. Failure invalidates computation, not the clinical hypothesis.
6. Audit burden, derived worsening, coverage, invalid rates, unmatched joins, and raw code-status/device-value frequencies by outcome, care unit, anchor-year group, and partition. Do not perform lexical goals-of-care classification.

Supportive results require all of: a prespecified direction for the worsening contrast; a locked-test Brier and/or calibration improvement for `B_traj` over `B_phys` with a paired interval excluding zero; risk separation that is not merely a tiny discrimination change; no material erasure after density-stratified, earlier-window, MAP-source, tie, horizon, and return-gap checks; and successful label permutation. An oxygen increment is secondary and supportive only if `B_full` improves over `B_phys` under the same locked evaluation; it remains charted oxygen, not delivered oxygen.

Adverse results are a null or reversed worsening association, no incremental Brier/calibration benefit, or disappearance after density, earlier-window, competing-risk, source, or selection checks. They argue that observed worsening adds no reliable information beyond current burden and observation intensity, or is mainly a documentation/general-acuity proxy. They do not prove ICU transfer is safe.

Inconclusive results include failed overall/paired/event gates, sparse or unstable intervals, contradictory sensitivity results, inability to separate trajectory from density, or failed computation. A failed permutation check is computational invalidity. No result supports a discharge or surveillance policy without external validation and prospective evaluation.

## What is and is not established

Computationally checkable claims are source/member/schema availability, fixed-item labels, exact cohort and index construction, timestamp ordering, coverage and invalid-value counts, exposure summaries, event labels, model estimates, uncertainty, calibration, missingness, density dependence, and falsification outputs.

This archive cannot adjudicate true delivered FiO2, work of breathing, bedside instability, functional/cognitive readiness, clinician discharge intent, staffing or ward capacity, treatment limitation from code-status text, or the adjudicated reason for ICU return. It cannot establish causal effects of discharge timing, monitoring, oxygen, treatment or surveillance; safe thresholds; external validity; transportability; or clinical utility. Those claims require clinician review of raw records and bedside context, richer prospective physiology and treatment data, external validation in another ICU system, and a prospective surveillance or target-trial study.
