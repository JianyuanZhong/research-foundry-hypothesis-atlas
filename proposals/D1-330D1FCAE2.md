# Discharge recovery versus observation intensity after TACE in HCC

## Status and hypothesis

This is an episode-4 substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`, using the same HCC snapshot. It is an observational prognostic study, not a treatment-effect, discharge-policy, or clinical-utility study.

The primary hypothesis is:

> Among adults with HCC who are discharged after a first observed TACE episode, discharge-proximal recovery information (the last 24 hours before discharge compared with the first 72 hours after TACE) adds reproducible held-out information about a subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days beyond baseline hepatic reserve, early post-TACE change, prior utilization, and the intensity of laboratory/encounter recording before discharge.

The clinically sharper nested hypothesis is:

> Persistent non-recovery at discharge is more strongly associated with an admission in days 0–7 than with an admission in days 8–30, and the association remains after adjustment/stratification for discharge duration and pre-discharge observation density.

This distinguishes a possible discharge-time vulnerability signal from a marker of planned surveillance or merely having more opportunity to measure. It does not establish that delaying discharge or changing monitoring improves outcomes.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The inspected natural-history demonstration (Shmatko et al., Nature 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09529-3) supports the value of time-ordered health trajectories and strict temporal testing, but it studies UK Biobank/Danish diagnosis histories and does not establish a post-TACE laboratory phenotype in this HCC source. The cancer demonstration's main article is unavailable; I inspected only its supplementary PDF/text, which documents large longitudinal multimodal cohorts, washout checks, validation and missingness-oriented architecture. It does not establish a valid HCC discharge phenotype here, and this HCC snapshot has no images, staging, validated treatment-response labels, or external cohort. The Bayesian EHR/genetic demonstration (Urbut et al., Nature 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10780-5) supports modelling longitudinal disease signatures and selection explicitly, but the HCC source has no validated germline/PRS modality and the present question is a small, time-anchored prognostic test rather than genetic discovery.

The strongest claim supported by those demonstrations and the locally inspected evidence is that longitudinal, temporally aligned clinical records can contain reproducible prognostic information when leakage and observation processes are handled. Existing evidence does not show that routine laboratory change after TACE, especially persistence at discharge, adds information beyond baseline reserve and care-record intensity for short-term return.

The unresolved claim is therefore whether a discharge-proximal recovery block has an observation-process-robust prognostic association. This matters because post-procedure teams decide whom to monitor closely and how to plan follow-up before discharge. A reproducible signal would justify a prospective study with adjudicated decompensation and a specified monitoring pathway; a null or process-sensitive result would caution against turning routine laboratory trajectories into an escalation rule.

## Outcomes

1. Primary: subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days.
2. Timing secondary: admission in days 0–7 and, separately, days 8–30 after discharge; do not double count an individual in both intervals.
3. Liver-focused secondary signal: a later encounter in the same window with a diagnosis name containing one of the prespecified observed terms `腹水`/`腹腔积液`, `肝性脑病`/`肝昏迷`, `食管胃底静脉曲张`, or `消化道出血`; report components separately and do not call this adjudicated decompensation.
4. Observation-process comparator: a later encounter with a nonmissing `就诊时间` but missing `入院时间` in the 30-day window, if the field availability audit supports this definition. Call it a non-inpatient-coded recorded encounter, not an outpatient visit; it is a negative/measurement-process comparator, not a clinical endpoint.

The liver-coded signal uses `diagnoses) joined to the later encounter. `clinical_documents` (`[internal dataset path]`, table `clinical_documents`) and `examinations` (`[internal dataset path]`, table `examinations`) are reserved for a small blinded expert-adjudication sample only if expert review is actually supplied. Documents have no temporal column and examinations have `开始时间`, `检查所见`, `检查诊断`, `检查`, `机器型号`, `检查号`; the catalog's lexical detector is not a clinical diagnosis extractor. Without an adjudication artifact, Harbor must not claim that coded liver signals are true decompensation.

## Analysis

Fit predeclared patient-level, fold-contained models:

* M0: age, sex, department, index calendar period, baseline core values, prior 30-day/encounter utilization, and recorded diagnosis-history indicators available before index.
* M1: M0 plus the early-minus-baseline core laboratory block.
* M2: M1 plus discharge-minus-early core block and observation-process covariates.
* M3: M2 plus missingness/process indicators, only as sensitivity.

Use penalized logistic regression as the transparent primary model, with a bounded gradient-boosted-tree alternative only if the event gate and resource limits permit. The same folds and predictors are used for both. Preprocess, impute and standardize within training folds. Split by participant, preserve discovery buckets, and report a chronological holdout by index date. No post-discharge data enters features.

The primary estimand is the held-out incremental predictive value of M2 over M1 for 30-day subsequent inpatient admission: paired out-of-fold log loss and Brier score difference, calibration slope/intercept and discrimination as secondary summaries, with bootstrap confidence intervals clustered by participant and fold. Also report the fitted discharge-block association with robust uncertainty, but do not treat it as causal. The nested discharge cohort is analyzed separately; it cannot replace the larger primary cohort.

Prespecify an adequacy gate before inferential fitting: at least 20 outcome events and at least 10 events per effective fitted degree of freedom after the discovery filter; otherwise provide feasibility/descriptive results only. Apply a stricter gate (at least 20 events and adequate events-per-degree) to the liver-coded and 0–7-day analyses. If gates fail, no positive or null clinical claim is made.

## Falsification and robustness

* Refit with and without observation-process covariates. A large attenuation after process adjustment is evidence that the apparent signal may be recording/discharge-selection driven.
* Stratify or interact by index length of stay (96–168 versus >168 hours, with prespecified alternative quantiles if cell sizes fail), calendar period, and department; do not interpret heterogeneous strata with inadequate events.
* Compare 0–7 versus 8–30-day associations. A signal only in the later interval or only for the non-inpatient-coded comparator is a surveillance concern.
* Run a timing placebo using a pre-index pseudo-window of equal duration, and a within-discovery permutation that preserves assay-wise missingness and participant-level event prevalence but breaks the laboratory trajectory/outcome pairing. These should be null within uncertainty.
* Recompute using expanded seven-assay features, complete cases and missingness indicators, and alternative medians (last valid value versus median) without changing the primary definition.
* Audit duplicate joins, impossible times, assay label drift, numeric parsing, units absent, lab density, discharge eligibility, and outcome counts in every split. No full-file feasibility count is an estimate.

## Interpretation

Supportive evidence requires adequate events, improvement of held-out M2 over M1 with uncertainty excluding no incremental value, directionally coherent assay-level effects, persistence after process adjustment and across temporal/LOS checks, stronger short-latency than late-only association, and null timing/permutation placebos. This supports a reproducible prognostic signal in this record, not a diagnosis, causal effect, discharge rule, or clinical benefit.

Adverse evidence is no incremental value, reversal across folds/time, substantial loss after observation adjustment, equal prediction of the non-inpatient-coded comparator, or non-null timing placebos. That would argue against interpreting the trajectory as a specific physiologic discharge-risk signal, though it cannot prove no biological relationship.

Inconclusive evidence includes failure of event gates, unstable estimates, missing/ambiguous assay semantics, incompatible joins, or absent adjudication. It warrants a scoped feasibility report or another study, not a forced conclusion. Clinical adjudication of decompensation, treatment intent, unplannedness, mortality, stage, transplant status, discharge appropriateness, and benefit from monitoring/delayed discharge requires expert review, richer data, or a prospective study.
