# Proposal: does the early component trajectory add information beyond a pre-index recorded care plan?

## Episode 23 successor and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves that parent’s exact HCC source bindings, encounter-constrained first TACE-like index, day-45 risk set, component-resolved AFP/albumin/total-bilirubin trajectory construction, strict-versus-broad competing pathway ontology, procedure coding/capture controls, patient-level temporal split, uncertainty, and evidence boundary.

The remaining clinically consequential ambiguity is different from endpoint classification: a later recorded TACE-containing episode may have been operationally anticipated before the day-45 biomarker window. An order is not treatment intent, completion, or a clinical plan, but a dated non-drug order can be an observable care-process precursor. If the trajectory only separates patients who already had a pre-index recorded therapeutic order, it is not evidence that the trajectory itself adds useful reassessment information. Conversely, a trajectory contrast that persists among patients without such a precursor would justify a more consequential next study of biomarker-informed reassessment.

Completion requires: (1) frozen training-only order vocabulary and audit; (2) the same index, landmark, trajectory and endpoint manifests as the parent; (3) locked predictions from transparent history/coding/plan and trajectory models; (4) a same-target joint latent trajectory/order-observation alternative or an explicit convergence deferral; (5) patient-clustered uncertainty, calibration, overlap and negative-control outputs; and (6) an interpretation manifest linking every claim to an estimate, interval, endpoint and frozen rule. ## Evidence-supported claim, unresolved question, and hypothesis

Available evidence supports only that the HCC snapshot contains dated procedures, encounters, laboratories, examinations and non-drug orders that can be related through `patient master index, encounter number`, and that the parent’s procedure vocabulary can distinguish strict, broad, diagnostic, mixed and non-liver recorded states. The source does not establish tumor burden, viable tumor, radiologic response, indication, treatment intent, technical completion, laboratory units, death, outside care, or benefit.

The unresolved question is:

> Among day-45-eligible adults after an encounter-constrained first recorded TACE-like episode, does the early joint AFP/albumin/total-bilirubin trajectory provide incremental information about a later strict TACE-containing recorded pathway after accounting for a pre-index recorded therapeutic-order signal, and is that incremental information different when such a signal is present versus absent?

Primary hypothesis, stated as an estimand: at 320 days after the day-45 landmark, the standardized difference in cumulative incidence of first `TACE_strict` between the parent’s prespecified favorable component pattern (AFP decrease with non-worsening albumin and total bilirubin) and its prespecified adverse contrast (AFP decrease with worsening of either hepatic component) is larger among patients with no qualifying pre-index therapeutic-order proxy than among patients with one. Formally, with `p=0` plan-negative and `p=1` plan-positive, the primary interaction is

`Gamma(320) = [CIF_strict(g_favorable | p=0) - CIF_strict(g_adverse | p=0)] - [CIF_strict(g_favorable | p=1) - CIF_strict(g_adverse | p=1)]`.

The prespecified direction is `Gamma(320) > 0`: the trajectory is more discriminating when a later pathway was not already visibly queued. The equal or opposite result is scientifically meaningful: it would favor a scheduling/care-process explanation over incremental trajectory information. The same contrast is reported at 30 and 90 post-landmark days and for the parent’s broad therapeutic, diagnostic-only, mixed/ambiguous and non-liver capture-control outcomes.

This does not estimate a treatment effect, a causal effect of an order, response, recurrence, survival, or utility. It is an associational incremental-recorded-pathway estimand. It could inform whether a future prospective reassessment study should target patients without an already recorded pathway, but it cannot support a treatment recommendation.

## Frozen order-plan exposure

The vocabulary is developed on 2010–2022 training data only, audited by raw label, row count, distinct key count, missingness and time-field coverage, and frozen before tuning/test evaluation. The strict plan vocabulary may include only labels with literal `TACE`, exact `transarterial chemoembolization`, or a training-audited explicit transcatheter hepatic-arterial embolization phrase. A label containing only `angiography`, `examination`, imaging language, or an unresolved compound/mixed meaning is not strict-plan positive. A broad-plan sensitivity may include additional explicitly therapeutic liver-directed terms, but its complete list, exclusions and ambiguous labels are published before locked evaluation.

Primary `plan_positive` means at least one strict-vocabulary order row for the patient with nonmissing `order time` strictly before the index episode’s `start time`, and with its order encounter joined to a valid encounter record. Restricting the primary proxy to order opening before index avoids treating a post-index order as pre-existing information. The primary lookback is 180 days before index; 365-day lookback and same-encounter-only definitions are prespecified sensitivities. Rows with missing order opening time cannot define plan status and are counted as unknown, not negative. The status/order name/time/encounter are preserved in an audit manifest.

A secondary `early_plan` descriptor counts strict-plan orders whose `order time` or `start time` lies from index time through day 45. It is not in the primary causal language and is not used to claim that an order caused a later procedure. It is reported as a mediator/care-process descriptor and in a sensitivity model because it may encode the pathway between the index and the landmark. `order status`, duration and frequency are descriptive covariates only; they do not prove execution.

## Outcomes and preserved endpoint semantics

Use the parent’s mutually exclusive first recorded states from days 46–365:

1. `TACE_strict` primary: timed episode containing literal `TACE` or a prespecified training-only arterial chemoembolization/embolization label that is not angiography-only and is not unresolved mixed-only.
2. `Liver_therapeutic_broad` secondary: parent broad liver-directed therapeutic ontology, including TACE, ablation, resection, transplant and other frozen therapeutic families, never angiography alone.
3. `Liver_diagnostic`: hepatic angiography, liver imaging/procedure work-up or diagnostic-only liver contact under the frozen lexical screen.
4. `Mixed_ambiguous`: compound/unresolved strings not safely classifiable as strict therapy.
5. `Nonliver_procedure`: first timed procedure outside the liver-directed therapeutic/diagnostic ontology, as capture control.
6. `No recorded transition` and administrative end of local observation, reported separately.

The strict-versus-broad ladder is fixed before evaluation, not selected for a favorable result. `Surgery Source` is retained for coding/source controls but never treated as intent. Episode construction is repeated with encounter-only grouping as a sensitivity.

## Baseline and substantive alternative on the same target

All models use the same day-45 cohort, plan definition, endpoints, split, information boundary and uncertainty procedure.

- `B0-history`: regularized discrete-time cause-specific competing hazards using age, sex, index year/department, pre-index HCC diagnosis/procedure counts, contact history and local follow-up opportunity.
- `Bcode/obs`: B0 plus measurement/capture features already permitted by the parent: encounter and procedure intensity, source mix, episode/coding density, assay availability, examinations/orders opportunity, missingness and ambiguous-label indicators.
- `Bplan`: Bcode/obs plus pre-index strict-plan status, order count, days from first qualifying order to index, same-encounter indicator and unknown status; order text is represented only by the frozen category, never a free-text embedding.
- `Btraj`: Bplan plus the parent’s separate standardized AFP, albumin and total-bilirubin changes, timing, missingness and component interaction; estimate `Gamma` directly with a plan-by-trajectory interaction. Fit both full-index and paired-observed versions.
- `Mplan` (substantive alternative): an irregular-time multi-output state-space model with separate assay-specific latent AFP, albumin and bilirubin states, an observed dated order/capture process, and competing transition hazards. The order process has a pre-index plan state and a post-index early-plan intensity, while the outcome hazard includes the latent component state and their interaction with plan state. It can reveal asynchronous component change and whether an apparent trajectory contrast disappears when informative ordering/observation is modeled continuously, information that the categorized Btraj model loses. It is fitted only through day 45 and evaluated on exactly the same `Gamma`, endpoint ladder and split; report posterior/standardized contrasts, log score, calibration, effective sample size/overlap and convergence diagnostics.

The transparent baseline is scientifically necessary because it answers the incremental question directly and shows whether the result survives prior history, coding and the order proxy. Mplan is retained because the ambiguity is not merely predictive accuracy: order timing and laboratory sampling may be informative processes, and categorizing them can hide asynchronous latent trajectories. A generic transformer is deferred; no image, validated response, intent, units, death or external-care dependency is solved by additional model capacity. If Mplan fails to converge or produces unstable observation-process sensitivity, Btraj remains a computable descriptive estimand and the stronger latent interpretation is deferred.

## Split, uncertainty, falsification and decision criteria

Use 2010–2022 for fitting, 2023 for tuning, and 2024 through 2025-01-01 as locked test when event support permits; otherwise use a deterministic patient-hash split declared before fitting. Patients cannot cross splits. Freeze procedure episode rules, order vocabulary, lookback, trajectory tolerances, endpoint ladder, sparse-cell rules, weighting and model tuning before the locked test.

Use patient bootstrap or patient-clustered sandwich uncertainty, never row bootstrap. Report cause-specific hazards, CIF at 30/90/320 days after day 45, calibration, log score, overlap/effective sample size, `Gamma` and component-specific absolute contrasts with intervals. Any observation weighting is predictive standardization only, fit on training data and positivity-gated; it is not causal adjustment.

Supportive computation requires: (a) `Gamma(320)` in the prespecified positive direction with a patient-level interval excluding the materiality margin, (b) compatible direction at 30 and 90 days and on strict and broad therapeutic tiers, (c) a trajectory contribution beyond Bplan rather than only beyond B0, (d) stronger specificity for therapeutic than diagnostic and non-liver outcomes, (e) no comparable pre-index placebo trajectory signal, no concentration in mixed labels or one assay variant, (f) stable encounter-only sensitivity, adequate plan-stratum overlap and calibration, and (g) compatible Mplan contrasts or a transparently documented reason the alternative was deferred. This supports only incremental association with a local recorded pathway, strongest in the observed plan stratum; it does not establish biological response or clinical benefit.

Adverse computation is a null/reversed interaction, a trajectory effect that vanishes after Bplan, similar effects for diagnostic/non-liver/placebo outcomes, concentration in plan-positive or ambiguous coding states, endpoint/episode instability, or order/trajectory effects that are not transportable across time. This favors prior scheduling, coding, observation or measurement explanations. Inconclusive computation is sparse strict events or plan strata, unknown-order dominance, positivity failure, insufficient follow-up, unstable assay vocabulary, temporal-split failure, intervals crossing the margin, or Mplan nonconvergence/observation sensitivity. These outcomes do not establish absence of biological response.

The verifier can check source paths and hashes, schemas, two-key aggregation, date inequalities, no leakage, index/risk-set construction, order vocabulary and plan status, endpoint exclusivity, competing-risk calculations, predictions, calibration, uncertainty and whether conclusions map to computed outputs. It cannot adjudicate whether an order represented intent, whether a procedure was completed or appropriate, radiologic response, viable tumor, recurrence, liver failure, death, survival, outside care, treatment benefit, utility or external validity. Those claims require clinical adjudication, richer linkage or another prospective/external study.
