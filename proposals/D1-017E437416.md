# Episode-9 strict repair: availability-safe post-shock covariates

## Child status and scope

This is a substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. It preserves the parent’s clinical question, 24-hour treatment window, delivery-coherent IV loop exposure, observation-bounded documented liberation estimand, combined ICU/hospital RRT endpoint, and corrected SHA256 discovery/reserved partition. It makes one material repair: every landmark and clone-hour covariate must be in the information set actually available at that time.

The parent’s other definitions remain operative unless explicitly replaced below. This is a precise repair specification for the compiler; it is not a new population, broader exposure, or claim about unobserved ward or post-discharge outcomes.

## Defect and why it matters

The source audit found that the parent names charttime/event time for several post-shock observations and says that predictors are “available by t0” or by each clone-hour, but does not impose storetime <= that analysis time uniformly. The audited MIMIC-IV 3.1 members have distinct documentation/availability fields: storetime exists in ICU chart, input, output and procedure events, hospital labs, and radiology notes. Thus, a late-entered MAP, urine/balance event, ventilator procedure, laboratory result, or radiology report could be treated as known at t0 or at a weighting hour even though it was stored later. This can alter eligibility, treatment-confounder adjustment and censoring weights, creating a verifier-accepted causal result that does not represent the stated decision-time estimand.

The repair is an availability-safe information set. For analysis time tau, a row is usable for eligibility, baseline adjustment, or a clone-hour model only when its clinical time is no later than tau and its nonmissing storetime is no later than tau. A row with clinical time before tau but storetime > tau is late-entry and unavailable at tau; it is excluded from that information set, counted, and never recovered by imputation from future records. This repairs causal design without changing the treatment question.

## Question, hypothesis and estimand retained

Among adults who are invasively ventilated at ICU hour 48 after vasopressor-treated shock, are stable off vasopressors for six hours, have at least 50 mL/kg documented positive ICU balance, adequate recent urine output, potassium at least 3.0 mmol/L, and no loop administration in the preceding 12 hours, does initiating or re-initiating a delivered ICU-recorded IV furosemide or bumetanide within (t0,t0+24 hours] increase the probability of documented successful liberation from invasive ventilation by day 7 compared with withholding every loop diuretic for 24 hours?

The primary estimand remains the per-protocol risk difference A minus B for this eligible start/restart population, conditional on consistency, positivity, correct measurement and no unmeasured confounding. It is partially identified when MIMIC observation ends or has gaps. A success remains a structured extubation by t0+5 days followed by 48 hours alive with no accepted re-intubation or invasive ventilation, with the entire confirmation interval continuously inside observed ICU intervals. Death before confirmed liberation is failure; ICU/ward gaps, alive discharge, external transfer and post-discharge time are unknown, not success.

The clinical relevance gate remains a lower-bound liberation risk difference greater than 5 percentage points with its 95% bootstrap lower limit above zero. Safety gates remain an upper 95% bound below 3 points for excess recorded 28-day mortality and below 5 points for excess observed new RRT by day 7 under both RRT date brackets. These are decision thresholds, not proof of efficacy or renal safety.

## Interpretation and limits

Supportive requires the parent’s full conjunction: lower 95% bound for RD_L above zero and point estimate above 5 points; both mortality and both RRT safety bounds pass; expected process direction; adequate arm/ESS, exposure and outcome capture; no material late-entry distortion; and passed falsification. It supports external validation or a pragmatic randomized trial, not practice change, efficacy as fact, renal safety, or proof of congestion.

Adverse is the parent’s prespecified branch: upper 95% bound for RD_U <=0, or a prespecified mortality/RRT harm bound crossed under the appropriate RRT bracket. Favorable balance cannot rescue respiratory or safety harm.

Inconclusive includes RD_L failing support while RD_U remains compatible with benefit, wide sampling/identification intervals, positivity or ESS failure, material late-entry exclusion or differential availability, exposure/endpoint sensitivity conflict, RRT bracket-dependent classification, residual imbalance, or missing ward/post-discharge observation. Nonsignificance is not equivalence or safety.

Computationally checkable claims include archive hashes/members/columns, dictionary labels, partition, joins, time boundaries, storetime information sets, exposure classification, endpoint classes, RRT allowlist/date brackets, attrition, weights/ESS, uncertainty and result branch. Treatment indication, exchangeability, text phenotype truth, complete ward dialysis/ventilation capture, post-discharge outcomes, renal-congestion biology, transportability and treatment recommendations require clinical adjudication, unavailable evidence, external data or another study.
