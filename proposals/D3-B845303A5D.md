# Directional macrophage-to-fibroblast communication in rheumatoid arthritis

## Question

Does macrophage-derived inflammatory signaling have a donor-aware, directional influence on fibroblast activation in rheumatoid arthritis, beyond shared disease state, donor-specific biology, or fibroblast-autonomous activation?

## Clinical importance

Fibroblast activation drives synovial inflammation, cartilage destruction, and bone erosion in rheumatoid arthritis. Macrophages and fibroblasts communicate bidirectionally in the synovial microenvironment. If a macrophage-conditioned state has a robust, donor-aware fibroblast response, it supports a testable intervention point for macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk. If the signal is donor-specific or absent after paired comparison, it argues against treating observed macrophage-fibroblast co-expression as a directional mechanism.

The clinical consequence is significant: if macrophage-derived inflammatory signaling is a primary driver of fibroblast activation, then targeting macrophage-fibroblast communication could provide a new therapeutic strategy for RA, particularly for patients with aggressive disease or inadequate response to current therapies.

## Evidence and closest prior work

The available expression datasets include E-MTAB-8873, E-MTAB-8316, and GSE95588, all from rheumatoid arthritis synovial tissue. These datasets provide paired donor information and cell-type-specific expression profiles that enable a donor-aware comparison of macrophage-exposed versus unexposed fibroblast states.

[K1] provides evidence for macrophage-fibroblast communication in RA synovial tissue, including ligand-receptor pairs and pathway-level interactions. The work supports the biological plausibility of directional communication but does not establish causality at the ligand-receptor level.

[K2] provides evidence for fibroblast activation signatures in RA, including pro-inflammatory and matrix-remodeling programs. The work supports the existence of a measurable fibroblast activation state but does not specify the upstream driver.

[K3] provides evidence for donor-specific variation in RA synovial tissue expression, including differences in immune cell composition and inflammatory signaling. The work supports the need for donor-aware analysis but does not test directional macrophage-to-fibroblast communication.

## Hypothesis

Macrophage-derived inflammatory signaling has a donor-aware, directional influence on fibroblast activation in rheumatoid arthritis, beyond shared disease state or donor-specific biology.

## Competing explanations

1. Shared disease state: both macrophages and fibroblasts are activated by a common upstream signal, producing correlated expression without directional communication.
2. Donor-specific biology: individual donors have distinct inflammatory profiles that drive both macrophage and fibroblast states independently.
3. Fibroblast-autonomous activation: fibroblasts activate through intrinsic mechanisms, and macrophage expression is a parallel consequence rather than a driver.
4. Assay/platform artifacts: technical differences between datasets or cell types create spurious correlations.

## Design

The experiment uses a paired donor-aware comparison of macrophage-exposed versus unexposed fibroblast states across E-MTAB-8873, E-MTAB-8316, and GSE95588. The primary contrast is the difference in fibroblast activation signature between macrophage-exposed and unexposed states within the same donor.

The analysis will:
1. Identify fibroblast activation genes from the RA fibroblast literature.
2. Compute the donor-aware contrast for each gene.
3. Test for a consistent activation signature across donors.
4. Compare the signal strength before and after paired comparison.

## Falsification criteria

- Supportive: a consistent activation signature is observed in the paired donor-aware comparison, with signal strength preserved or enhanced after pairing.
- Adverse: the signal is absent or reversed after paired comparison, supporting donor-specific or shared-state explanations.
- Inconclusive: the signal is weak or inconsistent across donors, requiring larger cohorts or independent validation.

## Limitations

The available expression data cannot establish ligand-receptor causality. A directional interpretation requires perturbation or independent validation. The proposal explicitly limits the claim to a donor-aware association and does not claim direct macrophage-to-fibroblast causation.

## Next test

A perturbation experiment in which macrophage-derived inflammatory signaling is blocked or enhanced, with measurement of fibroblast activation, would provide stronger evidence for directional communication. This would require cell culture or animal models and is beyond the scope of the available expression data.
