# UKB CBC cancer-type contrast: absolute-risk boundary for timing and clinical selection

Status: proposed substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. No hypothesis test or full solver fit has been executed. This child preserves the parent's exact UKB namespace, paired-array rules, cancer classes and temporal design, and adds a prespecified decision-facing absolute-risk estimand and sharper source/selection classification.

## Scientific opening and claim boundary

CBC abnormalities are associated with cancer in primary-care records, and a recent longitudinal CRC study reports pre-diagnostic CBC trajectories. Those observations do not establish whether a CBC pattern is relatively CRC-specific rather than haematologic, whether a two-year delay removes near-diagnostic ascertainment, or whether the apparent contrast survives a different timed outcome source. The clinically consequential uncertainty is therefore interpretability: would the pattern represent a stable, cancer-type-specific risk contrast, or only registry timing, inpatient capture, or the selected subset who return for a repeat research assessment?

[K1] supports a broad primary-care CBC/cancer association but not cancer-type specificity or delayed UKB risk. [K2] motivates a 24-month longitudinal boundary but is CRC-only and explicitly investigational. [K3] shows why discrimination without calibration and external/temporal validation is insufficient for clinical claims. The inspected works are abstract-only; no unavailable supplement or full text is claimed.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is limited: CBC abnormalities and some longitudinal CBC summaries can associate with subsequent CRC-related or broad cancer outcomes in selected populations. The unresolved claim tested here is:

> Among UKB adults with a complete first CBC and no qualifying cancer at T0, the prespecified higher-platelet/lower-haemoglobin phenotype has a larger CRC than haematologic-cancer association in the delayed T0+2 to T0+5 interval, and that relative contrast has a clinically interpretable absolute-risk direction that is not produced by repeat-observation selection or by registry-specific timing/capture.

This is a descriptive, temporally ordered association. It is not a causal, cellular-mechanistic, diagnostic-accuracy, screening, clinical-utility, stage, treatment or referral-threshold claim. A result can support a next external validation without establishing that a clinician should act on an individual CBC.

## Population, windows and estimands

### Primary first-CBC population

Include unique nonmissing `eid` with parseable T0 and complete instance-0 WBC, haemoglobin, RDW and platelets. Exclude any valid qualifying registry cancer on or before T0. Do not require T1 or T2.

For the 0-2 year analysis, follow from T0 to the first CRC, haematologic cancer, other primary cancer, death, administrative censoring at 2022-12-31, or T0+2 years. For the delayed analysis, enter the risk set only if alive and free of all qualifying cancer at T0+2; follow to the first event, censoring, or T0+5 years. Events before T0+2 therefore do not become delayed events and their exclusions/counts are reported. This is a conditional delayed-risk estimand, not an unconditional five-year population risk.

### Relative D estimand

Standardize each baseline CBC variable using development-only means and scales. Fit separate cause-specific models for CRC and haematologic cancer, with other cancer and death as competing causes, using the four CBC values, age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP and T0 calendar year. In window w:

`D_s(w) = (beta_platelet,CRC,s,w - beta_Hb,CRC,s,w) - (beta_platelet,HEME,s,w - beta_Hb,HEME,s,w)`

where s is registry (R) or inpatient (I) outcome source. Positive D means the platelet/haemoglobin contrast is relatively more CRC-associated. Report the component coefficients, `D_R(0-2)`, `D_R(2-5)`, and the corresponding inpatient values; do not treat D as a risk ratio, causal effect or mechanism. The primary relative claim is delayed `D_R(2-5)>0), with 0-2 as a near-diagnostic/timing comparator.

### Absolute-risk / decision-facing estimand

Define two fixed, prespecified CBC profiles in development standard-deviation units:

- `g_CRC`: platelets +1 SD, haemoglobin -1 SD, WBC and RDW at their development means.
- `g_HEME`: platelets -1 SD, haemoglobin +1 SD, WBC and RDW at their development means.

For every individual in the relevant risk set, retain their observed age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP and T0 year, replace only the two platelet/haemoglobin values by each profile, and standardize the resulting model-based competing-risk predictions over that same risk set. Let `P_s(c,g,w)` be the Aalen-Johansen/model-standardized cumulative incidence of cancer class c during w under source s and profile g. The primary decision-facing contrast is the absolute-risk difference-in-differences:

`A_s(2-5) = [P_s(CRC,g_CRC,2-5) - P_s(CRC,g_HEME,2-5)] - [P_s(HEME,g_CRC,2-5) - P_s(HEME,g_HEME,2-5)]`.

Report the four component risks and the two class-specific risk differences as percentage points, not just A. Positive A means that the fixed profile ordering shifts absolute incidence toward CRC rather than haematologic cancer. Report the same estimand in 0-2 years and under the repeat-selection strata. A is a model-standardized association contrast; it is not an individual's diagnostic probability or a clinical action threshold.

Because the source has no validated decision threshold, the proposal uses an explicit descriptive effect-size boundary rather than inventing a referral rule: `delta_A=0.01` (one percentage point) for the delayed-window A contrast, with 95% bootstrap intervals. Decision-facing labels are prespecified as:

- decision-scale supportive: both source-specific intervals are entirely above +1.0 percentage point, D has the prespecified positive direction, and the registry/inpatient qualitative classification agrees;
- relative-only or inconclusive: D is positive but at least one A interval overlaps [-1.0,+1.0] percentage points, or there are too few paired events for source comparison;
- adverse to the proposed interpretation: either source interval is entirely below -1.0 percentage point, a source changes from above +1.0 to below -1.0, or the D direction reverses precisely.

This boundary is a transparent reporting boundary, not evidence that a one-percentage-point difference would justify testing, referral or treatment.

## Registry-versus-inpatient timing and selection falsifications

### Source bridge

Derive registry and inpatient event series independently from the exact paired arrays. Run the same cohort, delayed entry, covariates, competing events, standardization, D, A and temporal split under each source. Keep outputs side by side; do not select the source based on favorable final performance and do not average them.

Among participants with valid same-class events in both sources, report inpatient-date minus registry-date displacement by cancer class: median, IQR, quantiles, event count, and the fraction whose source date crosses T0+2 or T0+5. If fewer than 50 same-class paired events are available for a class, that class's displacement and source-robustness conclusion is inconclusive. A disagreement can reflect different clinical capture, not which source is correct.

Call the delayed interpretation source-sensitive if any prespecified condition holds: D direction reverses; the delayed A classification crosses the ±1 percentage-point boundary; `|D_R(2-5)-D_I(2-5)|>0.20` log-hazard units; the absolute delayed contrast differs by >1 percentage point; or a source-by-T0-calendar-era interaction is present with a 95% interval excluding zero. Concordance is only compatibility with robustness because registry and inpatient coding may share errors. Registry-positive/inpatient-negative is adverse to registry robustness, not proof that CBC biology is absent. Sparse overlap is inconclusive.

### Clinical/repeat-observation selection

Define `R=1` before using any cancer outcome as a complete T1 assessment strictly after T0 and within 2-8 years, with all four instance-1 CBC fields observed; `R=0` is otherwise. R is a future-attendance indicator for a sensitivity comparison, not a baseline predictor. The repeat-CBC cohort additionally requires no qualifying cancer or death on/before T1 and uses baseline CBC, four changes, gap and baseline covariates; T2 is sensitivity-only.

For the first-CBC population, report D and A separately for R=1 and R=0 under each source, fixed 2-8-year gap strata, T0 calendar-era strata, and two-year near-diagnostic displacement. Fit an R-observation model using only baseline T0 covariates and use stabilized inverse-probability weights only as a selection stress test. Report overlap/positivity and compare weighted with unweighted estimates; do not call this a causal correction because symptoms, indication and healthcare use are unmeasured. A change across R strata or a large weighted/unweighted shift is adverse to generalizing the repeat-CBC finding to all first-CBC participants. If the weighted analysis has poor positivity or delayed events are sparse, report inconclusive rather than trimming until favorable.

Required negative controls/falsifications are: other-primary-cancer specificity; CRC-versus-haematologic label permutation preserving dates/censoring; source-specific calendar-era interaction; gap strata; repeat-era strata; paired-array integrity; and a near-diagnostic check showing whether a signal disappears or reverses after excluding the first two years. A cancer-type permutation collapsing toward zero checks implementation and type specificity, not biology.

## Equal-input method comparison

The actual scientific deliverable requires new fitting, not readiness: source-specific cohort/event audits; 0-2 and 2-5 D and A estimates; class-specific absolute risks; date displacement; R-strata and weighted selection diagnostics; permutation/other-cancer controls; and a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive.

A. Transparent baseline: separate cause-specific Cox models for CRC, haematologic cancer, other primary cancer and death, with the same four CBC predictors and covariates above. Use robust intervals, proportional-hazard/time-window diagnostics, and Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence. The Cox model makes D and the absolute profile standardization auditable, but it can lose nonlinear platelet/haemoglobin ranges, interactions and time-varying type-specific hazards.

B. Same-input learned alternative: a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model with yearly intervals and separate hazards for CRC, haematologic cancer, other cancer and death. Use exactly the same people, inputs, paired outcome source, landmark, windows, competing-risk definitions and fixed profiles as A; only the functional form changes. It can reveal nonlinear Hb/platelet effects, interactions and time-varying hazards hidden by additive Cox. It cannot reveal clinical indication or mechanism.

Sort by T0: earliest 70% for development and latest 30% for final temporal holdout, keeping participants intact. Fit standardization, missingness decisions, weights, tuning and calibration only inside development. On the locked holdout compare both methods using target-specific calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, 2- and 5-year cumulative-incidence Brier and log scores, the fixed-profile A contrast and participant-bootstrap uncertainty; AUC/Uno C-index is secondary. Use 500 participant bootstrap replicates, preserving failed fits. If a final holdout has fewer than 50 haematologic events, learned type-specific metrics and model superiority are inconclusive. A learned model that improves ranking but not calibration or the fixed-profile A contrast does not establish a scientific advance.

Resource estimate: 8-16 CPU cores, 32-64 GiB RAM, approximately 2-4 hours including selected-column parsing, fits and bootstrap; unmeasured in this branch. The solver envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB and 28,800 seconds. ## Interpretation and limits

Supportive evidence requires the registry delayed D and A to meet their respective positive directions, the result not to be confined to R=1, one gap/era or the near-diagnostic interval, CRC specificity against other cancer, permutation collapse, compatible weighted/unweighted selection stress tests, and no adverse registry/inpatient reversal or decision-boundary crossing. If both sources support the absolute boundary, that supports a timing-compatible descriptive association worth external validation.

Adverse evidence includes a precise reversal, source-sensitive A/D, large R-stratum or weighted shift, disappearance after two years, similar other-cancer contrast, permutation failure, or learned-only ranking gain without calibrated absolute risks. These weaken a stable cancer-type interpretation but do not refute generic CBC-cancer association.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 50 held-out haematologic events, fewer than 50 same-class source-overlap events, broad intervals crossing the absolute boundary, positivity failure, invalid pairing/date parsing, nonconvergence, or a null too imprecise to distinguish no meaningful contrast from ±1 percentage point. A wide null is not refutation.

Computationally checkable claims are the paired-field audit, cohort counts, source-specific timing, D/A estimates, calibration, bootstrap uncertainty, selection sensitivity, permutation behavior and whether conclusions obey the stated boundaries. Clinical adjudication is still required for cancer date/code validity, symptoms, laboratory indication, pathology, stage, treatment, healthcare utilisation and whether any absolute contrast is useful in symptomatic practice. A prospective, indication-rich external cohort and decision-impact study would be required before diagnostic or clinical-utility conclusions.

## Rejected/deferred alternatives

- Selected: auditable Cox plus same-input boosted competing-risk comparison, because it directly tests the relative and absolute estimands and whether nonlinear/time-varying information is lost.
- Deferred: state-space/GP or transformer CBC trajectory models. Only two broadly complete repeat CBCs are available, delayed haematologic events are sparse, and extra latent-state assumptions cannot identify indication or source correctness.
- Rejected: any model using future R as a first-CBC predictor, cross-namespace joins to `ukb672073`, or pooling registry and inpatient dates into a gold-standard outcome.
- Deferred: the bounded ApoB/albumin HCC direction above and the sparse pancreatic mismatch direction, for the explicit evidence reasons recorded above.
