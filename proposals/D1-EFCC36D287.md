# Provisional hypothesis: sustained early hypotension versus nadir for subsequent AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU stay with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of new or worsening acute kidney injury during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Clinical importance and unresolved question
A single nadir may be transient or artifact-prone, whereas sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved question is whether duration and trajectory add clinically meaningful information about subsequent AKI beyond the nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, measurement frequency, or treatment response. Resolving this could inform whether early AKI risk assessment should use duration/trajectory rather than a single minimum.

## Proposed computable experiment
Population: adults in a qualifying eICU ICU stay, with valid early blood-pressure and creatinine observations and sufficient follow-up through 72 hours; define exclusions and missingness before outcome inspection.
Exposure: cumulative minutes below 65 mmHg during 0–24 hours, plus nadir and measurement density.
Outcome: new or worsening AKI during 24–72 hours using a prespecified KDIGO-compatible creatinine rule; define baseline creatinine, minimum creatinine, and timing carefully.
Baseline: adjusted association of nadir alone with AKI, with severity, prior vasopressor exposure, hospital, and measurement density.
Alternative: a time-aware model using early BP trajectory/duration and creatinine trajectory, compared with the nadir-only baseline on a prespecified patient-level split and uncertainty estimates.
Falsification: no reproducible duration increment beyond nadir, attenuation after measurement-density/severity adjustment, inconsistent direction across hospitals, or failure of negative-control timing/label checks would weaken the hypothesis. A null with wide uncertainty is inconclusive, not refutation.

## Evidence status and limitations
The eICU README documents the relevant source files and table metadata, but this provisional draft has not yet completed the full schema/header audit or inspected the three required public works. No computed effect is claimed. Clinical adjudication of AKI, treatment intent, and the clinical meaning of a 65 mmHg threshold is unavailable from these fields alone. The required key-reference bundle, exact column verification, and a bounded feasibility check remain pending.
