# Biochemical hepatic trajectory as a context shift for lactate-by-measured-perfusion risk

## Status, parent and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive successor to assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`, the hepatic-context child of the mature MIMIC-IV lactate-by-measured-perfusion study. It preserves the parent’s first eligible adult live ICU-exit cohort, one-hour availability buffer, dual clinical/store-time feature rule, 12-hour window, lactate/perfusion phenotype, 48-hour competing R/D/S/N/U endpoint, L+B-to-C transport split, fixed 10% queue with 50% transparent-baseline reserve, uncertainty procedure, process falsifications and noncausal boundaries.

No cohort count, fitted estimate, prevalence, calibration result or clinical conclusion is claimed. The new scientific deliverable is a prespecified organ-trajectory interaction:

> Among patients with a late record-defined hepatic injury burden, does the observed lactate-by-measured-perfusion interaction differ when bilirubin/ALT/AST are declining versus nondeclining during the same pre-exit window?

The future Harbor solver must newly fit and estimate:

1. an all-eligible flow, parent strict coverage flow, and new hepatic-trajectory coverage flow, with all exclusion reasons and row-level provenance;
2. the parent’s hepatic burden H and lactate/perfusion cells exactly, plus a frozen biochemical trajectory state G;
3. the new three-way absolute-risk contrast `Theta_G`, its R- and D-specific decomposition, and standardized 48-hour competing-risk CIFs with subject-level uncertainty;
4. held-out fixed-capacity results comparing the parent’s hepatic model with the trajectory-augmented model under the inherited 10% queue and 50%-reserve policy, stratified by G where support permits;
5. matched transparent B1/Bdisc/Btraj/Bmask predictions and a small chronological M2 alternative on the identical subject split, source whitelist, target and queue;
6. all source/time/unit/gate audits, process and chronology falsifications, conclusion ledger, and explicit gate-failure semantics.

The primary new estimand is not a treatment effect, liver-clearance estimate, or proof of organ recovery. G is an observable biochemical EHR phenotype.

## Evidence and unresolved clinical question

The strongest available evidence is availability and design evidence only. The local MIMIC-IV 3.1 dictionary identifies total bilirubin (50885), ALT (50861), AST (50878), INR(PT) (51237), PT (51274), lactate (50813), creatinine (50912), and the parent MAP/pressor items. The labevents schema supplies subject/admission keys, item IDs, clinical `charttime`, recording `storetime`, numeric values and units. The expert asynchronous-organ-recovery seed is explicitly untested, and the parent has no fitted result. Neither establishes this interaction.

The parent asks whether persistent lactate has different observed near-term risk when measured perfusion improves or worsens, and whether late biochemical hepatic injury modifies that association. A late abnormal bilirubin/ALT/AST pattern may represent ongoing injury, a resolving episode, selective testing, or treatment/documentation intensity. A single late H category therefore cannot answer whether a hepatic-context signal is stable across recovery states.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Within the parent’s late elevated hepatic-burden group H+, the P-versus-NL contrast under improving measured perfusion I is less reassuring when the pre-exit bilirubin/ALT/AST trajectory is nonrecovering than when it is recovering.

Let `K_g = CIF_RD(P,I,H+,G=g) - CIF_RD(NL,I,H+,G=g)`, with S competing and R/D reported separately. The primary new contrast is:

`Theta_G = K_nonrecovering - K_recovering`.

The directional hypothesis is `Theta_G > 0`: persistent lactate remains more adverse, or the apparent reassurance of improving perfusion is attenuated, when hepatic markers are not declining. A clinically meaningful heterogeneity region is ±0.05 absolute 48-hour R/D CIF; an estimate inside that region is not support for a clinically material interaction. Worsening-perfusion I/W, H0, the neither/trajectory-indeterminate groups and INR sensitivity are secondary.

Support would justify testing whether a future monitoring protocol should display hepatic trajectory alongside lactate/perfusion rather than using a static hepatic flag. An adverse result is a reproducible null, reversal, or replication by process-only controls. Inconclusive results include sparse paired labs, high missingness, inadequate overlap, unstable bootstrap or a failed support gate. Any result establishes only observed prognostic heterogeneity under MIMIC documentation.

### Parent phenotype retained exactly

Persistent lactate P requires both six-hour half medians of item 50813 to be defined and at least 2.0 mmol/L; NL requires both defined and at least one below 2.0. A 4.0 mmol/L threshold is a fixed sensitivity.

Improving perfusion I requires late-minus-early median MAP >=5 mmHg and late target-pressor unioned minutes no greater than early minutes. W requires MAP change <=−5 mmHg and late target-pressor minutes no less than early minutes. Other combinations remain indeterminate. Each half requires at least one valid lactate, at least two MAP observations at distinct clinical times, and one positive-duration target-pressor interval. Missing pressor ascertainment is not zero exposure.

MAP uses ICU `chartevents` item IDs 220052, 220181 and 225312, with finite `valuenum), accepted units audited as mmHg, and clinical/recording times. Target pressors use `inputevents` item IDs 221906, 222315, 221289, 229617, 221749, 229630, 229631, 229632, 221662, 221653 and 221986. Use interval `starttime/endtime` for duration, `storetime) for availability, clip only to the feature window, collapse exact duplicates by available identity fields, and do not pool rate or amount into a dose.

Late hepatic burden H is the parent’s three-level record phenotype using bilirubin 50885, ALT 50861 and AST 50878. H0 is at least one valid marker with none at or above its training-only upper-tertile threshold; H1 is at least one marker at or above its corresponding threshold; H2 is at least two. Thresholds are calculated separately on finite training values in the eligible training population and frozen before validation, L-test or C outcomes. If a marker lacks support, its threshold/contribution is unavailable. The primary parent hepatic stratum is H+ = H1/H2; H2 is secondary. H means biochemical injury burden in the record, not liver failure, hepatic clearance impairment or etiology.

## New biochemical trajectory phenotype

G is defined before outcome reading and only within H+ for the primary interaction. It is not called “liver recovery” in the results table unless a clinical reviewer approves that terminology; the prespecified name is “recorded biochemical trajectory.”

For each of bilirubin 50885, ALT 50861 and AST 50878, require a finite numeric value, accepted canonical unit, valid `charttime/storetime`, and at least one valid value in each half. Use the half median. The primary paired-marker direction is frozen before fitting:

- bilirubin improves when late is at least 0.30 mg/dL lower than early or at least 15% lower, and worsens by the symmetric increase rule;
- ALT and AST improve when late is at least 20 U/L lower or at least 15% lower, and worsen by the symmetric increase rule.

For percentage calculations use `(early-late)/max(abs(early), marker-specific floor)`, with floors 1.0 mg/dL for bilirubin and 20 U/L for ALT/AST. Values between the symmetric thresholds are stable. No interpolation, last-observation carry-forward, missing-as-zero rule, or reference-range substitution is allowed. Unit conversion is permitted only for units explicitly observed and documented in the frozen unit audit; unsupported or ambiguous units are invalid.

A marker is paired only when both halves pass the same unit/time/value checks. G=recovering requires at least two paired markers, at least two improving directions, and no worsening direction. G=nonrecovering requires at least two paired markers and includes stable, mixed, or worsening patterns not meeting G=recovering. G=indeterminate covers fewer than two paired markers and is retained in the flow but excluded from the primary G interaction. A prespecified secondary splits nonrecovering into worsening versus stable/mixed; it cannot replace the primary after outcomes are seen.

INR(PT) 51237 and PT 51274 are available dictionary-verified sensitivity inputs, not primary G components: anticoagulation, sampling and synthetic-function interpretation are not adjudicated. An INR sensitivity adds INR only when its observed unit is accepted and paired; it uses a fixed 0.1 or 10% symmetric change rule. It cannot turn the primary result into a claim of hepatic synthetic recovery.

The new trajectory gate is endpoint-independent and frozen before any outcome/model result: at least two paired primary hepatic markers for G; at least 40 subjects and 10 known R/D events in every primary `P/NL × I × G` cell in each evaluated target; at least 8 subjects and 3 R/D events in each held-out test cell; U <=10% overall and <=20% per primary cell; and the parent’s original phenotype/support gates. No G cells are merged, cutoffs changed or cells dropped after outcome inspection. The parent’s H estimands remain reportable under the parent gate when the stricter trajectory gate fails, but `Theta_G` is then “not estimable.”

For each G stratum report standardized 48-hour CIFs for R, D and S, the composite A=R-or-D, and N/U accounting. The primary is `Theta_G) above under I. Report the corresponding W contrast, H0/H+ parent contrast, trajectory-by-H sensitivity, R-specific and D-specific decompositions, and 6/12/48-hour time profiles where support permits. Use simultaneous 95% subject-bootstrap intervals, preserving subjects and all admissions within subject; report effective sample size, overlap, bootstrap failures and gate status. If full refit is infeasible, label conditional-prediction bootstrap separately from full-refit uncertainty and report the completed replicate count.

Retain the parent’s fixed-capacity policy exactly. For held-out subjects, rank frozen predicted 48-hour A risk and select exactly the top 10% with deterministic score/subject-ID tie-breaking. The primary incremental policy comparison is Btraj versus Bdisc, with 50% of the 10% queue reserved for Bdisc and the remaining 50% filled by Btraj scores after excluding reserved subjects. Report queue overlap, A/R/D capture, PPV, rescue yield, random benchmark, and paired subject-bootstrap intervals overall and within G where support permits. Report the inherited 5% and 20% workload sensitivities only as secondary. This is a ranking/allocation operating characteristic, not patient benefit or treatment effect.

A clinically meaningful new policy interaction is prespecified as an absolute difference of at least 0.03 in trajectory-stratified safety-net gain, with intervals excluding zero in both L and C and direction concordant with `Theta_G). If the two eras disagree, the result is transport-inconclusive even if a pooled contrast is positive.

Supportive evidence requires both L and C to pass the new gate; `Theta_G) to exclude the ±0.05 null region in the hypothesized direction; R/D decomposition not to be driven by an unsupported component; Btraj’s queue interaction to meet its fixed criterion; and no comparable Bmask, testing-intensity or permutation signal. Adverse evidence includes a reproducible null, reversal, Bmask replication, chronology/permutation persistence, calibration deterioration, or queue behavior inconsistent with the risk interaction. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse G cells, high U, poor overlap, marker-unit failures, wide intervals or era disagreement.

## Matched models and alternatives

All models use the same strict trajectory-gated subjects, split, endpoint, source whitelist, target outcomes and frozen queue.

- **B1:** the parent’s transparent order-blind aggregate competing-risk baseline using the fixed 12-bin summaries, admission/demographic context, masks and organ/process summaries, without the new trajectory interaction.
- **Bdisc:** the parent transparent phenotype model adding the parent lactate/perfusion and H interactions, but no G terms. This is the direct incumbent for the new incremental question.
- **Btraj:** transparent extension of Bdisc adding G, lactate×perfusion×G terms within H+, and prespecified R/D/S cause-specific effects. It is the primary scientific model because every new coefficient and standardized CIF contrast is auditable.
- **Bmask:** retains counts, missingness, testing/process indicators and availability flags but removes physiologic values and G; similar `Theta_G) or queue interaction would weaken a biochemical interpretation.
- **M2:** a small masked chronological GRU over the identical 12 one-hour bins and raw whitelist: lactate, three hepatic markers, optional INR sensitivity, MAP, pressor exposure, demographic/admission context, H/G flags, values, missingness, counts and timing/process channels. Hidden size 32 or 64, five fixed seeds, dropout and early stopping are selected only in training/validation. It can reveal nonlinear/order-dependent coupling or recovery reversal lost by fixed half summaries. A gain without calibration, Btraj separation, or attenuation under chronology shuffle is not mechanistic evidence.

The baseline comparison is scientifically adequate because Bdisc tests the parent’s incumbent hepatic interpretation with no added trajectory information, while Btraj isolates whether the new recorded trajectory changes the estimand and fixed queue. M2 is substantive, not cosmetic: the baseline discards within-half ordering and nonlinear joint timing, while M2 can show whether a marker’s timing relative to lactate/perfusion carries information beyond the frozen direction state. M2 will be deferred if the same-input tensor cannot be constructed without post-`tL` values, if trajectory support fails, or if masked chronology checks are not reproducible.

Deferred branches are explicit: a treatment-effect or fluid-response model (no reliable intent, fluid responsiveness, counterfactual outcome or adequate time-varying confounding control); true liver-clearance or microcirculation inference (no measured clearance, portal/hepatic flow or microcirculation); note-based etiologic adjudication (notes are excluded from the primary whitelist and no note review is claimed); a full transformer (capacity does not resolve missing adjudication and adds no distinct uncertainty); AKI/dialysis and respiratory-recovery composites (not needed for this hepatic trajectory question and would introduce additional selective support and endpoint overlap); and external validation (no external cohort configured).

## Claim boundaries and required expert evidence

A supportive result establishes a reproducible association between a record-defined biochemical trajectory and observed risk/ranking heterogeneity at a first live ICU exit. It may motivate a prospectively adjudicated silent-mode study that measures liver disease etiology, synthetic function, treatment context and organ recovery before applying a hepatic-context alert.

It does not establish hepatic clearance of lactate, liver recovery, microcirculatory state, fluid responsiveness, treatment effect, preventability, appropriateness of ICU discharge, monitoring benefit, net benefit, or transportability beyond the frozen MIMIC eras. Bilirubin/ALT/AST can change for multiple reasons and may be selectively measured; INR is confounded by anticoagulation and is only a sensitivity. Clinical experts must adjudicate marker units, meaningful trajectory thresholds, etiologic liver injury, whether the endpoint represents clinically actionable deterioration, and whether a 10% review queue is operationally plausible. Another study with measured physiology, intent and external validation is required for stronger claims.
