# Independent medication-domain convergence audit: literal RT Vent Off/Suspended occurs in hospitals with no infusionDrug coverage

## Substantive child and clinical question

This child begins from assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves their literal RT-event extraction, exact-tuple and earliest-anchored 30-minute deduplication, fixed event-time windows, first-episode and common-support safeguards, competing ICU observation states, site-concentration warning, sequence nonexchangeability, and prohibition against calling any label extubation, liberation, reintubation, or bedside ventilator status. The new falsifiable question is whether the large Off-versus-Suspended respiratory-setting difference has convergent validity in an independently charted medication domain: timestamped sedative, analgesic, or neuromuscular-blocking infusion records.

The strongest inherited claim remains that Off and Suspended are different structured respiratory-charting regimes within four to five overlap hospitals, not clinical states. The untested claim was that Off should show a larger event-centered reduction in sedative/analgesic/neuromuscular infusion-record activity than Suspended. This matters because medication de-escalation would be independent of respiratoryCharting workflow and would justify proceeding to clinical adjudication. The executed audit instead found structural non-overlap: the RT-transition hospitals have no infusionDrug records at all. Therefore medication convergence is not merely null; it is unidentifiable in this eICU snapshot.

## Prespecified computable experiment and safeguards

The inherited cohort is unchanged: exact decisive tuples are collapsed, then sorted records are clustered from the earliest record through +30 minutes; mixed direction and mixed Off/Suspended clusters are excluded; require patient linkage, nonmissing ICU boundary, `T>=360`, and `T<=unitdischargeoffset`; do not exclude later Start. The full rescan reproduced 4,120 decisive rows, 4,094 clusters, and 1,063 eligible episodes: 713 Off and 350 Suspended.

Had overlap existed, drug names would be conservatively grouped as sedative (propofol, midazolam/Versed, lorazepam, diazepam, dexmedetomidine/Precedex, ketamine, etomidate), analgesic (fentanyl, morphine, hydromorphone/Dilaudid, oxycodone, hydrocodone, remifentanil, meperidine), and neuromuscular blocker (cisatracurium, vuronium/vecuronium, rocuronium, atracurium, succinylcholine, pancuronium), retaining exact names for audit. Primary windows were `[T-360,T)`, `[T,T+360)`, `[T+360,T+1440)`, and `[T+1440,T+2880)`. Outcomes were any record and row count by class; the primary change estimand was post-minus-pre change in episode-level any-record prevalence, contrasted Suspended minus Off. Analyses required first episodes, stay clustering, common-support hospitals with at least five fully observed episodes per label, equal-hospital and leave-one-hospital-out estimates. Vasopressor and maintenance-fluid infusion-record activity were negative controls for nonspecific medication-charting changes; a shifted sham event within the same stay was the temporal negative control. Support required a larger sedative/analgesic decrease after Off than Suspended, stable direction across common-support hospitals and first episodes, without comparable control-domain changes. Discordant directions were adverse; inadequate table overlap was prespecified inconclusive.

## Executed availability result

The complete infusionDrug file contains 4,803,719 rows in 73,547 stays. It has abundant relevant records globally: conservative string profiling identified 1,055,467 sedative rows, 622,641 analgesic rows, and 14,207 neuromuscular-blocker rows; leading exact names include Fentanyl, Propofol, Midazolam, and Dexmedetomidine in multiple recorded-unit forms. Thus the table is not globally empty.

Nevertheless, there were exactly 0 infusionDrug rows in the 748 stays containing the 1,063 eligible Off/Suspended episodes. The broader 2,505-stay decisive Start/Off/Suspended population also had 0 stay overlap with infusionDrug. This is explained at hospital level: all decisive events occur in hospitals 56, 58, 59, 60, 61, 63, 66, 67, 68, 69, 71, and 73, while infusionDrug has zero rows at every one of those hospitals. infusionDrug covers 152 other hospitals. Therefore no event-time window, baseline/change estimate, first-episode comparison, common-support hospital contrast, sham test, or medication negative control can be computed for this cohort. Treating missing rows as no sedation would be a severe false-negative error.

## Interpretation and advance

Result gate: **inconclusive because of structural source non-overlap**, not evidence of no medication use and not adverse physiologic convergence. The clinically useful advance is to falsify feasibility of the most direct independent medication-domain validation in this eICU snapshot and to identify a hospital-partitioned documentation mechanism. It also strengthens the warning that apparently multicenter eICU tables need not coexist within hospitals; absence in an event cohort cannot be interpreted clinically without hospital-level coverage auditing.

The evidence-supported claim is only that literal RT transitions and infusionDrug occupy disjoint hospitals/stays here. The unresolved claim remains whether Off is accompanied by medication de-escalation relative to Suspended. Exact additional evidence requires co-recorded infusion administration with start/change/stop times, independently timestamped airway/device status, nursing/RT narrative and blinded clinical adjudication sampled by hospital, label, and episode order. `medication` orders (`drugorderoffset`, `drugstartoffset`, `drugstopoffset`) could be a separate study but are not equivalent to administered infusion activity and must not repair this result mechanically.

Automatically checkable claims are source hashes/schema, literal extraction, clustering and eligibility counts, infusionDrug row/stay counts, name-class counts, exact stay intersection, and hospital coverage. Automated verification cannot establish administration, discontinuation, sedation depth, ventilator status, extubation, liberation, intent, safety, or causality.
