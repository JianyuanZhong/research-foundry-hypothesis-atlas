# Composition versus within-cell regulation in RA macrophage-fibroblast coculture

## Seed
D3-M1 (Composition versus within-cell regulation)

## Domain
Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses

## Question
In rheumatoid arthritis macrophage-fibroblast coculture, is the FLS-induced inflammatory activation of macrophages better explained by patient-level composition (donor baseline inflammatory state) or by within-cell drug-responsive regulation (consistent pathway-level drug responses)?

## Hypothesis
The variance in FLS-induced macrophage inflammatory activation is partitioned such that patient-level composition (donor effect) explains a larger fraction of variance than condition-specific drug responses for the core inflammatory gene set. This implies that patient-specific baseline state is the primary determinant of treatment response, and that a "one-size-fits-all" drug selection strategy is suboptimal.

## Clinical importance
RA treatment response is highly variable: ~30-40% of patients fail to achieve remission with first-line biologic DMARDs, and the failure of biomarker-driven trials stems from treating RA as a single entity rather than a spectrum of pathway-dominant endotypes [K3]. Understanding whether this variability is primarily driven by patient-specific baseline inflammatory state (composition) or by consistent drug-target pathway regulation (within-cell) has direct implications for treatment selection strategy. If patient composition dominates, then pre-treatment molecular profiling of the patient's inflammatory state could guide drug selection. If within-cell regulation dominates, then drug-target matching is more important than patient state. This question is unresolved: Kuo et al. demonstrated that different drugs redirect macrophages into different states in coculture, but did not quantify the relative contribution of patient vs. drug effects [K1]. Chatterjee et al. showed that variance partitioning in a cell therapy context revealed patient heterogeneity masking treatment effects, but in a different disease and assay system [K2].

## Analysis plan

### Step 1: Data preparation
1. Load counts matrix (23,710 × 70)
2. Filter genes: require count > 10 in at least 4 samples (one per patient) to remove low-expression noise
3. Log-transform: log2(count + 1) for variance analysis
4. Define sample metadata: patient (P1-P4), condition_type (M, MT, MTF, MTF_drug, MT_drug), drug (none, or specific drug), has_FLS (0/1)

### Step 2: Core inflammatory gene set
Define a core inflammatory gene set for focused analysis:
- TNF pathway: TNF, TNFAIP3, TNFSF11, TNFRSF1A, TNFRSF1B
- IL-6 pathway: IL6, IL6R, IL6ST, IL11, IL11RA, IL20
- Interferon: IFNG, IFNGR1, IFNGR2, STAT1, STAT2, IRF1, OAS1, OAS2, OAS3, MX1, MX2
- Chemokines: CCL2, CCL3, CCL4, CCL5, CCL20, CXCL8, CXCL9, CXCL10, CXCL11
- Antigen presentation: HLA-DRA, HLA-DQB1, HLA-DQA1, HLA-DRB1, CD80, CD86, CD40, CD70
- NF-κB: NFKB1, NFKB2, REL, RELA, RELB, CHUK, IKBKG, IKBKE
- Total: ~50-60 genes

### Step 3: Baseline analysis - Per-gene variance decomposition
For each gene, decompose variance into components:
- **Patient effect**: variance between patient means (P1 vs P2 vs P3 vs P4)
- **Condition effect**: variance between condition types (M vs MT vs MTF vs drug conditions)
- **Residual**: within-patient, within-condition variance

Method: One-way ANOVA per gene with patient as the factor (using all 70 samples).
- F_patient = MS_between_patients / MS_within_patients
- η²_patient = SS_between / SS_total
- This gives the fraction of total variance explained by patient identity

Repeat with condition_type as the factor:
- F_condition = MS_between_conditions / MS_within_conditions
- η²_condition = SS_between_conditions / SS_total

For the core inflammatory gene set, compute the distribution of η²_patient and η²_condition.

### Step 4: Alternative analysis - Mixed-effects model
For the core inflammatory gene set, fit a linear mixed-effects model:
```
gene_expression ~ condition_type + drug + (1 | patient)
```
- Fixed effects: condition_type (M, MT, MTF, drug), drug (specific drug, where applicable)
- Random effect: patient (4 levels)
- Extract variance components: σ²_patient, σ²_condition, σ²_residual
- Compute intraclass correlation: ICC = σ²_patient / (σ²_patient + σ²_condition + σ²_residual)

This model properly accounts for the nested design (multiple conditions within each patient) and gives a direct estimate of the patient-level variance fraction.

### Step 5: Pathway-level aggregation
For each pathway (TNF, IL-6, IFN, chemokines, AP, NF-κB):
- Compute the mean η²_patient and η²_condition across genes in the pathway
- Compare to the genome-wide distribution
- Identify pathways where patient effect is enriched vs. condition effect

### Step 6: Patient-drug interaction
For drugs tested in ≥2 patients:
- Fit interaction model: gene ~ patient × drug
- Test whether the drug effect differs significantly by patient
- Identify specific patient-drug combinations with divergent responses

### Step 7: Technical controls
- Check for batch effects: compare variance structure across patients
- Verify that patient effect is not driven by a single outlier sample
- Sensitivity analysis: exclude P4 (no drugs) and re-run

## Falsification criteria

### Supportive result (patient composition dominates)
- η²_patient > η²_condition for >60% of core inflammatory genes
- ICC > 0.4 in the mixed-effects model
- Patient effect is consistent across pathways (not concentrated in one)
- Drug responses show significant patient × drug interaction
- **Interpretation**: Patient baseline state is the primary determinant; pre-treatment profiling could guide drug selection

### Adverse result (within-cell regulation dominates)
- η²_condition > η²_patient for >60% of core inflammatory genes
- ICC < 0.2 in the mixed-effects model
- Drug effects are consistent across patients (low interaction)
- **Interpretation**: Drug-target matching is more important than patient state; one-size-fits-all drug selection may be adequate

### Inconclusive result
- η²_patient ≈ η²_condition (neither clearly dominates)
- High residual variance (technical noise or unmeasured biological variation)
- Patient effect concentrated in only 1-2 pathways
- **Interpretation**: Both patient state and drug regulation contribute; more patients or replicates needed to resolve

## Competing explanations
1. **Within-cell regulation dominates**: consistent drug-responsive pathways across patients (drug effect > patient effect). Prediction: low ICC, high condition η², low patient×drug interaction.
2. **Patient-drug interaction**: specific patient-drug combinations drive the variance. Prediction: significant interaction terms, but main effects of patient and drug both small.
3. **Technical/batch confounding**: library preparation or sequencing batch effects confound patient effects. Prediction: patient effect disappears after batch correction; residual variance is high.
4. **FLS cell line differences**: FLS from different patients have different activation potential, driving apparent patient effects. Prediction: MTF vs MT difference varies by patient, but M (macrophage-only) baseline does not.

## Method exploration
- **Baseline**: Per-gene one-way ANOVA (patient as factor) - simple, interpretable, no assumptions about normality beyond ANOVA
- **Alternative**: Linear mixed-effects model with patient as random effect - properly accounts for nested design, gives ICC estimate
- **What the alternative reveals**: Whether the patient effect is consistent across pathways or concentrated in specific inflammatory programs; whether the drug effect is additive or interactive with patient
- **Resource**: CPU only, small dataset (70 samples × 23k genes), fits in minutes. lme4 or statsmodels for mixed models.

## Limitations
1. **Small n**: 4 patients, no technical replicates. Statistical power is limited; ICC estimates will have wide confidence intervals.
2. **Unresolved donor identity**: design.json has donor_status="unresolved". We use patient labels (P1-P4) as the unit, but cannot verify that P1 macrophages and P1 FLS are from the same patient.
3. **FLS are cell lines**: Not primary FLS. Cell line behavior may differ from primary tissue.
4. **All conditions include TNF**: This is a stimulated system. The variance partitioning reflects drug effects on top of TNF stimulation, not resting state.
5. **No independent validation**: GSE95588 coculture r1/r2 includes earlier GSE57723 data.
6. **Cross-sectional**: No longitudinal data to track disease state changes.
