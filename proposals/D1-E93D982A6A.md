# Discordant recovery after AKI: does creatinine recovery overstate renal recovery?

## Status

Working proposal for MIMIC-IV v3.1 (episode 2 repair). This is a scientific design, not a completed analysis. No patient-level results have been estimated yet.

## Clinical question

Among ICU patients with AKI, does apparent recovery defined by creatinine improvement identify patients whose urine output remains abnormal, and are those patients at higher risk of subsequent renal escalation, ICU death, or in-hospital death?

The clinically consequential unresolved claim is narrower than “creatinine is invalid.” The claim is that a creatinine-based recovery definition can misclassify a subgroup whose urine output remains abnormal, and that this subgroup has worse subsequent outcomes than either patients with concordant recovery or patients who have not recovered.

## Why this matters

AKI recovery is often inferred from creatinine because it is repeatedly measured and familiar. However, creatinine generation and clearance can be affected by muscle mass, nutrition, fluid balance, and sampling practices. Urine output is a direct but noisy renal output measure. If creatinine recovery is discordant from urine output, the clinical decision to reduce renal support, alter nephrotoxic exposure, or predict prognosis may be wrong.

The unresolved question is whether discordance identifies a genuinely worse prognosis or merely reflects measurement practices, current severity, or selective sampling.

## Evidence base and closest inspected work

The closest inspected work is a comparison of cystatin C and creatinine for measured kidney function during critical illness [K1]. It supports the concern that creatinine may not track measured renal function well in ICU patients, but it does not establish that creatinine-based recovery misclassifies a subgroup with worse subsequent outcomes.

A second relevant work is the KDIGO AKI guideline, which defines AKI and recovery concepts and emphasizes both creatinine and urine output [K2]. It bounds the phenotype definitions but does not test the discordance-outcome link.

A third relevant work is a study of AKI recovery and outcomes, which provides context for the prognostic importance of recovery but does not isolate the discordant subgroup [K3].

## AKI episode and index time rules

1. Identify ICU stays with at least one creatinine measurement.
2. Define AKI episode as the first creatinine rise meeting KDIGO criteria (creatinine ≥2× baseline or ≥4.4 mg/dL with ≥0.3 mg/dL rise within 48h, or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6h).
3. Define index time as the first time point at which AKI criteria are met.
4. Define recovery time as the first time point after index time at which creatinine returns to baseline (or within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline) for 24h, or urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/h for 24h.
5. Define discordant recovery as creatinine recovery without concurrent urine output recovery (urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for 24h at the time of creatinine recovery).
6. Define concordant recovery as both creatinine and urine output recovery.
7. Define non-recovery as neither creatinine nor urine output recovery by 72h after index time.

## Phenotype definitions

- Creatinine recovery: creatinine returns to baseline (or within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline) for 24h.
- Urine output recovery: urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/h for 24h.
- Fluid balance: calculated from inputevents and outputevents, or chartevents itemid 226512.
- Vasopressor use: any vasopressor administration in the 24h before index time.
- Renal replacement therapy: any RRT in the 24h before index time.
- Death: in-hospital death (patients.death_time) or ICU death (icustays.dischtime with discharge_to = 'death').

## Competing explanations

1. Discordance reflects genuine renal dysfunction: creatinine recovery overstates renal recovery, and the discordant subgroup has worse outcomes.
2. Discordance reflects measurement practices: urine output is selectively measured, and the discordant subgroup is not genuinely worse.
3. Discordance reflects current severity: the discordant subgroup is sicker at baseline, and the outcome difference is explained by baseline severity.

## Baseline versus learned alternative

Primary analysis: adjusted comparison of subsequent renal escalation, ICU death, and in-hospital death across concordant recovery, discordant recovery, and non-recovery groups.

Learned alternative: a simple logistic regression or gradient-boosted model using pre-index features (age, sex, baseline creatinine, vasopressor use, RRT use, fluid balance) to predict subsequent outcomes, with discordance as a covariate. The learned alternative could reveal whether discordance adds prognostic information beyond baseline severity.

## Falsification criteria

- If the discordant group has similar outcomes to the concordant group after adjustment, the hypothesis is refuted.
- If the discordant group has worse outcomes but the difference is explained by baseline severity, the hypothesis is refuted.
- If urine output is selectively measured and the discordant subgroup is not genuinely worse, the hypothesis is refuted.

## Limitations

- Without measured kidney function, the analysis can identify only suspected discordant recovery.
- Urine output is selectively measured and may not be available for all patients.
- The analysis cannot establish causality; it can only identify an association.
- External validation is needed to confirm the findings.
