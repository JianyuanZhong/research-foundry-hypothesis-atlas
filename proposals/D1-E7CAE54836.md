# Discharge-time hepatic non-recovery after TACE and short-term return in HCC

## Status and hypothesis

This is a substantive episode-3 successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. It is an observational prognostic study, not a treatment-effect or discharge-policy trial.

The primary hypothesis is:

> Among adults with HCC who have a first observed TACE episode and are discharged, the early within-person laboratory trajectory from the 14 days before TACE to the first 72 hours after TACE adds reproducible information about a subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days beyond baseline hepatic reserve, prior utilization, demographics, and recorded disease history.

The new, clinically sharper nested hypothesis is:

> Among the subset with at least 96 hours between TACE start and discharge and complete core laboratory measurements, failure of bilirubin, albumin, and INR to recover by the last 24 hours before discharge adds information beyond the pre-TACE and early post-TACE measurements.

The second hypothesis asks whether early laboratory injury is transient or persists to the point at which discharge planning occurs. It is an incremental prognostic estimand: it does not estimate the causal effect of TACE, discharge timing, laboratory monitoring, or an intervention triggered by a laboratory result.

The primary endpoint is deliberately called **subsequent inpatient admission**, not unplanned readmission: the source does not encode scheduling intent reliably. A liver-focused secondary endpoint is a later admission carrying a prespecified diagnosis-coded signal for ascites, hepatic encephalopathy/coma, variceal disease, or gastrointestinal bleeding. That signal is not adjudicated hepatic decompensation.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported by the inspected external evidence is narrower. Sanai et al., *Scientific Reports* 2026 (DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-54466-4; PMCID PMC13457603; frozen source ID `[source checksum]`) studied TACE/TARE at four tertiary centers. Its full-text abstract defines hepatic decompensation using ascites, variceal bleeding, or portosystemic encephalopathy and reports baseline ALBI and treatment type as predictors of post-LRT decompensation. This supports the clinical plausibility that hepatic reserve and post-locoregional-treatment complications matter.

That evidence does not establish that routinely recorded, time-ordered laboratory change after TACE adds prognostic information beyond baseline values in this HCC source. It also does not establish that persistence of an abnormal trajectory at discharge identifies patients who would benefit from delayed discharge or intensified monitoring. The HCC source cannot calculate a validated ALBI score because laboratory units are absent.

The unresolved claim is therefore specific and falsifiable: conditional on baseline reserve and early post-TACE values, does a discharge-proximal recovery signal improve held-out prediction of a subsequent inpatient admission? This is clinically consequential because clinicians must decide how much post-procedure monitoring and follow-up to arrange before discharge. A reproducible incremental signal would motivate a prospective study with clinically adjudicated decompensation and a defined monitoring/discharge pathway; a null result would caution against using these routine trajectories as a stand-alone escalation rule.

The substantive advance over the parent is not a more complex model. It separates two biologically and clinically different claims:

1. early change may identify general short-term vulnerability; and
2. persistence or recovery by discharge may identify a discharge-time risk state.

The second claim is tested only in a nested cohort whose temporal design makes the discharge decision explicit. The design also reports the larger parent estimand so that a sparse discharge-complete subgroup cannot silently replace the clinically broader question.

## Exposure construction

Include a lab only after its two-key encounter join is verified and its timestamp falls in the prespecified window; do not assume all rows sharing an encounter were collected during the procedure.

For each participant and assay calculate medians separately:

- baseline: `[TACE start time - 14 calendar days, TACE start time)`;
- early: `[TACE start time, TACE start time + 72 hours]`;
- discharge-proximal: `[Discharge Time - 24 hours, Discharge Time]`.

The parent trajectory is early minus baseline for each assay. The new discharge non-recovery block is discharge-proximal minus early for each assay. Use raw assay-specific differences only for descriptive summaries; use training-fold median/IQR robust standardization in models. Do not compute an ALBI score, sum assays, or impose a clinical cutoff. Expected directions (not imposed in fitting) are higher bilirubin, INR, ALT, AST, creatinine and lower albumin/platelets as adverse.

The core complete-case analysis is primary for clinical interpretability. Repeat in the expanded seven-assay set as a secondary analysis, with complete-case and missingness-indicator versions. The missingness indicator is a property of this care record, not proof that a test was clinically indicated or that missingness is biologically informative.

## Baselines, estimands, and analysis

### Primary early-trajectory estimand

Fit a patient-level baseline-only model for the primary all-cause outcome containing age, sex, index department, the three baseline core assay medians, prior encounter count in the preceding 365 days, and prior recorded indicators for cirrhosis, portal-vein thrombosis, ascites, diabetes, and prior liver surgery. Construct prior indicators by date-aligned joins to encounters and diagnoses/procedures; diagnoses have no independent date. These are recorded-code utilization markers, not adjudicated comorbidities.

Fit a trajectory model adding the three assay-specific early-minus-baseline changes. Repeat with all seven assays only after reporting the expanded cohort gate. The primary incremental estimand is the global likelihood-ratio comparison of the trajectory model against the baseline-only model, complemented by adjusted odds ratios per training-fold IQR, absolute risk differences at representative covariate patterns, AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, and calibration intercept/slope. Use patient-level bootstrap intervals for incremental metrics and coefficient uncertainty.

### Nested discharge estimand

In the nested discharge cohort, compare:

- Model D0: the baseline covariates above plus early assay values (or equivalently a prespecified baseline-plus-early block; retain one parameterization as primary) and no discharge-proximal values;
- Model D1: Model D0 plus the three discharge-proximal-minus-early changes.

The primary nested incremental estimand is the out-of-fold and likelihood-ratio contribution of D1 over D0. Do not adjust the primary nested model for length of stay, post-index medications, post-index orders, or later laboratory values: they may mediate recovery or leak information. A length-of-stay adjustment is descriptive/sensitivity only.

Because the full-file audit found only 404 core-complete discharge participants and 8 liver-coded signals, use a prespecified adequacy gate before inferential modeling: publish counts and events after discovery filtering; if the all-cause nested outcome has fewer than 20 events or the liver-coded endpoint has fewer than 20 events, do not fit or interpret an unstable liver model as confirmatory. The liver-coded result is descriptive/inconclusive under that gate, regardless of p-value. Penalized logistic regression may be used as a stability sensitivity, not as a way to rescue inadequate events.

### Validation and robustness

Use patient-level five-fold cross-validation within discovery buckets 0–79; all preprocessing, imputation, standardization, and tuning occur inside training folds. Add a chronological split with 2010–2022 derivation and 2023–2025-12-02 testing, preserving participant separation. The reserved buckets are inaccessible and must not be described as external validation.

A bounded elastic-net logistic model and a shallow gradient-boosted model may compare functional form using identical feature blocks. Complexity is not evidence of clinical value. No GPU is needed. Report optimism-corrected out-of-fold Brier change, AUROC/AUPRC, calibration, and bootstrap intervals, not just in-sample discrimination.

Run the following prespecified checks:

- complete-case versus missingness-indicator and inverse-observation-weighting sensitivity analyses;
- minimum-observation and maximum-gap requirements in each laboratory window;
- exact assay-label and procedure-source sensitivity analyses;
- calendar-period and index-department selection tables;
- duplicate-key and join-multiplication assertions;
- a timing falsification that shuffles or reverses laboratory timestamps within participant while retaining values and assay labels; a genuinely time-ordered signal should attenuate;
- a negative temporal control using a post-discharge lab/order row, which must never enter features and is an explicit leakage audit, not a predictor;
- early-discharge sensitivity excluding the nested 96-hour rule from the main cohort, to show whether the discharge extension is transportable across hospital duration.

## Falsification and interpretation

Call the **primary early-trajectory hypothesis supportive** only if all are satisfied:

1. the prespecified trajectory block has global LR p < 0.05;
2. optimism-corrected out-of-fold Brier improvement over baseline has a 95% bootstrap interval excluding zero;
3. the direction/calibration conclusion is not reversed in the chronological split;
4. the result is not solely an in-sample or high-capacity-model gain and survives the key density/gap and timing-shuffle checks.

Call it adverse/falsified if the trajectory block has no incremental evidence and no held-out performance improvement, or if the apparent gain disappears/reverses chronologically, after density/gap handling, or under timing shuffling. A model that improves AUROC in-sample but worsens calibration or held-out Brier is not supportive.

Call the **nested discharge non-recovery hypothesis supportive** only if the nested all-cause outcome passes the same held-out incremental criteria against D0, has adequate event counts, and the conclusion is stable in the chronological and density/gap sensitivities. If early trajectory supports but discharge non-recovery does not, retain only the narrower conclusion that early change may have prognostic association; do not claim a discharge-readiness signal.

Call either analysis inconclusive if the TACE dictionary is not reproducible, the assay semantics or units cannot be independently validated, event timestamps cannot be reconstructed, complete-case selection is strongly calendar/department dependent, the nested event gate fails, or uncertainty is too wide to distinguish no incremental information from a clinically meaningful gain. A sparse liver-coded endpoint remains inconclusive even when the all-cause endpoint is supportive.

Supportive results justify a prospective study using clinically adjudicated decompensation, reliable treatment/scheduling information, and a prespecified monitoring or discharge pathway. Adverse results argue against treating routine laboratory trajectories as a stand-alone escalation rule. Inconclusive results identify the needed next data: laboratory units and provenance, medication/contrast/dose details, outpatient capture, treatment intent, tumor stage, and adjudicated outcomes. None of these results establishes causal TACE harm or benefit, a safe discharge threshold, unplanned readmission, recurrence, tumor response, mortality benefit, or clinical utility.
