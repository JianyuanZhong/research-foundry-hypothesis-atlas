# Provisional episode-18 hypothesis: does early hypotension duration add information beyond nadir for subsequent AKI?

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU admission with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. Cumulative duration is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is a non-causal association hypothesis. It does not claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Why this matters and what is unresolved

A single nadir may be transient or artifact-prone, whereas sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved claim is whether duration adds information beyond nadir after explicit pre-exposure creatinine anchoring, post-exposure outcome timing, and safeguards for observation intensity and hospital practice. Resolving this could determine whether early AKI risk assessment should use duration/trajectory rather than only a single minimum blood pressure.

The strongest evidence currently supports only the clinical relevance of hypotension burden and the dynamic, heterogeneous nature of ICU AKI; it does not establish this eICU duration-versus-nadir association. ## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results require a reproducible positive incremental association for duration beyond nadir in the prespecified held-out evaluation, with uncertainty that excludes a clinically negligible increment and robustness to observation-density and hospital strata. Adverse results are a null or reversed increment, instability across hospitals, or loss after timing/ascertainment safeguards; these would argue against using duration as an added signal. Inconclusive results include imprecise estimates, inadequate creatinine coverage, or unresolved urine-output semantics.

The experiment cannot establish causality, a treatment threshold, or clinical utility without additional expert review and prospective or externally validated data.
