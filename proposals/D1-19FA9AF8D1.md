# Does a laboratory-surveillance drop matter specifically when recorded instability persists?

Status: Episode 48 substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. This is a fixed-landmark observational recorded-care and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test of laboratory ordering, a treatment study, a physiologic-recovery study, a hospital-quality ranking, or evidence that a monitoring intervention improves outcomes.

## Unresolved question, strongest evidence and advance

The strongest claim supported by inspected evidence is limited: the configured eICU snapshot contains joinable, multi-hospital ICU-relative laboratory result/revision, periodic vital, recorded support, care-plan and ICU-disposition fields. Exact schemas and live gzip headers contain the required keys and offsets, and a bounded outcome-blind sample confirms pre-landmark rows in every planned stream. The assessed parent supplies a rigorous design for testing whether no revision-safe laboratory availability in minutes (300,360] after recorded instability in (180,300] is associated with later recorded ICU death rather than alive discharge. Neither that design nor this discovery episode has fitted a model or established a clinical result.

A central unresolved claim remains. The parent calls the exposure a surveillance fall “after instability,” but does not establish that recorded instability persisted during the hour in which laboratory availability fell. A patient may have stabilized before the laboratory gap; in that case the gap could be aligned with recovery rather than discordant with need.

The experiment will test:

> Among adult first ICU stays with recorded instability in (180,300], prior revision-safe laboratory surveillance, and adequate vital observation in (300,360], is the death-specific association of zero final-hour laboratory availability larger when recorded instability persists through (300,360] than when the same recorded instability has resolved?

This persistence-by-drop interaction is the primary unresolved claim. Its clinical value is that it distinguishes a generic post-instability workflow pattern from a more specific recorded mismatch between ongoing instability and falling surveillance. Supportive evidence would justify prospective clinical adjudication and silent-mode evaluation of this pattern. It would not show that an unmeasured biochemical abnormality existed, that tests were intentionally withheld, or that ordering more tests would improve survival.

The substantive advance over the parent is an outcome-blind, within-stay state transition and effect-modification test. It operationalizes the seed’s “while a patient remains unstable” requirement, contrasts persistent versus resolved recorded instability under matched observation support, and challenges the interpretation with competing discharge, support-documentation, interface, revision and temporal-order controls.

## Population, landmark, variables and estimand

Use only eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`.

From `patient`, include age >=18, recoding `> 89` to 90. Select the first ICU stay per `uniquepid` by smallest valid numeric `unitvisitnumber`, breaking ties by numeric `patientunitstayid`. Require numeric `unitdischargeoffset > 360`. Keep every stay for a `uniquepid` in one outer hospital fold. Time zero is ICU admission; calendar time-of-day fields are not used.

A qualifying periodic-vital time has numeric `observationoffset` and at least one nonmissing numeric value among `systemicmean`, `sao2`, `heartrate`, and `respiration`. Require at least two qualifying times separated by >=30 minutes in each of (180,300] and (300,360]. Report field-level missingness and require each persistence/resolution cell to have >=20 hospitals. Missing values never count as normal.

Define early recorded instability E=1 when at least two qualifying times separated by >=30 minutes in (180,300] each have any of: `systemicmean <65`, `sao2 <92`, `heartrate >=120`, or `respiration >=24`. Freeze thresholds before outcomes.

Among E=1 stays, define final-hour state:
- P=1, persistent recorded instability: at least two qualifying abnormal times separated by >=30 minutes in (300,360].
- P=0, resolved recorded instability: no qualifying abnormal time in (300,360], with the required two qualifying observation times separated by >=30 minutes.
- Intermediate/ambiguous patterns, including exactly one abnormal time, are excluded from the primary P interaction but retained in a separately reported sensitivity; they may not be relabeled as resolved.

For `lab`, retain a row only when numeric `labresultoffset` is in [0,360] and `labresultrevisedoffset` is missing or <=360. Define conservative `availability_offset=max(labresultoffset,labresultrevisedoffset)` when revision is numeric, otherwise `labresultoffset`. These are result/revision availability clocks, not specimen/order times.

Require >=2 distinct conservative availability times in (180,300]. Define D=1 when there are zero distinct availability times in (300,360], and D=0 when there are >=2 distinct availability times in (300,360]. Stays with exactly one final-hour time are outside the primary binary contrast and retained in a count-gradient sensitivity. A patient with no prior laboratory interface evidence cannot enter as D=1. Baseline counts, distinct key/interface coverage, last availability gap and revision-known/missing channels through minute 300 are covariates.

The primary estimand is the standardized difference-in-differences at 1,440 minutes after the landmark:
`[CIF_death(D=1)-CIF_death(D=0) | P=1] - [CIF_death(D=1)-CIF_death(D=0) | P=0]`,
conditional on the measured pre-landmark covariates and reported as a predictive association, not a causal contrast. Also report each P-specific contrast and the incremental hospital-held-out integrated competing-risk Brier score and calibration of the drop interaction.

Outcome time is (360,1800] in 12 60-minute intervals. Cause 1 is `unitdischargestatus=Expired` with numeric `unitdischargeoffset` in the window. Cause 2 is `unitdischargestatus=Alive` with offset in the window. No event by 1,800 is administrative censoring. Missing/other status is tabulated and excluded under a frozen rule. Do not impute death from destination, use hospital mortality, or claim outcomes after alive ICU discharge.

## Baseline and scientifically substantive alternative

Both methods use identical cohort rows, six 60-minute bins, fit-fold codebooks, admission/state/lab/support/EOL inputs, outcomes and hospital folds.

Transparent baseline:
- `B_state`: pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards for death and alive discharge. Inputs include admission context; six-bin vital values, slopes, missingness and observation burden; E/P state; lab result state and process only through minute 300; and timestamp-safe support/EOL/intake-output documentation through 360. It excludes final-hour lab-drop variables.
- `B_discord`: `B_state` plus D, final-hour lab count/gap/key/revision channels, P-by-D, and prespecified state-by-continuous-count terms. The primary inference is the P-by-D standardized CIF interaction and paired `B_discord-B_state` death/discharge performance.

Learned temporal alternative:
- `T_discord`: one-layer six-bin GRU with separate death/discharge hazard heads, using exactly the same channels and no hospital identifier. It can represent whether instability persists as laboratory availability falls, cross-domain ordering, duration and co-movement that binary P/D plus additive summaries discard.
- Fit a parameter-matched non-temporal MLP and within-patient time-order shuffle. A learned advantage supports temporal information only if it improves held-out death Brier/calibration with compatible uncertainty over both `B_discord` and the matched MLP, and attenuates under order shuffle. Otherwise retain the transparent interaction as primary and report T as adverse/inconclusive.

A mechanistic latent “need for surveillance” model is deferred because specimen/order times, assay units, raw waveforms, verified delivered support/doses, clinician intent and adjudicated physiologic states are absent. Revisit only when those dependencies permit an explicit observation/outcome model. This is a scientific dependency, not a CPU/GPU ban.

## Split, support, uncertainty and baselines

Use five outer folds holding out whole hospitals, approximately 20% each. Keep `uniquepid` together. Within development folds use a 75/25 hospital-grouped fit/validation split. Learn codebooks, support eligibility, scaling, imputation, regularization, calibration and early stopping only in fit hospitals. No outcome-dependent analyte, field, support-category or threshold selection.

Before fitting outcomes, report all E/P/D cells, hospitals, death/discharge/censor counts, observation density, lab interfaces and overlap. Primary interaction is classifiable only if each of the four P-by-D cells has >=200 episodes, >=20 hospitals and >=50 recorded deaths; the full risk set must have >=1,000 episodes and >=300 recorded deaths. Every support category requires >=1% fit prevalence and >=20 hospitals. If a gate fails, report the relevant claim inconclusive; do not alter windows/population to rescue it.

Primary uncertainty is >=1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions, or a predeclared hierarchical hospital/fold model if the bootstrap is unstable. Report 95% intervals for each standardized CIF contrast and interaction; macro-hospital and patient-weighted integrated Brier and calibration; and between-hospital heterogeneity. AUROC is secondary and cannot substitute for calibration or endpoint-specific evidence.

## Falsification and sensitivity

1. Permute final-hour lab process histories within hospital, P, prior lab intensity and baseline-risk strata. Patient-linked P-by-D evidence should attenuate.
2. Swap the (180,300] and (300,360] lab windows within support-compatible patients while preserving total counts, keys and revisions. Persistence-discordance evidence should attenuate.
3. Swap or permute vital-bin order within patient while preserving marginal values and observation burden. The P interaction and GRU temporal advantage should attenuate.
4. Replace the lab drop with matched drops in treatment/respiratory/intake-output documentation. A comparable or larger interaction indicates generic documentation loss rather than lab-specific information.
5. Compare death and alive-discharge increments. A similar/larger discharge interaction, especially near 360, is adverse to a deterioration-specific interpretation.
6. Exclude any EOL save/discussion in [0,360], then include timestamp channels. Absence is never called no limitation.
7. Compare conservative availability and result clocks on identical retained lab rows, revision-known-only rows, and common key/interface panels; any post-360 revision remains leakage audit only.
8. Repeat across outcome-free hospital surveillance regimes and leave each supported regime out. One-hospital/interface concentration or failed overlap is not transportable.
9. Re-run P using each individual vital threshold, a stricter requirement that the same abnormal field occurs twice, and an adjacent 20-minute separation. Direction reversal is instability/inconclusive.
10. Include the ambiguous one-final-abnormal-time group separately and fit the continuous count gradient. It cannot overwrite the primary contrast.
11. Mask (300,360] vital channels other than the frozen P definition, then mask support channels, to expose reliance on near-landmark documentation.
12. For T, require parameter-matched MLP and order-shuffle controls.
13. Audit every predictor for post-360 leakage, invalid revisions, `activeupondischarge`, target-fold preprocessing, hospital ID, and outcome-derived codebooks. Any material violation invalidates the fit.

## Interpretation criteria

Supportive evidence requires all support/overlap gates; a positive P-by-D standardized death-CIF interaction with its 95% interval excluding zero; compatible improvement in held-out death integrated Brier/calibration without material discharge degradation; a smaller discharge interaction; attenuation under patient-link and temporal falsifications; no matched generic-documentation replacement; and stability across revision/interface/regime/EOL challenges. This supports a transportable recorded-data association between persistent recorded instability plus falling lab availability and recorded ICU death. It does not establish unmet clinical need or intervention benefit.

Adverse evidence includes null/reversed P-by-D interaction, no `B_discord` increment, an equal/larger alive-discharge or generic-documentation interaction, persistence after broken patient/time linkage, concentration in one hospital/interface, or dependence on post-landmark/revision leakage. Such results reject the proposed interpretation even if overall prediction remains strong.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed P/D event or hospital gates, poor overlap, intervals spanning zero and a clinically meaningful interaction, unstable folds, excessive missing vital fields or revisions, availability/result-clock disagreement, threshold fragility, or learned improvement without temporal falsification. Inconclusive is not evidence for or against physiology.

## Actual deliverable, verification and compute

The future solver must newly build the cohort and fold manifest; freeze fit-fold codebooks; fit `B_state`, `B_discord`, matched documentation controls, clock sensitivities, `T_discord` and MLP/shuffle controls; produce held-out death/discharge CIFs, calibration and Brier outputs with clustered uncertainty; execute every support gate/falsification; and save an interpretation trace linking every conclusion to an output file, metric, cell and interval. Readiness or source checks do not complete the objective.

Harbor can verify paths, columns, joins, temporal boundaries, cohort arithmetic, model fitting, held-out predictions, uncertainty calculations, falsification execution and whether submitted conclusions match computed outputs. It cannot verify true physiologic instability, whether a test was ordered/collected, clinician intent, delivered treatment, complete goals of care, unrecorded death, causality, benefit or external validity. Those need chart adjudication, unit/order/specimen data, an external health system and prospective silent-mode followed by impact or randomized evaluation.

Measured discovery compute: a bounded Python gzip audit inspected headers and up to 100,000 rows in each planned source in 2.9 seconds on ordinary CPU; it found pre-360 offsets in every planned stream but did not form a cohort or inspect outcomes. The inherited parent records larger outcome-blind source/cohort audits; they are feasibility evidence, not fitted results.

Unverified future solver budget: streaming aggregation with 16 CPUs and <=192 GiB RAM for 2–4 hours; transparent fits/bootstrap <=4 CPU-hours after feature materialization; one allocated A100 (`cuda:0`) for <=2 hours for repeated GRU/MLP fits, with CPU fallback likely slower. This fits the planning envelope of 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds. Device availability was not probed because it does not settle scientific selection.

## Demonstrations, seeds and deferred alternatives

Natural-history/Delphi informs the equal-input temporal ordering test, but this ICU experiment neither reproduces UKB disease trajectories nor claims Danish external validation. The Bayesian demonstration motivates latent trajectories, but genetics and defensible biological latent states are unavailable; a bounded recorded-sequence comparison is used. Full Oncoformer reproduction is impossible here because local images and the main article/full STAR Methods are unavailable; only its accessible principle of comparing matched modalities/baselines informs the design.

All ten eICU seed candidates were retrieved/episode-visible and assessed as valid untested source material. Seed 08 directly motivates this child. Seed 02 is incorporated as hospital surveillance/interface transport. Seeds 01 and 10 are deferred because infusion/support records cannot establish delivered strategy, dose, liberation or hospital quality. Seed 03 would require artifact-aware blood-pressure duration and kidney-outcome work; seed 04 requires aligned PaO2/FiO2 and cannot use SpO2=100% as hyperoxemia; seed 05 requires verified complete ventilator mechanics; seed 06 lacks post-ICU outcome/staffing/bed-capacity evidence; seed 07 lacks the pre-transfer course; seed 09 has previously measured sparse conservative event support and missing delivered nutrition/administration evidence. Revisit these only when their stated dependencies or stronger support audits are available.

Not chosen: a proximal post-360 “vital deterioration” endpoint. It risks differential ascertainment because absence of vital records after discharge or interface loss is inseparable from no event, and persistence is already measured pre-landmark. Revisit only with a joint observation/outcome estimand and adequate monitored-at-risk support. Also not chosen: a hospital causal effect or instrument; hospital practice preference is not automatically an instrument.
