# JAK-STAT Dependence of Macrophage-Induced FLS Inflammatory Response in RA Synovial Tissue

## Scientific Opening

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue exhibits a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop between macrophages and fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS). The crosstalk is well documented: FLS secrete IL-34, CCL2, and CXCL12 that recruit and polarize macrophages toward the M1 phenotype, while M1 macrophage-derived TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β further activate FLS. However, the **specific signaling pathways** that mediate the macrophage-induced component of FLS activation remain incompletely characterized.

JAK-STAT signaling is one of the major signaling pathways that mediate cellular interactions in the RA synovial microenvironment, contributing to inflammatory persistence and progressive remodeling [K1]. JAK-STAT signaling is involved in the pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis [K2]. JAK-STAT inhibitors are effective in rheumatoid arthritis [K3].

Three non-equivalent explanations are compatible with the current evidence:

1. **JAK-STAT dependent model**: Macrophage-induced FLS inflammatory gene expression is selectively dependent on JAK-STAT signaling. JAK-STAT inhibition reduces the macrophage-induced component of FLS inflammatory gene expression, while the baseline FLS inflammatory state is less affected.

2. **Broad kinase inhibition model**: The observed inhibitor effect is not specific to JAK-STAT but reflects broad kinase inhibition. The inhibitor affects both the baseline FLS inflammatory state and the macrophage-induced component equally.

3. **Composition-driven model**: The apparent "inhibitor effect" is an artifact of changes in cell composition (e.g., reduced macrophage viability or altered FLS proliferation) rather than a genuine effect on signaling pathways.

This distinction is clinically consequential: if the macrophage-induced component of FLS activation is selectively dependent on JAK-STAT, then JAK-STAT inhibitors may be effective in RA by specifically disrupting the macrophage-FLS feedback loop. If the inhibitor effect is broad or composition-driven, then JAK-STAT inhibitors may not be effective in RA by this mechanism.

## Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: In RA FLS, macrophage coculture induces an inflammatory response that is selectively dependent on JAK-STAT signaling. Specifically, the macrophage-induced component of FLS inflammatory gene expression (i.e., the difference between coculture and FLS-only conditions) is reduced by JAK-STAT inhibition, while the baseline FLS inflammatory state is less affected.

## Competing Explanations

1. **JAK-STAT dependent model**: Macrophage-induced FLS inflammatory gene expression is selectively dependent on JAK-STAT signaling. JAK-STAT inhibition reduces the macrophage-induced component of FLS inflammatory gene expression, while the baseline FLS inflammatory state is less affected.
   - **Observable prediction**: The inhibitor effect is larger for the macrophage-induced component (coculture - FLS-only) than for the baseline FLS inflammatory state (FLS-only).

2. **Broad kinase inhibition model**: The observed inhibitor effect is not specific to JAK-STAT but reflects broad kinase inhibition. The inhibitor affects both the baseline FLS inflammatory state and the macrophage-induced component equally.
   - **Observable prediction**: The inhibitor effect is similar for the macrophage-induced component and the baseline FLS inflammatory state.

3. **Composition-driven model**: The apparent "inhibitor effect" is an artifact of changes in cell composition (e.g., reduced macrophage viability or altered FLS proliferation) rather than a genuine effect on signaling pathways.
   - **Observable prediction**: The inhibitor effect is accompanied by changes in cell composition (e.g., reduced macrophage viability or altered FLS proliferation).

## Falsifiable Test

**Test**: Compare the inhibitor effect on the macrophage-induced component of FLS inflammatory gene expression to the inhibitor effect on the baseline FLS inflammatory state.

**Supportive outcome**: The inhibitor effect is significantly larger for the macrophage-induced component (coculture - FLS-only) than for the baseline FLS inflammatory state (FLS-only). This supports the JAK-STAT dependent model.

**Adverse outcome**: The inhibitor effect is similar for the macrophage-induced component and the baseline FLS inflammatory state. This supports the broad kinase inhibition model.

**Inconclusive outcome**: The inhibitor effect is accompanied by changes in cell composition (e.g., reduced macrophage viability or altered FLS proliferation). This supports the composition-driven model.

## Limitations

- The inhibitor specificity is not confirmed in the available datasets.
- The effect size of JAK-STAT inhibition on the macrophage-induced component is not known a priori.
- The composition-driven model cannot be fully excluded without additional data on cell composition.
