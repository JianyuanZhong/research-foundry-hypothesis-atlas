# HCC recovery-to-deterioration hypothesis (working)

## Question
Among adult HCC inpatient encounters, does a short early inpatient recovery trajectory identify patients at increased risk of a subsequent clinically important deterioration, beyond what is explained by severity at the start of the observation period?

## Why it matters
Clinicians often use apparent improvement to de-escalate care. If early recovery can mask an impending deterioration, a validated signal could inform monitoring intensity and escalation decisions. A negative result would weaken the claim that routine recovery trajectories carry incremental prognostic information.

## Leading claim and strongest rival
- Leading claim: the temporal pattern of improvement followed by worsening contains information about subsequent instability.
- Rival: the pattern is explained by current severity, documentation intensity, coding practice, or delayed recognition of care already being escalated.

The experiment must compare a severity-adjusted baseline with a temporal model and test whether the temporal component improves prediction after controlling for current severity and encounter-level confounding.

Define:

2. An observation period (e.g., next 24–72 hours).
3. A clinically meaningful deterioration outcome, preferably a single adjudicated endpoint such as new organ support, escalation to ICU, or a defined acute deterioration event, with explicit temporal boundaries.

## Baseline versus alternative
- Baseline: severity-adjusted logistic model using current severity and baseline covariates only.
- Alternative: temporal model using the ordered early recovery trajectory (labs, vitals, orders/medications, procedures) to capture change over time.

The alternative can reveal whether the sequence and rate of change add information beyond a static severity snapshot. It is not justified merely for predictive gain; the comparison tests whether longitudinal recovery is prognostically informative after adjustment.

## Evaluation
Use time-based or encounter-level split to avoid leakage. Report outcome frequency, calibration, discrimination, uncertainty intervals, and comparison with the baseline. Define supportive, adverse and inconclusive outcomes. Supportive results suggest incremental prognostic signal; adverse results suggest no incremental value after severity adjustment; inconclusive results indicate insufficient events or uncertainty.

## Scientific deliverable
Fit and compare the two models on the same HCC population and outcome. Required outputs are event counts, model performance with uncertainty, baseline comparison, and a conclusion linked to computed results. No clinical causal claim is permitted from this observational design.

## Alternatives and limitations
Consider selection, timing, documentation and treatment artifacts. If the outcome requires clinical adjudication or is unavailable, state that explicitly. The experiment is a bounded prognostic association study, not a mechanistic or causal analysis.
