# Organ-Function Trajectory as an Effect Modifier of Antimicrobial De-escalation Safety in ICU: A Falsifiable MIMIC-IV Experiment

**Parent:** [prior hypothesis] (expert seed 7: "Following de-escalation, risk is lower in patients with clear evidence of infection and improving organ function than in patients who continue to deteriorate.")
**Dataset:** MIMIC-IV 3.1 (frozen archive, [source checksum])
**Status:** Data-bound proposal with verified schema, prespecified de-escalation phenotype, executed bounded feasibility probe, and three inspected key references. No full analysis was run.

---

## 1. Scientific Question

**Unresolved claim (what this experiment tests):**
> In adult ICU patients with culture-confirmed infection who receive broad-spectrum antibiotics and then have their regimen narrowed (de-escalation) after susceptibility results are available, the association between de-escalation and in-hospital mortality differs by organ-function trajectory (SOFA score change from landmark to 48h post-landmark) — i.e., organ-function trajectory is an effect modifier of the de-escalation–mortality relationship.

**Why this matters:**
Antimicrobial de-escalation is a critical stewardship decision in the ICU. The MINT trial and subsequent MIMIC-IV studies [K1] have shown that de-escalation at day 4 is not associated with higher mortality and may be associated with lower mortality. However, these studies did not stratify by organ-function trajectory. The clinical question is whether de-escalation is safe in *all* patients or whether it is only safe in patients who are clinically improving. If organ-function trajectory modifies the safety of de-escalation, this would change how clinicians make de-escalation decisions: de-escalation might be appropriate for improvers but risky for deteriorators.

**Strongest rival explanation:**
Confounding by indication: clinicians may de-escalate in patients who are already improving (and therefore have better outcomes regardless of de-escalation), and continue broad-spectrum coverage in patients who are deteriorating (and therefore have worse outcomes regardless of continued coverage). This would create a spurious association between de-escalation and better outcomes in improvers. The landmark design (susceptibility result availability) and SOFA trajectory adjustment partially address this, but residual confounding is possible.

## 2. Supported Claims (from prior literature, not re-tested here)

- [K1] Zhang et al. (2026) demonstrated that day-4 antipseudomonal and anti-MRSA de-escalation in MIMIC-IV sepsis patients was not associated with higher 30-day mortality and was associated with lower in-hospital death and more antibiotic-free days. However, this study did not stratify by organ-function trajectory.
- [K2] Sava et al. (2026) conducted a scoping review of 51 publications on de-escalation in critically ill adults with Gram-negative infections, finding that the evidence base is fragmented, definitions are heterogeneous, and reassessment typically occurs at 48-72h. The review identified standardized definitions and resistance-focused trials as research priorities.
- [K3] Zhuo et al. (2026) demonstrated that combined trajectories of serum sodium and urine output in the first 96h of ICU admission are associated with 30-day mortality in sepsis patients (MIMIC-IV), supporting the use of organ-function trajectories for prognostic stratification.

## 5. Organ-Function Trajectory Covariate

**SOFA score calculation:**
- Landmark SOFA: calculated at storetime (susceptibility result availability)
- 48h SOFA: calculated at storetime + 48h
- SOFA change: 48h SOFA - landmark SOFA

**SOFA components (from labevents/chartevents):**
- Creatinine (itemid 50912): 0-4 points
- Platelets (itemid 26510): 0-4 points
- Bilirubin (itemid 223905): 0-4 points
- PaO2/FiO2 (itemid 50821/52042 + FiO2 223835): 0-4 points
- GCS (itemid 220739): 0-4 points

**Trajectory categories:**
- Improver: SOFA change <= -1 (decrease of >= 1 point)
- Stable: SOFA change = 0
- Deteriorator: SOFA change >= +1 (increase of >= 1 point)

## 7. Analysis Plan

**Baseline model:**
Logistic regression: death ~ de-escalation + age + sex + landmark SOFA

**Extended model:**
Logistic regression: death ~ de-escalation + age + sex + landmark SOFA + SOFA change + de-escalation × SOFA change interaction

**Alternative:**
XGBoost (300 rounds, 5-fold CV) with same features

**Primary test:**
Interaction term (de-escalation × SOFA change) - tests whether the effect of de-escalation on mortality differs by organ-function trajectory

**Secondary analyses:**
- Incremental discrimination: ΔAUROC (extended vs baseline)
- Net reclassification improvement (NRI)
- Integrated discrimination improvement (IDI)
- Subgroup analysis: de-escalation effect in improvers vs deteriorators

---

## 8. Falsification Criteria

**Supportive:**
- Interaction term p < 0.05
- De-escalation associated with lower mortality in improvers (OR < 1) and higher mortality in deteriorators (OR > 1)
- ΔAUROC > 0.01 (extended vs baseline)

**Adverse:**
- No interaction (p > 0.10)
- De-escalation associated with higher mortality in both groups (OR > 1 in both improvers and deteriorators)

**Inconclusive:**
- Interaction p 0.05-0.10
- Insufficient power (n < 500 in either trajectory group)
- Wide confidence intervals crossing 1.0 in both subgroups

## 11. Limitations

1. **Confounding by indication:** Clinicians may de-escalate in patients who are already improving. The landmark design and SOFA trajectory adjustment partially address this, but residual confounding is possible.
2. **SOFA score coverage:** Not all SOFA components may be available at both time points (landmark and 48h post-landmark). Missing data handling will be needed.
3. **De-escalation definition:** The prespecified broad/narrow agent lists may not capture all de-escalation events. Some de-escalation may involve agents not on the list.
4. **Single-center data:** MIMIC-IV is from a single academic medical center. External validation is needed.
5. **Retrospective design:** Causal inference is limited. The results are hypothesis-generating.
