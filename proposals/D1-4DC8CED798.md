# Day-42 HCC exact-code repeat-TACE: patient-held-out state-versus-workflow test

Status: repair child of [prior hypothesis]. This is a design only; no cohort fit, model result, or positive finding is claimed.

## Repair and scientific deliverable

The parent’s clinical question and exact-code hypothesis are retained:

> Among patients observable at day 42 after a first locally recorded exact-code TACE, does adding the recorded assay-state channel at the day-42 landmark improve calibrated prediction of a later exact-code repeat-TACE event beyond matched observation and care-process information?

The inherited earliest-80%/latest-20% calendar holdout is not an estimable primary transport test in this snapshot. The Lead’s bounded audit found 951 eligible patients and 108 later events overall, but only 3 events in the latest-20% calendar test; order and calendar strata were empty or degenerate. Three events cannot support a transport conclusion, positive or negative. This child changes the primary temporal estimand, not the landmark, endpoint, channels, source bindings, or clinical question.

The primary estimand is the paired held-out difference in calibrated prediction loss for B=M0+P_obs+P_care+S versus A=M0+P_obs+P_care over the overall eligible target population under a deterministic patient-level 80/20 event-support-aware random split. The workflow increment is A versus M0+P_obs and the state-versus-workflow contrast is the difference between these increments. This is predictive/descriptive evidence about recorded information. It is not a causal assay effect, tumor-response estimate, treatment-benefit estimate, or treatment rule.

The actual deliverable is newly fitted, leakage-safe models and weights on development patients, frozen patient assignments, paired untouched-test predictions and losses, calibration/uncertainty estimates, support audits, falsification controls, and a claim-output map linking each conclusion to rows in those outputs. Completion is those artifacts with provenance, including an honest inconclusive result when a gate fails; completion is not confirmation of the hypothesis.

## Evidence, unresolved rival, and clinical advance

[K1] supports the closest inspected predictive evidence: a retrospective multicenter CT radiomics/deep-learning repeat-TACE study reported useful prediction but weaker external cohorts and identified overfitting, scanner and generalization limitations. Its imaging inputs are not available here. This proposal adds a routine longitudinal EHR test of whether a recorded assay state adds information beyond the process that makes the assay observable, with a patient-held-out estimand rather than a reproduction of [K1].

[K2] bounds the endpoint: the inspected abstract describes imaging and necrosis-aware mRECIST/RECIC assessment of HCC treatment response. The local procedure record cannot establish response, necrosis, progression, or treatment benefit.

[K3] is a consequential limitation/rival: its inspected abstract explicitly identifies missing imaging outcomes, ascertainment, exposure classification and confounding in a real-world HCC comparison, and does not identify a comparative treatment effect. The present study therefore treats observation and care process as a competing explanation, rather than interpreting an assay increment as biology.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that a later locally recorded repeat-TACE event may be predictable from routinely recorded longitudinal information. The unresolved claim is whether assay values/qualitative assay states carry held-out information after matching the observation and care workflow. The leading rival is workflow/selection: scheduled follow-up, orders, encounters, repeated testing, severity and planned retreatment can produce both more assay records and more recorded TACE. A second rival is timing/lead-time: records immediately before day 42 may encode a near-term plan. The state-consistent and workflow-consistent explanations differ on the paired channel ablations, the 7-day lead-time control, process-only permutations and assay-value/time permutations.

A supportive state increment would justify an imaging/intent/outside-care adjudication study sampling the supported population; it would not prove tumor biology. A workflow-dominant result would redirect data acquisition toward scheduling, intent, outside-care linkage and severity adjudication. An adverse or inconclusive result would prevent escalation and preserve the need for better evidence. The design can check computation, calibration, support and predictive contrasts; response, intent, outside care, causal mechanism, harms, costs and clinical utility require adjudication or another study.

## Disjoint channels and models

All dictionaries, encoders, scaling, imputation, cutpoints, weight models, hyperparameters and permutation schemes are fit inside development folds only.

M0 contains pre-index age, sex, department, encounter/admission history, diagnosis counts through matched encounters, procedure counts and exact-assay summaries in [-365,0). S contains only the eight assay labels in (0,42], audited numeric values, qualitative/inequality flags and assay-specific changes from a valid pre-index observation in [-365,0); it contains no request or missingness indicator. P_obs contains dated encounter, examination, medication/order/lab observation counts, distinct dates/gaps, assay-label request/result counts and availability/missingness indicators in (0,42], with no assay values or qualitative results. P_care contains dated non-TACE procedure tokens, non-drug-order tokens, medication starts/ends, department/visit marks and timing in (0,42], with no assay values.

The transparent primary baseline is elastic-net pooled-logistic discrete-time hazards for day bins 43–180 with fixed bins or a low-degree time spline. Fit M0, M0+P_obs, M0+P_care, A=M0+P_obs+P_care, M0+P_obs+S, and B=A+S. The confirmatory state contrast is A versus B; the matched workflow contrast is M0+P_obs versus A. Report weighted log loss, Brier/integrated Brier, calibration slope/intercept, and secondary AUROC/AUPRC. Differences use paired held-out predictions.

The scientifically substantive learned alternative is a compact time-aware GRU over the same -365 through +42 stream, with elapsed-time decay, event category/label, audited numeric value, qualitative/inequality flags, relative time, time since prior event and channel tag. It receives the same six ablations, target, split, IPCW/observation weights, metrics and three fixed development seeds. It may reveal nonlinear trajectories or timing patterns that elastic-net summaries lose; it cannot recover missing imaging, intent, outside care, units or reference ranges. The elastic-net remains primary because channel ablations, losses and calibration are auditable. The GRU is retained as a matched sensitivity because the scientific uncertainty includes trajectory shape, not merely a small predictive gain. Larger transformers and broad hyperparameter searches are deferred because they add flexibility without resolving the state/workflow ambiguity.

## Censoring, observation and uncertainty

Fit all-source right-censoring weights within development folds from information available before each risk interval, with a frozen tail rule and effective-sample-size audit. For S, cross-fit day-42 assay-observation weights using M0, pre-day-42 P_obs and P_care and the relevant prespecified support level. Report ordinary and observation-weighted estimates separately. No extrapolation is permitted.

The single confirmatory family contains the primary state and workflow contrasts over the overall held-out population and the two prespecified horizons. Use a max-|t| patient-bootstrap distribution over this family for simultaneous 95% intervals. Learned-model, repeated-seed, calendar and stratum results are labeled sensitivities and do not rescue a failed primary gate. Define D_state = loss(A)-loss(B), D_workflow = loss(M0+P_obs)-loss(A), and D_state-vs-workflow = D_state-D_workflow; positive means the added channel lowers loss.

Supportive overall state evidence requires a simultaneous lower 95% bound for standardized D_state >0, calibrated B, consistent direction under ordinary and observation-weighted scoring, and persistence under the 7-day lead-time control. State-consistent evidence is adverse if the increment is reproduced by assay-value/time permutations, disappears under lead-time/process controls, or is no larger than workflow evidence. A workflow-dominant result is D_workflow positive and comparable to or larger than D_state; mixed means both remain positive. An imprecise or support-failing result is inconclusive, not a null refutation. These margins are computational decision rules, not clinical MCIDs.

## Clinical limits and revisit rules

The snapshot lacks mortality, harms, costs, treatment intent, outside-care events, imaging files/adjudicated response, assay units/reference ranges and expert severity review. A positive held-out recorded-state increment can only justify the next adjudication study; it cannot be used as a patient-level treatment rule. Continue to imaging/intent/outside-care linkage if the primary state gate and calibration/lead-time checks pass. Prioritize workflow, scheduling and outside-care data if process evidence dominates. Defer calendar transport whenever its support gate fails, as expected from the three-event audit. Abandon the assay-state validation direction only after adequately supported repeated controls show that process/permutation features match the assay increment or the increment disappears under leakage-safe timing. A failed GRU alone never refutes the hypothesis.
