# Is a late ICU laboratory signal real surveillance timing or revision latency?

Status: Episode-26 targeted successor to [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational, prognostic measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that changing laboratory surveillance improves survival.

## Remaining ambiguity and substantive advance

The preceding candidate tests whether six pre-landmark laboratory-surveillance bins outperform aggregate counts. One clinically important ambiguity remains: a late bin can represent a late specimen/result pattern, or an earlier result whose final value was posted/revised late. eICU exposes `labresultoffset` and `labresultrevisedoffset`, but neither is a specimen-collection time and the lab schema has no separate unit column. Therefore an apparent timing signal could be an information-availability or interface process rather than a patient-linked deterioration signal.

The strongest available evidence is an outcome-blind source/header and feasibility audit, plus the parent’s inherited adult first-stay audit; no model has been fitted here. The available evidence supports only that the source contains result and revision offsets, vital observations, limited structured end-of-life documentation and recorded ICU disposition. It does not support that any laboratory was ordered at the recorded result time, that a revised value was unavailable earlier, or that surveillance causes outcome.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is narrower and clinically consequential: among adults observed at minute 360, does a laboratory-process signal that survives a conservative no-post-landmark-revision information audit carry more transportable, death-specific information than aggregate laboratory process and recorded physiology? The experiment will also test whether the signal changes materially when the same revision-safe rows are binned by result time versus conservative latest-availability time. This advances the prior work by separating a potentially actionable “information was becoming available late” pattern from a potentially misleading timestamp artifact. A positive result would justify a retrospective silent-mode review of information timing; it would not justify ordering more tests.

## Hypothesis, null and claim boundaries

Primary hypothesis (H1): replacing aggregate revision-safe laboratory-process summaries with six bins of conservative information-availability timing improves held-out-hospital prediction of recorded ICU death in (360,1800], measured by death-specific cumulative-incidence calibration and integrated competing-risk Brier score, after accounting for the same recorded laboratory state, vital state/process, admission context and pre-landmark carePlanEOL documentation.

Clock hypothesis (H1-clock): on the identical revision-safe row set, the availability-time increment is not materially worse than the result-time increment, and both increments are attenuated by destroying within-stay temporal order. This supports a recorded information-timing association, not specimen timing or physiology.

Nulls/falsifiers are that the availability-time increment is zero or unstable, that it is unchanged by order permutation, that the result-time increment exists only when post-landmark revisions are allowed, or that a matched vital-process increment is as large or larger. Such results would reject the proposed interpretation even if a high-capacity model predicts well.

No fitted result, effect estimate, clinical utility claim, causal claim, or external-validation claim is made in this proposal.

## Two comparable methods and why the alternative is informative

The simple transparent baseline B0 is pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic regression with the 12 hourly intervals and the following inputs: admission context; landmark lab state from the frozen 64-key codebook; aggregate revision-safe lab process (total numeric results, distinct result times, distinct keys/interfaces, revision-known/missing counts, observation span and gap summaries); landmark and aggregate vital state/process from both vital tables; and carePlanEOL presence/timing. It has no six-bin timing features. Regularization, imputation, scaling and calibration are learned only in development hospitals.

The primary transparent timing model B_avail uses exactly the same source rows, codebook, state channels, covariates, folds and hazard framework as B0, replacing aggregate lab-process timing summaries with the six-bin `availability_offset` channels and retaining the explicitly flagged revision channels. B_result repeats B_avail with the same revision-safe rows and six bins formed from `labresultoffset`. Thus the clock contrast is not confounded by a different cohort or codebook. A matched six-bin vital-process model is also fitted to test whether any advantage is generic documentation intensity.

The substantive learned alternative T is a small one-layer GRU (or temporal convolution if the solver documents a deterministic reason) over the six bin vectors containing the shared lab state/process, availability-time lab channels, vital channels, carePlanEOL channels and missingness indicators. It has separate death and discharge heads, hourly hazards, no hospital identifier and a parameter-matched non-temporal MLP control. It is fit only after B0/B_avail are frozen, with early stopping and hyperparameters selected in development hospitals. T can reveal persistence, bursts, co-movement and nonlinear interactions that B_avail’s additive coefficients lose. The result-time T_result is a paired sensitivity using the same rows and channel dimensions.

The learned alternative is selected for scientific information, not complexity, GPU use or a small predictive gain: retain it as a substantive result only if its held-out death calibration/Brier improvement over B_avail is uncertainty-compatible, it survives the temporal-order control, and it adds an interpretable pattern summary (for example, an early-persistent versus late-burst attribution) that is stable across folds. Otherwise report T as a prespecified negative/inconclusive branch and use B_avail for the primary clock inference. A mechanistic state-space model was considered but deferred: eICU has no verified specimen collection time, continuous physiology waveform or clinician-order process, so a mechanistic claim about biological deterioration would require unobserved transitions and unverifiable assumptions. It becomes worth revisiting only with collection/order timestamps and adjudicated physiologic states.

## Split, estimand, uncertainty and review diagnostic

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals. Keep all stays for each `uniquepid` together. Within each development fold use a 75/25 hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Learn codebooks, compatibility rules, imputation, scaling, feature selection, calibration and thresholds only from the fit portion; no outcome or endpoint-derived analyte choice is allowed in test folds.

The primary estimand is the macro-hospital mean held-out difference B_avail minus B0 in death-specific integrated competing-risk Brier score over 360--1800 and in calibration at 600, 1200 and 1800 minutes, with the discharge CIF evaluated in parallel. Secondary estimands are the clock difference B_avail minus B_result, the death-versus-discharge increment contrast, T minus B_avail, and hospital heterogeneity. Report calibration intercept/slope, CIF calibration plots/tables, integrated Brier decomposition where available, and patient-weighted results as secondary.

Use 95% uncertainty intervals from at least 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions, or a prespecified hierarchical fold/hospital model if bootstrap is computationally unstable. Report the number of hospitals, episodes, deaths, discharges and censored stays per fold and per clock representation. Do not use a normal patient-level interval as the primary transportability uncertainty.

At minute 360, provide a fixed-burden review diagnostic at predicted death-CIF thresholds 0.01, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10: reviews per 100 episodes, recorded-death capture, PPV, false-review burden and number needed to review, with B0/B_avail paired within held-out hospital. Require at least 200 held-out episodes, 20 recorded deaths, 20 alive discharges and 20 hospitals in a threshold cell; otherwise mark that cell unsupported. This is a label-relative review diagnostic, not net benefit, clinical utility or an instruction to order tests.

Support gates for the primary timing claim are at least 100 eligible hospitals, 50 event-bearing hospitals, 1,000 recorded deaths, 1,000 competing discharges, approximately 20 hospitals per outer fold where feasible, >=30% coverage for each retained vital field (or >=20% with at least two observations), and overlapping revision-safe state/process support in >=80% of held-out hospitals. Both early and late lab bins must have non-sparse support in the evaluated hospitals. Failure of a gate makes the corresponding claim inconclusive and does not authorize changing the cohort, landmark or outcome.

## Falsification and interpretation

Prespecify and save all outputs:

1. Within-patient permutation of six bin labels, separately for availability and result clocks, preserving total counts, codebook state, revision counts and missingness.
2. Hospital- and baseline-risk-stratified reassignment of lab-process histories across patients, preserving each hospital’s process distribution but breaking patient linkage.
3. Matched vital-process increment and a lab-only versus vital-only comparison.
4. Death-CIF versus alive-discharge-CIF increment contrast.
5. Strict revision-safe, common-analyte, revision-known-only and interface-stratified analyses.
6. Mask the (300,360] bin, then fit an early-only analysis; separately mask early bins to test whether the result is only a near-landmark artifact.
7. B_avail and B_result on identical retained rows; report results using any row with revision >360 only as a leakage audit.
8. T order-shuffle and parameter-matched non-temporal controls.
9. Repeat with carePlanEOL present/timing as a restriction or challenge, treating absent documentation as unknown.
10. Enforce no post-360 rows, no Apache mortality/APACHE APS fields, no hospital ID predictors, no target-fold preprocessing and no outcome-dependent codebook.

Supportive evidence requires all primary gates, an uncertainty interval for B_avail minus B0 compatible with a positive death-specific improvement without material death calibration harm, a smaller or non-positive discharge increment, attenuation after temporal-order permutation and patient-link permutation, no comparable vital-process gain, and stability across revision/interface/common-analyte restrictions. The availability-clock increment should not be an artifact confined to one interface or one late bin.

Adverse evidence is a null or adverse B_avail increment, unchanged performance after order permutation, equal/larger improvement for alive discharge, a gain reproduced by matched vital process, a gain present only when post-360 revisions are allowed, concentration in one interface/hospital regime, or reversal when revisions are handled conservatively. These outcomes reject the proposed patient-linked timing interpretation even if some model has high discrimination.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed event/overlap/temporal support gates, intervals spanning the prespecified meaningful effect and zero, unstable fold results, excessive revision-missingness, inability to reproduce the codebook, or an availability/result discrepancy that the two timestamps cannot adjudicate. A high-capacity model winning without stable falsification is not supportive by itself.

## Evidence limits and revisit criteria

The data contain recorded results, not verified orders, collection times, initial-posting times, true physiology, clinician intent or complete goals-of-care documentation. Narrative note sections are removed or not assumed to be genuine narrative; raw waveforms, laboratory units and external validation cohorts are unavailable. `carePlanEOL` is sparse and its absence cannot mean no limitation. The outcome is recorded ICU disposition, not complete mortality. Harbor can check joins, offsets, leakage, model arithmetic, uncertainty, falsification execution and whether narrative conclusions follow from computed outputs. It cannot establish that late information reflects biological decline, why a test was ordered, whether a clinician would review an alert, or that changing surveillance improves survival.

Clinical adjudication, source-system audit or a prospective silent-mode study with order/collection/result timestamps is required to distinguish testing from posting/revision workflow. External health-system validation is required for transportability beyond this eICU snapshot; an impact or randomized study is required for any claim that surveillance changes outcomes. Revisit the deferred mechanistic model only if timestamped orders/collections, assay units and adjudicated physiologic states become available.
