## Primary and alternative methods retained

The simple baseline is the parent’s prespecified logistic regression for any versus no reported falls, with the frozen covariates and linear MFI term; report the adjusted odds ratio per 1-SD MFI and 95% uncertainty interval. It supplies a low-complexity, readily interpretable estimate but assumes a linear MFI association on the log-odds scale and collapses single and recurrent reports.

The substantive alternative remains the parent’s same-cohort shrinkage-GAM, with the same binary endpoint, MFI, covariates, participants and parent-specified smoothness/selection procedure. Its purpose is to determine whether the MFI association shape departs materially from linearity without changing the clinical question; compare the prespecified linear and smooth terms using the parent’s comparison and uncertainty procedure, and show the estimated curve with uncertainty. It can reveal threshold-like or saturating association patterns that the linear baseline masks. It does not establish mechanism or improve clinical decision utility merely by fitting flexibly.

The ordinal analysis adds a distinct question about the distribution of reported burden, not another model of nonlinearity. Fit a cumulative-logit proportional-odds model with the same standardized continuous MFI and same fixed covariates. Its estimand is the common conditional odds ratio per 1-SD higher MFI for being at or above each cut (one-or-more versus none, and more-than-one versus none-or-one), under a common-slope assumption. If supported, this tests whether an association is ordered across fall-report burden categories; it does not show that MFI causes repeated falls. It is secondary and should be reported with interval estimates and its diagnostic even when inconclusive; do not promote it on the basis of a favorable p-value.

## Support, diagnostics, uncertainty, fallback

Before fitting the secondary models, tabulate all three valid response categories after applying the exact parent cohort/complete-case rules. Gate interpretation on at least 100 participants in each category and at least 10 observations in the smallest category per estimated effective slope/parameter in the ordinal model. These are minimum stability checks, not evidence of adequate power. If either fails, defer inferential ordinal/multinomial modeling; report the counts and say the burden contrast is too sparse for this design. Do not change the cohort or merge categories after seeing estimates to rescue the analysis. A descriptive three-category table may still be reported.

Evaluate proportional odds with a global score/likelihood-based test and category-specific slope diagnostics from the two cumulative splits. Treat the common-slope interpretation as untenable when the global diagnostic is p<0.01 **and** the estimated MFI slopes show consequential heterogeneity (e.g., opposite signs or a difference greater than 25% on the log-odds scale); report both diagnostic components. The stricter prespecified criterion reduces reliance on a single sensitive test. If the proportional-odds model fails this criterion and the category support gate is met, use multinomial logistic regression with no-fall reports as reference and report separate one-fall-versus-none and recurrent-versus-none conditional odds ratios with uncertainty. Do not call the multinomial coefficients a graded common effect. If the alternative is too sparse, unstable, or nonconvergent, defer inference rather than collapse categories or expand the study.

Use two-sided 95% uncertainty intervals for primary and secondary estimates and the parent’s existing robust/participant-bootstrap procedure, with 1,000 participant-level bootstrap resamples for secondary category contrasts if that is computationally stable; report bootstrap failures and effective replicate count. Participants are the resampling unit. If the parent interval procedure differs, preserve it for the common primary comparison and document any secondary-specific interval method before results. No family-wise confirmatory claim is made for the secondary outcome; characterize estimates and intervals, identify imprecision, and do not reinterpret an interval crossing the null as proof of no relationship. Report category-specific counts and denominators so conclusions can be audited.

## Interpretation and falsification

A pattern with little/no association for one-fall reports but a more positive, adequately precise recurrent-versus-none contrast (and an ordered proportional-odds estimate compatible with both cumulative splits) would be consistent with MFI being more closely associated with recurrent reported burden than isolated reports. It would motivate an independently validated analysis with prospectively/clinically adjudicated counts. It would not prove isolated falls are noise, that recurrent events are verified, or that MFI is causal or a mechanism.

Similar associations for one and recurrent categories would support a general association with any reported fall, not a recurrence-specific signal. A precise near-null ordinal and multinomial pattern would weaken the claim that MFI tracks reported burden in this selected, measured-covariate-adjusted cohort, while leaving unmeasured confounding, selection, outcome misclassification and clinical utility unresolved. An adverse/nonmonotone pattern, such as a clear association only with single reports or opposing category estimates, would contradict a simple graded burden interpretation and prompt review of reporting/selection and measurement explanations, not post hoc regrouping. Sparse categories, wide intervals, model instability, failed diagnostics without supported fallback, or discordance between methods yield an inconclusive/deferred ordinal result, not refutation of the primary hypothesis.

A recurrent-category association cannot distinguish genuine repeated falls from recall/heaping, report behavior, fall exposure time, frailty, disuse, illness, post-index events, selection into visit 3, or differential opportunity to report. Available data do not clinically adjudicate events, provide prospective event dates/counts, validate reporting, identify an intervention effect, establish mechanism, or assess decision utility. Those stronger claims require appropriate prospective/adjudicated outcomes, validation and expert review or another study. This is conditional association only; no causal or individual prediction claim is added.
