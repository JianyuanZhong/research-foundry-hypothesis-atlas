# Executed partial child: persistent numeric respiratory-setting de-escalation

## Parentage, assigned goal, and substantive advance

This proposal is a substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It follows the assigned goal: execute as much as feasible of the confirmatory frozen-cohort experiment asking whether exact or broader post-freeze Ventilation care-plan labels add transportable information for a persistent numeric respiratory-therapy-setting decrease.

The child preserves the inherited target-independent adult first qualifying eICU stay, the five fixed landmarks (1,440, 2,880, 4,320, 5,760, and 7,200 minutes), the four airway definitions, post-freeze care-plan exposure construction, disjoint pre/classification/outcome windows, hospital-held-out micro and equal-hospital estimands, and the restriction that exported fields are documentation proxies rather than bedside ventilation, weaning, SBT, extubation, benefit, harm, quality, or causal evidence.

The substantive executed advance is replacing a one-off minimum-setting proxy with an outcome-blind persistent endpoint based on repeated, separated, monotone numeric respiratory-chart observations, then checking independent respiratoryCare and treatment corroborators. The execution also quantifies endpoint sparsity and evaluates exact versus broader exposure models. It is deliberately published as a partial confirmatory execution: several inherited prespecifications were not completed by the available run and are listed explicitly below rather than silently imputed.

## Evidence already supported and claim tested

The parent evidence supports that the care-plan field is sparse and hospital-concentrated, that exhaustive broader Ventilation vocabulary adds very few rows in this snapshot, and that exact/broader labels do not establish bedside meaning. It does not establish that a care-plan label denotes a real respiratory decision or that a numeric chart value records a delivered setting.

The narrower falsifiable claim tested here is: conditional on the frozen target-independent landmark rows and their pre-landmark respiratory-chart state, does an exact or broader Ventilation exposure improve hospital-held-out prediction of a repeated persistent numeric reduction in at least one respiratory setting? The result is about prediction of a structured chart proxy only.

## Endpoint definition

From read-only `respiratoryCharting.csv.gz`, the executable used only the outcome-blind canonical labels fixed in advance:

- FiO2: `FiO2`, `FIO2 (%)`, and `Set Fraction of Inspired Oxygen (FIO2)`, converting percentage values to fractions and retaining 0.21–1.00;
- PEEP: `PEEP`, retaining 0–40;
- pressure support: `Pressure Support`, retaining 0–60;
- set rate: `Vent Rate`, retaining 0–80.

For each component, the pre-landmark reference was the median of the last three valid pre-window observations when at least two pre observations were separated by 30 minutes. A component remained missing if this requirement was not met. In the outcome window, at least two valid observations were required, separated by at least 120 minutes. A component-specific persistent decrease required the first and last qualifying observations to be at least the prespecified threshold below the pre reference (0.05 FiO2, 2 cm H2O PEEP, 2 cm H2O pressure support, or 2 breaths/min set rate), with the later qualifying value no higher than the earlier one. The primary binary endpoint was any qualifying component; a two-component endpoint was retained as a sparse secondary outcome. Component references, counts, spans, deltas, and rebound flags were retained in the executable's in-memory audit logic, while the compact enriched row artifact contains row-level endpoint and corroboration fields.

This is not continuous ventilator telemetry and does not prove an actual delivered therapy change. Missing observations are not evidence of no bedside de-escalation.

## Held-out models and observed direction

For each definition and endpoint, the executable fit logistic regression with median imputation plus missingness indicators, standardization, one-hot landmark handling, and hospital-held-out fitting. Hospitals were eligible for evaluation only when they had at least 20 rows and both outcome classes. The models were labeled baseline, process, exact, and broader. The current result JSON contains micro and equal-hospital macro log loss, Brier score, AUROC where defined, hospital counts, row counts, and per-hospital results.

For the primary persistent endpoint, exact-unbounded results were:

- baseline/process: micro log loss 0.233003 / macro 0.238628;
- exact: 0.232826 / 0.238146;
- broader: 0.233851 / 0.238330;
- 22 eligible hospitals, 3,041 evaluated rows, 206 events.

Thus the exact exposure was marginally better than the current baseline/process specification in this run, while the broader exposure was slightly worse than process in micro loss and slightly better than process in macro loss. This mixed, very small direction is not evidence of a clinically meaningful association.

Across definitions, broader-minus-process persistent log-loss deltas (micro / macro; positive is worse) were:

- exact_unbounded: +0.000848 / -0.000298;
- exact_airway_24h: +0.001221 / -0.000588;
- exact_airway_12h: +0.003528 / +0.000911;
- exact_airway_6h: +0.003458 / +0.000? (the stored rounded output should be used for the exact value);
- broad_parent: +0.000560 / -0.000091.

The displayed exact-airway-6h macro values were broader 0.288021 versus process 0.287168, i.e. +0.000853 worse. The current execution therefore does not meet the inherited supportive gate and is directionally adverse for broader micro loss in every displayed definition, with mixed macro results. Exact and broader comparisons are similarly small and mixed.

For the two-component endpoint, exact-unbounded had only 9 evaluated events across 1,964 rows and 6 eligible hospitals; endpoint sparsity makes model comparisons unstable and descriptive. The exact model was not uniformly superior and the broader model did not provide a reliable rescue.

## Interpretation and falsification

The observed execution is **inconclusive-to-adverse for the stronger claim that exact/broader labels provide transportable incremental information**. It fails the inherited supportive gate because broader exposure does not consistently improve held-out loss and because the corroborated endpoint is sparse. The small adverse micro-loss directions are compatible with sparse, site-specific documentation, endpoint measurement error, or model/feature limitations. They do not show that clinicians did not evaluate, wean, or reduce support.

A supportive result in a complete rerun would require the inherited gate: reproducible broader improvement over both process and exact models in micro and macro loss for the unbounded and at least two bounded definitions, uncertainty intervals supporting the direction, no deterioration in the two-component endpoint, and no materially similar improvement for placebo or unrelated-therapy controls. Even that would support only a transportable association with a structured proxy and would require blinded clinical adjudication.

An adverse result would be failure of broader improvement across bounded definitions together with negative controls that do not show a general charting signal and a passed endpoint-support audit. The current run is not sufficient to apply that full adverse gate because those controls and audits were not run.

An inconclusive result is the appropriate interpretation when endpoint support, corroboration, uncertainty, or negative controls are incomplete. The current output belongs in that category, with a modest adverse point-estimate direction but no bedside conclusion.

Automatic verification can check source and artifact hashes, filtering, joins, landmark inequalities, label-blind endpoint construction, repeated-observation rules, model arithmetic, and held-out separation. It cannot determine whether a chart value is a bedside setting, whether `No Artificial Airway` marks successful liberation, whether a treatment string denotes an executed intervention, or whether a reduction was clinically appropriate or beneficial. Those claims require direct device/ventilator data, SBT and extubation records, workflow/interface metadata, blinded clinician adjudication, and external validation.
