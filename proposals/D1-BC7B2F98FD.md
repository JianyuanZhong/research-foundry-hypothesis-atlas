# Episode 17: does the first acidemic PaO2 category add prognostic information beyond SpO2?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and substantive advance

The prior valid lineage established a reproducible concurrent mapping signal: in acidemic, probable-ventilation observations with released SpO2 94–95% versus 96–97%, lower-band observations tended to have lower PaO2, but the pre-locked material magnitude remained inconclusive. It did not test whether this recorded discordance matters for a patient-centered outcome.

The unresolved question is therefore: **among adults who have an eligible concurrent panel during the first 24 ICU hours, does PaO2 <70 mm Hg, compared with PaO2 70–89 mm Hg at the same acidemic pH and SpO2 94–97% context, identify at least a 10-percentage-point higher probability of hospital death after adjustment for pre-/concurrent measured context?**

This is a prognostic measurement hypothesis, not a treatment hypothesis. A supportive result would justify prospective validation of an ABG/monitor-discordance risk marker and a study of whether it changes triage or oxygen-assessment decisions. An adverse result would argue that the recorded PaO2 category adds little outcome information beyond the monitor and measured severity. An inconclusive result would identify insufficient outcome support or unresolved measurement selection. The advance over the parent is a patient-centered endpoint and an explicitly time-anchored risk-set design, rather than another PaO2 boundary refinement.

The 10-point risk-difference threshold is a transparent decision anchor, not a validated minimal clinically important difference. No conclusion about oxygen targets, harm from oxygen, or benefit from obtaining/changing treatment follows from this experiment.

## What evidence already supports and what this tests

The strongest available evidence supports only that the exact EICU extraction is computable and that lower SpO2-band observations in the inherited acidemic overlap population have a downward concurrent PaO2 ordering. The episode-15 category relocation claim was adverse at the 70-mm Hg boundary, while the episode-16 rank-shift successor supported direction but not locked magnitude. Neither establishes mortality association, causal oxygen injury, or that an ABG result should change treatment.

This experiment tests the new claim that, in a first-panel risk set with outcome measured after the panel, the observed PaO2 category has a clinically material adjusted association with hospital death. The outcome is not used to select the population, hospitals, cells, or exposure; all eligibility and the first observation are fixed before outcome modeling.

## Estimands, baselines, and analysis

The primary estimand is the marginal standardized risk difference in hospital death for PaO2 <70 versus PaO2 70–89 at the first eligible panel. Fit a Firth bias-reduced logistic model with exposure category; linear age, pH, PaCO2/10, charted FiO2/10, SpO2, and draw offset/360; indicators for gender, PaCO2 missingness, and unit type; and categorical hospital intercepts. When PaCO2 is missing set its numeric term to the primary-set median and activate the missingness indicator. Collapse unit type before outcome inspection to the released levels with fewer than 20 primary-set rows pooled as `Other`; this is the only allowed pooling. Standardize predictions over the complete primary risk set under each primary exposure category while retaining observed covariates. Do not add, remove, transform, or interact terms after seeing outcomes. Report crude risk differences as descriptive baselines, adjusted risk difference with a two-sided 95% interval, category-specific event counts, calibration, and the secondary >=90-versus-70–89 contrast from a separately labeled all-category model.

Hospital intercepts address measured site baseline differences but do not make the association transportable. Uncertainty uses 5,000 deterministic hierarchical bootstrap replicates: sample hospitals with replacement, then `uniquepid` clusters within each copied hospital, preserve all first-panel rows per copied person, assign new copy identifiers, refit the full model, and standardize. Report defined/failed replicates and the interval. Also report a person-cluster sandwich interval and equal-hospital-weighted estimates as sensitivity views. If a copied sample loses a necessary category, that replicate is failed rather than repaired by changing the model.

Before inspecting outcomes, define consistency hospitals as those with at least 10 first panels in each primary PaO2 category; require at least eight. Report the crude within-hospital risk difference for every such hospital without excluding sites for event counts. The primary support rule is joint: the lower bound of the adjusted PaO2 <70-minus-70–89 risk difference must be >=+10 percentage points; at least 75% of the fixed consistency hospitals must have positive crude differences; and no mandatory timing or measurement sensitivity may reverse direction. The threshold is interpreted only as a magnitude anchor.

Mandatory outcome-blind or pre-specified sensitivities are: first six hours instead of first 24 hours; first 72-hour panel with first-panel selection retained; complete-PaCO2 subset; no PaCO2 adjustment; acidemia <7.20 versus 7.20–<7.35 interaction; SpO2 lower versus higher band interaction; earliest retained revision rather than latest; last rather than median SpO2; and the parent’s all-observation clustered analysis as a non-primary descriptive check. Do not search additional PaO2 boundaries after results. Report missing-outcome exclusions and a negative-control timing check using draw offset after adjustment; this is diagnostic, not a clinical endpoint.

## Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive:** the adjusted risk-difference interval clears +10 points, event/category/hospital gates pass, the direction persists in the locked timing and measurement sensitivities, and the hospital consistency criterion passes. The strongest allowed conclusion is that the recorded first-panel PaO2 category is a reproducible, clinically material prognostic marker in this selected EICU risk set, warranting prospective synchronized validation.
- **Adverse to the claimed magnitude:** the upper interval is <+10 points. If the interval is wholly below zero, the data support an opposite prognostic association; if it includes zero, they do not establish an association. A precise +4 points is adverse to the +10 hypothesis, not proof of no relationship or equivalence.
- **Inconclusive:** the interval crosses +10, event/category/hospital feasibility gates fail, bootstrap failures exceed 10%, a necessary model cannot be fit, the hospital prediction interval spans clinically opposite directions, or timing/measurement sensitivities reverse direction. Inconclusive is not support.
- A correct computation paired with “PaO2 <70 causes death,” “94–95% is unsafe,” “96–97% should be targeted,” “ABG-guided oxygen therapy improves survival,” or “the monitor is calibrated/invalid” must fail verification. Correct computation with overconfident causal or treatment language must also fail.

## Evidence limits and required next study

EICU cannot establish arterial specimen identity, waveform synchronization or artifact, device make/calibration, delivered FiO2, exact ventilation persistence, clinician oxygen target or treatment timing, or an ignorable mechanism for selecting who receives an ABG. Mortality association is vulnerable to confounding by illness severity, treatment selection, and indication for blood-gas measurement even after measured adjustment. Clinical experts must adjudicate specimen/airway validity and plausibility. Independent data with synchronized ABG, waveform SpO2, delivered FiO2, treatment timing, and external hospitals are required for transportability and a bedside risk rule. A randomized study is required to establish whether obtaining an ABG or changing oxygen improves outcomes or causes harm.
