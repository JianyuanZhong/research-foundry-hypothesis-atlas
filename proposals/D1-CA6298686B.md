# Working proposal: temporal discordance between liver tests and albumin after decompensation

## Question and clinical importance

For adults with a liver cirrhosis diagnosis who experience a first inpatient decompensation event, does a worsening discordance between the trajectory of liver-specific tests (AST, ALT, ALP, bilirubin) and the trajectory of albumin predict a subsequent clinically meaningful deterioration, beyond what the current severity at the index event already predicts?

The clinical problem is that cirrhosis is heterogeneous. A patient may have relatively preserved synthetic function (albumin) while showing hepatocellular or cholestatic injury, or the reverse. This discordance may indicate different pathophysiologic states, treatment needs, or risk of progression. If it carries prognostic information beyond the index event, it could inform follow-up intensity and the need for hepatology review.

## Evidence and closest work

Routine laboratory trajectories are known to predict mortality and hospitalization in cirrhosis, and dynamic biomarkers are used in prognostic models. However, most existing work either treats laboratory values as contemporaneous predictors or does not explicitly model the discordance between different laboratory domains.

The closest relevant work includes:
- [K1] Studies of dynamic biomarkers in cirrhosis prognostic models, which show that laboratory trajectories carry prognostic information but do not explicitly model discordance between liver-specific tests and albumin.
- [K2] Studies of the heterogeneity of cirrhosis, which show that different pathophysiologic states may have different prognoses but do not test whether discordance between laboratory domains is an independent prognostic factor.
- [K3] Studies of the prognostic value of albumin in cirrhosis, which show that albumin is a strong predictor of mortality but do not test whether the discordance between albumin and liver-specific tests carries additional information.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The population is adults with a liver cirrhosis diagnosis who experience a first inpatient decompensation event. The index event is the first inpatient decompensation event. The follow-up period is from the index event to the next clinically meaningful deterioration or death.

## Estimand

The estimand is the hazard ratio for a subsequent clinically meaningful deterioration associated with a worsening discordance between the trajectory of liver-specific tests and the trajectory of albumin, adjusted for the current severity at the index event.

## Competing explanations

The discordance may reflect different pathophysiologic states, treatment needs, or risk of progression. It may also be a marker of measurement error or selection bias. The analysis must account for these competing explanations.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if the discordance does not predict a subsequent clinically meaningful deterioration beyond the current severity at the index event.
