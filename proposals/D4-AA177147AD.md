# UKB 05: Fracture pathways when bone and muscle decline out of sync

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

Those with low bone mineral density accompanied by decreased muscle strength have a higher fracture risk than those with low bone mineral density alone, and this additional risk is partly reflected in a higher incidence of falls.

## Study population and key data

Individuals with DXA, grip strength, and fall follow-up; fractures requiring hospitalization after imaging.

## Basic Approach

Analyze fractures and subsequent falls separately, compare bone density, grip strength, and their interaction, and validate the consistency of lumbar spine and femoral measurements.

## Significance of the Research

Identify two distinct pathways: bone strength and susceptibility to falls.

## Main Challenges

Hospitalization does not capture all fractures; concurrent falls cannot be used directly as causal mediators.

Aging and Organ Coordination

## Competing Explanations

Frailty, medication use, and insufficient activity simultaneously affect bone density, muscle strength, and falls.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

First assess event support in the cross-classification of low bone density and low grip strength; if the excess risk is entirely explained by prior falls, the claim should be revised.
