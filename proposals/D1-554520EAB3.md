# Does leakage-safe intermediate laboratory history improve prediction of a fixed next-day albumin–bilirubin panel after repeat TACE?

## Clinical question, supported evidence, and unresolved claim

For an adult with HCC who has already been selected for a recorded first repeat TACE, does one additional albumin or total-bilirubin value from the interval after TACE2 improve prediction of the early post-repeat hepatic laboratory state after the contemporaneous pre-repeat panel and the assay's observation process are known?

The clinical motivation is that liver-function evolution matters when repeat TACE is considered, but the assigned parent predicts whichever same-assay result happens to be nearest nominal +24 hours in a broad 72-hour interval. That endpoint is an observed assay, not a coherent event-aligned panel, and the prior audit showed its selected times cluster near +32 to +33 hours rather than +24 hours. A current public Europe PMC abstract record for Chen et al. (2025), DOI `10.12809/hkmj2311208`, describes 300 HCC patients and defines persistent ALBI-grade deterioration after initial TACE as worsening without recovery within 90 days; its findings link longitudinal albumin–bilirubin state, baseline liver condition, and tumour burden to suboptimal survival after repeat TACE. This abstract supports the clinical importance of longitudinal joint albumin–bilirubin state. It does not establish the present short-window prediction claim, validate this dataset's unitless values, or justify a treatment decision. No unavailable full text or supplementary methods are claimed to have been inspected. Frozen public source: Europe PMC search record `[source checksum]`.

The strongest claim supported by the HCC data is feasibility. A complete-source, no-sampling audit reproduced the frozen 319 systemic-record and 1,491 comparator patients and 159/526 selected first repeat-TACE events. Requiring both remote assay baselines, a common-timestamp pre-repeat albumin–bilirubin panel in the selected event encounter, and a common-timestamp post-repeat panel in the fixed `(event_time+24h,event_time+72h]` window yielded 39 systemic and 114 comparator patients. Both latest leakage-safe intermediate values were available in 37/39 and 106/114. The fixed-window post-panel median time was 32.299 hours (IQR 31.734–33.209) in systemic-record patients and 32.692 hours (IQR 32.212–33.789) in comparator patients. The audit inspected no history–outcome association and fitted no prediction model.

The unresolved, falsifiable claims are co-primary and assay-specific on this one aligned panel cohort:

1. Adding the latest eligible intermediate albumin value `L_albumin` improves out-of-sample prediction of fixed-window post-repeat albumin by at least 0.05 outcome SD beyond the current common-panel albumin value, the remote albumin anchor, compact pre-event covariates, and albumin observation-pattern controls.
2. Adding the latest eligible intermediate total-bilirubin value `L_bilirubin` analogously improves prediction of fixed-window post-repeat total bilirubin by at least 0.05 outcome SD.

Albumin and bilirubin remain native-value outcomes with separate models, errors, uncertainty intervals, and multiplicity correction. No ALBI score, cross-assay arithmetic, rank score, threshold search, or synthetic joint numeric outcome is permitted because the source lacks a laboratory unit field. A joint-panel statement is allowed only if both co-primary assays support their claim after Holm correction.

This is a prognostic-information experiment in selected, observed repeat-TACE episodes. It does not estimate toxicity, hepatic failure, treatment effect, repeat-TACE benefit, the value of ordering a test, or a treatment policy.

## Event-aligned B, P, fixed-window Y, and leakage-safe history

For every selected repeat event:

- `B_albumin` and `B_bilirubin`: separately, the latest valid result on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1. Both must exist.
- `P_panel`: the latest common albumin–bilirubin panel in `event_encounter` during `[event_time−72h,event_time)`. Exact event time is excluded. Its native values are `P_albumin` and `P_bilirubin`, and `P_lead_hours=event_time−P_time`.
- `Y_panel`: all common panels in the same `event_encounter` during `(event_time+24h,event_time+72h]`. The fixed-window outcome for each assay is the within-patient median of all its values in those panels. This does not select a timestamp by value direction and does not target an arbitrary nominal hour. Record panel count and first, median, and last relative hours.

The one evaluation cohort requires both `B` values, `P_panel`, and `Y_panel`. Expected complete counts are 39 systemic and 114 comparator. Reproduce them before inference. In the audit, post-panel count had Q1/median/Q3 of 1/1/1 in both arms, so the median is usually the sole fixed-window panel but remains deterministic if repeated panels exist.

For assay `a`, define `H_rows,a` by all conditions:

1. exact assay label `a` and valid uncensored parser;
2. `Test time >= tace2_time`;
3. `Test time < event_time−72h`;
4. `test time < P_time`;
5. normalized `visit number != event_encounter`;
6. locked duplicate handling.

Preserve all qualifying timestamps for observation-process controls. `N_H,a` is the number of distinct eligible timestamps; `H_any,a=1[N_H,a>0]`; `logN_a=log(1+N_H,a)`; `R_hours,a=event_time−L_time,a` when observed. `L_a` is the latest eligible value, with final source order resolving any exact-time tie. Do not derive slopes, splines, ranges, thresholds, embeddings, or clusters. Assert that no history row is in the event encounter, within the 72-hour current-state buffer, at or after P, or post-event.

Among the fixed `(24h,72h]` complete cohort, both `L` values were available in 37/39 systemic and 106/114 comparator patients. This is an availability fact only.

## One paired prognostic experiment per assay on the common cohort

Use all 153 common-panel patients, including those without `L`. For each assay separately compare models that differ only by its numeric history value:

`M_obs,a: Y_a ~ P_a + B_a + arm + age + sex + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_lead_hours + H_any_a + logN_a + R_hours_a`

`M_value,a: Y_a ~ P_a + B_a + L_a + arm + age + sex + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_lead_hours + H_any_a + logN_a + R_hours_a`

Thus `M_obs` knows whether, how often, and how recently the assay was measured but not the intermediate numeric value. The paired contrast isolates the incremental value of `L_a` conditional on that recorded observation process. Do not add the other assay's numeric history, interactions, nonlinear features, arm-specific effects, outcome-driven selection, or alternate algorithms.

Use deterministic leave-one-patient-out cross-fitting with ordinary least squares and an unpenalized intercept. Within each training set only:

- median-impute age and include age-missing only if it varies;
- encode literal male as reference, with fixed female and unknown/other/missing indicators, dropping a zero-variance indicator identically from shared terms;
- median-impute `R_hours_a` and `L_a` when `H_any_a=0`, retaining `H_any_a` in both models;
- center and scale continuous predictors;
- require finite nonzero variance for included continuous predictors and full column rank after deterministic shared-term dropping.

The sole primary estimand per assay is

`Delta_value,a = [RMSE_a(M_obs) − RMSE_a(M_value)] / SD_a(Y_a)`.

Higher values favor the numeric history value. Compute paired pooled LOOCV predictions and sample `SD(Y)` on the full 153-patient cohort. Report native RMSE and MAE for both models, standardized `Delta_MAE`, cross-fitted R-squared, prediction variance, calibration intercept/slope, arm-stratified descriptive errors, and patient-level paired squared-error contributions. Coefficients are descriptive and cannot replace out-of-sample performance.

No model may be fitted until the population, parser, clocks, and availability gates reproduce. The feasibility audit deliberately fitted no outcome model and performed no history–outcome comparison.

## Uncertainty, observation process, and falsification

1. Paired uncertainty and multiplicity: use 2,000 fixed-seed (`20260911`) patient bootstraps of frozen LOOCV tuples `(arm,Y,pred_obs,pred_value)`, sampling within arm and recomputing RMSEs, `SD(Y)`, and `Delta_value`. Require at least 95% estimable replicates. Report percentile 95% intervals and one-sided tail probabilities `(1+#Delta<=0)/(1+#estimable)`. Holm-correct across the two assays. State that uncertainty is conditional on the fitted cross-validation pipeline.
2. Patient-linkage destruction: among `H_any=1`, permute `L_a` within arm 500 times while preserving P, B, Y, covariates, `H_any`, `N_H`, `R_hours`, and the exact LOOCV procedure. Report unchanged singleton strata. Support requires observed `Delta_value` above the 95th permutation percentile.
3. Missing-history sensitivity: repeat the identical comparison among `H_any=1` patients. A sign reversal makes a favorable finding missing-history-sensitive/inconclusive.
4. Joint-panel observation selection: among all 159/526 selected repeat-event patients, model completion of both B values, common P, and fixed-window Y panel with five-fold cross-fitted logistic regression using only arm, age, sex, TACE2-to-repeat days, and event year. In complete patients, recompute paired errors with stabilized inverse-observation weights truncated at fixed 1st/99th percentiles. Report probability range, weight concentration, arm-specific effective sample size, and maximum weight share. A favorable result is selection-sensitive/inconclusive if systemic ESS <30, comparator ESS <80, one patient has >10% of total weight, weighted `Delta_value` reverses sign, or `|weighted−unweighted Delta_value|>0.05`. This MAR diagnostic cannot recover missing panels or remove collider bias.
5. Fixed-window sensitivities: without choosing among results, repeat with common Y panels in `(12h,36h]` and `(0h,48h]`, retaining within-window medians and recomputing complete cohorts and history. Outcome-blind audit counts were 34/97 and 37/104; both-history counts were 32/93 and 34/98. A sign reversal in a variant retaining at least 75% of the primary cohort makes favorable evidence timing-sensitive/inconclusive. Do not revive nearest-nominal timestamp selection.
6. Panel-density sensitivity: repeat using the first eligible common panel in the primary `(24h,72h]` window. Identify patients for whom this differs from the median-of-panels outcome. A sign reversal in an empirically distinct variant makes a favorable result panel-density-sensitive/inconclusive.
7. Influence: report patient contributions to paired squared-error reduction and recompute aggregate `Delta_value` after deleting each frozen LOOCV prediction tuple. If any deletion reverses sign or changes `Delta_value` by >0.05, a favorable result is influence-sensitive/inconclusive.
8. Directional clinical-context descriptor: report, without fitting a classifier or using it to choose models, the count with `Y_albumin<P_albumin` and `Y_bilirubin>P_bilirubin`, and its complement. Feasibility audit counts were 26/13 systemic and 68/46 comparator for joint directional deterioration/not deterioration. This direction-only descriptor lacks unit cutoffs and adjudication and is not the primary endpoint, a toxicity definition, or a validated clinically important change.
9. Repeat-event and observation cascade: report 319/1,491 pathways, 159/526 selected repeat events, 55/186 with both B and common P, and 39/114 complete primary panels. Report event timing, P lead time, post-panel timing/count, and arm-specific attrition. Do not weight to patients without a repeat event because their event-centered endpoint is undefined.

## Deterministic interpretation branches

Apply branches separately by assay; allow a joint-panel statement only when both assays pass the supportive branch after Holm correction.

1. Reconstruction/negative-feasibility: any unexplained snapshot, source, hash, pathway, event, parser, duplicate, common-panel, history-support, or clock mismatch stops inference. Do not widen windows after seeing outcomes.
2. Computationally inconclusive: any required training matrix is rank deficient, a required feature has nonfinite/zero training variance, `SD(Y)=0`, OLS fails, or fewer than 95% of bootstrap replicates are estimable.
3. Sensitivity-inconclusive: a favorable estimate fails linkage, missing-history, observation-selection, timing, panel-density, or influence checks. Report the conditional estimate and the exact failure.
4. Adverse degradation: computational gates pass and the 95% interval lies wholly below zero. Conclude only that adding `L_a` worsens generalization under the locked model.
5. Supportive numeric-history evidence: `Delta_value,a>=0.05`; its unadjusted 95% interval excludes zero; Holm-adjusted one-sided tail probability <0.05; `Delta_MAE>=0`; the gain exceeds the 95th linkage-permutation percentile; and no locked sensitivity reverses or materially destabilizes it. Conclude only that the one prespecified leakage-safe same-assay value adds reproducible prediction of that assay's fixed next-day panel value beyond contemporaneous state, remote anchor, compact covariates, and recorded observation intensity/timing in selected observed episodes.
6. Precise null for the design-sized increment: computational gates pass and the interval upper bound is below +0.05. Conclude that this design rules out a 0.05-standardized-RMSE gain under the locked model and conditional uncertainty convention. This supports parsimony for this forecast, not biological equivalence or a recommendation to omit testing.
7. No demonstrated increment: point estimate <0.05 or linkage permutation is comparable, without a wholly adverse or precise-null interval.
8. Inconclusive: interval spans both meaningful degradation and +0.05, or any remaining prespecified ambiguity exists.

If both assays satisfy branch 5 after Holm correction, one may state that numeric intermediate history improves prediction of both components of the fixed next-day albumin–bilirubin panel. Discordant assays must be reported separately. The 0.05 margin is a design convention, not a validated clinical minimum.

## What results would and would not mean

Support would justify retaining one leakage-safe numeric history value per assay for external validation of event-aligned panel forecasting. A precise null would suggest the contemporaneous panel, remote anchor, and observation pattern already contain any design-sized signal available in this source. Adverse results would favor the simpler observation-aware forecast. Inconclusive results would identify insufficient precision, instability, informative observation, or clock sensitivity rather than establish benefit or harm.

None establishes hepatic injury, decompensation, toxicity, causal treatment effect, repeat-TACE appropriateness, or decision utility. Missing laboratory units/reference ranges and analyzer harmonization prevent validated ALBI, clinical cutoffs, and cross-assay pooling. The source also lacks adjudicated hepatic decompensation, Child–Pugh/MELD context, tumour burden/radiographic response, technical TACE success and intent, albumin infusion/transfusion/fluid balance, bleeding/infection, verified medication administration, outside care, and reliable complete mortality. Repeat-event and panel-completion conditioning may induce selection/collider bias.

A computational verifier can check source hashes and scans, exact joins, population/event construction, parser and duplicate rules, B/P/Y panels and history clocks, training-only preprocessing, rank, predictions, paired metrics, bootstrap/Holm arithmetic, permutation, weights, timing/panel-density variants, influence, and branch precedence. It cannot establish clinical importance, toxicity, hepatic failure, treatment effect, mechanism, administration, or policy. Stronger claims require unit-validated assays, clinical adjudication, tumour and liver-function context, complete follow-up, external/prospective validation, and expert decision-curve or interventional evaluation.
