# Provisional successor: persistent discordance beyond current severity and measurement noise

Status: provisional research hypothesis, not a completed study result. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with an ICU stay and a prespecified creatinine-defined AKI episode who reach a 48-hour landmark, a *persistent* pattern of creatinine improvement without urine-output improvement will identify higher subsequent renal deterioration or death than concordant recovery, beyond the same landmark's current severity, treatment context, and measurement intensity. The unresolved claim is incremental prognostic information from the pre-landmark trajectory, not a mechanism, causal treatment effect, or proof of true renal recovery.

## Evidence and rival explanation
The inherited inspected evidence supports the measurement concern: creatinine-based eGFR can overestimate kidney function after prolonged critical illness, while the KDIGO framework combines creatinine and urine output [K1,K2]. A MIMIC/eICU landmark study used these variables and severity to predict observed CRRT initiation, but treated CRRT as clinician-driven rather than objective necessity [K3]. None establishes that discordant recovery adds information.

The strongest rival is that apparent discordance is a proxy for current illness, diuretic/vasopressor exposure, differential urine measurement intensity, or noise in a single creatinine/urine summary. The successor therefore tests persistence and ordering, not merely a cross-sectional mismatch.

A simple current-state model is compared with a compact trajectory model using pre-landmark slopes, persistence, nadir, and co-movement. Patient-level temporal splits, calibration, discrimination, uncertainty, and measurement-intensity sensitivity analyses are required.

## Falsification and limits
Support requires directionally consistent incremental information in held-out data after current severity, treatment, and measurement-intensity adjustment. Adverse results are no incremental signal, reversal, or instability under measurement perturbation. Inconclusive results include sparse discordant cells, inadequate urine coverage, or wide uncertainty. MIMIC lacks measured GFR, standardized cystatin C, reliable fluid-status adjudication, and complete urine measurement; clinical adjudication is needed before mechanistic or treatment claims. No numerical result is claimed.
