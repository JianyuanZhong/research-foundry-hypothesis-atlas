# Lineage-aware CBC cancer-type contrast beyond ascertainment

Status: proposed independent episode-6 successor of [prior hypothesis]; planned experiment, not an executed result.

## Scientific opening and clinical advance

The parent question showed why a platelet–haemoglobin contrast cannot be interpreted from a near-diagnostic window alone: diagnostic activity, symptoms and clinician concern may produce both the blood test and the imminent cancer diagnosis. The unresolved question is whether the contrast contains cancer-type information that survives a two-year washout and whether the additional CBC dimensions—WBC and RDW—change that interpretation.

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. Primary-care blood-test abnormalities and test ordering can rise in the months before CRC and can differ between cancer sites [K1]. Serial CBC trajectories can precede CRC diagnosis by about 24 months, but the inspected study is CRC-only and focused on metastatic presentation [K2]. Prediction-model discrimination is not enough for clinical adoption without calibration, appropriate populations and validation [K3]. None establishes that a research-assessment CBC pattern distinguishes CRC from haematologic cancer after the near-diagnostic period, or that it reflects a mechanism.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among adults with a complete first research-assessment CBC who have no registry-coded cancer at that assessment and remain alive and cancer-free for two years, a prespecified high-platelet/high-RDW/low-haemoglobin pattern without a corresponding WBC signal is more associated with CRC than with haematologic cancer during years 2–5, and this type contrast is not removed by baseline CRP and albumin adjustment.

This is a descriptive cancer-type association, not a claim that the CBC measures tumour biology. The clinically consequential advance would be a boundary on whether a future CBC-based decision aid should attempt cancer-site routing at all: a persistent CRC-versus-hematologic contrast would justify external validation; a washout-sensitive or inflammation-sensitive contrast would argue for broad risk assessment rather than a site-specific referral rule. The experiment cannot itself set a referral threshold.

The leading explanation is a persistent CRC-like systemic pattern: occult blood loss/inflammation may produce higher platelets and RDW with lower haemoglobin, while a haematologic process may contribute a different WBC/lineage pattern. The strongest rival is residual ascertainment or measurement context: near-diagnostic testing, broad inflammation, chronic illness, or selection of who reaches a research assessment creates apparent type differences that attenuate after the washout or CRP/albumin adjustment. A separate rival is generic cancer association: the same pattern should appear against other primary cancers if it is not CRC-specific.

## Population, timing and outcomes

Include nonmissing 'eid', parseable T0, and nonmissing instance-0 values of all four CBC fields. Exclude any paired registry cancer date on or before T0. Do not condition on a future repeat CBC or future laboratory availability.

The primary delayed-entry estimand starts at T0+2 only among participants who are alive and free of any qualifying registry cancer at T0+2. Follow to the first event, T0+5, or 2022-12-31, whichever comes first. Define the secondary near-diagnostic window as [T0,T0+2), and the primary persistence window as [T0+2,T0+5]. Dates exactly at the delayed-entry boundary are not in the delayed risk set. Registry code/date pairs remain paired.

Classify the first qualifying event as CRC (ICD-10 C18–C20), haematologic cancer (C81–C96), other primary malignant cancer (C00–C97 excluding CRC, C81–C96 and secondary C77–C79), death, or censoring. A person with an earlier competing cancer or death does not later contribute a target event. Report denominators, complete-case patterns, event counts, exclusions, person-time and administrative truncation before fitting.

For every CBC variable, standardize using development-set means and scales. In window w and cancer class c, let beta_{j,c,w} be the coefficient for standardized CBC component j in a cause-specific model. The primary lineage-aware contrast is:

L_w = (beta_P,CRC,w - beta_P,Heme,w)
    + (beta_RDW,CRC,w - beta_RDW,Heme,w)
    - (beta_WBC,CRC,w - beta_WBC,Heme,w)
    - (beta_Hb,CRC,w - beta_Hb,Heme,w).

Positive L means the prespecified platelet/RDW-high, haemoglobin-low, WBC-neutral direction is more CRC-associated than haematologic-associated. Report each component contrast, not only L, because a sum can hide cancellation. The primary test is L_2-5; report L_0-2 and A=L_0-2-L_2-5 as the ascertainment/timing comparison. Use a design threshold of 0.20 log-hazard units for a meaningful contrast; an interval crossing both zero and +0.20 is inconclusive, not a null finding. A complementary profile contrast must compare the same two fixed profiles: CRC-like (+1 SD platelets, +1 SD RDW, -1 SD haemoglobin, 0 WBC) and WBC-emphasis (+1 SD WBC, -1 SD platelets, 0 RDW, 0 haemoglobin). Profiles are fixed before seeing held-out outcomes.

The CRP/albumin analysis is a sensitivity estimand among the same delayed-entry definition with nonmissing '30710-0.0' and '30600-0.0'; use log1p(CRP) and standardized albumin. It is a measured inflammation/systemic-state stress test, not causal adjustment and not a reason to discard the primary cohort. Report complete-case differences and inverse-probability-of-chemistry observation weights only as sensitivity, with truncation fixed before analysis.

## Methods compared on the same question

Both methods use the same population, T0 boundaries, four CBC inputs, baseline covariates, competing outcomes, development/holdout split and fixed profile contrasts. CRP/albumin are added identically in the sensitivity analysis.

A. Transparent primary: fit separate piecewise cause-specific Cox models for CRC, haematologic cancer, other primary cancer and death. Use the four standardized CBC values, age, sex, BMI, smoking and T0 calendar year, with CBC-by-window interactions. Estimate L_0-2, L_2-5, A, component contrasts and robust confidence intervals. Use Aalen–Johansen cumulative-incidence estimates for each outcome at two and five years. Penalize only after documented separation or convergence failure.

B. Substantive learned alternative: fit a CPU discrete-time gradient-boosted competing-risk model with separate hazards for the same four outcomes and an indicator for the two windows, using exactly the same inputs. A fixed small tuning grid is selected in rolling development folds. It can reveal nonlinear WBC/RDW interactions, U-shaped WBC effects, platelet/RDW thresholds and time-window-specific patterns that the linear contrast averages away. On the locked holdout, report the two fixed profile contrasts, finite-difference effects for each CBC component, calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, 2- and 5-year cumulative-incidence Brier/log scores and participant-bootstrap intervals. AUC/C-index is secondary. Better ranking without a stable fixed-profile cancer-type contrast is predictive utility at most, not evidence of a lineage explanation.

Sort by T0; use the earliest 70% for development and latest 30% for the final holdout, preserving participants intact. Standardization, missingness handling, chemistry weights, tuning and calibration are fitted inside development folds. Use 500 participant bootstrap replicates for holdout predictions and contrasts. If the holdout contains fewer than 50 haematologic events, all type-specific learned metrics are automatically labelled imprecise and cannot be used to choose a favorable split. A registry/inpatient-code replication requires an actually available compatible field; it must not be fabricated from '40001'.

A small diagnostic has already established I/O feasibility: selected-column Parquet loading and bounded cohort/event counting completed in 12.5 seconds on 4 CPUs and 16 GiB RAM. ## Falsification and interpretation

Support for a persistent CRC-specific lineage contrast requires: (1) L_2-5 has the prespecified positive direction and its uncertainty is informative relative to +0.20; (2) attenuation A does not show a prespecified loss greater than 0.20 log-hazard units; (3) component effects are not solely a near-diagnostic WBC or platelet artifact; (4) the CRC contrast is stronger than the analogous lineage contrast against other primary cancer; (5) the CRP/albumin sensitivity does not reverse the direction or explain the full contrast; and (6) the learned model reproduces the fixed profile ordering and is acceptably calibrated on the temporal holdout. These criteria support a reproducible descriptive signal worth external validation, not a mechanism or referral rule.

Evidence for ascertainment/inflammation is a positive L_0-2 with attenuation or reversal in L_2-5, a large change after CRP/albumin, or a pattern shared by other cancers. Evidence adverse to CRC specificity is a precise WBC-dominant haematologic contrast, a precise reversal, or a comparable contrast against other primary cancer. Evidence against the proposed direction should stop CRC-specific interpretation but may motivate a broad-cancer or hematologic question.

Inconclusive results include a wide L_2-5 interval spanning no meaningful contrast and +0.20, fewer than 50 holdout haematologic events, unstable chemistry-complete selection, missing dates/codes, or model nonconvergence. An imprecise null does not refute the hypothesis. Continue only with a larger or clinically richer dataset, not repeated threshold or subgroup searching.

The source cannot observe symptoms, ferritin/iron, transferrin saturation, reticulocytes, FIT, infection timing, medications, transfusion, smear, marrow/pathology, morphology, molecular subtype, stage, test ordering, laboratory indication, primary-care contact or complete healthcare use. CRP and albumin are imperfect proxies for inflammation and systemic illness. Thus computation can check timed association, competing-risk contrasts, measured chemistry sensitivity, calibration, temporal transport and the prespecified falsification controls. It cannot establish clinical indication, a cellular mechanism, symptomatic-patient diagnostic accuracy, treatment benefit, clinical utility or a referral threshold. Those claims require clinical adjudication, linked indication-rich primary-care data, external validation and a prospective decision-impact study.

Required negative controls/audits: preserve date/code pairing; verify duplicate eids, invalid dates and implausible gaps; report missingness and complete-CBC selection; permute CRC and haematologic labels among development event dates while preserving event timing and verify L collapses toward zero; compare against other primary cancers; do not impute missing CBC as normal; never use future repeat attendance or future chemistry in the first-CBC model.

## Alternatives and actual scientific deliverable

The newly fitted deliverable is not a readiness probe: it is the locked development/temporal-holdout transparent and learned comparison, component contrasts and L_0-2/L_2-5/A, fixed-profile predictions, cumulative-incidence/calibration/Brier/log outputs, 500-bootstrap uncertainty, chemistry-complete selection/weighting sensitivity and the label-permutation control. Conclusions must quote the computed outputs and uncertainty.

The transparent lineage-aware Cox model is primary because L is auditable under sparse haematologic events and makes the delayed estimand visible. The gradient-boosted model is retained because nonlinear WBC/RDW/platelet interactions are scientifically relevant and may reveal information the baseline loses; it is not retained to chase a small AUC gain. A neural sequence model or latent GP/state-space model is deferred: the source has only 19,409 complete instance-1 and 5,862 complete instance-2 CBCs, and repeat hematologic events are sparse, so added temporal capacity would not identify indication or lineage. Future repeat-CBC change analyses remain secondary selection stress tests, not a reason to weaken this primary estimand. Cross-namespace augmentation and models using future repeat attendance are rejected.

## Key claim discussion

[K1] Rafiq et al. establishes that acute-phase and red-cell abnormalities and diagnostic activity differ near CRC versus lung-cancer diagnosis in linked primary care. It motivates the ascertainment rival; this proposal adds a cancer-free UKB population, hematologic comparator and two-year delayed-entry test.

[K2] Sala et al. motivates a 24-month CBC trajectory boundary for CRC risk, but its CRC-only metastatic design cannot establish type specificity. This proposal asks whether a fixed lineage-aware contrast survives beyond that boundary and against C81–C96 outcomes.

[K3] Hampton et al. shows why discrimination without calibration and appropriate validation is insufficient. This proposal therefore locks a temporal holdout, calibration and uncertainty evaluation; it does not claim clinical utility.

## Limitations retained

The delayed estimand conditions on survival and being cancer-free at two years, so it is not an unconditional natural-history effect. Registry diagnosis is not a test-order date. Chemistry sensitivity is subject to missingness and selection. UKB research-assessment participation and registry ascertainment may not represent symptomatic clinical populations. “Lineage-aware” describes dimensions of a CBC pattern, not measured cell lineage or mechanism. This is a bounded adaptation, not a reproduction of any cited work.
