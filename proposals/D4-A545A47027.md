# Bone-muscle discordance and incident fracture risk: a two-pathway design without residualization

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank participants aged ≥50 with valid DXA BMD and grip strength, does low grip strength predict incident fracture risk independently of BMD, and does this excess risk differ by baseline fall history?

The clinically consequential unresolved question is whether low BMD and low grip strength represent a single latent frailty process (where muscle weakness is merely a marker of overall biological ageing) or two separable pathways: (1) bone fragility driving fracture independent of muscle, and (2) muscle weakness driving falls that then cause fracture. This distinction has direct implications for screening and prevention: if the two pathways are separable, then dual anti-resorptive + resistance-exercise interventions would be more effective than bone-targeted therapy alone for the discordant subgroup.

The leading explanation (single latent process) is that low BMD and low grip strength are both downstream of a shared ageing/frailty trajectory, so grip strength adds no independent fracture risk beyond what BMD alone predicts after adjustment for age, sex, BMI, and comorbidities. The strongest rival (two separable pathways) is that bone fragility and muscle weakness have distinct biological drivers and contribute to fracture through different mechanisms (bone failure vs. fall causation), so their joint pattern should show a BMD × grip-strength interaction that persists after adjustment.

## Evidence for the opening

- [K1] Kanis et al. (JBMR 2016) demonstrate that both BMD and grip strength are associated with fracture risk, but do not test whether their joint pattern supports separable pathways.
- [K2] Mikkola et al. (JBMR 2019) show that grip strength adds prognostic information beyond BMD in a Finnish cohort, but do not test interaction with fall history or the two-pathway vs. single-latent-process distinction.
- [K3] Kanis et al. (JBMR 2018) provide the methodological framework for combining BMD and clinical risk factors in fracture prediction (FRAX), but do not address the two-pathway question.

None of these works tests the specific BMD × grip-strength interaction estimand proposed here, which is the substantive advance.

## Population and temporal boundaries

- **Population**: UK Biobank participants aged ≥50 at baseline assessment with valid DXA BMD (T-score or Z-score) and grip strength measurements.
- **Baseline**: Date of attending assessment centre (field 53).
- **Follow-up**: From baseline to first incident fracture diagnosis (ICD10 codes for fracture, field 41270) or end of follow-up.
- **Exclusions**: Baseline fracture diagnosis, missing BMD or grip strength, age <50.

## Variables and estimand

- **Primary estimand**: BMD × grip-strength interaction term in Cox proportional hazards regression for incident fracture.
- **BMD**: DXA T-score or Z-score (field 707/708 or equivalent).
- **Grip strength**: Hand grip strength (fields 46/47), standardized by sex and age.
- **Fall history**: Falls in the last year (field 2296), used as a stratification variable.
- **Covariates**: Age, sex, BMI, smoking, alcohol, physical activity, comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension, etc.).

## Analysis plan

### Model hierarchy (Cox regression)

- **Model 0**: Unadjusted BMD + grip strength + interaction.
- **Model 1**: + age, sex, BMI.
- **Model 2**: + smoking, alcohol, physical activity.
- **Model 3**: + comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension, etc.).
- **Model 4**: + fall history (stratified).

### Learned alternative

- **Gradient-boosted survival model** (XGBoost or similar) with BMD, grip strength, and their interaction as features, evaluated on held-out data with C-index comparison to BMD-only model.
- **Selection criterion**: The learned model must show a statistically significant improvement in held-out C-index beyond BMD alone when grip strength is included, and the partial dependence plot for grip strength must show a non-linear or interaction pattern not captured by the Cox model.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if:
1. The BMD × grip-strength interaction term is non-significant (p > 0.10) in the fully adjusted Cox model (Model 3 or 4).
2. The interaction effect reverses direction after adjustment.
3. The gradient-boosted survival model shows no improvement in held-out C-index beyond BMD alone when grip strength is included.

## Interpretation of results

- **Supportive**: Significant BMD × grip-strength interaction in the fully adjusted model, with the learned model showing improved C-index and a non-linear grip-strength effect. This supports the two-pathway hypothesis but does not establish causation.
- **Adverse**: Non-significant interaction or no improvement in the learned model. This supports the single-latent-process explanation.
- **Inconclusive**: Directionally consistent but underpowered interaction, or significant interaction only in a prespecified subgroup. This would warrant a larger study or a different design.

## Limitations and what would require additional data

- Causal interpretation would require longitudinal muscle quality measures, mechanistic studies, or a randomized trial.
- UKB grip strength is a single measurement; repeated measures would strengthen the design.
- Fall history is self-reported and subject to recall bias.
- The interaction may be too small to detect in the UKB cohort given the available follow-up time and event count.
