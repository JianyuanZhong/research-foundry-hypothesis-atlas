# Selective-measurement robustness of lactate–circulatory discordance after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and fit an auditable MIMIC-IV 3.1 study asking whether the parent’s recorded discordance signal survives selective lactate measurement and competing death. Completion requires a frozen, leakage-safe feature/outcome construction and two fitted held-out estimators, not a claim that the phenotype is true hypoperfusion or that ICU discharge policy should change.

The required outputs are:

2. `cohort_audit.parquet`: every adult subject’s earliest valid ICU stay, hospital linkage, alive-at-index rule, exclusions, and observation strata; do not discard subjects because lactate was not measured.
3. `feature_ledger.parquet`: all target lactate observations, measurement masks/counts, valid vasoactive intervals, MAP/physiology/support features and hourly rows in `[outtime-12h,outtime)`, plus the pre-window observation history used in the observation model.

5. `topology_outcome_audit.parquet`: every later ICU stay and transfer interval, the lower documented-bridge endpoint (L), upper any-later-ICU endpoint (U), first in-hospital death, alive hospital discharge, and all unknown/contradictory topology.

No fitted result is reported in this proposal.

## Existing evidence, unresolved question, and advance

The assigned parent ([prior hypothesis]) supports a carefully bounded measured-data question: among first-ICU-discharge adults with two qualifying lactates, persistent high lactate while recorded vasoactive support is off may differ in early ICU-return-compatible risk from low lactate while support is off, with death treated as a competing event. The parent also establishes the important evidence boundary: MIMIC records measurements, orders, transfers and death, but not tissue perfusion, clinician intent, fluid responsiveness, preventability, or a validated discharge-readiness label.

That parent does not resolve whether the comparison is mainly a consequence of who receives serial lactate testing. Requiring two measurements can condition on clinician concern, monitoring intensity, survival and discharge workflow. Conversely, treating unmeasured lactate as normal would manufacture a false low-risk reference. The clinically important unresolved claim is therefore:

> In the full adult first-ICU-discharge risk set, does the recorded persistent-high/recorded-off versus low/recorded-off contrast remain directionally present after explicit adjustment for the lactate observation process, and is its excess early burden return-dominant rather than only a death signal?

The falsifiable hypothesis is that the persistent-high/recorded-off contrast remains positive for (L) and has a compatible direction for (U) after observation-process transport, while its competing-risk composition differs from the low/recorded-off reference; it is not merely a consequence of more intensive testing. The design also prespecifies the alternatives: attenuation to the null after weighting, a reversal, a death-dominant rather than return-dominant contrast, or bounds that include the null all falsify or limit the claim.

This is a substantive advance over the parent because it makes measurement selection the object of the experiment and separates two clinically different consequences of a bad post-ICU trajectory: return-compatible escalation versus death before return. It remains prognostic and observational. It cannot show that continuing vasopressors, delaying discharge or treating lactate improves outcomes.

## Exposure/observation definition

The primary pre-index window is `[outtime-12h,outtime)`, left-closed and right-open. A valid lactate is numeric, has a verified target item, and has a charttime in the window. The repeated-lactate stratum (S=2) requires at least two valid observations at least two hours apart and a final observation in `[outtime-6h,outtime)`. Preserve S=0 (none), S=1 (one qualifying observation), and all failed/ambiguous rows; missingness is not low lactate.

For (S=2), lock classes before test evaluation:

- low: both qualifying values <2.0 mmol/L;
- persistent-high: both values >=2.0 mmol/L;
- resolving-high: prior >=2.0 and final <2.0 mmol/L;
- indeterminate: all other/contradictory cases.

Run assay-specific analyses for 50813 and 52442 and all verified blood lactates, and threshold/window sensitivities at 1.5/2.0/2.5 mmol/L and 6/12/24 hours. These are robustness analyses, not opportunities to choose a favorable result.

A valid vasoactive interval overlaps `[outtime-6h,outtime)`, has valid start/end/status fields and an asserted `d_items` label/link. “Recorded-off” requires a prior valid vasoactive interval, its end before the final six-hour window, and no valid interval overlapping that window. No support rows means unknown, not off. Record `continueinnextdept`, status ambiguity and boundary tolerances 0/15/30 minutes. Primary comparison cells are low/recorded-off, persistent-high/recorded-off, low/active-support and persistent-high/active-support; retain resolving, indeterminate and unknown cells in the audit.

Observation covariates (X) are available before the final lactate class: age/sex/anchor-year group, admission type/location, first/last careunit, 12 one-hour counts, medians and masks for MAP/HR/RR/SpO2/O2/FiO2/PEEP, vasoactive duration/count and ambiguity flags, and lactate-assay/order counts before the terminal 12-hour window. Do not use the target class, post-index care, or future outcome when fitting the observation model.

## Outcomes and estimands

Follow from `outtime` for 6, 24 and 48 hours, ending follow-up at the first event, hospital discharge, or valid hospital end. The competing event categories are:

1. documented-bridge ICU-return-compatible escalation (L): a later ICU stay begins within the horizon and is preceded by a documented non-ICU transfer interval after index outtime and before later ICU intime;
2. any later ICU stay (U): the upper endpoint, including returns whose transfer bridge is missing or contradictory;
3. first in-hospital death before a return;
4. alive hospital discharge before a return;
5. unknown/administratively censored time when neither endpoint can be resolved.

Use deterministic ties for death, ICU intime, transfer times and discharge. For each phenotype and time point report lower-bound (L) CIFs and upper-bound (U) CIFs, each with death and discharge as competing events. Do not call a return signal confirmed escalation when it appears only in (U).
