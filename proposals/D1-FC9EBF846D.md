## Clinical question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

After vasopressor-treated shock has resolved, should a markedly fluid-positive adult who remains invasively ventilated, with adequate recorded blood pressure, urine output, and potassium, follow a policy of starting or restarting an ICU-input-system recorded furosemide or bumetanide delivery during the next 12 hours while invasive ventilation continues, versus avoiding the same recorded delivery through that interval? The possible benefit is an earlier favorable recorded airway path; counterweights are recorded creatinine rise, renal replacement therapy (RRT), death, or treatment-limitation pathways.

The strongest available evidence supports narrower claims. The parent's inspected full XML of Kuriyama et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13476641; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100120; frozen source [source checksum]) reports heterogeneous diuretic trials with mostly low- or very-low-certainty outcomes and does not establish benefit from post-shock loop initiation. The parent's inspected full XML of Bircher et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13154636; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100075; frozen source [source checksum]) supports uncertainty about fluid-removal timing, amount, and duration. The inspected full XML of Napoli et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13399242; DOI 10.62675/2965-2774.20260480; frozen source [source checksum]; [source checksum]) uses reintubation within 48 hours as a primary extubation-failure outcome for another intervention and finds low-certainty, underpowered evidence. It supports the clinical relevance of a 48-hour window, not loop benefit or MIMIC validity. A focused model-mediated fetch of the official MIMIC procedureevents documentation (frozen response [source checksum], not a byte-exact download) says start/end are documented event times, instantaneous procedures can use artificial short duration, presence is affirmative documentation, and absence is not proof of nonoccurrence because documentation is not mandatory. The ambition papers are methodological demonstrations, not clinical evidence here; no unavailable cancer main text or STAR Methods is claimed as read.

The strongest source-grounded claim is that MIMIC can identify a reproducible adjacency between a recorded invasive-ventilation interval and a source-labeled extubation, and can identify later recorded ICU airway-rescue events while an admission remains observed. It cannot establish actual loop receipt/route, clinician intent/readiness, true extubation success, or complete ward capture.

The unresolved primary hypothesis is: in the frozen measured-overlap population, assignment-compatible follow-up under the 12-hour recorded-loop policy increases the lower bound of the five-day risk of a ventilation-adjacent recorded extubation followed by 48 hours alive in the observed admission without a recorded ICU airway-rescue event by more than 5 percentage points versus recorded avoidance. Adverse hypotheses remain increases of at least 3 points in all-eligible record-defined 48-hour creatinine rise or at least 5 points in observed RRT7. These are record-defined policy associations, not pharmacologic effects or proof of durable liberation.

## Falsification and interpretation

Recorded-respiratory support requires the one-sided 95% lower limit for the HA-RAE48-D5 lower-bound contrast >+0.05; ESS>=75/arm; >=100 treatment-satisfied A12 paths and >=100 B12 completions; no A satisfaction at/after C; shared pre-initiation closures <=15%; complete source/state/overlap/balance/control/ambiguity/contamination gates; and no material reversal under active-through-X, continuous-ICU, transfer-complete, 6-hour, or corrected 24-hour analyses. Material means sign reversal, absolute risk-difference movement >5 points, or branch change.

The only positive respiratory statement is: "In the measured-overlap discovery cohort, assignment-compatible follow-up under the 12-hour ICU-input-system recorded-loop policy was associated with at least a five-point increase in a ventilation-adjacent recorded extubation followed by 48 hours alive in the observed admission without a recorded ICU airway-rescue event." It must say shared early closures were not receipt effects; procedure absence is not proof of nonoccurrence; some paths may reflect treatment limitation; causality is not established. It may not say successful extubation, durable liberation, receipt, safety, or recommend treatment.

A decision-favorable signal additionally requires renal upper confidence limits below +.03 RCR48 and +.05 RRT7, no inherited death/discharge harm, and no material endpoint/timing reversal. Even then conclude only that the recorded benefit-harm pattern warrants prospective adjudication/randomization. If renal lower bounds cross margins, report recorded harm and an adverse/unresolved decision. If lower bounds do not cross but upper bounds do, renal safety and the decision remain unresolved.

Adverse is airway upper limit <=0, RCR48 lower >=+.03, RRT7 lower >=+.05 under both brackets, or inherited death/discharge harm. Margin-falsified is airway upper <=+.05. Inconclusive/infeasible includes inherited sample/positivity/balance/control/weight failures; <100 A satisfactions; any post-C A satisfaction; wide bounds; renal unknown >15% or >5-point imbalance; unresolved transfer coverage >15% or >5-point imbalance; exact CMO/brain-death imbalance >3 points; or material reversal under linkage, timing, continuous-ICU, positive-chart, ICU-return, status, store-lag, item/version, creatinine-readout, or RRT-date sensitivities. Null/adverse/margin-falsified/inconclusive cannot be called benefit, equivalence, or safety.
