# Early Post-TACE Hepatic Deterioration as a Prognostic Marker in HCC

## Question
In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who undergo transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does a composite measure of early hepatic deterioration during the first 7 days after the procedure predict subsequent hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days, after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and post-procedure observation intensity?

## Clinical importance
TACE is a common locoregional treatment for HCC, but a subset of patients experience early hepatic deterioration that may signal a need for escalation to systemic therapy or closer surveillance. Identifying these patients could inform treatment-selection decisions and resource allocation. The unresolved question is whether the early laboratory trajectory itself carries prognostic information beyond baseline liver function, or whether it merely reflects more intensive post-procedure monitoring.

## Evidence and closest prior work
- [K1] Decraecker et al. (2026) review the limitations of Child-Pugh scoring and the potential value of dynamic liver function monitoring and ALBI grade in HCC patients.
- [K2] A longitudinal EHR trajectory study demonstrates that routine clinical data can capture disease trajectories and predict adverse outcomes.
- [K3] A TACE outcome prediction study shows that post-procedure laboratory changes are associated with treatment failure and mortality.

## Data bindings
- Source: HCC dataset (exact source paths in datasets/README.md)
- Population: 20,064 TACE patients, ~16,000 with complete early labs
- Early hepatic deterioration composite: bilirubin, albumin, INR during days 1–7 (ALT/AST excluded due to zero early rows)
- Outcome: hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days (817 events)
- Adjustment: baseline Child-Pugh/ALBI, tumor burden, post-procedure observation intensity

## Analysis plan
- Baseline: logistic regression using pre-TACE liver function (Child-Pugh, ALBI) and tumor burden
- Alternative: time-aware survival model using the early hepatic deterioration composite
- Comparison: hazard ratio >1.2 with 95% CI excluding 1.0 after adjustment
- Sensitivity: negative-control analyses for observation intensity

## Falsification
If the hazard ratio is not significant after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and observation intensity, the hypothesis is not supported.
