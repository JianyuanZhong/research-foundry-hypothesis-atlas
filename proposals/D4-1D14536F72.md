# Scientific Proposal: Post-Infection Proteomic Recovery Trajectories Predict Long-term Organ Outcomes

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question**: Do distinct temporal proteomic recovery trajectories following hospitalization for infection predict long-term cardiovascular and renal outcomes, and do these trajectories differ based on the organ system most severely affected during the acute infection?

**Secondary Question**: Can machine learning models identify proteomic recovery "phenotypes" that stratify patients by risk for long-term organ dysfunction, outperforming traditional clinical risk scores?

## Clinical Importance

### 1. Early Identification of High-Risk Patients
- Current post-infection care relies heavily on clinical scores (SOFA, APACHE) that may not capture individual recovery trajectories
- Identifying patients with aberrant proteomic recovery could enable:
  - Early intervention strategies to prevent long-term organ damage
  - Targeted monitoring and follow-up
  - Personalized rehabilitation programs

### 2. Understanding Recovery Mechanisms
- Distinct proteomic recovery trajectories may reflect different biological pathways:
  - **Pro-inflammatory phenotype**: Sustained elevation of inflammatory markers
  - **Pro-coagulant phenotype**: Persistent coagulation activation
  - **Immunomodulatory phenotype**: Dysregulated immune response
  - **Metabolic phenotype**: Metabolic dysfunction during recovery

### 3. Bridging Acute and Chronic Care
- Most clinical research focuses on either acute infection management OR chronic disease prevention
- This study bridges the gap by examining the transition from acute infection to long-term organ outcomes

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

### Gap Addressed
The existing literature and expert seeds address:
- Pre-illness risk factors for infection
- Pre-illness predictors of infection severity
- Post-infection long-term risk for specific outcomes

**What remains unexamined**:
- **Temporal recovery trajectories** of proteomic profiles after infection
- How recovery trajectories differ by **organ system involvement** during acute infection
- Whether proteomic recovery phenotypes predict **long-term organ-specific outcomes**
- Whether machine learning can identify recovery trajectories better than traditional clinical scores

### Substantive Advance
This proposal explicitly:
1. Examines **temporal dynamics** of proteomic recovery (not just baseline or final states)
2. Tests whether recovery trajectories differ by **organ system** most affected during infection
3. Predicts **long-term outcomes** (cardiovascular, renal, mortality)
4. Compares proteomic recovery phenotypes against traditional clinical risk scores
5. Uses machine learning to identify optimal recovery trajectory features

- **Trajectory features**:
  - Rate of change (slope) between time points
  - Acceleration (change in slope)
  - Variability (standard deviation of changes)
  - Area under curve (AUC)

## Falsification Criteria

### Hypothesis Supported
- **Distinct recovery phenotypes**: Machine learning identifies proteomic recovery trajectories that significantly predict long-term outcomes, outperforming traditional clinical scores
- **Organ-specific signatures**: Recovery trajectories differ by organ system involved during acute infection
- **Biological plausibility**: Identified recovery phenotypes map to known biological pathways (inflammation, coagulation, etc.)

### Hypothesis Refuted
- **No predictive value**: Proteomic recovery trajectories do not predict long-term outcomes beyond traditional clinical scores
- **No organ-specificity**: Recovery trajectories are similar regardless of organ system involved
- **No biological mechanism**: Identified proteins do not map to known biological pathways

### Adverse Results
- **Poor performance**: Machine learning models perform no better than random guessing
- **Confounding**: Results disappear after adjusting for key confounders
- **Temporal mismatch**: Recovery trajectories measured at wrong time points

### Inconclusive Results
- **Intermediate performance**: Proteomic recovery trajectories show some predictive value but not clearly superior
- **Mixed organ-specificity**: Some but not all organ systems show distinct recovery patterns
- **Partial biological plausibility**: Some identified proteins map to known pathways, others do not
