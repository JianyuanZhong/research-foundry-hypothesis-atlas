# Donor-aware protective-versus-pathogenic target selectivity

Seed: D2-T2. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Does an intervention-associated transcript pattern preferentially suppress a pathogenic fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state, such that a target ranked from an averaged signal is misleading? This matters because macrophage and fibroblast programs can have opposing roles in fibrotic or inflammatory disease: an intervention-associated change in one compartment may reflect a protective response, a pathogenic program, or simply differential cell composition and stimulation. Resolving the question would change whether a target is pursued as context-specific, requires a cell-type-resolved intervention, or is deprioritized.

## Design

The experiment uses a donor-aware linear mixed model with donor random effects and condition fixed effects. The outcome is the contrast between a protective macrophage-linked state and a pathogenic fibroblast program, defined from the available conditions and transcript signatures. A negative-control gene set and a stimulation control test whether the apparent selectivity is explained by donor composition or general stimulation. Supportive results require a significant protective-versus-pathogenic contrast that persists after these controls; adverse results show no separation or a pattern driven by controls; inconclusive results reflect insufficient independent units or unstable donor effects.

## Competing explanations

Alternative explanations include donor composition, differential cell composition, stimulation artifacts, and general transcriptional response. The design explicitly tests these with a negative-control gene set and a stimulation control. A simpler alternative is to prioritize targets from an averaged signal, which the hypothesis predicts would be misleading if selectivity is real.

## Method comparison

The baseline is a simple linear model with condition fixed effects and no donor random effects. The learned alternative is a linear mixed model with donor random effects, which can reveal whether the apparent selectivity is driven by donor-specific baseline differences or is a robust intervention-associated signal. The mixed model is preferred when donor effects are substantial and the number of donors is sufficient to estimate them.

## Limits

The hypothesis is at hypothesis stage: no wet-lab validation and no claim of direct target action. The available evidence is descriptive and does not distinguish expression association from direct target action. Targeted assays do not measure absent genes. The design preserves the distinction between expression association and direct target action, targeted-assay limits, nested donors, and unavailable wet-lab validation.
