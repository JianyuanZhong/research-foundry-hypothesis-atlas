## Hypothesis and clinical importance

The assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]` is controlling. Its O1/O1_hold population, development split, time zero, A1 and AG1 models and rosters, AF1 model and roster, H1/D1 equations, parent uncertainty family, margins, missing-label rules, and noncausal limits are unchanged and must run first. This child opens one new, prespecified outcome question only.

> Among the exact 2,334-person O1_hold roster, does AG1, compared with the frozen AF1, yield a clinically recorded post-index kidney endpoint—first inpatient ICD-10 N18 diagnosis or death within five years of t1—by at least 10 per 1,000 assay slots?

This is decision-relevant because the parent’s H1 and D1 are equation-defined visit-1 phenotypes. A policy that enriches those phenotypes but does not enrich subsequent clinically recorded kidney disease or death may have limited value for deciding whom to give a cystatin-C assay. Conversely, an outcome-yield advantage would justify a prospective comparison of testing strategies using clinical endpoints. It would not show that either policy improves outcomes or that cystatin C should be ordered routinely.

The strongest available evidence supports association and internal computability, not this policy claim. The inherited full-text evidence for creatinine–cystatin discordance and low-albuminuria prognosis (Chen, DOI `10.1016/j.xkme.2024.100796`; Lees, DOI `10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.38300`) does not establish AG1-versus-AF1 yield, measured-GFR accuracy, or benefit. The inherited body-composition evidence (Lee, DOI `10.1002/jcsm.12523`) supports treating 23101 as an imperfect bioimpedance-derived mass comparator, not muscle truth. The unresolved claim here is the incremental *clinical-event targeting* claim.

## Falsification and interpretation limits

The computation must fail if any qualifying date is on or before t1, if an N18 code is not paired to its own position’s date, if dates beyond t1+5 years are counted, if main and health_outcomes duplicates disagree, if pre-index N18 remains after the parent’s eligibility exclusion, or if any outcome is used in model fitting, roster construction, or capacity. A frozen negative-control audit must report zero eligible pre-index N18 codes in O1_hold; a nonzero result indicates time-boundary or population leakage and is a falsification failure, not a finding.

Supportive results mean that AG1’s fixed roster contains more five-year recorded N18/death events than AF1 by the prespecified margin. Adverse results mean no joint material unique AG1 advantage is supported. Inconclusive results leave the question open. None supports a causal treatment effect, routine testing, measured or persistent CKD, measured GFR, mechanism, cost superiority, equity, or patient benefit.

N18 hospitalization is not persistent CKD and may reflect coding, acute illness, or ascertainment patterns; death is all-cause and has no adjudicated renal attribution. The configured data lack validated outpatient CKD trajectories, repeated clinically timed biomarkers, measured GFR, specimen/order/result timestamps, complete censoring/administrative follow-up metadata, treatment assignment, harms, cost, and external validation. Clinical adjudication by nephrologists and linkage to complete outpatient, kidney-replacement, mortality, and censoring records would be required for stronger conclusions, followed by an external prospective or randomized testing-strategy study.
