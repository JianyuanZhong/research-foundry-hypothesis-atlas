# Hypotension duration versus minimum pressure: a laboratory-based prognostic association in eICU

## Scientific opening, strongest supported claim, and unresolved hypothesis

The clinically important opening is whether the time that a patient's blood pressure remains below a recorded threshold contains information about later kidney injury that a minimum-pressure threshold alone cannot reveal. At the same recorded minimum pressure, a longer hypotension episode could reflect sustained low perfusion, whereas a short episode could reflect a transient pressure dip or a change in monitoring intensity.

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is narrower. The configured eICU snapshot contains structured longitudinal vitalPeriodic and lab tables with minute-level offsets. It does not contain raw waveforms, narrative clinical adjudication, or a validated clinical AKI label. Therefore the available evidence supports only a temporally bounded, computable association between recorded hypotension duration and subsequent laboratory-based AKI risk. It does not establish that hypotension causes AKI, that a duration threshold is a treatment target, or that the result generalizes beyond this cohort.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adults with a recorded ICU blood-pressure episode and a laboratory measurement at least 24 hours after episode start, the adjusted 14-day laboratory-based AKI risk difference is greater for a long recorded hypotension episode than for a short episode, after accounting for minimum pressure, baseline severity, and the observation process.

The primary estimand is the adjusted difference in 14-day risk of a laboratory-based AKI event between the prespecified long-duration and short-duration strata, conditional on the baseline covariates and the observed recording process. This is a laboratory-based prognostic association, not a causal effect of hypotension.

The strongest rival explanation is that the apparent duration signal is a recording-process artifact: pressure is recorded more frequently during prolonged hypotension, or the episode is selected because of dense or artifact-prone measurement. A second rival is that minimum pressure, baseline severity, competing care, or hospital practice drives the association. These rivals are observationally distinct only through prespecified comparisons: minimum pressure and baseline severity must explain the association in a baseline model; duration must add information beyond them; observation density and artifact indicators must be tested; and the association must be directionally stable across hospital-level validation and competing-care analyses. If the duration signal disappears after these controls, the strongest defensible conclusion is that the data do not support a duration-specific prognostic association.

## Population and episode construction

The population is adults (`age >= 18`) in a first ICU stay with at least one valid `systemicmean` observation and at least one valid `labresult` in `lab`. A valid pressure value is nonmissing and within a prespecified physiologic range, with duplicate offsets resolved by the latest valid value. The episode is a contiguous sequence of valid pressure observations with `systemicmean <= 70 mmHg`; the episode start is the first observation in the sequence and the episode end is the last valid observation before a new observation exceeds 70 mmHg or before the 14-day cutoff. The primary duration strata are short episodes with duration 0–2 hours and long episodes with duration 2–24 hours. Episodes lasting more than 24 hours are excluded from the primary analysis, with a prespecified sensitivity analysis of the >24-hour stratum.

The primary cohort requires at least one laboratory result at least 24 hours after episode start. The outcome is laboratory-based AKI defined as a new `labresult` for creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, or blood glucose with a result at least 24 hours after episode start and within 14 days, with a prespecified rule that a result is new only if it is not already present before the episode start or during the episode. The solver must report the exact creatinine, BUN, and glucose thresholds and units used, the number of laboratory fields, the number of observations, and the number of AKI events. The laboratory outcome is not a substitute for clinical adjudication.

The primary analysis excludes patients with an APACHE score of 40 or higher, because their baseline severity is so high that the duration contrast may be clinically uninterpretable. It also excludes patients with a recorded ICU discharge before episode start. The exclusion and sensitivity analyses are prespecified and are not selected after seeing the primary result.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The baseline model is a prespecified logistic model for 14-day laboratory-based AKI with the following inputs:

- Episode minimum `systemicmean` and episode duration in hours.
- Age, sex, ethnicity, admission height, admission weight, ICU admission source, and unit discharge status.
- Baseline APACHE-derived severity variables, including `meanbp`, `creatinine`, `temperature`, `respiratoryrate`, `heartrate`, `urine`, `wbc`, `sodium`, `ph`, `hematocrit`, `albumin`, `pao2`, `pco2`, `bun`, `glucose`, `bilirubin`, `fio2`, and the APACHE score when available.
- Hospital region, bed-size category, and teaching status from `hospital`.
- A prespecified observation-density variable: the number of valid `systemicmean` observations per 100 minutes from the episode start through the 14-day cutoff, capped at the 95th percentile of the full cohort, with a sensitivity analysis without the cap.
- A prespecified measurement-density flag: whether the episode has at least 20 valid pressure observations, at least 10 observations per hour, or at least 10 observations per 24 hours, with mutually exclusive categories and a sensitivity analysis that uses the number of observations as a continuous variable.
- A prespecified artifact indicator: the number of nonmissing versus missing pressure observations, and whether the observation pattern shows a monotone increase or decrease in missingness over time. This is a diagnostic, not a clinical variable.

The primary estimand is the adjusted risk difference for long versus short duration, with the baseline model including minimum pressure and duration together. The solver must report the baseline risk difference, 95% confidence interval, standard error, and the magnitude of the minimum-pressure coefficient. The duration coefficient and its confidence interval must be reported with the full model, not only with duration removed.

The substantive alternative is a prespecified multivariate logistic model that adds the observation-density variable and artifact indicators to the baseline model. It is not a mechanistic model: the available data do not identify the mechanism by which hypotension affects the kidney. The alternative can reveal whether the duration signal is robust to the recording process; if it is not, the baseline association is not a duration-specific prognostic signal. The alternative should be compared with the baseline on the same 14-day laboratory-based AKI outcome, using the same patient-level splits and uncertainty procedure.

A learned temporal alternative is deferred because the scientific comparison is not about predictive gain. A sequence model could learn ordering, but the key uncertainty is whether duration itself carries information after minimum pressure and observation density are controlled; adding a flexible sequence model would not resolve that uncertainty and would consume compute without a justified scientific advance. The learned alternative is revisitable if a future study has more reliable continuous pressure measurements, a validated clinical AKI label, and a clinically meaningful temporal question beyond the primary duration contrast.

## Analysis, uncertainty, and falsification

The primary analysis uses patient-level hospital-held-out validation, with the full cohort split by `hospitalid`. The solver must fit the baseline and alternative models separately in each hospital, then pool the risk differences and confidence intervals using a fixed-effect or robust variance procedure that is declared before analysis. The primary result is the pooled adjusted risk difference; the solver must also report each hospital's estimate and the range of estimates. The primary model is CPU-only logistic regression; the future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, and 28,800 seconds. The discovery diagnostic is a bounded read of the first rows of the relevant gzip files and is not the full analysis. The full analysis runtime is unverified.

The solver must report:

- Cohort size, episode counts, duration strata, minimum pressure distributions, duration distributions, laboratory field counts, AKI event counts, and the number of patients excluded by discharge or missingness.
- The baseline and alternative model coefficients, standardized risk differences, 95% confidence intervals, calibration, and discrimination (AUROC) as secondary metrics.
- The number of valid pressure observations, observation density, missingness patterns, and artifact indicators for each duration stratum.
- Sensitivity analyses for >24-hour episodes, creatinine/BUN/glucose thresholds and units, exclusion of APACHE score >=40, and hospital-held-out validation.
- A falsification analysis using a placebo episode: an episode of duration 0–2 hours created from a random 5-minute interval within the 14-day window, with the same minimum-pressure and density variables recomputed. The placebo is not a clinical claim. If the duration signal is a recording artifact, the placebo should reproduce the observed pattern; if it is a physiologic duration signal, the placebo should not.
- A negative-control outcome analysis using a prespecified non-AKI laboratory event, such as a new lab result for a field unrelated to kidney injury, with the same duration and density variables. A strong association here would weaken the claim that the duration signal is specific to kidney injury.
- A hospital-practice validation: repeat the primary analysis within each hospital and report whether the direction and magnitude of the duration effect are consistent. A strong duration effect confined to one hospital is evidence against generalizability.
- A competing-care analysis: report the 14-day risk among patients discharged alive before the 14-day cutoff and among patients remaining in the ICU. This is a descriptive competing-care analysis, not a causal treatment effect.

Falsification criteria are:

- The primary adjusted long-versus-short risk difference has a 95% confidence interval that includes zero, or the alternative model reverses the direction of the duration effect. This is inconclusive or adverse, depending on the interval.
- The duration signal disappears or reverses after adding observation density and artifact indicators. This supports the recording-process rival explanation.
- The placebo episode reproduces the direction and magnitude of the primary association. This supports the recording-process rival explanation.
- The association is concentrated in one hospital, or the negative-control outcome has a similarly strong association. These findings weaken the proposed duration-specific prognostic interpretation.
- The APACHE severity or discharge-censoring sensitivity changes the direction or magnitude materially. This indicates that the primary result is not robust to baseline severity or competing care.

The experiment is inconclusive if event counts are too sparse to estimate a stable risk difference, if the model is unstable, if the density/artifact diagnostics are too sparse, or if the laboratory outcome cannot be reliably reconstructed. An inconclusive result does not refute the hypothesis; it means that the available data cannot resolve it.

## Interpretation and clinical limits

A supportive result would establish a reproducible, adjusted laboratory-based prognostic association between longer recorded hypotension duration and subsequent laboratory-based AKI risk in this eICU cohort. It would not establish that hypotension causes AKI, that duration is a treatment target, or that the result applies to other hospitals, populations, or clinical settings.

An adverse result would show that the apparent duration effect is absent, reversed, or explained by minimum pressure, observation density, or artifact. It would support the alternative explanation that the available data do not support a duration-specific prognostic signal. It would not establish that short hypotension episodes are clinically safe.

An inconclusive result would mean that the available data cannot resolve the question. It would justify collecting more frequent or more reliable blood-pressure measurements, adjudicating AKI clinically, or studying a different population. It would not justify changing a clinical threshold.

The eICU snapshot lacks raw waveforms and narrative clinical adjudication. The solver must not infer continuous blood pressure from five-minute summaries or from sparse readings, and must not treat a missing pressure reading as a normal pressure or as evidence of no hypotension. The laboratory outcome is computationally checkable but is not a substitute for clinical adjudication. Causality, treatment effects, mechanisms, and clinical applicability require clinical adjudication, treatment data, a prospective study, or another study with more reliable physiology.

## Alternatives considered and selection

The baseline is selected because it directly tests the scientific uncertainty with a transparent logistic model and exact inputs, target, split, and uncertainty evaluation. The substantive multivariate alternative is selected because it can distinguish duration information from recording density and artifact while remaining computationally feasible. A learned sequence model is deferred: it could reveal ordering information, but the key rival explanation is observation density, and the available structured data do not provide a scientifically justified way to use it to resolve that uncertainty. Raw waveform reconstruction and clinical AKI adjudication are unavailable in this snapshot and therefore are not substituted for the primary estimand.

The actual scientific deliverable is the newly estimated adjusted 14-day laboratory-based AKI risk difference between long and short recorded hypotension episodes, with uncertainty, the baseline and alternative model results, density/artifact diagnostics, hospital validation, falsification analyses, and a conclusion that is linked to the computed outputs. Readiness alone is not a scientific result; the future solver must not report a fitted model as a scientific result unless the primary estimand and uncertainty are reported.
