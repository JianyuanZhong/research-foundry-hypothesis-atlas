# Provisional HCC hypothesis: post-hepatectomy laboratory trajectory and 30-day deterioration

Status: provisional checkpoint, episode 6; hypothesis and experiment design only, not a completed analysis or clinical conclusion.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with a recorded liver resection in the HCC snapshot, the early post-operative trajectory of liver-related laboratory abnormalities will be associated with a clinically important deterioration signal during the subsequent 30 days, beyond baseline disease severity and operative context. The unresolved claim is whether a reproducible trajectory, rather than a single post-operative value, identifies a distinct recovery/deterioration pattern that could inform surveillance or escalation research.

This is not established. The available HCC catalog supports the presence of longitudinal procedure, laboratory, encounter, diagnosis, medication, examination, pathology, and clinical-document data. It does not establish the proposed association, a causal mechanism, or clinical utility.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

A positive result would distinguish an early warning pattern that merits prospective validation from the simpler explanation that late deterioration is only a marker of baseline severity or a single post-operative value. A null or adverse result would argue against treating this trajectory as a distinct signal and would redirect attention to measurement timing, case selection, or endpoint validity. The substantive advance is a falsifiable comparison of a low-dimensional longitudinal pattern against a clinically interpretable baseline/single-value model, not a claim that the pattern is causal or ready for treatment.

## Data-bound experiment

Population: adults in the HCC snapshot with a liver-resection procedure record and a usable procedure time; exclude records whose procedure timestamp or patient linkage cannot be verified. The exact procedure-label cohort must be frozen from the procedure table after inspecting labels and timestamps.

Time zero: verified procedure start/end time. Baseline features are pre-operative and immediate post-operative values available before the prediction window. The prediction window is the first 30 days after time zero. Outcomes are a prespecified deterioration signal from subsequent encounters/transfers and clinically adjudicated events when available; if no validated endpoint exists, the analysis is exploratory and cannot support a clinical claim.

Required variables: patient identifier; procedure identifier and verified procedure time; liver-related laboratory measurements (including bilirubin, INR, ALT, and albumin where available) with timestamps and assay/unit metadata; baseline severity and operative context; subsequent encounter, transfer, diagnosis, and outcome timestamps. Use only records from the HCC source and its documented joins; do not join across HCC, MIMIC, eICU, or UKB identifiers.

Source bindings: the HCC source files listed in datasets/hcc/README.md and the corresponding table metadata under datasets/hcc/table-*.json, with the exact source paths and hashes in binding-template.json. The procedure, laboratory, encounter, transfer, diagnosis, and outcome tables must be joined on the documented patient/procedure/encounter keys and time fields. The solver must report the actual table names, columns, row counts, missingness, and any label/unit exclusions used.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Baseline: a prespecified logistic or survival model using baseline severity, operative context, and the first post-operative value of each laboratory, with temporal splitting by procedure time and uncertainty estimates.

Alternative: a low-dimensional trajectory model fitted to the same patients and time window (for example, restricted cubic splines or a small latent trajectory model), with the same covariates, split, outcome, and uncertainty evaluation. The alternative can reveal whether direction, slope, or persistence of change carries information lost by a single value; it is not selected for predictive novelty alone.

Evaluation: compare held-out discrimination/calibration and calibration of uncertainty, plus a prespecified trajectory-versus-baseline contrast. Report supportive, adverse, and inconclusive results. A supportive result requires reproducible incremental information and a clinically interpretable pattern; an adverse result is no incremental information or instability; an inconclusive result is insufficient endpoint quality, missing units/times, or imprecision.

## Falsification and interpretation

The hypothesis is falsified if the trajectory model adds no reproducible information beyond the baseline model, if the apparent signal disappears after temporal holdout or assay/unit checks, or if it is explained by observation intensity, missingness, or selection. A positive association does not identify a mechanism or justify treatment. Clinical adjudication, assay units, validated deterioration endpoint, and prospective/external validation are required for stronger conclusions.

The three inspected key-reference receipts and source snapshots are not yet attached. These limitations prevent selection, but do not invalidate the provisional question.
