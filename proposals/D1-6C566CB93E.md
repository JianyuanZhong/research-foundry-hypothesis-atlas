# Laboratory-observation history after recorded physiology and documented care limitation

Status: Episode-22 substantive successor to [prior hypothesis] and [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic, transportability, and measurement-process experiment, not a causal test-ordering study, hospital ranking, treatment recommendation, or evidence that an alert improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

At ICU-relative minute 360, does patient-linked laboratory observation history identify later recorded ICU death beyond recorded physiologic state, general vital-monitoring intensity, and documented care-limitation history? At the same fixed number of monitoring reviews, does adding laboratory process produce a more calibrated, transportable review set, or is it mainly a marker of residual illness, generic workflow, or an already documented decision not to escalate?

This is consequential because an apparently useful laboratory-process alert could instead signal severe illness or goals-of-care documentation, neither of which implies that more testing would help. The parent outcome-blind audit established feasibility only: 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals and 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes. These counts establish support for a proposed held-out-hospital experiment, not a fitted result, physiologic truth, clinician intent, or clinical utility. A bounded raw probe sampled 500,000 rows from each vital file and found early-window vital observations, but this is not a cohort-wide support estimate. The unresolved claim is that the lab-process increment remains patient-linked after measured state, vital-process intensity, and pre-landmark care-limitation documentation are challenged.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly freeze the cohort, analyte/value/interface/revision codebook, vital-field codebook, carePlanEOL codebook and five whole-hospital folds; fit paired competing-risk models Bv and BvP; fit the vital-process placebo BvV and EOL-aware diagnostic BvP-EOL; generate held-out predictions and calibration/Brier/uncertainty; evaluate fixed-burden review reclassification; and produce all prespecified falsifications plus an interpretation trace linked to output files. The legacy B0/B1 analysis remains an audit.

The primary deliverable is the held-out macro-hospital BvP-minus-Bv contrast for recorded death by 1,800 minutes, with alive discharge competing. The key repair is the care-limitation challenge: compare that contrast after adding only pre-landmark carePlanEOL presence/timing and in a separately reported cohort with no recorded EOL row by minute 360. This restriction means “no recorded row,” never “no treatment limitation.” If support fails, the EOL result is inconclusive and does not replace the unrestricted result.

A matched six-bin GRU or temporal convolution is a substantive alternative, not a complexity requirement. It uses the same supported channels, target, folds and preprocessing. It is interpretable only if it improves held-out calibration/Brier or review outputs beyond BvP, with order shuffle and parameter-matched non-temporal controls. It is deferred if support, convergence, or calibration gates fail.

## Models, split, uncertainty, and support

Bv is an elastic-net pooled discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly rows 360–1,800, two heads for recorded death and alive discharge, demographic/admission context, lab state/availability, and vital state/masks. Vital state uses first/last/min/max, slope only with at least two observations, prespecified instability flags, and field masks; it excludes vital observation counts/gaps. BvP adds laboratory process blocks (counts, distinct times, first/last, recency, spans/gaps, revision-known and stable interface/system indicators). BvP-EOL adds only pre-landmark carePlanEOL presence/timing. BvV adds vital observation-process summaries as a workflow placebo. BvP-rel uses outcome-free within-target-hospital percentiles from a 10% calibration subset, requiring 50 calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes per target hospital; it is site-adapted, not zero-shot.

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals, keeping all `uniquepid` episodes together; development hospitals split 75/25. No held-out outcomes, target outcome summaries, post-landmark records, or held-out preprocessing can influence fitting, coding, imputation, tuning, calibration or thresholds. Report macro-hospital paired differences first and patient-weighted results second, with hospital-clustered bootstrap/hierarchical intervals (>=1,000 resamples if feasible), per-hospital calibration slope/intercept, competing-risk integrated Brier, cumulative incidence, alert burden, capture, PPV, and uncertainty.

Decision cells require >=200 stays, >=30 recorded deaths overall, and >=10 stays in >=80% of contributing hospitals. The no-EOL restriction additionally requires >=2,000 stays and the same event/hospital requirements. Every retained vital field requires per-hospital nonmissing support and state/process overlap; otherwise the affected analysis is inconclusive.

## Decision diagnostic and falsification

At q={0.01,0.02,0.05,0.10}, define a retrospective review candidate at predicted recorded-death risk >=q. Report alerts/100, capture, PPV, and `100*(TP/N - FP/N*q/(1-q))`. Also match BvP to the Bv alert count per held-out hospital using outcome-free top-k selection, and report changed-up/down cases by Bv-risk strata. This is label-relative prioritization, not net clinical benefit.

Freeze: (1) compare B1-B0 with BvP-Bv; collapse after vital adjustment is adverse to patient-level lab interpretation; (2) compare BvP-Bv with BvP-EOL-Bv-EOL and no-EOL restriction; attenuation supports documented care-limitation workflow, persistence narrows the claim, and neither establishes unrecorded intent; (3) permute complete lab-process vectors within held-out hospital and Bv-risk stratum, retaining state/EOL/outcomes; attenuation toward Bv is required; (4) compare lab-process with vital-process increments; comparable gains support generic workflow; (5) compare raw and outcome-free relative process, hospital descriptors, common analyte panel, strict revision-only rows, and block ablations; gains confined to interface/revision/site coding support workflow; (6) prohibit post-360 rows, actual Apache mortality, outcome calibration in target hospitals, and leakage sentinels; (7) if support, positivity, finite metrics, convergence, or calibration gates fail, report inconclusive rather than substitute the legacy result.

Supportive evidence requires favorable uncertainty-supported BvP-Bv calibration/Brier or supported fixed-burden result, stable held-out review reclassification, permutation attenuation, no comparable vital/site increment, no coding reversal, and adequate EOL support for the EOL-specific claim. Adverse evidence is a reproducible null/worsening after state/EOL adjustment, workflow-matched gain, permutation failure, coding reversal, or fixed-burden disappearance. Inconclusive evidence includes failed vital/EOL coverage, sparse events/hospitals, unresolved units, no overlap, sparse no-EOL stratum, unstable temporal fit, or intervals spanning clinically meaningful ranges. A supportive computation cannot establish clinical benefit, safe thresholds, clinician intent, or whether testing should increase.

## Evidence limits, alternatives, and compute

The label is discharge status, not adjudicated cause or complete mortality. VitalPeriodic is summarized monitoring, not waveform. Lab rows do not establish orders, indication, collection quality, administration, or response. carePlanEOL is an incomplete documentation marker; absence is unknown. External hospitals, post-discharge mortality, complete interventions, alert response, patient preferences, validated narrative text, and causal assignment are unavailable. Expert adjudication, external validation, and prospective silent-mode/impact or randomized study are required for stronger clinical claims.

Measured discovery evidence includes exact catalog/schema/header availability, the bounded vital probe (4.6 seconds; 500,000 rows/file; early-window counts above), and parent feasibility counts. Unverified are full support, complete extraction time, compatibility, model convergence and all outcomes. Primary tabular work is estimated at 4–8 CPUs, 16–32 GiB RAM, 1–4 hours; this is unverified. T-v may use CPU, or one allocated A100-SXM4-80GB with 4 CPUs/32 GiB if repeated fits are too slow, explicitly using `cuda:0`. Discovery and the separate solver envelope remain distinct.

The method record in `support/episode-22-method-and-support.md` records the baseline/learned comparison and deferrals.
