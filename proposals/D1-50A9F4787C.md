# Provisional hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery in MIMIC-IV

Status: provisional repair of [prior hypothesis]. No cohort counts, model fits, or clinical conclusions are claimed.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in their first MIMIC-IV ICU stay with a documented creatinine-based AKI episode and observation through a prespecified 48-hour landmark, a creatinine-improved but urine-output-impaired phenotype will have worse subsequent in-hospital death and/or persistent or worsening kidney dysfunction than concordant creatinine-and-urine-output recovery. The comparison is prognostic, not causal.

The strongest rival is a monitoring/measurement artifact: creatinine is sampled at clinician-selected times, urine output is recorded only when a catheter or bedside collection is present, and fluid balance is a treatment/monitoring summary rather than a direct organ state. A second rival is residual illness severity and treatment selection: sicker patients may receive more invasive monitoring, diuresis, fluid administration, or renal replacement therapy. The design must therefore test incremental prognostic information beyond pre-landmark severity and monitoring intensity, and must not infer tubular injury, fluid overload, or treatment benefit from association.

## Evidence and claim boundary

The inherited branch contains a schema-bound proposal and an inspected source note for Tang et al. (2026), “An interpretable machine learning model for early prediction of subsequent observed CRRT initiation in patients with sepsis-associated acute kidney injury,” DOI 10.1007/s10238-026-02305-1, PMCID PMC13627203. That work supports the plausibility of using evolving creatinine, urine-output, fluid, severity, and treatment measures in a strict landmark design, and supports treating CRRT initiation as a treatment decision rather than an objective necessity. It does not establish the proposed discordant-recovery association or mechanism.

This provisional draft does not yet have the required three inspected key works with frozen receipts. The literature limitation is explicit; no additional citation or result is invented here.

## Population, time, exposure, and outcomes

Population: adults in the first ICU stay per subject_id, with a documented creatinine-based AKI episode and sufficient pre-landmark urine-output monitoring. The AKI rule, baseline/reference window, and minimum monitoring requirement must be fixed before outcome analysis. A single post-landmark creatinine cannot define AKI.

Time origin: icustays.intime. Primary landmark: 48 hours after intime. Exposure uses only clinical timestamps at or before the landmark: creatinine trajectory, urine-output category/trajectory, and secondary fluid-balance category. Events after the landmark are excluded from exposure construction.

Primary exposure: creatinine-improved/urine-output-impaired versus concordant recovery. Secondary comparisons: creatinine-improved/urine-output-impaired versus creatinine-improved/urine-output-recovered, and monitoring-stratified analyses.

Outcomes: in-hospital death through admissions.dischtime/deathtime and a prespecified post-landmark kidney outcome (persistent/worsening creatinine and/or renal replacement therapy), with follow-up through hospital discharge or a fixed 7-day window and explicit competing-risk handling. Exact outcome thresholds and the RRT item definition remain unresolved and must be fixed before fitting.

## Analysis and falsification

Simple baseline: prespecified logistic or cause-specific Cox model with age, sex, admission type, pre-landmark severity, creatinine value/trajectory, urine-output category, fluid-balance category, and monitoring intensity. This tests whether discordance adds interpretable prognostic information beyond creatinine recovery.

Substantive alternative: a time-aware landmark model using ordered pre-landmark creatinine, urine-output, and fluid-balance trajectories (discrete-time survival or regularized recurrent/functional model), with the same patient split, outcome, calibration, and uncertainty evaluation. It can reveal whether sequence and slope matter; it cannot establish mechanism. Compare incremental discrimination, calibration, and bootstrap uncertainty against the baseline. CPU is appropriate for the baseline; the time-aware model may use CPU or one allocated GPU only if trajectory fitting is materially slower.

Supportive result: a prespecified incremental association for discordance with uncertainty, persistence after monitoring-intensity adjustment, and no material reversal in fixed sensitivity analyses. Adverse result: no incremental association, disappearance after monitoring adjustment, or reversal. An imprecise null is inconclusive. Clinical adjudication of true AKI, recovery, fluid overload, and treatment indication is unavailable and would be required for stronger mechanistic or management claims.
