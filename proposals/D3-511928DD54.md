## 1. Unresolved question and what changed this episode

The parent proposed three competing explanations for FLS inflammatory output in RA synovial coculture: (A) macrophage-subset **composition** (MerTK/CD206neg fraction), (B) macrophage **MerTK pathway state** (per-cell signaling), (C) **FLS-intrinsic** donor dominance. The failed-resolution framework motivating resolution-restoring therapy in difficult-to-treat RA rests on arm A/B assumptions: a macrophage compartment stripped of pro-resolving efferocytic subsets instructing imprinted fibroblasts [K1], with efferocytosis-dependent macrophage state as the proposed resolution engine [K2] and subset balance as the driver of synovial inflammation [K3]. None of these reviews separates abundance from signaling state, and none tests whether stromal donor identity dominates.

This episode **executed the parent's planned primary screen** (managed compute job `[research job]`, state=succeeded; result `work/lead/screen_result.json`, [source checksum]). The pilot observes, on E-MTAB-8873 (n=33 samples; parent recorded 32 — corrected this episode; 440 genes after symbol collapse):

1. **(C) FLS-intrinsic dominance is refuted in-sample.** Sequential R² decomposition of M_flame: macrophage-condition factors 76.8% (batch-first order) to 81.5% (condition-first order); FLS donor 1.9%–11.9%; batch 0–14.7%. Whatever drives the FLS inflammatory module here, it is macrophage-condition-associated, not stromal-donor-associated.
2. **(A) The pre-specified monotone composition gradient is violated.** Group means across the MerTKneg-fraction ladder: Mpos −0.30, 3Mpos:1Mneg −1.13 (n=1), 1Mpos:3Mneg −0.37 (n=2), Mneg +0.19 — steps −0.83, +0.76, +0.56. The single 3:1 well (also lowest macrophage-marker content, M_mac −0.91) drives the violation. Paired Mneg-vs-Mpos contrasts (n=4 donor-matched pairs) are heterogeneous: −0.11, +1.20, +0.33, −0.01 — one R2 pair carries the entire effect.
3. **(B) MerTK inhibition does not raise FLS inflammatory output.** Paired UNC106238-vs-untreated Mpos contrasts (n=2): −0.42, −0.17 M_flame z-units — opposite to the pro-inflammatory direction pre-specified from Mneg>Mpos. design.json shows the "preInhibitor" samples carry the same treatment label (MerTK-inhibitor pretreated macrophages) in other batches, giving n=4 inhibitor-treated Mpos wells total (means −0.51, −0.11 by batch-set). No large direct FLS-side inhibitor effect (FLS_Inhibitor n=1: M_flame −0.89 vs FLS_only −0.63, unpaired descriptive).
4. **The dominant condition axis is macrophage inflammatory-state pretreatment, not subset identity.** LPS-pretreated macrophages strongly elevate M_flame (Mneg_preLPS 1.64 vs Mneg 0.19; Mpos_preLPS 1.81, n=1) and LOWER M_mac (MERTK/MRC1/CD14). The parent's positive control (LPS elevates M_mac) was mis-specified in direction; module responsiveness is confirmed, in the opposite direction.
5. Mneg cocultures consistently carry higher macrophage-state marker signal than Mpos (paired M_mac diffs +0.24, +0.79, +0.67, +0.17, n=4) — the mixed-well composition signal is detectable but does not translate into a consistent FLS-output effect.

**Refined unresolved claim (what this child tests):** macrophage-condition factors dominate FLS inflammatory output variance, but neither subset composition nor acute MerTK-pathway inhibition explains the dominant axis in the targeted panel. The remaining discriminating question: (i) does MerTK pathway state carry ANY FLS-output effect detectable transcriptome-wide (E-MTAB-8316 paired none-vs-UNC106238 contrast), and (ii) is the composition non-monotonicity a single-well artifact or a real property requiring crossed-donor mixed-ratio replication.

**Clinical consequence.** If arm B is null transcriptome-wide while macrophage inflammatory-state control dominates, resolution-restoring strategies aimed at MerTK-pathway activity alone [K1][K2] would be expected to underperform at the stromal-output level, and macrophage-state reprogramming rather than subset-number restoration or single-pathway blockade becomes the prioritized axis [K3]. If arm B is supported transcriptome-wide, single-ligand interventions retain stromal impact without needing subset restoration. Either outcome informs which resolution-restoring strategy to prioritize in persistent-inflammatory difficult-to-treat RA [K1].

- **Baseline**: donor-paired descriptive contrasts with bootstrap CIs (executed in pilot form; cheap, transparent, honest about n).
- **Substantive alternative**: composition-dose mixed model with FLS-donor random effect + variance-component decomposition — reveals how variance partitions across macrophage condition, stromal donor and batch, which the paired contrasts cannot. Pilot executed the fixed-effect sequential version; the mixed-model refinement is the solver's task.

## 5. Falsification criteria (pre-specified for the solver)

## 6. Competing explanations and limitations

- Mixed-well pseudobulk: cell-of-origin attribution is marker-based (MERTK/MRC1/CD14); unmeasured genes unavailable (TNF, HBEGF absent from panel).
- The 3:1 composition point is a single well; the composition gradient cannot be independently replicated in configured data (crossed-donor mixed-ratio replication is the stated missing design).
- Inhibitor off-target effects (UNC106238 selectivity outside measured scope); FLS_Inhibitor n=1.
- LPS effect is a pretreatment-state effect on macrophages, not evidence of a ligand-specific mechanism; directionality rests on pretreatment logic, stated as inference.
- Pilot module scores are z-score summaries within one panel; no cross-cohort validation in scope. GSE95588 r1/r2 cannot serve as independent validation of GSE57723-derived findings.
- Known NOTCH3/HBEGF/PGDH RA findings are not the subject and are not rebranded.
