# Provisional hypothesis: hypotension duration versus nadir for early AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in eICU with a qualifying ICU stay and at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during the first 24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of new or worsening acute kidney injury during the next 48 hours, after accounting for baseline severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target. The unresolved question is whether duration and trajectory add clinically meaningful information beyond nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, measurement frequency, or treatment response.

## Evidence currently supporting the opening

## Falsifiable test
Use adults aged at least 18 with one qualifying ICU admission, valid ICU start time, and at least one valid systolic BP <65 mmHg in ICU time 0–24 hours. Define exposure as cumulative minutes below 65 mmHg using prespecified piecewise-linear interpolation without extrapolation. Define the primary outcome as new or worsening KDIGO-compatible creatinine AKI in the subsequent 24–72 hours. Use the last valid creatinine before or at ICU time 0 when available, otherwise a prespecified pre-exposure creatinine; report missingness rather than treating it as normal.

Compare a hospital-held-out logistic baseline using nadir, age, sex, baseline creatinine, and available baseline severity/vasopressor context with a time-aware alternative adding cumulative hypotension minutes, interval-specific time below threshold, last BP before window end, and a simple recovery/trajectory feature. Use the same split, outcome definition, uncertainty procedure, calibration, and discrimination evaluation. Support requires reproducible incremental information from duration with prespecified direction and uncertainty, not confined to one hospital or explained solely by observation density. A null, reversal, artifact confinement, sparse AKI ascertainment, or unstable timestamp support is adverse or inconclusive as specified.
