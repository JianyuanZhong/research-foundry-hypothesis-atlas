# Plasma protein–disease associations: a shared "sickness/inflammation–aging" axis vs disease-specific pre-clinical signals (UKB Olink subset)

Episode: 2 (fresh Lead). Status: design drafted; bounded schema/feasibility probe executed; incidence/date fields still to confirm.

## Unresolved question and why it matters
Large Olink plasma-proteomic screens repeatedly nominate single proteins (e.g. GDF15, IL-6, CRP-family) as strong predictors of incident disease. It is unresolved whether such protein–disease associations reflect (A) a **disease-specific pre-clinical process** (a genuine early disease signal) or (B) a **shared, non-specific "sickness / inflammation / aging" axis** elevated before many diagnoses, partly driven by reverse causation (subclinical disease at blood draw). This distinction is clinically consequential: it decides whether a circulating protein signature is a plausible disease-specific early-detection/early-intervention target or only a generic risk/severity correlate. [K1] argues that a 14-protein "lung-cancer" signature is more parsimoniously explained by cumulative smoking exposure and is non-specific across smoking-related and cardiovascular conditions, with no Mendelian-randomisation support for causal influence. [K2] shows proteomic aging markers predict ADRD, consistent with a shared aging axis. [K3] shows proteomic models add prediction for heart failure in MASLD beyond clinical factors.

## Leading claim vs strongest rival
- Leading claim (H_shared): the proteins most strongly associated with incident disease in this subset are **non-specific**; a single data-derived shared axis (PC1 of all measured proteins) explains the majority of protein–disease associations, and disease-specific residual effects after shared-axis + age/sex adjustment are small.
- Rival (H_specific): proteins carry **disease-specific** pre-clinical signals that survive shared-axis adjustment, are stable in held-out disease outcomes, and persist in the distant-follow-up subgroup (diagnosis >5 years after baseline).
- Discriminating observation: the cross-disease association matrix and its residual structure after shared-axis adjustment, plus the early (<2 yr) vs distant (>5 yr) contrast.

## Analysis
1. Build the analysis frame: inner join olink `n_eid` to dta `n_eid`; keep age/sex-complete participants; exclude prevalent cases of the target disease if a date/prevalence field allows, else define ever-diagnosed.
2. Compute the shared axis: PC1 (and PC2) of z-scored protein columns.
3. Per-disease association: logistic (or Cox if dates exist) regressing disease on each protein, standardized; adjust for age, sex; then re-estimate adjusting for PC1.
4. Specificity: for each protein, compare standardized effects across the three diseases; compute overlap of top-k proteins per disease and an association-specificity ratio; test whether PC1 alone reproduces the ranking.
5. Reverse-causation contrast: protein–disease effect in diagnosis `<2 yr` vs `>5 yr` from baseline (requires dates).
6. Baseline vs substantive alternative:
   - Baseline (simple): age+sex, then add a single protein (e.g. GDF15).
   - Substantive alternative (learned): an elastic-net / gradient-boosted multi-protein model predicting each disease, evaluated by cross-validated AUC and by incremental AUC over age+sex+PC1; trained per disease with held-out disease transfer. Rationale: a learned multi-protein model can expose non-linear/higher-order shared structure that a single-protein or PC1 baseline loses; if it adds disease-specific discrimination beyond PC1, H_specific gains support.
   - Split: participant-level k-fold; report calibration and 95% CI; declare the learned model exploratory.

## Falsification criteria
- H_shared supported if: PC1 is among the strongest single predictors of each disease AND after shared-axis + age/sex adjustment, few/no proteins retain FDR-significant disease-specific effects AND top-k protein sets overlap substantially across diseases.
- H_specific supported if: proteins retain FDR-significant, disease-specific effects after shared-axis adjustment AND replicate in a held-out disease with distant follow-up.
- Inconclusive if: only metastable/weak specificity, or the shared axis and specificity are not separable at available power, or dates are missing so reverse causation cannot be assessed.

## Honest limits
- Cross-sectional baseline exposure; single blood draw cannot represent lifetime exposure; medication and prevalent subclinical disease affect levels.
- Diagnosis arrays may include prevalent events; without dates the temporal ordering is weak.
- This is a design proposal; only the schema/overlap probe has been executed. No association results are claimed.
