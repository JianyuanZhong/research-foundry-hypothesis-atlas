## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

After a current blood-biomarker assessment, does the earlier panel add information beyond current values and repeat-assessment spacing, and is that incremental information different for the first later cardiovascular, renal, and metabolic recorded event pathways?

This is a consequential triage question because cardiovascular review, kidney evaluation, and metabolic prevention are different clinical pathways. A pooled cardiorenal/metabolic endpoint can show a useful average while hiding that historical information helps one pathway and worsens calibration for another. The substantive advance over the parent is an endpoint-specific, direction-free heterogeneity estimand rather than another pooled score comparison.

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among participants with a valid paired panel and no qualifying target-domain record by the current-assessment landmark, the held-out incremental predictive value of prior-panel values beyond current values and repeat-spacing metadata is not equal across first cardiovascular, renal, and metabolic recorded-event pathways.

The primary estimand is the omnibus heterogeneity of
`Delta_d = Brier(M1b,d) - Brier(M1a,d)`
for d in C (cardiovascular), R (renal), and M (metabolic), using a common competing-category outcome and the same held-out participants. Negative `Delta_d` favors prior history for pathway d. Prespecified pairwise contrasts are C minus R and C minus M; they are descriptive if cell support is inadequate. The pooled C/R/M target is a secondary continuity estimand, and it must not substitute for the domain-specific test.

The strongest available evidence supports only that the export contains repeated structured fields, assessment landmarks, diagnosis-code/date arrays, and death-date fields. It does not establish biomarker analyte meaning, specimen timing, ICD code semantics, disease onset, or complete follow-up. The experiment therefore has a recorded-event mode and a separately gated prospective mode.

## Evidence gates and selection audit

Four gates are independent and must be reported with evidence paths:

1. Timing: assessment-date semantics are verified; specimen-prospective interpretation additionally requires specimen collection timing, which is not present in the local schema.
2. Biomarker semantics: a field dictionary verifies all eight field IDs, units, ranges, and missing-value meanings at both instances. The metadata currently certifies neither analyte identities nor units; negative values cannot be globally recoded.
3. Outcome semantics: a codebook verifies `41270/41280`, the C/R/M mapping, date meaning, and death-date meaning.
4. Ascertainment: an approved linkage record supplies individual observation end or complete five-year ascertainment. The local metadata currently does not supply this.

If gates 1–4 pass, report a separately labelled five-year prospective competing-risk estimand with censoring. If timing or ascertainment does not pass, the primary result remains recorded-event classification and must use no incidence, disease-onset, or survival language. If biomarker or outcome semantics fail, report a reproducible fail-closed audit and do not claim the hypothesis was tested.

Quantify repeat-selection by flowing from all participants with a valid T0 panel to valid T1 observations. Within training folds, model repeat observation using only T0 panel, age, sex, and T0 calendar year. Report repeat rate, covariate balance, overlap, effective sample size, and a fold-local inverse-probability-of-repeat sensitivity. No T1 value, future outcome, or post-T1 field may enter this model. The primary estimand remains conditional on a valid paired panel; weighting is a generalizability sensitivity, not a causal correction.

## Models, split, and scientific deliverable

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold split, fixed by a published hash of `eid`, shared by all models and weighting fits. Imputation, scaling, regularization, tuning, calibration, and repeat weights are fit within training folds only.

- M0: current instance-1 panel, approved age, approved sex, and T1 calendar year.
- M1a: M0 plus L, the elapsed assessment interval. This isolates repeat-spacing information.
- M1b: M1a plus the eight instance-0 values. This is the primary prior-history comparison.
- M1c: sensitivity replacing prior values with eight raw differences (current minus prior) plus L.
- M2: a substantive same-input learned alternative using M1b inputs, with prespecified current-by-prior discordance, current-by-L and prior-by-L interactions and smooth threshold terms. Use gradient-boosted multinomial probabilities, with fixed depth/learning-rate grid chosen inside training folds. M2 is for discovering stable nonlinear pathway-specific structure; it cannot replace the primary nested M1b versus M1a estimand.

For each outer fold, fit all models and emit held-out probabilities for C, R, M, joint, death and no-record for every eligible participant. The required scientific deliverable is newly fitted held-out predictions and estimates of `Delta_d`, the omnibus heterogeneity statistic, C-vs-R and C-vs-M contrasts, calibration by pathway, and fixed-risk-band triage changes; alternatively it is a reproducible fail-closed certificate if required gates fail. Include fold manifest, participant counts, event counts, preprocessing/tuning manifests, and source/header/key/integrity audits.

Primary evaluation is multiclass Brier score by pathway and overall, paired at the participant level. Also report calibration intercept/slope, calibration curves, log loss, discrimination only secondarily, and exploratory decision-curve net benefit at 0.05 and 0.10. These thresholds are not validated clinical action thresholds. Define the current-only middle band as fold-local M0 target risk q20 <= r < q80; compare fixed-capacity top-K review lists as a secondary decision analysis, with K=floor(0.10*n_early) fixed before late-era evaluation. Do not let M1b or outcomes define risk bands or K.

Estimate uncertainty with participant-level bootstrap preserving folds and pathway labels. Require at least 100 total target events and at least 50 events in each of C, R, and M, plus at least 200 participants per reported subgroup cell, before interpreting the omnibus or pairwise heterogeneity; otherwise mark the relevant result inconclusive. The future solver does not need to use a GPU: the planned regularized multinomial/boosted tabular workload is CPU-suitable within the separate 16-CPU, 256-GiB, 8-hour solver envelope. This estimate is unverified planning guidance, not measured discovery compute. A GPU is available (A100-SXM4-80GB through allocated jobs) but is not a scientific merit criterion.

## Falsification and interpretation

Retain source hashes, headers, row counts, key uniqueness, join coverage, overlap agreement, date plausibility, split manifests, leakage scans, and all gate certificates. Run:

- code/date position permutation and deliberately shifted-position controls;
- outcome-label/date permutation;
- within-current-risk and interval-stratum permutation of prior panels, preserving current values, L, missingness, and selection indicators;
- complete-case versus prespecified fold-local missingness sensitivity;
- raw-prior versus raw-change sensitivity;
- interval-regime and repeat-weight sensitivity with positivity and weight-truncation diagnostics;
- early-through-2012 fit and 2013 no-refit transport, where the late cohort meets support requirements;
- calibration and fold-instability checks, not discrimination alone.

Supportive evidence requires certified biomarker/outcome semantics, an explicit endpoint mode, at least the stated event/support thresholds, no code/date integrity failure, M1b better than M1a on held-out Brier in at least one pathway with a 95% upper confidence bound below zero, and a statistically/uncertainty-supported omnibus domain contrast that is not erased by selection weighting, interval regimes, or the no-refit era test. A stable M2 interaction pattern can explain the heterogeneity but is not required and cannot convert a small uncalibrated gain into support.

Adverse evidence is no incremental gain in every adequately supported pathway, equal or opposite pathway contrasts, gain explained by spacing rather than prior values, disappearance under permutation/selection controls, or pathway-specific calibration harm that outweighs score improvement. Inconclusive evidence includes failed semantic/timing/ascertainment gates, sparse domain events, poor overlap or effective sample size, unstable folds, conflicting code/date definitions, or materially conflicting recorded versus gated prospective results.

Computationally checkable claims are header/schema/key integrity, cohort construction, endpoint implementation conditional on the certified codebook, held-out metrics, uncertainty calculations, leakage/permutation behavior, and whether conclusions match those outputs. Clinical adjudication is required for disease onset, phenotype severity, specimen timing, clinical usefulness, action thresholds, and whether a recorded pathway should trigger a particular review. Causal effects of repeat testing or monitoring, treatment recommendations, mechanisms, transport beyond the paired cohort, and benefit of pathway-specific follow-up require additional data, expert review, external validation, or a prospective policy study.

## Alternatives and explicit deferrals

The transparent nested M0/M1a/M1b comparison is primary because it directly separates current values, repeat scheduling, and prior values. The learned M2 alternative uses the identical participants, split, inputs, outcomes, and held-out metrics; it can reveal threshold and discordance structure that an additive model loses. It is deferred from primary inference if tuning is unstable, calibration worsens, or any improvement is too small to alter the pathway heterogeneity estimand. A mechanistic organ-score model is not selected because local field labels, units, assay quality, and specimen dates are unverified. A disease-history transformer adaptation is deferred because the local UKB export is a sparse field-position array rather than a verified dated disease sequence, so it would not resolve the prior-versus-spacing uncertainty. Genetics/PRS is deferred because the catalog describes the genomics CSV as derived metadata, not a verified sequence/variant bundle or validated disease-specific score. Imaging, sleep accelerometry, infection severity, CHIP, and cancer-stage branches remain available expert ideas but lack the locally verified modality, staging, or chronology required here. These branches should be revisited only if the missing dependency is supplied.

The three demonstrations were used as methodological context, not reproduced. The methods guide reports verified author evidence for Delphi structured disease sequences and describes a bounded UKB internal adaptation; it also documents that ALADYNOULLI’s genetic analysis cannot be reproduced without verified genetics and that Oncoformer’s full multimodal experiment is unavailable because images and the main paper/STAR Methods are unavailable. This proposal uses none of those unavailable claims or files as if inspected, and its learned alternative is a modest same-input tabular adaptation. The demonstrations therefore motivate the baseline-versus-learned comparison but do not establish this hypothesis.
