## Scientific opening, importance, and bounded claims

The strongest evidence-supported claim is record-level and predictive: a longitudinal laboratory record may add held-out information about a later operational TACE record beyond a day-42 snapshot or an observation clock. The parent’s O4 makes that record narrower—an O3 event with a parseable procedure end time within 72 hours—but it remains an operational proxy.

The unresolved claim is whether any remaining assay-result increment is a change within a patient, rather than a stable severity/recording phenotype. A patient with persistently severe disease, a persistent pattern of ordering, or a stable tendency to be recorded densely can have both a distinctive pre-index assay profile and a later TACE record. A second rival is documentation workflow: encounters, orders, examinations, medications, and the opportunity to obtain a laboratory result can generate both the observed trajectory and the downstream endpoint. A third rival is timing/capture: only the most recent or best-observed patients may have a visible day-42 change.

This matters because the next clinical study differs by result. A residual change signal after stable-profile and values-blind workflow controls would justify assay calibration and blinded imaging/intent adjudication. If the stable profile or workflow explains it, the direction should be treated as severity or record-process prediction, not an assay-response signal. Neither result identifies biology, treatment intent, procedure completion, radiographic response, progression, causality, benefit, survival, or utility. [K1] supports temporal sequence modeling while warning that learned record biases affect interpretation. [K2] motivates explicit selection/observation handling but does not recover unobserved clinical meaning. [K3] distinguishes recorded time from biological and decision time and supports exposing timing and missingness. This child adds a concrete HCC stable-versus-change contrast using fields that are actually present.

## Hypothesis and estimand

Primary hypothesis:

> Among eligible HCC patients, baseline-referenced assay-result change observed through day 42 adds patient-held-out information for the later O4 closed-coherent operational TACE proxy beyond a stable pre-index assay profile and the same values-blind documentation/workflow and laboratory-opportunity information; that change increment is not reproduced for the capture-adequate pre-opened-order/no-strict-coherence process pattern.

This is a descriptive/predictive hypothesis about recorded data. It is not a causal or mechanistic hypothesis.

Let t0 be the first eligible TACE start. Define Hpre=[t0−365,t0), H42=[t0,t0+42 days], and the later risk window [t0+43,t0+181). Use the fixed 14-day grid [43,57), [57,71), …, [169,181). Let (L_w) be patient-level weighted pooled-logistic discrete-time hazard loss, with the parent’s risk masks and R42 or R28 weights.

Use nested information sets:

- X_clock: M0/B0, risk-bin/time-at-risk indicators, encounter/observation clock, and pre-landmark opportunity indicators; no assay result values.
- X_workflow: X_clock plus values-blind encounter, order, examination, medication, non-TACE procedure, and laboratory-opportunity features through t0+42. These retain identities, timestamps, status, specimen type, result-present flags and missingness, but exclude `定性结果` and `定量结果`.
- X_stable: X_workflow plus P_stable, the fold-fitted pre-index assay-content profile.
- X_change: X_stable plus Delta42, the fold-fitted baseline-referenced within-person assay-content change through day 42.

The primary new contrast is
[
G_change|stable,workflow,O4
= L_wX_stable,O4-L_wX_change,O4.
]
A positive value means that observed within-person result change improves held-out prediction beyond the stable profile and recorded workflow; it does not mean biological change. Report the same contrast for O0–O3 and `O_planproxy_cap`, but make O4 the strict endpoint decision. Report G_stable|workflow = L_w(X_workflow) - L_w(X_stable) as the stable-phenotype diagnostic, not as evidence for a mechanism. Report L_w(X_workflow) - L_w(X_change) as the total result-content increment.

### Exact assay decomposition

Use only the verified `labs` columns `检验`, `定性结果`, `定量结果`, `标本类型`, and `检验时间`, with `患者主索引` and `就诊号` as keys. Normalize only whitespace/Unicode/case for the assay and specimen labels; preserve the original labels in the audit. The assay channel is the exact pair (normalized `检验`, normalized `标本类型`). Do not pool numeric values across channels: the source has no lab-unit column.

For each patient/channel, P_stable uses valid result content in Hpre and a deterministic latest pre-index anchor. Numeric content is parsed only when the complete numeric field is parseable; qualitative content remains a categorical token. The development-fold scaler/imputation/token dictionary is channel-specific and fitted only in grouped development data. The stable block contains the anchor content and an explicit anchor-missing mask; it never replaces a missing anchor with zero. Pre-index count and timing belong to the values-blind opportunity/workflow block, so stable content is not silently a measurement-density surrogate.

For each patient/channel with an anchor, Delta42 is the ordered sequence of valid H42 results represented relative to that anchor: channel-specific scaled numeric difference or a categorical unchanged/changed transition, with the valid relative time and inter-event time retained. For the elastic-net, use prespecified summaries of this same sequence—first available change, last available change, median change, and any-change indicator—plus explicit no-post-result/no-anchor masks. For the GRU, use the same channel, specimen, content, time, inter-event, result-present and opportunity variables as event tokens; the stable anchor is supplied as a separate patient/channel context block. A post-index result without a pre-index anchor is not converted to a zero change; it is retained in the values-blind opportunity arm and marked unsupported for Delta42. The baseline-supported sensitivity restricts to patients with at least one development-defined channel anchor and reports the selection/availability change.

This decomposition is the substantive contrast: if G_stable|workflow is large but G_change|stable,workflow is null, the data support a stable severity/recording phenotype, not a change-specific assay signal. If the change contrast remains while the workflow-only gain and process-control contrast do not, the residual information is more specifically linked to result content, although still not to biology.

## Matched methods and method-selection record

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted patient-held-out predictions and uncertainty for the four nested information sets, each of O0–O4 and `O_planproxy_cap`, plus deterministic endpoint/capture audits and the stable-versus-change contrast. No fitted result is supplied here.

**Auditable baseline.** Fit pooled-logistic discrete-time hazards with risk-bin intercepts and elastic-net regularization (alpha=0.5). Fit assay dictionaries, channel-specific scaling, imputation, missingness flags, lambda, R42/R28 selection weights and any GRU early-stopping choice only in five chronological grouped development folds. The baseline uses fixed summaries of the exact source fields and yields coefficient/block audits, paired patient losses, calibration and risk-bin support.

**Compact learned alternative.** Fit a one-layer time-aware GRU (hidden size 32, dropout 0.10, 32/16 hazard head, Adam 1e-3, weight decay 1e-4, at most 50 epochs, eight-epoch early stopping, seeds 17/29/41). It consumes the same source variables, endpoint masks, patient split, weights and information boundary as the baseline, with ordered event tokens to retain irregular ordering and nonlinear assay combinations that fixed summaries may lose. It is not selected because it is complex; it is a substantive test of whether ordering/interactions carry information beyond stable content and workflow. A GRU gain without a positive stable-adjusted O4 contrast or without process-control separation is not evidence for assay biology.

Both methods must be run at equal inputs/splits: identical patients, t0 boundary, source-derived feature masks, endpoint labels, R42/R28 arms, development folds, untouched test set, bootstrap resamples and output rows. The baseline’s summary encoding and GRU’s event encoding are two representations of the same verified fields, not different cohorts or additional modalities. Revisit them only if this decomposition passes and expert intent/imaging data become available.

Sort index patients by t0: earliest 80% development and latest 20% untouched test, with no patient in both. Report endpoint/provenance counts, O3/O4 discordance, closure-invalid reasons, capture adequacy, unknown provenance, baseline/change availability, R42/R28 weight tails and ESS. No raw result rows or patient identifiers enter public search or proposal attachments.

Falsification is frozen before test scoring:

1. Mask `定性结果` and `定量结果` while retaining assay identity, specimen, times, result-present flags and opportunity. A persistent (G) after value masking falsifies a result-content interpretation.
2. Permute content/change tokens within patient and exact assay/sample channel while preserving availability and timing. A surviving contrast is adverse for the change interpretation.
3. Remove the last 7 days of lab results (t0+35,t0+42] and repeat; a gain confined to this lead-time band is timing-sensitive.
4. Repeat the decomposition at the R28/H28 boundary using only information allowed through day 28.
5. Compare O4 with O3/O0 and with `O_planproxy_cap`; a positive process-control contrast is adverse.
6. Fit workflow-only, stable-only, and change-added blocks on the same split; this makes stable phenotype versus residual change directly observable.
7. Audit within-patient joins, duplicate keys, post-boundary rows, closure validity and all endpoint-overlap decisions deterministically.

### Frozen decision rule

Call the result **SUPPORTIVE** only if all are true: (a) the primary O4 G_change|stable,workflow lower 95% simultaneous bound is positive in both elastic-net and GRU; (b) direction is retained in ordinary and R42 arms and is compatible with R28 and late-window removal; (c) the change signal is removed or materially attenuated by value masking and within-patient content permutation; (d) the stable-only and workflow-only comparisons do not account for the full change increment; (e) the O4 process-control contrast for `O_planproxy_cap` has an upper simultaneous bound at or below zero; and (f) test support is adequate—at least 10 distinct O4 patients and ESS≥20 for the relevant weighted contrast, with paired availability audited. Supportive means only residual recorded assay information predicts a narrower operational record beyond measured stable profile and workflow.

Call it **ADVERSE** if the stable-only model accounts for the apparent total while the change contrast is null/negative; the change contrast survives value masking/permutation; workflow-only or `O_planproxy_cap` is positive; O4 collapses relative to O3 because closure is missing/invalid; the signal is present in only one method/weighting arm; or a late lead-time/process control reproduces it. This favors stable severity, documentation, timing or endpoint artifacts but does not identify which one.

Call it **INCONCLUSIVE** if O4 has fewer than 10 distinct test patients, ESS<20, closure/capture provenance is too sparse, baseline/change support is unstable, paired test availability is poor, simultaneous intervals are wide, R42/R28 overlap fails, or methods disagree. Given the bounded diagnostic of only 7 nonblank closure times among all exact TACE rows, an O4 INCONCLUSIVE outcome is plausible and must not be rescued by switching to O3, changing the 72-hour rule, selecting a favorable assay, or reporting O0 as clinical evidence.

Only a supportive computational result would justify the next step of blinded expert adjudication of intent, completion and imaging response. An adverse result would redirect toward stable severity or capture/workflow measurement, with expert review of endpoint validity. An inconclusive result would require more complete closure/intent/imaging/outside-care data or another cohort. Automatic verification can check joins, boundaries, masks, held-out losses, uncertainty, falsifiers and output-linked gates; clinical adjudication and any claim about mechanism, response, benefit or utility require experts and another study.
