# Episode 17: is the audited acidemic SpO2/PaO2 phenotype prognostic of hospital death?

## Unresolved question, importance, and advance

Among invasively ventilated adults at their first eligible acidemic SpO2/PaO2 pair in the first 72 ICU hours, is a released monitor SpO2 of 94–95%, rather than 96–97%, associated with an absolute hospital-mortality risk at least 10 percentage points higher after standardization to the same recorded hospital, charted FiO2 stratum, PaCO2 stratum, pH stratum, and ICU day?

This is a **prognostic association between recorded phenotypes**, not an effect of assigning an SpO2 target, changing FiO2, drawing an arterial blood gas, or treating acidemia. “Higher mortality risk” means observed downstream risk enrichment only. It does not mean that the lower SpO2 band, a PaO2 category, oxygen therapy, or clinician behavior caused death.

The selected episode-15 parent established the strongest currently supported EICU claim: its exact 556-observation construction is computable, and within its 199-row measured-overlap population SpO2 94–95% mapped more often to observed PaO2 <70 and less often to PaO2 >=90 than SpO2 96–97%. The predeclared joint relocation hypothesis was falsified: the 70–89 contrast was -11.866 points (95% interval -35.168,+6.859), adverse to its +10-point requirement, while the <70 contrast was +29.027 (+2.697,+45.321), directionally increased but uncertain relative to its +10-point cap. That is measurement evidence, not outcome evidence.

The unresolved claim is whether that acidemic paired phenotype carries patient-centered prognostic information. Resolving it matters because a robust, materially elevated downstream risk could justify independent prospective validation of whether synchronized PaO2/pH assessment improves risk recognition when monitor SpO2 appears acceptable. An adverse result would be equally consequential: it would argue against promoting the parent’s striking concurrent category mapping into a mortality-risk rationale. Neither result can choose an oxygen target or establish the value of additional blood-gas testing.

Current external evidence motivates the outcome link but does not answer it. Ding and Chen (Critical Care 2025;29:473; DOI 10.1186/s13054-025-05754-4; PMCID PMC12587580) analyzed 582 mechanically ventilated MIMIC-IV patients using the first SF–PF pair with SpO2 <97% within 24 hours and 28-day mortality. Their main letter reports 61.3% SF/PF severity concordance, weaker outcome discrimination for SF than PF, and emphasizes prognosis rather than concordance alone. It concerns ARDS SF/PF categories, not acidemia, these SpO2 bands, this EICU construction, or the proposed 10-point risk difference. Its full-text XML was inspected; its supplementary methods were not inspected, so no claim depending on uninspected supplement detail is made. The inherited inspected Delgado cohort supports pH-dependent SpO2–PaO2 mapping plausibility, while the inherited inspected OXY-BREATHES review found no substantial overall 90-day mortality difference across heterogeneous randomized oxygen strategies. Neither supports this prognostic hypothesis or a causal target recommendation.

## Evidence-supported claim versus hypothesis tested

**Strongest evidence-supported claim before this experiment:** the audited concurrent EICU measurement phenotype exists and is boundary-sensitive; in acidemia, the lower SpO2 band maps disproportionately to recorded PaO2 <70 under measured overlap. The full-source feasibility audit also establishes that a temporally downstream hospital outcome is complete and that a one-stay index design is computable.

**Primary unresolved hypothesis:**

[
Delta_M =
P_w(	ext{hospital death}mid 	ext{index SpO2 }94	ext{–}95)
-
P_w(	ext{hospital death}mid 	ext{index SpO2 }96	ext{–}97)
ge 0.10.
]

The subscript (w) denotes the parent’s symmetric overlap target after rebuilding cells on one earliest index pair per ICU stay. The +10-point absolute margin is a predeclared material prognostic-risk threshold carried forward for interpretability; it is not a validated MCID and not a treatment-effect margin.

The primary exposure is the lower versus higher **recorded paired oxygenation phenotype**. PaO2 is required to construct and audit the same paired phenotype, but it is not part of the primary exposure contrast. The inherited PaO2 categories <70, 70–<90, and >=90 are reported as descriptive bridges only: they show whether mortality patterns occur in the same mapping categories that motivated the successor. Their sparse death counts prohibit separate confirmatory category claims.

## Exact population, index time, and temporal ordering

Use all rows without sampling from EICU snapshot `[source checksum]`.

1. From `patient`, retain numeric age >=18, interpreting released `age='> 89'` as 90 for eligibility/descriptive adjustment only; require `unitvisitnumber=1` and nonmissing `unitdischargeoffset`. Within `patienthealthsystemstayid`, retain the smallest `patientunitstayid` if duplicated. The parent audited 157,883 eligible rows and health-system stays, so this tie rule removed none. This means first recorded ICU unit visit in a health-system stay, not lifetime-first admission.
2. Reconstruct PaO2, pH, and optional PaCO2 exactly from `lab`: require `labtypeid=7` and exact `labname` in {`paO2`,`pH`,`paCO2`}; valid ranges PaO2 20–760 mm Hg, pH 6.80–7.80, PaCO2 10–150 mm Hg. Within `patientunitstayid + labresultoffset + labname`, retain greatest numeric `labresultrevisedoffset`, then greatest `labid`; exclude an analyte-time if rows tied at the retained revision have distinct valid values. Pivot exact-offset PaO2+pH and optional PaCO2. Set `draw=labresultoffset`. Exact offset is a panel proxy, not verified common arterial specimen.
3. Require `0 <= draw <= min(4320,unitdischargeoffset)`.
4. Require probable invasive ventilation at `draw` from `respiratoryCare`: exact `airwaytype` in {`Oral ETT`, `Nasal ETT`, `Tracheostomy`, `Double-Lumen Tube`, `Cricothyrotomy`}; `ventstartoffset<=draw`; and either a closed interval with `ventendoffset>ventstartoffset` and `draw<=ventendoffset`, or the released open sentinel `ventendoffset=0` with `draw<=respcarestatusoffset`.
5. Reconstruct charted FiO2 from `respiratoryCharting`: exact `respchartvaluelabel='FiO2'`, `draw-30<=respchartoffset<=draw`, and `respchartentryoffset<=draw`. Parse numeric values, convert 0.21–1.00 to percent, retain 21–100 before ranking, and choose greatest `respchartoffset`, then `respchartentryoffset`, then `respchartid`. Parse RFC CSV quoting strictly and stop on malformed records.
6. From `vitalPeriodic`, retain every `sao2` 70–100 with `draw-5<=observationoffset<=draw`; take the median over all rows, retaining duplicate offsets and possible half-point medians. Define lower band as 94, 94.5, or 95 and higher band as 96, 96.5, or 97. Exclude 95.5.
7. Require concurrent pH<7.35 and either band. This reproduces 556 observations: 227 lower, 329 higher; 440 ICU stays, 439 people, 41 hospitals; and 12 otherwise eligible acidemic observations at SpO2 95.5 excluded.
8. For prognosis, within each `patientunitstayid` retain the smallest `draw` after all prior rules, before inspecting PaO2 category or outcome. This yields 440 index stays (181 lower, 259 higher), 439 people. A person with two health-system stays can contribute both; uncertainty clusters on `uniquepid`.
9. Define hospital death only when `hospitaldischargestatus` is exactly `Expired`; define survival only when exactly `Alive`; require nonmissing `hospitaldischargeoffset` and strictly `hospitaldischargeoffset>draw`. Stop if any other status occurs or if the outcome is not future to the index. The audit found all 440 statuses and offsets complete, only Alive/Expired, and all 440 offsets after index; 122 deaths occurred (51/181 lower, 71/259 higher).

The index is selected without outcome, PaO2 category, future vital signs, length of stay, or death. Hospital mortality is downstream of the index by recorded offset, but the database does not provide cause or exact time of death.

## Outcome-blind measured overlap and primary estimand

On the 440 index rows, create the parent’s typed cells using only index-time fields:

- integer `hospitalid`;
- integer `floor(FiO2_percent/10)`;
- PaCO2 missing as its own typed level, otherwise integer `floor(PaCO2/20)`;
- pH <7.20, 7.20–7.29, or 7.30–<7.35;
- integer `floor(draw/1440)`, necessarily ICU day 0, 1, or 2.

Retain cells containing at least one index stay in each SpO2 band. Do not use PaO2, its categories, death, discharge offset, outcome counts, or `apachePatientResult` to create cells.

For cell (s) with (n_{Ls}) lower-band and (n_{Hs}) higher-band stays, weight each lower stay by (n_{Hs}/(n_{Ls}+n_{Hs})) and each higher stay by (n_{Ls}/(n_{Ls}+n_{Hs})), then normalize separately by band. Estimate (Delta_M) as the weighted lower-minus-higher difference in hospital-death risks.

Full-source feasibility rebuilding after earliest-stay indexing retains 156 stays in 47 cells across 9 hospitals {122,182,183,184,420,440,443,458,459}: 65 lower with 16 deaths/49 survivors and 91 higher with 21 deaths/70 survivors. Weight sums are 33.725 per band; ESS is 59.74 lower and 77.45 higher; maximum normalized row weights are 2.471% and 2.224%; maximum original-site total-weight fraction is 37.53%. Thus prespecified feasibility floors of >=15 deaths and >=30 survivors per band, ESS >=50 per band, >=8 hospitals, and no site >50% weight pass.

The feasibility run necessarily exposed a noninferential point diagnostic: weighted mortality was 24.19% lower versus 27.16% higher, (Delta_M=-2.97) points. No bootstrap interval or inferential classification was computed. This transparent adverse-looking diagnostic cannot redefine the hypothesis or margin; the frozen experiment must still quantify uncertainty and accept supportive, adverse, or inconclusive results.

## Uncertainty, baselines, sensitivities, and falsification gates

Use 5,000 deterministic hierarchical bootstrap replicates with seed 20260917, starting from the frozen 440 index rows:

1. sample the 41 original hospitals with replacement, taking 41 distinct hospital copies;
2. within each hospital copy, sample that hospital’s original number of `uniquepid` clusters with replacement, carrying all indexed stays for a sampled person;
3. assign new hospital-copy and person-copy identifiers;
4. rebuild the typed cells in each copied sample, discard copied cells lacking either band, recompute weights, and estimate (Delta_M).

Report percentile two-sided 95% intervals, defined/failed replicates, raw and weighted risks, risk difference, risk ratio as a scale diagnostic when both risks are nonzero, weight sums, ESS, maximum row weight, site fractions, and all cohort reconciliations. Never pool duplicate copies under original IDs.

Mandatory gates for support are: <=10% failed bootstrap replicates; >=15 deaths and >=30 survivors in each band; ESS >=50 in each band; >=8 contributing original hospitals; no original hospital >50% total weight; and no required sensitivity or deletion of a site with >=10 rows changes (Delta_M) to <=0. A gate failure cannot create support.

Fixed baselines/sensitivities are:

- unweighted hospital-mortality RD in all 440 index stays;
- the primary overlap construction without pH strata;
- first 24 hours only, rebuilding overlap;
- complete-PaCO2 only, rebuilding overlap;
- 12-hour time cells; five-point FiO2/ten-mm Hg PaCO2 cells; 0.10-pH and 0.05-pH cells, each rebuilt;
- earliest index per `uniquepid` rather than per stay;
- outcome sensitivity using exact `unitdischargestatus` with `unitdischargeoffset>draw`, explicitly a different and less patient-centered endpoint;
- leave-one-original-site-out estimates for every site, highlighting sites with >=10 rows;
- descriptive PaO2 category table (<70, 70–<90, >=90) by band with deaths/nondeaths and weighted risks, with no category-specific hypothesis test.

The audited primary category counts/deaths are sparse: higher band 9/2 (<70), 55/9 (70–<90), 27/10 (>=90); lower band 30/5, 27/7, and 8/4. These diagnostics may describe where events occur but cannot support claims that any PaO2 category is harmful, protective, or independently prognostic.

**Supportive:** if the 95% interval lower bound is >=+0.10 and every gate passes, report that the lower recorded acidemic paired phenotype is associated with at least 10 points greater observed hospital-mortality risk in this measured-overlap EICU target. The strongest action implication is independent prospective validation of risk enrichment and whether synchronized measurements add decision value.

**Adverse:** if the interval upper bound is <+0.10, the material prognostic hypothesis is falsified. If the interval crosses zero, direction remains unresolved and this is not “no association.” If the entire interval is below zero, report an opposite observed association, not protection from lower oxygenation and not evidence favoring a lower target.

**Inconclusive:** if the interval includes +0.10, or a mandatory gate fails without a robust adverse result, report that EICU cannot resolve the material claim. A positive point estimate below +10 is not support. Sensitivity conflict makes support inconclusive. Negative and inconclusive findings are scientifically valid.

## Unavailable evidence, adjudication, and verifier limits

EICU lacks verified arterial specimen identity, SaO2/co-oximetry, raw waveform and artifact flags, device make/calibration, delivered FiO2, pre-index oxygen-target intent, reliable duration of exposure to either SpO2 band, temperature and 2,3-DPG, complete pre-index severity and treatment limitation adjudication, cause/exact time of death, and post-discharge 28- or 90-day mortality. It cannot resolve selection into ABG testing, residual severity/confounding, mediation, transportability to current practice, or whether any intervention would improve outcomes. Clinical review is required for specimen/ventilation truth, implausible values, goals of care, and the meaning of a 10-point prognostic difference. Prospective synchronized external validation is required for prediction; a decision-impact study is required before recommending extra ABGs; an assigned-target trial with exposure duration, rescue rules, and patient-centered outcomes is required for benefit or safety.

The verifier can check hashes/headers, exact labels and ranges, joins, tie-breaks, all temporal inequalities, the 556/440/156 reconciliations, outcome levels/completeness, outcome-blind index and cell construction, weights, events/non-events, bootstrap copy IDs, intervals, gates, sensitivities, and whether the reported interpretation follows from computed outputs. It cannot adjudicate clinical truth, eliminate confounding, validate the +10 margin, prove prediction transportability, establish causality, safety, benefit, or a treatment/monitoring recommendation.

Verifier fixtures:

- RD +15 points (95% +11,+21), every gate passes: supportive only for material noncausal prognostic association.
- RD +6 (+2,+9): adverse to the +10 claim; report a smaller positive association, not “no association.”
- RD -3 (-12,+6): adverse to +10 with unresolved direction; do not call the lower band protective.
- RD -12 (-20,-4): opposite observed association and adverse; do not recommend lower SpO2 or infer benefit.
- RD +12 (-3,+24): inconclusive about +10.
- RD +15 (+11,+21) with ESS 44 or site fraction 60%: inconclusive because support gates fail.
- Correct computation paired with a causal oxygen/FiO2 effect, a lower/higher target recommendation, safety, harm, tissue injury, mortality benefit, device validity, or routine-ABG recommendation must fail.
- Category-specific harm/protection claims from the sparse descriptive PaO2 table must fail.
- Conclusions inconsistent with intervals, margins, or gates must fail; appropriate supportive, adverse, and inconclusive interpretations must pass.
