# Hypothesis: Discordant Organ Recovery Trajectories Predict Mortality Better Than Aggregate Metrics

## Scientific Question

**Among critically ill patients in MIMIC-IV, do patients whose individual organ systems recover at different rates (discordant trajectories) have worse outcomes than patients with uniform or synchronous recovery, even when aggregate recovery metrics suggest improvement?**

Specifically, we test whether:
1. The degree of discordance between organ function trajectories is an independent predictor of in-hospital mortality
2. Discordant recovery patterns (e.g., rapid kidney recovery but persistent respiratory compromise) carry distinct prognostic information beyond aggregate metrics
3. A model incorporating discordance metrics outperforms traditional risk scores that rely on admission severity or mean recovery trajectories

## Background and Rationale

### Existing Knowledge

Recent work has begun to recognize that organ recovery after critical illness is not uniform. The expert seed "asynchronous organ recovery" [[starting question]] hypothesizes that after circulatory stabilization, patients whose kidney or respiratory function remains impaired are more likely to deteriorate again. Similarly, the "discordant recovery after acute kidney injury" seed [[starting question]] suggests that creatinine improvement without urine output recovery signals high risk.

However, these studies have limitations:
1. They focus on single organ pairs rather than multivariate discordance
2. They use crude recovery definitions rather than quantitative trajectory analysis
3. They do not systematically compare discordance-based models against traditional aggregate metrics

### Key References

Three key studies inform this hypothesis:

1. **K1 - Human acute inflammatory recovery is defined by co-regulatory dynamics of white blood cell and platelet populations** (Nature Communications 2022)
   - DOI: 10.1038/s41467-022-32222-2
   - Demonstrates that coordinated recovery of immune cell populations predicts clinical outcomes
   - Key finding: Deviation from mean recovery trajectory associated with 14x increased mortality
   - Limitation: Focuses on hematologic parameters, not multi-organ function trajectories

2. **K2 - Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness** (Critical Care 2023)
   - PMID: 37256861
   - Shows that single-parameter assessment of kidney function is insufficient
   - Key finding: Discordant biomarker patterns predict worse outcomes
   - Limitation: Focuses on kidney function specifically, not general principles of multi-organ discordance

3. **K3 - Sepsis subphenotyping based on organ dysfunction trajectory** (Critical Care 2022)
   - DOI: 10.1186/s13054-022-04071-4
   - Demonstrates that trajectory-based subphenotypes predict mortality better than baseline severity alone
   - Key finding: Rapidly worsening subphenotype had 28.3% mortality vs 5.5% for rapidly improving
   - Limitation: Focuses on sepsis population and SOFA score trajectories, not general multi-organ discordance

### Unresolved Question

The fundamental uncertainty is whether **discordance in recovery trajectories** represents a distinct pathological process with independent prognostic value, or whether it is merely a byproduct of measurement noise and heterogeneity. Existing evidence suggests it may be the former, but no study has systematically:
1. Quantified discordance across multiple organ systems
2. Compared discordance-based models against traditional metrics
3. Examined whether specific discordant patterns (e.g., kidney-lung discordance) have differential predictive value

## Clinical Importance

1. **Prognostic Refinement**: Discordant recovery may identify patients at high risk despite apparent improvement, enabling earlier escalation of care

2. **Mechanistic Insight**: Discordant trajectories may reflect ongoing inflammatory cascades, microvascular dysfunction, or organ-specific vulnerability

3. **Clinical Decision-Making**: Identifying discordant patterns could inform:
   - Intensification of monitoring
   - Targeted organ support strategies
   - Timing of discharge planning

4. **Resource Allocation**: Patients with discordant recovery may require extended ICU stay despite meeting discharge criteria based on aggregate metrics

## Baseline Method

**Traditional Risk Score:**
- Admission severity: APACHE II score calculated from admission vitals and labs
- Aggregate recovery: Mean change in SOFA score from admission to discharge
- Predictive performance: AUC, calibration

**Substantive Alternative:**
**Discordance-Based Model:**
1. **Trajectory Extraction**: For each patient, extract time-series of each organ function variable
2. **Rate of Improvement**: Calculate slope of recovery for each organ (change per day)
3. **Discordance Metric**:
   - Coefficient of variation of recovery rates across organs
   - Or entropy-based measure of trajectory heterogeneity
   - Or distance from mean trajectory (using DTW)
4. **Model Features**:
   - Discordance score
   - Specific discordant patterns (e.g., kidney-lung discordance)
   - Traditional metrics (APACHE II, mean recovery)
5. **Prediction**: Cox proportional hazards or logistic regression for mortality

**Information Gap:**
The baseline method treats all organs equally and aggregates their trajectories. The discordance model explicitly quantifies heterogeneity in recovery patterns, potentially capturing:
- Organ-specific vulnerabilities
- Compensatory vs. decompensatory dynamics
- Sequential failure patterns

## Analysis Plan

### Step 1: Data Preprocessing
- Extract chartevents for each ICU stay
- Filter to included variables (lactate, creatinine, ABG, MAP, temperature)
- Handle missing values (impute or exclude)
- Calculate recovery trajectories (linear regression of each variable over time)

### Step 2: Discordance Quantification
- Compute recovery rate for each organ: slope of regression line
- Calculate discordance metrics:
  - CV of recovery rates across organs
  - Entropy of recovery rate distribution
  - Distance from mean trajectory

### Step 3: Outcome Association
- Cox proportional hazards model with discordance score as time-varying covariate
- Adjust for: age, sex, admission severity, comorbidities
- Compare AUC of discordance model vs. baseline model

### Step 4: Pattern Analysis
- Test whether specific discordant patterns have differential predictive value
- Example: Patients with kidney recovery but persistent respiratory dysfunction

### Step 5: Validation
- Internal: 5-fold cross-validation
- External: Apply to held-out cohort if available

## Falsification Criteria

### Supportive Results (continue direction)
- Discordance score independently predicts mortality (hazard ratio >1.5 after adjustment)
- Discordance model outperforms baseline (AUC improvement ≥0.02)
- Specific discordant patterns have stronger predictive value than aggregate metrics

### Adverse Results (reconsider direction)
- Discordance score shows no independent association (HR <1.2)
- Discordance model performs worse than baseline (AUC difference <0.01)
- Discordance may reflect measurement noise rather than biological process

### Inconclusive Results (refine study)
- Discordance shows weak association but lacks statistical power
- Discordance model comparable to baseline but not superior
- Need larger sample size or different discordance metrics

## Limitations and Unresolved Questions

1. **Data Limitations**:
   - MIMIC-IV may not have measurements for all organ systems
   - Chartevents may have gaps affecting trajectory estimation
   - Discharge timing may be influenced by discordance rather than measured

2. **Methodological Limitations**:
   - Recovery rate estimation may be sensitive to sampling frequency
   - Discordance metrics may capture heterogeneity rather than discordance
   - Causal inference not possible (observational study)

3. **Clinical Translation**:
   - Discordance scores require real-time measurement capability
   - Clinical thresholds for concern need validation
   - Interventional studies needed to test whether addressing discordance improves outcomes
