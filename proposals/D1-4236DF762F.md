# Proposal: Observed-first-stop association and a prospective gate for vasopressor discontinuation order

## Repair, clinical importance, and evidence boundary

This proposal is a substantive response to the completed negative action-positivity audit in the two parents. The clinically important bedside question remains whether the order in which clinicians discontinue norepinephrine (NE) and vasopressin (VP) is associated with short-term hemodynamic failure. The prior repeated-landmark strategy asked for an assigned NE-first versus VP-first contrast within a 60-minute grace period. In the frozen MIMIC-IV audit, however, among 6,040 pre-action MAP-eligible landmarks, NE-first occurred at 3.49% (211), VP-first at 2.76% (167), simultaneous action at 0.40% (24), and no action at 93.34% (5,638). Both directional action probabilities failed the prespecified 5% gate. This is evidence against the computability of that assigned-strategy emulation, not evidence that either discontinuation order is clinically ineffective.

The strongest available public evidence supports only a heterogeneous, short-term hemodynamic association. The inspected 2019 individual-patient meta-analysis (PMID 30893494; DOI 10.1002/phar.2265) reported more clinically significant hypotension after VP-first than NE-first, but no clear short-term mortality or length-of-stay difference and substantial heterogeneity. The inspected 2020 meta-analysis (PMID 31008869; DOI 10.1097/SHK.0000000000001345) likewise found a hypotension signal but judged evidence insufficient for firm inference. The inspected open-access 2025 cohort (PMCID PMC12487481; DOI 10.1186/s40560-025-00823-w) found no weighted hypotension difference. These sources do not establish causal superiority, mortality benefit, or a universal stopping rule. The demonstration papers in `references/research-ambition/README.md` were treated as methodological demonstrations, not as evidence for this vasopressor question; no unavailable main article or supplement content is claimed.

The unresolved and falsifiable MIMIC question is narrower: **among adult first-ICU-stay patients with concurrent low-dose NE and VP who have a documented, non-simultaneous first stop of one study pressor while the other remains active, is observed NE-first versus observed VP-first associated with a different 6-hour risk of a prespecified hemodynamic-failure endpoint?** The estimand is explicitly associational and indexed at the first observed stop. It is not an assigned-strategy, intention-to-treat, per-protocol, or causal effect. A result may motivate a prospective study; it cannot by itself recommend an order.

## Population and time zero without backward indexing

Use the first ICU stay per adult hospital admission/`subject_id` represented by the earliest `icu/icustays.intime` (ties resolved by `stay_id`), and the first qualifying dual-infusion episode in that stay. Adult age is calculated from `hosp/patients.anchor_age + year(hosp/admissions.admittime) - patients.anchor_year`, preserving the MIMIC convention for `anchor_age > 89` rather than treating it as a literal age. Patients must be alive and in the ICU at the candidate time, with both positive supported NE and VP infusion intervals overlapping for at least six hours before time zero.

Reconstruct positive infusion intervals from `icu/inputevents` using only records timestamped at or before the candidate stop for eligibility. The primary boundary is the first documented end of a reconstructed active interval after at least six hours of overlap, provided no positive interval for that same drug begins within the next 10 minutes. Union overlapping intervals and bridge a gap of at most five minutes only when `orderid` or `linkorderid` continues and the prior row is not explicitly stopped, paused, or cancelled. Keep a boundary-quality flag rather than treating `statusdescription` as ground truth. The stop time is the end of the active interval; a stop is eligible only if the comparator is active at that time. Exclude simultaneous stops whose recorded times differ by at most five minutes, symmetrically for NE and VP. Do not require future drug absence, future comparator coverage, future survival, or future MAP stability. A restart is therefore an outcome, not an exclusion.

Require at time zero, using the two hours before and including the stop:

- NE dose `>0` and `<=0.20 mcg/kg/min` and VP dose `>0` and `<=0.04 U/min` at the stop, with valid supported units and a plausible weight where weight normalization is needed.
- No increase in either study dose during the preceding 30 minutes, as a pre-stop stability descriptor.
- At least three valid MAP observations spanning at least three of four 30-minute bins, including one in the final 30 minutes; median MAP at least 65 mmHg and no MAP below 55 mmHg.
- No active epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine during the preceding two hours.
- No ICU exit or death at or before the stop.

The competing-pressor and mechanical-support rules are deliberately separated. Competing pressors are a pre-stop exclusion only when their reconstructed infusion interval is demonstrably active in the preceding two hours. Mechanical circulatory support (MCS) is a mandatory safety phenotype for analysis, not a reason to silently discard cases: classify each eligible stop as MCS-positive, MCS-negative, or MCS-unknown using the frozen dictionary and timestamped records described below. The primary association is reported in MCS-negative patients only if the classification audit supports that label; otherwise all cases are retained in a descriptive table and the association is labeled MCS-unresolved/inconclusive. Never code missing MCS documentation as absence.

The primary exposure is **observed first stop**: `NE_first` when NE stops first and VP remains active; `VP_first` under the mirror rule. Simultaneous stops are excluded from the two-arm association but reported. Episodes with no qualifying stop within the observed ICU record are not controls and are reported in the eligibility/action flow table. If a patient contributes more than one possible dual-infusion episode, retain only the first qualifying stop in the first ICU stay; this prevents recurrent landmark weighting and makes the analysis a single clinical decision episode. A secondary episode-level sensitivity may include later episodes with subject-clustered uncertainty, but it cannot change the primary population or exposure definition.

## Associational estimand and analysis

Let `A` be the observed first stop (`NE_first` or `VP_first`) and `Y6` the six-hour endpoint defined below. The primary estimand is the standardized observational risk difference

`E[Y6 | do not implied]` is not used; instead report `P(Y6=1 | A=NE_first, X)` standardized over the pooled eligible exposure population minus `P(Y6=1 | A=VP_first, X)` standardized over that same population,

where `X` consists only of pre-stop variables. This is a covariate-standardized association, not a counterfactual treatment contrast. Also report crude risks, risk ratio, and 95% confidence intervals. The primary effect-size description is “percentage-point difference in observed six-hour failure risk,” never “risk under stopping NE” or “effect of NE discontinuation.”

Fit a transparent logistic or pooled-binomial model for `Y6` with exposure and prespecified pre-stop covariates; use standardization to the pooled covariate distribution. Covariates include age, sex, admission type, ICU time, care unit, calendar/anchor-year group, ventilation and RRT status before time zero, MAP level/variability and observation density, NE/VP doses and recent slopes, duration of dual overlap, heart rate, lactate and creatinine trajectory when available, urine output/fluid balance, and timestamped steroid or crystalloid/albumin exposure. Diagnoses are descriptive or sensitivity covariates only; discharge-coded diagnoses cannot define shock phenotype at time zero. Missing covariates may use multiple imputation with missingness indicators, but never impute exposure boundaries, rescue events, death, or MCS status. Cluster confidence intervals by `subject_id` (one primary episode per subject) and use a prespecified bootstrap only for the full endpoint pipeline.

Propensity weighting is secondary and diagnostic. Report exposure counts, covariate balance, overlap, effective sample size, weight percentiles, and positivity by prespecified broad strata (care unit, baseline MAP bin, dose bin, ventilation status, and MCS classification). Do not trim or redefine eligibility after viewing the exposure counts. If either arm has fewer than 200 episodes, fewer than 40 endpoint events, any prespecified broad stratum with no observed arm, standardized mean difference at or above 0.10 after adjustment, or effective sample size below 30% of that arm, the comparative association is feasibility-only and no adjusted clinical interpretation is made. The earlier 5% action gate remains a permanent failure for the assigned-strategy emulation; it is not replaced by a lower threshold or by pooling non-action landmarks.

## Six-hour endpoint, rescue, mechanical support, and observation feasibility

The primary endpoint is a binary **observed six-hour hemodynamic failure** beginning at the documented first stop and ending at the earliest event through six hours, ICU exit, or death. It is the first of:

1. death before or at six hours;
2. restart or re-initiation of the stopped study agent, defined by a positive supported interval for that agent beginning after the stop (including a new interval after a gap), with time and dose recorded;
3. initiation of any rescue vasopressor not active in the two-hour pre-stop window: epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine, identified by the same item dictionary and positive-interval reconstruction;
4. NE escalation while VP remains the sole study pressor, defined as a subsequent valid NE dose at least 0.05 mcg/kg/min above the last pre-stop NE dose, sustained in a positive interval or confirmed by a second valid dose record within 30 minutes. For VP-first, the mirror rescue is restart of VP; NE escalation is retained as a clinically important component but is not double-counted as a stopped-agent restart;
5. two valid MAP values below 60 mmHg in separate 30-minute bins, separated by 5–120 minutes, with no valid MAP carried forward across a gap greater than 15 minutes; or
6. ICU exit before six hours with death, an active study or rescue pressor, documented infusion continuation into the next department, or any prior failure component.

The event clock is fixed at the first qualifying event. A MAP-only failure is not declared if the two values are from the same bin, are invalid/warning-marked, or are generated only by interpolation. MAP is assembled from `icu/chartevents` item 220052 (arterial) and 220181 (non-invasive), preferring arterial at duplicate `charttime`; retain both source types and an arterial-line/measurement-intensity indicator. Accept values 20–200 mmHg with blank/zero `warning`. The primary binary endpoint does not require complete MAP coverage when a monitoring-independent component (death, restart, rescue, or pressor-active ICU exit) occurs. For survivors without a monitoring-independent event, classify endpoint ascertainment as `failure`, `failure-free`, or `indeterminate`; do not call an unobserved trajectory a success.

ICU exit is thus a prespecified endpoint boundary, not silent censoring. Alive exit is failure if a study/rescue pressor is active, `continueinnextdept` indicates infusion continuation, or a prior component occurred; it is failure-free only if all recorded vasopressors are off, no prior failure occurred, and the exit time is within the six-hour window. If the exit record cannot establish these conditions, classify it indeterminate and provide best-case/worst-case bounds. Death is always failure, including death at or before exit. Report the composite and every component separately, including the number indeterminate from monitoring or exit evidence.

Complete rescue feasibility requires scanning all selected `inputevents` rows for NE 221906, VP 222315, epinephrine 221289/229617, phenylephrine 221749/229630/229631/229632, and dopamine 221662. Retain `amount`, `amountuom`, `rate`, `rateuom`, `patientweight`, `starttime`, `endtime`, `orderid`, `linkorderid`, `statusdescription`, and `continueinnextdept`; reject unsupported units, nonpositive rates, invalid intervals, and implausible normalized doses while publishing the rejection count. Do not infer a rescue from an order without a positive interval.

MCS feasibility must be completed before interpreting an MCS-restricted association. Use `icu/d_items` to freeze every relevant item whose label/category indicates IABP, Impella, ECMO, VAD, RVAD, LVAD, or other mechanical circulatory support, preserving `itemid`, `label`, `category`, `linksto`, `unitname`, and `param_type` in a mapping artifact. The bounded dictionary scan already shows that relevant MCS evidence is not confined to `procedureevents`: the matched labels include procedure-linked IABP line item 224272, but also `chartevents` items such as ECMO 224660, RVAD flow 220128, Impella/IABP hemodynamic fields, and `datetimeevents` IABP insertion item 225338. Therefore procedureevents-only exclusion is invalid. Scan the relevant `chartevents`, `datetimeevents`, and `procedureevents` records from ICU admission through time zero, retaining exact source item/time/value and a three-level adjudication flag. A positive flag requires a timestamped, dictionary-confirmed MCS record compatible with the ICU interval; absence of a record is not automatically negative. Sample blinded clinical/pharmacy review of positive and unknown timelines is required to estimate false-positive/false-negative boundary performance; without it, MCS-stratified results remain descriptive.

Report secondary outcomes only as exploratory: MAP area below 65 through six hours with explicit observation coverage, all-pressor-free status and hours at 24 hours, lactate change at 24 hours, ventilation and RRT initiation by 48 hours, AKI stage 2/3 when creatinine/urine-output coverage permits, ICU/hospital discharge, and in-hospital death. `hosp/admissions.deathtime` and `hospital_expire_flag` support in-hospital death; `hosp/patients.dod` is an audit only and does not guarantee outpatient mortality completeness. Do not make a 28-day mortality claim unless date shifting and ascertainment are explicitly validated.

## Falsification, boundary diagnostics, and interpretation

Prespecify the following checks before examining the arm-specific endpoint:

- A pseudo-stop six hours before each actual stop, while both agents remain active, testing whether the later observed arm label predicts pre-index MAP or lactate. A strong pseudo-association undermines temporal interpretation.
- A negative-control outcome in the pre-stop window and arm-specific MAP observation frequency, arterial-line availability, post-stop bin coverage, and endpoint-indeterminate fraction. Differential monitoring can create an apparent MAP association.
- Blinded review of a fixed random sample of NE and VP boundaries, including the raw `inputevents` rows, overlap/bridge rule, `statusdescription`, `continueinnextdept`, and subsequent restart. Report agreement/PPV and arm symmetry before using the association.
- Boundary sensitivities fixed in advance: 5- and 15-minute no-new-row stop windows, 0- and 10-minute bridge rules, 2- and 10-minute simultaneous windows, and MAP thresholds 55 and 65. No sensitivity may be chosen after seeing direction.
- Leave-one-care-unit and leave-one-anchor-year-group analyses, arterial-only and observation-density-restricted analyses, and exclusion of MCS-unknown cases as a sensitivity rather than an eligibility repair.
- Component-specific competing-risk tables for death, restart, rescue, MAP failure, and pressor-active exit. A composite association driven only by documentation intensity is not clinically supportive.

Supportive results are limited to a reproducible, adequately observed association favoring NE-first by at least five percentage points with a confidence interval excluding zero, balanced covariates/overlap, stable direction across reasonable boundary and monitoring analyses, no adverse death/rescue/RRT component pattern, and acceptable MCS adjudication. Even then, the conclusion is “observed NE-first was associated with lower six-hour failure in this MIMIC decision population”; it is not that NE-first causes fewer failures or is preferable at the bedside.

Adverse results include an association favoring VP-first by at least five points, or an apparent NE-first benefit accompanied by more death, rescue, restart, RRT, endpoint indeterminacy, or a boundary/monitoring reversal. These findings falsify the directional hypothesis or identify a documentation artifact and should not be translated into a recommendation. Inconclusive results include failed action overlap, small arms/events, MCS unknownness, boundary-review PPV below 0.80 or an arm difference of at least 0.10, effective sample size failure, unstable estimates/bounds crossing both clinically important benefit and harm, differential observation, or inability to classify ICU exits.

The current negative action-positivity audit already triggers the prospective-study gate for an assigned-strategy claim. A prospective study is warranted rather than a causal MIMIC claim if the observed-first-stop association is clinically large but remains confounded/unstable, if either action remains rare in clinically relevant strata, if MCS or true administration cannot be adjudicated, or if endpoint component ascertainment differs by arm. The prospective protocol should enroll an enriched population of adults on concurrent low-dose NE/VP with prespecified MAP/perfusion stability and MCS exclusions, record clinician intent and actual pump stop times, capture rescue and mechanical-support events continuously, and randomize discontinuation order only when equipoise and safety criteria are met. Its falsifiable primary hypothesis should be the same 6-hour composite, with a five-point superiority margin and noninferiority/safety monitoring for death and rescue. MIMIC cannot determine whether such randomization is ethical, establish intervention safety, or validate bedside clinical significance; those require expert review and prospective evidence.
