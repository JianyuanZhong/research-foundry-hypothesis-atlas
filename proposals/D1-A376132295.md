# Provisional eICU hypothesis: monitoring reduction during persistent instability

Status: provisional and untested. This is a design checkpoint, not a result.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with a continuous ICU stay who are physiologically unstable at a prespecified landmark, a reduction in laboratory-testing intensity during the preceding 12 hours is associated with a higher risk of a time-local respiratory or vasopressor/infusion deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond contemporaneous measured severity and recent support.

The association should attenuate when discharge/transfer, death, support de-escalation, and documented end-of-life care are handled explicitly. A residual association in hospital-held-out data would be evidence for a monitoring-process signal, not proof that changing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

## Why it matters

Reduced testing during instability could identify a potentially reviewable monitoring-process failure, while an association that disappears after handling end-of-stay and de-escalation contexts would show that the apparent signal is mostly severity, recovery, or documentation behavior. Either outcome would change how clinicians and health-system teams interpret a “monitoring gap.”

## Data-bound design

Use the configured eICU source files and their documented schemas. The within-dataset join key is `patientunitstayid`; use `hospitalid` only for hospital-level stratification or held-out validation. The relevant source tables are the longitudinal laboratory table (`lab.csv.gz`), vital-sign tables (`vitalAperiodic.csv.gz` and/or `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`), respiratory-care/charting tables, infusion/treatment tables, patient stay table, and hospital table. Exact column names and archive-member availability must be re-audited from the table metadata before execution; the current checkpoint does not claim they have been fully audited.

Construct one-hour or 12-hour landmarks within continuous stays. Define exposure as a prespecified reduction in the number of qualifying laboratory observations over the preceding 12 hours relative to a prior comparable window, with explicit handling of missingness and time since last test. Define outcome as a time-local deterioration in respiratory support or vasopressor/infusion intensity in the next 12 hours, using only records available at the landmark. Treat death, discharge/transfer, and end-of-life/de-escalation documentation as competing transitions or stratified contexts rather than silently coding them as non-events.

The baseline is an adjusted discrete-time model using landmark severity, recent support, time since last lab, and hospital/ICU context. A substantive alternative is a longitudinal recurrent-event or state-space model that represents laboratory monitoring and physiologic state as time-varying processes and estimates time-local risk while allowing competing transitions. The alternative can reveal whether the association is a persistent trajectory or a transient documentation gap; the baseline loses that temporal structure. Both must use the same population, landmark, outcome, split, and uncertainty evaluation.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence would be a prespecified positive association that persists after context adjustment and remains directionally consistent in hospital-held-out validation, with uncertainty estimates and negative controls not showing a comparable association for a non-causal timing control. Adverse evidence would be attenuation to null after explicit handling of EOL/discharge/de-escalation, failure to transport across hospitals, or a comparable signal for the negative control. Inconclusive evidence would be sparse qualifying landmarks, unstable endpoint ascertainment, or wide intervals; it would motivate a measurement/process study rather than a clinical recommendation.

## Missing evidence and limitations

No cohort counts, endpoint operationalization, source-row audit, model fit, or clinical result is claimed. The exact table columns and archive members still require inspection from the available metadata. Clinical adjudication of true deterioration, intent to reduce testing, and end-of-life status is unavailable or incomplete. The required three inspected key-reference receipts are not yet attached; literature claims are therefore intentionally not made here. No causal or treatment conclusion is authorized by this proposal.
