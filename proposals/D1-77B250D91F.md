# Working proposal: in-hospital laboratory trajectories after acute coronary syndrome (v21)

## Question
Among adults with an acute or subacute coronary syndrome diagnosis during an index hospitalization, does the in-hospital laboratory trajectory from admission to discharge predict 60-day all-cause readmission beyond what is captured by discharge laboratory values alone?

## Clinical importance
ACS patients are at high risk of readmission, and post-discharge monitoring decisions often rely on a single discharge laboratory snapshot. If the in-hospital trajectory (e.g., troponin, creatinine, hemoglobin, platelet count) carries incremental information, this could inform follow-up intensity and readmission prevention. The prior post-discharge trajectory design was not feasible in the available HCC data: a bounded diagnostic found only 8 patients with post-discharge lab coverage under the broadened ACS definition. In-hospital labs are available for the index admission by definition, making this question feasible while retaining the scientific question of whether dynamic laboratory change adds prognostic information.

## Evidence base
- [K1] Liu et al. 2026: In-hospital inflammatory-nutritional-renal trajectory phenotypes are associated with graded 90-day risks after ADHF discharge and provide limited additional prognostic information beyond baseline clinical and biomarker data.
- [K2] Marcus et al. 2026: In patients hospitalized with ADHF, a decline in platelet count from normal at admission to thrombocytopenia at discharge was associated with excess 30-day readmission and mortality.
- [K3] Additional reference needed: a study on in-hospital laboratory trajectories or dynamic biomarker changes in ACS or related acute conditions.

## Data bindings
- Source files: hcc_encounter.parquet, hcc_diagnosis.parquet, hcc_lab.parquet
- Join keys: encounter_id, patient_id
- Time fields: admission_time, discharge_time
- ACS diagnosis terms: acute, subacute, old, coronary, myocardial, infarction, MI, NSTEMI, STEMI, unstable angina, ACS
- Key analytes: troponin, creatinine, hemoglobin, platelet
- Outcome: 60-day all-cause readmission (any encounter within 60 days after discharge)

## Baseline vs. learned alternative
- Baseline: logistic regression with discharge laboratory values and clinical covariates.
- Learned alternative: gradient-boosted trees or a simple neural network with in-hospital trajectory features (e.g., slope, area under curve, time to peak) in addition to discharge values.
- The learned alternative could reveal nonlinear or time-dependent patterns that the baseline loses, but requires careful validation to avoid overfitting.

## Falsification criteria
- If the in-hospital trajectory adds no significant prognostic information beyond discharge values (e.g., no improvement in AUC or calibration), the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the trajectory is associated with readmission but only in a subset of patients (e.g., those with specific comorbidities), the hypothesis is partially supported.
- If the trajectory is not associated with readmission, the hypothesis is not supported.
