# Earlier natural menopause and cardiac structural remodelling beyond blood-pressure load

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank women with self-reported natural menopause, does earlier age at menopause remain associated with left-ventricular myocardial mass and left-atrial structure/function after adjustment for age, body composition, and measured blood-pressure load?

The clinically important unresolved question is whether menopause timing marks a subgroup with earlier subclinical cardiac structural change, or whether the apparent association is explained by residual haemodynamic load and shared metabolic ageing. This matters because menopause timing itself is not modifiable, but if it identifies a subgroup with earlier cardiac remodelling, it could motivate earlier cardiovascular surveillance and risk-factor control.

The leading explanation is that earlier natural menopause is associated with higher cumulative blood pressure and/or adverse body composition, which in turn drives left-ventricular hypertrophy and left-atrial remodelling. The strongest rival is a sex-hormone-related biological transition: loss of oestrogen may independently alter cardiac structure or vascular function. A third plausible explanation is selection/measurement: women with earlier menopause may differ in health-care contact, HRT use, or the probability of having a high-quality cardiac MRI. The available data can test whether the association persists after adjustment for measured blood pressure and body composition, but cannot establish causation or a hormonal mechanism.

## Closest inspected work

[K1] Wang et al. (2026) inspected a UK Biobank cohort of 170,250 women and found that HRT use is associated with elevated ASCVD risk, with risk varying by reproductive characteristics including age at menopause. This work supports the clinical relevance of reproductive timing for cardiovascular risk but does not test cardiac structural remodelling directly. The current proposal adds a direct test of cardiac MRI phenotypes, rather than relying on clinical events alone.

[K2] Chong et al. (2026) inspected 46,102 UK Biobank participants with carotid ultrasound and cardiac MRI and found that higher cIMT is associated with left ventricular hypertrophy and greater chamber volumes. This work establishes that vascular and cardiac structural traits are related in UK Biobank, but it does not test menopause timing as a predictor. The current proposal adds a test of whether menopause timing contributes beyond measured blood-pressure load.

[K3] Ometto et al. (2026) inspected UK Biobank cardiac MRI data and derived hundreds of cardiac MRI traits using 3D diffusion autoencoders. This work describes the availability and measurement properties of cardiac MRI in UK Biobank, but it does not test menopause timing as a predictor. The current proposal adds a focused test of menopause timing as a predictor of structural remodelling.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use UK Biobank participants with:
- self-reported natural menopause age (field 3581),
- no surgical menopause (field 2724),
- baseline cardiac MRI measures available (fields 24105, 24110, 24113),
- baseline and/or repeat blood pressure where available (fields 4080, 4079),
- and no prior cardiovascular diagnosis at the assessment visit.

Define the baseline visit as the assessment-centre date (field 53). For prospective outcomes, exclude diagnoses recorded before that date and use incident heart failure or atrial fibrillation after baseline. For structural remodelling, use cardiac MRI measures available at baseline: LV myocardial mass, LA maximum volume, and LA ejection fraction.

## Variables and estimand

Primary comparison: continuous age at natural menopause, with earlier menopause compared with later menopause, adjusted for age at assessment, sex-specific body composition, smoking, socioeconomic factors, and blood pressure. The primary estimand is an adjusted association with LV myocardial mass and LA function/volume, not a causal effect.

Key competing explanation: earlier menopause may co-occur with higher cumulative blood pressure, higher adiposity, lower physical activity, or earlier onset of other metabolic disease. Therefore the analysis must compare the association under:
1. minimal adjustment,
2. adjustment for baseline blood pressure and body composition,
3. adjustment for repeat blood pressure in the subset with repeat measurements,
4. a sensitivity analysis excluding early cardiovascular diagnoses and HRT users.

If the association disappears after repeat-blood-pressure adjustment, the leading interpretation is residual haemodynamic load rather than an independent menopause-timing signal.

## Outcomes and falsification

Primary outcomes: LV myocardial mass, LA maximum volume, and LA ejection fraction. Secondary outcomes: incident heart failure and atrial fibrillation. A supportive result requires a directionally consistent association with earlier menopause after adjustment, with stronger persistence in the repeat-blood-pressure subset. An adverse result is no association after adjustment or a direction inconsistent with the hypothesis. An inconclusive result is weak association, low overlap, or inability to distinguish menopause timing from blood-pressure load.

Falsification criteria:
- no association in the adjusted models,
- association explained by HRT use or prior cardiovascular disease,
- association confined to a small subgroup with poor data quality,
- or loss of signal after excluding early diagnoses.

## Method comparison

Baseline: multivariable linear/Cox regression with prespecified adjustment sets and inverse-probability weighting or missingness indicators. This is scientifically adequate for a descriptive association and can quantify uncertainty without requiring a neural model.

Alternative: a regularized elastic-net or gradient-boosted model predicting cardiac MRI phenotype from menopause timing, blood pressure, body composition, and demographic variables. This could reveal nonlinear interactions or a predictive signature, but it would not establish a mechanism and could overfit a small MRI subset. It is deferred unless the regression shows a robust association and the MRI sample supports stable fitting.

No GPU is required for either approach. The experiment is CPU-feasible.

## Deliverable and completion criterion

The solver must fit the adjusted regression models on the UKB subset, report effect estimates and 95% confidence intervals for each cardiac phenotype, and report whether the association persists after repeat-blood-pressure adjustment. Completion requires a reproducible analysis script, a summary table of estimates, and a written interpretation distinguishing supportive, adverse, and inconclusive results.

## Alternatives not chosen

A neural sequence model is not justified because the available measurements are sparse and the primary question is an adjusted association, not prediction. A causal mediation analysis is not justified because HRT and blood pressure are not fully measured and reverse causation is possible. A genetic instrument analysis is not available in this subset.
