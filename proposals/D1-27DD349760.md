# Provisional episode-12 checkpoint: timing-anchored eICU hypotension duration and AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU admission with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. Cumulative duration is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone. This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Grounding and unresolved question
The inherited evidence motivates the question but does not establish it: prior perioperative work studied time-weighted hypotension and did not answer the ICU duration-versus-nadir comparison; ICU AKI is dynamic and incompletely captured by creatinine; and EHR missingness can affect prediction and transportability. The unresolved claim is whether duration adds reproducible information after timing and ascertainment constraints. No eICU effect estimate has been computed in this episode.

## Exact available data and test

Join longitudinal tables by `patientunitstayid` and hospital context by `hospitalid`; do not join across datasets.

Qualify adults with valid ICU start and at least one valid systolic BP <65 in 0–24 hours. Derive cumulative minutes below threshold, nadir, and measurement counts from both vital tables, with prespecified physiologic filtering, piecewise-linear interpolation only between observations, and no extrapolation. Define the primary creatinine endpoint using the last valid creatinine at or before ICU time 0 when available; otherwise use a prespecified pre-exposure creatinine and report missingness rather than imputing normal. An event is the earliest rise >=0.3 mg/dL from baseline within 48 hours or >=1.5 times baseline if documented in the outcome window, with time to event retained. Do not use a post-exposure creatinine as baseline; classify missing baseline/outcome ascertainment as indeterminate. Urine-output AKI is secondary only after semantic and timing validation of intake/output labels, values, offsets, duplicates, and cumulative behavior.

Compare a simple hospital-stratified logistic baseline using nadir, age, sex, baseline creatinine, and available baseline severity/vasopressor context with a time-aware alternative adding cumulative hypotension minutes, interval-specific time below threshold, last BP before window end, and recovery/trajectory while retaining nadir. Use the same rows, outcome, uncertainty procedure, and hospital-held-out split. Report duration effect estimates and confidence intervals, calibration, and hospital-held-out discrimination as secondary descriptive outputs. A complex sequence model is deferred because the available data cannot distinguish hypotension-mediated injury from severity, treatment, and observation artifacts; revisit only if a bounded diagnostic shows the simpler representation loses material trajectory information.

## Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: duration adds reproducible information beyond nadir with prespecified direction and uncertainty, not explained solely by observation density or one hospital. Adverse: duration adds no information, reverses after prespecified adjustment, or is confined to artifact-prone measurements. Inconclusive: creatinine ascertainment, timestamps, or sampling are too sparse for reliable estimation; an imprecise null is not decisive refutation. Causal interpretation, treatment thresholds, and mechanism require clinical adjudication, richer treatment/etiology data, and prospective or quasi-experimental evidence.
