# Provisional hypothesis: body-fat distribution separates metabolic from functional/fall risk trajectories

Status: provisional, evidence-bound, and uncomputed. This is not a result or a selection.

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with valid second-assessment body-composition MRI, at similar total adiposity, higher visceral fat may be more specifically associated with subsequent metabolic disease, while higher posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) may be more specifically associated with subsequent functional decline or falls. The substantive advance is a temporally ordered, outcome-specific test of whether fat distribution contains information beyond total adiposity, rather than another generic health-risk prediction.

## Evidence and uncertainty

The parent branch reports that inspected evidence supports associations of MFI with metabolic and functional outcomes, that IMAT/MFI evidence is heterogeneous across imaging and disease contexts, and that UKB abdominal MRI phenotypes can improve internal prediction of incident type 2 diabetes. These claims motivate plausibility only; they do not establish the proposed divergence, causality, or clinical utility. No association has been computed in this episode. The strongest rival explanations are baseline severity, age/sex, measurement timing, selection into MRI, and observation/ascertainment practices.

Define time zero at the second assessment, exclude prevalent disease where identifiable, apply a prespecified one-year lag for metabolic outcomes, and use later repeated functional/fall measures where available.

Compare a simple adjusted total-adiposity model (age, sex, ethnicity, and measurement timing) with a prespecified composition model adding visceral fat and posterior-thigh MFI. Evaluate outcome-specific contrasts, held-out calibration/discrimination or trajectory summaries, and uncertainty. A learned sequence model is deferred unless repeated-measure support and missingness diagnostics justify it; it could reveal nonlinear time-varying decline that the baseline loses, but is not needed to answer the primary distribution question.

Supportive evidence would be stable, prespecified outcome-specific contrasts with uncertainty excluding the null after total-adiposity adjustment and sensitivity analyses. Adverse evidence would be no stable incremental information, reversal, or explanation by severity/timing/selection. Inconclusive evidence would be sparse events, insufficient overlap, missing dates, or wide uncertainty. None of these outcomes establishes mechanism or treatment benefit.
