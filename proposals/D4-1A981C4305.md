# UKB 21: Do changes in branched-chain amino acids precede deterioration in glucose metabolism

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

Those with increased branched-chain amino acids and decreased glycine between two measurements remain at higher subsequent diabetes risk, even when the second HbA1c is similar.

## Study population and key data

Individuals with repeat NMR and biochemical measurements who did not have diabetes at the second measurement.

## Basic Approach

Follow up from the second measurement onward, compare the information provided by current levels versus changes, adjust for weight change, and correct for measurement error.

## Significance of the Research

Determine whether metabolic changes provide precursor information beyond current glucose levels.

## Main Challenges

Two measurements cannot determine the exact turning point; sampling status and regression to the mean can affect the magnitude of change.

## Life Sciences

Disease prodrome and state transitions

## Competing Explanations

The changes may only reflect existing weight gain, medication use, or occult diabetes.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

First quantify test–retest reliability; if there is no incremental value after excluding early diabetes, weaken the preclinical interpretation.
