# Creatinine–cystatin C discordance, low muscle reserve, and prospectively recorded kidney events

## Child audit and preserved scientific question

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`.

The parent is scientifically valid and executable in principle. This child preserves its question, population concept, primary outcome and transparent Cox interaction. It repairs the remaining ambiguity in clinical risk interpretation and method comparison: death must be represented as a competing event in the nonlinear model and in reported five-year clinical risks, rather than being silently treated as ordinary censoring. It also makes the event-support and provenance prerequisites operational after the inherited diagnostic job was cancelled.

The unresolved question remains whether a lower cystatin-C-based eGFR relative to creatinine-based eGFR identifies prospectively recorded renal vulnerability in people whose low functional reserve may make creatinine-only assessment less informative. The primary hypothesis is:

> Among eligible UK Biobank participants without a previously recorded hospital N17 or N18 diagnosis, the hazard of a first subsequently recorded hospital N17 diagnosis associated with lower standardized discordance (D=eGFRcys-eGFRcr) is stronger at lower standardized baseline grip strength, conditional on eGFRcr, age, sex and BMI.

This is a prognostic association hypothesis, not a claim that cystatin C is a gold standard, that grip measures muscle mass, or that the interaction is causal.

The inspected Haines et al. CJASN 2023 article is a 38-person mechanically ventilated ICU study with serial creatinine, cystatin C, iohexol clearance and rectus-femoris ultrasound. It supports the physiological motivation that creatinine-based estimates can overestimate filtration during critical-illness muscle loss; it does not establish this community association, a UKB AKI/CKD accuracy claim, or clinical benefit from cystatin-C testing. N17 and N18 in this experiment remain recorded hospital diagnosis categories, not adjudicated laboratory-defined AKI/CKD.

## Exposure definitions

Convert creatinine from verified µmol/L to mg/dL by division by 88.4 and retain cystatin C in mg/L. Use age in years and the verified sex coding. Freeze the following equations before outcome fitting:

- Race-free CKD-EPI 2021 creatinine eGFR:
  `eGFRcr = 142 * min(Scr/k,1)^a * max(Scr/k,1)^(-1.200) * 0.9938^Age * 1.012^(Female)`, with `k=0.7, a=-0.241` for female and `k=0.9, a=-0.302` for male.
- Standalone CKD-EPI cystatin-C 2012 eGFR:
  `eGFRcys = 133 * min(Scys/0.8,1)^(-0.499) * max(Scys/0.8,1)^(-1.328) * 0.996^Age * 0.932^(Female)`.

Both are ml/min/1.73 m² estimates; neither is measured GFR. Define (D=eGFRcys-eGFRcr), where lower D is the prespecified direction compatible with creatinine-only overestimation. Standardize eGFRcr, D and mean grip using training data and freeze those transformations for validation, test and sensitivity scoring. Reject nonfinite or metadata-invalid values and report all counts. A raw-unit sensitivity uses log creatinine and log cystatin C with age and sex rather than eGFR equations; it tests equation dependence, not measurement accuracy. A repeat-biomarker sensitivity is permitted only if repeat timing is verified and its selected population is reported separately.

## Estimands, baselines and method comparison

### Primary transparent baseline

The primary scientific estimand is the interaction coefficient (eta_{D	imes G}) in a cause-specific Cox model for first post-index recorded N17, with death censored at its recorded date:

`eGFRcr_z + D_z + grip_z + D_z*grip_z + age_z + sex + BMI_z`.

The prespecified direction is (eta_{D	imes G}>0): because both lower D and lower grip are negative changes, a positive product term represents greater joint vulnerability. Define a minimally important interaction as (exp(eta_{D	imes G})ge1.20) per one-SD D-by-grip product contrast. Report the coefficient, 95% interval, proportional-hazard diagnostics, risk-set support, influence diagnostics and robust eid-clustered uncertainty. A null or opposite estimate with a sufficiently narrow interval is adverse evidence; a wide interval is inconclusive, not supportive.

The model-based clinical-risk output must not be described as a competing-risk probability. Separately estimate five-year N17 cumulative incidence using the fitted N17 and death cause-specific hazards and the Aalen–Johansen construction, standardized over the eligible cohort at prespecified grid points (D = -1, 0, +1 SD; grip = -1, 0, +1 SD, with eGFRcr/age/sex/BMI held at the observed covariate distribution). Report risk differences and intervals. This gives a clinically interpretable risk contrast while retaining the Cox interaction as the primary estimand. Fit an analogous death cause-specific Cox model for the CIF calculation. Repeat the design for N18 as a prespecified secondary, labelled recorded-hospital-CKD outcome. Fine–Gray is optional secondary sensitivity only and cannot replace the cause-specific estimand.

For method comparison, use identical complete-case participants, covariates, index, outcomes, and train/validation/test split. Assign participants by `eid` using a frozen seed to 60% train, 20% validation and 20% test. Fit the transparent Cox baseline on train, with tuning-free frozen specification, and use its N17 and death cause-specific hazards to predict five-year N17 CIF on test. Do not fit a model on test data.

### Same-input nonlinear competing-risk alternative

Fit a bounded one-year discrete-time competing-risk hazard model using exactly `eGFRcr_z, D_z, grip_z, age_z, sex, BMI_z`. Expand each participant into intervals from index until the first N17, death or administrative end; each interval target is one of no event, N17, or death, with death retained as a competing event rather than being treated as censoring. Use two shallow gradient-boosted hazard heads or a multiclass model with mutually exclusive probabilities, maximum tree depth 2–3, fixed regularization and minimum leaf size; select the small number of boosting iterations only on validation. All transformations, any imputation, tuning and censoring/administrative-loss weights are learned inside training. Report memory-safe streaming extraction and the actual resource use.

This alternative is scientifically informative because the Cox interaction imposes a single log-linear D-by-grip surface. The nonlinear model can reveal a threshold, saturation, or localized non-additive region while preserving the same biological inputs and the same competing event process. It is not justified by complexity or small metric gains. On the untouched test set, report five-year N17 CIF, competing-risk Brier score/integrated Brier score, time-dependent AUC, and calibration of the CIF with participant-level bootstrap intervals. Evaluate death-risk calibration as a safety check. Compare both methods at the same D-by-grip grid and report whether any nonlinear region changes the clinically interpretable CIF contrast, not merely whether AUC improves.

Treat administrative loss before five years as censoring with an explicitly reported IPCW model fit in training; do not use post-index diagnoses or future biomarkers as predictors. If the endpoint audit shows inadequate paired events or risk-set support, do not fit the nonlinear model. A practical adequacy gate is at least 100 post-index N17 events and at least 20 events in each of the four D-by-grip sign quadrants for an inferential interaction; the nonlinear surface additionally requires at least 200 N17 events, at least 100 competing deaths and usable support across the prespecified grid. These are design gates, not claims about the data.

The alternative is budgeted for CPU execution up to 4 CPUs, 8 GiB RAM and two hours, using streaming intervals and a bounded participant bootstrap. GPU use is neither required nor a merit criterion. If the surface is unstable, poorly calibrated, unsupported, or only trivially more predictive, defer it and retain the Cox result as the only fitted scientific analysis.

## Selection, reverse causation and falsification

The complete-case biomarker/assessment cohort is selected and may differ from all UKB adults. Report a flow from each source, join losses, missingness, exclusions, and baseline distributions of included versus excluded participants using pre-outcome fields. Do not claim population representativeness. One-sided grip and repeat-biomarker analyses remain separately labelled sensitivity cohorts.

Run a two-year landmark sensitivity: exclude anyone with recorded N17/N18 or death on or before index plus two years, retain only those alive and event-free at that landmark, and restart follow-up at the landmark while retaining baseline exposures. This tests dependence on occult baseline illness and early reverse causation, at the cost of survivor selection. A one-year landmark may be reported only as a labelled additional sensitivity. Same-day-code/death and strict code/date sensitivities are required.

Before outcome fitting, verify a nonrenal hospital negative control from the same paired arrays (for example a frozen K35 appendicitis category) and its event support. If verified and supported, run the same D-by-grip interaction with the same censoring rules. A similar negative-control association weakens renal specificity but does not prove confounding. Permute D within sex-by-age strata using a frozen seed; the Cox interaction and nonlinear D-by-grip surface should disappear. Failure of permutation indicates pipeline leakage or model-fitting error, not confirmation. Also compare strict N18 exclusion with an N17-only baseline exclusion, and compare the eGFR and raw-biomarker specifications.

Supportive evidence requires all provenance and event gates to pass; the primary interaction to be positive with its 95% interval excluding (log(1.20)) in the prespecified direction; a clinically meaningful and reasonably precise N17 CIF contrast; persistence under strict date/code and two-year landmark sensitivities; no comparable supported negative-control interaction; and, if fitted, a calibrated stable nonlinear surface that changes a clinically interpretable risk region rather than only a small prediction metric. This supports a prognostic association in the selected UKB hospital-record population.

Adverse evidence includes a null/opposite interaction with a sufficiently narrow interval, no incremental signal after eGFRcr adjustment, dependence on early or same-day records, a comparable negative-control association, or a nonlinear surface that is unstable, poorly calibrated or only trivially better. Negative findings remain scientifically valuable. Inconclusive evidence includes failed unit/field verification, inability to verify 41270 code semantics, death-date meaning or administrative cutoff, too few events, inadequate quadrant/grid support, substantial code/date discordance, failed proportional-hazard or calibration diagnostics, or cancelled/incomplete audit execution. Inconclusive results support no clinical conclusion.
