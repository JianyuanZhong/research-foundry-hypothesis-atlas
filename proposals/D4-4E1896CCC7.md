## Unresolved question and substantive advance

The parent tests whether an earlier eight-field biomarker panel adds prospective recorded-risk information beyond a current panel and repeat spacing, and whether that increment transports across calendar eras. This proposal does not use the repeated panel, repeat spacing, age-at-repeat estimand, or early-to-late transport. It asks a different clinical question:

> Among adults with the same baseline BMI, do central body shape and physical reserve identify different prospective disease vulnerabilities—more recorded cardiovascular/renal events for greater waist circumference, and more recorded serious infection for lower grip measurements?

This matters because BMI is often the available obesity measure but can conceal body-shape and muscle-reserve differences that could change who receives cardiometabolic versus vulnerability-focused review. The proposed advance is not a new BMI predictor leaderboard. It is a pre-specified outcome-specific contrast: whether waist information and grip information have different incremental, calibrated value conditional on the same BMI, age, sex, calendar time, and baseline observation. A null result would be useful because it would argue against adding these measurements for this recorded-risk purpose.

The expert seed suggested visceral-fat imaging, BMI, waist, grip, cardiovascular/kidney outcomes, infection, and disability. The local catalog contains no raw imaging and no verified disability outcome field, so this is a bounded non-imaging adaptation: waist is tested as a field-level anthropometric proxy only if its dictionary meaning and units pass; disability is removed rather than inferred. The seed and its reference motivate the question but do not establish the hypothesis or local field semantics.

- the catalog specifies one-to-one horizontal `eid` joins and ordinary files with no archive members.

This does not yet establish that 21001 is BMI, 48 is waist circumference, 46/47 are comparable grip measures, what their units or valid ranges are, that a code prefix is a clinically valid endpoint, that a recorded code marks disease onset, or that every participant has complete five-year observation. Those are gates for the solver.

The primary hypothesis is falsifiable and conditional:

> After passing field-semantic, baseline-date, prior-record, outcome-code/date, and ascertainment gates, adding baseline waist and the two baseline strength fields to age/sex/BMI will improve calibrated five-year prediction of a recorded cardiovascular/renal composite, while adding strength will improve calibrated prediction of a recorded infection endpoint more than waist does, with the two increments remaining directionally distinct under BMI-standardized comparisons and selection/observation sensitivities.

This is a prognostic-information hypothesis, not a causal effect of measuring waist or grip, a treatment effect, proof of visceral adiposity, proof of muscle mass, or a recommendation for screening.

## Outcomes and estimands

After a field-level code dictionary and clinical-domain review, predeclare:

- CVD/renal composite: an ICD-domain mapping consisting of cardiovascular codes in I00-I99 and kidney/renal codes in the predeclared renal domain (for example, N00-N29 only if the dictionary and clinical review justify that boundary);
- infection: A00-B99 only if the source code domain is verified as a diagnosis domain appropriate for this question.

The solver must publish the final exact code list before fitting. It must not silently replace the code list with a model-selected list. Same-position code/date pairs are mandatory; codes and dates may not be independently sorted. The first qualifying code after T0 is the event date. If an event and death share a verified date, death precedence is predeclared for the three-category outcome.

For each endpoint separately, define a three-category five-year recorded outcome: endpoint before or on the five-year horizon, death before endpoint, or no recorded endpoint/death through the verified horizon. If a participant has a competing event and the clinical adjudication defines it differently, retain the original category and report the adjudicated sensitivity separately. Without verified individual observation end and complete ascertainment, the solver must not use incidence, disease-free, or survival language; it may only report recorded-category prediction and label the result inconclusive for the clinical hypothesis.

For endpoint k, let B be the baseline model with age, sex, baseline BMI field 21001-0.0, and baseline calendar year. Let W add 48-0.0, G add 46-0.0/47-0.0 (or the verified strength summary), and WG add both body-shape and strength inputs. The primary incremental contrasts are:
`Delta_W,k = BS(W_k) - BS(B_k)` and `Delta_G,k = BS(G_k) - BS(B_k)`, with negative values indicating lower Brier loss after adding the feature family. The directional hypothesis is `Delta_W,CVD/renal < Delta_G,CVD/renal` and `Delta_G,infection < Delta_W,infection`, with practical margins declared before fitting. Report `WG` to test whether the two signals are complementary rather than additive substitutes.

To make “same BMI” explicit, report standardized predictions for waist and strength contrasts at a common, prespecified BMI distribution (for example, the pooled eligible BMI empirical distribution), with age/sex/calendar-year held to the same standardization distribution. These are conditional prognostic contrasts, not causal effects and not a claim that the intervention of changing waist or grip would change risk. A model-based BMI-by-waist and BMI-by-strength interaction sensitivity is secondary and must be regularized and predeclared.

## Split, models, uncertainty, and analysis

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on `eid`, published with its algorithm and manifest. All preprocessing, fold-local imputation/scaling, feature construction, hyperparameter tuning, calibration, selection modeling, and threshold definitions are training-fold-only. No `eid` appears in more than one fold.

Primary transparent nested models are fit separately for the two endpoint-specific three-category outcomes:

- B: age, sex, baseline calendar year, and field 21001-0.0;
- W: B plus literal field 48-0.0;
- G: B plus literal fields 46-0.0 and 47-0.0 (or their verified summary, with the choice frozen before outcome inspection);
- WG: B plus all three additional body-shape/strength fields;
- WG-int: WG plus only predeclared BMI-by-waist and BMI-by-strength terms as an interaction sensitivity.

Use regularized additive multinomial probability models. Generate outer-fold held-out probabilities, then fit final frozen versions on all eligible participants and retain one held-out prediction row per eligible `eid`. Primary uncertainty is a paired participant bootstrap over the retained predictions, preserving endpoint, fold, and feature-family pairing; report fold variability and a stratified bootstrap sensitivity.

A supportive result requires the directional feature-family contrasts to be stable in calibrated probabilities, not merely discrimination. Report multiclass Brier and log loss, reliability/resolution decomposition, calibration intercept/slope, observed-versus-predicted endpoint/death probabilities, and fixed illustrative endpoint-risk threshold movement at 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10%, and 15%. These thresholds are descriptive and do not define a clinical policy.

### Substantive learned alternative

Fit a bounded gradient-boosted multinomial model using exactly the B, W, G, and WG input sets and the same participants, outcome categories, five-fold split, fold-local preprocessing, and held-out metrics. Use a small predeclared depth/learning-rate/regularization grid and training-fold-only stopping/calibration. The primary scientific comparison remains the transparent feature-family contrasts; the learned model is not chosen as a leaderboard replacement.

The additive baseline can show whether waist or strength contributes in an inspectable, BMI-conditional direction, but it averages away nonlinear BMI-by-waist and BMI-by-strength thresholds and body-shape/strength discordance. The learned alternative can reveal whether the clinically relevant pattern is concentrated in, for example, high-waist-at-moderate-BMI or low-strength-at-high-BMI regions, provided the pattern is stable across outer folds, calibrated, and reproduced in predeclared shape summaries. A small learned improvement without stable calibration or region-level replication is adverse/inconclusive, not a clinical discovery.

A mechanistic organ-score model is deferred: the local catalog does not verify organ-specific field identities, units, specimen times, assay quality, or a valid mapping from these anthropometric fields to visceral fat or organ reserve. Imaging and a transformer-style disease history are also deferred because raw imaging is unavailable and the catalog has no verified longitudinal temporal columns. The evidence required to revisit them is field-level dictionary confirmation plus raw imaging, validated assay/organ mappings, or a new dated clinical-history source.

## Selection, confounding, and falsification

Report the full flow: population anchor; valid age/sex/T0; date-window eligibility; valid BMI/waist/strength fields; endpoint-specific prior-record exclusion; five-year observation/ascertainment gate; and endpoint categories. Verify key uniqueness, join coverage, overlapping-field agreement, missingness, invalid codes, date ranges, and baseline values. Use only pre-T0 variables for any observation/measurement-selection model.

Because anthropometric and strength measurements may be selectively recorded, fit an outcome-blind measurement-completeness propensity using age, sex, baseline calendar year, and other pre-T0 fields only if their semantics are verified. Apply stabilized inverse-probability weights, truncated at prespecified early-independent quantiles, as a transport-to-measured-feature sensitivity; report positivity, maximum weight, standardized differences, and effective sample size. This is not a causal correction for health-seeking behavior or confounding.

Predeclare these falsifications:

- Permute endpoint labels within fold and measurement-completeness strata; feature-family increments and their outcome-specific contrast should collapse toward null.
- Permute waist and strength within BMI band, age band, sex, calendar year, missingness, and measurement-selection strata while preserving outcome labels and baseline BMI; any retained directional result is not credible.
- Deliberately mispair outcome codes and dates and require the integrity audit to reject the analysis before model fitting.
- Compare B, W, G, and WG to distinguish body-shape information from strength information and to prevent a combined-model gain from being attributed to either family alone.
- Run a pre-T0 negative-control endpoint and a post-T0 leakage scan over every predictor, imputation statistic, selection variable, and code/date field.
- Compare complete-case analysis with one prespecified fold-local missingness strategy; do not let the choice be outcome-driven.
- Compare raw waist/strength terms with standardized and, only after units are verified, BMI-residual representations; no visceral-fat or muscle-mass label is allowed.
- Check calibration, Brier reliability/resolution, risk-band stability, fold stability, and selection-weight overlap; a discrimination-only gain is not supportive.
- Repeat the outcome-domain analysis with the final clinician-reviewed code list frozen; changing domains after seeing results is a failed preregistration.

Supportive evidence requires all semantic, timing, prior-record, code/date, and ascertainment gates to pass; at least 100 events for each claimed primary endpoint, adequate measurement-selection overlap (minimum ESS 200 and at least 50% of the corresponding unweighted group), no integrity or leakage failure, calibrated uncertainty intervals that support the predeclared directional margins, and stability under the feature-family, weighting, missingness, and fold sensitivities. A learned model adds support only when its shape summaries replicate and calibration does not materially worsen.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed outcome-specific pattern, gains explained by BMI calibration rather than added resolution, disappearance under measurement-selection weighting, unstable or poorly calibrated learned regions, or a combined-model gain that cannot be assigned to the prespecified feature families. This weakens the case for this BMI-independent recorded-risk distinction but does not prove that body composition has no biological or clinical value.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed field semantics or units, insufficient endpoint events, ambiguous diagnosis domains or onset, incomplete observation/ascertainment, sparse BMI/feature support, nonpositive weights, unstable folds, code/date corruption, or materially different results under prespecified endpoint/missingness sensitivities. Inconclusive is not evidence against the hypothesis.

## Compute and interpretation limits

The discovery audit was CPU-only and used bounded metadata/header/first-row reads; no model was fitted. The future solver estimate is unverified planning: 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM, and at most 28,800 seconds, inherited from the parent planning envelope. No GPU is required; the hardware inventory confirms A100 capacity exists, but absence of an allocation is not evidence against feasibility. If the solver elects a GPU for the learned sensitivity, it must request an allocated device and use `cuda:0`; that is a resource choice, not a scientific requirement.

Computationally checkable claims are source integrity, field presence, joins, cohort flow, date arithmetic, code/date pairing, predictions, proper-score contrasts, calibration/resolution, standardization arithmetic, bootstrap intervals, overlap/ESS, and falsification behavior. Clinical adjudication or additional data are required for field identity/units, disease onset, diagnosis validity, complete ascertainment, biological interpretation as visceral fat or muscle reserve, clinical action thresholds/utilities, causal effects of measurement or intervention, treatment benefit, and external transportability.

A supportive result would motivate clinician review of endpoint definitions and a prospective validation of BMI-independent risk stratification. An adverse result would discourage adding waist/grip for this recorded-risk distinction while leaving other biological questions open. Neither result is a clinical recommendation or proof of benefit.
