# MIMIC-IV renal-discordance repair: feasibility and design note

Status: repair-branch design plus bounded source availability probe. No patient cohort was built, no outcome counts were computed, no model was fitted, and no clinical conclusion is claimed.

Parent: [prior hypothesis]. This branch preserves its non-causal clinical question, ICU-discharge landmark, and 48-hour prognostic estimand while correcting the mounted source spelling and binding several fields directly to the verified schemas.

## 1. Scientific opening and claim boundary

The clinical question is whether an observed fall in serum creatinine without adequately observed urine-output recovery immediately before ICU discharge identifies a short-horizon prognostic phenotype, and whether a repeated longitudinal pattern is distinguishable from a brief low-documentation episode. The estimand remains prognostic, not causal: it is a standardized observational risk contrast at the ICU-discharge landmark, not the effect of delaying discharge, changing diuretics, or restoring urine output.

The strongest evidence inspected supports narrower claims. [K1] describes AKI definition/staging and evaluation as an evidence-based clinical assessment with creatinine and urine information but does not test this landmark phenotype. [K2] reports in a small mechanically ventilated cohort with iohexol clearance that creatinine-based eGFR can overestimate measured kidney function near ICU discharge as muscle is lost; it does not show that MIMIC discordance is prognostic. [K3] reports that dynamic kinetic-eGFR changes can aid renal-risk stratification while additional biomarkers provide modest/inconsistent incremental benefit. None establishes whether an EHR-defined creatinine/urine discordance is reproducible after urine-documentation and discharge-selection checks.

The falsifiable claim is:

> Among adults surviving a first ICU stay of at least 48 hours, and primarily among stays with dense, valid urine documentation, creatinine improvement in [t0-24 hours,t0) without recovery of measured urine output is associated with higher first 48-hour post-ICU-discharge risk of ICU readmission or in-hospital death after adjustment for measured pre-t0 severity and discharge-selection proxies; if the same pattern recurs in earlier 6-hour bins, its held-out risk contrast is larger and more stable than for a final-bin-only episode.

This would advance the question by separating three alternatives: residual illness/discharge selection, urine ascertainment/charting density, and a persistent observed longitudinal pattern. It cannot establish true GFR, complete urine collection, CKD, renal reserve, fluid balance, tubular injury, a renal mechanism, causality, treatment benefit, or transportability.

## 3. Population, landmark, predictors, and outcomes

Select adults with patients.anchor_age >= 18 whose first ICU stay per subject_id has valid intime < outtime, los >= 2 days, and is linked to an admission. Set t0 = icustays.outtime. Retain only patients alive at t0: admissions.deathtime is null or later than t0; rows with an inconsistent death time are a readiness failure. Require t0 <= admissions.dischtime. The subject-level first-stay rule is frozen before feature construction.

The primary outcome is the first event in (t0,t0+48 hours] and before/equal to admissions.dischtime:

1. in-hospital death at admissions.deathtime; or
2. a later icustays.intime for the same subject_id,hadm_id with intime > t0.

Keep the component outcomes separate. Secondary outcomes are ICU readmission alone, death alone, a 7-day in-hospital composite, and time to the primary component. An hospital_expire_flag=1 row without a usable deathtime is not silently assigned an event time; report it as a data-quality exception and prespecify whether it is excluded from time-to-first analyses.

All predictors are strictly pre-t0. The primary feature window is [t0-24 hours,t0). Longitudinal bins are non-overlapping 6-hour bins from ICU intime through t0; a partial first bin is retained with its duration recorded. Cross-subject calendar alignment is not used because the catalog states that timestamps are subject-shifted.

Creatinine: use only labevents.itemid=50912, valuenum finite and positive, and a verified compatible valueuom (canonical mg/dL; incompatible or unitless rows are reported and excluded rather than converted silently). Require at least two observations at least six hours apart, with the final observation in the 24-hour window. Let C_peak be the maximum and C_last the final valid value in the window; define creatinine improvement as C_peak-C_last >= 0.3 mg/dL. Retain count, observation span, time since peak, and the continuous values.

Urine: use only outputevents.itemid 226559/226560/226561, value finite and nonnegative, valueuom=mL, and charttime in the relevant stay/window. Sum valid row values; do not treat absent rows as zero. Record total volume, valid-row count, source-type indicators, zero fraction, earliest/latest chart time, and an observed-span proxy. The span is documentation timing, not proof of continuous collection. The primary dense-observation cohort requires at least two valid rows in each of the four final 6-hour bins and at least one verified source type. The sensitivity ladder is: any valid row; at least two final-window rows; at least one row in each final 6-hour bin; and the strict two-rows-per-bin rule. Report the full eligible cohort separately with documentation variables. Use <400 mL/24h as the unweighted low-output threshold; no weight-indexed claim is made.

Define four descriptive final-window groups: improved/high-output, improved/low-output (the discordant group), not-improved/high-output, and not-improved/low-output. These groups are not diagnoses.

Verified pre-t0 severity/selection proxies are age, gender, admission type, ICU LOS, admission-to-t0 duration, first/last ICU care unit, transfer-event context, creatinine level/trajectory and observation intensity, urine documentation variables, MAP item 220052 with last and preceding 6-hour summaries, vasopressor exposure from the four resolved input items, and invasive ventilation from item 225792. Support exposure uses starttime,endtime,statusdescription; ventilation uses procedure starttime,endtime,statusdescription.

Lactate/platelet item IDs in chartevents and renal-replacement-therapy item IDs were not resolved by this bounded probe. They are not primary predictors. If a future readiness manifest resolves them, they may be added only in a declared sensitivity model using their actual table and item dictionary rows; otherwise they remain deferred. In particular, labevents/d_labitems must be used for lab items, not an assumed chartevents binding.

## 4. Baseline and substantive learned alternative

The scientific deliverable is newly fitted, held-out evidence for whether the discordance and its persistence add information beyond measured severity and ascertainment—not a completed prediction leaderboard.

### Transparent baseline B0/B1

B0 is penalized logistic regression for the primary 48-hour composite with exact verified inputs: age, gender, admission type, ICU LOS, admission-to-t0 duration, first/last care unit, C_peak, C_last, continuous creatinine change, creatinine observation count/span/time-since-peak, 24-hour urine volume and low-output indicator, urine row count/observed-span/source indicators/zero fraction, MAP summaries and measurement indicators, vasopressor/ventilation indicators and duration/last-active status, and all corresponding missingness indicators.

B1 adds the four-level final-window group while retaining the continuous component measurements. The B1-versus-B0 contrast tests whether the clinically stated discordance grouping contains information beyond its raw ingredients and measured observation intensity. Do not select a model based only on AUROC: report held-out standardized group risks, risk difference and risk ratio, Brier score, log loss, AUROC/AUPRC as descriptive discrimination, calibration slope/intercept, and calibration plots.

Use a fixed patient-level 70/15/15 train/tune/test split, created once before fitting and reused by both methods. Fit preprocessing on train only, select the logistic penalty on tune only, and do not inspect test outcomes for feature or threshold changes. Use 200 patient-level bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions for uncertainty; report the number of test patients and events and avoid treating an interval crossing zero as proof of no association.

### Masked longitudinal HMM alternative

The substantive alternative is a three-state masked HMM over the 6-hour bins from ICU admission to t0. It uses the same channels as B0: creatinine level/change, urine volume, urine row count/source indicators/zero fraction/observed-span, MAP, support indicators, and explicit observation masks. Missing urine bins are missing emissions, not zero urine. The model is fitted on training subjects only; regularization/emission variance settings are selected on tune, and the test set remains untouched.

The scientific outputs are posterior state probability per bin, occupancy, transition count and timing, time since transition, posterior probability that final-window discordance persists across at least two earlier bins, and held-out outcome risk conditional on these summaries. State labels are assigned after fitting by their observed emission patterns and are not called biological renal states.

The discriminating comparison is repeated discordance versus final-window-only discordance, matched as closely as feasible on final-window observed discordance and B0 risk. A charting-noise explanation predicts concentration in low-density bins, low posterior persistence, unstable state assignments, and attenuation after documentation/support adjustment. A persistent observed-physiology explanation predicts reproducible persistence and a held-out risk contrast after those checks. Neither establishes renal mechanism.

Do not fit or interpret the HMM if the test set has fewer than 200 patients or 30 primary events, if observation masks cannot be represented without imputation, or if convergence/state separation fails. A pre-specified inconclusive rule is median maximum posterior state probability below 0.60, any fitted state occupying less than 5% of training bins, or failure of label-matched state summaries to remain directionally stable in at least 80% of bootstrap refits. These are feasibility criteria, not evidence for either rival.

A deterministic filtration/fluid-balance model was considered and deferred: the bound data lack measured GFR, reliable body weight for this design, fluid composition, complete diuretic exposure, and a validated creatinine-generation parameter. A transformer/LSTM was also deferred because its extra capacity would not yield a more direct observable for the repeated-versus-transient question; this is a scientific selection decision, not a blanket rejection of neural networks or GPUs. The HMM is retained because persistence, transition timing, and observation masking are directly interpretable outputs.

Approximate future solver envelope: CPU 8 cores, 64 GB RAM, and up to 4 hours for streaming extraction, fitting, bootstrap prediction, and diagnostics; the campaign planning envelope allows up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, and 28,800 seconds. These are planning estimates, not measured full-study timings. The bounded dictionary probe used 2 CPUs/8 GB and completed in 0.55 seconds, but it did not scan patient rows. Logistic regression and a small HMM should run on CPU; no GPU is required. If extraction or HMM fitting exceeds the CPU/time envelope, retain the complete B0/B1 analysis and defer the HMM rather than change the scientific estimand.

## 5. Falsification, interpretation, and next decisions

Supportive evidence requires all of the following: the dense-cohort discordant contrast is directionally stable and calibrated in held-out data after measured severity/support adjustment; the full-cohort result does not depend entirely on absent urine rows; repeated discordance has reproducible posterior persistence and a held-out risk contrast beyond final-window discordance at comparable B0 risk; and the direction is not destroyed by the pre-specified coverage, support, ICU-care-unit, RRT-available, and pseudo-landmark checks. This supports an observable prognostic phenotype and justifies external validation or adjudicated urine completeness. It does not justify changing discharge practice or claiming a renal mechanism.

Adverse evidence is attenuation to the null or reversal after documentation-density adjustment, concentration in sparse urine or heavily supported stays, disappearance in the no-support restriction, a permutation contrast comparable to the observed contrast, or an HMM pattern dominated by isolated low-density transitions. This supports ascertainment, residual severity, or discharge-selection explanations and should stop this direction unless a better measurement source is obtained.

Inconclusive evidence includes missing archive members, hash/schema mismatch, absent or duplicate dictionary labels, incompatible units, invalid time order, unresolved outcome timestamps, fewer than 30 test events, wide imprecision, calibration failure, unstable HMM states, or a dense cohort too small to distinguish patterns. Report the exact failure and do not convert it to a favorable subgroup or a null conclusion.

Falsification/robustness outputs are frozen before outcome review: discordance permutation within ICU-LOS/admission/B0-risk strata; 12-hour versus 48-hour follow-up; continuous creatinine change; final-window documentation ladder; measurement-density and care-unit strata; no-support restriction; optional RRT exclusion only after item resolution; creatinine-only and urine-only models; discordance interaction; and a pseudo-landmark 24 hours before t0 using only data before that pseudo-landmark. The pseudo-landmark is a timing diagnostic, not a second primary analysis.

Computationally checkable claims are archive/member/schema/item availability, cohort construction, leakage-free split, model fitting, held-out metrics, bootstrap uncertainty, persistence summaries, and whether conclusions match those outputs. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for complete urine collection, true filtration/GFR, CKD/renal reserve, mechanism, causal interpretation, clinical utility, and external transportability. The next decision is continue to external/adjudicated validation only after supportive held-out results; revise data measurement if results track documentation; defer if feasibility is inconclusive; abandon the direction if the dense and support-restricted phenotype is absent and no better measurement source is available.
