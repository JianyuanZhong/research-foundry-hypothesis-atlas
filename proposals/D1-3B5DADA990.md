# Hospital transportability of early ICU mortality models that use laboratory testing process

Status: Lead working proposal; not published as a candidate. Parent: `[prior hypothesis]` (expert seed `[starting question]`). This is a bounded adaptation of the seed, not a reproduction of another paper.

## Scientific question and hypothesis

Early ICU mortality prediction can exploit two different kinds of information:

1. measured clinical state (the values of vital signs and laboratory tests); and
2. the care/documentation process (which tests were recorded, how often, and when).

The unresolved question is whether the second kind of information is less transportable across hospitals than the first. This matters because an apparently accurate risk score that depends on local ordering, assay-interface, or documentation habits can miscalibrate triage, escalation, transfer, staffing, or bed-allocation decisions when moved to a new ICU.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among adult initial ICU admissions alive at a six-hour landmark, adding early laboratory testing-process features to the same measured values produces a larger loss in discrimination and calibration when evaluated in hospitals withheld from model development than when evaluated in hospitals represented during development. The excess transport penalty will be present for both a prespecified logistic model and a temporal GRU model.

The primary estimand is the process-versus-values difference in hospital transport gap. For metric m, define:

- G_m(model) = M_m(internal) - M_m(external) for AUROC, where larger positive values mean a larger transport loss;
- G_m(model) = M_m(external) - M_m(internal) for Brier score, where larger positive values mean a larger transport loss; and
- Delta_m = G_m(values+process) - G_m(values-only).

The confirmatory direction is Delta_AUROC > 0; the corresponding Brier direction is also (>0). A practical, predeclared threshold is 0.02 absolute metric units: a confidence interval entirely above zero supports a directional effect, while one whose lower bound also exceeds 0.02 supports a clearly non-trivial transport penalty. The analysis must report estimates and uncertainty even when neither threshold is met.

This is not a causal claim that testing causes mortality, nor a claim that missingness has one mechanism. The test is whether process-augmented prediction transports less well.

## What is already supported versus what remains untested

Supported by the local evidence:

- The eICU snapshot contains patient-level ICU stays, hospital identifiers, ICU-relative offsets, longitudinal laboratory results, and longitudinal vital-sign tables.
- In the bounded audit documented in `diagnostic-lab-audit.json`, the proposed cohort produced 140,801 adult initial ICU stays with at least 360 minutes of ICU stay across 207 hospitals; 2,339,035 lab rows met the early-window and revision-time rule, and 117,368 stays had at least one such lab row.
- The audited early laboratory names include the proposed clinical channels and show heterogeneous panel coverage across hospitals. This establishes feasibility of the data dependency, not clinical validity or transportability.
- The parent seed proposes that testing frequency and missingness patterns may be hospital-dependent and that whole-hospital holdout, calibration, and net benefit should be assessed. It explicitly calls the hypothesis untested.
- Current public search results show that dynamic EHR prediction and transportable representations remain active topics, but they do not establish this specific eICU hospital-held-out process-versus-values result. The search response is retained as untrusted provenance (Europe PMC source IDs `[source checksum]` and `[source checksum]`).

Untested:

- Whether testing-process variables add predictive information in this cohort.
- Whether any apparent gain is larger in development-site validation than in hospital-held-out validation.
- Whether process dependence is large enough to matter for calibration or decision thresholds.
- Whether the result is robust to analyte definition, temporal representation, or revision-time handling.

## Exact measurements and process variables

### Measured-value inputs

Use the following read-only longitudinal sources, always joined on `patientunitstayid` and filtered by their clinical observation offset:

- `[internal dataset path]`; catalog table `lab` (`datasets/eicu/table-79bdb33275339b1a.json`). Use `labresultoffset`, `labresult`, `labname`, `labmeasurenamesystem`, `labmeasurenameinterface`, and `labresultrevisedoffset`.
- `[internal dataset path]`; catalog table `vitalPeriodic` (`datasets/eicu/table-a22c6d6981a32279.json`). Use `observationoffset`, `temperature`, `sao2`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, and `systemicmean`.
- `[internal dataset path]`; catalog table `vitalAperiodic` (`datasets/eicu/table-72ace5b89971196b.json`). Use `observationoffset` and `noninvasivemean`.

The source convention states that offsets are minutes relative to ICU admission and that `vitalPeriodic` contains five-minute summary observations rather than raw waveforms. No waveform claim is made.

The primary lab panel is fixed before model fitting, using case-insensitive matching of `labname` and the audited unit field `labmeasurenamesystem`. The intended canonical names and units are:

| creatinine | mg/dL |

| platelets x 1000 | K/mcL |
| WBC x 1000 | K/mcL |
| lactate | mmol/L |
| pH | blank or missing unit |
| paO2 | mm Hg |
| paCO2 | mm Hg |

A row is a usable numeric measurement only if `labresult` parses as finite numeric and its laboratory result is available by the landmark: `0 <= labresultoffset <= 360` and, when `labresultrevisedoffset` is non-empty and numeric, `labresultrevisedoffset <= 360`. If the revision offset is empty, the result offset is used. This prevents a result revised after the prediction time from entering the feature vector. The full audit found all 15 names among common early rows; the executable analysis must still emit a coverage table and stop with a prerequisite failure rather than silently substituting similarly named tests if any required name/unit combination is absent.

For each continuous clinical channel, construct first observed value, last observed value, arithmetic mean, minimum, and maximum over the early window. For vital channels, the value source is the corresponding column above. For the 15 lab channels, use the canonical unit rows only. Values are not winsorized based on the outcome; any physiologic range checks must be fixed before examining model performance and reported as exclusions.

Use patient-context covariates available before or at ICU admission from `patient`: `gender`, `age`, `ethnicity`, `admissionheight`, `admissionweight`, `hospitaladmitsource`, `unitadmitsource`, `unittype`, and `unitstaytype`. These are context/clinical inputs, not testing-process inputs. Missing categorical values are an explicit “unknown” level; continuous values use training-set medians with no missingness indicators in the values-only models.

### Testing-process inputs

For each of the same 15 canonical lab channels, derive only from eligible `lab` rows:

- count of qualifying results in [0,360];
- any-result indicator;
- minutes from 0 to first result;
- minutes from last result to 360;
- number of distinct result timestamps;
- total observed lab rows across the 15 channels;
- number of distinct canonical analytes observed.

These are process variables because they encode whether and how often a lab was recorded, not the lab value itself. Do not use hospital ID, `labid`, interface labels, or unselected lab names as predictors in the primary analysis. `labmeasurenamesystem` and `labmeasurenameinterface` are used for unit harmonization and audit, not as model features. A sensitivity analysis may compare global versus within-hospital rank normalization of process counts, but it is secondary and must not replace the primary estimand.

### Deliberately excluded fields

Do not use `apacheApsVar` (`datasets/eicu/table-67711a86e012835e.json`), `apachePredVar` (`datasets/eicu/table-b1f86cc4a8d9f2a2.json`), or `apachePatientResult` (`datasets/eicu/table-754bebf64d3d9909.json`) in the primary model. They have no observation timestamps for a defensible six-hour cutoff and contain day-1/summary variables, mortality labels, or post-window information. In particular, `actualicumortality`, `actualhospitalmortality`, `predictedicumortality`, `diedinhospital`, and discharge fields are outcomes or leakage-prone benchmarks, not predictors. They may be reported only as a separate historical benchmark after the primary results, with the timing limitation made explicit.

No `note`, `diagnosis`, treatment, medication, or narrative feature is needed for the primary question. The metadata states that public narrative note sections are removed and structured note fields are not assumed to be genuine narrative; therefore the proposal does not claim to use clinical text.

## Comparable alternatives and method selection

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted, hospital-cross-validated estimates of the process-versus-values transport gap, with predictions, calibration summaries, and falsification outputs. No paper model is being reproduced.

### Alternative A: prespecified static penalized logistic regression (primary baseline)

Inputs: the patient context variables, 5 summaries per early vital channel, and 5 summaries per early canonical lab channel. Process-augmented version adds the fixed process variables above. Values-only version has no explicit missingness indicators, counts, observation times, masks, or hospital variables.

Fitting: one `StandardScaler`/one-hot encoder and logistic regression with fixed L2 regularization (`C=1`, `liblinear` or an equivalent deterministic solver), fitted only on the outer-training patients. Numeric imputation medians and categorical levels are also fitted only on outer-training data. The predicted probability is the estimand-relevant output.

Evaluation: AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, calibration curve/ECE, and decision-curve net benefit at 5%, 10%, and 20% risk thresholds. Uncertainty is hospital-clustered as specified below.

Information retained: this baseline is transparent and tests whether coarse clinical summaries and explicit testing counts are enough. It loses ordering and trajectory shape: it cannot distinguish, for example, a lactate that rises from one that falls if the same summaries result, and it treats irregular timing coarsely.

### Alternative B: substantive temporal GRU (confirmatory learned alternative)

Inputs are the same channels, discretized into twelve 30-minute bins over [0,360]. For each bin and continuous channel, summarize eligible observations in that bin by the mean; if more than one is present, retain the final observation time within the bin as a separate time feature only in the process-augmented model. Values-only sequences are filled with outer-training medians, with no observation masks or counts. The process-augmented sequence adds, for each canonical lab channel, a binary observed-in-bin mask, within-bin result count, and time since the latest qualifying result (capped at 360 minutes); it also adds the fixed early-window process totals. Patient context is appended to the final hidden state.

Fit a one-layer GRU with hidden width 64, dropout 0.1, a single sigmoid output, Adam learning rate 1e-3, batch size 256, class-weighted binary cross-entropy, and a maximum of 30 epochs. Use a patient-grouped 10% validation split from outer-training hospitals for early stopping with patience 5; no test-hospital data affects stopping, scaling, imputation, or hyperparameters. Fix random seeds 20260913 through 20260917 for the five outer repetitions. The GRU is a scientific alternative because it can expose time ordering, recovery/worsening trajectories, irregularly timed testing, and interactions that static summaries discard—not because it is more complex.

Fit both input variants in the same outer folds and compare the within-family process-versus-values transport gap. The main conclusion is not “GRU wins”; it is whether adding process information changes transport performance. If the learned alternative fails to converge or cannot fit the approved budget, retain the logistic analysis and record the GRU as a deferred alternative only if the feasibility failure is computational rather than scientific. Do not silently replace it with a different scientific question.

Approximate budget: feature extraction is a one-pass sequential read of `patient`, `lab`, `vitalPeriodic`, and `vitalAperiodic`, expected to be dominated by the 0.5-GB compressed lab and 1.8-GB compressed vital file. Logistic fitting and five repeated hospital splits should fit within minutes to tens of minutes on one CPU with 4–8 GiB RAM if features are materialized incrementally. The GRU should fit within the 7,200-second science budget on CPU with bounded epochs and early stopping; a GPU is optional, not required. The approved numeric budget in `inputs.json` is 7,200 science seconds, concurrency 2, and no mandatory GPU. The analysis must declare outputs and checkpoints if submitted for execution.

Selection: use both alternatives before narrowing. The logistic model is the prespecified baseline because it gives an auditable, low-resource estimate of the exact transport estimand. The GRU is retained because temporal order and irregular observation timing are scientifically meaningful mechanisms by which process dependence could appear. A simple-only result would leave open whether coarse aggregation masked dynamic process dependence; a GRU-only result would leave open whether the finding is a capacity artifact. Neither complexity nor CPU/GPU choice is treated as evidence of clinical value.

Alternatives not chosen:

- Apache-derived models are not chosen for the primary experiment because their fields lack timestamps and include day-1/summary variables, violating the six-hour landmark.
- A process-only model is not the primary comparison because it would answer how predictive hospital practice is, not whether process information harms transport conditional on measured state. It is a planned descriptive ablation.
- Transformer/large sequence models are deferred because no evidence currently shows that their added capacity is needed to resolve this scientific uncertainty; revisit only if the GRU shows reproducible temporal residuals that cannot be represented by the fixed baseline and an explicit budget is approved.
- Causal claims about testing, or an instrumental-variable analysis using hospital preference, are not selected because the data contain no randomized testing policy, order intent, staffing, bed-capacity, or complete pre-ICU history.

Revisit evidence: reconsider a richer temporal model if the values-only versus process contrast is stable in logistic regression but inconsistent in the GRU, if residual plots show systematic within-window trajectory information not represented by the static summaries, and if a bounded pilot demonstrates adequate event support and convergence. Reconsider a process-only or causal extension only after order-level data, testing intent, lab turnaround, staffing, or a suitable external validation dataset becomes available.

## Hospital-held-out validation and uncertainty

First construct the cohort and publish a data-audit table internally, including counts by hospital, outcome prevalence, lab coverage, and missingness. Retain hospitals with at least 200 eligible stays and at least 10 ICU deaths. If fewer than 20 hospitals meet this rule, do not report a definitive hospital-transport conclusion; report the prerequisite failure and descriptive internal results.

Use five repetitions of five-fold hospital-grouped cross-validation. In each repetition, assign whole eligible hospitals to folds using a fixed seed and approximate balance of stay count and deaths; no stay from a test hospital enters fitting, imputation, scaling, early stopping, or threshold selection. Within each outer training set, reserve a patient-grouped 10% validation subset within training hospitals for GRU early stopping. For each outer fold, calculate:

- internal validation performance on a patient-held-out subset of the outer-training hospitals, using the same fitted model and preprocessing;
- external performance on all eligible stays in the held-out hospitals.

The internal subset is an optimistic development-site comparator by design; the external set is the transport target. Do not tune on the external set or locally recalibrate before reporting primary external calibration.

Report both pooled out-of-fold metrics and the unweighted mean of hospital-level metrics. Primary inference uses paired bootstrap resampling of eligible hospital IDs (2,000 replicates, resampling complete hospital prediction sets), preserving the process/value pairing. Report 95% percentile intervals for (Delta_{mathrm{AUROC}}) and Delta_Brier, the distribution of per-hospital gaps, and the number and size of contributing hospitals. If a hospital has only one outcome class, it contributes to Brier/calibration summaries but not hospital-level AUROC; pooled AUROC remains secondary and its denominator is explicit.

Calibration is assessed without post-hoc correction by calibration intercept, slope, Brier score, and a fixed-bin calibration curve. Decision curves use the predeclared 5%, 10%, and 20% thresholds; they are descriptive because the database does not supply the harms, benefits, or actions associated with a risk threshold. Report AUPRC because ICU mortality is imbalanced.

The primary test is the sign and uncertainty of (Delta_{mathrm{AUROC}}), with Brier/calibration as co-primary clinical interpretation rather than a claim that one metric settles usefulness. A process model may retain AUROC but lose calibration; that is a clinically important adverse finding even if discrimination is unchanged.

## Falsification and integrity checks

All falsification checks are run without changing the primary estimand:

1. **Outcome-permutation negative control.** Permute the ICU-death label within each hospital using a fixed seed, refit all models, and repeat the outer evaluation. AUROC should approach 0.5, AUPRC should approach the permuted prevalence, and no reproducible process-specific transport advantage should remain. A large signal indicates leakage, split failure, or implementation error.

2. **Revision-time leakage audit.** Re-run the feature builder with `labresultrevisedoffset` deliberately ignored and compare with the primary cutoff-safe features. Any materially different primary estimate flags potential delayed-result leakage; the permissive run is not a valid result.

3. **Within-hospital process permutation.** After feature extraction, permute each patient's process-feature vector among patients within the same hospital while leaving measured-value features and labels unchanged, refit, and evaluate. The process increment should collapse toward the values-only model if it truly depends on patient-linked testing behavior. Persistent unchanged performance suggests process features are redundant, mislabeled, or a hospital proxy.

4. **Value-preserving timing shuffle.** For the temporal analysis, shuffle eligible lab offsets within stay while preserving lab values and total counts. This should attenuate any claimed timing-dependent process effect; the static count comparison may remain. Failure to distinguish these patterns is evidence that the temporal interpretation is overstated.

5. **Split and leakage audit.** Verify no `hospitalid`, `patientunitstayid`, `uniquepid`, discharge field, APACHE outcome, or post-360-minute row enters features. Verify preprocessing objects are fitted within each outer fold. Verify all process counts are computed only from `labresultoffset <= 360` and revision offsets <=360.

6. **Hospital-random split contrast.** A patient-level random split with hospital overlap is a descriptive optimistic benchmark. If the large process increment appears only in the random split and disappears under hospital holdout, that supports the transportability concern; if both are similar, hospital-specific process drift is less evident, though absence of drift is not proved.

A result is not supportive merely because the process model has higher internal AUROC. Support requires a larger external transport gap for process than values, directionally reproducible across the logistic and GRU families, with uncertainty and the integrity checks passed.

## Interpretation of possible results

Supportive result: process-augmented models perform better or similarly internally but have a materially larger held-out-hospital AUROC drop and/or Brier/calibration deterioration, with Delta above zero and ideally above 0.02, in both model families. This supports the narrow conclusion that early lab documentation features are less transportable in this eICU snapshot. It would justify external calibration, process-feature auditing, and values-first model design before deployment. It would not prove which hospital workflow caused the drift or prove patient harm.

Adverse result: process features do not increase internal performance, or their held-out-hospital performance is no worse than values-only, or the values-only model is itself equally unstable. This falsifies or weakens the stated hypothesis for this cohort and feature definition. It may support the practical use of process features in this setting, but not generalize to another health system.

Inconclusive result: too few hospitals/deaths, unstable site-level metrics, wide intervals spanning meaningful effects, failure of canonical-unit coverage, or disagreement between logistic and GRU families. Report the estimate and failure reason; do not choose the model with the favorable point estimate. An inconclusive result motivates a larger multi-system validation or richer data, not a positive claim.

Stronger conclusions require unavailable evidence or another study: whether clinicians ordered tests because of perceived risk; whether missing rows represent no order, unrecorded order, interface failure, or delayed result; whether process-dependent models change treatment or outcomes; whether a recalibration policy improves decisions; and whether the finding transports outside eICU. These require order/cancellation logs, lab workflow and turnaround data, staffing/bed-capacity information, expert review of data provenance, prospective silent deployment, or an independent health system.

## Data limitations and provenance

The local eICU source guide identifies snapshot `[source checksum]`. Source rows are read-only. Archive member for every listed source is an ordinary file inside the named `.csv.gz`; no additional archive member is assumed.

The bounded diagnostic used the exact read-only `patient.csv.gz` and `lab.csv.gz` paths above and wrote `diagnostic-lab-audit.json` in the workspace. It filtered adult `unitstaytype='admit'`, `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitdischargestatus` in `Alive/Expired`, `unitdischargeoffset>=360`, then counted lab rows with `0<=labresultoffset<=360` and `labresultrevisedoffset<=360` when present. It did not fit a model or estimate clinical performance.

The source metadata states that calendar dates are removed, offsets are ICU-relative, public narrative note sections are removed, structured note fields are not assumed to be genuine narrative, and `vitalPeriodic` is a five-minute summary rather than a raw waveform. The study therefore cannot test calendar drift, narrative-documentation effects, raw-monitor waveform robustness, or causal testing policy. Lab-result data do not establish that an unobserved test was not ordered. These limitations are part of the hypothesis boundary, not reasons to invent unavailable evidence.
