## 1. Scientific question

**Hypothesis.** In ICU patients who develop AKI, a fall in serum creatinine
without a matching recovery of urine output ("discordant recovery") identifies
patients who remain at higher risk of (a) in-hospital death and (b) persistent
AKI at ICU discharge than patients with concordant recovery (creatinine fall
AND urine-output recovery).

**Why this matters.** Creatinine-based recovery is the default bedside signal
that "the kidneys have recovered." Haines et al. [K1] show that creatinine
systematically overestimates true GFR after prolonged critical illness because
muscle wasting lowers creatinine generation independent of filtration. If a
falling creatinine can reflect muscle loss rather than filtration, then
creatinine-only recovery is a potentially misleading marker, and urine output —
a direct readout of filtration — should carry independent prognostic
information. The unresolved question is whether, in a large real-world ICU
cohort, the creatinine/urine-output discordance at a fixed post-peak time
window predicts worse downstream outcomes after adjustment for severity.

**Leading explanation vs. rival.**
- Leading: discordant recovery reflects persistent low GFR (true kidney
  dysfunction) that creatinine masks → higher mortality/persistent AKI.
- Rival 1 (severity confounding): discordant patients are simply sicker
  (older, higher APACHE, more sepsis) and the association is fully explained
  by baseline severity.
- Rival 2 (measurement artifact): urine output recording is sparse/incomplete
  in MIMIC (nurse documentation burden), so "no UO recovery" partly means
  "no UO recorded," and the association is a documentation artifact.
- Rival 3 (muscle-wasting direction): creatinine falls because of muscle loss
  (Haines mechanism), so discordant patients are the *most* wasted, i.e.
  the association is mediated by catabolism rather than kidney function.

The design below is built to separate these: severity adjustment tests Rival 1;
a documentation-sensitivity analysis (require ≥N UO entries in window) tests
Rival 2; the persistent-AKI-at-discharge outcome (which is also
creatinine-based) partially cross-checks Rival 3 (if discordance were pure
muscle wasting, discharge creatinine should be *low*, not high — so a
discordant group with high discharge creatinine supports the kidney-dysfunction
reading).

**What answering would change.** If discordant recovery is an independent risk
marker, a simple bedside rule (creatinine trend + hourly UO in the 48–72h
post-peak window) could flag patients for closer renal monitoring, earlier
nephrology involvement, or more cautious nephrotoxin use — without any new
biomarker. If the association vanishes after severity adjustment or under
documentation sensitivity, the seed's premise is refuted and creatinine-based
recovery remains adequate.

## 3. Population

**Inclusion (primary, lab-defined AKI):**
1. Adult ICU stay: `icustays.los >= 2` (days; the 48h exclusion below is
   enforced on charttime, not los, because los is rounded).
2. ≥1 serum creatinine in `chartevents` (itemid ∈ {220615, 229761}) with
   non-null `valuenum`, charttime within [intime, outtime].
3. **Baseline creatinine** available: a creatinine value with charttime in
   [intime, intime + 48h] (in-stay early baseline). If no pre-ICU baseline is
   available in this admission, use the earliest in-stay value as baseline and
   flag `baseline_source = 'in_stay'` (sensitivity: restrict to
   `baseline_source='early_window'`).
4. **AKI event** (KDIGO-style, creatinine criterion only — UO is reserved for
   the recovery phenotype, so the AKI definition is creatinine-based to keep
   the phenotype orthogonal to the index event):
   - `sCr_peak >= baseline + 0.3 mg/dL` at some charttime within 48h of the
     baseline value, OR
   - `sCr_peak >= 1.5 × baseline` within 7 days of baseline.
   - `sCr_peak` = maximum valuenum in [baseline_time, baseline_time + 7d].
   - Require `baseline > 0.4 mg/dL` (physiologic floor; avoids 1.5× of a
     spurious 0.2).
5. **Peak must be classifiable:** `sCr_peak >= 1.5 mg/dL` OR KDIGO criterion
   met (this is the "simpler alternative" merged in: if baseline availability
   is poor — to be measured — the fallback AKI definition is simply
   `sCr_peak >= 1.5 mg/dL` with in-stay baseline = earliest value; the
   primary definition is preferred if ≥60% of AKI-eligible stays have an
   early-window baseline — **unverified**, to be measured in the solver's
   feasibility step).
6. **Recovery window must fit in the stay:** `outtime >= t0 + 72h` where t0 is
   the peak time (Section 4). Stays that end before the window closes are
   excluded from the primary analysis (they cannot be classified as
   concordant/discordant/non-recovery) and reported in a sensitivity analysis
   with last-available values.
7. **UO data in window:** ≥1 urine-output entry (itemid ∈ {226559, 226560,
   226561, 226567}) with charttime in [t0+24h, t0+72h] and value ≥ 0. Stays
   with no UO entries in the window go to a separate "no UO data" group and
   are **excluded from the primary 3-group analysis** (documentation gap);
   their inclusion/exclusion is tested in sensitivity S3.

**Exclusions:**
- `los < 48h` (charttime-based: outtime − intime < 48h).
- No creatinine (rule 2).
- No weight (itemid 226512) in chartevents for the stay → default weight 70 kg
  with a `weight_defaulted` flag (see Section 5, UO formula). Weight
  availability in AKI stays: **unverified** (probe pending).
- Pediatric (anchor_age < 18 at admission).
- Multiple stays: **one row per (subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id)**; a patient
  with multiple ICU stays contributes each stay independently, but the
  primary analysis is restricted to the **first ICU stay per subject_id**
  (by icustays.intime) to avoid within-patient correlation; sensitivity S4
  includes all stays.

**Expected N (feasibility, see Section 8):** stays with ≥1 creatinine and ≥1
UO: **unverified** ([research job] running). Prior: AKI
prevalence in ICU 20–30% of stays with creatinine (literature prior, not
measured here). If ~30k stays have both creatinine and UO, expect ~6–9k AKI
stays, of which the recovery-window + UO-in-window filters may leave
~2–5k classifiable stays. This is a **feasibility risk**: if the classifiable
N is < 1,500, the 3-group comparison is underpowered and the design should be
simplified (drop the 3rd group, widen the window).

## 4. Anchor time and windows

- **t0 = time of creatinine peak** (charttime of the maximum valuenum in
  [baseline_time, baseline_time + 7d]; ties broken by earliest charttime).
- **Recovery window:** [t0 + 24h, t0 + 72h] (48h window). Rationale: 24h after
  peak allows the nadir to form; 72h matches the "day 3" recovery assessment
  used in the renal-recovery literature [K2, K3].
- **UO window:** same [t0 + 24h, t0 + 72h], aligned with the creatinine window.
- **Follow-up for outcomes:** starts at **ICU discharge (icustays.outtime)**
  for the persistent-AKI outcome (last 24h of stay) and at **index admission**
  for in-hospital death (deathtime within the same hadm_id). Using ICU
  discharge as the start for persistent-AKI avoids immortal-time bias: the
  recovery phenotype is fully measured before outtime (rule 6), so no
  follow-up time is required to be classified.

## 5. Phenotype definitions (exact formulas)

Let `W = [t0+24h, t0+72h]`.

**Creatinine recovery (CR):**
- `sCr_end` = last non-null creatinine valuenum with charttime in W. If no
  value in W, use the last value before outtime (flag `cre_end_imputed`).
- `CR_pct = (sCr_peak − sCr_end) / sCr_peak × 100`.
- **Creatinine-recovered** if `CR_pct >= 25` (i.e., sCr_end ≤ 0.75 × peak).
  The 25% threshold is a design parameter (unverified against data);
  sensitivity S1 varies it over {20, 25, 30, 50} and over "return to
  baseline" (sCr_end ≤ baseline × 1.25).

**Urine-output recovery (UOR):**
- Aggregate all UO entries in W: total mL = Σ value (itemid ∈ {226559,
  226560, 226561, 226567}, value ≥ 0, charttime in W).
- `window_hours` = (number of distinct hours in W with ≥1 UO entry) — a
  documentation-coverage measure.
- `weight_kg` = median of chartevents itemid 226512 values in the stay
  (typically one admission weight); if absent, 70 kg (flag
  `weight_defaulted`).
- **Hourly UO rate** = total_mL / (window_hours) — i.e., mean hourly output
  over the hours actually documented, not over the full 48h (this avoids
  penalizing sparse documentation as anuria; see Rival 2).
- **UO-recovered** if hourly rate ≥ 0.5 mL/kg/h (KDIGO oliguria threshold).
  Sensitivity S2 varies the threshold over {0.3, 0.5, 0.7} mL/kg/h and
  requires `window_hours >= 12` (≥25% of the 48h window documented) in the
  primary analysis; the `window_hours` requirement is the documentation
  guard against Rival 2.

**Three groups (primary):**
1. **Concordant recovery:** creatinine-recovered AND UO-recovered.
2. **Discordant recovery (creatinine-only):** creatinine-recovered AND NOT
   UO-recovered. *(the hypothesis group)*
3. **Non-recovery:** NOT creatinine-recovered (regardless of UO).

Stays that are UO-recovered but NOT creatinine-recovered are assigned to
group 3 (non-recovery) in the primary analysis; they are reported separately
in the baseline table because they are the "reverse discordant" group of
scientific interest (UO recovered, creatinine lagging — the Haines
muscle-wasting direction would predict this group to have *better* outcomes,
a directional cross-check).

**Edge cases:**
- `sCr_end` missing entirely (no creatinine after t0): assign to non-recovery,
  flag `cre_end_missing`.
- All UO values = 0 in W: UO-recovered = false (anuria).
- `window_hours = 0` (no UO in W): excluded from primary (rule 7).

## 6. Outcomes

**Primary:** in-hospital death — `admissions.deathtime` non-null for the
index hadm_id (equivalently `hospital_expire_flag = 1`). Follow-up from
admission to death or discharge.

**Secondary:** persistent AKI at ICU discharge — creatinine in the last 24h
of the ICU stay (max valuenum with charttime in [outtime − 24h, outtime])
satisfies either:
- `sCr_last24h >= 1.5 × baseline`, OR
- `sCr_last24h >= baseline + 0.3` (i.e., still above the AKI threshold
  relative to baseline).
This is a creatinine-based definition by necessity (no measured GFR); it is
the same construct the hypothesis says is misleading, so it is a secondary
cross-check, not the primary endpoint.

**Tertiary (descriptive):** ICU length of stay (outtime − t0), hospital LOS
(dischtime − admittime), discharge to non-home location
(`discharge_location` ∉ {LIVING, HOME...} — exact category list to be
enumerated from the data in the solver step; **unverified**).

## 7. Analysis plan

**(a) Baseline comparison.** 3-group (4-row incl. reverse-discordant) table:
age, sex, APACHE II (first value in stay, itemid 226743), SOFA (itemid
227428), AKI stage (peak/baseline ratio), baseline sCr, peak sCr, peak time
(t0 − intime), weight, UO window_hours, ICD comorbidities (diabetes E10-E14,
heart failure I50, sepsis A41.9/R65.2*, chronic kidney disease N18) from
diagnoses_icd for the index hadm_id. Continuous: mean±SD + Kruskal-Wallis;
categorical: % + chi-square.

**(b) Primary model — unadjusted (baseline analysis).**
- Death: 3-group comparison of crude death rates; discordant vs concordant
  risk ratio with 95% CI (Wald).
- Persistent AKI: same, group rates.

**(c) Adjusted model.**
- Cox proportional hazards for in-hospital death, groups as a categorical
  covariate (reference = concordant), covariates: age, sex, APACHE II, AKI
  stage, baseline sCr, t0 − intime, weight. Check PH assumption (Schoenfeld
  residuals); if violated, use a logistic model with time-to-discharge as a
  covariate or a landmark analysis at 7 days.
- Logistic regression for persistent AKI at discharge, same covariates.
- Report HR/OR with 95% CI for discordant vs concordant and non-recovery vs
  concordant.

**(d) Baseline-vs-adjusted comparison.** Report both (b) and (c) side by side.
The gap between unadjusted and adjusted estimates quantifies severity
confounding (Rival 1). If the unadjusted HR is large but the adjusted HR
collapses toward 1.0, the hypothesis is refuted by confounding.

**(e) Sensitivity analyses:**
- S1: creatinine-recovery threshold {20, 25, 30, 50}% and "return to
  baseline."
- S2: UO threshold {0.3, 0.5, 0.7} mL/kg/h; drop the `window_hours >= 12`
  requirement.
- S3: include the "no UO data in window" stays as a 4th group (tests Rival 2
  — if the no-UO group looks like the discordant group, the association is a
  documentation artifact).
- S4: all ICU stays (not just first per patient).
- S5: restrict to AKI defined by ICD codes (N17*, 585*) as the index event
  instead of lab-based (tests definition robustness).
- S6: exclude stays with RRT (procedureevents / ICD 39.95, 54.98* —
  **unverified** itemids; to be enumerated in solver step). RRT confounds UO
  (anuria on dialysis) and is a major clinical confounder.
- S7: restrict to `baseline_source = 'early_window'` (pre-ICU or first 48h
  baseline only).

**(f) Multiplicity:** primary hypothesis is a single discordant-vs-concordant
comparison for the primary outcome; sensitivity analyses are exploratory and
reported without multiplicity correction, labeled as such.

## 8. Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is **refuted** if, in the primary analysis (first ICU stay,
lab-based AKI, 48h recovery window, 0.5 mL/kg/h UO threshold, ≥12h UO
documentation):
- **F1 (death):** adjusted HR for in-hospital death, discordant vs concordant,
  ≤ 1.0 with 95% CI upper bound < 1.2 (i.e., no plausible clinically
  meaningful excess risk), OR the unadjusted and adjusted HRs both ≤ 1.2.
- **F2 (persistent AKI):** adjusted OR for persistent AKI at discharge,
  discordant vs concordant, ≤ 1.0 with 95% CI upper bound < 1.5.
- **F3 (confounding):** the unadjusted HR/OR is ≥ 1.5 but the adjusted
  estimate crosses 1.0 (CI includes 1.0) → the raw association is explained
  by severity (Rival 1 confirmed, hypothesis refuted in its independent-risk
  form).
- **F4 (documentation):** in S3, the "no UO data" group has outcome rates
  intermediate between concordant and discordant, and the discordant group's
  advantage over concordant shrinks by ≥50% when the no-UO group is excluded
  → association is a documentation artifact (Rival 2).

**Supportive result:** adjusted HR for death ≥ 1.5 with 95% CI lower bound >
1.0, AND adjusted OR for persistent AKI ≥ 1.5, stable across S1–S3.

**Inconclusive:** adjusted HR between 1.0 and 1.5 with CI crossing 1.0, or
classifiable N < 1,500 (underpowered). Inconclusive does not refute; it
mandates a larger/longer-window design or deferral.

**What would require more data:** a causal claim (discordance *causes* worse
outcomes) is not supported by this observational design; a supportive result
establishes an association, not a mechanism. Distinguishing Rival 3
(muscle wasting) from the kidney-dysfunction reading would require cystatin C
or measured GFR, which are unavailable in this snapshot.

## 10. Method alternatives (baseline vs. learned/mechanistic)

**Baseline (simple):** unadjusted 3-group comparison of outcome rates (Section
7b). This is the minimum viable analysis: it answers "do the groups differ
crude?" with no modeling assumptions. It loses: all confounding information —
if discordant patients are systematically older/sicker, the crude difference
overstates the kidney-specific effect.

**Alternative (adjusted):** Cox/logistic regression with severity covariates
(Section 7c). This reveals **how much of the crude association is severity
confounding** (Rival 1) — the single most important scientific question
beyond the raw association. It also gives a continuous, comparable effect
estimate (HR/OR) rather than a rate difference.

**Why both are needed:** the scientific claim is that discordance is an
*independent* risk marker. The unadjusted analysis alone cannot support that
claim; the adjusted analysis alone cannot show the magnitude of the raw
signal (and a null adjusted result is uninterpretable without the unadjusted
baseline — it could be a true null or a confounding collapse). Reporting both
side-by-side is what makes the F3 falsification criterion operable.

**Not chosen:** machine-learning risk models (gradient boosting, neural
networks). Reason: the question is a 3-group prognostic comparison with a
pre-specified phenotype, not a prediction task. A black-box model would add
no mechanistic insight, would be hard to falsify against the F1–F4 criteria,
and the tabular N (expected 2–5k) does not justify the complexity. A Cox
model is the scientifically adequate method here; this is a deliberate
choice, not a capability limit. A future extension could fit a
creatinine-trajectory model (e.g., linear mixed model on the sCr series) to
test whether the *shape* of the creatinine fall (not just the endpoint)
carries additional information — deferred because it requires a longer
creatinine series and is a separate scientific question.

## 11. Key references

**[K1] Haines RW, Fowler AJ, Liang K, Pearse RM, Larsson AO, Puthucheary Z,
Prowle JR. Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of
Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness. Clin J Am Soc Nephrol.
2023;18(8):997-1005. doi:10.2215/CJN.0000000000000203. PMID 37256861.**
- *Supports:* creatinine systematically overestimates GFR after prolonged
  critical illness (2-fold vs iohexol in 27 patients); muscle wasting drives
  the creatinine fall. This is the mechanistic basis for expecting
  creatinine-only recovery to be misleading.
- *Limitation:* n=38, single center, mechanically ventilated, >72h expected
  LOS; does not test outcomes (mortality) and does not use urine output.
  Full text inspected (references/expert-seeds/papers/kidney-function/
  article.readable.txt, 46,967 bytes).

**[K2] Abdel-Nabey M, Ghrenassia E, Mariotte E, et al. Acute Kidney Injury
Recovery Patterns in Critically Ill Patients: Results of a Retrospective
Cohort Study. Crit Care Med. 2021;49(7):e683-e692.
doi:10.1097/CCM.0000000000005008. PMID 33826581.**
- *Supports:* renal recovery defined as normalization of **both** creatinine
  and urine-output criteria is a valid, studied construct; recovery patterns
  are associated with outcomes; competing risks matter. Provides the
  methodological precedent for a dual-criterion recovery phenotype.
- *Limitation:* n=350, single center, 5-month enrollment; does not test
  discordance as a prognostic marker per se (it classifies trajectories);
  abstract only inspected.

**[K3] Luo X, Yan P, Zhang N, et al. Early recovery status and outcomes after
sepsis-associated acute kidney injury in critically ill patients. Zhong Nan
Da Xue Xue Bao Yi Xue Ban. 2022;47(5):535-545.
doi:10.11817/j.issn.1672-7347.2022.210368. PMID 35753723.**
- *Supports:* early (day-7) recovery status after AKI is an independent
  predictor of 90-day mortality (unrecovered vs complete recovery HR 3.50,
  95% CI 2.47-4.96) after adjustment — the closest published evidence that
  a recovery-status phenotype carries independent prognostic information.
- *Limitation:* sepsis-associated AKI only (not all AKI); recovery defined by
  creatinine trajectory (complete/partial/unrecovered), **not** by
  creatinine-UO discordance; 90-day outcome (not in-hospital); two Chinese
  centers; abstract only inspected.

*Additional context (not a key reference):* KDIGO 2012 AKI definition
(creatinine + urine output criteria) is the standard for the AKI index event
and the 0.5 mL/kg/h oliguria threshold; the 2020 Taiwan AKI consensus (PMID
34446340) confirms the creatinine/UO criteria remain in use and flags renal
recovery as an open research question.
