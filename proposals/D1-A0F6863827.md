# Is the ICI-specific low-baseline HBsAg decline in HBV-related HCC a real treatment effect, or a product of treatment-dependent observation? (A pre-specified "generic therapy" hypothesis, falsified here by its own criterion)

**HCC island, episode 2. Operation: restart** (`choose_work` seq 2; the two assessed board candidates
are transition context, not scientific ancestry).

**One-sentence hypothesis (H1-new).** The low-baseline-concentrated HBsAg decline after checkpoint
blockade in HBV-HCC **is ICI-specific** — contrary to this file's own earlier hypothesis — and the
unresolved question is now whether that excess is a treatment effect or an artefact of the fact that
**who gets an HBsAg test, and for how long, depends on both the treatment and the patient's course**:
the excess must survive correction for dependent censoring and observation intensity, and the
measured size of the excess depends 3-fold on how that correction is done.

**Revision notice — READ THIS FIRST. This file has been revised twice against its own interest, and
the second revision falsified its own primary hypothesis.**

1. *First revision.* The original hypothesis claimed the signal was continuation of a **pre-existing
   decline trajectory**, measured by a self-controlled slope-break estimand. A pre-committed sham
   audit (§1.2h) showed that estimand reproduces its stratified pattern **in full with no index
   event at all**, so it was withdrawn. A second audit (§1.2i) showed the *threshold* endpoint is
   genuinely post-index — low-stratum patients had **not** already fallen ≥1 log10 in the preceding
   two years — so the question moved to "ICI-specific or generic to any tumour-directed therapy?"
2. *Second revision (this one).* A blinded reviewer objected that median HBsAg follow-up (80 d ICI,
   0 d LOCO) cannot support an 18-month endpoint, and that censoring would bias the contrast toward
   this proposal's own predicted null. **The objection was correct and I executed it (§1.2k).** ICI
   low-stratum 18-month coverage is 5.69 pp *lower* than LOCO (CI excludes 0) — the bias ran in
   favour of my own answer. Once coverage is matched, the low-stratum ICI-minus-comparator difference
   is **+16.15 pp (+7.44, +25.64)** vs LOCO and **+19.23 pp (+10.26, +28.21)** vs TKI (0/44 events,
   mirroring [K1]'s 0/85). Even unmatched it is **+5.07** and **+6.92 pp** with intervals excluding 0.
   **The pre-specified falsification criterion in §3.4 — "≥ +5 pp with a 95% interval excluding 0
   against both comparators" — is MET. The generic-therapy hypothesis is falsified in this cohort,
   and [K1]'s ICI-specific reading is supported more strongly than my own discovery probes
   suggested.** The short-window nulls I had been reading as evidence for a generic effect were
   censoring-diluted.

What survives is *not* the old hypothesis with a new sign. It is a sharper and more consequential
question, stated as H1-new above and in §1.4: the two defensible estimators of the same quantity
differ 3-fold (+5 pp vs +16 pp) because one is diluted by dependent censoring and the other
conditions on a post-treatment survival variable, and **neither is right**. The assumption-free
bounds are ±70 pp wide, i.e. useless. So the scientific object of this proposal is now the
**observation process itself**, which is also why the hierarchical model with an observation-intensity
and dropout submodel (§3.5-A) is no longer an optional alternative but the only estimator that can
answer the question.

## 1. The opening

### 1.1 The specific scientific opening

PD-1 blockade is being actively discussed as a route to HBV functional cure, and the strongest
clinical evidence in HBV-HCC is [K1] (Mon et al., *J Hepatol* 2025): 118 + 44 HBsAg-positive
ICI-treated patients versus 85 HBV-HCC non-ICI controls, 6.8% and 9.1% HBsAg seroclearance over a
median 17.5 months versus **no case** in controls within 24 months, with the effect concentrated at
baseline HBsAg < 100 IU/mL (HR 6.274; 24-month cumulative incidence 38.4% in that stratum)
(PUBLISHED, abstract inspected). The premise study in chronic hepatitis B without cancer [K2] is
n = 24 at sub-oncologic dosing, with a mean 12-week HBsAg change of −0.30 log10 IU/mL and its single
seroclearance accompanied by a grade 3 ALT flare (PUBLISHED, abstract inspected).

The opening is not "nobody has looked". It is that **the published stratified effect has rival
explanations that the published design cannot exclude, and those rivals are directly measurable.**
In [K1] the comparator is a single 85-patient non-ICI HBV-HCC cohort with **zero** HBsAg-loss events,
so a comparator gradient of the same shape would be invisible by construction; the comparator's
own therapy (which in an HBV-HCC cohort is essentially never "none") is not reported in the abstract;
and baseline HBsAg level in a long-treated chronic-HBV population is not a randomly assigned
covariate — it marks both trajectory position and antigen dynamic range, with most patients on
nucleos(t)ide analogues (16/17 responders in [K1] were on NA, 94.1%, inspected in the frozen
abstract). Two rivals follow, and this episode separated them by measurement rather than argument:
**R-generic** (any tumour-directed therapy produces the same decline) and **R-artefact** (the
stratified estimator produces the pattern without any treatment effect).

### 1.3 The strongest claim the available evidence supports — explicitly NOT the contribution

That checkpoint-inhibitor-treated HBsAg-positive patients sometimes show large HBsAg decline and
occasional seroclearance, that such events are concentrated among patients with low baseline
HBsAg [K1], that the concentration is genuinely post-index rather than a continuation of an
already-completed decline (§1.2i), and — added by §1.2k, in [K1]'s favour and against this file's
earlier hypothesis — that the low-baseline excess **is larger after checkpoint blockade than after
embolisation or a TKI** by every estimator I computed (+5.07 to +19.23 pp vs LOCO). This proposal
does not claim to discover any of that, and no longer disputes [K1]'s ICI-specific reading. What is
*not* established is the **magnitude** of that excess, or that it is a treatment effect at all
rather than a property of who continues to be tested, and the magnitude is exactly what a clinical
decision would turn on.

### 1.4 The unresolved claim the solver will test

> **H1-new (primary, after the §1.2k falsification).** The ICI-specific low-baseline HBsAg-decline
> excess is **a treatment effect that survives the observation process**: under a jointly modelled
> HBsAg trajectory, HBsAg-testing intensity and dropout process (§3.5-A), the 18-month low-stratum
> ICI-minus-comparator difference in the cause-specific incidence of (≥1 log10 decline OR sub-cutoff
> HBsAg) remains **≥ +5 pp with a 95% credible interval excluding 0 against both comparators**, and
> a tipping-point analysis shows that explaining it away requires a degree of informative censoring
> larger than is observed for any measured prognostic variable in this cohort. H1-new is **falsified**
> if the jointly modelled difference falls below +5 pp or its interval covers 0, i.e. if the entire
> ICI-specific signal — including [K1]'s — is attributable to differential retention and testing.
> MEASURED starting point, and the reason this is a genuine test: the two defensible naive estimators
> of the same quantity differ 3-fold (**+5.07 pp** raw, **+16.15 pp** coverage-matched), one biased
> down by differential censoring (C-R1: ICI coverage −5.69 pp) and one biased up by survivor
> conditioning, with assumption-free bounds 138.8 pp wide. The scientific content of H1-new is the
> *resolution of that 3-fold gap*, not the sign of the effect, which §1.2k already settled.

> **H1-a (the discarded hypothesis, retained as a recorded negative result).** "The effect is generic
> to any tumour-directed therapy" was this file's primary hypothesis and is **falsified in this
> cohort by its own pre-specified criterion** (§1.2k, §3.4). It is retained here, not deleted,
> because the *reason* it looked true — short-window nulls diluted by dependent censoring (overall
> ICI-vs-comparator contrasts null at 365 and 730 d, §1.2e) — is a trap any analysis of routine HBsAg
> data in this population will fall into, and because the comparator arms are not event-free: LOCO
> low-stratum 18-month incidence is 3.08% among coverage-matched patients, so "generic" is wrong but
> "ICI-only" is also wrong.

> **H1b (mechanism split).** Any residual ICI-attributable decline is **cytolytic**, i.e. it is
> concentrated in patients with an on-treatment ALT flare, rather than reflecting non-cytolytic
> immune control. MEASURED context: flare lowers HBsAg in every arm and most strongly in the
> non-ICI LOCO arm (−0.145 vs −0.029), and flare prevalence is flat across baseline strata
> (ICI 32–37%, LOCO 42–48%), so flare does not itself generate the gradient.

> **H1c (responder fraction).** The arm-level mean is the wrong summary if response is confined to a
> subgroup. The solver must estimate a mixture responder fraction π per arm with uncertainty, and
> test whether π_ICI > π_comparator even where the means coincide. A credible interval on π that
> includes the comparator value is an informative bounded null, not a failure.

> **H2 (scale validity, gating).** Whether the ordinal index-scale strata can carry a dose–response
> claim at all. MEASURED warning: pre-index within-patient dynamic range is ~3× larger in the lowest
> stratum (median swing 0.257 log10, p90 0.779) than in the higher strata (0.062–0.088), while ≥1 log
> *rises* are rare everywhere (ICI 2.0% low stratum, 0.0% elsewhere). The asymmetry argues against
> pure symmetric noise, but the level-dependent dispersion must be quantified before any stratified
> effect size is interpreted as biology.

> **H3 (the new question §1.2i opens — what produces the generic decline?).** If the post-index
> low-baseline decline occurs equally after embolisation and after a TKI, the leading explanation is
> **loss of HBsAg-producing hepatocyte mass or function** caused by the index therapy (ischaemic
> necrosis, drug injury, immune flare), not restored antiviral immunity. Prediction: the decline
> scales with on-treatment ALT peak and with a rise in bilirubin / fall in albumin, is largest in
> the embolisation arm, and is attenuated in flare-free patients. **Strongest rival H3′:**
> antiviral pressure from nucleos(t)ide analogues started around the index. MEASURED evidence
> *against* H3′ already: the post-index fall is largest in patients with **no** recorded NA
> (−0.1383, frac large 0.176), intermediate in peri-index starters (−0.0862) and smallest in
> established NA users (−0.0507) — the opposite of what NA-driven suppression predicts, though the
> NA-none group is severity- and documentation-selected and this ordering is confounded by that.
> A third rival, **H3″ measurement**, is H2. H3 is secondary to H1 in this study and is powered as
> a mechanism-generating analysis, not a confirmatory one; it is stated so that a null H1 is not the
> end of the inquiry.

### 1.5 Why the answer matters either way

The clinical decision this bears on is concrete and is being made now: whether a low-HBsAg HBV-HCC
patient starting a checkpoint inhibitor should be managed as a candidate for HBV functional cure —
monitored for seroclearance, considered for NA cessation, or enrolled in a functional-cure trial —
and that decision is sensitive to the *size* of the effect, not merely its sign. The two defensible
naive estimates here differ 3-fold. At **+16 pp** (≈19% 18-month incidence in low-HBsAg patients)
checkpoint blockade would be among the most effective functional-cure interventions ever reported in
this population and prospective testing is clearly warranted. At **+5 pp** it is a real but modest
effect competing with finite-duration NA cessation and with the risk of immune-mediated hepatitis.
If H1-new is **falsified** — the excess dissolves under joint modelling of dropout and testing —
then [K1]'s 38.4% 24-month incidence and 6.3-fold hazard are substantially a retention artefact,
which would be a consequential negative result for a programme now being designed around them,
reached in a cohort with ~20× the ICI exposure of [K1] and with two active comparators [K1] lacked.

Three further things hold whatever H1-new shows. (i) §1.2k demonstrates, with numbers, that in
routine HBsAg data the *naive* and the *coverage-matched* estimator of one estimand differ 3-fold
and the assumption-free bounds are 139 pp wide — a measurement result that applies to every
observational HBsAg endpoint study, including NA-cessation and therapeutic-vaccine cohorts, and that
is why this proposal's deliverable is an estimator validated against its own negative controls
rather than another point estimate. (ii) §1.2h names and quantifies a distinct bias affecting **any**

## 3. PLANNED design

### 3.1 Population, arms, temporal boundaries

Index = first dated administration/procedure of the arm-defining therapy, on or after **2020-01-01**
(assay-era guard, §1.2f) through snapshot end. Arms as in §1.2. Co-administered TKI/anti-VEGF is
permitted in Arm I and recorded (it is standard of care); an ICI-monotherapy stratum is pre-specified.

Inclusion: HBsAg-positive baseline (last value days −60…−1, index ≥10, clear of the cutoff region);
≥1 post-index HBsAg within 730 d. Exclusion: liver transplant before or within the window (HBIG and
graft change the generative model); interferon exposure; baseline HBsAg-negative.

**Primary horizon 18 months** (feasible: 446 ICI patients observed beyond 420 d), with 24 months as a
pre-specified secondary (294 ICI beyond 540 d) and censoring handled in the time-to-event model rather
than by requiring long follow-up for inclusion. This horizon choice is forced by [K1]'s median
16.5 months to HBsAg loss or decline: a 6-month analysis cannot speak to that endpoint.

**Baseline strata** are the pre-registered quintile-boundary cuts MEASURED in the treated arms
(1048 / 1784 / 2365 index units), frozen here so the solver cannot re-tune them, plus a finer
decile analysis in the lowest stratum where the action is.

**The arms are not exchangeable and the proposal does not pretend they are.** Arm I patients have more
advanced disease. Defences, all pre-specified: (i) each patient is their own control, so time-invariant
between-patient confounding cancels; (ii) **pre-index trajectory is a conditioning variable, not a
gate to be passed**, and — revised by §1.2h — it must be entered through the hierarchical model's
per-patient latent slope with a level-dependent error term, **never** as a two-point pre-slope sharing
the junction observation with the stratifier, which is now measured to generate the entire stratified
break pattern on its own; (iii) overlap
weighting on baseline ALBI components, platelets, INR, AFP, PIVKA-II, baseline HBsAg stratum, NA state,
age, sex and calendar half-year, with weight diagnostics reported; (iv) negative-control outcome
(creatinine) must show no arm effect; (v) NA state recorded — MEASURED as nearly constant across strata
(ICI 76.2/75.8/76.4/77.2%; LOCO 66.0/64.6/66.2/69.1%), so NA cannot generate the gradient, though NA
*duration* is not recoverable and remains a limitation.

**Temporal split:** derivation = index before 2023-01-01; held-out temporal validation = 2023-01-01
onward. Regimens, NA prescribing and imaging practice all drift; a random split would hide that. No
post-index information enters any covariate.

### 3.2 Estimands

**The primary estimand was changed by §1.2h/§1.2i and the reason is recorded here.** The earlier
draft's primary was the slope-break contrast τ_s. The sham audit showed that estimator reproduces its
own stratified pattern with no index event, so it cannot be primary. The threshold endpoint passed the
same class of audit (backward-null, sham-non-reproducing) and is additionally [K1]'s own endpoint, so
it becomes primary. This is a demotion of the proposal's more distinctive estimand in favour of its
more auditable one.

1. **Primary (H1-new), and §1.2k changed what "primary" has to mean.** The estimand is unchanged in
   target — 18-month cause-specific incidence of (≥1 log10 HBsAg decline OR sub-cutoff HBsAg), by
   arm × baseline stratum, as arm contrasts within stratum and as the arm × stratum interaction —
   but it is now **defined jointly with the observation process**, because §1.2k measured that the
   estimand is not identified without it: the same quantity is +5.07 pp under naive censoring and
   +16.15 pp under coverage matching, with assumption-free bounds of width 138.8 pp. The solver must
   therefore report **all four** of the following for the low stratum, as one table, with the naive
   figure first and no single number promoted to "the" answer:
   (1a) naive cause-specific incidence with administrative censoring at last HBsAg observation
   (MEASURED anchors: 18-month low-stratum +5.07 pp vs LOCO, +6.92 pp vs TKI; 730-d +4.27 / +5.45);
   (1b) the same, **inverse-probability-of-censoring weighted** on time-varying predictors of
   dropout available in these tables (most recent ALT, bilirubin, albumin, AFP, platelet count,
   admission/encounter recency and count, arm, baseline stratum, calendar period) — this is the
   estimator that attempts identification rather than bracketing;
   (1c) the **coverage-matched** contrast, explicitly labelled as conditioning on a post-index
   treatment-affected variable (MEASURED: +16.15 pp vs LOCO, +19.23 pp vs TKI; rates 19.23% / 3.08%
   / 0.00% on n = 78 / 130 / 44) — reported as an upper-leaning comparison, never as the primary;
   (1d) a **tipping-point sensitivity analysis**: the magnitude of informative censoring (as a
   hazard ratio linking unmeasured dropout propensity to the HBsAg endpoint) required to move the
   IPCW low-stratum ICI-minus-comparator difference to 0 and to +5 pp, compared against the
   dropout–outcome association actually observed for each *measured* prognostic variable. This
   replaces the Manski bounds, which §1.2k measured to be uninformative (±70 pp) and which the
   solver must report once, as a width, to document that fact rather than as a result.
   Draw-count-banded contrasts (MEASURED: +1.38 / +8.58 / +2.74 pp at 1 / 2 / 3–4 draws) are a
   mandatory co-primary diagnostic for detection bias, since ICI patients average 2.81 forward draws
   against 1.58 (LOCO) and 1.61 (TKI). Comparison with [K1]'s endpoint remains limited by the
   ordinal-versus-IU/mL scale (§4).
2. **Secondary (continuous, junction-free and mandatory as specified):** τ_s = [slope change]_ICI,s −
   [slope change]_comparator,s from the hierarchical model's per-patient latent slopes, where the
   stratifier must **not** be an observation used in the pre-index slope. The two-point junction-anchored
   version may be reported only alongside its sham-index calibration from §1.2h. The scientific object
   remains the interaction τ_low − τ_high.
3. **H1c:** per-arm mixture responder fraction π with a credible interval, and π_ICI − π_comparator.
4. **H1b / H3:** the endpoint hazard, the slope change and π re-estimated with on-treatment ALT flare
   (≥3× 40 U/L), peak ALT, and change in bilirubin and albumin as effect modifiers; the flare-free
   subgroup carries any non-cytolytic claim, and the association of HBsAg fall with hepatocyte-injury
   markers *within* each arm is the H3 test. Pre-specified H3 reading: if the fall tracks ALT/bilirubin
   equally in LOCO and ICI, the generic-hepatocyte-loss explanation is supported over immune control.
5. **H2:** level-dependent measurement model — within-patient short-interval dispersion as a function
   of HBsAg level, fitted on pre-index data only, used to (a) report the ICC and (b) deflate the
   stratified effect sizes. If dispersion alone reproduces the observed gradient magnitude, the
   stratified claim is declared measurement-driven.
6. **Sensitivity (inert by MEASUREMENT, retained for completeness):** the AFP/PIVKA-II-conditioned
   intercept estimand from the earlier design; with elasticity −0.031 and R² 0.004 it is expected to
   be uninformative, and a solver finding otherwise would overturn §1.2f.

### 3.4 Falsification criteria, fixed in advance

Thresholds are on the **primary** estimand (18-month cumulative incidence, low-baseline stratum)
unless stated. They are stated in percentage points so the solver cannot re-scale them.

**First, the record of what already happened to the previous version of these criteria.** The
criterion below labelled "H1-a falsified" was written before `censoring_audit.py` ran and **was
met**: the low-stratum ICI-minus-comparator difference reached ≥ +5 pp with intervals excluding 0
against both comparators under two different estimators. The generic-therapy hypothesis is therefore
already falsified in this cohort (§1.2k) and the solver is **not** being asked to re-test it. The
criteria that bind the solver are those for H1-new.

- **H1-a (generic therapy) — FALSIFIED, this episode, by its own criterion.** Pre-specified text:
  "falsified if the low-stratum ICI-minus-comparator difference is ≥ +5 pp with a 95% interval
  excluding 0 against both comparators". MEASURED: +5.07 pp (+1.23, +9.16) vs LOCO and +6.92 pp
  (+1.94, +11.51) vs TKI naively; +16.15 pp (+7.44, +25.64) and +19.23 pp (+10.26, +28.21)
  coverage-matched. The two conditions I had additionally required for a *confirmatory* ICI-specific
  claim — persistence in the flare-free subgroup and in the 2023+ holdout — have **not** been tested
  and remain solver deliverables, so the honest statement is: *generic-therapy falsified;
  ICI-specific effect supported in direction, magnitude unresolved by a factor of 3, mechanism and
  temporal robustness untested.*
- **H1-new supported (ICI effect survives the observation process):** the IPCW low-stratum
  ICI-minus-comparator difference (estimand 1b) is **≥ +5 pp with a 95% interval excluding 0 against
  both comparators**, the arm × stratum interaction interval excludes 0, the effect persists in the
  draw-count-banded analysis and in the 2023+ temporal holdout, and the tipping-point analysis
  (estimand 1d) shows that nullifying it requires informative censoring **stronger than the observed
  dropout–outcome association of every measured prognostic variable**. ⇒ A real, ICI-specific,
  low-baseline-concentrated effect of a magnitude that the solver has bounded rather than merely
  signed, in a cohort ~20× [K1]'s ICI exposure with two active comparators. This is the outcome that
  would justify a prospective functional-cure trial, and it should report the effect as an interval
  between the IPCW estimate and the coverage-matched estimate, not as a point.
- **H1-new falsified (retention artefact):** the IPCW difference falls **below +5 pp or its interval
  covers 0**, or the tipping-point analysis shows a degree of informative censoring *weaker* than
  that observed for measured prognostic variables suffices to nullify it, or the excess disappears
  within matched draw-count bands. ⇒ The ICI-specific signal here, and by extension [K1]'s 38.4% /
  6.3-fold result, is substantially produced by ICI patients being retained and tested while
  comparator patients are tested once and discharged. This is a consequential negative result and
  must be reported as prominently as the positive one; it would argue against designing functional-cure
  trials on the strength of retrospective HBsAg-decline series.
- **Cytolytic / generic-hepatocyte-loss reading (H3):** effect present but concentrated in flare
  patients, or absent in the flare-free subgroup, or tracking ALT/bilirubin equally across arms.
  ⇒ Hepatocyte loss, not restored non-cytolytic immune control; this is the reading [K2]'s single
  flare-accompanied seroclearance and [K3]'s 38.3% ALT>100 U/L rate make the default expectation,
  and it is the explanation the LOCO arm's −0.145 flare-associated fall already favours.
- **Measurement-driven reading (H2):** the level-dependent dispersion model reproduces the stratified
  gradient magnitude, or the sham-index calibration required in §3.6(1) reproduces the primary
  endpoint gradient (it did **not** in discovery: +2.40 pp, CI −3.75 to +8.56). ⇒ Report the
  stratified literature as scale-dependent; IU/mL recalibration becomes the required next evidence.
- **Secondary-estimand-only outcomes must not be promoted.** Because §1.2h showed the slope-break
  estimator is self-generating, any finding that appears **only** in τ_s and not in the primary
  endpoint must be reported as hypothesis-generating with its sham calibration attached.
- **Inconclusive:** the IPCW low-stratum difference has a 95% interval wider than ±5 pp (low-stratum
  denominators 399 / 515 / 180, of which only 78 / 130 / 44 are observed to 18 months, so this is a
  realistic outcome), or the IPCW and coverage-matched estimates remain separated by more than a
  factor of 2 with no measured variable explaining the gap, or derivation and 2023+ holdout estimates
  disagree in sign. ⇒ Report as underpowered *for the magnitude question*, while stating that the
  *direction* question was settled against the generic-therapy hypothesis in §1.2k. Do **not**
  convert a wide interval into either claim. An imprecise estimate neither refutes nor confirms [K1];

### 3.5 Methods: a simple baseline and a substantive alternative on the same question

**Baseline (B1, primary-estimand baseline): overlap-weighted stratified cause-specific
cumulative-incidence regression (Fine–Gray or Aalen–Johansen with weights) on the threshold
endpoint, now with inverse-probability-of-censoring weights and a tipping-point sensitivity
analysis.** Inputs: baseline HBsAg stratum, arm, overlap weights from §3.1(iii), censoring at last
HBsAg observation, plus the time-varying dropout predictors listed in §3.2(1b). Target: the 18-month
arm × stratum contrasts of §3.2(1a–1d). This baseline is **simple, computable on the full analysis
set** (ICI 2,055 / LOCO 2,533 / TKI 878 with a forward value) and is the estimand that passed the
artefact audits, so it is not a straw man — with IPCW and the tipping-point analysis it can answer
H1-new on its own, and §1.2k makes clear that it must be run first and reported first.
**§1.2k also exposed its limit precisely.** B1 treats censoring as explainable by measured
covariates. Its naive and coverage-matched variants of one estimand differ 3-fold (+5.07 vs
+16.15 pp) and the assumption-free bounds are 139 pp wide, so the entire answer rests on whether the
dropout model is right. B1 can weight for dropout but cannot use the information in a patient's
*observed trajectory shape* to inform why they stopped being observed; nor can it separate a small
effect in many patients from a large effect in few (H1c), or level-dependent measurement noise from
level-dependent biology (H2) — and §1.2h proved that the naive way of using trajectory shape is
actively misleading.

**Baseline (B2, the discredited one, retained as a calibration target only): stratified
self-controlled two-point DiD with bootstrap CIs.** Inputs: baseline HBsAg, one pre-index slope, one
post-index slope, arm, stratum. Target: τ_s. Already executed in reduced form this episode. **Its
measured defects are now decisive and of two kinds:** (i) it requires ≥2 pre and ≥2 post values,
retaining only 504/245/189 patients — 16% of the ICI analysis set and 4% of LOCO — while discarding
the 3,107/5,575/1,710 with a baseline and some follow-up; (ii) more seriously, §1.2h measured it to
reproduce its own stratified pattern on sham data (+0.0737 versus the real +0.0613), because it
treats each patient's slope as noiseless and shares the junction observation with the stratifier.
B2 is therefore kept **only** as the quantity the hierarchical model and its sham calibration must
explain, never as evidence.

**Selected alternative (A): hierarchical Bayesian longitudinal trajectory model with a two-component
mixture and an observation-intensity submodel.** Per-patient random pre-index level and slope, a
slope change at index, arm × stratum fixed effects on the slope change, a two-component mixture
(responder fraction π per arm) on the post-index slope, a **level-dependent observation-error
variance** (the H2 model, so measurement noise is not confused with biology), and a submodel for
testing intensity (sicker patients are measured more often; MEASURED post-ICI sampling density is
non-random). Inputs: all HBsAg values in days −1095 to +730 for every eligible patient, with the
covariates in §3.1(iii). Target: joint posterior over slope change, τ_s, π and the noise model.
Split: derivation pre-2023, temporal holdout 2023+. Evaluation: posterior predictive checks on held-out
trajectories, **simulation-based calibration**, coverage of τ, and comparison against B on the subset
where B is computable. **Budget, revised upward because §1.2k made the dropout submodel mandatory.**
The diagnostics above are MEASURED at <2 min on one core, but that does not bound the MCMC. The
longitudinal-only model was estimated at 2–6 h on 8–16 CPUs; adding a shared-parameter survival
submodel for the end of observation roughly doubles the per-iteration cost and worsens geometry
(the latent slopes now appear in two likelihood terms), so the realistic estimate is **6–16 h on
16 CPUs including SBC replicates**, against the 16-CPU / 28,800 s (8 h) solver envelope in
`solver_planning_limits`. **This estimate is UNVERIFIED and may not fit**, and the honest options are
stated here rather than resolved by shrinking the science: (i) run SBC on a reduced replicate count
or as a separate job, since calibration and estimation are separately schedulable; (ii) use GPU-backed
vectorised sampling, which is a legitimate accelerant for repeated fits of the same model — the
deployment has 8×A100-80GB and per `skills/phai-hardware/SKILL.md` requires an explicit
`gpus=1` allocation, so a bounded timing probe should decide this rather than an assumption;
(iii) if neither fits, report B1+IPCW+tipping-point (which comfortably fits) as primary and model A
on the pre-2023 derivation set only, stating the reduction. What must **not** happen is dropping the
dropout submodel to save time, because that is the scientific content of H1-new.

**A's observation submodel is promoted from a refinement to the scientific core, and must become a
shared-parameter dropout model.** §1.2k changed A's role. The intensity submodel was originally
there to stop sampling density from distorting the slope estimate. It must now also model
**informative dropout**: the time of the last HBsAg observation is itself an outcome, correlated
with the latent trajectory through the patient's clinical course. The required specification is a
**shared-parameter (joint) model** — the per-patient latent level/slope (and the mixture component
membership) enter a submodel for the hazard of the observation process ending, so that a patient who
stops being tested is informative about their latent antigen path rather than being silently treated
as missing-at-random. This is the only estimator in this proposal that can place the +5.07/+16.15 pp
gap on a single scale, because it uses both the retained and the lost patients under one explicit
and checkable assumption, rather than choosing between discarding the censored (1c) or assuming they
behave like the observed given covariates (1b). It also makes A **falsifiable on the point that
matters**: the fitted dependence between dropout and the latent trajectory is a parameter with a
posterior, so the solver can report whether the data actually support informative dropout or not.

**Why A is evidence-selected rather than preferred on sophistication.** Two measured failures, not
one. (i) §1.2h measured the exact failure mode A's measurement model avoids: a two-point slope
difference anchored on a noisy junction value manufactures the stratified pattern, and Test B showed
the cheap junction-free fix is **underpowered** here (ICI 170, TKI 52, LOCO 6 patients with ≥3 pre
values). (ii) §1.2k measured that the threshold estimand is **not identified** without an
observation model, with a 3-fold estimator gap and useless assumption-free bounds. A addresses both,
and B1+IPCW addresses the second only under a missing-at-random assumption that A can test.
A must additionally survive two pre-specified ways to fail: **if A reproduces a stratified
slope-change gradient on sham-indexed data, A is as biased as B2 and its continuous estimand is
dropped** (§3.6(1)); and **if A's threshold-endpoint contrast differs from B1+IPCW by more than its
own credible interval width without the shared-parameter dropout dependence being credibly non-zero,
A's extra structure is not earning its keep and B1+IPCW is reported as primary.** Those are
pre-specified ways for the expensive model to lose, not a promise that it works.

**What A reveals that B loses:** (i) the 2,600 ICI patients B2 discards, which is the difference between
an inconclusive and a decisive answer on the continuous estimand; (ii) separation of level-dependent
measurement noise from level-dependent biology — the crux of H2, and impossible in B1/B2, neither of
which has a noise model;
(iii) the responder fraction π, so a subgroup effect hidden inside a null mean is detectable;
(iv) honest uncertainty under informative observation times.

**Rejected alternative, with its revival condition.** A two-compartment (hepatocyte + tumour) HBsAg
generative model was developed and rejected on MEASURED grounds: at the measured tumour share it is
**not recoverable** (simulation correlation 0.042–0.335), whereas the reduced decline-rate model is
(correlation 0.86, bias 0.002). It is also unnecessary, since the tumour share is ≈0 (§1.2f). Revive
only if IU/mL-calibrated HBsAg, HBV RNA / HBcrAg, or tumour HBV-integration status becomes available —
none is present in this snapshot.

## 4. What this study cannot settle

- **It cannot establish HBsAg seroclearance to clinical standards.** A single sub-cutoff index value is
  not confirmed seroclearance (which needs a confirmatory repeat and anti-HBs); the rates reported here
  are **upper bounds**, so a small number is the stronger statement. Clinical adjudication is required.
- **It cannot match [K1]'s IU/mL strata.** The index scale is uncalibrated and has no unit column; the
  comparison with "<100 IU/mL" is ordinal only. A refutation of [K1] *as stated in IU/mL* is therefore
  not available from this cohort — only a refutation of the stratified claim on an ordinal low-antigen
  stratum. This is the single most important limit on the contribution.
- **It cannot prove absence of a non-cytolytic effect.** Without HBV RNA, HBcrAg, anti-HBs titres or
  intrahepatic sampling, "no measurable HBsAg trajectory change" is not "no immunological effect".
- **It cannot settle NA contribution.** NA *state* is recorded but duration/adherence are not, and
  [K1]'s responders were almost all on NA.
- **It cannot speak to survival or tumour outcomes.** No mortality linkage in this snapshot. This also
  means the arms differ in unobserved competing risk: ICI patients are more advanced, so informative
  censoring by death is possible in the *direction that lowers* observed ICI event rates, and the
  cumulative-incidence analysis must treat loss of follow-up as a competing event and report the
  sensitivity of H1 to it.
- **It cannot identify the mechanism of the generic post-index decline (H3).** ALT, bilirubin and
  albumin are injury *proxies*, [K3] shows tumour progression drives most ALT elevation in exactly
  this population, and no histology, HBV RNA or intrahepatic antigen staining exists here. H3 can
  rank explanations by consistency, not establish one.
- **The audits in §1.2h/§1.2i bound the estimator, not the biology.** A sham-index analysis shows an
  estimator can generate a pattern; it does not show that any particular observed pattern *is*
  spurious. That is why the audit's consequence is a change of estimand plus a mandatory calibration
  deliverable, not a claim that [K1]'s result is an artefact.
- **It is single-centre and Chinese-genotype-dominant** (B/C), so generalisation to other genotypes is
  unsupported; external replication is required.
- **Follow-up is shorter than the published event horizon.** Median follow-up is ~4.6 months and only
  294 ICI patients are observed beyond 540 d, against [K1]'s median 16.5 months to the endpoint. The
  730-d endpoint rates MEASURED here barely exceed the 365-d rates, which argues the null is not purely
  a follow-up artefact, but the 24-month contrast remains underpowered and must be reported as such.

## 5. Disposition of the three expert research demonstrations

- **Delphi (Nature 2025, learned disease sequences).** Not adopted. A bounded adaptation —
  sequence-learned disease-history ordering as a covariate — fails on a concrete missing dependency:
  the HCC `diagnoses` table has **no date column at all** (`诊断名称`, `诊断类型` only), removing the
  dated-trajectory structure the method learns from. Not a dismissal of sequence models; the
  longitudinal part of *this* proposal is treated as the latent-trajectory problem it is (§3.5).
- **ALADYNOULLI (Nature 2026, Bayesian longitudinal latent structure).** The closest of the three,
  and its influence is explicit: §3.5's hierarchical latent-trajectory model is the same family of
  idea. Its genetic arm is not reproducible here (no genetics in this snapshot). Adaptation, not
  reproduction.
- **Oncoformer (Cell 2026, multimodal EHR + imaging).** Not adopted. Its main text and STAR Methods
  are **unavailable** in the local bundle (publisher 403/401; only supplement and bibliographic
  metadata present) and have not been read. Independently, this snapshot has **no image files** —
  `examinations` holds narrative reports only — so the multimodal experiment is impossible here.

## 7. UNRESOLVED objections

1. **Ordinal-versus-IU/mL mismatch (the most serious).** If the index scale's lowest stratum does not
   correspond to [K1]'s <100 IU/mL, this study tests a related but not identical claim. Unresolvable
   without assay recalibration; stated as a limit, not minimised.
2. **Conditioning on pre-index slope changes the estimand.** It is a defensible self-controlled
   contrast, but it is not the same quantity [K1] estimated, and the comparison must be made carefully
   rather than asserted. An adjusted association is still not a mechanism.
3. **Paired pre+post n is small** (504/245/189). Model A is motivated precisely by this, but partial
   pooling borrows strength under assumptions that SBC can check only partially.
4. **Informative observation times.** The intensity submodel is a model, not a fix; patients measured
   because they are deteriorating may differ in unmodelled ways.
5. **Index-event confounding.** ICI start is not random in time relative to disease course; the
   pre-index slope conditioning helps but cannot remove it.
6. **Flare proxy.** ALT ≥3× 40 U/L is a crude cytolysis proxy; [K3] shows tumour progression, not
   immune hepatitis, drives most ALT elevation here, so the flare split is confounded by progression.
7. **No untreated reference group exists** (§1.2g), so treatment-independence is inferred from
   cross-arm concordance and pre-index trends rather than demonstrated against an untreated arm.
8. **RESOLVED AGAINST THE PROPOSAL, and recorded as such.** The objection "the positive trajectory
   break could itself be artefactual" was executed as the pre-specified sham-break analysis (§1.2h).
   It came back **confirming the objection** on the interaction the proposal called its scientific
   object. Consequence: the primary estimand moved to the threshold endpoint, finding (d) was demoted
   to a bias measurement, the interpretation of (c) was withdrawn, and a sham calibration of the
   replacement model became a mandatory solver deliverable that can kill the continuous estimand.
9. **RESOLVED AGAINST THE PROPOSAL — this objection came true.** The text here previously read: "the
   low-stratum ICI excess of +4.3 pp is unexplained and may be real … it is currently the single most
   likely way the headline hypothesis is wrong." `censoring_audit.py` (§1.2k) was run on exactly that
   question and the excess strengthened under every estimator that corrects for the measured
   differential censoring: +5.07 pp naive at 18 months, +16.15 pp coverage-matched, both intervals
   excluding 0, against both comparators. The generic-therapy hypothesis is falsified (§3.4) and the
   proposal's question was rewritten rather than the result softened. Objections 1–8 and 10–13 are
   the ones that remain open.
10. **Test C's U-shaped sham pattern is unexplained** (21.17% low, 18.77% high, ~8–10% in the middle,
    with 16.92% sub-cutoff at high sham baseline). It does not reproduce the real monotone gradient,
    so it does not disqualify the primary endpoint, but a large decline rate at *high* sham baseline
    in a heavily sampled subgroup is an observation this proposal cannot currently explain, and it may
    indicate an assay-dilution or censoring behaviour at the top of the index range. Flagged for the
    solver's H2 analysis rather than hidden.
11. **The sham tests share the cohort with the real analysis.** They are internal negative controls,
    not independent replication; a design bias that is uniform across index definitions would evade
    them. External replication in an independent HBV-HCC cohort remains required.
12. **Arm definition by first dated administration ignores treatment switching.** LOCO and TKI patients
    may receive ICI later and are censored only by the never-ICI exclusion at cohort entry; the
    proposal's never-ICI arms exclude *any* recorded ICI, which conversely conditions on the future.
    Both choices are defensible and neither is neutral; the solver must report the primary contrast
    under both the as-entered and the never-exposed definitions.
13. **THE CENTRAL OPEN OBJECTION, and it is now the proposal's subject rather than a caveat.**
    Neither available estimator of the primary quantity is valid. The naive estimate (+5.07 pp) is
    biased **down** by differential censoring that §1.2k measured directly (ICI low-stratum 18-month
    coverage −5.69 pp vs LOCO, CI excluding 0). The coverage-matched estimate (+16.15 pp) conditions
    on *being observed to 18 months*, a post-index variable affected by treatment and by the patient's
    course, so it is a collider and is biased **up** by an unknown amount. The assumption-free bounds
    that would arbitrate are ±70 pp and therefore vacuous. IPCW (1b) and the shared-parameter model
    (A) both attempt identification but **under assumptions this dataset cannot verify**: HBsAg
    testing stops here for reasons — death, hospice transfer, referral to a local hospital, loss to
    follow-up — that are *not recorded in this snapshot* (no mortality linkage, §4; identifier-only
    `transfers`). The tipping-point analysis (1d) is a bound on how bad the unmeasured dependence
    would have to be, not a correction for it. **Honest consequence: this study can establish the
    direction of the ICI-specific effect and bracket its magnitude between a down-biased and an
    up-biased estimator, but it cannot deliver an unbiased point estimate.** The evidence that would
    is a cohort with protocol-scheduled HBsAg sampling and complete vital-status ascertainment, or a
    mortality linkage for this cohort; both are named in §4 as unavailable here.
14. **A single audit, not replicated, is now carrying a hypothesis reversal.** §1.2k was executed once
    by one actor in one extraction. It falsified this file's primary hypothesis, so it deserves more
    scepticism than a confirmatory result would, not less. Solver deliverable 0b therefore requires
    independent re-derivation of the coverage asymmetry before any effect claim is built on it, and
    the Lead must be told if C-R1 does not replicate.
