# Provisional hypothesis: context-specific MerTK-inhibitor response in E-MTAB-8873

## Status

This is a provisional, planned hypothesis for episode 17. No effect estimate, model fit, or clinical conclusion has been computed in this draft. It is not evidence that UNC106238 acts directly on MerTK, that the response is causal, or that any therapeutic benefit transfers to patients.

## Proposed relationship

In the available E-MTAB-8873 rheumatoid arthritis macrophage–fibroblast intervention assay, the transcriptional response associated with the MerTK inhibitor will differ between MerTK/CD206-positive and MerTK/CD206-negative macrophage contexts. The primary estimand is a donor-aware, condition-verified context-by-inhibitor interaction, not a generic mean-expression change. A secondary descriptive question is whether the interaction separates a macrophage-linked resolving/repair-associated program from a fibroblast-linked inflammatory or potentially harmful program.

This would matter clinically because a context-specific response could justify targeted mechanistic follow-up and more selective therapeutic-target evaluation, whereas a generic response would redirect attention to treatment, donor, composition, or assay artifacts. The unresolved claim is whether the apparent interaction is specific to the stated cellular context rather than reflecting pseudobulk composition, reused or mixed donors, or an unbalanced generic treatment effect.

## Evidence and uncertainty

The strongest evidence currently available supports biological plausibility: MerTK-positive synovial macrophage subsets have been associated with remission and repair-related fibroblast responses, macrophage reprogramming can alter inflammatory mediator production, and macrophage–fibroblast repair circuits are context-, timing-, and niche-dependent. These observations do not establish this assay-specific interaction, direct target action, causality, protection, efficacy, or patient benefit. The literature and exact source passages still need to be re-inspected and frozen for final delivery; no paper claim is being asserted here beyond this biological-plausibility framing.

The unresolved evidence is the actual E-MTAB-8873 interaction estimate and its uncertainty under a prespecified donor-aware analysis. The dataset is small and targeted, with 33 design records and 445 measured features in the available prepared view; temporal labels and clinical outcomes are not available. Mixed or reused donor structure, condition verification, and feature-definition ambiguity can make the result inconclusive.

## Falsifiable test

Use the exact E-MTAB-8873 prepared counts and design files, joined by sample_id, retaining all 33 samples and the 445 targeted features. Define the primary contrast as inhibitor versus no-inhibitor within each verified macrophage context, and test the difference between those contrasts (context-by-inhibitor interaction). Fit a simple donor-aware baseline that models sample-level effects and fixed context/treatment terms, then compare it with a substantive interaction alternative that allows the treatment effect to vary by context. Use donor-aware resampling or a prespecified mixed-effects/clustered uncertainty procedure appropriate to repeated and mixed donors; do not treat pseudobulk samples as independent biological replicates.

Prespecify negative controls: a composition negative control and a within-context treatment negative control, plus checks that the result is not driven by one donor, one condition, or a small set of features. The interaction is supportive if it is directionally consistent, statistically distinguishable under the uncertainty procedure, and not reproduced by the negative controls. It is adverse if the interaction is absent or reversed, or if the negative controls show a comparable generic/compositional effect. It is inconclusive if condition labels or feature definitions are unresolved, uncertainty is too wide, or donor dependence prevents a stable estimate.

## Method comparison and deliverable

The simple baseline is a donor-aware fixed-effects contrast with a common treatment effect across contexts. The learned/mechanistic alternative is a prespecified interaction model, with a transparent feature-level decomposition only if it reveals a reproducible program rather than merely improving prediction. The alternative can reveal whether the treatment effect changes with context and which gene program carries that change; the baseline loses this information. Selection is based on whether the alternative resolves the scientific uncertainty, not on a small predictive gain. CPU is sufficient for this small targeted assay; no GPU is needed for discovery or the proposed solver fit.

The actual scientific deliverable is a newly fitted donor-aware interaction estimate with uncertainty, negative-control results, and a gene-level sensitivity analysis. Completion requires the fitted outputs, not a confirmation of the hypothesis. Any claim of direct MerTK action, mechanism, causality, clinical efficacy, or patient benefit requires additional perturbation, clinical adjudication, or another study.
