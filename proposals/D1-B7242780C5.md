# Does hypotension add renal signal after dynamic testing and competing ICU exit are separated?

## Scientific deliverable and unresolved claim

The new deliverable is a fitted, uncertainty-quantified decomposition of the early hypotension association into:

1. the probability of obtaining a qualifying creatinine result;
2. the probability that the obtained result is a rise; and
3. the probability of unit death or alive unit exit before that result can be observed.

The solver must emit a frozen source/value audit and cohort flow, an interval-level analysis table, transparent nested competing-state fits, a joint observation/outcome multi-state fit, standardized contrasts with uncertainty, whole-hospital transport results, and every prespecified falsification analysis. Discovery has not fitted these models and claims no clinical result.

The strongest claim supported before fitting is only that the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative MAP summaries, ICU-keyed laboratory result and revision times, disposition fields, infusion records, admission/severity descriptors, and hospital identifiers that can be joined by the stated keys. The source audit does not establish an association, a biological AKI event, or a treatment effect.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adult ICU stays with an observed early creatinine baseline and adequate early MAP observation, does hypotension duration/intensity add prognostic information for a later *observed creatinine-rise signal* at the same MAP nadir and baseline risk, and how much of that increment is explained by dynamic creatinine-testing opportunity or by unit death/alive discharge ending observation?

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: at fixed minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, early MAP coverage, and measured admission risk, a higher 0–6-hour area-under-65 burden has a clinically meaningful positive contrast in the 6–48-hour cumulative incidence of an observed creatinine rise. The contrast will attenuate by at least 50% when interval-specific creatinine observation opportunity and competing unit-death/alive-exit states are modelled jointly. If a >=2 percentage-point burden contrast remains stable in the observation-aware model, is not explained by testing density or boundary handling, and transports to held-out hospitals, duration contains signal beyond the nadir and the recorded testing process. If the contrast disappears, the scalar duration claim is falsified as a robust database-observable increment.

This is an observational prognostic and ascertainment hypothesis. A positive observation-aware association does not show that hypotension causes AKI, that more testing is clinically indicated, or that changing MAP or vasopressors would prevent injury. A large testing-process component is clinically useful because it warns against treating a recorded creatinine event as an unbiased renal endpoint and identifies where a future adjudicated study is needed.

## Population, time origin, and competing states

Use the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot `[source checksum]`. The primary unit is one ICU stay keyed by `patientunitstayid); repeated stays are retained as separate observations. All stays sharing `uniquepid` are assigned to one split, with a `uniquepid`-clustered sensitivity analysis.

Time zero is ICU admission. All times are integer minutes relative to admission. The exposure window is [0, 360] minutes, the landmark is 360 minutes, and follow-up is from 360 through 2,880 minutes (48 hours). The primary cohort requires:

- age >=18; parse patient `age="> 89"` as 90 and retain an age-censored indicator;
- at least two numeric, plausible `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` values in [0,360], with values restricted to 20–200 mmHg;
- at least one numeric creatinine result in [0,60] minutes whose verified measurement-system and interface units are mg/dL;
- a nonnegative, nonmissing `unitdischargeoffset`, so the ICU observation boundary is known;
- no qualifying early observed rise: the first qualifying baseline is not followed by a valid creatinine >= baseline + 0.3 mg/dL at an offset after the baseline and no later than 360 minutes.

The first qualifying creatinine at the smallest `labresultoffset` in [0,60] is the baseline, not the minimum. Exact duplicate `labid` rows are deduplicated. If multiple valid rows remain at the selected offset, take their median and report the count. The primary event uses result time, not revision time. The label/unit map is frozen before any split-specific fitting; case-folded `labname="creatinine"` with mg/dL in the verified unit fields is eligible, whereas urinary creatinine is not. Invalid numeric values and unknown/incompatible units are excluded, not converted.

For an eligible stay, let `end_i=min(2880, unitdischargeoffset_i)`. A result at an offset strictly after 360 and no later than `end_i` is observable only if it precedes the unit-exit time; a same-minute tie is assigned to the unit-exit state. Unit death is identified by `unitdischargestatus="Expired"` or `unitdischargelocation="Death"` at the unit boundary. All other valid unit discharge/transfer locations with an alive status are the alive-unit-exit/observation-loss state. The disposition fields identify a unit boundary and status; they are not a precise death timestamp.

At 30-minute risk intervals after the landmark, the mutually exclusive first terminal outcomes are:

- observed creatinine-rise signal: the first qualifying result at or above baseline + 0.3 mg/dL;
- unit death before that signal;
- alive unit discharge/transfer before that signal;
- no signal through 2,880 minutes while the stay remains within the observable unit boundary.

A qualifying creatinine test with no rise is not terminal: it updates the observed testing history and the patient remains at risk. The analysis also records, for every interval, whether a qualifying creatinine was observed and whether it crossed the rise threshold. Thus the observed event is explicitly a conjunction of observation and a rise, rather than a claim that an unmeasured rise did not occur.

The primary standardized estimands are evaluated at 48 hours:

- `CIF_obs(B3)-CIF_obs(B2)`: the observed-rise CIF contrast when adding the prespecified burden to the same trajectory and process history;
- `CIF_test(B3)-CIF_test(B2)`: the corresponding probability of receiving a qualifying creatinine before unit death/alive exit;
- `P_rise|test(B3)-P_rise|test(B2)`: the model-based conditional rise contrast among intervals with a qualifying test, reported as a measurement-process decomposition rather than a causal effect.

The burden contrast is standardized between the development-set 25th and 75th percentiles of area-under-65, holding minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, MAP coverage, and baseline covariate distribution fixed. The clinical-importance margin is 2 absolute percentage points for the 48-hour observed-rise CIF. A contrast smaller than this may be reported but cannot support the primary clinical-importance claim. The conditional tested-result quantity is not interpreted as a population causal effect because conditioning on testing can select on illness and clinician response.

## Feature construction and dynamic observation process

Partition [0,360] into twelve 30-minute bins. Each bin feature uses only source rows whose event time is at or before that bin endpoint. Retain both observed and bridged MAP coverage. The physiology vector is:

- bin median and minimum MAP;
- observed/bridged covered minutes and uncovered-gap indicators;
- cumulative area-under-65 and time below 65, reserved for burden terms;
- longest consecutive low-MAP run, fraction of observed coverage below 65, low-episode count, and recovery slope from nadir to last valid MAP;
- last-bin MAP and a binary recovery-to-65 state requiring a valid last-bin observation;
- named vasopressor presence/onset and number of infusion offsets, descriptive only.

The burden variable is area-under-65; total minutes below 65 is a secondary correlated burden representation, not simultaneously entered in the primary contrast. The trajectory vector excludes both cumulative area-under-65 and total minutes below 65 so the burden-versus-shape comparison is not algebraically circular. Minimum MAP is always retained as a conditioning variable.

The observation-process vector is separate from physiology:

- pre-landmark qualifying-creatinine count and time since first/baseline result;
- pre-landmark all-numeric-lab count and MAP/lab coverage;
- at each post-landmark interval, qualifying-creatinine observed/not observed, all-lab count, time since the last qualifying creatinine, and time since the last any-lab row;
- interval start state and exact time available before any event.

The observation model predicts whether a qualifying creatinine is recorded in the next interval from prior physiology, prior testing history, prior non-terminal creatinine values, static covariates, and current observation boundary. It never uses the result that defines that interval's rise. A result without a qualifying unit map is treated as unavailable in the primary analysis and is counted in the audit.

## Matched transparent baseline and substantive alternative

All models use the same eligible stays, 30-minute interval rows, event ordering, split assignment, covariates, and held-out hospitals. The model comparison is scientific: it asks whether a transparent additive representation and an explicit observation/outcome state model agree about duration, testing, and competing exits. It is not a contest for a small predictive gain.

**Transparent baseline and nested estimands.** Fit regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic models for the four terminal states at each 30-minute interval. Use the same full engineered input ledger, with prespecified main-effect terms, missingness indicators, and frozen scaling learned in development hospitals.

- T0 contains baseline creatinine; minimum MAP; MAP coverage; age, sex, ethnicity; admission source/type and unit type; early vasopressor presence; hospital context; and interval time.
- T1 adds the 0–6-hour burden (area-under-65).
- T2 adds the non-burden trajectory vector: bin-level MAP, persistence, recovery, low-episode count, coverage, and vasopressor context.
- T3 adds burden to T2 and adds the interval testing-process features.
- T4 is T3 with burden removed. The primary transparent process-aware burden increment is T3 versus T4; the process-free comparison T2 plus burden versus T2 is reported to show how much the process terms change it.

The baseline emits cause-specific coefficients, event-specific calibration, and standardized 48-hour CIFs for observed rise, unit death, and alive exit. The design matrix correlation/condition number for burden versus trajectory is reported before T3; a numerically unstable contrast is inconclusive, not rescued by arbitrary regularization.

**Joint mechanistic alternative.** Fit a piecewise-exponential multi-state model on the same 30-minute rows and exact same source-derived inputs. The states are landmark/no observed rise, qualifying creatinine observed without a rise, observed creatinine rise, unit death, and alive-unit observation loss. The transition system has:

- an observation-intensity transition for a qualifying creatinine test;
- a conditional rise transition when a test occurs;
- separate unit-death and alive-exit hazards;
- a no-test continuation transition that carries the last observed history forward.

The physiology component uses the same MAP bins, minimum MAP, burden, persistence/recovery, coverage, vasopressor presence, baseline creatinine, and static/admission fields as the transparent model. The observation component uses the same lab-count, inter-test, recency, prior-result, and interval-boundary features. Piecewise baseline hazards are fixed to the 30-minute intervals. Training-hospital intercepts may be stratified or hierarchical; an unseen hospital uses the training-population baseline and no estimated test-hospital effect.

The alternative reveals whether a patient with the same nadir and scalar burden but a single recoverable dip differs from one with persistent/repeated low MAP, and whether that difference operates through the chance of being tested, the chance of rising when tested, or competing unit exit. The transparent model's marginal CIF alone loses this decomposition. The multi-state model is selected because the scientific uncertainty is a sequence of observation and state transitions, not because it is more complex or neural. A causal interpretation is explicitly out of scope.

For both model families, use grouped inner hospital validation for regularization, then fit once in development hospitals and evaluate in held-out hospitals. Preprocessing, label/unit confirmation, missing-value handling, scaling, and any splines must be learned only in development data. The split is made by hospital before fitting; all stays of one `uniquepid` remain in one partition.

## Analysis, uncertainty, transport, and required outputs

The primary reported contrast is the standardized 48-hour observed-rise CIF difference between the development 25th and 75th burden profiles at fixed minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, MAP coverage, and baseline covariates. Report it from T3 versus T4 and from the joint model. Also report the testing CIF and the conditional-rise decomposition, each with the exact same standardization. Cause-specific and Fine–Gray/subdistribution sensitivity estimates may be reported, but they remain database-observable process quantities.

Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates for confidence intervals, with a `uniquepid`-clustered bootstrap sensitivity. For observation-weighted sensitivity, estimate interval observation probabilities in grouped development folds, truncate inverse probabilities only at a prespecified development 1st percentile, and report the fraction truncated. Cross-fitting and truncation are sensitivity tools, not evidence of missing-not-at-random validity.

Transport evaluation holds out whole hospitals. Report hospital count, eligible-stay/event count, overlap of `uniquepid`, per-hospital contrasts, pooled held-out contrasts, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC as secondary summaries, and prediction coverage. The transport claim requires a pooled held-out contrast >=2 percentage points with the same sign as development, and at least 70% of held-out hospitals with >=50 eligible stays having the same sign; otherwise transport is adverse or inconclusive according to event/support counts. Do not rank hospitals or interpret residual hospital differences as care quality.

The solver must emit:

1. catalog/source/header hashes, full creatinine label/unit map, disposition map, vasopressor-name map, and exclusion counts;
2. cohort-flow counts at every criterion, including missing unit boundary, early creatinine rise, MAP coverage, and no post-landmark test;
3. stay-level and 30-minute interval analysis tables with keys, source times, coverage, MAP features, testing history, competing state, and split;
4. T0–T4 coefficients, joint transition/observation parameters, fitted nuisance probabilities, and convergence diagnostics;
5. standardized 48-hour observed-rise, test-opportunity, conditional-rise, death, and alive-exit contrasts with confidence intervals;
6. calibration, Brier, secondary discrimination, whole-hospital transport, per-hospital stability, and overlap reports;
7. no-interpolation, MAP-source, lab-revision, unit-boundary, confirmed-rise, `uniquepid` clustering, Apache-severity, and vasopressor-definition sensitivities;
8. a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse, or inconclusive, with each claim linked to an emitted output and with observational database evidence separated from clinical/causal claims.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Support for a robust duration increment requires all of the following:

- T3 versus T4 and the joint model agree in direction;
- the standardized observed-rise CIF contrast is >=2 percentage points and its uncertainty does not make the estimate compatible with a clinically trivial contrast as the sole interpretation;
- at least 50% of the process-free increment is not removed by explicit testing-process/competing-state modelling;
- the sign and calibration are preserved in held-out hospitals;
- the contrast is not eliminated by no-interpolation, non-invasive-MAP, lab-revision, confirmed-rise, unit-boundary, and vasopressor-definition sensitivities.

Support for an observation-dependent explanation requires a >=50% attenuation of the process-free duration contrast in both model families or a consistent joint-model decomposition, measurable between-stay variation in testing opportunity, and no evidence that the result is driven solely by one boundary or label rule. This supports ascertainment dependence of the recorded endpoint, not a biological null.

Adverse findings include a null or reversed burden contrast, a contrast fully explained by minimum MAP or trajectory, instability after density/revision/unit-boundary sensitivity, poor held-out calibration, or process effects that reverse across hospitals. These reject the corresponding prognostic interpretation but do not prove hypotension is safe, that testing caused the association, or that a different treatment is beneficial.

Inconclusive findings include unstable label/unit or disposition maps; too few hospitals, eligible stays, tests, rises, deaths, or alive exits; near-universal or near-absent testing; weak trajectory variation; a condition number or nonconvergence problem; inability to fit observation probabilities without extreme truncation; or intervals spanning the 2-point margin. Inconclusive is not support.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes and headers, schema conformity, row counts, joins, cohort criteria, offset ordering, deduplication, feature availability, split integrity, event/state construction, fitted parameters, standardization, uncertainty calculations, calibration, transport summaries, and sensitivity reproducibility. Clinical adjudication is required to call the endpoint KDIGO AKI, establish pre-ICU renal reserve or urine-output criteria, determine why testing was ordered or absent, validate treatment intent and dose, distinguish interface failure from clinical non-testing, and infer a causal MAP or vasopressor effect. A prospective or richer retrospective study with adjudicated renal outcomes and treatment timing is required for a treatment decision.

## Alternatives retained and revisit rules

The transparent nested model is retained as the matched auditable baseline. The joint piecewise-exponential multi-state model is selected as the substantive alternative because it encodes the observation process and competing exit states explicitly while receiving the same source rows, times, outcomes, split, and engineered input ledger.

A GRU or temporal-convolution model using the twelve MAP bins and laboratory-process history is deferred. This is not a blanket neural or GPU prohibition: it is deferred because the current question is whether interpretable physiology, testing opportunity, and competing exits explain the signal. Revisit it only if held-out residuals show reproducible sequence structure after T3 and the joint model, and only if it preserves the same observed-rise/test/exit estimands, splits, calibration, and process accounting. The evidence to justify it is stable residual miscalibration or shape-specific contrasts, not a small discrimination gain.

A pure “under-monitoring causes deterioration” study is not selected. The exact lab fields can describe testing dependence, but they do not contain order indication, clinician intent, interface completeness, or a valid counterfactual monitoring policy. Promote it only after expert-verified observation semantics or an external adjudicated outcome.

A treatment-effect study of MAP targets, vasopressor dose, or liberation is deferred because the available infusion table has mixed units and no universal dose semantics, while treatment is time-varying and confounded by evolving illness. It requires adjudicated renal outcomes, treatment intent/timing, and a causal design.

No learned model or full clinical conclusion was fitted in discovery. The actual scientific completion is a new fit of T0–T4 and the joint observation/outcome state model, followed by the specified standardized contrasts, uncertainty, transport, falsification, and evidence-limited interpretation.
