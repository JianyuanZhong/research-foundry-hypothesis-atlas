# Observation-robust renal and documented-nutrition signal during recorded insulin infusion

Status: episode-7 evidence-bound child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and observation-process study, not a causal treatment, administration, feeding, AKI, or alert-benefit study.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe eICU person-period cohort, freeze the label/unit codebook before outcome analysis, quantify glucose ascertainment, and estimate whether recorded renal worsening plus a documented nutrition gap predicts a newly detected low glucose during a recorded insulin infusion. It must report cohort flow, paired-support and hospital counts, the primary transparent model, observation weights and positivity, uncertainty, falsifications, and a conditional temporal-model comparison.

## Unresolved clinical question

The strongest supported claim is only that this frozen eICU v2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative patient/stay, recorded infusion, laboratory, intake/output, nurse-charting, hospital, and EOL fields. Headers do not establish that insulin was administered, nutrition calories were delivered, a gap means feed cessation, a low glucose was symptomatic, or creatinine change is adjudicated AKI.

The falsifiable claim is:

> Among adults in their first ICU stay with a recent valid glucose and a numeric exact-label recorded Insulin (units/hr) update, recorded creatinine worsening combined with absence of qualifying documented nutrition delivery in the preceding 12 hours is associated with a higher probability of a newly observed glucose <70 mg/dL in the next six hours, beyond insulin intensity, prior glucose trajectory and glucose-testing history; the contrast should survive ascertainment adjustment and be directionally reproducible across hospitals if support permits.

This could identify a transportable trigger for prospective chart review or intensified glucose surveillance. It cannot authorize insulin dose changes, feed restart, or a monitoring interval.

## Analysis and method comparison

First model glucose observation in (t,t+360] using history through t: testing density/gaps, interface, severity, hospital-fold training data and discharge/EOL context available at t. Estimate detected-low risk unweighted and with stabilized inverse-probability-of-observation weights, reporting truncation and positivity. This does not recover unobserved hypoglycemia.

B0 is a prespecified transparent pooled-logistic cause-specific model with insulin rate/updates, prior glucose values and gaps, demographics, weight, admission context, early severity, vasopressor context, coverage, hospital effects and competing-event handling, excluding the renal/nutrition block. B1 adds continuous renal change, strict documented-gap status, coverage categories and their prespecified interaction. Report standardized joint-versus-neither six-hour risks, uncertainty, calibration and fixed-alert-burden value beyond B0. Use patient-clustered bootstrap and hospital-aware transport intervals.

The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over twelve one-hour bins before t, with the same variables plus explicit missingness/observation channels and static context; heads predict detected low glucose, glucose observation and competing discharge/EOL. Compare full, physiology-only and observation-only ablations on identical periods and whole-hospital approximately 60/20/20 development/validation/transport folds, keeping `uniquepid` together. It can reveal whether ordering and trajectories carry information lost by summaries, or whether apparent signal is documentation/testing artifact. It is deferred as inferentially primary because the audit found only 9 primary <70 events. A GPU is optional and conditional: one allocated A100 with `cuda:0` may be used only if the event/support gate passes; B0/B1 are CPU-first. Future planning envelope: at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds; measured audit: 4 CPUs, 16 GiB, 27.2 seconds. Model convergence and event-fold sufficiency remain unverified.

## Falsification and interpretation

Permute insulin times within stay while preserving rates; shuffle renal/nutrition histories within hospital; use a pre-index placebo glucose window; repeat strict prior-delivery, complete-observable, direct-unit and no-carry-forward definitions; stratify laboratory versus bedside glucose; analyze insulin-free analogous periods; audit clinical versus revision timestamps; leave one hospital out with and without observation channels; and use newly observed non-glucose laboratory testing as a negative-control outcome.

Support requires adequate support/event gate, stable observation weights, a prespecified clinically meaningful joint-versus-neither risk difference (target 2 percentage points), uncertainty excluding zero, incremental calibration/alert value over B0, concordance of the temporal profile with B1, no single-hospital dominance, and failed placebo/permutation/negative-control falsifications. This supports only a prognostic monitoring signal for prospective chart-reviewed evaluation.

Adverse evidence is a null below target, reversal, disappearance after weighting or strict labels, poor held-out calibration, one-hospital/interface confinement, negative-control similarity, or reproduction in placebo/insulin-free periods. It refutes the measurable marker in this snapshot or suggests surveillance artifact; it does not prove insulin or feeding interruption harmless.

Inconclusive evidence is exactly the current feasibility warning if no additional support is obtained: inadequate events, sparse renal-gap cells, ambiguous labels, unstable weights, unsupported transport folds, competing-event miscalibration, or temporal nonconvergence. It should lead to data-quality, clinical-adjudication or prospective work, not a forced clinical conclusion.

Medication administration, delivered calories/feed-stop records, rescue treatment, clinician intent, symptomatic/confirmed hypoglycemia, adjudicated AKI and alert benefit require additional data, expert review or prospective/emulated-intervention study.
