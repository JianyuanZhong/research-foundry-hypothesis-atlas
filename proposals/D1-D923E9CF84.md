# Multi-Organ Instability Dynamics: Rate-of-Change vs. Pattern

## Scientific Question

**Primary Hypothesis:** The rate of change in multi-organ instability (temporal momentum: accelerating vs. stabilizing vs. decreasing) independently predicts ICU outcomes (death within 7 days, readmission within 30 days), beyond what is explained by:
- Absolute organ dysfunction levels
- Simple pattern classification (persistent vs. fluctuating)
- Static severity scores (SOFA, APACHE)

**Mechanistic Rationale:** Organ dysfunction in critically ill patients is not merely a static snapshot but a dynamic process driven by the interplay between inflammatory activation, organ reserve depletion, and therapeutic response. The *rate* at which instability evolves reflects:
1. The aggressiveness of the underlying pathophysiological process
2. The responsiveness of homeostatic mechanisms
3. The efficacy of early interventions

This temporal momentum may capture information about:
- Immune dysregulation trajectories (cytokine storm vs. immunosuppression)
- Organ reserve exhaustion dynamics
- Treatment responsiveness heterogeneity

## Hypothesis Evolution from Parent

**Parent ([prior hypothesis]):** Focused on *pattern* (persistent vs. fluctuating organ dysfunction) as a predictor of outcomes.

**Current Proposal:** Shifts to *rate-of-change* as the primary predictor. This advances beyond pattern-based approaches because:
1. **Temporal Momentum vs. State:** Rate-of-change captures the *direction and speed* of change, which may be more informative than current state alone
2. **Early Warning:** Accelerating instability may signal impending deterioration even before absolute scores become extreme
3. **Treatment Guidance:** Decreasing rate may indicate therapeutic responsiveness, regardless of absolute severity

## Data Bindings (MIMIC-IV)

### Population Definition
- **Source:** `baidu_downloads/eicu_mimic/mimic数据库/mimic-iv-3.1.zip`
- **Tables:**
  - `hosp/patients` (table-9154f8c46cade9af.json): Patient demographics, unique identifiers
  - `hosp/admissions` (table-e8ec3e6e4c4285599.json): Admission/discharge timestamps, admission source
  - `icu/icustays` (table-7d5c8feb0fb0dbd4.json): ICU stay records with timestamps
  - `icu/chartevents` (table-8208609a785ea7e8.json): Time-stamped chart events (vital signs, labs, interventions)
  - `hosp/labevents` (table-bf701d962c63287c.json): Laboratory events with timestamps
  - `hosp/d_labitems` (table-57ae65f0eb6cf1a6.json): Laboratory item definitions

### Population Selection Criteria
1. Adult patients (age ≥ 18)
2. ICU admissions with documented SOFA scores (from chartevents or derived)
3. Minimum ICU stay ≥ 24 hours
4. At least 3 organ systems with measurable dysfunction markers (respiratory, cardiovascular, renal, hepatic, coagulation)
5. Follow-up window: death or discharge within 30 days of ICU admission

### Outcomes
- **Primary Outcome 1:** ICU death within 7 days of admission
  - Field: `icu.icustays.hadm_id` + `icu.icustays.discharge_status` (0=alive, 1=dead)
  - Time: `icu.icustays.admit_time` + 7 days
- **Primary Outcome 2:** ICU readmission within 30 days
  - Field: `hosp.emar` (emergency room admissions) linked by `hosp.emar.patient_id`
  - Time: `hosp.emar.admit_time` within 30 days of index ICU discharge

### Rate-of-Change Metrics

#### 1. Multi-Organ SOFA Trajectory
For each organ system (respiratory, cardiovascular, renal, hepatic, coagulation):
- Extract SOFA component scores from `icu/chartevents` or calculate from vital signs/labs
- Compute daily SOFA scores for each organ system
- Define trajectory categories:
  - **Accelerating:** SOFA increase ≥ 1 point/day for ≥ 2 consecutive days
  - **Stabilizing:** SOFA change between -0.5 and +0.5 points/day
  - **Decreasing:** SOFA decrease ≥ 1 point/day for ≥ 2 consecutive days

#### 2. Composite Multi-Organ Instability Index
- Daily composite score: average across 5 organ systems
- Rate-of-change: linear regression slope of composite SOFA over ICU stay
- Trajectory classification based on slope direction and magnitude

#### 3. Temporal Momentum Features
- Second derivative (acceleration) of SOFA trajectory
- Time to peak instability
- Rate of change at specific timepoints (e.g., day 1-3, day 4-7)

## Method Comparison

### Baseline: Cox Regression with Static Features
- **Features:** Initial SOFA score, age, comorbidities (Charlson), infection source
- **Model:** Cox proportional hazards regression
- **Outcome:** ICU death within 7 days

### Alternative 1: Pattern-Based Cox Regression
- **Features:** Pattern category (persistent vs. fluctuating) derived from SOFA trajectory
- **Model:** Cox proportional hazards regression
- **Outcome:** ICU death within 7 days

### Alternative 2: Rate-of-Change Cox Regression (Primary Test)
- **Features:** Trajectory category (accelerating vs. stabilizing vs. decreasing)
- **Model:** Cox proportional hazards regression
- **Outcome:** ICU death within 7 days

### Alternative 3: Trajectory Modeling with Machine Learning
- **Features:** Time-varying SOFA scores, rate-of-change features, interaction terms
- **Model:** XGBoost or gradient boosting
- **Outcome:** ICU death within 7 days and readmission within 30 days
- **Evaluation:** AUROC, C-index, calibration plots

### Alternative 4: Mechanistic Trajectory Model
- **Approach:** Dynamic systems model of organ dysfunction evolution
- **Features:** State-space representation of multi-organ interactions
- **Model:** Hidden Markov Model or Kalman filter-based trajectory estimation
- **Outcome:** Same as Alternative 2

## Falsification Criteria

| Result Type | Hazard Ratio (HR) Range | Interpretation |
|-------------|------------------------|----------------|
| **Supportive** | HR < 0.5 for accelerating trajectory (vs. decreasing) | Accelerating instability strongly predicts poor outcomes; rate-of-change is clinically actionable |
| **Adverse** | HR > 2.0 for stabilizing trajectory (vs. decreasing) | Stabilizing instability predicts worse outcomes than decreasing; contradicts clinical intuition |
| **Inconclusive** | 0.5 < HR < 2.0 for all trajectories | Rate-of-change does not add predictive value beyond static severity; pattern may be more important |

### Statistical Significance
- Two-sided α = 0.05
- Confidence intervals must exclude null value (HR = 1.0)

## Limitations and Unresolved Questions

### Data Limitations
1. **SOFA Score Availability:** Not all chart events contain explicit SOFA scores; some must be calculated from vital signs and labs
2. **Organ System Definition:** Need to clearly define which chart events map to each organ system
3. **Missing Data:** Potential gaps in longitudinal measurements affecting trajectory estimation

### Clinical Adjudication Required
1. **Trajectory Thresholds:** Optimal thresholds for "accelerating" vs. "decreasing" need clinical validation
2. **Time Window:** Optimal time window for trajectory assessment (early vs. late ICU stay)
3. **Confounding:** Need to adjust for treatment intensity and other time-varying confounders

### Missing Evidence
1. **External Validation:** Requires validation on independent datasets (e.g., EICU-CRD, other hospitals)
2. **Causal Inference:** Observational design cannot establish causality; RCTs needed for treatment implications
3. **Biological Mechanism:** Need molecular/immunological data to explain rate-of-change patterns
