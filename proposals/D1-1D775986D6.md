# Ascertainment-transport test of early hepatic reserve after first TACE

## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

After a first transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) for HCC, does the early change in liver-function tests identify risk of a subsequent hepatic-decompensation record, or does the apparent signal mainly reflect which patients were kept in local care and repeatedly tested?

The primary hypothesis is that, among adults with a first qualifying TACE and an observable pre/post assay trajectory, adverse change in albumin, platelets, bilirubin and/or INR predicts a new day-8-to-day-90 coded decompensation encounter beyond pre-TACE state, prior history, and the care process. The incremental association and proper-score gain must persist after inverse-probability adjustment for post-window assay ascertainment and 90-day local-record follow-up, and must not disappear when the endpoint is restricted to prespecified severe or repeat-confirmed terms.

This is a prognostic-information hypothesis for a local coded endpoint. It is not a causal estimate of TACE toxicity, a claim of adjudicated liver failure, a treatment threshold, or evidence that monitoring improves outcomes.

## Established evidence, advance, and limits

The local source establishes dated TACE/procedure records, encounter dates, repeated numeric laboratory rows, diagnosis labels linked to encounters, and treatment-context fields. A prior acquired TACE study supports the plausibility of albumin/INR as reserve markers, but does not establish incremental value of an irregular early trajectory in this dataset or validate its lexical diagnosis endpoint. The strongest claim this experiment can support is conditional: under stated observation and record-follow-up assumptions, the trajectory adds prognostic information to a current-state/care-process model in this HCC source.

The substantive advance over the episode-5 parent is a target-population ascertainment analysis. The parent’s complete pre/post trajectory cohort is clinically interpretable but selected by having tests after TACE. This child first constructs the all-index TACE cohort and quantifies selection into the observed landmark, then estimates the trajectory claim in the observed landmark and transports it by stabilized inverse-ascertainment/follow-up weights to the all-index target under explicit positivity and missing-at-random assumptions. A missing trajectory is never silently imputed as normal. Endpoint-specificity tiers remain a test of whether a broad lexical signal is documentation intensity.

No available table supplies validated death capture, imaging, tumor stage/response, medication administration, or adjudicated liver failure. Encounters and diagnoses cannot prove that a patient remained alive outside this local record. Thus the experiment cannot establish mortality, true decompensation, treatment effect, clinical utility, or external validity. Manual blinded chart adjudication, linkage, and an external/prospective cohort would be required for those claims.

## Baseline, alternative, and method selection

B0 is rare-event penalized logistic regression using pre-window assay medians/missingness and counts, age, sex, prior encounter count, prior decompensation history, diagnosis-type stratum, and pre-index medication/treatment context. B1 adds post-window assay availability, count and median timing but no biological change; it is the primary care-process comparator.

A1 is the substantive learned/mechanistic alternative: an anchored one-factor assay-specific random-effects reserve model fitted to irregular assay observations at their actual `test times`. Robust standardization is training-fold only; albumin loading is +1, bilirubin/INR adverse loadings are constrained positive, and platelet loading is constrained opposite. Estimate patient pre-reserve, post-change/slope, discordance and posterior/empirical-Bayes uncertainty, then add these to B1. Fit A1 twice: observed-landmark and ascertainment/follow-up weighted. A signed training-fold robust change score is a transparent A2 sensitivity comparator, not the primary method.

This alternative retains measurement error, irregular timing, assay-specific scale and discordant recovery that B1 loses. A black-box sequence/image model is deferred because no local images or validated stage/response extraction exist and the endpoint is sparse; this is a question-specific dependency/identifiability decision, not a blanket neural-network or GPU ban. A1 should fit within the approved CPU budget using aggregated assay rows and deterministic patient folds; no GPU is required.

Use deterministic patient-level 60/20/20 train/tune/test partitions within buckets 0-79. Fit transforms, models, observation/follow-up weights, truncation thresholds and any regularization only on train/tune. Aggregate every one-to-many table before joins. No post-day-7 diagnosis, follow-up anchor, day-90 row, or future treatment enters a feature or observation model. Direct identifiers are keys only.

## Analysis, uncertainty, and falsification

Report adverse-IQR reserve-change odds ratio, paired test log-loss and Brier changes versus B1, calibration intercept/slope, reliability, descriptive AUROC/AUPRC, and exploratory decision curves at 5%, 10%, and 20%. Use 200 patient-level bootstrap replicates for intervals; if event or effective sample sizes are too small, report descriptive/interval outputs and no confirmatory interpretation. Report weight distributions, truncation, positivity and effective sample size separately for observation and follow-up.

Falsification checks are: B1/process-only performance; removal of post-window process variables; within-fold permutation of post-window assay values across patients while preserving assay/window/count; observed versus all-index transport weights; complete-four-assay versus any-primary-assay eligibility; unweighted versus weighted fits; unknown-follow-up pattern-mixture event-odds multipliers; diagnosis-type and admission-versus-discharge proxy; severe/repeat/care-concordant tiers; and simulation of irregular observations using the observed assay/window pattern to test A1 sign, scale and parameter recovery.

A supportive result requires at least 30 broad events overall and 8 test events, stable positivity/weights, successful simulation recovery, adverse-direction A1 association, improvement in both proper scores over B1 without material calibration harm in the observed and weighted analyses, no comparable process-only or permutation gain, and no material reversal in endpoint tiers. If these thresholds are not met, the output is inconclusive even if a point estimate is large. A broad-only signal that fails specificity tiers, or a signal that vanishes after ascertainment weighting, is adverse to the biological/prognostic interpretation and supports documentation/selection bias as the more plausible explanation. A null result with stable weights and adequate events falsifies the incremental trajectory claim in this snapshot, not hepatic-reserve biology.
