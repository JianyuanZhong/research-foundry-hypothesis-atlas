# Landmark transport of hypotension burden after documented rescue context

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must construct a frozen, auditable six-hour landmark cohort and 30-minute interval ledger from the read-only eICU 2.0 snapshot; fit matched transparent competing-risk models and an ordered mechanistic multi-state model; and emit uncertainty-quantified burden gradients, the prompt-versus-delayed/no documented pressor contrast, test-opportunity and ICU-exit decompositions, hospital heterogeneity, held-out transport, calibration, and all prespecified falsification sensitivities. Completion requires computed ledgers, fitted parameters, standardized contrasts and a conclusion linked to those outputs. Discovery has fitted no scientific model and claims no clinical result.

## Unresolved claim and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported before fitting is only data availability: the frozen snapshot contains ICU-relative MAP summaries, ICU-keyed laboratory results and revision offsets, named infusion rows with offsets, ICU disposition, admission descriptors, severity descriptors and hospital identifiers joinable on patientunitstayid. It does not establish pressor administration, treatment intent, hypotension causality, kidney injury, or transportability.

The unresolved question is whether documented rescue timing changes interpretation of early hypotension burden across hospitals, or whether the apparent renal signal is driven by measurement opportunity and competing ICU exit. Among adult ICU stays with a qualifying creatinine before the first observed low MAP, still observable after a six-hour landmark and without an early rise, does the 0–6 hour low-MAP burden gradient for a 6–48 hour *observed creatinine-rise signal* differ between prompt-after-low versus delayed/no documented target-pressor patterns, within hospitals and in held-out hospitals?

Primary directional hypothesis: conditional on minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, MAP coverage/trajectory, admission risk and hospital context, prompt documentation of a target pressor within 30 minutes after the first raw MAP <65 is associated with attenuation of the burden gradient relative to delayed/no documentation. This is a noncausal recorded-process hypothesis. It is falsified by a reproducible null, reversal or amplification, or by loss of the contrast within hospitals, in held-out hospitals, or after observation/exit decomposition. A reversal does not imply pressors cause renal injury.

A stable timing interaction could identify when early hypotension should be interpreted cautiously in surveillance or trial enrichment and motivate prospective renal monitoring studies. A stable burden gradient without timing modification would be a useful negative result supporting a more transportable prognostic descriptor. Neither result changes a vasopressor or MAP target.

For MAP, deduplicate stay/time by median; use raw observations to define first-low time and pressor timing. Primary burden is trapezoidal area below 65, linearly bridging only gaps <=30 minutes and retaining observed/bridged coverage, unbridged gaps and coverage. Secondary features are observed low minutes, longest run, episode count, minimum, recovery and trajectory. Minimum MAP and non-burden trajectory enter every model.

Define the first documented target-pressor record as the smallest infusionoffset in [0,360] whose case-folded drugname matches a frozen map for norepinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine or epinephrine. Categories are pre-low/concurrent (<= first low), prompt-after-low (> low and <=30 minutes), delayed-after-low (>30 minutes and <=360), and no record. Same-minute is pre-low/concurrent. Drugrate, infusionrate, drugamount, volumeoffluid and patientweight are audit fields only; no dose or administration is inferred.

Follow-up begins strictly after 360 and ends at end=min(2880, unitdischargeoffset). A post-landmark rise is the first qualifying result with offset >360, <=end, and >= baseline+0.3. In 30-minute intervals, competing terminal states are observed rise, unit death (unitdischargestatus=Expired or unitdischargelocation=Death), alive unit discharge/transfer before rise, or no terminal event by 2880/unit boundary. A qualifying non-rise test is not terminal. The observation process reports first qualifying test opportunity, rise conditional on test, death and alive exit. Predictors in each interval use prior history only; no future lab result may enter.

## Estimand, models and falsification

Let G_h,c be the standardized 48-hour observed-rise cumulative-incidence difference between development P75 and P25 burden within hospital h and timing category c, standardizing the same baseline creatinine, minimum MAP, coverage/trajectory, static covariates and admission risk. Define D_h=G_h,prompt−G_h,delayed/no, reporting delayed and no-record separately when supported. The primary clinical-interpretation margin is 2 percentage points absolute risk, not a treatment-effect margin.

Use a deterministic hospital hash split: 80% development and 20% held-out transport; no patient, uniquepid or hospital crosses splits. If connected hospital components are needed for multi-hospital uniquepid, report coverage loss. If fewer than 10 held-out hospitals or fewer than five supported held-out hospitals (>=50 cohort stays, both timing categories and burden overlap), transport is inconclusive.

Transparent matched baseline: regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic models for rise, death, alive exit and first test. B0 contains minimum MAP, trajectory, coverage, baseline creatinine, demographics/admission context, hospital context and admission-risk descriptors; B1 adds burden; B2 adds timing; B3 adds burden-by-timing; B4 adds interval observation history and exit boundary; B5 is B4 without the interaction; B6 adds development-hospital random slopes for the burden-by-timing contrast. The primary test is standardized D_h and B6 versus its no-interaction restriction, not AUROC.

Substantive alternative on identical rows, features, split and uncertainty: an ordered piecewise-exponential multi-state model with pre-landmark low-MAP/pressor/recovery history frozen as context and separate transitions for first test without rise, observed rise, unit death and alive exit, with hospital random effects where estimable. It can reveal whether apparent heterogeneity is carried by timing, MAP recovery, testing opportunity or competing exit; the baseline loses that ordering by marginalizing interval processes. A generic neural sequence model is deferred unless both models leave reproducible shape-specific residual miscalibration across held-out hospitals after process accounting; a small discrimination gain is insufficient.

Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates (uniquepid-clustered sensitivity), development-only preprocessing, calibration intercept/slope and Brier score, plus secondary AUROC/AUPRC. Report patient-standardized and equal-hospital held-out D, cluster uncertainty, sign consistency, burden/timing overlap, between-hospital variance and prediction interval. Support requires both models agree in direction, development and held-out patient-standardized D meet the 2-point margin with intervals excluding zero, equal-hospital held-out results agree, and the interaction survives testing/exit adjustment and sensitivities. Adverse results include null/reversed/amplified interaction, transport failure, or attenuation explained by testing/exit. Inconclusive results include sparse support, invalid unit/label maps, nonconvergence, boundary random effects, extreme weights, pooled/equal-hospital disagreement or intervals failing the margin.

Required sensitivities: no interpolation; non-invasive MAP alone; alternate 30/60-minute prompt cut; revised lab time; confirmed-rise requiring repeat result; alternate 48-hour/unit boundary; positive-rate-record and agent-map variants; Apache severity adjustment; connected-component split; broad baseline and late-entry cohorts. Sensitivities are not permission to choose a favorable result.
