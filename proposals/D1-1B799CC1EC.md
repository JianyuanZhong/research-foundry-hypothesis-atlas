# Surveillance-gated day-60 HCC marker decoupling after resection

Status: substantive child design; bounded source audits are observed, but no biomarker state or outcome model has been fitted. The question is narrowed from predicting a composite “recurrence-management event” to testing whether recurrence-report information is separable from the processes that generate imaging and treatment records.

## Scientific opening, claim boundary, and advance

The strongest evidence-supported claim is that a 4–6-week postoperative AFP response is prognostic in a selected high-AFP resection cohort [K1]. It is not evidence that persistent AFP is residual tumor. Persistent AFP without detected recurrence has been documented after apparently curative resection [K3]. A further threat is treatment meaning: post-hepatectomy TACE can be assigned as adjuvant management in patients treated as recurrence-free, and a current randomized protocol explicitly contrasts adjuvant TACE with intensive follow-up [K2]. Thus a post-landmark treatment code is not a recurrence diagnosis.

The unresolved claim is whether a day-60 AFP/PIVKA-II trajectory component that has decoupled from liver-injury recovery carries information about a subsequent untreated surveillance image, rather than merely predicting who is tested, imaged, or treated. The leading explanation is persistent tumor-associated signal. The strongest rival is a combination of postoperative hepatocyte injury/regeneration, assay or calendar drift, PIVKA-II perturbation by vitamin-K-related treatment, prior/adjuvant treatment selection, and informative surveillance.

Resolving this boundary is clinically consequential. A marker signal specific to independently ascertained recurrence could motivate a prospective imaging-prioritization study. A signal that mainly predicts testing or treatment would warn against using this EHR composite for occult-disease inference. This child advances the parent by removing treatment-only events from the recurrence-report outcome, representing surveillance and treatment as explicit competing observation processes, and requiring a first-untreated-image contrast before any tumor interpretation.

## Hypothesis and exact scientific deliverable

At a fixed day-60 landmark after first definitive HCC resection, a marker-persistence state learned from AFP and optional PIVKA-II will be distinguishable from a surgery-linked injury state only if it:

1. predicts an affirmative versus explicitly nonpositive first liver CT/MRI report obtained before any post-landmark HCC-directed treatment;
2. is more strongly associated with that report content than with the probability/timing of imaging or treatment itself;
3. remains stable across calendar eras, exact assay labels/specimen types, no-prior-treatment restrictions, and removal of PIVKA-II observations exposed to recorded vitamin-K/warfarin/antibiotic proxies; and
4. improves held-out report-content calibration and discrimination over the same-split static day-60 baseline.

The deliverable is newly fitted baseline and joint longitudinal models on one frozen cohort and split, producing: cohort and transition flows; assay/medication audits; first-transition cumulative incidences; content-model metrics; estimated injury and marker states/loadings; observation-intensity coefficients; state-identifiability checks; and conclusions mechanically mapped to supportive, adverse, or inconclusive criteria. Readiness is not completion.

The day-60 clinical question is preserved, but the biological-recurrence claim is gated. If first untreated surveillance reports or adjudicated positive/negative reports fail the thresholds below, the only valid deliverable is an ascertainment/measurement result; no recurrence-prediction model may be interpreted.

## Observed decision-changing audit

These are feasibility observations, not hypothesis results.

The parent’s broad audit found 6,880 landmark patients after early-treatment exclusion, but only 244 had recorded cross-sectional surveillance imaging in days 61–365, 672 had treatment, and 693 had either component. A new stricter audit required adult same-encounter HCC/resection in 2012–2023, numeric AFP before surgery and in days 0–14 and 15–60, an acute injury measurement, and no directed treatment in days -180:-1 or 0:60. After correcting a duplicate-procedure-row bug, 215 patients remained: 16 had liver CT/MRI, 46 had treatment, 36 had treatment before any recorded scan or no scan, and only nine had a scan before treatment (five lexical proxy-positive and four nonpositive). Pathology restriction and clinical adjudication were not applied in this bounded audit, so these are not final cohort counts.

The same audit found marked calendar distribution shifts for recorded AFP and PIVKA-II and many medication rows matching vitamin K or antibiotic terms; these are not proof of assay drift or PIVKA confounding, but they make pooled absolute thresholds indefensible. The failed nine-patient probe caused by treating same-episode procedure rows as repeat therapy is retained as an implementation warning: repeat resection must be in a different encounter and at least seven days after index.

This evidence warrants narrowing. A day-61–365 “no recurrence” label is not computable from record absence, and treatment cannot be pooled with affirmative imaging. The first untreated image is the only defensible computable disease-content contrast, and even it requires report-rule validation and expert review.

## Population and time boundaries

Primary audit population:

- age ≥18;
- earliest definitive `诊断名称` containing `肝细胞癌` or `原发性肝癌` without uncertainty language, plus same-encounter liver-resection text excluding transplant, donor, biopsy/puncture, cyst surgery, and explicitly recurrent surgery;
- index `开始时间` from 2012-01-01 through 2023-12-31 and within 30 days of the joined encounter date; otherwise quarantine;
- pathology-supported HCC for the clinical primary; same-encounter diagnostic support without pathology is a labeled sensitivity;
- no TACE/embolization, ablation, transplant, prior liver resection, or systemic-treatment proxy in days -180 to -1;
- no explicit residual/recurrence/metastasis or separate HCC-directed therapy from index through day 60;
- numeric exact-label AFP in days -90:-1 and at least one in days 15:60; at least two distinct postoperative dates among AFP or PIVKA-II and at least two distinct injury-marker dates in days 0:60 for the longitudinal alternative. The baseline requires only one acute and one recovery measurement. PIVKA-II is optional, never a membership requirement.

Predictors use day -90 through +60. One prediction is made at day 60. The marked observation window is day 61 through day 365, administrative end, or last observed dated record. Record absence is censoring, not a negative outcome.

## Outcomes and estimands

The first post-landmark transition is one of:

- **S: untreated surveillance report** — first dated liver/upper-abdominal CT or MRI report before any HCC-directed post-landmark procedure/systemic-treatment proxy;
- **T: treatment before qualifying imaging** — first TACE/embolization, ablation, repeat resection, transplant, or systemic-treatment proxy before S; this is a management transition, not recurrence;
- **C: censoring/no transition** — last recorded contact or administrative day 365.

Within S, the computable report-content mark is prespecified as affirmative recurrence/residual/new HCC/progression/metastasis, explicit nonpositive language, uncertain, or unclassified. The primary binary content contrast includes only affirmative versus explicit nonpositive reports after negation/temporality rules are frozen; uncertain/unclassified reports are reported and excluded. A report is “untreated” only relative to recorded HCC treatment; prior unrecorded outside treatment cannot be excluded.

Estimands:

1. content association: adjusted odds/hazard contrast for day-60 marker versus injury states on affirmative versus explicit nonpositive first untreated report;
2. ascertainment association: state-specific cause-specific hazards/cumulative incidences for S and T;
3. discrimination gain of the longitudinal alternative over the baseline for report content, conditional on passing the report gate;
4. differential association with generic non-HCC imaging/encounter contact as a negative-control ascertainment outcome.

No causal treatment effect is estimated. Treatment-intent text is summarized as explicit recurrence, explicit adjuvant, both, or ambiguous; ambiguous events stay ambiguous.

## Methods on the same split

### Simple baseline

Fit two linked but conventional models:

1. elastic-net cause-specific Cox models from day 60 for S and T, with C as censoring and cumulative-incidence reporting;
2. Firth logistic regression among S for affirmative versus explicit nonpositive content.

Inputs are age, sex, calendar year, service, pathology lexical proxies, preoperative AFP rank within year, latest day-15:60 AFP/preoperative log ratio, latest optional PIVKA-II ratio, acute peak and day-15:60 latest ALT/AST/bilirubin/INR/albumin/platelet ratios, recorded vitamin-K/warfarin/antibiotic flags, and pre-landmark encounter/lab/imaging counts and missingness. This preserves interpretability but loses irregular timing, recovery shape, cross-marker covariance, and dependence between measurement and observation.

### Substantive longitudinal alternative

Fit a constrained continuous-time joint marked point-process model:

- injury state (I_i(t)), initialized by surgery and allowed patient-specific recovery, loading on ALT/AST/bilirubin/INR and inversely on albumin/platelets;
- marker-persistence state (M_i(t)), allowed to clear, plateau, or rise, loading on AFP and optional PIVKA-II/AFP-L3;
- AFP may load on both states; PIVKA-II observation parameters include recorded vitamin-K/warfarin/antibiotic flags;
- exact-label/specimen observation models include calendar-era offsets, censoring, irregular times, missingness masks, and measurement intensity;
- shared random effects link (M_i(60)), (I_i(60)), baseline covariates, and prior contact to S and T intensities;
- if the report gate passes, the S mark model estimates affirmative versus explicit nonpositive content.

This alternative reveals whether the same latent component predicts report content, ordering/imaging, or treatment, which a static baseline conflates. It does not make the label “tumor” biological. Compare constrained and unconstrained two-factor fits, swap/leave-one-marker-out fits, three random seeds, posterior predictive checks, and simulated recovery of known states. A multivariate functional-PCA plus the same transition models is the CPU fallback if the joint model is non-identifiable. A transformer remains deferred because flexible sequence prediction would not resolve the observation-versus-content rival.

## Split, gates, and evaluation

Assign participants by `int(SHA256(患者主索引)[:8],16) mod 100`: 0–59 train, 60–79 validation, 80–99 locked test. All records for a participant remain together. Lexical rules, medication lists, cohort rules, and hyperparameters freeze before test access. Baseline and alternative use exactly the same eligible participants and split.

Before model fitting:

- observation-process gate: ≥500 eligible patients, ≥150 S-or-T transitions, and ≥30 transitions in each split;
- report-content gate: ≥300 first untreated S reports overall, ≥100 clinically reviewed affirmative and ≥100 reviewed explicit-negative reports, and ≥20 of each in locked test;
- blinded hepatobiliary/radiology review of a stratified sample must achieve PPV and NPV ≥0.85 for the frozen report rule; disagreements are adjudicated;
- observation weights/joint-model effective sample size must remain ≥50% of nominal and state simulation recovery must meet prespecified correlation ≥0.8 without factor swapping.

Failure of the observation gate stops outcome fitting. Passing observation but failing report/adjudication gates permits only the ascertainment experiment and marks the tumor-versus-injury question inconclusive.

Evaluate S/T cumulative incidence, cause-specific calibration and Brier score; report-content AUC/AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score; paired participant bootstrap intervals; state/loading/seed stability; calendar and no-prior-treatment sensitivity; AFP-only and PIVKA-restricted sensitivity; and generic-contact negative controls. Report event/component counts and weight tails.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of: stable separable states; (M(60)) more strongly associated with affirmative versus explicit-negative untreated imaging than (I(60)); the marker state not comparably associated with generic contact or merely S/T intensity; locked-test AUC gain ≥0.03 or Brier improvement ≥0.01 with a 95% paired interval excluding no improvement; and consistent direction across calendar eras, AFP-only, PIVKA medication-restricted, pathology-supported, and no-prior-treatment analyses. This supports prospective standardized imaging-prioritization research, not occult-tumor diagnosis or treatment benefit.

Adverse evidence includes AFP loading predominantly on injury; injury predicting report content at least as strongly; marker state predicting S/T or generic contact more strongly than report content; disappearance under calendar/PIVKA restrictions; factor instability; or the upper 95% bound excluding a 0.02 AUC gain with no calibration improvement. This favors injury, measurement, treatment-selection, or observation explanations and falsifies incremental adequacy of the day-60 marker model in this dataset.

Inconclusive evidence includes any failed gate, unavailable expert adjudication, incompatible assay eras, effective sample size <50%, unstable states, or intervals spanning clinically useful benefit and harm. Given the bounded audits, this is currently the expected outcome unless a frozen broader extraction materially increases untreated imaging. It does not refute biological decoupling; it means the retrospective data cannot distinguish it.

Computationally checkable claims are source/schema hashes, joins, times, cohort flow, no leakage, procedure episode deduplication, S/T ordering, report-rule outputs, splits, model fits, metrics, uncertainty, and consistency of conclusions with thresholds. Not automatically checkable are curative intent, true recurrence, treatment intent, outside surveillance/treatment, assay equivalence, biological identity of states, or clinical benefit of earlier imaging. Those require specialist review, missing assay metadata, prospective scheduled imaging, or another study.

## Compute and alternatives

Observed multi-gigabyte scans completed in about 65–170 seconds on 4 CPUs; this measures extraction feasibility only. Planned extraction, audit, baseline, and functional-PCA fallback: 8–16 CPUs, 64 GiB, 1–2 hours. Planned three-seed joint model: one allocated A100, 8 CPUs, 64 GiB, 2–4 hours unmeasured. Total fits within the 8-hour, 16-CPU/8-GPU/256-GiB solver planning envelope. GPU is a throughput choice.

Alternatives retained:

- Parent composite recurrence-management outcome: rejected because treatment may be adjuvant and record absence is not nonrecurrence; revisit only after validated intent and surveillance completeness.
- Imaging-only binary outcome in every observed scan: replaced by first untreated image to prevent treatment changing both markers and imaging interpretation.
- Inverse-probability weighting alone: retained as sensitivity, but insufficient when positivity is observed only after selective imaging; the joint transition/content model makes the dependence explicit.
- Prospective standardized AFP/PIVKA-II plus scheduled CT/MRI: preferred definitive study if gates fail.
- Generic transformer: revisit only after adequate adjudicated report support and residual nonlinear information; prediction gain alone would not resolve mechanism.
