# Observation-resolved target trial of IV loop initiation and remaining index-ventilation episode duration

**Parent:** `[prior hypothesis]`

## Substantive change and clinical question

This child preserves the parent’s population, hour-48 decision time, 24-hour strategies, clone-censor analysis, mortality gate and bracketed RRT ascertainment. It changes only the primary respiratory estimand. The parent’s VFD28 remains a bounded secondary outcome because a corrected-partition audit shows that it is structurally noninformative: among 362 near-final eligible subjects, mean individual VFD28 bound width was 14.93 days, median unknown time 18.59 days, and only 83 (22.9%) were point identified. Admissions, transfers, linked ICU readmissions, discharge destination and discharge text cannot establish post-discharge ventilation.

The bedside question remains: among invasively ventilated adults stabilized after vasopressor-treated shock but with substantial documented positive balance, does initiating/re-initiating an ICU-recorded IV loop within 24 hours shorten the remaining ICU-recorded invasive-ventilation episode already present at t0 without unacceptable death, early recurrent ventilation, RRT, hemodynamic or electrolyte harm?

The strongest evidence-supported claim is that heterogeneous conservative-fluid/deresuscitative bundles reduce recorded balance and may improve ventilator-free days. It does not establish benefit or renal safety from isolated IV furosemide/bumetanide initiation at this landmark. RADAR-2 was a bundled feasibility trial and underpowered for clinical outcomes. Recent endpoint-methods work also identifies death and ICU discharge as competing events that can make recovery outcomes nonrandomly missing (Boyle et al., 2026, DOI `10.1093/ajrccm/aamag238`; full Europe PMC XML inspected, frozen source `[source checksum]`). The unresolved claim tested here is narrower: actual IV loop initiation causes at least a 24-hour reduction in restricted remaining duration of the t0 ventilation record. Even a positive result would not establish durable extubation, post-discharge ventilator freedom, renal safety, or practice change.

## Hypothesis and estimand

Among the fixed eligible population below, strategy A versus B increases by at least 24 hours the day-7 restricted time saved to the end of the continuous ICU-recorded invasive-ventilation episode that overlaps t0, while meeting mortality, early recurrent-ventilation and observed-RRT gates.

Let valid `procedureevents` intervals for item 225792 be ordered within the index admission. The t0 component begins with every valid interval satisfying `starttime <= t0 < endtime` and is recursively extended through any later interval whose `starttime <= current_component_end`. No nonoverlapping gap is silently bridged. Its end `E` is the maximum endtime in that component. Define restricted remaining episode duration:

`RRED7 = 168 hours` if death occurs by t0+7 days, if no valid component end is observed, or if E is after t0+168 hours; otherwise `RRED7 = max(0, E-t0)` hours.

The per-protocol primary estimand is
`theta = weighted_mean_B(RRED7) - weighted_mean_A(RRED7)`;
positive values favor initiation. This measures a shorter ICU-recorded index episode, not days alive off ventilation and not extubation. The clinical gate is `theta >= 24 hours` and a subject-bootstrap 95% lower bound above 0; a stringent success analysis also requires the lower bound above 24 hours.

Primary interval validity requires `itemid=225792`, nonmissing `starttime,endtime,storetime`, `endtime>starttime`, `statusdescription=FinishedRunning`, and agreement within five minutes between `value` in `valueuom=min` and `endtime-starttime`. Emit failures. Rows marked `continueinnextdept=1` must be linked through `subject_id,hadm_id`, transfers and the next ICU stay; an unlinked continuation makes RRED7 unknown and the analysis infeasible if above 5%. Report endtimes within one hour of ICU outtime or a transfer boundary; repeat after excluding them. Sensitivities accept `Stopped`, recursively bridge gaps of at most one and six hours, and use the longest t0-overlapping row. A material (>24-hour, interval-excluding) conflict is inconclusive.

## Analysis, baselines and falsification

Fit stabilized clone-censor models hourly through 24 hours using lagged treatment, MAP/pressors, urine, balance, creatinine, lactate, potassium, FiO2, PEEP, oxygenation, respiratory support, sedation, crystalloid/albumin and result-available imaging. Baseline terms include age, sex, race, admission type/year, unit/service, prior completed-admission comorbidity, weight/balance, prior loop dose/time, KDIGO stage, pressor duration/time since cessation, physiology, fluids and pre-t0 radiology. Never use current-discharge diagnoses or post-t0 notes as covariates.

Report unadjusted, baseline overlap-weighted and clone-censor estimates; standardized differences, missingness, adherence probabilities, truncation, arm ESS, duration-quality diagnostics and bootstrap distributions. Use subject-clustered robust intervals and at least 500 subject bootstraps. Declare infeasible before interpretation if either adherent arm <100, ESS<75, >10% adherence probability <0.01, >5% unresolved continuation/invalid primary intervals, material eMAR conflict, or material outcome/safety ambiguity.

Required sensitivities retain the parent’s never-treated users; washouts; 30/100 mL/kg balance; strict accounting; 6/12/24-hour initiation; cardiac-surgery/heart-failure exclusion; KDIGO strata; no albumin co-initiation; calendar era; ICU/eMAR concordance; and quantitative unmeasured-indication analysis. Add primary interval status/boundary/gap definitions.

Temporal falsification tests whether eventual strategy predicts pre-t0 six-hour creatinine, urine-output or MAP slopes after baseline adjustment. Standardized difference >0.10 or multiplicity-controlled P<0.05 makes causal interpretation inconclusive. Greater urine and more-negative 24-hour balance are process controls, not patient benefit.

## Decision rules

Supportive requires: primary `theta>=24h` with 95% lower bound >0 (and label “stringent” only if >24h); upper 95% excess death <3 points; upper 95% excess observed RRT <5 points under both brackets; upper 95% excess day-7 death/48-hour recurrence <5 points under both discharge bounds; process direction; feasibility/positivity; passed falsification; and no material interval-definition conflict. This supports external validation and a randomized trial, not durable extubation or practice change.

Adverse is a primary 95% upper bound <=0, mortality-harm lower bound >=3 points, RRT-harm lower bound >=5 points under the prespecified bracket, or death/recurrence lower bound >=5 points. Favorable balance cannot rescue harm.

All other patterns are inconclusive/mixed, including a point estimate <24h, wide intervals, safety failure, bracket changes, nonpositivity, boundary-sensitive episode ends, eMAR conflict, residual pre-t0 imbalance, or VFD bounds spanning benefit and harm. Nonsignificance is not equivalence.
