# Procedure-corrobated respiratory transition and competing early failure after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and fit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study. Its primary scientific output is a held-out, uncertainty-quantified comparison of early competing failure pathways after different *recorded respiratory-support transitions*, with the parent's broad respiratory-versus-vasoactive estimand retained as a prespecified comparator.

Completion requires:

1. `run_manifest.json` containing the catalog and snapshot hashes, exact source hashes and paths, archive members, schema hashes, dictionary assertions, locked temporal/phenotype/topology/q rules, split seeds, software versions and output hashes.
2. `cohort_audit.parquet`, one row per subject's earliest eligible ICU stay, with all join, exclusion, temporal-integrity, alive-at-landmark, window, observation, and subtype-evidence flags.
3. `hourly_features.parquet`, twelve left-closed rows in [outtime-12h,outtime), containing chart-time physiology, respiratory/vasoactive evidence, source-specific masks, observation-process variables, and no post-outtime predictors.
4. `transition_ledger.parquet`, every qualifying respiratory support observation and procedure interval, its source, item label, valid interval, half-window state, contradiction, and subtype assignment or unknown reason.
5. `topology_outcomes.parquet`, every later ICU candidate within 48 hours, all transfer bridge candidates, ICU-unit/timing/tie flags, and conservative lower-bound L versus any-return upper-bound U labels.
6. `heldout_contrasts.parquet`, 6-, 24-, and 48-hour competing cumulative-incidence functions (CIFs), pairwise subtype contrasts, lower/upper compatible intervals, death and alive-discharge CIFs, calibration and uncertainty on fixed patient-level test splits.
7. `falsification_outputs.parquet` and `interpretation_map.parquet`, linking each claim to a computed estimate, interval, event/overlap/bound-width diagnostics, and its evidence limitation.

Readiness and discovery probes establish packaging or source feasibility only. No fitted association or clinical conclusion is asserted here.

## Supported evidence versus unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence is that the local MIMIC-IV archive contains timestamped ICU stays, charted physiology, respiratory/support-related item IDs, procedure intervals, vasoactive input intervals, transfers, admissions, and competing in-hospital endpoints. Prior board work also supports a methodological lower/upper framing: a later ICU return preceded by a uniquely documented non-ICU bridge is a conservative observed return-compatible endpoint L, while any later ICU return is an upper endpoint U for a latent transition. Prior audits did not estimate the subtype association below and are not evidence for its direction.

The unresolved claim is whether the recorded respiratory transition itself has clinically different early failure meaning depending on whether an invasive-liberation-compatible procedure is documented, versus a transition supported only by oxygen/NIV-related records. This is a prognostic/care-process measurement question, not an estimate of the causal effect of extubation or oxygen withdrawal.

Primary hypothesis, tested among all eligible adults with adequate respiratory/MAP observation and within the parent respiratory-transition strata:

- An extubation-corrobated transition (ET), defined as a valid extubation procedure interval in the pre-outtime window with temporally compatible preceding respiratory-support evidence, has a different and directionally higher 48-hour ICU-return-compatible CIF than an oxygen/NIV-record transition (ON), defined by valid respiratory evidence and no qualifying extubation corroboration, after standardization to the same final recorded burden, physiology, observation intensity and discharge-process covariates.
- The ET versus ON contrast is expected to be more pronounced for return-compatible escalation than for death; the solver must report the full vector rather than force this expectation. A null, reversed, topology-unstable, or observation-sensitive contrast falsifies the directional subtype claim.
- The parent's respiratory-only, vasoactive-only, and joint recent-withdrawal contrasts remain a primary family-level comparator. If the subtype refinement does not add stable information beyond the parent class, that is an informative adverse result for the proposed clinical refinement.

Procedure labels are transition corroboration, not proof of continuous mechanical ventilation. Any record that lacks adequate temporal compatibility, has contradictory intervals, or cannot be assigned to ET or ON is retained as unknown/insufficient evidence, not treated as ON.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

At ICU discharge, “respiratory support reduced” can describe very different states: liberation from an invasive airway, discontinuation of non-invasive support, or reduction of supplemental oxygen. Those states can imply different post-discharge surveillance concerns, but a single residual-instability score or aggregate ICU-return endpoint cannot distinguish them. The substantive advance is an evidence-tiered, competing-pathway estimand that tests whether transition provenance changes the meaning of a recorded respiratory withdrawal while preserving uncertainty about selective charting and transfer topology. A supportive result could motivate adjudicated prospective work on transition-specific discharge monitoring; it would not itself establish discharge readiness, extubation benefit, preventability, or a policy threshold.

## Estimand and analysis

The primary estimand is the standardized 6/24/48-hour CIF vector and pairwise contrast ET minus ON for L, U, death and alive discharge, conditional on the broad eligible population and reported with the compatible interval [L_ET-U_ON, U_ET-L_ON] for the latent return-compatible transition. Do not report a midpoint as identified. The parent family-level contrasts are co-primary context: respiratory-only, vasoactive-only and joint recent withdrawal versus durable recorded-off. Report return/death composition, bound widths, overlap, observation effective sample size, and q/topology frontiers.

The transparent baseline is pooled one-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards for L-compatible return, U-only return, death and alive discharge plus aggregate return, with subtype/class, preceding/final physiology summaries, support evidence, masks, observation/process variables, admission context, age, gender, anchor-year group and ICU LOS. Fit nested physiology/support-only, mask/process-only, and full models. Standardize held-out predictions to the common eligible population; use a descriptive coverage model only for observation standardization, not causal correction.

The substantive alternative is a two-domain observation-aware hidden semi-Markov model using the same hourly ledger, splits, q scenarios, covariates and outcomes. Respiratory states include duration-aware procedure-corroborated transition, oxygen/NIV-record transition, active, durable-off and unknown; hemodynamic states include active/withdrawn/unknown. The observation model represents masks, row counts and source channels; transition duration/order is the information a fixed summary loses. It predicts the same observable competing hazards and reports a latent transition hazard only within the declared topology sensitivity grid and only if it lies in [L,U]. It is exploratory if it fails held-out calibration or state stability. No complex model is preferred because it is complex.

Use subject bootstrap 95% intervals with bound propagation and repeat seeds 17/29/43. Report calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier, absolute CIF contrasts, event counts, overlap/positivity, effective sample size, subtype prevalence, bound widths, latent entropy and split stability; AUROC is secondary.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires the ET-versus-ON return/death contrast to be directionally stable at 6/24/48 hours, remain compatible after q and topology sensitivity, have adequate overlap/events, agree in direction between baseline and alternative, and disappear under transfer-topology permutation. It must not depend on storetime, one chart channel, one procedure source, or an implausibly narrow final-burden threshold. Supportive evidence establishes only a reproducible retrospective recorded-data association.

Adverse evidence is reversal or collapse after observation/process adjustment, a signal only for U with no L support, dependence on one procedure/channel/topology rule, persistence after transfer permutation, latent estimates outside [L,U], or a subtype effect explained by final burden alone. It argues against the stronger transition-provenance interpretation.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse ET/ON cells, fewer than 20 endpoint events, high subtype unknownness, wide overlapping intervals, poor positivity/effective sample size, unstable q/topology/tie frontiers, high latent entropy, poor calibration, or divergence between baseline and alternative. Inconclusive does not support the hypothesis.

Required falsifications include within-window time shuffling, storetime negative control, support/mask/physiology/process ablations, procedure-removal and channel-specific analyses, q/window/coverage/topology/tie sensitivity, transfer-careunit permutation, a +6-hour transfer shift, pre-landmark pseudo-return negative control, discharge-as-censoring sensitivity, first-careunit/admission-type/anchor-year/transfer-density strata, and subtype reassignment under endpoint tolerance. All final conclusions must map to computed outputs.

No result can establish causality of extubation, oxygen withdrawal, or discharge; clinician intent, readiness, treatment limitation, ward monitoring, planned/unplanned status, preventability, true ventilation state, microcirculation, external validity and clinical utility require adjudicated records, richer monitoring/evidence, prospective or causal studies, and external validation.

## Method comparison, compute and deferred alternatives

The simple baseline is primary because the scientific uncertainty is inspectable subtype and observation robustness; it directly exposes covariate adjustment, event counts, partial-identification intervals and calibration. The semi-Markov alternative is scientifically useful, not a complexity bonus: its duration/order and separate observation model can distinguish a true recorded transition pattern from a last-value or measurement-intensity artifact. Both use identical inputs and patient splits. A GRU/TCN/transformer is deferred because it adds sequence capacity without repairing missing-path endpoint semantics; revisit only if subtype event support is adequate and a held-out mechanistic/state alternative remains stable. Causal discharge-policy or treatment-emulation methods are deferred because intent, treatment limitations, source control and time-varying indications are not validated. CPU is the default; extraction and the baseline fit should use CPU, while repeated semi-Markov optimization may use one allocated A100 with cuda:0 only after profiling. Under the solver planning envelope (16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, up to 8 allocated A100s, 28800 seconds), unverified estimates are 10–30 minutes for streaming extraction, 30–60 minutes for baseline/bootstrap/bounds, and 1–4 hours for repeated semi-Markov/q/topology fits. These are planning estimates, not measured solver results. Discovery measured only source/catalog/header/schema/dictionary availability and prior board audits.
