# Hypothesis Proposal: Multisystem Organ Dysfunction and Physiological Reserve

## Unresolved Question

**Does the temporal convergence of multiple organ system dysfunctions during acute illness predict mortality better than single-organ trajectories, and does this relationship differ by pre-illness physiological reserve?**

This question addresses the **Multisystem Organ Dysfunction (MODD)** phenomenon, where simultaneous deterioration across multiple organ systems (cardiovascular, renal, respiratory, neurological, hematologic) predicts mortality more strongly than individual organ failures. Current risk stratification tools (SOFA, APACHE II) assess individual organs in isolation but fail to capture the *temporal convergence* of organ failures that characterizes the most lethal clinical trajectories.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Risk Stratification**: Understanding MODD trajectories could improve early identification of high-risk patients before organ failures become apparent, enabling earlier intervention.
2. **Treatment Prioritization**: Identifying patients at risk of MODD convergence could guide resource allocation and intensification of monitoring.
3. **Biological Mechanisms**: The relationship between MODD and physiological reserve (e.g., grip strength, baseline function) may reveal biological pathways of resilience that could be targeted therapeutically.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Existing research focuses on:
- Single-organ dysfunction trajectories
- Static risk scores (SOFA, APACHE)
- Individual organ aging clocks

This proposal advances by:
- Testing **temporal convergence** across multiple organ systems (not just individual trajectories)
- Examining **effect modification by physiological reserve** (a clinically relevant but underexplored effect modifier)
- Using UKB's longitudinal data to capture pre-illness reserve and post-illness organ trajectories

## Scientific Design

### Population
- **UK Biobank participants** with at least one acute illness episode (infection, cardiac event, or other acute condition)
- **Inclusion**: Participants with repeat metabolic measurements AND hospitalization records
- **Exclusion**: Participants without pre-illness reserve measures (grip strength, baseline function)

### Temporal Boundaries
- **Baseline**: At or before the second repeat metabolic measurement (to capture pre-illness reserve)
- **Follow-up**: From second measurement through hospitalization for acute illness
- **Outcome**: Time to mortality (all-cause) or hospitalization for severe organ dysfunction

### Key Variables

**Exposures:**
- `health_outcomes` table: Organ-specific outcomes (e.g., "28000-0.0" for cardiovascular, "28001-0.0" for renal, etc.)
- `population` table: Grip strength, age, sex, baseline function
- `additional_exposures` table: Pre-illness frailty measures

**Outcomes:**
- `health_outcomes` table: Mortality outcomes and organ dysfunction codes
- Temporal patterns of multiple organ system involvement

**Effect Modifier:**
- Pre-illness grip strength (from `population` table)
- Baseline physiological reserve indices

### Analysis Plan

1. **Baseline characterization**: Compare MODD patterns between high vs. low grip strength groups
2. **Convergence modeling**: Model the rate of multi-organ involvement over time
3. **Interaction testing**: Test whether grip strength modifies the MODD-mortality relationship
4. **Comparison**: Compare MODD-based prediction vs. single-organ trajectory prediction

### Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
- Single-organ trajectories predict mortality as well as or better than MODD convergence
- Physiological reserve does not modify the MODD-mortality relationship
- No temporal pattern of multi-organ involvement is observed
