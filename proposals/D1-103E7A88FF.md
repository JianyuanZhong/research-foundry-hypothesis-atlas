# Episode 69 successor: does late lactate add calibrated review value beyond APACHE IVa and the observed 24-hour state?

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

At a fixed 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed entry, is a newly released late lactate worth adding to a mortality-risk review model when the comparator already contains a routinely recognizable APACHE IVa predicted hospital-mortality score and the complete time-indexed laboratory/bedside state available at the landmark?

The parent established the right caution: an apparent raw late-lactate increment was inconclusive and the raw augmented model was miscalibrated. The parent’s nested, hospital-held-out recalibration asks whether late lactate adds threshold-averaged modeled value beyond the observed state, but it does not establish that this increment is useful over a standard severity score that clinicians may already recognize. This child makes that comparator explicit. It therefore tests a more consequential deployment claim without changing the late-lactate feature, threshold grid, or uncertainty principle.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis (HAPACHE):** in the APACHE-IVa-complete subset of the frozen 24-hour EICU risk set, the calibrated augmented model R1 (APACHE IVa score + the parent’s time-indexed laboratory/physiology state + released late lactate) has a copied-hospital 95% interval for integrated net benefit at least **+1.00 net true-positive-equivalent mortality-review decision per 100 people** over the identically calibrated comparator R0 (APACHE IVa score + the same state without late lactate), across the fixed review-risk grid 0.30 through 0.50.

This is a conditional prognostic/model-comparison hypothesis. “Review decision” is a mathematical proxy defined by the net-benefit formula; it is not a validated clinical action. A positive result would support only calibrated modeled incremental value in this selected EICU/APACHE population. It would not show that ordering a late lactate, displaying a score, changing treatment, or acting on the score improves survival or benefits patients.

## Evidence boundary and substantive advance

The strongest available evidence is narrower than HAPACHE. In the prior complete-source EICU analysis, adding late lactate to an APACHE-IVa-plus-entry-lactate comparator produced a favorable point estimate but uncertainty crossed the +1/100 margin. In the richer 33-hospital parent lineage, the raw augmented model also had favorable point behavior but poor calibration and an interval spanning the materiality margin. Thus existing evidence supports feasibility and possible serial prognostic information, not calibrated incremental review value beyond both a recognized severity benchmark and the full recorded 24-hour state.

The inspected Baysan et al. study (Crit Care Explor 2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750, PMCID PMC9444407; frozen full-text XML source and hash recorded in the parent lineage) reported that adding 24-hour lactate improved C-statistic in a different sepsis population. That supports modest serial prognostic information, not this hospital-held-out comparator or decision-value margin. The inspected Ye et al. study (2026, DOI 10.1186/s40842-026-00323-2, PMCID PMC13435416) supports relevance of lactate, respiratory rate and oxygen saturation in a selected sepsis/heart-failure model, not this estimand. Vickers et al. (PMCID PMC6261531) explains that net benefit depends on the action and false-positive/true-positive exchange rate; it does not validate this mortality-review action or threshold grid.

The three research-ambition demonstrations were inspected only to the availability stated in `references/research-ambition/README.md`. The natural-history and Bayesian articles have available full article files and mechanical text extractions, with figures/equations requiring originals. The cancer demonstration’s main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable; only its supplement and metadata are available. None is evidence for HAPACHE and no unavailable cancer text is claimed as read.

The substantive advance is not a more complex predictor. It is a clinically recognizable, leakage-safe comparator and an explicit test of whether the late measurement adds value after a score and observed state that could already be used at reassessment. It also makes the APACHE-completeness and APACHE-version assumptions auditable.

## Leakage-safe calibrated hospital-held-out analysis

Use a fixed, outcome-blind six-fold hospital role design. Sort the frozen hospitals by descending eligible count and greedily assign each to the currently smallest fold, breaking ties by hospitalid. For each evaluation fold k, use the next fold cyclically as calibration hospitals and the remaining four folds as development hospitals. This gives disjoint development, calibration, and evaluation hospitals and evaluates every person once.

For each rotation:

1. Fit R0 and R1 as unweighted L2 logistic regressions with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10,000, tol=1e-8, no class weighting, seed 20260961, on development hospitals only.
2. Within development data, winsorize continuous variables at training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize using training mean and population SD, and mean-impute missing continuous features after scaling to zero. Apply those constants unchanged outside development. A missing or zero-variation required feature makes the rotation not computable; no feature is dropped.
3. Predict calibration hospitals with each base model. Fit separate two-parameter Platt recalibrators on calibration-hospital outcomes: logistic D ~ 1 + logit(p_base), with C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10,000, tol=1e-8, no class weights. Clip finite probabilities only for logit to [1e-15,1-1e-15]; nonfinite values and failed fits are failures.
4. Apply each recalibrator only to evaluation-hospital raw predictions. Evaluation labels never influence fitting, preprocessing, calibration, thresholds, or feature choice.
5. Pool one finite calibrated q0 and q1 per person. The raw predictions are audit outputs, never a fallback.

The primary estimand is the trapezoid-integrated net-benefit difference over p={0.30,0.31,...,0.50}:
`NB(q,p)=TP/N - FP/N * p/(1-p)`;
`DeltaINBcal=100/(0.50-0.30) * integral[NB(q1,p)-NB(q0,p)]dp`.
Report every threshold cell, treat-all and treat-none references, AUROC, Brier, log loss, calibration-in-the-large/slope, probability changes, and hospital-level calibration. Treat-all/treat-none are guardrails and descriptive references, not the primary comparator.

Estimate uncertainty with 2,000 copied-hospital refit bootstrap replicates, seed 20260961. Sample hospitals with replacement within each fixed role fold so every role is nonempty; preserve copied hospitals as one held-out group and exclude their copies from development fitting. Repeat preprocessing, both fits, both calibration fits, predictions, all thresholds and DeltaINB. Require >=95% finite converged replicates. Record the sampled hospital indices and all failures. Fixed-prediction one-hospital deletions report DeltaINB and calibration diagnostics; they cannot rescue a failed primary gate.

Apply labels after all gates:

1. **Supportive of calibrated modeled value:** all gates pass; the copied-hospital 95% DeltaINBcal lower bound is >=+1.00; all threshold/default/site requirements pass; both Brier and log-loss point estimates improve. Wording must be “calibration-supported modeled incremental value beyond APACHE IVa and the observed state in this selected cohort.”
2. **Opposite:** all gates pass and the upper bound is <0. This opposes incremental modeled value for this fixed comparator and setting, not every model or any lactate use.
3. **Adverse to materiality:** all gates pass and the upper bound is >=0 but <+1.00. This refutes the locked materiality margin while allowing a smaller or uncertain increment.
4. **Inconclusive:** all remaining computable cases, including an interval spanning +1, unstable site patterns, mixed proper-score behavior, or a failed interpretive stability requirement.
5. **Not computable:** any computational gate failure, with the named failed gate.

If the calibrated result is supportive but the utility artifact is absent, output `utility_status=not_computable` and the combined conclusion exactly as “calibration-supported modeled value; clinical utility unvalidated.” The fixed threshold grid and +1/100 margin are scenario parameters, not elicited clinician utilities.

## What results would mean

A supportive result would show that late lactate adds a reproducible, calibrated statistical signal beyond a standard severity probability and contemporaneous recorded state. It would justify an independent external validation and a blinded clinician/stakeholder elicitation study defining the review action, burden, false-positive exchange rate, and acceptable thresholds. It would not justify ordering repeat lactate, changing pressors/fluids/oxygen, or claiming survival benefit.

An adverse-to-materiality result would be useful negative evidence against a >=+1/100 incremental modeled review-value claim for this APACHE-complete EICU setting and model class. It would not prove late lactate has no prognostic information, that repeat testing is useless, or that a different action/threshold/population would fail.

An opposite result would indicate that after calibration the added late-lactate model performs worse in the fixed modeled decision proxy than its APACHE/state comparator. It is not evidence that lactate causes harm.

An inconclusive result leaves the margin unresolved and calls for a larger, independently collected cohort or narrower predeclared scientific question; threshold chasing and post-hoc subgrouping are prohibited. Not computable means the data cannot answer even this prespecified comparison.
