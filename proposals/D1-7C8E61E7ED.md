
# Does hospital heterogeneity in newly documented post-rise renal support survive prior surveillance adjustment?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly estimate whether hospital heterogeneity in newly documented post-rise renal-support remains after adjustment for a leakage-controlled, hospital-level measure of pre-rise renal-support surveillance and source capture.

The primary deliverable is a paired comparison on the parent’s exact six-hour landmark cohort and strict no-prior-support risk set:

1. H_raw_support: the supported-hospital 90th-minus-10th percentile spread in standardized 48-hour cumulative incidence of a first strict renal-support record after the first qualifying recorded creatinine rise.
2. H_surv_support: the same spread after standardizing every hospital to a common pre-rise surveillance regime, represented by leave-one-stay-out hospital surveillance scores and prespecified source-specific score terms.
3. A_surv = H_raw_support - H_surv_support, with a joint hospital-cluster interval.
4. Treatment-source and intake/output-source support, repeat-creatinine opportunity, confirmation after a repeat, and unit death/alive/unknown exits.
5. The parent’s renal-escalation composite and recorded-confirmation estimands as secondary comparators.

Primary hypothesis: among adult eICU unit stays with a first qualifying recorded creatinine rise and no strict renal-support record from ICU time 0 through that rise, the hospital spread in newly documented post-rise renal support remains clinically material after leakage-controlled adjustment for pre-rise renal-support surveillance and source-specific ascertainment.

Supportive rule: H_surv_support >= 5 percentage points, its two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval excludes zero, and H_surv_support >= 0.50 H_raw_support when the raw spread is estimable. This is a documentation-process margin, not a treatment threshold.

The parent established a downstream renal-escalation documentation endpoint but left open whether its support component is a reproducible post-rise care process or a hospital/source-specific surveillance process. This child makes that mechanism primary while preserving the parent’s strict no-prior-support gate. Persistent adjusted spread means prior surveillance and source capture do not explain the observed heterogeneity; strong attenuation supports an ascertainment-calibration explanation. Neither result establishes biological AKI, dialysis initiation, treatment response, or hospital quality.

The future study must newly produce a frozen cohort and event ledger, fitted matched models, H_raw_support, H_surv_support, A_surv, component/process spreads, held-out-hospital transport, uncertainty, calibration, and a supportive/adverse/inconclusive interpretation. Discovery has fitted no model and claims no fitted result.

## Evidence-supported and unresolved claims

The inspected frozen eICU 2.0 evidence supports data availability and timing semantics only. The relevant catalog entries expose ICU-keyed laboratory, treatment, intake/output, disposition, hospital, vital-sign, infusion, and severity fields. The parent’s read-only source audit found 40,060 renal/dialysis-related treatment rows across 8,284 stays and 58,473 dialysis/CRRT/hemofiltration-labelled intake-output rows across 6,159 stays. These are feasibility audits, not index-cohort event rates, onset rates, treatment rates, or associations.

The strongest supported claim is that eICU can represent a recorded creatinine-rise process, pre-rise and post-rise renal-support documentation, source type, ICU-relative timing, hospital identity, and competing unit exits. It does not establish that a treatment row means administration or initiation, that a flowsheet row means a procedure, that support was clinically indicated, that chronic dialysis was absent before the ICU stay, or that the recorded rise is biological AKI.

The unresolved claim is whether residual hospital heterogeneity in a newly documented post-rise support record remains after using only pre-rise information to measure the hospital’s renal-support surveillance and source-capture regime. The claim is observational and noncausal.

## Strict renal-support outcome and source-specific ascertainment

The primary outcome is the first eligible post-rise strict support record s in (r,E] from either source, among the no-prior-support risk set. It is a newly documented post-rise renal-support record, not proven renal replacement initiation or treatment receipt.

Treatment-source support is a row in treatment whose case-folded treatmentstring contains a high-specificity renal modality, including patterns equivalent to renal dialysis, hemodialysis, CVVHD, CVVH, CAVHD, peritoneal dialysis, or SLED. Exclude catheter insertion/access surgery, line flushes, generic renal terms, and ultrafiltration (fluid removal only) from the strict primary endpoint. The solver must freeze and print the exact regex dictionary and excluded matches before fitting.

Intake/output-source support is a row in intakeOutput whose cellpath or celllabel identifies: Dialysis (ml)|In, Dialysis (ml)|Out, CRRT Actual Pt Fluid Removed, CRRT Out, CRRT - UF removed, CRRT- UF removed, or Hemofiltration, with valid cellvaluenumeric or nonzero dialysistotal. Generic urine, catheter, Foley, voided-amount, and occurrence labels are not strict support. Exact duplicate treatmentid or intakeoutputid rows are deduplicated. Use the earliest qualifying offset, and never use rows after unit discharge.

Report union support, treatment-only support, intake/output-only support, both-source same-stay support, a broad fluid-removal sensitivity, and prevalent-support ongoing-or-new documentation. The primary source-specific robustness test compares the union with treatment-only and intake/output-only definitions. A result supported only by a broad or one-source definition is not promoted to a clinical-escalation conclusion.

Urine-labelled intakeOutput rows are an observation-process audit only. The schema has offsets and values but no uniformly valid collection interval or time-varying denominator. Do not call low urine output oliguria, apply a KDIGO urine criterion, or combine heterogeneous urine labels into an AKI endpoint.

## Leakage-controlled pre-rise surveillance score

The adjustment must use no post-rise information, no post-rise support outcome, and no confirmation-row information.

Construct a reference cohort from all parent-eligible first-rise stays before applying the primary no-prior-support exclusion. For each reference stay, using only strict records in ICU time [0,r], define P_T for treatment-source support, P_IO for intake/output-source support, and P_U = 1-(1-P_T)(1-P_IO).

For each primary index stay i in hospital h, calculate leave-one-stay-out hospital scores from reference stays in the same hospital, excluding i:
- S_T(h,-i): pre-rise treatment-source documentation proportion;
- S_IO(h,-i): pre-rise intake/output-source documentation proportion; and
- S_U(h,-i): union proportion.

Freeze numerators and denominators before modeling. Use source-specific scores as the primary adjustment, with the union score as a collinearity-aware secondary representation; do not enter all three together. Use a prespecified empirical-logit shrinkage only if needed for zero/one proportions, with the shrinkage constant fixed before fitting. Report numerators, denominators, score distributions, leave-one-out behavior, and unsupported hospitals. A score is not a patient exposure and is not interpreted as true dialysis need.

A secondary score is standardized over the development reference distribution of age, sex, ethnicity, admission source/unit type, baseline creatinine, first-rise value/delta, early MAP ledger, prior creatinine-test count, documented pressor timing, and admission severity. The unstandardized score is primary because it represents local source capture. If score estimation is unstable or nearly collinear with hospital effects, the adjusted estimand is inconclusive rather than regularized into a claim.

Patient-level pre-rise observation covariates frozen at r include qualifying creatinine-test count, time since preceding test, preceding-result metadata completeness, and source-specific support-surveillance indicators for the reference audit. No post-rise lab, support, treatment, or outcome field enters these covariates.

## Primary estimands

Let R_h be primary no-prior-support first-rise stays in supported development hospital h. Let q_raw,h be the 48-hour CIF of first union strict post-rise support with recorded unit death and alive unit exit competing and unknown-status exit censored.

Do not use post-rise values, support rows, actual outcomes, or confirmation-row metadata as predictors.

Define H_raw_support = Q90(q_raw,h)-Q10(q_raw,h), among hospitals with at least 50 primary first-rise stays and at least 10 union strict post-rise support events. For the surveillance-adjusted model, estimate each hospital’s standardized CIF at a common development surveillance-score distribution, yielding q_surv,h and H_surv_support. The primary attenuation is A_surv = H_raw_support-H_surv_support, with a joint bootstrap interval. Also report treatment-only and intake/output-only spreads, repeat opportunity, recorded confirmation after repeat, death, alive exit, and unknown-status processes. Fewer than 10 supported hospitals, fewer than 10 events in a required source-specific analysis, or inadequate score overlap makes that claim inconclusive.

The parent renal-escalation composite remains secondary: first distinct later recorded confirmation or strict post-rise support. Report its component CIFs and the parent recorded-confirmation spread; do not let the composite replace support-only estimation.

## Ordered multi-state alternative

Fit a piecewise-exponential ordered multi-state model on exactly the same cohort, source dictionaries, time grid, split, covariates, common standardization distribution, hospital support rule, and 500-replicate uncertainty procedure.

States/transitions are first rise -> repeat-test opportunity -> confirmed recorded rise; first rise -> treatment-source support; first rise -> intake/output-source support; first rise -> union support; and competing recorded unit death, alive unit exit, and unknown-status censoring. Keep first-rise-to-repeat, repeat-to-confirmation, and post-rise-to-support distinct. The six-hour confirmation gap and strict t<U boundary are structural. Use transition-specific shrunken hospital effects and the source-specific pre-rise surveillance score. Report whether residual variation is carried by source, repeat opportunity, confirmation after repeat, support after confirmation, or competing exit.

The additive baseline provides transparent standardized CIFs but represents downstream causes in parallel. The ordered model can reveal a mechanism the baseline loses: whether surveillance adjustment attenuates opportunity, source capture, post-rise support, or exit. Agreement on H_surv_support and A_surv is stronger evidence; disagreement is model uncertainty and must be reported.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires H_surv_support >= 5 percentage points with a two-sided hospital-cluster interval excluding zero; at least half the raw spread remains when estimable; direction and qualitative magnitude reproduce in both models; support is present under strict union and at least one source-specific definition without material treatment/flowsheet contradiction; the result is not explained by repeat opportunity, unit exit, score non-overlap, or a single status/clock sensitivity; and the qualitative conclusion transports to held-out hospitals. This supports residual heterogeneity in a newly documented support process after measurable prior surveillance adjustment, not biological AKI, dialysis initiation, treatment effectiveness, or policy.

Adverse evidence is a null/reversed adjusted spread, interval compatible with zero, attenuation below 50%, disappearance after source-specific or surveillance adjustment, support only in broad fluid removal, or treatment/flowsheet disagreement. Large raw but small adjusted spread supports an ascertainment explanation and argues for harmonization or adjudication before using support as a portable endpoint. Large confirmation spread with null support spread means confirmation heterogeneity has not been shown to alter downstream support documentation. A spread only in prevalent-support analysis is not incident escalation.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 10 supported hospitals, sparse source-specific events, unsupported or nearly collinear scores, overlap failures, failed convergence, bootstrap instability, dependence on NULL-status handling, clock/source disagreement, semantic instability, or held-out calibration failure. Inconclusive is not evidence for or against renal intervention.

A process falsification reports hospital heterogeneity in a short-gap repeat measurement obtained within 0–6 hours after the first rise, expected to reflect surveillance variation and not a clinical endpoint. Failure to reproduce a detectable process control can indicate inadequate observation-process measurement or model instability. Additional sensitivities are revised lab clock, 3-hour/12-hour confirmation gaps, raw versus bridged MAP, non-invasive MAP, unit boundary/status/location, source definitions, broad fluid-removal inclusion, and second hospital hash.

Computationally checkable outputs are source/schema hashes, headers, joins, timing boundaries, cohort flow, support dictionaries, pre-rise scores and no-leakage audit, deduplication, state ledgers, split integrity, fitted coefficients, standardized CIFs, intervals, calibration, transport, and sensitivity reproducibility. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine biological AKI, renal replacement initiation, administration, intent, chronic dialysis before ICU, urine meaning, and whether residual heterogeneity reflects case mix, need, practice, or documentation. Expert review is required before changing surveillance, referral, support documentation, or treatment policy. No result establishes a causal pressor effect, MAP target, quality ranking, renal benefit/harm, or biological renal null.

## Method selection and retained alternatives

The transparent additive model is mandatory because it gives directly auditable hospital-standardized CIFs, source-specific components, and a clear attenuation estimand. The ordered multi-state model is mandatory because the uncertainty is process ordering: surveillance may alter repeat testing, source capture, support after a rise, or competing exit. It reveals transitions and mechanisms that the additive model loses while using identical inputs, split, outcome, and uncertainty.

A learned longitudinal/sequence model is deferred, not prohibited. Revisit only if both prespecified models show reproducible held-out nonlinear miscalibration or missing temporal structure in the same support estimand, with enough events to preserve source-specific decomposition and hospital-held-out evaluation. It would use the same pre-rise event sequence and no post-outcome fields, and be compared on H_surv_support, A_surv, calibration, process decomposition, and transport, not only discrimination. Its fitting time and GPU requirement are unverified; no GPU is required for the proposed tabular models.

A urine-output/KDIGO branch is deferred until collection-duration semantics, completeness, and a valid time-varying denominator are externally validated. A causal pressor branch is deferred because infusionDrug documents timing/name but not administration, dose, intent, indication, or complete exposure. A biological-AKI or dialysis-initiation claim requires adjudication, renal reserve, validated treatment/procedure semantics, and external evidence.
