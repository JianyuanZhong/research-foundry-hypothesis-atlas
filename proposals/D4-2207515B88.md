# Earlier natural menopause and cardiac structural remodelling beyond measured haemodynamic load

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank women with self-reported natural menopause, does earlier age at menopause remain associated with left-ventricular myocardial mass and left-atrial structure/function after adjustment for age, body composition, and measured blood-pressure load?

The clinically important unresolved question is whether menopause timing marks a subgroup with earlier subclinical cardiac structural change, or whether the apparent association is explained by residual haemodynamic load and shared metabolic ageing. This matters because menopause timing itself is not modifiable, but if it identifies a subgroup with earlier cardiac remodelling, it could motivate earlier cardiovascular surveillance and risk-factor control.

The leading explanation is that earlier natural menopause is associated with higher cumulative blood pressure and/or adverse body composition, which in turn drives left-ventricular hypertrophy and left-atrial remodelling. The strongest rival is a sex-hormone-related biological transition: loss of oestrogen may independently alter cardiac structure. The available data cannot distinguish these mechanisms; the experiment tests whether the association survives adjustment for measured haemodynamic load, not whether a hormonal mechanism exists.

## Hypothesis

Earlier natural menopause is associated with higher left-ventricular myocardial mass and larger left-atrial volume/index after adjustment for age, body composition, and baseline blood pressure, and the association is not fully explained by repeat blood-pressure measurements.

## Population and temporal boundaries

- **Population:** UK Biobank participants with self-reported natural menopause (field 3581 = 1), age 40–70 at baseline, with cardiac MRI data available.
- **Baseline visit:** Assessment-centre date (field 53).
- **Follow-up:** Prospective outcomes only; diagnoses recorded before the baseline date are excluded.
- **Menopause timing:** Age at natural menopause (field 2724), with menopause status confirmed by field 3581 and corroborated by fields 3536/3546 where available.

## Analysis plan

### Model hierarchy

- **Model 0:** Unadjusted association of age at menopause (per 5-year earlier) with LV myocardial mass and LA volume/index.
- **Model 1:** + age, BMI, waist-hip ratio, smoking, socioeconomic status.
- **Model 2:** + baseline systolic and diastolic BP.
- **Model 3:** + repeat BP (subset with repeat measurements).
- **Model 4:** Sensitivity with CV diagnosis exclusion.

### Primary estimand

Adjusted association per 5-year earlier menopause in Model 2, with a key comparison to Model 3. A material attenuation (>30% reduction with CI crossing the null) is interpreted as evidence that the association is largely explained by measured haemodynamic load.

### Baseline and learned alternative

- **Baseline:** Linear regression with the Model 0–4 hierarchy.
- **Learned alternative:** Gradient-boosted regression (CPU-feasible) with held-out R-squared improvement and partial-dependence criteria. The alternative tests whether non-linear or interaction effects explain the association beyond the linear model.

### Split and evaluation

- **Split:** 80% training / 20% held-out validation for the gradient-boosted alternative.
- **Evaluation:** Held-out R-squared improvement over the linear baseline; partial-dependence plots for age at menopause.
- **Uncertainty:** 95% confidence intervals for all regression coefficients; bootstrap for the gradient-boosted alternative.

### Falsification criteria

- If the association attenuates >30% after adding baseline BP (Model 1 to Model 2) with the CI crossing the null, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the association is absent in Model 3 (repeat BP subset), the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the gradient-boosted alternative does not improve held-out R-squared over the linear baseline, the linear model is sufficient.

## Resource and time budget

- **CPU:** 4 cores, 16 GB RAM.
- **Time:** ~2 hours for the regression hierarchy; ~4 hours for the gradient-boosted alternative.
- **GPU:** Not required.

## Selection and deferral reason

The linear regression hierarchy is the primary analysis because it directly tests the competing explanation (haemodynamic load) with an explicit adjustment hierarchy. The gradient-boosted alternative is a secondary check for non-linear effects. If the linear model is sufficient, the gradient-boosted alternative is deferred.
