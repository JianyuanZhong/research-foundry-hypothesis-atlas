# Provisional HCC hypothesis: ordered early laboratory trajectory versus care-process artifact

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with an exact documented HCC diagnosis (肝细胞癌), an ordered 7-day within-assay trajectory of liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker measurements from admission through day 7 will add held-out predictive information about a subsequent non-routine inpatient procedure beyond an admission snapshot that includes treatment indication and documentation/care-process intensity. The primary claim is predictive, not causal.

The strongest currently supported claim is only that this HCC snapshot contains joinable encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, and procedure records with timed procedure support. The unresolved claim is whether temporal laboratory information predicts a later non-routine escalation proxy after accounting for routine HCC treatment and observation intensity. The procedure endpoint is a computable proxy, not a validated measure of death, ICU admission, clinical harm, or treatment failure.

## Clinical importance and rival explanation

If the trajectory improves prediction, it would justify expert review of whether serial monitoring could identify patients needing reassessment, while not claiming that a procedure is equivalent to deterioration. If it does not improve prediction, or improves only for routine procedures, the result would favor the rival explanation that apparent value reflects treatment indication, care intensity, or documentation rather than evolving patient state.

## Exact data binding and falsifiable test

Use HCC snapshot [source checksum] and only HCC rows. Bind encounters to diagnoses, labs, and procedures using the documented patient/encounter keys and admission/visit time fields; preserve source-specific identifiers and do not join across HCC, MIMIC, or eICU. The planned analysis will define a strict post-day-7/pre-discharge procedure window, freeze a versioned non-routine procedure dictionary and routine-token exclusions, and audit ambiguous, duplicate, invalid-time, and untimed records before fitting.

The baseline is a snapshot model using admission demographics, HCC diagnosis, admission laboratory values, treatment/documentation indicators, and encounter timing. The substantive alternative is an ordered temporal model over the same assays and time axis, with a prespecified missingness/time representation. Compare held-out performance and uncertainty, with timing-shifted and order-negative controls; the trajectory claim is supported only if the incremental estimate is clinically nontrivial and its uncertainty excludes a trivial margin, while the negative controls do not show comparable gains.

## Uncertainties and missing evidence

Procedure semantics require clinical adjudication; available procedure text cannot establish that a code means deterioration or harm. Laboratory units, assay comparability, missingness mechanisms, and external validation are not established by the source catalog. No fitted result is claimed here. A supportive result establishes predictive association, not mechanism or treatment benefit; an adverse result weakens the temporal-monitoring rationale; an imprecise or null result is inconclusive and should trigger endpoint/assay refinement rather than a causal conclusion.

## Method alternatives

A simple snapshot model is the required baseline because it preserves interpretability and isolates incremental temporal information. The ordered temporal alternative is scientifically substantive because it can reveal whether direction, slope, persistence, or timing of change matters; it is not justified by a small predictive gain alone. CPU tabular fitting is appropriate for the planned baseline and bounded temporal model; GPU use is unnecessary unless a later solver chooses a more complex sequence model. The future solver must newly fit both models, estimate held-out incremental performance and uncertainty, and produce endpoint/timing audit outputs.

This draft is provisional. It deliberately records the unresolved endpoint semantics, missing clinical adjudication, and absent fitted results rather than inventing evidence.
