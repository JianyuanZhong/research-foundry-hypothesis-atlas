# Discharge synthetic-function recovery versus injury resolution after TACE in HCC

## Status, strongest supported claim, and unresolved hypothesis

This is an episode-7 substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. It is an observational prognostic experiment, not a treatment-effect, discharge-policy, or clinical-utility study.

The strongest claim supported by the evidence actually inspected is narrower: hepatic reserve and clinically important post-locoregional-treatment complications are plausible and clinically relevant. The frozen full text of Sanai et al. (*Scientific Reports* 2026, DOI `10.1038/s41598-026-54466-4`, PMCID `PMC13457603`, source ID `[source checksum]`) studied TACE/TARE at four tertiary centers and used ascites, variceal bleeding, or portosystemic encephalopathy in a decompensation definition. This supports plausibility, not the incremental value of a routinely recorded, time-ordered laboratory recovery signal in this HCC snapshot. The current Europe PMC search is preserved in support file `episode7_hcc_support.md`; search output is discovery evidence, not a novelty certificate.

The unresolved primary claim is:

> Among adults with HCC who have a first observed TACE episode, remain in the index encounter for at least 96 hours, and are discharged, does discharge-proximal non-recovery of synthetic-function assays (bilirubin, albumin, and INR), measured after the early post-TACE window, add held-out information about an inpatient encounter within 0–7 days beyond baseline reserve and early post-TACE response?

The substantive advance is a clinically interpretable contrast between transient biochemical injury and persistent discharge-time synthetic-function non-recovery. It preserves the broader parent estimand (early trajectory and a 30-day subsequent admission) so a sparse discharge-complete subset cannot silently replace the broader question. It also tests whether the discharge signal remains when laboratory-recording intensity and calendar/department practice are challenged. The estimand is incremental prognostic information; it is not the effect of delaying discharge or intensifying monitoring.

## Exposure blocks and outcomes

The primary exposure contrast is the discharge-minus-early change in the core synthetic block. Within each training fold, standardize each assay by training-fold median/IQR only. For interpretability, pre-specify direction labels (higher bilirubin or INR and lower albumin are worse within that assay); do not convert them to a validated score or clinical threshold. Report assay-specific changes, block coefficients/intervals, and a prespecified joint block test rather than hiding the result in one composite.

D0 contains baseline age, sex, index department, three baseline core medians, preceding-365-day encounter count, and recorded prior indicators for cirrhosis, portal-vein thrombosis, ascites, diabetes, and liver surgery generated only from date-aligned encounters, diagnoses, and procedures. D0 also contains the early core medians and early-minus-baseline changes. These are recorded utilization/code markers, not adjudicated disease.

D1 adds discharge-minus-early core changes. D2 is a bounded sensitivity model that adds discharge-minus-early ALT/AST changes, when complete, to ask whether the synthetic signal is merely a generic laboratory-injury signal. A prespecified discordance table classifies direction patterns (synthetic block non-recovery with improving versus non-improving ALT/AST); it is descriptive unless each cell has at least 20 events. Because units are unavailable, “recovery” means direction of the observed assay change after fold-contained robust standardization, not normalization to a clinical reference range.

The primary outcome is any subsequent inpatient encounter within 0–7 days after discharge. Secondary outcomes are any 30-day subsequent inpatient encounter; the mutually exclusive 8–30-day encounter; and the liver-code signal among later encounters containing one of `腹水`/`腹腔积液`, `肝性脑病`/`肝昏迷`, `食管胃底静脉曲张`, or `消化道出血`. Report each component separately and call this a diagnosis-coded signal, not adjudicated hepatic decompensation. If a later encounter has a TACE-class procedure, report it descriptively, but do not call the return planned or unplanned.

## Analysis, baselines, and uncertainty

Use patient-level five-fold cross-validation within discovery buckets 0–79 and a chronological split (2010–2022 derivation; 2023–2025-12-02 test). All label-dependent filtering, missingness decisions, imputation, scaling, feature selection, and tuning must be confined to training folds. The primary model is logistic regression for D0 versus D1; a bounded elastic-net logistic model is a secondary model-form check on the identical feature blocks. A shallow gradient-boosted model is optional only as a prespecified sensitivity, never as the scientific endpoint.

Report out-of-fold Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope, IQR-scaled odds ratios for the discharge block, risk differences at prespecified covariate patterns, global likelihood-ratio comparison, and patient-bootstrap 95% intervals. The key estimand is the D1-minus-D0 held-out performance contrast for 0–7-day events; the 30-day broad analysis remains primary if the nested cohort or event count is sparse.

Report complete-case results and a missingness-indicator sensitivity. Use inverse-observation-weighting only as a sensitivity, with weights learned within training folds. Record cohort attrition from all eligible TACE patients to complete baseline/early and to complete discharge windows. Require at least 20 events before inferential modeling of any primary outcome or discordance stratum; below that threshold publish event counts and treat effect/interaction estimates as inconclusive. The liver-code signal will usually be descriptive unless it passes the same gate.

Observation intensity is a threat, not a primary adjustment target: report laboratory count, hours covered, maximum gaps, pre-discharge lab density, index length of stay, department, and calendar strata. Do not adjust the primary D1 model for post-index medication/order rows or later labs. Use density-stratified and observation-weighted sensitivities to test whether an apparent gain is recording intensity rather than recovery. Department/calendar results are stability checks, not causal site effects.

## Falsification and interpretation

A supportive broad trajectory result requires a global LR p<0.05, optimism-corrected out-of-fold Brier improvement with a 95% bootstrap interval excluding zero, consistent direction/calibration in the chronological test, and no dependence solely on the high-capacity model, dense recording, or timestamp order.

A supportive nested discharge result additionally requires the D1 held-out Brier improvement over D0 for the 0–7-day outcome, the event gate, stability under density and missingness sensitivities, and no materially similar D1 gain in a pre-index placebo trajectory made from the same assay windows shifted earlier. A gain confined to the 8–30-day outcome weakens the discharge-proximal interpretation. A gain in D1 but not D2 supports only an incremental coded prognostic signal for the synthetic block conditional on the tested injury block; it does not establish biological mediation.

Call the result adverse/falsified when the incremental gain is absent, reverses chronologically, is explained by observation density or calendar/department selection, disappears after timestamp shuffling/reversal within participant, or improves in-sample metrics while worsening held-out Brier/calibration. A positive procedure-associated/non-procedure comparison is not required for this proposal and will not be used to manufacture an acute-vulnerability claim.

Call the result inconclusive if the TACE dictionary or assay labels cannot be reproduced, duplicate-key or time checks fail, missingness is strongly selection-dependent, the discharge cohort or outcome fails its event gate, procedure/outcome timing cannot be resolved, or uncertainty cannot distinguish no incremental information from a clinically meaningful gain. Harbor must write a conclusion-to-output ledger linking every reported claim to computed outputs.

Supportive computation would justify a prospective cohort with adjudicated hepatic decompensation, reliable scheduling and outpatient capture, stage/tumor burden, treatment dose/territory, and a predefined monitoring or discharge intervention. Adverse computation would caution against using routine trajectories as a stand-alone escalation rule. Neither result establishes safe discharge, unplannedness, treatment causality, mortality, tumor response/recurrence, clinical benefit/harm, or transportability. Those claims require clinical adjudication, missing evidence, or another study.
