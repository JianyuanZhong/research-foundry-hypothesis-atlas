# Overlap- and trajectory-calibrated post-shock IV-loop target trial

**Parent:** `[prior hypothesis]`

This proposal independently integrates and repairs the Episode-11 trajectory/overlap draft; it is published against the Lead-assigned parent.

## Episode-11 substantive repair

This successor preserves the parent’s corrected discovery partition, two-clock temporal contract, unit-safe cumulative fluid phenotype and weight hierarchy, delivered 24-hour ICU loop strategies, clone-censor framework, observation-bounded day-7 respiratory endpoint, mortality/RRT safety gates, exact MIMIC snapshot bindings, and evidence limits.

The remaining major threat is confounding by indication and clinical trajectory. At hour 48, clinicians may give a loop because a patient is improving and can tolerate diuresis, or because the patient is deteriorating from fluid overload; both the indication and the counter-indication evolve over hours. A treatment/no-treatment contrast among all eligible patients can therefore be badly biased, and a model can conceal rather than solve the problem when treatment positivity is poor. This child makes the estimand explicitly conditional on measured clinical equipoise and makes trajectory/adherence diagnostics decision gates.

## Evidence boundary, unresolved claim, and clinical advance

The strongest available evidence supports that protocolized conservative fluid management with active deresuscitation can remove fluid without a statistically detectable worsening of lactate or kidney-injury markers in a modest randomized RADAR-2 secondary analysis; clinically important effects were not excluded. McMullan et al., “The Effects of Conservative Fluid Management and Active Deresuscitation…,” *Critical Care Explorations* 2026;8:e1404, DOI `10.1097/CCE.0000000000001404`, frozen source `[source checksum]`, reports similar lactate and no statistically detectable between-group differences in AKIRisk or urinary cystatin-C, but emphasizes modest sample size and imprecision. That randomized strategy is not the same as an observational single-loop initiation decision.

The unresolved clinical claim is:

> Among invasively ventilated adults at ICU hour 48 who have recovered from vasopressor-treated shock, have unit-safe cumulative balance at least +50 mL/kg, retain urine output, potassium at least 3.0 mmol/L, and lie in measured treatment-equipoise after adjustment for their recent respiratory, hemodynamic, renal and fluid trajectories, initiating an actually delivered ICU IV loop within the next 24 hours increases the probability of being alive and durably liberated from invasive ventilation at day 7 by more than 5 percentage points versus withholding loops for that 24-hour window, without excess recorded 28-day death or observed new RRT.

The substantive advance is a transportable decision-boundary analysis: it estimates the effect only where MIMIC contains both treated and untreated trajectory-matched patients, quantifies how much of the nominal cohort is outside positivity, and reports whether a benefit-harm threshold is met. It does not turn observational residual confounding into a recommendation. Resolution would prioritize or de-prioritize a pragmatic trial of loop initiation in the explicitly defined equipoise population.

The MIMIC experiment cannot establish actual bedside awareness, unmeasured goals of care, clinician intent, exchangeability, renal safety, standard care, post-ICU outcomes, or a treatment recommendation. A positive result is trial-prioritization evidence only.

### Treatment strategies, adherence, and the repaired estimand

Strategy A is first qualifying actually delivered ICU IV furosemide item 221794/228340 or bumetanide 229639 in `(t0,t0+24h]`. Strategy B is no actual furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide or ethacrynic acid by any route in that interval. Primary ICU delivery requires valid clinical start/end/store times, accepted delivered status and a positive dose convertible to mg or positive mg/hour over positive duration. Exclude canceled, rewritten, not-started, order-only, nonpositive and unknown-unit rows. Deduplicate only overlapping same `orderid/linkorderid` segments.

For any-route washout and deviation, join `hosp/emar_detail` to `hosp/emar` on `subject_id,emar_id,emar_seq`, inherit `hadm_id,charttime,storetime`, and accept only actual-administration text with positive dose/product amount. Blank route is route-unknown, never proof of IV. Same-drug ICU/eMAR events within 15 minutes are concordant, not duplicate doses. Report concordant-ICU and explicit-IV-eMAR sensitivities.

Clone each eligible person to both strategies at t0. In each clone, censor at the first deviation through 24 hours; death during the window is retained as failure. ICU discharge before A initiation censors A; alive discharge with medication observation ending censors B. Post-hour-24 care is unrestricted. Report per-protocol effect only with inverse-probability adherence/censor weights; do not interpret the crude initiator contrast as causal.

The primary estimand is a 24-hour sustained-strategy per-protocol risk difference in ALDL7 among the **measured-overlap population**: subjects whose estimated probability of A initiation conditional on all pre-t0 covariates and trajectory history is between 0.10 and 0.90. The 0.10/0.90 rule is fixed before outcome inspection. The all-eligible intention-to-treat-style association is secondary and must not be used to claim bedside benefit if overlap is poor.

Construct the t0 propensity model using only decision-available pre-t0 information:

- demographics, admission type/year/unit/service, prior completed-admission comorbidity and prior loop exposure;
- weight, cumulative balance and its last 6-hour slope;
- urine output rate and slope, creatinine/lactate/potassium and slopes;
- MAP and MAP slope, recent vasopressor dose/presence and time since cessation;
- FiO2/PEEP/other support variables available from charted ICU items, sedation, crystalloid/albumin volume, and prior oxygenation;
- validated pre-t0 radiology flags only if the report’s clinical and store times meet the two-clock rule.

Use clinically meaningful summaries at t0, t0-3h, t0-6h and t0-12h, with missingness indicators and no future data. Do not use treatment-response variables after treatment begins, index-discharge diagnoses, future notes or a variable defined by the primary endpoint.

The model must report calibration, overlap histograms, positivity by trajectory stratum, and standardized differences before/after weighting. Use overlap weights for the baseline effect and stabilized inverse-probability-of-adherence/censor weights for the sustained strategy, truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles. A doubly robust weighted outcome model is primary; unweighted and covariate-adjusted models are checks. Hourly clone-censor models update only with lagged decision-available MAP/pressor, urine/balance, creatinine/lactate/potassium, FiO2/PEEP/support, sedation, crystalloid/albumin and imaging. Treatment-confounder feedback is handled by censor weights; never condition on an intermediate affected by the loop as if it were a baseline confounder.

Because propensity adjustment cannot remove unmeasured indication, add two prespecified stress tests:

1. **Trajectory restriction:** repeat the primary estimate in strata defined before treatment by respiratory slope (FiO2/PEEP/support), renal trajectory (creatinine/urine), and hemodynamic stability. Require each reported stratum to meet overlap/ESS rules. Heterogeneity is descriptive unless interaction uncertainty is acceptable.
2. **Quantitative residual-bias analysis:** report the minimum prevalence-difference/odds-ratio strength of one unmeasured binary “clinical readiness/trajectory” factor, associated with both loop initiation and ALDL7, needed to move the ALDL7 risk difference from its point estimate to 0 and to +0.05. Present a grid, not a single sensitivity value, and include negative-control pre-t0 slopes. This is a robustness statement, not proof of exchangeability.

Treatment probability below 0.01 or above 0.99 in >10% of eligible person-hours, any arm with fewer than 100 adherent subjects, effective sample size (ESS) <75 in either arm, or a large proportion outside overlap makes the per-protocol decision claim infeasible/inconclusive. No amount of model complexity or GPU use rescues failed positivity.

### Benefit-harm decision display

Do not combine incomparable outcomes into an unvalidated clinical utility score as though MIMIC estimated patient preferences. Display the ALDL7 benefit and death/RRT harms separately, then provide a prespecified threshold grid.

For a user-supplied harm weight `lambda_D` (ALDL7-equivalent loss per death) and `lambda_R` (ALDL7-equivalent loss per new observed RRT), calculate the conservative net-benefit bound:

`NB_L(lambda_D,lambda_R) = RD_L(ALDL7) - lambda_D * RD_U(death28) - lambda_R * RD_U(RRT7)`.

Use an explicitly labeled exploratory grid `lambda_D in {5,10,20}`, `lambda_R in {1,2,5}`; do not call these validated utilities. Also report the minimum ALDL7 benefit required to offset each observed harm bound, with uncertainty. The clinical decision gate remains the parent’s separate conservative requirements: ALDL7 `RD_L > +0.05` with 95% lower limit >0, upper 95% excess recorded death <+0.03, and upper 95% excess observed RRT <+0.05 under both date brackets. NB can be “favorable,” “unfavorable,” or “not identified” only within the displayed weight grid; it cannot establish net clinical benefit or a treatment recommendation.

### Falsification and result branches

Before fitting the outcome model, emit sequential attrition after partition for every criterion; final strategy assignments; grace/deviation events; outcome classes; missingness; units/status exclusions; route concordance; overlap; weights; ESS; and adherence probabilities.

The following are falsification or validity checks:

- eventual A versus B assignment must not predict pre-t0 six-hour creatinine, urine or MAP slopes after trajectory adjustment; standardized difference >0.10 or multiplicity-controlled P<0.05 blocks causal interpretation;
- baseline standardized differences after overlap weighting must be <=0.10 for prespecified trajectory variables;
- the direction of 24-hour balance/urine changes should be compatible with actual loop delivery, but these are exposure-process controls, not clinical benefit;
- estimates must be stable under 6/12-hour washout, 6/12/24-hour initiation, never-treated, balance +30/+100 and continuous balance, complete-hour capture, drain/irrigant exclusions, FinishedRunning-only input, planned-only extubation, 24/48/72-hour durability, event/end corroboration, cardiac-surgery/heart-failure exclusions, KDIGO strata, no albumin co-initiation, calendar era, first ICU per admission, ICU/eMAR exposure concordance, and alternative overlap thresholds 0.05/0.95;
- report the prespecified pre-t0 physiologic slopes as negative-control outcomes. Do not use future or after-window treatment as a negative-control exposure because evolving clinical course can determine it.

**Supportive:** In the measured-overlap, adherent analysis, `RD_L` point estimate is >+0.05 and its 95% lower limit >0; upper 95% excess recorded 28-day death is <+0.03; upper 95% excess observed day-7 RRT is <+0.05 under both date brackets; process controls have expected direction; positivity, ESS, temporal falsification and missingness checks pass; and no material conflict occurs across trajectory, fluid, exposure or endpoint sensitivities. A favorable NB grid strengthens prioritization only. The result supports external validation and a randomized trial, not prescribing loops.

**Adverse:** The 95% upper limit of `RD_U` is <=0, or the lower bound of mortality harm is >=+0.03, or the lower bound of observed RRT harm is >=+0.05 under the specified brackets. Better balance or urine cannot override durable-liberation failure, death or RRT harm. A clearly adverse result in a well-overlapped, well-observed stratum is clinically informative but still observational.

**Inconclusive:** `RD_L` fails support while `RD_U` permits benefit; the identification interval is wide; either safety gate fails; final successes/arm overlap/ESS are sparse; adherence or positivity fails; date brackets disagree; trajectory restriction materially changes the conclusion; the quantitative residual-bias grid shows modest unmeasured confounding could reverse the sign; temporal falsification fails; or the two-clock, fluid, exposure or endpoint sensitivities conflict. Nonsignificance is not equivalence or safety.

## What changed and what remains uncertain

Changed: the parent’s broad causal contrast is now explicitly secondary; the primary decision estimand is restricted to measured overlap and uses trajectory-calibrated baseline adjustment plus time-varying adherence/censor weights. Positivity, ESS, trajectory-stratum stability and a quantitative residual-indication grid are hard interpretation gates. The principal uncertainty remains unmeasured readiness/intent and incomplete observation after ICU transfer; if those threats are material, the correct result is inconclusive and the next step is adjudication or a randomized trial.
