# Outcome-blind ascertainment and partial-identification stress test for frozen HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B validation

## Purpose and substantive repair

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`, `[prior hypothesis]`, and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the frozen scientific schema: a noncausal prognostic comparison of a locked model using remote baseline `B` with versus without contemporaneous pre-repeat assay `P` for the subsequent assay `Y`, among selected adult HCC repeat-TACE records. It does not estimate a treatment effect, treatment response, safety, utility, clinical benefit, or patient-level decision rule.

The strongest supported evidence remains narrow. The executed full-source audit for the parent reproduced the frozen reconstruction and found one adequately supported future block (2022); its observed complete-triplet results support, at most, a local short-horizon prognostic association in that selected calendar mixture. The 2023 and 2024 blocks have insufficient systemic support and substantial loss of post-event assay observation, so favorable observed-triplet estimates there cannot establish transport.

The remaining substantive weakness is that support/observation diagnostics identify selection but do not quantify how much unobserved `Y` could alter the model contrast. The missing-outcome proposals also left this computation unexecuted and did not fully resolve the definition of a common-displacement tipping point or the distinction between sharp MSE bounds and bounds on an RMSE difference. This successor makes the missing-`Y` layer executable without changing the primary estimand, and makes ascertainment diagnostics explicitly descriptive rather than evidence of clinical follow-up.

The falsifiable question is:

> Among outcome-blind `B/P`-eligible repeat events, does the observed incremental predictive value of `P` remain favorable under an explicitly stated assay-specific support set for unobserved `Y`, and do observed assay-observation patterns localize rather than conceal the calendar-era loss of outcome information?

A bounded result is a sensitivity statement about the same prognostic contrast. It is not recovery of missing outcomes and does not assume missing at random.

## Computable partial-identification missing-Y stress test

Run this layer for every assay and origin, retaining every future `BP_eligible` episode, including those without Y. Fit M0 and M1 only on pre-origin observed complete triplets, freeze predictions `p0_i` and `p1_i`, and never refit on completed values.

### Support set and unsupported branch

Set `L_train` and `U_train` to the minimum and maximum observed training Y values for that assay, after the exact finite numeric parser. Construct the bound before inspecting future Y. If the training range is degenerate, or any *observed* future Y is outside `[L_train,U_train]`, label the temporal bound `unsupported` and do not widen it. A full-source assay range may be emitted only as a separately labeled, non-temporal descriptive check. If no future observed Y exists for a block, use the training observed-Y mean as the reference for tipping-point displacement and label the block low-information; do not invent a future observed mean.

### Row-level sharp error and contrast bounds

For observed Y, calculate

`e0_i = (Y_i-p0_i)^2`, `e1_i = (Y_i-p1_i)^2`, and `d_i = e0_i-e1_i`.

For a missing Y constrained only to the mathematical scenario `Y_i in [L_train,U_train]`, calculate model-specific squared-error bounds:

- `e_j,min = (clip(pj_i, L_train, U_train)-pj_i)^2`;
- `e_j,max = max((L_train-pj_i)^2, (U_train-pj_i)^2)`.

For the M0-versus-M1 improvement contrast, use the sharper direct affine expression

`d_i(y) = (y-p0_i)^2-(y-p1_i)^2 = 2*y*(p1_i-p0_i) + p0_i^2-p1_i^2`.

Thus missing-row contrast extrema occur at `L_train` and `U_train`; sum observed terms and these extrema to produce overall and arm-specific `Delta_lower` and `Delta_upper` for `SSE(M0)-SSE(M1)` over all BP-eligible rows. Also report lower/upper MSE for each model and missing counts/fractions. These intervals are sharp for the stated rectangular support scenario at the SSE/MSE level.

Do not incorrectly call a separately combined interval for `sqrt(MSE0)-sqrt(MSE1)` sharp. Report the primary standardized RMSE gain only on observed complete triplets. If desired, provide the conservative derived RMSE-difference enclosure `[sqrt(MSE0_lower)-sqrt(MSE1_upper), sqrt(MSE0_upper)-sqrt(MSE1_lower)]`, clearly labeled conservative and non-primary; do not standardize it by an unidentified test SD. A positive bounded `Delta` favors M1 because it means lower M1 squared error.

Use three locked endpoint patterns for an auditable table: all missing Y at `L_train`; all at `U_train`; and per-row least-favorable endpoint for the M1-versus-M0 improvement (the endpoint minimizing `d_i`). These are mathematical stress scenarios, not plausible imputations. Preserve row counts, arm counts, assay, origin, and block in aggregate outputs only.

### Deterministic tipping point

Let `m_ref` be the mean of observed future Y when at least one future Y is observed; otherwise let `m_ref` be the pre-origin observed training-Y mean and mark the reference as training-derived. For all missing rows simultaneously define

`y_i(delta) = clip(m_ref + delta, L_train, U_train)`.

Substitute these values only into the all-BP-eligible `Delta(delta)=SSE(M0)-SSE(M1)`. Find every root in `delta in [L_train-m_ref, U_train-m_ref]`, respecting clipping breakpoints; solve each resulting linear/quadratic segment analytically (and verify by deterministic endpoint evaluation). Report the root with smallest absolute `|delta|`; ties use the smallest delta. If no root exists, report sign-robust under this common-displacement scenario. If `Delta(0)=0`, report zero tipping displacement. This is a mathematical sensitivity coordinate, not an estimate of missing Y or a clinical plausibility claim. Also report whether the bounded endpoint interval crosses zero; crossing zero is selection-sensitive/unidentified under the stated support, regardless of a favorable observed-case result.

## Interpretation and falsification

Supportive evidence requires all reconstruction and clock assertions, both outcome-blind and complete-triplet arm gates, a prespecified primary RMSE result in an adequately supported block, no material ascertainment warning, and a positive all-BP-eligible bounded `Delta` throughout the declared training-range scenario. This supports only a selected short-horizon prognostic association in that calendar mixture under a mathematical outcome-support sensitivity analysis.

Adverse evidence is a nonpositive primary contrast in an adequately supported block, repeated M1 degradation, or a bounded `Delta` sign reversal. Selection-sensitive evidence is a favorable observed-case result with a bounded interval crossing zero, a tipping point near a support boundary, or a strong observation gradient. Inconclusive evidence includes failed arm gates, unsupported training range, unidentified test SD, low observation/transport ESS, influential cases, non-estimable small cells, or dependence on the non-temporal full-source range. Based on the executed parent audit, 2022 may remain locally supportive; 2023 and 2024 remain transport-inconclusive unless all gates genuinely pass. No favorable bound upgrades to multi-era transport.

A computation can verify source hashes, complete scans, headers, joins, duplicates, event reconstruction, exact assay/event clocks, block boundaries, outcome-blind risk-set construction, ascertainment counts, endpoint arithmetic, tipping-point arithmetic, and preservation of the primary complete-triplet fit. It cannot establish assay units/reference ranges, clinical severity, imaging response, treatment administration or intent, external-care capture, mortality, hepatic failure, clinical follow-up completion, causal effects, utility, actionability, or patient benefit. Those require clinical adjudication and an independent temporal/external/prospective cohort with validated assay metadata and reliable outcome capture.
