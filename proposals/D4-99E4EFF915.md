# Cystatin-C reclassification of renal vulnerability in people with preserved creatinine eGFR and low grip

## Episode 8 decision and unresolved claim

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`. This is a substantive successor, not a new topic or a reproduction of a reference paper. The parent established a well-bound association question and a five-year competing-risk interaction. This child makes the clinical decision uncertainty explicit: whether the discordance signal adds enough calibrated, held-out risk information beyond creatinine-based assessment to identify a subgroup in whom confirmatory cystatin-C testing or closer renal surveillance could reasonably be studied.

The strongest evidence available here is narrower than that decision. The locally read Haines et al. critical-illness study (the readable article at `references/expert-seeds/papers/kidney-function/article.readable.txt`) compared creatinine, cystatin C, measured GFR and muscle ultrasound in 38 mechanically ventilated ICU patients. It supports a physiological concern that creatinine-based eGFR can overestimate filtration during severe muscle loss. It does not establish a community UKB association, the grip interaction, hospital-code validity, measured-filtration accuracy in UKB, or benefit from ordering cystatin C.

The falsifiable claim is:

> Among UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 with apparently preserved baseline creatinine eGFR (primary: race-free eGFRcr >=90 mL/min/1.73 m2), valid baseline cystatin C and bilateral grip, and no previously recorded paired N17/N18 hospital diagnosis, lower cystatin-C-minus-creatinine eGFR discordance has a stronger association with the five-year cumulative incidence of a first subsequently recorded paired N17 hospital diagnosis at lower grip strength. Adding discordance to an eGFRcr-only model should improve calibrated five-year risk information and prespecified risk-threshold reclassification, if the interaction is real.

The population definition is intentionally narrower than the parent’s broad cohort because it tests the clinical situation in which routine creatinine eGFR appears reassuring. An eGFRcr 60–89 subgroup is a prespecified secondary transportability analysis, not a replacement chosen after seeing results.

A supportive result would justify prospective validation of selective cystatin-C measurement or surveillance in this subgroup. It would not show that cystatin C measures true filtration better in this cohort, that low grip is muscle mass, that a test changes treatment, or that the association is causal. An adverse result would weaken the case that this discordance identifies missed renal vulnerability when creatinine eGFR is preserved. A risk-model null with an association signal would mean statistical association has not translated into useful incremental prediction.

## Population and temporal boundaries

After the provenance gates and one-to-one joins:

1. Keep age 40–69, valid sex, valid BMI, valid bilateral initial grip, valid creatinine/cystatin C, and a valid `53-0.0` date. Use mean left/right grip in kg; do not impute a missing hand into the primary exposure.
2. Convert creatinine from micromol/L to mg/dL only after unit verification and retain cystatin C in mg/L. Compute the frozen race-free CKD-EPI 2021 creatinine and standalone cystatin-C equations below. Keep primary eGFRcr >=90; report the prespecified eGFRcr 60–89 secondary cohort separately.
3. Exclude any valid paired N17 or N18 code/date on or before the index date. An index-day code is temporally ambiguous and is excluded. This absence criterion covers only these linked hospital arrays, not outpatient diagnoses or unlinked disease.
4. Exclude death on or before index. Follow each participant from the assessment/index date to the first verified post-index paired N17 event, first death, the verified administrative end, or five years, whichever comes first. Death is a competing event, not routine censoring. A same-day N17/death is death-first in the primary ordering and N17-first in a sensitivity.
5. Require at least 100 verified post-index N17 events and at least 20 participants in each D/grip quadrant in the primary cohort. If the primary cohort fails, report only an inconclusive support result; do not silently broaden it. The secondary 60–89 cohort has its own support report.

Primary outcome is the first subsequently recorded, equal-suffix paired N17 hospital diagnosis. N18 is a prespecified secondary outcome using the same timing and competing-death rules. These are recorded hospital-code endpoints, not KDIGO AKI, CKD chronicity, outpatient disease, or complete renal-event ascertainment.

## Exposure and estimands

Freeze, before outcome fitting, the race-free CKD-EPI 2021 equations:

```
eGFRcr = 142 * min(Scr/k,1)^alpha * max(Scr/k,1)^(-1.200)
         * 0.9938^Age * 1.012^Female
```

with female `k=.7, alpha=-.241`, male `k=.9, alpha=-.302`, and

```
eGFRcys = 133 * min(Scys/.8,1)^(-.499) * max(Scys/.8,1)^(-1.328)
          * .996^Age * .932^Female.
```

Define `D=eGFRcys-eGFRcr`. Standardize eGFRcr, D and grip using training-partition parameters only. The prespecified exposure direction is lower D and lower grip; no threshold is selected from outcomes.

The primary scientific estimand is the five-year competing-risk risk-scale interaction in the primary eGFRcr >=90 cohort:

```
I5 = CIF_N17(-1,-1) - CIF_N17(+1,-1)
     - CIF_N17(-1,+1) + CIF_N17(+1,+1)
```

where coordinates are standardized D and grip at -1/+1 SD and risks are integrated over the observed age, sex and BMI distribution at a fixed reference eGFRcr within the primary range. Report all four CIFs, component contrasts, I5 and participant-bootstrap 95% intervals. The interaction is a hypothesis test, not a clinical threshold.

The decision-information estimand is incremental performance of:

- B0: eGFRcr, age, sex and BMI;
- B1: B0 plus grip;
- B2: B1 plus D;
- B3: B2 plus D×grip.

Fit cause-specific N17 and death hazards and obtain five-year Aalen–Johansen/model-based competing-risk risks. Compare B1 with B2/B3 using held-out IPCW Brier score, integrated Brier score, calibration slope/intercept, time-dependent discrimination, four-grid CIF calibration and decision curves/net benefit at a prespecified descriptive threshold grid of 1%, 2% and 5%. Report absolute changes and bootstrap intervals. These thresholds are sensitivity markers for risk stratification, not validated treatment thresholds or evidence of clinical utility. The primary claim of incremental information requires improvement beyond B1 that is stable, calibrated and not confined to one arbitrary threshold.

## Substantive learned alternative

Fit a same-input shallow discrete-time competing-risk model with separate N17 and death hazards. Inputs are exactly eGFRcr, D, grip, age, sex and BMI; no outcomes, post-index variables or repeat biomarker instances may enter. Use a frozen participant-level 60/20/20 train/validation/test split. Estimate transformations, imputation, hyperparameters and censoring weights in training only; use depth 2–3, fixed leaf-size/regularization limits and validation-selected iterations. Evaluate on the held-out test set with the same I5, four CIFs, IPCW Brier/integrated Brier, calibration, time-dependent discrimination, net benefit and participant-bootstrap intervals used for B0–B3.

The transparent nested model is scientifically necessary because it estimates the interpretable D×grip risk contrast and shows whether D adds anything to creatinine-only assessment. The learned alternative can reveal a threshold, saturation or curved D-by-grip region that one log-linear interaction loses. It is not selected for a small AUC improvement: it must change an interpretable held-out risk contrast, remain calibrated and supported, and agree in direction with the transparent estimand. CPU-only fitting is adequate (at most 4 CPUs, 8 GiB RAM, approximately two hours plus bounded bootstrap); no GPU is needed or rewarded. If support is too sparse for the learned model, defer it and retain the transparent estimate; if B2/B3 is not identifiable, the whole decision-information claim is inconclusive.

## Falsification and robustness

Prespecify:

- strict equal-suffix code/date pairing and same-day death-ordering;
- N17-only versus N17/N18 baseline exclusion;
- the eGFRcr >=90 primary cohort versus the separate 60–89 secondary cohort;
- a two-year survivor/landmark analysis, explicitly labelled as a changed population;
- raw-biomarker rather than equation-derived sensitivity, and repeat biomarkers only if their timing is independently verified;
- within sex-by-age-stratum permutation of D with a frozen seed. A persistent I5 or incremental score after permutation indicates leakage or implementation error;
- a nonrenal K35 control only if its exact code semantics, date pairing and event support are verified. A comparable K35 interaction weakens renal specificity; failure to support K35 leaves specificity unresolved;
- complete-case versus prespecified missingness/selection weighting, with all selection flow reported.

Supportive evidence requires all provenance and support gates, positive I5 with a 95% interval excluding zero, persistence under strict timing and landmarking, B2/B3 improvement over B1 in held-out calibrated risk scores, and no comparable supported K35 interaction. Adverse evidence includes a precise null or opposite I5, no incremental information over B1, disappearance under timing/landmark controls, comparable K35 association, or an unstable/poorly calibrated learned surface. Inconclusive evidence includes failed unit/code/date/death/cutoff/index gates, inadequate events or quadrants, unsupported risk cells, incomplete follow-up, or non-identifiable/calibration-failing models.

Computationally checkable claims are joins, duplicate handling, transformations, cohort flow, event ordering, CIFs, interaction estimates, prediction metrics, calibration, uncertainty, permutation behavior and adherence to the frozen split. Clinical adjudication is required for claims about true filtration, AKI stage, CKD chronicity, muscle loss, inflammation, disease mechanism, treatment selection, or patient benefit. Measured GFR, urine-output AKI criteria, serial clinical creatinine, reliable specimen dates, outpatient records, treatment context, complete cause-of-death data, independent validation and prospective decision-impact studies are unavailable here.
