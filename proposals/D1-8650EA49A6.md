# Residual instability before ICU-to-ward transfer: event-time surveillance prioritization

Status: proposed substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. No prediction model has been fit and no outcome result is claimed. This child preserves the fixed cohort, `t0`, final-24-hour information set, raw 48-hour primary target and S0-versus-T1 estimand, subject split, exact MIMIC-IV 3.1 bindings, and exactly three inspected references. Its new question is narrower: can the event-time extension support a consequential but explicitly hypothetical fixed-capacity surveillance-prioritization decision, rather than merely describe discharge selection?

## Scientific opening and claim boundary

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. A longitudinal GRU-D++ model predicted seven-day ICU readmission/death better than SWIFT in MIMIC-IV and an external hospital, while its authors identified operational factors, DNR status, and differing composite-component trajectories as limitations [K1]. A systematic review found heterogeneous readmission windows and outcomes and suggested advantages from longitudinal predictors, but did not establish a transportable monitoring decision [K2]. EHR measurement timing and frequency themselves carry information about healthcare processes [K3]. The parent's exact-cohort audit additionally establishes an observed data fact, not a fitted result: first states by 48 hours were ICU return for 1,999/49,157, death for 586, live discharge for 9,950, and continued hospitalization for 36,622; first ICU-return/death transitions numbered 827 by 12 hours, 1,550 by 24 hours, and 2,585 by 48 hours.

The evidence does **not** establish that trajectory information can improve a fixed-capacity ranking, that event-time modeling adds useful ranking information over a calibrated fixed-horizon model, or that surveillance changes any outcome.

**Frozen primary hypothesis.** In the fixed population and decision-time information set, T1's prespecified value-trajectory features improve held-out prediction of the raw 48-hour three-state outcome over S0. The primary paired estimand remains `Brier_raw48(S0)-Brier_raw48(T1)` with the parent's 0.002 materiality bound.

**New surveillance-prioritization hypothesis.** For an explicitly hypothetical program able to assign 24 hours of enhanced post-transfer surveillance to exactly 5% of transfers (50 per 1,000), an event-time T1 model's calibrated 24-hour cumulative incidence of literal first ICU return or death will capture at least 2 more such observed events per 1,000 transfers than a matched, calibrated fixed-horizon T1 model, while retaining an incremental advantage over its S0-matched event-time model. This is a prognostic prioritization hypothesis, not an intervention-effect hypothesis.

The leading explanation is that event-time learning separates early ICU return/death risk from the rapidly increasing live-discharge transition and therefore improves whom a fixed review quota selects. The strongest rival is operational selection: destination, service, treatment limitation, charting intensity, and discharge pathways determine both observed transitions and the ranking, so an event-time model may mostly decompose selection without improving event yield. They differ on a direct held-out comparison at the same quota, horizon, population, inputs, and outcome.

The audit reconciles `2,607 = 2,585 + 22`: 2,585 raw positives are literal first R/D transitions, while 22 have literal live discharge before a later raw event. Among those 22, 20 have a later death without a preceding return and two have a later ICU return and death. These records remain positive in `Y_raw48`, but are L-first and non-events for the primary in-hospital surveillance estimand. They are data chronology conflicts for an absorbing-discharge model, not evidence of post-discharge safety or failure.

Mandatory surveillance sensitivities are: (1) literal-first primary with all records; (2) exclusion of the 22; and (3) raw-label analysis counting their later R/D events while explicitly marking the violation of absorbing-discharge chronology. ## Hypothetical surveillance decision and estimands

The decision is made once at `t0`: assign a nonrenewable 24-hour surveillance flag to exactly `k_q=ceil(q*N_test)` held-out transfers ranked by predicted 24-hour first-R/D risk. The primary quota is `q=0.05`; `q=0.01,0.02,0.10` are prespecified sensitivities. Resolve exact score ties using a fixed SHA-256 ordering of `subject_id,hadm_id,stay_id,seed`, independent of outcomes. Quotas apply to the test population distribution, not simultaneous calendar-time beds or monitors. MIMIC's subject-specific date shifts and absent staffing/occupancy data prohibit reconstruction of real capacity.

For model `m`, horizon `h`, and quota `q`, define event capture per 1,000 transfers:

`C(m,q,h) = 1000/N_test * sum_i I(i in top_q(m)) * I(literal first R/D by h)`.

The primary new estimand is `Delta_ET = C(M1,0.05,24)-C(T1-24,0.05,24)`. The prespecified consequential magnitude is 2 additional observed events captured per 1,000 transfers. Matched feature-increment estimands are `C(T1-24)-C(S0-24)` and `C(M1)-C(M0)`. Also report PPV/event yield among flagged transfers, sensitivity/recall, number needed to review (`1/PPV`, descriptive only), top-set overlap/Jaccard, and observed R and D separately. The same frozen 24-hour assignments are evaluated for events by 12 and 48 hours to reveal early versus later yield; they are not re-ranked using outcomes.

Appropriate comparators are S0-24, T1-24, M0, M1, and random allocation's expectation `q` times the observed literal-first event count. Report an unattainable outcome-informed top-k bound only as an explicitly labeled mathematical ceiling, never as a model. Retain the parent's 2%, 5%, and 10% threshold decision curves as separate hypothetical exchange-rate analyses; they do not define capacity and cannot establish treatment utility.

Because every strategy flags the same number, `Delta_ET` is a paired difference in event yield rather than a cost-benefit estimate. It does not count events prevented, infer monitoring effectiveness, or value false positives, alarm burden, inequity, or opportunity cost.

## Falsification and result meanings

The raw 48-hour S0/T1 axis remains independently supportive, adverse, or inconclusive under the parent's rules.

**Event-time extension warrants workflow evaluation:** M1 is acceptably calibrated for 24-hour R+D; the 95% interval for `Delta_ET` is above zero; its point estimate is at least 2 additional events per 1,000; `C(M1)-C(M0)` has an interval above zero; and conclusions do not reverse after excluding the 22 conflicts. This supports incremental prognostic prioritization under the stated quota only.

**Simple baseline is sufficient:** T1-24 is calibrated and useful relative to random/S0-24, while the upper 95% bound for `Delta_ET` is below 2 per 1,000. Event-time decomposition may remain descriptively informative, but its added prioritization magnitude is ruled out.

**Adverse to the event-time extension:** the upper 95% bound for `Delta_ET` is at or below zero with adequate calibration and event support. This says M1 did not improve this ranking decision; it does not refute trajectory information or imply that monitoring is ineffective.

**Operational-selection dominant:** M1 improvement is confined to L prediction, disappears for R+D capture, or materially reverses by destination/service, treatment-limitation, or pathway proxy. This weakens a general surveillance-priority interpretation but does not identify the operational cause.

**Endpoint-ambiguous:** literal-first, conflict-excluded, and raw-label analyses yield opposed decisions, or R and D move in clinically opposed directions. Report each result without generic “deterioration” language.

**Inconclusive:** intervals span both zero and the 2-per-1,000 bound, calibration fails, bootstrap failure exceeds 5%, or component/subgroup support is insufficient. An imprecise null does not falsify the hypothesis.

No supportive result establishes that flagged events were preventable, that enhanced observation benefits patients, that ICU transfer should be delayed, that a return was unplanned, that discharge was safe, or that the quota is feasible or fair in practice. Those claims require a specified surveillance intervention, alarm/action protocol, actual arrival and staffing data, burden/cost and subgroup review, clinician adjudication, and prospective impact evaluation, preferably randomized or quasi-experimental.
