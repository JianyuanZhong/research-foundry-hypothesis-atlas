## 0. What changed vs the parent (the scientific defect repaired)

The parent's primary estimand was a **single composite binary** outcome
"successful liberation = extubated AND alive at day 7" that **conflates death with failed
liberation** — a patient who dies on day 2 is coded identically to one still ventilated, so a
"delayed liberation" HR can be driven by higher early mortality (a severity effect) rather than
by slower weaning. The parent's exposure was also a **binary** sustained-deep flag (≥50 % of RASS
in [-5,-3]), which discards the dose gradient and cannot tell a monotone causal dose-response from
a threshold/selection artifact.

This child makes three deltas, keeping every other element of the frozen design:
1. **Competing-risks estimand** (cause-specific hazard for liberation + Fine-Gray cumulative
   incidence), with death as the competing event — replaces the composite binary.
2. **Dose-response contrast** as the substantive alternative to the binary baseline.
3. **Prespecified supportive/adverse/inconclusive rules** extended to the CR and gradient outputs.

Everything else is inherited unchanged: landmark t*, [t*, t*+12 h] predictor window, locked
earliest-70 %/latest-30 % split by `icustays.intime`, M0→M1→M2 adjustment ladder,
negative-control analgesia exposure, permuted-label control.

## 1. Unresolved question and rival

After an invasively ventilated adult first meets stabilization (t*), does **continued deep
sedation causally delay successful liberation**, or is RASS depth chiefly a
**severity/encephalopathy marker** (confounding by indication / reverse causation)?

- **Leading claim (C):** sustained deep sedation in [t*, t*+12 h] causally prolongs time to
  successful extubation.
- **Strongest rival (R):** deep sedation tracks a single latent severity/arousal axis; the depth
  that looks like "treatment" partly *is* the sickness state it is credited with causing.
- **Conflation rival (R-death), new:** if death is treated as mere non-liberation, a severity-driven
  mortality difference is misread as delayed liberation.
- **Measurement rival (R2):** apparent depth reflects RASS charting practice, not delivered sedation.

**Separating observation.** Under C, the cause-specific HR for liberation is <1, the 7-day
cumulative-incidence difference (liberation) is negative, the effect survives M0–M2, the
continuous dose gradient is monotone, and the analgesia negative control is null. Under R,
the effect attenuates at M1 (neuro/GCS), the **cause-specific HR and the CIF both move toward the
null while the death hazard rises**, and the analgesia control shows a parallel spurious effect.

## 2. Clinical importance

Time to liberation drives ICU LOS, VAP and delirium. If depth is causal, a light-sedation target
is a real lever; if depth is a severity marker, depth-only protocols mislead. Separating C from R,
and separating **delayed liberation from increased death**, changes the decision either way.

## 4. Design (inherited, with CR recoding)

- **Population:** adults (≥18) with an ICU stay containing invasive ventilation (**225792**) that
  crosses a stabilization window; not restricted to sepsis. Exclude stays that end (extubation or
  death) before t*. Unplanned extubation (**225468/225477**) is not successful liberation.
- **Landmark t*:** first time with sustained MAP≥65 mmHg for ≥2 h **and** vasopressor-free for ≥2 h
  **and** still invasively ventilated (structured query over chartevents/inputevents/procedureevents).
- **Predictor window:** [t*, t*+12 h]. Continuous exposure = **fraction of RASS observations in
  [-5,-3]** (primary gradient); binary exposure = *sustained deep* if ≥50 % of observations deep,
  else *light/other* (inherited threshold, kept for the baseline).
- **Follow-up:** t* to 7 days; each stay contributes one of three mutually exclusive first events —
  **liberation** (extubation **227194** with no re-intubation within 48 h), **death** (competing),
  or **still ventilated / censored** at day 7.
- **Covariate ladder:** M0 = age, sex, admission type, pre-t* LOS, MAP min, lactate, vasopressor
  use, PEEP, PaO2/FiO2. M1 = M0 + GCS at t*. M2 = M1 + sedative drug class.
- **Split:** earliest 70 % / latest 30 % of stays by `icustays.intime` (locked before fitting).

## 5. Estimand, baseline vs substantive alternative (same question)

- **Primary estimand (CR):** with death as a competing event,
  (a) **cause-specific hazard ratio** for liberation (deep vs light, adjusted M0–M2), and
  (b) **7-day cumulative-incidence difference (CIF_d) for liberation** from a **Fine-Gray**
  subdistribution model. Both are reported; death CIF is reported as a secondary curve, never
  collapsed into non-liberation.
- **Baseline B0 (interpretable):** Cox / fixed-horizon logistic on the **binary** exposure with
  M0→M2 covariates. B0 answers "does observed depth predict liberation" but its composite binary
  outcome is exactly the conflation we are repairing, and it forces a linear-in-flag effect.
- **Substantive alternative A1 (dose-response + CR):** Fine-Gray CIF and cause-specific Cox with
  the **continuous deep-sedation fraction** (and a restricted-cubic-spline / decile gradient).
  A1 can reveal a **monotone causal dose-response** (C) versus a **threshold/selection artifact**
  (R), which the binary exposure cannot distinguish; because it is a subdistribution estimator it
  also keeps death as a competing event rather than as censoring.
- **Why two arms:** they test the same scientific uncertainty (C vs R and delayed-liberation vs
  increased-death) under different identifiability assumptions; A1 is the primary analysis and the
  binary B0 is retained as the transparent benchmark.
- **Approx budget:** CPU only, <1 h, `lifelines`/`statsmodels` Fine-Gray or a small NumPy
  subdistribution weight; **no GPU needed or claimed**.

## 6. Falsification criteria (prespecified)

- **Supportive (C, and not R-death):** adjusted **cause-specific HR <1** with 95 % CI excluding 1
  and **negative 7-day CIF_d** for liberation; survives M0, M1, M2; continuous **dose gradient
  monotone** (higher deep-fraction → lower liberation CIF); negative-control analgesia exposure
  null (CI includes 1); permutation control null. Death CIF not higher for the deep group in a way
  that could explain the liberation deficit.
- **Adverse (R / severity-marker):** deep-vs-light association attenuates to equivalence
  (cause-specific HR within 0.9–1.1) once M1 (neuro/GCS) is included, **or** the negative-control
  analgesia exposure shows a similarly "protective" effect, **or** the continuous gradient is
  **non-monotone / threshold-only** (binary captures the effect but the gradient does not).
- **Adverse (R-death):** with death as competing event the liberation effect largely disappears
  while the **death hazard/CIF is elevated** in the deep group — i.e. the parent's result was a
  mortality conflation.
- **Inconclusive:** wide CI; minimum-events power gate failed; deep-sedation class too rare (RASS
  sparsity); the signal is M2/drug-composition-driven; or **RASS documentation completeness too low**
  to estimate the [t*, t*+12 h] fraction with acceptable precision.

## 7. Interpretation of outcomes and limits

Supportive → supports targeting sedation depth as a causal lever for liberation (still needs RCT
confirmation). Adverse → depth is largely a severity marker and/or the parent's signal was a
death conflation; deprioritize depth-only protocols. Inconclusive → need better sedation
documentation before a decision.
**Limits:** single-centre retrospective; RASS charting completeness unknown ([K2] validates the
scale, not EHR documentation); confounding by indication only partly addressed; association is not
causation; no clinical truth claimed; cohort prevalence and power are **not** established in
discovery. **Computationally checkable:** the CR/gradient computations, event counts, HRs and
CIF_d. **Requires clinical adjudication or another study:** whether a residual latent severity
axis fully absorbs the effect, and any causal claim.

## 8. Key references

[K1] Shehabi 2012 (SPICE; early deep sedation → delayed extubation, HR 0.90) — closest prior
evidence **for** C, but observational, cannot separate C from R. [K2] Sessler 2002 — RASS validity

motivating the dose-response contrast. All three inspected at abstract level (locators/limits in
