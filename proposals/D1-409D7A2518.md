# eICU laboratory process: severity proxy or site signature?

**Status:** Proposed, not executed. Child revision of the valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. The fixed adult first-ICU-stay 24-hour landmark and subsequent in-hospital death outcome remain unchanged. No cohort counts, model fits, or results are claimed. Source files remain read-only; all four configured datasets remain accessible, although this experiment uses eICU only.

## Scientific opening and claim boundaries

**Unresolved question.** The parent compared first-day lab-result process features against vital signs, but its main rival remained open: does lab-process signal chiefly reflect site-specific measurement/capture practice, or individual illness severity/clinical concern that is only partly represented by vital signs? The important next step is not another model swap. It is to condition on measured lab severity and test whether the process signal is within-site, site-identifying, and transportable to unseen hospitals.

**Evidence already supported.** eICU is a multi-hospital ICU database with patient, lab, vital-sign and hospital tables [K1]. Cross-site EHR work reports that missing-lab patterns can reflect both disease course/clinical behavior and site-specific mapping or data-definition differences [K2]. A recent MIMIC-to-eICU study illustrates that transported model performance can be comparable while a more complex model does not consistently beat a simpler benchmark [K3]. These works motivate a discrimination test; none establishes that eICU testing frequency is a workflow cause of mortality.

**Leading account:** individual testing intensity reflects patient-level severity/clinician concern. If measured physiology and numerical lab burden capture much of that signal, process-feature gain should shrink after including the actual first-day lab values; any remaining association may persist within hospitals and transport across sites. **Strongest rival:** lab capture/order patterns carry substantial hospital-specific workflow, mapping, or documentation fingerprints. Then process features should help identify site beyond measured severity, and their mortality-prediction increment may vary or attenuate in hospitals excluded from fitting. A mixture is plausible. Between-site differences also reflect case mix, unmeasured severity, admission pathways, care practices, staffing and data curation; neither site association nor predictive gain identifies causal workflow.

**Why this matters.** A lab-process predictor may be a useful risk marker but brittle when ported, or it may carry redundant information once observed biochemical severity is included. Resolving that boundary informs whether a model may safely rely on recorded testing behavior across hospitals; it does not justify reducing/adding tests or changing care.

## Population, time, endpoint

Use one first ICU stay per person: `patient.unitvisitnumber == 1`, with a unique `uniquepid` among eligible first-unit stays (exclude/report duplicate first-stay ties rather than select by outcome). Adults require age numerically >=18; eICU's `>89` category qualifies as adult; unknown/non-numeric non-elderly ages are excluded and counted. Require alive and still in the ICU at minute 1,440: `unitdischargeoffset` must be greater than 1,440 or missing only where the source semantics confirm an ongoing stay; exclude documented earlier ICU discharge/death. Require ascertainable hospital disposition after the landmark. This conditions on survival and continued ICU residence for 24 hours and is not an all-admission estimate.

Features are events with offset >=0 and <=1,440 minutes from ICU admission. Use `labresultoffset` / `observationoffset`, not revised result times. A result appearing after the landmark is never a feature. Endpoint is hospital death after minute 1,440, based on `hospitaldischargestatus` and `hospitaldischargeoffset`; require status and offset semantics to be reviewed before coding `Expired` as death. This is later in-hospital death, not fixed-horizon post-discharge mortality.

## Comparisons and estimands

The simple baseline is penalized logistic regression with prespecified nonlinear spline terms for continuous severity summaries, fit to `S`. The matched added-feature model uses exactly `S` plus the defined lab process block `L`. The primary patient-weighted estimand is paired held-out Brier difference `Brier(S+L) - Brier(S)`; negative favors added process features. Report calibration intercept/slope and AUROC/AUPRC only as secondary diagnostics. Estimate this contrast in patient-grouped internal folds among training hospitals and in a held-out-hospital set; the transport contrast is `delta_heldout - delta_internal`. No conclusion is based on AUROC alone.

A substantive learned alternative on the same rows, inputs, outcome and splits is CPU `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (or a comparably preregistered tree-boosting implementation) with matched `S` and `S+L` arms. It can reveal nonlinear severity-by-process associations and interactions lost by the prespecified logistic form; it does not make workflow causal. Evaluate paired Brier, calibration, and the same transport contrast, with identical preprocessing fitted only on each training fold. Keep the simple baseline primary; the learned alternative is an explanatory sensitivity comparison, not a search for small AUC gains.

Add two direct mechanism-discrimination diagnostics, both observational. First, fit a site-ID classifier using severity block `S` and then `S+L` under patient-level stratified cross-validation within the training hospitals. Incremental site classification from `L` after `S` supports a site-specific measurement/capture signature, but can also reflect residual site case mix. Second, estimate hospital-level process-feature means and patient-level deviations among training sites and report each hospital's held-out `S+L` versus `S` score increment, with site-cluster uncertainty; consistency of within-site predictive increment and stable leave-site-out performance are more compatible with an individual severity proxy than with a purely site-specific shortcut. These are triangulation, not mediation or causal decomposition.

## Split, uncertainty, falsification

Before fitting, freeze one deterministic 20%-of-hospitals holdout by `hospitalid`; preserve all eligible `uniquepid` groups on one side, excluding/reporting any cross-partition identity conflict. Within remaining hospitals, produce 5-fold patient-grouped validation, stratified on outcome where feasible. No scaling, analyte selection, imputation, or mapping decisions may use outcomes or test-fold statistics. Train/evaluate both model families on identical folds. Summarize both patient-weighted and hospital-weighted performance so large hospitals cannot silently determine every conclusion. Use hospital-cluster bootstrap 95% intervals for paired score changes and the internal-to-held-out contrast; show per-hospital estimates and event counts. Do not interpret very sparse site estimates individually.

Evidence against useful added process signal: the `S+L` Brier increment is zero/worse internally and in held-out hospitals, or its interval excludes any prespecified clinically meaningful improvement after that margin is agreed by clinical reviewers before fitting. Evidence against portability: reproducible internal improvement with clearly worse held-out increment/calibration and site-to-site heterogeneity, not merely an imprecise null. Support for an observed-severity proxy: process increment attenuates after adding numeric lab severity and vitals, with no remaining material site-holdout loss. Support for site-specific signal: `L` improves site identification conditional on `S`, process effects vary materially across hospitals, and held-out-hospital gain attenuates. Predefine “material” margins for Brier/calibration with reviewers before outcomes are examined; otherwise classify intervals crossing zero as inconclusive, not falsification. Missing or unreliable assay harmonization, too few held-out hospital events, or wide site-cluster intervals make the result inconclusive.

**Interpretation branches.** Supportive of the site/workflow account means compatibility with site-specific recorded measurement patterns, not proof that clinical workflow caused the differences. Supportive of individual severity-proxy account means incremental patient-level association beyond the measured severity block and better portability; it does not rule out latent severity or clinician concern. Adverse results may show that process signals add no robust prediction beyond severity; this is useful evidence against relying on them, not proof there is no informational content. A null with wide intervals is unresolved. Stronger claims about clinical appropriateness, orders never resulted, exact provider intent, unmeasured acuity, causal testing effects, or safer/less testing require source order-level data, chart review, expert clinical adjudication, a prospective workflow study, or another external dataset.

## Deliverable, alternatives and resources

New deliverables required for completion are a frozen cohort-flow/exclusion table; analyte mapping and coverage audit; a reproducible train/test manifest; paired out-of-fold and held-out hospital predictions with uncertainty; baseline/alternative calibration and Brier summaries; site classifier and hospital heterogeneity diagnostics; and a short interpretation report mapping each claim to its computed output. No future result is asserted here.

The original baseline-versus-GRU sequence comparison is not selected: temporal sequence flexibility does not itself distinguish patient severity from site capture and adds cost without addressing that rival. A hierarchical varying-slope model was considered but deferred until site-level support and common analyte mapping are audited; if later used, it must estimate within-/between-hospital process associations with partial pooling and leave-site-out predictions, not merely replace the present model. Simple logistic is primary for transparent conditional comparison; boosted trees are the substantive nonlinear alternative.

Planned future solver envelope: CPU only, 8 CPUs, 32 GiB RAM, <=2 hours for two feature families x two model families, five internal folds and 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates. The 5.16-GB compressed lab table is the main input; stream/filter by stay and landmark rather than materialize the full raw table in RAM. This is a planning estimate, not a measured runtime. No GPU is justified for tabular logistic regression and moderate CPU boosting; deployment hardware facts are not a training requirement. Current episode discovery/fitting is intentionally absent.

**Other methods considered:** Delphi's learned disease-history ordering is not selected because the 24-hour lab-measurement process question has no disease-event chronology target; no fitting is needed to separate the site and observed-severity predictions. ALADYNOULLI does not require genetics for every adaptation, but reproducing its genetic analysis would lack its required verified genetic inputs and an EHR-only latent trajectory model would not discriminate this question better than the prespecified severity/process decomposition. Oncoformer’s main multimodal experiment needs chest X-rays unavailable here; a lab-only adaptation is possible in principle but a transformer does not resolve the workflow-versus-severity ambiguity. These are deliberate deferrals, not claims that the methods are generally infeasible.

## Inspected key works

[K1] Pollard et al. establishes the multi-hospital eICU structure and identifies distinct patient/stay join keys. It motivates explicit hospital and identity handling, but does not establish process-feature validity. [K2] Tan et al. reports site mapping/data-definition differences alongside potential severity and clinical-behavior signals in missing laboratory patterns; this motivates the rival accounts but uses COVID-19 patients across health systems, not the eICU ICU-mortality question. [K3] Lu et al. reports MIMIC-IV-to-eICU external validation and cautions that the complex model did not consistently outperform simple benchmarks; it supports matched comparator and transport evaluation, not the present hypothesis. Contribution beyond these works: a fixed first-day landmark mortality test that conditions process prediction on first-day numerical lab severity, separates site-identification from outcome prediction, and estimates whether patient-level incremental signal survives hospital holdout.
