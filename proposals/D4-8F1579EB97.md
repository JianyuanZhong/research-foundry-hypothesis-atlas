# Prior biomarker values beyond current values: a selection-aware UKB landmark experiment

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`  
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`  
Status: frozen research proposal; no model has been fitted and no clinical conclusion is claimed.

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

The clinical decision is risk triage after a current biomarker assessment: does retaining the preceding panel change prospective risk estimates enough to alter which people merit closer review or prevention research? The estimand is deliberately conditional: among people who have a valid paired panel and a verified current assessment landmark, does the prior panel add predictive information beyond the current panel and the fact and timing of repeat assessment? It is not an estimate of the effect of ordering a repeat test.

The falsifiable primary hypothesis is:

> In the paired-panel cohort, adding prior biomarker values to a current-value model plus assessment-interval metadata improves held-out five-year prediction of a later qualifying recorded cardiorenal/metabolic target, with the largest improvement in the middle two quintiles of current-only risk, and this improvement is not explained by the repeat interval alone.

The primary contrast is nested and decision-relevant:

- M0: current instance-1 panel, approved age, approved sex, and current assessment calendar year.
- M1a: M0 plus the elapsed interval between the two assessment dates, treated as assessment-spacing metadata.
- M1b: M1a plus the instance-0 panel (equivalently, a prespecified current/prior representation; raw changes are a sensitivity).

The main estimand is the held-out Brier contrast M1b minus M1a, not M1b minus M0. M0 versus M1a quantifies information in repeat scheduling/spacing; M1a versus M1b tests whether prior biomarker values add information after that selection-related metadata is known. A secondary interaction estimand asks whether M1b minus M1a is larger in the fold-local M0 middle-risk band than in the tails. This is a substantive refinement of the parent’s design because it prevents an apparent history effect from being attributed to interval length or repeat-selection information.

A result cannot establish that repeat testing, monitoring, treatment, or a threshold improves outcomes. It can establish only a conditional prognostic-information result for this export and cohort. The paired cohort is selected; the target population is not all UKB participants unless a later study supplies a transportable sampling design and the required measurements.

## What is known, what is unresolved, and what would advance knowledge

The local export demonstrably contains paired field-instance columns, two assessment-date columns, age, sex, participant identifiers, diagnosis-code/date arrays, and death-date columns. The local metadata also states that field-instance indices are not elapsed time, gives no per-field units or global missing-code interpretation, and reports no temporal columns. Therefore field IDs are not treated as analyte names without an approved dictionary, and assessment dates are not asserted to be specimen dates.

The strongest available evidence supports availability of candidate inputs and a computable recorded-event landmark, not disease onset, complete follow-up, or biomarker collection timing. The unresolved claim is whether the prior value itself improves out-of-sample risk estimation once current values and repeat-spacing information are held fixed. Existing average M0-versus-history comparisons do not answer whether the gain is concentrated in ambiguous current-risk patients or is merely a correlate of who returns and when.

Support would motivate external validation and a prospective study of selective repeat-testing policies. An adverse result would favor current values plus known spacing for this recorded-event prediction task and would weaken the case for carrying prior values solely for triage. Interval reversal, strong selection dependence, or a gain that disappears when code/date integrity is broken would identify a design artifact rather than useful history. None supports a biological mechanism or an intervention.

## Timing, ascertainment, and endpoint gates

Define T0 as `53-0.0` and T1 as `53-1.0` only after a supplied field dictionary verifies that these are comparable assessment dates and their parseable values pass date plausibility checks. Require T0 < T1, 0.25 < L <= 5 years, where L=(T1-T0)/365.2425, and T1 <= 2013-12-31 for a five-year administrative window within the observed assessment era. T0/T1 are assessment landmarks; they are not specimen dates.

Four independent gates must be reported:

- G1 timing: a supplied, inspected source or field dictionary verifies assessment-date semantics. To claim specimen-prospective ordering, it must additionally verify collection timing relative to `53`; otherwise specimen ordering fails.
- G2 biomarker semantics: a supplied dictionary verifies the meaning, units, allowed ranges and missing-value codes of all 8 field IDs at both instances. No global negative-code conversion is allowed.
- G3 outcome semantics: a supplied dictionary verifies the code system and domain mapping for `41270`/ `41280`, and death-date meaning for `40000`.
- G4 ascertainment: an approved linkage/data-quality record supplies an individual observation end or complete ascertainment through H=T1+5 years, with linkage type sufficient for right censoring.

The local metadata currently proves none of G1 specimen ordering, G2 per-field assay semantics, G3 target/death mapping, or G4 individual complete follow-up. The solver must record the status from actual supplied evidence and must not upgrade a failed gate because values parse.

The locally executable fallback is a landmark-prospective *recorded-event* endpoint, not incidence. After G2 and G3 code/date verification, pair each `41270-0.i` with its same-position `41280-0.i`, i=0,...,29. A qualifying target is the earliest date strictly after T1 and no later than H containing valid codes from two distinct prespecified domains (the intended metabolic, renal and cardiovascular prefixes may be used only if the supplied dictionary confirms them). A death category uses the earliest valid `40000-0.0/1.0` date after T1, with same-day death taking precedence, only if its dictionary gate passes. The three-category recorded outcome is target, death, or no qualifying record by H. “No qualifying record” is not disease-free and is not complete follow-up.

If G4 fails, the primary report remains this recorded-category outcome with no censoring adjustment and no survival/incidence language. If G2 or G3 fails, the solver must not invent a code mapping; it may report a complete fail-closed audit, but cannot claim the hypothesis was tested. If all four gates pass, a separately labelled sensitivity may use a competing-risk five-year cumulative-incidence estimand with individual censoring/observation ends. This prospective mode is an optional gated analysis, not a reason to conceal a failed local evidence gate.

## Population, selection audit, and temporal boundary

Join the four analytic tables and `population` horizontally by `eid`, requiring one-to-one keys, join coverage, and agreement of overlapping age/sex fields. Retain a paired-panel cohort with numeric, dictionary-valid values for all 16 biomarker columns, valid age and sex, valid T0/T1, and the interval boundary above. Exclude a qualifying target record on or before T1 only as “no recorded prior target”; never call participants disease-free. The target is strictly after T1.

To quantify the repeat-assessment selection problem, create a separate flow from all population-anchor participants to those with valid T0 biomarker values and then observed valid T1 values. Fit a fold-local repeat-observation model among T0-complete participants using only pre-T1 information: T0 biomarker values, age, sex, T0 calendar year, and any additional covariate only if its dictionary is verified. Report observed-repeat rates, standardized differences, and overlap/positivity. As a sensitivity, inverse-probability-of-repeat weights estimated inside training folds may reweight paired participants toward the T0-complete observed-repeat target under measured-selection assumptions. This is a transport/generalizability sensitivity, not a correction for unmeasured selection and not a causal effect of returning for assessment. It must not use T1 values, post-T1 data, or outcomes.

The main estimand is conditional on having a valid paired assessment. The solver must state this condition in every result table and must not extrapolate to participants without a repeat panel. Interval regimes are descriptive effect-modification strata, not randomized treatment groups: short (0.25,1], medium (1,3], and long (3,5]. Sparse regimes are inconclusive, not pooled after inspection.

## Models, split, and evaluation

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold split, fixed by a published hash of `eid`, shared by every model and selection-weight fit. All imputation, scaling, regularization, repeat-propensity fitting and tuning are training-fold-only. No person may occur in more than one fold.

For each outer fold:

1. Fit M0, M1a and M1b on the training fold using the same outcome and preprocessing family. For recorded categories use regularized multinomial probability models; for a gated prospective competing-risk analysis use a common regularized discrete-time cause-specific hazard model and cumulative-incidence predictions.
2. Generate held-out predictions. Derive M0 target-risk 20th and 80th percentile cut points from training-only cross-fitted M0 predictions, then assign lower, middle and upper current-risk bands to held-out people. M1b or outcome information must never define the bands.
3. Fit M2 only within the same training folds and produce held-out predictions.
4. Preserve a held-out prediction row for every participant with `eid`, fold, T0/T1/L, interval stratum, M0 band, outcome, and all model predictions.

M1b’s primary prior-history inputs are the eight T0 values plus L; M1c is a sensitivity using eight raw changes x1-x0 plus L; an annualized change version is secondary because L is an assessment interval with uncertain specimen relation. M0 and M1a retain current instance-1 values. This parameterization distinguishes prior levels from change and ensures the main contrast is not forced to use a potentially noisy annualization.

M2 is the learned alternative: the same current, prior, age, sex, calendar-year and L inputs as M1b, with prespecified nonlinear terms and interactions (current-by-prior discordance, current-by-L, prior-by-L, and smooth threshold terms), fit by gradient-boosted multinomial probabilities or gradient-boosted discrete-time hazards as gated. It can reveal threshold and discordant-current/prior patterns that the additive M1b loses. Its purpose is scientific shape discovery, not leaderboard optimization. M2 cannot replace the primary M1b-versus-M1a inference on the basis of a small predictive improvement.

The primary held-out estimands are:
`Delta_BS_history = BS(M1b)-BS(M1a)` overall and within each current-risk band and interval regime; and
`Delta_BS_middle-tail = Delta_BS_history(middle)-average(Delta_BS_history(lower), Delta_BS_history(upper))`.
Negative values favor prior history. Report paired participant-level bootstrap confidence intervals with folds preserved; for subgroup contrasts require a predeclared minimum of 200 participants and 30 target events per cell, otherwise mark the cell and interaction inconclusive. Report Brier score as primary, calibration intercept/slope and calibration plots, target/death/category counts, discrimination secondarily, and exploratory decision-curve net benefit at 0.05 and 0.10. These thresholds are not validated action thresholds. Report M1a-M0 and M1c-M1b to show what spacing and representation contribute.

## Falsification and sensitivity criteria

The solver must run and retain:

- source/header/schema/source-hash, row-count, key-uniqueness, join-coverage and overlap-agreement checks;
- date parse/plausibility, T0<T1, interval-bound and T1 administrative-boundary checks;
- G1-G4 certificates with exact evidence paths and a hard stop for unsupported semantics;
- participant split and fold-manifest audit;
- post-T1 leakage scan over every exposure field;
- paired code/date-position integrity checks, including deliberately broken date-position and code-position assignments;
- outcome-label/date permutation controls;
- within-current-risk/interval permutation of prior values or change vectors while preserving current values, L, missingness and selection indicators;
- raw-prior, raw-change and annualized-change comparison;
- complete-case versus prespecified fold-local missingness sensitivity;
- interval-regime and repeat-selection-weight sensitivity, including positivity and weight truncation diagnostics;
- calibration and fold-instability checks, not discrimination alone.

Supportive evidence requires G2 and G3, an explicit recorded or gated prospective endpoint, at least 100 target events overall, adequate subgroup support, no integrity failure, M1b improving M1a on the primary held-out Brier contrast with a 95% upper confidence bound below zero, and a middle-band advantage not contradicted by the predeclared interaction interval or selection-weight sensitivity. A coherent M2 shape may strengthen scientific interpretation only when it is stable across folds and improves subgroup calibration; it is not required for support.

Adverse evidence is a worsening or null history contrast, a gain attributable to M1a rather than M1b, reversal across interval regimes, disappearance after selection weighting or integrity controls, or calibration degradation that outweighs the score improvement. Inconclusive evidence includes failed semantic/timing gates, fewer than 100 target events, sparse cells, poor positivity, unstable folds, conflicting code/date definitions, or materially different conclusions under endpoint or missingness sensitivities.

No result in this experiment supports causality, disease onset, disease-free status, a treatment recommendation, an optimal repeat interval, benefit from monitoring, a biological mechanism, or transportability outside the defined paired cohort. Specimen-level timing, adjudicated onset, complete follow-up, treatment/context variables, external validation, and prospective evaluation of a repeat-testing policy require additional data, expert review, or another study.

## Alternatives and compute decision

The transparent alternative is the nested M0/M1a/M1b comparison above. It is scientifically preferable as the primary test because it exposes the interval-selection component and makes the incremental estimand auditable.

The learned alternative is M2, not a cosmetic model leaderboard. It can reveal nonlinear thresholds and current/prior discordance that a linear additive model cannot. It uses exactly the same participants, target, fold split and held-out metrics. It is deferred from primary inference if its tuning is unstable, its subgroup calibration is worse, or it offers only a small uncalibrated gain.

A mechanistic organ-score branch is not selected: the local export lacks verified field labels, units, assay quality and specimen dates, so directional organ assignment would manufacture biology. A disease-history transformer adaptation is also deferred: the available endpoint arrays are sparse field-position records without verified event semantics and dates are not a validated longitudinal disease sequence; a transformer would add complexity without resolving the central prior-value-versus-spacing uncertainty. A genetic interaction branch is deferred because the `genomics` table is derived metadata rather than a verified sequence/variant or polygenic-score input. The UKB seed directions based on raw imaging, proteomic aging clocks, sleep regularity, CHIP, infection severity, cancer staging and AKI are not discarded as ideas; their required modality, semantics or adjudication are not verified in this snapshot, and none answers the narrower repeat-history triage estimand better.

The actual scientific deliverable is a new, uncertainty-quantified held-out estimate of `Delta_BS_history` and its current-risk/interval heterogeneity, plus a selection/gate certificate. Completion is either those outputs with valid endpoint semantics or a reproducible fail-closed audit identifying the missing dependency; it is not a fitted result claimed in advance.

Discovery checks were CPU/source-audit work. The future solver planning envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM, and 28,800 seconds; this is a planning limit, not a completed run. The selected-column tabular fit is expected to use CPU and does not require a GPU. The deployment has eight A100-SXM4 80-GB GPUs, but GPU use is not a scientific criterion; an allocated benchmark is only warranted if the chosen implementation makes repeated nonlinear fits materially slower. No discovery estimate has been silently substituted for the future study.
