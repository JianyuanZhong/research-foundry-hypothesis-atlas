# Research Proposal: Duration-Weighted Hypotension Burden and Kidney Injury Risk

## Unresolved Question

**Current Knowledge**: Clinical guidelines emphasize maintaining systolic blood pressure (SBP) above 100-110 mmHg in septic shock. However, most risk models use **minimum SBP** as the key hypotension metric. This approach has limitations:
- Minimum SBP is a sparse summary statistic that loses temporal information
- Two patients with the same minimum SBP may have vastly different hypotension burdens (one had brief dip, the other sustained hypotension)
- Vasopressor exposure (a proxy for treatment intensity) may modify the relationship between hypotension and organ injury

**The Gap**: Does the **duration-weighted burden of hypotension** (accounting for both how low and how long BP remains below threshold) predict acute kidney injury (AKI) better than minimum SBP alone, and does this relationship vary by vasopressor burden?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Risk Stratification**: A duration-weighted hypotension metric could improve early AKI risk prediction in septic shock, potentially guiding more aggressive or de-escalated BP targets.

2. **Treatment Optimization**: Understanding how vasopressor burden modifies hypotension effects could inform titration strategies—whether to prioritize rapid recovery or sustained normotension.

3. **Quality Metrics**: Hospital-level analysis could identify whether differences in AKI outcomes are due to hypotension burden differences or treatment intensity variations.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Existing literature focuses on:
- **Minimum BP thresholds** as static risk factors
- **Vasopressor dose-response** as independent predictors
- **Time-to-AKI** as the primary outcome

This proposal advances by:
- **Temporal richness**: Using duration-weighted burden rather than summary statistics
- **Interaction effects**: Testing how vasopressor burden modifies hypotension effects
- **Clinical granularity**: Linking continuous hypotension burden to AKI risk in a way that respects the temporal dynamics of critical care

## Hypothesis to Test

**Primary Hypothesis**: After adjusting for severity, comorbidities, and hospital factors, patients with higher duration-weighted hypotension burden (DWHB) have increased risk of AKI, independent of minimum SBP. The magnitude of this association varies by vasopressor burden, with greater vasopressor use amplifying the risk.

**Secondary Hypothesis**: The DWHB-AKI relationship is stronger in patients with higher baseline severity (APACHE II > 30) compared to those with lower severity.

### Key Variables

**Exposure**: Duration-Weighted Hypotension Burden (DWHB)
- Definition: Sum over all time points where SBP < 100 mmHg of (100 - SBP) × duration
- Alternative: Time-weighted area under the hypotension curve

**Outcome**: AKI (KDIGO criteria or creatinine ≥0.3 mg/dL increase from baseline within 48 hours)

**Moderator**: Vasopressor Burden (sum of infusion rates across all vasopressors)

**Covariates**: APACHE II score, age, comorbidities (from diagnosis table), teaching hospital status

### Falsification Criteria
- **Supportive**: DWHB remains significant after adjusting for minimum SBP (p < 0.05); interaction term positive (higher vasopressor burden amplifies DWHB risk)
- **Adverse**: DWHB loses significance after adjustment; interaction term negative or null; minimum SBP remains the dominant predictor
- **Inconclusive**: DWHB significant but interaction null; need more data on temporal dynamics

### Sample Size Considerations
- Expected AKI rate in septic shock: ~30-50%
- For 80% power to detect OR=1.5 for DWHB (vs minimum SBP), need ~500 events
- With 50% AKI rate, need ~1,000 patients

## Methodological Alternatives

### Baseline Method: Logistic Regression with Minimum SBP
- **What it reveals**: Association between lowest BP and AKI
- **What it misses**: Temporal dynamics, duration effects, interaction with treatment intensity

### Learned Alternative: Gradient Boosted Trees (XGBoost/LightGBM)
- **What it reveals**: Non-linear relationships, feature interactions, importance rankings
- **What it adds**: Can capture complex interactions between BP trajectories and vasopressor use
- **Resource estimate**: CPU job, ~1-2 hours for training on ~10,000 patients

### Mechanistic Alternative: Time-series analysis with survival modeling
- **What it reveals**: How BP trajectories predict time-to-AKI
- **What it adds**: Temporal ordering, censoring handling
- **Resource estimate**: CPU job, ~30-60 minutes

### Selection Rationale
- Start with **baseline logistic regression** for simplicity and interpretability
- Use **GBDT** as substantive alternative to capture non-linearities
- **Time-to-event analysis** as mechanistic alternative for temporal dynamics
- All three can be compared in a single experiment
