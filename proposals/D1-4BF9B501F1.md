# Stopping empiric IV vancomycin after a negative MRSA surveillance result in ICU pneumonia

## Question and hypothesis

MRSA nasal surveillance has high negative predictive value for MRSA pneumonia and stewardship protocols reduce vancomycin exposure. A 2026 systematic/network/Bayesian meta-analysis of 15 mainly nonrandomized studies (2,872 patients; Timbrook et al., DOI 10.1093/ofid/ofag178; full text inspected from frozen source [source checksum]) found shorter MRSA-active therapy and fewer troughs with pharmacist+screen protocols, but its Bayesian probability of clinically important AKI or mortality benefit was <=36%. The 277-patient STOP-Vanc ICU RCT abstract (DOI 10.1093/ofid/ofaf695.125; frozen source [source checksum]) found NPV 0.989 but little evidence that testing itself changed vancomycin-free hours, AKI, or mortality; intervals were wide. Thus, evidence supports the screen's rule-out value and reduced exposure under active stewardship, not that the bedside act of early discontinuation improves renal outcomes while preserving safety in a broad, severely ill ICU population.

Hypothesis: **among adult ICU patients treated for imaging-compatible pneumonia with IV vancomycin plus an antipseudomonal beta-lactam at the time a MRSA surveillance culture is reported negative, stopping IV vancomycin within 24 hours reduces 7-day KDIGO stage 2/3 AKI versus continuing through 48 hours, without increasing 28-day mortality by more than 3 percentage points.**

This targets the actual decision after the result, unlike studies of whether ordering/reporting a test changes behavior. It can identify effect modification by baseline renal vulnerability and shock, but it cannot replace randomization.

## Target trial

Time zero t0 is the first hosp/microbiologyevents.storetime during an ICU stay for a MRSA SCREEN row with org_name null and comments matching the frozen negative-result vocabulary (including “No MRSA isolated.”). Use first eligible ICU episode per subject.

Include age >=18; t0 within icustays.intime/outtime; actual IV vancomycin administration in (t0-24h,t0]; an antipseudomonal beta-lactam (cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem, imipenem-cilastatin, ceftazidime, or aztreonam) administered in that interval; a lower-respiratory specimen collected in (t0-72h,t0+24h]; and a chest-radiology report in (t0-48h,t0] with a prespecified section/negation-aware pneumonia-compatible phenotype (new/progressive infiltrate, consolidation, or airspace opacity).

Exclude any MRSA or S. aureus organism from blood/lower-respiratory specimens with storetime <=t0; vancomycin allergy; receipt for a documented nonpulmonary invasive gram-positive indication available before t0 (positive blood/CSF/joint/wound culture or pre-t0 POE indication); chronic dialysis or RRT before t0; KDIGO stage 2/3 AKI already present; death/comfort-only status before t0. Do not infer exclusions from discharge diagnoses or discharge notes.

Strategies:

- A: last actually administered IV vancomycin dose occurs by t0+24h, with no further dose through t0+72h.
- B: continue, defined as at least one actual IV dose in (t0+24h,t0+48h].

Clone at t0 and censor for deviation during grace periods. Treatment from 24-48h followed by stopping is B; treatment restarted after compliant A is a secondary rescue outcome, not retrospective reclassification. Concomitant beta-lactam care is unrestricted and adjusted hourly.

Primary estimand: per-protocol risk ratio and risk difference for new KDIGO stage 2/3 AKI by day 7, using creatinine and urine-output criteria. Baseline creatinine is the lowest value from 7 days before admission through t0; sensitivity uses the last pre-t0 value and prior-year nadir. Secondary: 28-day mortality risk difference (noninferiority margin +3 points); vancomycin-free days alive through day 7; any new MRSA-positive blood/respiratory culture through day 14; vancomycin restart by 72h/day7; RRT; ICU/hospital-free days; and trough monitoring.

Prespecified modifiers: baseline KDIGO stage 1 versus none, vasopressor/ventilation at t0, community-onset versus hospital-onset pneumonia, and piperacillin-tazobactam co-treatment (because nephrotoxicity and prescribing patterns may differ).

## Analysis, baselines, and falsification

Use person-hour clone-censor-weighting through 72h with time-varying adherence models. Covariates available by each hour: age, sex, race, year, ICU/service, prior-admission comorbidity, pneumonia timing, ventilation, vasopressor dose, MAP, lactate, temperature, WBC, creatinine/eGFR trajectory, urine output, fluid balance, vancomycin doses/troughs, beta-lactam, contrast exposure, other nephrotoxins, microbiology collected/reported, and chest-report phenotype. No discharge coding/text enters models.

Report unadjusted landmark and 24h propensity-overlap-weighted baselines, then primary clone-censor estimates with subject-clustered robust intervals and 500 subject bootstraps. Report standardized differences, overlap, truncated weights, ESS, missingness, and calendar-era effects.

Sensitivity: require eMAR/inputevents/pharmacy concordance; vary stop grace to 12/36h; PCR-era versus culture-era turnaround; negative blood and respiratory cultures by t0; exclude piperacillin-tazobactam; exclude immunocompromised using only prior records; competing death; alternate AKI baselines; quantitative bias/E-values. Positive controls are fewer vancomycin doses/troughs. Falsification checks test association with pre-t0 creatinine slope and an outcome not plausibly altered within 24h (preexisting platelet trajectory). A strong association signals residual indication bias.

Declare infeasible/inconclusive if either arm <300, weighted ESS <200, >10% adherence probabilities <0.01, or chest phenotype PPV <0.80 on blinded adjudication.

- Supportive: AKI risk ratio 95% CI upper <1.0 and mortality risk-difference 95% CI upper <+3%, with no higher day-14 MRSA isolation whose interval excludes zero.
- Adverse: mortality lower 95% bound >=+3%, or clearly increased MRSA isolation; renal benefit cannot rescue safety harm.
- Inconclusive: all other patterns, including renal improvement with mortality margin not excluded, wide MRSA intervals, nonpositivity, or failed falsification. Nonsignificance is not noninferiority.

Support would strengthen rationale for implementation/external pragmatic trials, not prove universal safety. Adverse findings would identify groups needing continued coverage but cannot show the negative screen is diagnostically invalid. Clinical recommendation needs randomized multicenter evidence and organism/indication adjudication.

## Availability and verification

A direct preliminary query found 17,908 ICU-negative-result landmarks; 6,623 had an overlapping IV-vancomycin prescription, 4,814 also overlapped an antipseudomonal agent, 3,041 had prescription stop within 24h, and 795 had stop >=48h. These are not actual-administration counts and precede pneumonia/AKI exclusions; final attrition is mandatory.

Two blinded clinicians must adjudicate at least 200 chest reports and all sampled discordant/nonpulmonary indications. Without PPV validation, call this “text-derived pneumonia-compatible imaging.” The data cannot verify drug indication, outpatient creatinine, randomization, or post-discharge infection.

Verifier-checkable: result time, no pre-t0 pathogen leakage, treatment administrations/grace cloning, KDIGO construction, attrition, covariate timing, weights/ESS, intervals, sensitivity/falsification, and conclusion branch. Verifier challenge cases must reject: supportive computation called proof/standard care; AKI benefit despite failed mortality margin called supportive; adverse infection/mortality called safe; and wide intervals called equivalence. Indication validity, imaging truth, exchangeability, and practice recommendations require clinical review or another study.
