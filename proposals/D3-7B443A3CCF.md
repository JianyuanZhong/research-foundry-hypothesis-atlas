# Directional cell communication: macrophage-to-fibroblast versus fibroblast-to-macrophage signaling in RA

Seed: D3-M2
Domain: Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses

## Unresolved question

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), do macrophages drive fibroblast activation (macrophage-to-fibroblast direction), or do fibroblasts drive macrophage polarization (fibroblast-to-macrophage direction), or both? This question is consequential because the dominant signaling direction determines whether therapeutic intervention should target the immune compartment or the stromal compartment.

## Evidence for the opening

- [K1] Batashkov et al. (2026) describe RA as a biologically heterogeneous immune-mediated disease with substantial variability in therapeutic response. The review discusses tissue immunophenotype and biomarkers of treatment response, but does not directly test whether macrophage-fibroblast signaling is directional or bidirectional.
- [K2] Mathew et al. (2026) describe FLS as key tumor-like mediators of RA. In the inflamed synovium, they expand, resist apoptosis, and secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and proteases that sustain disease. The review does not test whether FLS-derived signals drive macrophage polarization or whether macrophage-derived signals drive FLS activation.
- [K3] Yi et al. (2026) describe how M1/M2 polarization imbalance drives RA progression. M1 macrophages maintain inflammation and tissue destruction, while M2 macrophages promote tissue repair. The review does not test whether the polarization state is driven by fibroblast-derived signals or by macrophage-autonomous mechanisms.

## Hypothesis

**Leading claim (H1):** In RA, macrophage-derived signals (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1β, CCL2) drive fibroblast activation (e.g., MMP-1, MMP-3, IL-6 upregulation), establishing a macrophage-to-fibroblast signaling direction.

**Strongest rival (H2):** In RA, fibroblast-derived signals (e.g., IL-6, CCL2, CXCL12) drive macrophage polarization toward an M1-like state, establishing a fibroblast-to-macrophage signaling direction.

**Alternative (H3):** Both directions operate simultaneously, with the dominant direction varying by disease stage or donor.

## Method comparison

### Simple baseline
- **Method:** Donor-aware paired t-test on ligand/receptor expression.
- **Inputs:** E-MTAB-8316 nested donor design; E-MTAB-8873 targeted assay.
- **Training/fitting target:** None (non-parametric test).
- **Split:** Donor-aware paired comparison (within-donor).
- **Outcome/uncertainty evaluation:** Effect size (Cohen's d) and 95% confidence interval for each ligand/receptor pair.
- **What it reveals:** Whether ligand/receptor expression differs significantly between conditions within the same donor.
- **What it loses:** Cannot capture non-linear relationships, interactions between ligands/receptors, or the direction of signaling.

### Learned/mechanistic alternative
- **Method:** Small neural network (e.g., 2-layer MLP) or mechanistic model (e.g., ordinary differential equation model) that captures the directional signaling.
- **Inputs:** E-MTAB-8316 nested donor design; E-MTAB-8873 targeted assay.
- **Training/fitting target:** Predict fibroblast activation markers (e.g., MMP-1, MMP-3, IL-6) from macrophage-derived signals (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1β, CCL2) and vice versa.
- **Split:** Donor-aware cross-validation (within-donor).
- **Outcome/uncertainty evaluation:** Prediction accuracy (R², RMSE) and 95% confidence interval for each direction.
- **What it reveals:** Whether the direction of signaling can be predicted from the data, and whether the learned model captures non-linear relationships or interactions that the baseline loses.
- **What it loses:** Interpretability (neural network) or requires mechanistic assumptions (ODE model).

### Comparison
- **What information the alternative could reveal that the baseline loses:** The learned/mechanistic alternative can capture non-linear relationships, interactions between ligands/receptors, and the direction of signaling, which the simple baseline cannot.
- **Selection or deferral reason:** The simple baseline is a necessary first step to establish whether ligand/receptor expression differs between conditions. The learned/mechanistic alternative is a more powerful test of the directional signaling hypothesis, but requires more data and computational resources. If the baseline does not show significant differences, the learned/mechanistic alternative is unlikely to reveal a direction.

## Discriminating test

**Observation or comparison that separates H1 from H2:**
- If macrophage-derived signals (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1β, CCL2) are upregulated in the macrophage-to-fibroblast direction, and fibroblast activation markers (e.g., MMP-1, MMP-3, IL-6) are upregulated in response, this supports H1.
- If fibroblast-derived signals (e.g., IL-6, CCL2, CXCL12) are upregulated in the fibroblast-to-macrophage direction, and macrophage polarization markers (e.g., M1-like markers) are upregulated in response, this supports H2.
- If both directions are supported, this supports H3.

**What each possible outcome would and would not establish:**
- **Supportive of H1:** Macrophage-derived signals drive fibroblast activation. This would not establish that the signaling is causal (only correlative) or that it is the dominant direction in all disease stages.
- **Supportive of H2:** Fibroblast-derived signals drive macrophage polarization. This would not establish that the signaling is causal (only correlative) or that it is the dominant direction in all disease stages.
- **Supportive of H3:** Both directions operate simultaneously. This would not establish which direction is dominant or whether the balance varies by disease stage or donor.
- **Adverse:** Neither direction is supported. This would suggest that the signaling is not directional or that the ligand/receptor candidates are not the relevant signals.
- **Inconclusive:** The data do not provide sufficient evidence to distinguish between H1, H2, and H3. This would suggest that the assay is not sensitive enough or that the ligand/receptor candidates are not the relevant signals.

## Falsification criteria

- **H1 is falsified if:** Macrophage-derived signals are not upregulated in the macrophage-to-fibroblast direction, or fibroblast activation markers are not upregulated in response.
- **H2 is falsified if:** Fibroblast-derived signals are not upregulated in the fibroblast-to-macrophage direction, or macrophage polarization markers are not upregulated in response.
- **H3 is falsified if:** Only one direction is supported, or neither direction is supported.

## Competing explanations

- **Composition-driven:** The observed differences in ligand/receptor expression may be driven by changes in cell composition (e.g., macrophage abundance) rather than within-cell state changes.
- **Within-cell state-driven:** The observed differences in ligand/receptor expression may be driven by within-cell state changes (e.g., polarization, activation) rather than changes in cell composition.
- **Both:** The observed differences may be driven by both changes in cell composition and within-cell state changes.

## Next test

- **Data required:** E-MTAB-8316 nested donor design; E-MTAB-8873 targeted assay; GSE95588 coculture data.
- **Outcome:** If the perturbation experiment supports the directional signaling hypothesis, this would provide stronger evidence for H1 or H2. If the perturbation experiment does not support the directional signaling hypothesis, this would suggest that the signaling is not directional or that the ligand/receptor candidates are not the relevant signals.
