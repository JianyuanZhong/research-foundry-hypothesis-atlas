# eICU monitoring-gap provisional hypothesis and method comparison

Status: provisional, untested design checkpoint. No cohort results, causal effects, or clinical recommendations are claimed.

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The configured eICU snapshot contains longitudinal, timestamped laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, patient, hospital, and end-of-life-plan records. The expert seed [starting question] supplies the opening that abrupt reductions in laboratory testing during instability may predict deterioration and vary across hospitals. This is an opening, not evidence that the association exists.

The provisional hypothesis is:

> Among adults with a continuous ICU stay who remain physiologically unstable at an hourly landmark, a reduction in laboratory-testing intensity during the preceding 12 hours is associated with higher risk of a time-local respiratory or vasopressor/infusion deterioration in the next 12 hours, beyond contemporaneous measured severity and recent support. The association should attenuate when end-of-life documentation, discharge/transfer context, and support de-escalation are handled explicitly. A residual held-out-hospital association would be a monitoring-process signal, not evidence that changing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

The strongest claim currently supported is only that the required longitudinal fields are available and can be joined within eICU by patientunitstayid. The unresolved claim is whether the exposure is predictive after conditioning on current state and whether the signal survives explicit documentation and competing-event handling.

## Why it matters and competing explanations

If reproducible, this could identify a monitoring-process signal for prospective review and clarify whether laboratory intensity contains information not captured by current vital signs and support. If it disappears after adjustment or negative controls, the apparent gap is more plausibly severity, recovery, discharge/transfer selection, end-of-life intent, or interface/documentation interruption. These explanations have different observable predictions:

- current-state/severity rival: association should attenuate after prespecified contemporaneous severity and recent support adjustment;
- discharge/selection rival: association should be concentrated near discharge/transfer or disappear when those competing events and context are handled;
- end-of-life/documentation rival: association should be modified by pre-index EOL-plan documentation or disappear in a prespecified EOL-stratified analysis;
- interface/documentation interruption rival: a non-laboratory charting timestamp control or within-stay timing control should show a comparable spurious signal if documentation interruption is the main explanation.

An association surviving adjustment does not identify a mechanism or justify ordering more tests.

## Population and temporal design

Use adult eICU stays with a valid unitdischargeoffset greater than 18 hours and a single continuous ICU interval. Create hourly landmarks t from 6 through unitdischargeoffset minus 12 hours. Require at least one valid vital observation and one laboratory observation in the preceding 24 hours. Treat missingness as missingness, not zero testing. Restrict the primary analysis to continuous stays.

At each t, define exposure from lab.csv.gz using patientunitstayid, labresultoffset, and labname. Count distinct analytes and distinct laboratory episodes in (t-12,t], using result time rather than treating a revised result as a new test. Compare (t-24,t-12] with (t-12,t]. The primary reduction is a positive prior count followed by zero current distinct analytes, or at least a 50% fall when the prior interval has at least two episodes; continuous change and episode count are secondary.

The primary outcome is the first qualifying deterioration in (t,t+12]: new or escalated respiratory support from respiratoryCare.csv.gz using respcarestatusoffset, airwaytype, ventstartoffset, ventendoffset, and highironoxlimit, or new/escalated vasopressor/infusion from infusionDrug.csv.gz using infusionoffset, drugname, drugrate, infusionrate, and patientweight. Do not use post-index laboratory values as the outcome. Death before the event and discharge/transfer before the event are competing events; mortality fields in apachePatientResult.csv.gz are secondary.

Adjust only for information available at or before t: vitalPeriodic.csv.gz (observationoffset, temperature, sao2, heartrate, respiration, systemic pressures), respiratory state from respiratoryCharting.csv.gz (respchartoffset, respchartentryoffset, type/value labels), recent infusion/support, age, sex, unit type, and hospital. Use carePlanEOL.csv.gz (cpleolsaveoffset, cpleoldiscussionoffset, activeupondischarge) to define prespecified EOL-context strata and a sensitivity analysis excluding documented EOL plans before t. Missing EOL documentation is not equivalent to no EOL care.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is a prespecified discrete-time logistic hazard model at the same hourly landmarks. It uses the primary reduction indicator, pre-index severity/support covariates, competing-event censoring, hospital fixed or clustered effects, and clustered uncertainty. It answers the narrow question of whether the landmark exposure is associated with the next 12-hour event after adjustment.

The substantive alternative is a longitudinal recurrent-event discrete-time hazard model using the same available inputs, with time-varying testing intensity and physiologic trajectories over consecutive landmarks. It can reveal whether the signal is transient, persistent, or preceded by a trajectory; it can distinguish a single landmark artifact from a changing monitoring pattern. It is selected for this scientific information, not for a small predictive gain. The model must use the same split, competing-event handling, and uncertainty evaluation as the baseline.

Use hospital-held-out validation: fit on training hospitals, evaluate on held-out hospitals, and repeat with prespecified folds. Report calibration, discrimination, absolute risk difference, and uncertainty intervals. Negative controls are within-hospital permutation of testing intervals preserving stay length and outcome, a post-index testing exposure that should not predict the prior deterioration, and a non-laboratory charting timestamp control where available.

Supportive results require a reproducible held-out-hospital association after state and EOL/discharge handling, with null negative controls. Adverse results are null, unstable, or attenuated associations after explicit rival adjustment. Inconclusive results include wide intervals, sparse events, or inability to distinguish interface interruption from clinical intent. None establishes causality or a recommendation to increase testing.

## Missing evidence and limitations

Exactly three inspected key works and frozen receipts are still required before final selection. Clinical adjudication of deterioration, direct interface-interruption flags, staffing/bed capacity, complete pre-ICU history, and reliable EOL intent are unavailable or incomplete. The public release removes narrative portions and calendar dates; offsets are minutes relative to ICU admission. No literature claim is made in this provisional draft.

## Alternatives not chosen

A static admission-level logistic model was not chosen because it loses the time-local exposure and competing-event structure. A generic black-box sequence model was deferred because it would not by itself distinguish the scientific rivals and would add tuning burden without a clear clinical interpretation. A mechanistic causal model was not chosen because the available data do not identify intervention effects or reliably measure intent and documentation interruptions. These alternatives should be revisited only if the bounded feasibility audit shows that the proposed longitudinal model cannot estimate the trajectory question or if additional adjudicated data become available.
