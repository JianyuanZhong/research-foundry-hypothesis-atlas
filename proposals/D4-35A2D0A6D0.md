# Same-current-level HbA1c trajectory history and transition to cardiometabolic multimorbidity

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`  
Seed: `[starting question]` (“Metabolic deterioration and multimorbidity”)  
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`  
Status: prospective observational hypothesis; no fitted result is claimed.

## What is known and what remains unresolved

The strongest directly relevant evidence available to this proposal is that static metabolic/atherogenic indices are associated with incident and progressive cardiometabolic multimorbidity in UK Biobank. The frozen Europe PMC search record for Xue et al. (DOI `10.1186/s12933-026-03255-4`, source `[source checksum]`) reports a prospective study of 346,868 participants, at least two of type 2 diabetes, coronary heart disease and stroke as multimorbidity, and positive associations for static indices. I inspected the retrieved bibliographic record/abstract, not the complete article methods or supplement; this record therefore supports the narrower static-association claim only.

It is not established that a second HbA1c measurement adds clinically material information beyond the current HbA1c, or that the prior-to-current trajectory identifies a different risk of crossing from no disease to a second distinct cardiometabolic/renal disease. The unresolved decision question is whether two people with the same current HbA1c should receive different risk interpretation because one arrived there from a lower prior HbA1c. This study can quantify prognostic information; it cannot show that acting on the trajectory improves outcomes.

A crucial estimand correction is required. In the parent proposal, a model containing repeat HbA1c and `repeat - baseline` HbA1c was described as testing whether a larger rise is harmful beyond the current value. Algebraically, holding repeat HbA1c fixed, a larger change means a *lower* baseline HbA1c. Thus that coefficient is a history-at-the-same-current-level contrast, not an ordinary “deterioration at fixed baseline” effect. This child makes that distinction explicit and estimates both contrasts without putting collinear current, baseline and change terms in the same model.

## Hypotheses

Primary hypothesis, stated in the clinically relevant direction:

> Among UK Biobank participants with a verified baseline and strictly later valid repeat HbA1c, who are alive and free of inpatient-coded type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease and heart failure at the repeat landmark, a trajectory history ending at the same current HbA1c is associated with a clinically material difference in 5-year risk of the second distinct disease family. The directional expectation is higher risk after a recent rise, but a clinically material association in the opposite direction is also scientifically informative; the primary null is no history increment.

The primary estimand is a model-based, noncausal standardized 5-year cumulative-incidence contrast:
[
RD_{history}=E_X{P(T_2\le5\mid C_i,\Delta=+0.5,X_i)
             -P(T_2\le5\mid C_i,\Delta=0,X_i)}.
]
Here (C_i) is each eligible participant’s observed repeat HbA1c, (Delta) is repeat minus baseline HbA1c after verified unit conversion to NGSP percentage points, and baseline is set to (C_i-Delta) for each displayed history. The standardization uses the empirical distribution of current HbA1c and baseline covariates in the eligible landmark cohort, and is restricted to common observed support; it must not extrapolate to impossible or sparsely observed baseline/current combinations. The primary contrast is therefore “same current value, 0.5 percentage-point rise versus stable history,” not a causal intervention and not a claim that the repeat measurement was randomly obtained.

A prespecified secondary estimand isolates ordinary deterioration:
[
RD_{change}=E_X{P(T_2\le5\mid B_i,\Delta=+0.5,X_i)
             -P(T_2\le5\mid B_i,\Delta=0,X_i)},
]
where (B_i) is baseline HbA1c and current HbA1c is (B_i+Delta), again only within observed support. This secondary contrast answers whether an increase itself is prognostically associated when the prior level is held fixed. The two estimates must not be conflated. A data-adaptive interquartile-range contrast is a sensitivity display, not a replacement for the 0.5-point clinical contrast. If the verified assay unit cannot be converted to percentage points, report standardized within-cohort contrasts but do not claim the clinical 0.5-point estimand.

The endpoint is the date of the second distinct component disease among type 2 diabetes (ICD-10 E11–E14), CKD (N18.x), and heart failure (I50.x). A same-date pair of distinct families counts as a second-family event. The endpoint is not “any diagnosis” and not a causal disease transition.

## Population and temporal design

Use one row per participant and horizontal joins by `eid`; never append tables as additional observations.

1. Baseline is assessment instance 0, with time field `53-0.0`. Require a valid baseline age, sex, HbA1c, repeat assessment date, repeat HbA1c, and prespecified covariates.
2. Repeat is assessment instance 1, with time field `53-1.0`. Require `53-1.0 > 53-0.0`, valid paired HbA1c values, and a verified mapping from biological-sample instances 0/1 to the two assessment dates. The local catalog says field-instance indices are not elapsed time and exact dates remain server-side; this mapping is a hard evidence gate, not an assumption.
3. The primary landmark is the repeat assessment date (L_i=53	ext{-}1.0). No person-time before (L_i) enters the primary risk model. This removes immortal person-time from the outcome analysis, but it does not remove selection/collider bias from conditioning on survival, disease-free status and return with a valid repeat.
4. Use aligned code/date pairs only. For each family (k), define (D_{ik}) as the earliest valid date strictly after (L_i) among matching `41270-j`/ `41280-j` cells with the same (j). A code with no aligned date is not silently treated as absent. A code-date pair on or before (L_i) excludes the participant from the primary disease-free landmark cohort; a code with unresolved date is an ascertainment failure and is reported separately.
5. Exclude anyone with a qualifying component diagnosis on or before (L_i), or death on or before (L_i). Report diagnoses/deaths between baseline and repeat as part of the selection audit rather than treating that interval as eligible follow-up.
6. For eligible participants, (T_{2i}) is the second order statistic of the available (D_{ik}) values for the three distinct families; equivalently, the later of the earliest post-landmark dates for the first two distinct families. Report the first-family and second-family identities and same-day ties.
7. Follow from (L_i) through (T_{2i}), death, or administrative end 2023-03-31, whichever occurs first. Death before (T_2) is a competing event, not ordinary noninformative censoring. A 5-year risk is estimated only under the stated censoring assumptions; the amount of administrative follow-up must be reported.
8. The 365-day delayed-outcome analysis starts counting (T_2) only after a one-year post-landmark washout and is a reverse-causation sensitivity, not the primary estimand. A prespecified 1–6-year baseline-to-repeat interval restriction is another sensitivity analysis. Participants who develop one component after (L_i) remain at risk for the second component.

The target population is explicitly a repeat-observed landmark population: people alive, disease-free for the three specified inpatient-coded families, and observed with a valid paired repeat. It is not the UK Biobank baseline population and not the causal effect of offering repeat HbA1c testing. We will compare eligible repeat-observed participants with baseline-eligible participants without a valid repeat. A stabilized inverse-probability-of-repeat weight based only on baseline variables, with truncation fixed before fitting and diagnostics reported, is a sensitivity analysis for measured selection; it cannot recover people who died or developed a component before the landmark and cannot remove unmeasured or time-varying selection.

## Analysis plan

First produce an auditable flow and selection report: source row counts; `eid` uniqueness/overlap; valid baseline and repeat assessment dates; paired HbA1c availability; interval distribution; field/unit/date gates; component-code/date cells; exclusions for pre-landmark disease/death; disease-order cells and ties; death and administrative censoring; follow-up and event counts; and comparison of repeat-observed versus no-valid-repeat baseline participants.

The primary model is a cause-specific hazards model for (T_2), with death as a competing event, using a flexible joint exposure surface in current HbA1c and baseline HbA1c (restricted cubic splines with a prespecified interaction/complexity rule). This parameterization is deliberate: `current + change` can estimate the same surface but makes “rise” easy to misread. The primary history contrast is generated by setting the baseline history to current minus 0.5 percentage points versus current, at each participant’s observed current HbA1c and covariates, only where both histories lie in empirical support. The secondary deterioration contrast uses baseline fixed and current equal to baseline plus 0.5 versus baseline. Use cause-specific hazards to obtain 5-year cumulative incidence with the Aalen–Johansen competing-risk calculation; report standardized absolute risk difference, risk ratio, 95% participant-bootstrap intervals, cause-specific hazard ratios, and cumulative-incidence curves. Fine–Gray is a sensitivity analysis, not the primary estimand.

Fit nested models in the identical landmark cohort:

- M0: current HbA1c plus baseline covariates.
- M1 (primary history model): current HbA1c plus baseline HbA1c/history surface plus baseline covariates.
- M2 (secondary deterioration parameterization): baseline HbA1c plus HbA1c change plus baseline covariates, with the same prespecified spline complexity.

Do not fit current, baseline and change as separate unrestricted terms in one model. They satisfy current = baseline + change, so the parent’s three-term wording is algebraically redundant and obscures the estimand. Compare calibration, Brier score and optimism-corrected incremental risk prediction with participant-level resampling; AUC or a small machine-learning improvement is descriptive and cannot establish clinical utility. Multiple imputation, if needed, must occur inside bootstrap/resampling partitions. The repeat-weighted analysis must report positivity, effective sample size and weight distribution.

## Falsification, evidence gates and interpretation

Before analysis, define the working threshold for clinical materiality as an absolute 5-year risk difference of 2 percentage points. This is a prespecified decision-relevance threshold, not a validated treatment threshold; expert review is required before translating it into care.

Support for the expected directional hypothesis requires:

- all unit, instance/date, code/date and death-date gates pass;
- at least 100 primary second-family events overall, at least 10 events in each displayed trajectory group/contrast stratum, and adequate empirical overlap for both standardized histories;
- the primary same-current-level contrast is positive, has a 95% interval excluding zero, and has a point estimate at least 2 percentage points; a lower confidence bound above 2 points is stronger evidence of materiality;
- the current-plus-history model is calibrated and improves the prespecified risk contrast over M0, with direction broadly consistent in the one-year washout, 1–6-year interval restriction, competing-risk and repeat-weighted analyses; and
- the fixed appendicitis negative-control diagnosis (K35.x) does not show an association as large as the primary result. Its event count must be reported; a sparse negative control is a limitation, not permission to substitute another control.

A clinically material negative history contrast is adverse to the expected “recent rise is higher risk” direction but would still show that trajectory history matters. A null contrast with a narrow interval that excludes the 2-point materiality threshold supports the conclusion that current HbA1c is sufficient for this endpoint in this selected landmark population. A strong current-level association with no history increment supports current-level risk stratification but not the dynamic claim. Disappearance after washout or repeat weighting is adverse to a near-term trajectory interpretation and suggests reverse causation or measured selection.

Results are inconclusive if units or temporal mapping fail; code/date alignment or death ascertainment is materially unresolved; event/overlap gates fail; repeat weights are unstable; the fitted surface is extrapolative; or uncertainty includes both a clinically material increase and a trivial/no increase. Fewer than 100 events, fewer than 10 events in a trajectory stratum, or sparse appendicitis does not make a secondary result a substitute for the primary endpoint.

Computation can establish field availability, selection counts, date ordering, inpatient-code-defined events, disease order, competing-risk estimates, standardized associations, uncertainty, calibration and whether the prespecified gates were met. It cannot establish outpatient disease, adjudicated diabetes/CKD/heart failure, disease stage or severity, treatment effects, causal benefit from repeat testing, representativeness, or that clinicians should change management. Those claims require validated dictionaries and dates, outpatient laboratory/medication data, clinical adjudication, external validation and a prospective testing-and-management study.
