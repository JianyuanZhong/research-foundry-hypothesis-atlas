The source audit did not estimate the association. The unresolved claim is whether, among adult ICU stays that reach a prespecified 6-hour landmark without an early creatinine-rise signal, duration/intensity of low MAP during ICU hours 0–6 is associated with a later *observed creatinine-rise signal* beyond the same minimum MAP and measured baseline risk.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: conditional on minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, MAP coverage, measured admission risk, and hospital context, greater time-weighted MAP burden below 65 mmHg during [0,360] minutes is associated with a higher cumulative incidence of a new creatinine rise during the observable post-landmark interval through 2,880 minutes. The claim is falsified by a null or reversed burden contrast, disappearance after coverage/artifact and observation-boundary sensitivities, or failure to preserve direction and calibration in hospitals held out before fitting. This is an observational prognostic claim, not evidence that changing MAP or vasopressor dose prevents AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

A stable burden increment could justify better renal surveillance and motivate a future causal/interventional study. It cannot diagnose KDIGO AKI because the configured data do not supply adjudicated baseline renal reserve, complete urine-output context, or reliable post-ICU laboratory follow-up.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results require a prespecified clinically nontrivial burden contrast beyond minimum MAP, uncertainty compatible with benefit and not merely statistical significance, consistent direction in the confirmed-rise endpoint, stability after coverage/no-interpolation/noninvasive-MAP/lab-revision/vasopressor sensitivities, and preserved direction/calibration in held-out hospitals. Mechanistic persistence/recovery contrasts strengthen interpretation but are not necessary.

Adverse results are a null/reversed burden contrast, loss after density or observation-boundary sensitivity, poor calibration or direction in held-out hospitals, or a signal explained by post-landmark measurement intensity. This favors a nadir-only or site-dependent-observation explanation; it does not show hypotension is physiologically harmless.

Inconclusive results include too few landmark stays, sparse MAP or creatinine coverage, unstable unit/value maps, competing unit discharge/death dominating follow-up, or intervals spanning clinically important effects. Report these without forcing support or harm.

Computationally checkable outputs are source/header and value maps, row and cohort flow, deduplication, units, coverage, joins, unit-observation boundaries, state counts/times, leakage-free splits, fitted contrasts, uncertainty, calibration, transport, and sensitivities. True AKI, pre-ICU renal reserve, urine-output adjudication, treatment intent, reasons for absent tests or discharge, causal treatment effects, and whether a MAP target should change care require clinical adjudication, richer data, or another study.
