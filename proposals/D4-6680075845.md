## Unresolved question and clinical importance
Plasma-proteomic screens repeatedly nominate single circulating proteins (GDF15, FABP1,
CHI3L1, IL-6/CRP family) as disease-specific preclinical biomarkers. It is unresolved
whether a protein–diagnosis association marks a disease-specific process (claim A) or
mainly a shared systemic morbidity/inflammation/aging axis elevated across many diagnoses
and tracking cumulative disease burden (claim B) [K2]. Prediction is not specificity: a signature
predictive of one disease can be more parsimoniously explained by a shared exposure and extend
across unrelated conditions [K1]. Measured plasma protein abundances also carry substantial
cis/trans genetic architecture, so exposed values are imperfect proxies of the underlying
process and measurement/model uncertainty must be reported [K3]. This decides whether a
circulating protein is a credible disease-specific early-detection target or only a generic
risk/severity correlate — decisive for any biomarker or prevention programme.

## Weakness of the parent discriminant (why a new one is needed)
The parent's estimand is a within-disease coefficient attenuation fraction
AF = 1 − |β_adj| / |β_unadj| after adding a shared-axis term. That discriminant conflates
two explanations: a genuine shared axis [K2], and a disease-specific signal whose proteins are
merely correlated with one another (so a data-driven axis absorbs it even when it is
disease-specific) [K1]. It also cannot carry the measurement-uncertainty caveat that protein
levels are imperfect proxies [K3]. It therefore cannot cleanly separate A from B, and it admits
over-adjustment/collider risk because the axis is fit on the same protein panel that the
disease may perturb.

## This child's scientific delta: a cross-disease predictive TRANSFER-SPECIFICITY test
Operationalize "shared" as *predictive non-specificity*, which is directly falsifiable:
1. **Participant-level sample splitting** (inherited). Fit all signatures/axes on a
   discovery half of `n_eid`; evaluate only on the held-out half.
2. **Transfer asymmetry (new, decisive).** For a target disease D (≥200 cases), train an
   L2-penalized logistic protein signature for D on the discovery half. On the held-out
   half report AUC(D) and the mean AUC over a pre-specified set of *unrelated* diseases D'
   (different ICD-10 chapters, each ≥200 cases). Define the transfer gap
   Δ_D = AUC(D) − mean_AUC(D').
   - Claim A predicts Δ_D > 0 and material: the signature carries target-specific
     information that does not transfer.
   - Claim B predicts Δ_D ≈ 0: the signature is a generic allowed predictor of many
     conditions.
3. **Per-protein contribution contrast (new).** For each protein, contrast its
   disease-preferential residual (target block vs negative-control block, see below) to
   quantify how many proteins drive any non-zero Δ_D.
4. **Collider / over-adjustment safeguard (new).** Refit the shared axis with disease
   status excluded from axis construction, and additionally report the attenuation
   estimand under an axis that is orthogonalized to the target disease indicator; a large
   change between the two axes bounds the over-adjustment sensitivity.
The parent's per-(protein,disease) attenuation fraction is retained as a *secondary*
estimand so the two discriminants can be compared, but Δ_D is now the primary
falsification route.

## Supportive / adverse / inconclusive
- Supportive of claim A: a non-empty protein set with Δ_D clearly positive beyond the
  permutation band for ≥1 high-burden disease, stable across the two split seeds.
- Supportive of claim B: Δ_D ≈ 0 for all diseases (within the permutation band) while
  AUC(D) is nonetheless above the age+sex baseline — i.e. models predict disease but not
  disease-specifically.
- Adverse / design caution: large in-sample-vs-held-out attenuation drop (axis overfit),
  or a large sensitivity of AF to the disease-orthogonalized axis (over-adjustment).
- Inconclusive: <200 cases for the tested blocks, non-identifiable axis (dominant PC
  degenerate), or permutation band overlapping all Δ_D.

## Episode-34 advance: an EXECUTED coherence diagnostic (no association result)
The parent/child designs all rest on an unstated assumption: that a dominant *coherent* shared axis
exists and is the object being tested. This episode quantified it read-only on the bound Olink table
(`[UKB data file]`, 53,014 x 2,924; details in `work/lead-ep34/coherence_diagnostic.json`):
- A 300-protein random subsample (seed 0), per-protein z-scored (10.3% missing cells mean-imputed),
  gives correlation eigenvalues with first-PC variance fraction f1 = 0.173 and cumulative top-5 = 0.293,
  top-10 = 0.360, against an independently-shuffled within-panel permutation band of f1 = 0.004,
  top-5 = 0.019 (median off-diagonal r = 0.096).
- Interpretation: a strong dominant shared component clearly exists (f1 far above the permutation band),
  consistent with claim B's premise [K2]; BUT it explains only ~17% of protein variance, so a single
  shared axis cannot by itself explain cross-disease protein-disease associations. The disease-specific
  residual test (out-of-sample transfer gap Delta_D, below) therefore remains the unresolved, falsifiable
  question, and a large f1 alone must not be read as proof of claim B (shared assay/batch and
  inflammation/severity structure would also inflate f1) [K3].
- Pre-specified solver threshold: claim B ("one coherent axis is sufficient") requires f1 above the
  within-panel permutation band 95th percentile AND held-out Delta_D within the null band. Claim A
  ("disease-specific residual") is supported only if Delta_D > 0 beyond the band and survives
  removal/orthogonalization of the shared axis.
This diagnostic is bounded (300-protein subsample, single panel seed, mean-imputed) and is an
illustrative coherence magnitude only; the solver must recompute on the full 2,923-protein matrix
with the same estimator and report the split manifest.
