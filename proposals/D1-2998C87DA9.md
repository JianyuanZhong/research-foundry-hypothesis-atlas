# Does the timing of laboratory surveillance add a death-specific ICU signal?

Status: Episode-25 substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that changing surveillance improves survival.

## Unresolved question and advance

The strongest available evidence is an outcome-blind feasibility audit and source/header inspection, not a fitted result. The parent established a transportable, competing-risk experiment asking whether laboratory observation history adds information beyond recorded physiology, vital-monitoring intensity, documented care limitation and hospital surveillance regime. A remaining threat is temporal aggregation: a total count, span or recency can conflate continuous early monitoring with a short late burst near minute 360.

The unresolved claim is whether the *within-stay timing and persistence* of laboratory observation before minute 360 adds transportable, death-specific information about later recorded ICU death after recorded state, care-limitation documentation and vital surveillance are accounted for. A late burst could indicate impending deterioration, but could instead reflect discharge preparation, transfer, escalation workflow, interface behavior or generic documentation.

This is clinically important because a surveillance-derived alert could mislabel workflow as hidden deterioration. Evidence of a robust timing signal would support retrospective risk review using records already available at a fixed landmark; it would not show that more testing, a particular test, or an alert changes outcomes. The substantive advance over the parent is a prespecified test of temporal pattern versus aggregate workflow, while preserving the parent’s endpoint-specific death-versus-discharge test.

## Hypothesis and what would falsify it

Primary hypothesis: replacing aggregate laboratory-process summaries with six time-resolved surveillance bins improves held-out-hospital calibration and integrated competing-risk Brier score for recorded ICU death in (360,1800], beyond the parent’s recorded-state-plus-aggregate-process baseline. The gain should be larger for recorded death than for alive ICU discharge and should persist after adjustment for time-resolved vital-observation intensity.

The stronger temporal interpretation predicts attenuation when each patient’s six laboratory-process bins are order-permuted while preserving total laboratory count, overall state and the hospital/risk-stratum surveillance distribution. A gain unchanged by this permutation supports aggregate workflow rather than temporal information. A gain concentrated only in the final bin, reproduced for alive discharge, reproduced by vital-process features, or reversed by common-panel/interface/revision restrictions is adverse evidence against a patient-linked deterioration interpretation.

No fitted result or clinical conclusion is claimed in this proposal.

## Features and two-method comparison

The simple transparent baseline B0 is a pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly outcome intervals (360,420] through (1740,1800]. It uses the same adult first-stay cohort, admission/demographic context, laboratory state and availability summaries, vital state and masks, aggregate laboratory/vital process summaries, and carePlanEOL presence/timing. It does not use six-bin laboratory timing.

The primary time-resolved transparent model Btime uses the same cause-specific hazard framework, same covariates and same folds, but replaces aggregate laboratory-process summaries with six prespecified bins: [0,60], (60,120], (120,180], (180,240], (240,300] and (300,360]. In each bin calculate numeric-result count, distinct result times, distinct analyte/interface groups, common-panel coverage, revision-known count and missing-bin indicators. Make corresponding six-bin vital observation count, distinct observation times, field coverage and missing-bin features. Overall span/gaps/recency may be secondary, explicitly labelled summaries.

The substantive learned alternative T is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over the same six bins and supported laboratory, vital, state, missingness and EOL channels, with separate death and discharge heads and no hospital ID. It can reveal ordering, persistence, bursts and co-movement lost by summaries. It is retained only if it improves held-out endpoint-specific calibration/Brier or fixed-burden review diagnostics over Btime under equal inputs and matched development/validation folds, with order-shuffle and parameter-matched non-temporal controls. Complexity or GPU use is not a selection criterion.

The baseline loses temporal ordering but is transparent, stable and auditable. Btime tests the specific temporal hypothesis with interpretable coefficients. T is deferred if it adds no uncertainty-supported information beyond Btime. This preserves a useful learned/mechanistic alternative without assuming that a neural model or GPU is required.

## Split, estimand, uncertainty and review diagnostic

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals; keep all stays for a uniquepid together. Within each development fold use a 75/25 fit/validation split. Learn codebooks, analyte compatibility rules, imputation, scaling, temporal feature selection, calibration and thresholds only in development hospitals. Primary comparisons are macro-hospital paired differences; patient-weighted summaries are secondary.

Report death- and discharge-specific cumulative-incidence calibration at prespecified times, calibration intercept/slope, integrated competing-risk Brier score, hospital-level paired differences for B0/Btime/T, and 95% hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals (at least 1,000 resamples if feasible). At minute 360, report death-risk review burden at thresholds 0.01, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10: alerts per 100, event capture, PPV, false-review burden, number needed to review and Btime/B0 reclassification. Require at least 200 held-out episodes, 20 recorded deaths and 20 hospitals per threshold cell. This is a label-relative review diagnostic, not clinical utility or net benefit.

Primary support gates are at least 100 eligible hospitals, 50 event-bearing hospitals, 1,000 recorded deaths, 1,000 competing discharges, approximately 20 hospitals per fold with both events where feasible, per-variable vital coverage >=30% and >=20% with two observations, and overlapping state/process support in >=80% of held-out hospitals. The temporal claim additionally requires support in early and late bins. Failed gates make that claim inconclusive, not a reason to change the endpoint.

## Falsification and interpretation criteria

Prespecify:

1. Within-patient six-bin order permutation preserving total laboratory count and overall state.
2. Hospital- and baseline-risk-stratified process permutation across patients.
3. Matched vital-process increment (Btime/B0 laboratory timing versus the corresponding vital-process model).
4. Death-versus-alive-discharge increment contrast.
5. Full, common-analyte, revision-known-only and interface-stratified codebooks.
6. Final-bin masking and early-only sensitivity analyses.
7. T order-shuffle and parameter-matched non-temporal controls.
8. Strict prohibition of post-360 rows, Apache mortality fields, target-fold preprocessing and outcome-dependent codebooks.

Supportive evidence requires valid gates, uncertainty-compatible Btime-over-B0 improvement for death, a larger death than discharge increment, attenuation after temporal-order permutation, no comparable vital/site increment, and stability across coding restrictions. Adverse evidence includes unchanged performance after temporal permutation, equal/larger discharge gain, concentration in the final bin or one interface/hospital regime, reproduction by vital process, or reversal under revision/common-panel restrictions. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse temporal cells, failed overlap, unstable fits, intervals spanning clinically meaningful effects, or inability to distinguish absent observation from absent physiology.
