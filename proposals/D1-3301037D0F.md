# Recorded recovery order and competing early failure after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct, fit and audit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study of whether the *recorded order of respiratory versus vasoactive support recovery* distinguishes two clinically different early failure pathways: ICU-return-compatible escalation versus death before return.

Completion is established only by computed, locked outputs:

1. `run_manifest.json`: source/catalog/snapshot/schema hashes, archive members, verified item labels, software versions, split seeds, thresholds, q/topology rules and hashes of every output.
2. `cohort_audit.parquet`: one row per subject's earliest eligible ICU stay, with joins, exclusions, temporal integrity, window coverage and observation-eligibility flags.
3. `hourly_features.parquet`: twelve left-closed bins in [`outtime-12h`, `outtime`), with physiology, support evidence, source-specific observation masks, row counts and no post-outtime predictors.
4. `transition_ledger.parquet`: every qualifying chart, interval and corroborating procedure row, validity status, half-window state, last-positive timestamps, contradictions, observed-off evidence, uninformative absence and q recoding.
5. `outcome_topology.parquet`: every later ICU candidate within the follow-up boundary, every candidate transfer bridge, topology/timing/tie flags, and the lower- and upper-bound event labels.
6. `sequence_contrasts.parquet`: held-out standardized 6/24/48-hour CIFs and pairwise contrasts for documented-bridge return, any ICU return, death and alive discharge, including the compatible interval for the latent transition event.
7. Locked patient-level test predictions from an auditable discrete-time baseline and a matched observation-aware two-domain HSMM, with calibration, IPCW Brier scores, subject-bootstrap uncertainty, split stability, convergence and state diagnostics.
8. `falsification_outputs.parquet` and `interpretation_map.parquet`: each scientific statement linked to a computed estimate, horizon, uncertainty interval, event count, bound width and evidence limitation.

A readiness check or discovery audit is not a clinical result. This proposal contains no fitted association and does not claim that any sequence is safer.

## Unresolved clinical question

The strongest available evidence is feasibility and endpoint-identification evidence, not a clinical conclusion. The inherited adult first-stay audit found 52,591 eligible first outtimes, 2,661 later ICU returns within 48 hours, 2,060 with a qualifying documented non-ICU bridge and 601 without one; 294 returns had multiple bridge candidates. It showed that recorded support, observation intensity and transfer topology can be represented, but it did not estimate whether a respiratory or hemodynamic pattern is associated with an outcome.

The unresolved question is:

> Among adults whose respiratory and vasoactive support both show a recent recorded transition off before the first ICU outtime, does the recorded order of those transitions identify a different short-term balance of return-compatible escalation and death?

This is a decision-relevant prognostic question about what kind of post-ICU review might be needed, not a claim that one treatment should be stopped earlier. The clinically interpretable, but explicitly proxy-defined, sequence groups are:

- **V-first**: the last valid vasoactive-positive evidence precedes the last valid respiratory-positive evidence by at least 60 minutes;
- **R-first**: the last valid respiratory-positive evidence precedes the last valid vasoactive-positive evidence by at least 60 minutes;
- **near-concordant**: the absolute recorded timestamp difference is less than 60 minutes;
- **unorderable/discordant**: contradictory, invalid or insufficient timestamp evidence; retained for audit and secondary analyses, not silently assigned an order.

“First” means first in the order of the last *recorded positive* evidence, not the true physiologic or treatment cessation time. The 60-minute margin is a prespecified protection against timestamp granularity and near-ties; 0- and 5-minute endpoint tolerances are separate topology sensitivities.

### Falsifiable primary hypothesis

At 48 hours after the index outtime, compared with R-first:

1. V-first has a higher cumulative-incidence contrast for a later ICU stay after a documented non-ICU bridge, compatible with escalation after ward transfer; and
2. R-first has a higher first-event in-hospital death contrast before ICU return.

For the return-compatible event, let (L_g(h)) be the CIF for a qualifying documented non-ICU bridge followed by a later ICU stay in group (g), and (U_g(h)) the CIF for any later ICU stay. The unknown transition-compatible CIF (T_g(h)) is reported only as (L_g(h) le T_g(h) le U_g(h)). The V-first minus R-first contrast is therefore the compatible interval

[
[,L_V(h)-U_R(h), U_V(h)-L_R(h),].
]

The primary claim is supportive only if the 48-hour interval is wholly above zero and the observed first-event death contrast (D_R(48)-D_V(48)) is wholly above zero, with 95% subject-level uncertainty intervals, at strict observed-off coding (q=0), and both directions remain stable across the prespecified q and topology sensitivities. A single component can be reported as partial support; it does not establish the whole two-pathway hypothesis. Six- and 24-hour estimates are time-localization checks, not substitutes for the primary horizon.

A secondary parent-preserving analysis compares the same four groups—durable recorded-off, recent respiratory withdrawal, recent vasoactive cessation and joint recent withdrawal—to determine whether the order result is genuinely new or merely a restatement of domain-specific withdrawal. The sequence question is primary within joint withdrawal; the broader four-group analysis is contextual and multiplicity-labeled.

## Clinical importance and advance

A simple “ICU readmission” endpoint conflates a potentially rescuable post-transfer escalation with death before return, and the latter can remove the opportunity to be readmitted. A simple residual-instability score also discards whether hemodynamic or respiratory support was the last domain to clear. The proposed contrast could inform which pathway deserves targeted review at ICU-to-ward transition—respiratory surveillance, hemodynamic reassessment, or both—if it is reproducible and clinically adjudicated later.

The substantive advance over the parent is a timing/discordance estimand: it asks whether the sequence of domain recovery carries information beyond the presence of recent withdrawal and final measurements. The learned alternative tests whether this order is a durable latent state transition rather than an artifact of one timestamp. Partial identification makes the clinical advance conditional: a strong result may distinguish pathways, while a wide bound may show that available records cannot support that interpretation.

Current public literature searches found broad ICU-readmission prediction and discharge-risk work, but the search outputs are not treated as proof of this MIMIC hypothesis and no unavailable full text or supplementary method is claimed. The three local demonstrations are methodological examples, not a topic mandate: the dated-sequence idea motivates the temporal alternative; the Bayesian longitudinal idea motivates observation-aware latent states; and the multimodal cancer demonstration cannot be reproduced because its main paper/full methods and image modality are unavailable here.

## Exposure construction and observation uncertainty

Create twelve one-hour bins and two six-hour halves: preceding (P=[outtime-12h,outtime-6h)) and final (F=[outtime-6h,outtime)).

Respiratory-positive evidence is valid charted O2 flow >2 L/min, FiO2 >0.21, PEEP >0, or corroborating intubation/NIV procedure evidence. Vasoactive-positive evidence is a valid listed input interval overlapping the bin. Record raw rows, interval clipping, status and channel. Observations are classified separately as positive, observed non-positive, uninformative absence, contradiction or invalid. For primary coverage, require at least two respiratory observation bins and two MAP observation bins in each half; retain excluded and inadequate strata for selection diagnostics.

For each domain and half, a recent-withdrawal indicator requires positive evidence in P and no positive evidence in F. At q=0, F must contain observed non-positive/off evidence; an absence with no informative observation remains unknown and is not called off. The locked q sensitivity set is {0,.10,.25,.50,.75,1.00}; only uninformative absence may be recoded under q, while observed non-positive values and contradictions are never recoded. The implementation must document whether intermediate q is represented by fixed fractional weights or a fixed hash-seeded stochastic recoding, and use the same rule in both models.

The primary sequence cohort is the intersection of recent respiratory withdrawal and recent vasoactive withdrawal under the relevant q scenario. For each domain, define the last-positive time from valid evidence in P∪F: `charttime` for chart evidence, procedure `starttime` for corroboration, and vasoactive interval `endtime` for an input interval. Keep channel-specific timestamps and a timestamp-quality flag. Classify the order only when both last-positive times are valid and their absolute difference is at least 60 minutes; otherwise use near-concordant or unorderable. These timestamps are proxies for documentation order, not actual support stop times.

The contextual four-group analysis uses durable recorded-off (no positive evidence in either half with informative final observation), recent respiratory withdrawal, recent vasoactive withdrawal and joint recent withdrawal. It also reports active/discordant/inadequate groups instead of conditioning them away. There is no imputation of missing support as physiologic recovery.

## Outcomes and partial identification

For each index stay, search all later ICU stays in the same admission with `later.intime > index.outtime) before the 48-hour/discharge boundary. The observed upper-bound event (U) is any such later ICU return.

The documented-bridge lower-bound event (L) requires at least one `hosp/transfers` row with non-null `careunit`, valid `intime < outtime), interval start no earlier than index outtime minus five minutes, interval end no later than later ICU intime plus five minutes, and a clipped non-ICU interval of at least 30 minutes between index outtime and later ICU intime. The bridge careunit must not equal the later stay's `first_careunit` or `last_careunit`. Record all qualifying candidates, not just the first. No bridge label implies intent, deterioration, preventability or an unplanned return.

The primary first-event outcomes within each horizon are L, U, death before either return, and alive hospital discharge before either return. Death uses `admissions.deathtime`; discharge uses `admissions.dischtime`. Death wins a one-minute tie, and a non-discharge event wins a discharge tie. Repeat topology with 0- and 5-minute tolerances. Report (L le T le U) at each horizon, the interval width, death CIF, alive-discharge CIF, event counts and all topology flags. Sensitivities include 0/5-minute endpoint tolerance, bridge durations 0/15/30/60 minutes, careunit-only versus interval logic, discharge-event exclusion, and ICU-unit matching. CIFs must sum consistently under the declared competing-event convention.

## Baseline and scientifically informative alternative

Both methods use identical raw inputs, q scenarios, temporal window, covariates, topology labels, split seeds and held-out population.

The auditable baseline is pooled one-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards for L, U, death and alive discharge, plus an aggregate-return model. Predictors are the sequence category, final and preceding support indicators, physiology summaries (MAP, HR, RR, SpO2, temperature), support duration/last-positive summaries, observation masks and row counts, admission context, age, gender, anchor-year group and ICU LOS. Include only prespecified sequence-by-final-burden and sequence-by-observation interactions. Fit nested support/physiology-only, observation/process-only and full models. Standardize held-out predictions to the common primary cohort and derive group CIFs and the compatible contrast interval. This baseline is directly auditable and tests the clinical contrast without relying on a complex representation.

The substantive alternative is a factorized two-domain observation-aware HSMM. Each respiratory and hemodynamic domain has duration-aware latent states {active support, recovering/transition, recorded-off, unknown}; emissions use the same physiology/support ledger, and a separate observation model uses source masks, row counts and channel availability. Constrain persistence and withdrawal transitions, fit the supervised competing-event head to the same observable outcomes, and fit all state/duration/regularization choices using train/validation only. The alternative retains order, duration and domain discordance that baseline summaries lose. It can reveal whether V-first/R-first is a short-lived timestamp artifact or part of a persistent domain-specific recovery state. It cannot identify missing transfers or actual cessation; latent transition estimates must remain within [L,U] and be reported with posterior/fit uncertainty and latent entropy.

Compare methods on held-out CIF calibration, IPCW Brier, calibration intercept/slope, event-specific reliability, subject-bootstrap intervals, split stability, positivity/ESS and latent-state stability. A small predictive gain is not a reason to prefer HSMM; the alternative earns a scientific role only if it provides stable, interpretable duration/order/state diagnostics that explain or challenge the prespecified contrast. A null or adverse HSMM result remains scientifically informative.

## Falsification and interpretation gates

Predeclare:

- timestamp and leakage audits showing no post-outtime predictors; `storetime`-only features must not reproduce the sequence contrast;
- within-window order reversal/time shuffling that preserves marginal support burden; a genuine order result should attenuate;
- support-channel ablations (chart only, input only, procedure removed) and each respiratory channel separately;
- q, window (6/12/24 hours), final-observation, topology, tie and bridge-duration sensitivities;
- transfer-careunit permutation and a +6-hour transfer timing shift; a topology-specific signal that survives implausible transfer structure is suspect;
- a pre-landmark pseudo-return/topology audit, which should not create valid post-outtime events;
- admission-type, anchor-year, first/last-careunit and transfer-density strata, with overlap and effective sample size;
- random exposure permutation as a coding sanity check and competing-CIF sum checks.

Supportive evidence requires both prespecified 48-hour directions to be stable at q=0 and across the locked q/topology frontiers, uncertainty compatible with nonzero effects, adequate event counts/overlap, no storetime or single-channel dependence, attenuation under order reversal, and baseline/HSMM agreement. It supports a retrospective record-compatible association between recorded transition order and competing outcomes.

Adverse evidence is reversal of either primary direction, a contrast that collapses after observation-process adjustment, a signal only for U but not L, a signal that survives transfer permutation or order reversal, dependence on one channel/topology rule, non-positivity, or HSMM latent outputs outside [L,U]. Wide intervals, sparse sequence strata, high latent entropy, unstable q frontiers, many convergence failures or poor overlap are inconclusive rather than negative.

The output-to-conclusion map must prevent a model score from becoming a clinical recommendation. Computationally checkable claims include cohort construction, timestamp ordering, event/CIF calculation, partial-identification bounds, calibration, uncertainty, split stability and falsification behavior. Clinical adjudication is required for actual respiratory/hemodynamic deterioration, treatment cessation, discharge appropriateness, intent, treatment limitation, monitoring adequacy, preventability, unplanned status and utility of a pathway-specific surveillance policy.

## Alternatives not chosen and evidence for revisiting

- The parent domain-only competing-pathway design remains a required contextual analysis; it would become primary again if sequence strata are too sparse or order is mostly unorderable.
- A renal asynchronous-recovery branch is deferred because direct GFR, volume status and validated kidney-recovery adjudication are unavailable in the configured MIMIC snapshot; revisiting requires verified lab definitions plus a prespecified kidney endpoint and sufficient support.
- Antimicrobial de-escalation is deferred because indication, source control, susceptibility-result availability and treatment intent are not adequately observed.
- A GRU/TCN is deferred because it adds capacity without repairing the missing-transfer and treatment-intent problem; revisit only if a preregistered representation test shows state/order information that the HSMM cannot express.
- A causal discharge-policy or dynamic-treatment analysis is not selected: treatment assignment, goals of care, ward surveillance and counterfactual outcomes are absent. It requires clinician-adjudicated treatment intent, richer post-discharge observation and preferably a causal/prospective design.
- Waveform/image and multimodal adaptations are unavailable: raw waveforms and MIMIC-CXR images are not in the configured snapshot. Radiology and discharge text are present, but validated context-aware extraction is unavailable; text is therefore limited to documentation availability audits.
