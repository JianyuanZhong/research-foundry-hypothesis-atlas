# Temporal Feedback Structure of Macrophage-FLS Inflammatory Signaling in RA Synovial Tissue

## Scientific Opening

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue exhibits a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop between macrophages and fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) [K1]. The crosstalk is well documented: FLS secrete IL-34, CCL2, and CXCL12 that recruit and polarize macrophages toward the M1 phenotype, while M1 macrophage-derived TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β further activate FLS [K1]. However, the **temporal ordering** of this feedback is unresolved. Three non-equivalent explanations are compatible with the current evidence:

1. **Unidirectional macrophage-driven model**: Macrophage activation precedes and drives the FLS inflammatory response. FLS are passive recipients of macrophage-derived signals.
2. **Bidirectional feedback loop**: Macrophage and FLS activation are mutually reinforcing with no clear temporal precedence. Both cell types can initiate the loop.
3. **Composition-driven model**: The apparent "feedback" is an artifact of changes in cell composition (e.g., increased macrophage infiltration) rather than a genuine dynamic interaction between cell types.

This distinction is clinically consequential. If the loop is unidirectional (macrophage-driven), targeting macrophage activation (e.g., MerTK inhibition, M-CSF blockade) should be sufficient to break the cycle. If it is bidirectional, both cell types must be targeted simultaneously. If it is composition-driven, depleting the infiltrating cell type may suffice without targeting the resident FLS.

The available datasets provide a unique opportunity to test this question using **perturbation-based experiments** (coculture with/without macrophages, MerTK inhibitor, LPS pre-treatment) across multiple donors, combined with **time-course data** (GSE95588 d1-d4) to assess temporal dynamics.

## Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: In RA synovial tissue, the inflammatory signaling between macrophages and FLS is **bidirectional with macrophage activation preceding FLS response** (i.e., a directed feedback loop with macrophages as the initiator). Specifically:

- Macrophage-derived signals (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β) drive the initial FLS inflammatory response.
- FLS-derived signals (IL-34, CCL2, CXCL12) then reinforce macrophage activation, creating a positive feedback loop.
- The loop is **not** merely composition-driven: the FLS response is proportional to macrophage activation state, not just macrophage number.
- The loop is **not** unidirectional: FLS-derived signals are necessary for sustained macrophage activation.

This hypothesis is falsifiable: if the FLS response is identical regardless of macrophage activation state (Mpos vs Mneg), the unidirectional model is supported. If the FLS response is absent without macrophage-derived signals (Mneg condition), the bidirectional model is supported. If the FLS response is proportional to macrophage number rather than activation state, the composition-driven model is supported.

## Evidence Supporting the Opening

- **Claim supported**: FLS-macrophage crosstalk forms a self-reinforcing vicious feedback loop in RA. IL-34 from FLS promotes macrophage M1 polarization; M1 macrophages further stimulate FLS. The loop amplifies RANKL-dependent osteoclastogenesis and bone erosion.
- **Contribution of this proposal**: Li et al. demonstrate the existence of the feedback loop but do not test its temporal ordering or directionality. Our proposal uses perturbation-based coculture data to test whether the loop is unidirectional, bidirectional, or composition-driven.
- **Limitation**: Li et al. used conditioned medium experiments and a mouse model (CIA). They did not use single-cell RNA-seq of primary human RA synovial tissue in coculture conditions.

- **Contribution of this proposal**: Romoff et al. demonstrate that FLS and macrophages interact in a complex, non-trivial way in the synovial microenvironment. Our proposal extends this to the inflammatory signaling axis (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, IL-34) and tests the temporal ordering of the feedback.
- **Limitation**: Romoff et al. focused on T cell activation, not the macrophage-FLS inflammatory feedback loop. They did not use time-course data or perturbation-based coculture experiments.

- **Claim supported**: M1-like macrophages exhibit higher core retention in 3D spheroid models of RA synovial tissue. TNF and synovial fluid increase macrophage core density. M1 spheroids show elevated expression of M1 markers, chemokines, and EC-activating signatures.

- **Limitation**: Philippon et al. used a 3D spheroid model with bulk RNA-seq (n=4). They did not use single-cell RNA-seq or time-course data. The 3D model may not capture the temporal dynamics of the feedback loop.

## Competing Explanations with Specific Observable Predictions

### 1. Unidirectional macrophage-driven model
- **Mechanism**: Macrophage activation precedes and drives the FLS inflammatory response. FLS are passive recipients of macrophage-derived signals (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β). FLS-derived signals (IL-34, CCL2, CXCL12) do not reinforce macrophage activation.
- **Specific observable predictions**:
  - (a) In E-MTAB-8873, FLS inflammatory gene expression (TNF, IL6, IL1B) is significantly higher in Mpos vs Mneg conditions (donor-aware mixed model, p < 0.05 FDR-corrected).
  - (b) FLS-derived signal genes (IL34, CCL2, CXCL12) are NOT significantly upregulated in Mpos vs Mneg conditions (p > 0.1).
  - (c) In GSE95588 time-course, macrophage activation genes (CD68, CD163, TNF) are upregulated at d1, while FLS inflammatory genes (TNF, IL6, IL1B) are upregulated at d2-d4, with a clear temporal lag.
  - (d) MerTK inhibitor treatment does NOT reduce FLS inflammatory gene expression (the FLS response is not dependent on macrophage activation state).
- **Clinical implication**: Targeting macrophage activation alone should be sufficient to break the inflammatory loop.
- **Severity**: If true, MerTK inhibition or M-CSF blockade would be the primary therapeutic strategy.

### 2. Bidirectional feedback loop
- **Mechanism**: Macrophage and FLS activation are mutually reinforcing. Macrophage-derived signals drive FLS activation, and FLS-derived signals reinforce macrophage activation. The loop is self-sustaining.
- **Specific observable predictions**:
  - (a) In E-MTAB-8873, FLS inflammatory gene expression (TNF, IL6, IL1B) is significantly higher in Mpos vs Mneg conditions (donor-aware mixed model, p < 0.05 FDR-corrected).
  - (b) FLS-derived signal genes (IL34, CCL2, CXCL12) ARE significantly upregulated in Mpos vs Mneg conditions (p < 0.05 FDR-corrected), indicating that FLS respond to macrophage activation by producing signals that reinforce macrophage activation.
  - (c) In GSE95588 time-course, macrophage activation genes are upregulated at d1, FLS inflammatory genes at d2-d4, AND FLS-derived signal genes (IL34, CCL2) are upregulated at d3-d4, indicating a feedback loop with temporal ordering.
  - (d) MerTK inhibitor treatment significantly reduces FLS inflammatory gene expression (p < 0.05), indicating that the FLS response is dependent on macrophage activation state.
  - (e) LPS pre-treatment of macrophages significantly increases FLS inflammatory gene expression (p < 0.05), indicating that macrophage activation state drives FLS response.
- **Clinical implication**: Both macrophage and FLS must be targeted simultaneously to break the loop.
- **Severity**: If true, combination therapy (e.g., MerTK inhibition + FLS-targeted therapy) would be required.

### 3. Composition-driven model
- **Mechanism**: The apparent "feedback" is an artifact of changes in cell composition (e.g., increased macrophage infiltration) rather than a genuine dynamic interaction between cell types. FLS response is proportional to macrophage number, not activation state.
- **Specific observable predictions**:
  - (a) In E-MTAB-8873, FLS inflammatory gene expression is NOT significantly different between Mpos and Mneg conditions when macrophage number is matched (p > 0.1).
  - (b) FLS inflammatory gene expression is proportional to macrophage number (e.g., in the "co-cultured with MerTK/CD206neg and MerTK/CD206pos macrophages" condition, which has more macrophages, FLS response is higher than in Mpos or Mneg alone).
  - (c) In GSE95588 time-course, macrophage and FLS genes are upregulated simultaneously (both at d1), with no temporal lag.
  - (d) MerTK inhibitor treatment does NOT reduce FLS inflammatory gene expression (the FLS response is not dependent on macrophage activation state, only number).
  - (e) LPS pre-treatment of macrophages does NOT significantly increase FLS inflammatory gene expression (the FLS response is not dependent on macrophage activation state).
- **Clinical implication**: Depleting the infiltrating cell type may suffice without targeting the resident FLS.
- **Severity**: If true, macrophage depletion (e.g., CSF-1R inhibition) would be the primary therapeutic strategy.

### 4. Donor-specific baseline
- **Mechanism**: FLS inflammatory response varies by donor, independent of macrophage condition. The feedback loop is not a general feature of RA but a donor-specific phenomenon.
- **Specific observable predictions**:
  - (a) In E-MTAB-8873, the donor-specific baseline (intercept) explains most of the variance in FLS inflammatory gene expression (R² > 0.5).
  - (b) The macrophage condition effect (Mpos vs Mneg) is NOT significant after accounting for donor (p > 0.1).
  - (c) The direction of the macrophage condition effect varies by donor (some donors show Mpos > Mneg, others show Mneg > Mpos).
- **Clinical implication**: The feedback loop is not a general feature of RA but a donor-specific phenomenon.
- **Severity**: If true, the feedback loop is not a reliable therapeutic target.

## Falsifiable Test

### Primary test: Macrophage activation state drives FLS response
- **Data**: E-MTAB-8873 coculture conditions (Mpos vs Mneg) across 5 donors (SA256A, SA257A, SA258R, SA263R, SA266A).
- **Analysis**: Donor-aware mixed model comparing FLS inflammatory gene expression (TNF, IL6, IL1B, IL34, CCL2, CXCL12) in Mpos vs Mneg conditions.
- **Estimand**: The fixed effect of macrophage activation state (Mpos vs Mneg) on FLS inflammatory gene expression, adjusted for donor-specific baseline (random intercept).
- **Supportive result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is significantly higher in Mpos vs Mneg (p < 0.05, FDR-corrected). This supports the bidirectional feedback loop.
- **Adverse result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is identical in Mpos vs Mneg (p > 0.1). This supports the unidirectional macrophage-driven model.
- **Inconclusive result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is higher in Mpos vs Mneg but not significant (0.05 < p < 0.1). This is inconclusive and requires additional data.

### Secondary test: MerTK inhibitor blocks the feedback
- **Data**: E-MTAB-8873 inhibitor conditions (Inhibitor vs none) across 5 donors.
- **Analysis**: Donor-aware mixed model comparing FLS inflammatory gene expression in Inhibitor vs none conditions.
- **Estimand**: The fixed effect of MerTK inhibitor treatment on FLS inflammatory gene expression, adjusted for donor-specific baseline (random intercept).
- **Supportive result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is significantly lower in Inhibitor vs none (p < 0.05, FDR-corrected). This supports the bidirectional feedback loop (MerTK inhibition blocks macrophage activation, which in turn blocks FLS response).
- **Adverse result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is identical in Inhibitor vs none (p > 0.1). This suggests the feedback loop is not MerTK-dependent.
- **Inconclusive result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is lower in Inhibitor vs none but not significant (0.05 < p < 0.1). This is inconclusive and requires additional data.

### Tertiary test: Time-course dynamics
- **Data**: GSE95588 d1-d4 time-course data (70 samples).
- **Analysis**: Time-series analysis of macrophage and FLS inflammatory gene expression.
- **Estimand**: The temporal ordering of macrophage and FLS activation (macrophage genes upregulated at d1, FLS genes upregulated at d2-d4).
- **Supportive result**: Macrophage activation precedes FLS response (macrophage genes upregulated at d1, FLS genes upregulated at d2-d4). This supports the bidirectional feedback loop with macrophages as the initiator.
- **Adverse result**: FLS response precedes macrophage activation (FLS genes upregulated at d1, macrophage genes upregulated at d2-d4). This supports the unidirectional FLS-driven model.
- **Inconclusive result**: Macrophage and FLS genes are upregulated simultaneously (both at d1). This is inconclusive and requires additional data.

**Note**: GSE95588 donor identity is unresolved (mapping_note warns against statistical inference without GSM metadata inspection). The time-course analysis is exploratory and should be interpreted with caution.

## Method Alternatives

- **Target**: FLS inflammatory gene expression (TNF, IL6, IL1B, IL34, CCL2, CXCL12).
- **Split**: Donor-aware cross-validation (5 donors, leave-one-donor-out).

## Limitations

1. **Assay registry reconciliation**: The assay registry (`[internal dataset path]`) must be inspected to verify cross-dataset compatibility. The E-MTAB-8873 RSEC matrices are in MolsPerCell units, while GSE95588 counts are in raw counts. Normalization is required for cross-dataset comparison.

2. **Inhibitor cleanliness**: The MerTK inhibitor used in E-MTAB-8873 is not specified in the design file. The inhibitor may have off-target effects that confound the interpretation of the feedback loop.

3. **Donor-specific baseline**: The 5 donors in E-MTAB-8873 may have different baseline inflammatory states. The donor-aware mixed model accounts for this, but the small number of donors limits the power of the analysis.

4. **Time-course donor identity**: GSE95588 donor identity is unresolved. The time-course analysis is exploratory and should be interpreted with caution.

5. **Causal interpretation**: The perturbation-based experiments (coculture with/without macrophages, MerTK inhibitor, LPS pre-treatment) provide evidence for causal relationships, but they are not randomized controlled trials. The causal interpretation is limited by the experimental design.

6. **Single-cell resolution**: The E-MTAB-8873 RSEC matrices are in MolsPerCell units, which provides single-cell resolution. However, the RSEC method may introduce technical noise that affects the interpretation of the feedback loop.

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if:
1. FLS inflammatory gene expression is identical in Mpos vs Mneg conditions (p > 0.1), supporting the unidirectional macrophage-driven model.
2. FLS inflammatory gene expression is proportional to macrophage number rather than activation state, supporting the composition-driven model.
3. The macrophage condition effect is not significant after accounting for donor (p > 0.1), supporting the donor-specific baseline model.
4. MerTK inhibitor treatment does not reduce FLS inflammatory gene expression (p > 0.1), suggesting the feedback loop is not MerTK-dependent.
5. LPS pre-treatment of macrophages does not significantly increase FLS inflammatory gene expression (p > 0.1), suggesting the FLS response is not dependent on macrophage activation state.

- **Inconclusive result**: If the result is inconclusive, the next step would be to increase the number of donors and improve the time-course data (resolve GSE95588 donor identity) to increase the power of the analysis.

## Clinical Implications

The temporal ordering of the macrophage-FLS feedback loop has direct clinical implications for RA therapy:
- If the loop is unidirectional (macrophage-driven), targeting macrophage activation (e.g., MerTK inhibition, M-CSF blockade) should be sufficient to break the cycle.
- If it is bidirectional, both cell types must be targeted simultaneously (e.g., MerTK inhibition + FLS-targeted therapy).
- If it is composition-driven, depleting the infiltrating cell type (e.g., CSF-1R inhibition) may suffice without targeting the resident FLS.

This distinction could inform the design of combination therapy for RA, potentially improving treatment response and reducing the risk of drug resistance.
