## Unresolved question and substantive advance

The parent asks whether waist and strength add different BMI-conditional information for recorded cardiovascular/renal versus infection outcomes. A newly retrieved and read UK Biomedicine paper (Wirler et al., BMC Medicine 2026, DOI `10.1186/s12916-026-04907-8`, acquired PMCID `PMC13154465`, source [source checksum]) reports waist/grip associations and sex- and age-heterogeneity for type 2 diabetes, but uses association models and explicitly identifies incremental predictive utility as unresolved. Its diabetes result is not reproduced here.

The new clinical question is:

> At the same BMI and baseline age, does the incremental calibrated value of waist versus grip differ by sex for separate five-year recorded cardiovascular/renal and infection outcomes?

This could inform whether one inexpensive measurement is informative for a particular risk-review context, while recognizing that UKB recorded outcomes cannot by themselves establish a clinical policy. The advance is an interaction estimand about measurement-family-by-sex specificity and endpoint specificity, not another joint-association or predictor leaderboard. It is falsifiable: the sex difference in the waist-versus-grip incremental proper-score contrast may be zero, reversed, or unstable.

The strongest available evidence supports only field presence, parseable values, horizontal `eid` linkage, and dated code/date arrays. It does not establish the clinical identity or units of the local fields, that recorded codes represent onset, complete observation, or that a measurement changes outcomes. Those dependencies are hard gates.

## Hypothesis, estimands, and interpretation

For sex group (sin{0,1}) and endpoint (k), define the baseline B as age, sex, baseline BMI and baseline calendar year. W adds waist; G adds the two grip fields; WG adds both. Let (Delta_{W,s,k}=BS(W)-BS(B)) and (Delta_{G,s,k}=BS(G)-BS(B)), where lower Brier score is better. The primary interaction estimand is
(H_k=[Delta_{W,1,k}-Delta_{G,1,k}]-[Delta_{W,0,k}-Delta_{G,0,k}]), evaluated on sex-standardized BMI/age/year distributions. A predeclared practical margin and confidence interval must be specified before fitting.

Primary hypotheses are that the waist-versus-grip contrast is not exchangeable across sex, and that its direction differs between cardiovascular/renal and infection endpoints. The study does not assume which sex has the larger increment. A secondary continuous-sex interaction model and a sex-by-BMI sensitivity are allowed only after passing field semantics.

The CVD/renal recorded endpoint uses a clinician-reviewed, predeclared ICD-domain list (cardiovascular I00-I99 plus a renal domain only if locally justified); infection uses A00-B99 only if the code domain is verified. The first same-position code/date after baseline is used. For each endpoint, use a three-category five-year recorded outcome: endpoint by horizon, death before endpoint, or neither recorded through horizon. Exclude prior qualifying records only after code/date semantics pass. No participant without a prior record is called disease-free.

Supportive evidence requires semantic, timing, code/date, and ascertainment gates; at least 100 events per claimed endpoint and adequate sex-specific overlap/ESS; calibrated held-out H_k intervals supporting the predeclared margin; and stability under weighting, missingness, folds, and endpoint definitions. Adverse evidence is a null/reversed sex interaction, an apparent interaction explained by calibration or selection, or instability. Failed semantics, incomplete ascertainment, sparse sex cells, or ambiguous onset make the result inconclusive, not negative. No result establishes causal effects, optimal measurement, screening thresholds, treatment benefit, mechanism, or external transport.

## Models and analysis

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on `eid`, with all imputation, scaling, feature construction, selection modeling, tuning, calibration and thresholds fold-local. Fit endpoint-specific regularized additive multinomial models for B, W, G, WG and prespecified sex interaction terms. Produce one held-out prediction per eligible `eid`, paired by fold and endpoint. Estimate H_k with a participant bootstrap and fold variability; report Brier/log loss, calibration intercept/slope, reliability/resolution, discrimination as secondary, sex-standardized predictions, risk-band movement, and intervals.

The substantive alternative uses the exact same B/W/G/WG inputs, participants, split, outcomes and held-out metrics in a bounded gradient-boosted multinomial model with a small training-fold-only hyperparameter grid. The additive model reveals inspectable sex-specific family increments but can miss nonlinear BMI-by-waist, BMI-by-grip, and discordance regions. The learned model can reveal whether interaction is concentrated in high-waist/low-grip combinations or BMI-specific regions; it is informative only if calibrated, fold-stable, and reproduced by predeclared shape summaries. A small uncalibrated improvement is not supportive.

Model measurement completeness using only verified pre-T0 variables; report stabilized inverse-probability-weight sensitivity, positivity, standardized differences, truncation, and ESS separately by sex and endpoint. Repeat complete-case versus one fold-local missingness strategy. Permute endpoint labels within fold/selection strata and permute waist/grip within BMI-age-sex-calendar/missingness strata. Deliberate code/date mispairing must be detected before fitting; scan for post-T0 leakage and run a pre-T0 negative-control endpoint. Do not change code lists after seeing results.
