## Question, evidence boundary, and substantive clinical advance

This is an independent substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the Lead-frozen scientific schema exactly: 540 person-landmark rows, 443 distinct first qualifying ICU stays/persons, 74 hospitals; the first qualifying stay per `uniquepid`; landmarks `L ∈ {1440, 2880, 4320, 5760, 7200}` minutes after ICU admission; the first exact target label in `[L,L+360]`; decision time `D = cplitemoffset`; inherited pre-landmark exclusions; exact literal labels; repeated-landmark dependence clustered by `patientunitstayid`; fixed windows and parser; and the five-state all-row primary accounting.

The unresolved claim remains narrower: does the exact current label add reproducible information about later respiratory chart state beyond the pre-`D` state and documentation process, after preserving hospitals, opportunity, repeated landmarks, and competing ICU exits?

The substantive advance is a **state-triangulated, decision-oriented audit**. It asks whether any label increment is useful for a clearly operational and falsifiable task—prioritizing a row for manual respiratory review under a fixed review capacity—while requiring agreement with an independently defined physiologic-alert proxy from `vitalPeriodic`. This makes the result more consequential than a generic association without converting chart proxies into bedside outcomes. The physiologic proxy, review action, relative costs, and thresholds are frozen before arm-stratified results. Competing exits and unobservable rows remain visible in an all-row state vector and receive explicit unresolved utility, never silent exclusion or imputation.

A supportive result would therefore mean only that the label improves transported prediction and review prioritization for two structured eICU constructs, with a concordant physiologic-alert signal. It would not validate the label's bedside meaning. An adverse result would mean that the label does not add robust decision-relevant information for these constructs or that its apparent value is explained by opportunity, persistence, exits, or site composition. An inconclusive result would identify inadequate physiologic opportunity, support, calibration, or transport. Bedside semantics, causal benefit/harm, and a recommendation to initiate or withhold an intervention require adjudicated workflow outcomes and another study.

## New independent physiologic-alert proxy

This is a secondary triangulation outcome; it does not alter `K`, the primary estimand, the cohort, or any frozen respiratory-setting rule. It uses only fields actually present in the exact `vitalPeriodic` source.

For each row, first apply the same exit precedence using `patient.unitdischargeoffset <= D+2160`. Among rows not assigned `competing_exit` and with valid exit timing, construct a value-blind opportunity mask from `vitalPeriodic` before reading `sao2` or `respiration` values:

* `V_E` is present if at least two finite vital rows occur at distinct `observationoffset` values in `(D,D+720]` and the span is at least 5 minutes;
* `V_T` is present if at least two finite vital rows occur at distinct `observationoffset` values in `(D+720,D+2160]` and the span is at least 5 minutes;
* retain separate component-level counts and missingness for `sao2` and `respiration`.

The fixed alert parser accepts finite numeric `sao2` in percent and `respiration` in breaths/min only. It marks a window `physiologic-alert` if, in that window, at least two valid observations at distinct clinical offsets satisfy either `sao2 < 88` or `respiration > 30`; a single qualifying value is retained as a descriptive one-point signal but does not define the alert. Values outside finite numeric parsing are missing, not normal. These thresholds are explicitly operational proxy thresholds, not adjudicated hypoxemia, tachypnea, respiratory failure, or harm. If the source audit cannot verify the documented `vitalPeriodic` units/convention, the physiologic-alert analysis is unavailable and its decision-oriented conclusion is inconclusive.

Define separate window-specific physiologic states, never a pooled or post-hoc “relevant window” target. For each row with valid exit timing and no competing exit, derive `H_E` from `(D,D+720]` and `H_T` from `(D+720,D+2160]`:

`H_w ∈ {alert, no_alert, physiologic_unobservable}` for `w ∈ {E,T}`.

A window has valid opportunity `V_w` only if it contains at least two finite, clinically distinct `observationoffset` values spanning at least 5 minutes, with component-level `sao2` and `respiration` counts retained separately. Within a window, `alert` requires at least two distinct valid observations satisfying either `sao2 < 88` or `respiration > 30`; `no_alert` requires `V_w=1` and no alert; otherwise the state is `physiologic_unobservable`. Exits take precedence globally in the separate all-row field `H_exit`; invalid exit timing is `H_invalid_exit_time`. Thus an E target is evaluated only on E-opportunity rows and a T target only on T-opportunity rows; E and T denominators are reported separately and are never pooled. The primary physiologic state record is `H ∈ {competing_exit, invalid_exit_time, E_alert, E_no_alert, E_unobservable, T_alert, T_no_alert, T_unobservable}` with the deterministic precedence `competing_exit`, `invalid_exit_time`, then the applicable E/T states. No row is assigned a pooled no-alert state merely because one window lacks observations.

The primary triangulation table is the all-row joint distribution `P(A,K,H)` and its arm contrast, with exit and each window-specific unobservable state retained. Report `P(K=classifiable-escalation,H_E=alert)` on the E-opportunity denominator and `P(K=classifiable-escalation,H_T=alert)` on the T-opportunity denominator, plus discordant cells. Concordance is descriptive evidence of two chart signals, not proof that either is a clinical event. A label gain present only in `K` but absent from both separately tested `H_E` and `H_T`, or present only through opportunity differences, is adverse to the stronger patient-state interpretation; it does not prove the label is meaningless.

## Decision-oriented threshold and utility analysis

This analysis is a prespecified secondary translation of held-out predictions, not a treatment-effect analysis and not a bedside recommendation. Its decision is `R=1`, prioritize the row for manual respiratory chart review, versus `R=0`, do not prioritize it in a limited review queue. It is deliberately a chart-operations decision so that its utility can be computed without claiming that review changes outcomes.

Two targets are evaluated separately and never silently combined:

1. **Setting-escalation review target:** `Y_K=1` for `K=classifiable-escalation`; all other observed K states are retained as explicit non-target states, with competing exits and `non_exit_unobservable` reported separately.
2. **Physiologic-alert review target:** `Y_H=1` for `H=alert`; `exit`, `physiologic_unobservable`, and `no_alert` remain distinct states.

For each target and each fixed threshold/cost pair, emit a complete action-by-state table. In the observed-target view, `R_τ=1{p>=τ}` is evaluated only within the target-opportunity denominator (`K` classifiable for `Y_K`; `V_E=1` for `Y_{H_E}`; `V_T=1` for `Y_{H_T}`); classifiable non-target rows are explicit false-positive candidates and no-alert rows are explicit true-negative candidates. Competing exits, invalid exit times, and physiologic-unobservable rows are outside that conditional denominator and are reported as unresolved counts, never coded as negatives.

In the all-row unresolved-aware view, use three actions: `review` if `p>=τ_high`, `no_review` if `p<=τ_low`, and `abstain` otherwise. The deterministic scoring table is: review plus observed target `+1`; review plus observed non-target `-c`; no_review plus observed target `0`; no_review plus observed non-target `0`; abstention for any state `0`; and no_review plus any unresolved state (competing exit, invalid exit time, or the applicable unobservable state) `-u`. Review plus unresolved is `-c_u`, with a separate prespecified unresolved-review cost grid `c_u ∈ {0.00,0.05,0.10}`; this prevents unresolved states from being silently rewarded or treated as known targets. All K/H cells, including classifiable stable/mixed, classifiable de-escalation, classifiable escalation, exit, and unobservable states, are tabulated under these actions. The scoring function is an operational sensitivity analysis for allocating chart-review slots, not a clinical utility, mortality cost, harm estimate, or causal estimand. Report the full state counts, each component of the score, and results over `c ∈ {0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30,0.50}`, `c_u` grid, and `u ∈ {0.25,0.50,1.00}`.

Compare M1 with M0 at every prespecified threshold/cost pair, and against treat-none, treat-all-observed-target, and fixed-capacity review policies at 10%, 20%, and 30% of the applicable target-opportunity rows, with deterministic tie breaking by `patientunitstayid` then landmark. Report incremental net benefit, recall, PPV, review fraction, abstention fraction, unresolved-review fraction, and calibration within held-out predictions. A label may be called decision-useful for a named structured target only if, in the unbounded threshold grid, M1 improves both held-out micro and equal-hospital macro log loss for that target's all-row state accounting and its target-opportunity view, and its incremental net benefit is positive versus M0 at at least two adjacent cost values and at least one fixed-capacity policy, with the stay-cluster interval excluding zero. This gate is applied separately to `Y_K`, `Y_{H_E}`, and `Y_{H_T}`; a gain in one cannot be transferred to another.

No threshold is selected after inspecting the arm contrast or favorable result. Thresholds and costs are not clinical recommendations. A positive chart-review score can justify only prospective assessment of whether review is useful, not automated extubation, ventilator change, discharge, or withholding care.
