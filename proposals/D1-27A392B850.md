# Discordant renal recovery in MIMIC-IV: a falsifiable, temporally separated prognostic study

## Question and clinical importance

Among adult ICU patients with KDIGO acute kidney injury (AKI), does a discordance between creatinine-based and urine-output-based recovery at a fixed 72-hour post-onset landmark predict worse 28-day in-hospital mortality beyond what is explained by current illness severity?

The clinical decision is consequential: clinicians must decide whether to escalate monitoring, involve nephrology, initiate renal replacement therapy (RRT), or modify fluid management when creatinine and urine output tell different stories about renal recovery. A model that identifies patients whose discordance reflects true residual kidney injury rather than measurement or severity artifacts could support earlier nephrology review and more informed RRT decisions. This study does not claim that discordance itself causes worse outcomes.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

**Already supported by evidence:** AKI is common in ICU patients and associated with increased mortality; creatinine and urine output can disagree in AKI staging and recovery; AKI recovery is an important prognostic marker. These are established in the literature and in MIMIC-IV analyses.

**Unresolved claim (the hypothesis):** In a temporally separated design, a 72-hour discordance between creatinine-based and urine-output-based AKI recovery is an independent predictor of 28-day in-hospital mortality after adjustment for current illness severity, and this association is not fully explained by delayed creatinine kinetics or selective measurement patterns.

**What the experiment will test:** Whether the discordance signal survives a strict landmark analysis with pre-specified adjustment for current severity, and whether a learned model that captures the temporal trajectory of creatinine and urine output adds prognostic information beyond a simple baseline.

## Leading claim and strongest rivals

**Leading claim:** Discordant renal recovery at 72 hours reflects a true biological mismatch in renal recovery that carries independent prognostic information for 28-day mortality.

**Rival 1 — Current severity:** The discordance is a proxy for overall illness severity. Sicker patients have more volatile creatinine and urine output, and their mortality risk is already captured by SOFA or APACHE scores. *Separating observation:* If the discordance signal disappears after adjustment for SOFA, APACHE, and other severity measures at the landmark, the leading claim is weakened.

**Rival 2 — Delayed creatinine:** Creatinine has a slow half-life and may lag behind true renal recovery. A patient whose creatinine is still elevated at 72 hours may have already recovered, while a patient with normal creatinine may still be injured. The discordance may therefore reflect measurement delay rather than true biological discordance. *Separating observation:* If the discordance signal is stronger when creatinine is rising (suggesting ongoing injury) versus falling (suggesting delayed clearance), the delayed-creatinine rival is supported. If the signal persists regardless of creatinine direction, the leading claim is strengthened.

**Rival 3 — Selective measurement:** Urine output is recorded more frequently in sicker patients or in patients with indwelling catheters. The discordance may reflect differential measurement intensity rather than true biological discordance. *Separating observation:* If the discordance signal is concentrated in patients with high urine-output measurement frequency or indwelling catheters, the selective-measurement rival is supported. If the signal persists in a subset with consistent measurement, the leading claim is strengthened.

## Population and temporal design

### Population
- Adult ICU patients (age ≥ 18) in MIMIC-IV.
- First ICU admission only (to avoid immortal-time bias from repeated admissions).
- KDIGO AKI stage ≥ 1 at any point during the ICU stay, defined by either creatinine or urine output criteria.
- Exclusions: patients who die before the 72-hour landmark, patients with no creatinine or urine output measurement at the landmark, patients with pre-existing dialysis.

### AKI onset
- AKI onset is the first time the patient meets KDIGO criteria (creatinine ≥ 2× baseline or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours).
- Baseline creatinine is the lowest creatinine in the 7 days before ICU admission, or the first creatinine in the ICU if no prior value exists.

### Landmark and recovery assessment
- Landmark: 72 hours after AKI onset.
- Creatinine-based recovery: creatinine at the landmark is ≤ 1.5× baseline.
- Urine-output-based recovery: mean urine output over the 24 hours before the landmark is ≥ 0.5 mL/kg/h.
- Discordance: creatinine-based recovery and urine-output-based recovery disagree.
- Four discordance groups: (1) both recovered, (2) creatinine recovered but urine output did not, (3) urine output recovered but creatinine did not, (4) neither recovered.

### Follow-up and outcomes
- Follow-up starts at the 72-hour landmark.
- Primary outcome: 28-day in-hospital mortality.
- Secondary outcomes: RRT initiation within 7 days after the landmark, ICU length of stay, hospital length of stay.
- No outcome is observed before the landmark (no future leakage).

## Simple baseline

A logistic regression model with the following predictors at the 72-hour landmark:
- Age, sex, race (from `patients`).
- SOFA score at the landmark (computed from MIMIC-IV variables).
- APACHE IV score at the landmark (computed from MIMIC-IV variables).
- Creatinine at the landmark (from `labevents`).
- Mean urine output over the 24 hours before the landmark (from `chartevents`).
- Fluid balance over the 24 hours before the landmark (from `chartevents`).
- RRT status at the landmark (from `chartevents` or `prescriptions`).
- Discordance group (4 categories).

The baseline is a simple, interpretable model that captures the main prognostic variables and the discordance signal.

## Substantive learned alternative

A gradient-boosted tree model (e.g., XGBoost or LightGBM) with the following predictors at the 72-hour landmark:
- All predictors from the baseline model.
- Temporal features: creatinine slope over the 24 hours before the landmark, urine output slope over the 24 hours before the landmark, fluid balance slope over the 24 hours before the landmark.
- Interaction terms: discordance group × SOFA score, discordance group × creatinine slope.
- Non-linear terms: creatinine × urine output, creatinine × fluid balance.

The learned model can capture non-linear relationships and interactions that the baseline logistic regression cannot. It may reveal whether the discordance signal is modified by current severity or by the direction of creatinine change.

### Training/fitting target
- Primary outcome: 28-day in-hospital mortality (binary).
- Secondary outcomes: RRT initiation within 7 days (binary), ICU length of stay (continuous), hospital length of stay (continuous).

### Split
- Training set: 70% of patients.
- Validation set: 15% of patients.
- Test set: 15% of patients.
- Stratified by discordance group and 28-day mortality.

### Outcome/uncertainty evaluation
- Primary metric: AUC (area under the ROC curve) for 28-day mortality.
- Secondary metrics: calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow test, calibration slope), Brier score, log loss.
- Uncertainty: 95% confidence intervals for AUC and Brier score, computed via bootstrap (1000 resamples).
- Comparison: baseline logistic regression vs. learned gradient-boosted tree model.

### Approximate resource/time budget
- CPU: 8 cores, 32 GB RAM.
- Time: ~2-4 hours for cohort construction, ~1-2 hours for model fitting, ~1 hour for evaluation.
- Total: ~4-7 hours on a single CPU node.

### Selection/deferral reason
The learned model is selected because it can capture non-linear relationships and interactions that the baseline logistic regression cannot. If the learned model does not significantly outperform the baseline (AUC difference < 0.01), the baseline is preferred for interpretability. If the learned model outperforms the baseline, the non-linear relationships and interactions are of scientific interest and warrant further investigation.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if:
1. The discordance signal disappears after adjustment for SOFA, APACHE, and other severity measures at the landmark (Rival 1 supported).
2. The discordance signal is stronger when creatinine is rising versus falling (Rival 2 supported).
3. The discordance signal is concentrated in patients with high urine-output measurement frequency or indwelling catheters (Rival 3 supported).
4. The learned model does not significantly outperform the baseline (AUC difference < 0.01), suggesting that the discordance signal is not captured by non-linear relationships or interactions.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive interpretations

**Supportive:** The discordance signal persists after adjustment for current severity, is not concentrated in patients with high measurement frequency, and the learned model significantly outperforms the baseline. This supports the leading claim that discordant renal recovery reflects a true biological mismatch.

**Adverse:** The discordance signal disappears after adjustment for current severity, is concentrated in patients with high measurement frequency, or the learned model does not significantly outperform the baseline. This supports one of the rival explanations and weakens the leading claim.

**Inconclusive:** The discordance signal persists after adjustment for current severity but is concentrated in patients with high measurement frequency, or the learned model significantly outperforms the baseline but the discordance signal is not captured by non-linear relationships or interactions. This suggests that the discordance signal is real but may be driven by measurement artifacts or may not be captured by the current model.

## Explicit limitations

1. **No measured GFR:** MIMIC-IV does not include measured GFR. Creatinine and urine output are surrogate markers of renal function and may not accurately reflect true GFR.
2. **Selective urine/fluid recording:** Urine output and fluid balance are recorded more frequently in sicker patients or in patients with indwelling catheters. This may introduce measurement bias.
3. **Causal claims:** This is an observational study. The discordance signal is associated with, but not causally linked to, 28-day mortality. Confounding by indication, selection bias, and other unmeasured confounders may be present.
4. **Single-center data:** MIMIC-IV is a single-center dataset. The results may not generalize to other populations.
5. **No external validation:** The model is not externally validated. The results may not generalize to other datasets.
