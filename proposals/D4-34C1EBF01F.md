# UKB-07 repaired hypothesis: CRP-discordant GlycA and delayed infection admissions

## Scientific opening and claim boundary

The inspected evidence supports three narrower claims. GlycA is a composite NMR acute-phase signal that is associated with cardiovascular disease even after hsCRP adjustment, but it is correlated with obesity and other metabolic risk factors [K1]. A broad inflammatory/metabolomic signature is associated with hospital-recorded respiratory infection, but GlycA was not isolated from CRP [K2]. Repeated NMR scores have only moderate temporal correlation and repeat participation is observational and subject to survivor bias; changing scores do not establish persistent biology or clinical utility [K3].

The unresolved question is therefore:

> Among UK Biobank participants with baseline GlycA and CRP, does GlycA elevation unexplained by CRP predict a clinically consequential inpatient infection diagnosis after the first post-assessment year, with an association that is stronger or more specific than its association with vascular inpatient diagnoses and generic mortality, after adjustment for measured demographic, smoking, obesity and blood-cell covariates?

The hypothesis is an association hypothesis, not a causal or mechanistic claim. “Persistent inflammation” is an interpretation to be tested indirectly: a delayed association and, secondarily, stable direction across two assays would be compatible with persistence. Neither result proves that inflammation persisted between blood draws. The strongest competing explanations are (1) occult illness or reverse causation near T0, (2) obesity/metabolic confounding, (3) assay timing and survivor/return-attendance selection, (4) nonspecific inflammatory frailty that predicts any bad outcome, and (5) inpatient coding and ascertainment artifacts.

A positive result would add a clinically meaningful outcome distinction only if the infection association survives the delayed-entry and covariate checks and is materially larger than the vascular comparison rather than merely tracking mortality. It would not establish treatment benefit, infection prevention utility, biological mechanism, or causality. A null or imprecise result is useful: it would limit the persistence/specificity interpretation and direct the next study toward repeated assay validation, outpatient infection ascertainment, or clinical adjudication.

## Actual scientific deliverable

The solver must newly fit and report, with held-out uncertainty:

1. A prespecified CRP-discordance exposure transformation learned in the training period.
2. A cause-specific survival/competing-risk analysis for first infection, first vascular admission, and all-cause death after delayed entry.
3. A matched-input gradient-boosted discrete-time competing-risk model.
4. Held-out infection and vascular cumulative-incidence estimates, discrimination and calibration, with 95% uncertainty intervals, plus the infection-versus-vascular contrast and the early-versus-delayed comparison.
5. A two-wave stability sensitivity analysis, reported as a sensitivity and not as proof of persistent biology.

Completion requires saved model specifications, cohort counts and exclusions, transformation parameters, event tables, held-out predictions, calibration/discrimination summaries, confidence intervals, and a conclusion that explicitly labels supportive, adverse, or inconclusive evidence. No participant-level rows belong in the proposal or public search evidence.

## Exposure construction and confounding control

Within the chronological training partition only, use `log1p` only if required by validated nonpositive-code handling; otherwise use natural logs of positive GlycA and CRP. Standardize each using training mean and SD. Fit a prespecified linear residual model:
`z(log GlycA) = alpha + beta*z(log CRP) + error`.
The exposure is the training-fitted residual, then standardized with training residual SD. Larger values mean GlycA higher than expected for CRP. Report the primary continuous per-SD estimate and a fixed categorical contrast (top quartile versus bottom three quartiles of the training residual distribution). For interpretability, also tabulate the four quadrants defined by training medians of standardized GlycA and CRP; the high-GlycA/low-CRP quadrant is the named discordant group. All cut points and coefficients are frozen before validation.

Adjust the baseline survival model for T0 age, sex, BMI, smoking, and blood-cell measurements when available (WBC, lymphocytes and neutrophils); report missingness and use a single prespecified strategy (complete-case primary, with missing-indicator/multiple-imputation sensitivity only if executable). Do not call this full metabolic adjustment: the binding lacks verified diabetes, lipid, medication, symptom, antibiotic, and outpatient-infection variables. A model with CRP and GlycA entered separately, and a model adding the residual to CRP, are nested checks of incremental information.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

The directional claim is falsified in the held-out primary delayed-entry cohort if the residual-GlycA infection association is directionally null or adverse by the prespecified estimate: HR < 1 with a 95% interval entirely below 1, or high-versus-low 5-year infection risk difference <=0 with a 95% interval entirely <=0. An imprecise interval crossing the null is inconclusive, not a refutation. The “clinically meaningful distinction” claim is not supported if infection is not higher than vascular in the predeclared coefficient/risk contrast, or if infection and vascular effects are similar while mortality is also elevated; that pattern favors nonspecific inflammatory frailty. If the association appears only in the T0-to-one-year analysis and attenuates after delayed entry, near-term occult illness is the leading explanation and the persistence claim is adverse. If adjustment for BMI, smoking and blood-cell fields removes it, confounding is favored. If only the learned model detects a small ranking gain without calibrated absolute-risk or outcome-specific improvement, report descriptive predictive utility only, not a scientific advance.

Supportive evidence requires all of: a positive held-out delayed-entry infection estimate with uncertainty compatible with a nonzero effect; a prespecified high-discordance risk contrast in the same direction; materially stronger infection than vascular association or a clearly different infection cumulative-incidence pattern; and no dependence solely on the first post-T0 year. A directionally consistent two-wave sensitivity strengthens stability but cannot establish persistent inflammation. Adverse evidence follows the falsification rules above. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse two-wave infection events, wide intervals, violated missingness assumptions, or a validation cohort too small for the contrast. These criteria do not make a causal, mechanistic, treatment, or clinical-utility claim.

## Limitations and deferred evidence

The four bound tables lack symptom onset, outpatient infection, antibiotic use, pathogen, severity, treatment, adjudication, admission identifiers, assay collection times/batches, medication/diabetes/lipid data, and a clinical decision threshold. ICD-10 ranges can misclassify infection and vascular diagnoses; repeated measurements are selected by survival and return attendance. The data cannot distinguish a true persistent inflammatory state from stable trait, residual confounding, measurement error, or healthcare-use propensity. Underlying cause `40001-0.0` is not sufficient to adjudicate infection mechanism, and death dates do not establish cause-specific clinical pathways.

Deferred alternatives are: (a) a joint longitudinal biomarker model, deferred because only two biomarker waves and sparse delayed infection events make random-effects persistence unstable and because it would not add unavailable symptom/assay-timing evidence; (b) a neural sequence model, deferred because there is no richer repeated sequence and its complexity would not resolve the key causal/measurement rivals; (c) a genetic instrumental-variable analysis, deferred because no genetic instrument is bound in the four source tables; and (d) external validation or clinical utility analysis, deferred because no external cohort, treatment decisions, or adjudicated endpoint is available. Revisit these only if repeat assays with collection metadata, richer clinical covariates, adjudicated events, or an external validation cohort become available.
