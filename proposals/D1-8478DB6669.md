# Does a laboratory-surveillance signal survive broad chart-workflow adjustment?

Status: Episode 50 substantive child of assessed candidates [prior hypothesis] and [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test of laboratory ordering, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that changing surveillance improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence is feasibility, not a fitted association. The assessed parent audit reports 135,680 eligible adult first ICU stays, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, and 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes. It also reports 1,433 carePlanEOL rows and 1,230 unique stays, with 717 discussion and 249 save offsets in the [0,360] landmark; this does not establish complete documentation of treatment limitation.

The unresolved claim is whether laboratory-observation history contains patient-linked, death-specific early-warning information after adjustment for recorded physiology, vital-monitoring intensity, documented care limitation, and the broader intensity of nursing/respiratory charting. A laboratory-process increment that survives vital adjustment can still be a proxy for a generally intensively observed chart. The clinically important decision is whether a retrospective review signal is interpretable enough to justify external validation and prospective silent-mode evaluation, rather than being deployed as if it represented hidden deterioration.

This child adds two substantive tests to the inherited death-specificity and surveillance-regime designs:

1. A nested observer-workflow challenge: compare the lab increment after adding broad charting-process controls, not only vital-process controls.
2. A lead-time challenge: compare competing-risk predictions at 720, 1080 and 1800 ICU-relative minutes. A signal present only near disposition is not an early-warning signal.

## Hypothesis and falsification

Primary hypothesis: among eligible adult first ICU stays at minute 360, adding laboratory-observation process to a model containing recorded state and broad chart-workflow process improves held-out-hospital calibration and integrated competing-risk Brier score for recorded ICU death, with a larger increment for death than for alive ICU discharge, and the increment is present at more than one post-landmark horizon.

Let B0 contain admission/context, laboratory state and availability, vital state and masks, and carePlanEOL presence/timing. Let B_lab add lab process; B_chart add nursing/respiratory chart process; and B_all add both process blocks. The primary estimand is the macro-hospital paired difference in B_all minus B_chart for death-specific integrated/time-specific Brier and calibration at H in {720,1080,1800}. The endpoint-specific contrast is the lab increment for death minus the lab increment for alive ICU discharge. A secondary lead-time estimand compares this death-specific increment across the three H values.

Falsification hypothesis: if the apparent laboratory signal is generic documentation workflow or local interface behavior, it will be similar for death and alive discharge, be reproduced by chart-process/site descriptors, disappear after broad chart adjustment, persist under within-hospital process permutation, reverse under interface/revision restrictions, or occur only at H=1800. Any of these adverse patterns refutes the patient-linked early-warning interpretation even if raw mortality discrimination improves.

## Analysis, baseline and learned alternative

Fit a transparent nested elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly post-landmark bins and separate death/discharge heads:

- B0: demographics/admission context, lab state/availability, vital state/masks, and EOL presence/timing;
- B_lab: B0 plus lab observation process;
- B_chart: B0 plus nursing/respiratory documentation process;
- B_all: B0 plus both process blocks.

State features are first/last/median/range/slope when supported, with missingness masks. Lab process includes counts, distinct result times/analytes, first/last time, recency, spans/gaps, revision-known/stable interface/system indicators and common-panel blocks. Vital process is included in B0 only as the prespecified general monitoring adjustment; its incremental placebo is also reported. Chart process uses only outcome-blind aggregate timing/category features.

Use five outer folds holding out whole hospitals, keeping all `uniquepid` episodes together. Development hospitals use a 75/25 fit/validation split. All codebooks, imputation, scaling, category locks, percentiles, tuning and calibration are development-only. Report macro-hospital paired differences first, patient-weighted results second, with hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals and per-hospital calibration. Use at least 1,000 resamples if feasible.

The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU over six one-hour bins in [0,360], with exactly the B_all channels, masks and predeclared process categories, two competing-risk heads and the same horizons/folds. It can reveal ordering, persistence and co-movement between laboratory and chart processes lost by landmark summaries. Fit and early-stop only in development hospitals, assess in held-out hospitals, and compare with a parameter-matched non-temporal MLP and bin-order shuffle. Interpret a temporal gain only if order shuffling attenuates it and the model improves held-out endpoint-specific calibration/Brier or fixed-burden outputs over B_all.

CPU elastic-net fitting is expected to take minutes-to-hours, but full-file preprocessing, resampling and GRU convergence are not measured. One allocated A100 using `cuda:0` is optional for repeated temporal fits; ordinary shells do not establish GPU absence. The future solver must request declared resources within the separate solver envelope and preserve checkpoints. No discovery fit is claimed.

A joint multivariate observation/point-process model is deferred: it could estimate laboratory intensity conditional on state, but its missingness and interval-censoring assumptions and runtime are unverified and are not needed to test the primary chart-workflow uncertainty. A latent GP/Bayesian trajectory model is deferred because genetic inputs and a validated latent physiologic target are unavailable and the immediate question is observer-process attribution. Full multimodal/waveform models are unavailable. Causal test-ordering or alert-impact analysis is deferred because orders, clinician review, responses, harms and patient-centered outcomes are absent.

## Falsification, support and interpretation

Lock these checks before outcome fitting:

1. Permute lab-process features within held-out hospital, B0-risk and vital-coverage strata while retaining state, chart process and outcomes.
2. Compare B_all-B_chart, B_lab-B0 and B_chart-B0, and compare against hospital-descriptor-only and chart-only models.
3. Repeat common-analyte, revision-known-only and interface-stratified codebooks.
4. Compare H=720, 1080 and 1800 for lead time.
5. Repeat excluding documented EOL-by-360; absent documentation remains unknown.
6. Leave one surveillance regime or hospital out.
7. Use GRU order-shuffle and matched non-temporal controls.
8. At q in {0.01,0.02,0.05,0.10}, enforce equal per-hospital review burden and report alerts per 100, capture, PPV, false-review burden and number needed to review.
9. Prohibit target-fold preprocessing, target-outcome calibration, post-360 rows and Apache mortality fields.

Require at least 100 eligible hospitals, 50 event-bearing hospitals, 1,000 recorded deaths and 1,000 competing discharges overall, overlap in at least 80% of held-out hospitals, and adequate per-horizon/per-endpoint support (targeting at least 20 hospitals with both events where feasible). Failed gates make that claim inconclusive rather than motivating a changed endpoint.

Supportive evidence requires valid gates, uncertainty-supported chart-adjusted death-specific improvement, a larger death than discharge increment, persistence at more than one horizon and across held-out hospitals, attenuation under process permutation, and no comparable chart/site/interface/revision explanation. Adverse evidence includes disappearance after chart adjustment, equal/larger discharge improvement, improvement only at H=1800, permutation persistence, coding reversal, or a GRU gain surviving order shuffle. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse horizon cells, failed overlap/coverage, unstable folds, intervals spanning meaningful favorable and adverse values, codebook ambiguity or unsupported temporal fitting.

The solver must newly save the cohort flow, outcome-blind codebooks, five-fold hospital manifest, feature matrices, B0/B_lab/B_chart/B_all fits and predictions, conditional GRU/controls, horizon-specific CIF/Brier/calibration, death-specificity and lead-time contrasts, uncertainty, equal-burden diagnostics, falsification outputs and an interpretation trace linking every conclusion to a computed file and uncertainty. This fitted artifact, not confirmation of the hypothesis, is the scientific deliverable.
