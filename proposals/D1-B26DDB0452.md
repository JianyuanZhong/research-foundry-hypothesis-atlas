# When does laboratory surveillance justify monitoring review after recorded physiology is measured?

Status: Episode-19 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This preserves the parent’s adult first-stay cohort, exact six-minute-hour landmark ([0,360]) minutes, competing recorded-death estimand over ((360,1800]), vital-state adjustment, whole-hospital transport design, and primary `Bp−Bv` comparison. It adds a prespecified equal-review-burden decision layer and elevates adjustment for general vital-observation intensity to a strict actionability check. This remains an observational prognostic and measurement-process study: it is not a causal test-ordering study, a clinical-alert trial, a hospital ranking, or evidence that review improves survival.

## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

At minute 360 of an ICU stay, should a monitoring reviewer receive a patient-linked laboratory-surveillance signal after the recorded vital state has already been measured? A positive answer requires more than a small discrimination gain. The laboratory process must improve calibrated identification of later recorded ICU death in held-out hospitals and change which patients would be reviewed at a fixed review burden, while the improvement attenuates when general monitoring intensity is controlled and when patient-process labels are permuted within physiologic-risk strata.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among adults reaching ICU-relative minute 360, patient-level laboratory observation process adds transportable information for recorded ICU death by minute 1,800 beyond admission context, revision-safe laboratory state/availability, and contemporaneously recorded vital state/availability (`Bp−Bv`). The increment remains directionally useful in the stricter comparison that also adjusts for vital-observation intensity (`BpV−BvV`), and at a prespecified review burden the laboratory model captures more recorded deaths or has higher PPV than the vital-state policy.

This is falsifiable. A null or adverse `BpV−BvV`, no equal-burden capture/PPV improvement, attenuation under state- and monitoring-intensity-matched permutation, or reproduction by hospital descriptors rejects the patient-linked/actionable interpretation even if the unadjusted process model predicts.

The strongest available evidence is the parent’s outcome-blind feasibility audit: 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows known by minute 360. These counts support a bounded hospital-transport experiment, not a fitted prediction result, causal effect, clinician intent, or clinical benefit. The unresolved claim is whether the lab-observation increment survives physiologic and general-monitoring adjustment and is large and stable enough to alter a transparent review policy.

Estimate the cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death by minute 1,800 with alive ICU discharge before 1,800 as the competing event. A stay still present at minute 1,800 is administratively censored. Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, and latent or unrecorded death are outside the estimand.

## Feature blocks and policies

All codebook resolution, imputation, scaling, regularization, temporal tuning, and policy thresholds are learned within development hospitals. No outcome, post-landmark row, hospital ID, or hospital descriptor enters a primary patient predictor.

- **`Bv`: state baseline.** Pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic death and alive-discharge heads over hourly rows from minute 360 through 1,800. Inputs are admission context, revision-safe lab state summaries and lab availability, and recorded vital-state summaries/availability. Lab state uses predeclared first/last/median/range/slope where support permits, threshold flags, and analyte masks. Vital state uses predeclared first/last/median/range/slope and an availability indicator for each retained physiologic field. This is the parent’s state-adequate baseline.

- **`Bp`: patient-linked laboratory-process extension.** Exactly `Bv` inputs plus patient-level lab-process features: total valid rows, distinct analytes, distinct result times, first/last result time, recency to minute 360, span, inter-result-gap summaries, analyte-specific counts/recency, revision-known status, and verified interface/system indicators. It excludes `hospitalid`. The parent’s primary estimand remains the paired held-out-hospital `Bp−Bv` increment.

- **`BvV` and `BpV`: strict general-monitoring control.** Add to `Bv` and `Bp`, respectively, only vital-observation-intensity features derived from the same [0,360] rows: periodic/aperiodic row counts, distinct observation times, last observation time, span, and coverage by six one-hour bins. The strict actionability estimand is `BpV−BvV`: incremental lab process after both recorded vital state and general vital monitoring intensity are accounted for. This does not claim to remove all workflow or missing physiology; it is a prespecified sensitivity/control contrast.

- **`T`: equal-input temporal alternative.** A one-layer GRU or temporal convolution, selected before outcome evaluation, over six one-hour bins in [0,360]. Each bin contains the same lab state, lab availability, lab-process, vital state, vital availability, and vital-observation-intensity channels used to derive `BpV`; admission context is concatenated to the sequence representation. It has competing death and alive-discharge heads for the same hourly outcome rows, no hospital ID, and no post-landmark input. Fit on development hospitals with development-only early stopping and tuning. Compare `T` with `BpV` using identical episodes, labels, folds, channels, and uncertainty. The temporal model is scientifically useful only if ordering, persistence, or co-movement yields a reproducible calibration or review-policy improvement; a small AUROC increase alone is not a substantive finding.

### Review-policy estimand

For each held-out hospital and each prespecified risk level q ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, define a baseline review policy that reviews an episode at minute 360 when its Bv predicted cumulative incidence by minute 1,800 is at least q. In development hospitals, choose the Bp, BpV, and T thresholds that match the Bv review fraction within ±1 percentage point; freeze them before held-out evaluation. If matching is impossible because of ties or sparse scores, use a prespecified top-k rule with k equal to the Bv review count and record the exception.

For each policy report macro-hospital and pooled: review fraction, recorded-death capture, positive predictive value, calibration among reviewed and non-reviewed episodes, incremental reviews (new alerts minus Bv alerts), and the standard decision-curve net benefit over a cost-to-benefit grid rather than one asserted utility weight. The minimum clinically interpretable output is a paired held-out-hospital capture/PPV comparison at equal review burden. Report q-specific results only when the alert support gate passes. These are retrospective label-relative review diagnostics, not evidence that a clinician should act.

## Splits, uncertainty, support gates, and falsification

Use five grouped outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once; keep every `uniquepid` together and stratify by eligible episode and event counts when feasible. All preprocessing and policy thresholds are development-only. The primary summary is the macro-hospital paired difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score and calibration for `Bp−Bv`; `BpV−BvV` and equal-burden policy metrics are prespecified secondary actionability tests. CIF at 1,800 and policy capture/PPV are secondary outcome summaries.

Before fitting, write a support manifest. The primary interpretation is permitted only if:

1. At least 1,000 recorded deaths and 50 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes are present in the frozen cohort; for event-specific policy interpretation, at least 50 hospitals have at least two recorded deaths and at least 20 eligible episodes. The parent audit’s 2,470 deaths, 195 ≥20-episode hospitals, and 175 event-bearing hospitals are feasibility evidence, not a substitute for this recheck.
2. At least 20 hospitals contribute to every outer-fold paired evaluation, with no evaluation hospital having fewer than 20 eligible episodes. Hospitals failing this may remain in descriptive sensitivity tables only.
3. A retained vital field has nonmissing observations in [0,360] for at least 30% of eligible episodes overall and at least 10% in at least 80% of evaluation hospitals; fields failing this are excluded from the primary state block, not globally imputed. Missingness indicators remain explicit.
4. For every q reported as an actionability result, the equal-burden policy has at least 20 review-eligible evaluation hospitals, at least 10 alerts per evaluation hospital on average, and at least 100 alerted episodes with at least 20 recorded deaths pooled across held-out folds. Otherwise q is exploratory and cannot support an implementation-oriented conclusion.
5. The strict comparison has overlapping Bv-risk and vital-coverage strata: every retained evaluation hospital must have at least 10 episodes in each populated prespecified Bv-risk quintile/coverage cell used for permutation; otherwise the affected hospital/stratum is reported and the strict test is inconclusive.
6. Every primary model has successful convergence in all five folds, finite predictions for all eligible evaluation episodes, no feature timestamp beyond 360, and no development/evaluation patient or hospital leakage.

Use hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals with at least 1,000 resamples if feasible, and simultaneous intervals for q-specific policy cells. Report per-hospital episode, death, discharge, alert, and missing-vital support; do not turn unsupported hospitals into pooled certainty.

Falsification and interpretation checks are locked before labels:

- permute lab-process channels within hospital, Bv-risk quintile, and vital-coverage stratum while holding lab/vital state fixed;
- repeat after adding vital-observation intensity (`BpV−BvV`);
- use strict common-panel and revision-known lab codebooks;
- compare with hospital-descriptor-only and vital-observation-only models;
- inspect whether gains are concentrated in one hospital, interface, analyte family, or revision convention;
- compare raw process with outcome-free target-hospital percentile normalization as the parent’s diagnostic, requiring at least 50 calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes per hospital, with no target outcomes or post-landmark data;
- repeat policy metrics at the equal-burden operating point, so a gain caused only by issuing more reviews cannot count.

Supportive results require: all primary support gates pass; held-out-hospital `Bp−Bv` improves calibration/Brier in a paired direction; `BpV−BvV` remains directionally favorable with uncertainty excluding no improvement for the declared primary metric; the equal-burden lab policy captures more recorded deaths or has higher PPV without unacceptable calibration loss; matched permutation attenuates the increment; and no site descriptor, single hospital, interface, or lab family explains it.

Adverse results include: the increment becomes null or reverses after vital-state or vital-intensity adjustment; equal-burden capture/PPV does not improve or calibration worsens; permutation leaves the gain intact; site descriptors reproduce it; or strict codebook/revision handling removes it. These findings support workflow, missing-physiology, interface, or revision explanations and argue against a patient-linked review signal.

Inconclusive results include: failed vital coverage/overlap, fewer than the event or alert supports above, fold reversals, unstable temporal fits, unsupported q cells, leakage, or wide intervals spanning clinically relevant adverse and favorable effects. Inconclusive is not confirmation or refutation.

## Evidence limits and unavailable dependencies

This snapshot has no validated lab-order time, clinician reason for ordering, human review event, alert acknowledgement, treatment response, bedside waveform, independent physiologic adjudication, or prospective outcome under a review policy. Structured charting, medication, infusion, intake/output, respiratory, and nursing tables do not by themselves establish those missing constructs. Recorded vital values can be wrong or missing; a process increment that remains after vital adjustment can still encode unmeasured physiology or local workflow.

Therefore a supportive result establishes only that patient-linked recorded laboratory-observation history improves a retrospective, held-out-hospital recorded-death review proxy under stated support gates. It does not establish that laboratory review improves care, that a clinician would act, or that the signal is causal. Those stronger claims require expert adjudication of observation intent and physiologic correctness, external-hospital validation, prospective silent-alert testing, and ultimately an impact or stepped-wedge evaluation with review burden, responses, harms, and patient-centered outcomes.
