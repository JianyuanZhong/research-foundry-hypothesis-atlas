# Executed HCC 144-hour recovery diagnostic

Status: executed on the unchanged parent cohort and locked temporal split. Results are adverse to the prespecified trajectory hypothesis.

## Frozen question and evidence boundary

The evidence inspected before this analysis supports three limited facts: selected preoperative and operative factors have been associated with postoperative length of stay in HCC [K1]; POD1 bilirubin plus clinical factors predicted adjudicated grade B/C PHLF in a major-hepatectomy cohort [K2]; and ISGLS PHLF requires concomitant INR and bilirubin abnormalities on/after POD5 plus management-based grading [K3]. None establishes that the path to POD5 adds information about discharge delay.

The tested claim was that 0–144-hour INR/bilirubin trajectory—especially bilirubin lag despite INR improvement—would improve 2024–2025 prediction of discharge after 240 hours beyond a POD5 snapshot. The strongest rival was that current POD5 state, operative burden, measurement practice, and era explain the apparent signal. The endpoint remains delayed recorded discharge, not PHLF, morbidity, mortality, or discharge readiness.

The reconstructed complete cohort exactly matched the frozen audit: train 2013–2021 N=6,975/events=1,851; validation 2022–2023 N=2,562/events=428; locked test 2024–2025 N=2,700/events=387. Patients were unique. Validation was divided before fitting: 2022 (N=1,025/events=212) tuned B1/T1; 2023 (N=1,537/events=216) fit logistic recalibration only. Test was evaluated after all choices and controls were frozen.

B0 was spline-penalized logistic regression on age, sex, operation duration/missingness, laparoscopic and coarse major-resection flags, cirrhosis, year, late measurement offsets, late log INR/bilirubin, and their interaction. B1 was elastic-net logistic regression on matched trajectory features; validation chose C=0.03 and l1_ratio=0.2. T1 was depth-3 histogram gradient boosting; validation chose learning rate 0.03, 150 iterations, minimum leaf 80, and L2=1. Controls were a timing-only model, T1 without offsets, within-train-year × major × laparoscopic early/middle temporal shuffle preserving late state, and B0 plus ALT history.

## Executed results

On locked test, B0 Brier was 0.11763 (log loss 0.39191; AUROC 0.650). T1 was worse: Brier 0.11857, paired difference T1−B0 +0.00094 (2,000-patient bootstrap 95% CI −0.00046 to +0.00231), log-loss difference +0.00393 (−0.00096 to +0.00861), and AUROC 0.645. T1 calibration slope was 1.342 and intercept 0.453, outside the prespecified 0.8–1.2 and ±0.05 bounds.

Transparent B1 was only trivially better in Brier: 0.11741, difference −0.00022 (95% CI −0.00180 to +0.00133), below the 0.005 threshold and compatible with no improvement. Its AUROC was 0.668, which is hypothesis-generating rank separation but does not rescue the failed proper-score/calibration criteria.

The train-weighted, late-state/severity-overlap bilirubin-lag contrast was +0.30 percentage points versus concordant recovery (95% CI −0.41 to +1.01; effective N 313 vs 710), far below the required +3 points. The weighted observed outcome difference was −0.19 points. These results are adverse to the proposed bilirubin-lag phenotype for this endpoint.

Controls did not reveal a hidden favorable interpretation:

- Temporal shuffle was worse than B0 by +0.00312 Brier (95% CI +0.00180 to +0.00446), showing that early/middle linkage contains some rank information, but T1 itself still did not beat B0; the required “half the gain removed” criterion is undefined because there was no positive T1 gain.
- Timing-only was worse than B0 by +0.00359 (95% CI +0.00149 to +0.00561), so timing alone did not explain a favorable trajectory gain.
- ALT history was worse than B0 by +0.00057 (95% CI −0.00128 to +0.00239), providing no evidence that nonspecific injury history adds a robust gain.
- T1 without measurement offsets remained worse than B0 (+0.00055 Brier), so offsets were not masking a useful trajectory result.
- T1−B0 directions were inconsistent across major/minor and cirrhosis strata; the coarse major flag had only 58 test patients and is not stable enough for a strong modern-era subgroup claim.

Annual outcome rates fell from 26.2% in 2021 to 20.7% in 2022, 14.1% in 2023, 15.9% in 2024, and 12.4% in 2025. Median late bilirubin remained about 19 across 2022–2025, while median late collection shifted from ~129 hours in 2024 to ~119 hours in 2025. Complete-trajectory coverage also rose from 31.0% in 2013 to 76.4% in 2025. This supports material discharge/measurement-era drift, not a gross bilirubin scale discontinuity.

## Interpretation and decision

The strongest computed claim is adverse: in this unchanged cohort, the prespecified learned INR/bilirubin trajectory did not improve locked-test proper scores or calibration over the POD5 snapshot, and bilirubin lag did not show the required adverse risk contrast. Current late state plus ordinary covariates should remain the preferred parsimonious model class for this recorded-discharge endpoint. The small B1 AUROC increase does not justify clinical use or a mechanistic label.

This result does not establish that biological recovery path never matters. It tests one routinely observed, complete-case, three-window representation against recorded discharge in one institution. The bootstrap resampled locked-test patients with frozen fitted/calibrated models; it did not refit the complete training/calibration pipeline, so its intervals omit model-fitting uncertainty. The first-versus-last value sensitivity was not fitted; all 12,237 primary complete cases had first values, making it feasible but unnecessary to overturn the decisive primary failure. Selection weighting and 336-hour outcome were not executed in this bounded diagnostic. These omissions narrow precision and generalizability but do not turn the failed prespecified primary gates into support.

The branch should be parked for this endpoint. Revisit only with adjudicated PHLF/complications, laboratory units/reference limits, reliable operative extent, timestamped interventions and disposition/death, preferably external validation. Those data could test liver-specific recovery rather than institutional discharge practice.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive meanings

Supportive would have required all five frozen gates: Brier gain ≥0.005 with CI below zero, acceptable calibration, bilirubin-lag risk difference ≥3 points with CI above zero, ≥50% gain loss under shuffle, and consistent subgroup direction. None passed.

The executed result is adverse to this exact prognostic phenotype and model comparison. It is not proof of equivalence, absence of liver injury, or absence of PHLF. A future adjudicated study could reach a different conclusion.

An inconclusive label would have applied for inadequate events, gross scale drift, poor overlap, or intervals spanning clinically opposite bilirubin-lag effects. Events and weighted overlap were adequate, no gross bilirubin shift appeared, and the lag CI excluded the prespecified clinically relevant +3-point threshold; therefore “adverse” is more accurate than “inconclusive.”
