# Testing gaps during instability and acute ICU deterioration

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

When an ICU patient remains physiologically unstable, an abrupt reduction in laboratory testing is associated with a higher risk of acute deterioration in the next 24 hours, and the association is less transportable across hospitals when the model uses testing-process features than when it uses clinical state alone.

The unresolved claim is not that reduced testing causes deterioration. It is whether, after adjustment for measured time-local physiology and existing support, the timing of observed laboratory testing contains a reproducible signal of impending deterioration, whether that signal is concentrated in unstable patients, and whether it transports across hospitals. The expert seeds eICU-02 and eICU-08 propose this question but do not establish it.

Existing evidence supports only the narrower premise that EHR observation/missingness patterns can encode care processes and that longitudinal clinical prediction should be evaluated across environments. It does not establish the direction, interaction, causal meaning, or transportability of this eICU association. Resolution would advance monitoring-risk assessment: a robust signal could justify a prospective study of monitoring adequacy; a null or nontransportable signal would caution against using an apparent “quiet chart” as a universal warning rule.

## Population and time

Use the first ICU stay for each `uniquepid` in `patient.csv.gz` (`unitvisitnumber=1`, adult age; parse `age` values such as `> 89` as 90). Require a numeric `unitadmit.../unitdischargeoffset` and at least 36 hours of ICU observation. Candidate landmarks are every 6 hours from ICU offset 12 hours through 72 hours, retained only when at least 24 hours of follow-up remains. For the primary analysis, select one eligible landmark per stay with a fixed random seed; use all eligible landmarks in a secondary discrete-time analysis clustered by `uniquepid`.

Exclude landmarks with an end-of-life discussion recorded at or before the landmark in `carePlanEOL`; report this as an imperfect proxy because the table does not encode all treatment limitations. Exclude alive discharges/transfers before the 24-hour outcome window; retain deaths as observed events. Do not use records after the landmark in any predictor. Keep hospital IDs for hospital-stratified inference and held-out validation, but do not use hospital ID as a predictor.

## Exact exposure and instability definition

Deduplicate laboratory observations within `patientunitstayid, labname, labresultoffset`, retaining the earliest `labresultrevisedoffset`; require numeric `labresult`. The prespecified core panel is the available, unit-audited set of creatinine, BUN, glucose, sodium, potassium, WBC, platelet and lactate labels, using `labname`, `labmeasurenamesystem` and `labmeasurenameinterface`. Label matching is case-normalized and documented; a core analyte with fewer than 1,000 observed stays is dropped before fitting and reported, never silently substituted. Testing counts are distinct analyte names with observations in a time window.

At landmark t:
- prior testing intensity is the number of distinct core analytes in [t-12h,t-6h);
- recent testing intensity is the number in [t-6h,t];
- require prior intensity >=4;
- define an abrupt reduction as recent intensity <=50% of prior intensity and no core-lab observation for at least 8 hours before t. Preserve continuous alternatives (recent-minus-prior count and longest gap) for dose-response sensitivity.
- define instability using only pre-landmark data: any MAP <65 mmHg in [t-6h,t] from `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` or `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean`, or a pressor infusion in that window. The pressor list is normalized from `infusionDrug.drugname` to norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine or dopamine. A stable comparison stratum has neither criterion. This definition is intentionally observable and not a clinical diagnosis.

## Outcome and estimand

The primary outcome is acute deterioration within (t,t+24h]: ICU death, new pressor initiation after t among patients without a pressor in [t-6h,t], or new invasive ventilation start after t among patients without ventilation crossing t. Ventilation is defined from `respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset` and `ventendoffset`; ambiguous/open intervals are retained with a documented rule and sensitivity analysis. Death is `patient.unitdischargestatus=Expired` with `unitdischargeoffset` in the window.

The primary estimand is the standardized 24-hour risk difference for abrupt reduction versus no abrupt reduction, separately at unstable and stable landmarks, plus the interaction contrast (difference in risk differences). Estimate it from the prespecified state-plus-process baseline with hospital-stratified patient bootstrap 95% CIs. The key transport estimand is the change in calibration/Brier score and risk-difference reproducibility when testing-process variables are removed and when each sufficiently large hospital is held out in turn.

Secondary outcomes are the individual composite components, ICU death alone, and a cause-specific competing-risk analysis treating alive ICU discharge/transfer before 24 hours as a competing event. Report hospital-specific effects with shrinkage and no hospital ranking.

## Models and analysis

Primary baseline B0 is a regularized pooled logistic model for the composite outcome using only clinical state: demographics, time since ICU admission, admission APACHE variables/scores, last and 6-hour summaries/slopes of MAP/heart rate/respiration/SpO2, recent core-lab values/slopes, current ventilation/pressor indicators, and missing-value indicators for these state variables. B1 adds testing-process features: prior/recent counts, longest gap, analyte-specific observation indicators and the abrupt-reduction indicator. Use the same eligible landmarks and fixed patient-level/hospital-held-out splits. Estimate interaction and standardized risks from B1; compare B0 versus B1 to test whether process information adds a stable signal.

Alternative A is a small GRU or causal temporal-convolution model over four prior 6-hour bins with exactly the same state and process inputs, including explicit masks and event counts. Fit state-only A0 and state-plus-process A1 on training patients only, early-stop on a validation hospital-independent patient split, and evaluate untouched hospitals. The learned model is scientifically useful because it can detect nonlinear lagged state-by-gap interactions and expose hospital-specific process shortcuts that the linear baseline may miss; it is not selected for a small AUROC gain.

Report risk differences/interaction with 95% CIs, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC as secondary, and decision-curve net benefit at thresholds selected before testing. Use patient bootstrap nested within hospitals and report the distribution of leave-one-hospital-out estimates. Do not claim clinical utility unless an external prospective validation and clinical review support it.

## Falsification and robustness

1. Repeat with multiple gap thresholds (6, 8, 12 hours) and continuous testing-count changes; a one-threshold-only signal is weak.
2. Shuffle lab event times within patient while preserving the number of labs and hospital/stay duration; the exposure interaction and process-feature transport signal should attenuate.
3. Test the pre-specified stable stratum as a negative-control context. A large identical association in stable patients suggests a general documentation/discharge artifact rather than instability-specific risk.
4. Exclude landmarks within 12 hours of any discharge/transfer, with active EOL discussion, or with abrupt loss of all source-table coverage; repeat with and without the exclusion.
5. Vary MAP source priority and pressor normalization; report missingness and event support by hospital.
6. Verify no exposure or predictor uses `labresultrevisedoffset` after t or any post-landmark data. If labels, event counts, or hospital-held-out support are inadequate, return an inconclusive feasibility result rather than change the question silently.

## Interpretation

Supportive: the unstable-stratum interaction is directionally positive with a CI excluding zero, persists across prespecified definitions, and the state-plus-process signal is reproducible in held-out hospitals; B1/A1 calibration does not collapse relative to B0. This supports an observational monitoring-risk marker and justifies prospective monitoring studies, not a treatment recommendation or causal claim.

Adverse: the interaction is near zero or negative with adequate precision, or it disappears after timestamp/shuffling and censoring checks; if B1/A1 degrades materially across hospitals, testing-process features behave as site-specific shortcuts. This would argue against a universal “reduced testing” warning rule and would still be clinically useful.

Inconclusive: sparse core-panel coverage, too few eligible landmarks/events, unstable label/unit mapping, severe hospital heterogeneity, or wide CIs prevents distinguishing these cases. Report the missing evidence and do not select thresholds.

Computationally checkable outputs are cohort counts/filtering, feature timestamps, fitted coefficients/risks, calibration, held-out metrics, bootstrap intervals, and falsification results. Clinical adjudication is still required to determine whether a composite event represents meaningful deterioration, whether a pressor/ventilation change was indicated, whether missing labs reflect interface failure or deliberate monitoring, and whether any prospective intervention is safe. Staffing, bed capacity, clinician intent, pre-ICU events, and complete treatment-limitation status are unavailable; no mechanism, quality ranking, or causal effect can be inferred.
