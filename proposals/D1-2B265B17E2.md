# Can post-adequacy inspired-oxygen exposure be separated from evolving severity and observation in acute pulmonary injury?

## Status, opening, and deliverable

This is a planned eICU study and an estimand-focused child of `[prior hypothesis]`; it reports no outcome result. ICU-ROX showed that an oxygen-conservative protocol reduced oxygen exposure in a broad mechanically ventilated population without significantly changing ventilator-free days [K1]. LOCO2 found no 28-day survival benefit from a very low oxygen target in ARDS and stopped early for safety and futility [K2]. A current mechanistic review distinguishes inspired hyperoxia from arterial hyperoxaemia and describes preclinical synergy between oxygen and mechanical stress, while emphasizing inconsistent clinical mortality evidence [K3].

The strongest claim supported by those works is limited: oxygen targets change exposure; overly low targets may be unsafe in ARDS; and elevated inspired oxygen can expose pulmonary tissue even when saturation does not quantify arterial hyperoxaemia. They do not establish that post-adequacy FiO2 harms eICU patients, that pulmonary injury modifies such harm, or that EHR oxygen settings identify a causal treatment effect.

The unresolved hypothesis is: **among adult first ICU stays alive and still in the ICU at hour 36, with a PaO2 of 80–120 mm Hg paired to FiO2 during hours 18–24, the hour-24 acute-pulmonary-injury proxy modifies the association between mean excess FiO2 during hours 24–36 and seven-day in-hospital death after hour 36; a pulmonary-specific, delayed association will remain distinguishable from measured severity, ventilation, treatment-selection, hospital, and observation-process explanations.**

The primary computed estimand is descriptive: the difference between pulmonary strata in the covariate-standardized seven-day death cumulative-incidence risk difference associated with a +0.10 contrast in mean `(FiO2-0.40)+`, over shared observed exposure support. Standardization uses only information fixed by minute 1,440. It is an exposure-response contrast among hour-36 survivors, not the effect of oxygen initiation and not a total causal effect.

The deliverable is a frozen cohort and hourly panel, a transparent landmark estimate, a sequential temporal model on the same hospital-held-out splits, and a prespecified attribution/falsification report. The strongest result this experiment can support is “compatible with pulmonary-specific delayed oxygen toxicity after measured rivals were not reproduced.” It cannot establish oxygen toxicity, an ARDS mechanism, or a treatment recommendation.

## Clinical importance and rival predictions

A credible pulmonary-specific delayed pattern would justify phenotype-stratified prospective oxygen research. A precise absence of a clinically consequential interaction would argue against enriching a trial with this eICU proxy. If the signal follows worsening, ventilator intensity, chart density, or particular hospitals, the useful next study is treatment/measurement validation rather than oxygen restriction.

The leading explanation is delayed oxygen–mechanical-stress injury in already injured lungs. The principal rivals make different predictions:

- **Severity and clinician selection:** oxygenation, haemodynamics, or ventilator support worsen before or at FiO2 escalation; a pre-exposure deterioration control predicts later exposure; the pulmonary interaction attenuates when temporal history is represented.
- **Ventilation co-treatment:** pressure/volume burden, not FiO2, predicts deterioration; an apparent oxygen interaction disappears when lagged mechanical stress and invasive-ventilation state are separated.
- **Measurement and hospital practice:** the interaction tracks FiO2/ABG chart density, entry delay, source label, or a few hospitals and is reproduced by non-dose charting variables.
- **Pulmonary-phenotype error:** diagnosis timing and P/F support identify different case mixes or misclassify low-P/F patients without an acute diagnosis; blinded clinical review does not validate the proxy.
- **Toxicity-compatible temporal pattern:** after pre-decision oxygenation is adequate, excess FiO2 precedes rather than follows deterioration, the excess-FiO2 by lagged-mechanical-stress term is stronger in the injury proxy, and the pattern survives held-hospital, source, and observation checks.

The last pattern is compatibility evidence only. Unmeasured clinician intent, undocumented procedures, imperfect event times, and prior oxygen can leave these explanations observationally indistinguishable.

## Population and immutable clocks

The index is `patient.patientunitstayid`. Include age >=18 (map `age='> 89'` to 90 only for adjustment), the first ICU stay in each `patienthealthsystemstayid` (lowest valid `unitvisitnumber`, then smallest `patientunitstayid`), and `unitdischargeoffset>2160`. The patient must be alive and in the ICU at minute 2,160.

Exclude impossible/negative landmark chronology, burns, extracorporeal support when documented before minute 1,440, and `carePlanGeneral.cplgroup='Care Limitation'` with `cplitemvalue='Comfort measures only'` by minute 1,440. DNR, no-CPR, no-intubation, and `carePlanEOL` discussion rows are not silently equated with comfort-only care: retain them as baseline treatment-limitation indicators and report exclusion sensitivities.

Fixed boundaries are:

- baseline and pulmonary classification: admission through minute 1,440;
- adequacy anchor: the last valid PaO2 80–120 mm Hg in minutes 1,080–1,440, paired to the closest valid FiO2 within ±60 minutes; equal-distance ties prefer the preceding FiO2;
- exposure: minute 1,440 inclusive to 2,160 exclusive;
- outcome time zero: minute 2,160;
- follow-up: `(2160,12240]`, seven days after hour 36.

No diagnosis, physiology, exposure, or event after minute 2,160 enters eligibility or the hour-24 phenotype. Requiring survival and ICU presence through the common exposure window prevents exposure-duration immortal time. It does not solve selection among 36-hour survivors or reverse causation within hours 24–36.

## Hour-24 pulmonary phenotype and required validation

The **acute-pulmonary-injury proxy** requires both (1) a diagnosis/admission-diagnosis entered by minute 1,440 containing a frozen acute term—ARDS/acute respiratory distress, pneumonia, aspiration, pulmonary oedema, or acute respiratory failure—and (2) adequacy-anchor PaO2/FiO2 <=300. The primary comparator has no prespecified acute pulmonary term by minute 1,440, regardless of P/F ratio. A pulmonary term with P/F >300 is a discordant sensitivity group. A strict comparator requiring no term and P/F >300 is sensitivity-only because the completed support audit found only 173 such stays and one sustained-exposure case.

This proxy is not Berlin ARDS. Before any “pulmonary-injury-specific” interpretation, two ICU clinicians blinded to hour-24–36 exposure and outcome must independently review a prespecified stratified sample of 240 stays: 60 proxy-positive, 60 no-term/PF<=300, 60 no-term/PF>300, and 60 term/PF>300, sampled across hospitals and source-completeness quartiles. Review is restricted to pre-minute-1,440 `diagnosis`, `admissionDx`, `note`, `treatment`, blood gas, and respiratory-support evidence. They classify probable acute parenchymal pulmonary injury, absent, or indeterminate and separately verify the oxygenation pair. Report agreement, adjudicated PPV for the positive proxy, and contamination in the comparator. A PPV <0.75, comparator contamination >0.20, or >0.20 indeterminate prevents a biological phenotype claim and makes an otherwise positive proxy interaction inconclusive for trial enrichment. Because images, uniform reports, and a reliable cardiogenic-oedema exclusion are unavailable, even successful review cannot establish Berlin ARDS.

## Exposure and outcome

Normalize FiO2 values of 0.21–1.00 directly and values 21–100 by division by 100; reject all others. For each of 12 exposure hours, use the median valid event-time FiO2 from respiratory charting. Carry the last setting forward for at most 120 minutes; never carry a future value backward. Lab FiO2 is a same-time validation channel, not an automatic substitute. Require at least six supported hours.

The primary exposure is the covered-time mean of `(FiO2-0.40)+` across the 12-hour window. Uncovered time remains missing, is never imputed as room air, and is handled through explicit coverage indicators and an observation model. Report the distribution and common support continuously. “Sustained” (>0.40 in >=75% of supported hours) and “limited” (<=25%) are display and overlap categories only.

Crucially, primary exposure is **not conditioned on same-hour SpO2 or PaO2**, because oxygenation measured after FiO2 can be a treatment response and selecting “adequate hours” can induce post-treatment selection. For temporal analyses, an hour is pre-decision adequate only when the last oxygenation value strictly preceding the hour’s FiO2 record is SaO2/SpO2 >=94% within 30 minutes or PaO2 >=80 within 120 minutes. It is pre-decision hypoxaemic if SpO2 <92% or PaO2 <60; otherwise adequacy is unknown. Adequacy-tagged excess burden and thresholds 0.30, 0.50, and 0.60 are sensitivities. SpO2 never quantifies arterial hyperoxaemia.

Primary outcome is in-hospital death after minute 2,160 through minute 12,240 from `patient.hospitaldischargestatus='Expired'` and `hospitaldischargeoffset`. Live hospital discharge in that interval is a competing event; remaining in hospital at day seven is administrative censoring. Report Aalen–Johansen risks. Secondary outcomes are ICU death, death by hospital discharge, and hour-36–72 respiratory deterioration only if timestamp coverage passes. `actualventdays` is never a timestamped endpoint.

## Estimand-relative classification of hour-24–36 variables

A variable’s role depends on when it was observed relative to FiO2; “adjusting for the exposure window” is not one operation.

| Information | Estimand role | Transparent baseline | Sequential alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demographics, admission state, APACHE/physiology, diagnoses, oxygenation, ventilation, shock and limitations fixed by minute 1,440 | Pre-exposure confounders `C0`; pulmonary proxy `P` is effect modifier | Primary standardization set | Initial history/state |
| Severity and oxygenation strictly before hourly FiO2, including prior PaO2/SaO2, pH/PaCO2, MAP, heart rate and vasopressors | Time-varying confounders for later FiO2 that may themselves be affected by earlier FiO2 | Not in primary total-window adjustment; summarized only in an explicitly noncausal attenuation analysis | Enter history before treatment assignment |
| PEEP, plateau pressure, tidal volume, rate and invasive-ventilation state strictly before hourly FiO2 | Co-treatment, potential confounder and effect modifier | Baseline levels in `C0`; within-window summaries only in attribution analysis | Lagged history plus prespecified oxygen × mechanical-stress interaction |
| Oxygenation, severity, or ventilation observed at/after hourly FiO2 | Potential mediator/response; same-hour ordering may be ambiguous | Never represented as pre-treatment confounder; included only in labeled conditional attenuation | Transition target; cannot be used to justify the same treatment decision |
| Whether/when FiO2, ABG, saturation or settings were charted; event-to-entry/revision delay | Measurement process `R_t`, affected by hospital and severity | Coverage/source variables and sensitivity analyses, not biological confounders | Separate observation head and inverse-observation sensitivity |

Thus adding hour-24–36 severity, ventilation, or oxygenation to a regression changes the contrast. Attenuation may be due to confounding control, mediation removal, measurement error, or collider conditioning; it is not proof of a direct oxygen effect or its absence.

## Two matched analyses

Both analyses use the same cohort, raw variables, five frozen hospital-held-out folds, exposure/outcome definitions, and fold map. Hospitals are grouped without splitting `hospitalid` or `uniquepid`; folds are balanced on hospital size, region, and training-fold outcome rate. No threshold or model is selected after viewing the primary interaction.

### B0: transparent landmark baseline

Fit cause-specific pooled logistic models for death and live discharge after hour 36. Predictors are a restricted cubic spline for continuous excess FiO2, pulmonary stratum, their interaction, and `C0`: age, sex, ethnicity, admission source/type, unit type; hospital region/size/teaching and a partial-pooling hospital intercept; pre-hour-24 APACHE IV score/predicted mortality; baseline PaO2, FiO2, P/F, pH, PaCO2, lactate, creatinine, bilirubin, platelets, WBC; pre-hour-24 vasopressor and invasive-ventilation status; PEEP, plateau pressure, tidal volume and rate; diagnoses/comorbidities; and treatment-limitation indicators. Untimed APACHE “day 1” fields are sensitivity-only.

Standardize both cause-specific hazards to each stratum and derive seven-day cumulative incidence. Report the interaction as the difference in standardized risk-difference slopes per +0.10 exposure over common support with hospital-cluster bootstrap 95% intervals. Also report unadjusted Aalen–Johansen risks, calibration, exposure support, and a generalized-propensity sensitivity using `C0` only with prespecified 1st/99th percentile truncation.

A frozen attribution ladder then adds, in order, (A) hours 18–24 trends, (B) hour-24–36 severity/oxygenation summaries, (C) ventilation summaries, and (D) measurement density/source/delay. These are residual conditional associations, not alternate estimates of a total causal effect. B0 is primary because its time zero, covariates, and risk standardization are auditable. It loses ordering and cannot distinguish a high setting before deterioration from one chosen because deterioration began.

### S1: sequential state/measurement model

Fit a recurrent state-space sequential g-computation model to the identical hourly fields. At each hour, a latent respiratory-severity state is inferred only from history available before that hour; observed-state emissions are PaO2/SaO2, pH/PaCO2, haemodynamics, PEEP, plateau pressure, tidal volume, respiratory rate, invasive ventilation, and vasopressor state. Separate heads predict the next FiO2 value and each observation mask. The transition after FiO2 predicts next-hour physiology. A cumulative lagged excess-FiO2 term and a prespecified `excess FiO2 × lagged plateau-or-PEEP` term enter future-state and event hazards; they do not enter pre-treatment state inference.

Train treatment, transition, and observation components without outcome labels on training hospitals, then fit competing event heads. Evaluate held-hospital FiO2 density, observation probability, next-hour PaO2/SaO2/PEEP calibration, event calibration, Brier score, and posterior predictive distributions. A flexible neural implementation may use one allocated A100; a Bayesian/regularized recurrent implementation is acceptable if it preserves the same targets and folds.

S1 must first reproduce B0’s observed-data exposure interaction on shared support. It then estimates a model-based scenario contrast between the observed path and a bounded rule `A_t=min(A_t,0.40)` only when oxygenation was adequate **before** the decision; hypoxaemic or unknown hours remain observed. Report pulmonary-minus-comparator seven-day risk differences. This scenario requires sequential exchangeability, consistency, positivity, correctly ordered chart times, and a correctly specified transition/observation model. Because clinician intent and true setting-change times are incomplete, call it a “g-computation scenario,” never an identified trial effect.

S1 adds scientific information only if held-hospital state and observation calibration pass and it separates “deterioration then oxygen” from “oxygen then delayed deterioration.” It is rejected as explanatory evidence if it merely improves AUROC, if treatment/observation calibration fails, or if inferred effects require unsupported exposure extrapolation.

## Verified feasibility, not a result

The frozen eICU snapshot is `[source checksum]`, with 200,859 stays at 208 hospitals. Prior full-file audits found 420,762 labelled PaO2 rows, 3,075,308 respiratory-charting FiO2 rows plus 360,107 lab FiO2 rows, 1,464,808 PEEP rows, 372,398 plateau-pressure rows, and broad pre-hour-24 acute-term support.

A narrow feasibility screen using the preserved landmarks found 2,280 adult hour-36 stays across 124 hospitals: 993 injury-proxy stays and 1,166 with no acute pulmonary term. Before final exclusions and oxygenation/coverage rules, injury-proxy sustained/limited FiO2 cells contained 217/207 stays with 48/35 seven-day deaths; no-term cells contained 166/311 stays with 50/41 deaths. The strict no-term/PF>300 group had only 173 stays and is not primary. These aggregate counts establish potential support only; they are not adjusted findings and do not validate the final cohort.

Stop before outcome modeling if either primary stratum has <150 analyzable stays, <40 deaths, fewer than 20 represented hospitals, continuous-exposure effective sample size <100 after weighting, or <5% representation in any prespecified generalized-propensity support band. Sequential scenario results are suppressed if >20% of adequate decision-hours require treatment extrapolation outside the training-fold conditional 2.5th–97.5th percentile or if fewer than 100 patients per stratum contribute at least six pre-decision-classifiable hours.

## Falsification and decision rules

All gates and the +3 percentage-point interaction threshold are frozen before outcomes are inspected.

**Supportive of the unresolved association, not causality:** B0 estimates a pulmonary-minus-comparator interaction of at least +3 percentage points per +0.10 excess FiO2, its hospital-bootstrap 95% interval excludes zero, and an admissible S1 has the same direction with a pulmonary-specific delayed signal and scenario contrast whose 95% interval excludes zero. Overlap, event, held-hospital calibration, observation, and phenotype-review gates pass. The negative temporal control is null, the signal is not restricted to hypoxaemic decision-hours, and no single hospital/source-removal analysis changes direction. This justifies prospective validation and trial-enrichment planning, not oxygen restriction.

**Adverse to the hypothesized clinically consequential interaction:** the B0 upper 95% bound is below +3 points; the association is equal or larger in the comparator; direction reverses; deterioration consistently precedes FiO2; pressure/volume burden remains while oxygen terms disappear; the pre-exposure deterioration control reproduces the interaction; or measurement/hospital controls abolish it. A failed phenotype review is adverse to the biological label even if the proxy association remains real. These outcomes support abandoning or revising this proxy-specific enrichment rule.

**Inconclusive:** intervals include both zero and +3 points; any population/event/positivity/coverage gate fails; B0 and an otherwise admissible S1 materially disagree; S1 state, treatment, or observation calibration fails; results depend on a few hospitals; or clinical review is unavailable/indeterminate. An imprecise null does not refute toxicity.

Prespecified falsification analyses are:

1. predict an hours-12–18 deterioration composite from later hour-24–36 burden using only pre-hour-12 covariates; a comparable pulmonary interaction is evidence of shared severity/documentation, because later exposure cannot cause the earlier event;
2. compare `L_{t-1}->A_t`, `A_t->L_{t+1}`, and 2–12-hour distributed lags; a same-hour association with no delayed component favors clinician response;
3. repeat with lagged PEEP/plateau/tidal-volume burden and among invasively ventilated stays; pressure-only persistence is adverse to oxygen specificity;
4. fit observation hazards, inverse-observation sensitivity, high-coverage-hospital restriction, dense-site downsampling, label-source removal, and event-versus-entry-time sensitivity;
5. test charting-entry delay and LPM oxygen as negative exposure controls conditioned on measured FiO2; reproduction of the interaction favors documentation practice;
6. leave out each region and the highest-volume hospitals; report between-hospital exposure and measurement variance;
7. report no-term/PF<=300, no-term/PF>300, term/PF>300, strict ARDS-term, and P/F 200/300 sensitivities together; none becomes confirmatory.

## Alternatives not selected and revisit conditions

A regression that adjusts for every hour-24–36 variable was rejected as a causal analysis because it mixes confounders, mediators, and observation processes. A maximum PaO2 exposure was rejected because it conflates sampling with burden and misses inspired hyperoxia [K3]. Hospital oxygen preference was rejected as an instrument because exclusion, independence, and monotonicity are implausible. A fully nonparametric marginal structural model should be revisited only if hourly treatment and censoring positivity are adequate. A pure mortality predictor was rejected because predictive gain does not resolve the oxygen-specific explanation. S1 is retained because ordering and observation modeling can reveal information that B0’s summary loses, but it is deferred as explanatory evidence if its held-hospital transition and observation targets fail.
