# Discordant Recovery After Acute Kidney Injury: Urine Output vs. Creatinine Trajectories and Subsequent Adverse Outcomes

## Parent
- Expert seed: `[prior hypothesis]` ([starting question])
- Dataset: MIMIC-IV 3.1

## Scientific Question

In ICU patients with AKI, does "discordant recovery" — defined as creatinine returning toward baseline while urine output remains oliguric (<0.5 mL/kg/h) — independently predict worse subsequent outcomes (hospital mortality, need for renal replacement therapy) compared to "concordant recovery" (both creatinine and urine output normalize)?

## Unresolved Claim

Current AKI recovery assessments rely primarily on creatinine trajectories. However, creatinine is a delayed and confounded marker of GFR (affected by muscle mass, fluid status, and tubular secretion). Urine output reflects real-time renal perfusion and tubular function. The hypothesis is that patients whose creatinine "recovers" but whose urine output remains low represent a distinct phenotype with persistent occult renal dysfunction and higher risk of subsequent adverse events. This discordance has not been systematically quantified in a large ICU cohort with both markers available.

## Clinical Importance

- AKI affects ~25-50% of ICU patients; incomplete recovery is common and associated with CKD and mortality.
- Current practice uses creatinine-based AKI staging (KDIGO) for both diagnosis and recovery assessment.
- If discordant recovery is a meaningful phenotype, it would:
  1. Improve early identification of patients at risk of persistent kidney dysfunction
  2. Inform monitoring intensity and follow-up after ICU discharge
  3. Potentially identify candidates for earlier nephrology consultation or renal replacement therapy
  4. Refine AKI recovery definitions beyond creatinine alone

## Evidence Base and Rival Explanations

**Supporting evidence:**
- [K1] Haines et al. (2023, CJASN): eGFR creatinine systematically overestimates kidney function after prolonged critical illness due to muscle loss; cystatin C better estimates true GFR. This establishes that creatinine-based assessment is confounded in the ICU setting.
- [K2] KDIGO 2012 AKI Guideline: Defines AKI using both creatinine AND urine output criteria, acknowledging that UO is an independent marker of renal function. However, the guideline does not specifically address discordant recovery patterns.
- [K3] Jonkers et al. (2026, Critical Care): Distributional model predicting joint creatinine and urine output trajectories in ICU patients (MIMIC-IV, eICU). Demonstrates that UO trajectories carry prognostic information beyond creatinine alone, and that joint modeling improves AKI risk stratification.

**Rival explanations:**
1. **Measurement artifact**: UO may be under-recorded in MIMIC (foley catheter not in place, documentation gaps). This would bias toward missing UO data rather than true oliguria.
2. **Confounding by severity**: Patients with worse overall illness may have both lower UO and higher mortality, with the UO effect being a proxy for severity.
3. **Reverse causation**: Low UO may be a consequence of fluid restriction or diuretic use rather than true renal dysfunction.
4. **Creatinine lag**: The "discordance" may simply reflect the known lag between GFR recovery and creatinine normalization, not true persistent dysfunction.

## Study Design

### Population
- Adult ICU patients (age ≥18) in MIMIC-IV 3.1
- Must have ≥2 creatinine measurements during ICU stay
- Must have ≥1 urine output measurement during ICU stay
- Must have an identifiable AKI event (KDIGO Stage 1-3 by creatinine criteria)
- Exclude: ICU stay <24h, pre-existing dialysis

### Temporal Boundaries
- **Index time**: Time of AKI peak (highest creatinine or lowest UO, whichever is later)
- **Recovery assessment window**: 48 hours after AKI peak
- **Outcome window**: From recovery assessment time to hospital discharge or death

### Variables

**Exposure (Recovery Pattern at 48h post-peak):**
- *Concordant recovery*: Creatinine returned to ≤1.5× baseline AND UO ≥0.5 mL/kg/h
- *Creatinine-only recovery (discordant)*: Creatinine returned to ≤1.5× baseline BUT UO <0.5 mL/kg/h
- *UO-only recovery (discordant)*: UO ≥0.5 mL/kg/h BUT creatinine still >1.5× baseline
- *No recovery*: Neither marker recovered

**Covariates (measured at index time):**
- Age, sex (patients table)
- APACHE II or SOFA score components (chartevents, labevents)
- Sepsis status (ICD codes or SIRS criteria)
- Baseline creatinine (first measurement before AKI)
- Peak AKI stage (KDIGO)
- Vasopressor use (inputevents)
- Mechanical ventilation (chartevents)
- Fluid balance in 24h before index (inputevents, outputevents)

**Outcomes:**
- Primary: Hospital mortality (admissions.hospital_expire_flag)
- Secondary:
  - Need for RRT (ICD procedure codes or chartevents)
  - ICU length of stay
  - 28-day mortality (if survived to 28 days)

### Analysis Plan

1. **Cohort construction**: Identify AKI events using KDIGO creatinine criteria (≥0.3 mg/dl increase from baseline within 48h, or ≥1.5× baseline within 7 days). Use the first qualifying event per admission.

2. **Recovery classification**: At 48h post-peak, classify each patient into one of four recovery patterns. For UO, use the sum of all UO sources (Foley + Void + Condom Cath + etc.) in the 48h window, normalized by body weight (or use absolute mL/h if weight unavailable).

3. **Primary analysis**: Cox proportional hazards model for hospital mortality, comparing discordant (creatinine-only) recovery vs. concordant recovery, adjusted for covariates. Report hazard ratio with 95% CI.

4. **Sensitivity analyses**:
   - Restrict to patients with ≥3 UO measurements in the recovery window
   - Exclude patients with RRT during the recovery window
   - Use 24h and 72h recovery windows
   - Propensity score matching on baseline severity

5. **Falsification criteria**:
   - **Supportive**: HR for discordant vs. concordant recovery ≥1.3 with 95% CI excluding 1.0
   - **Adverse**: HR ≤1.0 (no increased risk or protective)
   - **Inconclusive**: 95% CI spans 1.0 with wide interval (imprecise estimate)

### Method Comparison

**Baseline**: Unadjusted comparison of mortality by recovery pattern (descriptive).

**Learned alternative**: A gradient-boosted classifier (e.g., XGBoost) predicting 28-day mortality using recovery pattern + covariates, compared to a Cox model. The ML model could reveal:
- Non-linear interactions between UO trajectory shape and creatinine trajectory
- Subgroups where discordance is more/less prognostic
- Whether UO trajectory slope (not just level at 48h) adds information

**Why both**: The Cox model provides interpretable hazard ratios for clinical decision-making. The ML model tests whether the relationship is more complex than a single binary discordance variable. If the ML model substantially outperforms the Cox model, it suggests that the simple binary classification loses important information about trajectory shape.

**Resource estimate**:
- Cohort construction: ~5 min on 8 CPU (reading labevents, outputevents)
- Cox model: <5 min
- XGBoost: <10 min (CPU, 16 cores)
- Total: <30 min on CPU

### Limitations
1. UO data may be incomplete (foley catheter not always in place)
2. Creatinine is a lagging marker; "recovery" may overestimate true GFR recovery
3. Single-center data (MIMIC-IV = Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center)
4. Cannot distinguish true oliguria from documentation gaps
5. RRT confounds UO measurement (anuria during dialysis)
6. No measured GFR available (iohexol, inulin)

### What Would Change Understanding
- If discordant recovery is independently associated with worse outcomes: validates UO as a critical component of AKI recovery assessment, supports incorporating UO into recovery monitoring protocols
- If no association: suggests creatinine recovery is sufficient, or that UO discordance is a marker of severity rather than independent renal dysfunction
- If interaction with severity: suggests discordance matters most in moderate-severity patients where it could change management

## Key References

[K1] Haines RW, Fowler AJ, Liang K, Pearse RM, Larsson AO, Puthucheary Z, Prowle JR. Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness. Clin J Am Soc Nephrol. 2023;18(8):997-1005. DOI: 10.2215/cjn.0000000000000203. PMID: 37256861.
- Claim: eGFR creatinine systematically overestimates kidney function after prolonged critical illness (2-fold overestimation vs. iohexol gold standard); cystatin C is less confounded by muscle loss.
- Contribution: This study extends the creatinine-confounding argument to the recovery phase specifically, using UO as a real-time functional marker.
- Limitation: Small single-center study (n=38); does not address UO or discordant recovery patterns.

[K2] KDIGO Clinical Practice Guideline for Acute Kidney Injury (2012). Kidney Int Suppl. 2012;2(1):1-138. DOI: 10.1038/kisup.2011.38.
- Claim: AKI definition incorporates both creatinine AND urine output criteria; UO <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6h is an independent AKI criterion.
- Contribution: This study extends the KDIGO framework from diagnosis to recovery assessment, specifically testing whether UO-based recovery adds prognostic information beyond creatinine-based recovery.
- Limitation: Guideline document; does not provide empirical evidence on discordant recovery patterns or their prognostic value.

[K3] Jonkers J, Rouzé A, Verhaeghe J, et al. Beyond binary AKI classification: development and external validation of a distributional model predicting serum creatinine and urine output trajectories in ICU patients. Crit Care. 2026;30:249. DOI: 10.1186/s13054-026-06017-6. PMID: 42108463.
- Claim: Joint prediction of creatinine and UO trajectories improves AKI risk stratification beyond creatinine alone; UO carries independent prognostic information in ICU patients.
- Contribution: This study specifically tests the prognostic value of discordant recovery patterns (not just AKI onset/persistence) and quantifies the magnitude of the discordance effect on hard outcomes (mortality, RRT).
- Limitation: Focuses on AKI prediction (onset/persistence), not recovery patterns; uses MIMIC-IV but for a different clinical question.

## Disposition of Research-Ambition Demonstrations

1. **Natural history transformer (Nature 2025)**: Not pursued as a method. The question here is a specific prognostic comparison, not a generative modeling of disease trajectories. The transformer approach would be overkill for a binary recovery classification. However, the concept of learning natural history from longitudinal data informs the ML alternative (trajectory-based prediction).

2. **Cancer detection from longitudinal EHR (Cell 2026)**: Not applicable. Different disease domain (cancer vs. AKI), different data structure (outpatient longitudinal vs. ICU intensive monitoring).

3. **Bayesian longitudinal EHR framework (Nature 2026)**: The Bayesian approach to longitudinal data is relevant to the sensitivity analysis (uncertainty in recovery classification). However, for the primary analysis, a standard Cox model is more interpretable and clinically actionable. The Bayesian framework could be used in a future extension to model the full trajectory uncertainty.
