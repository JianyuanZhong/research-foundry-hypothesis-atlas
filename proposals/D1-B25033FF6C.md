# Can eICU support a hospital-comparable measure of sustained liberation from invasive ventilation?

## Status, opening, hypothesis, and deliverable

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` and a planned two-stage experiment, not an executed result. It preserves the parent's adult 24-hour invasive-mechanical-ventilation (IMV) landmark population, hospital-aligned clocks, 14-day multistate outcome, competing events, exact read-only eICU bindings, and transparent versus learned recovery models. It repairs the parent's fatal measurement flaw: high positive predictive value (PPV) among detected events cannot rule out hospital-specific false negatives, especially when explicit ventilator-off and oxygen-device records are sparse and concentrated.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is that IMV discontinuation practices vary and death before separation is common [K1], that successful liberation must account for reintubation, death, and transfer [K2], and that longitudinal EHR respiratory models and labels can be institution- and documentation-dependent [K3]. The inherited full-source audit additionally establishes only a data fact: eICU's current `ventendoffset` is almost always zero, historical intervals carry most candidate ends, and explicit off/device evidence is site-concentrated. None of this establishes comparable sensitivity across eICU hospitals or true hospital recovery differences.

**Measurement hypothesis (must be tested first).** After probability-sampled blinded review of both algorithm-positive and algorithm-negative 14-day risk windows, the sustained-liberation phenotype has adequate PPV and sensitivity in each retained hospital, and the simultaneous upper bound on a P90-P10 hospital contrast generated solely by differential ascertainment is at most 5 percentage points. This is falsifiable before any recovery-effect model is fit.

**Recovery hypothesis (conditionally testable).** Only if the measurement gate passes, among adults still invasively ventilated 24 hours after a documented first IMV start, the target-standardized 14-day probability of sustained liberation differs across eligible hospitals after baseline-severity adjustment and remains materially heterogeneous after a longitudinal model accounts for preceding respiratory recovery and observation processes. Support requires a measurement-corrected P90-P10 contrast of at least 10 percentage points with a 95% lower bound above 5 points in both admissible recovery models.

The principal scientific deliverable is therefore either:

1. a hospital-stratified estimate or partial-identification bound for sustained-liberation sensitivity, PPV, specificity, timing error, and maximum ascertainment-only hospital contrast, followed—only if admissible—by two newly fitted recovery models and standardized hospital contrasts; or
2. a measurement-validation result showing that eICU cannot support this hospital comparison.

A future solver must construct and freeze the cohort and sampling frame; produce blinded packets; ingest clinician labels; estimate the measurement quantities and gate; and, only after a recorded pass, fit Models B and L and generate the required outputs. No recovery result is claimed here.

4. The measurement sampling frame includes every hospital with at least 50 index episodes, regardless of raw detected-event count, so a low-sensitivity hospital is not screened out before validation. The recovery population retains the parent's final threshold: after validation, a hospital must have at least 50 index episodes and at least 20 adjudication-compatible sustained-liberation endpoints. At least 30 hospitals must pass all measurement gates. Freeze inclusion before recovery fitting.
5. Follow from L through L+14 days, hospital death, or observation-ending discharge/transfer, whichever comes first. Model predictors end strictly before L.

The preserved states are:

- **V:** invasively ventilated at L.
- **P:** provisional liberation at the first high-confidence end of an invasive interval.
- **S:** alive and observed for 48 hours after P without reconstructed reinstitution of IMV; absorbing primary success.
- **R:** reconstructed reinstitution within 48 hours after P; may return to P.
- **D:** death while in V, P, or R from unit/hospital discharge status and timing; absorbing competing event.
- **X:** alive hospital discharge, external transfer, or ICU exit after which the linked hospitalization cannot establish respiratory state through the required 48 hours; absorbing competing event and reported by subtype.
- **C:** still V/P/R at day 14; administrative end.
- Tracheostomy after L is a separate competing transition, not liberation.

Chart silence cannot create P. Version 0 requires a valid historical end interval or explicit `RT Vent On/Off=Off/off/Suspended` plus a subsequent noninvasive/room-air device state; `treatment` weaning is contextual only. A later high-confidence invasive start defines R. The 48-hour primary sustain window follows [K3]; seven-day sustain follows [K2]. Phase-I disagreements may motivate exactly one phenotype revision. Version 1 is frozen before Phase II and before any hospital recovery analysis.

The primary recovery estimand remains, for each eligible hospital, the standardized 14-day cumulative incidence of S if that hospital's fitted transition effects applied to the pooled eligible-cohort baseline covariate distribution, while preserving D, X, R, and tracheostomy as competing events. Primary heterogeneity is the P90-P10 absolute standardized risk difference. Secondary estimands are transition-specific hospital random-effect SD/MOR and standardized D, X, R, tracheostomy, and still-ventilated risks. No ratio among survivors is allowed.

## Two-phase blinded adjudication and comparability gate

### Sampling unit and source strata

The validation unit is the complete patient-specific endpoint risk window ([L,min(L+14	ext{ d},D,X)]), including 12 hours before a candidate transition and 48 hours after it when available. Algorithm-positive (A=1) means phenotype v1 assigns S by day 14; algorithm-negative (A=0) means it does not. Review therefore directly validates the stay-level endpoint used by the hospital estimand, not merely isolated positive events.

Packets contain relative-time rows from patient exits, respiratoryCare, respiratoryCharting, and treatment, plus APACHE agreement fields and plotted ventilator-setting/device trajectories. They omit `hospitalid`, hospital attributes, the algorithm label, source-stratum label, and derived model predictions. Reviewers see source names because clinical interpretation requires them, but not which source triggered the algorithm.

Prespecified source signatures are: historical interval only; interval plus invasive airway; explicit RT off; ventilator-to-noninvasive/room-air device transition; treatment-only corroboration; multiple concordant sources; and negative windows with (a) abrupt cessation of previously regular invasive settings, (b) airway/device change not meeting v1, (c) weaning/reintubation text only, or (d) no liberation sentinel. Hospital volume and respiratory-documentation density terciles are crossed with these signatures.

### Phase I: rule development, excluded from performance estimation

Two ICU clinicians independently review 240 windows: 120 A=1 and 120 A=0, balanced over hospital-volume/documentation terciles and source signatures, with no more than one window per person. They classify IMV at L, provisional liberation time as an interval, 48-hour reinstitution, D/X/tracheostomy, and sustained liberation as **definite**, **probable**, **definitely absent**, or **indeterminate because eICU evidence is insufficient**. A third clinician resolves disagreements after recording initial labels.

The team may revise v0 once using only Phase I. It must publish the changed rule, disagreement taxonomy, and expected direction of each change. Phase-I packets never enter final accuracy estimates. Then freeze v1, the full sampling frame, random seed, inclusion probabilities, packet generator, manual, and source strata.

### Phase II: independent hospital-stratified validation

For every hospital with at least 50 index episodes:

- Review a stratified probability sample of (min(40,N_{h,+})) A=1 windows, allocating at least five where available to each represented source signature and retaining exact inclusion probabilities.
- Review every A=0 window if their total is at most 1,200 (the inherited provisional audit suggests about 553). If there are more than 1,200, review a fixed 1,200-window stratified probability sample with at least 24 per hospital where available, one random base component plus sentinel-enriched components, and retain Horvitz-Thompson weights. This cap is fixed before labels are seen.
- One ICU clinician reviews every packet. A blinded 30% random replicate, every probable/indeterminate packet, and every packet with logically discordant transition labels receives a second independent review; a third clinician arbitrates after initial labels are locked.
- Report raw and weighted counts, effective sample size, packet exclusions, review time, pairwise agreement, prevalence-adjusted agreement, and kappa for each component. No hospital identifiers appear in reviewer materials or public outputs.

For hospital (h), let weighted counts (D_{ah},N_{ah},U_{ah}) denote definite/probable event, definitely absent, and indeterminate reference states within algorithm stratum (A=a). “Probable” is included with definite only in the central estimate and moved to U in the conservative analysis. Design-weighted central sensitivity, PPV, specificity, and NPV are reported. Without pretending U is resolved, partial-identification intervals include:

- (PPV_h in [D_{1h}/T_{1h},(D_{1h}+U_{1h})/T_{1h}]);
- (Se_h in [D_{1h}/(D_{1h}+D_{0h}+U_{0h}), (D_{1h}+U_{1h})/(D_{1h}+U_{1h}+D_{0h})]);
- analogous specificity bounds obtained by interchanging definite event/absence and A strata.

Use survey-weighted hospital/person-cluster bootstrap with simultaneous 95% intervals. Also report event-time agreement within 6 and 12 hours and hospital-specific median signed timing error.

The transparent measurement baseline M0 is these design-weighted confusion tables and worst-case bounds. The scientifically substantive alternative M1 is a clinician-anchored hierarchical Bayesian observation model: source indicators and their interactions generate the observed algorithm/source pattern conditional on latent S; hospital random effects are estimated separately for sensitivity and false-positive probability; adjudicated windows anchor the latent state; sampling weights enter a pseudo-likelihood; and weakly informative priors are fixed before Phase II. M1 can reveal whether apparent misses track a source mix or an irreducible hospital effect that M0 aggregates away. It is admissible only if posterior predictive checks reproduce weighted source-by-hospital confusion tables and its conclusions lie within M0 partial-identification bounds; it cannot “rescue” a failed M0 gate.

For each validation bootstrap/posterior draw and true event prevalence (p) over the observed adjudication-supported range, compute the observed event probability expected under identical recovery but hospital-specific ascertainment:
(q_h(p)=Se_h p+(1-Sp_h)(1-p)).
Define (Delta_{asc}) as the maximum over p of the hospital P90-P10 of (q_h(p)). This translates sensitivity/specificity differences into the same absolute-risk scale as the recovery estimand.

### Gate fixed before recovery modeling

No hospital recovery random effect, P90-P10 recovery contrast, hospital ranking, or Model B/L fit may be computed or viewed until all of the following pass on Phase II alone:

1. sustained-liberation central PPV and sensitivity and landmark-IMV PPV are each at least 0.85, with pooled simultaneous lower 95% bounds at least 0.80; the indexed-window design does not claim landmark-IMV sensitivity;
2. every retained hospital has conservative lower 95% bounds at least 0.70 for both PPV and sensitivity, and at least 20 reviewed A=1 windows plus either an A=0 census or weighted A=0 effective sample size at least 20;
3. the simultaneous 95% upper bound for (Delta_{asc}) is at most 5 absolute percentage points under both M0 bounds and admissible M1;
4. no source signature has a sensitivity difference whose simultaneous upper bound exceeds 10 points after hospital adjustment; no one source produces more than 90% of endpoints in more than 25% of retained hospitals;
5. indeterminate reference status is at most 10% overall and 20% in every retained hospital; median signed timing error is within ±6 hours in every hospital and the hospital P90-P10 timing-bias spread is at most 12 hours;
6. kappa is at least 0.70 for sustained liberation and reinstitution; at least 30 hospitals retain at least 50 index cases and 20 adjudication-compatible endpoints.

Failure of any item ends the hospital-recovery branch. Report a measurement-only result, including whether failure arose from low PPV, low sensitivity, differential source ascertainment, timing, imprecision, or insufficient hospitals. Do not relax thresholds, inspect recovery contrasts, replace the outcome with `actualventdays`, merge sites, or drop adverse hospitals after seeing labels. If M0 passes but M1 fails calibration, proceed only with M0-based correction; if M0 fails, M1 cannot override it.

## Common recovery inputs, split, and measurement correction

After a pass, both recovery models use identical fixed demographics/admission/APACHE/EOL variables and four six-hour bins from t0 to L containing respiratory settings, oxygenation/ventilation, vital signs, selected labs, sedative/opioid/pressor exposure, measurement counts, missingness masks, time since last observation, and chart-entry delay. The transparent model receives prespecified first/last/min/max, slope, area-outside-range, and observation-count summaries; the learned model receives the same binned tensor and masks. Neither receives post-L physiology, `actualventdays`, outcome-period discharge destination, hospital volume, or an outcome-derived feature.

Assign five folds by deterministic hash of `uniquepid`, stratified so every hospital contributes to each fold. Fit units, plausibility/winsorization limits, imputation, feature selection, hyperparameters, and the measurement calibration on training data or the separately adjudicated validation set only. No patient's repeated stays cross folds. Keep the same folds for both models.

The frozen adjudication model supplies hospital/source-specific emission probabilities for reconstructed P/S/R labels. Primary Model B and L fits use either a joint latent-state likelihood or at least 200 multiple imputations of true transitions from those emissions; combine model and sampling uncertainty and nest the hospital/person bootstrap outside imputation. The naive v1 label analysis is a labeled sensitivity only. Recovery support must hold over the entire admissible M0 partial-identification region, not merely M1 posterior means.

## Model B: transparent competing-risk multistate baseline

Preserve the hierarchical semi-Markov piecewise-exponential model for V→P, P→S, P→R, R→P, and V/P/R→D or X, plus tracheostomy, with six-hour intervals for days 1–3 and daily intervals thereafter. Use prespecified restricted cubic splines for continuous baseline summaries, diagnosis group and unit type, and partially pooled hospital random intercepts for each transition. Integrate over the adjudication-calibrated emission model. Standardize 14-day state occupation by g-computation over the pooled baseline distribution. Report coefficients, shrinkage, P90-P10 contrasts, and nested bootstrap/imputation intervals.

Model B is interpretable and keeps competing paths explicit, but loses within-day order, nonlinear covariation, and observation dynamics beyond summaries.

## Model L: structured learned longitudinal alternative

Preserve the two-layer, 64-hidden-unit GRU with dropout 0.2 over the four six-hour bins. It outputs:

1. a physiologic recovery state, supervised by next-bin respiratory burden (FiO2, PEEP, pressure/volume, SpO2/PaO2, vasopressor, neurologic summaries); and
2. an observation state, supervised by next-bin label availability, counts, and entry delays.

Both states and fixed covariates feed the same calibrated semi-Markov transitions as Model B. A gradient-reversal penalty suppresses hospital prediction from the physiologic state conditional on case mix; the observation state may encode workflow. Hospital effects appear only in transition heads, not patient embeddings. Tune on training folds lexicographically: multistate likelihood/calibration first, then leakage, never desired heterogeneity. Reject Model L if hospital macro-AUROC from the physiologic state exceeds 0.65, state calibration materially worsens, or the observation model contradicts adjudicated source errors. Ablate observation state, recovery state, adversary, and missingness/count inputs.

Model L can test whether nonlinear temporal ordering and explicit observation dynamics account for variation that Model B attributes to hospital context. It does not discover a biological mechanism. The recovery hypothesis requires both admissible models; a technically failed L permits a valid baseline analysis but not the two-model persistence claim.

## Evaluation, falsification, and interpretation

Held-out evaluation includes integrated Brier score, transition likelihood, state calibration at days 3/7/14, 48-hour R discrimination/calibration, standardized S/D/X/R/tracheostomy/ventilated risks, P90-P10 and hospital random-effect uncertainty, fold stability, and subgroup results by age, sex, broad race/ethnicity, surgical/medical status, and baseline severity. Sensitivities include strict dual-source episodes, source-balanced reweighting, timing perturbations drawn from adjudicated intervals, 72-hour and seven-day sustain, exclusion of pre-L EOL records, and profiling the full admissible measurement bounds. Use 1,000 nested cluster bootstraps sampling hospitals then people.

- **Supportive:** the measurement gate passes; both corrected admissible models show P90-P10 S-risk at least 10 points with lower 95% bound above 5 over all admissible measurement parameters; fold and primary-sensitivity ordering Spearman is at least 0.70; source-balanced/strict dual-source directions persist; and no D/X/R/tracheostomy tradeoff explains the apparent advantage. This supports persistent adjusted hospital-context heterogeneity in eICU-documented sustained liberation, not causation or quality. If Model L reduces Model B hospital variance by at least 25% while residual support remains, nonlinear pre-landmark trajectory/observation information explains part of the descriptive variation.
- **Adverse/falsifying:** the measurement gate fails; or after a pass either admissible model has contrast below 5 points/interval including zero, unstable ordering, reversal under measurement bounds/source balancing/timing, a compensating rise in D/X/R, or failed learned-model leakage/calibration. Conclude that eICU does not support a stable recovery difference; differential ascertainment, case mix, competing outcomes, or model instability is the stronger explanation.
- **Inconclusive:** measurement bounds span both admissible and inadmissible comparability, fewer than 30 hospitals remain, corrected intervals span both <5 and ≥10 points, models materially disagree without a resolved diagnostic, or reviewer uncertainty is too high. Report precisely what additional adjudication or source data could narrow the result; do not call an imprecise null falsification.
- **Measurement-only:** low or differential sensitivity/PPV, source dependence, or timing error blocks recovery modeling. This is a substantive result about nonportable eICU ventilation ascertainment.

Even a supportive association cannot identify weaning, sedation, mobility, staffing, or other practices as causes. Causal or quality conclusions require reliable time-varying practice measures, readiness/SBT data, goals-of-care and clinician judgment, positivity and causal assumptions, ICU expert review, and preferably prospective or quasi-experimental replication with source-chart validation. Public rankings are prohibited.
