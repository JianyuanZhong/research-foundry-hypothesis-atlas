## Unresolved question and advance

The strongest relevant evidence available here supports only that static metabolic measures and longitudinal disease histories are associated with later disease burden. The local full readable natural-history demonstration (Shmatko et al., DOI `10.1038/s41586-025-09529-3`) supports the design lesson that disease order, competing events, temporal validation and calibration matter; it does not establish this HbA1c question. The local full readable Bayesian demonstration (Urbut et al., DOI `10.1038/s41586-026-10780-5`) supports explicit time-aware likelihoods and selection-aware prediction; it does not establish this hypothesis. The cancer demonstration's main paper and STAR Methods were unavailable; only its supplement and metadata were inspected, so no complete-paper claim is used.

The unresolved clinical claim is whether two adults with the same current HbA1c have different near-term risk because one arrived there from a lower prior value. This matters for interpreting a repeat HbA1c and deciding whether current-level risk communication is sufficient or history should trigger closer cardiometabolic review. The substantive advance over a static association is a delayed-entry, disease-free, same-current-level history contrast with explicit competing death and selection limits.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UKB participants with valid baseline and strictly later paired repeat HbA1c, alive and free of inpatient-coded type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease and heart failure at the repeat landmark, a 0.5 percentage-point lower baseline HbA1c at the same current repeat HbA1c is associated with a higher 5-year cumulative incidence of a second distinct disease family among those three families.

This is a prognostic association hypothesis, not a causal effect of HbA1c change or proof that repeat testing improves care. A material opposite contrast supports history dependence but refutes the expected direction; a sufficiently precise null refutes the added-history claim.

## Population and temporal design

Use one record per participant and horizontal one-to-one joins on `eid`; never append tables as observations.

- From population, require `21022-0.0` age and `31-0.0` sex. From assessment, use instance-0 date `53-0.0` and instance-1 date `53-1.0`; require parseable dates with instance 1 strictly later and a repeat interval of 1–6 years for the primary cohort (report the unrestricted valid-repeat sensitivity).
- Require baseline/repeat HbA1c `30750-0.0` and `30750-1.0` from biological samples, numeric and finite. Verify the HbA1c unit and that sample instances 0/1 correspond to the selected assessment dates. Field-instance indices are not elapsed time by assumption.
- The time zero is the repeat assessment date. Exclude any aligned qualifying component diagnosis or death on/before that date. Participants without a valid repeat are not silently treated as negative controls; compare repeat-observed and no-valid-repeat participants on baseline fields.
- Let B be baseline HbA1c and C current repeat HbA1c. The primary estimand is the standardized, noncausal 5-year cumulative-incidence contrast at the same C: B=C-0.5 versus B=C, generated only over observed common support and standardized over age, sex, BMI, blood pressure and verified renal covariates. A separate secondary estimand compares C=B+0.5 versus C=B at fixed B. Do not put unrestricted C, B and C-B in one model because of algebraic redundancy.
- Follow from the repeat date through 5 years, administrative end 2023-03-31, the second-family event, or death. Death before the event is a competing event. Prespecify a 365-day post-landmark washout and the 1–6-year repeat-interval restriction as reverse-causation sensitivities.

## Outcome and exact code/date construction

In `health_outcomes`, pair each `41270-j` inpatient ICD-10 code with `41280-j` at the identical index j (j=0,...,258); never sort codes and dates independently or cross-match suffixes. A code with no valid aligned date is ascertainment failure, not evidence of no disease.

For each participant, the first post-landmark date in each family is:

- type 2 diabetes: E11–E14;
- chronic kidney disease: N18.x;
- heart failure: I50.x.

The primary outcome is the later date among the first two distinct families. Same-date first occurrences of two families count as a second-family event. Report family identity, same-date ties, first-family ordering, death competing events, administrative censoring and alignment failures. Secondary outcomes are each component, any first component, all-cause death, and an expanded second-family endpoint adding I20–I25 and I60–I69. Secondary outcomes cannot rescue an infeasible primary endpoint.

## Analysis and baselines

First produce an audit/flow table: row counts and hashes; `eid` uniqueness and overlap agreement; baseline/repeat date validity and interval distribution; HbA1c missing, nonnumeric, negative and out-of-range counts; unit and instance/date verification; pre-landmark exclusions; aligned code/date failures; component family order and ties; follow-up, events, deaths and censoring; and characteristics of repeat-observed versus no-valid-repeat participants.

Primary analysis is a cause-specific hazards model for the second-family event with death as competing event, using a prespecified flexible joint surface in C and B plus age spline, sex, BMI, systolic/diastolic BP and verified baseline renal covariates. Standardize predicted 5-year cumulative incidence using Aalen–Johansen and report risk difference, risk ratio, cause-specific hazard ratio, curves and participant-bootstrap 95% intervals. Fine–Gray is sensitivity only. Use nested models on the same landmark cohort:

- M0: C plus baseline covariates;
- M1: C and B/history surface plus baseline covariates;
- M2: B and C-B plus baseline covariates, with identical complexity.

Use multiple imputation only within bootstrap partitions. Repeat-selection inverse-probability weighting is a sensitivity with prespecified truncation, positivity and effective-sample-size diagnostics; it does not remove unmeasured selection. A bounded regularized spline or shallow gradient-boosting analysis may be secondary, with all tuning inside resampling partitions and calibration/Brier reporting. AUC, small performance gains and any machine-learning result are descriptive, not evidence of clinical utility.

## Falsification and interpretation

Set a 2-percentage-point absolute 5-year risk difference as a research materiality threshold before fitting; it is not a treatment threshold. Support for the expected history claim requires all hard data gates to pass, at least 100 primary events overall and 10 in each displayed history/change stratum, common-support coverage, a positive history contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero and point estimate at least 2 points, broadly consistent direction after washout, repeat-interval, competing-risk and weighting sensitivities, calibration of M1, and no equally large positive association for the fixed negative-control inpatient appendicitis endpoint K35.x (report its event count).

A material opposite contrast is adverse to the expected direction but supports history dependence. A narrow null whose interval excludes the 2-point threshold supports current-level sufficiency for this endpoint in this selected population. A current-level association without a history increment supports static risk association but falsifies the added-history claim. Disappearance after washout suggests reverse causation; disappearance after repeat weighting suggests measured selection. Results are inconclusive for failed hard gates, fewer than 100 events, sparse strata/control events, extrapolative support, unstable weights, or uncertainty spanning trivial and material effects.

Computation can check availability, filtering, temporal order, code-defined inpatient family events, event order, competing-risk estimates, calibration, uncertainty and gate status. It cannot establish outpatient disease, adjudicated diagnoses, severity, laboratory trajectories between two measurements, causal effects, representativeness, treatment benefit or clinical utility. Those require validated dictionaries and dates, outpatient labs/medications, clinical adjudication, external validation, expert review and a prospective testing-and-management study.
