# Episode 66 result-bearing successor: late lactate after a time-safe laboratory and bedside-physiology state

## Result and strongest supported claim

The frozen complete-source EICU experiment is **computable** and its ordered result is **Inconclusive**.

In the exact 1,229-person, 33-hospital landmark population, adding only ln(late lactate) and its square to the frozen R0 laboratory-plus-physiology model produced a leave-one-hospital-out threshold-averaged increment of **+1.0097 net true-positive-equivalent reviews per 100 people**. The prespecified 2,000-replicate copied-hospital refit-bootstrap 95% percentile interval was **−0.2906 to +2.0575 per 100**. Because this interval spans both zero and the +1 materiality margin, the experiment neither supports nor falsifies the material-increment hypothesis.

The strongest claim supported by these data is narrower: the locked R1 model had a positive point increment at every one of the 21 thresholds, improved point AUROC and both proper scores, and remained positive in all 33 fixed-prediction site deletions. Copied-hospital uncertainty nevertheless permits no incremental value, and R1 was overfit/miscalibrated by the frozen slope guardrail (calibration slope 0.589). These results do not establish value from ordering lactate, benefit from acting on a score, or patient benefit.

The unresolved claim remains the parent's hypothesis: among similarly selected patients, late lactate yields at least +1/100 modeled mortality-review value beyond a time-indexed laboratory and cardiorespiratory state. Resolving it requires another adequately powered, independently synchronized cohort and clinically elicited utilities, not post hoc threshold narrowing or model expansion.

## Frozen question, population, and clocks

At L24=t0+1,440 minutes after the earliest recorded norepinephrine/Levophed infusion entry in ICU minutes 0–1,440, does adding the released late lactate improve modeled hospital-mortality review decisions beyond R0?

The complete EICU snapshot [source checksum] was used without source-row sampling. Adults were age >=18 after mapping literal `> 89` and numeric ages >89 to 90. One stay per `uniquepid` was selected by lexicographically smallest (t0, patientunitstayid). Eligible people had known hospital discharge status and hospitaldischargeoffset >=L24.

Baseline B used valid lactate 0.2–30 mmol/L collected in [t0−360,t0] with revision <=t0+60; the latest valid collection was selected and B>=2 required. Follow-up F used the same validity rules in [t0+720,L24] with revision <=L24, selecting nearest t0+1080, then the earlier collection, then smallest labid. At a collection offset, only the greatest revision survived; tied greatest revisions with different values invalidated the collection and identical ties collapsed to smallest labid. Hospitals were frozen outcome-blind by requiring >=5 people in each historical X category, where X=1[(B−F)/B<0.20]. X was not modeled or analyzed.

The independently recomputed flow was exact:

- 200,859 patient rows; 200,234 adults, including 7,081 older-age mappings.
- 16,299 early-pressor stays; 14,786 selected people.
- 14,672 known outcomes; 13,023 hospitalized through L24.
- 5,077 valid baselines; 3,117 with B>=2; 1,506 with eligible repeat lactate.
- 1,229 people in the identical 33 outcome-blind hospitals: 507 deaths and 722 survivors.

Every hospital contained both outcomes; the largest hospital contributed 9.36%, below 15%.

## Frozen predictors and analysis

R0 retained exactly 166 predictors: age and age squared; Female and Male indicators with Other/Unknown omitted; t0/1,440 and its square; baseline lag, ln(B), and ln(B) squared; follow-up measurement age without F; six frozen summaries for each of 23 non-lactate markers (missing, log1p count, last, recency, minimum, maximum); and last, median, minimum, maximum, resolved-timestamp count, and missingness for heart rate, respiration, and SpO2 in [L24−360,L24]. R1 had 168 predictors and added only ln(F) and ln(F) squared. Hospital, X, outcomes, clearance, F/B, treatments, diagnoses, notes, post-L24 rows, and the optional R2 shape diagnostic were absent.

Lab observations were collection-bounded to [t0,L24], revision-bounded to <=L24, and resolved at patient/marker/collection offset by the frozen revision/conflict/tie rules. All 23 markers passed richness: post-resolution coverage ranged from 756/1,229 (61.5%) to 1,213/1,229 (98.7%), across 31–33 hospitals.

VitalPeriodic values were collection-bounded to [L24−360,L24], resolved separately by stream at patient/timestamp, and restricted to heart rate 20–250/min, respiration 2–80/min, and SpO2 40–100%. There were 86,335 qualifying export rows. Post-QC coverage was 1,218/1,229 (99.10%) for heart rate, 1,126/1,229 (91.62%) for respiration, and 1,176/1,229 (95.69%) for SpO2; no contradictory in-range timestamp was found.

Both models used unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10,000, tol=1e−8, no class weighting, and seed 20260961. Within each LOHO training fold, observed continuous features were winsorized at 1%/99%, standardized by training mean and population SD, and mean-imputed to zero after scaling; held-out hospitals used only their training-fold constants. All continuous training SDs were finite and positive, all 33 point folds converged, and each person received one finite prediction from each model.

At thresholds 0.30–0.50 by 0.01, net benefit was TP/N−FP/N×p/(1−p). The sole primary contrast was R1−R0, integrated by trapezoid and expressed per 100. Bootstrap sampling drew 33 hospitals with replacement 2,000 times; copies of an original hospital remained one held-out group, were wholly excluded from that fold's training data, and entered training and evaluation as row copies. Preprocessing and both models were refit in every replicate.
