# MerTK-inhibitor selectivity in RA synovial macrophage–fibroblast cocultures

## Scientific question

**Unresolved hypothesis.** In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), MerTK/CD206-positive synovial tissue macrophages (STMs) are associated with a reparative rather than inflammatory macrophage state. Does pre-treatment of those macrophages with a MerTK inhibitor reduce a harmful fibroblast inflammatory/destructive program while preserving a macrophage repair-linked identity and a fibroblast matrix/TGF-beta repair program?

This is not established by prior evidence. The experiment tests **perturbation-response selectivity**, not target causality, patient benefit, or a direct fibroblast-intrinsic mechanism.

## Clinical importance

RA synovial fibroblasts drive inflammation and cartilage/bone destruction, while macrophage-fibroblast repair circuits can resolve inflammation or become maladaptive. A therapy that suppresses the harmful fibroblast program while erasing the protective macrophage/repair program could be counterproductive; one that separates the two programs would prioritize a useful stromal-myeloid intervention axis. Conversely, a null or adverse result can prevent premature enthusiasm for MerTK blockade in this cell state.

## Existing evidence and advance

- **Observed evidence.** The E-MTAB-8873 repository states that MerTK/CD206-negative STMs induced an inflammatory fibroblast cluster, whereas MerTK/CD206-positive STMs—especially from sustained remission—induced collagen/TGF-beta repair responses. It does not report the effect of MerTK inhibition on those programs.
- **Existing knowledge.** Reviews support macrophage-fibroblast repair circuits [K2] and pathogenic fibroblast targeting [K3]; a preprint identifies an inflammatory-to-destructive fibroblast transition [K1].
- **New claim tested.** MerTK inhibition in the MerTK/CD206-positive context either reduces the harmful fibroblast program selectively, broadly suppresses repair programs, or produces no coherent program-level effect. This is a bounded, falsifiable transition from cell-state association to intervention-response selectivity.

## Baseline analysis

1. Parse the prepared TSV as a feature-by-sample matrix and join each sample column to `E-MTAB-8873.design.json` by `sample_id`.
2. For each exact feature row, compute `log2(value + 1)`.
3. For each of the three matched units, compute the paired difference `inhibitor - untreated` for each program score, where a score is the mean of its exact-feature log2 values.
4. Report the three paired differences, their median, a nonparametric sign/rank interval, and an exact restricted sign-flip null distribution over the three units (eight possible sign assignments). Do not use an ordinary three-donor t-test.
5. Report all three per-gene paired log2 differences for program genes, flagged as exploratory, and the same score for the single Mneg unit as a specificity check.
6. Sensitivity analysis: use panel-median scaling and a rank-based score; repeat without zero-heavy MMP13. If conclusions change, label the result inconclusive.

**Primary estimand.** Median paired program-score change caused by comparing MerTK-inhibitor-pre-treated versus untreated MerTK/CD206-positive macrophages in these recorded ex vivo units.

## Substantive alternative and comparison

A simple score baseline is scientifically adequate for the primary question because the gene sets are prespecified and the effective sample size is three.

A substantive **rank-based multivariate profile alternative** can still reveal information the mean score loses: whether the inhibitor produces a coordinated program-wide shift or is driven by one gene. Fit no supervised predictive model. For each program, rank the paired per-gene log2 differences, compute the signed rank-sum statistic, and obtain an exact restricted permutation p-value by sign-flipping all genes within each matched unit. Compare this with the mean-score result. Report gene-level contributions and whether the harmful, repair, and macrophage identity programs move together.

A sparse PLS or neural model is deferred because three matched units cannot support stable feature selection, hyperparameter tuning, or honest held-out validation; fitting it would add unjustified complexity. If more matched units become available, revisit sparse PLS with leave-one-unit-out prediction and held-out program-score estimation. Estimated solver resources are CPU only, under one minute and 4 GB for this 445-by-33 matrix; GPU use is unnecessary.

## Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive.** The harmful score falls in all three MerTK/CD206-positive units, the exact sign-flip interval is directionally consistent, and the macrophage repair-linked and matrix/TGF scores are not broadly reduced. This supports a bounded selectivity hypothesis in this assay only.
- **Adverse.** The harmful score does not fall, or repair-linked macrophage and fibroblast matrix/TGF scores fall together with it. This challenges MerTK blockade in this context and would favor a more selective target or timing.
- **Inconclusive.** Effects are discordant across units, score methods disagree, zero-heavy genes dominate, or confidence intervals cross zero. The correct conclusion is uncertainty, not therapeutic failure.
- **Rival explanations.** Off-target inhibitor effects, macrophage donor composition, run/batch, cell-count/quality differences, pre-treatment timing, 445-gene ascertainment, and pseudobulk cell-origin mixing can all explain the result. The negative-state R1 unit and run labels are checks, not causal controls.
- **Claims requiring more evidence.** Target causality, clinical benefit, safety, patient-level heterogeneity, or generalization beyond these RA cocultures require vehicle controls, genetic MerTK perturbation, cell-type-resolved measurements, additional independent donor-matched units, and prospective expert/wet-lab validation.

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must compute the three paired program-score differences, per-gene contributions, exact restricted permutation inference, sensitivity analyses, and a clear supportive/adverse/inconclusive classification. No patient-level or clinical claim is computationally checkable from this assay.
