# Recovery shape after hypotension and observed renal deterioration: an eICU transport experiment

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent question established a useful unresolved comparison: at similar observed MAP nadir and pre-window state, does accumulated low-MAP exposure carry information about a subsequent renal deterioration signal beyond minimum MAP? This successor asks a more clinically actionable refinement: among patients with the same minimum MAP and similar total low-MAP burden, does *recovery timing* matter?

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is limited to data availability and methodological plausibility: the eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative MAP observations, serial labelled laboratory results, ICU discharge status/time, hospital identifiers and structured support records. The seed and prior proposals do not establish that hypotension causes kidney injury, that 65 mmHg is a treatment target, or that an EHR creatinine change is adjudicated AKI.

The unresolved claim is whether a late-persistent low-MAP trajectory has a higher 24-hour risk of an observed creatinine deterioration signal than an early low-MAP episode that recovers, after conditioning on minimum MAP, total low-MAP minutes/depth, baseline creatinine trajectory and observation support. The primary falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Within prespecified minimum-MAP and total-burden support strata, landmarks with low MAP persisting into the final two hours of the preceding six-hour window have a positive, non-zero standardized 24-hour risk difference for a subsequent creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal compared with landmarks with comparable total low-MAP burden but recovery before that final two-hour period; this recovery-shape contrast reproduces in hospitals held out from fitting.

This is an observational risk-marker hypothesis. A positive result could motivate prospective study of recovery-aware hemodynamic surveillance; it cannot establish a causal effect of permitting hypotension, a MAP treatment threshold, or benefit from a pressor.

This is a substantive advance over the parent’s scalar duration/minimum comparison: it separates two patients with similar nadir, duration and depth but different temporal order. The clinically relevant information is whether low pressure is resolving at the landmark, which a minimum-plus-total-burden summary loses.

## Population, landmarks and temporal boundaries

Retain the first ICU stay per person: `age >=18` after mapping `> 89` to 90 and recording nonnumeric ages, `unitvisitnumber=1`, nonmissing `hospitalid`, numeric `unitdischargeoffset), and at least 36 hours from offset 0 to discharge. If multiple valid `unitvisitnumber=1` rows exist for a `uniquepid`, retain the earliest valid admission row and report the conflict rather than count the person twice.

Candidate landmarks are t = 12, 18, ..., 72 hours after ICU admission. Retain only a landmark with at least six hours before t and at least 24 hours until `unitdischargeoffset`. Exclude any landmark with an EOL save/discussion offset <= t. Use all eligible landmarks for the primary patient-clustered analysis; one fixed-seed eligible landmark per stay is a sensitivity analysis. Every predictor timestamp, including `labresultrevisedoffset`, must be <= t.

## Exposure construction

Primary MAP uses valid numeric `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` in a prespecified plausible range of [-50, 300] mmHg. Sort by `observationoffset`; each observation represents only the following `min(5, next_valid_offset-current_offset)` minutes, clipped to [t-360,t), with no carry-forward over gaps and no interval beyond t. Require at least 80% observed/covered minutes; repeat at 70% and 90%. Report invalid values, coverage, recording density and gaps by hospital.

For each landmark calculate:

- `minimum_map`;
- `low_map_minutes`: covered minutes with MAP <65;
- `low_map_depth_area`: sum over covered minutes of (65-MAP) for MAP <65, in mmHg-min;
- `early_low_minutes` in [t-360,t-120);
- `late_low_minutes` in [t-120,t);
- `recovery_shape`: primary indicator late_low_minutes = 0 and early_low_minutes > 0, versus persistent indicator late_low_minutes > 0, with total low minutes and depth area retained as covariates.

The primary contrast is persistent versus recovered within minimum-MAP strata (55-64, 45-54, <45 when both states have support) and total low-MAP burden bands (0-60, >60-120, >120 minutes when both states are present). A continuous secondary estimand is the standardized risk difference for a 60-minute increase in late-low minutes at fixed total low minutes/minimum MAP using a prespecified spline or interaction, not post-hoc threshold search. The exposure is deliberately an observed-monitoring summary, not continuous hypotension.

Repeat all primary exposure construction using `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean`, and a prespecified periodic-first/aperiodic-only-when-periodic-absent sensitivity. Repeat MAP cutoffs 60 and 70 mmHg.

## Outcome, censoring and estimand

The primary outcome is a **creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal**, not adjudicated AKI. Require a stable pre-landmark renal baseline: two positive numeric creatinine results with comparable source label/system/interface, timestamps in [t-48h,t], both revised by <=t and separated by 6-48 hours. Define the baseline as the last pre-t result and record the pre-t slope from the two values. Among landmarks with this baseline and at least one future creatinine in (t,t+24h], define the first future value as an event if it is >= baseline + 0.3 on the retained source scale or >=1.5 times baseline. The compiled audit must verify label/unit comparability and plausible values before interpreting this endpoint. If comparability fails, return an inconclusive feasibility result rather than relabeling it KDIGO AKI.

A missing future creatinine is observation censoring, never a negative. ICU death in (t,t+24h] and alive ICU discharge/transfer before 24 hours are competing events for the 24-hour renal cumulative-incidence analysis. Landmarks without an observed future creatinine and without a competing event are censored at the last observable opportunity; report an inverse-probability-of-observation analysis based only on pre-t variables, plus complete-case and unweighted sensitivities. Do not use future creatinine as a predictor. Secondary outcomes are ICU death and each discharge/death competing event separately.

The primary estimand is the standardized 24-hour cumulative-incidence risk difference for persistent versus recovered recovery shape within the prespecified minimum-MAP/total-burden support strata, under the eligible-landmark covariate distribution. Fit a cause-specific regularized model with patient-clustered inference and standardize predictions; bootstrap patients within hospital for 95% intervals. Report the interaction between recovery shape, total burden and minimum MAP only if the predeclared support gate is met. Transport is evaluated by calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, standardized risk difference and interval in each held-out hospital with >=200 eligible landmarks and both exposure/outcome levels.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The transparent baseline is a regularized pooled logistic/cause-specific risk model using admission demographics, time since admission, admission-only Apache variables/scores, baseline creatinine, two-result pre-t creatinine slope, MAP minimum/mean/SD/coverage/last value, total low-MAP minutes, depth area, recovery-shape indicator, pre-window heart rate/respiration/SpO2 summaries and slopes, documented ventilation and normalized pressor-record indicators, and missingness indicators. Hospital is a stratification variable, not a feature. The duration/depth model without recovery-shape is the nested comparator; the primary test is the incremental standardized risk and calibration contribution of recovery shape.

The substantive learned alternative is a small causal temporal-convolution model over six preceding one-hour bins with the same admission covariates and time-safe hourly MAP, heart rate, respiration, SpO2, creatinine, pressor-record and ventilation-record values plus explicit masks and gap/coverage channels. Fit matched duration/depth-only and full-order variants to the same renal endpoint. The full model can reveal early dip versus late persistence, partial recovery and irregular observation patterns that the baseline’s single recovery indicator loses. Train on patient-held-out training hospitals, use a fixed patient-level validation split for early stopping, and evaluate untouched hospitals; do not use hospital ID. Use three fixed seeds and patient/hospital-stratified bootstrap uncertainty. Compare standardized risks, calibration, Brier score and prespecified decision curves; AUROC is secondary and cannot determine clinical value.

This comparison is not a complexity contest. The baseline is selected for interpretability and a directly estimable recovery contrast. The temporal model is retained because ordering is the scientific object, not merely because it is more flexible. CPU fitting is preferred: streaming extraction and regularized models are expected to take under 45 minutes, and the small temporal variants roughly 45-90 minutes within the 7,200-second science budget using two CPUs and <=8 GiB. GPU use is deferred because no distinct scientific question requires it. If the temporal model is not feasible, the baseline recovery-shape estimate remains the primary deliverable; the alternative is not silently replaced by an easier target.

## Falsification, support gates and interpretation

Before fitting, report cohort/stay/landmark counts; MAP coverage and observation density; exposure-state support by minimum MAP and total-burden stratum; exact creatinine label/system/interface map, unit comparability audit, two-result baseline support, future-lab support, competing events and all counts by hospital. Require both recovery states, both renal outcome states, adequate site support and stable label mapping. Otherwise report an inconclusive feasibility result without a clinical conclusion.

Falsification and robustness checks are:

1. Permute the order of valid MAP observations within each pre-t window while preserving MAP values, counts, coverage and gaps. A recovery-shape association should attenuate while total burden/minimum summaries remain comparable.
2. Apply the same creatinine-rise rule as a pre-landmark placebo in [t-24h,t), using only results revised by <=t. A similarly strong placebo suggests reverse-time severity or documentation bias.
3. Repeat MAP source priority, cutoffs (60/65/70), coverage (70/80/90%), burden bands and the stable two-result versus one-result creatinine rule.
4. Exclude landmarks within 12 hours of discharge/transfer, landmarks with EOL proxies, and landmarks with abrupt loss of all source coverage.
5. Repeat with one landmark per stay and leave-one-hospital-out evaluation. Do not rank hospitals.
6. Test whether adjustment for documented pressor/ventilation records changes the contrast; because pressor records lack stop times and support records are incomplete, this is not causal treatment adjustment.
7. Compare the recovery contrast with the parent monitoring-gap comparator using non-overlapping lab-count windows [t-18h,t-6h) and [t-6h,t), without claiming superiority from AUROC.

Supportive results require a positive persistent-versus-recovered risk difference with a 95% interval excluding zero, persistence across source/coverage/threshold and censoring checks, attenuation under time permutation, weak pre-landmark placebo, and either calibrated recovery-shape contribution in held-out hospitals or a prespecified temporal pattern that reproduces there. This supports a transportable observational marker of renal-risk context and justifies prospective recovery-aware monitoring research, not a treatment threshold.

Adverse results are a precise null/negative contrast, disappearance under time permutation or censoring controls, a strong placebo, or failure in held-out hospitals. This would indicate that total burden/minimum MAP or documentation severity carries the reproducible signal rather than recovery timing.

Inconclusive results include inadequate MAP coverage, no within-stratum recovery contrast, unstable creatinine label/unit mapping, too few two-result baselines or future labs, insufficient hospital support, unresolved observation censoring, or intervals too wide to distinguish supportive from adverse results. No clinical recommendation follows.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/snapshot, exact filters/joins, ICU-relative timestamps, MAP arithmetic, exposure support, creatinine parsing and timing, model fitting, split integrity, standardized risks, intervals, calibration/Brier scores, site transport and falsification outputs. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether MAP observations reflect true perfusion, whether creatinine changes are KDIGO AKI, whether unobserved urine output or outpatient baseline changes the phenotype, what clinicians intended, whether pressors/fluids were given or stopped, whether missing labs reflect interface failure or deliberate care, and whether acting on a marker improves patient outcomes.

## Demonstration dispositions and seed provenance

The three research demonstrations were read as permitted by the local bundle and are not treated as a topic list. Delphi supports a bounded adaptation to ordered eICU trajectories, but its UK Biobank training/external setting is unavailable and reproduction is not claimed; the temporal model here is a question-specific adaptation. ALADYNOULLI motivates longitudinal latent structure, but verified genetic inputs and the original full reproduction dependencies are unavailable; an EHR-only latent model would be a different study and is deferred because the current temporal alternative directly tests recovery ordering. Oncoformer’s main article/STAR Methods remain unavailable and images are absent; a lab-only adaptation is not relevant to this hemodynamic question and is deferred.

The ten eICU expert seeds were preserved and assessed before parent selection. Seed 03 (duration of hypotension) is the parent lineage; seed 02 (testing-practice transportability) and seed 08 (reduced testing/deterioration) remain prespecified comparators. Seeds 01 (vasopressor strategy heterogeneity), 04 (hyperoxia), 05 (ventilatory burden), 06 (night discharge), 07 (transfer risk), 09 (insulin/hypoglycemia) and 10 (hospital recovery speed) remain deferred: each is clinically relevant, but its exact treatment/measurement semantics, outcome support, or unavailable organizational/clinical evidence was not sufficient to displace this verified renal/recovery design. In particular, nutrition cessation cannot be inferred from missing intake-output records, infusionDrug has no end field, and eICU lacks adjudicated outcomes, raw waveforms, images and narrative notes.
