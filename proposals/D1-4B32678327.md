# Provisional hypothesis

## Proposed relationship
Among patients with an acute clinical deterioration, the temporal ordering and rate of recovery of organ dysfunction may distinguish a reversible recovery process from persistent severity-driven decline, and may identify patients for whom escalation decisions should be reconsidered.

This is provisional. It is not yet claimed that recovery ordering is causal, that it improves outcomes, or that it is distinguishable from observation intensity.

## Supporting observation and evidence status
The motivating observation is that organ recovery can appear asynchronous: one organ may improve while another remains impaired. The clinical consequence is that a single severity snapshot can misclassify the trajectory and delay or accelerate escalation. I have not yet verified this observation in the configured cohort or inspected a complete literature source for this episode.

## Exact data required (to be verified)
- Cohort: adult acute-care encounters with time-stamped organ function measurements and a defined deterioration event.
- Inputs: sequential organ scores or component measures, time since deterioration, treatment/escalation markers, observation frequency, and discharge or clinical outcome.
- Outcome: subsequent deterioration, need for escalation, or validated clinical endpoint, with a prespecified temporal boundary.
- Missing/unavailable evidence: clinical adjudication of reversible versus irreversible injury, treatment intent, confounding by indication, and whether measurements reflect physiology or observation practice.

## Falsifiable test
Compare patients with similar current severity but different recovery ordering. A supportive result would show that the recovery pattern predicts subsequent trajectory beyond current severity, with a prespecified effect direction and uncertainty interval. An adverse result would show no incremental information after adjustment or a pattern consistent with observation intensity. An inconclusive result would be an imprecise estimate or inadequate time-stamped coverage.

## Method comparison
Baseline: adjusted regression or a time-to-event model using current severity and static covariates. Alternative: a sequence model or mechanistic recovery-state model using the same inputs and held-out temporal split. The alternative could reveal whether ordering and recovery rate carry information lost by a snapshot, but only if observation intensity and treatment timing are addressed.
