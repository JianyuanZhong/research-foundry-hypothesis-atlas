# Event-anchored case-only branch for the sparse-CBC/CRC experiment

## Parent, purpose, and substantive repair

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`.

The parent correctly makes release-matched cancer/death linkage coverage a hard gate for population-level survival inference. This proposal adds a secondary, explicitly narrower branch that can be run when that certificate is absent. It does **not** rescue incidence, survival, cumulative incidence, calibration, PPV, sensitivity, capacity yield, or a prognostic risk claim. Instead, it asks a falsifiable case-case question using only recorded malignant events:

> Among UK Biobank participants with a first **recorded** malignant diagnosis after the day-366 landmark, is the prespecified adverse CBC trajectory (falling haemoglobin and MCV and rising platelets between the two protocolized assessments) more strongly represented among recorded colorectal-cancer (CRC) cases than among recorded non-CRC malignant-cancer cases, after matching on diagnosis-lag stratum and adjusting for age, sex, and landmark CBC values?

A second, event-anchored temporal question is:

> Within recorded CRC cases, is the adverse CBC trajectory more common when the recorded CRC diagnosis is closer to the landmark, with the same lag-gradient tested in recorded non-CRC cases as a specificity check?

The first question is a case-case etiologic/prognostic-pattern contrast conditional on being a recorded cancer case; the second is a prediagnostic-timing gradient. Neither estimates the probability that an arbitrary landmark participant will develop CRC. Their value is diagnostic: they can determine whether the sparse protocolized CBC pattern is CRC-specific among observed cancer diagnoses, or instead is a general feature of recorded malignancy or of late/early ascertainment. This is a substantive, falsifiable result even when absent registry rows cannot be treated as non-events, but it is not a clinical decision rule.

## What is supported and what remains untested

The available parent audit supports only that the rectangular export contains paired fields, repeat-assessment dates, and some suffix-paired cancer date/code records, and that a raw-export flow can be constructed. It does not establish that every participant was observable for cancer or death through any date. The parent audit reports, before any coverage gate, 16,298 strict all-malignant-free delayed-entry candidates, 75 nominal first CRC statuses and 921 nominal first non-CRC statuses through the nominal horizon in that primary set. These are recorded-export counts, not population event counts.

The untested claim is a contrast in CBC trajectory distributions between recorded CRC and recorded non-CRC cases, and whether that contrast varies with recorded diagnosis lag. The case-only branch does not require a non-event control to be called a control, and therefore does not silently convert an absent registry row into a non-event. However, it still depends on the recorded case set containing enough valid paired events and on the selection into recorded cases not being so outcome- or CBC-dependent that the conditional contrast loses its intended meaning. Those assumptions are made explicit and sensitivity-bounded rather than hidden.

## Case construction, timing, and outcome definition

Set `E=L+366 days` and `H=L+1825 days`. The exposure is fully determined at `L`; no outcome-derived value may enter it.

1. Require `t0<L`, `365 <= date_diff(t0,L) <= 2922` days, age 40–75 at `L`, finite paired values for all three primary markers, and an externally certified plausible range/unit check.
2. For every suffix-paired cancer record, normalize only whitespace and case after syntax certification. Accept three-character ICD-10 codes and valid dotted or undotted child codes. A malignant code begins `C00`–`C97`; CRC is `C18`, `C19`, or `C20` including children; non-CRC is malignant excluding C18–C20. Collapse exact duplicate `(eid,event_date,code)` rows.
3. For the primary case-only analysis, use the first recorded malignant date `T` satisfying `E <= T <= H` among participants with no **recorded** malignant diagnosis before `L` and no **recorded** malignant event in `[L,E)`. The label is `Y=1` if the codes on `T` include CRC and `Y=0` if they include only non-CRC malignancy. If both CRC and non-CRC codes occur on the same date, classify `Y=1` for the CRC-versus-other contrast and retain all codes in the audit. This is a date-level first-recorded-event rule, not proof of incident cancer in the underlying population.
4. Exclude a case from the primary case-case contrast if the first event date is after the earliest recorded death date, and classify a same-day cancer/death event as death-first in the primary analysis because within-day ordering is unavailable. Report a cancer-first sensitivity rule. No cancer date after death may be used as an endpoint.
5. Define diagnosis lag `A=date_diff(L,T)` in days and prespecified strata `[366,730]`, `[731,1095]`, `[1096,1460]`, `[1461,1825]`. Exact boundary inclusion is frozen. The lag is an event-anchoring variable, not a baseline predictor; matching/stratifying on it prevents an apparent CRC effect from being a simple difference in follow-up opportunity or ascertainment timing.

The strict recorded-history-free case set is the principal branch because it aligns with the parent risk-set construction. A secondary all-recorded-case analysis may include prior recorded malignancy, labelled a different estimand and not pooled with the principal analysis. It must not be described as cancer-free.

## Exposure and estimands

For each marker `m`, calculate the annualized change

`S_m = (m_1-m_0) / ((L-t0)/365.2425)`.

The primary three-dimensional exposure is the vector `(S_Hb,S_MCV,S_Plt)`, with the prespecified adverse directions falling haemoglobin, falling MCV, and rising platelets. Use continuous terms; do not select thresholds after viewing cases. A locked direction-concordance indicator can be secondary, but it must not replace the continuous primary estimand.

The primary estimand is the stratum-standardized case-case odds ratio for CRC versus non-CRC among first recorded malignant cases, comparing the trajectory model `(age_L, sex, Hb1, MCV1, Plt1, S_Hb, S_MCV, S_Plt)` with the current-values comparator `(age_L, sex, Hb1, MCV1, Plt1)`. Standardize to the empirical distribution of lag strata and sex; report coefficient contrasts and model-based risk differences only as **case composition** differences. The inferential target is not `P(CRC | landmark participant)` and not a hazard ratio.

The secondary temporal estimand is the case-only lag gradient: within recorded CRC cases, the association between each adverse slope and ordered lag stratum, tested using a locked ordinal trend score and the same trend in non-CRC cases. A stronger prediagnostic signal predicts larger adverse slopes in the shortest-lag CRC stratum, but a null result is compatible with a signal detectable only in clinically ordered, denser testing or with no CBC-specific biology.

## Analysis and valid baselines

Use a predeclared, low-dimensional ridge-penalized logistic regression for `Y` with lag strata included as fixed effects and the comparator/current-value covariates listed above. Use identical participant-level repeated outer splits only for descriptive out-of-sample case-composition performance; all imputation, scaling, penalty selection, and calibration must occur within training folds. Because only recorded cases are analyzed, do not use IPCW for unobserved non-events and do not report population AUC, C-index, Brier score, cumulative incidence, or calibration. Case-case AUC, if reported, must be labelled discrimination between recorded CRC and recorded non-CRC cases, with a minimum event gate and no clinical-action interpretation.

The primary contrast is trajectory versus current-value comparator on the identical recorded-case set and identical folds. The baseline is deliberately clinically interpretable: age, sex, and the three landmark CBC values. Do not add other fields, registry-derived predictors, post-L values, diagnosis lag as a free-form predictive variable, or the death date as a predictor. A prespecified age/sex-only descriptive model can be an audit baseline but cannot be called the parent prognostic comparator.

With the parent’s nominal 75 CRC and 921 non-CRC first-event counts, the case-only model may be computationally feasible but is not necessarily clinically precise. Require at least 30 recorded CRC cases for the locked three-slope association; with 15–29, fit only the prespecified univariate/parsimonious association and report wide uncertainty; with fewer than 15, report case flow and raw trajectory distributions only. These gates concern recorded cases and do not imply coverage validity.

## Partial identification and ascertainment sensitivity

Let `R=1` denote that a true malignant event is present in the rectangular export, and let `Y*` be its true CRC/non-CRC subtype. The observed case-case contrast identifies the distribution conditional on `R=1`, not the distribution among all true cases. It becomes a population case-subtype contrast only under a strong, unverified assumption that ascertainment is independent of trajectory conditional on subtype and measured covariates, or under a validated selection model.

Therefore report two layers:

* **Always permissible without a coverage certificate:** the exact conditional statement about recorded cases, including the recorded-case flow, trajectory distributions, lag-stratum composition, and the held-out case-case contrast. No absent row is assigned `Y=0`.
* **Partial-identification sensitivity:** if an external linkage audit can bound the ratio of recording probabilities for two cases with the same measured covariates and trajectory by `Gamma`, refit weighted case-case contrasts over a prespecified grid `Gamma in {1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 3, 5, infinity}`. `Gamma=1` is the exchangeable-recording assumption; `Gamma=infinity` is the no-assumption limit. Report the range of the trajectory contrast, not a selected favorable value. The bound must be justified by an actual release-matched audit; it must not be inferred from the maximum date, the number of blank cells, or national incidence.

Without any ascertainment audit, the population case-case contrast has only the trivial no-assumption bounds; no clinically meaningful population claim is identified. This is a useful negative result because it determines exactly what an external audit must establish. A valid audit should contain, by nation and release, the number of true cancer cases, numbers linkable into this export, dates of coverage and update lag, and whether linkage probability varies by cancer type, death, age, sex, or CBC-related clinical contact. If those quantities are unavailable, retain only the recorded-case conditional claim.

## Why valid controls cannot be sampled absent coverage

Risk-set controls sampled at each CRC diagnosis time would need to be demonstrably alive, eligible, cancer-free through that time, and observable for the relevant registry and death endpoints. In this export, an absent 40005/40006 record does not establish any of those conditions. Consequently, neither incidence-density sampling nor a nested case-control hazard-ratio analysis is valid without the parent’s release-matched coverage certificate (or participant-level coverage indicators plus a validated censoring model). Sampling controls from the rows with no recorded event would create unobserved non-events and can induce immortal-time and differential ascertainment bias. The event-anchored case-only branch avoids this invalid step by conditioning on observed cases, while accepting the narrower estimand and possible case-selection bias.

## Falsification criteria and audits

All of the following are frozen before fitting:

1. Permute CRC/non-CRC labels within exact lag, sex, and five-year age strata. The trajectory increment should be centered at the null; systematic retained signal indicates leakage, coding imbalance, or implementation error.
2. Use an impossible temporal exposure check: replace the two CBC slopes with a post-`L` value if any is available in the specified fields (there is no permitted post-`L` CBC field in the primary branch). Any apparent result from an outcome-derived or post-diagnosis value fails the audit.
3. Run a negative-control case subtype contrast using a malignant non-CRC subset split by a prespecified code-family grouping that is not CRC. A large effect only for an arbitrary code split suggests ascertainment or coding artifacts; this is an audit, not a new scientific endpoint.
4. Repeat the case-only analysis with cancer-first rather than death-first same-day handling, with exact duplicate rows retained versus collapsed, and with dotted/undotted code normalization. Material changes must be reported, not resolved by choosing the favorable rule.
5. Verify that all case labels use suffix-identical date/code pairs, that `T=E` is included, that `T=H` is included, and that no code/date after death enters the primary analysis.
6. Compare the lag distribution and CBC distributions between CRC and non-CRC cases before and after fold assignment. A split-dependent difference indicates leakage or broken matching.
7. Repeat the analysis in recorded-history-free cases only versus the all-recorded-case sensitivity set. A result that exists only after including prior recorded malignancy is not evidence for the parent cancer-free landmark question.

## Interpretation matrix

**Supportive for a recorded-case CRC-specific signal:** the trajectory model has a prespecified adverse-direction contrast favoring CRC over non-CRC with uncertainty excluding the case-composition null, the contrast persists under lag-stratum standardization and reasonable ascertainment-sensitivity values supported by an audit, the shortest-lag CRC stratum shows the prespecified adverse gradient, and all permutation, suffix, boundary, death-ordering, and fold-isolation checks pass. The conclusion remains: CBC trajectory is associated with subtype and timing among recorded cases. It does not establish population CRC risk or clinical utility.

**Adverse:** the trajectory contrast is precisely null or favors non-CRC, and the lag gradient is absent or opposite with clean falsification checks. This argues against a CRC-specific signal in this sparse protocolized case set, but does not refute clinically ordered CBC models or a signal requiring denser tests.

**Inconclusive:** fewer than the event gate, broad/unstable intervals, strong dependence on same-day or code parsing rules, failed permutation tests, no ascertainment audit for any broader claim, or a trajectory contrast that reverses over plausible `Gamma` values. An absent coverage certificate is not itself a failed computation; it limits the claim to the recorded-case conditional layer.

## Claim limits and required next evidence

Computation can verify row counts, uniqueness, joins, exact suffix pairing, field transformations, case labels, timing, split isolation, case-case contrasts, and ascertainment-sensitivity calculations. It cannot verify that recorded cancer cases are complete, that a missing event means no event, that registry dates are incident rather than corrected, that same-day ordering is known, or that CBC changes are clinically actionable.

The branch cannot establish screening benefit, FIT or colonoscopy referral benefit, stage shift, treatment benefit, mortality benefit, harms, cost-effectiveness, individual thresholds, or transportability outside selected UKB repeat attenders. Those require release-matched linkage documentation, clinical adjudication of field units and cancer dates, external validation in clinically ordered CBC data, and a prospective impact study. The narrowest additional evidence needed to upgrade beyond the recorded-case claim is a release-matched cancer/death linkage certificate; to make an unselected population case-case claim under partial identification, an audit must additionally quantify subtype- and covariate-specific recording probabilities or defensible bounds on their ratios.
