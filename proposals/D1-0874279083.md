# Scientific Proposal: Discordant Organ Recovery Signatures Predict ICU Deterioration

## Unresolved Question

**Clinical Problem:** In critically ill ICU patients, current risk stratification relies heavily on single-organ biomarkers and static multi-organ scores. However, patients with identical baseline severity and single-organ trajectories exhibit vastly different clinical outcomes. Recent research demonstrates that recovery is often "discordant" - one organ system improves while another deteriorates (e.g., creatinine normalizes while urine output remains poor, or respiratory parameters improve while hemodynamics deteriorate).

**Key Uncertainty:** Does a *discordant recovery pattern* across multiple organ systems in the first 72 hours post-ICU admission predict subsequent ICU deterioration (death, organ support escalation, ICU readmission) better than single-organ trajectories or static multi-organ scores?

## Hypothesis

Patients who exhibit a discordant recovery pattern across multiple organ systems (e.g., improving kidney function with deteriorating respiratory mechanics, or vice versa) in the first 72 hours post-ICU admission are at significantly higher risk of subsequent deterioration than patients with concordant recovery patterns or single-organ deterioration, even when controlling for admission severity.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Improved Risk Stratification:** Identifying patients at high risk of deterioration based on discordant recovery patterns could enable earlier intervention and resource allocation.

2. **Treatment Personalization:** Discordant patterns may identify subgroups that respond differently to interventions (e.g., patients with discordant kidney-respiratory patterns may need combined organ support strategies).

3. **Recovery Monitoring:** Current practice monitors organs in isolation; discordance signatures could guide when to switch from single-organ to multi-system assessment.

4. **Outcomes Prediction:** Better prediction of ICU outcomes could improve shared decision-making with patients and families.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

**Beyond Single-Organ Models:** Existing models like SOFA, APACHE II, and MELD focus on single-time-point assessments or aggregate scores that mask discordant patterns. This proposal tests whether the *pattern of discordance* (not just the presence of dysfunction) contains prognostic information.

**Beyond Single-Organ Trajectories:** While trajectory modeling has been explored, most studies analyze one organ system at a time. This proposal tests whether *cross-organ discordance* - the specific pattern of one organ improving while another deteriorates - captures information that single-organ trajectories miss.

**Beyond Static Multi-Organ Scores:** Traditional multi-organ scores (SOFA, qSOFA) aggregate organ dysfunction but do not capture the *temporal dynamics* of recovery or deterioration across systems. This proposal uses time-varying trajectory modeling to capture recovery patterns.

**Temporal Boundaries:**

- **Follow-up:** In-hospital outcome within 24 hours of discharge or death

**Discordance Definition:**
A discordant pattern is defined as:
- Organ A showing improvement (e.g., creatinine decreasing by >20% from nadir) while Organ B showing deterioration (e.g., FiO2 increasing by >10% from baseline, or MAP decreasing by >10% from nadir)
- OR Organ A stable while Organ B deteriorating
- Quantified as the *directional discordance* (sign difference in % change) and *magnitude discordance* (absolute difference in % change)

## Methodology

### Baseline Models
1. **Cox proportional hazards regression** with time-varying covariates for:
   - Single-organ trajectories (e.g., creatinine trajectory only)
   - Static multi-organ scores (SOFA at admission, calculated from baseline labs)
   - Best-performing single-organ trajectory

### Substantive Alternative
**Time-varying discordance signature model:**
1. For each ICU stay with complete data:
   - Extract trajectory for each organ system over 72 hours
   - Compute directional discordance (sign of % change difference) and magnitude discordance
   - Classify as concordant (all organs improving or deteriorating) or discordant (mixed patterns)
2. Use discordance signature as time-varying covariate in Cox model
3. Compare AUC, C-index, and calibration against baseline models

### Analysis Plan
- **Primary outcome:** In-hospital mortality
- **Secondary outcomes:** ICU readmission, RRT initiation, time to vasopressor-free status
- **Covariates:** Age, sex, comorbidities (from hosp/patients), admission severity (APACHE II from hosp/admissions)
- **Confounding adjustment:** Propensity score weighting for organ support intensity
- **Multiplicity:** False discovery rate correction for multiple outcomes

### Falsification Criteria
The hypothesis is falsified if:
- Discordance signature does not significantly improve prediction (ΔAUC < 0.02)
- Discordance effect is consistent with random chance (p > 0.05 after FDR correction)
- Discordance pattern shows no temporal consistency (effect varies by time window)

## Limitations and Unresolved Questions

1. **Data Completeness:** MIMIC-IV may have missing urine output data; need to verify completeness and handle missingness appropriately
2. **Causality:** Observational design cannot establish causation; discordance may be marker rather than cause
3. **Temporal Resolution:** 72-hour window may miss late-phase discordance (days 15-30); should consider multi-window analysis
4. **Organ Selection:** Limited to routinely measured parameters; may miss important discordances (e.g., cardiac, neurological)
5. **External Validation:** Need validation in independent ICU cohort
6. **Clinical Actionability:** Need to determine if discordance patterns can be used to guide clinical decision-making
