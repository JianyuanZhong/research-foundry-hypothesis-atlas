# Provisional HCC early-trajectory hypothesis (episode 3)

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with a documented HCC diagnosis and an inpatient encounter in the HCC snapshot, a prespecified 7-day trajectory of liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker laboratory measurements may add information about subsequent inpatient escalation beyond an admission-baseline laboratory snapshot, age/sex, admission department, treatment/procedure indicators, and documentation intensity. The direction and magnitude are not assumed.

This is a hypothesis, not an observed result. The proposed outcome is an escalation proxy, not validated death, ICU admission, or clinical harm.

## Unresolved evidence and limitations

The exact index-cohort rule, assay-to-clinical-domain mapping, laboratory units, event support, and whether procedure/transfer signals can be used as an interpretable escalation proxy remain to be audited. The source has no validated death or ICU outcome. Clinical documents may support adjudication but lexical extraction is not a validated diagnosis model. No fitted result, effect size, or causal relationship has been established.

## Falsifiable test to be specified

Compare a simple admission snapshot baseline with a time-aware representation of measurements from admission through day 7, using a prespecified held-out split and uncertainty estimates. The temporal model is supportive only if it improves held-out prediction of the escalation proxy beyond the baseline and the improvement is not explained by documentation intensity or treatment/procedure timing alone. Failure to improve, instability across prespecified assay domains, or inability to define a defensible outcome would be adverse or inconclusive, respectively. Clinical interpretation requires expert review or additional validated outcome data.
