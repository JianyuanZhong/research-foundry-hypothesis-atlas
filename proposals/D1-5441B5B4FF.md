# Does the laboratory-observation increment travel across hospital surveillance regimes?

Status: Episode-30 substantive, targeted successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It preserves the parent’s adult first-stay cohort, ICU-relative minute-360 landmark, revision-safe laboratory information rule, competing recorded ICU-death versus alive ICU-discharge outcome through minute 1,800, whole-hospital evaluation, exact equal-review-burden policy comparison, uncertainty, support gates and clinical evidence limits. Its nontrivial advance is an outcome-free hospital-surveillance-regime transport test: it asks whether the laboratory-process increment survives not only patient-level aggregate nursing/respiratory adjustment, but also transport into hospitals whose overall surveillance behavior is different.

No model has been fitted in this discovery episode, and no clinical benefit, physiologic-truth, clinician-intent, causal, or external-validation claim is made.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The parent establishes the right immediate comparison: laboratory observation process after recorded laboratory/vital state, vital-observation intensity, and aggregate nursing/respiratory documentation. A remaining uncertainty is at the hospital level. Two hospitals can have patients with the same individual documentation counts but different surveillance regimes: one may record many laboratory observations and sparse bedside charting, another may record dense nursing and respiratory data with fewer laboratory results, and their interfaces may differ in timestamp and revision practice. A pooled laboratory increment could therefore be a local surveillance convention that appears patient-linked within hospitals but does not transport to a hospital with another observation regime.

The strongest available evidence is outcome-blind feasibility and schema evidence, not a result. The parent reports 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows known by minute 360. Those inherited counts support a bounded whole-hospital experiment; they do not establish that a laboratory result reflects physiology, clinician review, intent, or benefit.

The unresolved claim is:

> At the fixed six-hour landmark, the laboratory-observation increment is patient-linked and transportable across outcome-free hospital surveillance regimes, rather than an artifact of hospital-wide testing, charting, interface, or observation intensity.

This matters clinically because a retrospective risk-review score that works only under one hospital’s documentation regime should not be treated as a portable monitoring aid. Conversely, a laboratory increment that remains directionally useful at the same review burden in low-, intermediate-, and high-surveillance hospitals would justify a more serious external and prospective evaluation. Either result is informative: disappearance identifies a workflow/transport limitation, while persistence narrows (but does not remove) the measurement-process explanation.

## Falsifiable hypothesis

Primary hypothesis:

> In adults reaching ICU-relative minute 360, adding patient-level laboratory-observation process features (`Lp`` to the parent’s documentation-adjusted baseline (`Bd`` improves held-out-hospital macro-hospital integrated competing-risk Brier score for recorded ICU death by minute 1,800, and the increment remains favorable in each supported, pre-specified outcome-free hospital surveillance regime. At the same outcome-free, per-held-out-hospital review burden, `BpD` captures at least as many recorded ICU deaths and has at least as high recorded-death PPV as `Bd` in each supported regime without material calibration harm.

The claim is falsified or materially weakened if the `BpD-Bd` increment is null or reversed overall, fails in one or more supported regimes, loses equal-burden capture/PPV in a regime, is explained by regime assignment or site descriptors, or disappears after defensible revision/common-panel controls. A finding that both models degrade in a regime does not by itself refute the laboratory increment; the paired increment, calibration and absolute performance must be reported. Persistence only in one regime is heterogeneity, not general transport.

The temporal extension is a separate hypothesis:

> A compact temporal model using the same six-hour channels adds information beyond the transparent summaries only when its advantage is stable across surveillance regimes, attenuates after six-bin order permutation, and exceeds a parameter-count-matched non-temporal control.

A supportive computation would establish only a retrospective, recorded-outcome, patient-linked risk association under these data rules. It would not establish that testing, reviewing, or changing surveillance improves patient outcomes.

## Population, temporal boundaries, outcome and estimand

Use eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`.

Construct exactly one row per person’s first valid ICU stay from `patient`:

- require numeric `patientunitstayid` and `unitdischargeoffset > 360`;
- retain only stays reaching the ICU-relative minute-360 landmark.

The predictor window is ICU-relative [0, 360] minutes. A laboratory row is eligible only when `labresult` is numeric, `labresultoffset` is in [0, 360], and `labresultrevisedoffset <= 360` or null. Vital and charting rows are eligible only when their event/observation offset is in [0, 360]. The solver must keep result/event time separate from revision/entry time, audit negative or impossible offsets, invalid IDs, duplicate-stay assignment and unexpected discharge values, and never silently remap them. A record not known by minute 360 cannot enter a landmark predictor.

The first post-landmark discharge event represented by the selected patient row is the outcome:

- event of interest: normalized `unitdischargestatus = Expired` with `unitdischargeoffset` in (360, 1800];
- competing event: normalized `unitdischargestatus = Alive` with `unitdischargeoffset` in (360, 1800];
- administrative censoring: neither event through minute 1,800.

Use 24 one-hour discrete-time bins covering (360, 1800] and estimate cause-specific hazards, then derive cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death with alive ICU discharge competing. Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, latent death, and post-ICU outcomes are outside the estimand.

## Outcome-blind patient features

Freeze the codebook and support rules using development hospitals only.

Admission context `A`: `gender`, `ethnicity`, `admissionweight`, `hospitaladmitoffset`, `hospitaladmitsource`, and `unitstaytype`.

Laboratory state `Ls`: predeclared first, last, median, range and slope (or robust equivalent` for supported analytes, with explicit availability masks. Candidate names are potassium, sodium, hemoglobin, hematocrit, glucose, creatinine, BUN, calcium, WBC, platelets, bicarbonate, FiO2, PaO2, PaCO2, pH, anion gap, HCO3, oxygen saturation, magnesium and base excess, subject to observed encoding support. The schema has no separate unit column. A name does not verify unit compatibility; unresolved unit, interface or system support means exclusion or a separately supported stratum. Do not infer unit or physiologic state from `labresulttext`.

Laboratory process `Lp`: total valid rows, distinct analytes, distinct result times, first/last result time, recency, span, inter-result-gap summaries, analyte-specific counts/recency, and revision-known status. No post-landmark row is eligible.

Vital state `Vs`: the same predeclared summaries and masks over supported fields from `vitalPeriodic` and `vitalAperiodic`. Vital intensity `Vd`: periodic and aperiodic row counts, distinct observation times, last time, span and six-bin coverage. It is an ascertainment control, not complete physiology or monitoring.

Documentation intensity `D`: for nurse and respiratory charting separately and together, row counts, distinct event time counts, first/last event time, span, six-bin coverage, and audited entry-lag summaries (`entry offset - event offset``. Use all rows regardless of numeric value. Primary `D` excludes chart values, label text and category/type names; these timestamps are documentation metadata, not review, action, intent, physiology or treatment response. Category/type groupings, if used, are a development-frozen workflow diagnostic only.

## Outcome-free surveillance regimes

The regime is a hospital-level evaluation context, not a patient predictor.

For each hospital and outer fold, form a predictor-only fingerprint from the eligible first-stay episodes’ [0,360] windows. Use robust, log1p-transformed per-episode hospital summaries for:

1. laboratory observation: median valid-row count, median distinct result-time count, and fraction with any revision-safe laboratory result;
2. vital observation: median periodic and aperiodic row counts, median distinct observation-time counts, and six-bin coverage;
3. nursing/respiratory documentation: median row counts and event-time counts separately and combined, six-bin coverage, and median valid entry lag.

Do not use `unitdischargestatus`, discharge time, death, alive discharge, post-landmark data or hospital descriptors to construct the fingerprint. A hospital’s eligible episode count is used only for the support gate and optional weighting, not a regime feature.

Within each outer training set, standardize these fingerprint components using training hospitals only and fit deterministic three-means clustering with fixed seed, multiple fixed starts, and a frozen tie rule. Order the resulting regimes by the training-centroid value of the total standardized surveillance-intensity score (low, intermediate, high); the labels have no clinical meaning beyond this ordering. Assign each held-out hospital to its nearest training centroid using its predictor-only fingerprint. This is label-free transductive assignment: no held-out outcome, calibration, threshold, or model refit may influence it. Save the fingerprint, centroids, assignment distance and support audit.

The primary hospital-held-out analysis reports `BpD-Bd` separately within each assigned regime. A pre-specified transport stress test is regime-held-out: for each regime with adequate support, fit the paired models on development hospitals in the other regimes and evaluate on hospitals assigned to the omitted regime. This does not claim independent external validation; it tests zero-label adaptation to a distinct observed surveillance environment. No regime label enters `Bd`, `BpD`, `T0` or `T1` in the primary scores, so a regime result cannot be produced merely by giving the model a site proxy.

As an outcome-free sensitivity, repeat regime assignment using only `Vd+D` components (excluding `Lp` and report whether conclusions depend on defining the environment partly by laboratory intensity. This sensitivity is frozen before outcomes are inspected and is not a license to select the most favorable regime definition.

## Transparent baseline and substantive learned alternative

Fit pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards with identical episodes, preprocessing, hyperparameter selection, grouped folds and 24 labels:

- `Bv = A + Ls + Vs`;
- `BvV = Bv + Vd`;
- `Bd = BvV + D`;
- `BpV = BvV + Lp`;
- `BpD = Bd + Lp`.

The primary scientific contrast is `BpD-Bd`, evaluated overall, by surveillance regime, and in regime-held-out stress tests. `BpV-BvV` is retained as the parent/intermediate contrast. Regime fingerprints are not patient covariates in these primary models. A secondary descriptive model may estimate regime-by-`Lp` interactions only if every regime passes support; its purpose is heterogeneity estimation, not the primary policy score.

The same-input learned alternative is two identical compact one-layer GRUs: `T0` receives the channels corresponding to `Bd`, and `T1` receives exactly `T0` plus `Lp). Use six fixed half-open bins [0,60), [60,120), [120,180), [180,240), [240,300), [300,360); records at exactly 360 belong to landmark summaries but not a sequence bin unless the convention is frozen before fitting. Channels include laboratory state/masks, vital state/masks, vital intensity, aggregate D count/coverage/entry-lag, and (for T1` Lp. Concatenate `A` after the sequence. Use hidden size 32, one layer, dropout 0.1 and development-only early stopping; both models have death and alive-discharge heads for the same 24 post-landmark bins and no hospital ID or regime label.

The parent summaries intentionally lose within-window order, persistence and co-movement. The GRUs can reveal whether the laboratory increment depends on a late/early sequence, persistence, or joint documentation trajectory rather than on aggregate quantity. A temporal result is substantive only with regime-stable calibration or equal-burden policy evidence, attenuation under order permutation, and improvement over a parameter-count-matched non-temporal model with the same channels and labels. A small AUROC difference alone is not a temporal or clinical result.

## Hospital splits, uncertainty and equal review burden

Use five grouped outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once. Keep all stays for a `uniquepid` together and stratify by eligible episodes and recorded deaths where feasible. No hospital is split across training and evaluation. Codebooks, valid ranges, revision rules, transformations, imputation, scaling, regularization, clustering centroids, temporal hyperparameters and policy definitions are frozen from development data. The held-out fingerprint may be used only for outcome-free regime assignment and evaluation stratification.

The primary performance estimand is the macro-hospital paired difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score over (360,1800] for `BpD` versus `Bd`, with lower Brier favorable. Secondary outputs are CIF at minute 1,800, time-specific Brier, calibration slope/intercept or observed-versus-predicted summaries, `BpV-BvV`, `T1-T0`, per-regime contrasts, and regime-held-out contrasts. Report every hospital and fold, pooled values only alongside macro-hospital values, and 95% hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals with at least 1,000 resamples if feasible. Preserve multiplicity across all predeclared contrasts; do not select the conclusion from whichever metric is favorable.

For each held-out hospital h and q in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, define `Bd`’s cumulative-incidence review policy as episodes with `Bd) risk at least q. Let k(h,q` be its alert count. Rank exactly the same held-out episodes by `BpD`, `BpV`, `T1) and controls and select exactly the top k(h,q), using deterministic ties. No held-out outcome is used. If k(h,q`=0, the cell has no PPV or capture assignment. Report review fraction, recorded-death capture, recorded-death PPV, calibration among reviewed and non-reviewed episodes, and incremental membership relative to `Bd`, overall and by regime. Macro-hospital paired differences are primary; pooled values are secondary. This is a label-relative retrospective review comparison, not net clinical benefit.

Implementation-oriented interpretation requires at least 20 evaluation hospitals, at least 10 alerts per evaluation hospital on average, at least 100 alerted episodes, and at least 20 recorded deaths across supported held-out cells for the reported q. For a regime-specific or regime-held-out interpretation, require at least 10 evaluation hospitals and 20 recorded deaths in that regime across supported cells, with at least 20 eligible episodes per hospital. Otherwise report the cell as descriptive or inconclusive.

## Locked falsifications and robustness

1. Permute `Lp` within each held-out hospital, stratified by held-out `Bd`-risk quintile, vital-coverage stratum and D-coverage stratum, holding `Ls`, `Vs`, `Vd` and D fixed. A patient-linked increment should attenuate; persistence indicates process confounding or an implementation error.
2. Compare `BpV-BvV` with `BpD-Bd`. Disappearance only after D adjustment supports a broader documentation-workflow explanation; it does not prove absence of physiology.
3. Repeat with a strict common analyte panel and revision-known-only rows. Loss under defensible unit/interface/revision rules prevents a robust analyte interpretation.
4. Repeat with only `Vd+D`-based regime fingerprints. Large changes indicate that the apparent transport result is partly a consequence of defining regimes with the tested laboratory process.
5. Perform the regime-held-out stress test. If the increment is favorable only when the target regime is represented in training, the result is interpolation, not transport to a different surveillance regime.
6. Normalize patient `Lp` and D features within hospital using development-defined, outcome-free quantiles and repeat evaluation. A loss suggests absolute local workflow scale rather than portable patient information.
7. Fit documentation-only, vital-intensity-only and hospital-descriptor-only diagnostics. Reproduction by `D`, `Vd`, `numbedscategory`, `teachingstatus`, `region`, interface or one site undermines a general patient-linked interpretation. Hospital descriptors are never added to the primary score.
8. For `T0/T1`, permute six-bin order and compare with parameter-count-matched non-temporal controls. A temporal conclusion requires order attenuation and matched-control separation in supported regimes.
9. Recompute every policy metric with identical per-hospital k(h,q); more alerts cannot create an apparent gain.
10. Report leave-one-hospital-out influence, fold direction, regime direction and per-hospital calibration. A pooled improvement driven by one hospital or one regime is not transport evidence.
11. Shift or permute entry-lag features within held-out documentation strata while retaining event counts. A large change from arbitrary entry metadata marks workflow-sensitive evidence, not clinical timing.
12. Audit regime assignments against held-out labels only after all primary analyses are frozen, as a verifier diagnostic. If labels entered construction, discard the regime claim and preserve only the label-free computation.

## Supportive, adverse and inconclusive interpretations

Supportive evidence requires all relevant gates, a favorable overall macro-hospital `BpD-Bd` Brier contrast with uncertainty excluding no improvement, favorable direction in a prespecified majority of folds, and no material calibration harm. The increment must remain favorable in every supported surveillance regime under the primary regime definition, or any exception must be reported as a falsification of the stated all-regime hypothesis. Equal-burden capture or PPV should improve without increasing review burden. The increment should attenuate under `Lp` permutation, not be reproduced by site/documentation diagnostics, and remain credible under common-panel/revision controls. `T1` supports a temporal extension only after its order and matched-control criteria pass. These results still justify only external validation planning.

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversed overall increment, regime-specific reversal, no equal-burden improvement, calibration harm, persistence under process permutation, reproduction by D or site descriptors, dependence on one interface/analyte family, or loss under defensible revision/common-panel rules. These findings support workflow, interface, revision, missing-physiology or regime-shift explanations and argue against a portable laboratory-process review score.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed or unstable regime support, sparse deaths or policy cells, large assignment distances, nonconvergence, leakage/source-join failure, unstable fold or regime directions, wide intervals spanning clinically important favorable and adverse values, or unsupported temporal fits. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.

## Evidence limits, demonstrations and deferred alternatives

The snapshot lacks validated laboratory order time, specimen-collection time, clinician reason for ordering, human review/acknowledgement, treatment response, independent physiologic adjudication, raw waveform, prospective monitoring policy, patient-centered harm/benefit endpoint and complete care-limitation documentation. `labresultoffset` and `labresultrevisedoffset` are not specimen and review timestamps. Nurse/respiratory entry and event offsets are documentation timestamps. `vitalPeriodic` is a five-minute summary, not a waveform. The laboratory schema has no separate unit column. Structured note/chart fields do not establish intent or clinical meaning.

Thus even supportive computation cannot establish causal benefit, clinician actionability, physiologic truth, hospital quality, or superiority of a monitoring program. Those conclusions require expert analyte/unit adjudication, independent physiologic evidence, external hospital validation, prospective silent-alert testing, and an impact or stepped-wedge study measuring review burden, response, harms and patient-centered outcomes.

## Method comparison, resources and revisit conditions

The simple nested elastic-net hazards are primary because the scientific estimand is a directly auditable increment after explicit documentation adjustment and because coefficient/block inclusion, calibration and hospital-level uncertainty are transparent. They lose detailed within-window order and nonlinear co-movement.

The paired GRUs are retained as a substantive same-input alternative, not as a complexity bonus. They can reveal whether persistence, ordering and joint lab/documentation trajectories carry information hidden by six-hour summaries, while the matched non-temporal and order-shuffled controls test whether that information is genuinely temporal. Both methods use identical episodes, labels, source channels, splits and evaluation metrics. The GRU is conditional on support and convergence; it is not allowed to rescue a failed transparent design.

Measured in this episode: local dataset guide, complete eICU catalog, relevant JSON schemas, source paths/hashes and raw source headers/first rows were inspected; no feature build, model fit, bootstrap, clustering result or clinical analysis was run. Unverified estimates: full-file decompression/feature construction, five-fold elastic-net fitting and permutations may require minutes to hours on CPU depending on implementation; paired GRU training may require minutes to hours on one GPU depending on convergence. These are planning estimates, not measurements.
