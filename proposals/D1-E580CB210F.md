# Episode 8: establish the concurrent pH-conditioned SpO2 blind spot, and falsify an unsupported pre-draw alert

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and advance

At a monitor SpO2 of 96–97%, clinicians may infer that arterial oxygen tension is not markedly excessive. Acidemia shifts the oxygen–hemoglobin dissociation relation, but it remains unresolved whether this creates a clinically material, reproducible failure of that inference across heterogeneous ICUs.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis:** among the exact EICU concurrent observations defined below, the observation-standardized probability of observed PaO2 >120 mm Hg is at least 10 percentage points higher when same-panel pH is <7.35 than when pH is 7.35–7.45. Full support additionally requires a positive acidemic-minus-normal contrast in at least six of eight prespecified exposure/documentation-adequate hospitals.

This is a concurrent measurement association. It is not an effect of changing pH, FiO2, SpO2, or oxygen treatment and is not a deployable pre-blood-gas alert.

The strongest existing evidence supports only a narrower claim. The inspected full-text XML of Delgado et al. (Antioxidants 2026; DOI 10.3390/antiox15020235; PMCID PMC12938712) reports a single tertiary center in 2017–2022, 21,406 adults, and 717,064 paired measurements. Arterial gases were aligned to preceding five-minute SpO2 and FiO2; acidotic states had higher PaO2 at comparable SpO2, SpO2 96–97% generally corresponded to physiologic PaO2, and FiO2 modified hyperoxemia probability. It does not establish the prespecified EICU +10-point contrast, multi-hospital consistency, causality, or treatment benefit.

The substantive episode-8 advance is an honest actionability boundary. The episode-7 child proposed pH sampled 5–60 minutes earlier as a pre-draw trigger, but had no complete-file feasibility evidence and did not operationalize result availability. A no-sampling reconstruction now requires both prior sample time and `labresultrevisedoffset <= index draw`: only 28 target-band rows have an available pH 5–60 minutes earlier (17 acidemic, 7 normal, 4 alkalemic), from 23 stays and six hospitals; no hospital has at least 5 acidemic and 10 normal observations. The acidemic/normal cohort is 24, not the proposed minimum 200. Therefore no lagged inferential model is permitted. The available EICU data can resolve the concurrent measurement/transportability question but cannot resolve clinical utility of a prior-pH alert.

## Variables, estimand, baselines, and analysis

Exposure is same-panel acidemia (pH <7.35) versus normal pH (7.35–7.45). Primary outcome is observed PaO2 >120 mm Hg. Secondary outcomes are PaO2 >150, continuous PaO2, and PaO2 <60; the last describes an arterial-gas state, not tissue hypoxia.

The primary estimand is the observation-weighted marginal standardized risk difference among all 705 rows. Fit B2; set acidemia to 1 and 0 for each row while preserving observed FiO2, median SpO2, PaCO2/missingness, age, sex, unit type, admission source, and six-hour block; average predictions over all rows and subtract normal from acidemic. This is descriptive g-computation, not a causal intervention.

Fixed baselines and model are:

- B0: intercept plus median SpO2 centered at 96.5.
- B1: B0 plus linear FiO2 per 10 points, age per decade, sex, unit type, unit admission source, six-hour block 0–11, PaCO2 per 10 mm Hg, and PaCO2-missing indicator.
- B2: B1 plus acidemia and acidemia × linear FiO2.

Categorical missingness is a level. Collapse levels with fewer than 10 primary rows to Other using predictor counts before PaO2 inspection. No hospital indicator enters B0–B2. The fixed functional-form sensitivity uses FiO2 bands 21–39, 40–59, and 60–100.

Fit population-average logistic GEE with exchangeable correlation within `uniquepid`. Estimate standardized RD and a two-sided 95% interval with 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples. Carry every patient/row in a sampled hospital and assign copied hospital and person clusters new IDs. Report convergence and failed fits.

Repeated-observation sensitivities are earliest eligible observation per stay, equal-stay weighting, and earliest draw per stay-specific six-hour block. Other fixed sensitivities are first 24 hours; last rather than median SpO2; 60-minute FiO2 lookback; chart-offset FiO2 without entry restriction; alternate FiO2 labels separately; complete PaCO2; fixed FiO2 bands; closed nonzero ventilation intervals; broader airway proxy; and deletion of each adequate hospital. APACHE variables are QC only because they lack event timestamps.

For missing linkage, model retention of strict FiO2 and SpO2 among the 20,946 probable-airway panels using patient/hospital descriptors, draw block, and pre-draw chart availability—never PaO2 or post-draw data. Refit B2 with stabilized inverse-linkage weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles. This sensitivity depends on an unverifiable missing-at-random assumption.

## Hospital consistency and portability

For each of the eight frozen sites, report group denominators, PaO2 >120 events, and unadjusted RD with a `uniquepid` cluster-bootstrap interval. No site disappears for zero events, separation, or an unfavorable result. Full transportability requires positive point estimates in at least six of eight. Pool empirical RDs by random-effects meta-analysis and report tau-squared, I-squared, and a 95% prediction interval.

Separately perform leave-one-hospital-out evaluation on the same eight sites without hospital ID or hospital-derived features. Pool held-out predictions and report calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, log loss, AUROC/AUPRC, and B2–B1 changes. B2 is not materially worse only if Brier increase <=0.01, log-loss increase <=0.02, and calibration slope is 0.8–1.2. This tests portability of a same-panel measurement model, not a deployable alert.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive for the pooled +10-point hypothesis** requires the B2 standardized RD lower 95% bound >=+10 points. A point estimate >=+10 with a lower bound below +10 is not enough. **Full support including transportability** additionally requires at least six positive site RDs, no adequate-site deletion reversing direction, a heterogeneity prediction interval not extending to <=-5, and passing held-out limits. The strongest allowed conclusion is that a clinically material concurrent pH-conditioned measurement blind spot is reproducible enough to justify prospective synchronized validation.

**Adverse to material magnitude** means the upper 95% bound is <+10, even if a smaller positive association exists. An upper bound <0 supports an opposite association. Fewer than six positive sites is adverse to transportability, not proof of no pooled association. A precise +6-point RD is adverse to +10 but supports a smaller association.

**Inconclusive** means the interval includes +10; more than 10% of bootstrap fits fail; strict linkage or overlap is inadequate; the prediction interval spans <=-5 and >=+10; or direction reverses under entry timing, ventilation, weighting, repeated-observation, or leave-site-out analyses. Pooled support with failed site criteria must be reported as pooled concurrent support with transportability adverse/inconclusive.

The pre-draw alert branch is already **computationally infeasible/inconclusive in this snapshot**, not adverse to biology: 24 eligible 5–60 minute acidemic/normal rows cannot test its +10-point effect or hospital transportability. Concurrent support can never rescue or validate that lagged alert.

Secondary outcomes cannot rescue a failed primary hypothesis. A wide -2 to +22 interval is inconclusive, not supportive.
