The unresolved clinical question remains whether baseline cystatin-C unexpectedly high relative to creatinine is associated with later creatinine after conditioning on baseline creatinine, and reciprocally whether unexpectedly high creatinine is associated with later cystatin-C. Discordance can affect CKD staging, drug dosing, monitoring, and referral, but two protocol measurements per marker cannot establish measured GFR decline, CKD progression, mechanism, causality, or benefit from testing. The computable advance is therefore deliberately narrow: quantify cross-marker persistence in a large, exactly audited repeat-attender cohort, then determine whether the observed pattern is specific enough to justify stronger follow-up.

The tested hypothesis is an adjusted cross-lag association in repeat attenders. The strongest claim supported by this execution is weaker than renal specificity because both nonrenal diagnostic controls were also associated.

## Frozen estimands and the analysis actually executed

For eligible participants, all four renal measurements were required to be finite and positive and were natural-log transformed. P1 exposure was the full-cohort standardized residual from fixed-core OLS of baseline log cystatin-C on baseline log creatinine, centered `21022-0.0` age, sex, and baseline-center indicators. P1 was the exposure coefficient in fixed-core OLS for repeat log creatinine conditional on baseline log creatinine, centered age, sex, elapsed years, changed-center indicator, and the residual exposure.

P2 was symmetric: the standardized full-cohort residual from baseline log creatinine on baseline log cystatin-C, age, sex, and baseline-center indicators was tested in fixed-core OLS for repeat log cystatin-C conditional on baseline log cystatin-C, age, sex, elapsed years, and changed-center indicator.

The executed outcome regressions did **not** include baseline-center fixed effects; only exposure residualization included them, while outcome models included a changed-center indicator. Reported standard errors are classical homoskedastic OLS standard errors and intervals are `coefficient ± 1.96 SE`. Thus these outputs are not heteroskedasticity-robust or center-clustered estimates. Coefficients are log-outcome differences per one full-cohort SD of the residual exposure; they are not outcome-standardized effect sizes.

For each control, the already estimated renal residual exposure was tested among eligible renal-cohort participants with positive paired control values. Repeat log LDL was conditioned on baseline log LDL and the same outcome-model core covariates; repeat log vitamin D was treated analogously. These are diagnostic controls on different complete-case subsets, not matched-sample reruns of P1 and not guaranteed biological nulls.

## Executed outputs and interpretation

Managed job `[research job]` succeeded. Its aggregate output is `[internal dataset path]`, [source checksum].

* P1, baseline cystatin-C residual to repeat creatinine: `n=16,546`, coefficient `0.0172105`, model-based SE `0.0008907`, model-based 95% CI `[0.0154647, 0.0189563]`.
* P2, baseline creatinine residual to repeat cystatin-C: `n=16,546`, coefficient `0.0064249`, model-based SE `0.0006965`, model-based 95% CI `[0.0050598, 0.0077899]`.
* LDL control: `n=16,467`, coefficient `-0.0116023`, model-based 95% CI `[-0.0145956, -0.0086089]`.
* Vitamin-D control: `n=15,357`, coefficient `-0.0237543`, model-based 95% CI `[-0.0301731, -0.0173354]`.

The executed fixed-core OLS therefore found positive cross-lag associations in both directions. P1 is statistically separated from zero by the model-based interval, but its coefficient `0.0172` is below the proposal’s provisional `0.05` reporting threshold. Because the outcome was not standardized, that threshold is interpreted here on the proposal’s stated log-outcome coefficient scale; no clinical-importance claim follows. More importantly, both LDL and vitamin-D controls were associated with the same renal discordance exposure; vitamin D had a larger absolute coefficient than P1 and LDL was also nonzero. Signs and scales across different biomarkers do not support a simple magnitude equivalence claim, but these results are adverse to renal specificity and prohibit interpreting P1/P2 as a specific persistent renal signal. The strongest supported conclusion is: in this selected repeat-attender cohort, baseline cross-marker discordance was associated with later values of the other protocol renal biomarker under a parsimonious fixed-core OLS model, while simultaneous nonrenal associations leave shared health, selection, center, season, medication, and other non-GFR pathways unresolved.

This is not evidence of measured GFR decline, CKD progression, renal pathology, causality, diagnostic validity, drug-dosing benefit, cystatin-C ordering benefit, or generalizability beyond UKB repeat attenders.

## Preserved falsification and decision rules

For a future full prespecified execution, support for the narrow associational claim requires positive P1 uncertainty excluding zero, reasonable center/interval robustness, no material date/placebo failure, and no gross selection explanation. A result below `0.05` is detectable but scientifically small and not clinically actionable. Concordant P2 may support cross-marker persistence, but a shared renal-signal interpretation additionally requires materially weaker diagnostic controls.

Adverse evidence includes a null or negative P1 precise enough to exclude `0.05`, an equal/larger diagnostic-control association, a center-specific estimate, a failed date placebo, or material instability. On currently executed evidence, the associated LDL and vitamin-D controls are already adverse to specificity. This does not erase the estimated cross-lag coefficients; it limits their interpretation.

Inconclusive evidence includes wide uncertainty, inadequate paired cohort, severe center/interval nonpositivity, uncertified assay identity/range, sensitivity to unexecuted robustness analyses, or dependence of latent interpretation on unidentified reliability/error assumptions. Neither adverse nor inconclusive findings prove absence of biological progression.

## What would require additional evidence

Renal specificity requires at minimum the unexecuted matched-sample controls and center/date/attendance sensitivities, richer certified non-GFR covariates, and external replication. True kidney-function decline requires measured GFR or adjudicated renal outcomes and preferably additional waves or external reliability information. Clinical staging, medication decisions, ordering benefit, and prospective utility require expert clinical adjudication and a separate validation or impact study. An automatic computational verifier can check source bindings, cohort counts, model formulas, and aggregate arithmetic; it cannot establish assay semantics not certified locally, renal mechanism, causal validity, clinical importance, or benefit.
