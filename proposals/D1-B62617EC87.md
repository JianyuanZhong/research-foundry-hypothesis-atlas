# Compiler-ready within-stay temporal-dependence and exposure-persistence stress test for the frozen eICU respiratory-label trajectory

## Question, evidence boundary, and substantive advance

This targeted child advances the assigned assessed-valid lineage `[prior hypothesis]`, `[prior hypothesis]`, and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the Lead-frozen scientific schema exactly: 540 person-landmarks, 443 unique first qualifying ICU stays/persons, 74 hospitals; the inherited first qualifying stay and pre-landmark exclusions; landmarks `L ∈ {1440, 2880, 4320, 5760, 7200}` minutes after ICU admission; the first exact target label in `[L,L+360]`; `D = cplitemoffset`; the exact two care-plan strings; fixed B/E/T respiratory windows, parser rules and raw thresholds; the mutually exclusive five-state all-row outcome; repeated landmarks clustered by `patientunitstayid`; and a descriptive, noncausal estimand.

The strongest available evidence supports only that the literal labels are sparse, hospital-concentrated structured documentation and that prior chart-proxy associations are unstable. It does not establish that either label denotes an SBT, extubation evaluation, clinician intent, delivered setting, readiness, treatment, benefit, harm, quality, or causal intervention. The unresolved executable claim is narrower: does exact-label information about a later independent respiratory-chart trajectory survive not only hospital/documentation-opportunity audits, but also the dependence and temporal-persistence structure created when multiple landmarks from one ICU stay share care-plan documentation and respiratory measurement history?

The substantive advance is a predeclared **within-stay temporal-dependence and exposure-persistence layer** that does not change the frozen primary cohort or endpoint. Existing hospital-held-out metrics and stay-cluster uncertainty prevent random-row leakage and understate no independence, but they do not reveal whether a pooled label increment is driven by long stays contributing many landmarks, labels persisting across adjacent landmarks, or a label at one landmark being a near-duplicate of another label in the same stay. This child therefore adds:

1. an all-row, row-preserving audit of label persistence, inter-label timing, landmark spacing, and repeated-label transitions within each `patientunitstayid`;
2. a prespecified stay-balanced descriptive sensitivity selecting one qualifying landmark per stay by a deterministic rule, while retaining all 540 rows as primary;
3. a leave-one-landmark-block-out temporal sensitivity in which all landmarks from a stay remain together and the model cannot use another landmark from the same stay as training evidence;
4. a within-hospital-by-landmark, stay-level label permutation that preserves the number of labelled stays rather than treating repeated landmarks as independent; and
5. a stricter gate requiring any purported patient-state increment to survive the persistence audit, the stay-balanced sensitivity, and a temporal-holdout analysis, or else be reported as stay/documentation persistence information or inconclusive.

These additions test a clinically relevant residual threat—whether the label reflects a durable episode-level charting regime rather than information newly available at a particular decision time—without introducing a new clinical schema, changing the primary endpoint, or fabricating outcomes.

## New exposure-persistence and within-stay temporal layer

### 1. Exposure provenance and persistence audit

Using only the frozen action-row artifact and the exposure-verification fields from `carePlanGeneral`, create one audit row per frozen landmark and one stay-level audit row. The audit must report:

* exact `patientunitstayid`, `uniquepid`, `hospitalid`, landmark `L`, `D`, label, and source care-plan identifier where available;
* the number and timestamps of all exact-target labels in the inherited eligible interval `[L,L+360]`, including whether the chosen row is the unique first target label or whether same-time contradictory targets were excluded under the inherited rule;
* previous and next frozen landmark within the same stay, their exact `D` differences, labels, and whether the same label persists, switches, or becomes absent;
* time from the current `D` to the nearest prior and next exact target-label row in `carePlanGeneral`, restricted to the inherited target strings and explicitly separated from the selected action row;
* whether the current label is the first target label in the stay, follows a switch, or repeats a prior target label; and
* the count of labelled landmarks per stay, maximum and median landmark spacing, and whether any action-row label is reused at an identical `D`.

The audit is descriptive and cannot revise the frozen exposure. It must not infer that a repeated label is a persistent clinical state: it is only persistence of a literal structured record. If the action artifact does not retain sufficient neighbouring-label provenance, report that field unavailable and do not recover it by using post-outcome fields.

### 2. Stay-balanced descriptive sensitivity

Retain all 540 rows and the primary `p_k(a)` as the prespecified result. Add a fixed stay-balanced sensitivity using exactly one frozen landmark per `patientunitstayid`: choose the earliest landmark among the five with a valid frozen action row; break ties by smaller `L`, then smaller `D`, then stable artifact row order. No endpoint availability, post-D value, outcome status, label direction, hospital support, or effect estimate may enter this selection. Report the selected landmark count, arm counts, hospitals, all five K states, and attrition from the 540-row primary table.

Compute the same descriptive all-row vector, conditional classifiable display, M0/M1 held-out metrics, opportunity diagnostics, and endpoint sensitivities on this stay-balanced subset. This is a robustness estimand about an episode-weighted subset, not a replacement cohort and not a causal effect. If fewer than 50% of unique stays have a valid selected landmark after artifact verification, the sensitivity is unavailable/inconclusive; never fill missing stays or switch to an outcome-selected landmark. The primary all-row results remain reportable if the subset is unavailable.

For transparency, also report a purely descriptive landmark-weighted comparison: each stay contributes total weight one divided equally among its valid frozen landmarks, and all five landmarks remain in the calculation. This weighted summary is not used to select rows, fit the primary model, or alter uncertainty clustering.

### 3. Temporal holdout and no-cross-landmark leakage

In addition to leave-one-hospital-out, run a prespecified grouped temporal sensitivity. Partition stays, not rows, into five deterministic folds by the hash of `patientunitstayid` concatenated with fixed seed `61061`, stratified only as feasible by hospital and the frozen landmark set; all landmarks from a stay must remain in one fold. Within each fold, fit M0 and M1 on the other stays, with all preprocessing and calibration training-only, and report held-out predictions for all five K states. Hospital is not a patient predictor and held-out target-rate calibration remains prohibited.

This is a sensitivity, not a new primary transport estimand. Its purpose is to test whether an increment persists when no landmark from the same stay appears in the training partition. Report the exact fold assignment manifest, stay/row counts, label counts, landmark counts, hospital overlap, class support, exit/unobservability counts, and whether each loss contrast is estimable. If a fold has inadequate support, report its all-row metrics where calculable but no fabricated arm direction. Compare paired M1-M0 micro and equal-fold macro log loss/Brier score with the identical M0 preprocessing. Repeated landmarks must never be split across folds.

The sensitivity is computationally interpretable only as a grouped out-of-sample chart-construct test. It cannot establish prospective performance or bedside usefulness.

### 4. Stay-level label permutation null

Retain the parent within-hospital-by-landmark row-level label permutation, but add a stay-level null. Construct strata using only hospital and landmark `L`; within each stratum, permute the label assignment among eligible **stays**, carrying the permuted label to all of that stay's frozen landmark rows. A stay is eligible only if it contributes at least one frozen action row in that stratum and the permutation preserves the exact number of evaluation and no-trial stays. Repeated landmarks and their dependence are thereby preserved; no row-level relabelling is allowed in this null.

Run the same complete prediction/contrast pipeline for a fixed predeclared number of permutations, record attempted, valid, failed, and effective-stratum counts, and preserve all arm counts. A valid permutation requires at least 20 nonempty hospital-by-landmark stay strata with both labels and finite cluster support; below that threshold the stay-level null is unavailable/inconclusive, not evidence for or against the hypothesis. Compare the observed increment to both the parent row-preserving null and this stay-level null. A result typical under the stay-level null is adverse to a landmark-specific patient-state-information interpretation, even if it is unusual under a broad row-level null.

### 5. Exposure-switch and label-first stratified diagnostics

Report M1-M0 and five-state/conditional contrasts separately for (i) first-target-label landmarks, (ii) repeated same-label landmarks, and (iii) landmarks following an exact target-label switch, only when each stratum has predeclared minimum support of 10 rows per label and at least five hospitals with both labels. These are diagnostics, not post-hoc subgroup claims. If support fails, the stratum is unavailable/inconclusive. No subgroup may be selected based on direction or endpoint availability.

A signal limited to repeated same-label landmarks or to one side of a within-stay switch is evidence of exposure persistence/episode documentation, not evidence that the current label adds new patient-state information at `D`. A signal limited to first-label landmarks may be compatible with a newly informative episode marker but still requires all parent endpoint, opportunity, transport, placebo, and airway gates.

## Mechanical gates and interpretation

Run gates from machine-readable outputs before interpreting any label contrast or prediction increment. The following parent gates remain mandatory:

1. parent artifact and source/schema/hash integrity, exact labels, exact boundaries, parser freeze, independent endpoint isolation, and no post-D baseline leakage;
2. exactly one K state for each of 540 rows with competing-exit precedence and no unobservable row called stable/mixed;
3. explicit observability and competing-exit accounting, with supportive feasibility at least 40% classifiable and at least 10 hospitals with both labels among classifiable rows; below 20% classifiable or below five such hospitals is adverse feasibility, and intermediate is inconclusive;
4. transport support, site-balanced versus row-weighted concordance, no hospital dominance above 25%, and at least five adequately supported hospitals before any cross-hospital patient-state interpretation;
5. full `(C,O_B,O_E,O_T,V_T)` decomposition and observation-only/value-masked diagnostics, with opportunity-only or mask-only gains adverse to patient-state interpretation;
6. M1 improvement in both micro and equal-hospital macro held-out loss for all-row K and classifiable trajectory in the unbounded primary plus at least two frozen sensitivities, favorable stay-cluster uncertainty excluding zero, and no more than 25% reversing estimable held-out hospitals;
7. trajectory coherence, pre-D/lead-time placebos, documentation negative control, source/component ablation, and valid permutation support.

Add these within-stay gates:

8. **Persistence accounting gate.** The persistence audit must enumerate all available same-stay target-label timing and landmark-transition fields before subgroup summaries. Missing provenance is reported, not reconstructed. Any use of future labels, post-D care-plan rows, or endpoint-derived fields to define an exposure-persistence stratum invalidates that diagnostic.
9. **Stay-balance gate.** The one-landmark-per-stay sensitivity must use the fixed earliest-landmark rule and remain secondary. A supportive patient-state interpretation requires its direction and all-row K loss increment to be concordant with the primary in the available subset; if unavailable, the result is inconclusive for episode-balanced robustness, not adverse evidence against the endpoint.
10. **No-cross-landmark gate.** A supportive temporal-dependence result requires the grouped stay-fold M1-M0 increment to favor M1 for all-row K and classifiable trajectory in at least three estimable folds, with no more than one reversal and no gain confined to exit/unobservability/opportunity masks. Fewer than three estimable folds is inconclusive.
11. **Stay-level null gate.** A patient-state interpretation requires the observed increment to be more favorable than the valid stay-level opportunity-preserving null under the predeclared comparison; fewer than 20 valid strata or failed positivity is inconclusive. A typical stay-level-null increment is adverse to a landmark-specific claim, not evidence that labels have no clinical meaning.
12. **Persistence interpretation gate.** A gain restricted to repeated labels, same-stay persistence, one label switch direction, or first-label/site composition is classified as episode/documentation information unless it also survives the primary endpoint, opportunity decomposition, stay-balanced subset, grouped temporal holdout, and supported held-out hospital criteria. It cannot be called a current decision-time patient-state signal.

Supportive results would establish, at most, a reproducible association/prediction increment for the structured eICU chart construct that is less sensitive to hospital mix, documentation opportunity, repeated-landmark weighting, and label persistence. Adverse results would show that the increment is unstable, opportunity-driven, site-driven, or episode-persistent for this snapshot; they would not establish absence of bedside evaluation, harm, or lack of treatment. Inconclusive results would identify failed artifact recovery, sparse stay-level overlap, insufficient temporal folds, failed permutation support, or unavailable label provenance. Stronger claims require adjudicated bedside SBT/extubation/reintubation outcomes, synchronized device logs, local interface dictionaries, workflow metadata, post-ICU follow-up, expert clinical review, and prospective or external replication.
