# Synchronous versus discordant recorded organ recovery after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and fit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 study of whether the *alignment* of recorded renal and respiratory recovery at the first ICU discharge identifies a different early failure pathway after recorded vasoactive support withdrawal.

Completion requires, on a locked patient-level test set:

1. `run_manifest.json`, containing catalog [source checksum], MIMIC snapshot `[source checksum]`, source/archive/schema hashes, dictionary assertions, temporal rules, thresholds, split seeds, software versions, and output hashes.
2. `cohort_audit.parquet`, one row per subject's earliest eligible ICU stay, including admission/ICU join status, temporal contradictions, alive-at-landmark status, support eligibility, renal/urine/respiratory observation coverage, and every exclusion.
3. `organ_ledger.parquet`, the complete pre-landmark ledger, with 24 one-hour rows in `[outtime-24h,outtime)`, raw values, timestamps, source-specific masks, duplicate and validity flags, and provenance. No absence may be converted into a zero or a normal value.
4. `organ_phenotype_audit.parquet`, with creatinine-pair and urine-coverage audits, respiratory-support channel audits, renal and respiratory recorded states, synchrony class, unclassified/contradictory cases, and all locked sensitivity recodings.
5. `topology_outcome_audit.parquet`, with later same-admission ICU stays, transfer rows and bridge status, return candidates, death/discharge timing, and the lower/upper endpoint labels.
6. Held-out predictions and estimands from both methods: 6-, 24-, and 48-hour competing-risk CIFs for documented-bridge return (L), any later ICU return (U), U-only/unknown-topology return, death, and alive discharge; calibration and proper scoring; observation-weight diagnostics; topology/phenotype bounds; uncertainty; and a conclusion-to-output map in `interpretation.md`.

The primary scientific deliverable is a bound-aware estimate of whether renal-predominant and respiratory-predominant residual instability have different first-failure compositions after recorded vasoactive withdrawal, compared with recorded concordant recovery. It is not a bedside score or a causal estimate of ICU discharge.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claim

The local expert seed `[starting question]` proposes asynchronous organ recovery, and seed `mimic-10` proposes residual oxygen/vital instability before ICU discharge. Seed `mimic-03` specifically cautions that creatinine-only recovery can disagree with urine output. These are untested expert hypotheses, not findings. The local evidence establishes that this snapshot contains dated ICU stays, hospital boundaries, transfer records, creatinine/lactate laboratory records, charted respiratory/vital values, vasoactive input intervals, and numeric output records. The dictionary and archive audit below verifies the specific items used here. It does not establish that any recorded value measures true organ recovery, volume status, tissue perfusion, treatment intent, or preventable deterioration.

The parent candidate showed why a single metabolic marker must be separated from selective measurement, support state, competing death, and uncertain ICU-return topology. The unresolved successor question is:

> Among adults alive at a first ICU outtime, particularly those with a documented prior vasoactive interval that is recorded off in the final six hours, does discordance between recorded renal recovery and recorded respiratory-support burden identify a different early failure pathway—more death for renal-predominant instability and more ICU return for respiratory-predominant instability—than concordant recorded recovery, after selective observation and death competing with return are handled explicitly?

The directional hypothesis is prespecified, not assumed true:

- renal-predominant residual burden (recorded renal impairment with no persistent high respiratory-support burden) will have a larger 48-hour death CIF than concordant favorable recorded recovery;
- respiratory-predominant residual burden (persistent high recorded respiratory support with no renal impairment) will have a larger 48-hour L/U return CIF than concordant favorable recorded recovery;
- both-domain burden will have the largest total early first-event burden, but its return-versus-death composition is unresolved.

The primary comparison is within the recorded-off-support stratum. All eligible alive first-ICU discharges remain the target population through an observed-pair analysis, a conditional observation-standardized analysis, and assumption-light phenotype/topology intervals. A null, reversal, death-only contrast, or contrast explained by observation intensity is an adverse result for the proposed pathway interpretation. These predictions are deliberately falsifiable; they are not claimed results.

The clinical importance is a discharge-surveillance decision: whether a patient who is no longer receiving recorded vasoactive support should be treated as one homogeneous recovery state, or whether an auditable renal/respiratory mismatch warrants different monitoring hypotheses. The substantive advance over a generic discharge score or the parent’s lactate contrast is a pathway-specific, multi-organ estimand that tests whether *which organ remains abnormal* changes the mode of early failure. It can motivate prospective monitoring research only if robust; it cannot justify discharging, delaying discharge, changing oxygen, giving fluid, or restarting vasoactive treatment.

## Renal-respiratory exposure and estimands

For each subject, retain all valid creatinine rows and selected urine rows. Define an observed creatinine pair only when there is at least one valid creatinine in each of the early and late halves; use the latest in each half. Let `DeltaCr = late - early` in mg/dL. Primary recorded renal impairment is `DeltaCr >= 0.30` or `urine-low=1`, provided both the creatinine pair and three-bin urine coverage are present. Primary favorable renal status is `DeltaCr <= 0.10` and `urine-low=0`. Values between thresholds, conflicting assays, missing coverage, and output-context contradictions are intermediate/unknown. The creatinine thresholds are recording rules, not AKI diagnoses; sensitivities are `0.20/0.30/0.50` for worsening and `0/0.10/0.20` for favorable change, with the urine thresholds and assay-specific recodings above. A secondary renal definition uses creatinine alone and reports how much the urine requirement changes ascertainment.

Define the primary four observed synchrony cells only when both domains are determined:

- concordant favorable: renal favorable + respiratory favorable;
- renal-predominant burden: renal impaired + respiratory favorable;
- respiratory-predominant burden: renal favorable + respiratory-persistent;
- concordant burden: renal impaired + respiratory-persistent.

Intermediate/unknown renal or respiratory states, R=0 observation histories, contradictory rows, and recorded-off/active/unknown support are never silently folded into a cell. The primary stratum is recorded-off support; the all-cohort analysis includes support state as a prespecified stratification/nested covariate.

For horizon h in {6,24,48 hours}, estimate:

1. `Delta_death^L/U(h)`: renal-predominant minus concordant-favorable death CIF contrast and respiratory-predominant minus concordant-favorable L/U-return CIF contrast;
2. `Delta_return^L/U(h)`: corresponding return CIF contrasts with death and alive discharge competing;
3. the CIF of concordant burden and the return-versus-death composition when its denominator is nonzero;
4. the width and direction of the joint identification interval when organ phenotype and L/U topology are uncertain.

Report, in this order, (a) the complete observed-pair estimand, (b) the all-eligible-cohort estimand transported under a declared conditional-observation assumption, and (c) assumption-light intervals allowing every unresolved phenotype/topology assignment compatible with the recorded audits. The observation-standardized estimand is a recorded-data association, not causal. Keep death as a competing event; do not call a larger return CIF a readmission-risk increase if death also changes.

For L, a later same-admission ICU stay must begin in the horizon and have a documented non-ICU-labeled `hosp/transfers` interval with `eventtype=transfer`, non-null `careunit`, parseable interval, and temporal placement between index `outtime` and later ICU `intime`. A careunit containing ICU/CCU labels is not accepted as the lower-bound bridge; ambiguous or missing topology is unknown. U is any later same-admission ICU stay in the horizon. U-only is a U event without verifiable L bridge and is included in U but not described as confirmed escalation. Death uses `admissions.deathtime`; if `hospital_expire_flag=1` but deathtime is absent, classify death but mark timing unavailable, use dischtime as an administrative primary time, and interval-censor it in sensitivity. Primary ties are death-first; return-first is sensitivity. Alive discharge is a competing event when it precedes other events.

For observation standardization, estimate the probability of determined renal/respiratory phenotype from pre-landmark values, masks, counts, timestamps, support state, static covariates, and admission type only. Fit on training subjects, cap stabilized weights at 10 (sensitivities 5 and 20), report uncapped positivity/ESS, and vary a locked selection multiplier Gamma in {0.5,1,2}. No post-outtime variable may enter. The all-cohort target is not redefined as the selected complete-case subset.

For assumption-light bounds, keep each recorded event/censoring time and first-event label fixed, but assign subjects with unknown renal/respiratory phenotype and ambiguous L/U topology to every deterministic-compatible cell/topology assignment. Minimize/maximize each contrast and composition at each horizon using an exact finite-assignment or linear-programming implementation and publish assignment counts. These are identification intervals, not confidence intervals. Bootstrap uncertainty around fixed endpoints and uncertainty in weighted estimates must remain separate.

## Matched methods and scientific comparison

Use subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits, seeds 17, 29, and 43. No subject may cross splits. Thresholds, unit rules, feature construction, weight caps, model tuning, and latent initialization are locked using training/validation only. The test set is evaluated once per split.

### Alternative A: transparent primary baseline

Fit pooled one-hour cause-specific penalized logistic hazards over eight six-hour post-landmark bins for L, U-only, death, and alive discharge. Fit L and U endpoint definitions separately. Predictors are the prespecified synchrony cell, support state, all renal/urine/respiratory values and masks, parent-compatible lactate values/masks, vital values/masks, measurement intensity, static covariates, and time-bin indicators. Fit nested versions:

A. synchrony cell and support state only;
B. renal/respiratory values and support/physiology without organ cell labels;
C. observation masks/counts/intensity only;
D. organ trajectories and masks;
E. full identical ledger.

Standardize held-out cause-specific hazards to the target population, then use Aalen-Johansen recursion to obtain CIFs with competing death/discharge. The observation-weighted and finite-assignment analyses apply to the full model. Report absolute CIFs, prespecified contrasts, return/death composition, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier/log loss where defined, weight positivity/ESS, and subject-bootstrap intervals across the three splits. The baseline is the primary inferential result because every term, mask, threshold and endpoint can be audited.

### Alternative B: joint observation-aware dynamic Bayesian/semi-Markov model

Fit a compact two-domain latent state-space model on the exact same 24-bin ledger and splits. The renal domain has latent favorable, impaired, intermediate, and unknown states; the respiratory domain has favorable, persistent-high, intermediate, and unknown states. A semi-Markov transition component models persistence and ordered transitions across the 24 pre-landmark bins. Separate emission/observation components model creatinine values, urine-bin values and coverage, FiO2/O2-flow/PEEP observations, lactate if included, and their masks. A coupled event head predicts the same L, U-only, death, and alive-discharge hazards in the same eight bins. It is trained on training subjects, selected on validation, and evaluated once on the locked test subjects; no post-landmark source is allowed.

Required outputs are posterior domain states and entropy, transition/duration summaries, observation-model propensities/calibration, held-out CIFs and the same contrasts/compositions, proper scores/calibration, and multi-initialization or bootstrap uncertainty. What this alternative can reveal that the baseline loses is whether a short-lived abnormal measurement versus a persistent, ordered renal-to-respiratory or respiratory-to-renal trajectory drives the contrast, and whether missingness is itself the dominant process. It can also quantify uncertainty in asynchronous states rather than forcing an observed endpoint cell. It cannot recover unrecorded true GFR, fluid balance, ventilator settings, perfusion, or clinical intent.

Compare methods on the scientific outputs—not merely AUROC: direction and magnitude of the predeclared CIF contrasts, death/return composition, width of observation/topology intervals, held-out calibration and proper scores, observation-model calibration, latent entropy, transition stability across seeds, and agreement under falsification. Retain the transparent baseline as primary if the dynamic model is high-entropy, poorly calibrated, initialization-unstable, changes the estimand, or narrows uncertainty only by unverifiable latent assumptions. The learned alternative is scientifically justified by trajectory/observation decomposition, not by model complexity or a small predictive gain.

## Falsification and interpretation gates

Prespecify before locked test evaluation:

- Permute creatinine/urine/respiratory values and their temporal order within support-intensity and observation-coverage strata while preserving masks and timestamps. Organ-synchrony contrasts should attenuate toward the mask-only result.
- Permute respiratory-support interval timing and vasoactive end timing within support-intensity strata while preserving counts and interval lengths.
- Fit observation-only, support/vital-only, renal-only, respiratory-only, lactate-only, and full-ledger models. If observation-only reproduces the full contrast, an organ-recovery interpretation is weakened.
- Replace primary `charttime`, `starttime/endtime` with `storetime` as timestamp negative controls.
- Repeat at pseudo-landmarks 6 and 12 hours before the true outtime, shifting feature and outcome origins together. A highly landmark-specific signal should not appear at arbitrary misalignment.
- Repeat creatinine assays separately, urine item sets separately, each respiratory channel separately, thresholds and coverage rules, support boundary tolerances, transfer bridge rules, death timing, and return/death tie order.
- Report stratification by first/last careunit, admission type, age group and anchor-year group without treating deidentified cross-subject dates as calendar time.
- Report event support, cell counts, missingness, contradiction rates, observation-model calibration, ESS, latent entropy, and joint phenotype/topology interval width.

Supportive evidence requires adequate positive counts and observation positivity; a directionally consistent renal-death and/or respiratory-return contrast in the prespecified observed and observation-standardized analyses; a non-vacuous assumption-light interval or an explicit reason it is vacuous; acceptable calibration; attenuation after temporal/value permutation; and stability across assays, urine rules, respiratory channels, thresholds, support boundaries, transfer topology and seeds. The learned model need not score better, but it must reproduce the contrast with a stable trajectory/observation decomposition.

Adverse evidence includes a null or reversal after observation adjustment, a signal reproduced by masks alone, persistence after value permutation, dependence on one output channel or missingness rule, a death-only contrast for the respiratory-return claim, or a broad U-only contrast with null/wide L endpoint. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse cells, low positivity, high unknown prevalence, vacuous bounds, unstable weights, unresolved death time, high latent entropy, model disagreement, or inadequate event support. Inconclusive must not be converted into a null.

Computation can establish reproducible recorded feature construction, observed-pair and observation-standardized recorded-data associations conditional on their assumptions, competing-risk CIFs, finite-assignment bounds, held-out calibration and sensitivity behavior. It cannot establish true organ recovery, AKI, oliguria as a clinical diagnosis, fluid status, respiratory failure, treatment response, clinician intent, treatment limitations, discharge readiness, planned versus unplanned return, preventability, clinical utility, or post-discharge outcomes. Those require adjudicated charts and measurements (for example measured kidney function, fluid balance, respiratory device/settings, treatment-limitation and ward-monitoring information), expert review, external validation, and likely prospective or causal study.
