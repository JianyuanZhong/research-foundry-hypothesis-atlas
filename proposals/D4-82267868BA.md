# Longitudinal Proteomic Trajectories and Multimorbidity Progression

## Hypothesis to Test

Among individuals with repeated proteomic measurements, distinct longitudinal proteomic trajectory patterns (characterized by the temporal ordering and clustering of proteome changes) predict differential rates of multimorbidity progression (cardiometabolic syndrome, cardiorenal syndrome, and other major disease clusters) beyond what static baseline proteomic profiles predict.

Specifically:
1. Individuals exhibiting rapid proteome turnover (high inter-measurement variability) followed by sustained proteomic change will have higher rates of cardiometabolic and cardiorenal multimorbidity progression compared to those with stable proteome trajectories
2. The predictive value of proteomic trajectories will be attenuated when adjusting for static baseline proteomic profiles, suggesting trajectories capture unique temporal dynamics
3. These trajectory-based predictions will be independent of traditional risk factors (age, sex, BMI, smoking, alcohol)

## Study Population and Main Data

**Population**: UK Biobank participants with:
- Repeated proteomic measurements (≥2 time points)
- Incident diagnoses of cardiometabolic syndrome (ICD-10 E10-E14, E78, R73.0) and/or cardiorenal syndrome (ICD-10 I10-I16, N18-N19) during follow-up
- Complete baseline covariates (age, sex, BMI, smoking, alcohol)

- Minimum follow-up: 1 year (to ensure meaningful trajectory estimation)

## Scientific Significance

This hypothesis advances beyond existing knowledge in three key ways:

1. **Temporal Dynamics**: While prior organ-specific proteomic aging clocks (Wang et al., Nature Aging 2025) have demonstrated that multiple organ-age clocks predict disease and longevity, these studies typically assess static baseline proteomic profiles. This proposal tests whether the *temporal dynamics* of proteome changes—captured through longitudinal trajectory analysis—provide additional predictive power beyond static measures.

2. **Multi-Protein Trajectories**: Unlike studies focusing on single biomarker discordance (Williams & Gerlach, Pharmacotherapy 2025), this proposal examines multi-protein trajectory patterns, testing whether coordinated proteomic evolution (rather than individual protein changes) predicts disease risk.

3. **Clinical Utility**: By identifying distinct proteomic trajectory patterns associated with multimorbidity progression, this research could enable earlier intervention strategies and personalized monitoring for disease clusters, addressing a critical gap in chronic disease management.

## Main Limitations

1. **Measurement Frequency**: Repeated proteomic measurements may be sparse, limiting trajectory estimation accuracy
2. **Reverse Causation**: Undiagnosed disease may affect proteomic measurements before outcome
3. **Confounding**: Traditional risk factors (age, BMI, smoking) may mediate the relationship
4. **External Validation**: Results should be validated in independent cohorts (e.g., MIMIC-IV, eICU)
5. **Data Availability**: Proteomic measurement metadata (assay type, QC flags) requires verification

## Proposed Approach

**Baseline Model**: Cox proportional hazards regression using static baseline proteomic profiles (mean of all measurements at first visit) to predict time to multimorbidity onset.

**Alternative Model**: Hidden Markov Model (HMM) or Gaussian Process Regression to learn latent proteomic trajectory states from repeated measurements.

**Evaluation**: Compare AUC, calibration, and clinical utility between baseline and trajectory models.
