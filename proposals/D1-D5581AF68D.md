Goal
-----

The estimand is prognostic: among the frozen complete laboratory-triplet episodes observed in the comparator or recorded-systemic arm, what is the difference in prediction error when a model trained only on earlier episodes is applied to later episodes? Evidence already supported
--------------------------
The inherited executed benchmark supports the narrower claim that M1 improves on uncalibrated raw persistence in apparent internal validation, but its incremental advantage over an outcome-trained affine persistence forecast is not established. Thus a raw-persistence comparison is not sufficient evidence for incremental value.

Falsification and interpretation rules
---------------------------------------
Supportive: M1 has lower paired late-era error than frozen affine persistence, with an uncertainty interval compatible with a positive gain, and no material calibration deterioration; this supports incremental prognostic transport only, not clinical utility or causality.

Adverse: M1 loses to affine persistence or its calibration/error deteriorates materially in the late era. This falsifies the claim of transportable incremental value under this cohort and clock definition; it may indicate temporal drift, overfit, or selection shift, not patient harm.

Null/inconclusive: the point estimate is small or uncertain, or late systemic cells are too sparse. Then conclude that incremental value was not established; do not equate this with equivalence. A wide interval caused by 52/54 total late observations is an information limitation.

A large raw-persistence gain but little or no M1-over-affine gain should be interpreted as calibration transport or baseline-shift evidence, not evidence that M1 adds clinically useful information. Any claims about exchangeability of systemic versus comparator arms, treatment benefit, toxicity, recovery, patient-important outcomes, or deployment utility require clinical adjudication, richer covariates, or another prospective/external study.
