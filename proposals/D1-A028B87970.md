# EP13 Proposal: Paired Post-Operative Biomarker Kinetics After Curative Hepatectomy for HCC

**Branch:** work/lead/ (EP13 promotion of EP12 restart candidate)
**Status:** deliverable, data-bound, discovery-phase (not yet fitted)
**Episode:** 13 of 20 (HCC island)
**Parent:** none (restart — new question, not a child of parked EP7)

---

## 1. Unresolved claim and clinical consequence

**Unresolved claim (H1 — primary).** After curative-intent hepatectomy for HCC, the EARLY POST-OP
(days 1-42) trajectory of the PAIRED biomarker set {AFP, PIVKA-II, AFP-L3%} independently predicts
early treatment re-escalation (first TACE/ablation/systemic therapy within 365d after surgery; a
relapse surrogate) BEYOND pre-op values and static clinicopathological features.

**Unresolved claim (H2 — discriminator).** A pre-specified 3-test rival-explanation discriminator
(pre-op biomarker stratum, liver-reserve interaction, timing day1-7 vs day8-42) separates the
**tumor-biology account** (post-op values reflect residual/recurrent tumor) from the
**hepatic-regeneration account** (post-op values reflect post-resection hepatocyte turnover).

**Clinical importance.** 30-70% of HCC patients recur within 2 years of curative resection.
Current surveillance and adjuvant-therapy decisions use static pre-operative features. Post-op
AFP failure to normalize (tumor half-life 5-7d for AFP<400 ng/mL) is a recognized clinical signal
of residual/recurrent disease, but hepatic regeneration after resection also transiently elevates
AFP. A model using routinely drawn post-op biomarkers to (a) identify high-risk patients and
(b) determine whether the signal is tumor-specific vs regeneration artifact directly informs
surveillance intensity and early adjuvant therapy (e.g., atezolizumab-bevacizumab per IMbrave050).

**Why this is new vs parked EP7 ([prior hypothesis], compiled 3x).**
EP7 used a single marker (AFP) and only touched AFP-L3 as a sparse subcohort. PIVKA-II is a
distinct tumor-secreted factor (protein induced by vitamin K absence/antagonism-II, a des-gamma-
carboxylated prothrombin variant) that is NOT a regeneration marker — it is specifically secreted
by HCC cells and is elevated in ~50% of HCC even when AFP is normal. A PAIRED kinetics model
(AFP + PIVKA-II + AFP-L3%) can separate tumor-specific signal from regeneration artifact much
more cleanly than AFP alone: if the post-op signal is driven by regeneration, PIVKA-II (a
non-regeneration marker) should NOT track with AFP; if driven by residual tumor, both should
co-vary. This is a substantive scientific advance, not a repackaging.

**Closest prior work (inspected).**
- [K1] Wen 2026 JHC (PMC13401935): combined PRE-op AFP+PIVKA-II for post-hepatectomy OS/RFS,
  C-index 0.728, n=553. Establishes paired AFP+PIVKA-II prognostic value but at PRE-OP timepoint
  only — does NOT use post-op kinetics.
- [K2] Zhou 2025 Discov Oncol (PMC12858684): dynamic NLR changes (pre-op → 3-month post-TACE)
  predict disease control, n=157. Demonstrates the "kinetics > baseline" principle for HCC
  biomarkers but uses NLR (not AFP/PIVKA-II) and post-TACE (not post-hepatectomy).
- [K3] Palihati 2026 Front Immunol (PMC13381516): review explicitly identifying "dynamic serum
  biomarkers such as AFP kinetics" as a key advance for predicting HCC treatment efficacy, but
  not yet systematically studied post-hepatectomy for recurrence prediction.

**Gap.** No prior work has (a) used the PAIRED post-op kinetics of AFP + PIVKA-II + AFP-L3%
(days 1-42) to predict early re-escalation, and (b) applied a pre-specified rival-explanation
discriminator to separate tumor-biology from hepatic-regeneration accounts. This is the gap.

## 2. Rival explanations and their differing predictions

| Account | Prediction | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Tumor-biology** (post-op values reflect residual/recurrent tumor) | PIVKA-II (non-regeneration marker) co-varies with AFP; both predict re-escalation; signal strongest in pre-op double-positive stratum; timing: early (day1-7) signal reflects residual tumor at surgery | H2 Test 1 (pre-op stratum), Test 2 (liver-reserve interaction), Test 3 (timing) |
| **Hepatic-regeneration** (post-op values reflect post-resection hepatocyte turnover) | AFP elevated but PIVKA-II NOT (regeneration does not elevate PIVKA-II); signal strongest in patients with large resection volume / poor liver reserve; timing: early (day1-7) signal reflects regeneration peak | H2 Test 2 (liver-reserve interaction), Test 3 (timing) |
| **Surveillance/selection bias** (clinicians re-escalate based on imaging, not biomarkers) | Biomarker kinetics add no predictive value beyond imaging-based surveillance; C-index does not improve over baseline | H1 (C-index comparison) |

**Key distinguishing observation.** PIVKA-II is the critical discriminator: it is a tumor-secreted
factor that is NOT elevated by hepatocyte regeneration. If the post-op signal is tumor-driven,
PIVKA-II kinetics should independently predict re-escalation AND co-vary with AFP. If
regeneration-driven, PIVKA-II should be uninformative while AFP (which IS elevated by
regeneration) may still predict — but the liver-reserve interaction would be strong.

## 3. Pre-specified 3-test rival-explanation discriminator (H2)

**Test 1 — Pre-op biomarker stratum (tumor burden).**
Stratify by pre-op AFP (>400 vs ≤400) × PIVKA-II (>40 vs ≤40) into DP/SP/N groups (per [K1]).
- Tumor-biology prediction: post-op kinetics predict re-escalation STRONGLY in DP group, weakly
  in N group (residual tumor burden drives the signal).
- Regeneration prediction: post-op kinetics predict re-escalation EQUALLY across strata
  (regeneration is independent of pre-op tumor burden).
- **Discriminative:** if the kinetics effect is concentrated in DP, tumor-biology is supported.

**Test 2 — Liver-reserve interaction (regeneration capacity).**
Interact post-op biomarker kinetics with pre-op liver reserve (albumin, platelet, bilirubin,
PT — composite Child-Pugh approximation).
- Tumor-biology prediction: NO significant interaction (tumor signal is independent of liver
  reserve).
- Regeneration prediction: SIGNIFICANT interaction — post-op AFP effect is stronger in patients
  with poor liver reserve (more regeneration) or large resection.
- **Discriminative:** a significant interaction supports regeneration; absence supports tumor-biology.

**Test 3 — Timing (day1-7 vs day8-42).**
Split post-op window into early (day1-7, regeneration peak) and late (day8-42, tumor half-life
window).
- Tumor-biology prediction: LATE (day8-42) signal is more predictive (residual tumor persists
  while regeneration resolves); early signal is confounded by regeneration.
- Regeneration prediction: EARLY (day1-7) signal is more predictive (regeneration peak); late
  signal resolves as regeneration completes.
- **Discriminative:** if late > early, tumor-biology; if early > late, regeneration.

**Decision rule (pre-specified).**
- ≥2 of 3 tests support tumor-biology → tumor-biology account favored.
- ≥2 of 3 tests support regeneration → regeneration account favored.
- Mixed/inconclusive → accounts not separable with available data; report honestly.

## 5. Baseline vs kinetics vs learned comparison

**Model A — Baseline (Cox static pre-op).**
Cox proportional hazards: re-escalation ~ log(pre-op AFP) + log(pre-op PIVKA-II) + AFP-L3% + age + sex + liver reserve (albumin, PLT, bilirubin, PT) + tumor size (if available) + BCLC stage (if available).
- This is the "static features only" model. Establishes the baseline C-index.
- EP8 result: baseline Cox C-index = 0.4835 (below 0.5) on n=10811, events=3370. Headroom for post-op kinetics.

**Model B — Kinetics (Cox + post-op trajectory terms).**
Cox proportional hazards: re-escalation ~ [Model A terms] + log(post-op AFP day1-7) + log(post-op AFP day8-42) + log(post-op PIVKA-II day1-7) + log(post-op PIVKA-II day8-42) + AFP-L3% post-op + delta_AFP (post-op - pre-op) + delta_PIVKA-II (post-op - pre-op) + normalization indicator (post-op AFP < 10 ng/mL).
- This adds the post-op trajectory terms. Tests H1 (do post-op kinetics add predictive value beyond pre-op?).
- Also fits the 3-test discriminator (H2) as stratified/interaction terms.

**Model C — Learned (XGBoost survival).**
XGBoost survival (e.g., xgboost's survival objective or a Cox partial likelihood objective): re-escalation ~ [all features from A + B] + interaction terms.
- This is the "learned" model that can capture non-linear interactions between post-op kinetics and pre-op stratum / liver reserve.
- Tests whether the paired kinetics signal is non-linear (which Cox would miss).

**Comparison metric:** C-index (concordance index) with 95% CI (bootstrap, 1000 resamples).
- **Supportive:** Model B C-index > Model A C-index by ≥0.02 (clinically meaningful improvement), AND Model C C-index ≥ Model B C-index.
- **Adverse:** Model B C-index ≤ Model A C-index (post-op kinetics add no value).
- **Inconclusive:** Model B C-index > Model A but <0.02 improvement (statistically significant but clinically marginal).

**H2 discriminator evaluation:**
- Report the 3-test results (Test 1, 2, 3) with p-values and effect sizes.
- Apply the pre-specified decision rule (≥2/3 → favored account).
- Report whether the accounts are separable with available data.

## 6. Pre-specified falsification criteria

**H1 (primary — post-op kinetics predict re-escalation beyond pre-op):**
- **Supportive:** Model B C-index > Model A C-index by ≥0.02 (95% CI non-overlapping), AND at least one post-op kinetics term (log post-op AFP, log post-op PIVKA-II, delta_AFP, normalization) is independently significant (p<0.05) in the Cox model.
- **Adverse:** Model B C-index ≤ Model A C-index, OR no post-op kinetics term is independently significant.
- **Inconclusive:** Model B C-index > Model A but <0.02 improvement, OR post-op kinetics terms are significant but C-index improvement is <0.02.

**H2 (discriminator — tumor-biology vs regeneration):**
- **Supportive (tumor-biology):** ≥2 of 3 tests support tumor-biology (Test 1: DP > N; Test 2: no liver-reserve interaction; Test 3: late > early).
- **Supportive (regeneration):** ≥2 of 3 tests support regeneration (Test 1: equal across strata; Test 2: liver-reserve interaction; Test 3: early > late).
- **Inconclusive:** Mixed results (1-1-1 or 1-2 mixed), OR insufficient power (too few events in strata).

**Stronger conclusions requiring additional data:**
- Causal claims (post-op biomarkers CAUSE re-escalation) require RCT or instrumental variable — NOT supported by this observational study.
- Mechanistic claims (PIVKA-II is a specific tumor-secreted factor) require experimental validation — NOT supported by this study.
- External validation requires an independent cohort — NOT available in this dataset.
- Imaging-based surveillance bias cannot be fully excluded (no imaging data in this dataset) — only partially testable via the C-index comparison.

## 7. Limitations

1. **Surrogate outcome.** Re-escalation (TACE/ablation/systemic) is a surrogate for recurrence,
   not a direct measure of tumor recurrence. Clinicians may re-escalate based on imaging, not
   biomarkers, introducing surveillance/selection bias.
2. **Single center.** One hospital (Guangxi Medical University Cancer Hospital context). No
   external validation.
3. **No lab units.** AFP/PIVKA-II values are unitless in source. Log-transform assumed.
4. **Right-censoring.** Patients without re-escalation by 365d are censored. Follow-up may be
   incomplete.
5. **AFP-L3% is categorical.** Stored as percentile strings in 定性结果, not clean numeric.
   Limits the precision of the AFP-L3% kinetics term.
6. **No imaging data.** Cannot directly measure tumor recurrence or exclude imaging-based
   surveillance bias.
7. **Interaction is association-level.** The liver-reserve interaction (Test 2) is an
   association, not a mechanistic proof of regeneration.
8. **3-variable composite liver reserve.** Albumin + PLT + bilirubin + PT is an approximation
   of Child-Pugh, not the full score (no ascites/encephalopathy).
9. **Surveillance bias partially testable.** The C-index comparison (Model B vs A) partially
   tests whether biomarkers add value beyond imaging-based surveillance, but cannot fully
   exclude it.
