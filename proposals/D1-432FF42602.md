# Coverage-gated lactate-by-perfusion heterogeneity with a fixed early-landmark rescue estimand

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly produce a source-audited, leakage-controlled comparison of two pre-specified estimands for the same clinical uncertainty: whether the prognostic meaning of persistent hyperlactatemia differs when measured systemic perfusion is improving versus worsening.

The live-exit estimand inherited from the selected parent remains primary because it is directly relevant to risk communication at an observed ICU exit. A fixed early-landmark analysis is a substantive robustness estimand, not a post hoc replacement:

1. construct an all-eligible flow and a separately reported strict coverage-complete flow for each estimand;
2. produce a row-level provenance manifest for clinical time, recording time, item dictionary, units, interval clipping, deduplication and joins;
3. apply the same endpoint-independent coverage gate and frozen cell/event-support rules before any fit or effect estimate;
4. estimate the parent’s exact 48-hour live-exit persistent-lactate-by-perfusion interaction with competing R/D/S states;
5. estimate the fixed, non-outcome-dependent 24-hour early-landmark interaction for ICU-level rescue/readmission versus death, with alive discharge/observation as a competing state;
6. fit matched transparent latest-state/phenotype hazard models, an order-blind history model, a process-only mask control, and a small chronological learned model on identical subjects, inputs and partitions within each estimand;
7. report held-out competing-risk predictions, CIFs, calibration, Brier scores, component-specific uncertainty, selection/coverage diagnostics and falsification outputs;
8. issue a claim ledger that links every conclusion to computed outputs and separates prognostic association from physiology, treatment effect, rescue appropriateness, discharge policy, utility and transportability.

No fitted result or clinical conclusion is claimed in this proposal. A gate failure yields a complete audit and descriptive flow but makes the affected inferential claim inconclusive; no window, threshold, cell definition or endpoint will be loosened after inspecting results.

## Unresolved question, clinical importance and evidence boundary

The strongest available evidence before the experiment is only that the configured MIMIC-IV 3.1 snapshot contains timestamped ICU boundaries, repeated lactate and MAP observations, vasoactive intervals, organ laboratories, urine output, ICU procedures, and admission discharge/death fields from which an operational trajectory can be constructed. The expert seed about asynchronous organ recovery is explicitly untested. The seed and the related paper are rationale, not validation of this lactate/perfusion phenotype. The local research-ambition demonstrations support comparing transparent summaries with longitudinal learned representations; they do not establish this phenotype, its causal meaning, or a clinical benefit. A public literature search was unavailable during this design pass (HTTP 503), so no additional paper claim is made.

The primary unresolved claim is:

> Among the first eligible live ICU exits with repeated lactate and paired, adequately ascertained MAP/vasoactive measurements, is the 48-hour excess risk associated with persistent hyperlactatemia smaller when measured perfusion is improving than when it is worsening?

The fixed-landmark robustness claim is:

> Among first eligible ICU stays that are still under ICU observation and alive at a fixed 12 hours after ICU entry, is the 24-hour risk of an operational ICU-level rescue/readmission or death associated with persistent hyperlactatemia modified by improving versus worsening measured perfusion?

The interaction estimand for either analysis is the difference in standardized cumulative incidence of the adverse composite between persistent and nonpersistent lactate across perfusion directions:

`Delta = [CIF(A | persistent, improving) - CIF(A | nonpersistent, improving)] - [CIF(A | persistent, worsening) - CIF(A | nonpersistent, worsening)]`.

For the live-exit analysis, A is first ICU readmission or timed in-hospital death within 48 hours. For the fixed analysis, A is first qualifying ICU-level rescue/readmission or timed in-hospital death within 24 hours. The directional hypothesis is Delta < 0. The clinically meaningful null region is -2 to +2 percentage points; a negative estimate inside that region is not primary support.

This is clinically important because the same lactate value may represent unresolved circulatory stress, adrenergic metabolism, impaired clearance, or a measurement/process pattern. If the interaction is robust across a fixed early landmark and the live-exit context, it could justify prospective testing of context-aware risk communication. If it appears only at live exit, that would implicate late selection or discharge context rather than a general early recovery signal. If it appears only at the fixed landmark, it may be more useful for early deterioration surveillance but would not establish a discharge decision rule. Neither estimand establishes microcirculatory dysfunction, lactate etiology, fluid responsiveness, treatment benefit, appropriate rescue, or an improved discharge policy.

## Population and exact time-zero rules

### Primary live-exit estimand

Use one index per `hadm_id): the earliest ICU stay row in `icu/icustays` with non-null `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, outtime`, `intime < outtime`, a matching `hosp/admissions` row, and a live exit at `t0 = outtime`. Require `t0 < dischtime` for the primary post-exit estimand so that an alive hospital discharge at the same instant is not treated as a 48-hour observation period. Require `deathtime` null or `deathtime > t0`; a contradictory `hospital_expire_flag=1` with missing `deathtime` is retained as unresolved U in the flow and cannot be silently treated as alive. Report reversed/missing intervals, missing admission links, immediate hospital discharge, and death-at/before-exit exclusions separately.

Define `tL = t0 - 1 hour`. Require `intime <= tL - 12 hours`. The primary feature window is `[tL-12h, tL]`, split into early `[tL-12h, tL-6h]` and late `(tL-6h, tL]`. This one-hour buffer makes the feature availability boundary explicit and prevents the ICU outtime record from being used as a clinical measurement.

### Fixed early-landmark estimand

Use one index per `hadm_id`: the earliest valid ICU stay with `intime < outtime), a matching admission, and `tF = intime + 12 hours < outtime`. Require `deathtime` null or `deathtime > tF`, `dischtime > tF`, and no contradictory death status. This is a fixed clinical-time rule determined by ICU entry, not by discharge, later measurements, endpoint status, or model output. Patients discharged from ICU before `tF`, dead by `tF`, or lacking adequate follow-up are reported in the all-eligible flow and not re-indexed at a later time.

The fixed feature window is `[intime, tF]`, split into early `[intime, intime+6h]` and late `(intime+6h, tF]`. The fixed analysis is not a claim that circulatory stabilization occurred at 12 hours: MIMIC has no adjudicated stabilization time. It is an operational early landmark chosen before outcome inspection. A 6-hour landmark is a fixed, labeled sensitivity only; it cannot replace the 12-hour estimand if coverage or event support fails.

No subject crosses analysis partitions. If a subject has multiple admissions, each estimand selects at most one index per admission, but the split is by `subject_id` so admissions from a subject cannot be distributed across train, validation and test.

## Feature timing and source-level validity

For charted measurements, require finite clinical `charttime <=` the estimand’s `tL` or `tF` and finite `storetime <=` the same cutoff. For interval inputs and procedures, require finite `starttime < endtime`, use the clinical interval for overlap/direction, require `storetime <=` the cutoff for features, and clip only to the feature window. No post-cutoff clinical time may enter a feature matrix even if stored earlier. Exact repeated input/procedure rows are collapsed using their complete available identity keys before interval union. Unsupported units, nonnumeric values, reversed intervals and impossible values are rejected and counted, not converted by assumption.

Persistent hyperlactatemia means the early and late median of valid lactate values is at least 2.0 mmol/L. Lactate threshold 4.0 mmol/L is a fixed sensitivity, not a selectable threshold. The primary measured-perfusion direction uses the median MAP in each half and unioned minutes of valid target-agent intervals in each half:

- improving: late MAP minus early MAP >= 5 mmHg and late target-agent minutes <= early minutes;
- worsening: late MAP minus early MAP <= -5 mmHg and late target-agent minutes >= early minutes;
- indeterminate: all remaining cases, retained descriptively and never merged into either primary cell.

Rates and amounts are not pooled into a pseudo-dose. A missing pressor interval is missing ascertainment, never evidence of zero support. Foley output is corroborative only and cannot rescue missing MAP/pressor coverage.

## Endpoint-independent coverage and frozen event-support gate

The coverage gate is calculated before outcome modeling, without endpoint labels, event times, association direction, test predictions or effect estimates. It has one row of flags and reason codes per eligible index and is identical in rule and thresholds for the live-exit and fixed analyses after their respective time origins.

In each early and late half, record:

- valid lactate count (distinct `labevent_id`), distinct lactate clinical times, accepted normalized `valueuom=mmol/L`, and chart/store-time lag;
- valid MAP count and distinct clinical times from items 220052, 220181 and 225312, numeric `valuenum`, `valueuom=mmHg`, with same-time item duplicates collapsed by median;
- a MAP item-presence bit vector;
- valid target-pressor interval count, target-item-presence bit vector, exact duplicate count, unioned overlap minutes and invalid/reversed interval counts;
- all-valid-input interval count as an observation-process audit, never as evidence of no pressor;
- Foley item 226559 valid numeric row count, distinct times, unit failures and chart/store lag;
- per-analyte value/test indicators for lactate 50813, ALT 50861, AST 50878, bilirubin 50885, creatinine 50912, bicarbonate 50882, hemoglobin 51222, platelets 51265 and WBC 51300;
- counts before and after every timing, unit and value filter, plus missingness and process intensity.

The strict primary coverage gate requires in both halves: at least one valid lactate, at least two valid MAP observations at distinct clinical times, and at least one valid target-pressor interval. Patients failing the gate remain in the all-eligible flow and in fixed MAP-only/coverage sensitivities but cannot enter the strict concordant interaction. The gate is intentionally conservative and narrows generalizability.

After gate variables and all endpoint labels are frozen, apply the same pre-specified support rule to each four-cell interaction (persistent/nonpersistent lactate by improving/worsening perfusion):

- >=40 subjects in the full gated cohort;
- >=10 adverse-composite events in each full cell;
- >=8 subjects and >=3 adverse-composite events in each deterministic held-out test cell;
- U <=10% overall and <=20% in every cell.

These event counts are a fixed adequacy check, not a choice based on observed effect or predictive performance. No cell may be dropped, merged with indeterminate, or redefined. If integer allocation prevents the test support, the affected estimand is not estimable. Fewer than five component R or five component D events in a cell preclude component-specific interaction claims even when the composite gate passes. Report all counts and gate failures.

For live-exit selection, fit the parent’s outcome-free q audit using pooled ICU-stay hours, one-hour bins, live-exit hours versus eligible non-exit hours, and only information available by each hour. In each interaction cell require at least 90% of index exits with q in [0.05,0.95], at least 20 non-exit hours in each occupied q decile of the exit distribution, and inverse-odds ESS >=50. q failure does not repair the cohort: the unweighted estimate remains descriptive and selection-robust interpretation is inconclusive.

For the fixed landmark, fit an outcome-free coverage-completeness model among all fixed-eligible stays using only demographics, admission/ICU context and first-half observation/process summaries available before the fixed cutoff. Require the same 90% overlap and ESS >=50 rules. Poor overlap makes the weighted fixed-landmark sensitivity inconclusive, without altering the unweighted gated estimate.

## Exact competing-risk endpoints

### Live-exit: 48-hour post-exit states

Order first transitions in `(t0, t0+48h]):

- R: a later `icu/icustays` row with the same `subject_id, hadm_id`, a different `stay_id`, and `intime > t0`; use the first qualifying `intime`;
- D: non-null `hosp/admissions.deathtime` with `deathtime > t0`, `deathtime <= t0+48h`, and, when `dischtime` is non-null, `deathtime <= dischtime`;
- S: alive `dischtime` in `(t0,t0+48h]`, before R and D, with `hospital_expire_flag=0` and `deathtime` null or later than `dischtime`; S is an observation boundary, not a favorable outcome;
- N: no R, D or S by 48 hours while the admission remains ascertainable;
- U: missing timed death with `hospital_expire_flag=1`, contradictory endpoint fields, or another record-level condition preventing the first state from being decided.

If R and D have equal timestamps, assign D first. If a later ICU stay occurs after S, S remains the first state for this same-admission estimand. Report R, D, S, N, U, the composite A=R-or-D, 12/24/48-hour CIFs, cause-specific hazards and separate R/D interactions. A fixed 7-day horizon and adverse/non-adverse U assignment are sensitivities.

### Fixed-landmark: 24-hour early deterioration states

The fixed endpoint is deliberately an operational ICU-record endpoint, not a clinical adjudication of rescue. Order first transitions in `(tF,tF+24h]):

- R: the earliest qualifying ICU-level rescue/readmission record: (a) a new target-pressor interval in `icu/inputevents` with target item ID and clinical `starttime > tF`; (b) a `procedureevents` interval with `starttime > tF` for intubation 224385, invasive ventilation 225792, non-invasive ventilation 225794, hemodialysis 225441, CRRT 225802, CVVHD 225803, peritoneal dialysis 225805, CVVHDF 225809 or SCUF 225955; or (c) a later ICU stay in `icu/icustays` with same `subject_id,hadm_id`, different `stay_id`, and `intime > tF`. Require valid finite clinical intervals for support starts, collapse exact duplicates, and retain component type and first time. A pressor or procedure already active across `tF` is not a new start;
- D: timed in-hospital death with `deathtime > tF` and `deathtime <= tF+24h`, no later than any tied R; when `dischtime` is non-null require death no later than discharge;
- S: alive hospital `dischtime` in `(tF,tF+24h]` before R/D, with consistent live status; S is the observation boundary because ICU support records do not provide complete ward-level rescue ascertainment;
- N: no R, D or S by 24 hours while ascertainable;
- U: missing/contradictory timed death or an endpoint record contradiction that prevents ordering the first state.

A later ICU admission and a support start at the same time are deduplicated as one R event with component flags. If R and D tie, D precedes R. The primary fixed estimand is the 24-hour CIF of A=R-or-D, with R, D, S and N separately reported. This endpoint does not claim that all clinical rescue is captured: ward oxygen escalation, unrecorded bedside decisions, treatment intent, appropriateness and preventability are unavailable.

## Matched transparent and learned alternatives

All models within each estimand use the same strict gated subjects, phenotype cells, source whitelist, endpoint labels, deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 split, preprocessing discipline, held-out test set and subject bootstrap. The fixed-landmark models use the same input variables as one another; the live-exit models do likewise. No test outcome selects thresholds, bins, features, clipping, imputation or model form.

B0 is a penalized discrete-time cause-specific complementary-log-log hazard model for R, D and S. It uses demographics and admission context (`anchor_age, gender, anchor_year_group, admission_type, admission_location`), first/last ICU care unit, index ICU elapsed time at the cutoff, terminal valid lactate/MAP/organ values, active support status, terminal masks and measurement-process summaries. It is the transparent latest-state baseline.

B1 adds order-blind summaries over twelve one-hour bins in the 12-hour feature window: medians, minima, maxima, SD/IQR, abnormal fractions, valid-bin counts, distinct measurement counts, pressor presence/minutes, Foley charting and laboratory testing indicators. B1 tests whether generic history and observation intensity explain the interaction without using temporal order.

Bmask receives context, terminal masks, counts, time-since-observation, test/process intensity, Foley-charting, pressor-presence and support-process history, but no physiologic values. If Bmask matches B1/Bdisc, a physiology-specific interpretation is weakened.

Bdisc is B1 plus the fixed persistent-lactate indicator, early/late lactate medians, fixed improving/indeterminate/worsening direction, early/late MAP and pressor changes, their lactate interaction, organ laboratory values and separate availability indicators. It is the primary transparent scientific model because it tests an interpretable cross-domain effect modification rather than a small predictive gain.

J1 is a small masked chronological GRU with six two-hour bins, using the same raw source whitelist, numeric values, masks, measurement/process indicators, support flags and context available to Bdisc. Hidden size 32 or 64, dropout, early stopping and five fixed random seeds are selected only on training/validation. The output is a discrete-time three-cause head for R, D and S. J1 is scientifically useful because it can reveal persistence, reversal, time-local coupling and nonlinear trajectories discarded by Bdisc and B1. A J1 gain without calibration, without Bdisc/Bmask separation, or without chronology-shuffle attenuation is not evidence of the lactate/perfusion mechanism.

A two-component diagonal-covariance mixture on early-to-late lactate/MAP/pressor changes was considered as a compact mechanistic/latent sensitivity. It is deferred from primary status because components can be driven by selective treatment recording and cannot validate a biological perfusion state. A larger transformer is also deferred: the scientific uncertainty is a fixed interaction and ascertainment boundary, not generic sequence scale. These are evidence-bound deferrals, not CPU/GPU prohibitions.

## Analysis and uncertainty

Fit cause-specific discrete-time hazards and derive 6-, 12-, 24- and, for live exit, 48-hour CIFs for R, D, S and A=R-or-D. Report the standardized phenotype interaction Delta, the four cell CIFs, component-specific contrasts, transition-aware multiclass and composite Brier scores, calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, AUROC/AUPRC as secondary operating characteristics, and paired Bdisc-B1, B1-B0 and J1-Bdisc differences on the held-out subjects.

Use 1,000 subject-level bootstrap replicates where the solver envelope permits; otherwise report the exact completed number and distinguish conditional test-prediction bootstrap from full refit uncertainty. Bootstrap all standardized contrasts, CIFs, Brier differences and calibration quantities. Report train/validation/test counts, event counts, U and indeterminate fractions, overlap, weight percentiles and ESS. Keep the fixed 2-point null region separate from uncertainty intervals; do not call a clinically meaningful result from a small AUROC change.

Required falsifications and robustness checks are:

- source dictionary label/link/unit audit, accepted and rejected units, impossible-value audit, interval clipping and duplicate audit;
- join integrity, first-index/first-exit audit, no subject crossing splits, prohibited-column scan and no post-cutoff clinical-time scan;
- chart-time-only versus chart-plus-store-time availability sensitivity;
- Bdisc versus Bmask and a phenotype-only/process-only control;
- within-subject early/late permutation preserving counts and masks;
- endpoint-label permutation within each partition with complete refit;
- 2.0 versus fixed 4.0 mmol/L lactate threshold;
- arterial-MAP-only, all-MAP, MAP-only, pressor-only, Foley corroboration, and 6-hour fixed-landmark sensitivities;
- 4/6/8-bin history, terminal-bin omission, and no-rate/no-amount pooling audits;
- live-exit q audit and fixed-landmark coverage-completeness weighting with overlap/ESS;
- separate R, D and S results, 7-day live-exit horizon, and adverse/non-adverse U bounds;
- fixed-landmark support components separately: pressor start, ventilation/intubation, dialysis and later ICU readmission;
- care-unit, anchor-year-group, age/sex and measurement-intensity strata where fixed support permits.

## Interpretation and falsification criteria

A supportive cross-estimand result requires the strict coverage and event gates for both reported estimands, Delta below -2 percentage points with an interval entirely below the null region for the relevant estimand, lower persistent-lactate A CIF under improving perfusion, calibrated held-out Bdisc improvement over B1, Bdisc exceeding Bmask, and stability across timing, component, coverage, selection and U-bound sensitivities. Stronger confidence requires concordance of the fixed early and live-exit estimates, but discordance is scientifically informative rather than automatically adverse.

A fixed-landmark-only result means the association is detectable before live-exit selection but does not establish discharge appropriateness. A live-exit-only result means it may be a late-state or exit-selection phenomenon and requires the q audit and further study before generalizing to early deterioration. Concordant estimates support a reproducible prognostic association across two time origins, not a causal mechanism.

Adverse evidence is a reliably reversed interaction beyond the null region, a signal explained by Bmask/process intensity, disappearance under available-by-cutoff timing, a failed label/unit/timestamp audit, persistence after outcome permutation, a J1 gain that survives chronology permutation, or a result confined to S/N rather than R/D. An adverse computational result challenges the operational hypothesis; it does not prove improving perfusion is protective or worsening perfusion causes rescue.

Inconclusive evidence is any coverage or event-support failure, sparse component events, high U or indeterminate fractions, incompatible units, poor overlap/ESS, unstable calibration, intervals wider than six percentage points, or disagreement that cannot distinguish selection from timing. A failed fixed-landmark gate does not weaken the live-exit estimand and cannot license moving the fixed time origin.

Computationally checkable claims are source/archive/member provenance, schema and dictionary labels, joins, time ordering, cohort flow, gate flags, phenotype construction, split integrity, model fits, predictions, CIF/Brier/calibration, contrasts, uncertainty, overlap and falsifications. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for microcirculatory dysfunction, lactate etiology, true circulatory stabilization, complete renal/respiratory recovery, treatment intent, fluid/pressor/ventilator/dialysis effects, rescue appropriateness or preventability, discharge policy, monitoring benefit, net utility and transportability.

## Alternative selection and method record

The selected parent’s live-exit Bdisc is retained because it answers a clinically consequential post-ICU risk question and has the strongest ascertainment/selection safeguards. The fixed early-landmark alternative is selected as a required robustness estimand, not as an unqualified replacement: it removes the possibility that conditioning on observed live exit defines the entire signal, uses a pre-outcome time origin, and aligns the endpoint with near-term deterioration. Its tradeoff is a less complete rescue label and possible sparse new-support events. This is why both estimands are retained and why the fixed gate can make only its own claim inconclusive.

The transparent B0/B1/Bdisc family and J1 use exact identical whitelist inputs within each estimand, with the same target and split. Bdisc exposes interpretable lactate-by-MAP/pressor effect modification; J1 can reveal temporal persistence, reversal and coupled trajectories that Bdisc discards. A learned gain alone is not a scientific win. The latent mixture and large transformer are deferred for the evidence-bound reasons above. A causal treatment-response model is deferred because the available inputevents do not provide reliable treatment intent, fluid responsiveness, counterfactual outcomes or adequate time-varying confounding control.

## Compute, deliverable and uncertainty of feasibility estimates

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted B0, B1, Bmask, Bdisc and J1 models for the gate-passing live-exit and fixed-landmark populations, held-out predictions, both estimand-specific CIF/interaction tables, component and U-bound sensitivities, selection/coverage manifests, uncertainty, falsifications, resource logs and a conclusion ledger. If either gate fails, completion is a complete audited flow and explicit not-estimable result for that estimand, not a smaller substitute study.

Measured during discovery are archive/catalog availability, table schemas, archive members, relevant dictionary labels, links and units. Unmeasured are full feature/event scans, strict-gate prevalence, event support, q/coverage overlap, ESS, repeated bootstrap runtime and J1 convergence. The future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, up to 8 allocated A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs and 28,800 seconds. Chunked extraction, B0/B1/Bdisc fits and competing-risk summaries are expected to be CPU-first but this is unverified. J1 is intentionally small and expected to be CPU-feasible, also unverified; if repeated seeds/bootstrap threaten the envelope, request an allocated GPU via `job_submit` and use `cuda:0` inside the allocation. Ordinary-shell CUDA absence is not evidence against feasibility. No GPU is required by the science, and no solver/proposer training is authorized.
