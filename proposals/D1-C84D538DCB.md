# Temporal order of respiratory and circulatory withdrawal at first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and fit an auditable, leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study of whether the *order* of two recorded support transitions separates early post-ICU pathways beyond the parent’s low-final-burden respiratory subtype contrast.

Completion requires all of the following, with no clinical result inferred from readiness:

- `run_manifest.json`: MIMIC snapshot and catalog hashes, source paths, archive members, schema hashes, item-label/unit assertions, software versions, split seeds, locked thresholds, timestamp tolerances, topology rules and output hashes.
- `cohort_audit.parquet`: one earliest eligible ICU stay per subject, joins, exclusions, support-transition eligibility, coverage, exposure class, and primary/audit strata.
- `hourly_features.parquet`: 12 one-hour rows in `[outtime-12h,outtime)`, including physiology, support evidence, source-specific observation masks, event counts and pre-index process covariates.
- `transition_ledger.parquet`: every candidate respiratory and vasoactive row/event, validity checks, evidence channel, time interval, transition-time uncertainty, contradiction and order assignment.
- `outcome_topology.parquet`: every candidate later ICU stay, transfer bridge, event time, topology class, tie status and lower/upper escalation endpoint.
- `cif_order_contrasts.parquet`: cause-specific and Aalen–Johansen estimates for the ordered exposure classes, standardized to the eligible low-burden population, with the partial-identification interval for escalation.
- `test_predictions.parquet` and `calibration_uncertainty.parquet`: locked patient-level test predictions, calibration, IPCW Brier scores, bootstrap intervals, overlap and effective sample size.
- `latent_order_diagnostics.parquet`: held-out state occupancy, transition-duration distributions, posterior entropy, mask-process diagnostics and seed stability for the alternative.
- `falsification.parquet` and `interpretation.md`: every prespecified negative control, sensitivity, bound and conclusion linked to computed outputs.

The actual scientific work is to estimate the ordered-transition competing-risk contrasts and determine whether a duration/order-aware model reveals reproducible, clinically interpretable separation that survives observation-process and topology checks. No association, utility, causality or fitted result is claimed in this proposal.

## Supported evidence and unresolved claim

The local data audit supports only that MIMIC contains dated ICU outtimes, later ICU stays, transfer intervals, charted respiratory physiology/support markers, vasoactive infusion intervals and procedure markers. The MIMIC metadata states that timestamps are subject-specific shifted dates with within-subject intervals preserved; cross-subject calendar alignment is invalid. The relevant item dictionary confirms the intended labels: O2 Flow (223834), Inspired O2 Fraction (223835), PEEP set (220339), Intubation (224385), Non-invasive Ventilation (225794), Extubation (227194), and vasoactive items norepinephrine (221906), epinephrine (221289, 229617), dopamine (221662), phenylephrine (221749, 229630, 229631, 229632) and vasopressin (222315).

The residual-instability seed and asynchronous-organ-recovery seed motivate the question, but they do not establish it. A charted procedure is not proof of continuous ventilation, an infusion interval is not proof of physiologic circulatory recovery, and absence of a row is not proof that support was absent. A later ICU stay is not adjudicated deterioration.

The parent supports a feasible matched contrast between invasive-liberation-compatible and oxygen-only recorded respiratory transitions, with a reachable respiratory-by-vasoactive cross-product and lower/upper transfer-topology bounds. It does not test whether the *temporal ordering* of respiratory and circulatory withdrawal carries information beyond their final joint state. The unresolved claim is:

> In adults reaching a first valid ICU outtime with low final recorded respiratory and MAP burden and adequate observation, among those with event-supported transitions in both domains, the order of recorded respiratory withdrawal and recorded vasoactive cessation identifies different early competing pathways: respiratory-first and circulatory-first patients will have different 48-hour cumulative incidences of bridge-confirmed ICU escalation, any ICU return, death and alive discharge, after adjustment for support intensity and observable discharge-process differences.

This is a retrospective prognostic/measurement claim about recorded transition order. It is not an effect of extubation, vasopressor cessation or ICU discharge. The directional prior is that respiratory-first may mark unresolved circulatory reserve and circulatory-first may mark unresolved respiratory reserve, but the study must treat either direction, a null, or a reversal as possible. The primary test is a two-sided contrast; the directional expectation is secondary and cannot rescue a failed primary test.

## Exposure construction: ordered recorded transitions

Keep every raw candidate row and every contradiction in the ledger. The primary exposure is not a claim of true organ recovery.

### Respiratory transition time

Construct a respiratory ledger from the locked respiratory item IDs and procedure IDs:

- Respiratory-positive evidence is O2 flow item 223834 with `valuenum > 2` L/min, FiO2 item 223835 with `valuenum > .21`, PEEP item 220339 with `valuenum > 0`, or an NIV/intubation procedure marker (225794/224385) with valid `starttime`.
- A respiratory transition is event-supported only if there is respiratory-positive evidence in the preceding six hours and one of:
  - extubation procedure 227194 with valid `starttime` (primary timestamp `starttime`, with `endtime` sensitivity);
  - a first qualifying non-positive respiratory chart in the final six-hour window: O2 flow <=2, FiO2 <=.21, and no PEEP-positive/NIV-positive evidence in the locked observation neighborhood, with at least two final-half respiratory observations;
  - a source-concordant positive-to-non-positive change on the same respiratory channel, retained as a separate lower-evidence subgroup.
- The primary respiratory event time is the procedure `starttime` for extubation or the `charttime` of the first qualifying non-positive chart for oxygen/FiO2. Tie duplicate chart rows at the same timestamp after retaining their source and value.
- Classify the respiratory channel as invasive-compatible, oxygen/FiO2-only, or other/unknown. Invasive-compatible requires extubation 227194 and preceding PEEP or FiO2 evidence; it remains compatibility evidence, never proof of continuous ventilation. O2/FiO2-only cannot be called extubation or liberation.

Do not assign an exact respiratory transition time from absence alone. The broader half-window state (positive evidence in preceding half, no positive evidence in final half) is an audit/sensitivity feature and is labeled “recorded withdrawal-compatible,” not “off.”

### Vasoactive cessation time

From `icu/inputevents`, retain only the locked vasoactive IDs: norepinephrine 221906; epinephrine 221289 and 229617; dopamine 221662; phenylephrine 221749, 229630, 229631 and 229632; vasopressin 222315.

A valid listed infusion interval requires non-null `starttime,endtime`, `starttime < endtime`, and overlap with `[outtime-12h,outtime)`. Its cessation time is `endtime), with interval status and `storetime` retained as process fields. If more than one vasoactive overlaps, preserve each interval and define the domain cessation as the latest valid endtime before outtime only when no locked vasoactive interval remains active at that time. If an interval extends through outtime, classify the domain as active/unknown, not ceased. A vasoactive cessation requires a valid preceding active interval; no-recorded-interval is never treated as cessation.

### Order classes and uncertainty

Let (t_R) be the qualifying recorded respiratory transition time and (t_V) the qualifying vasoactive cessation time. Use a prespecified time tolerance (delta=15) minutes, locked before test access:

- **Respiratory-first**: (t_R+delta < t_V).
- **Vasoactive-first**: (t_V+delta < t_R).
- **Near-synchronous**: (|t_R-t_V|ledelta).
- **Indeterminate**: one or both times lack event-supported evidence, timestamps conflict, an active interval overlaps outtime, or source coverage cannot distinguish the order.

The primary contrast is respiratory-first versus vasoactive-first. Near-synchronous and indeterminate are retained rather than collapsed; near-synchronous is a prespecified secondary exposure. Repeat with (delta=0) and 30 minutes, extubation `endtime`, and six-hour coarse order only as sensitivity analyses. Report the fraction excluded and the order-specific evidence channels.

To preserve reachability and comparability with the parent, also derive the parent’s independent respiratory states and vasoactive states from the same ledger, then form the reachable cross-product. Never define “joint withdrawal” by requiring mutually incompatible single-domain rules. The order analysis is an added field over patients with two supported domain transitions, not a replacement for the parent’s cross-product audit.

## Outcomes and transfer topology

For horizons (hin{6,24,48}) hours after outtime, define:

- (U_h): any later `icu/icustays.intime > outtime` for the same `subject_id,hadm_id` within (h);
- (L_h): a later ICU stay satisfying (U_h) and preceded by a documented non-ICU transfer bridge in `hosp/transfers`: non-null `careunit`, a qualifying interval beginning after outtime and ending before later ICU `intime`, bridge duration at least 30 minutes, and careunit distinct from the later ICU stay’s first and last careunit where the comparison is defined.

Record every bridge and all topology alternatives. The true clinical escalation (T_h) is unobserved and reported only as (L_hle T_hle U_h). Do not call any return planned, unplanned, deterioration or preventable.

The first event in each horizon is one of bridge-confirmed ICU escalation, topology-unconfirmed ICU return, in-hospital death before return, and alive hospital discharge before return. Apply a one-minute tie rule, with 0- and 5-minute sensitivities. For the order contrast A versus B, report the compatible risk-difference interval ([L_A-U_B, U_A-L_B]), not a midpoint. Primary interpretation requires evidence in (L); a result present only in (U) is evidence about record topology, not strong evidence of clinical escalation.

## Baseline and substantive alternative on identical inputs

Use fixed subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits with seeds 17, 29 and 43. Split by `subject_id` before any feature fitting; no patient contributes to more than one split. Thresholds, winsorization, missingness handling, model parameters and (delta) selection are fit/locked using training/validation only.

### Transparent baseline

Fit a pooled one-hour discrete-time cause-specific competing-hazard model for L, U-only, death and alive discharge, with a separate aggregate-return model. Inputs are exactly the derived 12 hourly rows and pre-index covariates: ordered exposure class, respiratory/vasoactive channel and evidence tier, final and preceding physiology, support intensity/duration, source-specific masks and row counts, transition timestamps/uncertainty, pre-index transfer-process proxies, admission context, age, gender, anchor-year group and ICU LOS. Use penalized logistic regression with prespecified splines or linear terms and no post-index variables. Report nested models (physiology/support-only, observation-only, process-only and full) to show which information carries the contrast. Standardize held-out CIFs to the primary low-burden, two-transition population.

### Scientifically meaningful alternative

Fit an observation-aware factorized hidden semi-Markov model (HSMM) on the same event ledger and hourly rows. Its latent respiratory states are invasive-compatible, PEEP/NIV-supported, oxygen-only, recorded-off and unknown; its hemodynamic states are vasoactive, recorded-off and unknown. It has duration-dependent transitions over eight six-hour intervals, explicit order/discordance states, and a separate observation-mask/intensity process. Post-landmark location is ICU, documented non-ICU or unknown. The model’s fitting target is the same four cause-specific post-outtime event hazards/CIFs and held-out subject outcomes as the baseline, not reconstruction of unavailable physiologic truth.

The HSMM can reveal whether ordered duration and discordance patterns explain risk that half-window summaries lose, and whether an apparent order signal is generated by the observation process. It cannot recover unrecorded support, clinician intent, true ward monitoring or adjudicated deterioration. Compare models on held-out CIF calibration, IPCW Brier score, log loss where defined, overlap, uncertainty and stability—not on a small predictive gain alone. The baseline remains the primary scientific result if latent states have high posterior entropy, poor seed stability, weak held-out calibration or fail to reproduce the ordered contrast.

A GRU/TCN is deferred: it could learn flexible sequences, but without an additional clinical label or richer measurement process it would add capacity without a clearer answer to the order-versus-observation question. Causal treatment/discharge-policy estimation, validated intent NLP, continuous waveforms, MIMIC-CXR image modeling, external validation and outside-hospital outcomes are deferred because the required evidence is unavailable, not because of a blanket CPU/GPU rule.

## Uncertainty, falsification and interpretation gates

Estimate subject-bootstrap 95% intervals, split stability across the three seeds, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier, event counts, group overlap/effective sample size, unknown/contradictory rates, topology-bound width and HSMM posterior entropy. Use censoring weights fit without post-index leakage and report sensitivity to discharge censoring.

Prespecified falsifications and sensitivities are:

- permute respiratory-versus-vasoactive order within matched support-intensity strata while preserving exposure counts;
- shuffle within-window event order while preserving each domain’s marginal counts and observation masks;
- remove procedure markers, then analyze chart-only evidence;
- mask process features, remove physiology, remove source masks, and fit observation-only/process-only models;
- substitute `storetime` for `charttime/starttime/endtime` as a negative control;
- use pseudo-landmarks 6 and 12 hours before outtime with outcomes shifted consistently;
- vary (deltain{0,15,30}) minutes, bridge duration ({0,15,30,60}) minutes, tie tolerance ({0,1,5}) minutes, and respiratory windows ({6,12,24}) hours;
- compare lower-bound bridge-confirmed escalation L with upper-bound any-return U, including transfer permutations and +6-hour transfer shifts;
- stratify by first/last ICU careunit, admission type and anchor-year group, without cross-subject calendar comparisons.

Supportive evidence requires adequate event support and positivity, a non-wide lower-bound contrast with uncertainty excluding the locked null when a directional claim is made, agreement in direction and magnitude between baseline and alternative, calibration acceptable in held-out data, order sensitivity not explained by masks/process variables, and attenuation under order permutation. The conclusion must identify whether it is supported for L, only for U, or neither.

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversal, disappearance after observation/process adjustment, dependence on one channel or procedure, persistence under order permutation, poor calibration, or a signal only in U. A signal only in U is adverse to the stronger clinical escalation interpretation but may document transfer topology.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse ordered cells, poor positivity, high indeterminate/unknown prevalence, broad ([L_A-U_B,U_A-L_B]) intervals, unstable timestamp/window frontiers, high latent entropy, split instability or failure to reproduce the contrast across methods. Do not merge sparse groups or treat model disagreement as confirmation.

Computation can establish the recorded-data estimand, reproducible feature construction, held-out prediction/calibration, competing-risk estimates, topology bounds and sensitivity consistency. It cannot establish extubation success, physiologic recovery, clinician intent, readiness for discharge, preventability, causal effects of support withdrawal, clinical utility, planned versus unplanned return, or outcomes after leaving the hospital. Those claims require expert adjudication of charts/clinical trajectories, unavailable ward monitoring and treatment-limitation evidence, external validation, and likely a prospective or causal study.

## Compute and method-selection record

Measured discovery facts are the configured 7,200-second discovery allowance, the exact archive/header/item-label audit, read-only availability of the listed members, and the declared solver envelope of at most 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB RAM, 8 A100 80-GB GPUs and 28,800 seconds. Full extraction, bootstrap and HSMM fitting have not been run here; their runtime is unverified. The baseline is expected to be CPU-suitable. Begin the HSMM with one CPU allocation and profile; request one allocated A100 only if the actual sequence fit is matrix- or repetition-limited. Ordinary shell CUDA absence is not evidence of hardware absence; allocated jobs use `cuda:0` if a GPU is requested. No GPU is mandatory.

The alternative was selected because duration and ordered discordance are the scientific object, not because it is more complex. It uses exactly the same source-derived observations, has the same target/splits/outcomes, and exposes latent-state identifiability and observation-process failure modes. The transparent baseline was retained as the primary estimand anchor because it is auditable and less assumption-dependent. The flexible GRU/TCN was explicitly deferred; evidence to revisit it would be a well-supported, adequately populated order phenotype that remains unexplained by the HSMM and a documented computational budget for a preregistered sequence ablation. Clinical causal models, NLP and unavailable modalities should be revisited only after adjudicated labels or new data become available.
