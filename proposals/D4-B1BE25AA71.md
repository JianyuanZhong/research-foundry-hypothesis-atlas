## Frozen question and evidence boundary

The tested claim is whether, among UK Biobank participants who were at or above the protocol-derived q=60 threshold on the direction-specific target marker at baseline and returned for a qualifying first-repeat assessment 2–8 years later, baseline discordance on the other marker is associated with first-repeat crossing below 60 on the target marker after the frozen adjustment.

The strongest computable evidence is an association within selected qualifying repeat attenders. This execution cannot establish CKD, chronicity, measured GFR decline, renal specificity, causality, benefit from monitoring or treatment, clinical utility, risk among nonattenders, or transport to the baseline-eligible population. Assay reference-material traceability and clinical adjudication remain unavailable.

## Selected-attender estimates and frozen multiplicity family

For A, standardized selected-attender risk was 0.0172307 under E=0 and 0.0374283 under E=1, giving `RD_A^S=0.0201976` (2.02 percentage points). The participant-frequency bootstrap had 2,000 successful replicates and no failures. Its percentile 95% interval was `[0.0107114, 0.0314255]`; its two-direction Bonferroni simultaneous interval was `[0.00871668, 0.0329637]`. The frozen null-centered two-sided bootstrap p-value was 0.00049975 and Holm-adjusted p-value was 0.00099950. A was rejected by the frozen sequential Holm rule.

For B, standardized risk was 0.0349350 under E=0 and 0.0521354 under E=1, giving `RD_B^S=0.0172004` (1.72 percentage points). Its bootstrap also had 2,000 successful replicates and no failures. The percentile 95% interval was `[-0.00447163, 0.0408221]`; the simultaneous interval was `[-0.00714566, 0.0441957]`. The raw and Holm-adjusted p-value was 0.137431. B was not rejected and is inconclusive, not evidence of no association.

## Attendance diagnostic and support-gate qualification

Attendance was not modeled into weights, imputations, transported outcomes, or all-baseline risks. For A, qualifying-repeat proportions were 3.604% in E=0 and 2.058% in E=1. For B they were 3.604% in E=0 and 3.250% in E=1. These differences reinforce that the reported estimands are selected-attender quantities only; they do not identify nonattender outcomes or an all-`T_d` contrast.

A crucial qualification is that the parent's phrase “stable agreement with the crude selected-attender risk difference” had no numeric tolerance. The execution's mechanical gate operationalized this only as common sign. Although signs agreed, magnitudes did not: A's crude RD was 0.139259 versus adjusted 0.020198 (absolute attenuation 0.119061), and B's crude RD was 0.157859 versus adjusted 0.017200 (absolute attenuation 0.140659). Therefore this child does not claim magnitude stability or certify that the underdefined stability gate passed. It reports A as a robust multiplicity-controlled positive fixed-model association, while explicitly treating the large attenuation as adverse to any stronger claim about unadjusted robustness. B fails the Holm and simultaneous-interval gates and remains inconclusive.

## Interpretation and falsification

The strongest supported conclusion is direction-specific: among qualifying repeat attenders with baseline creatinine-derived eGFR at least 60, baseline cystatin-C-derived eGFR below 60 was associated with an approximately 2.02-percentage-point higher model-standardized risk of first-repeat creatinine-derived eGFR below 60 under the frozen adjustment, with multiplicity-controlled uncertainty excluding zero. The reciprocal direction was not established. The large crude-to-adjusted attenuation means the observed crude contrast was largely explained by the fixed covariate distribution and prevents a claim of stable magnitude across crude and adjusted analyses.

A null or reversed adjusted A estimate, simultaneous interval including zero, Holm non-rejection, failed support, separation, or nonreproducible bootstrap would have been adverse or inconclusive; these occurred inferentially for B but not A. Neither result resolves assay traceability, chronicity, measured filtration, selection mechanisms, causal effects, clinical actionability, or external validity. Those stronger claims require laboratory/clinical adjudication and another study with appropriate outcomes and follow-up.
