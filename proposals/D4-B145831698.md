## Interpretation and falsification

First compute a local Episode-95 result, then apply the parent hierarchy.

- **Sex-age transportability supportive:** all parent-required kidney, record-density, competing-death, biochemical, and deployment gates/labels needed for the frozen hierarchy are supportive; all Episode-95 gates pass; the sharp lower bound and simultaneous lower limit for `Delta_std` are strictly above 0; every cell’s sharp lower bound and simultaneous lower limit is at least 0; and at least one cell’s simultaneous lower limit is strictly above 0. - **Sex-age adverse/reversal:** all relevant computational gates pass and either `Delta_std` has sharp upper bound and simultaneous upper limit strictly below 0, or any cell has sharp upper bound and simultaneous upper limit strictly below 0. If one cell is adverse while another has a simultaneous lower limit above 0, additionally label `sex_age_mixed_direction`. This falsifies an unqualified nonreversal claim. It does not prove AF1 superior for an individual or that AFG1 causes harm.
- **Heterogeneous but nonreversing:** no cell is adverse, all point contrasts are nonnegative, and at least one adjusted interaction interval excludes 0, but the strict supportive rule is not met. Report as inconclusive for universal transportability with evidence of heterogeneity; do not call it fair or equivalent.
- **Sex-age inconclusive:** any integrity, supportability, missingness, sparse-event, variance, finite-draw, decomposition, bound, multiplicity, parent, or control gate fails; bounds cross the relevant decision region; only marginal groups are informative; or the pattern meets neither support nor adversity. ## Limits and next evidence

A supportive result would support only the statement that, in this selected UKB repeat-assessment holdout, the recorded E18 enrichment contrast was positive after standardizing to the same observed sex-age distribution and showed no statistically resolved reversal in the four prespecified cells. It would justify designing an external prospective comparison with prespecified recruitment and monitoring by age and sex.

An adverse result would reject an unqualified subgroup-consistency claim in this snapshot. It would not show that AFG1 causes kidney events or harms a protected group. An inconclusive result, especially from sparse cells, means the available holdout cannot qualify subgroup consistency and must not be converted into reassurance.

No result here establishes equity. Field 31 is binary recorded sex, not gender; age/sex are already model and eGFR-equation inputs; UKB repeat-assessment participation is selected; inpatient N18 is administrative and not adjudicated CKD; capture, censor-individual censoring, outpatient/primary-care events, kidney replacement therapy, migration, cause-specific mortality, and workflow timing are incomplete or unavailable. FFM and grip have different acquisition burdens and missingness mechanisms. Fairness additionally requires access, burden, error costs, patient preferences, socioeconomic and racial/ethnic context, disability/accessibility review, and clinically meaningful harms and benefits.

Stronger claims require external cohorts with broader representation; complete inpatient/outpatient/primary-care/kidney-replacement and censoring linkage; repeated timed creatinine, cystatin C, and UACR; measured GFR; nephrologist/chart adjudication; calibration and decision-curve review by subgroup; workflow, cost, accessibility, and ethics expertise; and ultimately a prospective comparative or randomized testing-strategy study with patient-centered outcomes.
