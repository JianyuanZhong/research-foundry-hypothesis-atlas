# Does laboratory surveillance add information when recorded vital trajectories appear to recover?

Status: Episode-44 substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test of ordering tests, a physiologic-truth study, a treatment recommendation, a hospital-quality ranking, or evidence that an alert or additional surveillance improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest evidence currently available is outcome-blind source/schema inspection and inherited feasibility auditing. The eICU snapshot contains patient-linked laboratory result and revision offsets, periodic and aperiodic vital observations, recorded ICU discharge status/offset, hospital descriptors and sparse carePlanEOL timing. No model has been fitted in discovery. Existing work in this line can test whether revision-safe laboratory observation timing transports beyond recorded vital state and monitoring intensity, but it does not answer whether the laboratory increment is most informative when the recorded vital trajectory is failing to recover or when it appears stable/improving.

The unresolved claim is:

> At ICU-relative minute 360, among adult first ICU stays, does revision-safe laboratory observation history add a death-specific, held-out-hospital signal beyond recorded vital level, vital observation intensity and vital trajectory—and is that increment larger in episodes whose recorded vital trajectory appears stable/improving than in episodes with clearly non-improving recorded trajectories?

This is clinically consequential because a laboratory-process signal in a visibly deteriorating patient may simply restate severity and have limited incremental review value. A signal during an apparently recovering recorded vital trajectory could identify a subgroup for retrospective review of unrepresented biochemical or care-process information. Conversely, if the signal is strongest only during non-recovery, it may be a severity/workflow marker rather than evidence of occult risk. Either result refines how a future silent review system should be interpreted; neither establishes that more tests or a different treatment would help.

The substantive advance over the parent is a prespecified effect-modification question about *when* laboratory observation history is informative, using a reproducible recorded-trajectory phenotype rather than another clock or another pooled transport score. It separates incremental patient-linked information from a generic “sicker patients are monitored more” explanation while preserving the parent’s conservative revision handling.

## Evidence-supported and untested claims

Available evidence supports only the existence of the listed fields and recorded outcomes. It does not establish that a result offset equals specimen collection, that revision timing equals information availability, that missing observations mean physiologic absence, that a vital trajectory is true recovery, that clinicians intended any test, or that ICU discharge status captures all mortality.

The primary hypothesis (H1) predicts that adding revision-safe laboratory process to a vital state-plus-trajectory baseline improves death-specific cumulative-incidence calibration and integrated competing-risk Brier score in held-out hospitals, with a prespecified interaction: the increment is nonzero and at least as large in the stable/improving recorded-vital stratum as in the non-improving stratum. The neutral hypothesis is no incremental or no effect-modified information. This is a prognostic hypothesis about recorded outcomes, not a causal or biological hypothesis.

## Models and scientifically informative alternative

The transparent baseline B_v uses pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards with admission context, landmark vital levels, aggregate vital observation intensity, recorded trajectory channels/stratum, landmark laboratory state, carePlanEOL channels, and no patient-level laboratory-process timing channels beyond a minimal count/coverage audit needed to define the observation set.

The primary incremental model B_vP adds the revision-safe laboratory process channels (including conservative availability timing) and a prespecified interaction between the laboratory-process score and the frozen trajectory stratum. B_vP and B_v use identical rows, codebook, splits, outcome intervals, preprocessing rules and vital channels. B_result is a paired clock sensitivity replacing availability timing with result timing on identical retained rows. A matched vital-process interaction model and a lab-process-only model test whether the claimed interaction is generic monitoring intensity or an artifact of combining domains.

The substantive learned alternative T_vP is a small one-layer six-bin GRU, with separate death/discharge heads, using exactly the same lab, vital, trajectory, carePlanEOL, admission and missingness inputs; it has no hospital identifier. A parameter-matched non-temporal MLP is mandatory. T can reveal whether persistence, late-vs-early ordering, cross-vital/lab co-movement and nonlinear interactions are lost by the transparent additive and interaction terms. It is retained as scientific evidence only if it improves held-out death calibration/Brier over B_vP with uncertainty compatible with improvement, survives time-order shuffle and adds a stable interpretable pattern summary. Otherwise B_vP remains the primary inference and T is reported as a negative/inconclusive branch. CPU is appropriate for streaming aggregation and elastic-net; one allocated A100 with explicit `cuda:0` placement is optional for T. GPU use is not a merit criterion.

A mechanistic recovery state-space model is deferred because no verified specimen/order timestamps, assay units, continuous waveform or adjudicated physiologic state exists. The required dependency for revisiting it is an external timestamped, unit-aware source with expert-defined recovery states.

## Splits, uncertainty and fixed review diagnostic

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals. Keep all `uniquepid` records together. Within each development fold use a 75/25 hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Learn the 64-key codebook, field-support rule, scaling, imputation, trajectory median, regularization, calibration and thresholds only in the fit portion. No outcome-derived analyte or trajectory-field selection is permitted.

Primary uncertainty is 95% intervals from at least 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions, or a prespecified hierarchical hospital/fold model if bootstrap is unstable. Report macro-hospital and patient-weighted secondary estimates, fold/hospital episode counts, death/discharge/censor counts, and trajectory-stratum support.

At landmark minute 360, compare B_v and B_vP at predicted death-CIF thresholds 0.01, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10 with equal review burden: reviews per 100 episodes, recorded-death capture, PPV, false-review burden and number needed to review. This is a label-relative retrospective review diagnostic, not net benefit or clinical utility. A threshold cell is unsupported unless it has >=200 held-out episodes, >=20 recorded deaths, >=20 alive discharges and >=20 hospitals.

## Falsification criteria

All outputs below are prespecified and saved with the interpretation trace:

1. Permute six lab-bin labels within patient, preserving total counts, key composition, revision channels and missingness.
2. Reassign lab-process histories across patients within hospital and baseline B_v risk strata, preserving hospital process distributions but breaking patient linkage.
3. Permute the recorded vital-bin order within patient and rerun the trajectory/GRU analysis; preserve marginal observations and counts.
4. Fit matched vital-process and vital-process-by-trajectory interaction models. A comparable or larger vital-process increment weakens the lab-specific interpretation.
5. Compare death and alive-discharge increments. A large equal/larger discharge increment suggests generic length-of-stay or workflow information.
6. Repeat with carePlanEOL restriction/challenge, treating absent documentation as unknown; report sparse-support failure as inconclusive.
7. Repeat on common-analyte, revision-known-only and interface-stratified subsets; compare availability and result clocks on identical rows. Any row with revision >360 is only a leakage audit.
8. Mask the (300,360] vital and lab bins and then mask early bins to test near-landmark leakage/artifact.
9. Run T order shuffle and parameter-matched non-temporal controls.
10. Repeat by hospital surveillance regime and leave one supported regime out; do not use regime labels as patient predictors.
11. Enforce no post-360 rows, no Apache mortality/APACHE APS fields, no hospital ID predictors, no target-fold preprocessing and no outcome-dependent codebook/trajectory-field choice.

Supportive evidence requires all support gates; B_vP must show uncertainty-compatible improvement in death-specific integrated Brier and calibration without material harm, with a pre-specified stable/improving-stratum increment at least as large as the non-improving increment; discharge increment smaller; attenuation after lab-link and temporal permutations; no matched vital-process replacement; and stability across revision/common-analyte/interface/regime restrictions. It supports a recorded trajectory-conditioned prognostic association, not occult physiologic deterioration or a surveillance intervention.

Adverse evidence is a null/adverse B_vP increment, interaction reversal or instability, unchanged performance after lab/vital order permutation, a larger increment in discharge or matched vital process, concentration in one hospital/interface, or reliance on post-landmark revisions. These results reject the proposed interpretation even if a model discriminates well.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed event/stratum/overlap gates, intervals spanning zero and the meaningful effect, unstable hospital folds, insufficient two-window vital support, excessive revision missingness, irreproducible outcome-blind field/codebook rules, or an unresolvable availability/result discrepancy. A complex model winning without the falsification pattern is not supportive.

## Actual scientific deliverable and evidence limits

The solver must newly fit B_v, B_vP, B_result, matched vital-process controls and conditional T; create cohort-flow and data-quality counts; save fold-specific codebooks/trajectory-field manifests; produce held-out death/discharge hazards and CIFs; calculate Brier/calibration/fixed-burden metrics with hospital-cluster uncertainty; execute every falsification and sensitivity; and save an interpretation trace mapping each conclusion to an output file, metric/row and interval. The inherited audit recorded 200,860 patient rows, 39,132,532 lab rows, 146,671,643 periodic-vital rows, 25,075,075 aperiodic-vital rows, 1,434 carePlanEOL rows and 209 hospital rows including headers. The source files remain read-only.

Harbor can verify joins, offsets, cohort arithmetic, fitting, held-out predictions, uncertainty, falsification execution and whether conclusions follow from outputs. It cannot establish true physiology, specimen/order timing, clinician intent, complete goals-of-care status, unrecorded death, causality, clinical benefit or external validity. Those require timestamped order/collection data, assay units, expert adjudication, an external health system and a prospective silent-mode/impact or randomized study.
