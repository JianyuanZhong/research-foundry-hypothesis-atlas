## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

Transferring a patient out of the ICU is a high-stakes, weakly-standardised decision; unplanned ICU
readmission and post-discharge death are the adverse outcomes it is meant to prevent. Clinicians judge
"readiness" dynamically — is oxygen demand still climbing, are vitals still swinging? Static severity
scores ignore that trajectory. The open question: **does a dynamic residual-instability footprint in the
fixed 24 h before ICU discharge carry prognostic information beyond a static last-day severity snapshot,
or is the apparent signal an artifact of overall severity and of observation intensity** (sicker patients
are measured more often, which mechanically inflates any variability summary)? [K3] shows, on MIMIC-IV,
that apparent readmission predictors can be non-actionable severity/frailty/administrative confounding.
Resolving this decides whether discharge-readiness tools must model trajectories or can rely on levels.

## 2. Leading claim vs strongest rival (discriminating observation)

- **Leading claim (H1):** among patients discharged alive from the ICU, a prespecified dynamic
  instability footprint in the 24 h before ICU discharge adds held-out discrimination for 48 h unplanned
  ICU readmission / 7-day post-discharge death beyond a static last-day severity model.
- **Strongest rival (H0):** the footprint is fully explained by (a) overall severity/comorbidity and
  (b) observation intensity (measurement/review frequency), i.e. sampling, not a distinct physiologic
  state [K3].
- **Discriminating observation:** the held-out **incremental** discrimination (ΔAUROC / ΔAUPRC) of the
  dynamic model over the static model, and whether that increment survives an observation-frequency
  control (M-obs) and a permuted-label negative control.
- **What each result would / would not establish:** a positive increment establishes PREDICTIVE utility
  only — not that delaying discharge or lightening support improves outcomes (that needs prospective /
  target-trial work). A null increment does not prove instability is meaningless; it bounds what routine
  pre-discharge vitals encode. [K1] predicts 48 h readmission from pre-discharge data with an opaque
  ensemble (not MIMIC-IV); [K2] shows the readmission-prediction literature is heterogeneous and
  high-risk-of-bias, so an incremental, control-tested claim is the substantive advance.

## 5. Method ladder (same question, explicit comparison)

- **B0 — static logistic:** last-24 h SOFA-surrogate levels (platelets, bilirubin, creatinine, MAP/pressor
  proxy from chartevents), nearest-to-t0 lactate `50813`, age, admission_type, Charlson, ICU LOS.
- **B1 — static + summary instability:** B0 plus hand-engineered instability features over `[t0-24h, t0)`:
  SD / range / slope of HR, SBP, RR, SpO2; count of SpO2 < 90 %; FiO2 and O2-flow trend/escalation.
- **A1 — sequence model:** a GRU over the irregular 24 h multivariate series (masked time-since-t0
  encoding for the verified vitals/O2 channels), predicted on the same held-out split.
  **What A1 can reveal that B0/B1 cannot:** whether the *temporal ordering/shape* of instability (e.g.
  late-window acceleration vs an early spike) carries information that scalar variability summaries
  compress away — i.e. whether the signal is trajectory-structured or level/review-structured.
- **Controls (same question, no new estimand):** (M-obs) observation-frequency-only model using counts
  of measurements/reviews (value-free) — if the dynamic increment is reproduced by M-obs alone it is
  sampling, not physiology; (perm) label-permuted negative control — increments must collapse to ~0.

## 6. Evaluation, split, uncertainty and prespecified rules

- Split: locked 70/30 by t0. **Observed boundary: t0 = 2169-10-11 22:55:40; development n=43,726,
  held-out n=18,740.**
- Uncertainty: 1000-sample bootstrap CIs for AUROC/AUPRC and for the paired Δ(B1−B0), Δ(A1−B0).
- **Supportive:** Δ(A1−B0) AUROC >= 0.02 with 95 % bootstrap CI excluding 0 on the held-out split, AND
  the increment survives M-obs (M-obs adds little when added alongside A1) AND the permuted-label
  increment collapses to ~0.
- **Adverse:** Δ(A1−B0) <= 0.01, or CI includes 0, or the increment is materially attenuated (>50 % of
  the gap removed) by adding M-obs — i.e. the rival H0 explains the footprint.
- **Inconclusive:** wide CI / miscalibration / too few held-out events to separate the two (see gate).
