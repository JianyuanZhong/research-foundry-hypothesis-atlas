# Provisional repair: discordant renal recovery after AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with an acute kidney injury episode in MIMIC-IV, patients whose creatinine has returned toward baseline but whose urine output and/or fluid balance remain abnormal at a prespecified 48-hour post-landmark assessment will have a higher risk of subsequent in-hospital death or persistent/recurrent kidney dysfunction than patients with concordant creatinine and urine-output recovery, after accounting for baseline illness severity and treatment context.

This is a hypothesis, not an observed result. The clinical opening is that creatinine-based recovery may be a misleading surrogate: creatinine can fall because of dilution, altered production, or recovery while residual tubular dysfunction or fluid overload persists. Resolving this would clarify whether a single creatinine criterion is sufficient for judging renal recovery and for deciding whether continued monitoring or renal-risk assessment is warranted.

Define creatinine recovery, urine-output recovery, and fluid-balance recovery using fixed item IDs and time windows. Compare discordant versus concordant recovery groups for subsequent in-hospital death and persistent/recurrent kidney dysfunction, with a prespecified severity-adjusted model and sensitivity analyses for measurement missingness and treatment. The hypothesis is supported if discordance is associated with materially worse outcomes beyond the prespecified effect threshold; it is challenged if the association is null or reverses after adequate adjustment; it is inconclusive if event counts, valid landmarks, or measurement coverage are insufficient.

A substantive alternative is a time-to-event or recurrent-event model that uses the trajectory of creatinine, urine output, and fluid balance (with missingness indicators and treatment time-varying covariates) to test whether the ordering and persistence of discordance, rather than a single 48-hour snapshot, carries prognostic information. The alternative could reveal whether residual dysfunction is a dynamic process or merely a threshold artifact; it must be compared on the same cohort, outcomes, split, uncertainty, and calibration/decision criteria. Neither method establishes causality.
