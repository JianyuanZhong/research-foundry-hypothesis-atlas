# Fixed-time eICU label-to-action audit: no reproducible positive 12-hour de-escalation signal

## Status, parents, and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the first parent’s completed negative finding: the eICU fields do not support a defensible durable-liberation association because independent liberation markers are sparse and strict 48-hour observation is incomplete. It does not retry, bridge, or reinterpret that endpoint.

The second parent established that two narrower outcomes are computable in the frozen 540-row cohort. This child completes the deferred association analysis, including exact cohort/exposure verification, available-case ascertainment, restriction to hospitals with both labels, uncertainty, pre-D trajectory comparison, component decomposition, removal of the dominant peak-pressure component, landmark strata, and leave-one-hospital-out checks.

The strongest new claim supported is adverse to the directional hypothesis: in the only three hospitals with adequate label overlap, the exact daily-evaluation label did not identify a reproducible excess of independently charted 12-hour ventilator-setting or sedation-infusion-rate de-escalation. The prespecified setting composite was less frequent after the daily-evaluation label, but that contrast was dominated by peak-pressure changes and became small and imprecise when peak pressure was removed. Sedation-rate results were imprecise and reversed across hospitals. Thus these labels cannot be treated as a validated positive implementation-fidelity marker in this snapshot.

This is a noncausal chart-association and measurement result. It is not evidence about actual SBT performance, true extubation, durable liberation, bedside delivery, appropriateness, or patient benefit.

## Clinical question, hypothesis, and evidence boundary

Among the parent’s strictly screened ventilated person-landmarks, does the first exact care-plan label `Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation`, versus `Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial`, at fixed decision time D predict more independently recorded ventilator-setting de-escalation or same-drug sedation-infusion-rate de-escalation during `(D,D+720]` minutes?

Directional hypothesis: the daily-evaluation label will be followed by a higher probability of each charted de-escalation outcome, after restricting to hospitals that use both labels and accounting descriptively for pre-D physiology, landmark, decision delay, and repeated person-landmarks.

Falsification/adverse criteria are: a null or inverse contrast with clinically informative uncertainty; no larger post-D contrast than the analogous pre-D trajectory diagnostic; dependence on peak-pressure charting; reversal across hospitals or landmarks; or differential ascertainment that prevents a stable comparison. The executed results meet several adverse/inconclusive criteria and do not support the directional hypothesis.

### Ventilator-setting outcome

For each row and conceptual component, require a numeric pre-D value strictly before D and a numeric value in `(D,D+720]`; an unpaired component is unavailable, never “no action.” A qualifying reduction is FiO2 by at least 5 percentage points, PEEP/PEEP-CPAP by at least 1, pressure support by at least 1, set ventilator rate by at least 1, or peak inspiratory pressure by at least 1. The primary composite is any qualifying reduction among paired components. A locked sensitivity removes peak pressure because the feasibility scan showed that component supplies most events. Component-specific results are mandatory.

The analogous pre-D trajectory diagnostic compares `(D-1440,D-720]` with `(D-720,D]`. It tests whether trajectories were already different before label assignment; it is not a no-treatment control and cannot validate causal exchangeability.

### Sedation-infusion-rate outcome

Sedative/analgesic infusion names are matched to propofol, fentanyl, midazolam, dexmedetomidine, lorazepam, ketamine, or morphine. Require a numeric same-exact-drug-name rate in `(D-1440,D)` and a numeric same-drug rate in `(D,D+720]`. A reduction is at least 10% of baseline or at least 0.01 documented rate units. The estimand among paired rows is conditional on rate documentation. It is not a sedation interruption, because no complete stop/hold algorithm is available and units may differ across drugs and institutions.

## Executed analysis and uncertainty

The executable job was `[research job]`; it completed without row sampling. Person-landmark is the analysis unit. Descriptive risk differences compare evaluation minus no-trial. Primary rows are the three overlap hospitals and outcome-available rows. Hospital-standardized risk differences weight hospital-specific contrasts by analyzed hospital row count. A secondary linear-probability model includes hospital and landmark fixed effects plus standardized pre-D FiO2, PEEP, SpO2 median, respiratory rate, MAP median, and decision delay; uncertainty is clustered by `uniquepid` to account for repeated landmarks. With only three overlap hospitals, neither conventional hospital-clustered inference nor generalization to the 70-hospital source population is defensible; fixed-hospital results and person-clustered intervals are therefore descriptive uncertainty, not population-level causal inference.

### Ventilator-setting de-escalation

In overlap hospitals, primary setting ascertainment was 127/130 (97.7%) evaluation and 170/173 (98.3%) no-trial, difference -0.6 percentage points (Wald 95% interval -3.8 to +2.7). Among available rows, reductions were 39/127 (30.7%) versus 72/170 (42.4%): risk difference -11.6 points (Wald 95% interval -22.6 to -0.7). All three hospital-specific contrasts were inverse: -17.6, -10.7, and -5.0 points; the row-count-standardized contrast was -10.7 points. The pre-D-adjusted, person-clustered model estimated -10.9 points (95% interval -23.0 to +1.2; p=0.078). This does not support the hypothesized positive label-to-de-escalation association.

The result is not robust as a general support-reduction signal. Removing peak pressure leaves 5/126 (4.0%) versus 12/170 (7.1%), risk difference -3.1 points (95% interval -8.2 to +2.1), hospital-standardized -2.9 points, and adjusted -3.2 points (95% interval -8.6 to +2.1). Peak-pressure alone was 36/118 (30.5%) versus 63/165 (38.2%), difference -7.7 points (95% interval -18.8 to +3.5). In the overlap hospitals, FiO2, PEEP, pressure-support, and rate reductions were individually sparse and imprecise. Across all 540 rows, the primary available-case difference was -5.2 points (91/282 versus 85/227; 95% interval -13.5 to +3.2), while the no-peak difference reversed to +2.9 points (28/281 versus 16/227; 95% interval -1.9 to +7.7). This component and population dependence blocks a stable claim that either label identifies broader ventilator-support de-escalation.

The pre-D diagnostic in overlap hospitals was already common: 63/130 (48.5%) versus 76/172 (44.2%), difference +4.3 points (95% interval -7.1 to +15.6), whereas the post-D primary contrast was inverse. Among all rows observed in both windows, the post-D difference was -4.8 points and the pre-D difference +1.7 points; the difference of differences was -6.5 points. In the overlap-hospital adjusted difference-score model it was -14.1 points (95% interval -32.0 to +3.8). This is adverse to a positive implementation-fidelity interpretation, although it does not estimate a causal effect.

Landmark-specific primary setting differences in overlap hospitals were -22.5, -11.8, -7.2, -27.8, and +12.4 points from 24 through 120 hours, each based on small strata. The late reversal and wide intervals reinforce that the label is not a stable generalizable positive marker.

### Sedation-infusion-rate de-escalation

Paired-rate ascertainment in overlap hospitals was 70/130 (53.8%) evaluation and 110/173 (63.6%) no-trial, difference -9.7 points (95% interval -20.9 to +1.4). Among paired rows, reductions were 10/70 (14.3%) versus 15/110 (13.6%): difference +0.6 points (95% interval -9.8 to +11.1). The hospital-standardized difference was +5.1 points, but hospital-specific differences were -8.0, +18.3, and -6.3 points. Omitting hospital 252 reversed the standardized contrast to -7.2 points; omitting 248 or 420 yielded +10.1 or +8.8 points. The adjusted person-clustered estimate was +8.1 points (95% interval -3.6 to +19.7; p=0.175). Across all 540 rows, the available-case contrast was +2.0 points (30/151 versus 25/140; 95% interval -7.0 to +11.0).

These results are inconclusive and hospital-dependent, not supportive evidence of a reproducible label-to-sedation-de-escalation association. They also cannot establish sedation interruption or bedside delivery.

## Gate decision and interpretation

- Exact cohort, labels, and D: pass.
- Outcome computation from independent tables: pass for charted numeric changes.
- Within-hospital overlap: limited pass in exactly three hospitals; transportability remains poor.
- Setting ascertainment in overlap hospitals: pass and balanced.
- Sedation paired-rate ascertainment: partial and somewhat lower under evaluation; available-case results are conditional on charting.
- Positive temporal ordering: pass mechanically, but the pre-D diagnostic does not support a new positive post-label shift.
- Component robustness: fail; the setting composite is dominated by peak pressure and changes materially when peak pressure is removed or the population is broadened.
- Cross-hospital consistency: setting direction is inverse in all three hospitals, while sedation direction reverses.
- Directional hypothesis: not supported. The setting result is adverse/inverse but measurement-dependent; the sedation result is inconclusive.
- Durable liberation: remains failed under the first parent’s audit and is not re-estimated.

Supportive evidence would have required a positive post-D association in overlap hospitals, balanced ascertainment, persistence after removing peak pressure, consistent hospital and landmark direction, and no comparable pre-D trajectory difference. That pattern was not observed. Adverse evidence is an inverse setting contrast, component dependence, and no distinct positive post-label shift. Inconclusive evidence is the imprecise and heterogeneous sedation association and sparse component-specific setting changes.

The scientifically defensible conclusion is narrow: the exact daily-evaluation label has no validated, reproducible positive 12-hour meaning as a marker of the two computed de-escalation proxies in this snapshot. The inverse primary setting association may reflect care-plan semantics, baseline clinical state, charting workflow, routine measurement variation, or true management differences; this design cannot distinguish them.

## What is computationally checkable and what is not

An automatic verifier can check the cohort hash and row count; exact exposure labels; one-to-one joins; D equality; temporal boundaries; numeric thresholds; missing-as-unavailable logic; overlap-hospital restriction; counts, rates, risk differences, intervals, fixed-effect model specification, component sensitivities, pre-D diagnostic, and whether conclusions match supportive, adverse, or inconclusive outputs.

Clinical adjudication or another study is required to establish whether a charted setting/rate was actually delivered, whether peak pressure represented a controlled setting rather than a measured value or documentation convention, whether any rate change was intentional interruption, whether an SBT occurred or was appropriate, whether a patient was extubated, and whether any action improved outcomes. Prospective bedside/device data, standardized ventilator semantics and drug units, explicit SBT/sedation-interruption records, true extubation/reintubation adjudication, and broader hospital overlap are required before clinical or causal claims.
