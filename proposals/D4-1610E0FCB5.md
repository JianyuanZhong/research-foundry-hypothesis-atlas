# Dated two-panel metabolic change and transition to cardiometabolic multimorbidity

## Successor change and scientific deliverable

This is a measurement-repaired successor to [prior hypothesis]. The parent compile audit found that the focused assessment source does not contain repeated biomarker fields: those columns are in main only for instances 0 and 1, while assessment contains assessment dates and repeated anthropometric/strength fields. This proposal removes every unsupported biomarker instance 2–3 claim and binds the two observed biomarker panels to their dates.

The deliverable is a newly fitted, locked comparison of (1) a current-level cause-specific Cox baseline and (2) a transition-specific multistate model that adds dated annualized change. Completion requires a frozen cohort/codebook, counts and measurement-process audit, fitted coefficients with uncertainty, locked held-out performance/calibration/decision-curve outputs, and negative-control/permutation results. No conclusion is complete without its computed output and uncertainty.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that baseline glycaemic, lipid, renal and anthropometric measurements are prognostically associated with later coded cardiometabolic disease. The local data and method references do not establish that a dated within-person change measure adds clinically relevant information after conditioning on the latest level, nor whether any added information is specific to progression from one disease domain to a second.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 with no cardiometabolic multimorbidity at a dated repeat assessment, annualized worsening between the two available biomarker panels—higher HbA1c, higher triglycerides, and lower HDL—has a nonzero joint association with time to a second cardiometabolic disease domain beyond the latest biomarker levels and baseline disease state. The incremental association is larger for the 1-domain-to-2-domain transition than for the 0-domain-to-1-domain transition, and it is not reproduced for a prespecified non-cardiometabolic negative-control outcome.

This is a prognostic association estimand, not the causal effect of changing a biomarker and not evidence that a monitoring intervention improves outcomes.

## Clinical importance and advance

A patient may have an acceptable current value but a recent deterioration that prompts earlier review, medication reconciliation or repeat testing. Conversely, if change adds no reliable information after the current level and measurement intensity are accounted for, adding noisy trajectory alerts could waste clinical attention. The advance is a direct, date-bound test of incremental information and of the transition it informs, rather than another cross-sectional association or an uncalibrated black-box risk score.

The two-panel restriction is scientifically important. It prevents instance labels from being treated as elapsed time and prevents a two-point difference from being presented as a latent long-term trajectory. The question is deliberately narrowed to observed interval change.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The primary estimand population is the UKB repeat-panel cohort:

1. age 40–69 inclusive at recruitment from population.21022-0.0;
2. valid sex from population.31-0.0;
3. complete core laboratory panel (HbA1c, triglycerides, HDL) at both main instances 0 and 1;
4. valid assessment.53-0.0 and assessment.53-1.0, with date 1 later than date 0 and an interval of 1–10 years in the primary analysis;
5. no date-order contradiction among nonmissing assessment dates 53-0 through 53-3;
6. no coded cardiometabolic multimorbidity (two or more domains) before the index date, where the index is date 1;
7. not deceased before the index date.

The primary start time is date 1 plus a 365-day washout. Events in the washout are excluded from the primary risk set. Follow-up ends at the first dated second-domain event, death, the administrative end, or five years after entry, whichever comes first. The administrative end is the maximum trusted date in the outcome snapshot; the solver must report it and the number with a complete five-year opportunity. A full-follow-up cause-specific analysis is secondary. Sensitivities use 0, 2 and 5 years of washout and intervals of 0.5–15 years.

Participants with exactly one domain before index are eligible; their baseline state is 1 and the outcome is the first additional domain after index. Participants with zero domains begin in state 0. Participants with two or more domains at index are excluded.

Use the catalog’s deterministic participant partition rule, with buckets 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for validation/model-selection decisions, 70–79 for the locked test, and 80–99 reserved and never read for fitting, tuning or threshold selection. The split is by eid, not rows, dates or events. The exact catalog namespace and hash construction must be recorded in the run manifest.

## Exposure construction and estimand

For each core marker j, define observed annualized change:

g_j = (value at date 1 − value at date 0) / (date difference in years).

Reverse-code HDL so worsening has the same positive direction as HbA1c and triglycerides. Standardize each g_j using the training-partition mean and SD only. Latest panel values and interval length remain separate covariates.

The primary estimand is the vector of conditional cause-specific log-hazard coefficients for the three standardized changes in the model for first transition to a second cardiometabolic domain, adjusted for latest levels, baseline disease state, age, sex and prespecified baseline clinical covariates. The primary null is the joint Wald/Likelihood-ratio test that all three change coefficients equal zero. Report each coefficient and 95% confidence interval, the joint test, and the difference in 5-year cumulative incidence between prespecified low/typical/high change profiles evaluated at the same latest levels; this is prognostic standardization, not a causal intervention contrast.

The primary clinically meaningful threshold is fixed before fitting: an improvement must be directionally consistent, have a 95% interval excluding the joint null, and improve locked-test 5-year Brier score or calibration/decision-curve net benefit by at least 0.01 in absolute scale without worsening calibration in either sex or either baseline-state subgroup. Statistical detectability alone is not called clinical usefulness.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

### Simple baseline: current-level Cox

Fit M0, a cause-specific Cox model for time to first second-domain event with latest HbA1c, triglycerides, HDL and, in a prespecified secondary model, latest creatinine and cystatin C; baseline state (0 versus 1 domain); age, sex, latest BMI, valid baseline systolic/diastolic field IDs and smoking-status field ID; interval length; and an indicator for secondary covariate missingness. Use restricted cubic splines with knots fixed from training data, robust participant-level standard errors and no test-set tuning. Every comparison uses the same eligible population, delayed entry, censoring, codebook and split.

### Substantive alternative: transition-specific multistate illness-deepening model

Fit M1 on the same participants and split with states 0 (no domain), 1 (exactly one domain), 2 (at least two domains) and D (death, absorbing). Permit 0→1, 0→D, 1→2 and 1→D. Retain a direct 0→2 transition if dated outcomes establish it; otherwise treat it as interval-censored in a prespecified sensitivity analysis. Use the same latest levels and covariates as M0, then add the three dated changes, with change effects allowed to differ for 0→1 and 1→2.

The scientific comparison is transition-specific information, not merely a small predictive score increase: it asks whether change identifies disease deepening differently from disease onset. M1 can reveal a change signal concentrated in accumulation of a second domain, which a single Cox endpoint loses. It is appropriate to the verified two-panel design and does not pretend to learn a long sequence.

Evaluate once on locked 70–79 test buckets after codebook, knots, model, threshold and weight decisions are frozen. Report transition-specific coefficients, robust/bootstrapped 95% intervals, 5-year cause-specific and competing-death cumulative incidence, time-dependent and integrated Brier score, discrimination, calibration slope/intercept, integrated calibration error, decision-curve net benefit at fixed 5%, 10% and 20% thresholds, restricted mean event-free survival, and results by sex, age below/above 55 and baseline state 0/1. Training/validation may use only buckets 0–69. Reserved buckets 80–99 are never inspected. The permutation falsification test shuffles change vectors within baseline state and sex in training/validation, repeats the locked pipeline with a frozen seed, and cannot choose the primary model.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires the prespecified joint change test to reject the null in the worsening direction; reasonably consistent direction across HbA1c, triglycerides/HDL and the 1→2 transition; locked-test Brier, calibration or decision-curve improvement by the fixed criterion without subgroup calibration harm; persistence across washout, interval and competing-death sensitivities; and an acute-appendicitis association that is null or materially weaker while the within-state permutation produces no comparable signal.

This supports incremental prognostic information in the selected repeat-measured UKB population. It does not support a causal treatment effect, a universal monitoring policy or biological mediation.

Adverse evidence is a null/reverse-direction joint change association, loss after latest levels and interval length are included, an appendicitis signal of similar size, a permutation signal, or a gain that disappears on locked-split/calibration analysis. This falsifies the proposed incremental-information or disease-specificity claim; it does not prove biomarker change harmless.

Inconclusive evidence is too few complete dated pairs, insufficient events in the 1→2 transition or negative control, date/code correspondence failure, poor repeat-measurement positivity, wide intervals spanning clinically important effects, or severe subgroup calibration failure. Report feasibility and uncertainty only.

Computationally checkable claims include source bindings and hashes, column presence, cohort counts, date ordering, valid-value rules, code/date extraction, split integrity, model fits, confidence intervals, cumulative incidence, calibration, Brier/decision-curve outputs, permutation behavior and sensitivities. Clinical adjudication is unavailable: local coded outcomes are not chart-reviewed; exact assay dictionaries and treatment changes are incomplete; repeat-assessment reasons, medication adherence, socioeconomic confounding and referral/measurement indication are unavailable or unresolved. Validated UKB phenotype algorithms, chart review, treatment data, external validation and an intervention study would be required for stronger clinical claims.

## Alternatives considered and revisit criteria

- The parent’s four-instance slope/mixed-effects design is rejected as written because biomarker fields at instances 2–3 are not present in main; it is replaced by a dated two-panel change estimand.
- The parent’s recurrent/temporal-attention alternative is deferred because two laboratory panels cannot support a rich sequence representation. Revisit if a verified source adds at least three dated biomarker panels or dense ordered event histories.
- Proteomic organ-age, fat-distribution/imaging, CHIP sequence, infection-timing, sleep-regularity, cancer-staging/treatment and disease-specific PRS directions remain deferred for the catalog limitations recorded by the parent.
- A lab-only neural model is not forbidden, but is not selected: it adds representation complexity without resolving the two-panel limitation. Revisit only with a prespecified target showing transition heterogeneity beyond M1, participant-level locked calibration/uncertainty, and measured runtime benefit.
