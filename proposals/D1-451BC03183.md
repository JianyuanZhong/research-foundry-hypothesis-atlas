# Episode 4 branch memo: index-anchored timing and competing-observation sensitivity

Status: proposed Harbor design; no cohort scan, model fit, endpoint adjudication, or result has been executed. This is an evolution of the frozen episode-3 HCC child `[prior hypothesis]`. It does not change the scientific goal, population, time zero, laboratory windows, or the parent primary outcome.

## Scientific boundary and hypothesis

The parent has established a testable prognostic opening, not a finding: after TACE, a short-lived aminotransferase flare may be different from coordinated loss of hepatic synthetic/renal reserve. The parent’s unresolved timing rival is stronger than a generic “length-of-stay confounding” label. A discharge-anchored event is only observable after a patient is discharged, so it can combine reserve loss with (a) how long the index admission lasts, (b) whether and when a patient is discharged, (c) early readmission or other acute-care observation during days 0–7, and (d) whether the diagnosis/documentation system records the endpoint. The available evidence supports only that these are measurable or auditable processes in the HCC tables; it does not support that the 0–7-day trajectory predicts decompensation.

The frozen primary hypothesis remains:

> In adults with HCC and a first observed TACE, a 0–7-day trajectory showing worsening synthetic/renal reserve adds held-out prognostic information beyond baseline for a new high-specificity decompensation-coded admission during days 8–90 after index discharge.

The episode-4 boundary hypothesis is:

> If the incremental trajectory signal reflects post-TACE reserve loss rather than discharge selection, early-care utilization, or endpoint ascertainment, its direction and calibrated incremental value should remain when the same day-0–7 features are evaluated with an index-anchored time-to-first-event estimand that does not require discharge, while accounting for early acute-care events and the observable competing-event process.

A supportive result would strengthen an incremental prognostic interpretation only. It would not establish causality, a biological mechanism, treatment benefit, discharge policy, clinical utility, or a safe action threshold. An adverse result would be informative: if the gain exists only after discharge, the scientifically correct conclusion may be “prediction conditional on a discharge/observation regime,” not “post-TACE reserve trajectory predicts clinical deterioration.” An inconclusive result would identify missing endpoint timing, competing-event ascertainment, or sparse risk sets as the next dependency.

## Index-anchored survival and competing-event estimands

This is a labeled sensitivity/child estimand, not a replacement for `D_D`.

### Index-anchored cause-specific target

Define `D_I` as the first distinct post-index encounter whose `admission time` is in [index+8 days, index+90 days], regardless of whether an index `discharge time` exists or precedes it, and whose diagnosis meets the same high-specificity component rule. The event time is the encounter `admission time` minus the earliest qualifying procedure `start time`. A separate exploratory endpoint uses `visit time` for any-encounter ascertainment; it is not pooled with the admission endpoint because outpatient and inpatient observation are different processes.

A diagnosis has no independent timestamp in this release. Therefore, the index-anchored event date is the linked encounter time, not an invented diagnosis time. A continued index encounter with a newly appearing diagnosis cannot be temporally adjudicated from these tables and is excluded from the primary `D_I` definition or placed in an explicitly “unresolvable timing” stratum. This is a limitation, not evidence against the hypothesis.

Estimate the cause-specific hazard and cumulative incidence `F_D^I(t)` for t=8,…,90, and compare baseline-only versus dynamic risks at days 30, 60 and 90. Patients remain in the risk set without discharge conditioning. Follow-up ends at day 90, the verified source horizon, or an explicitly documented loss of source observation. Do not treat an absent later encounter as proof of recovery.

### Early events are an observation/state process, not automatically a competing clinical endpoint

Define `A_{0–7}` as the first distinct post-index acute-care encounter in days 0–7, using parseable `Admission Time` (and, in a separately labeled any-encounter analysis, `Encounter Time`). Because this dataset lacks a planned/unplanned field, no such status is inferred. Early readmission/acute care can change later laboratory observation and endpoint ascertainment, but it does not mathematically preclude a later decompensation event. It is therefore represented as:

1. a prespecified availability/selection stratum and time-varying observation-state descriptor;
2. a secondary multi-state path (index → early acute event or no early event → `D_I`); and
3. a negative-control target for checking whether the trajectory is mainly predicting care intensity.

It is not adjusted for in the primary 0–7-day prediction as though it were a baseline confounder, and it is not mislabeled as a competing risk.

### Conditional competing-risk estimand and its availability boundary

The clinically meaningful competing event would be an adjudicated terminal event such as death/hospice before decompensation. The HCC catalog provides no mortality linkage, and `clinical_documents` has text but no temporal columns; a death mention is not a validated death date. Thus a full clinical competing-risk claim is unavailable from the current release.

If blinded clinical review can validate a terminal event and its linked encounter date from `clinical_documents.discharge status`, `discharge diagnosis` or `course of treatment` plus `encounters.admission time/discharge time`, estimate the prespecified observed-data competing-risk quantity `F_D^{I,CR}(90)`: cumulative incidence of `D_I` by day 90 with terminal events as a mutually exclusive competing cause. Report cause-specific hazards and Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence; a Fine–Gray subdistribution model is secondary and descriptive. If terminal-event adjudication is absent, sparse, or temporally ambiguous, mark `F_D^{I,CR}` not identified and report only the cause-specific/index-anchored and observation-process analyses. Do not substitute last encounter, missing discharge, or a lexical “death” flag for mortality.

The main observation-process sensitivity treats the last observed encounter/eligible record as right censoring and estimates inverse-probability-of-observation weights from variables known by day 0 (baseline severity/utilization, department, calendar era, and discharge observability), with stabilized predeclared truncation. This is not a cure for unmeasured loss to follow-up. A non-decomp admission that does not terminate observation is a state transition, not a competing event.

## Endpoint and measurement audit

Retain the parent’s training-only assay dictionary and parse only numeric `labs.Quantitative result`. Candidate concepts remain bilirubin, albumin, platelets, PT/INR or PT ratio, creatinine, sodium, AST, ALT and AFP, subject to demonstrated presence and assay comparability. There is no lab-unit column: never pool raw thresholds across unverified assay regimes or claim ALBI/MELD/Child-Pugh without expert unit review. Use deterministic [0,1–2,3–4,5–7] bins, retain raw timestamps, and record assay/name, specimen type, first/last lab time, and observation counts.

For each eligible index, create a timing/ascertainment ledger before outcome modeling:

* index time, index encounter, discharge time and discharge lag;
* discharge missingness, discharge before day 8, discharge on/after day 8, and no subsequent discharge-observable state;
* first 0–7-day acute-care encounter and whether it is the index encounter or a later encounter;
* lab observation density and whether labs came from the index or later encounter;
* `D_D` event time relative to both discharge and index;
* `D_I` event time relative to index;
* diagnosis-only, document-confirmed, discordant, missing-document and unresolvable-timing labels;
* endpoint source table/field and whether the same encounter carries competing/other acute diagnoses.

The endpoint rule must be frozen before looking at model performance. A blinded clinician adjudication packet must sample events and non-events across discharge strata, early-event strata, calendar/departments, assay regimes, and diagnosis/document discordance. Labels must include true new/worsening decompensation, pre-existing disease, planned/unclear encounter status, competing illness/HCC progression, TACE injury, terminal-event status if claimed, and text validity. The lexical rule is a computable screen, not clinical truth.

## Matched baseline and substantive survival/state-space alternative

The baseline is deliberately available in two matched forms:

* `B_D`: parent L2-regularized logistic regression for `D_D`, using time-zero age, sex, department, HCC/cirrhosis/portal-vein-thrombus/prior-decompensation flags, procedure context, pre-index utilization, baseline assay-specific values and missingness.
* `B_I`: the same baseline covariates in a cause-specific survival model for `D_I` (regularized Cox or prespecified flexible parametric hazard), with no post-index variables. A binary 90-day L2 result remains the direct comparator for the parent estimand; survival metrics are not substituted for the parent’s Brier comparison.

The substantive alternative is `SS-CR`, a joint irregular-time state/observation/survival model. It uses exactly the parent’s baseline and `labs` inputs plus laboratory timestamps, assay/name regime, observation indicators/counts, department/calendar, and the encounter/order/examination/medication process fields available by day 7. A two-factor latent state separates transient injury from reserve; assay-specific measurement error and irregular evolution are fit on train only. A cause-specific hazard head predicts `D_I` at days 8–90 from the day-7 posterior state and baseline covariates. Separate observation/discharge and early-acute-event heads model when the patient is observed, without using post-day-7 data. If adjudicated terminal events exist, add the terminal cause; otherwise retain the observed-data cause-specific model and state that clinical competing-risk inference is unavailable.

The alternative can reveal information the baseline loses: whether the incremental association is tied to event timing rather than a binary label, whether reserve/injury trajectories predict hazard after removing discharge conditioning, how much risk is attributable to observation intensity, and whether dynamic state uncertainty differs in patients with early acute events. It does not identify a biological mechanism; latent factors are representations. Its assumptions (state evolution, missingness/observation model, partial pooling and within-regime exchangeability) can fail, so the model is a discriminator of the timing rival, not a complexity bonus.

A neural sequence model is deferred. It could learn timestamp order, but before the survival/state-space comparison it would add capacity without separating discharge selection, nonterminal early admissions, missing endpoint dates, or unobserved death. Revisit it only if SS-CR shows a stable residual ordering effect that is not explained by observation/state assumptions and if event support justifies a separate model.

## Splits, uncertainty, falsification, and interpretation

Freeze patient-level chronological partitions before outcome inspection: train 2010-07-27–2018-12-31, validation 2019-01-01–2021-12-31, and test 2022-01-01–2025-10-03 (or an equivalent recomputed complete-follow-up cutoff). Tune penalties, hazard/state dimensions, observation-process shrinkage and any competing-risk sensitivity on train/validation only. No patient may cross partitions through multiple encounters.

For `D_D`, retain the parent’s paired test-set Brier/calibration/discrimination/net-benefit comparison. For `D_I`, report cause-specific hazard ratios only as model summaries, cumulative incidence/survival at 30/60/90, time-dependent ROC/PR-AUC, IPCW Brier and integrated Brier over days 8–90, calibration at each horizon, and paired patient-cluster bootstrap 2,000 replicates or the configured bounded equivalent. Report effective risk-set size, event counts, missing-discharge/early-event strata, censoring-weight diagnostics, convergence, and state/observation uncertainty. An interval crossing no incremental difference is inconclusive unless precision was prespecified as adequate.

Required falsification and boundary checks are:

1. Within-patient timestamp permutation preserving lab values and counts.
2. A matched pre-index pseudo-trajectory with the same modeling and timing machinery.
3. Counts/lag/department/era-only prediction of `D_D` and `D_I`.
4. Prediction of discharge lag and `A_{0–7}` as process-control outcomes.
5. AST/ALT-only versus reserve-panel-only blocks.
6. Any-admission and repeat-TACE outcomes, kept secondary and non-equivalent.
7. Discharge-time permutation or a discharge-stratum label shuffle performed only within train/validation as a selection-process control.
8. A feature-time audit proving that no post-day-7 lab, encounter, discharge outcome, endpoint text, or later process variable enters the dynamic features.
9. Endpoint concordance tables and blinded adjudication, including terminal-event ascertainment if the competing-risk quantity is claimed.

Support requires that the dynamic model improve the untouched chronological `D_D` comparison and show directionally compatible incremental performance for `D_I`, with calibration not confined to discharged-by-day-7 or early-observed patients, no material pre-index/timestamp-permutation reproduction, and no dominance by counts or discharge-lag prediction. If a clinically adjudicated terminal event is available, the competing-risk interpretation must not reverse the conclusion.

Adverse evidence is a gain that disappears under index anchoring, is confined to discharged patients or one early-care regime, primarily predicts discharge/acute-care observation, reproduces in the pre-index pseudo-window, fails calibration under index-anchored follow-up, or is erased by endpoint adjudication/competing-event handling. This revises the claim to a discharge-conditioned observed-care association and may justify stopping the trajectory direction.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse index-anchored events, non-identifiable terminal timing, high censoring or unstable IPCW weights, missing discharge, diagnosis/document discordance, unresolved assay units, inadequate common support, or state-space nonconvergence. Continue only when the parent and index-anchored analyses are both computationally valid and adjudication is adequate; narrow to an observed-care or regime-specific claim when only that estimand is estimable; defer clinical interpretation when endpoint or terminal-event adjudication is missing; abandon only after an adequately supported, predeclared analysis repeatedly fails.

Computationally checkable claims are source/header/hash integrity, within-HCC joins, cohort and timing ledgers, no leakage, split integrity, fitted predictions, survival metrics, calibration, paired uncertainty, censoring weights, event-process contrasts and negative-control behavior. Clinical adjudication, assay/unit verification, planned status, true decompensation, competing illness, death/hospice, external validation and clinical action thresholds require expert review or another study.
