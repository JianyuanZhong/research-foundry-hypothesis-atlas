# Working proposal: recent AFP elevation and subsequent HCC recurrence risk

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The strongest evidence-supported claim is that AFP (alpha-fetoprotein) is used clinically as a tumor marker for HCC and that AFP is associated with HCC outcomes in published literature. The local HCC snapshot supports a bounded adaptation: the labs table has 28,159,928 rows and 707,463 AFP/甲胎蛋白-containing rows; the diagnoses table has 1,810,646 rows and 792,628 HCC-related rows; and the procedures table has 11,389 TACE-related rows. This diagnostic does not establish that these counts are the target cohort or that AFP elevation causes recurrence.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among patients with a documented HCC diagnosis and a prior AFP measurement, a recent increase in AFP is associated with a higher risk of a subsequent documented HCC recurrence event, after accounting for pre-existing tumor burden and time since the last AFP measurement.

The clinically important decision this could inform is whether an AFP-guided review of surveillance or treatment response is warranted after a patient has had an HCC recurrence. A positive result would justify prospective validation before any treatment decision; a null or adverse result would argue against using this marker as a stand-alone escalation signal.

## Closest inspected work and substantive advance

The proposal will cite three works. K1 is the current 2026 abstract for “SREBP2 as a Novel Biomarker for Posttransplant Hepatocellular Carcinoma Recurrence: A New Prognostic Nomogram” (PMCID: PMC13094809), which reports an SREBP2-integrated nomogram for recurrence-free survival. K2 is the 2022 systematic review/meta-analysis “Predictors of post-recurrence survival in hepatocellular carcinoma patients following liver transplantation” (PMID 34999555), which identifies AFP level at recurrence as a recurrence predictor. K3 is the 2025 review “Criteria and prognostic models for patients with hepatocellular carcinoma undergoing liver transplantation” (DOI 10.3350/cmh.2024.0323), which documents that dynamic response markers and prognostic models are used to establish surveillance strategies and improve outcomes after HCC liver transplantation.

The advance is not to claim a new mechanism. It is to test a specific, falsifiable temporal association in this local cohort and to determine whether AFP elevation adds information beyond documented HCC burden and AFP timing. The local data do not contain validated tumor-burden imaging or pathology labels, so the baseline will use only explicitly documented HCC-related encounters and the strongest available lab history. This is a prognostic/risk-stratification question, not a causal treatment claim.

## Leading rival and distinguishing comparison

The strongest rival explanation is severity/indication bias: patients whose AFP is high may have more aggressive or extensive disease, more treatment, or more surveillance, rather than AFP itself indicating future recurrence. A second rival is temporal ordering: AFP may reflect the most recent HCC event rather than an event that occurred later.

The baseline and alternative must differ in information and therefore predict different patterns. The baseline will use only the earliest documented HCC diagnosis, pre-index HCC encounters, and a fixed pre-index AFP history. The alternative will add the index AFP level and the time from the index AFP to the outcome. If the alternative adds information only because it is conditioning on the event that generated the AFP rise, its association should attenuate or reverse when the index AFP is not used. If the alternative adds information beyond documented tumor burden and timing, that supports a marker-related association. Neither result identifies causation.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Baseline (B): a regularized logistic regression or a prespecified tree ensemble using only baseline covariates and pre-index AFP history. The target is the 12-month TACE event. This baseline preserves the marker-free information available before the index AFP and tests whether adding AFP timing/level changes the estimate.

Alternative (A): B plus index AFP level and index-to-outcome lag. A is scientifically substantive because it tests whether a recent AFP rise adds information beyond documented tumor burden and time; it also tests temporal ordering. A will use the same patient-level split, outcome, and regularization as B.

A secondary mechanistic-style analysis is a causal mediation-style analysis with an explicit DAG: index AFP -> recent HCC activity (mediator) -> outcome. It is not causal. It estimates whether the association attenuates after conditioning on recent HCC activity. This alternative could reveal whether the apparent AFP signal is driven by recent tumor activity; the baseline cannot reveal that. If it is not supported by the data, defer it and report why.

## Analysis, uncertainty, and falsification

Use patient-level splitting: no outcome or index-lag information from a patient in the validation/test set. Fit B and A in development, evaluate calibration, discrimination, and net benefit in validation, and report test-set performance. Use bootstrap resampling for 95% confidence intervals and uncertainty in risk ratios. Report standardization/SHAP summaries only as descriptive. Do not claim an external cohort or a causal treatment effect.

Falsification criteria:

1. Severity/indication bias: if the association persists after B but disappears in A, or is reversed when recent HCC activity is included, the data support the severity/indication explanation rather than a marker-specific association.
2. Temporal ordering: if the index AFP is not associated with the outcome, or the association is only present when lag is omitted, the marker is likely a surrogate for the generating HCC event.
3. Negative result: a narrow 95% CI around RR=1 with no clinically meaningful net benefit is adverse to the marker-guided escalation hypothesis; it does not prove that AFP is useless.
4. Inconclusive: missing outcome dates, inadequate event counts, wide intervals, or a non-reproducible pattern across lag/sensitivity analyses are inconclusive.
5. Supportive: a reproducible, clinically meaningful RR>1 with a 95% CI excluding 1, improved calibration/net benefit in A, and attenuation when recent HCC activity is added, supports a marker-associated prognostic signal pending external validation.

## Evidence limits and required next study

The local snapshot is not a validated clinical cohort. It lacks a front-page table with patient-level clinical data, raw images, waveforms, and a validated HCC recurrence adjudication process. transfers, vitals, and front_page are identifier-only; clinical_documents has no time field and its rule-based HCC extraction is not a validated diagnosis detector. Units are not separately available for labs, and height/weight units are unverified. Therefore, the strongest claim is an observed association in this dataset, not a clinical recommendation.

A future study must validate HCC recurrence adjudication, verify AFP assay units and cross-assay comparability, establish treatment indication and severity, and externally validate the model. Only then can a treatment or surveillance decision be informed.
