# Provisional hypothesis: discordant kidney recovery after ICU AKI

Status: provisional and evidence-bounded. This is a design draft, not a completed cohort analysis. No MIMIC-IV counts, fitted models, or clinical conclusions are claimed.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in their first MIMIC-IV ICU stay with a documented creatinine-based acute kidney injury (AKI) episode and adequate observation through a prespecified 48-hour landmark, a creatinine-improved but urine-output-impaired recovery phenotype will have worse subsequent in-hospital death and/or persistent or worsening kidney dysfunction than concordant creatinine-and-urine-output recovery.

This is a prognostic association hypothesis, not a causal or mechanistic claim. The clinically important unresolved question is whether discordance identifies a subgroup at higher near-term risk after apparent biochemical improvement, or whether it is mainly a monitoring and treatment artifact.

## Evidence and opening

The inherited board work identifies a plausible opening: creatinine and urine output can recover at different rates, but the available records do not establish that discordance is a stable biological recovery phenotype. The strongest supported claim is only that MIMIC-IV contains dated laboratory, urine-output, treatment, organ-support and outcome fields that can be used to test this comparison. The unresolved claim is whether discordance adds prognostic information beyond pre-landmark illness severity, current organ state, monitoring intensity and treatment proxies.

The strongest rival explanation is measurement/monitoring artifact: creatinine is sampled at clinician-selected times, urine output is recorded mainly when a catheter or bedside collection is present, and fluid balance is a treatment/monitoring summary. A second rival is residual severity and treatment selection, including diuresis, fluid administration, vasopressors and renal replacement therapy. A result that persists after prespecified monitoring and treatment adjustment would support a prognostic signal, but would not identify tubular injury, fluid overload, or a mechanism.

## Proposed computable experiment

Population and time:
- Adults in their first eligible MIMIC-IV ICU stay, with a creatinine-based AKI episode and a 48-hour post-AKI landmark.
- Use only records available before the landmark for exposure and covariates; outcomes begin at the landmark.
- Require a prespecified minimum number of creatinine observations and urine-output coverage around the landmark; report coverage and censoring rather than silently treating missing urine output as normal.

Exposure:
- Define creatinine improvement and urine-output impairment using fixed, clinically interpretable thresholds derived from the available item definitions and KDIGO-style conventions. The exact item IDs, units, and thresholds must be audited before execution.
- Compare discordant recovery (creatinine improved, urine output impaired) with concordant recovery (both improved), with a prespecified reference/other category.

Outcomes:
- Primary: subsequent in-hospital death through a fixed horizon after the landmark.
- Secondary: persistent/worsening kidney dysfunction, escalation of renal support, and other prespecified organ-support deterioration.
- Use competing-risk or landmarked survival methods appropriate to discharge and death, with uncertainty intervals.

Baseline and substantive alternative:
- Baseline: a prespecified landmark model using pre-landmark demographics, severity, current creatinine/urine state, treatment and monitoring proxies, with a simple adjusted comparison of discordant versus concordant recovery.
- Alternative: a time-aware model using the ordered pre-landmark laboratory, urine-output and treatment trajectories, evaluated on the same patient-level split, outcome, calibration and uncertainty criteria. The alternative can reveal whether the apparent discordance is explained by trajectory shape, timing and monitoring intensity rather than a single landmark contrast; it does not establish mechanism.
- Compare incremental discrimination, calibration and uncertainty, but treat a small predictive gain as insufficient unless it changes the scientific interpretation of the discordance comparison.

Falsification and interpretation:
- Supportive: discordance remains associated with worse outcomes after monitoring/treatment adjustment, is stable in prespecified sensitivity analyses, and the time-aware model shows a reproducible trajectory pattern rather than a single measurement artifact.
- Adverse: the association disappears with monitoring intensity, urine-output coverage, treatment or severity adjustment, or is confined to poorly observed patients; this favors the measurement/severity rival and argues against a broad recovery-phenotype claim.
- Inconclusive: sparse urine-output coverage, unstable thresholds, wide uncertainty, or inability to define a valid landmark would not refute the hypothesis and would require a narrower or new study.
