# Persistent hyperlactatemia with divergent perfusion trajectories: a MIMIC-IV study

## Scientific question

Among ICU patients with persistently elevated lactate during the first 24 ICU hours, does the association between subsequent crystalloid administration and 72-hour perfusion recovery differ by the concurrent trajectory of perfusion markers (mean arterial pressure, urine output, vasopressor exposure)?

More specifically, we test whether a 1000-mL increase in crystalloid during hours 24–48 is associated with a larger improvement in a perfusion composite at hour 72 among patients whose early perfusion markers are improving despite persistent lactate than among those whose perfusion markers are worsening.

## Clinical importance and unresolved claim

Lactate is commonly used to guide resuscitation, but persistent elevation can reflect inadequate tissue perfusion, impaired clearance, mitochondrial dysfunction, sepsis-related metabolic derangement, or observation/medication effects [K1]. The seed hypothesis is that the clinical meaning of persistent hyperlactatemia depends on whether perfusion is improving or worsening. This matters because fluid administration can improve preload-dependent perfusion, but can also worsen pulmonary edema, capillary leak, or organ congestion when the underlying problem is not underfilling.

The evidence supports that lactate is associated with severity and outcomes, and that lactate clearance has prognostic value [K1][K2]. It does not establish that two patients with the same lactate trajectory have the same response to fluids, or that fluid should be withheld or intensified based on a lactate–perfusion discordance. The unresolved claim is therefore heterogeneous treatment association: the causal or prognostic association between early fluid administration and later perfusion recovery differs by early perfusion trajectory.

## Population and temporal design

### Population

- One ICU stay per patient, using the first ICU stay after the first hospital admission in the selected MIMIC-IV snapshot.
- Adult patients, age >= 18 at admission.
- ICU length of stay >= 72 hours, so the 72-hour outcome can be observed.
- At least two lactate measurements in the first 24 ICU hours, with at least one measurement in hours 0–12 and at least one in hours 12–24.
- At least one MAP measurement in each of the early and late early windows.
- At least one urine-output measurement or fluid-balance measurement in hours 0–24, and at least one in hours 24–48.
- Exclude stays with missing ICU admission time, impossible timestamps, or lactate values outside the plausible clinical range used by the lab dictionary.

### Time zero and windows

Time zero is `icustays.intime`.

- Baseline window: ICU hours 0–24.
- Exposure window: ICU hours 24–48.
- Outcome window: ICU hours 48–72.

All timestamps are converted to ICU-relative hours using `icustays.intime`. A measurement is assigned to a window only if its timestamp falls inside the closed interval; ties are assigned to the later window for exposure and outcome to avoid using information before the exposure window begins.

### Persistent hyperlactatemia

Define persistent hyperlactatemia as:

- mean lactate in hours 0–24 >= 4.0 mmol/L, and
- at least one lactate measurement >= 4.0 mmol/L in hours 12–24.

This is a working operational definition, not a diagnosis. Sensitivity analyses use mean >= 2.0 and >= 3.0, and require the late measurement to be >= 2.0, >= 3.0, and >= 4.0 respectively. The primary analysis is restricted to the >=4.0 definition because it represents a clinically severe and more clearly persistent state.

### Perfusion trajectory

Construct a standardized perfusion score from three components available in MIMIC-IV:

1. MAP: mean of available MAP values in hours 0–12 and 12–24.
2. Urine output: hourly urine output in hours 0–12 and 12–24, calculated from output events and corrected for observation time when possible.
3. Vasopressor burden: norepinephrine-equivalent dose in hours 0–12 and 12–24, using input events and a documented conversion table; if conversion is not available, use norepinephrine dose directly and report the limitation.

For each component, calculate the change from the first early window to the second early window. Standardize each change within the persistent-hyperlactatemia population using mean and standard deviation from the training split only. The perfusion trajectory score is the sum of the three standardized changes. A positive score indicates improving perfusion; a negative score indicates worsening perfusion.

Define the primary trajectory groups:

- Improving perfusion: score >= 0.
- Worsening perfusion: score < 0.

A sensitivity analysis uses the top and bottom quartiles of the score to reduce ambiguity near zero.

### Exposure

Exposure is total crystalloid volume administered during ICU hours 24–48, calculated from `icu/ingredientevents` using the documented fluid categories. The primary exposure is continuous, modeled per 1000 mL. A secondary binary exposure is >= 1000 mL versus < 1000 mL. We do not claim that fluid administration is randomly assigned; this is an observational estimand.

### Outcome

Primary outcome is the change in the same perfusion score from hours 24–48 to hours 48–72, using MAP, urine output, and vasopressor burden. A positive change indicates improvement.

Secondary outcomes:

- 72-hour ICU survival.
- 72-hour ICU discharge.
- Change in MAP.
- Change in hourly urine output.
- Change in vasopressor dose.
- 72-hour new or worsening renal dysfunction, using creatinine and urine output when available.

## Competing explanations

1. **Pathophysiologic heterogeneity**: persistent lactate with improving perfusion reflects resolving underfilling or improving delivery, whereas persistent lactate with worsening perfusion reflects ongoing shock, congestion, or mitochondrial dysfunction. Fluids should help the first group more than the second.
2. **Severity/selection artifact**: patients with improving perfusion are less severely ill or have better reserve, so they improve later regardless of fluids. Worsening patients may be sicker and less able to respond.
3. **Treatment confounding**: clinicians may give more fluids to patients who appear underfilled and fewer to those who are congested or deteriorating. The observed association may reflect treatment selection rather than a fluid effect.
4. **Measurement artifact**: lactate, MAP, urine output, and vasopressors are measured at different intervals and may be selectively obtained. A sparse measurement pattern can create an apparent trajectory.

The design distinguishes these explanations by conditioning on the early perfusion trajectory and by using a temporal exposure window after the trajectory is defined. It does not fully eliminate treatment confounding. Propensity weighting or target-trial emulation is a sensitivity analysis, not the primary causal claim.

## Baseline and learned alternative

### Baseline: stratified linear model

Fit a linear model for the 72-hour perfusion-score change with:

- fluid volume in hours 24–48 (continuous),
- trajectory group (improving versus worsening),
- fluid-by-trajectory interaction,
- baseline age, sex, ICU length of stay, baseline lactate, baseline MAP, baseline urine output, baseline vasopressor dose, and early lactate slope.

The primary estimand is the difference in the fluid coefficient between the improving and worsening trajectory groups. Use bootstrap confidence intervals and report the interaction p-value as secondary.

This baseline estimates an average linear association within two trajectory groups. It is transparent and easy to audit.

### Learned alternative: gradient-boosted heterogeneous-effect model

Fit a gradient-boosted regression model for the same 72-hour perfusion-score change using the same inputs and the same trajectory groups. Use a two-stage approach:

1. Fit outcome models separately in each trajectory group.
2. Estimate the conditional fluid effect as the difference in predicted outcomes for a 1000-mL fluid increase, using the same covariates.
3. Report the mean effect and 90% prediction interval in each group, and the difference in mean effects.

The learned model can reveal nonlinear fluid-response curves and interactions with baseline severity that the linear baseline loses. It can also show whether the apparent heterogeneity is concentrated in a subset with high baseline vasopressor dose or low urine output.

The alternative does not establish causality. It is a descriptive heterogeneous-association model. If it adds little predictive improvement over the linear baseline, the simpler model is preferred. If it reveals a clinically interpretable nonlinear threshold or subgroup pattern, that is a substantive finding worth replication.

### Split and evaluation

Use a 70/30 train/test split by patient, stratified by trajectory group. Standardize variables using training means and standard deviations only. Evaluate:

- mean absolute error for the 72-hour perfusion-score change,
- calibration slope and intercept,
- difference in mean predicted fluid effect between trajectory groups,
- 90% prediction interval coverage.

The primary scientific comparison is not predictive accuracy alone. It is whether the learned model reveals a stable, clinically interpretable difference in fluid association that the linear baseline misses.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if:

- the fluid-by-trajectory interaction is not present in the primary linear model and the learned model does not reveal a stable, clinically interpretable alternative pattern;
- the apparent interaction disappears after restricting to the top and bottom quartiles of the perfusion trajectory;
- the association is driven by patients with fewer than two early lactate measurements or by a single vasopressor agent;
- the learned model's subgroup pattern is unstable across the train/test split and bootstrap resamples.

## Interpretation of results

### Supportive result

A positive fluid-by-trajectory interaction, with a larger positive association in the improving-perfusion group and a smaller or null association in the worsening-perfusion group, would support the claim that persistent hyperlactatemia has heterogeneous associations with fluid administration depending on early perfusion trajectory. This would justify further prospective validation and a clinical study of fluid strategies.

### Adverse result

A stronger or equal association in the worsening-perfusion group, or a null interaction, would argue against the seed hypothesis as stated. It would suggest that persistent lactate with worsening perfusion is not less responsive to fluids, or that the trajectory definition does not capture the relevant pathophysiologic distinction.

### Inconclusive result

A small interaction with wide confidence intervals, or a pattern that is present in the linear model but not stable in the learned model, would be inconclusive. It would not refute the hypothesis. It would require a larger dataset, better perfusion measurement, or a prospective study.

## What would require additional data or expert review

- Microcirculatory perfusion measures (e.g., sublingual flow, capnography, tissue oximetry) are not in MIMIC-IV.
- Fluid response is not randomized; causal claims require a prospective trial or a stronger target-trial design.
- The perfusion score is a composite and may not capture all relevant organ dysfunction.
- Clinical adjudication of shock etiology is not possible from MIMIC-IV alone.
