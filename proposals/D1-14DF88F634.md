# Proposal: when an early post-TACE trajectory predicts the next recorded care pathway

## Episode and deliverable

This is a substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. The scientific deliverable is a newly constructed, leakage-safe HCC cohort and a locked-test comparison of transparent competing-action hazard models with an observation-aware longitudinal model. The solver must fit/estimate:

1. B0: pre-index history plus measured capture features;
2. B1: B0 plus a prespecified early AFP/hepatic trajectory;
3. B2: B1 plus day-45-known measurement and encounter-process features;
4. M3: a low-rank irregular-time latent trajectory plus assay/examination observation submodel and competing action hazards; and
5. an all-index missingness-transport sensitivity.

Completion requires patient-level cohort/split manifests, cause-specific predictions and cumulative incidences, model parameters, calibration/log-score and bootstrap intervals, procedure-vocabulary and examination-proxy audits, negative-control and non-liver capture-control results, and an interpretation file linking every conclusion to computed outputs and a prespecified rule. Discovery has not solved the question.

## Unresolved clinical question and hypothesis

After TACE for HCC, a clinician must decide whether to arrange early reassessment or prepare for additional liver-directed treatment. The strongest claim supported by the inspected local evidence is only that this source contains structured HCC diagnoses, many dated procedures, dated laboratory measurements for AFP and hepatic-function proxies, and dated examination records. Public literature and the demonstrations motivate longitudinal biomarker and observation-process analyses, but do not establish this local cohort’s residual tumour burden, imaging response, treatment intent, or clinical utility.

The unresolved claim is whether the early trajectory is informative about the subsequent recorded care pathway beyond who is measured and remains in local care.

> Among adults with a structured HCC diagnosis and a first recorded TACE-like procedure, an early transformed-AFP improvement accompanied by non-deteriorating hepatic-marker proxies is associated with a lower cause-specific hazard and 365-day cumulative incidence of a subsequently recorded repeat TACE-like action, after adjustment for measured observation intensity; the association is more specific to liver-directed actions than to unrelated recorded procedures and is weaker for a pre-index analogue of the trajectory.

This is a prognostic association with a recorded action. It is not a claim of recurrence, viable tumour, radiologic response, treatment benefit, or a treatment recommendation.

## Population, temporal boundaries and outcomes

Use one index episode per patient. In table `procedures`, select the earliest row with case-insensitive `TACE`, `Transarterial chemoembolization`, or both `Hepatic artery` and `Embolization` in `Surgery`. Collapse qualifying rows for a patient within 24 hours into one index episode while retaining raw names, row counts, and a vocabulary audit. Use `Start Time`; report any fallback because it is missing (the source audit found no missing procedure start in the file).

Require age >=18 and nonmissing `sex` from the joined index encounter; a `diagnosis name` containing literal `hepatocellular carcinoma` in a diagnosis joined to an encounter from 180 days before through 7 days after index; index time no later than 2025-01-01; and sufficient local ascertainment to day 365. Unknown follow-up is excluded from the primary event denominator and counted, never coded as no event.

The single prediction landmark is day 45 after index. No record after day 45 can be a feature. The primary event window is days 46–365. Define mutually exclusive first subsequent recorded actions:

- repeat TACE-like: the same frozen TACE-like rule;
- alternate liver-directed: a procedure name containing `resection`, `ablation`, `radiofrequency`, or `microwave`, but not TACE-like;
- non-liver capture control: the first procedure in neither category.

Report cause-specific discrete-time hazards and competing-risk cumulative incidence for each category, plus a broad liver-directed category combining the first two. A non-event is not called treatment success. Report a literal-name sensitivity restricted to `TACE` or `transarterial chemoembolization`, and 90-/180-day outcome windows.

## Exposure construction and estimands

For the primary paired cohort require one parseable AFP in days -30 to -1 and one in days 7–45. Use last pre and first early-post values. Let z=log1p(AFP), define transformed improvement as z_post <= 0.5 z_pre, and retain continuous z_post-z_pre, missingness and inequality flags. For each hepatic assay, use last pre and first early-post values. Define within-assay non-deterioration as early >= pre for albumin and platelets, and early <= pre for total bilirubin and the coagulation-ratio proxy. The joint phenotype is AFP improvement plus non-deterioration in at least two of four hepatic assays. This is a proxy phenotype, not a clinical response threshold.

The primary estimand is the adjusted association between this phenotype/continuous trajectory and each first recorded competing action in the paired cohort. The all-index estimand is separate: model paired-biomarker completeness using training data only, inspect weight overlap, and apply stabilized inverse-probability weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles. Failed overlap is inconclusive.

The negative-control exposure repeats the construction in days -120 to -91 and -90 to -46, before index. It is not called a TACE response. If it predicts later actions as strongly as the post-index phenotype, the post-TACE-specific interpretation is falsified, though a generic prognostic association may remain.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

B0 is a regularized cause-specific discrete-time hazard/multinomial model with age, sex, index department and procedure family; pre-index HCC/cirrhosis/portal-vein/liver-, lung- and bone-metastasis/ascites diagnosis indicators and counts; prior liver-directed procedure counts; pre-window assay values/missingness; and measured capture through day 45.

B1 adds the frozen phenotype and continuous changes. B2 adds only day-45-known process variables: distinct encounter dates, lab rows/dates, assay-specific first-observation timing and availability, examination counts/days-to-first/family indicators, and explicitly labeled completed-record proxies if available.

M3 is a scientifically substantive alternative, not a complexity bonus: two low-rank latent proxy states over days -30 to 45 (AFP-informed tumour-activity proxy and four-assay hepatic-function proxy), irregular-time random intercepts/slopes or a low-rank state-space model, and an observation submodel for each assay/examination conditional on prior observations, baseline covariates and latent state. Posterior day-45 states, slopes, uncertainty and capture intensity feed competing action hazards; future data cannot smooth the landmark. M3 can reveal whether asynchronous trajectory shape and informative measurement timing carry information lost by snapshots, but cannot recover tumour burden, response, or access.

The simple baseline is scientifically necessary because it tests incremental information, not merely predictive rank. A transformer adaptation of the natural-history demonstration is deferred: structured dated history is present, but it would add little identifiable information beyond M3 for this single institutional action endpoint and would make vocabulary/leakage auditing harder. The multimodal cancer demonstration cannot be reproduced because image files are absent; a lab-only adaptation is represented by B1/B2/M3. The Bayesian latent-trajectory demonstration supports M3’s modeling idea, but the local HCC study has no verified genetics and therefore does not claim a genetic reproduction. These are dispositions, not claims that the demonstrations establish this hypothesis.

## Splits, uncertainty and falsification

Use index-year splits: 2010–2022 fit, 2023 tuning, and 2024–2025-01-01 locked test, based on procedure start; if calendar parsing or support fails, use fixed patient-hash buckets and record the fallback. No patient crosses a split. Parsers, imputation, penalties, latent settings, missingness models and weights are fit without locked-test outcomes.

For each action and the broad liver-directed outcome report cause-specific contrasts, cumulative incidence at 365 days, AUROC/PR-AUC only where meaningful, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, mean log score, event counts and patient-bootstrap or declared robust intervals. Compare B2 and M3 on the same paired cohort. The 0.02 mean-log-score difference is a model-comparison margin, not clinical utility.

Supportive evidence requires a repeat-TACE phenotype contrast <=0.80 with a 95% interval entirely below 1.0; direction preserved under literal naming and 90-/180-day windows; <30% attenuation after B2 and weighting; stronger association for liver-directed than non-liver capture-control actions; no competing-action reversal; a weaker pre-index negative control; and M3 mean-log-score improvement >=0.02 without materially worse calibration. These are operational rules, not treatment thresholds.

Adverse evidence is null/reversed association, >=30% attenuation after process adjustment/weighting, a strong phenotype-by-testing interaction, similar association for the non-liver control, a pre-index analogue as strong as the post-index phenotype, instability under frozen sensitivities, poor calibration, or an M3 gain that vanishes after leakage/observation controls. An opposite or common alternate liver-directed action makes a repeat-TACE-only conclusion adverse/incomplete.

Inconclusive evidence is too few paired patients/events, failed overlap, wide intervals, high unknown follow-up, date-incomplete examinations, failed M3 convergence, or absent clinical adjudication. Inconclusive is not confirmation.

## Clinical advance and limits

If supportive, the study would establish a reproducible, observation-adjusted association between an early multi-marker trajectory and a subsequent local liver-directed care pathway, and determine whether it is specific to repeat TACE or simply reflects return/testing intensity. This could justify a prospective study of biomarker-triggered reassessment. If adverse, it would warn against treating AFP/hepatic trajectories as disease-specific retreatment signals and identify capture processes as the dominant explanation. If inconclusive, it identifies the missing follow-up, event support, or adjudication needed before changing care.

The available data cannot establish radiologic response, viable tumour, BCLC stage, treatment intent, death, outside-care actions, true decompensation, causal treatment effects, clinical utility, or external validity. The non-liver procedure is only a capture control, not proven biologically unrelated. Those claims require time-aligned expert imaging/pathology review, mortality/claims linkage, treatment intent and an external or prospective cohort. The verifier can check cohort construction, leakage, parsing, predictions, intervals and interpretation-to-output links; it cannot establish those clinical claims.
