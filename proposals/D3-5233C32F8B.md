# Context-Specific Pathway Activity in RA Synovial Tissue: Fixed Membership vs. Data-Driven Inference

## Scientific Opening

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue exhibits dysregulated signaling pathways (NF-κB, JAK-STAT, MAPK) that drive inflammation and joint destruction. Standard pathway analysis assumes fixed pathway membership (e.g., a gene is either in the NF-κB pathway or not) and then measures the aggregate expression of member genes. However, pathway membership is context-dependent: a gene may participate in multiple pathways, and the functional relevance of a pathway may depend on the cell type, disease state, or microenvironment.

The D3-M3 expert seed asks: "Can pathway-level activity be inferred from gene expression without assuming a fixed pathway membership?" This is clinically consequential because if fixed-pathway enrichment misses context-specific pathway activity, then pathway-based interventions (e.g., JAK inhibitors) may be misdirected. Resolving this question changes the next mechanistic experiment: if data-driven inference reveals context-specific pathway activity not captured by fixed membership, then pathway-targeted therapies should be re-evaluated in the specific disease context.

## Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: In RA synovial tissue, fixed-pathway membership (e.g., standard NF-κB, JAK-STAT, MAPK gene sets) is insufficient to capture the full spectrum of pathway activity. Data-driven pathway activity inference (e.g., using gene co-expression networks or pathway embedding methods) reveals context-specific pathway activity that is not captured by fixed membership, and this context-specific activity is associated with disease severity and treatment response.

## Competing Explanations

1. **Fixed-pathway sufficient**: Standard pathway membership captures all relevant pathway activity. Data-driven inference adds no new information beyond what fixed membership provides.

2. **Context-specific activity exists**: Pathway membership is context-dependent, and data-driven inference reveals activity patterns that are missed by fixed membership. This activity is associated with disease severity and treatment response.

3. **Both needed**: Fixed membership captures the core pathway activity, but data-driven inference adds complementary information about context-specific modulation.

## Evidence Opening

The available datasets provide single-cell and pseudobulk expression data from RA synovial tissue:
- **E-MTAB-8873**: Targeted assay with 445 measured feature columns, including FLS and coculture samples.
- **E-MTAB-8322**: Pseudobulk contains measured macrophages only; not whole tissue or a cell-state-resolved analysis.
- **E-MTAB-8316**: Four FLS donor clusters, six nested monocyte donors, twelve condition samples.

These datasets provide the expression data needed to test whether fixed-pathway membership is sufficient or whether context-specific pathway activity is needed.

## Falsifiable Test

**Test**: Compare fixed-pathway enrichment (e.g., GSEA using standard NF-κB, JAK-STAT, MAPK gene sets) with data-driven pathway activity inference (e.g., using gene co-expression networks or pathway embedding methods) in RA synovial tissue.

**Supportive outcome**: Data-driven inference reveals context-specific pathway activity that is not captured by fixed membership, and this activity is associated with disease severity and treatment response.

**Adverse outcome**: Fixed-pathway membership captures all relevant pathway activity. Data-driven inference adds no new information beyond what fixed membership provides.

**Inconclusive outcome**: Data-driven inference reveals some context-specific activity, but the association with disease severity and treatment response is not clear.

## Method Alternatives

1. **Fixed-pathway GSEA**: Standard gene set enrichment analysis using predefined pathway gene sets.
2. **Data-driven pathway embedding**: Methods that infer pathway activity from gene co-expression networks or other data-driven approaches.
3. **Hybrid approach**: Combine fixed membership with data-driven inference to capture both core and context-specific activity.
