# Compiler-ready child: residual instability at ICU discharge and 7-day post-ICU failure

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]` (assessed valid; highest-ranked MIMIC parent in the assigned board)

Dataset: MIMIC-IV v3.1 configured snapshot `[source checksum]`

## 1. Question, evidence boundary, and clinical advance

The unresolved question is whether a repeatedly observed, physiologically defined residual-instability phenotype at the end of an ICU stay identifies a clinically distinct subgroup at risk of a first ICU readmission or death before readmission within seven days. The consequential decision is where to target post-ICU surveillance and which signals merit prospective discharge-readiness evaluation.

The falsifiable prognostic hypothesis is:

> In adults discharged alive from an ICU, higher final-24-hour vital-instability burden and higher charted oxygen-support intensity are associated with higher seven-day cause-specific risk of first ICU readmission and of death before readmission, beyond information available at ICU discharge; adding these features improves calibrated prediction in a locked source-era holdout.

This is a prognostic association/prediction hypothesis. It is not a claim that ICU discharge caused the event, that the measured instability caused it, or that delaying discharge, changing oxygen, or increasing monitoring would improve outcomes.

Before this experiment, the available evidence supports only that the configured MIMIC-IV snapshot contains linked admissions, ICU stays, timestamped chart events, item definitions, and in-hospital outcomes. The seed and parent suggest the clinical hypothesis but do not establish it. The experiment will add an operational phenotype, competing-event estimands, measurement-coverage audit, leakage controls, incremental baselines, and locked evaluation. A supportive result would justify prospective adjudication and external validation; it would not justify a discharge policy by itself. A null or unstable result would be clinically useful evidence against relying on these charted measures alone.

The substantive advance over the parent is fourfold: adults and ICU-time boundaries are explicit; the primary association population and the all-eligible prediction population are separated; an executable feasibility artifact with fixed minimum event/coverage gates is required before interpretation; and the prediction model, association estimand, uncertainty, and interpretation rules are no longer conflated.

## 2. Population and index

Use all five available MIMIC anchor-year groups and report every flow count by `anchor_year_group`.

Join:

- `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `subject_id, hadm_id`;
- `hosp/admissions` to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`.

An eligible ICU discharge row must satisfy:

1. `anchor_age >= 18`;
2. non-null `intime`, `outtime`, and admission `dischtime`;
3. `intime < outtime < dischtime`;
4. admission `deathtime` is null or strictly later than `outtime`.

The admission death time is used only to establish survival at index and to define the competing outcome. It is never a predictor. MIMIC’s `anchor_age=91` represents age greater than 89 and is retained as 91 without interpreting it as exact.

The primary index is the earliest eligible ICU discharge by `outtime` for each `subject_id, hadm_id`. Apply this first-index rule before calculating either exposure or outcome. The primary population therefore has one index per hospitalization and avoids converting a later ICU stay into both a new index and an outcome. A secondary episode-level analysis includes every eligible ICU discharge and clusters inference by `hadm_id`; it is not allowed to replace the primary result.

The index time is the ICU `outtime). All prediction features must be computable from rows with clinical `charttime < outtime). `storetime` is audit-only and must never define availability. The primary exposure window is the intersection of the final 24 hours and the ICU stay:
`[max(intime, outtime - 24 hours), outtime)`.
The six fixed four-hour bins are defined relative to `outtime`: bin 0 is `[outtime-24h,outtime-20h)`, through bin 5 `[outtime-4h,outtime)`. Rows before `intime`, at `outtime), or after `outtime` are excluded, leaving early-short-stay bins legitimately unobserved.

The audit and final analysis must report index counts and exposure coverage separately for the five `anchor_year_group` values. Subject-level splitting is mandatory. Since MIMIC dates are shifted independently by subject, anchor-year groups are treated as a source-era partition for locked evaluation, not as proof of real calendar trends.

## 3. Outcomes and estimands

For each index, define administrative follow-up end as:
`min(outtime + 7 days, dischtime)).

A first ICU readmission is the earliest later row in `icu/icustays` for the same `subject_id, hadm_id` with `intime > index outtime` and `intime <= follow-up end`. The primary definition accepts any positive gap; report the gap distribution. A sensitivity definition requires `intime >= outtime + 2 hours` to assess near-contiguous administrative stays.

The competing death event is admission `deathtime` with `outtime < deathtime <= follow-up end`, occurring before the readmission. If readmission and death have an exact timestamp tie, classify death first and report the number of ties. If neither event occurs before follow-up end, censor at hospital discharge or seven days. A later death after readmission is not relabeled as death-before-readmission; it is a secondary all-cause in-hospital death outcome.

The primary estimands are:

- the cause-specific hazard ratio for first ICU readmission per 0.10 increase in the vital-instability proportion, and separately per 1 L/min increase in `flow_max` and per 0.10 increase in `fio2_max`, conditional on the minimum discharge-time covariates;
- the analogous cause-specific hazard ratio for death before readmission;
- seven-day Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence for each event by prespecified exposure strata;
- incremental seven-day prediction performance of residual features over the simple baseline.

The high-support phenotype is descriptive, fixed before outcome inspection:
`flow_max >= 4 L/min OR fio2_max >= 0.40`, when the corresponding oxygen measure is observed. It is a sensitivity/communication stratum, not a treatment threshold.

The primary association estimand is conditional on measurement adequacy: at least four of six bins contain at least one valid vital measurement. This makes “repeatedly observed instability” interpretable and makes its selection fraction explicit. The target population remains all eligible adult ICU discharges. The all-eligible analysis is the transport-oriented prediction estimand: it retains missingness and density indicators and does not treat unobserved physiology as stable. Results must state which of these two populations they describe.

## 4. Exposure construction

Read only `icu/chartevents` rows whose `stay_id` is the primary index stay, whose `charttime` lies in the window above, and whose `itemid` is in the fixed list below. Join to `icu/d_items` on `itemid` and verify label, unitname, linksto, and param_type before feature construction. Retain raw `value`, `valuenum`, `valueuom`, `warning`, `charttime`, and `storetime) in an audit extract; do not silently substitute similarly named items.

Oxygen items:

- 223834, “O2 Flow”, L/min;
- 227287, “O2 Flow (additional cannula)”, L/min;
- 227582, “BiPap O2 Flow”, L/min;
- 223835, “Inspired O2 Fraction”, dictionary unit “None”;
- 226732, “O2 Delivery Device(s)”, text.

For the three flow items, retain nonnegative numeric values and set `flow_max` to their maximum; do not add streams whose semantics may overlap. For item 223835, accept values in [0.21, 1.0] as fractions and values in (1,100] after division by 100; all other values are invalid and counted, not repaired. Set `fio2_max` to the maximum valid normalized fraction. The text device item is used only for raw/descriptive frequency tables; it is not converted into a device category without clinician review. Record whether each oxygen item was observed, and define high-support only when a valid value exists.

Vital items and validity/abnormality rules:

| Signal | itemid(s) | valid values | abnormal value |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Heart rate | 220045 | 20–250 bpm | <50 or >100 |
| Respiratory rate | 220210 | 4–80 insp/min | <8 or >24 |
| SpO2 | 220277 | 50–100% | <92% |
| MAP | 220181, 220052 | 20–200 mmHg | <65 |
| Temperature | 223761, 223762 | 30–43°C after conversion | <36 or >=38°C |

For MAP, combine valid non-invasive mean and arterial mean by taking the minimum within each bin, retain source indicators, and provide separate-source sensitivity results. Convert Fahrenheit by `(F-32)/1.8). Do not use alarm items, APACHE-derived items, arterial oxygen pressure/saturation, or ventilator-set respiratory rate in the primary phenotype.

For each of six bins, set `valid_block=1` if at least one valid measurement from the five vital signals is present, and `abnormal_block=1` if any valid measurement is abnormal. Define:
`vital_instability = sum(abnormal_block) / sum(valid_block)` when at least one valid block exists.
The primary association set requires `sum(valid_block) >= 4`. Report the all-eligible feature with its denominator, valid-block count, abnormal-block count, and missingness indicator; no missing block is coded as stable. Also report the fraction of observed valid vital measurements that are abnormal, recognizing that measurement-level counts weight densely measured signals differently.

The bounded residual feature set has at most 16 additions to B0: `flow_max`, `fio2_max`, `vital_instability`, abnormal-measurement fraction, six block-validity/count features, the three oxygen-observation indicators, MAP-source indicators, and the fixed high-support indicator. The exact feature manifest and missing-value rule must be written before fitting. For prediction only, continuous missing values are imputed with the training-set median and paired with a missingness indicator; no median is used to create an exposure value in the primary complete-observation association analysis.

## 5. Covariates, treatment limitation, and unavailable evidence

B0 contains only information available by `outtime):

- `hosp/patients`: `anchor_age`, `gender`, `anchor_year_group`;
- `hosp/admissions`: `admission_type`, `admission_location`, `insurance), and elapsed `outtime-admittime`;
- `icu/icustays`: index `first_careunit`, `last_careunit`, `los`;
- the count of earlier ICU stays in the same hospitalization, derived from `icu/icustays.intime/outtime) before the index.

Use one-hot encoding for categorical fields, prespecified linear terms for continuous fields, and explicit missing categories/indicators. Do not use `deathtime`, `hospital_expire_flag`, `discharge_location`, `dischtime), future ICU rows, or any post-index field as an adjustment variable. Diagnoses, procedures, notes, and orders may be reported descriptively but are not B0/B1 covariates unless a separate analysis proves availability by `outtime); the compiler should not infer that availability from table membership.

Treatment limitation is a central unmeasured confounder. Item 223758 (“Code Status”, text) is available in `icu/chartevents`; audit its final-window observation rate and raw values. No lexical mapping to DNR, DNI, comfort measures, or a treatment-limitation category is authorized in the primary run. If and only if a clinician-reviewed mapping is supplied, add the latest pre-index category and a missingness indicator as a labelled sensitivity analysis. Discharge notes cannot repair this limitation: `note/discharge` is a separate note source, may be post-index, and is unstructured. Do not use it in the primary analysis. Functional status, cognition, ward staffing, post-discharge oxygen access, clinician intent, and complete goals-of-care status are unavailable or not clinically adjudicated here.

## 6. Computation, baselines, validation, and uncertainty

First produce a cohort-flow table, by anchor-year group, containing all ICU stays, adult stays, eligible alive-discharge stays, first-index count, measurement-eligible count, oxygen coverage, code-status coverage, readmission count, death-before-readmission count, censoring count, and the number of possible <2-hour readmissions. Also produce per-item row counts, invalid numeric counts, valid-block distributions, and exact event-time ties. These are prerequisite audits, not scientific estimates.

B0 is the simple baseline. For association, fit separate cause-specific Cox models for readmission and death-before-readmission with B0 only. For prediction, fit a three-class seven-day multinomial model (readmission, death-before-readmission, neither by seven days) with B0 only.

B1 is the bounded residual model. For association, add the fixed residual features to B0 in separate cause-specific Cox models. Report unadjusted exposure summaries, B0-adjusted associations, and B1-adjusted associations so adjustment is not mistaken for causality. For prediction, add the fixed residual features to B0 in ridge-penalized multinomial regression, with penalty selected in the training data only. No automated feature selection, neural network, transformer, GPU, or outcome-driven threshold search is permitted.

For time-to-event analysis, report robust sandwich standard errors clustered by `subject_id`; the primary one-index-per-admission design minimizes within-admission dependence. Estimate 95% confidence intervals for hazard ratios and cumulative incidence. Use Aalen–Johansen estimates at 24 hours, 72 hours, and seven days with death as a competing event for readmission and vice versa. A subdistribution hazard may be shown as descriptive sensitivity only and must not be called a causal effect.

Use subject-level partitions: train on `2008 - 2010`, `2011 - 2013`, and `2014 - 2016`; validate on `2017 - 2019`; lock the test on `2020 - 2022`. Keep every hospitalization from one subject in one partition. This is a source-era holdout under subject-shifted dates, not external validation. If an anchor group is absent, or any partition has fewer than 20 readmission events or 20 death-before-readmission events for fitting, preserve the planned split and label that endpoint infeasible; do not reassign years. For locked-test calibration claims require at least 10 of each event and 30 total first events; otherwise report only descriptive test counts.

Report AUROC and AUPRC for each event, seven-day class-specific Brier score, multiclass Brier/log loss, calibration intercept and slope, reliability tables by decile, and absolute observed versus predicted cumulative incidence. Use 500 subject-level bootstrap resamples for prediction metric intervals where event counts permit; use the Cox robust intervals for association and state when bootstrap strata fail. A performance difference is clinically meaningful only if calibration and absolute-risk separation are credible; an AUC increment alone is not a clinical utility claim. Decision-curve analysis is optional descriptive context and cannot imply a treatment benefit.

## 7. Leakage assertions and falsification

The executable pipeline must fail closed if any assertion fails:

- every primary exposure row has `charttime < outtime` and `charttime >= intime`;
- `storetime` is never used as availability time;
- every exposure row joins to exactly one indexed `stay_id`;
- no later ICU stay contributes exposure rows;
- the first-index rule is applied before feature or outcome calculation;
- no outcome field or post-index data enters B0/B1;
- `d_items` labels, units, linkage, and parameter types match the fixed item manifest;
- all source paths and archive members match the configured snapshot and recorded SHA-256.

The computational negative control is a within-partition permutation of the complete final-window residual feature vectors, preserving missingness/density structure but breaking subject-outcome linkage. B1 must lose its incremental locked-test performance over B0 within uncertainty. If it does not, investigate leakage, split contamination, or an overly permissive metric pipeline; do not interpret it as biology.

The fixed interpretation rules are:

- Supportive: in the prespecified primary association set, the direction is adverse and consistent for continuous vital burden and the fixed high-support stratum for at least one primary event; the 95% interval excludes the null for that event; Aalen–Johansen estimates agree; B1 improves locked-test calibration and meaningful absolute-risk separation over B0; and the result is not confined to a single oxygen item, MAP source, or dense-charting subgroup.
- Adverse: the adjusted association is consistently protective or the cumulative incidence is lower with greater burden, or the adverse direction fails in the complete-observation and all-eligible sensitivity frameworks. This falsifies the stated adverse prognostic hypothesis; it does not prove a protective biological effect.
- Inconclusive: event or coverage gates fail, intervals are wide, estimates reverse across window/gap/coverage definitions, or the signal is confined to measurement-dense patients. Sparse oxygen items make oxygen-specific conclusions inconclusive even if vital burden is estimable.
- A death-only signal with no readmission signal is interpreted as a possible marker of treatment limitation, non-readmission death, or ascertainment, not as evidence of ward-readmission risk.
- A readmission signal that disappears after treating death as a competing event invalidates a naive composite/logistic interpretation.
- Any signal requiring post-`outtime` rows, discharge-note text, or future ICU information is invalid leakage.
- A statistically significant but very small prediction change without calibration or actionable absolute-risk separation is prognostic evidence only, not a discharge-policy recommendation.

Computationally checkable claims are row eligibility, join uniqueness, temporal exclusion, feature arithmetic, event labeling, model fitting, uncertainty calculations, split integrity, and metric differences. Clinical adjudication is required for treatment limitation, actual oxygen delivery/device use, clinical meaning of abnormality, and discharge readiness. Causal discharge claims, intervention benefit, external transportability, and clinical utility require prospective or external studies.

## 9. Demonstration and alternative-seed dispositions

I read the available material named by `references/research-ambition/README.md). The full natural-history article and its available supplements support the design principle of strict prediction-time temporal constraints and calibration/transport checks; they do not establish this ICU hypothesis. The cancer main article and STAR Methods remain unavailable through the checked endpoints; only its supplement was inspected, so no claim about the main article is used. Its cohort-flow, washout, calibration, and validation illustrations motivate reporting discipline only. The full Bayesian longitudinal article was inspected; its joint longitudinal/competing-process framing motivates keeping event types and time explicit, but its biobank/genetic setting does not validate this MIMIC phenotype. None of these papers supplies a MIMIC clinical conclusion.

I retained seed 10 because its residual-instability question is directly aligned with the available ICU chart and outcome tables. I considered the bounded MIMIC seed directions on asynchronous/discordant organ recovery (seeds 01 and 03) and creatinine during diuresis (seed 08). Those would require an AKI/congestion definition and, for stronger claims, measured kidney function or clinically adjudicated fluid status; the configured snapshot does not provide those adjudications. They are not stronger bounded alternatives for this episode. This proposal preserves the seed’s caution that repeated measures are selective and that a recovery phenotype cannot be treated as a causal treatment effect.

## 10. What changed and what remains uncertain

Changed from the parent: explicit adult restriction; ICU-stay-bounded exposure bins; a precise primary measurement-eligible association estimand versus all-eligible prediction estimand; tie handling and fixed event/coverage gates; a fixed three-class prediction target; a locked output/manifest contract; 500-bootstrap uncertainty; fail-closed leakage assertions; source-era wording for anchor-year holdout; and explicit demonstration/alternative-seed dispositions.

Remaining uncertainty: MIMIC charting is selective, oxygen item semantics may not reflect actual delivery, code status lacks adjudicated mapping, and unobserved ward and functional factors may dominate risk. The execution may show sparse coverage or insufficient events; in that case the correct conclusion is infeasibility/inconclusive for the affected endpoint, not a substituted easier question. Even a robust result remains an association within one deidentified hospital system and cannot support a causal discharge intervention without clinician review, external validation, and prospective evaluation.
