## Proposed relationship

In the available E-MTAB-8873 RA macrophage–fibroblast coculture assay, the MerTK-inhibitor-associated transcriptional response differs between MerTK/CD206-positive and MerTK/CD206-negative macrophage contexts. The primary estimand is a donor-aware, condition-verified context-by-inhibitor interaction, not a generic mean-expression change. A secondary descriptive question is whether the interaction separates a macrophage-linked resolving/repair-associated program from a fibroblast-linked inflammatory or harmful program.

The strongest available evidence supports biological plausibility of MerTK-positive resolving macrophage states and macrophage–fibroblast repair interactions, but does not establish this assay-specific response, direct action of UNC106238, causality, or clinical benefit. The unresolved claim is whether the apparent interaction survives donor-aware contrasts and prespecified negative controls.

## Falsifiable test

Fit a donor-aware baseline comparing treatment-associated changes within each context, then test the prespecified context-by-inhibitor interaction. Use composition negative controls and a within-context treatment negative control to distinguish specificity from pseudobulk composition, donor reuse, or generic treatment effects. Support requires a stable interaction with uncertainty compatible with the prespecified effect and no comparable signal in the negative controls. Adverse evidence is a generic treatment/composition signal, context reversal, or failure under donor-aware analysis. Inconclusive evidence includes insufficient independent donors, unstable feature definitions, wide uncertainty, or contradictory controls; it does not refute the hypothesis.

The simple baseline is a donor-aware matched contrast with context and treatment main effects. The alternative is scientifically useful only if it improves interpretation of the interaction and uncertainty, not merely predictive performance.
