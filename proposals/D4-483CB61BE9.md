# Weight loss during HbA1c worsening as a near-term pancreatic-cancer signal

## Unresolved question and clinical importance
The strongest inspected evidence supports an association between recent-onset diabetes/worsening glycemia and weight loss with subsequent pancreatic cancer, but it does not establish whether this phenotype is specific to a near-term occult-cancer window in an unselected population, or whether it is explained by treatment, obesity-related metabolic deterioration, or another catabolic illness [K1][K2][K3]. Resolving this matters because a reproducible, temporally specific phenotype could justify expert review of targeted pancreatic evaluation, while a null or treatment-driven result would argue against indiscriminate imaging.

## Hypothesis
Among UK Biobank participants with two dated assessment visits, no pancreatic cancer before the later visit, and comparable change in HbA1c, clinically meaningful weight loss is associated with higher incidence of pancreatic cancer diagnosed within 2 years after the later visit than stable/increasing weight. The HbA1c-worsening × weight-loss interaction will be stronger in the 0–2-year window than in the 2–5-year window.

## Population, index and outcomes
Use only the UKB/ukb672073 identifier namespace and join on eid. Include adults with nonmissing HbA1c and weight at two assessment-center dates, a positive pre-specified interval (initially 1–10 years), and no ICD-10 C25 diagnosis before the later date. The index is the later assessment date. Primary outcome is first C25 diagnosis in 0–2 years; secondary outcome is 2–5 years. Censor at death or the fixed administrative end available in the frozen catalog. Use a landmark design; no post-index measurement is a predictor.

## Analysis and method comparison
Primary model: cause-specific discrete-time or Cox model for 0–2-year C25 with HbA1c change, weight change, their interaction, age, sex, BMI, glucose, interval and recorded diabetes treatment; report interaction, absolute risk difference, calibration and robust uncertainty. Sensitivities use treatment-naive participants, stricter intervals, missingness indicators/inverse weighting, and the later window.

Baseline: the prespecified additive/interaction model above. Substantive alternative: a regularized latent longitudinal trajectory model (or mechanistic joint model if event support permits) using the same two dated HbA1c/glucose/weight observations, assessment timing, covariates and outcome, with patient-level time-dependent splits. It can reveal whether trajectory shape/timing carries information lost by two scalar changes, but cannot identify a mechanism. Compare on identical held-out participants using calibration, discrimination and uncertainty; do not select for a tiny predictive gain. CPU is appropriate for the baseline; the alternative is planned within the solver envelope (16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 2 GPUs, 28,800 seconds), with GPU use optional and unverified until a bounded fit.

## Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: reproducible positive interaction and excess near-term risk in held-out data, weaker later-window association, and stability under treatment/interval/missingness analyses. Adverse: no interaction, later-window-only association, or attenuation after treatment/missingness controls, favoring treatment, ascertainment or general catabolic explanations. Inconclusive: too few C25 events, sparse joint measurements, or wide confidence intervals; do not broaden to all cancers. A positive result establishes an association/prognostic pattern, not causality or a pancreatic mechanism. Complete prescriptions, symptoms, imaging, clinical adjudication and external validation are required for stronger claims.
