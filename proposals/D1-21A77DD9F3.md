## Unresolved claim and clinical importance

The parent establishes no empirical result; its strongest available claim is a well-specified, untested observational question about whether a recorded bilirubin/ALT/AST trajectory modifies the lactate-by-measured-perfusion association. The expert MIMIC-05 card likewise proposes, but does not establish, that acute glucose should be interpreted relative to chronic glycemia. The local dictionary confirms fields, not biological validity.

The unresolved clinical question is:

> Among adults with late record-defined hepatic injury at a first live ICU exit, is the prognostic meaning of a nonrecovering versus recovering biochemical trajectory different when acute glucose is high relative to the patient's recent chronic glycemic background?

This matters because acute stress hyperglycemia, chronic diabetes, insulin treatment, anemia and transfusion can all affect the relation between an observed glucose and an HbA1c-derived background. If the same biochemical nonrecovery signal has materially different near-term risk under high versus low relative glycemic stress, a future monitoring display may need metabolic context rather than treating the trajectory as a uniform warning. If it does not, this supports a simpler interpretation and avoids adding a selective HbA1c feature. This is a prognostic/interpretive question, not a treatment-effect test.

Define chronic estimated average glucose as `eAG = 28.7 * HbA1c_percent - 46.7` mg/dL. Let acute glucose be the median of valid blood glucose measurements in `[tL-6h,tL]`, and define `Q=high` when acute glucose/eAG is at least 1.20 and `Q=low` when it is below 1.20; Q is indeterminate when either component is unavailable or invalid. The 1.20 threshold and formula are frozen before outcome reading. A sensitivity uses acute-minus-eAG >=30 mg/dL and does not replace the primary.

Within the parent's H+ late hepatic-burden group and the parent's paired-marker recorded biochemical trajectory gate, let `K_q` be the standardized 48-hour CIF for R-or-D for `G=nonrecovering` minus `G=recovering`, standardized over the parent P/NL lactate and I/W perfusion cells using training-era weights. The primary new estimand is `Theta_Q = K_high - K_low`; report R-specific and D-specific versions and S as a competing event. The directional hypothesis is `Theta_Q > 0`: nonrecovering biochemical markers are less specifically reassuring, or more strongly associated with R/D, when acute glucose is high relative to chronic glycemia. A 0.05 absolute CIF-difference null region is clinically meaningful; estimates inside it are not material evidence.

Supportive evidence requires the prespecified support gates in both L and C, `Theta_Q` and the relevant R/D components to exclude the ±0.05 null region in the hypothesized direction, concordant era direction, and a corresponding held-out fixed-queue improvement for Bgly over Btraj without a similar Bmask/process signal. Adverse evidence is a reproducible null or reversal, no calibrated incremental value, a process-only replication, or persistence after Q permutation/order shuffling. Inconclusive evidence includes inadequate HbA1c pairing, unit failure, sparse Q×G×phenotype cells, poor overlap, high unresolved U, unstable bootstrap, or L/C disagreement. None establishes clinical benefit or mechanism.

## Falsification, alternatives and claim limits

1. Audit dictionary labels, accepted units, values, duplicates, specimen types, HbA1c timing, chart/store lag, anemia/transfusion proxies if available, insulin observation and glucose/HbA1c testing intensity.
2. Refit a Bmask process-only model using glucose/HbA1c availability, test counts, store lag, care-unit and intervention channels but no biochemical values. Similar `Theta_Q` weakens a metabolic interpretation.
3. Permute Q within era/partition/H/G support strata and refit; the interaction and queue gain must collapse toward the permutation benchmark. Shuffle early/late glucose ordering while preserving value counts and masks; persistence weakens a temporal claim.
4. Repeat with HbA1c window 30–180 days, acute glucose threshold 1.20 versus absolute gap 30 mg/dL, laboratory-only versus ICU-chart sensitivity, and without insulin/process channels. These are frozen sensitivities, not post hoc replacements.
5. Audit anemia, transfusion, steroid/insulin availability where locally represented; these are confounding/measurement flags, not validated corrections. A sensitivity can stratify by available proxies but cannot establish true chronic glycemia.
6. Repeat parent lactate/perfusion phenotype with inherited MAP modality and pressor missingness sensitivities; do not interpret persistence as causal physiology.

A supportive result establishes only a reproducible association between a record-defined acute/chronic glycemia phenotype and observed heterogeneity in the prognostic association of biochemical trajectory at live ICU exit. It does not establish stress physiology, hepatic clearance, diabetes diagnosis, liver recovery, treatment effect, preventability, monitoring benefit, net benefit or external validity. HbA1c may be selectively ordered and distorted by anemia, transfusion, hemoglobinopathy, renal disease or recent treatment; MIMIC lacks adjudicated diabetes status, reliable pre-illness glycemic history, insulin intent, full steroid/nutrition context, laboratory assay harmonization, prospective discharge-readiness adjudication and external validation. Clinical experts must review Q thresholds, unit normalization, meaningfulness of the R/D endpoint, HbA1c validity and operational plausibility. A prospective silent-mode study with adjudicated metabolic status and external validation is required for stronger clinical claims.
