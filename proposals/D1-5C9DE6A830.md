## Missing-Y computation and exact output contract

For each assay, origin, and future block, use all `BP_eligible` rows. Fit M0/M1 only on pre-origin observed complete triplets and freeze finite `p0_i,p1_i` for every BP row. Any nonfinite required prediction stops that block with `computationally_invalid`; do not drop that row. Let `N` be all BP-eligible rows and `n_mis` the missing count.

Define `L_train=min(Y_train)` and `U_train=max(Y_train)` from the exact finite training parser before inspecting future Y. If the range is empty or degenerate, or any observed future Y lies outside `[L_train,U_train]`, set bound status `unsupported` and do not widen the range using future or full-source values. A full-source range, if computed, is a separately labeled descriptive check. If no future Y exists, a training observed-Y mean may be recorded only as a training-derived tipping reference; it does not create a future outcome.

For observed rows, `e0=(Y-p0)^2`, `e1=(Y-p1)^2`, and `d=e0-e1`. For missing rows and model `j`, use

```
e_j,min = (clip(p_j,L_train,U_train)-p_j)^2
e_j,max = max((L_train-p_j)^2,(U_train-p_j)^2)
```

For the signed contrast use the exact affine identity

```
d(y) = 2*y*(p1-p0) + p0^2 - p1^2.
```

Therefore each missing row contributes `min(d(L),d(U))` and `max(d(L),d(U))`; sum observed contributions and divide by fixed `N` for sharp conditional `Delta_lower` and `Delta_upper` MSE bounds, overall and separately by arm. Report missing counts/fractions and three deterministic scenarios: all missing at L, all at U, and each missing row at its least-favorable endpoint for the lower M0-minus-M1 contrast. A positive Delta means lower M1 error.

For each model separately, report SSE and MSE bounds. The only RMSE-difference enclosure is the conservative non-primary interval `[sqrt(MSE0_lower)-sqrt(MSE1_upper), sqrt(MSE0_upper)-sqrt(MSE1_lower)]`; never call it sharp or standardize it by an unidentified test SD. If test Y SD is zero/nonfinite, primary G and its CI are `nonestimable`, while valid unstandardized bound arithmetic may remain available.

The common-displacement sensitivity sets every missing value to `clip(m_ref+delta,L_train,U_train)`, where `m_ref` is the observed future-Y mean when available, otherwise the explicitly marked training-derived mean. Since d is affine and clipping is shared, Delta(delta) is continuous piecewise linear with breakpoints only at `L_train-m_ref` and `U_train-m_ref`. Solve each segment analytically, retain roots only within the segment, verify by direct evaluation, choose the smallest absolute root with ties by smallest delta, and report whether the endpoint interval crosses zero. This is a sensitivity coordinate, not an imputation or clinical plausibility claim.

The aggregate output must contain, for each assay/origin/block: snapshot and catalog hashes; source/schema IDs and hashes; sampling statement; frozen denominator assertions; `N`, observed and missing counts/fractions overall/by arm; training range and its status; prediction finite/stop status; observed primary G and bootstrap bookkeeping if estimable; MSE/Delta endpoints and endpoint scenarios; tipping domain, roots, selected root, and zero-crossing flag; and interpretation status. Allowed status values are `valid`, `unsupported`, `computationally_invalid`, and `nonestimable`, with a required machine-readable reason. No patient IDs, raw values, notes, or row-level timestamps are serialized.

## Interpretation, falsification, and remaining limitations

Supportive missingness-robust evidence requires successful reconstruction and clock assertions, adequate outcome-blind and complete-triplet gates, finite predictions, an estimable primary result, a supported training range, and Delta strictly positive throughout the declared range. This would support only a selected short-horizon prognostic association in that calendar mixture.

Adverse evidence is a nonpositive gated primary contrast, repeated M1 degradation, or a bounded Delta sign reversal. Selection-sensitive evidence is favorable observed-case performance with a bound crossing zero, a near-boundary tipping point, a strong observation gradient, or discrepant ascertainment. Inconclusive evidence includes failed arm gates, unsupported/degenerate range, nonfinite predictions, zero/nonfinite SD, insufficient observed patient clusters, low ESS, influential clusters, or dependence on an unvalidated full-source range. These labels must not be collapsed into a favorable result.

Computational verification can establish source hashes, schema/header presence, duplicate-safe joins, frozen denominators, exact clocks, outcome-blind risk-set construction, endpoint arithmetic, status precedence, piecewise-linear roots, finite predictions, and bootstrap bookkeeping. It cannot establish laboratory units/reference ranges, clinical severity, tumor burden, imaging response, repeat-TACE intent, medication administration, transfusion/fluid exposure, external-care capture, mortality, hepatic failure, completed follow-up, causality, clinical utility, actionability, or patient benefit. Those require clinical adjudication and an independent temporal, external, or prospective cohort.
