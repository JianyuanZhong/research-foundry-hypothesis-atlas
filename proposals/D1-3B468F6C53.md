# What “respiratory support withdrawal” means at first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly fit and audit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study testing whether the type of recorded respiratory transition—not merely a pooled respiratory-positive/negative label—differentiates early post-discharge failure pathways. It must produce:

Completion is these newly fitted estimates and audits. Discovery checks and readiness validation are not clinical results, and this proposal makes no association claim.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the assessed parent and the available evidence is narrower than a clinical rule: recorded respiratory and vasoactive support transitions may be associated with different competing early outcomes, but respiratory evidence is heterogeneous. Oxygen flow/FiO2, PEEP and procedure markers do not have the same clinical meaning, and a procedure row is not continuous ventilation. The prior topology audit was feasibility evidence only: under its stricter adult first-stay and observation rules it reported 52,591 eligible first outtimes, 2,661 later ICU returns within 48 hours, 2,060 with a qualifying non-ICU bridge and 601 without; 294 returns had multiple bridge candidates. It did not estimate the proposed subtype associations.

The unresolved claim is:

Among adults discharged from their first valid ICU stay who have adequate pre-landmark respiratory and MAP observation, does an invasive-liberation-compatible respiratory transition have a different early ICU-escalation/death signature from an oxygen-only respiratory transition or vasoactive cessation, after accounting for measured physiology, support intensity, observation intensity and observable discharge process?

The primary hypothesis is a prespecified contrast hypothesis:

- an invasive-liberation-compatible recent respiratory transition has a larger 48-hour transition-compatible ICU-escalation contrast than an oxygen-only recent respiratory transition;
- a recent vasoactive-only cessation has a larger 48-hour in-hospital-death contrast than either respiratory subtype;
- joint respiratory-plus-vasoactive withdrawal has the largest total early-failure burden, but its return and death components need not be the same.

These are hypotheses about retrospective record-compatible associations and pathway contrasts, not causal effects of extubation, oxygen weaning or ICU discharge.

The primary exposure categories are mutually exclusive and assigned using only [outtime-12 hours, outtime), with a separate unknown/inadequate stratum that is not silently recoded into “off”:

1. durable recorded-off: both respiratory and vasoactive domains have no positive evidence in either half and meet the prespecified observation-coverage requirement;
2. oxygen-only recent respiratory transition: valid O2-flow or FiO2 evidence in the preceding six-hour half, no positive O2-flow/FiO2 evidence in the final half, no qualifying PEEP or invasive/NIV procedure marker in the locked transition window, and no vasoactive-positive interval;
3. invasive-liberation-compatible recent respiratory transition: a valid extubation marker (procedureevents itemid 227194) in the locked preceding/final boundary window, evidence of prior ventilatory support from PEEP or FiO2 in the preceding half, no positive PEEP/FiO2 evidence in the final half, and no vasoactive-positive interval;
4. recent vasoactive-only cessation: a valid listed vasoactive interval in the preceding half, no such interval in the final half, and no respiratory transition in categories 2 or 3;
5. joint recent withdrawal: the respiratory definition in category 2 or 3 and the vasoactive definition together;
6. discordant or unknown: overlapping contradictory markers, inadequate coverage, or evidence that cannot establish the subtype.

A 15-minute and 60-minute procedure-marker boundary sensitivity, procedure-free secondary classification, and a broad respiratory definition matching the parent are locked secondary analyses. The invasive label is deliberately “compatible,” not a claim of continuous ventilation or successful extubation. If the invasive or oxygen-only stratum has inadequate events or overlap, that result is inconclusive; the solver must not merge subtypes merely to obtain a result.

For each exposure group and horizon h, define L_h as the CIF of the first later ICU stay preceded by a documented non-ICU transfer bridge, and U_h as the CIF of any later ICU return. The latent ICU-escalation CIF T_h is only partially identified: L_h ≤ T_h ≤ U_h. For an exposure contrast A minus B, report the compatible interval [L_A − U_B, U_A − L_B], with no midpoint interpretation. Death before return and alive hospital discharge are competing first events. Report L, U, death and discharge at 6, 24 and 48 hours, with subject-bootstrap intervals and all locked q/topology sensitivities.

The subtype hypothesis is falsified if its worst-case contrast intervals include zero or reverse direction, fail at one or more prespecified horizons, collapse after observation/process adjustment, depend on one support channel or procedure rule, or survive transfer-topology permutation. A positive U contrast with a null or adverse L contrast is evidence against the stronger transition interpretation. Wide intervals, sparse subtype counts, poor positivity, high unknown prevalence, or unstable q/topology results are inconclusive.

## Clinical importance and advance

At ICU discharge, a patient recently liberated from invasive support may need a different surveillance posture from one whose record shows only a reduction in low-flow oxygen, even when both are labeled “respiratory support off.” Conversely, vasopressor cessation may be more closely associated with death before a return can occur. A pooled residual-instability score cannot distinguish these pathways, and an aggregate ICU-return endpoint can mix ward escalation with uncertain transfer topology.

The substantive advance is a clinically interpretable measurement test: it asks whether a support-transition subtype, with a transparent evidence tier, changes competing early-failure contrasts after accounting for documentation intensity. It can yield a robust subtype association, show that the apparent hierarchy is only a broad respiratory/documentation signal, or show that endpoint and observation uncertainty make the clinical distinction non-identifiable. It does not by itself justify discharge policy, monitoring allocation or treatment changes.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods use identical cohort flags, mutually exclusive subtype ledger, q scenarios, topology outcomes, covariates, target standardization and subject splits.

The transparent baseline is a pooled one-hour discrete-time cause-specific competing-hazard model for L, U, death and alive discharge, plus an aggregate-return model. Include subtype indicators, preceding/final-half physiology summaries, support intensity and duration, source-specific masks, row counts and process proxies, admission context, age, gender, anchor-year group and ICU LOS. Fit nested physiology/support-only, observation/process-only and full specifications. Use a descriptive landmark coverage model only to standardize predictions to the common primary population, never as a causal correction. Return held-out subtype CIFs, bound-aware contrasts, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier score, overlap, effective sample size and bootstrap intervals.

The alternative is an observation-aware factorized hidden semi-Markov model with respiratory states invasive-liberation-compatible, non-invasive/PEEP-supported, oxygen-only, off and unknown, plus hemodynamic states vasoactive, off and unknown. Constrain persistence and plausible transition ordering, include duration-dependent state transitions, emissions from the same physiology/support ledger, and an observation model for masks, row counts and source channels. Add post-landmark location states ICU, documented non-ICU and unknown only for the outcome process. Fit state, duration and regularization parameters using training/validation data, lock the test outputs, and evaluate any model-based latent transition estimate across declared bridge-capture/false-bridge grids. Reject any latent estimate outside [L,U].

The baseline answers the primary observable association with maximal auditability. The semi-Markov alternative can reveal ordering, duration, respiratory/hemodynamic discordance and a recovery buffer that fixed half-window summaries lose. It cannot identify true ventilation, clinician intent, unrecorded ward care or a biological transition; it is subordinate to the nonparametric L/U bounds.

A generic GRU/TCN is deferred because it would add sequence capacity without resolving the respiratory evidence tier or topology ambiguity. Causal discharge-policy or treatment-effect estimation, validated goals-of-care/readiness NLP, ward-monitoring adequacy, external validation, continuous ventilator waveforms, MIMIC-CXR images and outside-hospital readmissions are deferred because the needed adjudication or modalities are unavailable. The catalog explicitly notes that radiology text is present but images and raw waveforms are not.

## Uncertainty, falsification and interpretation

Use subject-level bootstrap 95% intervals for all CIFs and compatible contrast intervals, preserving bound logic; repeat the locked split seeds. Report subtype event counts, unknown/discordant rates, positivity, effective sample size, calibration, IPCW Brier, bound widths, semi-Markov state entropy and test split stability.

Predeclare:

- timestamp/leakage audits and a storetime-only negative control;
- within-window order shuffling, support-mask-only, physiology-only, process-only and combined ablations;
- removal of procedure markers, channel-specific analyses, broad-parent respiratory recoding and q/window/coverage sensitivities;
- 0/5-minute tie, 0/15/30/60-minute bridge and careunit/topology sensitivities;
- transfer-careunit permutation and a +6-hour transfer shift;
- pre-landmark pseudo-return negative control;
- discharge-as-censoring sensitivity;
- first-careunit, admission-type, anchor-year and transfer-density strata;
- latent-state stability and rejection of out-of-bound latent estimates.

Supportive evidence requires the prespecified invasive-compatible versus oxygen-only and vasoactive-only contrasts to be directionally stable in worst-case bounds, uncertainty to exclude the null where a directional claim is made, adequate events and overlap, agreement between baseline and alternative on observable outcomes, loss of topology signal after transfer permutation, and no dependence on storetime or one channel. Adverse evidence includes subtype reversal/collapse after q or observation adjustment, a signal only in U but not L, dependence on one procedure/topology rule, persistence after topology permutation, non-positivity, or latent estimates outside bounds. Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals, sparse subtypes, high unknown prevalence, unstable q/topology frontiers, high state entropy or poor transport overlap. A broad parent signal with no subtype separation is adverse to the new subtype hypothesis but may support only a nonspecific recorded-instability association.

Every reported scientific conclusion must map to a row in the computed outputs and state whether it concerns L, U, death, discharge, the bound interval or predictive performance. Automatic verification can check source bindings, transformations, timestamps, model outputs, uncertainty calculations, calibration and whether prose matches computed results. It cannot establish that the recorded transition was clinically intended, that ICU discharge was appropriate, that an ICU return was preventable, or that a monitoring policy would improve outcomes.
