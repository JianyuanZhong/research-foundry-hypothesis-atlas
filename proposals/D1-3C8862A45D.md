# Partial-identification of early post-ICU escalation after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must newly fit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study that repairs the parent’s hard split between “transfer-confirmed” and “unconfirmed” ICU return. The primary deliverable is a topology-uncertainty analysis: a documented non-ICU bridge is a lower-bound observation of a latent ward-to-ICU transition, while any later ICU return is an upper bound. The exact aggregate ICU-return/death/alive-discharge estimand remains the comparator and upper endpoint of this interval.

## Unresolved question, supported claim, and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent’s strongest supported contribution is measurement discipline: it separates recorded positive support evidence, observed non-positive values, uninformative absence, contradiction, and invalid timing; uses a locked missing-support `q)-grid; and preserves first ICU return, death, and alive discharge as competing first events. Its remaining endpoint claim is too strong. A transfer row documenting a non-ICU interval does not prove the return was unplanned, clinically deteriorating, preventable, or even that a missing transfer row means no non-ICU interval occurred.

A bounded read-only audit of the exact MIMIC archive and the parent’s adult first-stay/12-hour eligibility rule found 52,591 eligible first ICU outtimes, 2,661 later ICU returns within 48 hours, 2,060 (77.4%) satisfying the frozen bridge rule, and 601 (22.6%) without a qualifying bridge. Among return audits, 294 had more than one qualifying bridge candidate before deterministic tie-breaking. This is feasibility and endpoint-ambiguity evidence, not an association result; no support exposure restriction or model fit was used in this audit.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults at the first ICU outtime with adequate recorded-support observation, does recent recorded respiratory and/or vasoactive withdrawal, compared with durable recorded-off evidence, have a higher 48-hour risk of the latent clinically relevant event “ICU return after an intervening non-ICU interval,” after observation/process adjustment, throughout the plausible range of missing transfer-path assignments?

The primary estimand is deliberately partially identified. For each exposure group and horizon, let (L=CIF(	ext{documented non-ICU bridge followed by ICU return})) and (U=CIF(	ext{any later ICU return})). The latent transition risk is only known to satisfy (L le T le U). For recent withdrawal (r) versus durable off (d), the compatible risk-difference interval is:

[
[L_r-U_d,; U_r-L_d].
]

Death and alive discharge remain mutually exclusive competing first events; the lower/upper transition interval is calculated within that same first-event process, not by censoring deaths or discharges. The primary directional hypothesis is that the entire compatible interval for the recent-minus-durable latent transition contrast is positive at 48 hours and directionally stable at 6 and 24 hours, across the declared `q)-grid and topology-rule variants. A weaker, separately reported hypothesis is that the observed confirmed-return lower-bound contrast is positive. The hypothesis is falsified if the worst-case lower contrast is non-positive, if the interval contains clinically important opposite signs, or if any apparent signal is removed by observation/process adjustment or prespecified falsifications.

The earlier parent claim—“recent withdrawal is associated with a higher confirmed-return CIF”—is retained as an observable lower-bound association, not promoted to the latent clinical event. The parent’s aggregate return is retained as an ascertainment-complete comparator and upper bound. This repair prevents a positive association with transfer documentation from being presented as proof of a ward-to-ICU escalation association.

A 2-percentage-point absolute difference remains an analysis gate only: it is clinically interesting if the uncertainty interval for the compatible contrast can distinguish it, but it is not a bedside threshold, discharge rule, or utility claim.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

Rapid ICU return after discharge is consequential for patient safety and bed capacity, but an aggregate return mixes different record pathways. A topology-confirmed return may be more interpretable than an immediate return, yet missing or duplicated transfer documentation can make a hard binary endpoint sensitive to the record-generation process. The clinically important question is therefore whether the evidence supports a robust association with a transition-compatible escalation, or only with “some ICU return” or “being documented in the transfer pathway.”

The substantive repair is an estimand repair, not a more complex classifier:

- confirmed return is explicitly the observed lower bound;
- aggregate return is explicitly the upper bound;
- unconfirmed returns are ambiguity mass, not a separate biological competing disease;
- competing death and alive discharge are retained;
- exposure and topology coverage are audited separately;
- a model-based latent-state sensitivity analysis may narrow the interval only under stated, non-identified assumptions and can never override the nonparametric bounds.

Supportive results would mean that the observed data and declared assumptions are compatible with a robust retrospective association between recorded withdrawal and a topology-consistent post-ICU escalation. They would not show causality, readiness, inappropriate discharge, unplanned intent, preventability, or benefit from delaying discharge. If only the upper bound is positive, the scientifically correct result is that topology ambiguity prevents the stronger claim. If the aggregate association is positive but the confirmed lower-bound association is null, that is evidence that return ascertainment or missing pathway documentation matters.

## Matched transparent baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods use the identical cohort, support ledger, twelve-bin features, masks, context/process variables, q scenarios, topology audit, outcome times, and patient-level splits. All thresholds, imputations, q handling, bridge rules, and tuning are locked using training/validation only.

### Transparent baseline

Fit pooled one-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards through 48 hours for the four observable first-event labels (confirmed return, unconfirmed return, death, alive discharge), plus the parent three-event aggregate-return/death/discharge model. Predictors are fixed preceding/final physiology summaries, support exposure states, observation masks, process/context covariates, and their prespecified interactions. Fit nested recorded-physiology/support, masks-only, process/context-only, and full models.

On held-out predictions, calculate observed confirmed-return CIF (L), observed any-return CIF (U), the nonparametric bound interval ([L,U]), and the worst-case recent-minus-durable contrast interval. Standardize to the common primary population; separately fit a landmark-only coverage inclusion model and stabilized inverse-odds weights to describe the relationship between broad eligible and adequate-coverage populations. Use locked weight truncation and report positivity; this is not causal correction.

The baseline is deliberately transparent and is the primary estimator for the interval because it makes every component of the bound visible. It loses hourly order, support persistence and transfer-path timing beyond fixed summaries.

### Mechanistic learned alternative

Fit a constrained, observation-aware latent location/support semi-Markov model using the same pre-landmark twelve-bin ledger and post-landmark outcome audit. The pre-landmark component has separate respiratory and hemodynamic duration states with constrained persistence/withdrawal transitions and an explicit observation model for masks, row counts and source channels. The post-landmark component has ICU, documented non-ICU, and unknown-location states, with observed ICU intervals treated as high-specificity state evidence and transfer rows as imperfect observations. It predicts the same four observable hazards and a latent non-ICU-to-ICU transition hazard.

Because the sensitivity and false-negative rate of `hosp/transfers` are not validated, do not present the latent model’s point estimate as identified truth. Fit/lock duration, regularization and state count on validation; evaluate the latent estimate across a predeclared grid of transfer-capture sensitivities and false-bridge rates, and always display it against the nonparametric ([L,U]) bounds. If the model-implied interval falls outside those bounds, treat it as a bug or reject the model. If it narrows the interval only under untestable assumptions, label it model-based sensitivity, not evidence that the bounds are wrong.

This alternative adds scientific information that the baseline loses: ordered support duration and withdrawal sequence, time spent in observed versus unknown location, and whether return timing is compatible with a persistent non-ICU interval rather than an immediate ICU-to-ICU artifact. Its value is endpoint interpretation and assumption stress-testing, not a small AUROC gain. Report latent states only if state meaning, split stability, entropy, calibration, and topology-permutation behavior are acceptable.

A flexible GRU/TCN is deferred: without an observation model or interpretable location constraints it would add capacity without resolving the missing-path estimand. A causal discharge policy model, notes-as-intent model, renal endpoint, waveform/image model, and external validation are deferred because treatment assignment, validated intent/readiness adjudication, renal ground truth, images/waveforms, and another transfer-semantic cohort are unavailable.

Primary outputs are bound endpoints and worst-case contrast intervals at 6, 24, and 48 hours for respiratory withdrawal, vasoactive cessation, and joint withdrawal versus durable recorded-off. Report confirmed lower-bound CIF, aggregate upper-bound CIF, interval width, contrast interval, and all competing death/discharge CIFs. Use subject-level bootstrap 95% intervals, with a prespecified percentile or bootstrap-t confidence procedure and the full bootstrap draws retained. Resample subjects, never hourly rows; repeat for seeds 17, 29, and 43.

Report event counts for every observable category and horizon, calibration intercept/slope and plots for each observable cause, IPCW Brier scores, AUROC only as secondary, overlap, positivity, effective sample size, missingness/coverage strata, and the coverage-transport diagnostic. Do not use a model’s discrimination as evidence that a latent topology label is clinically valid.

Predeclare these falsifications and negative controls:

- prove no post-outtime predictor leakage by timestamp audit and feature-to-outcome dependency audit;
- shuffle hourly time labels within the pre-landmark window;
- replace `charttime` with `storetime` as a negative timing control;
- compare masks-only, support-only, physiology-only, process-only, and full models;
- remove procedure corroboration; separate arterial/NIBP MAP and O2-flow/FiO2/PEEP channels;
- vary q, window length, exposure thresholds, support channel and observation coverage;
- vary bridge tolerance, minimum duration, careunit logic, eventtype exclusion, ICU matching, and death/return tie rules;
- permute transfer careunit labels within admission while preserving ICU times and transfer durations;
- shift post-landmark transfer intervals by +6 hours and use a pre-landmark pseudo-return window as a negative control;
- compare confirmed lower bound, aggregate upper bound, interval width, and latent model output;
- perform complete-case, coverage-restricted, broad-target-standardized, first-careunit, admission-type, anchor-year and transfer-density analyses;
- run split-seed stability and latent-state label/order reproducibility checks.

Supportive evidence requires a positive worst-case lower contrast with uncertainty excluding zero, adequate overlap and event support, stable direction over the declared q and topology grids, no material storetime or single-channel dependence, loss of topology-specific signal after transfer-careunit permutation, and agreement between transparent bounds and a valid mechanistic model. It supports only an association with a record-compatible transition, not a clinical decision rule.

Adverse evidence includes a non-positive worst-case lower contrast, reversal across plausible topology/q settings, a positive aggregate upper contrast with null/negative lower bound, dependence on one transfer rule or documentation channel, persistence after topology permutation, non-positivity, or a latent estimate outside the nonparametric bounds. Inconclusive evidence includes a wide bound interval, sparse category/event counts, poor coverage transport, contradictory transfer topology, unstable splits, high latent-state entropy, or sensitivity parameters that cannot be justified. Never convert an inconclusive interval into a confirmed null or a positive result.
