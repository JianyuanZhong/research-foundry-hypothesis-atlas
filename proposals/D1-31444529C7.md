# When is laboratory observation history clinically interpretable rather than a workflow fingerprint?

Status: episode-16 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent is the highest-mu assessed-valid parent assigned for this episode. This child preserves its fixed ICU-relative landmark, whole-hospital transport estimand, competing recorded-death outcome, and observation-process framing, while adding a prespecified state-stratified operating-point analysis. It is an observational prognostic and transportability study, not a causal test-ordering study, hospital ranking, or demonstration of alert benefit.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly:

1. freeze an adult first-ICU-stay cohort, analyte/value/revision codebook, fixed ICU-relative windows, and five grouped whole-hospital outer folds;
2. fit a transparent state-plus-availability baseline (B0), a matched transparent patient-level process model (B1), and, only after support checks, a matched temporal model (T) using the same episode inputs;
3. generate zero-shot held-out-hospital cumulative-incidence predictions for recorded ICU death by 1,800 ICU-relative minutes, retaining alive ICU discharge as a competing event;
4. estimate the parent primary transport quantity: the paired macro-hospital difference in integrated Brier score between B1 and B0;
5. add a prespecified clinical-interpretation panel: calibration, alerts per 100 stays, observed deaths flagged, positive predictive value, and hypothetical decision-curve net benefit at fixed alert thresholds, all stratified by the B0 state-risk bands; and
6. produce permutation, site-fingerprint, common-panel, revision/unit, and temporal-order falsifications plus an output-linked interpretation trace.

Completion requires a frozen data dictionary and cohort flow; source/header/schema/unit/interface/revision audit; fold manifest; development-only preprocessing; held-out predictions; macro-hospital and patient-weighted scores; calibration and uncertainty; state-stratum operating-point tables; permutation contrasts; model ablations; and a written trace mapping every scientific conclusion to a computed file and uncertainty interval. No fitted result is asserted here.

## Supported evidence versus unresolved claim

The evidence currently supports feasibility only. The parent’s outcome-blind audit reported 135,680 eligible adult first stays, 2,470 recorded ICU-death events by 1,800 minutes, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows, and 111,655 episodes with at least one eligible landmark-known laboratory row. These counts support a whole-hospital experiment; they do not establish that testing history predicts death, transports to new hospitals, reflects clinician intent, or improves care. They are not results of the proposed fits.

The assessed eICU expert seed `[starting question]` proposes that testing-frequency and missingness-dependent models lose performance when transported to another hospital. The parent already makes that question data-bound. The remaining clinical question is sharper:

> Within a baseline patient-state risk stratum, does patient-level laboratory observation timing and intensity provide stable, calibrated information for a prospective ICU mortality-surveillance decision in a new hospital, or does any apparent operating-point advantage reduce to local workflow?

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In adult first ICU stays observed through a fixed six-hour ICU-relative landmark, adding patient-level laboratory observation timing/intensity to measured laboratory state and explicit availability will improve zero-shot held-out-hospital calibration and integrated Brier performance for 24-hour recorded ICU-death risk. Any decision-relevant gain will be concentrated in prespecified B0 risk strata and remain larger than a state-risk-stratified within-hospital process permutation; if it is reproduced by the permutation or by hospital descriptors, it is a workflow fingerprint, not evidence that a patient-specific process signal is transportable.

“Decision-relevant” here means a stable change in the hypothetical operating characteristics of an alert for enhanced chart/laboratory surveillance review. It does not mean that testing, reviewing, or treatment changes mortality.

## Population, landmark, outcome, and estimand

Use the frozen eICU snapshot `[source checksum]).

Select one first ICU stay per `uniquepid`:

- age `age >= 18`, treating numeric age greater than 89 as 90;
- positive numeric `unitdischargeoffset`;
- smallest valid `unitvisitnumber` per `uniquepid`, with ties broken by `patientunitstayid`;
- `unitdischargeoffset > 360`, so every included stay reaches the landmark.

The primary predictor window is ICU-relative minutes [0, 360]. The fixed ICU-relative origin is the eICU ICU-admission offset convention; there is no invented `unitadmitoffset`. `hospitaladmitoffset` is admission context only and is not used to redefine the primary clock. A separately labeled sensitivity analysis may use [max(`hospitaladmitoffset`, -360), 360] and must not pool pre-ICU features with the primary features.

The primary endpoint is first recorded ICU death with `unitdischargestatus` exactly normalized to `Expired` and `unitdischargeoffset` in (360, 1,800]. Alive ICU discharge before 1,800, identified by the exact recorded discharge status (normally `Alive`) and offset, is a competing event. A stay still in the ICU without recorded death at 1,800 is administratively observed at the horizon. Missing or noncanonical discharge statuses are audited and excluded from the primary event classification unless an a priori normalization rule is documented. This is recorded ICU death, not all-hospital mortality, latent death, post-ICU mortality, or a causal mortality outcome. Do not use actual-outcome fields from `apachePatientResult`.

The primary statistical estimand remains the paired outer-test difference in macro-hospital integrated cause-specific Brier score for the cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death by 1,800 minutes:

`Delta_Brier = IBS_macro(B1) - IBS_macro(B0)`.

Negative values favor B1. Patient-weighted IBS, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope/intercept, calibration error, cause-specific discrimination, and alive-discharge cumulative-incidence calibration are secondary. The new clinical-interpretation estimands are descriptive/decision-analytic functions of the same held-out predictions:

- alert burden and observed death capture at thresholds `p_t in {0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05}`;
- paired B1-minus-B0 differences in alerts per 100 eligible stays, deaths flagged per 100 deaths, and positive predictive value;
- hypothetical label-relative net benefit `NB(p_t) = TP/N - FP/N * p_t/(1-p_t)`, evaluated separately by B0 state-risk stratum and averaged by hospital as well as patient-weighted.

The net-benefit calculation assumes, only for this mathematical characterization, that an alert triggers an intervention/review with benefit-to-harm odds represented by `p_t/(1-p_t)`. No such intervention, benefit, harm, or utility is observed in eICU. Therefore net benefit is not evidence of clinical utility and cannot justify deploying an alert.

## Feature blocks

For each retained analyte, construct only from eligible rows in [0, 360]:

- measured state: first, last, last-minus-first slope when at least two valid values exist, range when at least two values exist, and prespecified clinical-range flags;
- explicit availability: any valid result, count of distinct measurement times, and number of valid result rows;
- patient-level process: time from landmark to last result, first-to-last span, inter-test interval summaries, total distinct analytes, per-analyte timing/count features, stable audited system/interface categories, and revision-known indicators.

State values, availability indicators, and process channels are defined before looking at outcomes. Training-fold medians, scales, missing-value indicators, spline knots, category maps, and any model hyperparameters are fit only in development hospitals. A process feature must never encode a row or revision after 360. Hospital ID and post-landmark quantities are prohibited.

## Models, matched inputs, and method selection

Freeze codebook, cohort, outcomes, folds, feature transforms, thresholds, and support rules before fitting.

**B0: transparent state/availability baseline.** On one row per stay, use an elastic-net logistic model for the 24-hour recorded-death cumulative-incidence risk with an explicit competing-discharge adjustment, or an equivalent discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly bins 360–1,800 and death/discharge heads. Inputs are demographics/admission context, laboratory value summaries and threshold flags, and explicit analyte availability. The same exact patient episodes and folds are used in every comparison. A value-only ablation omitting availability is a lower-bound sensitivity.

**B1: transparent patient-process extension.** Use the same model family, target, episode-hour rows, competing event, folds, and B0 inputs, adding only the prespecified patient-level observation timing/intensity block. Use splines only for predeclared count/recency variables. B1 is the primary test of incremental process information, with coefficient/standardized-risk summaries and ablations by process channel.

**T: substantive temporal alternative at matched inputs.** If support gates pass, fit a compact one-layer GRU or temporal-convolution model over six one-hour bins in [0, 360]. It receives exactly the channels available to B1: laboratory values, availability, counts, elapsed time, audited interface/system categories, and revision-known status. Its heads estimate recorded death and alive discharge; an auxiliary next-bin-observation head may characterize temporal observation structure but is not a clinical endpoint. T can reveal whether ordering, persistence, abrupt co-movement, and within-window timing contain information discarded by B1’s summaries. Compare T against B1 on identical episodes, folds, outcomes, and held-out hospitals, not as a search for a small predictive gain. If T is poorly calibrated, fails convergence, lacks common feature support, or has too few events for stable estimation, defer it and preserve B0/B1 as the scientific analysis.

The selected deliverable is not “the most accurate model.” It is the newly estimated transport difference and its state-stratum operating characteristics. T is retained as an alternative because it tests whether temporal organization, rather than just process quantity, matters; it is deferred if that scientific comparison is not supported.

## Splits, uncertainty, and support gates

Use five grouped outer folds, each holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once. Keep all stays sharing a `uniquepid` together. Within each outer training partition, split hospitals approximately 75%/25% into development/validation groups for regularization, early stopping, preprocessing and codebook decisions. Stratify hospital assignment by eligible and event counts where possible. The primary evaluation is zero-shot on held-out hospitals; a patient-random split is secondary and explicitly optimistic.

Use paired fold predictions and a hospital-level bootstrap or hierarchical random-effects interval that resamples hospitals, not individual rows alone. Report fold dispersion, macro-hospital and patient-weighted estimates, and the number of event-bearing hospitals contributing to each stratum and threshold.

Before fitting, the solver must report:

- cohort flow, missing demographics/statuses, first-stay selection, hospital counts, and all source/header/schema checks;
- analyte mapping, numeric-result, system/interface, revision-timing, duplicate and conflicting-unit counts;
- counts of eligible stays, recorded deaths, alive discharges, administrative horizon observations, stays with landmark-known labs, and supported hospitals;
- per-stratum and per-threshold numbers of stays, deaths, hospitals, alerts, and competing discharges.

The operating-point panel is inferential only when each displayed stratum/threshold has at least 30 recorded deaths pooled across at least 10 held-out hospitals and the paired prediction set has nonzero alerts in both models. Otherwise it is descriptive, adjacent B0 strata may be predeclared as merged before outcome interpretation, and the affected claim is inconclusive. T additionally requires at least 50 recorded deaths in the training partition, at least 10 event-bearing development hospitals, positive common-channel coverage, finite validation loss, and calibration that is not materially worse than B1 under a prespecified tolerance. Failure is a reason to defer T, not permission to change the hypothesis.

## Prespecified clinical-interpretation analysis

Define B0 state-risk bands using fixed predicted-risk cut points, not fold-specific outcome quantiles:

- low: `p_B0 < 0.01`;
- intermediate: `0.01 <= p_B0 < 0.05`;
- high: `p_B0 >= 0.05`.

The cut points are operating labels for interpretation, not validated triage thresholds. They are applied to held-out predictions produced without held-out outcomes. For each band, report B0 and B1 (and T if eligible) calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope/error, integrated Brier score, risk distributions, alert burden, deaths flagged, PPV, and the paired model difference. Also report a process-intensity descriptive split using development-fold median total distinct measurement times within each B0 band; this split is not a causal exposure contrast and is not used to tune the model.

At `p_t = 0.5%, 1%, 2%, 5%`, calculate the label-relative net benefit for each model and each B0 band, with hospital-level intervals. A useful operating-point pattern would be a calibrated B1 increase in death capture or net benefit at a tolerable alert burden that repeats across held-out hospitals and is absent after process permutation. It remains only a candidate for prospective surveillance review. If B1’s apparent gain occurs only in one band, its clinical relevance is limited to that band; if it increases alerts without calibrated capture, it is operational burden rather than value. The low-risk band is especially important: a large apparent net benefit there can be driven by many false positives even when PPV is low.

The primary decision interpretation is therefore support-gated and two-dimensional:

- patient-level transport signal: original B1 improvement versus its state-risk-stratified process permutation and site-only diagnostic;
- operating relevance: stable calibration and alert-burden/death-capture tradeoff at a fixed `p_t`, without claiming a benefit from the hypothetical review.

## Falsification and sensitivity tests

1. **State-risk-stratified process permutation.** Within each hospital and B0 risk band, permute each B1 process vector across stays while preserving its marginal distribution; keep B0 features and outcomes fixed. Refit/re-score under the same training/evaluation rules. If original B1 and permuted B1 are indistinguishable, the process gain is consistent with hospital workflow or coarse state strata rather than patient-specific information. If original B1 exceeds the permutation on held-out hospitals, that supports a transportable association, not clinician intent or causal value. If the permutation has no support in a sparse cell, report the failure rather than silently relaxing the strata.

2. **Site-fingerprint diagnostic.** Fit a secondary model using only `hospitalid` during development, and separately `numbedscategory`, `teachingstatus`, and `region`. It is evaluated with the same hospital-held-out design and cannot enter B0/B1/T. A site-only performance comparable to B1, or a B1 gain that disappears when hospital composition is controlled, flags workflow fingerprinting.

3. **Common-panel sensitivity.** Restrict all hospitals to the outcome-blind analyte/system/interface panel with adequate support, refit B0/B1, and compare the same state-strata operating points. A process gain that depends on a hospital-specific analyte interface is not robust transport evidence.

4. **Revision and time placebo.** Refit after removing revision-known channels and after restricting to rows with explicit revision timing; verify that no post-landmark revision contributes. Reverse the order of the six temporal bins for T while preserving values and counts. If T retains the same gain after reversal, temporal ordering was not the claimed information.

5. **Process-channel ablations.** Separately remove timing/recency, count/intensity, and system/interface/revision channels. Report which component changes Brier/calibration/alert characteristics, rather than interpreting an aggregate process score as mechanism.

6. **Outcome and split checks.** Permute death labels within development hospitals only as a negative-control computation; compare a patient-random split only as an optimism diagnostic; repeat leave-one-hospital-out summaries for event-bearing hospitals. A label-permutation gain or a patient-random-only gain invalidates the corresponding claim.

7. **Clock and horizon sensitivity.** Repeat with a 180-minute landmark-known subset only as a secondary fixed ICU-relative sensitivity and with a separately labeled pre-ICU window. Repeat a six-hour prediction horizon only if the same competing-event and status support are present. These analyses cannot replace the primary [0, 360] to 1,800-minute estimand.

## Interpretation gates

**Supportive, but observational:** B1 improves the primary macro-hospital IBS over B0 with a hospital-level interval and consistent fold direction; calibration is not degraded; the gain is larger than the state-risk-stratified permutation; and at least one fixed threshold/stratum shows repeatable calibration and alert-burden/death-capture improvement across supported held-out hospitals. This supports patient-level laboratory process as transportable prognostic information and identifies a subgroup for prospective surveillance evaluation. It does not support changing test ordering, infer clinician intention, or establish mortality benefit. A T improvement additionally supports information in temporal organization only if T passes its gate and beats B1 with uncertainty.

**Adverse:** B1 worsens held-out calibration/IBS, creates excessive alert burden for little observed death capture, fails to transport, or matches the permutation/site-only diagnostic. The appropriate conclusion is to omit the process block, use state/availability with local recalibration, or treat testing-process shift as a deployment hazard. This is scientifically useful and does not prove that testing is clinically harmful.

**Inconclusive:** event/stratum/hospital support fails; analyte mapping or revision timing is unresolved; intervals are too wide; fold results conflict; outcome status coverage is inadequate; or B1/T cannot be separated from the permutation. Do not convert an inconclusive result into a null or positive claim.

The verifier can check cohort construction, time exclusion, source joins, outcome coding, model fits, held-out predictions, score arithmetic, intervals, alert tables, permutation behavior, and whether conclusions are linked to outputs. It cannot establish clinical utility, clinician intent, true physiologic state, causal benefit, or whether a real ICU team would act on an alert.

## Alternatives considered and disposition

- **Transparent B0/B1 (chosen primary pair):** CPU-feasible, auditable, and directly answers incremental process information with matched inputs. It preserves coefficients, calibration, strata, and alert-burden traces.
- **GRU/temporal convolution T (retained conditional alternative):** same channels/splits/targets; scientifically informative about order, persistence, abrupt changes and co-movement that summaries lose. A future solver may use at most one allocated A100, but GPU use is not a merit criterion. Defer for event/common-support/convergence/calibration failure.
- **Mechanistic latent-state model:** deferred. The three named sources do not provide a validated physiologic latent state or clinician-intent label; inferring mechanism from observation timing would overclaim. Revisit only with richer time-matched vitals, interventions, chart adjudication, or a prespecified generative measurement model.
- **Hospital hierarchical/random-effects prediction:** retained as uncertainty and decomposition, not the primary patient model. Hospital/site descriptors are too few to identify workflow mechanisms and cannot substitute for held-out transport.
- **Full observation-standardized or causal missing-data intervention analysis:** deferred. Missingness is informative and the local data do not identify what would have been measured under an alternative testing policy; no causal test-ordering or mortality effect is estimable.
- **The parent’s secondary pre-ICU window:** retained only as labeled sensitivity because hospital admission timing is available, but the fixed ICU-relative primary window remains frozen.
- **Additional eICU tables such as `vitalPeriodic`, medication, nursing, Apache, and notes:** not silently substituted. The catalog says `vitalPeriodic` contains derived five-minute summaries rather than raw waveforms, while narrative note sections are removed/limited. Adding them would change the question and requires a new source audit; the present child remains bound to patient/hospital/lab.
- **Expert seed `[starting question]` (reduced testing and deterioration):** relevant safeguard for process gaps, but deferred as a separate outcome question because “unstable,” abrupt reduction, interface failure, discharge, and goals-of-care explanations require additional definitions and could duplicate the present process signal. Its caution motivates the permutation and interface falsifications.
- **Expert seed `[starting question]` (hypoglycemia after insulin):** clinically important but the prior audited branch found sparse conservative support and cannot be silently broadened by treating documentation as administration, nutrition, or true hypoglycemia. It remains deferred pending stronger support and adjudication.
- **Expert seed `[starting question]` (vasopressor strategies):** requires treatment exposure harmonization, timing/titration, and confounding control unavailable in this three-table question; no instrumental-variable interpretation is made.
- **The three demonstrations:** Delphi’s verified learned dated-history example motivates the bounded temporal comparison, but the original UK Biobank trajectories/Danish external validation are unavailable and reproduction is not claimed. ALADYNOULLI’s longitudinal latent structure is not reproduced because its genetic/latent assumptions and validated physiologic inputs are not established here; an eICU-only latent model is explicitly deferred. For Oncoformer, the local bundle states that the main article and complete STAR Methods are unavailable; only the supplement is available, and eICU lacks the chest X-ray modality. No full multimodal reproduction or unavailable method claim is made. These references guide alternative reasoning; they do not determine this topic.

## Required unavailable evidence

The local sources do not provide clinician intent for ordering, reasons for missing tests, analyzer/interface downtime, true physiologic state when no laboratory result is recorded, symptoms, interventions triggered by an alert, treatment response, or post-ICU outcomes. `labresulttext` and system/interface fields cannot establish these facts. Recorded ICU death is not adjudicated clinical mortality, and a testing-process association cannot establish that testing caused or prevented death.

A supportive computation would justify an observational transportability result and a candidate surveillance-review operating point only. Stronger claims require chart or expert adjudication of physiologic deterioration and clinical intent, external hospital validation, a prospective silent deployment, and an impact study comparing outcomes under a defined alert/review policy. Causal claims about changing testing or mortality require a different design with treatment-policy information and appropriate confounding control.
