# Retention-separated postoperative HCC surveillance selection

Status: substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. A bounded aggregate audit exposed two consequential limits in the parent: 10,685/15,575 patients (68.6%) have no new encounter entry after day 60, and the laboratory target/control pair is nearly nested (3,125 LM+NL weeks, 777 NL-only, only 86 LM-only). Late-period marks remain supported and the imaging discordant cells are balanced. The design therefore keeps the outcome-independent full grid but separates local return, laboratory panel add-on, and imaging routing rather than treating two heterogeneous controls as interchangeable. No hypothesis model has been fitted.

## Scientific opening, evidence boundary, and advance

The strongest clinical claim supported by inspected evidence is that incomplete AFP response at a 4–6-week postoperative visit was associated with worse recurrence-free and overall survival among 549 selected resected HCC patients with preoperative AFP >400 ng/mL [K1]. It does not establish that persistent AFP is residual tumor, that follow-up availability is ignorable, or that surveillance changes outcomes.

Routine EHR observation is generated by care. In 669,452 patients at two hospitals, presence and timing of common laboratory orders were often associated with survival independently of values [K2]. A methodological review defines informative presence/observation, describes explicit observation-process and latent approaches, and warns that prediction and explanation require different interpretations [K3]. These works justify modeling observation; they do not establish why any local HCC record appears.

The unresolved hypothesis is that pre-landmark marker history is associated with a target-specific local surveillance decision after day 60, beyond measured severity and the propensity to return to this institution. The leading explanation is target-specific surveillance selection. The strongest rival is patient retention: marker history, postoperative morbidity, or unmeasured severity predicts whether a patient reappears locally, and all recorded marks follow. A second rival is modality-specific availability: AFP is added to a generic laboratory panel, while hepatic and nonhepatic imaging arise in different workflows; a pooled “negative-control” result could therefore be an artifact.

The advance is a falsifiable decomposition on one outcome-independent risk set: (1) encounter return, (2) laboratory panel composition, and (3) imaging routing. The experiment can determine whether a residual association is specific to one modality, shared with generic return, or unsupported. It remains an institutional ascertainment study—not a recurrence, clinician-intent, surveillance-benefit, or causal treatment study.

## Variables and estimands

A(t) is outcome-independent encounter occupancy: an encounters-table interval from admission/visit/start through valid discharge overlaps week t; if no valid interval exists, the admission, else visit, else discharge fallback date lies in t. A0 uses the start date only. Labs, examinations, procedures, medications, targets, and controls never define A or eligibility.

H is pre-landmark marker history. In the full cohort first contrast marker-observed versus marker-absent. Among marker-observed patients use train-scaled continuous preoperative rank and latest day-15:60/preoperative log ratio within exact marker label, specimen, and calendar era; report the prespecified high/persistent versus low/clearing standardized contrast. Never impute a marker value when absent or pool raw values across labels/eras because units and platforms are unavailable.

Primary marks are LM (AFP/PIVKA-II/AFP-L3 measurement), NL (sodium/creatinine/hemoglobin), S (hepatic/upper-abdominal CT/MR), and NI (nonhepatic CT/MR). LI (ALT/AST/bilirubin/albumin/INR/platelets) and T (recorded HCC-directed procedure/systemic medication) are secondary. T occurrence does not establish indication.

Three linked estimands use the same 685,300 patient-weeks:

1. **Local-return estimand:** standardized weekly and 365-day risk of A by H using only baseline and lagged history. This quantifies the retention rival; it is not a loss-to-follow-up model.
2. **Laboratory add-on estimand:** a full-grid four-state outcome—neither, NL-only, LM+NL, LM-only. The primary laboratory contrast is the H association with LM+NL versus NL-only; LM-only is reported but not treated as a symmetric control because only 86 weeks exist. Also report the inherited marginal Δ_lab and ratio-of-risk-ratios.
3. **Imaging-routing estimand:** a full-grid four-state outcome—neither, S-only, NI-only, both. Estimate the H association with S-only versus NI-only and the inherited marginal Δ_img and ratio-of-risk-ratios. The 386 S-only and 397 NI-only weeks make this the cleanest negative-control comparison.

Standardize over the same empirical baseline covariates and observed A histories. Report component risks, risk differences, ratios, cluster-bootstrap 95% intervals, and effective patient counts. The A=1 common-denominator composition analysis is required corroboration, never a replacement for the full-grid estimands. Report A0 and lagged-A-only sensitivity results.

## Matched methods and what each reveals

**Transparent baseline.** On the identical full grid, fit (a) pooled-logistic/GEE A-return model and (b) two stacked multinomial models for laboratory and imaging joint states, with week spline, H, A/current and lagged A, index era, service, age, sex, index LOS, day1–60 extra encounter, pathology/severity, prior treatment, injury history, exact label/specimen, and only t− history. Include prespecified H×state interactions and H×era/LOS/early-return tests. Use elastic-net selection in training, refit the frozen basis with cluster-robust inference, and patient-cluster bootstrap standardized risks. It makes retention and pair-specific composition visible but compresses long gaps and bundles.

**Scientifically substantive alternative.** Fit a multivariate marked-process state-space model on the same grid and split. U is generic local-record activity anchored by A; B is laboratory-panel propensity loading on LM and NL; Q is imaging-access/routing propensity loading on S and NI; V is target enrichment, constrained to zero on A and loading differentially on LM/S versus NL/NI. Transitions use baseline and t− history only. Compare U+B+Q against U+B+Q+V, with separate laboratory and imaging posterior predictions. This can reveal persistent target enrichment after explicitly representing retention and modality availability—information the baseline’s weekly interactions lose. It cannot identify clinician intent or biology.

Require null/shared-retention/target-specific/control-mismatch simulations, three seeds, recovery correlation ≥0.8, no factor swapping, 90–98% interval coverage, calibrated held-out component risks, and ≥1% validation target/composition deviance gain without ≥1% control gain. If these fail, the transparent analysis completes the study and latent claims are prohibited.

Planned future-solver resources: baseline 8–16 CPUs, 64 GiB, 1–2 hours; alternative one allocated A100, 16 CPUs, 128 GiB, 4–6 hours with checkpoints. These are unverified estimates within the configured 16-CPU/8-GPU/256-GiB/8-hour envelope. The final bounded extraction took 90.6 seconds on ordinary CPU; no GPU was needed.

## Gates, falsification, and interpretation

Before fitting, require ≥20 validation and test patients per component mark; ≥100 effective patients per standardized H contrast; blinded review of ≥100 laboratory labels and ≥100 examination titles with PPV/NPV ≥0.90; and exact reporting of full-grid/A=1 pair cells, era, timing, LOS, early-return, and marker-absent support. T requires separate pharmacy/hepatobiliary review (≥100 positive and ≥100 oncology-like negative snippets, PPV/NPV ≥0.85) or remains unclassified treatment occurrence.

Retain the inherited materiality thresholds: absolute standardized specificity risk difference ≥0.02 and ratio-of-risk-ratios ≥1.25, with Holm-adjusted 95% intervals, locked-test directional replication, absolute calibration error ≤0.02, and train-only H permutation within era, pre-landmark observation, LOS, and early-return strata.

**Supportive for imaging routing:** Δ_img and its ratio meet both inferential and materiality gates; S-only versus NI-only points in the same direction on the full grid and A=1 frame; direction persists across eras and baseline morbidity/retention-proxy strata; permutation removes it; and the result is not reproduced for A. This supports a local target-specific imaging-recording association.

**Supportive for laboratory add-on:** LM+NL versus NL-only meets the same gates and replicates; do not call this exchange between target and control or generalize it to imaging. An overall target-selection claim requires both modality-specific conclusions to be supportive. One supportive pair yields only that pair’s conclusion.

**Adverse:** target interactions are precisely below both materiality thresholds, the H association is shared with A/control states, and U+B+Q matches U+B+Q+V within the 1% threshold. This favors local retention/severity/modality availability as an adequate observed explanation.

**Inconclusive:** intervals include material effects; calendar or LOS/early-return direction reverses; control dictionaries or effective support fail; full-grid and A=1 directions conflict; calibration fails; the marker-history association with A cannot be separated from mark associations; or latent recovery/stability fails. An imprecise null is not adverse. The 10,685 patients without a new post-day60 encounter make failure to separate retention a central inconclusive result, not evidence of no surveillance.

No result can establish complete follow-up, death, outside care, scheduled surveillance, clinician motivation, recurrence, treatment benefit, or biological meaning for V. Those require linked mortality/claims, prospective schedules, validated clinical adjudication, and another study.
