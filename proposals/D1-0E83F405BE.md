# Monitoring de-intensification while unstable predicts short-term deterioration in ICU patients: an eICU test of monitoring–clinical-state alignment (episode-3 child with measured feasibility evidence)

Child of [prior hypothesis] (episode 1, valid, mu 29.4). This version adds **executed probe evidence** ([research job], work/lead/probe_ep2.py → work/lead/probe_ep2_results.json), two design corrections discovered against real data, and threshold/count-calibrated falsification criteria. No outcome modeling has been performed; the probe is an exposure-prevalence and feasibility measurement, not a result about the hypothesis.

## 1. Unresolved question (unchanged from parent)

Among ICU patients who are objectively unstable at time t, does an abrupt (≥50%) reduction in laboratory testing rate over the next 24 h predict a composite 48-h deterioration outcome (organ-support escalation, ICU death, or post-discharge bounce-back) beyond APACHE severity and concurrent physiologic state — i.e., does monitoring–state *misalignment* carry early risk information — and does the signal vary across hospitals? Clinical importance: de-implementation programs (WICH-ICU [K1]; surgical-ICU QI [K3]) reduce testing at unit level without patient-level instability stratification, and discharge-readiness models (GRU-D++ [K2]) ignore the monitoring-intensity trajectory.

## 2. NEW: executed exposure-prevalence probe ([research job])

Day-granularity successor-guided diagnostic over the frozen eICU 2.0 CSVs (all counts measured, none projected):

- **Population (verified):** 200,859 unit stays; 75,122 eligible (age ≥ 18, stay ≥ 48 h) across **206 hospitals**; 73,975 (98.5%) have ≥ 8 labs in the first 24 h, so the baseline testing criterion barely restricts the cohort.
- **Testing-rate dynamics:** 207,762 eligible consecutive-day transitions with prior-day count ≥ 8; day-to-day lab-count ratio percentiles: p5 = 0.261, p25 = 0.60, median = 0.897, p75 = 1.08. A ≥ 50% drop (ratio ≤ 0.5) therefore lies well beyond routine day-to-day fluctuation (it is below the 25th percentile), while still selecting a large group.
- **Exposure prevalence (threshold scan):** ≥ 50% drop on vasopressor-active (unstable-proxy) days: **5,817 patient-days = 19.84% of 29,322 vasopressor days**; ≥ 30% drop: 10,248 (35.0%); ≥ 40%: 7,936 (27.1%); ≥ 60%: 3,583 (12.2%). Unexposed-unstable reference (ratio ≥ 0.8): 13,186 days. **First qualifying index per patient: 5,344 exposed-unstable patients** vs 5,256 unexposed-unstable.
- **Crude 48-h death (first qualifying index, no adjustment):** exposed-unstable 285/5,344 (5.33%); unexposed-unstable 442/5,256 (8.41%); exposed-stable 547/25,842 (2.12%). Interpretation: the *crude* association runs opposite to H1's direction — maintaining high testing among unstable patients marks sicker patients, and/or clinicians de-escalate in unstable patients they believe are improving. This materially strengthens the prespecified **clinician-foresight rival (H0)** and makes adjustment for the pre-event physiologic trajectory (not just APACHE at admission) scientifically mandatory rather than optional. It also means a naive/unadjusted analysis would be uninformative; the design below conditions on measured trajectory.
- **Artifact bound:** 0 of 5,817 exposed-unstable days have zero infusionDrug rows — an exposed-unstable day *cannot* arise from a documentation outage (vasopressor-active classification requires real infusion rows). The remaining outage risk (a global absence day being misread as an unexposed/drop day) is handled by the multi-source absence filter below.
- **Hospital spread:** 125 hospitals have ≥ 1 exposed-unstable event; **84 hospitals have ≥ 10 events** (top: hospital 420 with 371). Hospital heterogeneity and held-out-hospital evaluation are therefore well powered.
- **Power implication:** death alone among exposed-unstable patients (285 events) is underpowered for the parent's ≥ 500-event threshold; the **composite outcome (escalation-dominated) is required as primary**, exactly as the parent specified. Feasibility of the ≥ 500-event criterion with the composite will be verified at solver time; the probe bounds it from below at 285 death-only events plus all escalations/readmissions.

## 3. Design corrections discovered against real data (the concrete scientific changes)

1. **`unitstaytype` values are literally `admit` (154,948), `readmit` (9,947), `stepdown/other` (25,239), `transfer` (10,725)** — the parent's filter `unitstaytype in (admission, readmission)` would silently select zero rows. Corrected filter: `unitstaytype in ('admit','readmit')`.
2. **Outage filter reformulated:** eICU offsets are per-patient ICU-relative minutes with no calendar timestamps, so the parent's cross-patient hospital-day outage rule is not computable as written. Corrected rule (patient-level multi-source absence): exclude any candidate window where, on the index day, rows are simultaneously absent across independent sources (lab.csv, infusionDrug.csv, vitalPeriodic.csv) — a global documentation outage signature. eICU's lack of calendar time is also why the composite readmission outcome uses `patienthealthsystemstayid`/`unitvisitnumber` linkage rather than wall-clock gaps.
3. **Threshold calibration:** the ≥ 50% drop threshold is retained as primary because it sits below the measured p25 of the ratio distribution (0.60), i.e., beyond routine fluctuation; 40% and 60% are prespecified sensitivity analyses (their prevalences are now measured: 27.1% / 12.2% of vasopressor days).

## 4. Harbor experiment (binding unchanged from parent except where corrected)

Data: eICU v2.0 read-only CSVs (paths/hashes in datasets/eicu/README.md). Join key `patientunitstayid`; hospital `hospitalid`; readmission linkage `uniquepid` + `patienthealthsystemstayid` + `unitvisitnumber`.

- **Population:** adults (age ≥ 18), `unitstaytype in ('admit','readmit')`, stay ≥ 48 h (`unitdischargeoffset ≥ 2880`), ≥ 8 labs in first 24 h, APACHE available (apachePatientResult.csv, prefer apacheversion '4').
- **Exposure (index windows every 8 h from hour 24):** R24_before ≥ hospital-median daily testing rate; R24_after ≤ 0.5 × R24_before; ≥ 24 h in-unit after index. **Instability at t (within 4 h before t):** vasopressor active (infusionDrug.csv drugname match + drugrate > 0) OR MAP < 65 mmHg (vitalPeriodic.csv systemicmean; vitalAperiodic.csv noninvasivemean fallback) OR invasive ventilation / FiO2 ≥ 0.5 (respiratoryCharting.csv). The probe used vasopressor-active days as the unstable proxy; the full analysis adds the MAP and ventilation criteria.
- **Trajectory adjustment (newly mandatory):** the pre-index 24-h physiologic trajectory (MAP slope, vasopressor-rate change, ventilator/FiO2 change) enters the adjustment set, because the crude reversal (§2) shows severity-at-admission alone cannot separate H1 from clinician foresight.
- **Outcome (48 h after t, timestamp-adjudicated):** composite of ICU death (`unitdischargestatus='Expired'` with offset ≤ t+2880), organ-support escalation (new vasopressor start or ≥ 50% rate increase; new invasive ventilation), or unit discharge within 48 h followed by unplanned ICU readmission within 72 h of unit discharge (same patienthealthsystemstayid, next unitvisitnumber). Secondary: hospital death.
- **Exclusions:** active carePlanEOL entry before t; multi-source-absence outage windows (§3.2); discharge/transfer windows censored.
- **Analysis:** (1) descriptive alignment cross-tab with hospital variation; (2) baseline severity-adjusted logistic regression (APACHE, age, sex, unit type, instability indicators, pre-index trajectory) with hospital random intercept; (3) primary H1 test: adjusted OR for de-intensification-while-unstable vs unstable-without (ratio ≥ 0.8), cluster-robust by patient; (4) learned alternative: gradient-boosted model adding testing-trajectory features, 60/40 hospital split, held-out ΔAUROC; (5) hospital heterogeneity by testing-intensity quartile. CPU-only; measured data passes (awk full-file scans ≈ 33 s) confirm compute feasibility.

## 5. Falsification criteria (count-calibrated)

- **Supportive:** adjusted OR ≥ 1.15 with CI excluding 1.0, and/or held-out ΔAUROC ≥ 0.02 → misalignment carries early risk information; a candidate exemption rule for de-implementation programs and a cheap feature for readiness models. Not causal proof.
- **Adverse:** adjusted OR ≤ 1.0 with CI excluding 1.0, or association confined to EOL-adjacent stays → patient-targeted de-escalation looks safe at the dynamic level; supports [K1]'s program and strengthens the reading that clinicians titrate testing to recovery appropriately.
- **Inconclusive:** CI spanning 1.0 with width > 0.15, or < 500 composite events among exposed-unstable after exclusions → underpowered; report counts honestly and defer. Death-only secondary is already known to be underpowered from the probe (285 crude events) — an inconclusive death-only secondary is NOT an inconclusive study.
- **Not answerable here:** causality (needs randomized testing-reduction stratified by instability), mechanism, ward/post-discharge outcomes.

## 6. What this child adds beyond parent and inspected works

The parent established the question and verified bindings but had **zero measured evidence** that the exposure occurs often enough to study. This child converts feasibility from assumption to measurement: ≥ 50%-drop-while-vasopressor-active occurs on 1 in 5 vasopressor days (5,817 days; 5,344 patients; 84 hospitals with ≥ 10 events), the exposure is not a documentation artifact, the ≥ 50% threshold is calibrated against the measured ratio distribution, and two real-data corrections (unitstaytype literal values; computable outage filter) remove silent-failure modes. The measured crude direction reversal (5.33% vs 8.41%) sharpens the scientific contest between H1 and the clinician-foresight rival rather than weakening the study: it demonstrates the answer genuinely depends on trajectory adjustment, i.e., the experiment has discriminating content. [K1] cannot deliver this (unit-level endpoints); [K2] lacks monitoring features; [K3] lacks stratification — each a distinct, inspected gap this design fills.
