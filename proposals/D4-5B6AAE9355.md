# Pre-illness creatinine–cystatin C discordance, grip strength, and hospitalized AKI: specificity and measurement stress test

Status: prospective study design; no exposure–outcome association has been fitted. This child preserves the parent’s primary population, 10-year estimand, and hypothesis, but makes the censoring gate, control roles, cross-outcome comparisons, measurement sensitivity, learned alternative, and solver outputs deterministic.

## Supported evidence, unresolved claim, and clinical advance

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. Large eGFRcys−eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have multiple non-GFR predictors [K1]. Discordance is associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes in SPRINT [K2]. During prolonged critical illness, muscle loss can make creatinine eGFR substantially overestimate measured GFR [K3]. These findings do not establish incident AKI prediction in ambulatory adults, a low-grip interaction, a muscle mechanism at baseline, or benefit from cystatin-C-guided care.

Unchanged primary hypothesis: among UK Biobank participants with baseline race-free 2021 CKD-EPI eGFRcr >=60 mL/min/1.73m2 and no prior inpatient N17 code, a 15-unit lower 2012 CKD-EPI eGFRcys minus eGFRcr predicts a higher 10-year cumulative incidence of first hospitalized coded AKI after adjustment for eGFRcr and prespecified covariates; the contrast is larger at lower sex-specific grip strength.

The leading interpretation is prognostic renal vulnerability obscured by creatinine in people with low muscle reserve. Rivals are: (R1) cystatin-C and creatinine non-GFR determinants, including inflammation, adiposity, smoking, diabetes, and muscle mass; (R2) generalized frailty; (R3) acute-admission or coding opportunity; and (R4) N17 measurement error or secondary-position coding during another illness. Resolution could justify prospective enrichment or a future cystatin-C-guided trial. It cannot establish true GFR, mechanism, or treatment benefit.

## Cox/Aalen–Johansen baseline

Primary adjusted model: cause-specific Cox hazards for N17 and death. Use eGFRcr, delta=eGFRcys−eGFRcr, sex-standardized grip, delta×grip, age, sex, raw categorical ethnicity/smoking/diabetes/center, assessment year, BMI, waist, averaged BP, CRP, glycemia, and albuminuria when units are verified. Use restricted cubic splines with knots fixed at the 5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th cohort percentiles for continuous main effects. Never enter eGFRcr, eGFRcys, and their algebraic difference together. Impute covariates only, within training/bootstrap samples; do not impute exposure, outcome, or dates. Report complete-case sensitivity.

Aalen–Johansen curves are unadjusted descriptions. Adjusted cumulative incidence comes from g-computation over the empirical covariate distribution using both fitted cause-specific hazards. The unchanged estimand is the standardized 10-year cumulative-incidence difference and ratio for delta −15 versus 0 at eGFRcr 90, separately at the sex-specific 25th and 75th grip percentiles. Report cause-specific HRs, the delta×grip interaction, 95% bootstrap intervals, proportional-hazards diagnostics, first-year exclusion, alternative discordance definitions, Fine–Gray sensitivity, and the primary-position N17 sensitivity.

## Control-outcome logic

Appendicitis and hip fracture are complementary but not interchangeable controls.

- First inpatient `K35*` is a negative-control outcome for a broad acute-admission/coding-opportunity association. It is imperfect: its age/sex pattern differs from AKI and it does not measure longitudinal admission frequency, lab surveillance, or nephrotoxic exposure.
- First inpatient `S72*` is a frailty-sensitive rival outcome, not a negative control. Low grip and generalized vulnerability can genuinely predict hip fracture, so requiring a null S72 association would contradict the purpose of the control.
- The available first-diagnosis arrays lack encounter identifiers and repeated admissions. Therefore neither control can falsify differential renal testing or coding within a hospitalization. That interpretation requires episode-level HES/admission records or adjudication and is unavailable here.

Keep the primary AKI cohort unchanged. For direct specificity contrasts, create a secondary common-risk subset that additionally has no K35 or S72 date on/before day 30. Fit the same covariates, censoring rule, death competitor, and 10-year contrasts for all three outcomes. Standardize all outcomes to this same subset. Because baseline incidences differ, do not compare raw risk differences across outcomes. Let theta_j be the log standardized 10-year risk ratio for delta −15 versus 0 and D_j=theta_AKI−theta_j for j=K35,S72; obtain joint participant-bootstrap intervals. Repeat for the low-minus-high-grip modification contrast.

Interpretation is two-dimensional:

- Primary prognostic support: AKI risk-difference lower 95% bound >0. Clinical usefulness additionally requires lower bound >0.5 percentage points.
- Renal specificity supported relative to a control only if the corresponding D lower bound >0; adverse relative to that control if D upper bound <=0; otherwise inconclusive. A wide control interval is inconclusive, not reassuring.
- AKI positive, K35 null/smaller, and S72 positive but smaller favors a renal-plus-shared-frailty interpretation.
- AKI and K35 effects of similar size favor generalized acute-admission/coding opportunity.
- AKI and S72 effects of similar size, with K35 smaller, favor shared frailty/physiological reserve.
- Primary-position N17 materially attenuated toward the controls favors outcome-coding artifact.
- AKI upper 95% risk-difference bound <=0 is adverse for the primary hypothesis regardless of controls. Controls never rescue a null AKI result.

These rules bound interpretation; they do not prove a muscle or renal mechanism.

## Scientifically substantive learned alternative

Fit a multi-task discrete-time survival model on the same baseline inputs and the same common-risk subset. Construct ten one-year intervals. A two-layer shared neural representation (64 and 32 ReLU units, dropout 0.1) feeds task-specific yearly hazard heads for N17, K35, S72, and death. Nonfatal heads remain at risk until their own event, death, loss, or administrative end; one nonfatal control does not censor another. Use participant-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splitting, stratified by sex and observed N17/death status, fixed seed 20260923; tune only learning rate and L2 penalty on validation loss, with early stopping. Repeat seeds 20260923, 20260924, and 20260925 as a stability analysis.

From predicted cause-specific hazards, compute each endpoint’s cumulative incidence under the same delta/grip interventions and empirical standardization as the Cox model. Compare held-out calibration-in-the-large and slope at 10 years, IPCW integrated Brier score, time-dependent AUC, and AKI decision curves. Report the cross-outcome D contrasts and delta×grip surface across seeds. A shared-head ablation that constrains delta/grip effects across outcomes tests whether task-specific structure materially improves held-out loss and specificity contrasts.

This alternative answers a scientific question the Cox baseline loses: whether nonlinear delta–grip risk structure is shared across frailty/acute-admission outcomes or is reproducibly AKI-specific. It is not selected for the primary claim merely for a small AUC gain. Retain it only if calibration is acceptable, the task-specific model improves held-out Brier score or net benefit, and the direction of D and the interaction surface is stable across all three seeds. Otherwise defer it and retain Cox/AJ inference. A latent true-GFR mechanistic model is not identifiable from two baseline filtration markers without measured GFR or validated repeat measurement error and is explicitly rejected.
