# Successor B: does the late lactate trajectory improve 24-hour prognosis beyond the late value itself?

## Clinical question, supported claim, and hypothesis

At the 24-hour reassessment, the clinician can see the most recent eligible late lactate. The parent showed that people with less than 20% clearance have higher crude hospital mortality in a feasible EICU risk set, but that contrast does **not** answer whether the trajectory adds information: nonclearance is calculated from the baseline and late values and is strongly constrained by the late value. Treating its crude risk difference as incremental evidence would be circular.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is narrower. In several critical-care populations, late lactate is prognostic; inspected studies disagree about whether serial history or clearance improves prediction beyond the contemporaneous value. Baysan et al. (2022; DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; acquired full-text XML source `[source checksum]`) found that admission plus 24-hour lactate modestly improved model fit in sepsis and that adding 24-hour lactate to a recalibrated APACHE model increased C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64. Klemm et al. (2024; DOI 10.3390/jcm13071932; source `[source checksum]`) found the 24-hour absolute value outperformed clearance in 64 cardiogenic-shock patients. Cao et al. (2022; DOI 10.1186/s12871-022-01878-0; source `[source checksum]`) reported limited prediction by 24-hour clearance alone but better performance in combinations. A current 2026 review (Levy et al.; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100061; source `[source checksum]`) emphasizes serial interpretation while warning that adrenergic stimulation, inflammation, ischemia-reperfusion, and hepatic/renal clearance can all shape lactate. None tests this exact multicenter EICU phenotype or establishes bedside benefit.

The unresolved, falsifiable hypothesis is: **in the parent’s fixed 24-hour EICU risk set, adding the continuous baseline-to-late lactate trajectory to a model already containing the absolute late lactate will increase out-of-hospital discrimination for subsequent hospital mortality by at least 0.02 AUROC, without worsening Brier score.** A 0.02 AUROC increment is frozen as the minimum material prognostic gain because it matches the order of the modest late-lactate increment reported by Baysan et al.; it is a development threshold, not a universal clinical utility constant.

The substantive advance over the parent is therefore an incremental, transport-focused prediction estimand, not another association between a derived group and death. It asks whether retaining the prior value is prognostically worthwhile once the current value is known. It does not ask whether lactate caused death, whether failure to clear proves hypoperfusion or failed treatment, or whether acting on the trajectory benefits patients.

## Frozen population, landmark, measurements, and outcome

Use the complete EICU snapshot `[source checksum]`, read-only and unsampled. The parent’s population and time rules are unchanged.

1. Map `patient.age` `>89` to 90 and retain age at least 18. For each stay, define candidate `t0` as the minimum `infusionDrug.infusionoffset` in [0,1440] whose case-folded `drugname` contains `norepinephrine` or `levophed`. For each `uniquepid`, retain the lexicographically smallest (`t0`, integer `patientunitstayid`). A matching row is evidence of a recorded infusion entry, not verified administration or dose.
2. Set `L24=t0+1440` minutes. Require `hospitaldischargestatus` exactly Alive or Expired and `hospitaldischargeoffset>=L24`. This defines people still in the hospital risk set at L24 with a known eventual outcome; it excludes earlier live discharge as well as earlier death. `D=1` only for Expired. EICU provides no separately adjudicated death time or cause.
3. Baseline `B`: case-insensitive `labname='lactate'`, case-insensitive `labmeasurenamesystem='mmol/L'`, finite numeric `labresult` in [0.2,30], collection `labresultoffset` in [t0-360,t0], and `labresultrevisedoffset<=t0+60`. At each collection time take rows at the greatest eligible revision; if tied rows disagree, invalidate that collection; identical ties use smallest integer `labid`. Select the latest valid collection, then smallest `labid`, and require `B>=2`.
4. Late value `F`: apply the same analyte, unit, range, revision, conflict, and tie rules with collection in [t0+720,t0+1440] and revision by L24. Select nearest t0+1080, then earlier collection, then smallest `labid`. This is the absolute late value available at L24, although its collection can be 0–720 minutes old at the landmark.
5. Define continuous trajectory `T=ln(F/B)`; higher T means less clearance or a rise. Define the parent’s binary `X=1[(B-F)/B<0.20]` only as a secondary implementation heuristic. Retain the actual baseline and follow-up collection offsets. All predictors exist by L24; outcome is read only after the cohort and outcome-blind site gate are frozen.

The target outcome is subsequent in-hospital mortality among this measured 24-hour risk set. The target population is explicitly measurement-selected and pressor-entry-selected; it is not all shock, sepsis, or ICU patients.

## Locked models and estimand

Construct three fixed logistic models. There is no hyperparameter search and no use of hospital identity as a predictor.

- Sanity baseline: training-set mortality prevalence only.
- Current-value model M0: `age, age^2`; fixed gender indicators Female, Male, and Other/Unknown (one omitted); `t0/1440` and its square; baseline collection lag `(t0-B_offset)/360`; follow-up age at landmark `(L24-F_offset)/720`; `ln(F)` and `ln(F)^2`.
- Continuous-trajectory model M1: every M0 term plus `T=ln(F/B)` and `T^2`.
- Binary heuristic model M2: every M0 term plus X, replacing T terms. M2 is secondary and cannot rescue or reverse the primary classification.

M0 deliberately excludes the value B: conditional on F, adding T is algebraically equivalent to adding the prior B value. Including B in M0 would make the incremental question tautological. Its timestamp remains in both models to control the opportunity and spacing of the prior measurement.

Within each training set, winsorize continuous raw features at training 1st/99th percentiles and standardize to training mean/SD; apply those constants unchanged to the held-out site. Fit unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, `C=1.0`, `lbfgs`, maximum 10,000 iterations, and tolerance `1e-8`; use no class weights. Fixed category columns prevent unseen-category errors. Clip probabilities only for log loss at [1e-6,1-1e-6].

Generate one prediction per person by leave-one-hospital-out validation: for each of the 33 retained hospitals, train each model on the other 32 and predict the held-out hospital. Pool only these out-of-hospital predictions. The primary estimand is
`ΔAUC = AUROC(M1)-AUROC(M0)`.
Secondary proper-score estimands are `ΔBrier=Brier(M0)-Brier(M1)` and `Δlogloss=logloss(M0)-logloss(M1)`, so positive values favor trajectory. Report each model’s calibration-in-the-large and calibration slope from pooled held-out predictions, with no post-hoc recalibration. Report corresponding M2-minus-M0 metrics descriptively, but suppress an adjusted binary group-effect interpretation because its overlap gate is already failed.

For uncertainty, use 2,000 copied-hospital bootstrap replicates with seed `20260924`. Sample 33 hospitals with replacement; all copies of the same original hospital remain a single held-out group, models are refit using the sampled multiplicities of all other original hospitals, and predicted rows are repeated by their sampled multiplicity. This prevents the same original site from entering both training and test sets. Use percentile 95% intervals. For deletion stability, recompute pooled metric differences after deleting each original hospital from the fixed out-of-hospital predictions.

## Gates and result meanings

Before interpreting M1, require: exactly the frozen source hashes and cohort rules; at least 25 eligible hospitals, 800 people, 200 deaths, and 200 survivors; every retained hospital with both outcomes; maximum site share at most 15%; all 33 original M0/M1 fits converged with finite predictions; at least 95% of bootstrap replicates finite and converged; and finite ΔAUC after deleting at least 80% of sites. These gates currently pass through population/events/site concentration but model execution remains untested. The binary overlap gate fails prospectively as reported and affects only M2/group interpretation.

Classify the primary result in this order:

- **Opposite/harmful:** all computability gates pass, the upper 95% bound for ΔAUC is below 0, and the upper bound for ΔBrier is below 0.
- **Supportive of clinically material incremental prognosis:** all gates pass; the lower 95% bound for ΔAUC is at least +0.02; the lower bound for ΔBrier is at least 0; M1 calibration slope is 0.80–1.20 and absolute calibration-in-the-large is at most 0.05; and at least 80% of finite site-deletion ΔAUC values are positive.
- **Adverse to the prespecified material-gain claim:** all computability gates pass and the upper 95% bound for ΔAUC is below +0.02. This can coexist with a smaller positive gain and does not prove zero information; Brier, log loss, and calibration must still be reported.
- **Inconclusive:** any computability gate fails; the ΔAUC interval includes +0.02; or the AUROC criterion is met but the Brier, calibration, or site-stability guard fails.

Always report point estimates, intervals, denominators, site/event counts, and gate outcomes. A p-value or a positive trajectory coefficient cannot substitute for these rules. No result from M2 changes the primary label.

A supportive result would justify external/prospective validation of a serial-lactate prognostic feature. An adverse result would argue that, in this measured EICU population and model class, keeping the prior value does not add the prespecified material discrimination beyond the late value; it would not show that serial measurement is clinically useless. An opposite result would indicate failed transport or overfitting of the trajectory feature. An inconclusive result leaves the material-gain claim unresolved.
