# Early laboratory trajectories and competing liver-directed procedure pathways in HCC

## Scientific deliverable and falsifiable hypothesis

The scientific deliverable is a newly fitted, locked cause-specific prognostic analysis of whether the first 48 hours of dated laboratory information differentially identifies two subsequent recorded liver-directed procedure pathways: (E) embolization/interventional treatment and (R) liver resection or ablation. Completion requires a frozen cohort, exact procedure-family dictionary, competing-event and censoring audit, patient-level split, transparent cause-specific baseline, a trajectory model with family-specific heads, held-out predictions and uncertainty, cumulative-incidence contrasts, falsification analyses, and a claim-to-output interpretation table.

The primary hypothesis is:

> Among adults with a first observed HCC-coded inpatient admission and no recorded liver-directed procedure in hours 0–48, worsening early hepatic/renal laboratory trajectories are differentially associated with the first subsequent recorded liver-directed pathway in hours 48–120: the association with embolization/interventional treatment is stronger than the association with resection/ablation, after accounting for admission and pre-admission information.

“Stronger” is an estimand, not a premise: it is the family contrast in the coefficient or standardized cause-specific risk contrast for a prespecified worsening-reserve pattern. The direction may be wrong. If a clinically coherent dictionary or sufficient events cannot be established, the result is inconclusive rather than evidence for or against the biological hypothesis.

The endpoint is a first local recorded procedure pathway, not treatment indication, treatment choice under equal eligibility, progression, treatment failure, or benefit. A procedure record has no reliable field for intent, scheduling, imaging stage, tumor burden, resectability, or clinical necessity. The proposed advance over the parent is to resolve the parent’s heterogeneous composite endpoint: an association that predicts “some liver-directed procedure” can conceal opposite pathway relationships. The successor asks whether the same early state carries distinguishable information about the pathway actually recorded.

## Evidence versus unresolved claim and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence is feasibility and data structure, not a clinical conclusion. The HCC snapshot contains encounters, diagnosis-linked HCC lexical proxies, dated laboratory records, and dated procedure records. The parent’s bounded audits reported 42,692 provisional first observed adult HCC-coded admissions and 29,086 provisional admissions without a liver-directed procedure in hours 0–48; its provisional 48–120-hour composite transition count was 10,918. A successor bounded audit using explicit family rules found approximately 28,588 provisional no-early-procedure index encounters before the still-inpatient-at-48-hour filter, 2,653 patients whose first eligible 48–120-hour pathway was embolization/interventional, 11,969 whose first was resection/ablation, and 28 same-time mixed-family cases. These are feasibility diagnostics, not locked results: the solver must regenerate them after freezing exact strings, duplicate handling, event precedence, discharge competing-event handling, the landmark risk-set filter, and split assignment.

The unresolved claim is not that laboratory deterioration causes embolization or that stable laboratories select resection/ablation. It is whether early laboratory trajectories contain reproducible, held-out, pathway-differential prognostic information beyond admission/pre-admission features, and whether that information survives observation-intensity and timestamp falsification. This matters because multidisciplinary review and procedural capacity planning distinguish catheter-based/interventional pathways from surgery or ablation. A validated pathway-specific signal could motivate prospective review of physiologic reserve and procedural suitability. It would not by itself select a treatment, establish eligibility, or justify changing care.

The parent’s conclusion that a composite recorded liver-directed transition is computable does not establish this family contrast. The three demonstrations are method context only: Delphi supports evaluating dated structured histories with patient-level held-out calibration; ALADYNOULLI motivates a bounded EHR-only latent longitudinal adaptation, not reproduction of its unavailable genetic analysis; Oncoformer supports comparing learned longitudinal representations with simpler baselines, while its main article/full STAR Methods and local HCC images are unavailable. No demonstration establishes this HCC hypothesis.

## Population, landmark, competing-risk estimand and native-time rules

Use the configured HCC participant buckets only: fitting buckets 0–59, selection buckets 60–69, and locked internal test buckets 70–79. Buckets 80–99 are inaccessible; no external-validation claim is permitted.

1. Build the eligible encounter set from encounters joined to diagnoses on the exact pair (patient master index, encounter number). Normalize only whitespace/encoding. Require age >= 18, parseable native admission time and discharge time, discharge time >= admission time, admission time >= 2011-01-01 00:00:00, discharge time < 2026-01-01 00:00:00, and at least one diagnosis name matching the frozen HCC lexical rule (initially hepatocellular carcinoma or liver cancer after exact review). Retain diagnosis type as a descriptor; it is not a confirmation or stage label.

2. Select the earliest eligible encounter per Patient Master Index, ordering by native Admission Time and then Encounter Number. Report duplicate encounter keys, diagnosis multiplicity/types, unmatched diagnosis keys, invalid or ongoing times, overlapping admissions, and earlier HCC-coded encounters excluded or not resolvable. Do not silently replace an invalid earliest candidate with a later row.

3. Define the landmark risk set as selected encounters with no valid-start procedure in the frozen liver-directed dictionary (E, R, X and any other included liver-directed therapeutic family) during [0, 48) hours from admission time and with native discharge time >= admission time + 48 hours. Thus every primary subject is still observed at the landmark. A procedure with missing/invalid start time is not evidence of no procedure. Report its count and perform a prespecified missing-time sensitivity only if its scale and interpretation are acceptable. The early exclusion and landmark-inpatient requirement are cohort rules, not predictors.

4. The landmark is Admission time + 48 hours. A procedure row is linked only when the exact pair (Patient master index, Visit number) matches the index encounter. Use native procedure Start time, never End time, to define onset. The primary risk interval is [48, 120) hours from admission, represented as [0, 72) hours from the landmark, in 6-hour bins for the pooled-hazard implementation. Require procedure start <= native Discharge time. Procedures after discharge are ineligible.

5. Define a first-event competing-risk outcome:
   - Cause E: first eligible row/string in the frozen embolization/interventional family, including explicit hepatic-artery embolization, TACE/chemoembolization and documented therapeutic hepatic-artery catheter intervention.
   - Cause R: first eligible row/string in the frozen liver resection or liver-lesion ablation family, including hepatic resection and explicit liver tumor/lesion radiofrequency, microwave, cryoablation or percutaneous ethanol ablation.
   - Cause X: a first eligible liver-directed row that is clinically relevant but ambiguous/mixed between E and R, or another prespecified liver-directed therapeutic family not assigned to E or R. Same-timestamp E+R rows are X unless an exact composite string has a prespecified primary family before outcome modelling.
   - Cause D: native discharge before 120 hours without a prior E, R or X event; discharge is an observed loss of inpatient procedural opportunity and therefore a competing event, not a zero-risk non-event.
   - Administrative censoring: no E/R/X/D event before 120 hours. No death event is available; death cannot be treated as a competing risk. Procedure starts after native discharge are ineligible.
   
   The primary estimand is the family-specific cumulative incidence at 72 landmark hours, CIF_E(72) and CIF_R(72), and the contrast in standardized risk for a prespecified one-standard-deviation worsening hepatic/renal trajectory. The primary differential estimand is the contrast of family-specific trajectory coefficients (or equivalently the difference in standardized cause-specific risk contrasts), Δ_E-R. Estimate cause-specific hazards for E, R, X and D with all other causes competing; report subdistribution/CIF summaries rather than interpreting a cause-specific hazard as a marginal probability. Do not use a cause-specific hazard ratio as a treatment effect.

6. The primary directional test is whether Δ_E-R > 0 for the worsening-reserve pattern, with a two-sided 95% interval and a prespecified scientific relevance threshold chosen before test inspection (for example, at least a 10% relative difference between the two standardized family-specific risk contrasts, if event support makes that scale stable). If the threshold is not estimable without arbitrary post hoc tuning, report the estimate and uncertainty without declaring directional support. The principal analysis is not a multiple-testing search across assays: exact channels and trajectory contrasts are frozen before fitting. The 48–168-hour window is a secondary sensitivity, with identical cause definitions and discharge rules.

## Baseline, substantive alternative and method-selection record

The comparison must use the same population, cause definitions, time bins, splits, outcomes and missingness policy.

### B0: transparent admission/pre-admission cause-specific baseline

Fit two one-vs-all cause-specific elastic-net pooled-logistic hazard models over 6-hour bins from landmark to 120 hours. For each bin, the event label is E versus still at risk (including R/X/D as competing failures), or R versus still at risk; after any competing event, including discharge, later bins are not at risk. Inputs are admission age, sex, admitting-department family, calendar era, admission time-of-day, diagnosis-type indicators, frozen pre-admission HCC/liver-disease lexical proxies, prior encounter/inpatient counts, prior liver-directed procedure counts in the 90-day lookback, and per-exact-assay pre-admission nearest value, recency, count, missingness and slope when at least two dated values exist. Include a bin-time indicator and identical preprocessing across causes. No post-admission laboratory, procedure, order, medication, examination, document, pathology, discharge or length-of-stay information enters B0.

### B1: transparent early trajectory comparator

B1 adds only first 48-hour summaries for each frozen exact assay: first value, last value, difference, elapsed time between first and last, count, number of distinct valid timestamps, missingness/availability, and slope when at least two values exist. It also adds prespecified cross-channel contrasts only if units are identical within the exact assay and the contrast has a defensible sign; otherwise keep channels separate. B1 is the primary transparent comparator for the alternative. A stronger B1 prevents the learned model from claiming an advance over an artificially weak admission model.

### Selected alternative: irregular coupled reserve model with competing-risk heads

Fit a low-dimensional continuous-time observation/state model using every valid dated numeric token from the same early assay channels, assay identity, elapsed time, qualitative-result/missingness indicators and measurement density. Use separate latent hepatic-reserve and renal-stress states with patient-level shrinkage/random intercepts and slopes, assay-specific observation offsets/noise, irregular time gaps and an explicit observation-process term. At the landmark, the cause-specific heads use latent level, recent state slope, coupled-state contrast, posterior state uncertainty, and B0 covariates to predict E and R hazards; X and D are included as competing heads when event support allows; otherwise their rows remain competing failures in the E/R heads. Estimate family-specific CIFs by standardizing predicted cause-specific hazards over the observed competing-risk process.

The substantive information this model could reveal that B1 loses is whether irregularly timed, coupled movement in reserve and renal stress—rather than only first/last change—has distinct pathway associations, and whether apparent laboratory deterioration is actually a measurement-intensity/observation-process signal. The model’s “reserve” and “stress” states are fitted summaries, not validated hepatic failure or acute kidney injury diagnoses. State dimensions, process noise, shrinkage, outcome regularization and observation-process specification are selected only in buckets 0–59 and 60–69; the locked test is untouched.

### Deferred alternative and reason

A one-layer GRU or temporal-attention model over identical early laboratory tokens is a scientifically plausible deferred alternative. It could identify nonlinear order and interactions, but it is not needed to answer the first pathway-differential question if the latent model is stable and interpretable. Revisit only if locked residuals and predeclared diagnostics show reproducible nonlinear timing interactions that the latent model cannot represent. Revisit requires a new child proposal if it changes the estimand or population. A text model is deferred because documents have no native temporal columns, pathology has no native pathology time, local text extraction is unvalidated, and expert labels are unavailable. GPU availability is not a selection criterion: B0/B1 and the low-dimensional latent model are expected to fit on CPU; a later neural sequence fit could request one allocated A100 and use cuda:0, subject to a bounded solver budget.

Produce a cohort flow from all encounters through diagnosis match, adult/valid-time eligibility, first-patient selection, early-procedure exclusion, exact dictionary classification, valid early-lab availability, event/discharge/censoring status and final split. Preserve raw row audits separately from modelling aggregation. Primary held-out evaluation on buckets 70–79:

- cause-specific hazard discrimination and calibration for E and R by time bin;
- cause-specific Brier score and integrated Brier score with competing-risk appropriate weighting;
- time-dependent AUROC/PR-AUC only as secondary summaries because event prevalence differs by cause;
- 72-hour CIF calibration plots, observed versus predicted CIF by prespecified risk bins, calibration intercept/slope, and standardized CIF_E/CIF_R contrasts;
- paired patient bootstrap 95% intervals with at least 500 resamples for B1–B0 and latent–B1 differences, Δ_E-R, CIF contrasts and calibration quantities;
- report both absolute and relative pathway-specific risk differences, event prevalence, and threshold-based review counts as exploratory planning summaries, not a validated policy.

All preprocessing, dictionary decisions, assay selection, scaling and hyperparameter selection are training/selection-only. Splits are patient-level by patient master index so multiple encounters/rows cannot cross partitions. No post-landmark data enter primary predictors.

Prespecified falsification and sensitivity analyses:

1. Add/remove measurement count, missingness, timestamp gap and observation-process terms. If the pathway contrast disappears, label it surveillance/selection signal rather than organ-state evidence.
2. Permute laboratory timestamps within patient and exact assay while preserving values and assay identity. A timing-specific claim should attenuate; unchanged performance indicates value/density rather than trajectory timing.
3. Use pre-admission laboratory timestamps as a negative timing control and verify no early token precedes admission or exceeds the 48-hour landmark.
4. Repeat under a frozen same-assay/same-timestamp aggregation rule and after excluding duplicate rows.
5. Repeat the 48–168-hour window, by admitting department, calendar era, assay availability and measurement density. These are heterogeneity descriptions, not causal effect modification.
6. Repeat with X retained as a competing event and, separately, exclude X from the estimand while reporting its frequency. If conclusions depend on forcing ambiguous mixed procedures into E or R, the pathway claim fails.
7. Report first eligible procedure family, row count, same-time duplicate count, and exact-string contribution. Refit after excluding low-frequency or clinically ambiguous strings using a rule frozen before seeing model results.
8. Use a negative-control procedure endpoint made from central-line/venipuncture terms. Comparable pathway discrimination for this workflow endpoint implicates care intensity and undermines physiologic interpretation.
9. Repeat after stratifying or excluding procedures with order records in the early window as a planned-care sensitivity, but do not call orders intent labels and do not interpret the result causally.
10. Include post-landmark procedure/order features only in a labelled leakage demonstration, never in the primary analysis.
11. A cause-specific model that ignores competing events is a deliberate negative analysis; quantify how much its estimates differ from the competing-risk implementation. Do not use it as the primary result.
12. Preserve the parent composite endpoint as a bridge analysis only. It cannot replace the pathway-specific estimand.

## Interpretation criteria

Supportive evidence requires:

- a clinically coherent, preregistered direction for the reserve/renal trajectory and a nonzero Δ_E-R with uncertainty reported;
- B1 improves calibrated prediction over B0 for at least one pathway without a major calibration failure in the other;
- the latent model improves over B1 in held-out competing-risk calibration/CIF or a prespecified pathway-risk stratification metric, with an absolute integrated Brier improvement threshold of 0.002 or another threshold frozen before results;
- the family contrast is not explained by measurement intensity, timestamp permutation, one department/era, one assay, one exact procedure string, or the X classification;
- the signal is not reproduced by the central-line/venipuncture negative control, and competing-risk estimates are materially more faithful than the naive composite analysis.

Support would justify external validation and expert review of whether the signal corresponds to procedural suitability or pathway planning. It would not justify selecting embolization over surgery/ablation, denying treatment, or claiming benefit.

Adverse evidence includes a null or opposite Δ_E-R, no meaningful latent-versus-B1 gain, poor or unstable CIF calibration, disappearance after observation-process adjustment, persistence under timestamp permutation, equivalent performance for the negative-control endpoint, or strong dependence on local procedure-name artifacts, scheduling proxies, one department/era or ambiguous mixed strings. An adverse result is scientifically useful: it would show that the apparent composite association does not resolve into a robust laboratory-linked pathway signal.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 100 events for either primary cause after frozen exclusions, inability to freeze a coherent exact dictionary, excessive missing/invalid procedure times, substantial unresolved X events, incomplete discharge ascertainment, inadequate dated assay support, nonconvergence, unstable CIF weighting, or intervals too wide to distinguish a meaningful pathway difference from no difference. Inconclusive means this snapshot cannot resolve the question.

## Clinical evidence limits and what requires another study

Computationally checkable claims are the cohort counts, exact-string and timestamp audits, split integrity, leakage absence, model convergence, held-out predictions, cause-specific metrics, CIF estimates, bootstrap uncertainty, and whether the prespecified falsification patterns occurred.

The data cannot establish validated HCC diagnosis, pathology confirmation, radiographic stage, tumor burden, vascular invasion, resectability, treatment intent, scheduling status, multidisciplinary recommendation, procedure necessity, clinical benefit, toxicity, mortality, transplant eligibility, recurrence, progression, organ failure, AKI, outside-hospital events, or causal effects. No death field, complete outside-hospital capture, lab units/reference ranges, or validated imaging/pathology timing is available. The diagnosis table has no timestamp; examinations and documents are not validated indication labels; pathology has no native pathology time; vitals and transfers are identifier-only.

Stronger conclusions require expert adjudication linking exact procedure strings to therapeutic intent and pathway, radiology/pathology review with acquisition timestamps, laboratory units/reference ranges, tumor stage/resectability and liver-function adjudication, treatment scheduling/indication fields, mortality/transplant and multi-hospital linkage, external validation, and a prospective clinical or causal/implementation study. The proposed analysis is prognostic and observational; it does not estimate the effect of choosing one procedure family.
