# Patient-linked laboratory surveillance and a transportable monitoring-review candidate

Status: Episode 18 substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic, transportability and decision-reclassification study. It is not a causal test-ordering study, hospital-quality ranking, alert-impact trial, or claim that changing monitoring improves survival.

## Clinical question and hypothesis

The parent established that the six-hour laboratory observation process can be studied across held-out hospitals, but a mean prediction increment does not by itself answer the clinically important question: would the process signal identify a reproducible set of patients who are newly candidates for intensified monitoring review, and is that set patient-linked rather than a workflow fingerprint?

At the fixed ICU-relative minute-360 landmark, the hypothesis is:

> Adding patient-level laboratory observation timing and intensity to laboratory state and minimal availability will improve calibrated competing-risk prediction of recorded ICU death through minute 1,800 and will create a supported, cross-hospital group that crosses a fixed monitoring-review risk threshold from below to above. That increment and the new-group event contrast should attenuate when complete process vectors are permuted within held-out hospitals and matched on out-of-fold baseline risk and unit-stay type. If the gain disappears under within-hospital percentile normalization or matches hospital descriptors, the stronger interpretation is workflow-scale dependence, not patient-specific surveillance information.

This is a sharper, falsifiable refinement of the parent’s raw-versus-relative transport question. The “monitoring-review candidate” is a retrospective model-defined subgroup, not observed monitoring escalation.

## Equal-input models and reclassification

All models share the same episodes, codebook, [0,360] features, [360,1800] labels, folds and support gates.

B0 is an elastic-net pooled discrete-time cause-specific logistic baseline with death and alive-discharge heads on hourly rows. It uses admission context, analyte value summaries/range/slope, clinical value flags and one availability flag per analyte. It excludes detailed process, hospital ID and descriptors.

B1 is the transparent matched primary alternative: B0 plus analyte counts, distinct result-time counts, first/last times, recency, span/inter-result gaps, revision-known flags, stable system/interface indicators, total row count, distinct-analyte count and total span. The primary transport estimand is the held-out macro-hospital B1-minus-B0 calibration/Brier difference.

B2 retains the parent’s outcome-free target-hospital percentile normalization of B1 process variables. It is an explicitly labeled unsupervised adaptation diagnostic, not strict zero-shot transport.

T is the learned alternative: one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins with the exact B1 state, availability, count, recency, revision and interface/system channels, no hospital ID, and competing death/discharge heads. It is selected only if support and calibration/convergence pass, and its scientific question is whether ordering/persistence/co-movement adds information beyond B1.

For fixed q in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, define the held-out new-review group as B0 CIF < q and B1 CIF >= q; calculate the analogous B2 group where supported. Report group size, hospital coverage, recorded-death CIF, alive-discharge CIF, calibration, capture, PPV, alert burden and label-relative net benefit. Use fixed B0 bands low <.01, intermediate .01–<.05, high >=.05. A group is not evidence of actual monitoring benefit.

## Split, uncertainty and gates

Use five grouped outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals, keeping `uniquepid` together. Development-only imputation, scaling, codebook, hyperparameter and early-stopping decisions are mandatory. Report macro-hospital paired results first, patient-weighted results second. Use hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals, at least 1,000 resamples when feasible, with simultaneous 95% intervals over q and B0 bands.

Require at least 100 deaths overall, 10 event-bearing hospitals per retained fold, 20 eligible episodes per contributing hospital, positive state/process overlap and finite metrics. A q/band cell additionally needs 200 held-out stays, 30 deaths, and 10 stays in at least 80% of contributing hospitals. B2 requires 50 target calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes per hospital. T requires stable validation/held-out calibration. Gate failures are reported as unsupported/inconclusive, never silently pooled.

## Falsification and result meaning

Within each held-out hospital, stratify on out-of-fold B0 risk ventiles and `unitstaytype` (coarsen sparse cells), then permute complete process vectors. The B1 increment and new-review contrast should attenuate. Persistence means residual state/site structure, leakage or a flawed null—not actionability. Compare a site-only model using hospital bed category, teaching status and region; a comparable gain indicates workflow. Compare raw B1 with B2 local percentiles; raw-only gain supports workflow dependence, while B2 persistence supports only relative-surveillance portability with an unlabeled target stream. Repeat common-panel and strict-revision analyses, process ablations, and a within-bin temporal shuffle for T.

Supportive evidence requires favorable held-out B1-minus-B0 calibration/Brier, a supported fixed q/band reclassification or net-benefit result with simultaneous uncertainty excluding zero, matched-permutation attenuation, no comparable site-only signal, and no sensitivity reversal. Adverse evidence is null/worsening, calibration harm, disappearance under matching/normalization, persistence under permutation, site-only equivalence or revision/panel reversal. Inconclusive evidence is any support/overlap failure, unsupported B2, unstable folds/T, or intervals spanning meaningful ranges. No fitted result is asserted here.

## Clinical evidence limits and deferred branches

The data record results, not order intent, indication, absent-test meaning, physiologic truth, clinician response, alert burden harm, treatment response, preferences, staffing, external validation or post-discharge mortality. Recorded ICU death is a discharge-status label and not adjudicated cause. Supportive computation cannot establish that clinicians should increase testing, that a monitoring alert improves survival, or that a process variable is causal. Those claims require expert chart adjudication, observed orders/monitoring and responses, external hospitals, and prospective impact or randomized evidence.

Causal test-ordering, treatment-effect, vital/nurse/respiratory multimodal, NLP/waveform, image/full-demonstration reproduction, and Bayesian genetic/latent reproduction are deferred for missing inputs or a different estimand. The sparse renal/nutrition/insulin branch remains deferred rather than revived by weakening documentation.
