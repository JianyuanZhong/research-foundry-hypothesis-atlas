# Directional macrophage–FLS crosstalk in rheumatoid arthritis

## Unresolved question

Does co-culture with MerTK/CD206-positive versus MerTK/CD206-negative synovial macrophages produce a reproducible, **phenotype-specific FLS inflammatory program**, rather than a generic co-culture response or a donor/condition artifact? This is not a claim that co-expression proves directionality; it is a falsifiable, donor-aware, perturbation-based test.

## Clinical importance

Rheumatoid arthritis synovium contains both pathogenic fibroblasts and macrophage subsets that can either sustain inflammation or promote resolution. If macrophage phenotype, not merely co-culture or donor baseline, changes a measurable FLS program, this would provide a mechanistic target for therapies intended to restore resolution rather than only suppress inflammatory output.

## Evidence and hypothesis

Prior work supports bidirectional stromal–myeloid signaling and macrophage heterogeneity in RA, but the available targeted assay allows a sharper comparison:
- **Baseline claim (already supported):** synovial macrophages and FLS interact, and macrophage subsets are heterogeneous.
- **Unresolved claim (to be tested):** MerTK/CD206-positive versus -negative macrophage phenotype specifically shifts a coherent FLS transcriptional program beyond a phenotype-insensitive co-culture baseline.
- **Strong rival:** any apparent phenotype effect is donor-dependent, batch-driven, or a generic co-culture response.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

- **Baseline:** donor-aware, phenotype-insensitive linear model comparing any co-culture to FLS-only, plus a separate indicator for MerTK/CD206 phenotype. This tests whether co-culture itself changes the FLS program and whether phenotype adds information.
- **Substantive alternative:** leave-one-FLS-donor-out multivariate model trained only on measured inflammatory and matrix-remodeling genes to predict macrophage phenotype from FLS expression. If a held-out-donor phenotype-specific score remains stable, this supports a coherent program not reducible to a single donor or a generic co-culture response.
- **Split:** leave-one-FLS-donor-out cross-validation.
- **Evaluation:** held-out log score or AUROC with donor-cluster bootstrap; gene-level and program-level effect sizes with uncertainty.
- **Falsification:** if the phenotype-specific score does not exceed the phenotype-insensitive baseline, or if held-out predictions are unstable across donor clusters, the phenotype-specific claim is not supported.
- **Compute:** CPU; minutes-scale for 33 samples and 445 features. No GPU required.

## Outcomes

- **Supportive:** reproducible phenotype-specific FLS program in held-out donors, consistent direction across at least two independent FLS donor clusters, and perturbation effects aligned with macrophage-directed MerTK inhibition.
- **Adverse:** no stable phenotype effect after donor adjustment, or effects confined to a single donor/condition.
- **Inconclusive:** weak or unstable effects due to small n, targeted-panel limitation, or unmodeled donor dependence; this would not prove absence of crosstalk.

## Limits

- Co-culture expression cannot prove which ligand-receptor pair mediates directionality.
- Targeted panel cannot claim whole-transcriptome novelty.
- Cross-sectional perturbations cannot establish longitudinal disease transitions.
- Clinical translation requires orthogonal validation in independent tissue cohorts and expert clinical adjudication.
