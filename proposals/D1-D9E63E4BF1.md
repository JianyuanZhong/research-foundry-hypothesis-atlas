# Day-60 HCC marker-versus-injury ascertainment after resection

Status: reference-complete crossover child. A bounded source audit resolved the parents’ decisive feasibility objection. The disease-content route is retained as a frozen gate but is not authorized for fitting: even a deliberately broad, procedure-only “untreated” upper-bound cohort produced 18 first liver CT/MRI reports before later treatment. The executable deliverable is therefore the ascertainment/measurement study (route B). No biomarker or outcome model has been fitted.

## Scientific opening, claim boundary, and advance

The strongest supported claim is that AFP response at the 4–6-week postoperative visit is prognostic for recurrence and survival in a selected cohort of 549 resected HCC patients with preoperative AFP >400 ng/mL [K1]. This does not show that persistent AFP is residual tumor. A documented patient had persistent postoperative AFP with repeated imaging showing no recurrence over two years [K3]. Postoperative treatment is also not a recurrence label: a current randomized protocol assigns high-risk, apparently curatively resected patients to adjuvant TACE versus intensive follow-up, and notes that TACE’s angiographic component may itself detect occult lesions [K2].

The unresolved biological claim is whether an AFP/PIVKA-II component at day 60, separated from postoperative liver injury, carries information about subsequently detected untreated tumor. Its leading interpretation is persistent tumor-associated signal. Its strongest rival is a compound process: injury/regeneration changes AFP, assay/calendar and vitamin-K-related effects alter measurements, clinicians selectively remeasure or image concerning patients, treatment is selected before imaging, and outside surveillance is missing.

The available snapshot cannot adequately test the biological claim. The substantive advance is instead to quantify the observation mechanism that makes that claim non-identifiable here: whether marker and injury trajectories, their missingness, and prior clinical contact predict who is remeasured, imaged, or treated and how treatment intent changes the apparent endpoint. This can prevent an EHR-derived “recurrence” score from confusing tumor biology with care selection and specifies the prospective evidence needed to reopen the clinical question.

## Hypotheses and selected deliverable

Biological hypothesis retained but not currently estimable (route A): among patients with a first observed HCC resection and no HCC-directed therapy through day 60, a day-60 marker-persistence state (M_i(60)), separated from an injury-recovery state (I_i(60)), predicts an affirmative versus explicitly nonpositive first liver CT/MRI report obtained before later treatment, more strongly than it predicts imaging or treatment occurrence.

Selected ascertainment hypothesis (route B): among first observed HCC resections without recorded HCC-directed therapy through day 60, the availability and apparent prognostic association of day-60 tumor-marker trajectories are materially shaped by informative measurement and management. Specifically, models that jointly represent marker/injury values and observation intensity will show that remeasurement, first imaging, and treatment-intent transitions depend on prior contact, marker/injury measurement history, and latent injury state; complete-case marker associations will attenuate or change after this process is modeled.

The route-B scientific deliverable is newly fitted, same-split baseline and longitudinal models that produce: a frozen cohort flow; assay/specimen/calendar and missingness audit; marker-versus-injury measurement-state frequencies through day 60; post-landmark marker/injury remeasurement intensities; first S/T/D/C transition incidences with T intent marks; baseline and joint-model coefficients, calibration and uncertainty; latent-state/loadings and identifiability diagnostics; complete-case versus observation-aware contrasts; negative controls; and a conclusion mapped to supportive, adverse, or inconclusive criteria. It estimates ascertainment, not recurrence, treatment effect, or biological tumor state.

## Decisive bounded audit

The new aggregate-only audit read the HCC source snapshot directly. It defined an adult’s earliest dated liver resection in 2012–2023 in the same encounter as a non-uncertain HCC diagnosis, excluded transplant/donor/biopsy/puncture/cyst/recurrent-procedure wording, required surgery within 30 days of the encounter date, and deduplicated all index-encounter procedure rows.

Among 19,208 index patients, 15,641 had no procedure-defined HCC treatment through day 60. The deliberately broadened marker cohort required only numeric exact-label AFP in days -90:-1 and 15:60 plus two injury-marker dates in days 0:60; it retained prior treatment as a state and omitted pathology and medication-treatment exclusions. It contained 295 patients, but only 18 had a strict first hepatic/upper-abdominal CT/MR report in days 61–365 before a later procedure treatment. An expanded report-text rescue also yielded 18. Lexical feasibility labels were 8 affirmative, 3 explicitly nonpositive, 1 uncertain, and 6 unclassified; validation and locked-test each contained only one report. The pathology-supported broad cohort had 13 first untreated scans; excluding prior procedure treatment left 17; the parent-like cohort had 15.

These are upper bounds because the audit ignored medication-defined HCC therapy. It did not clinically adjudicate pathology, scan purpose, report content, treatment intent, or outside care. The report-content gate (300 reports, 100 reviewed affirmative, 100 reviewed explicit-negative, and 20 of each in locked test) therefore fails by more than an order of magnitude. Relaxing to all 15,641 no-early-procedure-treatment patients yields 339 strict first scans before later procedure treatment, but those patients do not have the paired marker/injury information needed for the biological comparison. Widening on that basis would change the question rather than solve missing follow-up. Route A is formally deferred; the audit is not an outcome result.

The audit script, aggregate JSON, source hashes, definitions, and measured runtime are attached. It scanned the multi-gigabyte lab and examination files in 125.9 seconds on 4 CPUs with 64 GiB requested; this establishes extraction feasibility only.

## Population, landmark, and observation states

Route B primary population:

- age ≥18;
- earliest observed non-uncertain `hepatocellular carcinoma` or `primary liver cancer` diagnosis joined to a liver resection in the same encounter, with dated surgery in 2012–2023 and within 30 days of encounter date;
- exclude transplant, donor, biopsy/puncture, cyst operation, and explicitly recurrent/repeat resection as index;
- alive/observed at day 60 insofar as the local record permits, and no recorded TACE/embolization, ablation, transplant, separate repeat resection, or systemic antitumor therapy from index+1 through day 60 after the medication dictionary is frozen;
- do not require biomarker measurement. Prior therapy in days -180:-1 is retained as a four-level measured state (none, locoregional, systemic, ambiguous) and the never-prior-treated subset is prespecified.

Predictor window is day -90 through +60; landmark is day 60; follow-up is day 61 through day 365, last observed dated contact, or administrative end. Absence is censoring or a measurement state, never nonrecurrence.

At day 60, classify observed measurement state as paired marker+injury trajectory, marker-only, injury-only, or neither. Marker trajectory means AFP/PIVKA-II/AFP-L3 values; injury trajectory means ALT/AST/bilirubin/INR/albumin/platelets. Counts, times, exact labels/specimens, censor flags, and missingness are data, not imputed clinical normality.

Post-landmark processes are:

- `L-M`: AFP/PIVKA-II/AFP-L3 measurement;
- `L-I`: injury-panel measurement;
- `S`: first dated liver/upper-abdominal CT/MRI before recorded HCC treatment;
- `T-R`, `T-A`, `T-B`, `T-U`: treatment before S with same-encounter text respectively indicating recurrence/residual/progression/metastasis, adjuvant/preventive intent, both, or unclassified intent;
- `D`: explicit in-hospital death text dated by linked discharge, analyzed only as an unvalidated sensitivity;
- `C`: censor at the earlier of day 365 or last dated local contact. No post-day-60 record censors at day 60.

S is report-detection time, not recurrence onset. Same-day S/T ties are a separate state. For affirmative S reports, report the descriptive interval from latest prior explicit-negative scan (or day 60) to first affirmative scan; do not call it biological onset.

## Two matched methods on the same question

Use `int(SHA256(patient master index)[:8],16) mod 100`: 0–59 train, 60–69 validation, 70–79 locked test, 80–99 untouched reserve. Freeze cohort code, dictionaries, report rules, hyperparameters, and gates before test access. Both methods use identical patients, outcomes, covariates, and split.

Simple baseline:

1. Multinomial elastic-net regression for the day-60 measurement state.
2. Cause-specific elastic-net Cox or weekly pooled-logistic models for first S, T-R/T-A/T-B/T-U, L-M, and L-I; report cumulative incidence for S/T and recurrent-count calibration for labs.
3. On the subset with values, Firth/penalized models using preoperative AFP rank within calendar year, latest day-15:60 AFP/preoperative log ratio, optional PIVKA-II ratio, acute peak and latest injury ratios, age, sex, year, service, pathology proxies, prior-treatment state, medication flags, and pre-landmark encounter/lab/imaging counts. Explicit missingness indicators are retained.

This baseline is clinically legible but collapses irregular timing and recovery shape and treats observation history as hand-built summaries.

Substantive longitudinal alternative: fit a constrained continuous-time joint marked point-process model. An injury state (I_i(t)), initialized by surgery with patient-specific recovery, loads on ALT/AST/bilirubin/INR and inversely on albumin/platelets. A marker-persistence state (M_i(t)), allowed to clear, plateau, or rise, loads on AFP and optional PIVKA-II/AFP-L3; AFP may load on both. Observation submodels include exact label/specimen, calendar-era offsets, censoring, missingness masks, and vitamin-K/warfarin/antibiotic flags. Shared random effects link (M_i(60)), (I_i(60)), measurement intensities, prior contact, and baseline covariates to L-M/L-I and S/marked-T intensities.

The alternative can reveal whether an apparent marker component predicts values, the act of measurement, surveillance, or treatment selection; the baseline loses this distinction. It cannot make either latent state biologically “tumor.” Compare constrained/unconstrained two-factor fits, leave-one-marker-out and AFP-only fits, three seeds, posterior predictive checks, factor-swap checks, and simulated recovery of known states. A multivariate functional-PCA plus the same transition models is the CPU fallback if the joint model is non-identifiable. A generic transformer is deferred because flexible prediction does not resolve the observation-process rival.

## Gates, evaluation, and falsification

Route A remains frozen and may be reopened only by a new source or prospective collection meeting all of: ≥300 first untreated S reports; ≥100 blinded-review affirmative and ≥100 explicit-negative reports; ≥20 of each in locked test; PPV and NPV ≥0.85 after adjudication; and adequate paired day-60 marker/injury support. No resampling, report-text rescue, treatment-as-recurrence substitution, or test-set pooling can satisfy this gate.

Route B proceeds only after the full procedure-plus-medication cohort extraction confirms ≥500 eligible patients, ≥150 S-or-T transitions overall, ≥30 transitions in train/validation/test, and ≥100 participants per nonempty day-60 measurement-state category used for inference. The joint state model additionally requires ≥500 participants with at least two distinct pre-landmark observation dates and simulation recovery correlation ≥0.8 without factor swapping. If it fails, the baseline measurement audit remains the deliverable and latent-state claims are inconclusive.

Evaluate weekly observation-rate calibration, time-dependent Brier score and calibration for S/T, multinomial log loss/calibration, held-out likelihood, state/loadings and seed stability, and paired participant bootstrap 95% intervals. Primary scientific contrasts are: complete-case versus observation-aware marker coefficients; marker-state versus injury-state associations with L-M/L-I, S, and each T intent mark; and change in calibration after adding observation intensity. Sensitivities include pathology-supported, no-prior-treatment, calendar era, AFP-only, PIVKA medication-restricted, strict versus expanded imaging, and generic nonhepatic imaging/contact negative controls. Report weight tails and effective sample size; inverse-probability weighting is a sensitivity, not a cure for absent outcomes.

Support for the route-B hypothesis requires stable state separation plus materially different observation/transition hazards by measurement history or latent injury state, and attenuation or sign change of the complete-case marker association after joint observation modeling, with paired 95% intervals excluding no change and consistent direction across calendar/no-prior-treatment sensitivities. This supports an informative-ascertainment warning, not tumor biology.

Adverse evidence is stable near-null observation-intensity effects, unchanged calibrated marker associations after observation modeling, and no association with generic contact or treatment-selection states. This weakens the proposed ascertainment explanation but still cannot support the biological hypothesis because route A failed.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed route-B support gates, nonpositivity/effective sample size <50%, unstable/factor-swapped states, incompatible assay eras, intent-rule PPV/NPV <0.85, unavailable clinical review, or intervals spanning meaningful attenuation and amplification. Such a result means this snapshot cannot quantify either biological decoupling or ascertainment reliably.

Computationally checkable claims are source/schema hashes, joins, dates, cohort flow, procedure-episode deduplication, measurement-state and S/T/D/C construction, split isolation, model fits, diagnostics, metrics, uncertainty, and whether the reported conclusion follows the thresholds. Curative intent, true recurrence, outside care, assay equivalence, treatment intent, latent-state biology, and benefit from earlier imaging require hepatobiliary/radiology review, assay metadata, linked external records, or a prospective scheduled-imaging study. An automatic verifier must not certify them.
