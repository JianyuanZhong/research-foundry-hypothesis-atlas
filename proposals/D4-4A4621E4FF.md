## Clinical question, evidence boundary, and advance

When current and prior values from an eight-field biochemical panel are already available, does adding inexpensive waist and grip measurements provide decision-relevant information for identifying a later recorded cardiorenal/metabolic event? If it does, is that increment preserved when models fitted using repeat assessments through 31 December 2012 are applied without refitting to repeat assessments in calendar year 2013?

This is a measurement-value and temporal-transport question. It is not an estimate of the effect of ordering waist or grip, returning for a repeat assessment, changing treatment, or monitoring a patient. “Decision-relevant” is operationalized prospectively as a prespecified, uncertainty-quantified improvement in proper scoring and stable risk movement in the current+prior-panel ambiguity band; no clinical utility is assumed.

The strongest available local evidence is structural: the snapshot contains the literal field-instance cells, assessment landmarks, position-matched outcome code/date arrays, death-date fields, and unique `eid` joins. The catalog does not itself establish the names, units, allowable ranges, missing-value meanings, specimen collection times, disease onset, clinical code domains, or complete follow-up. Those are gates, not assumptions. The experiment must fail closed before fitting the prospective hypothesis if those dependencies cannot be certified.

The substantive advance over the parents is a distinct cheap-measurement question. It separates:

1. information in the current panel;
2. information in repeat-assessment spacing;
3. information in the prior panel conditional on current values and spacing; and
4. information in waist/grip conditional on both panel occasions and spacing.

It then asks whether the fourth component survives a calendar-era shift, and whether an apparent waist/grip gain is retained after the prior panel (complement) or largely disappears (proxy).

## Falsifiable hypotheses

Let `C` denote age, sex, current panel, and current-assessment calendar year; let `I` add the verified interval between assessment landmarks; let `CP` add the prior panel; and let `CPWG` add current waist and current left/right grip.

Primary hypothesis:

> In participants with a valid paired assessment and certified five-year recorded-outcome ascertainment, `CPWG` improves held-out prediction beyond `CP` by more than a prespecified practical margin in the central 60% of current+prior-panel target risk, and that conditional increment is not materially changed when early-through-2012 fits are applied without refitting to 2013 participants.

Complement-versus-proxy hypothesis:

> The waist/grip increment after `CP` is retained relative to the waist/grip increment after `C+I`; specifically, the gain lost on adding the prior panel is no more than the prespecified margin. A large loss would support the proxy explanation, whereas a retained increment would support complementary information.

The nulls are zero or practically negligible conditional waist/grip information, and/or an early-to-late reversal or deterioration. The direction is not assumed. No hypothesis says that waist is biologically visceral fat, grip is physiological reserve, or either measurement causes or prevents disease.

## Estimands and endpoint

Use a three-category, mutually exclusive recorded endpoint:

- `T`: the earliest qualifying target record strictly after T1 and through H;
- `D`: death before a qualifying target, or death tied with a target under the prespecified precedence rule;
- `N`: neither qualifying target nor death record through H.

The primary target is a predeclared cardiorenal/metabolic composite assembled only after a field dictionary and code-domain review. The solver must freeze the code list, domain labels, and code-date position rule before inspecting outcome associations. A qualifying target must meet the preregistered domain requirement (at least two distinct clinical domains if that definition is retained after review); domain-specific targets are secondary, not replacements for the primary estimand. Codes and dates are paired by identical array position and may never be independently sorted.

For each participant, T1 is the later assessment landmark and H = T1 + 5 years. A record on or before T1 excludes the participant from the primary incident-like analysis only when the target code semantics and dates are certified. Otherwise the analysis uses only the bounded recorded-category endpoint and says “no qualifying record in the available source,” never “disease-free,” “unaffected,” or “incident.” If individual follow-up completion through H cannot be certified, the primary five-year interpretation is inconclusive. A separately labelled bounded recorded-category analysis may still be computed only if code/date semantics and landmark timing pass; it must not use incidence, censoring, disease-free, or complete-ascertainment language. If target and death share a date, death takes precedence, fixed before fitting.

For evaluation set E and fixed band g, let `BS(M,g,E)` be the weighted multiclass Brier score over T/D/N. Define:

- `I_CP,e,g = BS(CP,g,e) - BS(CPWG,g,e)`: waist/grip gain after current+prior panel;
- `I_C,e,g = BS(C+I,g,e) - BS(C+IWG,g,e)`: waist/grip gain before adding the prior panel;
- `A_e,g = I_C,e,g - I_CP,e,g`: gain attenuated by adding the prior panel; positive values indicate proxy-like attenuation;
- `tau_g = I_CP,late,g - I_CP,early,g`: no-refit temporal change in the conditional cheap-measurement increment.

The prespecified primary band is the central 60% of early current+prior-panel target risk: lower tail L is r < q20, ambiguity band A is q20 <= r < q80, and upper tail U is r >= q80. Here r is the cross-fitted `CP` probability of T. q20 and q80 are empirical early-only 0.20 and 0.80 quantiles using one recorded quantile algorithm. The half-open labels are exhaustive and are frozen for all models, weighting, bootstrap and permutations. The band is a research stratum, not a validated clinical threshold.

The primary transported estimand is `I_CP,early,A`, `I_CP,late,A`, and `tau_A`. The complement-versus-proxy contrast is `A_e,A`. The practical Brier margin is `delta = 0.002`; this is a prespecified analysis margin, not a clinical-benefit threshold. Report pooled, band-specific, sex-specific, and endpoint-specific secondary contrasts without replacing the primary estimand.

## Selection, fitting split, and frozen risk band

The primary analysis is a complete required-measurement cohort after field-specific missing-code handling. Do not allow a missing-value sentinel to enter a model. Report a flow from the population anchor through valid age/sex, T0, paired T1, interval, complete current/prior panel, complete waist/grip, prior-target exclusion, early/late era, risk band, and T/D/N outcome.

To diagnose repeat-measurement selection, among T0-complete participants define S=1 as having a valid paired T1 plus complete current/prior panel and waist/grip. Fit a regularized selection model in early data using only pre-T1 variables: T0 panel, age, sex, T0 calendar year, and T0 waist/grip if their semantics and availability allow. Apply it unchanged to late T0-complete people. Use stabilized weights P(S=1)/p-hat(S=1|X), with the numerator from early training data and truncation at the early 1st and 99th percentiles. Report covariate overlap, maximum weight, standardized differences and weighted effective sample size by era and sex. This is a measured-selection sensitivity under a missing-at-random assumption, not causal correction and not evidence that attending a repeat assessment causes benefit. If the selection model cannot be defined without post-T1 data, omit the weight and report the exact failed sensitivity gate.

Use a deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on `eid` within early data. All imputation, scaling, regularization, hyperparameter selection, calibration and selection modeling are training-fold-only. For each early fold, fit `CP` on four folds and produce held-out probabilities for every eligible early participant. Compute q20/q80 from all eligible early held-out `CP` probabilities only. For late participants, apply each of the five early-fitted fold models without refitting or recalibration and average their predicted probabilities; use the frozen early q20/q80. No late value, outcome, band, calibration, tuning or missingness decision may affect an early-fitted model.

Require, before interpreting the primary interaction/transport claim:

- at least 100 target events overall;
- at least 200 eligible participants and 30 target events in each early-L, early-A, early-U, late-L, late-A and late-U cell;
- each late weighted ESS at least 200 and at least 50% of its unweighted cell size;

These are support requirements, not post hoc reasons to change the band or endpoint. If unmet, report the audit and classify the transport/interaction claim inconclusive.

## Transparent models and substantive nonlinear alternative

Fit these regularized multinomial models with identical participants, outcome, fold assignment, weighting conventions and held-out evaluation:

- `C`: age, sex, T1 calendar year, and eight current panel values;
- `I`: `C` plus verified spacing L;
- `CP`: `I` plus eight prior panel values;
- `CPW`: `CP` plus current waist; `CPG`: `CP` plus current left and right grip; `CPWG`: `CP` plus current waist and both grip fields;
- `CIWG`: `I` plus current waist and both current grip fields, used only for the proxy comparison.

The primary transparent increment is `CP` versus `CPWG`, with `CPW` and `CPG` as secondary family decompositions. The nested `C`→`I`→`CP` sequence prevents a cheap-measurement gain from being mislabeled as prior-panel information or repeat-spacing/selection information. Use standardized continuous inputs after training-fold-only transformations; retain left/right grip separately in primary models and define any combined-grip sensitivity in advance.

The matched substantive alternative is a small-grid gradient-boosted multinomial model on exactly the `CPWG` inputs, with a matched `CP` boosted model. Fix the grid before fitting (for example, shallow depth 2–3, a small learning-rate/regularization grid, fixed number of trees and early-stopping rule based only on inner training folds). The alternative can reveal nonlinear waist-by-current-panel, grip-by-waist, current/prior discordance, and threshold interactions that an additive model averages away. It is scientifically informative only if its shape summaries are stable across early folds, calibration is acceptable, and the same fixed early models behave coherently in late no-refit evaluation. A small uncalibrated score gain is not a scientific conclusion and cannot replace the transparent nested contrast.

A mechanistic body-composition or organ-reserve model is deferred. The local catalog has no raw imaging and does not certify analyte identities, assay units, specimen timing, or a validated latent organ construct; inventing such a mechanism would exceed the evidence. The deferred alternative could be revisited if field dictionaries, raw imaging or validated derived measures and timing become available.

## Interpretation and fail-closed criteria

Supportive evidence requires all age/sex, assessment-date, biomarker-identity/unit, specimen-timing, code/date, prior-record and ascertainment gates to pass; the event and six-cell support requirements; adequate overlap and weighted ESS; no leakage or pairing failure; and:

- `I_CP,early,A > delta` and `I_CP,late,A > delta`, each with a 95% lower bound above `delta`;
- `A_early,A <= delta` and `A_late,A <= delta`, with the upper 95% bound below `delta` (the retained gain is not materially smaller after adding the prior panel);
- the selection-weighted sensitivity does not reverse the conditional waist/grip direction;
- `tau_A` has a complete 95% interval inside [-0.002, 0.002];
- calibration does not breach the declared rule;
- the nonlinear alternative does not show unstable shape or a late no-refit failure.

This supports complementary conditional recorded-risk information in the measured paired-assessment population. It does not establish clinical incidence, biological mechanism, treatment benefit, an optimal threshold, causal monitoring value, or transport outside the observed support.

Adverse evidence is a nonpositive or practically negligible `I_CP,A`, a materially attenuated `I_CP` after adding the prior panel (`A > delta`), a gain attributable only to `C+I` spacing or selection, a direction reversal under weighting, permutation survival, calibration deterioration, fold instability, or early-to-late reversal. This weakens the case for retaining waist/grip for this recorded-risk task; it does not show that the measurements have no association or value in another population.

Inconclusive evidence includes any failed semantic/specimen-timing/ascertainment gate, sparse event or era-band support, non-overlap, unstable weights, nonidentifiable quantiles, code/date mismatch, incomplete follow-up, wide intervals crossing the practical margin, materially conflicting missingness/endpoint analyses, or a nonlinear shape that cannot be distinguished from noise. Inconclusive is not evidence against the hypothesis. If a semantic or specimen-timing gate fails, the solver must return a complete gate certificate and no fitted prospective conclusion. If only complete ascertainment fails, it may return the bounded recorded-category analysis as a secondary computational result, while classifying the five-year/clinical interpretation as inconclusive.

Computationally checkable claims are file integrity, selected-column availability, joins, gate counts, endpoint construction under the frozen code list, fold assignment, model fits, held-out predictions, scores, contrasts, calibration, bootstrap intervals, overlap/ESS, band assignments and permutation behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for biomarker meaning, specimen timing if not present in the local export, disease onset, complete ascertainment, action utilities, causal effects of measuring or acting, mechanisms, and external transport.
