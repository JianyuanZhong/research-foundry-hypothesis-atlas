# Recovery discordance at ICU discharge and cause-specific early failure

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe first-ICU-discharge cohort, estimate a prespecified paired respiratory/hemodynamic recovery phenotype, and fit two cause-specific prognostic models on that same cohort. Completion requires: (1) an auditable cohort/exclusion table; (2) a 24-row-per-stay feature table and coverage/missingness report; (3) locked test predictions for first ICU return and death as separate competing events; (4) phenotype-specific cumulative-incidence estimates, adjusted associations, calibration and uncertainty; and (5) a falsification/interpretation report tied to those files. No discovery result or readiness check is a clinical conclusion.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent established a useful but ambiguous question: whether generic residual instability in the last 24 hours before ICU outtime predicts early ICU return or death. “Residual instability” can mean at least two clinically different states: a patient still needing respiratory support, a patient with unresolved circulatory instability, or both. A composite also hides whether the signal would call for respiratory observation, hemodynamic observation, or simply reflects a high-risk patient who dies before a return.

This successor tests the prespecified hypothesis:

> Among adults at a valid first ICU-discharge landmark, patients with an isolated residual respiratory burden, isolated residual hemodynamic burden, or dual residual burden in the final 6 hours have different and higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU return or in-hospital death than patients with low/resolved burden in both domains, after adjustment for discharge context. The direction and size of risk will differ by event type, and the paired phenotype will discriminate early ICU return from death better than a single generic instability score.

A secondary information hypothesis is that a masked temporal model using the same 24-hour physiologic streams improves cause-specific calibration and cumulative-incidence estimation over the fixed-feature baseline because it can retain persistence, recovery, transient excursions, observation masks and cross-domain ordering. This is a scientific test of whether the proposed recovery construct captures information lost by a final snapshot, not a contest for a small AUROC gain.

What is already supported is narrower: the parent’s read-only diagnostic found 546,028 admissions and 94,458 ICU stays; 70,138 first-hospital-ICU stays had a linked admission, a valid ICU interval and dischtime after outtime; 3,872 had a subsequent ICU return within 48 hours, 856 had an exact-timestamp death in that window, and 4,630 met the parent composite. Those are feasibility counts, not evidence that either phenotype is associated with outcome. Feature coverage, exclusions, phenotype prevalence and uncertainty remain unknown until the solver computes them.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

ICU discharge is a disposition and monitoring decision. A patient with persistent oxygen/ventilatory burden but recovered blood pressure may need respiratory-focused observation; a patient with the opposite pattern may need hemodynamic surveillance. Treating both as one instability score cannot establish that distinction. Separating first ICU return from death also matters: a phenotype associated with return is not automatically the same phenotype associated with near-term mortality.

A supportive result would provide a reproducible prognostic phenotype for prospective review of discharge readiness and targeted monitoring. It would not show that delaying discharge, changing oxygen, or changing vasopressors improves outcomes. The advance over the parent is a clinically interpretable, cause-specific estimand—paired organ-support state at a fixed landmark—rather than a generic residual-risk score.

This is a bounded adaptation of expert seed 10, “Residual instability before ICU discharge” ([prior hypothesis]), and a child of the assessed valid parent [prior hypothesis]. It is not a reproduction of any demonstration. The inspected demonstration methods support dated longitudinal modeling, calibration, missingness/order ablations and explicit uncertainty. The cancer demonstration’s main paper and full STAR Methods were unavailable; only its supplement was inspected. The Bayesian demonstration’s available article and supplement concern longitudinal EHR/genetic discovery with unavailable genetic/multi-biobank dependencies. None establishes this MIMIC hypothesis.

## Population, landmark and temporal boundaries

Use MIMIC-IV 3.1. Include adults with hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18 (retain MIMIC’s top-coded age limitation for ages greater than 89), a valid linked admission, and the first ICU stay within each subject_id, hadm_id hospitalization ordered by icu/icustays.intime. Require non-null intime and outtime, outtime > intime, and outtime < admissions.dischtime. Exclude a stay if an earlier ICU stay in the same hospitalization ends after its intime, if the admission link is inconsistent, or if the 24-hour feature window cannot be represented. Retain patients who later die. Patient-level splitting is mandatory; no patient may occur in more than one partition.

The index is icu/icustays.outtime. For all predictors, use only icu/chartevents.charttime in [outtime - 24 hours, outtime). Do not use storetime, any chart at or after outtime, dod, discharge disposition, later notes, or events from a subsequent ICU stay as predictors. The final 6-hour phenotype window is [outtime - 6 hours, outtime); its comparison window is [outtime - 12 hours, outtime - 6 hours). The full temporal model uses 24 one-hour bins ending at outtime. A measurement exactly at outtime is excluded.

Follow every eligible stay for at most 48 hours after outtime and no later than the linked admissions.dischtime. The primary event is the first event after outtime:

1. ICU return: a subsequent icu/icustays row with the same subject_id and hadm_id, intime > outtime, and intime <= min(outtime + 48 hours, dischtime).
2. Death: a non-null admissions.deathtime with deathtime > outtime and within the same horizon, occurring before any ICU return. If exact timestamps tie, classify death first as the conservative prespecified rule.

Patients without an event are administratively censored at the earlier of 48 hours and dischtime; patients with a missing exact death time are not assigned an artificial event time. The primary estimand is 48-hour cumulative incidence for first ICU return and first death as competing events, with cause-specific hazard ratios as secondary associations. The parent composite (return or death) is retained as a secondary binary endpoint, not the primary scientific estimand. Secondary analyses are 7-day ICU return, in-hospital death before dischtime, and a sensitivity using hospital_expire_flag only as an outcome-status check without inventing a death time. MIMIC does not reliably distinguish planned from unplanned ICU return, so the outcome is “ICU return,” never “unplanned readmission.”

## Recovery phenotype

Resample each core numeric stream into one-hour bins over [outtime-24h, outtime), retaining the median and an observation mask. For MAP, preserve source (220181 versus 220052) and use non-invasive mean in the primary analysis; repeat with arterial mean only as a sensitivity and never average the two sources as identical. Convert Fahrenheit only if temperature is included; do not duplicate same-time Celsius/Fahrenheit observations. Do not impute normal values for absent measurements.
