# Repaired UKB seed 23: repeated CBC discordance and cancer-type-specific prediagnostic risk

Status: delivery-ready design conditional on the explicit administrative-follow-up check below. The bounded audit is observed; model results are not yet available.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The strongest inspected evidence supports two narrower claims. First, a population study found that haemoglobin trajectories were lower in future colorectal-cancer cases than matched primary-care controls roughly 20–30 months before diagnosis, even while values remained in the normal range, but it did not establish clinical benefit or distinguish colorectal from haematologic cancer [K1]. Second, a large primary-care preprint found that abnormal platelet counts were associated with one-year cancer incidence and reported colorectal-cancer diagnostic associations, but it did not test two-time-point CBC trajectories or the competing hematologic-cancer explanation [K2]. A small AML study shows that CBC-based machine learning can be predictive in a diagnostic setting, but its 510-record, single-setting design does not establish prospective risk prediction or external validity [K3].

The unresolved, clinically consequential question is whether a repeated CBC pattern can separate two actions that are otherwise conflated: gastrointestinal evaluation for occult colorectal disease versus hematologic evaluation for a marrow/lymphoid process. The key rival explanation is not a different classifier but timing and selection: a CBC signal may simply become abnormal close to any diagnosis, reflect infection/inflammation or testing intensity, or be distorted by the exceptionally complete participants who return for repeat assessment.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: in participants free of recorded target cancer at the second assessment, a prespecified discordance consisting of haemoglobin decline with RDW and platelet rise is associated more strongly with first recorded colorectal than first recorded hematologic-family cancer during the delayed-entry follow-up window. A marrow-discordance pattern consisting of haemoglobin decline with large absolute WBC/platelet changes is the competing pattern expected to be relatively more hematologic. This is a cancer-type-specific association hypothesis, not a claim of iron deficiency, clonal disease, mechanism, causality, or diagnostic utility.

Substantive advance over K1–K3: the study tests a same-person, two-time-point CBC change and its cancer-type interaction in one identifier namespace, separates immediate reverse-causation from a one-year-washout signal, and evaluates a transparent prespecified contrast against a nonlinear learned model on the same question. It can show that the apparent CBC signal is CRC-specific, hematologic-specific, generic, timing-driven, or too imprecise to resolve.

## Population, time, and outcomes

Source bindings, exact columns, code families, missingness, and observed feasibility counts are in [temporal-audit.md](temporal-audit.md). Use only `[UKB data file]`, table `ukb671626` Parquet metadata `table-5d49a6760e7ffdbd.json`, key `eid`, namespace `ukb671626`.

Let `T=53-1.0`, `X0` be the CBC vector at instance 0, and `X1` the same vector at instance 1. The analytic population is:

1. one row per `eid`, complete for `30000,30020,30070,30080` at both instances;
2. nonmissing `53-0.0` and `53-1.0`, with `53-1.0 > 53-0.0`;
3. no colorectal- or hematologic-family code/date on or before T;
4. for the primary delayed-entry analysis, no target-family event in [T,T+365 days);
5. `T+5 years <= A`, where `A` is computed once as the maximum parseable `40005-{i}.0` date in the frozen view. The report must state A and whether the registry snapshot justifies treating the absence of a recorded event as observed follow-up. If this cannot be justified, the study reports only the case-only target contrast as confirmatory and labels incidence estimates descriptive.

The primary exposure is the four-component change vector `X1-X0`. Within the training split only, standardize each change and define:

- CRC-like score `S_CRC = -z(Delta-Hb) + z(Delta-RDW) + z(Delta-platelet)`;
- marrow-discordance score `S_HEM = -z(Delta-Hb) + abs(z(Delta-WBC)) + abs(z(Delta-platelet))`.

The scores are summaries for prespecified interpretation; the full change vector remains in the model. A current-state baseline uses instance-1 CBC values. CRP `30710-0.0/1.0` is a sensitivity covariate only because it is missing for some repeat participants. Demographic adjustment uses `31-0.0`, `21003-0.0`, `21001-0.0`, and `20116-0.0` if their missingness and coding are verified before fitting.

Primary outcomes are first recorded post-landmark diagnoses, paired by cancer-slot index: colorectal prefixes C18/C19/C20; hematologic prefixes C81,C82,C83,C84,C85,C86,C88,C90,C91,C92,C93,C94,C95,C96. A target event at T or earlier is prevalent/leakage and excluded. The primary delayed-entry window is [T+365 days, min(T+5 years,A)]; 0–365 days is a reverse-causation sensitivity and two years is a second washout sensitivity. If both target families occur, earliest date wins; exact ties are reported. Other cancer codes are a negative-control outcome family using the same date/code pairing rules.

The primary estimand is the CRC-versus-hematologic contrast in recorded five-year event probability, expressed as the ratio of the `S_CRC` association with CRC versus its association with hematologic cancer, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. A cause-specific time-to-event analysis is secondary if a valid administrative censoring date is established. Case-only multinomial CRC-versus-hematologic odds is the confirmatory estimand when non-event follow-up cannot be defended.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative

The simple baseline answers the scientific question transparently:

- nested baseline 0: age/sex plus instance-1 CBC;
- primary linear baseline: age, sex, BMI, smoking, instance-1 CBC, the four CBC changes, and the two prespecified scores in a multinomial logistic model for colorectal / hematologic / no target event;
- fit and standardize in training only; use ridge regularization fixed before test inspection;
- report coefficient estimates, score contrasts, calibration, Brier score, multiclass log loss, one-vs-rest AUROC/AUPRC, and the held-out CRC-versus-hematologic odds contrast.

The substantive alternative is a CPU HistGradientBoosting multi-class model using exactly the same eligible participants, labels, and temporal windows, with current CBC, all four changes, both assessment dates as elapsed gap, CRP level/change when retained, and the same prespecified covariates. It can reveal nonlinear thresholds and interactions—e.g., whether platelet direction matters only after a haemoglobin fall or whether a WBC deviation is asymmetric—that the linear baseline loses. It is not justified by a small predictive gain alone; its scientific output is the held-out partial-dependence/conditional-risk pattern for the two target classes and whether that pattern agrees with the prespecified score direction.

Split once by participant and calendar order of T: earliest 70% for fitting, next 15% for tuning, latest 15% held out for confirmation. No outcome-derived random split, repeated test peeking, or row-level duplication is permitted. Imputation, scaling, score parameters, and model selection use fit data only. If either target has fewer than 30 held-out cases, report the model comparison as inconclusive rather than claiming validation. Use 2,000 fixed-seed participant bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions for uncertainty; report event counts and interval width, not only p-values.

Approximate future solver budget: CPU only, 4 CPUs, 16 GiB RAM, 20 minutes for cohort construction and both fits, plus up to 30 minutes for bootstrap uncertainty. The observed discovery scan used 2 CPUs/8 GiB and took 40 seconds. GPU is not scientifically required for 18,377 rows and two-time-point structured features; the learned alternative remains allowed but CPU is the proportionate choice. Future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 256 GiB, 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds; no GPU allocation is requested for this design.

## Required deliverable and evaluation

Completion requires newly fitted, held-out baseline and learned models and the following files:

- `cohort_counts.json`: exact filters, missingness, dates, event counts, A, and split counts;
- `baseline_estimates.json`: score coefficients/contrasts, class risks, calibration and uncertainty;
- `learned_estimates.json`: test predictions, performance, feature/interaction summaries and uncertainty;
- `test_predictions.parquet`: `eid`, T, true first-event class/date, observed-window flag, both model predictions;
- `falsification_report.md`: washout, reverse-causation, negative-control, missingness, sex/age, and registry-completeness checks;
- `interpretation.md`: supportive, adverse and inconclusive decision against the stated hypothesis.

Supportive evidence requires the same direction for the prespecified CRC-like contrast in held-out data, a clinically non-negligible and reasonably precise CRC-versus-hematologic contrast, persistence after the one-year washout and directionally after two years, and no comparable negative-control association. Agreement between baseline and learned conditional patterns strengthens descriptive regularity; it does not prove mechanism or causality.

Adverse evidence is reversal, attenuation restricted to the immediate window, disappearance after excluding early events, or replication in non-target cancers. This would favor occult-disease timing, generic inflammation, measurement selection, or code/test artifacts over a cancer-type-specific prediagnostic signature. A learned improvement without stable class-specific pattern is predictive utility only, not explanatory evidence.

Inconclusive evidence is wide intervals, sparse held-out target events, inability to establish A/follow-up completeness, or strong missingness/selection distortion. It should trigger expert review or another linked clinical dataset, not be reported as a definitive null. Clinical implementation would additionally require adjudicated symptoms, iron studies, repeat clinical CBCs, medication/infection context, blood smear/flow cytometry, histology, stage, referral pathways and external validation—all unavailable here.

## Alternatives considered and selection record

A fixed linear score-only analysis was retained as the interpretability anchor because K1 supports a temporal haemoglobin signal but does not establish that a joint CBC pattern discriminates disease type. A learned gradient-boosted multi-class model was retained as a substantive alternative because K3 demonstrates that CBC combinations can carry nonlinear diagnostic information, while its small single-setting design makes an internal, temporally held-out UKB test scientifically useful rather than a reproduction. A high-capacity sequence transformer was deferred: this source has only two CBC instances for the target analysis, and the extra capacity would not resolve the central confounding by diagnostic timing or unavailable clinical adjudication. Proteomic or multimodal alternatives were not selected because those inputs are not bound to this same `ukb671626` population; missing dependencies prevent that comparison, not the use of structured learning here.

Revisit the learned alternative only if held-out calibration fails because of clear nonlinear misfit, the baseline shows a stable but heterogeneous class pattern, and the same source fields remain available. Revisit the whole direction only with better cancer ascertainment, clinical context, or external validation.
