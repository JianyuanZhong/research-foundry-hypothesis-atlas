# Interval- and decision-stratified value of repeated UKB biomarker history

Parent: [prior hypothesis]  
Status: frozen successor proposal; no scientific model has been fitted and no clinical conclusion is claimed.

## Scientific question, clinical importance and advance

The parent asks whether a repeated eight-field blood-biomarker panel adds information beyond the current panel for a later recorded cardiorenal/metabolic event. The remaining clinical bottleneck is not another average discrimination estimate: it is unknown whether any history signal is concentrated in people for whom the current panel is ambiguous, and whether it is stable across the interval between assessments. These are the conditions under which retaining a prior result could plausibly change follow-up triage; a population-average gain can conceal no useful gain in the decision-relevant group or a gain caused by repeat-spacing selection.

The new question is:

> Among participants with a verified paired panel, does prior-panel history improve five-year prediction of a later qualifying recorded target specifically within the middle of the current-only risk distribution, and does that incremental value vary across short, medium and long assessment intervals?

The primary hypothesis is a prespecified interaction hypothesis: the history model M1 will have a more favorable held-out target-record Brier contrast and research decision-curve net benefit than the current-only M0 in the current-only middle-risk band (the fold-local 20th–80th percentile of M0 target risk), while the contrast will be smaller or absent in the lower and upper tails; the middle-band contrast will not reverse across assessment-interval regimes (0.25 < L <= 1, 1 < L <= 3, and 3 < L <= 5 years). This is a prognostic-information hypothesis, not a claim that repeat testing, monitoring, treatment, or a threshold improves outcomes.

This is a substantive advance over the parent’s marginal M1-versus-M0 comparison. It estimates where and when history could matter for a decision, tests whether annualized change is robust to irregular spacing, and can support a future selective-repeat study even if the overall average gain is small. It cannot establish an optimal retesting interval because the repeat assessment was not randomized and assessment dates are not proven specimen dates.

## Models and decision-stratified estimand

All arms use the same deterministic participant-level five-fold split, outcome definition, horizon, missingness policy and fold-local preprocessing. A fixed hash of `eid` must define folds and be published.

M0 is the transparent current-only baseline: the eight instance-1 fields, age, sex, T1 calendar year and L. Its held-out target probability is used to define current-only risk strata without using the history model.

M1 is the primary transparent history model: M0 plus the eight raw changes, with a prespecified annualized-change version as a sensitivity. The raw-change choice tests whether an apparent gain is a within-person difference rather than an artifact of dividing by a noisy, unverified assessment interval. Fit a common regularized linear/logistic discrete-time hazard family in prospective mode, or a regularized multinomial probability model in recorded-event mode. Scaling, imputation and regularization selection are fold-local.

M2 is the scientifically meaningful learned alternative: M1 inputs plus fold-local nonlinear current-level, raw-change and L terms and their prespecified current-by-change and change-by-L interactions, fit with gradient-boosted discrete-time hazards or a gradient-boosted multiclass model as appropriate. M2 can reveal threshold, discordant-current-versus-history and interval-dependent patterns that the additive M1 loses. It is secondary and cannot supersede M1 for a small predictive gain without coherent subgroup calibration and stable interaction results.

The primary stratified estimand is the held-out contrast in each outer-fold test set:

DeltaBS(g,s) = BS(g,s; M1) - BS(g,s; M0).

where (g) is current-only risk band and (s) is interval regime. For each outer fold, fit M0 on training data; derive the 20th and 80th percentiles from training-only cross-fitted M0 target-risk predictions; apply those cut points to held-out M0 predictions; and label lower-tail, middle-band and upper-tail participants. This prevents M1 or held-out outcomes from defining the strata. The principal heterogeneity contrast is DeltaBS_middle - DeltaBS_tails, with interval modification reported as the difference in DeltaBS across short/medium/long regimes. If subgroup counts are sparse, the result is inconclusive rather than pooled opportunistically.

Report overall and stratified Brier contrasts, calibration intercept/slope and calibration curves, discrimination as a secondary measure, target/death/category counts, and decision-curve net-benefit contrasts at 0.05 and 0.10. These are analysis thresholds, not validated action thresholds. Report cross-fitted reclassification from M0 to M1 in the middle band, including the fraction moved across each threshold and whether calibration improves. Report M2-versus-M1 only as a model-form diagnostic, not as evidence that a complex model is clinically superior.

Use participant-clustered bootstrap intervals over held-out predictions while preserving folds; for interaction contrasts, resample participants within strata with a predeclared minimum count. A failed prospective gate switches the primary report to recorded-event Brier/calibration and the same stratified contrasts, explicitly labeled recorded. No fitted result is implied by this proposal.

## Falsification and integrity checks

The solver must run:

- participant-level split and fold-membership audit, with no eid in multiple folds;
- header/schema/hash, key uniqueness, join-coverage and overlap-agreement checks;
- no post-T1 leakage audit;
- permutation of slope/change vectors within current-risk and interval strata while retaining current values, L, missingness and outcomes;
- permutation of outcome labels/dates;
- raw versus annualized change, interval-bound, same-day-tie and code-domain sensitivities;
- complete-case versus prespecified fold-local missingness handling;
- checks that breaking code/date positions or reassigning outcome dates cannot improve the primary contrast;
- calibration and fold-instability checks, not discrimination alone;
- subgroup support and positivity audit, including minimum counts before interaction interpretation.

For prospective mode, support requires Gates A and B, at least 100 target events, no integrity failure, a middle-band DeltaBS 95% upper bound below zero, a middle-versus-tail interaction compatible with the stated direction, positive net benefit at both analysis thresholds without material calibration degradation, and compatibility across raw/annualized, interval and domain sensitivities. Adverse evidence is no gain or worsening in the middle band, reversal across interval regimes, a confidence interval excluding any prespecified decision-relevant gain, or disappearance under permutation/integrity controls. Inconclusive evidence includes a failed gate, fewer than 100 target events, sparse strata, incomplete ascertainment, intervals spanning useful gain and harm, fold instability, or endpoint/missingness sensitivity changing the conclusion.

For recorded-event mode, support means only that M1 improves prediction of future recorded target categories in the middle current-risk band under this export and passes the same falsification controls. Adverse evidence favors M0 for that recorded-event task. Inconclusive evidence includes unavailable code semantics, sparse target/death categories or subgroups, unstable folds, failed integrity checks or conflicting timing representations. Neither mode supports a causal effect, disease onset, disease-free status, optimal repeat interval, monitoring benefit, treatment recommendation, mechanism or transportability to another population.
