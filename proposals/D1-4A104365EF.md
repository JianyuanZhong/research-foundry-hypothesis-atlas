# Early FiO2 de-escalation after invasive ventilation: a six-hour landmark study in eICU

## Clinically important unresolved hypothesis

Among adult ICU stays with invasive ventilation already present and a delivered FiO2 >=50% within the first 120 minutes after ICU admission, patients who remain invasively ventilated but have a contemporaneously charted FiO2 <=40% at a six-hour landmark will have a shorter subsequent time to successful liberation from invasive ventilation, without more post-landmark severe hypoxemia or ICU/hospital mortality, than otherwise comparable patients whose contemporaneous FiO2 remains >40%.

This tests an **observational six-hour treatment-state association**, not the claim that lowering FiO2 causes liberation. The causal clinical question—whether an active de-escalation protocol improves outcomes—is consequential because oxygen is ubiquitous and modifiable, hyperoxia is biologically concerning, and clinical outcome evidence does not establish a universally beneficial threshold. A robust multicenter association with acceptable safety could justify and parameterize a pragmatic trial; it cannot itself set an oxygen target.

## Evidence boundary and advance

The inspected eICU catalog establishes that respiratory settings, oxygen saturation, ventilation intervals, outcomes, severity variables, and hospital identifiers are available with ICU-relative offsets. The prior candidate's frozen literature source (Nadeau et al., European Respiratory Review 2026; PMID 42386311; PMCID PMC13320619) supports biological concern about hyperoxia and uncertainty in clinical mortality evidence, not benefit from FiO2 <=40%. The research-ambition demonstrations were inspected only as methodological exemplars: they emphasize temporal evaluation, calibration/validation, and explicit limitations; they provide no evidence for this oxygen threshold.

The unresolved claim is whether a timed, actionable early de-escalated state predicts faster liberation with no important safety penalty across heterogeneous hospitals. The substantive advance over admission-FiO2 prognostic studies is a prespecified six-hour landmark, a contemporaneous exposure definition, explicit ventilation and oxygenation corroboration, hospital-level transportability checks, and adverse/inconclusive as well as supportive interpretations.

## Computable Harbor experiment

### Unit, time zero, and population

* Unit: one `patientunitstayid`. For a person with multiple qualifying stays, retain the first qualifying stay by `uniquepid` as primary; include all stays with person-clustered uncertainty as sensitivity.
* ICU-relative time zero: offset 0. Exposure landmark: 360 minutes. Follow-up begins strictly after 360 minutes.
* Adults: parse `patient.age`; map `> 89` to 90 only for inclusion/adjustment and retain an indicator; exclude missing/unparseable age and age <18.
* Primary admissions: `unitstaytype='admit'`; require `unitdischargeoffset>360` and not dead/discharged before the landmark.
* Initial high FiO2: first valid numeric invasive-ventilation FiO2 in offsets [0,120] is >=50%.
* Invasive ventilation must be corroborated near both initial observation and landmark using a `respiratoryCare` ventilation interval/ETT or tracheostomy record; a prespecified sensitivity permits `respiratoryCharting` `RT Vent On/Off` plus `apacheApsVar.intubated=1` or `vent=1`.
* Require at least one valid FiO2 entry in [300,360] so exposure ascertainment is equally current in both groups. Exclude FiO2 outside 21–100 and never convert L/min oxygen to FiO2.
* Do not require absence of desaturation to enter the de-escalated group: that would condition exposure assignment on a post-setting consequence.
* Exclude documented comfort-only/EOL limitation saved before 360 minutes in primary analysis if a reproducible structured mapping is possible; otherwise report inability and perform sensitivity excluding any pre-landmark `carePlanEOL` entry. ECMO exclusion is attempted through prespecified `treatmentstring` mappings and otherwise stated as incomplete.

### Exposure

At the 360-minute landmark, take the last valid FiO2 setting in [300,360], breaking identical-offset ties by `respchartentryoffset` then `respchartid`:

* de-escalated state: FiO2 <=40%;
* comparator state: FiO2 >40%.

Require the FiO2 record to occur while invasive ventilation is corroborated. Report alternate thresholds (<=35%, <=45%, <=50%), windows [270,360] and [330,360], and no-carry-forward as sensitivity analyses. Continuous time-weighted FiO2 over 0–360 is secondary and uses a maximum 120-minute carry-forward, with chart-label provenance retained.

### Outcomes

1. **Primary:** time from minute 360 to successful liberation from invasive ventilation, defined from validated `respiratoryCare.ventendoffset`/subsequent intervals as the first end followed by >=48 hours alive without a new invasive interval. Death before liberation is a competing event. If interval validation fails prespecified completeness/logic checks, the primary is declared not computable rather than silently replaced.
2. **Corroborating duration:** `apachePatientResult.unabridgedactualventdays` and `actualventdays`, clearly labeled total recorded ventilator days rather than post-landmark ventilator-free days.
3. **Safety:** post-landmark severe hypoxemia (any `vitalPeriodic.sao2` or respiratory-charted SaO2 <88% sustained in two observations 5–30 minutes apart; alternate <85%); `actualicumortality`; `actualhospitalmortality`; and `patient.unitdischargestatus`.
4. **Secondary:** `unabridgedunitlos`, ICU discharge offset, and recurrent invasive ventilation. No claim of extubation failure is made without clinical adjudication.

### Estimand and analysis

Primary estimand: the adjusted difference in restricted mean time to successful liberation through ICU day 14 (with death handled as a competing event), and adjusted risk differences for severe hypoxemia and mortality, comparing the two observed six-hour FiO2 states in the overlap population. This is explicitly an association conditional on measured history.

Before seeing outcomes, freeze label harmonization and interval validation. Report a full flow diagram, exposure counts, hospital counts, missingness, charting density, and unadjusted outcomes. Estimate a propensity score for six-hour FiO2 state using only information available by the landmark: age, sex, ethnicity, admission source/type, diagnosis, weight, hospital, APACHE physiology, initial FiO2, pre-landmark FiO2 trajectory, PaO2/pH/PaCO2 when available, PEEP and tidal-volume fields, saturation burden, respiratory rate, MAP, vasopressor evidence, and baseline comorbidity. Because some trajectory variables can be consequences of earlier oxygen treatment, this model estimates a landmark-state association, not a baseline causal effect.

Use overlap weighting, standardized mean differences, effective sample size, propensity distributions, hospital-clustered bootstrap confidence intervals, and a Fine–Gray/cause-specific analysis plus restricted-mean-time analysis. Include hospital fixed effects where within-hospital exposure variation is sufficient; otherwise use random intercepts and state the assumption. Compare against (i) unadjusted, (ii) demographics/initial severity only, and (iii) APACHE-score-only baselines. Multiple imputation is permitted for baseline covariates; outcomes are never imputed. Prespecified heterogeneity is limited to synchronized initial PaO2/FiO2 severity and ARDS-like admission diagnosis, with interaction intervals and no underpowered subgroup claims.

An exploratory hourly clone–censor–weight analysis may emulate sustained FiO2 regimes, but it is not primary unless hourly measurement support and time-varying confounder positivity pass prespecified diagnostics.

### Falsification and robustness

* Balance must achieve absolute standardized differences <0.10 for core severity variables; failure is inconclusive.
* Positivity fails if either arm has effective sample size <200 overall, fewer than 10 adequately represented hospitals, or material propensity mass outside common support.
* Negative-control outcome: pre-landmark events cannot be outcomes of landmark state; model associations with pre-landmark saturation burden/discharge-selection indicators as bias diagnostics, not causal endpoints.
* Pseudo-landmarks at 3 and 9 hours must not produce effects explainable solely by survival/measurement selection; discordance triggers an inconclusive interpretation.
* Compare hospitals with high versus low FiO2 and saturation chart completeness, leave one hospital out, restrict to synchronized PaO2/FiO2, vary invasive-ventilation mapping, threshold, landmark window, and treatment-limitation handling.
* Quantify informative charting by number/timing of FiO2 and SaO2 observations. A similar apparent benefit when exposure is assigned from stale rather than contemporaneous measurements falsifies the proposed timing mechanism.
* Report E-values or quantitative bias analysis for an unmeasured severity confounder as sensitivity only; do not present it as elimination of confounding.

## Decision rules and evidence limits

* **Supportive:** faster adjusted liberation with a clinically meaningful effect and confidence interval excluding important mortality/hypoxemia harm, adequate balance/overlap, and consistent direction across hospital and measurement sensitivities. This supports trial prioritization only.
* **Adverse:** slower liberation, increased severe hypoxemia, or increased mortality robustly associated with the de-escalated state; this argues against indiscriminate protocolization and motivates severity-stratified study.
* **Inconclusive:** poor overlap, invalid/incomplete ventilation intervals, sparse contemporaneous settings, residual imbalance, wide intervals spanning meaningful benefit and harm, or contradictory sensitivity results. A null estimate with these defects is not evidence of equivalence.

Computationally checkable claims include cohort construction, timestamps, exposure/outcome mappings, counts, balance, overlap, model outputs, uncertainty, and sensitivity concordance. Whether a setting was truly delivered, why clinicians lowered oxygen, whether ARDS/extubation readiness was present, and whether de-escalation caused an outcome require chart adjudication, modalities absent from public eICU (continuous delivered settings, imaging, waveforms, clinician rationale), external replication, expert review, and ultimately a randomized/pragmatic study.
