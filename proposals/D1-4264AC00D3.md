## 1. Unresolved question and opening

Patients discharged alive from the ICU are frequently readmitted or die in hospital. Clinical
discharge decisions are made from the *current* state plus a gestalt of "looks unstable", but
ICU outcome models score the *mean level* of vitals (last values / early-warning scores).
It is unresolved whether pre-discharge physiological **instability** — variability and trend
of continuously-charted vital signs and a rising oxygen requirement in a fixed window before
ICU discharge — carries prognostic information **beyond the mean level of the same signals**.

Two explanations predict different observables:
- **H-instability (leading claim):** genuine residual physiologic dysregulation → variability/
  trend features add discrimination beyond level on a held-out split.
- **R-level/measurement (strongest rival):** apparent "instability" is fully explained by (a)
  the mean severity level at discharge and (b) observation/review intensity — sicker patients
  are charted and reviewed more often, and the number/timing of measurements is itself an
  outcome-correlated, value-free artifact. If a value-free **observation-frequency-only**
  control predicts outcome as well as the variability model, H-instability is not identified.

This is a falsifiable test, not another expression plot: the rival makes a distinct prediction
on a value-free negative-control exposure.

## 2. Hypothesis vs already-supported conclusions

Already supported (NOT re-tested): vital-sign *dynamics* are informative inside the ICU [K3];
dynamic variability tracks mortality in other settings/analytes [K2]; ICU-readmission models
are confounded and only moderately discriminative [K1]. None of these separates pre-discharge
**variability from mean level**, none controls **observation intensity**, and none targets the
**discharge decision**. The unresolved, falsifiable claim tested here is exactly that separation.

## 4. Estimand, baselines and the learned/mechanistic alternative

Same locked split for both; primary estimand = **incremental discrimination ΔAUROC (M1 − M0)**
with a prespecified **equivalence margin 0.01**, plus calibration (slope/intercept) and a
decision-curve net-benefit check at the observed readmission/death rate.

- **B0 (interpretable baseline):** penalized logistic regression on **snapshot level** features
  only — last pre-discharge MAP, HR, RR, SpO2, FiO2, temperature, vasopressor active, ventilation
  active, plus age/sex and ICU LOS. This is what a level-only discharge score sees.
- **B1 (primary instability model):** B0 **+** within-window variability/trend features per signal
  (SD, IQR, range, coefficient of variation, Theil–Sen slope, fraction of time outside a
  predeclared "unstable" band) and O2-requirement trend.
- **A1 (substantive learned alternative, the thing B0 can lose):** a small sequence model on the
  irregular 12-h multivariate series (GRU/1-D temporal conv with time-since-last-observation
  channels) trained on the same split. It can represent *order and coupling* of fluctuations that
  summary-variability features (B1) compress away. **Question the alternative answers:** does the
  temporal *structure* of residual instability add information beyond its scalar variability
  summary? CPU-only baseline first; the sequence model may use one GPU if it materially helps
  convergence (deferred decision; not required for the baseline).

**Measurement-selection negative control (value-free):** `M-obs` = {n chartevents/hour in window,
time since last measurement, fraction of window charts in last hour} — contains NO physiologic
values. Prediction of the rival R: M-obs alone predicts outcome, and (much of) B1's gain over B0
disappears once M-obs is included.
**Permutation control:** labels shuffled (S1) → AUROC ≈ 0.5.

## 5. Analysis and split

Temporal split by `patients.anchor_year_group` (train earliest groups, lock a later group as test);
no random split, to avoid leakage and to respect care-practice drift. Robustness: rerun on an
alternative split boundary; restrict to stays without any `225792` ventilation in-window (to test
whether instability == lingering ventilation). Report AUROC/AUPRC with bootstrap 95% CIs
(cluster bootstrap by `subject_id`).

## 6. Falsification criteria (prespecified)

- **Supportive of H-instability:** on the locked test split, ΔAUROC(B1 − B0) CI lower bound > 0
  AND point estimate > 0.01 (equivalence margin); M-obs adds ≤ 0.01 beyond B1; permutation ≈ 0.5.
- **Adverse (rival R supported):** ΔAUROC(B1 − B0) ≤ 0.01 (equivalence) OR M-obs alone carries
  the gain (its addition collapses B1's incremental value). An informative negative.
- **Inconclusive:** event count below the predeclared power gate (e.g. <150 primary events or
  <40 in the smallest split arm) or wide CIs crossing both 0 and 0.01 → honest inconclusive;
  must be reported, not spun.

## 7. Clinical importance & advance over inspected work

If variability/trend adds nothing beyond level + observation intensity, that is a directly useful
negative: pre-discharge "looks unstable" scores should use level and review intensity, not
variability, and variability-based early-warning additions are unwarranted. If it adds real
information, a physician-ready discharge-instability score could be iterated. This advances beyond
K1/K3 (no variability-vs-level and no observation-intensity control, and K3's target is within-ICU
deterioration, not the discharge boundary) and beyond K2 (electrolyte variability, no frequency
control).

## 8. Honest limits and required adjudication

- Single-centre retrospective; association/prognostic-information only, **no causal claim**.
- "Unplanned" readmission cannot be fully distinguished from planned return; needs **clinical
  adjudication** and may require the note text (deferred).
- Observation intensity is tested, not removed; residual confounding by discharge destination/
  care continuity (available via `admissions.discharge_location`) is possible.
- ICU-vs-ward measurement cadence differs; the 12-h window and M-obs are designed to expose, not
  hide, this.
- Power/cohort counts are an explicit **compile/runtime gate**; not yet observed (design stage).
