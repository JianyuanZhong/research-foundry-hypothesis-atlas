# Preoperative HCC MVI trajectories in an explicitly graded pathology target

Status: substantive ascertainment child of [prior hypothesis]. A new aggregate full-denominator audit was executed; no MVI outcome model was fitted. The clinical question, prediction cutoff, outcome, day −90:−1 feature window, temporal split, data bindings, and matched baseline/learned trajectory alternatives are preserved. The scientific repair is an explicit recorded-grade target population and a prohibition on transporting model performance to ungraded resections without additional pathology evidence.

## Question, supported evidence, unresolved claim, and advance

The clinical question remains whether longitudinal routine-care measurements available before first recorded HCC resection improve prediction of pathology-recorded M1/M2 microvascular invasion (MVI) over matched latest snapshots. A reproducible increment could inform surgical approach/margin discussions, neoadjuvant-study eligibility, and counseling; it cannot establish treatment benefit.

The strongest evidence-supported claims are narrower than the hypothesis. A static AFP/tumor-size/margin score achieved C-statistics near 0.81 in two Chinese validation cohorts, and MVI requires pathology [K1]. M0/M1/M2 have explicit histopathologic definitions, with M2 associated with recurrence and mortality in a selected retrospective resection series [K2]. Missing or selectively observed reference-standard outcomes can bias complete-case accuracy estimates; weighting and imputation depend on assumptions about outcome missingness that can be unverifiable [K3].

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adults with same-encounter pathology-confirmed HCC, one nonconflicting explicit M0/M1/M2 grade, no recorded procedure-defined treatment on days −180:−1, and strict numeric laboratories on at least two preoperative days, transparent day −90:−1 trajectory summaries will improve temporally held-out M1/M2-versus-M0 prediction over the latest snapshot. Any stronger learned-order claim additionally requires an ordinal sequence model to beat matched transparent value summaries and an unordered learned set model, and requires order destruction to attenuate its gain.

This is a recorded-grade estimand, not prediction for every resection candidate. The substantive advance over the parent is to audit the full eligible resection denominator and separate three populations: all first qualifying resections, same-encounter pathology-confirmed HCC resections, and explicitly graded HCC resections. The audit shows explicit grade recording is strongly template-era dependent, so weighting cannot identify transport to ungraded patients. The trajectory hypothesis remains unresolved.

## Executed full-denominator ascertainment audit

The read-only snapshot was scanned in 102.6 seconds on 4 CPUs/32 GiB; no row-level records or free text were emitted. The script and aggregate outputs are attached.

For 2014–2021, the broad denominator contains 12,574 adults at their earliest recorded dated qualifying liver resection with no recorded procedure-defined treatment on days −180:−1. Of these, 11,725 have same-encounter pathology, 11,405 have pathology text explicitly supporting HCC, and 849 have no linked pathology despite an HCC diagnosis row. Diagnosis has no timestamp and is audit-only.

Within the 11,405 pathology-HCC target:

- 9,084 (79.65%) have exactly one nonconflicting explicit M0/M1/M2 grade;
- 30 have conflicting explicit grades;
- 177 have generic MVI language or the grade heading without an extractable grade;
- 2,114 have HCC pathology but no explicit MVI-grade or generic-MVI text;
- 3,644 observed grades are M1/M2 (40.11% among graded cases);
- without assumptions, target-population M1/M2 prevalence is bounded only by 31.95%–52.30%; these prevalence bounds do not bound AUROC, calibration, or net benefit.

Grade availability among pathology-HCC resections changed sharply by year: 0.20% in 2014, 49.60% in 2015, 96.07% in 2016, 98.06% in 2017, 97.35% in 2018, 96.97% in 2019, 97.09% in 2020, and 50.30% in 2021. It was 78.04% in the 2014–2018 train era, 96.97% in 2019 validation, and 73.81% in the 2020–2021 test era. This is adverse evidence against stable ascertainment and unrestricted temporal transport.

Age and sex differences in grade availability were small in the aggregate, but service ranged from 78.20% to 86.73% among services with adequate counts. Availability was 80.73% with versus 78.58% without serial numeric labs, 80.85% with versus 70.63% without a lexical preoperative tumor report, and 80.57% with versus 65.10% without a size-like report. These are descriptive selection diagnostics, not causal explanations and not evidence that the missing outcome is conditionally random.

The reconstruction exactly reconciles the parent's all-year strict graded denominator (10,171) and serial cohort (5,204). The frozen primary periods contain 4,567 serial patients: 2,210/935 M1/M2 in 2014–2018, 992/463 in 2019, and 1,365/510 in 2020–2021. The remaining all-year serial records are report-only later-era data, including the 2024–2025 stress set; they cannot rescue primary failure.

A transparent grade-availability logistic model and a nonlinear histogram gradient-boosting alternative used year, age, sex, service, numeric-lab availability, serial-lab availability, preoperative-exam availability, lexical tumor-report availability, and size-like-report availability. Both were fit in 2014–2018 and evaluated without refitting in 2019 and 2020–2021. Neither transported (logistic AUROC 0.502/0.483; learned alternative 0.437/0.471), and the learned alternative was grossly miscalibrated after the template shift. A full-period observed-covariate propensity diagnostic produced a maximum stabilized weight of 63.0 and effective sample size 5,857 of 9,084 before clipping (8,307 after 1st/99th-percentile clipping). This is adverse to relying on IPW, not proof of no selection.

## Identification decision and selection repair

Primary inference is restricted to patients whose qualifying first resection encounter has pathology-confirmed HCC and exactly one explicit nonconflicting M0/M1/M2 grade. All claims must say “among explicitly graded cases.” This is a target-population restriction, not a claim that grading is random or representative.

The following are mandatory alongside every model result:

1. The full-denominator flow from qualifying resection through pathology link, pathology-HCC, explicit/nonconflicting grade, and serial-lab eligibility, overall and by year/split.
2. Standardized differences and grade-availability rates by age, sex, service, year, any/serial labs, preoperative exam, tumor-report availability, and size-like-report availability.
3. The transparent logistic and nonlinear selection diagnostics on the frozen temporal split, calibration, probability overlap, stabilized-weight distribution, clipping fraction, and effective sample size.
4. Unweighted primary outcome-model metrics and a secondary stabilized-IPW analysis standardized only over observed covariates among pathology-HCC resections. The weighted analysis is labeled assumption-dependent; it cannot upgrade the target.
5. Worst-case M1/M2 prevalence bounds by split. Extreme all-ungraded-M0 and all-ungraded-M1/M2 assignments may be shown as outcome-prevalence scenarios only, not as identified model-performance estimates.
6. Year-specific outcome-model metrics when support permits, with particular attention to 2014–2015 and 2021 template discontinuities. A 2016–2020 high-ascertainment sensitivity is secondary and cannot replace the locked 2020–2021 test.

Weighting becomes interpretable only if selection probabilities have overlap (validation/test minimum predicted probability ≥0.10, maximum ≤0.95; unclipped effective sample size ≥70% of selected N; <5% weights clipped at prespecified 1st/99th percentiles), weighted and unweighted conclusions agree, and expert review supports a plausibly ignorable recording process conditional on modeled variables. The executed max weight of 63 and temporal model failure mean these gates are not met now. Even if later met, unmeasured template, specimen, and sampling factors remain a limitation.

Expansion to all pathology-HCC resections requires blinded hepatopathology adjudication of a stratified sample of graded, generic-ungraded, no-MVI-text, conflicting, and no-linked-pathology encounters across 2014–2015, 2016–2020, and 2021. Review must determine whether absent grade means true M0, omitted template field, nonresection specimen, inadequate sampling, or failed linkage and must verify specimen/index correspondence. The current source lacks pathology issue time, specimen/block identity, block count, and sampling protocol; these cannot be repaired automatically.

## Frozen population, clocks, outcome, and split

Index remains the earliest recorded qualifying liver-resection start per patient. A qualifying procedure name contains liver and resection and excludes transplant/donor, biopsy/puncture, drainage, abscess/cyst, and isolated cholecystectomy. Exact duplicate procedure rows are collapsed. The pathology-HCC target requires pathology in the same (Patient Master Index, Encounter Number) encounter and HCC wording in Examination Findings or Examination Diagnosis. The analysis cohort additionally requires exactly one unique grade following “MVI indicates risk grade:” across HCC pathology rows.

Include age ≥18. Exclude missing procedure time, conflicting/no explicit grade from outcome-model fitting, an earlier recorded qualifying resection, and recorded embolization/intervention, ablation/radiofrequency/microwave, infusion/chemotherapy/radiotherapy, or targeted systemic treatment on days −180:−1. “No recorded prior treatment” is institutional-record limited.

Prediction cutoff remains start of calendar day −1. Inputs have event days −90:−1; surgery-day and later events are prohibited. Strict numeric labs on at least two distinct days are required for the outcome-model cohort. Exact duplicate labs are collapsed; same-label/time duplicates use the median only if all are strict numeric, otherwise they are ambiguous. Inequalities are not converted. Exact labels are not pooled because units/platform/reference ranges are absent. Training-era label/year robust scaling is frozen.

Primary outcome remains pathology-recorded M1/M2 versus M0; ordered grade is secondary and M2 versus M0/M1 exploratory. Pathology is outcome/selection audit data only and is never a feature. Split remains 2014–2018 train, 2019 validation, and 2020–2021 locked test; 2022–2023 is report-only and 2024–2025 drift stress only. Five-fold patient-level cross-fitting occurs within train; no patient crosses sets. All outcome models use the same 4,567 primary-period patients, event window, static inputs, and eligible report rules.

## Matched baseline and substantive learned alternatives

All dictionaries, transformations, penalties, hyperparameters, thresholds, and selection rules are learned in train and frozen through validation before test. Learned models use five fixed seeds.

S-value is penalized logistic regression on age, sex, year, service, the latest strict value and missing indicator for each exact label observed in at least 100 training patients, prespecified AFP, PIVKA-II, platelets, albumin, bilirubin, ALT, AST, INR/PT, GGT, ALP, WBC, hemoglobin, creatinine, and latest eligible report-derived size, multiplicity, irregular margin/capsule, and modality. S-full adds days since each latest value and total distinct measurement days. Prespecified continuous terms use splines; other labels use elastic net. Report the reduced AFP/size/margin benchmark [K1].

V is the transparent value-trajectory alternative on S-value's inputs and vocabulary. Per label add first/latest/minimum/maximum/median/range/IQR or MAD/SD, latest-minus-first, rank-block medians, Theil–Sen slope over observation rank, ordinal min/max positions, direction changes, and availability. Prespecified cross-marker interactions are AFP–PIVKA-II, ALT–AST, albumin–bilirubin, INR–platelets, GGT–ALP, and WBC–platelets.

F is the transparent full trajectory/timing alternative. It adds V plus per-label count, distinct days, span, recency, median/max gap, calendar day of first/latest/min/max, Theil–Sen slope per day, −90:−31/−30:−8/−7:−1 medians and changes, shared-day co-change summaries for the six pairs, and total event/day/panel and early/middle/late intensity. F can reveal interpretable trends, extrema, variability, synchronization, timing, and observation intensity that a snapshot loses, but timing gain may represent workflow rather than tumor biology.

D-bag is a permutation-invariant Deep Sets model over all exact-label/scaled-value tokens plus S-value static/report inputs, without date, gap, position, panel, or count tokens. It tests nonlinear cross-marker information without chronology.

T-value uses permutation-invariant within-day pooling followed by an ordinal day transformer (≤4 layers, width ≤128, ≤8 heads) plus S-value inputs. It sees day order but no absolute date, gap, panel size, or observation count. T-shuffle uses the same architecture with deterministic patient-level day-order permutations and 20 locked evaluation permutations. T-full adds day-to-index, interday gap, panel composition/size, and intensity plus S-full inputs. Timing-only and value–day reassignment controls are mandatory. Cap at 512 raw tokens before within-day pooling using a train-frozen deterministic day-stratified rule; report truncation by split/outcome.

The substantive learned models can reveal nonlinear unordered interactions, residual ordinal dependence, and timing/process dependence that V/F may lose. They do not identify mechanism. Multivariate GP/state-space latent factors remain deferred because sparse exact-label repetition and absent units make latent factors fragile; revisit only after stable transparent gain plus simulation recovery. Gradient boosting on F is secondary, not a substitute for the matched ladder. Images are unavailable, so radiomics is infeasible.

## Outcome-model estimands, metrics, and falsification

Report paired AUROC, AUPRC, log loss, Brier, calibration intercept/slope/in-the-large, and decision net benefit at risks 0.20/0.30/0.40/0.50 on identical locked-test patients with 2,000 paired patient bootstraps. Report sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV at a validation-selected 80% sensitivity and calibration by sex, age <65/≥65, tumor size ≤5/>5 cm, service, year, and ascertainment era when ≥20 events/cell.

Fixed comparisons are V−S-value; F−S-full; T-value−V and T-value−D-bag; T-value−T-shuffle plus frozen-model order destruction; and T-full−F/T-value plus timing-only and value–day reassignment controls.

Transparent value-trajectory support requires locked-test V versus S-value AUROC improvement ≥0.02 and Brier reduction ≥0.01, both paired intervals excluding zero, no material inferiority to S-full (lower AUROC bound >−0.01 and Brier increase <0.005), calibration slope 0.8–1.2, net-benefit gain at at least two adjacent thresholds without material harm elsewhere, and no direction reversal between 2019 and 2020–2021. F versus S-full uses the same gates for complete transparent longitudinal support.

A learned order-specific claim requires T-value to improve AUROC ≥0.02 and Brier ≥0.01 versus V with intervals excluding zero; improve both versus D-bag; beat T-shuffle; lose at least 50% of its Brier gain over V with material AUROC attenuation after order destruction; pass calibration/net-benefit gates; and avoid era reversal. T-full alone cannot satisfy a value/order claim.

Mandatory falsifications/sensitivities are day −7 cutoff, latest-day-only, report-free run, conservative rejection of grade/pathology/postoperative report language, exclusion of >1-day qualifying-start spans and 13 encounter-clock anomalies, any-prior-recorded-treatment exclusion, labs-only/report-only/demographics-and-observation-only controls, same-day panel collapse, exact-label restriction, leave-one-year-out fits, outcome permutation, automated feature-time leakage tests, and the parent’s blinded pathology/radiology reviews.

Supportive results establish only internal temporally held-out prediction among explicitly graded cases. If V or F supports but T-value fails, interpretable trajectories help and learned-order value is rejected. If D-bag matches T-value, unordered nonlinear interactions suffice. If only F/T-full improves, care timing/process predicts and tumor-biological interpretation is prohibited.

Adverse results include precise exclusion of V/F gains, V or D-bag/T-shuffle matching T-value, no attenuation after order destruction, gain confined to F/T-full, learned-model miscalibration, era/subgroup reversal, or dependence on 2014–2015/2021 template discontinuities. The executed ascertainment audit is already adverse to all-resection transport.

Inconclusive results include broad intervals, poor calibration, severe truncation, dependency/seed instability, failed adjudication, or gain confined to day −1/flagged reports. An imprecise null does not refute a biological relationship. No result here establishes causality, tumor biology, external transport, sampling adequacy, or treatment benefit.
