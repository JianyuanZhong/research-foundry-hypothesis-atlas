# Calibrated late-lactate decision value in a hospital-held-out EICU cohort

## Child audit and substantive change

Parent: [prior hypothesis].

The parent is a valid, result-bearing experiment, but its clinically important claim remains unresolved. In the exact 1,229-person/33-hospital cohort, raw R1 (late lactate added to R0) had a point delta-INB of +1.0097 per 100, while the copied-hospital refit 95% interval was -0.2906 to +2.0575 per 100. Thus the interval crossed both zero and the +1 materiality margin. At the same time, the R1 calibration slope was 0.58921, below the parent guardrail. The favorable point AUROC, Brier/log-loss changes, and positive site deletions cannot establish that raw probabilities support reliable review decisions.

The substantive child is a calibration-validity experiment. It keeps the parent predictors, cohort, outcome, landmark, thresholds, hospital-held-out validation, uncertainty principle, and noncausal boundary fixed. It adds one prespecified two-parameter calibration operation, learned without using an evaluation hospital's outcomes, and makes calibrated decision value—not raw score separation—the primary estimand. This addresses the failed guardrail without changing model complexity or searching for a favorable threshold.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is: in this selected EICU cohort, the locked raw R1 point estimate was favorable, but its copied-hospital uncertainty permits no incremental value and its absolute-risk scale was miscalibrated. The untested successor claim is: after leakage-safe calibration learned in development hospitals, late lactate yields at least one additional net true-positive-equivalent mortality-review decision per 100 people beyond a similarly calibrated R0, with acceptable calibration and site stability. This remains a predictive decision-value claim, not a causal or patient-benefit claim.

A recent public cardiac-arrest study illustrates the current evidence gap: Gao et al. (Emergency Medicine International, 2026, DOI 10.1155/emmi/3964486; PMCID PMC13478800) assessed serial lactate-related predictors using logistic/ROC analyses and reported internal and external discrimination. That is relevant prognostic context, but it is a different population and does not resolve calibrated incremental net benefit in early-pressor general ICU patients. The present child advances the question from association/discrimination to a preregistered, absolute-risk decision-value estimand.

## Hypothesis, population, and outcome

Primary hypothesis (H1): among adults with an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed entry, baseline lactate at least 2 mmol/L, and an eligible repeat lactate by the 24-hour landmark, the calibrated late-lactate model R1 has a hospital-held-out threshold-integrated net-benefit increment of at least +1.00 per 100 over calibrated R0.

The target population is exactly the parent population, with no source-row sampling:

- Snapshot: EICU [source checksum].
- Adults: age >=18; literal > 89 and numeric ages >89 map to 90, as in the parent.
- One ICU stay per uniquepid: lexicographically smallest (t0, patientunitstayid).
- t0: earliest qualifying norepinephrine/Levophed infusion entry with infusionoffset in ICU minutes [0, 1,440], using the parent's locked drug-name matcher.
- Landmark L24 = t0 + 1,440 minutes.
- Require known hospital discharge status and hospitaldischargeoffset >= L24.
- Baseline lactate B: valid numeric lactate 0.2--30 mmol/L in [t0-360, t0], with labresultrevisedoffset <= t0+60; select the latest valid collection and require B >= 2.
- Follow-up lactate F: the same validity rules in [t0+720, L24], with labresultrevisedoffset <= L24; select the collection nearest t0+1080, then the earlier collection, then smallest labid.
- At a patient/marker/collection offset, retain only the greatest revision; conflicting values at the greatest revision invalidate that collection, while identical ties collapse to the smallest labid.
- Preserve the parent's outcome-blind hospital richness rule: each historical X category must have at least five people in a hospital, where X = 1[(B-F)/B < 0.20]; X is not a predictor, stratifier, or outcome.
- Expected complete-source flow, a computability gate rather than a new analysis result: 200,859 patient rows; 200,234 adults; 16,299 early-pressor stays; 14,786 selected people; 14,672 known outcomes; 13,023 through L24; 5,077 valid baselines; 3,117 with B >= 2; 1,506 eligible repeat lactates; and 1,229 people in 33 outcome-blind hospitals, with 507 deaths and 722 survivors.

Outcome Y is hospital mortality, coded from the known hospitaldischargestatus at discharge. This is a binary prediction outcome observed after L24; no time-to-event, cause-of-death, or intervention outcome is claimed.

## Predictors and fixed baselines

R0 is exactly the parent's 166-predictor laboratory-plus-bedside-physiology model:

- age and age squared;
- Female and Male indicators, with Other/Unknown omitted;
- t0/1440 and its square;
- baseline lag, ln(B), and ln(B)^2;
- follow-up measurement age without F;
- six fixed summaries for each of the parent's 23 non-lactate markers: missingness, log1p count, last, recency, minimum, maximum;
- last, median, minimum, maximum, resolved-timestamp count, and missingness for each of heart rate, respiration, and SpO2 in [L24-360,L24].

R1 is exactly R0 plus only ln(F) and ln(F)^2. No hospital identifier, X, F/B, clearance, treatment, diagnosis, note, post-L24 measurement, or optional shape diagnostic enters either primary model. The source-resolution rules above apply to all lab features; vital values are collection-bounded to [L24-360,L24], resolved separately by stream at patient/timestamp, and restricted to heart rate 20--250/min, respiration 2--80/min, and SpO2 40--100%.

Within every training fit, retain the parent preprocessing and model specification: unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10,000, tol=1e-8, no class weighting, and seed 20260961. Continuous training features are winsorized at the training 1st/99th percentiles, standardized by training mean and population SD, and mean-imputed to zero after scaling. Held-out hospitals use only training-fold constants.

The baseline is not treat-all or treat-none. The primary contrast is calibrated R1 versus calibrated R0, both subjected to the same calibration procedure. Treat-all and treat-none net benefit are secondary descriptive comparators only.

## Three-way hospital-held-out calibration

The parent's failed slope guardrail is repaired by a fixed, two-parameter calibration layer that cannot see evaluation-hospital outcomes. To make this executable with 2,000 refits, assign the 33 hospitals to six fixed, outcome-blind folds before any outcome analysis. Sort hospitals by descending eligible-patient count and greedily assign each to the currently smallest fold, breaking ties by hospitalid. Use the same assignment in every analysis and bootstrap replicate.

For evaluation fold k, use the next fold cyclically as calibration hospitals and the remaining four folds as development hospitals. Rotate k through all six folds, so every patient is evaluated once, every hospital is absent from its evaluation model, and calibration and evaluation hospitals are disjoint. This is a three-way hospital cross-fit, not a patient split. It preserves hospital-held-out validation while avoiding indirect leakage from calibrating on predictions whose base models saw the evaluation hospital. It requires only 12 base fits and 6 calibration fits per complete pass, rather than millions of nested fits.

For each rotation:

1. Fit unchanged base R0 and R1 on development hospitals only, including all development-patient preprocessing constants.
2. Predict the separate calibration hospitals with those base models. Fit, separately for each model, the prespecified logistic recalibration logit(P(Y=1)) = a + b*logit(p_base) on calibration-hospital outcomes. This is an intercept and one slope only; no spline, isotonic fit, interaction, threshold, or model-selection step is allowed. Use finite probabilities clipped only for the logit calculation at 1e-6 and 1-1e-6. A failed finite two-parameter fit is a computability failure.
3. Apply the resulting (a,b) to the evaluation-fold base predictions. Evaluation-hospital labels are never used to fit its base model, preprocessing, or calibration.

Repeat all six rotations independently in every bootstrap replicate. Within each fixed fold, sample that fold's original hospitals with replacement, preserving the six nonempty role groups and treating copied rows as copies of their original hospital. Thus every evaluation fold remains hospital-held-out, calibration uncertainty is refit, and the bootstrap cannot fail merely because an unrestricted hospital resample omitted a role fold. The stratified cluster bootstrap estimates uncertainty conditional on the fixed, outcome-blind hospital-fold design and is reported as such.

## Estimand and analysis

For each fixed threshold p in {0.30, 0.31, ..., 0.50}, define NB(p) = TP/N - FP/N * p/(1-p), where a person is reviewed when the calibrated predicted hospital-mortality risk is at least p. The primary scalar estimand is delta-INB = 100 * integral from 0.30 to 0.50 of [NB_R1_cal(p) - NB_R0_cal(p)] dp / 0.20, computed by trapezoidal integration over the 21 prespecified thresholds. The +1.00 per 100 margin is retained from the parent and is not inferred from this experiment's results.

Primary uncertainty is a stratified copied-hospital refit bootstrap with 2,000 replicates: within each of the six fixed folds, sample that fold's original hospitals with replacement; copies are row copies, retain original hospital identity, refit all six development models, calibration fits, preprocessing, and predictions, then recompute the primary contrast. Set the bootstrap RNG to the parent seed 20260961 and record all sampled hospital indices. Do not use an IID patient bootstrap, unrestricted resampling that can empty a role fold, or a normal approximation.

Secondary, non-gating outputs are AUROC, Brier score, log loss, mean absolute calibrated R1-R0 probability change, threshold-specific delta-NB at all 21 thresholds, calibration intercept and slope for each model from pooled evaluation-hospital predictions, and fixed-prediction leave-one-hospital-out deletions. Raw parent R0/R1 metrics are reproduced as an audit comparison, never as a rescue for a failed calibrated primary gate.

## Computability and calibration gates

The experiment is computable only if all of the following pass:

- exact catalog snapshot and four source hashes/headers match;
- the full parent flow counts, cohort, 33 hospitals, outcome counts, and one-person/one-prediction rule match;
- all 23 parent non-lactate markers pass the parent's richness and resolution rules;
- all vital QC and coverage rules pass;
- six three-way rotations have nonempty development, calibration, and evaluation roles, and all role assignments are outcome-blind;
- all development model fits converge with finite preprocessing constants and predictions;
- every calibration fit has finite intercept and slope, and every evaluation prediction is finite;
- all 2,000 copied-hospital refits complete and are finite, or the prespecified failure count is reported and the experiment is labeled not computable rather than selectively repaired.

The calibration/usability gate is fixed before execution and is evaluated on pooled evaluation-hospital predictions. The calibration slope is b from the unpenalized logistic model with intercept and logit prediction as covariate. Calibration-in-the-large is estimated separately as the intercept from a logistic model with logit prediction as a fixed offset (slope fixed at 1):

- each of calibrated R0 and calibrated R1 must have calibration slope in [0.80, 1.25];
- each must have calibration-in-the-large (offset intercept) in [-0.05, +0.05];
- the lower-tail and upper-tail probability clipping count must be reported and cannot be used to alter the model;
- the 33 fixed-prediction site deletions must have delta-INB > -0.50 per 100, and no prespecified threshold-specific deletion may be <= -0.50 per 100.

These are usability/stability gates, not evidence of clinical safety. The bounds are fixed tolerances for this experiment; they may not be widened after results are seen.

## Ordered interpretation and falsification

Apply the following order exactly:

1. Not computable: any source, deterministic flow, feature/time, fold-role, convergence, finite-prediction, three-way-calibration, or bootstrap-completion gate fails. No scientific direction is assigned.
2. Supportive of the calibrated material hypothesis: all gates pass, the calibrated primary 95% interval lower bound is at least +1.00 per 100, and all stability requirements pass.
3. Adverse/opposite direction: all calibration and stability gates pass and the calibrated primary 95% interval upper bound is below 0. This falsifies even a nonnegative incremental review-value claim under the stated proxy.
4. Adverse to materiality: all calibration and stability gates pass, the upper bound is at least 0 but below +1.00 per 100. This falsifies the prespecified materiality claim while allowing a smaller or uncertain benefit.
5. Inconclusive: all other computable cases, including an interval crossing +1.00, a calibration-gate failure, a stability-gate failure, or a mixed pattern that prevents the ordered supportive/adverse conditions.

A point estimate above +1 cannot override a lower interval bound below +1. Raw-model improvements cannot override failed calibration. Threshold-specific or site-specific favorable results cannot replace the single integrated primary contrast. Conversely, a negative finding is informative only within this prespecified population and calibration procedure.

## Automatic versus clinical claims

Automatically checkable claims include source identity and hashes, table columns, joins, offsets, revision/tie resolution, cohort counts, feature inclusion/exclusion, leakage prevention, fixed six-fold role membership, model convergence, calibration equations, predictions, NB/delta-INB, proper scores, bootstrap interval, site deletions, gates, and the ordered label.

This experiment cannot establish that an infusion entry proves norepinephrine was administered, that a lactate was available or displayed to clinicians at L24, or that the retrospective database revision offset equals clinical result availability. It cannot establish that a review action is appropriate, that the +1/100 margin represents an elicited clinician utility, or that any action changes treatment, mortality, safety, length of stay, or patient benefit. It does not identify a causal effect of lactate measurement or treatment, and the same-database hospital split is not external validation. Calibration tolerances require clinical review before implementation, and bedside availability, utility weights, transportability, and patient impact require prospective synchronized measurement, independent external validation, clinician adjudication, and an impact study.

## What changed and what remains uncertain

Changed: the estimand now evaluates a calibrated, absolute-risk review decision; calibration uses disjoint hospital-held-out development/calibration/evaluation roles; calibration is a formal prerequisite for a supportive/adverse interpretation; and the stratified refit bootstrap propagates model and calibration uncertainty in every replicate.

Preserved: the parent's exact four-file EICU binding, 1,229-person population, t0/L24 landmark, baseline and repeat-lactate windows, R0/R1 feature schemas, fixed logistic model, 0.30--0.50 threshold grid, +1/100 margin, hospital-held-out structure, copied-hospital uncertainty principle, source read-only policy, and noncausal claim boundaries. The only changed validation detail is the explicitly justified six-fold three-way role design needed to make leakage-safe calibration executable.

Still uncertain: whether the available 33 hospitals provide enough information for a narrow calibrated interval; whether two-parameter calibration transfers to a truly new hospital; whether late lactate can be obtained and acted on without delay; and whether any modeled review value improves clinical outcomes. The successor is designed to make those uncertainties explicit and falsifiable, not to claim they have already been resolved.
