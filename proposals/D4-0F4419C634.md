title: "Do colorectal-cancer CBC trends transport to protocolized testing, or depend on clinically triggered blood tests?"

clinical_question: >-
  In cancer-free UK Biobank repeat-assessment participants, do prespecified adverse changes in
  haemoglobin, mean corpuscular volume (MCV), and platelet count add two-year colorectal-cancer
  risk information beyond age, sex, and the same biomarkers' landmark values? This is a
  transportability stress test of a signal already validated in clinically ordered primary-care
  tests, not another claim that longitudinal CBC prediction is novel. If protocolized measurements
  carry the signal, they may reflect occult biology; failure to transport would suggest that
  indication, test timing, or primary-care context contributes materially to published performance.

supported_vs_unresolved:
  strongest_current_support: >-
    The full text of the 2026 BLOODTRACC external validation (PMCID PMC13295383; DOI
    10.1186/s12885-026-16179-9) was inspected. It externally validated sex-specific joint models
    using age plus up to five years of clinically ordered haemoglobin, MCV, and platelet histories
    to estimate two-year CRC risk in more than 6.5 million primary-care patients, reporting
    c-statistics about 0.73-0.74 and calibration slopes 0.92-0.95. Thus, whether longitudinal FBC
    trends can predict CRC in primary care is no longer the unresolved claim. The inspected BLOTTED
    protocol (PMCID PMC9830700) also makes clear that selection for cancer investigation is the
    intended clinical use, but protocolized UKB repeat measurements differ fundamentally from
    indication-driven testing.
  unresolved_hypothesis: >-
    Among UKB participants with protocolized CBC measurement at assessment instances 0 and 1 and
    no CRC before the landmark, adverse annualized changes in haemoglobin, MCV, and platelets will
    be directionally associated with registry-recorded CRC within two years and will improve
    optimism-corrected prediction beyond landmark values. The competing adverse hypothesis is that
    the signal does not transport, implying published primary-care performance partly depends on
    clinically triggered testing or denser histories.
  estimand: >-
    Prognostic, not causal: cause-specific hazard ratios for three prespecified annualized changes,
    a joint likelihood-ratio test for their incremental association, and cross-validated changes in
    two-year discrimination, calibration, and referral-capacity event yield. No effect of FIT,
    colonoscopy, referral, screening, or treatment is estimated.
  substantive_advance: >-
    BLOODTRACC establishes external prediction in clinically ordered primary-care data. This study
    asks a different mechanistic/transportability question using protocolized repeat assessments:
    does within-person biology survive removal of test-indication and visit-frequency information?
    Either a positive or a well-estimated null result would refine where CBC-trend triage can be
    expected to work.

predictors_and_comparators:
  primary_changes: >-
    Annualized changes (instance 1 minus instance 0 divided by exact elapsed years) in haemoglobin,
    MCV, and platelet count. Directions are prespecified as lower haemoglobin, lower MCV, and higher
    platelets. Represent each continuously with one linear degree of freedom after training-fold
    standardization; no marker selection, categorization, or opaque average score.
  interpretable_transition_rule: >-
    Secondary rule: at least two of three adverse changes cross a prespecified magnitude of 0.5
    training-fold standard deviations over the observed interval. This is an interpretable
    distributional transition, not clinical anaemia/microcytosis/thrombocytosis. Clinical cutoffs
    may be added only after authoritative units and sex-specific reference ranges are frozen and
    reviewed; they remain secondary because UKB assessments are not diagnostic encounters.
  baseline_model: >-
    Restricted-linear landmark age, sex, and the three landmark values (haemoglobin, MCV, platelets).
    This deliberately mirrors the information content of the published age-plus-FBC model while
    keeping degrees of freedom compatible with the expected event count. A secondary clinical
    model may add landmark BMI and smoking only if coding is validated and event count supports it.
  trajectory_model: "Baseline model plus the three continuous annualized changes."
  rdw: "RDW current value and change are a separately prespecified exploratory extension, not selected by performance."

analysis:
  descriptive: >-
    Report the cohort flow, missingness, visit interval, marker distributions, event counts,
    two-year Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence with death competing, and CRC counts in each washout
    interval. Compare included repeat attenders with those having instance-0 data but no instance-1
    visit to quantify selection; do not inverse-weight unless predictors of repeat attendance are
    sufficiently observed and positivity is plausible.
  association: >-
    Fit Firth-penalized cause-specific Cox models (or ridge Cox if Firth survival software is not
    validated), starting at day 181. Report each direction-coded change HR per SD and a single
    3-df joint test, with bootstrap 95% CIs. Death censors the cause-specific model; a Fine-Gray
    subdistribution model and Aalen-Johansen risks are sensitivity analyses. Check proportional
    hazards graphically and with residual tests.
  prediction: >-
    Only if at least 30 events remain, compare baseline and trajectory models with identical repeated
    stratified 5-fold cross-validation; if any validation fold has fewer than five events, use
    leave-one-event-out or bootstrap optimism correction instead. All standardization and any
    imputation occur within training resamples. Use fixed ridge penalties selected inside training
    data and report paired changes in Uno C-index, two-year time-dependent AUC, integrated Brier

falsification_and_interpretation:
  supportive: >-
    The three changes have the prespecified joint direction, the 3-df incremental test excludes the
    null at two-sided 0.05, and resampled estimates show improved prediction/event concentration
    without materially worse Brier score or calibration. The strongest supported conclusion would
    be that a CBC-change signal exists even under protocolized measurement and merits external
    validation with clinically timed tests. It would not establish a referral threshold or benefit.
  adverse: >-
    Precisely null or opposite-direction joint effects, no improvement in resampled prediction, or
    worse calibration/event yield would be useful adverse evidence that BLOODTRACC-like performance
    does not transport to sparse protocolized repeats and may depend on indication, test density,
    or population context. It would not refute BLOODTRACC in primary care.
  inconclusive: >-
    Fewer than 30 events, confidence intervals spanning clinically important benefit and harm,
    unstable resamples, uncertain registry coverage, or unresolved field metadata makes the result
    inconclusive. Report the estimable bounds and do not escalate to a more flexible model.
