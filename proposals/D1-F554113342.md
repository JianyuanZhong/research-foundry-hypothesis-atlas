# Renal recovery discordance after apparent ICU-discharge normalization

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe adult first-ICU-discharge cohort; reproduce the parent’s support-context and competing-event features; derive a prespecified renal recovery trajectory from serum creatinine and urine-output observations; fit a transparent competing-event baseline and a duration-aware latent organ-recovery alternative; and produce locked patient-level held-out estimates.

Completion requires:

1. an auditable cohort, exclusions, temporal-integrity and renal-coverage table;
2. hourly cardiorespiratory/support features and two 12-hour renal-window summaries with explicit missingness and source provenance;
3. counts and uncertainty for renal recovery/discordance strata, including inadequate and contradictory documentation;
4. cause-specific and Aalen–Johansen cumulative-incidence estimates for first ICU return, in-hospital death, and alive hospital discharge at 6, 24 and 48 hours;
5. adjusted associations and held-out risks for the renal phenotype conditional on the parent support-buffer context;
6. calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier scores, bootstrap intervals and split-seed variability;
7. latent-state emission/transition diagnostics and a conclusion-to-output map; and
8. prespecified renal-source, observation-mask, timing, support-adjustment and discharge-process falsifications.

No readiness result, feasibility count or fitted result is a clinical conclusion.

## Unresolved claim and falsifiable hypothesis

The selected parent supports only a data-bound prognostic question: among apparently normalized ICU discharges, active/recently withdrawn support and the duration of a support-free buffer may carry different early risks when alive discharge is treated as a competing event. It does not establish that the support signal is the mechanism of failure, that a discharge should be delayed, or that a creatinine trajectory measures true GFR.

A clinically important remaining uncertainty is whether cardiorespiratory normalization can hide discordant renal recovery. A creatinine fall can reflect dilution or reduced creatinine generation rather than recovering filtration, while urine output is a different, imperfectly observed physiological signal. The available critical-illness study by Haines et al. found creatinine-based kidney-function estimates could overestimate measured function in a small prospective cohort, but it used cystatin C, iohexol clearance and muscle measurements that are unavailable here; it therefore motivates, not validates, this MIMIC question. The expert seed on discordant AKI recovery is likewise untested.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among adults alive at a valid first ICU-discharge landmark who have low final cardiorespiratory burden and adequate renal observation in both windows, a discordant renal trajectory—creatinine stable/falling while documented urine-output rate is stable/falling, or creatinine rising together with falling urine output—has a higher 48-hour cumulative incidence and cause-specific hazard of first ICU return or in-hospital death than concordant renal recovery, after adjustment for the parent support-buffer context and prespecified case mix. The primary contrast is the first discordance versus concordant improvement; the second pattern is a severity-positive control. The interaction hypothesis is that discordance is most informative among recent support withdrawal/short-buffer patients, not among patients with active support or clearly long stable off-support buffers.

This is a prognostic information hypothesis, not a treatment effect. Supportive evidence requires adequate positivity, reproducible direction across renal-source definitions and support strata, calibrated held-out incremental information over the parent baseline, and survival of mask/timing/discharge falsifications. Adverse evidence is a null or reversed contrast, dependence on one urine source or creatinine measurement, disappearance after observation-mask adjustment, or risk explained entirely by support-buffer strata. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse paired trajectories/events, non-positivity, contradictory timestamps or intervals, wide uncertainty crossing a prespecified clinical margin, or disagreement between urine sources. None justifies withholding ICU discharge, continuing support, or calling a creatinine rise harmless.

## Population, temporal boundary and outcomes

Use MIMIC-IV 3.1 adults with `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18` (retain the MIMIC >89 representation), the earliest `icu/icustays.intime` within each `subject_id,hadm_id`, and alive ICU discharge with `intime < outtime < admissions.dischtime` and no `admissions.deathtime <= outtime`. The landmark is `icustays.outtime`. No source timestamp at or after outtime may enter features.

Use parent support features in [outtime-12h,outtime), split into two six-hour halves. Use renal windows [outtime-24h,outtime-12h) and [outtime-12h,outtime), split into one-hour bins by `charttime`. For creatinine use the median valid numeric `valuenum` for item 220615 in each observed hour. For urine, sum valid `value` in mL per hour from the explicitly labelled urinary output items 226559 (Foley), 226560 (Void), 226561 (Condom Cath), 226563 (Suprapubic), 226564 (R Nephrostomy), 226565 (L Nephrostomy), and 226567 (Straight Cath). Exclude irrigant and perioperative output items (226566, 226627, 226631) and APACHE-derived urine scores; do not convert an absent row into zero. Record source identity and retain source-specific sensitivity analyses. Require at least two creatinine observations and at least 8 of 12 urine-observed hours in each renal half-window for the primary adequate-coverage analysis; retain lower-coverage stays as a separately reported unavailable/mixed stratum. A weight-normalized secondary analysis is allowed only if a weight field is verified before fitting; it is not assumed.

Define continuous changes as final-window minus preceding-window median creatinine and urine-output rate. Define the prespecified four-level renal state using the signs of these changes, with a small measurement-error deadband fixed from training-only repeatability/quantization diagnostics: concordant improvement (creatinine not rising and urine rate rising), creatinine-only improvement/urine non-improvement (primary discordance), urine-only improvement/creatinine non-improvement, and concordant worsening. If a defensible deadband cannot be estimated without leakage, use continuous changes as primary and report the four-level state descriptively; do not tune the clinical state on the test set. Flag renal-replacement-therapy evidence and perform an exclusion/stratified analysis using ultrafiltrate/hemodialysis chart items where available; urine output is not interpreted as filtration during treatment.

Follow to `min(outtime+48h, admissions.dischtime)`. First events are later ICU `intime > outtime` for the same hospitalization within 48h; death with `deathtime > outtime` before ICU return; or alive hospital discharge with `dischtime > outtime` before the other events. Ties use death before ICU return before discharge with a prespecified timestamp tolerance sensitivity. If no event occurs by 48h, censor administratively. Require non-null disposition for the competing-discharge analysis and report exclusions. Do not infer planned/unplanned return, bedside readiness, or post-discharge outcomes.

## Baseline, substantive alternative, split and uncertainty

The simple baseline is the parent’s cause-specific Cox competing-event analysis with support context/buffer, final and change-in-burden summaries, masks, age, gender, ICU LOS, careunit, admission type and prespecified admission covariates. Compare nested versions: parent baseline without renal features; baseline plus continuous creatinine/urine changes; and baseline plus the locked four-level renal state. Report cause-specific associations, Aalen–Johansen CIFs and absolute CIF differences at 6/24/48h for ICU return, death and alive discharge, with calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier scores and subject-level bootstrap intervals.

The substantive alternative is a constrained joint latent organ-recovery model fitted only on training data. Hourly emissions are the same creatinine, urine rate, support, physiology and observation masks. Persistent states represent cardiorespiratory recovery with concordant renal recovery, creatinine-only/urine-discordant recovery, urine-only/creatinine-discordant recovery, and global/support-dependent instability. Sparse transitions and competing discrete hazards are estimated over eight six-hour post-index bins; posterior state probabilities, rather than hard labels, enter outcome hazards. This can reveal whether persistence and order of renal versus support recovery explain risk beyond fixed summaries; it does not establish that the states are biological entities. Both methods use identical inputs, patient-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits, three fixed seeds, training-only scaling/deadband/parameters, and locked test predictions. Compare calibration, integrated Brier score and CIF separation with bootstrap uncertainty, not AUROC alone. Predeclare a clinically meaningful absolute-CIF margin if a defensible clinical source exists; otherwise report estimates and intervals without a binary clinical-benefit claim.

Discovery feasibility evidence is limited: an allocated CPU source diagnostic read the MIMIC archive and verified dictionary labels/items in under one second; the first full cohort scan failed on empty timestamps and was cancelled, so no cohort/event count from it is evidence. Future streaming extraction/Cox fitting is estimated, not measured, at <=16 CPUs, <64 GB RAM and roughly 1–4 hours; the latent model is unverified and budgeted up to 8 hours within the 28,800-second solver-planning envelope. No GPU is required for the tabular baseline; an A100 masked-sequence sensitivity is optional only if a bounded CPU latent fit demonstrates reproducible order information that cannot be represented compactly.

## Falsification, interpretation and evidence limits

Falsifications are locked before test evaluation: renal timestamp permutation within stay; charttime versus storetime; urine-source restricted analyses; output coverage and mask-only/mask-removed models; creatinine-only, urine-only and support-only ablations; support-context adjustment and interaction removal; dialysis-flag exclusion; 6/12/24-hour window perturbations; deadband perturbation; arterial versus NIBP MAP; procedure-removed support coding; and comparison of alive-discharge-as-competing-event versus censoring. A renal association driven by testing intensity, one source, interval artifacts, treatment-related urine recording, or discharge timing is not support for discordant recovery.

Supportive results would establish a reproducible prognostic association and incremental, calibrated held-out information for the renal discordance, especially within recent withdrawal, with robust source/mask sensitivity. Adverse results would weaken the proposed kidney-versus-support interpretation and favor the parent support-buffer explanation. Inconclusive results would motivate better urine coverage, a larger/external cohort, or clinician adjudication rather than a null clinical claim. The verifier can check cohort construction, temporal integrity, computed contrasts, uncertainty, calibration and whether conclusions match outputs. It cannot adjudicate true GFR, muscle mass, urine completeness, work of breathing, dialysis indication, clinician intent, treatment limitations, discharge rationale, planned versus unplanned ICU return, or whether any policy improves care.

MIMIC lacks cystatin C, measured GFR/iohexol, muscle mass, complete fluid/congestion status, reliable body-weight normalization for all stays, raw waveforms, imaging, post-discharge outcomes, staffing and bedside readiness. External validation, renal/critical-care expert review of the proxy phenotype, and prospective or causal study would be required before changing ICU-discharge or renal-monitoring policy.

## Demonstration and seed dispositions

The Delphi natural-history demonstration motivates ordered clinical sequences, but UKB trajectories, Danish external validation and the original training environment are unavailable; this is an EHR-only bounded adaptation. The ALADYNOULLI demonstration motivates latent longitudinal states, but genetics are absent; the joint latent model is an explicitly changed EHR-only adaptation. The Oncoformer demonstration’s main paper and full STAR Methods remain unavailable and images are absent; no multimodal reproduction is claimed and an image branch is deferred.

This proposal develops the MIMIC seed 03 question because creatinine and explicit urine-output item families are locally available, while downgrading its strongest biological language to “suspected discordant recovery.” Seed 08 (creatinine during decongestion) remains deferred because a validated congestion phenotype and treatment emulation are not available. Seeds 01–02 and 04–07, 09–10 remain deferred because they require, respectively, richer organ recovery adjudication, valid diuretic treatment emulation, microcirculatory/ischemia status, sedation depth, source-control or transfusion indication; seed 10 is the selected parent line rather than a new treatment claim. The Haines kidney paper is related physiological evidence, not validation of this MIMIC phenotype. All four configured dataset source catalogs remain available read-only; only this proposal and derived work files are writable.
