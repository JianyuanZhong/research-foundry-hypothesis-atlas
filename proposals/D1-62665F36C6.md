## Unresolved Question

Among ICU patients discharged alive, is the excess risk of hospital mortality associated with nighttime discharge (22:00–06:00) concentrated among patients with residual hemodynamic instability at the time of discharge?

**Leading claim:** Nighttime discharge is associated with higher hospital mortality, and this association is driven by patients who are still hemodynamically unstable (on vasopressors, with low blood pressure, or with high heart rate) at the time of discharge.

**Strongest rival:** The nighttime discharge–mortality association is uniform across stability strata, reflecting either (a) a measurement artifact (e.g., nighttime discharges are coded differently), (b) a staffing/monitoring effect that applies to all patients regardless of stability, or (c) no true association (the apparent effect is due to confounding by illness severity).

**What would distinguish them:** If the association is concentrated in the unstable stratum (interaction p < 0.05), the leading claim is supported. If the association is uniform or absent in the unstable stratum, the rival is favored.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Discharge timing is a modifiable decision.** If nighttime discharge of unstable patients is harmful, this directly informs ICU discharge protocols and staffing policies.
2. **Resource allocation.** Identifying the high-risk subgroup (unstable + nighttime) enables targeted interventions (e.g., step-down unit, extended monitoring).
3. **Policy relevance.** The eICU-CRD spans 208 hospitals; a consistent finding would support system-level guidelines.

## Evidence and Novelty

### Closest prior work (inspected):

**[K1] Shirakawa et al. 2026** (PMC13639130, Acute Med Surg)
- Hour-by-hour ICU *admission* timing and 24-h mortality in a single Japanese ICU (n=5,724)
- Found higher 24-h mortality in early morning admissions; no clear nighttime effect
- **Limitation:** Studies admission, not discharge. Single center with 24/7 intensivist coverage. Does not examine interaction with pre-event clinical status.
- **What this proposal adds:** Discharge timing (not admission), multi-hospital (208), and interaction with pre-discharge hemodynamic status.

**[K2] Jin et al. 2026** (PMID:41418669, Aust Crit Care)
- After-hours ICU discharge increased from ~10% to 15.6% after patient flow centre implementation (n=8,383, single Australian ICU)
- No increase in ICU readmission; lower hospital mortality in post-PFC group
- **Limitation:** Single center. Does not stratify by patient stability at discharge. The lower mortality may reflect overall system improvements, not the safety of after-hours discharge per se.
- **What this proposal adds:** Multi-hospital, stratification by hemodynamic stability, direct test of the interaction.

**[K3] Stephan et al. 2026** (PMC13218118, Ann Intensive Care)
- CRRT weaning in septic AKI: 52% of patients still on norepinephrine at weaning (median 0.22 μg/kg/min)
- Highlights that "liberation" decisions are often made under residual vasopressor dependency
- **Limitation:** Studies CRRT weaning, not ICU discharge. Single pathology (septic AKI). Does not examine time-of-day effects.
- **What this proposal adds:** Generalizes the "residual instability at liberation" concept to ICU discharge timing, across all diagnoses, in a multi-hospital cohort.

### Novelty
No inspected study has tested whether the nighttime ICU discharge–mortality association is modified by pre-discharge hemodynamic status in a multi-hospital cohort. The interaction test is the specific scientific contribution.

## Study Design

### Population
- All ICU stays in eICU-CRD with alive discharge (unitdischargestatus ≠ 'Deceased')
- Require: parseable unitdischargetime24, APACHE score available, ≥1 vital sign in the 24h before discharge
- Estimated eligible: ~100,000+ stays (based on 200,859 total × ~50% with 24h vital coverage)

### Exposure
- **Nighttime discharge:** unitdischargetime24 hour in [22, 23, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5] (i.e., 22:00–06:00)
- **Daytime discharge:** hour in [6, ..., 21] (i.e., 06:00–22:00)

### Effect Modifier (the key variable)
- **Residual hemodynamic instability** (composite, defined as ≥1 of):
  - On vasopressor in the 6h before discharge (infusionDrug: drugname contains 'norepinephrine', 'epinephrine', 'vasopressin', 'phenylephrine', 'dopamine')
  - Mean arterial pressure < 65 mmHg in the 6h before discharge (vitalPeriodic: systemicsystolic, systemicdiastolic → MAP = (SBP + 2×DBP)/3)
  - Heart rate > 110 bpm in the 6h before discharge (vitalPeriodic: heartrate)
- **Stable:** none of the above

### Outcome
- **Primary:** Hospital mortality (apachePatientResult.actualhospitalmortality)
- **Secondary:** ICU mortality (apachePatientResult.actualicumortality), 7-day hospital mortality (proxy: actualhospitalmortality with hospitaldischargeoffset ≤ 7 days)

### Analysis
1. **Descriptive:** Compare baseline characteristics (APACHE, age, gender, ICU LOS) between nighttime and daytime discharge, overall and by stability stratum.
2. **Primary model:** Multivariable logistic regression:
   - Outcome: hospital mortality
   - Predictors: nighttime discharge (binary), stability (binary), **nighttime × stability interaction**, APACHE score, age, gender, ICU LOS (log), hospital (random effect or fixed effect)
   - The interaction term is the primary estimand.
3. **Sensitivity analyses:**
   - Restrict to stays with ≥3 vital signs in 6h before discharge (reduce measurement noise)
   - Use 12h and 24h windows for instability definition
   - Exclude stays with ICU LOS < 24h (too short for meaningful instability assessment)
   - Hospital-level clustering (GEE or mixed model)

### Falsification Criteria
- **Supportive:** Nighttime × unstable interaction OR > 1.5 with 95% CI excluding 1.0, and the nighttime effect in the unstable stratum is at least 2× the effect in the stable stratum.
- **Adverse:** No significant interaction (p > 0.10), or the nighttime effect is larger in the stable stratum.
- **Inconclusive:** Interaction is significant but the absolute risk difference is < 1 percentage point (clinically trivial), or the eligible population is < 5,000 stays.

### Method Comparison
- **Baseline:** Unadjusted comparison of nighttime vs daytime mortality by stability stratum.
- **Primary model:** Multivariable logistic regression with interaction (as above).
- **Alternative:** Gradient-boosted trees with hospital as a feature, to check if the interaction is robust to non-linear effects.
- **Rationale for multivariable regression as primary:** The interaction term is directly interpretable, the sample size is large, and the effect is expected to be modest. Trees are used as a robustness check, not the primary analysis.

### Limitations
1. **Vital sign sparsity:** eICU vitalPeriodic is sparse; only 32.6% of stays have ≥1 vital in the last 6h. Mitigation: use 24h window, require ≥3 vitals for primary analysis.
2. **No staffing data:** Cannot distinguish "nighttime is worse because of fewer staff" from "nighttime is worse for unstable patients because they need more monitoring."
3. **Retrospective design:** Cannot establish causality. The interaction is an association, not a causal effect.
4. **Single outcome:** Hospital mortality is the primary outcome; 7-day mortality and ICU mortality are secondary.
5. **eICU-CRD limitations:** No continuous monitoring data; vital signs are periodic (every 4-8h typically).

### What Would Require Additional Data
- Staffing and bed-capacity data (not in eICU)
- Continuous hemodynamic monitoring (not in eICU)
- Readmission data (not in eICU)
- Clinical adjudication of "appropriate" vs "inappropriate" discharge timing
