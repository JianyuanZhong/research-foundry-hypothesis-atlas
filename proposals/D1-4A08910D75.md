# Schema repair note: discordant AKI recovery in MIMIC-IV

Status: schema/evidence repair only. No cohort counts, model fits, or clinical results are claimed.

## Question and claim boundary

Provisional hypothesis: among adults in their first MIMIC-IV ICU stay with a documented AKI episode and observation through a prespecified 48-hour landmark, a creatinine-improved but urine-output-impaired phenotype has worse subsequent in-hospital death and/or persistent or worsening kidney dysfunction than concordant creatinine-and-urine-output recovery. This is a prognostic association, not a causal or mechanistic claim.

The strongest rival is monitoring/measurement artifact: creatinine sampling is clinician-selected, urine output requires a catheter or bedside collection, and fluid balance is a treatment/monitoring summary. The experiment must include monitoring intensity and treatment proxies and must not infer tubular injury, fluid overload, or treatment benefit from association.

## Population, exposure, outcomes, and analysis

Population: adults in the first ICU stay per subject_id, with a documented creatinine-based AKI episode and sufficient pre-landmark urine-output monitoring. The exact AKI episode rule must be fixed before outcome analysis, using creatinine item IDs above and a prespecified baseline/reference window; do not infer AKI from a single post-landmark value.

Time origin: ICU intime. Primary landmark: 48 hours after intime. Exposure uses only data with clinical timestamps at or before the landmark: creatinine trajectory, urine-output category/trajectory, and secondary fluid-balance category. Exclude events after the landmark from exposure construction.

Primary comparison: creatinine-improved/urine-output-impaired versus concordant recovery. Secondary comparisons include creatinine-improved/urine-output-impaired versus creatinine-improved/urine-output-recovered, and prespecified monitoring-stratified analyses.

Outcomes: in-hospital death through admissions.dischtime/deathtime and a prespecified post-landmark kidney outcome (persistent/worsening creatinine and/or renal replacement therapy), with follow-up through hospital discharge or a fixed 7-day window and explicit competing-risk handling. The exact outcome thresholds and RRT item definition remain unresolved and must be fixed before fitting.

Simple baseline: prespecified logistic or cause-specific Cox model with age, sex, admission type, pre-landmark severity, creatinine value/trajectory, urine-output category, fluid-balance category, and monitoring intensity. It tests whether discordance adds interpretable prognostic information beyond creatinine recovery.

Substantive alternative: time-aware landmark model using ordered pre-landmark creatinine, urine-output, and fluid-balance trajectories (discrete-time survival or regularized recurrent/functional model), with the same patient split, outcome, calibration, and uncertainty evaluation. It can reveal whether sequence and slope matter; it cannot establish mechanism. Compare incremental discrimination, calibration, and bootstrap uncertainty against the baseline. CPU is appropriate for the baseline; the time-aware model may use CPU or one allocated GPU only if trajectory fitting is materially slower.

Supportive result: prespecified incremental association for discordance with uncertainty, persistence after monitoring-intensity adjustment, and no material reversal in fixed sensitivity analyses. Adverse result: no incremental association, disappearance after monitoring adjustment, or reversal. An imprecise null is inconclusive. Clinical adjudication of true AKI, recovery, fluid overload, and treatment indication is unavailable and would be required for stronger mechanistic or management claims.
