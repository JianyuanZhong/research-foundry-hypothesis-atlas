# Robustness of time-weighted hypotension burden for early AKI: summary choice, measurement-density and baseline-severity controls

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP), after controlling for measurement density, baseline illness severity, and after showing that the signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined)?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density. The strongest rival is that the apparent burden signal is an artifact of recording density, measurement timing, or baseline illness severity, or that it is specific to one arbitrary choice of blood-pressure summary. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

MAP is the common bedside summary and a natural baseline for hemodynamic risk. Minimum systolic blood pressure alone can overstate a single low reading, while MAP may understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU 2.0 adult ICU admissions with at least one systolic blood pressure measurement in the first 24 hours of ICU care. The primary population is patients with adequate systolic and diastolic measurements (at least 10 measurements in the first 24 hours). The outcome is AKI within 48 hours, defined by KDIGO criteria (creatinine increase or urine output criteria). The exposure window is the first 24 hours of ICU care. The outcome window is the 48 hours after ICU admission.

## Exposure and outcome definitions

- **Hypotension burden**: Time-weighted measure of hypotension in the first 24 hours. Define hypotension as systolic BP < 90 mmHg or MAP < 65 mmHg. The burden is the fraction of time with hypotension, weighted by the duration of each measurement interval.
- **MAP**: 24-hour mean arterial pressure, calculated as the mean of all MAP measurements in the first 24 hours.
- **AKI**: KDIGO criteria within 48 hours. Creatinine increase >= 2x baseline or >= 0.3 mg/dL increase within 48 hours, or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for >= 6 hours or < 0.3 mL/kg/h for >= 12 hours.

## Analysis plan

1. **Primary analysis**: Compare the burden model to the MAP model for AKI prediction within 48 hours. Adjust for measurement density, baseline illness severity (APACHE IV with SOFA fallback), vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, and hospital.
2. **Robustness analysis**: Compare the burden signal across MAP, systolic, diastolic, and combined blood-pressure summaries. The signal is robust if it is consistent across all four summaries.
3. **Severity conditioning**: Stratify by baseline illness severity (APACHE IV with SOFA fallback). The signal is robust if it is consistent across severity strata.
4. **Missing-severity sensitivity analysis**: Exclude patients with missing APACHE IV and SOFA scores. The signal is robust if it is consistent in the complete-case analysis.
5. **Hospital transportability analysis**: Fit the model within each hospital and compare the burden signal across hospitals. The signal is transportable if it is consistent across hospitals.

## Solver deliverable

The solver must fit the following models and report the following outputs:
- **Models**: Burden model, MAP model, and adjusted models (with measurement density, baseline severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, and hospital).
- **Outputs**: Model coefficients, AUC, calibration metrics (calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope), confidence intervals, and hospital-level analysis.
- **Uncertainty**: Report 95% confidence intervals for all effect estimates. Use bootstrap or Bayesian methods for uncertainty estimation.
- **Interpretation**: Report whether the burden signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary, measurement density, baseline severity, and hospital.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if:
- The burden signal disappears after adjustment for measurement density, baseline severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, and hospital.
- The burden signal is not robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined).
- The burden signal is not stable across hospitals.
- The burden signal is not consistent across severity strata.

## Limitations

- eICU 2.0 is a retrospective cohort study. Causal inference is limited by residual confounding and selection bias.
- APACHE IV and SOFA scores may be missing for some patients. The missing-severity sensitivity analysis addresses this limitation.
- Hospital-level analysis may be limited by the number of hospitals in the dataset.
- The study does not establish causality. A positive result would support further validation and prospective study.
