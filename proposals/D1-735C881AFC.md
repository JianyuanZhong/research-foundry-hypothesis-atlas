## 1. Hypothesis

### Supported claim (established by prior literature, not re-tested here)
Vital-sign variability and oxygen requirement during ICU stay are associated with adverse outcomes. [K1] Wang et al. demonstrated that heart-rate-derived variability measures (HR SD, HR RMSSD) calculated from routine ICU charted data in the first 24 hours are independently associated with in-hospital mortality (HR SD OR 1.855, 95% CI 1.211–2.841). [K2] Kalinoski et al. showed that persistent critical illness (ICU LOS ≥ 10 days) is associated with a threefold increase in mortality risk (HR 3.01, 95% CI 2.72–3.33). [K3] Orosz et al. demonstrated that post-ICU clinical deterioration (captured by MET calls) is strongly associated with ICU readmission (27.5% vs 2.2%, p < 0.001).

### Unresolved claim (this experiment tests)
**Patients with residual physiological instability in the last 24 hours before ICU discharge — defined as persistently elevated oxygen requirement (FiO2 ≥ 0.4, or SpO2 < 94% when FiO2 unavailable) or abnormal vital-sign variability (SD of MAP > 10 mmHg or SD of HR > 20 bpm) — have a higher risk of ICU readmission (same hospitalization) and in-hospital death, independent of baseline severity and discharge destination.**

This claim is unresolved because:
1. Prior studies have examined variability at ICU *admission* (first 24 h) [K1], not at ICU *discharge* (last 24 h).
2. No study has combined oxygen requirement and vital-sign variability into a unified "residual instability" phenotype at the discharge transition.
3. The association with ICU *readmission* (as opposed to mortality alone) has not been tested with a prespecified, data-derived instability indicator.

### What answering this question would change
If supported: a simple, computable phenotype from routine ICU monitoring could identify patients at elevated risk of post-discharge deterioration, informing monitoring intensity after ICU discharge and potentially reducing preventable ICU readmissions. If adverse (no association): residual instability in the last 24 h is either not independently prognostic beyond baseline severity, or the chosen thresholds/timing do not capture the relevant signal.

## 2. Study Population (unchanged from parent)

### Inclusion criteria
- Adult patients (`anchor_age` ≥ 18 at first encounter; `hosp/patients`)
- ICU length of stay ≥ 1.0 day (`icu/icustays.los`)
- At least one chartevent in the last 24 h of the ICU stay (data availability)
- Discharged alive from the ICU (`outtime` IS NOT NULL)
- **First ICU stay of the hospitalization only** (min `stay_id` per `subject_id`+`hadm_id`) — avoids double-counting re-entries

### Exclusion criteria
- ICU stay < 1.0 day
- No chartevent data in the last 24 h for HR, MAP or SpO2
- Non-first ICU stay of the hospitalization

### Temporal boundaries
- **Exposure window:** [outtime − 24 h, outtime] of the index ICU stay
- **Primary outcome (ICU readmission, Definition A):** any subsequent `icustay` with the same `subject_id` + `hadm_id` and `intime` > index `outtime`
- **Sensitivity outcome (Definition B):** any subsequent `icustay` with the same `subject_id`, different `hadm_id`, `intime` within 72 h of index `outtime`
- **Secondary outcome (in-hospital death):** `deathtime` IS NOT NULL OR `hospital_expire_flag` = 1 for the index `hadm_id`

## 4. Prespecified Residual Instability Phenotype (unchanged from parent)

In the **last 24 hours before ICU discharge** (`charttime` ∈ [outtime − 24 h, outtime]):

### Component 1: High Oxygen Requirement
- **Primary:** FiO2 (itemid 223835) ≥ 0.4 — ≥ 1 measurement with `valuenum` ≥ 0.40, OR mean FiO2 ≥ 0.40
- **Fallback (if no FiO2 in window):** SpO2 (itemid 220277) < 94% — ≥ 1 measurement with `valuenum` < 94

### Component 2: Vital-Sign Fluctuation
- **MAP variability:** SD of MAP (itemid 220181, fallback 220052) > 10 mmHg
- **HR variability:** SD of HR (itemid 220045) > 20 bpm

### Composite
**Residual instability = (High Oxygen Requirement) OR (MAP SD > 10) OR (HR SD > 20)**

### Sensitivity analyses
- FiO2 threshold: 0.30, 0.40, 0.50; MAP SD: 5, 10, 15 mmHg; HR SD: 10, 20, 30 bpm; window: 12 h, 24 h, 48 h

## 6. Analysis Plan (unchanged from parent)

### Baseline model
Multivariable logistic regression (readmission) / Cox PH (death):
- Age (per 10 years), Sex (male), ICU LOS (per day), Discharge destination (home vs non-home)

### Extended model
Baseline + **residual instability indicator** (binary 0/1)

### Model comparison
- Adjusted OR (95% CI) for residual instability
- Harrell C-index baseline vs extended; NRI and IDI; likelihood ratio test

### Method comparison: baseline vs substantive alternative
- **Baseline:** logistic regression with the binary instability indicator
- **Alternative:** gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost) with continuous features (FiO2 mean, FiO2 max, MAP SD, HR SD, HR mean, SpO2 min)
  - **What the alternative reveals:** whether the relationship is nonlinear/threshold-dependent, which component contributes most to discrimination, and whether component interactions (e.g., high FiO2 + high HR SD) are stronger than either alone. If the tree substantially outperforms the logistic model, the binary phenotype loses information and thresholds may need refinement; if similar, the simple indicator is clinically sufficient.

### Falsification criteria (unchanged)
- **Supportive:** adjusted OR ≥ 1.5, 95% CI excluding 1.0, AND ΔC-index ≥ 0.01 (or NRI > 0, p < 0.05)
- **Adverse:** adjusted OR < 1.2, 95% CI including 1.0, AND no incremental discrimination (ΔC-index < 0.005, NRI not significant)
- **Inconclusive:** < 100 patients with residual instability, OR < 50 outcome events, OR > 50% missing data in the exposure window

### Interpretation
- **Supportive:** residual instability is an independent prognostic marker for post-ICU-discharge deterioration; would justify prospective validation and integration into discharge readiness checklists.
- **Adverse:** residual instability in the last 24 h is not independently associated with readmission or death beyond baseline severity; the discharge decision may already account for these factors, or the signal occurs earlier in the ICU course.
- **Inconclusive:** insufficient events or data; would require a larger dataset or different time window.

### Step 2 — Cohort table (budget: 120 s)
DuckDB SQL over the extracted CSVs:
```sql
CREATE TABLE cohort AS
SELECT p.subject_id, p.gender, p.anchor_age,
       i.hadm_id, i.stay_id, i.intime, i.outtime, i.los,
       a.dischtime, a.deathtime, a.hospital_expire_flag, a.discharge_location
FROM icu_icustays i
JOIN hosp_patients p USING (subject_id)
JOIN hosp_admissions a USING (subject_id, hadm_id)
WHERE p.anchor_age >= 18
  AND i.los >= 1.0
  AND i.outtime IS NOT NULL
  AND i.stay_id = (SELECT MIN(stay_id) FROM icu_icustays i2
                   WHERE i2.subject_id = i.subject_id AND i2.hadm_id = i.hadm_id);
```
- Expected: ≈ 55–75 k rows (parent probe: 74,829 all-stays; first-stay-only reduces this).

### Step 3 — Last-24h chartevents prefilter (budget: 600 s; the dominant cost)
**Key reduction:** filter `chartevents` to the last-24h window of cohort stays BEFORE any aggregation, using a single SQL join on `stay_id` with the time predicate in the join condition:
```sql
CREATE TABLE last24 AS
SELECT c.stay_id, c.itemid, c.charttime, c.valuenum
FROM icu_chartevents c
JOIN cohort i ON c.stay_id = i.stay_id
WHERE c.itemid IN (220045, 220181, 220052, 223835, 220277)
  AND c.charttime >= i.outtime - INTERVAL 24 HOUR
  AND c.charttime <= i.outtime;
```
- Expected: ≈ 1–3 M rows (parent probe: 99.8% of stays have ≥ 1 of the 5 items in the window).
- **Fallback if > 600 s:** run the same query per 100 k `stay_id` chunks and append; or reduce to the 3 essential items (220045, 220181, 220277) and mark FiO2/ABP-MAP as missing.

### Step 4 — Phenotype features (budget: 120 s)
Per-stay aggregation in DuckDB (or pandas on the `last24` table):
- HR: count, mean, SD (itemid 220045)
- MAP: count, mean, SD (itemid 220181; fallback 220052 if no NBP rows)
- FiO2: count, mean, max (itemid 223835); verify value range is 0–1 (unit ambiguity documented in parent)
- SpO2: count, min (itemid 220277)
- **Residual instability flag:** (FiO2 max ≥ 0.4 OR FiO2 mean ≥ 0.4) OR (no FiO2 AND SpO2 min < 94) OR (MAP SD > 10) OR (HR SD > 20)
- Missing rule: a component is "missing" if its item has 0 rows in the window; the composite flag uses available components only, and a stay with < 2 available components is excluded from the primary analysis (recorded in the output).

### Step 5 — Outcome flags (budget: 60 s)
```sql
-- Definition A: same-hadm ICU readmission
CREATE TABLE outcomes AS
SELECT i.stay_id,
       CASE WHEN EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM icu_icustays i2
                         WHERE i2.subject_id = i.subject_id
                           AND i2.hadm_id = i.hadm_id
                           AND i2.intime > i.outtime) THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS readmit_A,
       CASE WHEN (a.deathtime IS NOT NULL OR a.hospital_expire_flag = 1) THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS ihd
FROM cohort i JOIN hosp_admissions a USING (subject_id, hadm_id);
```
- Definition B (cross-admission 72 h) computed as a sensitivity in the same step.

## 8. Limitations and Alternatives (unchanged from parent)

- **Selection bias:** the last-24h window conditions on survival to that point; ICU LOS is a covariate; sensitivity excludes stays > 14 days (PerCI).
- **Discharge destination confounding:** included as a covariate; stratified sensitivity.
- **Competing risk of death:** Fine-Gray primary; Cox sensitivity.
- **No external validation:** single-center; internal bootstrap/CV only.
- **Measurement:** FiO2 (223835) unitname is "None" in d_items; values assumed 0–1 fractions — verify with a range check in Step 4. HR SD is from irregularly spaced charted values, not continuous ECG.
- **Alternatives not chosen:** SOFA/APACHE scores (deferred; not computable for all patients in the last 24 h); eICU external validation (different itemid namespace; requires a separate crosswalk study).
