## Question (falsifiable)
Does MerTK inhibition (UNC106238 250 uM, last 2h of 4h LPS stimulation) of
monocyte-derived macrophages ATTENUATE the macrophage-driven inflammatory program in
co-cultured RA fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS), and is the effect consistent across
the 6 nested donor pairs?

## Cell-type reconciliation (critical, verified)
E-MTAB-8316 measures FLS, NOT macrophages. Verified from:
- repository.json: "Sequencing of synovial tissue fibroblasts from RA patients that were
  co-cultured with inflammatory macrophages."
- Protocol (idf.txt): CD14+ monocytes (6 healthy donors) -> M-CSF 3d -> macrophages;
  LPS 1 ng/ml 4h (last 2h + UNC106238 250 uM); macrophages WASHED; FLS from 4 RA patients
  in 0.4 um transwell; 48h co-culture; FLS lysed, RNA isolated.
- design.json cell_type="monocyte" is a METADATA MISLABEL (monocyte = donor identity of
  the macrophage pre-treatment arm); sequenced cells are FLS.
- Therefore this dataset measures the FLS response to macrophage-derived paracrine signals
  (soluble factors, no direct cell-cell contact due to 0.4 um transwell).

## Method
- Baseline: per-pair log2FC (UNC106238 - LPS) on CPM-normalized counts; paired t-test
  genome-wide (n=6 pairs), BH FDR; direction consistency (6/6 pairs same sign).
- Alternative (module-level): pre-specified FLS modules (inflammatory: IL6, TNF, CXCL8,
  S100A8/A9, NFKBIA, IL1B, CCL2, CXCL10, CXCL1, CXCL2; matrix-remodeling: MMP1/3/9/13/14,
  ADAMTS5/10, TIMP1, COL1A1/A2; metabolic: HIF1A, LDHA, SLC2A1, PKM, GAPDH, HK2) tested
  with paired t-test on module mean log2FC. Reveals WHICH program is modulated, which
  genome-wide FDR loses at n=6.
- Donor-dependence: per-FLS-patient consistency (both pairs of SA049/SA114 same direction?);
  leave-one-pair-out on module means.

## Classification: ADVERSE (hypothesis NOT supported; opposite direction observed)
- Main hypothesis (UNC106238 attenuates FLS inflammatory program): NOT supported.
- Observed: UNC106238 INCREASES the FLS inflammatory program in 6/6 pairs (module p=0.0084).
- Rival 2 (MerTK anti-inflammatory in this context): DEScriptively supported (6/6 increase).
- Rival 3 (off-target at 250 uM): CANNOT be excluded (no MerTK-specific control in dataset).
- Rival 4 (FLS-patient confound): PARTIALLY supported (high discordance in reused pairs).

## What the result does and does not establish
- DOES establish: (1) UNC106238 on LPS macrophages does NOT attenuate the FLS
  inflammatory program in this system; (2) the direction is opposite (increase, 6/6 pairs);
  (3) genome-wide FDR is null at n=6 (underpowered); (4) FLS-patient-specific effects are
  large (high discordance).
- DOES NOT establish: (1) that the effect is MerTK-specific (no genetic/pharmacological
  control); (2) a causal mechanism (paracrine, 48h, single time-point); (3) clinical
  relevance (ex vivo system, healthy monocytes, not RA patient macrophages); (4) that
  MerTK inhibition is harmful in vivo (could be a system artifact).

## Falsification criteria (pre-specified, evaluated)
- SUPPORTIVE: consistent attenuation (>=4/6 pairs) of inflammatory module, FDR<0.1 on
  >=1 module, effect consistent within FLS patients. NOT MET (opposite direction).
- ADVERSE: no module-specific effect; uniform genome-wide change; or INCREASE of
  inflammatory program. MET (inflammatory module INCREASES 6/6, p=0.0084).
- INCONCLUSIVE: n=6 underpowered (genome-wide FDR null); FLS-patient confound dominates.
  ALSO APPLIES (genome-wide 0 FDR; high discordance).
- FINAL: ADVERSE (opposite direction) with INCONCLUSIVE caveats (off-target, FLS confound).

## Clinical importance
MerTK is an active therapeutic target for RA (pro-resolving macrophage axis; De Lima 2023
identified TAM/MERTK receptors in synovial niche restoration [K2]; Wang 2026 showed MerTK
signaling suppresses NF-kB/MAPK in macrophages [K1]). If MerTK inhibition INCREASES FLS
activation (as observed here), this BOUNDS the therapeutic claim: MerTK-targeted therapy
may not reduce FLS-driven inflammation via macrophage paracrine signaling in this system,
and could theoretically worsen it. This is a useful negative finding for target
prioritization: it argues for (a) MerTK-specific controls before clinical translation,
(b) macrophage-intrinsic readouts (not just FLS), and (c) patient-specific FLS
sensitivity as a stratification factor [K3].

## Competing explanations
1. MerTK signaling in LPS macrophages is ANTI-inflammatory (suppresses FLS activation);
   blocking it removes the brake -> more FLS inflammation. (Supported by 6/6 direction.)
2. Off-target: UNC106238 at 250 uM has non-MerTK effects on macrophages. (Cannot be
   excluded; no control in dataset.)
3. FLS-patient-specific effects dominate (2 FLS reused across monocyte donors). (Partially
   supported: high discordance.)
4. Transwell paracrine artifact: 0.4 um pore, 48h, single time-point; FLS may adapt.
5. Healthy monocytes, not RA patient macrophages: may not represent the disease state.

## Method alternatives (recorded)
- Baseline (used): per-pair log2FC + paired t-test genome-wide + module-level tests.
  Appropriate for n=6; transparent; donor-aware.
- Alternative (deferred): DESeq2/edgeR negative-binomial model on raw counts. Heavier;
  unlikely to change the module-level conclusion (direction 6/6 is robust to normalization).
- Alternative (deferred): Macrophage-intrinsic readout (sequence macrophages, not FLS).
  Would separate macrophage MerTK effect from FLS response. Requires new experiment
  (not in dataset).
- Alternative (deferred): MerTK-specific control (MERTK knockdown, second inhibitor,
  dose-response). Requires new experiment.

## Honest limitations
1. **No MerTK-specific control**: UNC106238 at 250 uM may have off-target effects;
   cannot attribute the effect specifically to MerTK inhibition.
2. **n=6 pairs**: genome-wide FDR is null (underpowered); module-level p=0.0084 is
   nominal (no FDR correction for 3 modules).
3. **FLS-patient confound**: 2 FLS patients reused across monocyte donors; high
   discordance in reused pairs; FLS-specific effects may dominate.
4. **Healthy monocytes**: not RA patient macrophages; may not represent the disease state.
5. **Paracrine only**: 0.4 um transwell, no direct cell-cell contact; effect is
   soluble-factor mediated.
6. **Single time-point**: 48h co-culture; FLS may adapt; no time-course.
7. **No wet-lab validation**: transcript response does not establish a mechanism.
8. **Cross-sectional**: no causal/temporal claim.

## Residual (unresolved) question
Whether the observed INCREASE in FLS inflammatory program is MerTK-specific (vs
off-target) and whether it replicates with a MerTK-specific perturbation (knockdown,
second inhibitor, dose-response). The current dataset cannot answer this.

## Next test
1. MerTK-specific control: MERTK knockdown in macrophages, or a second MerTK inhibitor,
   or dose-response of UNC106238 (10/50/250 uM) to assess specificity.
2. Macrophage-intrinsic readout: sequence macrophages (not FLS) to separate macrophage
   MerTK effect from FLS response.
3. More monocyte donors (n>=12 pairs) for genome-wide FDR power.
4. RA patient macrophages (not healthy) to represent the disease state.
