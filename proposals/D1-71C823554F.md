# When creatinine is moving, does a kinetic clearance estimate predict renal drug elimination better than the steady-state equations used to dose drugs?

A prespecified, stratified, large-scale test — and error decomposition — of kinetic eGFR
against measured serum vancomycin concentrations in MIMIC-IV 3.1.

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]` (expert seed 03, "Discordant recovery after
acute kidney injury"). Branch A. Supporting files: `bindings.md`,
`method-comparison.md`, `key-references.json`, `refs/`.

**Status marker.** Everything in sections 3 (bindings/cohort) is **observed** — computed
in this workspace in `[research job]`. Everything in sections 5-8
(analysis, falsification, interpretation) is **planned**. No estimator has been fitted
and no prediction error has been computed. A planned analysis is not a result.

---

## 1. Why the seed's original anchor had to be replaced, and what replaces it

Expert seed 03 argues that serum creatinine misrepresents kidney recovery in critical
illness, and its expert reference resolves this with cystatin C and measured GFR.
**That anchor is unavailable here: `hosp/d_labitems` in MIMIC-IV 3.1 contains no cystatin
C item at all** (case-insensitive scan, zero rows; independently reproduced). Timed
urinary creatinine clearance does exist (itemid 51080) but with **717 rows in the entire
database**, which cannot carry a reference standard.

So the seed's question needs a different objective readout of kidney function — one that
is physiologic rather than a second creatinine-based formula. This proposal uses
**renal drug elimination measured directly in the patient**: therapeutic drug monitoring
concentrations of intravenous vancomycin, a drug cleared ~80-90% unchanged by glomerular
filtration and dosed at the bedside from a creatinine-based clearance estimate. The
measured concentration is not an estimate of kidney function; it is a consequence of it.
This keeps the seed's scientific concern (creatinine misrepresents renal function when it
is not at steady state) while anchoring it to an outcome that is independently measured,
clinically consequential, and abundant in this source (**119,231 vancomycin levels**).

It also makes the question decision-linked in a way the seed's original framing was not:
the steady-state equations under test are the ones actually used to pick a milligram dose.

## 2. The scientific opening, stated honestly

### 2.1 The strongest claim the existing evidence already supports

1. **Steady-state eGFR equations are mathematically mis-specified when creatinine is
   changing.** This is not in dispute. Cockcroft-Gault, MDRD and CKD-EPI all invert a
   single creatinine under the assumption that production equals excretion. [K1] states
   the premise explicitly — current equations "do not work if the plasma creatinine is
   changing rapidly" — and derives the kinetic estimate (KeGFR) from creatinine mass
   balance: initial creatinine content, volume of distribution, production rate, the
   measured change between consecutive creatinines, and the maximum anuric rise per day.
   Note the limit of what [K1] establishes: the inspected abstract presents a derivation
   and illustrative AKI/recovery scenarios, **not** a validation against measured GFR or
   against drug clearance.
2. **Correcting the estimator for creatinine kinetics has already been tried against
   vancomycin concentrations, and it failed.** [K2] (Harada 2018) compared KeGFR with
   three established eGFR formulae for predicting serum vancomycin concentrations in 122
   critically ill adults and reported KeGFR with the **highest** root-mean-squared
   prediction error of the four (6.67 ug/mL; mean error 0.81, mean absolute error 4.46
   ug/mL), concluding that "the predictability of KeGFR formula did not show good
   accuracy in critically ill patients."
3. **A large component of renal drug-clearance variation in the ICU is invisible to any
   creatinine-based estimator.** [K3] (Udy 2014) measured 8-hour urinary creatinine
   clearance daily in 281 ICU patients with **normal** plasma creatinine (<120 umol/L)
   and found clearance >= 130 mL/min/1.73 m2 on at least one occasion in **65.1%**, with
   74% of those sustaining it on >= half of their measures, and day-1 augmented clearance
   predicting sustained elevation through the first ICU week (p = 0.019).

Taken together, (1)-(3) are already a coherent story: the kinetic correction is
*algebraically* right and *empirically* unhelpful, plausibly because the dominant error
is elsewhere. **This proposal does not assert that kinetic GFR predicts drug clearance
better.** Asserting that would contradict the only directly relevant work I inspected.

### 2.2 The unresolved claim this experiment tests

[K2] is the closest prior work and it is also, on inspection, a weak test of the specific
mechanism:

- **It did not condition on the thing the mechanism requires.** KeGFR and a steady-state
  equation converge to the same number as the creatinine delta goes to zero; the kinetic
  term only does work when creatinine is moving. [K2]'s abstract reports no restriction to
  moving-creatinine patients and no creatinine-slope distribution. In this source the
  median ICU vancomycin level is drawn at a **creatinine slope of exactly 0.000
  mg/dL/24 h** (observed, n = 14,670). An unselected cohort is therefore mostly a
  comparison of an estimator against itself, and a null there is only weakly informative
  about the stratum where the estimators actually disagree.
- **n = 122 patients, with no reported interval on the error differences.** An imprecise
  null is not a refutation.
- **The prediction pipeline was proprietary.** Concentrations were "calculated using
  software provided by the manufacturer of vancomycin"; the reported error is a property
  of (formula + unspecified software), not of the formula.
- **It reported only ME/MAE/RMSE.** It did not decompose the residual into clearance
  misspecification versus volume of distribution / fluid balance versus sampling-time
  error versus irreducible between-patient variation — which is the quantity that would
  explain *why* the kinetic correction does not help.

**Unresolved claim (H1, directional and stratum-specific).** Among ICU vancomycin levels
drawn while serum creatinine is demonstrably *not* at steady state, the steady-state
estimators carry a **signed, slope-direction-dependent bias** in predicted vancomycin
concentration: they over-state clearance (and therefore under-predict the measured
concentration) while creatinine is rising, and under-state clearance (over-predict the
concentration) while creatinine is falling. Replacing the steady-state clearance with
the kinetic estimate removes that signed bias and reduces absolute prediction error.

**Unresolved claim (H2, the quantity that makes a null informative).** The residual
variance in measured vancomycin concentration not explained by the clearance estimator is
dominated by volume-of-distribution / fluid-balance misspecification and by
patient-specific clearance deviation (the augmented-clearance phenomenon of [K3]) rather
than by creatinine non-steady-state. H2 is testable whether or not H1 holds, and if H1
fails, H2 says what *would* have to be fixed instead.

### 2.3 Why the signed-bias prediction, and not just the error reduction, is the discriminating test

The four rival explanations below all predict that prediction error is *larger* in
unstable patients. Only the creatinine-kinetics mechanism predicts that the **sign** of
the error flips with the **direction** of the creatinine trajectory, with magnitude
scaling in the slope, after conditioning on severity, dose, weight, fluid balance and
hours-since-dose. That interaction is the falsifiable core.

| rival | mechanism | its prediction | how it is separated |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(i) Vd / fluid overload and augmented renal clearance** [K3] | error driven by distribution volume and by true clearance variation uncorrelated with creatinine | error inflated in both slope directions; no sign flip; error scales with cumulative fluid balance and with low-creatinine-high-clearance phenotype | H2 variance decomposition; fluid-balance and ARC-phenotype interaction terms; inspect whether the slope x estimator interaction survives their inclusion |
| **(ii) clinicians already anticipate the trajectory** | the measured level reflects prescribing behaviour and TDM-triggered dose changes, not estimator accuracy | observed concentrations are pulled *toward* target in moving-creatinine patients, so the apparent bias is attenuated or absent; mg/kg dose correlates with creatinine slope | model prescribed mg/kg on creatinine slope directly; repeat H1 restricted to the **first** level under an order whose mg/kg dose was unchanged from the previous interval, where anticipation cannot have acted yet |
| **(iii) selection — levels drawn preferentially in unstable or suspected-toxic patients** | sampling of the outcome depends on expected abnormality | error inflated, and concentration distribution shifted, in patients sampled more frequently; bias present even at flat creatinine | condition on draw intensity (levels per ICU day) and on whether the draw is protocolised (6-18 h post-dose, 45.84% observed) vs off-schedule; estimate H1 within the protocolised window only |
| **(iv) assay / timing artifacts** | peak vs trough vs random draws, infusion still running, continuous infusion | error concentrated at short dose-to-draw intervals; no dependence on creatinine slope | exclude draws < 2 h post-dose (2.19% observed); stratify by hours-since-dose; analyse the continuous-infusion subset (`administration_type = Medication Infusion`, 29,797 rows) separately; report that infusion duration is unrecorded and must be assumed |

### 2.4 What answering this changes

If H1 holds in the moving-creatinine stratum, then a free, two-line change to the
clearance input of every AUC-guided vancomycin dosing calculator reduces systematic
under-dosing during AKI onset and over-dosing during recovery — the stratum in which
vancomycin nephrotoxicity and treatment failure are both concentrated. If H1 fails at
~100x [K2]'s sample size with prespecified stratification, that converts a small
single-centre negative result into a well-powered one and, through H2, redirects effort
from the clearance term to volume-of-distribution/fluid status and early individualised
Bayesian feedback. Both outcomes are usable; the design is not built to confirm.

## 3. Population, temporal boundaries and observed cohort

MIMIC-IV 3.1 (one zip, SHA-256 `0dd448e...af09` per `datasets/mimic/README.md`; full
receipts in `bindings.md`). Unit of analysis = one measured vancomycin concentration
(a *level episode*), clustered within patient.

Inclusion (all verified in data; counts are **observed**):
- serum vancomycin level, `hosp/labevents.itemid = 51009`, numeric `valuenum`;
- index time = that level's `charttime`, falling inside an `icu/icustays`
  `[intime, outtime]` window;
- >= 1 qualifying IV vancomycin administration in the **prior 96 h**
  (`hosp/emar.event_txt` in the administered/started sets, route `IV` obtained via
  `emar_detail.pharmacy_id -> pharmacy.route`);
- >= 2 serum creatinine values (itemid 50912/52546, 0 < value <= 40 mg/dL) in the
  **prior 48 h**, spanning >= 6 h;
- age >= 18 at the index time.

Exclusion: any RRT in the **prior 7 days** (`icu/procedureevents` CRRT / Hemodialysis /
CRRT Filter Change / Peritoneal Dialysis, union `pharmacy.frequency = "HD PROTOCOL"`);
non-IV vancomycin only (oral/enema/ophthalmic/intrathecal/catheter-lock — 15,579 oral
liquid and 763 enema orders observed); levels drawn < 2 h after a dose (distribution
phase); non-numeric level values.

Observed cascade (full detail in `bindings.md` section 7):

```
119,231 levels (itemid 51009)
 ->  39,592 during an ICU stay
 ->  17,595 with a qualifying administration within 96 h
 ->  17,504 with >= 2 creatinine within 48 h
 ->  14,703 with no RRT within 7 d
 ->  14,670 analysable levels, 6,414 patients, 7,341 ICU stays
```

Prespecified strata (observed sizes): **non-steady-state 8,444** (rising 4,658, falling
3,786) vs **steady-state 6,226**, where non-steady-state means
`(|dCr| >= 0.3 mg/dL and |slope| >= 0.3 mg/dL/24h)` or `Cr_last/Cr_first >= 1.15` or
`<= 0.87`. The steady-state stratum is the built-in negative control: the kinetic and
steady-state estimators nearly coincide there, so H1 predicts **no** improvement in it.

No calendar-time window is imposed, because MIMIC-IV dates are per-patient shifted; the
`anchor_year_group` field is instead used as a secular-practice covariate (vancomycin
target changed from trough-guided to AUC-guided over the source period), and the
estimator comparison is also reported within `anchor_year_group` strata.

## 4. Variables

### 4.1 Predictors (clearance estimators) — exact formulas

With `Cr` in mg/dL, age in years, weight = `inputevents.patientweight` in kg (100% stay
coverage observed), sex from `hosp/patients.gender`:

**Cockcroft-Gault (the bedside default):**
`CrCl_CG = ((140 - age) * W_kg / (72 * Cr)) * (0.85 if female)`, with a prespecified
sensitivity using adjusted body weight when BMI > 30 (BMI from `hosp/omr` height where
available).

**CKD-EPI 2021 (race-free):**
`eGFR = 142 * min(Cr/k, 1)^a * max(Cr/k, 1)^-1.200 * 0.9938^age * (1.012 if female)`,
with `k = 0.7` and `a = -0.241` for females, `k = 0.9` and `a = -0.302` for males;
de-indexed to mL/min by `* BSA/1.73` (Du Bois BSA).

**MDRD (4-variable, no race term):**
`eGFR = 175 * Cr^-1.154 * age^-0.203 * (0.742 if female)`; the race coefficient is
omitted because MIMIC-IV 3.1 carries `race`, not the historical dichotomy — recorded as
an explicit deviation from the published equation.

**Kinetic eGFR, the form adopted [K1]:**

```
KeGFR = ( CrCl_ss * Cr_ss / mean(Cr1, Cr2) )
        * ( 1 - ( 24 * (Cr2 - Cr1) ) / ( dt_hours * dCr_max_per_day ) )
```

where `Cr1, Cr2` are the first and last creatinine in the 48 h window, `dt_hours` their
separation, `Cr_ss` a reference steady-state creatinine with its steady-state clearance
`CrCl_ss` (primary definition: the patient's lowest creatinine in the 365 d before ICU
admission from `hosp/labevents`, falling back to the ICU-admission creatinine, with the
choice reported as a sensitivity), and `dCr_max_per_day` the maximum anuric rise
(primary 1.5 mg/dL/day; sensitivity over 1.0-2.0, and a per-patient version computed
from creatinine production rate / creatinine volume of distribution = 0.6 * W_kg).

**Explicit honesty limit.** [K1] was inspected **abstract only** (subscription full text;
see `refs/K1-chen-2013-kegfr-europepmc.txt`). The abstract names every input of the
formula but does not print the algebra, the recommended `dCr_max_per_day`, or the Vd
fraction. The equation above is therefore stated as *the form this proposal adopts* and
must be checked against the published equations by a reader with full-text access before
execution. This is a required pre-execution adjudication step, not an optional one.

### 4.2 Outcome

Primary: the measured vancomycin concentration at the index time, `valuenum` in ug/mL.

Derived classification (secondary, decision-linked): **sub-therapeutic < 10**,
**therapeutic 10-20**, **supra-therapeutic > 20** ug/mL for trough-window draws
(6-18 h post-dose), with a prespecified sensitivity using 15-20 for serious MRSA
infection, and a separate AUC24-based classification (target 400-600 mg*h/L) for the
subset where two levels in one interval permit an AUC estimate. The threshold choice is
reported both ways because the source period spans the trough-to-AUC guideline change.

### 4.3 Covariates

Dose and timing: last dose mg (`prescriptions.dose_val_rx` /
`emar_detail.dose_given`), mg/kg, hours since last administration (observed median
16.50 h), cumulative dose in the prior 48 h, dosing frequency
(`pharmacy.frequency`: Q12H 89,812, Q24H 40,341, Q8H 23,051, ...), infusion volume from
`prod_strength`/`product_description`, continuous-infusion flag.
Physiology/severity: weight, age, sex, cumulative fluid balance
(`inputevents` mL rows minus `outputevents` urine, with a missingness indicator —
66,876 of 94,458 stays have urine output), urine output rate in the prior 24 h, albumin
(itemid 50862, 1,032,456 rows), BUN (51006, 4,202,807 rows), vasopressor exposure from
`inputevents` `01-Drips`, mechanical ventilation, ICU day, `first_careunit`.
ARC phenotype: indicator for `CrCl_CG >= 130 mL/min/1.73 m2` with creatinine below the
upper reference limit — the operational analogue of [K3]'s definition in this source.
Behavioural: levels per ICU day (draw intensity), whether the mg/kg dose changed from the
previous interval, `anchor_year_group`.

## 5. Analysis plan

**Design.** Patient-level split: 70% of the 6,414 patients to a development set, 30% held
out, stratified on non-steady-state status, split by `subject_id` so no patient appears in
both. All model selection, threshold tuning and covariate screening happen in development
only; the held-out set is scored once per prespecified hypothesis. Exploratory analyses
are labelled as such and are not used to support H1 or H2.

**Baseline (B0).** Closed-form one-compartment prediction of the measured concentration
from the steady-state estimator: `CL = f(CrCl_CG)`, `V = 0.7 L/kg * W_kg`,
`C(t) = (Dose/(CL*tau_inf)) * (1 - exp(-k*tau_inf)) * exp(-k*(t - tau_inf))` accumulated
over the prior 96 h of recorded doses, with `k = CL/V`. Repeated with CKD-EPI 2021 and
MDRD in place of CG. Population constants (`CL/CrCl` ratio, `V/kg`) are fitted on the
development patients only.

**Index model (B1).** Identical, with `CrCl_CG` replaced by `KeGFR`. B1 and B0 differ in
exactly one input, which is what makes the comparison interpretable.

**Primary estimand (H1).** Within the non-steady-state stratum of the held-out set, the
paired difference in mean absolute prediction error, `MAE(B0) - MAE(B1)`, in ug/mL, with
a 95% CI from patient-clustered bootstrap (2,000 resamples of patients).

**Discriminating estimand (H1b).** In a mixed-effects regression of signed prediction
error on `creatinine slope x estimator`, with a patient random intercept and adjustment
for hours-since-dose, mg/kg, weight, fluid balance, ARC indicator, draw intensity and
`anchor_year_group`: the slope-by-estimator interaction coefficient. H1 predicts a
negative signed error (under-prediction) for B0 at positive slope and a positive signed
error at negative slope, with the interaction attenuating toward zero under B1.

**H2 variance decomposition.** Fit the hierarchical model of `method-comparison.md` and
partition residual variance in log-concentration into (a) clearance-estimator block,
(b) Vd/weight/fluid block, (c) sampling-time block, (d) patient random effects,
(e) residual. Report each as a fraction, with posterior intervals.

**Prespecified sensitivity / rival-separation analyses.** (1) protocolised-window only
(6-18 h post-dose, 6,725 levels observed); (2) first level under an unchanged mg/kg order
(rival ii); (3) stratified by draw intensity (rival iii); (4) continuous-infusion subset
analysed separately (rival iv); (5) `dCr_max_per_day` varied 1.0-2.0 and `Cr_ss` defined
three ways; (6) steady-state stratum as negative control; (7) within-`anchor_year_group`.
Multiplicity: H1 and H2 are the only confirmatory tests; Holm correction across the two,
and all sensitivity analyses are reported as a pre-listed panel with CIs rather than
re-tested for significance.

## 6. Prespecified falsification criteria (numeric)

Minimum clinically relevant difference, set before any fitting: **0.75 ug/mL** of mean
absolute error, i.e. 5% of a 15 ug/mL trough target, and about 11% of [K2]'s reported
RMSE.

**Supportive (H1 holds):** in the held-out non-steady-state stratum,
`MAE(B0) - MAE(B1) >= 0.75 ug/mL` with the 95% patient-clustered bootstrap CI excluding
0; **and** the slope-by-estimator interaction has the predicted sign with p < 0.01;
**and** no comparable improvement appears in the steady-state negative-control stratum
(point estimate < 0.375 ug/mL); **and** the direction survives sensitivity analyses
(1)-(3).

**Adverse / refuting (H1 fails — a replication of [K2] at scale):** the 95% CI for
`MAE(B0) - MAE(B1)` lies entirely below 0.75 ug/mL (including CIs that favour the
steady-state estimator), **or** the slope-by-estimator interaction CI includes 0. With
8,444 non-steady-state levels in 6,414 patients, a CI half-width below 0.75 ug/mL on a
paired error difference is expected to be achievable; this will be confirmed by a
development-set-only precision check before the held-out set is opened.

**Inconclusive:** bootstrap CI width for the primary estimand > 1.5 ug/mL and spanning
0.75 — i.e. the data cannot distinguish a clinically relevant improvement from none. The
pre-committed response is to report the interval, not to reinterpret it.

**H2 decision rule:** if blocks (b) + (d) carry more than **3x** the variance of block
(a), declare rival (i) dominant and the clearance-estimator choice a second-order problem
for vancomycin exposure prediction in the ICU; if block (a) exceeds block (b), the
clearance term is the binding constraint and estimator work is worth continuing.

## 7. What each outcome would and would not establish

**Supportive.** Establishes a *predictive* and *measurement* claim: a kinetic clearance
input reduces systematic error in predicting measured drug concentration in the
moving-creatinine stratum. It does **not** establish that kinetic eGFR measures true GFR
(no tracer or cystatin C exists here), that dosing by it improves infection cure or
reduces nephrotoxicity (that requires a trial with clinical endpoints), or that the
signed bias is causal rather than a residual of unmeasured severity. The honest next step
would be a prospective dosing study, not a guideline change.

**Adverse.** Establishes a well-powered, prespecified, stratified null that corroborates
and substantially strengthens [K2] — including in the stratum where the mechanism should
have been strongest, which [K2] could not examine. Combined with H2 this is a positive
scientific result: it locates the dominant error in volume of distribution, fluid status
and patient-specific clearance ([K3]), and argues that further refinement of
creatinine-based clearance equations is a poor investment relative to early Bayesian
feedback from a measured level. It does **not** establish that creatinine kinetics are
irrelevant to kidney-function assessment generally — only that they do not materially
improve prediction of this drug's concentration under these data constraints.

**Inconclusive.** Would most likely reflect error dominated by unrecorded infusion
duration and sparse sampling. It would **not** be reported as weak support, and the
pre-committed interpretation is that this question requires a source with documented
infusion start/stop times and at least two levels per dosing interval.

## 8. Conclusions that require data or adjudication not available here

1. **True GFR.** No cystatin C item exists in `d_labitems`; measured timed urinary CrCl
   (itemid 51080) has only **717 rows** database-wide. Any claim that one estimator is
   closer to *true* GFR is out of reach and is not made. The 717 rows are used only as an
   opportunistic concordance description with explicit n.
2. **Assay provenance.** The vancomycin assay method, platform and lot are not recorded,
   so immunoassay cross-reactivity and inter-period drift cannot be excluded.
   Expert laboratory adjudication would be needed before attributing residual bias to
   physiology rather than measurement.
3. **Infusion duration.** `emar_detail.infusion_rate` is populated on **79 of 888,194**
   vancomycin rows and `infusion_complete` on 1,149. The one-compartment prediction must
   assume an infusion duration; this is a named, quantified assumption, not a hidden one.
4. **Rich PK sampling.** MIMIC contains clinical TDM draws only; individual CL and V are
   not separately identifiable per occasion and are recovered only through population
   shrinkage. Any individual-level pharmacokinetic claim requires a dedicated PK study.
5. **Dosing intent.** Whether a dose was deliberately reduced in anticipation of a rising
   creatinine is not recorded as intent; rival (ii) is addressed by design (unchanged-dose
   restriction) but not eliminated. Chart-note adjudication of a sample by a clinician
   would strengthen this and is identified as the single most valuable manual review.
6. **Generalisation beyond vancomycin** (e.g. to aminoglycosides, beta-lactams,
   direct oral anticoagulants) requires separate analyses; vancomycin's high renal
   fraction makes it the most favourable case, so a null here is the stronger negative.

## 9. Relationship to the parent seed

Preserved from seed 03: the core concern that creatinine is an inadequate instrument for
kidney function in critical illness, and the caution that apparent "recovery" by
creatinine may not be physiologic recovery. Changed, and why: the seed's cystatin
C / measured-GFR anchor is **absent from this source** and could not be substituted by
another creatinine formula without circularity, so the readout was replaced with a
measured physiologic consequence of renal clearance. Added: an explicit, inspected prior
negative result [K2] that the seed did not reference, the stratification that makes the
mechanism testable, and an error decomposition against [K3]'s rival that makes a null
informative rather than merely disappointing.

## 10. Novelty check — reported honestly

I searched explicitly for prior work validating kinetic GFR against therapeutic drug
monitoring concentrations (Europe PMC and OpenAlex title/abstract searches on kinetic
eGFR + vancomycin / therapeutic drug monitoring / drug clearance; targeted retrieval by
PMID/DOI thereafter). **Such work exists and it is negative: [K2], Harada et al. 2018,
"Predictability of serum vancomycin concentrations using the kinetic estimated glomerular
filtration rate formula for critically ill patients", Int J Clin Pharmacol Ther
56(12):612-616, PMID 30220291** — 122 critically ill adults, KeGFR versus three
established eGFR formulae, KeGFR with the highest RMSE of the four. The Branch A leading
claim as originally posed is therefore **not novel and is contradicted by the closest
inspected evidence.**

What remains unaddressed, and is this proposal's contribution: stratification on whether
creatinine was actually moving (the median level in this source sits at zero slope);
roughly 50x the patient count and 120x the level count; a transparent, specified
one-compartment prediction instead of proprietary manufacturer software; the
signed-direction interaction that distinguishes the kinetic mechanism from severity,
selection and timing artifacts; and the H2 variance decomposition that attributes the
residual. I make no claim of an exhaustive novelty search. A full-text reading of [K2]
(subscription-only; I inspected the abstract only) could reveal that some of these were
in fact addressed, which would further narrow the contribution; that check is listed as a
pre-execution requirement.
