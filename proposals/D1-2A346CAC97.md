# Episode 59: does late lactate add decision-relevant information beyond the released 24-hour laboratory record?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and advance

At a 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine entry, an observed late lactate may change perceived mortality risk. Episode 58 showed that it improved a model containing pressor-entry lactate and retrospective APACHE IVa predicted mortality. That comparator is clinically incomplete: `apachePatientResult` has no availability timestamp, and it omits the other laboratory results that are commonly visible by the reassessment. The unresolved question is therefore whether late lactate contributes material information beyond the rest of the released, leave-lactate-out laboratory record, or whether the favorable Episode 58 signal mainly reflects contemporaneous derangement that other tests already encode.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis:** among the inherited age-inclusive EICU cohort of adults with an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed entry, baseline lactate at least 2 mmol/L, survival in hospital through the 24-hour landmark, and a released 12-to-24-hour lactate, adding that late lactate to a prespecified model containing pressor-entry lactate and a fixed leave-lactate-out laboratory snapshot available by the landmark increases uniformly threshold-averaged net benefit over mortality-risk thresholds 0.30-0.50 by at least **1 net true-positive-equivalent review per 100 people**.

This is an incremental-prognostic-information hypothesis conditional on the test having been obtained. It is not an effect of ordering lactate, treating lactate, or acting on a prediction. The substantive advance over Episode 58 is a stricter comparator whose collection and revision times are auditable. A negative result would distinguish “late lactate improves a sparse retrospective score” from “late lactate adds material information beyond the contemporaneous record.”

## What is already supported and what remains unresolved

The definitive Episode 58 parent reconstructed 1,084 APACHE-complete people in 31 hospitals and completed 2,000 copied-hospital leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO) refits. Adding late lactate to APACHE IVa predicted mortality plus entry lactate yielded ΔINB +1.982 per 100 (95% interval +0.483 to +3.351), ΔAUROC +0.0313, improved Brier and log loss, and positive deletion estimates in all 31 hospitals. This supports a positive **modeled** increment in that selected cohort and threshold scenario. It did not support the locked ≥+1 materiality claim because +1 remained inside the interval, and it did not establish ordering utility, causality, or patient benefit.

The trajectory parent and its executed successor found only +0.448 ΔINB per 100 (95% -0.463 to +1.580) when trajectory was added after the late value in 1,229 people across 33 hospitals. Thus binary clearance or trajectory cannot rescue this experiment.

Baysan et al. (2022; DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407) was inspected in frozen full-text XML (source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]). In 4,440 critically ill adults with sepsis, adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV increased the C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64; admission plus 24-hour lactate had the best reported fit. This supports modest serial prognostic information, not incremental decision value beyond a released contemporaneous laboratory record.

A current 2026 report by Li et al. (DOI 10.2196/92838; PMCID PMC13478678) was acquired as full-text XML (source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]). Its abstract and relevant retrieved passages report trajectory modeling among selected patients with at least three lactate/albumin measurements, incremental NRI/IDI, and modest external discrimination. It does not answer this fixed-landmark, leave-lactate-out decision question. No supplementary material was inspected.

The Vickers et al. decision-curve guide (PMCID PMC6261531; frozen HTML source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) was inspected. It supports comparison with treat-all and treat-none across clinically reasonable thresholds and emphasizes that thresholds encode benefit-harm preferences. It does not validate 0.30-0.50, uniform weighting, or +1 per 100 for this review decision.

The research-ambition README and its availability restrictions were inspected; none of its three demonstrations is treated as evidence for this hypothesis.

## Population and temporal boundaries

No source rows are sampled. Complete gzip CSVs are scanned; chunking is only for memory control.

1. Start with all 200,859 `patient` rows. Parse age; map literal `> 89` and numeric ages above 89 to 90; retain age ≥18.
2. In `infusionDrug`, define `t0` as the earliest `infusionoffset` in [0,1440] whose case-folded `drugname` contains literal `norepinephrine` or `levophed`. This is a recorded entry, not verified administration or dose.
3. Per `uniquepid`, retain the stay with lexicographically smallest `(t0, patientunitstayid)`. Define `L24=t0+1440`.
4. Require `hospitaldischargestatus` in {Alive, Expired} and `hospitaldischargeoffset >= L24`.
5. Baseline lactate `B`: normalized `labname=lactate`, unit `mmol/L`, finite value [0.2,30], collection `labresultoffset` in [t0-360,t0], and `labresultrevisedoffset <= t0+60`. At a collection time retain the greatest eligible revision; conflicting values at that revision invalidate the collection; identical ties use smallest `labid`. Choose the latest valid collection and require B≥2.
6. Follow-up lactate `F`: the same analyte/unit/value/revision/conflict rules, collection in [t0+720,t0+1440], revision by L24; choose nearest t0+1080, then earlier collection, then smallest `labid`.
7. Preserve the inherited outcome-blind hospital gate requiring at least five people with binary clearance ≥20% and five with <20%. This binary is used only to freeze the parent-comparable hospital set; it is not a predictor, estimand, subgroup, or rescue analysis.
8. Only after the hospital set is frozen define `D=1` for Expired and 0 for Alive.

The complete-file audit (`episode59_availability_audit.py/json`) reproduces **1,229 people, 507 deaths, 722 survivors, and 33 hospitals**; no discharge occurs exactly at L24. The cohort remains selected by survival, recorded pressor entry, clinician lactate ordering, laboratory release, and the inherited site gate.

## Released leave-lactate-out laboratory snapshot

For each fixed analyte below, consider collections in [t0,L24] with finite numeric `labresult` and `labresultrevisedoffset <= L24`. Apply the same greatest-revision, conflict-invalidation, and smallest-`labid` tie rules, then choose the latest valid collection. Lactate rows are excluded. The fixed keys are case-insensitive trimmed `labname | labmeasurenamesystem` pairs:

- `potassium|mmol/L`, `sodium|mmol/L`, `chloride|mmol/L`, `BUN|mg/dL`, `glucose|mg/dL`, `creatinine|mg/dL`, `bicarbonate|mmol/L`, `calcium|mg/dL`;
- `Hgb|g/dL`, `platelets x 1000|K/mcL`, `WBC x 1000|K/mcL`;
- `albumin|g/dL`, `total bilirubin|mg/dL`;
- `paO2|mm Hg`, `paCO2|mm Hg`; and
- `pH` with `labmeasurenamesystem` missing or blank.

The outcome-blind full-source audit found patient coverage of 93.0%-98.7% for the core chemistry/CBC keys, 75.8%-78.8% for pH/PaO2/PaCO2, and 67.7%-68.7% for albumin/bilirubin. Raw ranges are retained in the audit for QC. No complete-case exclusion is allowed.

## Models, baselines, estimand, and uncertainty

`R0`, the primary comparator, contains age and age squared; Female and Male indicators with Other/Unknown omitted; t0/1440 and its square; baseline collection lag `(t0-B_offset)/360`; ln(B) and ln(B)²; follow-up measurement age `(L24-F_offset)/720`; and, for every fixed non-lactate analyte, its latest value, measurement age `(L24-offset)/1440`, and missingness indicator. Including F measurement age in both models conditions on when the observed test was collected.

`R1` adds only ln(F) and ln(F)². It does not add clearance, B×F interaction, outcome-derived feature selection, or measurement count.

Within each LOHO training fold, observed continuous values are winsorized at training 1st/99th percentiles and standardized with training mean and population SD. Missing continuous values are set to zero after standardization (the training mean) and paired with fixed missing indicators. A feature with no observed training values makes that fold noncomputable. Transformations are applied unchanged to the held-out hospital.

Both models are unweighted L2 logistic regressions with intercept, `C=1`, `lbfgs`, `max_iter=10000`, `tol=1e-8`, no class weighting, and seed 20260959. No held-out outcome informs preprocessing, fitting, calibration, thresholds, or feature selection.

At each p in {0.30,0.31,...,0.50}, compute `NB=TP/N-FP/N*p/(1-p)`. Treat-none is zero; treat-all uses pooled prevalence. R0 is the primary comparator. The primary estimand is

`ΔINB = 100/(0.50-0.30) × integral[0.30,0.50] (NB_R1(p)-NB_R0(p)) dp`

using the trapezoid rule over all 21 points.

Sample the 33 hospitals with replacement 2,000 times using seed 20260959. Copies of a sampled hospital remain one held-out group; all copies are excluded from that fold's training data; sampled multiplicity weights training and held-out contributions. Refit preprocessing and both models in every replicate. Use percentile 95% intervals.

Prespecified secondary checks cannot rescue the primary: ΔAUROC, Brier improvement, log-loss improvement, mean absolute probability change, threshold-specific ΔNB, and all 33 fixed-prediction hospital deletions. In the 1,084-person/31-hospital APACHE-complete subset, add `logit(predictedhospitalmortality)` and its square to both R0 and R1 as a retrospective sensitivity. Because `apachePatientResult` has no timestamp, this sensitivity is not evidence of operational availability. A nonpositive sensitivity point estimate is a mixed-result guardrail; a positive result cannot upgrade the primary label. Adding trajectory after F is descriptive only and cannot rescue the result.

Computability requires exact source hashes/headers; exact audited flow; ≥1,000 people, ≥30 hospitals, ≥200 deaths and ≥200 survivors; both outcomes in every hospital; maximum site share ≤15%; every fixed feature observed in every training fold; finite unique held-out predictions; all 21 thresholds; ≥95% finite converged refits; and ≥80% finite deletions.

## Falsification and interpretation

After computability gates:

- **Supportive:** the 95% lower bound for ΔINB is ≥+1, R1 calibration slope is 0.80-1.20, absolute calibration-in-the-large ≤0.05, R1 beats the better default at all 21 thresholds, no threshold ΔNB is below -0.5 per 100, at least 80% of finite site deletions are positive, and the APACHE sensitivity point estimate is >0.
- **Adverse to materiality:** the 95% upper bound is <+1 but ≥0, with no opposite branch. This refutes the locked +1 increment for this model and setting, not every smaller increment.
- **Opposite:** the 95% upper bound is <0. This means the fixed extended model worsened modeled decisions; it does not mean lactate testing caused harm.
- **Inconclusive/mixed:** the interval includes +1 or zero, or the lower bound clears +1 but any guardrail fails.
- **Not computable:** any required source, cohort, feature, fit, bootstrap, deletion, or threshold gate fails.

Support would establish only a material out-of-hospital modeled increment among selected repeatedly measured EICU survivors under unadjudicated review utilities. Adverse findings would weaken the claim that the observed late value adds consequential information once other released labs are known. Opposite findings would challenge this fixed modeling use. Inconclusive findings would require independent data rather than threshold narrowing, binary clearance, post-result feature removal, or a favorable secondary metric.

## Verifier contract, privacy, and evidence that remains unavailable

The verifier can check source hashes and headers; all filters, joins, clocks, revisions and conflicts; one-person and hospital selection; the fixed lab keys and audited coverage; leakage-safe fold preprocessing; imputation and missing indicators; model settings; prediction uniqueness; every decision-curve cell and integral; copied-hospital multiplicities and refits; intervals, proper scores, calibration, deletions, guardrails, and deterministic label. It must test supportive, adverse, opposite, mixed/inconclusive, and noncomputable fixtures, and reject correct numerical outputs paired with unsupported conclusions such as “repeat testing improves survival.”

Neither computation nor an automatic verifier can establish arterial versus venous lactate; specimen/panel identity; actual norepinephrine administration or dose; why any test was ordered; whether every released result was visible to clinicians; intervening fluids, beta-agonists, source control, liver/renal handling, tissue perfusion, limitations of treatment, exact death time/cause, preventability, workflow burden, cost, or what action followed a result. Clinical informatics review is required for analyte/specimen/workflow semantics. Clinician and patient review is required to validate the 0.30-0.50 thresholds and +1 margin. Independent prospective external validation requires explicit ordering indications and view/action timestamps; an impact study, ideally randomized if an ordering or treatment policy is proposed, is required for changed decisions, harms, costs, or patient benefit.

The availability audit is aggregate support. The private person-level cohort remains only in the workspace and must not be published.
