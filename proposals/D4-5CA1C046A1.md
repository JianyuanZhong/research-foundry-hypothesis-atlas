# Is repeat Lp(a) testing clinically redundant after a valid baseline measurement?

## Question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

Guidelines increasingly support measuring lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] at least once in adulthood because it is largely genetically determined. That recommendation does not resolve whether repeating Lp(a) years later changes cardiovascular-risk decisions, especially near the 125 nmol/L risk-enhancer threshold, or whether apparent changes reflect assay limits, illness, or regression to the mean.

**Supported claim:** elevated Lp(a) is an independent ASCVD risk marker, and UKB field 30790 is an immunoturbidimetric plasma measurement in nmol/L. Existing evidence does not establish that a second measurement has no decision value. **Unresolved claim:** among adults with two valid measurements and no ASCVD at the second assessment, the baseline measurement is noninferior to the repeat measurement for post-landmark 8-year MACE risk and treatment-threshold classification.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: replacing repeat Lp(a) with the earlier baseline value in an otherwise identical locked clinical risk model increases integrated Brier score by no more than 0.002 and decreases standardized net benefit by no more than 0.002 across 5%–15% 8-year treatment thresholds; additionally, >=95% of people are classified on the same side of 125 nmol/L. This is a test of clinical redundancy, not proof that biology never changes. It could support avoiding low-value repeat testing or identify a subgroup (near threshold, kidney/inflammatory change, statin initiation) needing retesting.

Relevant frozen evidence includes the 2022 European Atherosclerosis Society consensus search record `[source checksum]`; claims beyond its bibliographic/abstract evidence are not made. The research-ambition demonstrations were inspected for standards—temporal landmarking, leakage prevention, calibration, and honest UKB selection-bias limits—not treated as topic prescriptions. The cancer main article was unavailable; only its supplement was inspected.

## Outcomes and models

Primary outcome is first post-landmark MACE: algorithmic MI, algorithmic ischemic stroke, or cardiovascular death (underlying I00–I99), with all-cause death as competing event. Clinician review must lock the phenotype hierarchy before model evaluation. Secondary outcomes are components and all-cause mortality.

Create three models, all developed on participant hash buckets 0–59 and evaluated once on buckets 60–79 (reserved 80–99 are inaccessible):

- **C (clinical):** age at date1, sex, validated ethnicity, smoking1, mean BP1, BMI1, diabetes1, total/HDL cholesterol1, medication indicators1, kidney function1, CRP1, assessment calendar time, and interval from date0.
- **C+B:** C plus baseline Lp(a) `30790-0.0`.
- **C+R:** identical C plus repeat Lp(a) `30790-1.0`.
- Secondary **C+B+R** tests whether both measurements add information.

Use a prespecified cause-specific Cox model with restricted cubic splines and recalibrate baseline hazard in development data. No generic high-dimensional model is needed. Handle covariates with 20-fold multiple imputation fitted inside development resamples; never impute Lp(a), exposure dates, or outcomes. Evaluate 8-year time-dependent AUC, calibration intercept/slope and plot, integrated Brier score, and decision-curve net benefit at 5%–15% risk thresholds. Bootstrap `eid` 1,000 times for paired C+B minus C+R differences. If follow-up cannot support 8 years, use the largest common horizon >=5 years chosen without outcomes by exposure and label the changed estimand.

Measurement agreement is co-primary context: median absolute/relative change, intraclass correlation, Bland–Altman limits, and threshold crossing at 75, 125, and 250 nmol/L. Distinguish analytic values beyond reportable range from missingness. Prespecified subgroup interactions: baseline within 25 nmol/L of 125; change in eGFR category; CRP >10 mg/L at either visit; interval >=4 years; initiation of lipid-lowering medication between arrays using a frozen official Coding 1004 map. These are FDR-controlled and not causal.

Compare repeat attenders with eligible baseline-only participants on baseline characteristics and baseline-model predicted risk. Use inverse odds-of-reattendance weighting as a transport sensitivity, while stating it cannot correct unmeasured selection.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive of redundancy:** C+B is noninferior to C+R on both margins (upper 95% CI for Brier increase <0.002; lower 95% CI for standardized net-benefit difference >−0.002 at every threshold), >=95% threshold agreement at 125 nmol/L, and C+B+R does not improve Brier score by >=0.002 or net benefit by >=0.002. This supports no measurable decision value of routine repeat testing in similar repeat attenders.

**Adverse:** either noninferiority margin fails with a precise advantage for C+R, threshold agreement <90%, or a prespecified subgroup has repeat-model net-benefit gain >=0.005 with its lower 95% CI >0. This refutes general redundancy and motivates targeted retesting validation.

**Inconclusive:** CIs cross margins, <200 MACE events or <20 per model parameter, inadequate 8-year follow-up, materially poor calibration, strong reattendance-weight instability, assay/reportability ambiguity, or sensitivity estimates change sign. Failure to prove noninferiority is not proof repeat testing helps.

The verifier can check joins, dates, exclusions, split isolation, models, paired metrics/CIs, margins, and whether conclusions obey these rules. It must reject a correct computation paired with claims of biological invariance, population-wide generalizability, or a guideline recommendation. It should test synthetic supportive, adverse, and inconclusive outputs.

Automated computation cannot verify assay comparability, clinical validity of MACE, unmeasured reasons for retesting/reattendance, treatment decisions that clinicians would actually make, or cost-effectiveness. Clinical laboratory review is required for reportability and assay-era interpretation; cardiology review is required for outcome definitions and decision thresholds. Changing practice needs external prospective validation in an unselected contemporary cohort and an implementation/cost study.
