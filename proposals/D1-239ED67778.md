## The repaired measurement boundary

MIMIC documentation describes `starttime` and `endtime` as the recorded clinical input interval and `storetime` as the time the observation was manually entered or validated by clinical staff. The public page does not define `storetime` as first clinician awareness, a decision time, bedside availability, intent, medication availability, or actual administration. A store time can follow the clinical start, and a store time at or before the start still does not establish those stronger facts.

Therefore the primary exposure remains exactly the parent’s `inputevents` record:

- Clinical `starttime`/ `endtime`, half-open intervals, define the recorded input timing. `storetime` never moves an interval.
- A12 is the first qualifying clinical onset strictly in `(t0,C0)`; B12 has no qualifying clinical onset through `C0`. Equal A12/B12 clones, early V0 closure, and every inherited ambiguity rule remain fixed.
- `storetime` is a descriptive documentation-timing variable only. Define lag as `storetime-starttime` when both parse, and report missing/unparseable, negative, zero, and positive lag categories and prespecified quantiles.
- No `E_DA` or “decision-observable” cohort exists. No row is excluded, reassigned, censored, reweighted, or given a different outcome because its store time is late, early, missing, or tied.
- For a linked/touching segment sequence, report row-level and sequence-level lag summaries. Do not require all component rows to be on-time, and do not infer that a late component proves retrospective care or that an early component proves awareness.
- A post-`C0` store time for a clinically earlier loop row is “post-window documentation” in the audit. It is not a contradiction of B12 and cannot change A12/B12 assignment.
- eMAR/pharmacy/prescription data remain non-linkable to ICU input rows for exact automated dose confirmation because they lack a shared ICU `stay_id`, `itemid`, `orderid`, or equivalent key. Their `storetime`, `charttime`, medication text, and time proximity cannot be used to manufacture decision timing or prove receipt. eMAR absence never proves B12.

This repair is substantive: it makes the measurable object documentation lag, while preserving the clinical-time estimand. It avoids converting an EHR workflow timestamp into a latent mental state or a bedside event.
