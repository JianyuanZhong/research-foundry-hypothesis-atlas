# Monotone whole-accession onset states for the HCC documentary-M2 coverage/preference frontier

## Focused repair and clinical question

This is a focused successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves that candidate's adult first eligible liver-resection population, documentary same-encounter M2 endpoint, chronology quarantine, whole-accession semantic units, fixed-capacity locked comparators, 24-hour confirmatory decision time, exact finite-state computation, and fail-closed clinical interpretation.

The repair addresses one defect: the parent applies one binary accession mask to 72-, 48-, 24- and 12-hour cutoffs. That makes report visibility a time-invariant property even though a report can become available between those cutoffs. The child replaces that mask with a per-episode, per-whole-accession monotone onset state. Availability at 24 hours is the sole confirmatory estimand; 12, 48 and 72 hours are sensitivity descriptions and cannot veto a primary result.

The consequential question is whether a fixed-capacity review service can identify more eventual documentary-M2 first-resection cases than acquisition-only and random-review comparators using only report content that was actually available by the confirmatory 24-hour decision time, while exposing the patient-coverage and stakeholder-preference tradeoff. The result remains a retrospective documentary allocation result, not a treatment recommendation and not evidence of biological MVI, report viewing, clinical action or patient benefit.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that the HCC snapshot contains linked procedures, encounters, examination rows with acquisition-like times and report narratives, and same-encounter pathology narratives. The exact source cannot establish report authoring, finalization, release, ingestion, retrievability, readability, amendment history or viewing before a cutoff. The unresolved claim is that an exactly locked semantic ranking has a nonempty primary 24-hour coverage/preference frontier that is superior to independent acquisition-only, report-surface and random-review baselines under every retained operation, source, reader, pathology and deletion state. That claim is testable only as a documentary historical estimand; action compatibility requires an external report-archive audit and outcome-blind preference elicitation.

The substantive advance over the parent is temporal validity of the availability estimand. Each accession now has a single ordered onset state, and the four observed cutoff indicators are derived from that state rather than independently or jointly assigning one binary mask. An external audit maps finalization/readability evidence to the same state. This makes an accession that becomes readable between 48 and 24 hours visible at 24 but not 48, without allowing it to disappear at a later cutoff.

## Operation/source worlds and the monotone onset state

Let i index a patient-episode and let d be one of the ordered cutoffs
72 < 48 < 24 < 12 hours before the operation. For each retained operation state o, define cutoff times t_{i,72}, t_{i,48}, t_{i,24}, t_{i,12} in chronological order. For each whole accession u attached to i, the source/operation world defines

`E_{i,d,u}=1` iff the operation state is valid, u is source-clean and nonblank, its CT/MRI membership is valid, and its validated acquisition interval lies wholly inside [t_{i,d}-90 days,t_{i,d}). Otherwise `E_{i,d,u}=0`. Thus an accession acquired after an earlier cutoff cannot be exposed at that cutoff, even if its report is later readable. Operation ambiguity and source ambiguity are never cross-combined across incompatible worlds.

Conditional on E=1, assign one onset category to the whole accession for this episode:

- `O=0`: the audited whole source version is final, released/ingested and readable no later than t_{i,72};
- `O=1`: first eligible whole-version readability is in (t_{i,72}, t_{i,48}];
- `O=2`: first eligible whole-version readability is in (t_{i,48}, t_{i,24}];
- `O=3`: first eligible whole-version readability is in (t_{i,24}, t_{i,12}];
- `O=4`: it is not eligible/readable by t_{i,12}, or the audit can only establish a later/never time.

The onset is a property of the whole source version, not a phrase or report component. Its derived visibility vector is

`V(O)=(1{O=0}, 1{O<=1}, 1{O<=2}, 1{O<=3})`

in the ordered 72/48/24/12 columns. It is monotone by construction: once the whole version is available it remains available at later decision times. The observed exposure is

`M_{i,d,u}=E_{i,d,u} V_d(O_{i,u})`.

There is no shared binary mask across cutoffs. There is one onset state per whole accession within an operation episode, and four cutoff-specific exposure bits derived from it. If the source accession itself is not a valid whole unit, M is zero in every arm and the corresponding state is retained as an unavailable source world. Missing external version evidence is different: a source-clean unit remains in the onset uncertainty set, but action compatibility is bridge-pending. An explicitly invalid audit crosswalk, hash mismatch, or contradictory version lineage forces that unit unavailable rather than allowing a favorable onset state.

For an audit interval rather than an exact onset time, use the contiguous allowed category set implied by the interval; do not choose a midpoint. With no external audit, the HCC-only computational sensitivity set is {0,1,2,3,4} for each otherwise source-clean accession; it represents interval-censored availability, not observed visibility. This is explicitly bridge-pending and cannot produce action-compatible support. This distinction prevents missing availability evidence from being silently treated as visibility or as permanent invisibility.
