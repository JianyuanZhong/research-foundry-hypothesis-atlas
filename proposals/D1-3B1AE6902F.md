# Observed post-discharge inpatient burden after first observed TACE in HCC: an endpoint-audited trajectory study

## Scientific question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

Among adults with HCC who have a first observed TACE-type procedure in the local HCC snapshot, does the early post-procedure trajectory of AFP, hepatic-reserve laboratories, and creatinine improve prediction of a subsequent **observed inpatient encounter lasting at least 24 hours within 30 days after discharge**, beyond a pre-index clinical/laboratory baseline?

The clinical importance is triage of post-discharge follow-up burden: if a trajectory signal is reproducible and calibrated, it could motivate prospective validation of a risk-stratified surveillance tool. The endpoint is intentionally an observed-care-burden endpoint. It is not “unplanned readmission,” treatment failure, recurrence, radiographic progression, hepatic failure, AKI, mortality, or a causal effect of TACE.

The prespecified hypothesis is that a coupled deterioration pattern—rising or non-recovering AFP and worsening standardized reserve/renal laboratory trajectories by discharge—will be associated with higher 30-day observed inpatient-event risk and will improve held-out calibration and risk stratification over the pre-index baseline. The strongest available evidence supports only that the snapshot contains dated encounters, procedures, diagnoses and laboratory values that can construct this estimand. It does not support the prognostic claim. A read-only provisional audit found 18,963 complete-window candidates and 1,406 first qualifying events, but those numbers must be regenerated after the solver freezes dictionaries, cohort rules, joins and catalog partitioning.

## Endpoint decision and ascertainment limitations

The primary endpoint is the first subsequent encounter for the same patient with:

- a different `就诊号`;
- `入院时间 > index 出院时间`;
- admission no later than 30 days after index discharge;
- nonmissing `出院时间`;
- `出院时间 - 入院时间 >= 24 hours`.

The analysis calls this an **observed subsequent inpatient encounter ≥24 hours**. It does not call it planned or unplanned. The source has no validated admission-intent, planned/unplanned, admission-reason, death, or outside-hospital field. A same-source endpoint is therefore clinically interpretable as local observed inpatient burden, but not as complete readmission risk.

The earlier non-procedural primary proposal is not retained as primary. Excluding a return because a procedure occurred in its first 24 hours conditions the endpoint on a post-landmark management decision and can make planned-care ambiguity look like clinical adjudication. Instead, every qualifying ≥24-hour return contributes to the primary endpoint. For the first qualifying return, report mutually exclusive descriptive strata:

1. a TACE-type procedure recorded in the first 24 hours of that return;
2. no TACE-type procedure recorded in that interval.

The second label is “no recorded early TACE,” never “non-procedural,” because procedure capture may be incomplete and other planned procedures are not necessarily identifiable. These strata are secondary competing/descriptive outcomes, not exclusions from the primary endpoint.

Also report: any subsequent encounter within 30 days including stays shorter than 24 hours; first ≥24-hour return with/without a recorded early TACE; number of ≥24-hour returns; repeat TACE during days 31–180; and a sensitivity outcome for any prespecified liver-directed procedure dictionary. None is interpreted as recurrence, response, progression, or treatment failure.

Short stays, missing-discharge encounters, overlapping admissions, and admissions at or before index discharge are not silently treated as non-events. They receive separate counts and sensitivity analyses. The primary complete-follow-up rule requires index discharge no later than `2025-12-01 23:59:59`, using the snapshot boundary of `2026-01-01`; late/incomplete cases are reported as excluded from the primary analysis.

## Population, index, and temporal rules

Use only discovery catalog hash buckets 0–79. Buckets 80–99 are inaccessible and are not external validation. Use the configured participant partitioner; do not reconstruct a different split from row order.

1. Include patients aged at least 18 with parseable index age. Index is the earliest observed qualifying TACE-type procedure with valid `手术.开始时间` on or after `2011-01-01`. The final procedure dictionary is frozen before outcome counting. It must include the observed exact family `经导管肝动脉栓塞术`, `动脉化疗栓塞`, `经导管肝动脉造影化疗栓塞术（TACE）`, and clearly equivalent combined TACE/embolization strings confirmed in a pre-outcome vocabulary audit. Angiography-only rows are excluded.

2. Require a diagnosis name containing `肝细胞癌` or `肝癌` on a diagnosis row joined to an encounter at or before the procedure start. Diagnoses have no native temporal field; the joined encounter’s `就诊时间` is the only temporal anchor. Treat lexical and copied-forward diagnosis flags as cohort proxies, not adjudicated HCC or comorbidity diagnoses.

3. Join the procedure to an encounter on `患者主索引, 就诊号`. Require valid `入院时间` and `出院时间`, procedure start within the index admission-discharge interval, and discharge at or after procedure start. Exclude any earlier observed qualifying TACE for the patient. This is first observed, not first-ever treatment.

4. The prediction landmark is index `出院时间`. The baseline uses only information available by procedure start; the trajectory model uses dated information from procedure start through index discharge. No post-discharge lab, diagnosis, procedure, encounter, length-of-stay-derived outcome label, or future information may enter features.

## Models and scientific comparison

### Pre-index regularized logistic baseline

Fit a training-only standardized elastic-net logistic regression for the primary binary event. Covariates, all available by procedure start, are age, sex, index department, calendar era, TACE subtype, pre-index HCC/liver-disease/portal-hypertension/ascites/metastasis proxies, prior inpatient count and prior liver-directed/TACE count in 180 days, and for each exact assay the nearest value, days since measurement, pre-index count, missingness and a slope only when at least two valid dated values exist in days −90 through 0. Do not include index length of stay in the primary model. All imputation, scaling, dictionary expansion and regularization selection are training/selection-only.

### Selected substantive alternative: irregular latent organ-state model

Fit an interpretable continuous-time, low-dimensional state-space model using the same patients, outcome, split, baseline covariates and exact dated laboratory tokens. Use separate assay-specific observation models for AFP, albumin, bilirubin, coagulation, platelets and creatinine. A latent reserve state and a latent renal-stress state evolve with patient-specific random intercepts/slopes and shrinkage across days −90 through discharge; AFP remains a distinct marker state. Each observation contributes value, assay identity, elapsed time, and missingness/measurement-density information. At discharge, a regularized logistic head uses the baseline covariates plus latent state level, recent slope, and posterior uncertainty to predict the primary endpoint.

Fit the joint lab observation and outcome objective on the fitting partition, select state dimension, process-noise and shrinkage on the selection partition, and report assay loadings, state-level/slope contrasts and uncertainty. Standardized latent states are interpretable as data-derived reserve/renal-stress summaries, not proven physiology. The alternative can reveal whether direction, timing, coupled organ change and uncertainty contain information lost by nearest pre-index values and counts. It is nested scientifically in the same question; its added information is post-procedure trajectory, not an unshared outcome or population.

A one-layer GRU or temporal-attention model using the identical token schema and target is recorded but deferred. It would be revisited only if the state model fails convergence or held-out residual checks show reproducible nonlinear timing interactions. A small predictive gain alone is insufficient. A multimodal transformer is not selected because HCC has no image files and the cancer demonstration’s main paper and complete STAR Methods were unavailable; no reproduction claim is made. The learned alternative is therefore not rejected categorically, but the interpretable state model better separates trajectory information from coding and surveillance artifacts for this endpoint.

## Split, analysis, uncertainty, and surveillance checks

Use deterministic participant-level catalog buckets 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for hyperparameter/regularization selection, and 70–79 as the locked internal test set. No patient, encounter, lab or procedure may cross partitions. A latest-era chronological stress test is secondary and is not external validation.

Before test evaluation, save the frozen dictionaries, cohort flow, every exclusion, key multiplicity, timestamp audit, assay counts, follow-up completeness and procedure-stratum counts. Report baseline and trajectory measurement availability, median lab count/gaps, and the number of index discharges with short, missing or overlapping follow-up.

On the same locked test patients, report AUROC, PR-AUC, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, calibration plots, observed-versus-predicted risk bins, and paired patient-bootstrap 95% intervals for model differences. Use 500 or more patient-level bootstrap resamples, with all model refitting confined to the fitting/selection partitions when needed. Decision curves are exploratory only; no threshold is a clinical policy. Prespecify a minimum absolute Brier improvement of 0.002 for a practically meaningful gain; a smaller statistically positive gain is labeled trivial rather than clinically supportive.

Address surveillance and capture explicitly:

- compare the state model with and without measurement count, gap, missingness and observation-process terms;
- compare with and without pre-index encounter density;
- permute laboratory dates within patient while preserving values and assays; trajectory-specific improvement should attenuate;
- exclude all labs whose native `检验时间` is after discharge and audit any lab/encounter interval overlap;
- exclude index length of stay, then include it only as a labeled discharge-severity sensitivity;
- stratify by calendar era, department, assay availability and measurement density;
- compare all-event primary results with early-TACE and no-recorded-early-TACE strata and with short-stay returns;
- repeat using exact AFP only and a predeclared related-label sensitivity without pooling.

If fewer than 100 primary events remain, if any primary assay dictionary cannot be frozen because semantics/units are ambiguous, if missing/overlapping time fields dominate, or if follow-up completeness cannot be quantified, the primary result is inconclusive. Do not shrink the cohort or relax the endpoint to manufacture event support.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results require all of: a directionally coherent prespecified trajectory association; improved held-out calibration and at least one prespecified risk metric over the baseline with uncertainty; at least the 0.002 Brier threshold or a comparably prespecified calibrated risk-stratification improvement; stability after measurement-intensity, date-permutation, era and department checks; and no timestamp leakage. This supports only a local prognostic signal for observed inpatient-care burden and justifies prospective/external validation.

Adverse results are a null or opposite trajectory association, no meaningful held-out improvement, poor calibration, instability across strata, or disappearance after accounting for measurement intensity. This argues against using early AFP/reserve/creatinine trajectories alone to prioritize post-discharge surveillance and against the proposed state interpretation.

Inconclusive results include too few events, nonconvergence, assay ambiguity, wide intervals spanning meaningful benefit and harm, incomplete local follow-up, or a result driven by early recorded TACE, coding era, department, missingness or surveillance intensity. These identify a data limitation rather than refuting or supporting the biology.

Even a supportive computation cannot establish complete readmission risk, planned/unplanned status, outside-hospital capture, radiographic response, recurrence, progression, hepatic failure, AKI, survival, transplant eligibility, treatment intent, or benefit from changing follow-up. Those require clinical/radiology adjudication, validated staging and intent, laboratory units/reference ranges, death/transplant linkage, complete multi-hospital capture, expert review, external validation, or a prospective causal/implementation study.

## Solver deliverable and compute

The solver must newly fit both models and output: (1) source hashes and frozen procedure/diagnosis/assay dictionaries; (2) cohort flow, joins, duplicates, timestamp and follow-up audit; (3) patient-level split and locked predictions; (4) baseline coefficients and latent-state/loadings/uncertainty summaries; (5) discrimination, calibration, Brier/PR-AUC differences and patient-bootstrap intervals; (6) all surveillance, date-permutation, short-stay, procedure-stratum, era and LOS sensitivity outputs; and (7) a conclusion table mapping every scientific claim to a computed output and marking clinical claims that require adjudication or another study.

The discovery endpoint audit used 4 CPUs/16 GiB and completed in 6.3 seconds for encounters, diagnoses and procedures; a prior bounded full procedure/lab label scan took approximately 146.4 seconds with the same resources. The future solver planning envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 256 GiB RAM and 28,800 seconds. CPU is appropriate for the elastic-net and low-dimensional state-space fits; a GPU is not required. A deferred GRU could use one allocated A100-SXM4-80GB, but that is a future task-specific option, not a discovery result or mandatory dependency. The sources remain read-only; all cohort tables, predictions and derived files belong in the workspace.
