# Working proposal: urine output trajectory and early ICU deterioration

## Scientific opening

The consequential decision is whether an ICU team should intensify monitoring or intervention when urine output is falling despite apparent hemodynamic stability. The leading explanation is that the trajectory of urine output contains information about evolving renal/organ dysfunction that is not captured by a single latest value or admission severity. A rival explanation is that apparent signal is merely current illness severity, treatment (diuretics, fluids, vasopressors), selection into ICU, or documentation/measurement practice.

## Hypothesis

Among adult MIMIC-IV ICU stays, a sustained downward trajectory in urine output over the preceding 6–12 hours will predict a clinically meaningful deterioration event in the next 12 hours beyond a model using only current organ state and admission/early severity. The learned trajectory model will have materially better held-out discrimination/calibration than a simple current-state baseline, and the incremental signal will remain when urine-output measurement intensity and treatment exposure are explicitly represented.

This is an association/prediction hypothesis, not a causal claim. It does not establish that changing fluid or diuretic treatment improves outcomes.

## Population, landmark, and estimand

Use adult ICU stays from `icu/icustays` joined to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`. Restrict to stays with `outtime - intime >= 24 hours`, a valid prespecified urine-output dictionary, and a landmark at ICU hour 12. Do not use later ICU events to define eligibility.

The primary estimand is the risk of the first mutually exclusive deterioration event in `(landmark, landmark+12 hours]`: new renal replacement therapy, new invasive mechanical ventilation, or death. An event already present before the landmark is excluded. Censor at ICU `outtime` when earlier. Report the three components separately; do not allow overlapping repeated windows or multiple events to inflate risk. Non-invasive ventilation is not automatically counted as invasive ventilation. If RRT or ventilation item mapping is not reproducible, the result is inconclusive rather than silently redefined.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Use staged, same-outcome comparators. B0 is a regularized logistic model for the first-event endpoint using current/last observed urine output, current vital signs, demographics, admission type, care unit, and preceding 6-hour treatment. B1 adds urine-output observation count, coverage, time since last urine record, and chart/store-time gaps. B2 adds prespecified fluid, diuretic, and vasopressor exposure summaries and timing. The primary scientific contrast is T1 versus B2.

T1 is a prespecified trajectory/persistence model over hourly urine-output bins, with slope, persistence, recovery, and explicit missingness; a small GRU/Transformer is a deferred alternative only if the bounded trajectory model is feasible and the artifact-adjusted contrast remains unresolved. The alternative can reveal ordered decline and recovery information discarded by the latest value, but model capacity is not the scientific claim.

Learned alternative: a temporal model (for example, a gradient-boosted sequence encoder or a small GRU/Transformer) over hourly binned urine output, vital signs, fluid/vasopressor/diuretic exposure, and measurement-intensity indicators for the preceding 12 hours. Fit the same outcome, split, and calibration evaluation as the baseline. The alternative can reveal whether slope, persistence, recovery, and irregular measurement patterns carry information that a current-state summary discards. It is not selected for a small predictive gain alone; a substantive result requires consistent held-out improvement, calibration, and interpretability/ablation showing trajectory rather than mere treatment or documentation intensity.

## Analysis and falsification

Use subject-level train/validation/test splits, no patient leakage, and bootstrap or repeated-split uncertainty. Compare discrimination (AUROC/AUPRC), calibration intercept/slope, and decision-curve/net benefit at prespecified alert thresholds. Report subgroup analyses by care unit, admission type, and measurement density. Ablate urine-output trajectory, treatment features, and measurement-intensity features.

Supportive: T1 improves held-out discrimination and calibration beyond B2, with stable direction and clinically meaningful net benefit; the gain persists in high-coverage strata and is not reproduced by a shuffled trajectory or pre-landmark charting-gap negative control.
Adverse: no incremental held-out gain, poor calibration, gain confined to low-coverage strata, or disappearance after treatment/measurement adjustment; this weakens the claim that trajectory adds information.
Inconclusive: insufficient eligible landmarks, sparse urine output, unstable item mapping, or wide uncertainty; do not interpret as no signal.

## Evidence and limitations

[K1] Haines et al. (2023) inspected the full-text excerpt for a prospective study of 38 mechanically ventilated critically ill patients. It found that creatinine-based eGFR systematically overestimated kidney function after prolonged critical illness, with cystatin C closer to measured GFR; this supports the proposal's caution against treating creatinine or a single bedside measure as renal recovery. The study was small, single-center, and did not test urine-output trajectories or near-term deterioration.

[K2] Tang et al. (2026) inspected the full-text excerpt for a multicenter MIMIC-IV/eICU-CRD landmark study predicting observed CRRT initiation. Urine output was among retained predictors, but external calibration was poor and incremental value was limited; the authors explicitly did not interpret prediction as CRRT indication or treatment benefit. This bounds the present proposal's claim and motivates calibration, incremental-value, and artifact analyses rather than a treatment recommendation.

[K3] Jakobsen et al. (2026) inspected the full-text excerpt for a systematic review of experimental porcine sepsis studies. It found that AKI classification depends on measurement strategy: urine-output criteria require sustained oliguria, creatinine may be blunted, and GFR reporting was associated with higher criterion fulfilment. Although the population is experimental and heterogeneous, this supports treating urine output as an observable measurement with context-dependent meaning, not as measured GFR.

No clinical adjudication of RRT/ventilation indications, fluid balance validity, or treatment causality is available automatically; those stronger conclusions require expert review or another study.
