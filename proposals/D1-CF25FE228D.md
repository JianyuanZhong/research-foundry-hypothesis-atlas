# Persistent versus transient recovery-context mismatch before first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and fit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study that estimates whether *persistent, ordered* respiratory/hemodynamic instability after recorded support withdrawal is associated with a different early post-ICU competing-event pathway than a transient final-window abnormality. Completion requires:

1. `run_manifest.json` containing the catalog and all source/archive/schema hashes, verified item labels, cohort and temporal rules, trajectory definitions, topology/tie rules, split seeds, software versions and output hashes.
2. `cohort_audit.parquet`, one row per subject's earliest eligible ICU stay, with all join, timing, death, window-coverage, support-state and exclusion flags.
3. `hourly_trajectory.parquet`, 24 one-hour left-closed bins in [outtime-24h,outtime), with chart-time physiology, interval support, procedure corroboration, masks, row counts and source-channel indicators; no post-outtime predictor.
4. `persistence_ledger.parquet`, prespecified support-transition timing and mutually exclusive transient/persistent/discordant phenotypes for respiratory and hemodynamic domains.
5. `transfer_outcome_audit.parquet` and `bound_cif.parquet`, with every later ICU candidate, documented non-ICU bridge, lower-bound return L, any-return upper-bound U, death and alive-discharge competing event, and compatible contrast intervals at 6, 24 and 48 hours.
6. Locked patient-level test predictions from a transparent pooled discrete-time competing-hazard baseline and a same-input observation-aware two-domain semi-Markov trajectory model, with calibration, IPCW Brier score, bootstrap/split uncertainty, event support, positivity/ESS and model comparison.
7. `falsification_outputs.parquet` and `interpretation_map.parquet`, linking each conclusion to computed estimates, uncertainty, bound width, sensitivity results and evidence limitations.

No fitted association or clinical conclusion is claimed in this proposal. Readiness checks establish packaging and bounded input feasibility only.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The parent evidence supports a narrower claim: recorded support withdrawal and final-six-hour physiology can be separated from observation intensity, and documented non-ICU bridging is a lower-bound marker (L) while any later ICU stay is an upper-bound marker (U). The parent has not established whether mismatch is a persistent physiologic trajectory or a transient measurement/documentation state.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adults leaving their first ICU stay alive, after a recorded respiratory or vasoactive withdrawal, persistent instability across the preceding 24 hours—especially instability that precedes and remains after withdrawal—has a larger ICU-return-compatible CIF than a transient final-six-hour abnormality; persistent hemodynamic mismatch has a larger competing death CIF than persistent respiratory mismatch.

This is a prognostic association about recorded support/physiology, not a causal effect of withdrawing support and not a discharge-readiness rule. It is falsified if persistent and transient groups have compatible null/reversed contrasts, if the distinction disappears after process standardization, if temporal order permutations reproduce it, or if it is driven only by one channel, missingness, or the broad U endpoint.

Primary estimands are standardized absolute differences in first-event CIF at h in {6,24,48} between persistent and transient phenotype groups, separately for L, U, death and alive discharge, with each competing event retained. For each group g, report L_g(h), U_g(h), D_g(h), and A_g(h). For the latent transition-compatible return, report the nonparametrically compatible interval [L_A-U_B, U_A-L_B] for contrast A versus B rather than choosing a midpoint. Report L and U separately; a U-only contrast is not called a confirmed escalation pathway. The primary comparison is persistent versus transient within the same support domain and recent-withdrawal stratum; respiratory-versus-hemodynamic differences are secondary and require adequate cell support.

## Exposure and persistence ledger

Verify labels in `icu/d_items` before extraction. Respiratory positive support evidence is chartevents item 223834 O2 flow >2 L/min, 223835 FiO2 >0.21, 220339 PEEP >0, or corroborating procedureevents item 224385 intubation, 227194 extubation or 225794 non-invasive ventilation. Vasoactive evidence is overlapping inputevents for 221906 norepinephrine, 221289/229617 epinephrine, 221662 dopamine, 221749/229630/229631/229632 phenylephrine, or 222315 vasopressin. Procedures corroborate a transition and are not continuous ventilation; interval status and duration are retained.

Split the pre-landmark period into four six-hour blocks: [outtime-24h,-18h), [-18h,-12h), [-12h,-6h), [-6h,0). Define a recent withdrawal in a domain as positive support in at least one of the first three blocks and no positive evidence in the final block, subject to an uninformative-absence q scenario q in {0,.10,.25,.50,.75,1.0}; observed non-positive evidence is never recoded. Require at least two respiratory and two MAP observation bins per six-hour block for the primary determined population. Keep active, discordant, inadequate and unknown strata in audits and report them.

For every block, compute respiratory instability fraction among valid hourly bins with SpO2 <90% (220277) or RR >=30/min (220210), and hemodynamic instability fraction among valid bins with MAP <65 using arterial MAP 220052 or NIBP MAP 220181. Missing bins are not stable; store denominators. The primary abnormal-block indicator is >1 abnormal hourly bin per block, with any-bin and >2-bin sensitivities. Temperature 223761/223762 and HR 220045 are covariates/audit features, not definitions of organ failure.

Among recent-withdrawal subjects, define a domain-specific trajectory phenotype using the four blocks:
- persistent mismatch: abnormal in at least two consecutive pre-withdrawal blocks and in the final block, with support withdrawal occurring between the earliest abnormal block and outtime;
- transient final mismatch: abnormal only in the final block and not in either preceding block;
- recovering/absent mismatch: no final-block abnormality;
- unresolved/discordant: all other patterns, inadequate observation or contradictory support evidence.

The primary comparison is persistent versus transient within respiratory and hemodynamic domains. A joint multisystem persistent group and the parent’s concordant/mismatch hierarchy are secondary. Preserve continuous instability burden, run length, first-abnormal-block, withdrawal-to-outtime interval W, support intensity and source-channel masks so persistence is not reduced to a single arbitrary cutoff.

## Baseline and scientifically substantive alternative

The primary baseline is pooled one-hour cause-specific penalized logistic hazards for L, U, death and alive discharge, plus aggregate return. It uses the same trajectory ledger: phenotype, block burdens, W, support intensity, observation masks/counts, admission context, age, gender, anchor-year group, ICU LOS and prespecified domain interactions. Nested fits are physiology/support only, observation/process only and full. Standardize held-out predictions to the common determined population and compute competing-event CIFs by Aalen-Johansen recursion. Report absolute CIF contrasts, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier score, bootstrap 95% intervals, split stability, event counts, overlap and ESS. This baseline is auditable but loses exact temporal order, run length and duration.

The substantive alternative is a compact observation-aware factorized two-domain HSMM on the identical 24-bin ledger and splits. Respiratory and hemodynamic latent states include favorable, persistent abnormal, transient/transition and unknown states, with explicit duration and support-withdrawal transitions; emissions use the same measured values and support indicators; a separate observation model uses masks, counts and source-channel availability. Its event head predicts the same L/U/death/discharge hazards in eight post-landmark bins. Fit and tune only on train/validation, evaluate locked test once per split, and never let post-outtime data enter latent state construction. Constrain or flag any model-based latent-return estimate outside the observed [L,U] interval.

The alternative can reveal whether persistence is a stable ordered state, whether abnormality precedes versus follows withdrawal, whether respiratory and hemodynamic durations differ, and whether missingness explains the apparent persistence. The baseline loses this order/duration decomposition. Compare scientific outputs, not a small score gain: contrast direction/magnitude, death-return composition, bound width, calibration/proper scores, observation-model calibration, latent entropy, transition stability and falsification behavior. Retain the baseline as primary if the HSMM is high-entropy, poorly calibrated, initialization-unstable, changes the estimand, or narrows uncertainty only through unverifiable latent assumptions.

Use CPU for extraction, ledger, baseline and bootstrap (planning: 4-16 CPUs, 32-128 GiB). The HSMM can begin on CPU; a bounded profile may request one allocated A100 using `cuda:0` for repeated optimization only if CPU timing makes device choice material. Future solver planning envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB RAM, 8 allocated GPUs and 28800 seconds. ## Falsification and interpretation

Pre-register: subject/time leakage audits; within-window permutation of values and their temporal order preserving masks; permutation of support interval timing within duration/intensity strata; W permutation within 6-12 and 12-24-hour strata; storetime negative control; pseudo-landmarks 6 and 12 hours earlier; channel-specific removal; q/coverage/threshold and bridge topology grids; and stratification by careunit, admission type, age and anchor-year group. A persistence-specific association should attenuate after temporal permutation and should not be recreated by observation masks alone.

Supportive evidence requires adequate persistent/transient counts and positivity; directionally consistent persistent-versus-transient L/U and/or death contrasts in observed and process-standardized analyses; non-vacuous compatible intervals; acceptable calibration; attenuation under value/order permutations; and stability across channels, thresholds, q, W, topology, splits and HSMM initializations. The learned alternative need not outperform, but it should reproduce the contrast with stable order/duration decomposition.

Adverse evidence is null/reversal after process adjustment, masks-only reproduction, survival of the signal under value/order permutation, dependence on one channel or arbitrary cutoff, death-only evidence for a return claim, or broad U with null/wide L. Inconclusive evidence is sparse cells, low positivity/ESS, high unknown prevalence, vacuous bounds, unstable weights, unresolved death timing, high latent entropy, model disagreement or inadequate events. Inconclusive is not a null.

Computation can establish reproducible recorded trajectories, observed-pair/process-standardized associations under stated assumptions, competing-risk CIFs, finite topology/phenotype bounds, calibration and sensitivity. It cannot establish true physiologic recovery, AKI, fluid status, measured kidney function, ventilator settings, treatment response, clinical intent, treatment limitations, discharge readiness, planned/unplanned or preventable return, ward monitoring, or clinical utility. Those require adjudicated charts, device settings, fluid balance, treatment-limitation documentation, expert review, external validation and likely prospective/causal study. Discharge/radiology text is available only as documentation availability; local lexical extraction is unvalidated and excluded from readiness/intent labels. MIMIC-CXR images and raw waveforms are unavailable.
