# Bounded outcome-missingness sensitivity for the frozen HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B validation

## Targeted child and clinical advance

This is a substantive child of assessed valid candidates `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent design already establishes a useful but narrow question: among selected repeat-TACE episodes with a complete laboratory triplet, does the contemporaneous pre-repeat assay value `P` improve short-horizon prediction of the next observed same-assay value `Y` beyond remote baseline `B` and the identical compact decision-time core? The predecessor's full-source audit also shows why a later-era claim is not yet identified: the 2022 block has 28 observed outcomes among 29 outcome-blind B/P-eligible episodes, 2023 has 29/43, and 2024 has 14/70 for albumin (16/70 for bilirubin), with only 1 observed 2024 systemic outcome for either assay's relevant arm accounting.

The unresolved, falsifiable question is therefore not whether a missing assay can be recovered, and not whether a treatment caused a laboratory change. It is:

> For each fixed annual future block, does the sign of the same P-beyond-B predictive error contrast remain unchanged for every unobserved post-event assay value in a transparently bounded, assay-specific range learned before reading future outcomes; if not, how far into that range must the missing values move to erase the observed contrast?

The substantive advance is partial identification of robustness. It distinguishes a predictive contrast that survives all declared completions from one that is entirely selection-sensitive, without silently treating missing `Y` as a negative result, invoking missing-at-random, changing the observed complete-triplet estimand, or converting the systemic-record indicator into a causal treatment comparison. This is clinically useful as an evidence boundary: it can show whether a monitoring signal is robust to outcome-recording failure in the selected pathway, while honestly identifying when the available EHR cannot resolve the question.

The strongest claim supported before this child remains restricted to the observed complete-triplet population: only the 2022 annual block passes the inherited outcome-blind and complete-triplet support gates and has high outcome observation. The new experiment tests robustness of that predictive interpretation under explicit mathematical bounds. It cannot establish multi-era transport, clinical laboratory validity, hepatic recovery/failure, survival, safety, treatment effect, utility, actionability, or patient benefit.

## New bounded partial-identification layer

For every assay and origin:

1. Construct the full future BP-eligible risk set before selecting or reading any future `Y`. Retain only aggregate row accounting in published outputs. Each internal row has arm, calendar block, pre-Y covariates, `Y_observed`, and, only if observed, `Y`.
2. Fit M0 and M1 on the same pre-origin observed complete triplets and generate fixed predictions `p0_i` and `p1_i` for every BP-eligible future episode. The sensitivity layer never refits a model after completing missing outcomes.
3. Define assay- and-origin-specific bounds from training observed `Y` only, after the exact numeric parser: `L = min(Y_train)` and `U = max(Y_train)`. If the training range is empty, degenerate, or any observed future `Y` lies outside `[L,U]`, mark that assay/origin `bounded_support = unsupported`; do not widen the range, fabricate an interval, or interpret finite-looking calculations. A secondary full-source assay range may be reported only as a non-temporal descriptive check and never as transport evidence.
4. Let `N` be the number of all future BP-eligible episodes, `O` the observed subset, and `M` the missing subset. For observed rows calculate `e0_i=(Y_i-p0_i)^2`, `e1_i=(Y_i-p1_i)^2`, and `d_i=e0_i-e1_i`. For a missing row and candidate `y ∈ [L,U]`, calculate `e_j(y)=(y-pj_i)^2` and `d_i(y)=e0_i(y)-e1_i(y)`.
5. Compute sharp, deterministic bounds on each model's all-risk-set MSE. For model `j`, the missing-row minimum is at `clip(pj_i,L,U)` and the maximum is `max((L-pj_i)^2,(U-pj_i)^2)`. Add observed errors and divide by `N`. Compute the sharp contrast interval directly, not by subtracting unrelated marginal intervals: because `d_i(y)=2y(p1_i-p0_i)+p0_i^2-p1_i^2` is affine in `y`, each missing row's extrema occur at `L` or `U`; sum with observed `d_i` and divide by `N`. Define positive `Delta_MSE = MSE0-MSE1` as favorable to added P.
6. Report the bounded RMSE-difference interval from the joint endpoint optimization of `sqrt(MSE0)-sqrt(MSE1)` over the independent row-wise box `y_i ∈ [L,U]`, or report a mathematically conservative interval if implementation uses separate model extrema. The compiler must label the interval method explicitly; it must not call marginal-MSE subtraction a sharp RMSE interval. Report primary standardized RMSE gain only for observed complete triplets.
7. Calculate the tipping point separately overall and by descriptive arm without pooling arms into a causal contrast. Let `Ybar_obs` be the observed future-case mean when estimable. For a common completion level `z ∈ [L,U]`, set every missing row's hypothetical outcome to `z` and calculate `Delta_MSE(z)`. Solve the piecewise-linear equation `Delta_MSE(z)=0`; because all missing rows share `z`, it has a closed-form root on each relevant interval (with endpoint checks). Report the smallest absolute displacement `|z−Ybar_obs|` among valid roots, the corresponding `z`, and the fraction of the training range traversed. If no root lies in `[L,U]`, the bounded sign is robust. If the observed-case mean is unavailable, report tipping displacement as not estimable but retain endpoint bounds. This definition replaces an ambiguous “common displacement” phrase with a reproducible scalar completion rule.
8. Also report three locked stress patterns for each assay, origin, block, and descriptive arm: all missing outcomes set to `L`; all set to `U`; and the least-favorable endpoint per row for the sign of `Delta_MSE` (choose whichever of `L` and `U` minimizes `d_i(y)` for a favorable-to-M1 contrast, with the maximizing endpoint as the opposite stress). These are mathematical completions, not plausible imputation or clinical predictions.
9. Report missing counts and fractions, observed-case primary metrics and inherited paired bootstrap interval, all-BP-eligible bounded MSE/RMSE contrast intervals, tipping values, and endpoint-pattern labels. Preserve the annual block identity; never pool 2022–2024 to make a transport claim. Use fixed training-only `SD_train(Y)` only for an optional dimensionless secondary contrast, clearly distinct from the primary standardized test RMSE gain.

The computation must assert one row per patient-assay-event, preserve arm and calendar labels, use no future `Y` in risk-set construction or prediction, and emit aggregate JSON/CSV only. Outputs must contain source hashes, schema checks, denominator assertions, support flags, bound provenance, formulas/version, row counts, and branch labels, but no patient identifiers, raw clinical values, clinical notes, or free text.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

- **Boundedly supportive:** reconstruction and clocks pass; both outcome-blind and complete-triplet gates pass; the observed complete-triplet primary contrast meets the inherited prespecified RMSE/uncertainty criterion; and the all-BP-eligible `Delta_MSE` remains positive under the entire `[L,U]` box, with no material arm-specific reversal. This supports robustness of a short-horizon prognostic association under the declared assay-support scenario for that selected calendar block only.
- **Adverse:** the locked observed-case contrast is nonpositive in an adequately supported block, M1 degrades repeatedly, or a bounded completion reverses the sign in a supported block. This challenges incremental prediction or shows selection sensitivity; it does not show that B is sufficient, identify a mechanism, or imply treatment harm.
- **Selection-sensitive:** the observed complete-triplet estimate is favorable but the bounded interval includes zero, or the tipping completion is close to the observed-case range. The result is unidentified under the declared bounds, not evidence of transport.
- **Inconclusive/unbounded:** any support gate fails; arm counts are below threshold; training bounds are empty/degenerate; an observed future outcome lies outside the training range; the test SD is unidentified; the interval is numerically unstable; or a conclusion depends on the non-temporal full-source range. This is the prespecified expectation for later sparse blocks, especially 2024 systemic.

A favorable bounded sign does not establish multi-era transport, laboratory-unit comparability, clinical outcome validity, treatment effect, causality, toxicity, recovery, survival, mortality, safety, utility, actionability, or patient benefit. A negative result does not establish no P information; it may reflect support, observation, or model limitations. A null is not equivalence. The systemic-record label remains a recorded-pathway covariate and composition descriptor, never an exposure contrast.
