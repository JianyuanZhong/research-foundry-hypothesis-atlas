# Does the laboratory observation process buy ICU mortality discrimination that does not transport across hospitals?

Lead working proposal — eICU 2.0 island, episode 2. Seed parent: `[starting question]` ([prior hypothesis]).

Status: design frozen by Lead for compilation. Data probe delegated to Generation; observed numbers to be inserted before selection.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICU severity/prognosis models built on EHR data are increasingly deployed at sites other than the development site. A recurring design choice is whether to feed the model information about the *measurement process* itself — how many labs were drawn, how many distinct analytes were ordered, which analytes were never measured. If that process information partly reflects local surveillance conventions (order sets, protocols, staffing) rather than patient physiology, then a model that gains apparent internal performance from it will mis-calibrate when transported, and internal validation will not reveal the risk. Resolving this changes a consequential modeling decision: which feature families are safe to deploy, and whether target-site recalibration is required.

## Claims

- **H1 (leading).** Observation-process features added to a value-anchored model increase internal (within-hospital) discrimination but incur a larger whole-hospital transport penalty — worse calibration slope and observed/expected (O:E) ratio — than the value-only model of matched complexity.
- **R1 (rival, severity).** Process features largely proxy true illness severity (sicker patients are tested more), so they transport at least as well as value summaries; any penalty matches the value-only model.
- **R2 (rival, model class).** Any penalty is a linear-model artifact and disappears under gradient-boosted trees.

Distinguishing observation: the *difference-in-penalty* between V+P and V under leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO) transport. Supportive H1 needs the V+P penalty to exceed V's; R1 predicts near-zero difference; R2 predicts the difference shrinks under GBT.

## Design

- **Population.** Adult ICU stays in eICU 2.0 with numeric `age` >= 16, `unitdischargeoffset` >= 1440 min (so a first-24h window fits), and a decisive hospital outcome (`patient.hospitaldischargestatus` in {Expired, Alive}). One row per `patientunitstayid`.
- **Outcome.** Hospital mortality (`patient.hospitaldischargestatus == 'Expired'`; cross-checked against `apachePatientResult.actualhospitalmortality`).
- **Temporal boundary.** Features from the first 24h only (`lab.labresultoffset` in [0, 1440] minutes from unit admit). No exposure to post-24h labs, no `labresultrevisedoffset` after the window.
- **Feature sets.**
  - **V (value-only):** first-24h min/max/mean of 10 prespecified analytes (creatinine, lactate, WBC, platelets, bilirubin, sodium, potassium, glucose, BUN, hemoglobin — exact `labname` strings bound from the data probe), plus age, gender, and a coarse APACHE admission-dx category. Missing value = absent (no imputation of the value).
  - **V+P:** V plus observation-process features: total lab rows in [0,1440], count of distinct `labname`, and one measured/not-measured indicator per analyte.
- **Models.** L2-penalized logistic regression (baseline) and gradient-boosted trees (substantive nonlinear alternative). APACHE IVa `predictedhospitalmortality` is an existing-score comparator.
- **Validation.** (i) Development: within-hospital stratified 5-fold. (ii) Transport: leave-one-hospital-out, restricted to hospitals with >= 200 stays (whole hospitals held out; no patient overlap).
- **Metrics.** AUROC, calibration slope, O:E ratio, Brier score, and net benefit at 5% and 10% risk thresholds.
- **Primary estimand.** Difference-in-penalty between feature sets: Δ = [(AUROC_internal − AUROC_LOHO) for V+P] − [same for V]; and the analogous contrast for calibration slope.
- **Falsification (pre-specified).** H1 supported only if the V+P AUROC transport drop exceeds V's by >= 0.02 **and** the V+P calibration slope is at least 0.1 lower under transport than V's. H1 refuted if the V+P penalty is <= the V penalty. Otherwise inconclusive.
- **Exploratory (labeled).** Association between per-target-hospital V+P calibration error and that hospital's median first-24h lab count per stay, adjusted for case-mix. Not a mechanism claim.

## Baseline vs learned/mechanistic alternative

The logistic baseline tests whether a simple linear combination of value summaries transports. The GBT alternative tests the same feature sets with learned nonlinearities and interactions (e.g., surveillance intensity × severity), revealing whether H1's penalty is a model-class artifact (R2) or persists — which the baseline cannot separate. Deliverable established by: fitted V and V+P models under both model classes, with internal and LOHO AUROC/calibration/O:E/Brier/net-benefit outputs.

## What each result means

- **Supportive:** transport penalty concentrated in process features → recommend excluding observation-process features, or mandating target-site recalibration, before deployment.
- **Adverse:** process features transport at least as well as value summaries → the severity-signal reading (R1) is strengthened and process features are not a transportability hazard.
- **Inconclusive:** wide CIs or too few hospitals in the transport set → requires pooled MIMIC+eICU replication and prospective site validation.

All outcomes are computational statements about model behavior. They are **not** proof of a causal mechanism, and **not** a hospital quality ranking. Interpreting "surveillance practice" causally needs chart/order-set/workflow data that eICU does not contain (explicit missing dependency; clinical adjudication required).

## Limitations and honest scope

- eICU 2.0 is a US 2014–2015 snapshot; transport here is across eICU hospitals only, not external health systems.
- Absence of a lab row is not always equivalent to "not ordered"; ambiguous cases are treated as not-measured and this is stated as a measurement limitation.
- No staffing, bed-capacity or order-set data; hospital-level attribution stays exploratory.
- Analytes are prespecified to control for the multiplicity of lab names; the exact strings must be verified in the probe before fitting.

## Alternatives not chosen (deferred)

- Deep sequence models over continuous vitals/ventilation (`vitalPeriodic.csv.gz` 1.7 GB, `respiratoryCharting.csv.gz`): disproportionate to an estimand about summary feature families; revisit if temporal resolution itself is the question.
- Instrumental-variable/"hospital preference" designs (seed 01/10): no valid instrument with these fields.
