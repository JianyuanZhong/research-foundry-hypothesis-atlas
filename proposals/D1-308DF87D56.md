# Care-plan labels and an independently defined respiratory patient-state trajectory

## Question and advance

The two exact eICU care-plan values `Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation` and `Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial` have already failed transportability and vocabulary-rescue tests. Those results establish sparse, hospital-concentrated documentation and do not establish whether a label contains any information about a clinically consequential respiratory course. The unresolved question is narrower and testable: **among the frozen eligible labelled person-landmarks, does the label predict a reproducible 36-hour respiratory support trajectory after accounting for the measured respiratory state immediately before the label?**

The proposed advance is to replace a single post-label setting reduction with an independently defined, mutually exclusive trajectory endpoint: sustained de-escalation, escalation, or neither. It uses only time-stamped respiratory settings, airway status, and vital signs; it does not use care-plan values, care-plan counts, notes, or treatment strings to define the primary endpoint. This tests incremental patient-state information rather than semantic agreement between documentation fields. The estimand is predictive/associational, not a causal effect of assigning a care-plan label.

Evidence already supported by the parents is limited to failure of common multicenter label meaning and failure of outcome-blind vocabulary expansion for a short-horizon chart proxy. The experiment does **not** test whether a label means that an SBT or extubation evaluation occurred, whether extubation was appropriate, or whether a patient benefited.

## Independently defined state and trajectory

Freeze the label-to-component mapping and numerical parsing before reading or summarizing care-plan labels by outcome. For each row, construct three non-overlapping windows relative to D:

1. **Baseline** `B=(-12h,0h]`.
2. **Early** `E=(0h,12h]`.
3. **Late** `T=(12h,36h]`.

For each window and each of the four setting components, use the median of valid numeric measurements; retain the number of observations and the first/last timestamp. A window is component-observed only if it has at least two measurements separated by at least 30 minutes, reducing one-off transcription artifacts. A window is state-observed if at least three of four setting components are observed. If a component is absent, it is not treated as unchanged or as zero.

Define component change from B to T using fixed clinical thresholds: FiO2 decrease >=0.10 (absolute fraction, after converting percentage values to fractions), PEEP decrease >=2 cmH2O, pressure-support decrease >=2 cmH2O, and set-rate decrease >=2 breaths/min. An increase uses the corresponding positive threshold. A component is neutral if its change is smaller than the threshold or unavailable. The primary trajectory is:

* **Sustained de-escalation** if state-observed in B and T, at least two components decrease, no component increases, and the E window is not an escalation (no two-component increase versus B).
* **Escalation** if state-observed in B and T, at least two components increase, or an invasive airway is newly documented in E/T after no invasive evidence in B.
* **Stable/mixed** otherwise, including one-component changes, opposing changes, or insufficient state observations.

To make “patient-state” more than a settings-only artifact, report a prespecified safety-concordance flag separately: among trajectory-classifiable rows, whether the median `sao2` in E and T is at least 90% and median respiratory rate is at most 30/min when those vitals are observed at least twice in each window. The primary estimand remains the three-class setting/airway trajectory; safety-concordance is not silently used to redefine missing outcomes. A secondary stringent improvement endpoint requires sustained de-escalation plus safety concordance, with the denominator explicitly restricted to rows with adequate vital observation.

Invasive evidence is defined without care plans using `respiratoryCare.airwaytype` values that, after a frozen source-wide enumeration, contain an endotracheal/tracheostomy/invasive-airway term or have a nonmissing active ventilation interval (`ventstartoffset <= time` and (`ventendoffset` missing or `ventendoffset >= time`)). The exact lexical rule, all distinct airwaytype values and counts, and a sensitivity using only nonmissing vent intervals must be printed. No claim is made that this is bedside truth.

## Baseline adjustment and estimands

The primary question is incremental information, not a causal label effect. Fit a multinomial model for the three-class trajectory with hospital-held-out evaluation. The baseline model uses only pre-D patient-state information: hospital-independent one-hot landmark, baseline component medians, component observation counts/missing indicators, baseline invasive-airway indicator, baseline median SaO2 and respiratory rate, age category, and inherited ICU/admission covariates available before D. A second model adds the exact three-state care-plan label (evaluation, no-trial, and neither exact target is not present by construction; retain a two-level label indicator). A process-control sensitivity adds only pre-D documentation density (`respchart` count/distinct labels), never post-D care-plan counts. The care-plan label is not used to choose the state windows, map respiratory labels, or define the endpoint.

Evaluate row-weighted micro log loss and equal-hospital macro log loss, multiclass Brier score, one-vs-rest calibration, and class-specific observed-vs-predicted rates. Hold out hospitals, not random rows; eligible held-out hospitals require at least 20 rows and at least two trajectory classes. Repeated landmarks are clustered by `patientunitstayid` in bootstrap uncertainty. Use 100 nested stay-level bootstrap replicates, refitting preprocessing and model in every hospital-held-out fold. Report the exact number of eligible hospitals, rows, and class counts before fitting. No numerical improvement is clinically meaningful by itself; the key result is whether the label adds stable information across hospitals and to the stringent improvement endpoint.

For descriptive association, report label-stratified class risks and hospital-specific multinomial contrasts in the prespecified overlap hospitals 248, 252, and 420 only when each label has at least ten class-observed rows. Do not pool sparse hospitals to manufacture overlap. Report all 74 hospitals descriptively, with label concentration and endpoint missingness.

## Falsification and gates

Use the following fixed gates before viewing label-stratified results.

1. **Independent-outcome integrity.** Pass only if the primary trajectory code reads no care-plan columns after D is obtained, no treatment strings, and no outcome-derived feature selection; all endpoint mappings and thresholds are source- and outcome-blind. A violation invalidates the analysis rather than producing an adverse clinical result.
2. **State observability.** Supportive feasibility requires at least 40% of the frozen 540 rows to be classifiable and at least 10 hospitals with both labels represented among classifiable rows. Adverse feasibility is <20% classifiable or <5 such hospitals. Otherwise inconclusive. This prevents absence of charted settings from being mistaken for stable patient state.
3. **Incremental predictive value.** Supportive requires exact label versus baseline to improve both micro and macro log loss in at least the unbounded cohort and two airway-definition sensitivities, with nested 97.5th percentile delta below zero and no reversal in the sign of the hospital-specific delta in more than 25% of eligible held-out hospitals. Adverse requires no improvement in any bounded sensitivity and nested probability >=0.75 that the label worsens macro loss in at least two definitions. Otherwise inconclusive.
4. **Clinical trajectory coherence.** Supportive requires the label contrast to be directionally consistent for sustained de-escalation and stringent safety-concordant improvement in at least two of the three overlap hospitals, with the same direction in early and late windows. Adverse is a label contrast that reverses between the three hospitals or is present only for the settings-only endpoint while disappearing for the safety-concordant endpoint. Inconclusive applies with sparse safety observations.
5. **Negative-control timing.** Recompute the same B/E/T trajectory using a placebo decision time `D-24h`, keeping the original label fixed and requiring no overlap with D-centered outcome windows. A supportive label-specific post-D result must exceed the placebo contrast by at least 5 percentage points without hospital-direction reversal. A comparable/larger placebo contrast or reversal is adverse to a newly informative label interpretation; otherwise inconclusive.
6. **Hard-event concordance.** ETT-removal and reintubation strings from `treatment` are secondary only. Supportive validation requires at least 10 concordant events under each label across at least five hospitals with adequate observation. Fewer events or absent standardized terminology is adverse to validation, not evidence that liberation did not occur.

Supportive results would show that exact care-plan labels add transportable information about a reproducible, independently measured respiratory trajectory and would motivate prospective interface/clinical adjudication. Adverse results would show that labels add no stable information beyond measured state, strengthening the case against using them for multicenter decision support, while not proving documentation conventions or patient harm. Inconclusive results would identify missingness, sparse label overlap, or endpoint instability as the next data requirement.

## Evidence limits and verification boundary

The strongest computable claim is about incremental prediction of a prespecified eICU chart-derived trajectory. Respiratory settings, airway intervals, vital signs, and treatment strings are proxies; they do not establish actual ventilator delivery, SBT performance, extubation readiness, extubation, reintubation, appropriateness, quality, or benefit. A care-plan label remains an observational exposure affected by clinician selection and hospital workflow; no causal effect estimate is authorized. Device-linked ventilator waveforms, synchronized bedside orders, standardized units, validated extubation/SBT adjudication, clinician review, and external replication would be required for stronger conclusions.

A verifier can check cohort identity and hashes, label/D reconstruction, source-column isolation of the primary endpoint, fixed windows and thresholds, all-window missingness, trajectory class assignment, held-out hospital separation, bootstrap clustering, model deltas, placebo timing, and gate logic. It cannot verify intended semantics of `cplitemvalue`, whether numeric chart entries reflect delivered settings, or whether an apparent trajectory represents clinical improvement; those require site metadata, expert adjudication, or another study.
