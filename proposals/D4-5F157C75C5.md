# UKB successor: landmarked metabolic change and route-specific transition to a second cardiometabolic domain

## Scientific deliverable and unresolved claim

The deliverable is a newly computed, source-audited comparison of two prognostic models in a precisely defined two-panel UK Biobank cohort. Completion requires a frozen codebook and cohort-flow table; an audit of repeat-panel selection and timing; fitted route/death hazards; route-specific five-year cumulative-incidence predictions; participant-level uncertainty; locked-test calibration, Brier, discrimination and decision-curve results; and the immediate-versus-lagged sensitivity analysis described below. A readiness probe, a significant coefficient, or a better fit is not a completed scientific result.

The inspected evidence supports the narrower claim that baseline metabolic indices are associated with cardiometabolic disease and that multistate models can describe first-disease-to-multimorbidity transitions. A recent UKB study of composite metabolic indices reported associations with incidence and progression and modest incremental prediction, but it did not establish whether a dated within-person change between two locally verified assay panels adds information beyond the current level after a single cardiometabolic domain is already established. The frozen Europe PMC record read for context is DOI 10.1186/s12933-026-03260-7, source ID [source checksum]; its population and index definitions differ from this design.

The falsifiable primary hypothesis is:

> In UKB participants aged 40–69 with exactly one first-coded domain (diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or chronic kidney disease) present by panel 0 and still no second domain at a later valid panel, annualized worsening of HbA1c, triglycerides and/or HDL contains route-specific prognostic information for acquisition of a second coded domain after a one-year lag, beyond panel-1 levels, existing-domain identity, disease duration and prespecified covariates.

This is a prognostic and internal-prediction claim about coded events in a repeat-tested subset. It is not a causal effect of changing a biomarker, evidence that repeat monitoring improves outcomes, a treatment recommendation, or a claim of adjudicated disease onset.

## Population, phenotype and the repaired temporal estimand

Freeze the phenotype codebook before fitting:

- diabetes: ICD-10 E10–E14 or ICD-9 250;
- cardiovascular disease: ICD-10 I20–I25, I50, I60–I69 or ICD-9 410–414, 428, 430–438;
- chronic kidney disease: ICD-10 N18/N19 or ICD-9 585;
- negative control: acute appendicitis, ICD-10 K35 or ICD-9 540.

Scan every available code/date suffix. Pair a code only with the date at the identical suffix; require a valid parsed date; take the earliest valid paired date for each domain, not the first array position. Code-only/date-only mismatches are audited and do not create events. The resulting dates are first coded occurrences in this administrative export, not confirmed clinical onset.

The baseline panel is `d0=53-0.0`; the repeat panel is `d1=53-1.0`. Require age 40–69 at recruitment, valid sex, d0<d1, a 365–3,650-day gap, agreement of main and assessment dates, and finite HbA1c, triglycerides and HDL at both panels. This is a material repair to the prior complete-case rule: the primary metabolic question no longer conditions on creatinine and cystatin-C availability. Those markers are a labeled secondary renal-extension cohort, not hidden eligibility criteria.

At d0, require exactly one of the three domains with its first valid coded date on or before d0. At d1, require the same one domain, no second domain, and survival through d1. Define disease duration as (d1 minus the first coded date)/365.25 and include it in both models. The primary landmark is L=d1+365 days. The primary risk set consists only of people alive and with no second coded domain through L; follow-up starts at L and ends at the first new domain, death, administrative end, or five years after L. Death is absorbing and a same-day event/death tie assigns the event to death, with the reverse convention as a sensitivity.

This makes the estimand explicit:

> Five-year route-specific cumulative incidence, conditional on the participant having a valid repeat metabolic panel, exactly one coded domain at d1, and being alive and second-domain-free at the one-year landmark L.

The one-year lag is a scientific guard against a panel taken during an incipient second disease. It is also a selection event. The solver must report the number and characteristics of participants removed by the lag, not describe the primary result as risk from the general UKB population.

A prespecified secondary estimand starts follow-up at d1 and counts second-domain events and death immediately, while retaining the same d0/d1 eligibility. It tests whether the conclusion is driven by the washout. A further sensitivity varies the lag over 0, 2 and 5 years, the panel gap over 182–5,475 days, and the same-day death tie. Participants whose first domain occurs only in (d0,d1] remain a labeled interval-onset analysis; they are not pooled into the primary established-domain estimand.

For marker j, compute annualized change g_j=(x_j1−x_j0)/((d1−d0)/365.25), reversing HDL so larger values mean worsening. Standardize using fit-partition quantities only. The primary three-marker feature set is the estimand-defining metabolic input. The secondary renal extension adds creatinine and cystatin-C levels and changes, only if route support and missingness permit; it cannot rewrite the primary result.

## Repeat-panel selection repair

The repeat panel is not a random follow-up visit. First, construct a d0-eligible source pool using only information available at d0: age, sex, existing domain and coded-domain duration, panel-0 metabolic values, BMI/BP/smoking/center when valid, prior code counts and death/diagnosis history through d0. Define R=1 only when a valid d1 date, all three primary assays, and date agreement are present within the prespecified gap. Report R by domain, sex, age, center, baseline values, disease duration and gap.

Among people who remain alive and second-domain-free at d1, fit a prespecified observation model for R using the d0 variables, with cross-fitting inside the training partition. Check overlap/positivity by domain and use stabilized inverse-probability-of-repeat weights only if the observed probability range and effective sample size are adequate. Truncate weights at fit-defined training quantiles, freeze those rules, and report weighted versus unweighted results. If positivity fails, the weighted analysis is inconclusive and the primary claim remains explicitly conditional on repeat-panel availability. No weight can recover people who died or acquired a second domain before d1; that limitation is part of the estimand.

The primary analysis is therefore a transparent conditional complete-case landmark analysis, not a claim that change predicts progression for all UKB participants. The weighted analysis is a transport sensitivity, not a license to assert population-level clinical utility.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

Both models use exactly the primary landmark cohort, route/death outcomes, censoring, split, feature preprocessing, missingness policy, and uncertainty procedure.

M0 is the simple current-level competing-risk Cox baseline. It fits cause-specific hazards for each supported transition from the established domain (D→C, D→K, C→D, C→K, K→D, K→C) and a death hazard. Predictors are panel-1 HbA1c, triglycerides and HDL, existing domain, disease duration, age, sex, panel gap, and the prespecified latest BMI/BP/smoking/center covariates. Current metabolic terms and continuous covariates use fit-frozen restricted cubic splines so that any M1 gain is not merely a current-level linearity artifact. Unsupported routes are not pooled to manufacture power. Five-year route-specific cumulative incidence is obtained from the fitted competing hazards, with “any second domain” reported only as a derived summary.

M1 is a mechanistic, route-aware metabolic-axis transition model on the identical state space. It adds the dated changes as three prespecified worsening axes—glycaemic change (HbA1c), atherogenic change (TG increase and HDL decrease), and their joint worsening—and allows each axis to have a route-specific effect and a prespecified interaction with the established domain. It uses partial pooling/shrinkage across routes only when the training partition shows adequate event support; otherwise it reports the route as unsupported rather than changing the estimand. The death hazard is kept identical in form and predictors in M0 and M1. This alternative can reveal whether historical metabolic deterioration is selectively associated with a particular next domain, or only indicates nonspecific frailty; M0, which sees the latest level, cannot answer that pathway-order question.

The primary scientific contrast is the route-specific M1 change-axis association and the five-year cumulative-incidence contrast at fixed current levels, disease duration and covariates. The predictive contrast is paired M1−M0 performance on the locked test set. A small predictive gain without stable route direction, calibration improvement or interpretable axis pattern is not evidence for a clinically useful progression mechanism.

A learned nonlinear competing-risk GAM with the same inputs, routes, split and locked metrics was developed as a possible extension. It could reveal thresholded or nonlinear change/current-level combinations, but the two-panel sparse subset and administrative phenotype make a high-capacity result difficult to distinguish from repeat-testing indication and regression to the mean. It is deferred unless M1 shows stable signal and a bounded locked diagnostic demonstrates value beyond M1. A recurrent/attention model is likewise deferred: two verified assay panels provide little sequence structure, not because neural models or GPUs are categorically excluded. A four-instance slope or mixed-effects trajectory is rejected because the complete main header contains no verified assay instances 2/3.

The selected M1 is CPU-first: small tabular Cox/multistate fits and 200 participant resamples are proportionate to the question. The future solver planning envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM and 28,800 seconds; full-fit and bootstrap time are unmeasured. Discovery had a separate 7,200-second science budget. The deployment has allocated-GPU capability, but no GPU is scientifically needed for the selected design; any future learned branch must request an allocated GPU explicitly and report measured resources. This is a compute tradeoff, not a scientific restriction.

## Split, uncertainty and required outputs

Hash participants, never rows, as SHA-256 of the frozen namespace `ehr-hypothesis-discovery-v1`, dataset and `eid`, modulo 100: 0–59 fit, 60–69 model/weight/threshold decisions, 70–79 locked test, and 80–99 reserved and unread. No model, threshold, weight rule or axis interaction is selected using the locked test.

Report:

1. source hashes, exact headers, join checks, code/date mismatch counts and complete cohort flow;
2. d0/d1 date-gap, repeat-panel selection, landmark attrition, route event-support and effective-sample-size diagnostics;
3. M0/M1 route coefficients or smooth/axis contrasts with 95% intervals;
4. five-year route-specific CIFs, survival/death CIF, integrated/time-dependent Brier score, calibration intercept/slope/error, discrimination, restricted mean event-free survival, and paired participant-bootstrap intervals for M1−M0;
5. fixed 5%, 10% and 20% five-year operating points, with sensitivity, specificity, PPV, number needed to review and decision-curve net benefit. These are hypothetical review operating points, not validated clinical cutoffs;
6. unweighted/weighted repeat-panel analyses, 0/2/5-year lag analyses, gap and phenotype sensitivities, renal extension, negative-control and permutation results.

Use 200 participant-level bootstrap resamples, preserving the frozen split and all preprocessing decisions. Preserve seeds, indices, convergence warnings, weight truncation, route support and failed fits. If a route lacks prespecified support, do not merge it after seeing results.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following at a level compatible with the available event support: a worsening-direction M1 axis association after current levels and disease duration; route specificity or a coherent nonspecific-versus-route-specific pattern; locked-test calibration/Brier or decision-curve improvement that is not a chance-compatible isolated discrimination change; stability across reasonable lag/gap, missingness and repeat-selection analyses; no comparable appendicitis signal; and no axis signal after within-domain, sex- and gap-bin permutation of changes. Immediate-post-panel and one-year-landmark results should be compared rather than selectively reported.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed change association with useful precision, disappearance after disease-duration/current-level adjustment, worse locked calibration or Brier performance, a comparable appendicitis association, a strong repeat-selection dependence, or a permutation signal. This falsifies the specified incremental prognostic claim in the repeat-tested landmark cohort; it does not prove that metabolic change is biologically harmless or has no causal effect.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few repeat panels or route events, failed positivity, substantial main/assessment date disagreement, severe subgroup calibration failure, nonconvergence, intervals spanning both clinically meaningful and negligible effects, or a result that changes materially between coded-phenotype definitions. It is also inconclusive for clinical policy if a performance result has no expert-approved action, burden/cost specification or external validation.

Computationally checkable claims include source/header hashes, joins, date parsing, code/date pairing, first coded-domain dates, cohort flow, landmark eligibility, selection weights, fitted hazards, predictions, uncertainty, calibration, Brier, decision curves, permutations and sensitivity analyses. Clinical adjudication of onset, medication/adherence and repeat-testing indication; treatment effects; external transport; review burden; and benefit from acting on a risk score require unavailable evidence, expert review or another study. Administrative codes cannot establish that a second domain truly began at its recorded date.

The natural-history/Delphi material supports learning from ordered dated histories, but this is not a reproduction and no external Danish validation is claimed. The Bayesian/ALADYNOULLI material motivates latent longitudinal alternatives, but verified local genetic inputs are not required or available for this question and no genetic reproduction is claimed. The relevant expert seed was UKB-09, “metabolic deterioration and multimorbidity.” It was materially repaired with a stable established-domain estimand, exact two-panel bindings, explicit landmark selection and route-specific outcomes. Other UKB seeds remain deferred because their imaging, accelerometry, proteomics, CHIP sequence/clone, cancer staging/treatment or polygenic inputs are unavailable or unverified; deferral is not disproof. The future solver must report a conclusion linked to computed outputs and uncertainty, and must not upgrade this proposal into causal or clinical-truth claims.
