# MerTK-dependent macrophage-to-fibroblast signaling drives fibroblast activation in rheumatoid arthritis

## Seed
D3-M2 (Directional cell communication)

## Domain
Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses

## Question
In rheumatoid arthritis synovial fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS), does MerTK inhibition specifically block the pro-inflammatory signaling from MerTK/CD206-positive macrophages that drives fibroblast activation, or does the inhibitor have off-target effects on FLS independent of macrophage contact?

## Hypothesis
MerTK-positive (Mpos) macrophages activate FLS through a MerTK-dependent pathway. Inhibiting MerTK on macrophages should reduce FLS activation markers (IL6, TNF, IL1B, CCL2, CXCL8, TGFB1) in co-culture, but should not affect FLS in the absence of macrophages. If the inhibitor has off-target effects on FLS, we would expect to see changes in FLS-only + Inhibitor samples.

## Clinical importance
Rheumatoid arthritis is characterized by synovial inflammation and joint destruction. Macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk is a key driver of disease progression [K1]. Understanding whether MerTK is a specific mediator of macrophage-to-fibroblast signaling could identify a therapeutic target for blocking this interaction [K2]. MerTK is already being explored as a target in other inflammatory diseases, and this study would provide mechanistic evidence for its role in RA [K3].

## Data
- **E-MTAB-8873** (targeted assay, 445 features, 33 samples):
  - FLS-only samples (4 donors: SA256A, SA257A, SA258R, SA263R)
  - FLS + Mpos co-culture (4 donors)
  - FLS + Mneg co-culture (4 donors)
  - FLS + Mpos + Mneg co-culture (2 donors)
  - FLS + Inhibitor (4 donors)
  - FLS + Mpos + Inhibitor (4 donors)
  - FLS + Mneg + Inhibitor (4 donors)
  - FLS + Mpos + preLPS (4 donors)
  - FLS + Mneg + preLPS (4 donors)
  - FLS + Mpos + preInhibitor (2 donors)
  - FLS + Mneg + preInhibitor (2 donors)
  - FLS + Mpos + Mneg + 1Mpos + 3Mneg (2 donors)
  - FLS + Mpos + Mneg + 3Mpos + 1Mneg (2 donors)

## Methods
### Baseline: Donor-aware pseudobulk comparison
1. For each FLS donor, average the repeated measurements within each condition
2. Compare FLS-only vs FLS+Mpos (Mpos effect)
3. Compare FLS+Mpos vs FLS+Mpos+Inhibitor (MerTK-specific effect)
4. Compare FLS+Mneg vs FLS+Mneg+Inhibitor (off-target control)
5. Use paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test for each donor pair
6. Report effect sizes and p-values for each gene

### Alternative: Mixed-effects model
1. Fit a linear mixed-effects model with donor as a random effect
2. Fixed effects: condition (FLS-only, Mpos, Mneg, Mpos+Mneg), treatment (none, inhibitor, LPS)
3. Test the interaction between condition and treatment
4. Report gene-level coefficients and p-values

### Key genes
- MERTK (receptor)
- IL6, TNF, IL1B, CCL2, CXCL8, TGFB1 (inflammatory cytokines)
- CD163, MRC1, CD80, CD86 (macrophage markers)
- AXL, TREM2, APOE (related receptors)
- GAPDH (housekeeping)

## Falsification criteria
- **Supportive**: MerTK inhibition reduces FLS activation markers in Mpos co-culture but not in Mneg co-culture or FLS-only
- **Adverse**: MerTK inhibition has no effect on FLS activation markers, or has similar effects in Mpos and Mneg co-culture
- **Inconclusive**: Insufficient power due to small sample size, or conflicting results across donors

## Limitations
- Targeted assay (445 genes), not whole-transcriptome
- Multiple donors per sample (requires_contributing_donor_model)
- No independent validation dataset
- Cross-sectional design (no time course)

## Next steps

2. Fit the mixed-effects model
3. Identify genes that are specifically affected by MerTK inhibition in Mpos co-culture
4. Propose a follow-up experiment with whole-transcriptome sequencing and more donors
