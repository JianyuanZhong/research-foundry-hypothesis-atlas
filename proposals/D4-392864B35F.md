# Episode 22 audit: route-tie and administrative-boundary repair

## Decision

The assigned candidate is scientifically strong and its participation/completeness repair should be retained. I found one substantive endpoint defect that warrants a child: it defines a death tie but not a tie in which two or three new domains receive the same first coded date. That ambiguity directly affects route-specific cause-specific hazards, cumulative incidence, event counts, and the supportive/adverse criteria.

A bounded read-only probe of the first 10,000 rows of the exact health-outcomes source found 158 earliest-domain records with simultaneous first dates for at least two target domains: 100 diabetes+CVD, 34 CKD+CVD, 22 CKD+diabetes, and 2 all three. This is not a cohort estimate, but it demonstrates that the omission is operationally material.

The child retains the parent’s conditional coded-event prognosis question and repairs route/event construction. It also makes the absence of a documented administrative completeness cutoff an explicit gate rather than allowing the maximum observed date to be silently treated as a censoring date.

## Repaired endpoint

Use the parent’s exact code families:

- diabetes: ICD-10 E10--E14 or ICD-9 250;
- CVD: ICD-10 I20--I25, I50, I60--I69 or ICD-9 410--414, 428, 430--438;
- CKD: ICD-10 N18/N19 or ICD-9 585.

Normalize code case and whitespace. A domain observation exists only for a same-array-position code/date pair with a parseable ISO date. For each domain, its recorded date is the minimum valid paired date across all 259 ICD-10 and 47 ICD-9 positions. Code-only and date-only cells are audited but do not create an event.

The population, d0/d1 dates, gap, age, sex, paired assays, one-domain stability, death exclusion, duration and assessment-linked annualized changes remain exactly as in the parent. Primary time zero remains d1, and coded dates are recorded-event dates, not adjudicated clinical onset.

For every participant after d1, calculate the earliest date strictly greater than d1 on which a previously absent second target domain has a valid paired event. Define the primary event as:

- `SECOND_DOMAIN_UNIQUE`: exactly one new domain has the earliest date;
- `SECOND_DOMAIN_MULTI`: at least two new domains share that earliest date;
- `DEATH`: death is on or before the earliest new-domain date, including a same-day tie;
- `CENSOR`: neither event occurs before the applicable censoring boundary.

The primary scientific estimand is time to any second-domain event, with `SECOND_DOMAIN_MULTI` retained as a distinct event cause. This avoids arbitrary route assignment and uses all supported events. Route-specific claims are secondary cause-specific analyses for DM, CVD, CKD and the multi-domain cause; a route is interpreted only when it is the unique first new domain. If the same domain has multiple qualifying code/date positions, use its minimum date once.

Report a full tie table by existing d1 domain, event category, exact event date, split, and locked-test status. Report counts of same-day multi-domain events and same-day death ties. The verifier must be able to recompute the event category from the frozen hashed participant file and paired outcome fields.

## Administrative boundary repair

The catalog metadata says exact dates remain a source limitation and does not provide a documented outcome-registry closure date. Therefore the maximum finite date observed in `41280-*`, `41281-*`, and `40000-*` is named `C_obs`, not `C_admin`. It may be reported as a candidate observed-date bound, but it is not evidence by itself that all participants have complete follow-up through that date.

The solver must emit `censoring_audit.json` containing the source-level cutoff evidence, `C_obs`, date range, dates after each candidate boundary, and the number of participants whose nominal five-year horizon exceeds the supported boundary. If no source-level closure evidence is available in the configured files, five-year cumulative-incidence, calibration and decision-curve claims are marked administratively unverified and cannot receive a supportive conclusion. The analysis may still show predictions and coded-event associations under the explicit observed-boundary assumption, but the interpretation is conditional on that assumption. This is an evidence-bound limitation, not a reason to invent a cutoff.

## Models and comparison

Preserve matched B0/M1/M2:

- B0: route/multi-domain/death cause-specific Cox hazards using panel-1 levels, existing domain, duration, age, sex, gap, BMI, SBP, DBP, smoking and center.
- M1: B0 plus three assessment-linked changes, with pre-specified change-by-existing-domain terms only where event support permits.
- M2: B0 plus fixed fit-partition common-axis deterioration, `deltaS`, and pre-specified domain interactions.

All use the same stable one-domain cohort, time zero, causes including `SECOND_DOMAIN_MULTI` and death, preprocessing, participant split, observed-boundary handling, weights, uncertainty and locked evaluation. M1 is the incremental-information test; M2 is the scientifically interpretable common-axis alternative. A small score difference alone is not a scientific attribution.

The two-stage participation flow remains: d0 eligibility; observed d1 date/return; d1 stable one-domain survival; complete d0 assays; complete d1 assays; final endpoint support. Stage-3 return modeling is descriptive. Assay-completeness IPCW is conditional on observed d1 and is not a correction for nonreturners. Any return weighting is exploratory and assumption-dependent.

## Verifier-ready outputs and interpretation

Require:

- source/header/hash and one-to-one join audit;
- participant-level flow and missingness table;
- paired code/date audit, domain first-date table and tie table;
- `C_obs`/administrative-evidence audit;
- frozen hashed participant event file with d1, event date, event category, cause, death tie flag, censor boundary and split;
- B0/M1/M2 coefficients, route/death/multi-domain cumulative incidence, locked predictions, calibration, Brier, discrimination, RMST and paired 200-resample intervals;
- M1--B0, M2--B0 and M1--M2 contrasts, including composite and supported unique-route results;
- assay-completeness, lag, gap, phenotype, appendicitis and within-state permutation sensitivities;
- a conclusion whose label is linked to these files.

Supportive requires at least 20 total and 5 locked-test primary composite second-domain events, adequate route/multi-domain support for any route-specific claim, no material tie-construction or weighting failure, a pre-specified worsening-direction interval excluding the null, and locked-test uncertainty supporting an incremental metric improvement versus B0. Five-year claims additionally require administrative cutoff evidence; otherwise the conclusion is at most supportive of the observed-boundary coded-event association and inconclusive for five-year prognosis.

Adverse is an informative null/reversal, disappearance after duration or assay-completeness adjustment, comparable appendicitis/permutation signal, or no incremental information. Inconclusive includes inadequate composite or route support, positivity/effective-sample-size failure, unstable paired dates/ties, unverified administrative completeness for a claimed horizon, poor calibration/convergence, or intervals spanning meaningful benefit and no benefit. These labels concern conditional coded-event prognosis only.

Clinical onset, treatment/adherence, causal effects, monitoring benefit, nonreturner transport, utility, policy validity and route meaning beyond recorded coding require adjudication, unavailable evidence, expert review or another study.
