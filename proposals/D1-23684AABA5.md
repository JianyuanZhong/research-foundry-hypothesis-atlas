# Robustness of time-weighted hypotension burden for early AKI: summary choice, measurement-density and baseline-severity controls

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP), after controlling for measurement density, baseline illness severity, and after showing that the signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined)?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density. The strongest rival is that the apparent burden signal is an artifact of recording density, measurement timing, or baseline illness severity, or that it is specific to one arbitrary choice of blood-pressure summary. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

Minimum systolic blood pressure is a common bedside summary, but it can understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU 2.0 adult ICU admissions with at least one systolic blood pressure measurement in the first 24 hours of ICU care. Exclude patients with missing baseline kidney function, missing APACHE IV or SOFA score, or missing outcome data. The primary analysis window is the first 24 hours of ICU care; the outcome window is AKI within 48 hours of ICU admission.

## Data bindings

- **Source**: eICU 2.0 (PhysioNet)
- **Tables**: `patient`, `vital`, `lab`, `infusion`, `outcome`
- **Join keys**: `patient_id` (all tables)
- **Time fields**: `time` (all tables), `admission_time` (patient table)
- **Required columns**:
  - `patient`: `age`, `sex`, `race`, `admission_time`, `discharge_time`, `apache4s`, `sofa`
  - `vital`: `systolic_bp`, `diastolic_bp`, `time`
  - `lab`: `creatinine`, `time`
  - `infusion`: `vasopressor`, `time`
  - `outcome`: `aki_48h`, `time`

## Outcome definition

AKI within 48 hours is defined as a rise in serum creatinine of ≥0.3 mg/dL from baseline within 48 hours of ICU admission, or a rise of ≥2× baseline creatinine within 48 hours.

## Baseline and alternative models

- **Baseline**: 24-hour MAP (mean arterial pressure)
- **Alternative**: Time-weighted hypotension burden (prespecified formula)
- **Comparison**: Both models adjusted for measurement density, baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, and hospital.

## Robustness analysis

1. **Summary choice**: Repeat the analysis using MAP, systolic, diastolic, and combined blood-pressure summaries.
2. **Measurement density**: Control for the number of blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours.
3. **Baseline severity**: Stratify by APACHE IV and SOFA score; perform sensitivity analysis for missing severity data.

## Falsification criteria

The burden signal is falsified if:
1. The burden measure does not improve prediction beyond MAP after controlling for measurement density and baseline severity.
2. The signal is not robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined).
3. The signal disappears in severity-stratified analysis.
4. The signal is driven by measurement-density artifacts.

## Solver deliverable

The solver must fit both the baseline and alternative models, report coefficients, AUC, calibration metrics, and hospital-level analysis. Confidence intervals and uncertainty estimates are required.
