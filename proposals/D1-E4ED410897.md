# Episode 9 child proposal: temporal and competing-operational-care boundary for day-42 HCC assay state

Status: design only. This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. No cohort fit, model output, favorable finding, clinical response, treatment-intent, causal, or benefit claim is made.

## Scientific question and deliverable

The primary question remains:

> Among patients observable at day 42 after a first locally recorded exact-code TACE, does the recorded assay-state channel add patient-held-out information about a later locally recorded exact-code repeat-TACE event in [43,181) beyond matched observation and care-process information?

The Episode-9 repair addresses one remaining interpretation boundary: a pooled [43,181) assay increment could be concentrated in the first weeks after the landmark, when planned review and operational care are most intense, rather than represent information that remains useful later. The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Conditional on the inherited day-42 population and feature channels, the assay-state increment is not solely a near-term workflow marker: adding assay state will improve held-out prediction for the overall [43,181) repeat-TACE endpoint, and a supported late [91,181) increment will remain after early [43,91) risk and first recorded non-TACE operational care are treated as competing first-recorded events.

This is deliberately narrower than “the assays measure tumor response.” The experiment can test predictive/descriptive persistence in the local record. It cannot identify biological state, treatment intent, imaging response, mechanism, causality, or benefit.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted, patient-held-out estimates and uncertainty for:

1. the inherited primary `D_state = L_Y(A)-L_Y(B)` for the first repeat TACE in [43,181);
2. an early cause-specific increment `D_E = L_E(A)-L_E(B)` for a first repeat TACE in [43,91);
3. a late cause-specific increment `D_L = L_L(A)-L_L(B)` in the risk set still free of early repeat TACE and first competing non-TACE care through day 90;
4. a competing operational-care increment `D_C = L_C(A)-L_C(B)` for the first non-TACE procedure before any repeat TACE in [43,181).

Completion requires frozen cohort/event audits, development-only preprocessing and fits, untouched-test predictions and losses for both models and all supported contrasts, calibration/weighted-ESS reports, paired patient-bootstrap intervals, timing/process falsifications, a prespecified multiplicity report, and a claim-to-output map. A favorable result is not completion.

## Evidence, opening, and competing explanations

[K1] shows that a CT/radiomics/deep-learning model can predict prognosis around repeat TACE in multicenter cohorts, but its imaging payload and external-cohort limitations are not available in this HCC snapshot. [K2] makes the clinical response boundary explicit: HCC treatment effect is assessed using modality- and criterion-specific imaging, including necrosis-aware mRECIST/RECICL concepts. [K3] shows why recorded response comparisons remain vulnerable to missing outcomes, ascertainment, exposure classification, and treatment-selection confounding. These works support the unresolved opening but do not answer it.

The leading explanation is that assay values/states contain durable recorded information about a patient’s state. The strongest rivals are:

- near-term workflow or planned follow-up: assay recording and review occur close to a subsequent repeat code;
- generic operational care: an assay increment predicts a first non-TACE procedure just as it predicts repeat TACE;
- severity/measurement: latent severity drives both assay measurement and later procedures, and timing only changes the intensity of observation.

The timing/competing-care comparison distinguishes observable predictions, not mechanisms. Durable-state-consistent evidence is a primary increment plus a supported late increment that remains after the prespecified timing controls and is not comparable to `D_C`. Workflow-consistent evidence is an early-only increment, attenuation after removing days 36–42 or truncating process information at day 28, or a similar assay increment for `C`. A late increment is still only recorded predictive information. A null or imprecise decomposition is inconclusive, not evidence that no biological signal exists.

## Population, landmark, and primary endpoint

All elapsed times are integer days from `T0`, the index procedure start.

- Index: the first row in `procedures` with nonmissing `患者主索引`, `就诊号`, and `开始时间`, and trimmed, Unicode-case-folded `手术` exactly equal to `tace`. “First” means first in this supplied snapshot, not first-ever TACE.
- Collapse exact duplicate patient/start rows for event counting while retaining duplicate counts in the audit. Undated strict-code rows are retained in an audit and cannot be timed.
- Eligibility: maximum valid timestamp across the bound sources reaches `T0+42`. Valid timestamps are source-specific as listed below; invalid/missing timestamps never extend observation.
- Exclude from the day-42 landmark risk set any later exact-code TACE with `0 < Delta < 43`; retain all such rows and counts in `early_repeat_audit.csv`. An untimed repeat is not a timed negative.
- Primary `Y`: first later unique patient/start exact-code TACE with `43 <= Delta < 181`. Follow-up ends at this event, valid all-source `obs_end`, or day 181. Primary person-day bins are [43,91) and [91,181); complete 180-day follow-up is sensitivity only.
- No predictor uses future events, `obs_end`, post-index TACE rows, or any record after the day-42 feature cutoff. Exact TACE rows never enter predictors.

The primary population, landmark, endpoint family, and evidence limits are unchanged from the parent.

## Minimal temporal/competing-operational-care decomposition

The primary `Y` remains the union endpoint and the only primary decision. The new decomposition is a secondary family on the same A/B channel contrast.

### Early and late repeat-TACE causes

- `E`: first unique repeat-TACE event with [43,91) days.
- `L`: first unique repeat-TACE event with [91,181) days, in the risk set that has no `E` and no prior `C` first-operational event through day 90.
- For the timing risk sets, a first `C` event exits the risk set as a competing operational event; this is a cause-specific first-recorded-event estimand, not a claim that the procedure biologically prevents later TACE.
- The late estimate is explicitly conditional on surviving the early/competing-care selection. It is not a marginal late risk and is not substituted for `D_state`.
- For an exact same patient/start timestamp containing both exact TACE and non-TACE rows, classify the first event as TACE for the first-event decomposition; preserve the tie audit. Exact duplicate patient/start rows are otherwise collapsed.

### Competing operational-care event

`C` is the first unique non-TACE procedure row for the same patient with nonmissing start time, a trimmed Unicode-case-folded `手术` token not exactly equal to `tace`, and [43,181), provided no repeat-TACE event occurs earlier. It uses no semantic procedure list and no inference about intent. It is an operational first-recorded-care event, not a clinical competing risk, response, planned treatment, or harm endpoint.

For `C`, exact repeat TACE is the competing event. For `E` and `L`, `C` is a competing first-event exit. Administrative/all-source observation loss remains right censoring with development-fitted weights. There is no mortality or other clinical competing event in this snapshot. The inherited parent `Y_NC` secondary is retained unchanged as a support-gated, non-substitutable horizon analysis; `C` is a separate mutually exclusive first-event decomposition and cannot rescue or redefine `Y_NC`.

### Support and precision gates

Before interpreting any timing result:

- The inherited overall primary gate must pass: at least 50 primary events overall, at least 15 untouched-test primary events, at least 100 test patients, at least 15 development events, completed duplicate/timing/`obs_end` audits, finite weights, and weighted ESS >= 100.
- Each of `E`, `L`, and `C` needs at least 15 development events, 15 untouched-test events, 100 relevant test patients where defined, finite weights, and weighted ESS >= 100. The late gate counts only the late conditional risk set. These are feasibility gates, not clinical importance thresholds.
- If a type fails support, retain a row with `inconclusive_support_failure`, exact counts and reason; do not pool event types, substitute `Y_NC`, or report a directional result.
- A supported timing contrast is called precise only if the two-sided 95% patient-bootstrap interval half-width is <=0.05 for log-loss contrast and <=0.02 for Brier contrast, with finite calibration outputs. Otherwise it is `inconclusive_precision_failure`, even if the point estimate is positive.
- The inherited exact `W_packet` and `Y_NC` support/precision gates remain exactly as in the parent. No proxy-stratum selection or post hoc support rescue is permitted.

## Models, estimand, split, and uncertainty

### Primary transparent model

Fit an elastic-net pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific hazard with fixed intercepts for [43,91) and [91,181). The union endpoint uses the inherited person-days and censoring. The temporal first-event decomposition uses cause-specific heads for E, L, and C; when estimating E/L, the other first-event type exits the risk set. No Fine–Gray estimand, counterfactual risk, or intervention interpretation is used.

Fit the same prespecified channel series for every supported outcome: `M0`, `A=M0+P_obs+P_care`, `B=A+S`. The primary and each secondary increment is paired held-out loss `L(A)-L(B)`, positive when assay state lowers loss. The parent-defined `A*`/`B*` W adjustment, `Y_NC`, and their exact support gates are fitted only as specified by the parent.

Report weighted log loss as primary, Brier/integrated Brier and calibration slope/intercept as required secondary metrics, and AUROC/AUPRC descriptively. Report ordinary and observation-weighted estimates, censoring/assay-observation weight tails and ESS. Fit all encoders, scaling, imputation, dictionaries, cutpoints, hyperparameters, and weights inside development folds.

### Matched time-aware learned alternative

Fit a compact irregular-event GRU on the same (-365,+42] stream, patient split, outcome heads, A/B ablations, weights, metrics, and falsification controls. Each event token contains only allowed category/assay label, audited numeric value when in S, qualitative/inequality flag, relative time, elapsed time since prior event, decay, and channel tag. Use a discrete-time cause-specific likelihood, development-only normalization, fixed three development seeds, and development-only early stopping. The GRU is a substantive sensitivity because it can preserve nonlinear trajectories and order-to-exam timing that the summary elastic-net loses; it cannot identify intent, response, units, outside care, mechanism, or causality.

### Split and uncertainty

Create one deterministic patient split before fitting: 80% development/20% untouched test, stratified only on the union primary event indicator, seed 271828, no patient crosses partitions. Seeds 314159 and 161803 are fixed sensitivity splits and cannot select a favorable result. All folds, dictionaries, preprocessing, weight models and support summaries are development-only. Score the test once for the prespecified analysis.

Use patient-bootstrap resampling of the untouched test set, keeping all person-days and fixed weights together, for paired 95% intervals. Use one max-|t| family across the six named state contrasts `D_state`, `D_E`, `D_L`, `D_C`, supported `D_Wadj`, and supported parent `D_NC`; unsupported members are NA with exact reasons and are not silently counted as nulls. GRU, alternate splits, lead-time/process truncation, complete follow-up, and permutation outputs are sensitivities, not additional confirmatory hypotheses.

## Falsification and interpretation

Use the identical split, development folds, weights, support rules, and output schema for:

- remove all assay/process records in days (35,42] while keeping the day-42 landmark;
- truncate process features at day 28 while retaining assay state through day 42;
- permute assay timestamps within patient while preserving assay/result multisets and process counts;
- shuffle quantitative assay values within calendar and baseline-history blocks;
- permute process marks within calendar and event-count strata;
- permute `W_packet` labels within patient/index-calendar blocks;
- alternate valid `obs_end`, exclude undated strict-TACE histories, and run exact-code-only versus a separately frozen high-specificity alias sensitivity;
- development outcome-label permutation scored against untouched true-label test outcomes.

Supportive evidence for the narrow “not solely near-term workflow” claim requires the overall primary gate; a positive familywise lower bound for `D_state`; ordinary and observation-weighted agreement; acceptable A/B calibration; no comparable assay value/time-permutation increment; and a supported `D_L` that is not eliminated by the seven-day/process timing controls. This supports persistent recorded predictive information only.

Evidence adverse to that interpretation includes: an early-only `D_E` with unsupported `D_L`; attenuation after the day-35 removal or day-28 process truncation; a comparable `D_C`; or reproduction by assay timestamp/value permutations. A positive `D_C` is evidence that assay state tracks generic first recorded operational care, not proof of a biological or treatment-specific signal. A persistent `D_L` after C competition is compatible with durable recorded information but remains compatible with unmeasured severity and cannot establish tumor response or intent.

If `E`, `L`, or `C` lacks support or precision, report the exact inconclusive status and interpret only the supported primary/parent members. A mixed pattern routes to workflow/scheduling and severity review. A workflow-dominant pattern prioritizes scheduling/planning and outside-care linkage. A supported persistent pattern prioritizes linkage to adjudicated imaging, treatment intent, assay units/reference ranges, and expert severity review. No result supports selecting, withholding, or comparing TACE treatment.

Elastic-net is selected as primary because its channel increments, cause-specific risk sets, C competition, and proxy exclusions are directly auditable. If GPU is used, the solver must request an allocated device and use `cuda:0` inside that job; an unallocated shell CUDA check is not a feasibility test.

Rejected/deferred alternatives: W-stratum selection, broad proxy or semantic order lists, examination-finding NLP, a large transformer, an unconstrained latent multi-state model, Fine–Gray substitution, a treatment-intent or response-label model, and a mortality/benefit analysis. They either add post hoc multiplicity, require missing clinical semantics, or change the endpoint/evidence boundary without resolving the named timing question. Revisit the GRU only if it changes the timing/workflow interpretation on the same held-out estimand; revisit a larger latent model only after adjudicated imaging/intent or externally linked state targets exist. Revisit competing-care definitions only with a predeclared new source or clinical adjudication, never by selecting a favorable procedure subset.

## Evidence limits and next decision

This snapshot lacks imaging payloads, adjudicated mRECIST/RECICL response, treatment intent/scheduling, outside-care events, assay units/reference ranges, mortality, harms, costs, and expert severity review. Examination findings/diagnoses are not validated response adjudication and are not used for response labels or NLP. Therefore the strongest possible result is a reproducible local recorded-state increment with timing and workflow behavior; it is not clinical response, treatment benefit, causality, mechanism, or utility.

The next decision is data acquisition: workflow-dominant evidence prioritizes scheduling/intent/outside-care linkage; persistent late evidence prioritizes imaging/intent/units/severity adjudication; support or precision failure defers interpretation. This proposal makes no fitted-result claim.
