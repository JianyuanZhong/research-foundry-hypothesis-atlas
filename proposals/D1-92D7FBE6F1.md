# Suspected discordant renal recovery after early ICU AKI: episode 3 repair

## Scientific deliverable

Fit and evaluate a prespecified prognostic analysis in MIMIC-IV v3.1. The primary new estimate is the post-landmark in-hospital death contrast between patients with creatinine recovery but non-recovered Foley-recorded urine output and patients with recovery of both measures. The computational deliverable is complete only if it includes:

1. a provenance manifest, cohort flow, exact item/stay support, duplicate-resolution log, phenotype prevalence, outcome counts, coverage and missingness by phenotype;
2. a prespecified adjusted risk contrast with 95% confidence intervals and a time-to-event sensitivity analysis;
3. two newly fitted models on an identical subject-level split: a transparent creatinine-centered baseline and a nonlinear temporal model incorporating urine/output and fluid trajectories;
4. held-out AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, bootstrap uncertainty, and ablations showing whether urine/output information adds beyond creatinine;
5. threshold, measurement-density, source-specific-creatinine, unweighted-output and pre-landmark-RRT sensitivity analyses;
6. prespecified supportive, adverse and inconclusive reporting, including a no-go result if the phenotype or event support is inadequate.

No fitted clinical result is asserted in this proposal.

## Unresolved clinical claim

The strongest relevant available evidence supports a measurement concern, not the proposed prognostic claim. The inspected Haines et al. critical-illness study (CJASN 2023, DOI 10.2215/CJN.0000000000000203; local readable text at `references/expert-seeds/papers/kidney-function/article.readable.txt`) enrolled 38 selected mechanically ventilated patients and compared creatinine, cystatin C, iohexol clearance and muscle measurements. It reported increasing disagreement between creatinine-based and cystatin-C-based estimates during prolonged ICU stay and overestimation of measured GFR by creatinine near ICU discharge. That study did not establish the prevalence, prognostic value or mechanism of a Foley-output/creatinine discordance phenotype in this MIMIC snapshot. Cystatin C, measured GFR, muscle ultrasound and clinician adjudication are unavailable here.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among adults alive in their first ICU stay at a fixed 72-hour landmark who meet an observed early ICU creatinine-rise criterion and have dense paired measurements, creatinine recovery without concurrent Foley-recorded urine-output recovery is associated with higher subsequent in-hospital mortality than recovery of both measures.

This is a prognostic association with an observed phenotype. A supportive result would justify external validation using cystatin C or measured GFR and prospective renal-monitoring research; it would not show that creatinine recovery is harmful, that oliguria is the mechanism, or that a clinician should change treatment.

## Population and time zero

Use one index row per `hadm_id`: the earliest valid adult `icu/icustays` row for that hospitalization. Join:

- `icu/icustays` on `subject_id, hadm_id`, retaining `stay_id, intime, outtime, first_careunit, last_careunit, los`;
- `hosp/admissions` on `subject_id, hadm_id`, retaining `admittime, dischtime, deathtime, discharge_location, hospital_expire_flag`;
- `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`, retaining `anchor_age, gender`.

Exclude missing or invalid intervals, `anchor_age < 18`, later ICU stays for the primary analysis, and stays with `t0 = intime + 72 hours >= outtime`. Exclude a stay if `admissions.deathtime <= t0`. The analytic time zero is exactly `t0); all predictors and phenotype measurements must have timestamps strictly before or at `t0`, and follow-up starts after `t0`. MIMIC timestamps are subject-specific shifted times: calculate within-subject intervals only and do not compare calendar dates between subjects.

Use these non-overlapping windows relative to `intime):

- early: [0, 24) hours;
- rise: [24, 48) hours;
- recovery: [48, 72) hours.

Eligibility requires one valid creatinine in the early window, a valid creatinine in the rise window, and a valid creatinine in the recovery window. The requirement for a later-window rise prevents an early-only maximum from being mislabeled as the initiating rise.

## Creatinine phenotype

Use both of the available serum-creatinine representations:

- `icu/chartevents`, itemid 220615 (“Creatinine (serum)”), columns `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, charttime, valuenum, valueuom, itemid`;
- `hosp/labevents`, itemids 50912 (“Creatinine”) and 52546 (“Creatinine”), columns `subject_id, hadm_id, itemid, charttime, valuenum, valueuom, labevent_id`. Link the hospital rows to the index ICU by `subject_id,hadm_id` and timestamp containment in that ICU stay.

Join labels through `icu/d_items` and `hosp/d_labitems`. Retain positive numeric values in mg/dL, record the source table/itemid, remove exact duplicate event rows, and use a prespecified deterministic resolution for same-time cross-source duplicates (prefer the ICU chart value when the numeric values agree; otherwise retain a source-discordance flag and use the median for the primary summary, with source-specific analyses). Do not use non-mg/dL rows or textual values as numeric measurements.

Let:

- `C_early` be the minimum valid creatinine in [0,24);
- `C_rise` be the maximum valid creatinine in [24,48);
- `C_peak` be the maximum valid creatinine in [0,48);
- `C_late` be the minimum valid creatinine in [48,72).

Define observed early creatinine rise as `C_rise >= C_early + 0.3 mg/dL` OR `C_rise/C_early >= 1.5`. Define apparent creatinine recovery as `C_late <= C_peak - 0.3 mg/dL` OR `C_late/C_peak <= 0.75`. Preserve the continuous absolute and relative changes; do not call this a complete KDIGO adjudication because the available window lacks a reliable pre-ICU baseline and the phenotype is partly observation-dependent.

## Foley-output phenotype and measurement bias

Use `icu/outputevents`, linked by `stay_id`, with itemid 226559 (“Foley”), columns `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, charttime, storetime, itemid, value, valueuom`. Retain nonnegative numeric mL values. Exact duplicate rows (same stay, item, charttime, storetime and value) count once. Do not convert a missing chart to zero.

Bin valid Foley events by hour relative to `intime). For each 24-hour window require at least 18 distinct observed hourly bins, a first-to-last observed span of at least 20 hours, and no inter-observation gap over 4 hours. These are coverage criteria, not evidence that all urine was captured. Calculate observed 24-hour urine volume as the sum of deduplicated values divided by the full 24-hour window and weight; never divide by the number of observed bins. Weight is the latest positive kilogram measurement at or before `t0) from `icu/chartevents` itemids 226512 (“Admission Weight (Kg)”) or 224639 (“Daily Weight”), columns `stay_id, charttime, valuenum, valueuom`. If no valid weight exists, report an explicitly separate unweighted analysis and do not mix it into the primary weighted contrast.

Define late Foley-output recovery as observed volume >=0.5 mL/kg/hour in the recovery window. The primary contrast is:

- discordant recovery: apparent creatinine recovery plus late Foley-output non-recovery;
- concordant recovery reference: apparent creatinine recovery plus late Foley-output recovery.

The two other combinations are retained descriptively and in secondary models. The primary phenotype is “suspected discordant recovery,” not persistent renal dysfunction, measured low GFR or a validated oliguria diagnosis. The compiled audit must report coverage and missingness separately by phenotype; if the coverage rule produces too few patients or events, the primary result is inconclusive rather than imputed.

## Outcomes

The primary outcome is death after `t0) and no later than the same admission’s `dischtime`, using `admissions.deathtime` with the event required to satisfy `deathtime > t0` and `deathtime <= dischtime`. Use `hospital_expire_flag` only as a consistency check, not as an event time. Report absolute risks, risk difference and an adjusted association with 95% intervals; a cause-specific time-to-event model is secondary.

Secondary outcomes are:

- first later ICU readmission after the index `outtime) and before `dischtime`, from a later `icu/icustays` row with the same `subject_id,hadm_id`; analyze only among those discharged alive from the index ICU and treat death before readmission as a competing event;
- new post-landmark renal replacement, from `icu/procedureevents` itemids 225441 (Hemodialysis), 225802 (Dialysis-CRRT), 225803 (CVVHD), 225805 (Peritoneal Dialysis), 225809 (CVVHDF) or 225955 (SCUF), using `stay_id,itemid,starttime,endtime`;
- death or new renal replacement as a descriptive composite, never as a replacement for the primary outcome.

ICU and hospital length of stay are descriptive only because discharge and death induce selection.

## Predictors and leakage controls

Prespecify all predictors from data at or before `t0). Include age, sex, first care unit, ICU/hospital context, creatinine levels and slopes, measurement counts and missingness, and pre-landmark physiology:

- `icu/chartevents`: itemids 220181 (NBP mean), 220045 (heart rate), 220210 (respiratory rate), 220277 (SpO2), with `stay_id, charttime, valuenum, valueuom`;
- `hosp/labevents`: lactate itemids 50813, 52442 or 53154, with `subject_id,hadm_id,charttime,valuenum,valueuom`;
- `icu/inputevents`: timestamped exposure rows with `stay_id,starttime,endtime,itemid,amount,amountuom,rate,rateuom`; prespecified vasoactive itemids 221906, 221662, 221749, 229630, 229631, 229632, 221289 or 229617;
- `icu/procedureevents`: ventilation itemids 225792 and 225794, and the listed renal-replacement itemids;
- `icu/outputevents`: Foley output and a separate input-minus-output proxy using timestamped input/output rows;
- `hosp/diagnoses_icd` joined to `hosp/d_icd_diagnoses` by `icd_code,icd_version`, for an admission-coded CKD/ESRD proxy only.

Do not use `deathtime`, `hospital_expire_flag`, later ICU rows, post-`t0) treatments, discharge information, or post-landmark outcomes as predictors. Split by `subject_id), even if a subject has multiple admissions.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is a penalized logistic model for post-`t0) death using demographics/context, creatinine summaries and pre-landmark physiology/treatment indicators, with missingness indicators and no Foley-output or fluid-trajectory features. Its scientific role is to test whether the proposed urine-output information adds value beyond a transparent creatinine-centered risk model.

The substantive alternative is a CPU-fitted `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (or an equivalent Harbor-available nonlinear tree model) for the same binary outcome, population, time zero and predictor boundary. Create 6-hour bins over [0,72) for creatinine, Foley output, weight-standardized output, input-minus-output, MAP, heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO2, lactate, vasoactive exposure, ventilation and RRT. For each bin retain last, mean, minimum, maximum, slope/variation where identifiable, measurement count and missingness. Fit baseline and full models on the same 60/20/20 subject-level train/validation/test split; use grouped validation for tuning and never use the held-out test set for selection. Use a urine/fluid ablation to test the substantive information claim.

The alternative can reveal nonlinear timing, trajectory persistence and interactions—such as whether output non-recovery matters mainly after a creatinine peak—that a terminal creatinine summary loses. It is not allowed to rescue a failed phenotype contrast or to substitute predictive performance for clinical meaning. Budget approximately 2 CPUs, 8 GiB RAM and at most 90 minutes for extraction/feature construction/fitting after the support audit; no GPU is required. Preserve checkpoints and fail as inconclusive if calibration or event support makes uncertainty uninterpretable.

## Estimation, falsification and interpretation

First produce the frozen cohort and support audit. Then:

1. describe support, coverage, missingness and phenotype/outcome counts without suppressing exclusions;
2. estimate the primary contrast using a prespecified adjusted binomial/logistic model with robust subject-level uncertainty and standardized risks; report unadjusted and adjusted results;
3. fit the baseline and alternative under the identical split and report AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope and bootstrap intervals;
4. perform threshold sensitivity for absolute/relative creatinine recovery, 12/18/20-hour output coverage, unweighted output, each creatinine source, removal of pre-landmark RRT, and an expanded urinary-output set (`Foley`, `Void`, `Condom Cath`, `Suprapubic`, `Nephrostomy`, `Straight Cath`) only as a secondary measurement-sensitivity analysis;
5. run falsification checks: shuffle phenotype labels within the eligible cohort, permute event times within subject, compare an outcome window before `t0) where possible, and test whether source/table or chart-density indicators alone reproduce the apparent association;
6. report uncertainty intervals and instability rather than selecting the most favorable threshold.

Supportive evidence requires a directionally higher discordant primary risk with an interval excluding the null, persistence under dense coverage and source/threshold checks, and reproducible incremental information from urine/fluid trajectories beyond the baseline. Adverse evidence is a near-null or reversed primary contrast, especially with dense coverage; a better nonlinear score alone does not rescue it. Inconclusive evidence includes inadequate paired support, sparse events, extreme phenotype-dependent missingness, failed calibration, threshold instability, leakage, or inability to reproduce the support audit.

Computational verification can establish joins, timing, reproducibility, fitted estimates, uncertainty and whether conclusions follow from held-out outputs. It cannot establish true GFR, mechanism, completeness of urine capture, clinical utility, a causal treatment effect, or external validity. Those claims require cystatin C/measured GFR, muscle and volume-status assessment, treatment indications, clinician adjudication, and an external or prospective study.

## Demonstrations, seeds and deferred alternatives

The three demonstrations are not being reproduced. Delphi's dated structured-history idea is adapted only to the temporal comparison; its UK Biobank cohort and Danish external validation are unavailable. ALADYNOULLI's genetics-dependent analysis is not reproduced; an EHR-only latent trajectory model was considered but deferred because it would add assumptions without resolving the kidney measurement question. Oncoformer's multimodal image/EHR experiment is not reproduced because MIMIC-CXR images, the main article and complete STAR Methods are unavailable; a lab-only cancer adaptation is out of scope.

The MIMIC expert seeds were preserved and assessed as repairable. The selected branch derives from seed 03 (discordant recovery), with seed 01 asynchronous organ recovery and seed 10 ICU-discharge instability retained as alternatives. Diuretic timing/creatinine-during-diuresis (seeds 02 and 08) are deferred because treatment indication and volume status are incompletely observed; transfusion heterogeneity (09), antimicrobial de-escalation (07), persistent sedation (06), and stress hyperglycemia relative to HbA1c (05) remain clinically important but require different confounding or selective-measurement audits. A failure of renal support should trigger revisiting seed 10, not silently changing the outcome or population.

Supporting method evidence is `references/research-ambition/methods-and-compute.md`; the audited MIMIC source guide and exact table schemas are the binding evidence. The pending bounded support audit is a prerequisite and has produced no counts yet.
