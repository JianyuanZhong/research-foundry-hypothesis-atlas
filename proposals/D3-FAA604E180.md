# Permutation test for state-specific metabolic reprogramming in treatment-resistant RA macrophages

**Child of [prior hypothesis]** (D3-M3, E-MTAB-8322).
**Status: EXECUTED. Permutation test completed ([research job]).**

## Scientific question

The parent candidate (candidate-75da3dd9) executed a 5-condition gradient residual analysis on E-MTAB-8322 (n=25 independent donors) and found a nominally significant OLS Resistant coefficient (0.7900, p=0.0333) indicating that treatment-resistant RA macrophages have higher metabolic gene expression than predicted by their inflammatory state. However, non-parametric tests (Kruskal-Wallis p=0.083, Jonckheere-Terpstra p=0.134) did not reach significance.

**Question**: Is the OLS Resistant coefficient a real effect or an artifact of OLS model assumptions (normality, homoscedasticity)? A permutation test on the OLS residual provides a model-free p-value that does not depend on these assumptions.

## Hypothesis

Treatment-resistant RA synovial macrophages exhibit a state-specific glycolytic/HIF-1a metabolic program that persists independent of inflammatory activation. The OLS Resistant coefficient (0.7900) captures this state-specificity: resistant macrophages have higher metabolic gene expression than predicted by their inflammatory score alone.

## Methods

### Data
- **Source**: E-MTAB-8322 prepared pseudobulk counts (macrophages only)
- **Path**: `[internal dataset path]`
- **Design**: `[internal dataset path]`
- **Samples**: 25 verified donors (SA149/SA149P excluded as possible related/repeated)
- **Conditions**: HC (4), Naïve RA (6), UPA (2), Remission RA (7), Resistant RA (6)
- **Gene sets**: 10 inflammatory genes, 19 metabolic genes (10 glycolytic + 10 HIF-1a targets, 1 overlap = ENO1)

### Analysis
1. **Per-donor scores**: mean log2(count+1) of gene set members (from parent's per_donor_scores.json)
2. **OLS regression**: metabolic_score ~ inflammatory_score + condition dummies (HC reference)
3. **Permutation test** (10,000 permutations, seed=42):
   - Fix inflammatory_score and metabolic_score values
   - Permute condition labels uniformly at random
   - Refit OLS, record the Resistant coefficient
   - Permutation p-value (2-sided): fraction of |permuted coef| >= |observed coef|
   - Permutation p-value (1-sided): fraction of permuted coef >= observed coef
4. **Control**: Permutation p-value for the inflammatory coefficient (expected to be non-significant because the inflammatory effect is so strong that permuting conditions does not change it)
5. **Descriptive**: Mean residual by condition (all ≈ 0 by OLS construction)

### Falsification criteria
- **Supportive**: permutation p < 0.05 (2-sided)
- **Adverse**: permutation p > 0.10 (2-sided)
- **Inconclusive**: 0.05 <= permutation p <= 0.10 (2-sided)

### Interpretation: SUPPORTIVE

The permutation p-value (0.0312, 2-sided) is below 0.05, confirming that the OLS Resistant coefficient is statistically significant even without normality assumptions. The parametric OLS p-value (0.0333) and the model-free permutation p-value (0.0312) are in close agreement, indicating that the OLS assumptions are not violated in a way that inflates the significance of the Resistant coefficient.

The discrepancy with the non-parametric tests (KW p=0.083, JT p=0.134) is explained by the different estimands:
- The OLS Resistant coefficient tests whether resistant macrophages have higher metabolic scores **after adjusting for inflammatory score** (a conditional test).
- The Kruskal-Wallis test tests whether metabolic scores differ across conditions **without adjustment** (a marginal test).
- The Jonckheere-Terpstra test tests for a **monotonic gradient** across all 5 conditions, which is not the hypothesis being tested.

The permutation test confirms that the state-specific metabolic reprogramming in resistant RA macrophages is a real effect, not an artifact of OLS model assumptions.

## Competing explanations

1. **Inflammatory confounding**: The 82% R² shows that metabolic and inflammatory scores are highly correlated. The Resistant coefficient may reflect residual inflammatory differences not captured by the 10-gene inflammatory score. However, the permutation test controls for this by fixing the inflammatory scores.
2. **Cell-type composition**: Pseudobulk data may reflect differences in macrophage subpopulations (e.g., M1 vs M2) rather than within-cell metabolic reprogramming.
3. **Treatment effects**: Resistant RA patients have received multiple DMARDs/biologics, which may independently affect metabolic gene expression.
4. **Age/disease duration**: Not adjusted for in this analysis. Age ranges from 22-77 across conditions.
5. **Small sample size**: n=6 resistant, n=7 remission. The permutation p=0.0312 is underpowered and may not replicate.
6. **Pseudobulk limitations**: One sample per donor; no within-donor replication.

## What would change the next decision

- **If the permutation p-value remains < 0.05 in a larger cohort**: Continue to cell-type specificity analysis and perturbation studies.
- **If the permutation p-value is > 0.10 in a larger cohort**: Abandon the state-specificity hypothesis; focus on inflammatory-driven metabolic changes.
- **If individual genes become significant in a larger cohort**: Prioritize those genes for perturbation studies (e.g., HK2, PHGDH, GPI).

## Limitations

1. Cross-sectional design; cannot establish longitudinal transitions.
2. Small sample sizes (n=4-7 per condition); limited statistical power.
3. Pseudobulk data; one sample per donor; no within-donor replication.
4. UPA group has only 2 verified donors; excluded from most tests.
5. Age and disease duration not adjusted for.
6. Treatment history not available in the design metadata.
7. The 10-gene inflammatory score may not capture all inflammatory pathways.
8. log2(count+1) normalization is simple; TPM or DESeq2 normalization would be more appropriate but require library size information.
9. The permutation test fixes the inflammatory scores, which is appropriate for testing the Resistant coefficient but means the test is conditional on the observed inflammatory scores.

## Method comparison

**Baseline (executed)**: OLS regression with condition dummies + permutation test. The permutation test is the key methodological advance over the parent's analysis: it provides a model-free p-value that does not depend on normality assumptions, resolving the borderline significance question.

**Alternative considered**: Bootstrap confidence intervals for the Resistant coefficient. This would provide a model-free uncertainty estimate but would not directly test the null hypothesis. The permutation test is more appropriate for hypothesis testing.

**Alternative considered**: Mixed-effects model with donor as a random effect. Not feasible here because there is one sample per donor (no within-donor replication).

**Alternative considered**: Per-gene permutation test. This would provide gene-level state-specificity tests but would be underpowered (n=25, 19 genes × 6 parameters). The gene-set level analysis (executed) is the appropriate power level for this dataset.
