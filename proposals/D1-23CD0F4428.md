## 1. Scientific question, why it matters, and rival explanations

**Question.** In adult ICU patients discharged alive, does residual physiological instability during a fixed 24 h pre-discharge window — high/persistent oxygen requirement, ongoing vasopressor infusion, and high vital-sign variability — predict (a) post-ICU in-hospital death and (b) ICU readmission within the same hospitalization, after adjustment for illness severity and discharge destination?

**Why it matters.** The ICU-to-ward transition is a high-risk interval: patients who deteriorate after leaving the ICU are often caught late because monitoring intensity drops. A bedside, charting-data-only instability score computed in the last 24 h of the ICU stay would give the discharging team a concrete, prespecified signal to escalate post-ICU monitoring (step-down vs ward) or to delay discharge. The decision targeted is *post-ICU monitoring intensity / discharge readiness*, which is distinct from the in-ICU mortality prediction problems already well studied.

**Leading claim (hypothesis).** Residual instability in W is an *independent* prognostic marker: the association with post-ICU death and readmission survives adjustment for severity (age, ICU LOS, ventilation duration, pre-window vasopressor exposure, ICD comorbidity load) and discharge destination.

**Rival explanations the design must separate:**

- **R1 — severity confounding.** Unstable patients were simply sicker. *Design response:* severity-adjusted logistic regression; the unadjusted-vs-adjusted gap is itself a reported quantity. Charted APACHE/SOFA are unusable in this snapshot (charted in only 14/6 stays, verified in a prior episode), so the severity proxy is the covariate set above.
- **R2 — treatment limitation / weaning failure.** "Instability" may reflect a patient who cannot be weaned (still intubated at discharge), i.e., a label of ongoing criticality rather than a risk marker. *Design response:* stratify ventilator-free vs ventilated-at-discharge; the primary analysis **excludes** ventilated-at-discharge stays (measured: 97/32,528 = 0.30% of the primary population — a negligible exclusion, reported in a sensitivity that includes them).
- **R3 — discharge destination confounding.** Unstable patients may be sent to step-down/long-term care, which changes both monitoring and outcome risk. *Design response:* adjust for `discharge_location`; report crude rates by destination.

**What answering would change.** A supportive result (adjusted ORs materially > 1 with a graded score–outcome relation) would justify a prospective study of a monitoring-escalation rule for post-ICU patients with score ≥ 1. An adverse result (F3 below: association fully explained by severity) would argue that the instability signal is redundant with standard severity assessment and that discharge decisions should rely on the existing severity tools. Either outcome changes the next decision about whether to pursue this signal.

## 4. Anchor and window

Anchor = `outtime` of the index stay. Primary window **W = [outtime − 24 h, outtime]**, closed interval, `charttime`/`starttime` inclusive. Sensitivities: 12 h and 48 h windows.

## 5. Phenotype (exact formulas)

Computed per stay in W (primary ventilator-free stratum):

- **Oxygen requirement (O).** `med_SpO2` = median of `valuenum` for itemid 220277 in W (require ≥ 1 entry; 99.5% have one). Abnormal if `med_SpO2 < 92`. Secondary (ventilated stratum only): median FiO2 (itemid 223835) in W, abnormal if > 0.4.
- **Vasopressor use (V).** Abnormal if any `inputevents` row with a vasopressor itemid and `starttime` in W (measured: 579/32,528 = 1.8%).
- **HR variability (H).** SD of itemid 220045 values in W, computed only if ≥ 6 entries (documentation guard; 99.1% qualify). Abnormal if SD > 15 bpm.
- **SBP variability (B).** SD of itemid 220179 values in W, computed only if ≥ 6 entries (90.9% qualify). Abnormal if SD > 20 mmHg.

**Composite residual-instability score** = number of abnormal components among {O, V, H, B}, range 0–4. Primary analysis uses the composite (score 0 vs ≥ 1, and the full 0–4 graded series). Component-level analyses are secondary.

**Edge cases.** (i) Missing component (e.g., no SBP entries) → component scored 0 (not abnormal) and the stay is retained; the number of assessable components is recorded and reported. (ii) Out-of-range physiologic values (e.g., SpO2 = 100 held constant) are not winsorized; the median/SD are robust to sensor drift by construction of the guard. (iii) FiO2 is sparse (18.1%) and is used only in the ventilated stratum, never in the primary composite. (iv) A stay with a component abnormal *and* ventilated at outtime is excluded from the primary stratum (R2).

## 6. Outcomes (all within the same hospitalization, after `outtime`)

- **Primary: post-ICU in-hospital death** = `admissions.deathtime` not null and `deathtime > outtime`. Measured prevalence in the first-stay population: 1,641/32,528 = **5.04%**.
- **Secondary 1: ICU readmission** = a second `icustays` row with the same `subject_id, hadm_id` and `intime > outtime`. Measured: 4,787/32,528 = **14.72%**; median gap from first `outtime` 19.8 h; 2,981 (62.3%) within 48 h, 3,365 (70.3%) within 72 h.
- **Secondary 2: 48 h / 72 h post-ICU escalation** = any of (new vasopressor `inputevents` row with `starttime` in (outtime, outtime+48/72 h], new invasive-ventilation `procedureevents` row with `starttime` in that interval, or death in that interval). *Unverified count — to be computed by the solver.*
- **Competing risk.** Death precedes readmission for some patients; report crude rates for both and note the competing-risk structure. Fine–Gray subdistribution hazard for readmission is an optional sensitivity, not primary.

## 7. Analysis plan

1. **Baseline table** by composite score (0, 1, 2, 3, 4): age, sex, ICU LOS, ventilation duration, pre-window vasopressor use, ICD comorbidity load, discharge destination, and each phenotype component.
2. **Unadjusted outcome rates** by score with 95% CIs (Wald).
3. **Adjusted logistic regression** (death; readmission separately): covariates = age, sex, ICU LOS, ventilation duration (h), pre-window vasopressor use (any), ICD comorbidity load (distinct diagnosis count), `discharge_location` (categorical). Score entered as 0 vs ≥ 1 (primary) and as the full graded series (trend test).
4. **Unadjusted-vs-adjusted gap** reported explicitly (this is the R1 readout).
5. **Prespecified sensitivities:** window {12 h, 24 h, 48 h}; threshold variations (SpO2 < 90/94; HR SD > 10/20; SBP SD > 15/25); include ventilated-at-discharge; all stays (not just first); exclude deaths within 24 h of discharge (immortal-time check).

## 8. Falsification criteria (prespecified)

- **F1 (death):** adjusted OR for post-ICU death, score ≥ 1 vs 0, ≤ 1.0 with CI upper bound < 1.3 → no supported prognostic signal for death.
- **F2 (readmission):** adjusted OR for readmission, score ≥ 1 vs 0, ≤ 1.0 with CI upper bound < 1.2 → no supported signal for readmission.
- **F3 (severity explains it):** unadjusted OR ≥ 1.5 but adjusted OR crosses 1.0 → the raw association is severity confounding (R1 confirmed); the instability phenotype adds nothing beyond severity.
- **F4 (flat):** outcome rates flat across the 0–4 score series (no monotone trend, p_trend > 0.2) → no dose–response structure.

**Interpretations.** *Supportive:* adjusted ORs > 1 for both outcomes with a graded trend and a small unadjusted-vs-adjusted gap → residual instability is an independent, bedside-computable signal; next step is a prospective monitoring-escalation study. *Adverse (F3):* association fully explained by severity → do not pursue the phenotype; rely on existing severity tools for discharge decisions. *Inconclusive:* wide CIs crossing 1.0 with unadjusted ORs ~1.2–1.5 → underpowered or weak signal; do not claim refutation; consider a larger multi-hospital dataset (eICU) before abandoning.

## 9. Method alternatives (recorded reasoning)

- **Baseline:** unadjusted outcome rates by score + crude comparison (Section 7.2).
- **Substantive learned/mechanistic alternative:** severity-adjusted logistic regression (Section 7.3). *What it reveals that the baseline loses:* the **confounding structure** — whether the instability signal is independent of (R1) severity, (R2) weaning status and (R3) destination. This is the scientific core of the question; a predictive model would not answer it.
- **Deferred:** gradient-boosted trees / survival (Fine–Gray) models. Justification: the scientific value here is the *association and its confounding structure*, not prediction accuracy; a bedside rule needs interpretability (a 0–4 count of charted abnormalities); the incremental AUC over logistic regression would be small and would not change the decision. Fine–Gray is retained only as an optional competing-risk sensitivity for readmission. CPU budget is ample for all of the above; no GPU is needed.

## 11. Limitations

- Single-center (BIDMC) administrative/physiology data; no external validation — a supportive result motivates, but does not establish, a monitoring rule.
- Severity is proxied (charted APACHE/SOFA unusable in this snapshot); residual severity confounding is possible.
- `discharge_location` is a coarse proxy for post-ICU monitoring intensity.
- SpO2/FiO2 are charted, not continuously streamed; documentation gaps are handled by the entry-count guards, but missingness is informative (sicker patients may have more monitoring).
- First-stay restriction improves identifiability but reduces generalizability to repeat ICU admissions.
- The escalation outcome (Section 6) is *unverified* in this episode and must be computed by the solver.
