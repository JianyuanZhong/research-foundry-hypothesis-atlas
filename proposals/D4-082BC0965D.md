# UKB-05 (child): Does grip strength add independent fracture risk beyond BMD?
## Re-specified estimand after event-support audit

### Why this child exists (scientific change from parent [prior hypothesis])
Episode-2 data audit (jobs [research job], [research job],
[research job]) shows the parent's primary 4-group categorical
interaction is underpowered:
- Cohort (DXA inst2 + grip inst2 + sex): 45,125 (23,212 F / 21,913 M), age 44-85,
  mean 64.4.
- Derived T-score (sex/site-specific NHANES-style references; no T-score field
  exists in the frozen data): low BMD (T<-1) n=5,849; osteoporosis (T<-2.5) n=176.
- Post-DXA inpatient fracture (ICD10 S02-S82, any instance, first event after
  DXA date): 69 events total; 62 within 5y; 10y group events: 42/15/7/5 across
  the four BMD x grip groups.
A categorical BMD x grip interaction with 5-42 events per cell cannot resolve the
hypothesis. This child re-specifies the estimand to a continuous-exposure design
with adequate power, keeps the same scientific question, and states explicit
supportive/adverse/inconclusive criteria.

### Unresolved question (unchanged)
In adults with DXA-verified BMD, does low hand grip strength independently predict
subsequent inpatient fracture beyond BMD, and is the association concentrated in
low-BMD individuals?

### Re-specified design
**Population:** UKB participants with (a) L1-L4 BMD (23204-2.0) or femoral neck
BMD (23208-2.0) at assessment instance 2, (b) grip strength (46-2.0 left, 47-2.0
right, take better hand) at instance 2, (c) sex (31-0.0 in
[UKB data file]), (d) no inpatient fracture (41270 S-codes with
41280 date) before the instance-2 attendance date (53-2.0).
Verified size: 45,125 with DXA+grip; ~44,867 also with falls (2296-2.0).

**Exposures (continuous, sex-standardized):**
- BMD: T-score derived as (BMD - ref_mean)/ref_sd using sex- and site-specific
  young-adult references (documented in analysis script; sensitivity: raw BMD
  z-score within cohort).
- Grip: sex-specific z-score of (46-2.0 or 47-2.0, better hand).
- Categorical overlays for description: low BMD (T<-1), low grip (below
  sex-specific median: 22.0 F / 36.0 M, observed).

**Outcome:** first inpatient fracture after DXA date from 41270-*.0 (ICD10 S02-S82)
with 41280-*.0 dates, instances 0-3. Verified: 69 post-DXA events (62 by 5y).
Sensitivity outcome: hip fracture or hip replacement (S72, Z96.6/M25.5) reported
descriptively (expected few events; not primary).
**Competing risk:** death (40000-0.0) after DXA date (781 deaths verified).

**Covariates:** age at DXA (21003-2.0), sex (31-0.0), BMI (21001-2.0),
falls in last year (2296-2.0, as covariate in primary model; as mediator only in
a pre-specified secondary analysis with the temporal limitation stated).

**Analysis (primary):**
1. Cox PH, cause-specific hazards with death as competing event:
   - M1: T-score (per SD) -> fracture
   - M2: grip z (per SD) -> fracture
   - M3: T-score + grip z (independent effects)
   - M4: T-score + grip z + T-score x grip z (interaction, continuous)
   - M5: M4 + age, BMI, falls
   Primary estimands: HR per SD lower grip in M3 (independent effect beyond BMD)
   and the interaction term in M4/M5 (concentration in low-BMD).
2. Baseline comparison: M5 Cox vs gradient-boosting survival model (XGBoost
   survival or random-survival-forest) on the same features; compare AUROC/C-index
   and calibration. The learned model tests whether non-linear or higher-order
   structure (e.g., grip matters only below a BMD threshold) improves prediction
   beyond the linear interaction.
3. Secondary (descriptive): 4-group incidence rates and HRs (as in parent),
   explicitly labeled underpowered; falls mediation only as descriptive
   proportion-of-risk with the single-timepoint limitation stated.

**Power statement (computed, not assumed):** with 69 events, a continuous HR per SD
can be estimated with 95% CI half-width ~1.5/sqrt(69) on the log scale for the
main effect; the interaction term is detectable only for large effect sizes
(approx. interaction HR per SD of T-score x grip >1.8 or <0.55 at 80% power,
alpha 0.05). The design therefore tests the main independent effect of grip with
reasonable precision, and treats the interaction as exploratory.

### Falsification criteria
- **Adverse:** HR per SD lower grip in M3/M5 within 95% CI of 1.0 (e.g., 0.85-1.18)
  AND no meaningful C-index gain from the learned model: grip strength does not
  independently predict inpatient fracture beyond BMD in this population.
- **Inconclusive:** HR point estimate >1.2 or <0.8 but CI crosses 1.0, or interaction
  borderline: report as suggestive; requires replication or larger fracture
  ascertainment (e.g., outpatient codes, self-report 20002).
- **Supportive:** HR per SD lower grip >1.2 with 95% CI excluding 1.0 in M5,
  consistent direction in M2-M4, and (optionally) interaction HR >1.3 in low-BMD
  stratum; learned model C-index gain >=0.01 supports non-linear structure.

### What results would/would not establish
- Supportive: grip adds independent prognostic value beyond BMD for inpatient
  fracture; would NOT establish causality or that muscle interventions prevent
  fracture (needs RCT); inpatient-only ascertainment limits sensitivity.
- Adverse: BMD dominates fracture prediction in this DXA subcohort; does not rule
  out muscle effects in very old/institutionized populations or for hip-specific
  outcomes with better ascertainment.
- Inconclusive: power limited by event count; next step would be broader fracture
  ascertainment or a different muscle-function measure.

### Method comparison (baseline vs learned)
- Baseline: Cox PH with continuous T-score, grip z, interaction + covariates.
  Interpretable, standard for time-to-event, directly yields the HR estimands.
- Learned alternative: XGBoost survival (or random survival forest) on the same
  features. Reveals non-linear/threshold structure the linear interaction misses
  (e.g., grip effect only below a BMD threshold); C-index and calibration
  comparison quantifies the information gain.
- Budget: both fit in minutes on CPU (45k rows, <1 hour total incl. data prep).
  No GPU needed.

### Limitations
1. Single DXA and grip measurement (instance 2); no longitudinal muscle/bone change.
2. T-scores derived from published reference means/SDs (no T-score field in frozen
   data); sensitivity analysis with cohort z-scores required.
3. Inpatient fracture only (69 events); outpatient/self-reported fractures missed.
4. Falls measured once (2296-2.0); mediation is descriptive only.
5. Residual confounding (frailty, bisphosphonates, steroids, activity).
6. UKB selection (healthier, age 44-85 here); limited generalizability to
   institutionalized or very old adults.

### Essential clinical adjudication
ICD10 S-codes include high-energy traumatic fractures; a fragility-only outcome
would require clinical adjudication of a case sample, which is outside this
computational study. The primary outcome is therefore "inpatient fracture" with
the limitation stated.
