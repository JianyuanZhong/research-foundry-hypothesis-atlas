## Clinical question and evidence boundary

After resuscitation, an invasively ventilated patient may still have marked positive fluid balance but has stabilized off vasopressors. Should clinicians initiate or re-initiate an ICU-recorded IV loop diuretic, or withhold loop diuretics for the next day? The answer could change ventilator duration, death, renal replacement therapy (RRT), and fluid-removal practice.

Existing evidence supports a narrower claim: heterogeneous conservative-fluid/deresuscitation bundles can reduce recorded fluid balance and may increase ventilator-free days. The indexed 2017 systematic-review record cited in the research-ambition guide reported 11 heterogeneous randomized trials (2,051 participants), increased ventilator-free days, and no precise mortality benefit. The indexed RADAR-2 report concerns a bundled strategy, not isolated loop initiation; its 2026 secondary analysis did not detect several kidney/perfusion biomarker differences but was explicitly too small and imprecise to establish safety. A 2026 review described renal-congestion claims as hypothesis-generating without validated diagnostic criteria or outcome-driven intervention evidence. The local research-ambition README and its stated availability limitations were inspected; unavailable full papers are not claimed to have been read here.

Thus, evidence does not establish the effect of starting actual IV furosemide/bumetanide at this landmark, establish mortality or RRT safety, validate an EHR congestion phenotype, or justify routine care.

### Hypothesis

Among adults invasively ventilated at ICU hour 48 after vasopressor-treated shock, stable off vasopressors for six hours and with at least 50 mL/kg documented positive ICU balance, initiating/re-initiating ICU-recorded IV furosemide or bumetanide within 24 hours increases the true 28-day VFD28 by at least one day versus withholding every loop diuretic for 24 hours, while not causing more than 3 percentage points excess recorded 28-day mortality or 5 points excess 7-day new RRT.

The estimand is the per-protocol mean VFD28 contrast (A minus B) in this eligible start/restart population, conditional on consistency, positivity, correct measurement, and no unmeasured confounding. The primary VFD result is partially identified because the source does not record ventilation outside observed ICU care. The experiment may support the hypothesis only when the worst-case VFD benefit still clears +1 day and both conservative safety bounds pass. It may falsify benefit when even the best-case VFD contrast is at or below zero. Other results are inconclusive, not evidence of equivalence.

## Treatment strategies and actual administration

Earlier loops from ICU admission through `t0-12h` are allowed. Adjust for cumulative prior loop dose and time since the last loop and report a never-treated-since-admission new-user sensitivity. This is a start/restart estimand, not an all-loop-naive estimand.

Clone each eligible person to both strategies at t0:

- **A:** the first positive, non-rewritten ICU `inputevents` administration of furosemide (221794 or 228340) or bumetanide (229639) starting in `(t0,t0+24h]`; no accepted oral loop may precede that initiation in the grace period. Subsequent care is unrestricted after hour 24.
- **B:** no furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide, or ethacrynic acid by any route in `(t0,t0+24h]); subsequent care is unrestricted after hour 24.

For ICU medication exposures, require numeric positive administered amount and the delivered-segment statuses above. Orders, prescriptions, and pharmacy records never define treatment.

For hospital eMAR, reduce `hosp/emar_detail` to one event per `subject_id,emar_id,emar_seq`, then join `hosp/emar` on those keys and `hadm_id`. Normalize exact medication names. Accept `event_txt` {Administered, Delayed Administered, Partial Administered, Administered in Other Location}, or Started/Restarted only when positive event-level dose is reconstructed from the detail rows. Reject Not Given, Hold, Confirmed/order-only, Stopped, and Not Started. Route is any nonblank detail-row route after aggregation; blank route is unknown, never IV. Deduplicate same-drug ICU/eMAR events within 15 minutes for concordance only.

Censor clone A at first nonprotocol oral loop, ICU discharge before qualifying initiation, or hour 24 if untreated. Censor clone B at first loop or alive discharge with unknown medication during the grace period. Death during the grace period remains in both clones with VFD=0 and is never censored as nonadherence. Estimate inverse-probability adherence/censoring weights.

Report ICU/eMAR overlap and discordance. Mandatory exposure sensitivities are (1) A restricted to ICU events corroborated by an accepted same-drug eMAR within 15 minutes and (2) primary ICU exposure augmented only by eMAR events with explicit IV route. Because these are different administration systems, no duplication-rate threshold is imposed. A material exposure conflict—opposite-sign VFD contrast with its 95% CI excluding zero, or an absolute change >1 day whose paired bootstrap CI excludes zero—forces the causal interpretation to inconclusive. Imprecision in the corroborated subset alone does not prove exposure invalidity.

## Outcomes: observation-aware primary estimand

### Primary VFD28 bounds

Define the conventional target as days alive and free of invasive ventilation from t0 through day 28, with the standard death rule: death by day 28 means VFD28=0. Use the earliest available `admissions.deathtime`, `patients.dod`, or hospital death flag as a death signal, and analyze date-only DOD with early/late boundary scenarios. A death date after discharge still sets VFD28 to zero when it falls within the 28-day window.

For survivors, do not carry an alive discharge forward. Construct valid invasive-ventilation intervals from `procedureevents.itemid=225792` with usable `starttime,endtime`, excluding canceled, rewritten, or not-started records, and clip them to the linked `icustays.intime,outtime` and to `[t0,t0+28d]`. Union overlapping intervals across all ICU stays and observed readmissions linked by `subject_id,hadm_id). The observation set is the union of those linked ICU-stay intervals. Within an observed ICU interval, absence of a valid 225792 interval is recorded as non-ventilated, subject to the procedure-capture audit; malformed or ambiguous overlapping records are unknown.

For each clone/person surviving to day 28, calculate day-equivalents:

- `L) (lower VFD): observed alive, non-ventilated time in the ICU observation set; every alive interval outside that set is unknown and conservatively assigned ventilated.
- `U) (upper VFD): `L) plus all alive time outside the ICU observation set, assigning every such interval non-ventilated.

Both are divided by 24 hours and lie in [0,28]. If death is known by day 28, set both to zero. If date-only DOD timing changes death-by-day-28 status or an endpoint bound, report the two scenarios and do not call the result supportive unless the conclusion is unchanged. Emit per-person observed time, unknown time, ventilated time, L, U, and death status. The interval is not a confidence interval: it is uncertainty from missing ventilation observation.

The primary causal interval is computed after clone/censor weighting, using paired subject-level resampling:

```
Delta_L = weighted_mean(A, L) - weighted_mean(B, U)
Delta_U = weighted_mean(A, U) - weighted_mean(B, L).
```

Report both arm means, the [Delta_L, Delta_U] identification interval, 95% subject-bootstrap intervals for each endpoint, and the corresponding optimistic carry-forward and hospital-observed VFD sensitivities. The old alive-discharge carry-forward is a sensitivity completion, never the primary result. A hospital-observed VFD is also descriptive because it rewards or penalizes discharge timing and is not the conventional 28-day target.

### Safety outcomes

Report recorded 28-day mortality separately. Do not call it fully population mortality unless death-field completeness is demonstrated; `deathtime` and `dod` are the available follow-up sources and date-only boundary uncertainty must be shown.

For new RRT by day 7, use the six prespecified RRT procedure IDs and treat death before RRT as a competing event. Because procedureevents are ICU-recorded, apply the same observation principle: a documented RRT is event=1; a documented alive person observed in ICU through day 7 without RRT is event=0; an alive person with any unobserved post-discharge/non-ICU time before day 7 has RRT interval [0,1] unless RRT is documented later in an observed linked ICU stay. Report lower and upper weighted arm risks and the treatment risk interval [A_lower−B_upper, A_upper−B_lower]. If death occurs before day 7, RRT is 0 for the competing-event endpoint. The upper safety bound, not a missing-as-no-event estimate, is used for the safety gate.

Secondary outcomes are KDIGO creatinine AKI through day 7, creatinine and potassium change, K<3.0, new vasopressor use, MAP<55, 24/48-hour documented balance, and urine output. Use `hosp/labevents.charttime` for clinical time and `storetime` for availability, with creatinine 50912/52546 and potassium 50971/52610 after emitting `hosp/d_labitems` labels and units. Post-discharge missing labs are unknown, not normal. Balance and urine are process checks, not patient benefit.

## Falsification, feasibility gates, and result logic

Temporal falsification tests whether eventual strategy predicts already observed pre-t0 six-hour creatinine, urine-output, or MAP slopes after the same baseline adjustment. A material standardized difference >0.10 or multiplicity-controlled P<0.05 forces causal interpretation to inconclusive. More-negative 24-hour balance and greater urine output under A are process-positive controls; failure raises exposure/strategy concern but cannot prove inefficacy.

Declare the analysis infeasible/inconclusive before outcome claims if either observed adherent arm has <100 people, ESS<75, >10% of person-hours have adherence probability <0.01, or material balance/outcome missingness exceeds 10%. Also report the number and fraction whose VFD identification interval has any unknown time and the RRT interval is nondegenerate. A large observation interval is not silently repaired by carry-forward; if it prevents the clinical gate, the result is inconclusive.

Supportive requires all of the following:

1. the lower 95% bootstrap bound for `Delta_L` is >+1 VFD day (benefit persists under the worst admissible ventilation completion);
2. the upper 95% bound for excess recorded 28-day mortality is <+3 percentage points;
3. the upper 95% bound for excess 7-day RRT risk uses the conservative RRT upper-risk contrast and is <+5 points;
4. balance/urine process directions are as expected;
5. all feasibility, positivity, exposure, missingness, death-boundary, and pre-t0 falsification gates pass.

This supports external validation and a pragmatic randomized trial. It does not establish clinical benefit as fact, exchangeability, or a practice recommendation.

Adverse means VFD benefit is refuted when the upper 95% bound for `Delta_U` is <=0, or a prespecified safety-harm lower bound is >=+3 mortality points or >=+5 RRT points. Favorable fluid balance cannot rescue respiratory or safety harm. If `Delta_L` does not clear +1 but `Delta_U` is >0, the effect is compatible with benefit but unresolved because observation is insufficient. Inconclusive/mixed also includes wide bounds, incomplete safety bounds, nonpositivity, exposure-sensitivity conflict, residual pre-t0 imbalance, material missingness, unvalidated imaging interaction, or contradictory death-date scenarios. Nonsignificance is neither equivalence nor safety. A null or adverse validated imaging interaction falsifies only the modifier, not the primary hypothesis.

## Claims the verifier can and cannot establish

Computational verification can establish source hashes/members/columns, joins, item maps, time boundaries, deterministic sampling, attrition, exposure reduction, clone/censor rules, balance arithmetic, observation intervals, bound arithmetic, death/RRT coding, weights, ESS, bootstrap intervals, sensitivities, falsification statistics, and whether a submitted conclusion matches the prespecified branch and cites its computed output.

It cannot establish treatment indication, goals of care, completeness of post-discharge death reporting, validity of the text edema classifier, clinical truth of a radiology phenotype, exchangeability, positivity outside recorded data, renal congestion, transportability, or a treatment recommendation. Those require clinical adjudication, missing modalities/evidence, external validation, and preferably randomization. A computed supportive branch is evidence for the explicitly bounded observational estimand, not proof of bedside efficacy.

Challenge cases must fail verification: calling documented balance patient benefit; treating blank eMAR route as IV; censoring early death; using carry-forward as the primary VFD result; calling a +1-day optimistic estimate supportive when the lower bound or conservative safety bound fails; calling wide bounds no effect/equivalence; calling an unadjudicated text interaction congestion; or calling observational support proof or standard care.
