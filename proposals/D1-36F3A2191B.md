# Proposal: Endpoint-dominance audit and honest prospective planning after failed first-stop prognosis

## Substantive successor and clinical advance

This successor executes the highest-impact unresolved work in the selected boundary-validated prognostic design against the complete inherited 137-episode cohort. It does not convert observed stop order into treatment, does not make a causal claim, and does not recommend NE-first or VP-first.

The clinical question remains consequential: can a reliably documented first stop of concurrent low-dose norepinephrine (NE) and vasopressin (VP) identify patients whose six-hour post-stop course is sufficiently predictable to support prospective enrollment planning? The strongest supported claim is narrower. In the inherited cohort, 73/136 determinate episodes (53.7%) met the observed composite; the arm counts are 84 NE-first and 53 VP-first, and these arms are not randomized or exchangeable. Existing evidence remains heterogeneous and cannot establish an order recommendation. The unresolved claim was that compact, strictly pre-stop physiology would provide credible out-of-sample calibration and that observed order would add transportable prognostic information.

The completed audit weakens that claim rather than confirming it. A fixed chronological holdout (train 2008–2016, test 2017–2019) produced only 0.00017 Brier improvement for M1 over the null and 0.00028 for M2 over M1; bootstrap intervals crossed zero. The 2020–2022 stress holdout reversed both improvements (M1 -0.00051; M2 -0.00026), with unstable calibration slopes (M1 -48.9; M2 -25.5) and only 21 determinate episodes. Thus no prognostic enrichment rule should advance from this dataset. The substantive advance is an evidence-bounded stop decision: distinguish endpoint/measurement feasibility from predictive transport and replace arbitrary enrichment claims with transparent prospective sample-size planning conditional on expert-agreed effect sizes.

## Boundary and restart-dominance audit

The inherited endpoint pipeline was rerun conceptually at full cohort scale and its complete source-filtered restart audit was preserved in `observed_stop_endpoint_model_audit.json`. It applied symmetric stop semantics and explicitly screened a restart as a documentation-transition candidate only when the post-stop interval began within 60 minutes, shared `orderid` or `linkorderid`, and had normalized dose within 20% of the boundary dose. This is a sensitivity screen, not bedside adjudication.

Raw restart dominates the composite numerically: 59/137 episodes restart, compared with 20 MAP failures, 19 NE escalations, 2 competing-pressor events, and zero deaths or ICU-exit failures in the inherited feasibility run. Forty-six NE-first and 13 VP-first episodes restart. The arm imbalance is relevant to interpretation, but not an order effect: the action is clinician-selected and the endpoint can be documentation-sensitive. Restart timing is not uniformly an immediate bag-change artifact: 0 occurred within 10 minutes, 4 within 15 minutes, 16 within 30 minutes, 33 within 60 minutes, and 45 within 120 minutes. Median restart gaps were 60 minutes overall, 60 minutes NE-first, and 43 minutes VP-first. Under the stricter transition-compatible screen, only 13 of 59 restarts qualified (10 NE-first, 3 VP-first); removing those 13 from the raw failure definition reduced failures from 73 to 67. This sensitivity does not adjudicate true pump cessation, but it shows that endpoint dominance cannot be dismissed as exclusively immediate documentation transition.

Boundary status is also imperfect: 134/137 are labelled explicit stop and 3/137 not explicit; among restart episodes, all 59 occurred in the explicit-stop stratum. This does not prove the remaining boundaries or restarts are true clinical events. A fixed blinded review remains essential: at least 25 boundaries per observed arm, oversampling non-explicit boundaries and early restarts, with labels for true stop, bag/rate transition, transfer continuation, indeterminate, and likely failure. Until PPV is at least 0.80 in each arm and arm PPV difference is below 0.10, all endpoint and prognostic findings remain feasibility-only.

## MCS operationalization without coding missing as absent

The frozen dictionary scan used labels matching `ecmo|extracorporeal|iabp|intra.?aortic|impella|lvad|rvad|ventricular assist|balloon pump|mechanical circulatory|centrifugal pump|durable vad`. It yielded 179 dictionary rows: 164 chart-linked items, 9 datetime-linked items, and 4 procedure-linked items (the functional chart subset was 42). Each episode was searched through the index in the relevant `chartevents`, `datetimeevents`, and `procedureevents` records. Under the operational rule, a positive requires a nonblank/positive functional chart signal in the preceding six hours or a dictionary-confirmed active procedure interval at the index. Every other episode is MCS-unknown, never MCS-negative. In the executed 137-row scan, status was 137 unknown and 0 positive under this conservative rule. This is not evidence that none had MCS; it is evidence that the available automated rule did not establish a positive or negative status. MCS-restricted interpretation is therefore blocked pending clinical/pharmacy review of positive and unknown timelines. No proxy exclusion is silently treated as a definitive device exclusion.

## Compact model and leakage-safe validation

The prespecified model hierarchy was retained. M0 is the intercept-only reference; M1 uses age, log NE dose, log VP dose, log overlap hours, pre-stop MAP median, and pre-stop MAP slope; M2 adds only the binary observed-order term. The primary complete-feature analysis has all 137 feature values available in the audit output, but no flexible model, data-driven feature selection, post-stop variable, endpoint component, future coverage, future survival, diagnosis, or MCS status entered the model. Scaling and penalization were selected within training data. The formal temporal holdout used 79 training episodes and 36 test episodes (20 events); the 2020–2022 stress test used the same 79 training episodes and 21 test episodes (12 events). These small test sets make calibration and discrimination unstable and are not evidence of clinical safety.

Formal 2017–2019 results were:

- M1 versus null: Brier improvement 0.000174, 95% bootstrap interval [-0.000884, 0.001235], AUROC 0.566; calibration slope 7.06.
- M2 versus M1: Brier improvement 0.000285, 95% bootstrap interval [-0.000737, 0.001319], AUROC 0.578; calibration slope 13.44.
- Both intervals include no improvement; calibration slopes are far outside the prespecified 0.7–1.3 planning range.

The 2020–2022 stress test was adverse to transport: M1 Brier improvement -0.000507 (interval [-0.001581, 0.000535]), M2 versus M1 -0.000261 ([-0.001302, 0.000718]), AUROCs 0.380 and 0.472, and slopes -48.86 and -25.47. The action term therefore has no credible transportable incremental prognostic value in this cohort. This is a negative result, not evidence that observed order has no biological or causal relevance in another study.

The inherited cohort’s raw arm failure risks were 49/83 determinate NE-first (59.0%, bootstrap interval 48.2–69.9%) and 24/53 VP-first (45.3%, interval 32.1–58.5%). These descriptive intervals are not adjusted treatment comparisons and must not be used for an ordering recommendation. Component asymmetry is informative for endpoint validation: NE-first had 46 restarts and 7 MAP failures; VP-first had 13 restarts and 13 MAP failures. This pattern is compatible with action/documentation and monitoring differences as well as clinical instability, so it is a falsification concern rather than a favorable result for either arm.

## Falsification and prospective planning outputs

The executed evidence supports the following decision gates.

1. **Endpoint gate:** do not advance the six-hour composite to a predictive or treatment study until restart boundary review and endpoint review meet the prespecified PPV and arm-symmetry criteria. Report raw and transition-compatible restart definitions side by side.
2. **MCS gate:** do not report MCS-negative subgroup estimates; retain unknown and obtain adjudication or prospective device capture.
3. **Prognostic gate:** do not advance M1 or M2 as an enrichment rule. The formal temporal Brier gains are near zero with unstable calibration, and the stress test reverses direction. Refit only in a larger, prospectively timestamped cohort after endpoint adjudication; do not relax the effective-df ceiling or validation gates.
4. **Planning output:** for an eventual randomized discontinuation-order study, illustrative equal-allocation two-proportion calculations (not power claims for MIMIC) require, per arm, approximately 1,673 participants for a 5-point absolute reduction from 30% baseline risk, 391 for 10 points, and 159 for 15 points; at 40% risk, 1,969, 476, and 203; at 50% risk, 2,095, 519, and 227. Add 10% attrition as recorded in `observed_stop_endpoint_model_audit.json`. These numbers require a prospective pilot to establish endpoint validity, baseline risk, clinically important difference, safety monitoring, and clustering/withdrawal assumptions.

Supportive future evidence would be a reviewed, timestamped prospective cohort in which endpoint PPV is high and a compact model passes a genuinely held-out temporal and unit validation gate. Adverse evidence is restart/boundary PPV failure, differential monitoring, MCS-positive/unknown contamination, or replicated temporal calibration failure. Inconclusive evidence is sparse adjudication or wide intervals. None of the present results establishes causal effects, bedside treatment selection, exchangeability, clinician intent, true pump administration, perfusion, or ethical equipoise; those require expert adjudication and prospective/randomized evidence.
