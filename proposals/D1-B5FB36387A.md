## Unresolved question, importance, and advance

At ICU hour 48, clinicians often face a consequential de-resuscitation decision: in a still invasively ventilated adult who recently required vasopressors, is substantially fluid-positive, and now meets prespecified perfusion, urine, potassium, RRT, and ECMO safeguards, should a recorded IV loop-diuretic course be started during the next shift? Fluid removal could accelerate ICU de-escalation, but it could also worsen renal perfusion or mark patients already expected to improve.

The strongest evidence available before this experiment is narrower than the hypothesis. Kuriyama et al.'s full public XML (2026, DOI `10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100120`, PMCID `PMC13476641`; frozen source `[source checksum]`) reports 26 randomized trials and 1,652 participants comparing diuretic strategies, mostly in heart-failure or post-cardiovascular-surgery populations; most outcome evidence was low or very-low certainty. It does not resolve initiating/restarting a loop versus withholding it in this post-shock ventilated phenotype. Orieux et al.'s inspected full XML (2026, DOI `10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100083`, PMCID `PMC13218125`; frozen source `[source checksum]`) explicitly calls ICU renal congestion hypothesis-generating and says interventional evidence and clinical utility are lacking. These support equipoise and physiologic plausibility, not benefit.

The parent made day-7 non-ICU success destination-neutral, but set `D7=t0+7d`, which is approximately ICU day 9 because `t0` is ICU hour 48. Its own broad evidence did not establish that the strict endpoint could meet the <=10% unknown gate. A new treatment- and effect-blind source audit (`audit_topology_horizons.py`, output `topology_horizon_audit.json`) applied the exact discovery hash, earliest ICU stay, adult/hour-48 boundaries, and unique accepted item-225792 `V0` filter to 5,752 records. At `t0+7d`, the corresponding counts were 928, 2,412, 1,524, 799, and 89 (15.44% unknown). No treatment, clinical endpoint, or effect was estimated. This demonstrates a source-driven observability gradient, not final eligibility or efficacy.
