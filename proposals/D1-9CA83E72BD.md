# Post-insulin hypoglycemia after kidney-function deterioration in eICU

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The unresolved question is whether, at the same insulin intensity, ICU patients with worsening kidney function over the preceding 24 hours have a higher risk of subsequent hypoglycemia, and whether this risk is modified by observed nutrition interruption. This matters because hypoglycemia is a high-severity, potentially preventable adverse event. Worsening kidney function can reduce renal insulin clearance, while interrupted nutrition can reduce glucose supply. If kidney deterioration adds prognostic information beyond insulin intensity and current glucose, this could inform insulin dosing, glucose monitoring, and nutrition management in the ICU.

The strongest claim already supported by the available evidence is that eICU contains timestamped insulin medication orders, laboratory results including glucose and creatinine, and timestamped intake/output records. The unresolved claim is whether kidney-function deterioration is an independent predictor of subsequent hypoglycemia after accounting for insulin intensity and current glycemic state. The proposed experiment tests this conditional temporal association; it does not establish that kidney dysfunction causes hypoglycemia.

## Scientific opening and competing explanations

The leading explanation is that deteriorating kidney function prolongs insulin exposure and therefore increases the probability of a low glucose. A strong rival is that sicker patients both receive more insulin and have more frequent glucose measurements, so the association is driven by severity and surveillance rather than renal clearance. A second rival is that apparent nutrition interruption is a documentation artifact: a missing intake/output record may mean that nutrition was not recorded, not that it stopped. The available data can distinguish some of these explanations by conditioning on current glucose and insulin intensity and by treating missing nutrition as unknown. They remain partially indistinguishable because eICU lacks complete nutrition orders, clinician intent, and direct renal clearance measurements.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU admissions with at least one non-cancelled insulin administration and a subsequent glucose measurement. The analysis unit is an insulin exposure window, not a patient, with clustering by `patientunitstayid` and `uniquepid` for uncertainty.

1. Define an insulin exposure window beginning at the first qualifying insulin administration after ICU admission and ending 24 hours later.
2. Require at least one glucose measurement in the preceding 6 hours and at least one subsequent glucose measurement in the following 12 hours.
3. Exclude insulin orders with `drugordercancelled='Yes'`, orders that end before they start, and windows without a valid subsequent glucose observation.
4. Restrict to patients with at least two creatinine measurements in the preceding 24 hours and at least one urine-output record in the preceding 24 hours. Patients without these observations are not silently included as stable.

The outcome is a subsequent glucose result below 70 mg/dL within 12 hours after the exposure window, using the first qualifying result. A secondary outcome is a clinically documented hypoglycemic episode or treatment, identified from note text and medication records; this requires manual adjudication of a sample and is not assumed to be fully available.

## Data bindings

All tables join on `patientunitstayid`; time is the table-specific offset field, interpreted as hours from the admission reference documented in the eICU source. `uniquepid` is used only for clustering and never as a cross-dataset join key.

- **Insulin exposure:** `medication.csv.gz`, fields `patientunitstayid`, `medicationid`, `drugname`, `drugstartoffset`, `drugstopoffset`, `drugorderoffset`, `drugordercancelled`, `dosage`, `routeadmin`, `frequency`, `prn`, and `gtc`. Select insulin by a prespecified drug-name dictionary (for example, names containing insulin), then parse numeric dose from `dosage` where possible. Infusion rate is not directly available; use administered dose and route as the intensity measure and report the limitation.
- **Glucose and kidney function:** `lab.csv.gz`, fields `patientunitstayid`, `labid`, `labname`, `labresult`, `labresulttext`, `labresultoffset`, `labmeasurenamesystem`, and `labresultrevisedoffset`. Select glucose and creatinine by a prespecified name dictionary. Use the earliest unrevised result in each window; if a revised offset exists, use the revised value when available.
- **Urine output:** `intakeOutput.csv.gz`, fields `patientunitstayid`, `intakeoutputid`, `intakeoutputoffset`, `outputtotal`, `intakeoutputentryoffset`, `cellpath`, `celllabel`, `cellvaluenumeric`, and `cellvaluetext`. Sum output volumes in the preceding 24 hours and calculate urine output per hour using admission weight from `patient.csv.gz` (`admissionweight`) when available.
- **Nutrition:** `intakeOutput.csv.gz`, fields `intakeoutputoffset`, `intaketotal`, `cellpath`, `celllabel`, `cellvaluenumeric`, and `cellvaluetext`. Define observed oral/enteral nutrition only from explicit intake labels and volumes. Missing records are coded as unknown, not as cessation. A sensitivity analysis restricts to windows with at least one nutrition record in each 6-hour half of the preceding 24 hours.
- **Current state and severity:** `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`, fields `patientunitstayid`, `vitalperiodicid`, `observationoffset`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, `systemicsystolic`, `systemicdiastolic`, `systemicmean`, and `sao2`; `patient.csv.gz`, fields `patientunitstayid`, `uniquepid`, `age`, `gender`, `ethnicity`, `unittype`, `apacheadmissiondx`, `admissionweight`, and `hospitaladmittime24`. These are included as prespecified confounders, not as post-outcome predictors.

The source files are ordinary gzip CSV files under the exact paths listed in `datasets/eicu/README.md`. The inspected headers confirm the fields above; no archive member is required.

## Exposure and outcome definitions

Define insulin intensity as the total parsed insulin dose over the preceding 24 hours, standardized within hospital and route. Define kidney deterioration as an increase in creatinine of at least 0.3 mg/dL over the preceding 24 hours, or a urine-output decline of at least 30% from the first 12 to the second 12 hours, when both measurements exist. Define current glycemic state as the mean and minimum glucose in the preceding 6 hours. Define observed nutrition as the total explicit oral/enteral intake volume in the preceding 24 hours, with a separate unknown-nutrition indicator.

The primary estimand is the adjusted association between kidney deterioration and the 12-hour probability of a subsequent glucose below 70 mg/dL, conditional on insulin intensity and current glycemic state. The interaction with observed nutrition is exploratory because missing nutrition is not equivalent to absence.

## Methods and comparison

### Simple baseline

Fit a logistic regression for the subsequent hypoglycemia outcome using prespecified covariates: insulin intensity, kidney deterioration, current mean and minimum glucose, observed nutrition volume, unknown-nutrition indicator, age, sex, unit type, admission diagnosis, and vital signs. Use hospital fixed effects or hospital-stratified analysis if the number of hospitals supports it. Estimate uncertainty with patient-level bootstrap resampling or cluster-robust standard errors.

### Learned alternative

Fit a small temporal sequence model, such as a recurrent neural network or transformer with a 24-hour horizon, using the ordered insulin administrations, glucose results, creatinine results, urine-output records, and explicit nutrition records. The model receives time since admission, event type, numeric value, and missingness indicators; it predicts the 12-hour hypoglycemia outcome. Use a time-based split: train on earlier admissions, validate on later admissions, and repeat with multiple splits. Compare with the baseline using calibration (Brier score and calibration-in-the-large/separation) and the direction and uncertainty of the kidney-deterioration effect.

The learned alternative can reveal whether the timing and trajectory of kidney function and nutrition matter. The baseline collapses a 24-hour trajectory into one summary and may miss a recent creatinine rise or a change in nutrition just before insulin administration. The alternative does not prove a mechanism; it tests whether temporal structure improves prediction and whether its learned sensitivity to recent kidney deterioration is consistent with the prespecified hypothesis. If the learned model improves only marginally or its effect is unstable, the simpler baseline remains preferable.

## Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive result:** kidney deterioration remains positively associated with subsequent hypoglycemia after adjustment for insulin intensity and current glucose, with a prespecified direction and uncertainty interval excluding the null. A stronger result would show that the association is larger when explicit nutrition is low and that the learned model identifies recent kidney deterioration as important.
- **Adverse result:** no association after adjustment, or an opposite direction, would weaken the proposed renal-clearance explanation and favor insulin intensity, current glucose, severity, or surveillance as the dominant drivers.
- **Inconclusive result:** sparse outcomes, wide uncertainty, insufficient temporal overlap, or materially different results across splits would require a larger cohort, a different endpoint, or external validation.

A null result does not prove that kidney function is irrelevant; it may reflect measurement sparsity, threshold choices, or unmeasured nutrition. The study cannot establish causality or identify a safe insulin dose without prospective treatment assignment or a stronger causal design.

## Key references

- [K1] Olsen et al. (2026) studied AKI in hospitalized patients with type 2 diabetes and found that AKI was associated with reduced time in range, driven by hyperglycemia, without a significant increase in time below range. This challenges a simple assumption that AKI always increases hypoglycemia and motivates the need to condition on insulin intensity and current glucose. The present proposal adds a temporal ICU analysis of subsequent hypoglycemia after insulin exposure, rather than a cross-sectional association with CGM time-in-range.
- [K2] Jiang et al. (2026) studied critically ill patients and found that CGM-guided glucose management was associated with fewer severe hypoglycemic events, while explicitly noting that observational differences in measurement density and residual confounding prevent causal interpretation. This supports the clinical importance of hypoglycemia in the ICU and the need to account for monitoring intensity. The present proposal tests a specific risk factor—kidney deterioration—rather than a monitoring intervention.
- [K3] Tang et al. (2026) used the eICU Collaborative Research Database as an external validation cohort in a multicenter retrospective study, demonstrating that eICU can support temporal ICU analyses but also that incremental predictive value of a marker may be limited. This bounds what can be inferred from eICU data. The present proposal applies eICU to a new adverse-event endpoint and explicitly treats missing nutrition as unknown.

## Limitations

- Insulin dose parsing from free text may be imperfect; infusion rate is not directly available.
- A clinically documented hypoglycemic episode endpoint requires manual adjudication.
- Missing nutrition is coded as unknown, limiting the nutrition interaction analysis.
- The learned model is proposed, not completed; its feasibility and performance are unverified.
