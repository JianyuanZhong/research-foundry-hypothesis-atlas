# Episode 35: extubation-ordered initiation policy and hospital-observed airway repair

Parent: [prior hypothesis]

## Unresolved question, evidence boundary, and falsifiable hypothesis

After vasopressor-treated shock has resolved, should a markedly fluid-positive, invasively ventilated adult with adequate blood pressure, urine output, and potassium follow a policy of starting or restarting an ICU-input-system recorded furosemide or bumetanide delivery during the next ICU shift, while the patient remains invasively ventilated, versus avoiding such recorded delivery during that interval? The potential benefit is earlier durable liberation from an endotracheal airway; countervailing harms are early recorded creatinine rise, renal replacement therapy (RRT), or death.

The strongest available evidence supports narrower claims. The inspected full XML of Kuriyama et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13476641; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100120; frozen source [source checksum]) reports 26 randomized trials with 1,652 participants comparing diuretic strategies; most outcome evidence was low or very-low certainty and clinically heterogeneous, largely from heart-failure and post-cardiovascular-surgery populations. It does not establish the effect of initiating a loop after resolved shock. The inspected full XML of Bircher et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13154636; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100075; frozen source [source checksum]) states that optimal timing, volume, and duration of fluid removal remain uncertain and care is individualized. The inspected full XML of Hansford et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC12927355; DOI 10.1136/bmjmed-2024-001280; frozen source [source checksum]) supports aligning eligibility, strategy assignment, and follow-up and explicitly notes that target-trial emulation does not remove confounding. These sources support the importance and uncertainty of the question, not benefit, renal safety, a 12-hour biologic threshold, or causality. The previously inspected Chanda et al. full XML supports separating record-defined creatinine change from clinical AKI, not a loop effect. The ambition papers remain methodological demonstrations and are not clinical evidence for this question; no unavailable text is claimed as read.

The unresolved primary hypothesis is: in the frozen measured-overlap population, assignment-compatible follow-up under a policy of recorded loop initiation by 12 hours while still invasively ventilated increases the lower bound of five-day hospital-observed 48-hour extubation success by more than 5 percentage points versus recorded avoidance through 12 hours. Separate adverse hypotheses are an increase of at least 3 points in all-eligible record-defined 48-hour creatinine rise or at least 5 points in observed RRT7. These are associations between record-defined dynamic policies and record-defined outcomes. They do not identify a pharmacologic effect among recipients.

## Consequential defect and smallest substantive repair

The parent allows initiation at any time in 24 hours and says outcomes before initiation remain in both clones. More importantly, extubation does not close treatment assignment, so a person can be extubated first, receive a loop later for another indication, satisfy the initiation clone, and contribute an airway success that preceded exposure. That computation can represent assignment to a broad grace-period regime, but the parent's supportive wording calls it an association with the recorded-loop strategy without explaining that early outcomes are shared policy branches. It therefore invites a treatment-effect interpretation unsupported by event order.

A direct treatment-blind audit of discovery buckets 0-79 in the frozen MIMIC archive selected each subject's earliest valid ICU stay before clinical gates, required adult hour-48 structural eligibility, active accepted 225792 ventilation at t0, and applied the qualifying-row overlap component of the parent's washout; ambiguous washout rows were not excluded in this broad audit. It did not apply shock, balance, MAP, urine, potassium, ECMO/RRT, airway-linkage, or final outcome gates and estimated no arm effect. Among 4,658 broad structural landmarks, 897 had a qualifying loop start by 24 hours, with median start lag 11.47 hours; 72 of those had an item-227194 extubation record before the loop; under a 12-hour grace this was 19 of 466. There were 758 extubation records, 163 deaths, and 289 index-stay exits by 24 hours. A 6-hour grace retained only 232 broad initiators; 12 hours retained 466, while halving the opportunity for treatment-independent evolution. Thus a near-zero grace is not source-feasible, 24 hours creates a material mixture, and 12 hours is a treatment-blind feasibility compromise corresponding approximately to the observed median start lag, not a proven clinical optimum.

Replace G=t0+24h with G12=t0+12h for the primary policy and make the first accepted extubation or verified cessation of invasive ventilation a shared treatment-assignment closure. A later loop can never satisfy the initiation policy for an airway outcome already under way. Early extubation remains a legitimate common branch of both baseline policies, so the primary estimand is the effect of assignment to a dynamic “initiate while still ventilated by 12 hours” policy, not the effect of actually receiving loop before every outcome. Preserve the repaired effective starttime clock and overlap washout. Keep 6-hour and corrected 24-hour policies as feasibility/sensitivity estimands; neither may replace the primary after effects are seen.

The same audit identified an observation defect. Of 2,098 broad item-227194 candidates by day 5, only 1,054 had 48 hours of continuous ICU observation, but 1,746 remained alive in the same hospital admission for 48 hours; 702 were hospital-observed but failed the ICU-only rule. The parent therefore maps many favorable ward transfers to unknown. Replace the primary durability contract with same-admission hospital observation for a record-defined ICU-airway-failure endpoint; retain continuous-ICU durability as a required conservative sensitivity. This does not assert complete ward airway capture.

## Falsification and result interpretation

Supportive evidence requires the one-sided 95% lower limit for the HA-E48-D5 lower-bound contrast to exceed +0.05; ESS at least 75 per arm; at least 100 A12 satisfactions and 100 B12 compatible completions; no A12 treatment satisfaction after or tied with airway cessation; shared pre-initiation extubation reported and no more than 15% of eligible participants; and all inherited source, partition, state, overlap, balance, negative-control, ambiguity, contamination, renal-observation, and sensitivity gates. Continuous-ICU AA-E48-D5, 6-hour when feasible, and corrected 24-hour estimates must not materially reverse direction. The only permitted respiratory conclusion is: “In the measured-overlap discovery cohort, assignment-compatible follow-up under a policy of ICU-input-system recorded loop initiation by 12 hours while invasive ventilation continued was associated with at least a five-point increase in the record-defined hospital-observed airway outcome versus recorded avoidance.” It must add that shared early extubations were not effects of loop receipt and that causality is not established.

If RCR48 or RRT lower bounds cross +0.03 or +0.05 respectively, report a record-defined renal-harm signal and leave the clinical decision adverse/unresolved. If their lower bounds do not cross, say only that no prespecified recorded harm margin was detected; if upper bounds cross, renal safety and the combined decision remain unresolved.

Adverse evidence is HA-E48-D5 upper limit <=0, all-eligible RCR48 lower limit >=+0.03, observed-RRT7 lower limit >=+0.05 under both date brackets, or inherited death/alive-discharge harm. Margin-falsified is airway upper limit <=+0.05. Inconclusive/infeasible includes inherited sample, positivity, overlap, balance, control, contamination, or weight failures; early common extubation >15%; any treatment-satisfied A12 airway event not strictly after loop onset; fewer than 100 A12 satisfactions; wide bounds; renal unknown >15% or >5-point arm imbalance; or material reversal under 6/24-hour, continuous-ICU, store-lag, status-conflict, loop-item/version, amount/rate, airway-linkage, creatinine-readout, or RRT-date sensitivities. Null, adverse, and inconclusive outputs must never be narrated as benefit, renal safety, or equivalence.
