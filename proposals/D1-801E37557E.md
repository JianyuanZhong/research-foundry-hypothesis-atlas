# When to initiate loop diuretics: a timed target-trial experiment in MIMIC-IV

## Scientific opening and unresolved question

In critically ill patients with fluid overload and ongoing hypoperfusion, the decision to start a loop diuretic is consequential. The parent seed states that treatment effects may differ between stable perfusion and hypoperfusion, but it does not specify how to define that decision time, what outcome is measured, or how to separate diuretic initiation from the clinical state that prompted it.

The unresolved claim is not that diuretics improve patients. It is that, after explicitly accounting for the time-varying state that predicts treatment, the effect of initiating a loop diuretic at one ICU decision time differs from delaying that initiation by a prespecified interval. The competing proposal is an adaptation of the parent diuretic-timing question: it uses the available MIMIC-IV 3.1 structured data to estimate a decision-time treatment effect, while treating the result as a feasibility and decision-relevance study, not as proof of benefit.

The leading explanation is that early diuretic initiation can reduce fluid accumulation and subsequent organ stress, but may also be initiated because the patient is already critically ill. The strongest rival is that the observed association reflects selection into treatment or the severity of the state that prompted treatment. A third rival is that the timing decision is driven by a latent clinical state that is measured incompletely. The experiment tests these rivals by comparing initiation and delayed initiation within the same ICU encounter, using serial measurements, and by explicitly modeling time-varying treatment eligibility.

## Population and temporal boundaries

1. Restrict to adult ICU stays with `intime <= 2025-01-01` and `outtime > intime`.
2. Require at least one eligible ICU decision opportunity, defined below.
3. Require at least one loop-diuretic administration record in `inputevents` or a valid pharmacy record in `pharmacy` before the opportunity. This is a feasibility condition, not an eligibility restriction.
4. Exclude patients with `anchor_year_group >= 2020` or a death record in `patients.dod` before the opportunity.
5. For each eligible ICU stay, define the first decision opportunity after `intime + 24 hours` and before `outtime`. This avoids using post-decision information and is the primary decision time. A secondary sensitivity uses the earliest opportunity after `intime + 6 hours`.
6. A decision opportunity is eligible only if the patient has no loop-diuretic administration in the preceding 12 hours and is still in the ICU at the opportunity time.
7. Define the treatment group as initiation of a loop diuretic at the primary opportunity. The comparator is delayed initiation at the same opportunity, with no loop-diuretic administration in the next 24 hours, followed by an opportunity at `opportunity + 24 hours`. If no such opportunity exists, classify the observation as delayed/no-opportunity and do not use it as an effective treatment comparison.
8. Exclude opportunities where the patient dies before the comparator opportunity. This makes the primary comparison a conditional treatment effect among patients who remain alive and in the ICU until the comparator window.
9. Do not use MIMIC-CXR images or raw waveforms; they are unavailable in this configured snapshot.

## Exposure and clinical state

The exposure is a positive administration of a loop diuretic in `inputevents` or `pharmacy`. Use `inputevents` as the primary exposure source because its `starttime, endtime, amount, amountuom, rate, rateuom` fields can define an administration and stop time. Use `pharmacy` as a secondary source and reconcile duplicate administrations.

Use `icu/inputevents` to define the following time-varying covariates, all measured before each decision time:

- prior loop-diuretic administration in the preceding 24 hours, with a 6-hour lag for the primary analysis;
- vasopressor administration in the preceding 24 hours, using norepinephrine (221906), epinephrine (221794/229617), phenylephrine (221749/229630/229631), and vasopressin (222315), with positive `amount` and `rate` and a 12-hour lag;
- fluid input in the preceding 24 hours, using Ringer's lactate (220955), D5RL (220967), D10RL (220968), and Hetastarch (220980), with positive `amount` and `rate`;
- urine output in the preceding 24 hours, using OR urine (226627), PACU urine (226631), and the APACHE IV urine output field (227519), with positive `value` or `valuenum`;
- creatinine in the preceding 24 hours, using serum creatinine (50912/52546), urine creatinine (51082), and APACHE IV creatinine fields (226751/226752/227005), with positive numeric values;
- lactate in the preceding 24 hours, using blood lactate (50813/52442/53154), with positive numeric values;
- blood pressure from `chartevents` (item 38866) and heart rate from item 38809, with numeric values and a 6-hour lag;
- respiratory support from `procedureevents` and `datetimeevents` using the catalogued item IDs for intubation and mechanical ventilation, with a 12-hour lag.

The primary clinical state is a composite of fluid accumulation, organ stress, and hemodynamic instability at the decision time. It is not a validated diagnosis:

- fluid accumulation: recent positive fluid input, recent urine output below a prespecified threshold, and recent vasopressor exposure;
- organ stress: recent elevated creatinine, elevated lactate, or reduced urine output;
- hemodynamic instability: recent vasopressor exposure or low blood pressure.

The thresholds and item mappings are fixed before outcome analysis. They are not estimated from the outcome.

## Outcomes

The primary outcome is a prespecified composite of the next 72 hours:

- death in `admissions.deathtime` before the end of the 72-hour window;
- documented escalation to invasive mechanical ventilation or ECMO, using `procedureevents` and `datetimeevents` after the decision time;
- a prespecified composite of creatinine increase of at least 0.3 mg/dL from the baseline value or at least one AKI stage using the RIFLE/KDIGO criteria, using `labevents` and `chartevents`;
- ICU-free discharge before the 72-hour window.

The primary estimand is the standardized 72-hour cumulative incidence difference between loop-diuretic initiation and delayed initiation, among patients with the same baseline state and who remain alive and in ICU until the comparator opportunity. Report the absolute risk difference, risk ratio, and number needed to treat/harm.

Secondary outcomes are the component events above, the time from initiation to the first event, and the number of loop-diuretic administrations in the next 72 hours. These secondary outcomes are not used to define the primary estimand.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

### Simple baseline

Fit a target-trial propensity model with a logistic treatment model and a separate 72-hour outcome model. The treatment model includes the decision-time state, baseline state, age, sex, ICU length of stay, and the number of prior observations. The outcome model uses the same variables plus the treatment indicator. Use inverse-probability-of-treatment weights with truncation at the 1st and 99th percentiles, stabilized weights, and a prespecified sensitivity analysis without truncation. Estimate the standardized treatment effect by comparing the weighted outcome model predictions for initiation and delayed initiation.

This baseline is transparent and directly interpretable. It can reveal whether the observed treatment effect is associated with the measured state and whether treatment assignment is concentrated in high-risk patients.

### Learned longitudinal alternative

Fit a discrete-time recurrent model for the 24-hour treatment and outcome processes. The target is the sequence of treatment decisions and outcomes across the ICU stay, with state features from `chartevents`, `inputevents`, `outputevents`, `labevents`, `procedureevents`, and `datetimeevents` entered as lagged observations. Use a recurrent neural network or a gradient-boosted recurrent model, with patient-level split validation and no data from later observations. Evaluate calibration, discrimination, and the standardized treatment effect with the same outcome model. Compare the recurrent model with the baseline model using a predeclared test set and a held-out patient cohort.

The alternative can reveal whether the state trajectory itself carries information about both treatment and outcome that the baseline loses, and whether a learned representation improves the treatment-effect estimate. It is not selected for a small predictive gain alone. The recurrent model is deferred if the required item IDs or event counts are insufficient, if the recurrent fit does not converge, or if the held-out effect estimate is materially less stable than the baseline.

Approximate future-solver budget: the baseline can be CPU-only with 16 CPUs, 64 GiB RAM, and about 2–4 hours; the recurrent alternative may use 16 CPUs and 8–16 GiB RAM, with an estimated 4–8 hours, including convergence checks. These are planning estimates, not measured solver runtimes. No GPU is required for the baseline and is not mandated for the recurrent model.

## Time-varying confounding and selection

The primary analysis uses a marginal structural model with inverse-probability-of-treatment weights and a G-computation outcome model. This is an adaptation, not a reproduction of the parent seed. It explicitly conditions on pre-decision covariates and avoids using post-decision state to define treatment.

Use multiple imputation for missing covariates, with missingness indicators and a prespecified imputation model. Perform an outcome-model sensitivity analysis with and without imputation. Use a negative-control outcome—change in a laboratory value that should not be affected by diuretic initiation, such as a non-diuretic-related electrolyte change—only if the item and timing are clinically justified. A positive negative-control association is evidence that residual confounding or measurement error may be present; it is not proof of a mechanism.

The selection of ICU stays, the decision opportunity, and the availability of measurements is acknowledged. The primary comparison is conditional on being alive and in ICU until the comparator window. Report the proportion of eligible patients who qualify for the comparator window and the reasons for exclusion. A negative-control analysis or an external cohort with richer adjudicated fluid and organ-state data is required for stronger causal claims.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results for the hypothesis would be a statistically precise, clinically meaningful standardized 72-hour risk difference in the direction that early initiation reduces the composite, with the effect persisting after weighting and sensitivity analyses. The result would justify a prospective trial of timed diuretic initiation, not establish that the intervention is safe or beneficial in all patients.

Adverse results would include no difference, a larger risk with initiation, or an effect that disappears after controlling for time-varying state. This would argue against initiating diuretics on the basis of this decision-time signal and would suggest that the parent hypothesis needs a different timing or population.

Inconclusive results include sparse comparator opportunities, unstable weights, wide confidence intervals, or a large difference between the baseline and learned alternative. These findings should not be treated as evidence that diuretics are ineffective; they indicate that the available data cannot resolve the uncertainty.

Falsification tests are:

- a placebo timing analysis using a fixed interval that is not clinically justified, which should show no treatment effect;
- a placebo outcome using a laboratory value that is not plausibly affected by diuretic initiation;
- an analysis excluding patients with prior vasopressor exposure, prior fluid overload, or prior organ dysfunction;
- an analysis using the secondary 6-hour opportunity and excluding patients who die before the comparator window.

These tests are designed to detect timing artifacts, selection, and residual confounding. A positive placebo effect is a failure of the experiment, not evidence that the clinical hypothesis is true.

## Clinical adjudication limits and additional evidence

MIMIC-IV does not provide a validated diagnosis of fluid overload, microcirculatory dysfunction, or septic shock. It does not provide validated urine output measurements for all patients, reliable bedside hemodynamics, or expert adjudication of treatment decisions. Therefore, the experiment can estimate a treatment effect among patients with available structured measurements, but it cannot establish that the state was truly fluid-overloaded, that the decision was clinically optimal, or that the outcome was adjudicated correctly.

Stronger conclusions require clinical adjudication of fluid status and organ function, validated urine-output and hemodynamic measurements, expert review of treatment decisions, and an external validation cohort. A randomized trial is required to determine whether early initiation improves survival, organ recovery, or other patient-important outcomes.
