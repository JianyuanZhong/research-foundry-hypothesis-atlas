# Body-size and mechanics-adjusted ventilatory burden versus tidal volume alone for recorded liberation

## Episode 5 successor and decision

This is a substantive new direction grounded in unused eICU seed 05 and the episode-5 parent assignment. It asks whether an early ventilatory summary should represent respiratory-mechanics burden, not just tidal volume, when identifying adults who may need continued ventilatory support and closer liberation assessment. The practical decision is whether a future surveillance model should treat tidal volume alone as sufficient, or should incorporate body-size normalization and contemporaneous plateau/PEEP measurements.

This is an observational prognostic and transportability experiment. It will not estimate the effect of changing ventilator settings, define a safe ventilator target, rank hospitals, or establish that a setting caused delayed liberation.

## Evidence-supported claim and unresolved hypothesis

Before execution, the strongest supported claim is structural and feasibility-related. The frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative respiratory records with exact fields for FiO2, PEEP, set tidal volume, tidal volume per ideal body weight, plateau pressure, peak inspiratory pressure, respiratory rate, and ventilator start/end offsets. A read-only audit found 20,168,176 respiratoryCharting rows across 124,598 stays; exact labels included FiO2 (2,926,039), PEEP (1,464,808), Tidal Volume (set) (1,034,549), TV/kg IBW (1,024,017), and Plateau Pressure (353,455). respiratoryCare contained 865,381 rows across 44,772 stays, with 180,176 nonzero ventstartoffset records. These counts establish that fields are present, not that their semantics are uniform, complete, clinically adjudicated, or associated with the outcome.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adult eICU stays with a recorded ventilatory-support episode and sufficient early measurements, a six-hour summary that adds body-size-normalized tidal volume and respiratory-mechanics measures (especially plateau pressure, PEEP, and their driving-pressure proxy) predicts delayed recorded ventilatory liberation and transports better across hospitals than a matched model using tidal volume alone.

The primary null is no incremental association or no held-out improvement in the mechanics-augmented model after hospital grouping, measurement-density adjustment, competing discharge/death, and endpoint-ascertainment checks.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use one patientunitstayid as the index unit and retain uniquepid for a person-clustered sensitivity. Include adults with numeric patient.age >= 18, treating the eICU > 89 convention as age 90 and reporting missing-age exclusions. Require unitdischargeoffset >= 360.

Define a candidate ventilatory episode using respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset >= 0 or a predeclared, audited exact respiratory-chart rule indicating ventilator-on status. The primary analysis requires the episode to overlap minute 0–360 and at least one valid early tidal-volume measure plus either a valid plateau-pressure/PEEP pair or an explicit reason for missing mechanics. Do not call every oxygen-support record invasive mechanical ventilation. Before modeling, enumerate airwaytype values and respiratory-chart status values; if available values cannot distinguish invasive ventilation from noninvasive or oxygen-only support, label the population as recorded ventilatory support and do not claim invasive ventilation.

The landmark is minute 360. Exposure summaries use only source observations with 0 <= respchartoffset <= 360 or 0 <= respcarestatusoffset <= 360. For paired mechanics, match plateau pressure and PEEP within 15 minutes within stay; retain the pairing gap and number of observations. Use only values passing fixed, outcome-independent plausibility rules established before outcome analysis. Do not carry a ventilator setting across gaps longer than 60 minutes. Summaries are first, last, median, maximum, interquartile range, slope, observation count, covered fraction, and maximum gap.

Primary derived measures:

- Tidal-volume burden: median and maximum numeric Tidal Volume (set) normalized by predicted body weight calculated from patient.admissionheight and patient.gender, when height and sex are valid.
- Mechanics burden: paired plateau pressure, PEEP, and plateau minus PEEP (a recorded driving-pressure proxy), with no claim that it equals clinically adjudicated driving pressure.
- Secondary directly charted TV/kg IBW, Tidal Volume (delivered), and Exhaled TV (machine) analyses, each kept separate by exact label.
- Supporting respiratory context: FiO2, respiratory rate, mean airway pressure and pressure support, never treated as equivalent modalities.

Height, sex, or units that are missing or ambiguous remain missing. The primary cohort does not impute an unobserved mechanical measurement as a normal setting.

Follow-up begins after minute 360 and ends at the earliest of minute 2,880, ICU discharge, ICU death, or a valid recorded liberation event. Define the primary event as the first post-landmark respiratoryCare.ventendoffset that is positive and within follow-up, conditional on an audited record being a valid ventilatory-episode end. A secondary endpoint uses the first exact respiratoryCharting RT Vent On/Off or equivalent status transition to an audited off state. Report the two endpoint definitions separately; do not combine them unless their concordance is prespecified and adequate.

Ascertainment states are: recorded liberation; still recorded on support at 48 hours; ICU death/discharge before a qualifying liberation record; and unknown because a ventilatory episode or end status cannot be ascertained. Death/discharge is not a successful liberation and absence of an end record is not proof of ongoing ventilation.

## Models, baselines, and uncertainty

Fit pooled discrete-time cause-specific hazards over 6–12, 12–24, 24–36, and 36–48 hours. The event is first recorded liberation; ICU death and discharge before a qualifying record are competing absorbing states, while unascertainable support status is reported as unknown and handled in a prespecified observation-weighted sensitivity.

Use matched model families and preprocessing frozen within training hospitals:

- M0: age, gender, ethnicity, hospital admission source, unit type, unit stay type, and fixed early measurement-density summaries;
- Mtv: M0 plus early tidal-volume summaries, including normalized tidal volume where calculable;
- Mmech: Mtv plus paired plateau pressure, PEEP, driving-pressure proxy, and prespecified respiratory-rate/FiO2 context;
- Mdirect: a sensitivity replacing calculated normalization with exact TV/kg IBW and exact delivered-volume labels, never pooling labels with different semantics.

The primary incremental comparison is Mmech versus Mtv, with Mtv versus M0 reported separately. A transparent linear-logit model is primary; fixed-knot splines are a prespecified sensitivity, not selected after seeing outcomes. All label inventories, plausibility bounds, normalization, imputation, and tuning are learned from training hospitals only.

Use five grouped hospital folds with all stays from a hospital held out together. Report fold- and hospital-level held-out Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC and area under the precision-recall curve, cause-specific hazard ratios or risk contrasts with 95% uncertainty, and the paired Mmech minus Mtv change. Use hospital-cluster bootstrap or fold-level intervals, and a person-clustered sensitivity by uniquepid. Report counts for every ascertainment state and the distribution of exposure missingness and pairing gaps.

Because documentation and liberation ascertainment may be informative, estimate observation weights using only pre-landmark data: demographics, admission source, unit, hospital, early support evidence, number of respiratory observations, number of distinct labels, baseline density, and whether a qualifying early mechanics pair exists. Show positivity, truncation, and sensitivity to no weighting. Weighting does not repair unmeasured clinician decisions or missing ventilator status.

## Falsification and interpretation

Computationally adverse or falsifying results include:

- no stable incremental association or held-out improvement for Mmech over Mtv;
- direction changes across hospitals, endpoint definitions, threshold/gap rules, or calculated-versus-direct normalization;
- apparent improvement restricted to one hospital, complete-follow-up cases, long documentation gaps, one respiratory label, or a random patient split;
- the same mechanics signal predicting a pre-landmark pseudo-liberation or a permuted post-landmark endpoint;
- shuffling mechanics within hospital and broad tidal-volume/measurement-density strata failing to remove the claimed incremental performance;
- performance depending on respchartentryoffset, Apache first-24-hour aggregates, discharge location, or other future/leakage fields;
- mechanics being available primarily for high-density or selected patients, with nonpositive or unstable observation weights.

Supportive results require a directionally consistent, clinically interpretable mechanics contribution conditional on tidal volume, preserved calibration or lower held-out Brier error, and reproducibility across multiple hospitals and both prespecified endpoint definitions. This supports a portable prognostic marker for recorded liberation ascertainment, not a ventilator-setting target or treatment effect.

Adverse results favor tidal volume alone, show that mechanics are not transportable, or show that the signal is a documentation-density artifact. They do not show that plateau pressure or PEEP are biologically irrelevant.

Inconclusive results include insufficient hospital overlap, too few valid endpoint events, inability to distinguish ventilatory support from oxygen-only care, poor label/unit concordance, sparse paired mechanics, nonpositive observation weights, or discordance between respiratoryCare and respiratoryCharting endpoint definitions. The appropriate next study requires validated ventilator-device exports and expert respiratory adjudication, not relaxed exclusions or a new cutoff.

The computation can verify file integrity, exact joins, temporal ordering, formulas, cohort flow, endpoint-state accounting, held-out predictions, uncertainty, and permutation/leakage checks. It cannot establish invasive-ventilation status from ambiguous labels, true delivered mechanical power, clinician intent, causal harm from a setting, successful physiologic liberation, or clinical utility. Those require respiratory expert adjudication, richer ventilator/device data, and prospective or quasi-experimental evaluation.

## Demonstration dispositions

The three research-ambition demonstrations are references, not evidence for this hypothesis.

1. Learning the natural history of human disease with generative transformers (DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09529-3): the local article text and available supplements were inspected. Its temporal ordering and external-validation discipline motivate hospital-held-out evaluation here, but eICU lacks its lifetime population/genetic context and disease-trajectory target; this is not a reproduction.

2. Advancing cancer detection and treatment using longitudinal routine clinical data (DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2026.07.009): the README states that the main paper and full STAR Methods remain unavailable. Only its metadata and supplement were inspected; no unavailable main-paper claim is used. Its cancer-specific modalities are not configured in eICU, so it is not pursued.

3. A Bayesian framework for longitudinal EHR and genetic discovery (DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10780-5): the local article and available supplement/reporting material were inspected. Only its general emphasis on uncertainty and longitudinal selection motivates the present design; eICU lacks configured germline data and this is not a genetic discovery study.
