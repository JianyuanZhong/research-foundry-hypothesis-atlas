# Discharge-boundary nodule handoff with timestamp-safe all-opportunity states

## Clinical question and advance

An incidental pulmonary-nodule recommendation can fail at two distinct transition points: the recommendation-bearing radiology state may not be stored by discharge, or a discharge artifact stored by discharge may not faithfully carry the actionable interval. A third possibility is only apparent failure caused by stale summaries, addenda entered after the original report, or a timestamp that does not represent user visibility. The clinically consequential decision is whether a prospective workflow should first validate/fix discharge-artifact finalization and availability or reconcile radiology recommendations into the discharge plan.

The falsifiable MIMIC question is: among all first, adjudicated, incidental, unconditional, interval-specific chest-CT recommendation opportunities whose knowledge state is resolvable at the actual discharge boundary, what mutually exclusive discharge-boundary process states occur, and does strict artifact-delay prevalence exceed strict content-failure prevalence by a prespecified operational margin? Same-system imaging is secondary and observation-limited; it is never called adherence, benefit, or an effect of documentation.

This synthesis retains the high-mu parent's exact source bindings, full lexical feasibility scan, observation-aware downstream framing, blinded clinical adjudication, and negative controls, and the second parent's all-opportunity state architecture, reciprocal addendum audit, sole-DS rule, and partial-identification intent. It repairs several remaining estimand/timestamp vulnerabilities: `N` is frozen before any discharge artifact or future follow-up is inspected; charttime cannot establish artifact visibility; recommendation states cannot use an addendum with future event-time merely because it was stored early; stale-summary chronology is a coded state rather than silent omission; the primary contrast is an ordinary multinomial contrast rather than a falsely “paired” comparison; and date-only death is interval-censored rather than treated as an exact timestamp.

## Evidence boundary and unresolved claim

Prior published work supports a communication/documentation problem and feasibility of tracking, including an association between discharge-summary mention and timely imaging, but does not establish that documentation causes surveillance or improves cancer outcomes. The available MIMIC audits are feasibility evidence, not clinical truth: the complete radiology source contains 2,321,355 rows; the complete discharge source contains 331,794 DS rows, one per admission key in the audited snapshot; the reciprocal radiology graph contains 25,735 directed edges, with 25,600 present chronological same-admission RR-to-AR pairs and 135 edges involving absent nodes. Delayed DS storage is common in the audit, but `storetime` is not signature, user visibility, transmission, or physical departure.

The testable process claim is therefore only that one strict, observable state is more prevalent than another. A positive result nominates a prospective workflow target; it does not prove communication failure, recommendation appropriateness, or patient benefit. The study cannot infer outside-care nonadherence, malignancy prevention, or causal surveillance effects.

## Report/addendum state and eligibility

Scan all radiology rows; lexical rules are high-sensitivity retrieval, never the endpoint. Link a base `RR` to an admission only when `(subject_id,hadm_id)` matches and `admittime-12h <= base.charttime <= dischtime`; repeat with zero-hour and `edregtime/edouttime` boundaries. Confirm CT/CTA chest (or abdominal CT with lung bases) from all `radiology_detail` fields and text. Exclude screening, explicit nodule surveillance, cancer staging/oncologic surveillance, active primary lung malignancy, nonpulmonary nodules, conditional/optional recommendations, explicit no-follow-up recommendations, hospice/comfort-only transition, in-hospital death, and a pre-discharge CT explicitly satisfying/superseding the recommendation.

Reconstruct the reciprocal graph from both parent and addendum fields. For each base, use only reciprocal, subject-consistent, admission-consistent edges; do not infer edges from row order or one direction. At boundary `D=dischtime`, build the knowledge state from the base and linked addenda with nonmissing `storetime <= D`, but apply this timestamp-consistency rule before lexical eligibility:

1. Every component used to assert the recommendation must have `charttime <= D` or a clinically adjudicated event-time interpretation that is explicitly marked uncertain. A component with `charttime > D` but `storetime <= D` is a `timestamp-discordant` report state, not silently treated as pre-discharge knowledge.
2. If the recommendation exists only in a post-D addendum, it is not a pre-discharge opportunity. It remains in the unconditional screening cascade and a delayed-addendum table, never in the primary denominator.
3. Missing required node, missing required `storetime`, cross-admission/subject edge, contradictory reciprocal direction, non-deterministic cycle, or unresolved charttime/storetime conflict is `U_R`. Report `U_R` as a bound and do not recode it as omission or availability failure.

The recommendation must be patient-specific, affirmed, unconditional, and specify chest/thoracic CT at exactly 3, 6, or 12 **calendar months**. Do not require guideline concordance, pack-years, or “new” status; abstract size, morphology, multiplicity, stability/newness, and prior local imaging only when stated. Two radiology-trained reviewers adjudicate every rule-screened candidate using report text and radiology history available at `D`, blinded to discharge text and later outcomes. Select the first eligible admission per subject by base `charttime`, then base `storetime`, then `note_id`; never use DS content, later notes, or follow-up to select it.

Define the primary opportunity denominator `N` before reading DS text/content, DS storage timing, or any post-discharge observation: first-subject opportunities with a resolvable, pre-discharge recommendation state after the above timestamp rule. A missing DS row, unknown DS `storetime`, DS delay, DS content, and later trace do not alter membership in `N`. Report all lexical candidates, metadata-confirmed candidates, clinical exclusions, `U_R`, late-addendum candidates, and `N` in an unconditional cascade.

## One-final-summary rule and mutually exclusive strict states

Restrict to `DS` rows. Validate the complete source and count rows per `(subject_id,hadm_id)` before analysis. In this snapshot, the audit found exactly one DS row per 331,794 admission keys and no within-admission version ties. Therefore the sole DS row is canonical, even if its `charttime` precedes or its `storetime` follows discharge. Do not choose “latest storetime,” substitute an earlier artifact, or treat charttime as storage. If a future refresh has multiple DS rows, stop and freeze a new version rule before analysis.

For each `i in N`, assign exactly one strict state at `D`:

* `A`: canonical DS exists, `storetime` is known and `storetime > D`. This is only an observed storage-delay signal, not proof of invisibility, unsigned status, unavailable UI, absent departure artifact, or failed communication.
* `F`: canonical DS has known `storetime <= D`, text is classifiable, and blinded transition adjudication finds active patient-specific pulmonary nodule plus chest/thoracic CT action plus exact 3/6/12-month interval or an unambiguous equivalent calendar due date.
* `C_O`: DS stored by `D`, classifiable, but no active patient-specific actionable nodule plan.
* `C_I`: DS stored by `D`, classifiable, but nodule/action/timing is incomplete or interval/modality discordant without an explicit alternate resolution. Approximate “in a few months” or a range is not exact unless the codebook defines an unambiguous due date.
* `R`: DS stored by `D` and explicitly documents an alternate resolution (for example a documented goals-of-care decision, refusal/preference, specialist takeover with concrete alternative, or scheduled outside follow-up). This is documentation of an alternate plan, neither faithful success nor content failure; it must be explicit, not inferred.
* `U_D`: no linked DS row, missing DS `storetime`, unreadable/contradictory DS linkage, or unclassifiable text. Unknown is not `A`, omission, or discordance.

`A,F,C_O,C_I,R,U_D` are mutually exclusive and exhaustive over `N`. Define `p_A=n_A/N`, `p_C=(n_CO+n_CI)/N`, and `Delta=p_A-p_C`. This is a one-sample multinomial contrast with joint covariance, not a paired treatment comparison. Report the full state vector, `C_O` and `C_I` separately, and `Delta`; never compare `A` to `C` after selecting only stored artifacts. The conditional observable-content description is `N_obs=n_F+n_CO+n_CI+n_R`, with `(F,C_O,C_I,R)/N_obs`, and is not a substitute for the all-opportunity estimand.

## Stale summaries, addenda, and delayed transitions

Record four chronology markers when available: base report `charttime/storetime`, first recommendation-introducing component `charttime/storetime`, DS `charttime`, and DS `storetime`. If the sole DS was stored before the recommendation became available but the recommendation is available by `D`, classify its content as `C_O`/`C_I` with a `stale-artifact` subtype; do not call it a writer's failure to incorporate information unavailable at DS creation. If DS is stored after `D`, it remains `A` regardless of whether later text is faithful or omitted. If DS charttime is before `D` but storetime after `D`, it remains `A`; charttime only describes discordance.

Construct transitions from strict states at +24 hours and +7 days without changing the primary state. A delayed `A` may later be `F`, `C_O`, `C_I`, or `R`; a later state does not retroactively change `A`. A post-D addendum cannot change eligibility or strict exposure. Report all late addenda that negate, reinforce, or change interval as descriptive transition types. These rules prevent a stale summary, delayed addendum, or later final text from being counted both as availability and content failure.

## Primary hypothesis, uncertainty, and timestamp bounds

The operational decision hypothesis is `Delta > 0.05`, with sensitivity margins 0 and 0.10. The 0.05 is an intervention-prioritization margin, not a clinical-quality threshold. Availability-first support requires a simultaneous two-sided 95% lower bound for `Delta` above 0.05, all adjudication and precision gates, and bounds over unresolved states that do not reverse the conclusion. Content-first support requires the simultaneous upper bound below -0.05 and reliable concordance adjudication. Otherwise choose neither mechanism or propose a bundled/factorial prospective test.

Use 9,999 fixed-seed subject-level bootstrap replicates, recomputing the full multinomial state vector, `p_A`, `p_C`, `p_F`, conditional content vector, and `Delta`; use max-|t| simultaneous intervals, with nonstudentized max-deviation and exact multinomial intervals if a component is zero. A dominance claim requires at least 200 `N`, at least 20 `A` and 20 `C` events, `U_D <=5%`, all annotation gates, and simultaneous `Delta` interval width <=0.10. If `U_D` exceeds 5%, assign unresolved states entirely to A and entirely to C for partial-identification bounds; no point estimate selects an intervention unless the conclusion holds across bounds.

Timestamp uncertainty is reported separately from missingness. `storetime` remains an observed storage timestamp, not visibility. For every boundary ordering, retain raw minute/second differences and a `charttime/storetime` discordance flag. Recompute strict states under (a) exact recorded timestamps, (b) a conservative boundary tie rule that treats events within the documented timestamp resolution as unresolved, and (c) an interval-censored ordering rule for any date-only `dod`: death is in `[dod 00:00, dod+1 day)` and is not an exact competing-event time. If an ordering can change eligibility or a surveillance/death state under this interval, report lower and upper state bounds rather than choosing a direction. Never use `dod` to claim death preceded a CT when intervals overlap. The same principle applies to any missing or coarsened report/addendum time. A reversal between exact and conservative timing is boundary dependence and makes the mechanism result inconclusive.

## Secondary observation-aware surveillance

Do not use downstream imaging to select availability versus content intervention. For every `N`, define due date as base report `charttime +` 3/6/12 calendar months using calendar arithmetic, not 30-day substitution. Time zero is `D` for the all-opportunity outcome; for descriptive artifact-timing analyses also report `max(D,DS.storetime)` without changing the primary denominator. The event is first same-system chest CT/CTA with `charttime` after time zero and within due date +/-42 days; nodule-focused status requires blinded adjudication. Report +/-30 and +/-90-day windows and separate later acute-admission CT from nodule-surveillance evidence.

Assign an all-`N` joint observation state by fixed precedence: (1) timed CT before recorded/interval-certain death, (2) death before window end with no prior timed CT, (3) no timed CT/death but a later MIMIC radiology or admission trace after due+42, (4) no later trace/unknown. For date-only death or overlapping CT/death intervals, retain an ambiguity category and bound states rather than force precedence. Report the all-N risk of state 1 by strict state descriptively; never condition the primary secondary estimand on later trace, `N_obs`, or observed-only follow-up. Continued trace is itself an outcome. Any inverse-probability-of-observation result is a sensitivity under unverifiable missing-at-random assumptions. MIMIC absence is not nonadherence or absent outside care.

Falsification checks are a pre-index temporal placebo window, non-chest imaging in the due window, differential continued-trace rates, nodule-focused versus generic/acute CT specificity, and timestamp-bound sensitivity. A placebo or generic-utilization association as large as the surveillance association, association driven by acute/non-nodule CT, or materially different observation states makes the secondary association inconclusive. No causal adjustment or benefit claim is permitted.

## Adjudication, feasibility, and interpretation

Two independent reviewers adjudicate incidental status, CT coverage, unconditionality, exact interval, report/addendum meaning, timestamp discordance, faithful text, stale artifact, incomplete/discordant content, alternate resolution, and nodule-focused follow-up. Report raw agreement, Gwet AC1 and kappa with subject-bootstrap intervals for eligibility, exact interval, and states. Confirmatory inference requires raw agreement >=0.80, AC1 >=0.70, and complete consensus for each domain; persistent failure yields reviewer-specific bounds only. Validate lexical screening against consensus (sensitivity >=0.95 and lower Wilson bound >0.90); automated state labels require PPV >=0.90 with lower bound >0.80 to replace adjudication.

Automatic verification can check source hashes, complete-file counts, archive members, reciprocal edges, sole-DS rule, admission joins, strict timestamp filters, first-subject selection, mutually exclusive state assignment, stale/delayed transition matrices, no post-index denominator conditioning, calendar-month arithmetic, bootstrap intervals, timestamp bounds, observation precedence, and conclusion-to-output linkage. Experts and another study are required for guideline appropriateness, actual visibility/signature/transmission/departure, clinical intent, patient receipt, outside imaging, adherence, malignancy, radiation harm, survival, or patient benefit. A prospective local timestamp crosswalk is required before calling `A` a true availability failure; randomized/stepped-wedge or factorial evaluation with outside-system capture is required for causal intervention claims.

Supportive result: one mechanism dominates across exact and conservative timestamp bounds, with precision and adjudication gates passing; this prioritizes validation/testing of that workflow only. Adverse result: both process gaps are low/precise or alternate resolutions predominate, redirecting work to tracking, scheduling, communication, or cross-system exchange. Inconclusive result: sparse `N`, unresolved states, agreement failure, interval crossing the margin, timing reversal, or control/observation bias. None establishes clinical benefit.

## Changes and remaining limits

Compared with the two assessed parents, this child keeps the stronger source/audit and observational discipline while making the denominator and timing rules executable at the compiler boundary. It adds a formal charttime-versus-storetime conflict state, prohibits future-event-time addenda from silently entering a pre-discharge knowledge state, explicitly codes stale summaries, retains the sole-DS rule, corrects the primary contrast's statistical description, and bounds date-only death and any coarsened timestamp rather than treating it as exact. The central unresolved limits remain the semantics of `storetime`, missing outside care, undocumented alternate plans, incomplete mortality, sparse clinically adjudicated opportunities, and lack of pixels/orders/communication/outcomes. The correct endpoint remains an evidence-bounded process decomposition, not adherence, causality, or patient benefit.
