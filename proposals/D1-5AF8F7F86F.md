# Episode 71 successor: is an optionally observed repeat lactate useful in a calibrated hospital mortality-review queue?

## Unresolved clinical question and advance

At a fixed 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed entry, should a mortality-review service use a repeat lactate when it is already available, despite the fact that repeat testing is selectively performed and its result may not be visible at the bedside? The leading EICU line has established a strict, reproducible 1,229-person/507-death/722-survivor/33-hospital cohort and has asked whether late lactate adds value conditional on a repeat result. That conditional result is not yet a workflow result: a queue cannot prioritize people whose repeat result is absent unless missingness is explicitly part of the policy.

This successor makes the workflow question primary. It evaluates an availability-preserving model in the complete baseline/L24 risk set: the repeat lactate is an optional feature, with its availability and measurement age represented separately from its numeric value. It then retains the inherited measured-overlap comparison as a prespecified decomposition of numeric value versus measurement process. The advance is not a more complex predictor. It is an estimand that distinguishes (1) whether the observed repeat-lactate process changes a fixed workload queue and (2) whether the number itself adds information once available.

Baysan et al. (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407; inspected full-text XML source [source checksum]) externally validated a model in 4,440 MIMIC patients and reported recalibrated C-statistic 0.62 for APACHE IV versus 0.64 after adding 24-hour lactate. That supports a modest prognostic increment, not a calibrated fixed-capacity review policy, not transportability across multiple hospitals, and not benefit from ordering or acting on a repeat test.

## Hypothesis, estimands, and evidence boundaries

Primary hypothesis H_workflow: among adults reaching the 24-hour landmark with a valid early lactate, a calibrated model that adds the observed optional repeat-lactate process and value captures at least 3 percentage points more of eventual hospital deaths in a within-hospital top-10% mortality-review queue than the same model without repeat-lactate information, when predictions are generated for hospitals held out from fitting.

For hospital h, let n_h be the number in the complete baseline/L24 analysis population and k_h=ceil(0.10 n_h). Let Q_full,h and Q_base,h be the k_h highest predicted risks from the optional-repeat and no-repeat models, with ascending patientunitstayid as the tie-break. Let D=1 when hospitaldischargestatus=Expired and D=0 when it is Alive. The primary estimand is:

  Delta10_workflow = sum_h sum_{i in Q_full,h} D_i / sum_h sum_i D_i
                    - sum_h sum_{i in Q_base,h} D_i / sum_h sum_i D_i.

The locked materiality threshold is +3 percentage points, approximately the scale of 15 deaths in the inherited 507-death cohort; it is a decision-relevance anchor, not a validated clinical-utility threshold. A fixed 20% within-hospital queue is secondary and is not allowed to rescue the primary result.

Primary model decomposition:

- R_base: the common L24 snapshot, with no repeat-lactate availability, age, or value variables.
- R_process: R_base plus F_available and, only where available, repeat-lactate measurement age (L24-F_offset)/720, with missing-age indicator.
- R_full: R_process plus ln(F) and ln(F)^2; missing F is training-imputed after preprocessing and has the explicit F_available indicator.

The primary contrast is R_full versus R_base because it answers the actual optional-result workflow question. R_process versus R_base quantifies process information. Among the inherited measured-overlap population only, R_full versus R_process is the conditional numeric-value contrast; it is reported with the parent’s fixed quota and is not substituted for the primary. If the process increment is as large as or larger than the full increment, a positive primary result is labelled process-dependent and cannot justify a repeat-testing policy.

The strongest supported claim before this experiment is that late lactate can be associated with subsequent in-hospital mortality and may add modest prediction beyond a prior severity model. The unresolved claim is H_workflow. Neither the prior evidence nor a supportive result establishes that lactate should be ordered, that a reviewer can see the result, that review changes treatment, or that mortality improves.

## Hospital-held-out analysis and uncertainty

Use  hospital-level leave-one-hospital-out prediction on the fixed primary hospital set. Fit each R model on all other hospitals using unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10000, tol=1e-8, and seed 20260971. Winsorize continuous predictors at training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize by training mean and population SD, and mean-impute missing continuous predictors after scaling to zero. Do not scale missing indicators. A feature with no finite training values or zero post-winsorization SD fails the fold; no feature may be dropped.

For every outer training set, generate inner hospital-out-of-fold raw predictions and fit a two-parameter Platt recalibrator using only those predictions: logistic regression of D on (1, logit(p_raw)), same L2/solver/tolerance settings. Apply q=logistic(a+b logit(p_raw)) only to outer held-out predictions. Clip finite probabilities to [1e-15,1-1e-15] before logits; any nonfinite or failed inner fit is noncomputable. No outer outcome may affect preprocessing, calibration, quotas, or feature choice.

For each model, require pooled calibration-in-the-large absolute risk difference <=0.05, pooled calibration slope 0.80-1.20, and at least 80% of hospitals with finite predictions whose mean predicted risk is within 0.10 of observed death rate. These guardrails determine whether probability-based secondary decision curves are interpretable; queue capture remains the primary estimand.

Within each hospital, select ceil(0.10 n_h) and secondary ceil(0.20 n_h) by descending q and ascending patientunitstayid. Report capture, sensitivity, precision, false positives, deaths not flagged, paired additions/removals, hospital-specific contrasts, and queue overlap. Also report process and conditional-value contrasts, F availability overall and by hospital, and rank-switch fractions.

Use 2,000 copied-hospital bootstrap replicates, seed 20260971. Sample hospitals with replacement; each copied original remains one held-out group and is excluded from the corresponding training data. Refit outer models, inner calibration, and queues in every replicate. Require >=95% finite converged replicates and report percentile 95% intervals for primary, secondary, process, and conditional contrasts. Fixed-prediction deletion diagnostics report contrasts and calibration, but never rescue a failed primary result. A supportive interpretation requires >=80% finite hospital deletions with positive Delta10_workflow and a non-opposite 20% result.

The secondary decision-curve diagnostic uses thresholds 0.30, 0.35, ..., 0.50 and NB=TP/N-FP/N*p/(1-p), with the exact trapezoid average over width 0.20. Treat-all and treat-none are reference strategies only; DCA cannot relabel the queue hypothesis or compensate for failed calibration.

## Falsification and interpretation

Apply computability gates first.

- Not computable: any source/header/hash, frame, population, time, revision, feature, fold, calibration, prediction, quota, bootstrap, or deletion gate fails. Do not substitute random patient splits, pooled calibration, raw probabilities, altered windows, or the measured-overlap cohort.
- Supportive workflow increment: all gates pass; both models pass calibration guardrails; the copied-hospital 95% lower bound for Delta10_workflow is >=+3 points; >=80% of finite hospital deletions are positive; and the 20% contrast is not directionally opposite.
- Process-dependent: the primary workflow result meets support, but Delta10_process is at least as large as the full increment, or the conditional numeric-value contrast is not positive. This supports only an observed measurement-process association, not ordering or repeat-testing.
- Calibration/operational failure: the interval meets +3 but either model fails calibration, site stability, or the fixed-quota decision contract.
- Opposite: the primary interval upper bound is <0. This opposes optional repeat-lactate information for this comparator, quota and selected population.
- Adverse to materiality: the upper bound is <+3 but >=0. This rejects the locked three-point workflow increment, not all smaller information gains.
- Inconclusive: the interval spans +3, deletion direction is unstable, the 20% contrast is opposite without a wholly negative primary interval, or process and full contrasts are materially discordant without a clean branch.

A supportive result means only that adding an observed optional repeat-lactate process/value to a calibrated, hospital-held-out model improves modeled fixed-capacity re-ranking at L24. An adverse result weakens the prespecified three-point increment. An opposite result means the augmented model worsened modeled queue capture for this setting. Inconclusive means the evidence cannot distinguish the locked margin.

No result establishes that repeat lactate should be ordered, that its result is visible, that a queue review changes care, or that mortality, safety, workload, cost, or equity improves. Clinical informatics adjudication is needed for test ordering, specimen/assay identity, result revision semantics, device validity, and review timestamps. Clinician/stakeholder work is needed to validate the 10%/20% workload quotas and materiality margin. Prospective external validation should predefine the workflow, assay, display and review timestamps; an impact study, ideally randomized for any ordering or treatment policy, is required for causal patient benefit.
