## Scientific opening and falsifiable claim

The parent’s respiratoryCare start can be corroborated by respiratoryCharting, but both may be documentation interfaces. The treatment table adds a third stored stream with a treatment label and ICU-relative offset. The clinically important unresolved boundary is whether a later strict respiratoryCare interval-start record is reproduced in a separately stored treatment-record stream beyond generic record availability, shared workflow, timing coincidence, and exit/EOL selection.

The strongest evidence-supported claims are bounded. Pollard et al. describe eICU as a multi-center, high-granularity archive containing care documentation and treatment information, while warning that only parts of continuously streamed bedside information are archived and that table/query definitions can be ambiguous [K1]. Rajkomar et al. show that temporally ordered EHR representations can support cross-center prediction without site-specific harmonization, but this does not validate eICU treatment semantics [K2]. Tang et al. describe reproducible preprocessing for irregular structured EHR data, including an eICU proof of concept, but do not establish that a treatment row is an order, administration, delivery, or clinical event [K3]. None of these works tests the proposed three-stream concordance.

**Hypothesis.** In the unchanged adult first-ICU S6 risk set with no valid documented active respiratoryCare vent interval at minute 360, the pre-landmark parent state/process vector will show a coherent hospital-held-out increment for the strict documented respiratoryCare interval-start outcome when, and only when, the start is temporally concordant with an outcome-blind, source-labeled respiratory row in treatment. The increment must persist in treatment-observable risk sets and availability-weighted sensitivity analyses, across prespecified ±10/±30/±60-minute windows, away from ICU exit/EOL, across hospitals, and against schedule-preserving treatment-offset nulls. If the signal predicts treatment-row opportunity or generic documentation density, is explained by shared schedule, or is concentrated at exit/backfill times, the treatment stream does not add corroboration.

This is a documentation hypothesis, not a treatment hypothesis. The estimand is a recorded cross-table transition. A positive result cannot establish that therapy was ordered, administered, delivered, effective, indicated, or causal.

## Interpretation and limits

Supportive requires: frozen raw-string classes before outcomes; exact source/hash/header and joins pass; at least five viable hospitals with positive opportunity and strict event support; adequate positivity and ESS; directionally coherent held-out R_start_doc and C_RT_doc results in both matched model families; the paired process increment exceeds the inherited negligible margin (0.01 AUC where defined) with uncertainty; and the result is not dominated by availability weighting, T_nonresp_control, schedule null, one hospital, one vocabulary, duplicates, or EOL. This supports only a cross-table recorded respiratory-documentation transition.

Adverse includes an increment for any-treatment/opportunity but not C_RT_doc; disappearance after weighting; equality with T_nonresp_control; large changes across ±10/±30/±60 or shifted windows; exit/EOL concentration; schedule-null explanation; one-hospital/class dependence; or a boosting-only gain. If charting concordance remains but treatment concordance fails, retain the parent charting interpretation and conclude that treatment adds no corroboration.

Inconclusive/deferral includes fewer than five viable hospitals, zero/near-zero positivity in a prespecified stratum, unstable weights or raw vocabulary, too few strict starts, intervals spanning meaningful and negligible margins, unresolved offset semantics, or unavailable independent treatment provenance. An imprecise null is not refutation. Order/administration/device evidence and external validation are required before claims about delivered therapy, initiation, mechanism, benefit, causality, or clinical actionability.
