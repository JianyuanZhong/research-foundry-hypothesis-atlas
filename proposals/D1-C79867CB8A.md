# Provisional discordant AKI recovery hypothesis

Parent: [prior hypothesis]

## Proposed relationship

Among ICU patients with an acute kidney injury (AKI) episode and a preceding circulatory-stabilization landmark, persistent or delayed kidney recovery after respiratory recovery (discordant recovery) is associated with a higher risk of subsequent clinical deterioration than concordant recovery, after accounting for baseline severity and the current organ state at the landmark.

This is a provisional, falsifiable association hypothesis. It does not claim that discordance is causal or that the available data identify a mechanism.

## Concrete design to verify

- Population: MIMIC-IV ICU stays with an AKI episode and a prespecified landmark after circulatory stabilization.
- Temporal boundary: derive AKI, respiratory status, and covariates only from data available before the landmark; outcomes begin at the landmark.
- Primary exposure: kidney-versus-respiratory recovery discordance at the landmark, with recovery thresholds to be fixed from the inspected MIMIC-IV item definitions and clinical conventions.
- Outcomes: subsequent escalation of organ support and death, with a prespecified follow-up window.
- Baseline comparison: a simple landmark Cox/logistic or landmark survival model using current severity and organ state.
- Alternative: a time-aware model using pre-landmark trajectories of creatinine, urine output, respiratory support, and hemodynamics to test whether the incremental information is temporal discordance rather than current severity alone.
- Falsification: discordance should not predict deterioration if it is fully explained by current severity and pre-landmark trajectory; a null or reversed association would revise or reject the leading explanation.
