# Hypotension duration beyond the nadir and observed incident kidney injury after ICU admission

## Scientific question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

The unresolved claim is whether, among adult ICU stays surviving and remaining observable at a prespecified 6-hour landmark with no early creatinine-rise signal, the duration/intensity of low MAP during ICU hours 0–6 is associated with a subsequent observed creatinine-rise signal during hours 6–48 after conditioning on the same minimum MAP and measured baseline risk.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: conditional on minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, early coverage, measured patient/admission risk, and hospital, greater time-weighted MAP burden below 65 mmHg during hours 0–6 has a higher 6–48-hour cumulative incidence of an observed incident creatinine-rise signal. A null, reversal, disappearance after coverage/artifact sensitivity, or failure to transport across held-out hospitals falsifies the incremental burden claim. This is an observational prognostic association, not a claim that changing MAP or vasopressors prevents AKI.

The strongest evidence available before the experiment is only that this frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative MAP, laboratory, disposition, patient, severity, and infusion fields that can be aligned. A bounded source audit verified the required headers and exact disposition labels in the local files; it did not establish the hypothesis or estimate effects. eICU public data lack reliable pre-ICU renal reserve, urine-output adjudication, treatment intent, and clinical AKI adjudication. The primary endpoint is therefore explicitly an observed creatinine-rise signal, not KDIGO AKI.

Clinical importance is that minimum pressure may miss prolonged moderate hypotension. If burden adds calibrated, transportable information beyond the nadir, it could identify patients needing closer renal surveillance and motivate a future causal or interventional study. It cannot establish a vasopressor target.

## Baseline versus substantive mechanistic alternative

The transparent baseline and alternative answer the same scientific question and use the same landmark population, source rows, split, competing states, and outcomes.

Baseline: fit a regularized cause-specific pooled-logistic model in 30-minute intervals from 360 to 2880 minutes, with baseline creatinine, minimum observed MAP, area coverage, patient/admission covariates, hospital fixed effects or training-hospital effects, and no MAP-burden term. Add primary area-under-65 in a prespecified nested model. Estimate cause-specific hazards and 48-hour cumulative incidence by standardization at fixed minimum MAP. Report the adjusted burden contrast per 60 mmHg-min and 25th-to-75th percentile, absolute risk/CIF, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC only as secondary summaries, and 95% intervals from hospital bootstrap or robust clustered inference.

Mechanistic alternative: fit a cause-specific piecewise-exponential multi-state model with 30-minute exposure-history segments from hours 0–6 and post-landmark risk intervals. Each exposure segment contains covered minutes, area below 65, minutes below 65, minimum MAP, recovery slope to the next valid segment, longest low-MAP run, measurement indicators, and early vasopressor indicator; add the same baseline creatinine and static/admission covariates. States are landmark/no observed signal, observed creatinine-rise signal, death, and alive discharge/observation loss. Fit transitions only using information available before each event interval, include hospital random intercepts or stratified hospital effects, and obtain standardized 48-hour CIF contrasts at identical minimum MAP and baseline risk.

The mechanistic model can reveal whether the burden association is concentrated in persistent low MAP, failure to recover, or repeated episodes, while the scalar baseline collapses those trajectories. It is scientifically useful only if these predeclared trajectory contrasts are stable and uncertainty excludes clinically trivial effects; a small predictive gain alone is not success. Both models use patient-level `uniquepid` splitting and a final entire-hospital holdout selected before fitting. The holdout is transport within this snapshot, not independent external validation.

A GRU/temporal-convolution sequence model is recorded as not selected: eICU supports structured sequences, but the mechanistic model directly tests the duration/recovery uncertainty with clearer missingness semantics and CPU-feasible fitting. It may be revisited only if mechanistic residuals show reproducible unmodelled trajectory structure and a prespecified learned model can use the same inputs, target, split, calibration metrics, and hospital holdout. No conclusion may be based on an unrun learned model.

Compute planning is separate from discovery. The source-header/disposition audit was measured with read-only streaming; full MAP/lab construction and repeated hospital bootstrap are not yet measured. Plan within the solver envelope of 16 CPU, up to 8 GPUs, 262144 MiB memory, and 28800 seconds. ## Falsification, interpretation, and evidence limits

Supportive results require: a prespecified positive burden contrast beyond minimum MAP; clinically nontrivial absolute-risk difference with uncertainty; consistent direction in the conservative confirmed-rise analysis; stable estimates after coverage, interpolation, noninvasive-MAP, lab-revision, and vasopressor sensitivities; and direction/calibration that persist in held-out hospitals. Mechanistic evidence that recovery/persistence explains the increment strengthens the substantive interpretation but is not required to claim the prognostic association.

Adverse results are a null/reversed burden contrast, loss of calibration or direction on held-out hospitals, or disappearance after measurement-density/artifact sensitivity. This would favor the nadir-only or site-dependent-observation explanation, not prove that hypotension is physiologically harmless.

Inconclusive results include too few landmark stays, insufficient post-landmark creatinine observation, sparse MAP coverage, unstable lab-unit/name rules, competing-event dominance, or intervals spanning clinically important benefit and harm. Report these rather than forcing a label.

Computationally checkable claims are exact row counts and flow, units/value maps, interval construction, deduplication, missingness, joins, leakage-free splits, state counts, fitted contrasts, uncertainty, calibration, transport, and sensitivity outputs. True AKI diagnosis, pre-ICU renal reserve, urine-output criteria, reasons for absent labs/discharge, treatment intent, and whether a MAP target should change care require adjudication, richer data, or another study. No automatic verifier can establish those clinical claims.
