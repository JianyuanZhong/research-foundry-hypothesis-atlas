# Discharge-process-adjusted topology of early post-ICU failure

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly fit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 landmark study that determines whether the parent’s durable-versus-recent recorded-support contrast is specific to a post-transition ICU return after an observed non-ICU interval, or is explained by the observed ICU-discharge process and topology of the record.

Completion requires:

1. an auditable adult first-ICU-discharge cohort and all parent support-buffer fields;
2. a pre-landmark discharge-process covariate/overlap table and a measured landmark-selection model;
3. mutually exclusive 6-, 24- and 48-hour CIFs for transition-confirmed return, transition-unconfirmed/immediate return, death, and alive hospital discharge;
4. transparent process-adjusted estimates of the recent-minus-durable contrast and its topology interaction;
5. a prespecified respiratory-dominant versus vasoactive-dominant recorded-support subgroup analysis, retained as a proxy subgroup rather than a clinical mechanism claim;
6. a simple cause-specific baseline and a duration-aware hidden semi-Markov alternative using the same patient-level split and the same pre-outtime information;
7. locked held-out predictions, calibration, IPCW Brier scores, patient-bootstrap uncertainty, positivity/selection-weight diagnostics, topology validity, and falsification results; and
8. an interpretation file tying every conclusion to computed outputs and labelling supportive, adverse, or inconclusive evidence.

Required workspace outputs are:

- `cohort_audit.parquet`: one row per eligible first ICU stay with `subject_id,hadm_id,stay_id,intime,outtime`, inclusion flags, temporal checks and exclusion counts.
- `discharge_process.parquet`: ICU LOS, first/last ICU unit, landmark clock fields, admission/ED timing and pre-landmark transfer-process summaries; no post-outtime field.
- `landmark_selection.parquet`: all adult first ICU candidates, selection indicator, entry-only covariates, fitted selection probability, stabilized/truncated weight and overlap diagnostics.
- `hourly_features.parquet`: one row per stay-hour in `[outtime-12h,outtime)`, with physiology, explicit observation masks, support-channel evidence, and source/timing flags.
- `support_buffer_audit.parquet`: valid vasoactive and respiratory ledgers, procedure corroboration, recent/durable buffers, discordant and unobserved strata.
- `outcomes_topology.csv`: first event, event time, aggregate ICU-return flag, transition class, transfer interval, topology observability, ties and follow-up.
- `baseline_test_predictions.parquet` and `semimarkov_test_predictions.parquet`: locked test risks/CIFs for all four causes at 6/24/48 hours, plus aggregate-return sensitivity.
- `process_adjusted_cif.csv`, `calibration_brier.csv`, `bootstrap_intervals.csv`, `selection_diagnostics.csv`, `semimarkov_state_diagnostics.csv`, `falsification_results.csv`, and `interpretation.md`.

No model estimate or clinical conclusion is asserted by this proposal.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The parent established a source-concordant recent-versus-durable recorded-support exposure and separated ICU return after an observed non-ICU interval from return without one. Its remaining scientific ambiguity is selection: an apparent recent-support association may reflect which patients are discharged from ICU, from which unit, at what time, and through which recorded transfer pathway. Conversely, adjustment that removes the association may be over-adjustment for a clinically meaningful discharge state. This is a consequential distinction for post-ICU surveillance research: a signal specific to a transition-confirmed return could motivate clinician-adjudicated transition monitoring; a signal that disappears after process adjustment would argue that the database association is primarily a discharge/recording artifact.

The primary falsifiable contrast is defined for the recent and durable groups within the parent’s low-final-burden, adequately observed, source-concordant population:

- `Delta_TC(48) = CIF_transition-confirmed(48 | recent) - CIF_transition-confirmed(48 | durable)`
- `Delta_TU(48) = CIF_transition-unconfirmed(48 | recent) - CIF_transition-unconfirmed(48 | durable)`

The primary hypothesis is `Delta_TC(48) - Delta_TU(48) > 0`, after adjustment for prespecified clinical summaries and observed discharge-process covariates, with the same direction expected at 24 hours if event support permits. The estimand is an observational prognostic contrast among recorded ICU discharges. It is not a treatment effect and does not say that recorded support withdrawal causes deterioration.

A secondary, prespecified proxy-subgroup hypothesis is that the topology interaction differs by recorded support domain: recent respiratory-dominant withdrawal will have a larger transition-confirmed-return contrast than recent vasoactive-dominant withdrawal, whereas active/residual joint support will be more concentrated in immediate/unconfirmed return or death. This is a hypothesis about recorded trajectories, not proof of respiratory or circulatory mechanism. It is reported only if each subgroup has prespecified minimum event and overlap support; otherwise it is inconclusive.

## Evidence already supported versus claims to test

The inspected catalog and archive establish that MIMIC-IV 3.1 contains dated ICU stays, admissions, hospital transfers, chart observations, input intervals, procedure intervals, item dictionary labels, laboratory observations and discharge notes. The item dictionary confirms the parent’s listed physiologic, support and procedure IDs. A bounded read-only archive audit measured 94,458 ICU stays, 70,136 adult first-ICU landmarks meeting the parent’s broad temporal criteria, 3,872 broad 48-hour same-admission ICU returns, and 1,681 of those returns with a strict observed non-ICU transfer interval. These counts were not restricted to the parent’s low-final-burden or support-coverage strata and are feasibility diagnostics, not study results.

The unresolved claim is that the recent-versus-durable contrast contains information about a post-transition failure process beyond measured discharge-process selection and is not confined to transition-unconfirmed re-entry. The experiment can test this claim as a reproducible association with uncertainty. It cannot establish readiness, intent, planned/unplanned status, preventability, or clinical utility.

## Population, landmark and outcomes

Use the parent population exactly:

- adult `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`, retaining MIMIC’s `anchor_age=91` representation;
- earliest `icu/icustays.intime` per `subject_id,hadm_id` (also report any same-subject/same-admission ordering anomalies);
- valid joins to `hosp/admissions` and `hosp/patients`;
- non-null `intime < outtime < admissions.dischtime`;
- no `admissions.deathtime <= outtime`; and
- `admissions.dischtime` available.

The landmark is `icu/icustays.outtime`. Primary predictors use only records in `[outtime-12 hours,outtime)). Divide this into 12 one-hour bins and two six-hour halves. Use `charttime` for chart observations and `starttime,endtime` for intervals. `storetime` is an audit/falsification field only and never the primary clinical time. No post-outtime row, transfer, note or discharge field is a predictor.

Follow until `min(outtime+48 hours, admissions.dischtime)`. The mutually exclusive first events are:

1. transition-confirmed ICU return: a later `icustays.intime > outtime` in the same `subject_id,hadm_id` within 48 hours, preceded by at least one valid `hosp/transfers` interval with `intime >= outtime`, `outtime <= later icustays.intime`, `intime < outtime), and `careunit` outside the frozen ICU-careunit set;
2. transition-unconfirmed/immediate ICU return: the same later ICU return with no observed qualifying non-ICU interval;
3. in-hospital death: `admissions.deathtime > outtime` before other events and within 48 hours; and
4. alive hospital discharge: `admissions.dischtime > outtime` before other events and within 48 hours.

The ICU-careunit set is the distinct non-null values of `icustays.first_careunit` and `last_careunit` in the frozen snapshot; no unverified string heuristic is allowed. Later ICU stays, not transfer rows, define ICU return. Keep invalid/missing transfer intervals as topology-observability diagnostics. Use death, transition-confirmed return, transition-unconfirmed return, then alive discharge as the primary tie precedence, with a timestamp-tolerance sensitivity. Never call a return planned or unplanned. Collapse the two return classes for the parent aggregate-return sensitivity.

Primary estimands are standardized group-specific CIFs and absolute recent-minus-durable differences at 6, 24 and 48 hours for each cause, plus the interaction contrast `Delta_TC - Delta_TU`. Cause-specific hazard ratios are secondary. A discharge-as-censoring analysis is a sensitivity demonstrating the estimand change, never a replacement for alive discharge as a competing event.

## Observed discharge-process adjustment and selection sensitivity

Define the observed process vector `P` before or at `outtime` from:

- `icustays.los`, `first_careunit`, `last_careunit`, ICU `intime/outtime` clock hour/day and elapsed `admittime-to-outtime`;
- `admissions.admission_type`, `admission_location`, `insurance`, `language`, `marital_status`, `race`, and ED timing `edregtime,edouttime` where ordered;
- counts and last observed careunit/event type from `hosp/transfers` with transfer `outtime <= index outtime), never rows after the landmark; and
- demographics `gender,anchor_age,anchor_year_group`.

`discharge_location` is retained for descriptive post-outcome audit only and is never a predictor. The process vector is not a claim that administrative variables capture clinical readiness; it is an explicit adjustment for measured discharge/recording context.

The primary process-adjusted analysis fits the baseline and alternative conditional on the same `P` and clinical feature set `X), then standardizes predictions to the common-overlap distribution of `P). Report estimates before and after adding `P`, overlap counts, and the fraction of the recent/durable contrast lost after adjustment.

As a prespecified sensitivity, construct all adult first ICU candidates with valid entry and admission joins, including those that do not reach an eligible alive ICU-discharge landmark. Define selection `S=1` by the parent’s landmark eligibility conditions. Fit a train-only logistic selection model using entry-available variables only: age, sex, admission type/location, first ICU careunit, admission-to-ICU-entry time and ICU-entry clock fields. Apply stabilized inverse-`P(S=1)` weights, with training-derived 1st/99th percentile truncation and a weight-sensitivity grid. Use these weights only as an observational selection sensitivity among the eligible landmarks. Do not describe them as causal correction or as recovering outcomes for people who died in ICU. If positivity fails, the selection sensitivity is inconclusive and the conditional landmark analysis remains primary.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

Use patient-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits by `subject_id`, fixed seeds 17, 29 and 43. Fit all encoders, imputation, standardization, selection weights, tuning and thresholds on training data only. No subject can occur in more than one split.

The identical information set is `X + P`: the locked exposure tier/buffer and domain indicators; final and preceding MAP/respiratory burdens; all one-hour values/masks and source/channel flags; HR/RR/SpO2 summaries; optional audited pre-index labs; demographics; ICU LOS and unit fields; admission covariates; and pre-outtime transfer-process summaries. Outcome topology, `discharge_location`, post-outtime transfers/notes and any event time are excluded.

Transparent baseline: fit four cause-specific Cox models, one for each event, with prespecified main effects for recent/durable exposure, support-domain subgroup, the exposure-by-topology estimand represented through separate event causes, compact trajectory summaries, and `P`. Use a nested snapshot-only baseline that removes buffer/order and retains final values, masks, support presence and case mix. Standardize cause-specific predictions into Aalen–Johansen CIFs; report adjusted CIF contrasts, calibration, IPCW Brier scores, event counts, effective sample size and bootstrap intervals. This baseline directly answers whether topology and process adjustment change the substantive contrast and remains auditable.

Substantive alternative: a constrained hidden semi-Markov competing-risk model on the same 12 hourly rows and static `P`. Use prespecified states for durable recorded-off/low burden, recent withdrawal/low burden, active recorded support, residual/discordant support, and observation-limited status. Learn emissions, sparse transitions, dwell-time distributions and four discrete event hazards over eight six-hour bins from the training patients; choose only state restrictions and regularization on validation; lock test posterior state probabilities and CIFs. The model’s scientific purpose is to reveal whether ordered support/physiology and dwell distinguish a transition-confirmed pathway from an immediate/uncertain pathway after the same process context. It is not retained for a small predictive gain. Retain it only if state occupancy/dwell and topology-specific hazard diagnostics are identifiable and reproducible and if held-out CIF calibration or the prespecified topology interaction is materially more informative under a locked margin. If states are non-identifiable, report the transparent baseline and the failure as a scientific result.

The alternative is deferred if the exact same-input comparison cannot be fit within the solver envelope or if sparse topology causes non-identifiability; a masked GRU/TCN is not substituted merely to improve prediction. Revisit it only if the semi-Markov diagnostics demonstrate stable ordered information that a constrained state model cannot represent.

## Uncertainty, falsification and gates

Use patient-level bootstrap within the locked test evaluation for 95% intervals, with stratified resampling diagnostics for rare topology/exposure cells. Report overlap, effective sample size, selection-weight distribution, event counts, transfer-observability rates and topology disagreements. Primary uncertainty is the interaction contrast and its standardized CIFs, not a model score.

Predeclare:

- order-shuffle hourly features within stay while preserving values and masks;
- mask-only, mask-removed, physiology-only and support-removed variants;
- `storetime` substituted for `charttime` and last-value coding substituted for interval overlap;
- procedure corroboration excluded, then used only as corroboration;
- arterial/NIBP and FiO2/O2-flow/PEEP channels separated;
- 6-hour buffer and 6/12-hour window perturbations;
- joint, vasoactive-only and respiratory-only exposure strata;
- topology collapsed to aggregate return;
- strict versus timestamp-tolerance non-ICU interval rules;
- transfer missingness/careunit-map diagnostics and a negative-control pseudo-landmark before `outtime`;
- before/after `P` adjustment and selection-weight sensitivity;
- first/last ICU unit and admission-type strata; and
- alive discharge as competing event versus discharge-censored sensitivity.

Supportive evidence requires adequate cell sizes/positivity and transfer observability; a stable positive `Delta_TC-Delta_TU` after `P` adjustment and, if feasible, selection weighting; no concentration solely in transition-unconfirmed return; calibrated held-out CIFs; and reproducible support-domain/state diagnostics. This supports a retrospective prognostic marker and a testable target for external and clinician-adjudicated validation, not a discharge rule.

Adverse evidence is a null/reversed interaction, disappearance after process adjustment, concentration only in unconfirmed return or discharge pathway, strong dependence on coding/topology/timing rules, poor calibration, extreme selection weights, or non-identifiable states. It weakens or falsifies the claim that recorded durability marks a distinct post-transition failure process.

Inconclusive evidence is rare events, non-positivity, missing/contradictory transfer intervals, unstable ties, topology disagreement, wide intervals, unavailable discharge/death times, or failure of the selection model. No clinical rule is issued in that case.
