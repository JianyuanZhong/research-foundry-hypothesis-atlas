# Episode 19 selection-boundary evolution — landmark physiology versus operational transition

Status: proposed substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. No cohort count, fitted coefficient, performance, confidence interval, or clinical result has been observed or is asserted.

## Unresolved claim and smallest advance

The parent can test whether a respiratoryCare interval-start is concordant with treatment timing and respiratoryCharting timing, but its primary risk set is conditioned on `patient.unitdischargeoffset > 360`. That conditioning is clinically consequential: a later recorded respiratory transition may reflect prolonged ICU stay, early discharge/death selection, end-of-life workflow, denser observation, or the chance to ascertain an event rather than a transition linked to patient state.

The strongest supported claim remains narrower: the eICU tables contain reproducible ICU-relative offsets and separately stored vital, respiratory-care, charting, treatment, discharge, and EOL records suitable for a bounded multi-center recorded-event analysis. The inspected works support structured temporal EHR analysis and the need to audit transport and measurement [K1,K2,K3]. They do not establish that `treatmentoffset`, `ventstartoffset`, or `respchartoffset` means order, administration, delivery, physiologic onset, or bedside action.

Falsifiable child hypothesis: among adult first-ICU stays that remain observable at minute 360 and lack a valid active respiratoryCare interval there, a treatment/respiratory clinical-offset concordance increment is not explained away by (i) pre-landmark observed vital trajectory, (ii) baseline observation density and respiratory-source availability, (iii) EOL signals, (iv) proximity to discharge and competing expired discharge, or (v) the selection mechanism that produced the 360-minute risk set. The claim is only a reproducible recorded-event association. It is not a claim that deterioration caused treatment, that treatment was delivered, or that the vitals are a gold-standard physiologic trajectory.

The leading operational rival predicts that the association is primarily a function of remaining in the ICU and being observed: it should be strong in the selected S6 group, track observation counts/gaps and discharge proximity, persist in generic or non-respiratory controls, and attenuate after a prespecified landmark-selection audit or vital/availability adjustment. A patient-state rival predicts that the event is more frequent after a worsening or persistently abnormal trajectory in the separate vital tables, remains within hospitals after held-out evaluation, and is not fully reproduced by density and exit controls. An ascertainment rival predicts that baseline respiratoryCharting/respiratoryCare row availability and post-landmark documentation opportunity dominate, with weak or unstable associations in low-availability strata. These comparisons can separate descriptive regularities, but cannot identify a biologic mechanism or eliminate unmeasured severity.

## Population, landmark, risk sets, and estimands

Time zero is ICU admission; every offset is ICU-relative minutes.

1. **All-eligible selection cohort (audit only).** Start with patient rows having finite `patientunitstayid`, `hospitalid`, `unitdischargeoffset`, and `unitdischargestatus`; `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitstaytype=admit`, and source `age >= 18` (retain source age values such as >89). Within `uniquepid`, keep the smallest finite `unitadmitoffset`, breaking ties by `patientunitstayid`. Do not require survival to 360. Classify mutually exclusive landmark status from `unitdischargeoffset`: early alive discharge (`<=360` with a non-expired status), early expired discharge (`<=360` with an expired status), landmark-eligible (`>360`), and missing/contradictory discharge status. This cohort is used only to describe selection and fit a landmark-inclusion model; no endpoint after 360 is assigned to early exits.

2. **Primary S6 risk set.** Retain exactly the parent S6 stays with `unitdischargeoffset > 360), finite discharge status, and no valid documented respiratoryCare interval covering minute 360. Keep the parent's four starting strata rather than pooling them: active interval; respiratory record but no auditable active invasive interval; no respiratoryCare record in [0,360]; and unknown/contradictory respiratory source state. The primary estimand is conditional on this operational risk set and is not a population-average causal effect.

3. **Post-landmark follow-up.** Follow in strict `(360,720]`, ending at `min(720, unitdischargeoffset)`. A first valid `R_start_doc` is the first `respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset` in that interval with finite start, a finite end only if end > start or explicitly open, no valid interval covering the immediately preceding minute, and duplicate starts collapsed with row IDs retained. Malformed, contradictory, same-offset, and ambiguous interval rows are audited and excluded. Treatment concordance remains `C_clin(w)): outcome-blind frozen `T_resp_any` within `[t-w,t+w]` of the event clinical offset at prespecified w=10, 30, and 60 minutes. The parent `R_stop_doc`, chart clinical/entry anchors, exact-tie/backfill strata, and schedule/source-coupling nulls remain unchanged.

4. **Competing exits and censoring.** If discharge occurs before an event, censor at `unitdischargeoffset` for the primary recorded-event analysis; an expired discharge is a competing exit at that recorded offset and is never converted to a death timestamp. Alive discharge is a separate competing exit. No row after discharge is eligible. Report cause-specific discrete-time hazards and cumulative incidence for R_start_doc versus alive and expired discharge, with risk-set denominators at each interval. A 720-minute administrative end is right censoring. The primary analysis is not rescued by imputation of unrecorded death times.

## New selection and measured-trajectory design

The added module uses only separately sourced vital observations available by the minute-360 landmark. It is deliberately descriptive: it measures observed-vital trajectory, not latent physiology.

- From `vitalPeriodic`, use `vitalperiodicid`, `patientunitstayid`, `observationoffset`, `temperature`, `sao2`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, `cvp`, `etco2`, `systemicsystolic`, `systemicdiastolic`, `systemicmean`, `pasystolic`, `padiastolic`, `pamean`, `st1`, `st2`, `st3`, and `icp`. From `vitalAperiodic`, use `patientunitstayid`, `observationoffset`, `noninvasivesystolic`, `noninvasivediastolic`, and `noninvasivemean`. Keep each table's row ID and source; do not treat the two tables as duplicate measurements.
- Include observations with finite `observationoffset` in [0,360] only. For each variable, create prespecified 60-minute bins [0,60], (60,120], ..., (300,360], using the last finite value, within-bin median, count, time from last observation to 360, and a robust within-stay slope when at least three distinct offsets exist. Also retain trajectory missingness and maximum gap. No value after 360 enters a predictor, selection model, or baseline.
- Define observation-density/ascertainment covariates before modeling: counts and maximum gaps in vitalPeriodic, vitalAperiodic, respiratoryCare, respiratoryCharting, and treatment over [0,360]; whether each source has any finite time field; and exact duplicate/contradiction flags. These are adjustment and stratification variables, not evidence that a row is clinically meaningful.
- Define EOL audit flags from carePlanEOL `cpleolsaveoffset` and `cpleoldiscussionoffset` in [0,360], retaining their separate offsets and row IDs. Do not use Apache outcomes or future chart/treatment rows as predictors. `apachePatientResult` remains an outcome-blind audit only.
- For the landmark-selection audit, fit/describe the probability of reaching 360 versus early alive/expired discharge using time-zero patient/hospital fields and summaries observed only through `min(360, unitdischargeoffset)`; early-exit rows therefore never contribute post-discharge values. Mark truncated observation windows explicitly, report overlap and effective sample size by hospital, and do not silently inverse-weight if positivity is inadequate.
- For the S6 endpoint analysis, report unweighted results as primary and a prespecified stabilized landmark-inclusion weighting sensitivity based only on the selection model, truncated at the 1st/99th training-fold percentiles. If any hospital or stratum has no overlap, suppress the weighted estimate and label the analysis unresolved. This weighting addresses measured selection only; it does not turn the conditional estimand into a causal effect.

## Analysis, baseline, learned alternative, and uncertainty

The scientific deliverable is newly estimated, not a result presumed from the parent: (a) a landmark-selection table and overlap/ESS report, (b) pre-landmark vital-trajectory features and their held-out incremental association with R_start_doc and competing exits, (c) treatment concordance increments after measured severity/availability/selection safeguards, and (d) a conclusion table mapping each claim to an output and uncertainty interval.

Use the same outcome, risk set, windows, frozen outcome-blind treatment classes, hospital-held-out folds, and 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap or equivalent cluster uncertainty for both methods.

- **Transparent state/process baseline:** fold-isolated cause-specific discrete-time elastic-net hazards. Inputs are the parent [0,360] state/process and availability modules plus the explicit vital trajectory summaries, EOL flags, discharge proximity/censoring indicators, timing/tie/backfill variables, and treatment/control features. Fit R_start_doc, alive discharge, and expired discharge as separate causes at discrete intervals; choose penalty by training-fold likelihood. The selection model is fitted only in the all-eligible audit cohort and its weights are applied only as a sensitivity.
- **Learned alternative:** capacity-matched histogram gradient boosting with the identical row-level feature list, endpoint, competing causes, hospital folds, weighting rule, and bootstrap procedure. Its substantive role is to reveal nonlinear threshold/interactions—e.g., whether treatment concordance appears only at high observation density or within a narrow vital-slope/exit-proximity region—that the linear baseline can miss. A score improvement alone is not evidence for the hypothesis. No neural sequence model is needed: the design has fixed, coarse 60-minute summaries and cannot recover treatment-side timestamps or physiologic gold-standard labels. Revisit a sequence/latent-state model only if raw high-frequency measurements, adequate support, and an adjudicated target are available.

Report held-out-hospital AUROC/PR-AUC only as secondary predictive diagnostics; primary outputs are cause-specific hazard ratios or marginal risk contrasts for treatment concordance, the vital-trajectory block, density/EOL/exit blocks, and weighting sensitivity, with 95% hospital-cluster intervals, per-hospital and starting-stratum tables, calibration/Brier, and positivity/ESS. Do not interpret a predictive score as a clinical utility result.

Compute plan: gzip streaming, interval joins, 60-minute aggregation, elastic-net/boosting, and cluster bootstrap are provisionally CPU-feasible within the parent future-solver envelope (up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM, 28,800 seconds); these estimates are planning, not measured in this episode. Discovery inputs permit 9,000 science seconds and two GPU slots, but no GPU is necessary or scientifically rewarded. The alternative is selected for information about nonlinear selection/trajectory interactions, not complexity.

## Prespecified falsification, interpretation, and limits

Support for the narrow hypothesis requires: at least five hospitals with positive primary opportunity and overlap; stable frozen treatment vocabulary and endpoint audit; vital trajectory block showing coherent held-out association beyond baseline state/density; concordance increment remaining directionally and materially present in unweighted and valid weighted analyses; no dominance by one hospital, starting stratum, EOL flag, discharge-proximity bin, exact tie/backfill bin, or high-density bin; no similar increment in T_any_record or T_nonresp_control; and survival across w=10/30/60, entry-aligned exclusion, schedule/source-coupling nulls, and hospital-held-out folds. This supports only a reproducible cross-table recorded association compatible with, but not proof of, a patient-state-linked operational transition.

Adverse evidence: early-exit selection is large or lacks overlap; the increment attenuates to the generic/density/EOL/exit controls; it is concentrated in high observation density or discharge-adjacent rows; the vital block does not transport across hospitals; treatment aligns more strongly with chart-entry than clinical time; or schedule/null and non-respiratory controls reproduce it. Then conclude that the observed concordance is compatible with operational ascertainment/selection and do not claim a physiologic transition.

Inconclusive evidence: fewer than five viable hospitals, insufficient positive opportunity or effective sample size, unstable source semantics, missing/contradictory discharge times, unmeasured selection after weighting, vital measurements too sparse for trajectory estimation, or wide intervals that cannot distinguish the prespecified adverse/supportive regions. A low-precision null is not refutation.

Clinical adjudication remains essential for claims of treatment order, administration, delivery, indication, ventilator initiation, bedside action, physiologic deterioration, or treatment benefit. The tables have no treatment order/administration/device provenance, indication or assignment, reliable death timestamp, interface ID, raw waveform, or clinical adjudication. Apache outcomes are not a gold standard for the respiratory endpoint. No result from this experiment can establish causality or mechanism.

## Alternatives retained and revisit rule

The selected pair is elastic-net versus histogram boosting because both can share the exact endpoint, censoring, folds, uncertainty, and feature budget while answering whether nonlinear measured-state/selection interactions explain the association. A mechanistic latent-state model is deferred: without an adjudicated physiologic target and with treatment-side timing absent, it would add untestable structure rather than resolve provenance. A neural raw-sequence model is likewise deferred for the same scientific reason, not because neural methods or GPUs are disallowed. Revisit either only if a bounded diagnostic shows sufficient longitudinal support and a clinically adjudicated target or new treatment provenance becomes available. Causal treatment-effect modeling remains deferred until assignment, indication, administration/device exposure, and confounders are observed.
