# HCC early trajectory versus care-process artifact (episode 7)

## Unresolved question and clinical importance
Among adults admitted with a documented HCC diagnosis, does an ordered 7-day laboratory trajectory add information about a subsequent inpatient procedure-based escalation proxy beyond an admission snapshot, after representing treatment indication and documentation intensity? The key rival is that apparent trajectory value reflects routine HCC treatment, care intensity, or documentation rather than evolving patient state. Resolving this matters because it determines whether temporal monitoring could inform expert review, while avoiding an unsupported claim that a procedure equals death, ICU admission, or clinical harm.

## Hypothesis and strongest supported claim
A time-aware representation of within-assay liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker measurements from admission through day 7 will improve held-out prediction of a prespecified non-routine post-window procedure over a snapshot model, with uncertainty excluding a trivial margin; the incremental value should be smaller for routine HCC treatment procedures and absent in a timing-shifted negative control. This is a hypothesis, not an observed result. The strongest claim currently supported is only that the HCC snapshot contains joinable encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, and procedure records with substantial temporal procedure support.

## Audit and endpoint
The bounded source audit found 105,044 encounter rows, 101,457 valid admission times, 104,554 exact HCC patient/admission keys, 338,040 procedure rows, 7,781 labels, 321,207 timed HCC procedure rows, 15,774 untimed HCC procedure rows, and 20,052 HCC procedure rows after day 7 and before discharge (6,779 keys). These are support counts, not outcome validity. The primary endpoint is the first post-day-7, pre-discharge procedure whose label is not in a frozen routine-HCC dictionary (TACE/arterial embolization/arterial chemoembolization, hepatic resection, ablation, transplantation, and documented compound variants). The broader procedure proxy is secondary. Untimed procedures are excluded and reported as ascertainment limitation. The dictionary and event counts must be frozen before fitting; clinical experts must adjudicate whether any endpoint represents deterioration.

## Experiment
Cohort: exact HCC diagnosis, nonempty admission time, and the four liver-tumor departments `肝肿瘤内科, 肝胆肿瘤与肝移植外科, 肝胆肿瘤内科, 肝肿瘤外科`; unrestricted exact-diagnosis cohort is sensitivity. Feature window: admission through admission+7 days and no later than discharge. Primary features are within-assay values, time since admission, specimen type, missingness, and time-zero treatment/process indicators; no unsupported cross-assay pooling because labs have no unit column.
Baseline: penalized logistic regression using age, sex, department, first available assay values, time-zero routine-treatment and medication indicators, and documentation counts. Alternative: patient-level ordered sequence/state-space model with the same covariates and temporal assay information. Split by `患者主索引`, use nested time-aware validation and repeated bootstrap uncertainty. Evaluate held-out discrimination, calibration, and the incremental difference; use post-outcome measurements and within-patient order permutation as negative controls. The alternative can reveal persistence, ordering, and cross-marker interactions lost by a snapshot.
Supportive: non-routine endpoint shows a nontrivial held-out trajectory advantage with uncertainty excluding the margin, stable across department/documentation/missingness/assay sensitivities, and absent in the timing-shifted control. Adverse: no improvement, degradation, advantage confined to routine treatment, disappearance after process adjustment, or positive negative control. Inconclusive: sparse non-routine events, unstable dictionary, missing timing, imprecise intervals, or inability to adjudicate clinical meaning.

## Evidence context
[K1] Yang et al. (2026) reported that routine admission bilirubin, direct bilirubin, AST, and INR predicted day-7 total bilirubin in a small HBV cirrhosis cohort, supporting short-term laboratory response and motivating a snapshot comparator; it does not establish HCC prognosis or causality.
[K2] Bui et al. (2026) reported that stage, microvascular invasion, resection margin, and Child-Pugh status predicted postoperative disease-free survival in a retrospective HCC resection cohort, bounding the claim that static factors are already important; it does not test early inpatient trajectories or escalation.
[K3] Millarch et al. (2026) reported that a temporal EHR model using ordered vitals, labs, and medications predicted mortality across a nonlinear trauma trajectory and outperformed static severity scores, motivating the sequence alternative; it is not HCC evidence and does not transfer automatically.
