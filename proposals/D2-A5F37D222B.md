# Protective versus pathogenic macrophage–fibroblast target selectivity

Seed: D2-T2. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Does an intervention-associated transcript pattern reduce a pathogenic fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state, such that a target prioritized from an averaged signal would be misleading? This matters because macrophage and fibroblast programs can have opposing roles in fibrotic or inflammatory disease: an intervention-associated change in one compartment may reflect a protective response, a pathogenic program, or simply differential cell composition and stimulation. Resolving the question would change whether a target is pursued as context-specific, requires a cell-type-resolved intervention, or is deprioritized.

## Strongest currently supported claim

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that the four configured sources contain macrophage/fibroblast-related conditions and intervention-associated transcript changes. The datasets are available as prepared counts and design metadata. This is descriptive evidence, not evidence that a specific transcriptomic signature is protective or pathogenic, or that an intervention acts directly on a target.

## Unresolved claim

The unresolved claim is whether a protective macrophage-linked state and a pathogenic fibroblast program can be separated by an intervention-associated transcript response without overinterpreting expression as direct target action. Specifically, we test whether the intervention-associated pattern is selectively associated with reduced pathogenic fibroblast expression and preserved protective macrophage-linked expression, after accounting for donor, condition, and cell-composition artifacts.

## Operational definitions of the two states

The datasets do not contain explicit cell-type labels for every sample. We therefore define the states operationally from the available conditions and transcript signatures:

- **Protective macrophage-linked state**: samples in macrophage-enriched or macrophage-conditioned conditions (e.g., Mpos, Mneg, macrophage-only or macrophage-coculture conditions) with expression of macrophage-associated genes that are not shared with the pathogenic fibroblast signature. The protective score is the mean log2 fold-change of a predefined macrophage-associated gene set (e.g., genes enriched in macrophage efferocytosis and resolution) relative to a macrophage-negative or unstimulated baseline.
- **Pathogenic fibroblast program**: samples in fibroblast-enriched or fibroblast-conditioned conditions (e.g., FLS-only, FLS-coculture, or fibroblast-only conditions) with expression of fibroblast-associated genes that are not shared with the protective macrophage signature. The pathogenic score is the mean log2 fold-change of a predefined fibroblast-associated gene set (e.g., genes enriched in fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix remodeling) relative to a fibroblast-negative or unstimulated baseline.

These definitions are reasonable proxies because the conditions in the datasets are explicitly labeled by cell type (macrophage, fibroblast, coculture) and by stimulation (LPS, inhibitor, pre-LPS, pre-inhibitor). The gene sets are chosen from the available transcript data and are not assumed to be exhaustive. The protective and pathogenic scores are computed within each donor to control for donor-specific baseline differences.

## Competing explanations and how they are tested

The central competing explanation is that the observed intervention-associated pattern is driven by differential cell composition or stimulation rather than by a specific protective-versus-pathogenic target-selective effect. We test this explicitly:

1. **Donor-aware condition contrasts**: We compare the intervention-associated pattern within each donor, using donor-specific baselines. This controls for donor-specific baseline differences and reduces the chance that the pattern is driven by donor composition.
2. **Composition-adjusted analysis**: We use a negative-control gene set that should not differ if the signal is not driven by composition. The negative-control set is chosen from genes that are not enriched in either the protective macrophage or pathogenic fibroblast signatures. If the intervention-associated pattern is driven by composition, the negative-control set should show a similar magnitude of change. If the pattern is specific to the protective-versus-pathogenic contrast, the negative-control set should show little or no change.
3. **Stimulation control**: We compare the intervention-associated pattern in stimulated (LPS, inhibitor) versus unstimulated (pre-LPS, pre-inhibitor) conditions. If the pattern is driven by stimulation, it should be present in both stimulated and unstimulated conditions. If the pattern is specific to the intervention, it should be present only in the intervention-associated conditions.

## Variables and outcomes

- Exposure: intervention-associated transcript pattern.
- Outcome: separation of protective macrophage-linked and pathogenic fibroblast states.
- Covariates: donor_id, cell_type, condition, timepoint.
- Competing explanations: differential cell composition, stimulation, measurement artifact.

## Analysis

A donor-aware linear mixed model baseline is compared with a composition-adjusted alternative. The baseline uses donor random effects and condition fixed effects. The alternative uses donor-aware regularization and a held-out split. The outcome/uncertainty evaluation is based on the separation of protective and pathogenic states.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if the intervention-associated pattern does not separate protective and pathogenic states, or if the separation is driven by differential cell composition or stimulation. Specifically:

- If the negative-control gene set shows a similar magnitude of change as the protective-versus-pathogenic contrast, the hypothesis is falsified.
- If the intervention-associated pattern is present in both stimulated and unstimulated conditions, the hypothesis is falsified.
- If the pattern does not separate protective and pathogenic states after donor-aware adjustment, the hypothesis is falsified.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive outcomes

- **Supportive**: the intervention-associated pattern separates protective and pathogenic states, the negative-control gene set shows little or no change, and the pattern is present only in the intervention-associated conditions.
- **Adverse**: the pattern does not separate the states, or the separation is driven by artifact (e.g., the negative-control gene set shows a similar magnitude of change, or the pattern is present in both stimulated and unstimulated conditions).
- **Inconclusive**: the pattern is ambiguous or the data are insufficient (e.g., the negative-control gene set shows a small but non-zero change, or the pattern is present in some but not all intervention-associated conditions).

## Method comparison

The donor-aware linear mixed model baseline is simple and interpretable. The composition-adjusted alternative could reveal nonlinear or high-dimensional patterns that the baseline loses. The baseline is selected for its interpretability and the alternative is deferred for future work.
