## Clinical question, strongest supported claim, and unresolved hypothesis

After vasopressor-treated shock has resolved, should a markedly fluid-positive adult who remains invasively ventilated at ICU hour 48, with adequate recorded MAP, urine output, and potassium, start or restart an ICU-input-system recorded furosemide or bumetanide delivery during the next 12 hours while the linked recorded ventilation interval remains open, or avoid such delivery until that interval closes or 12 hours elapse?

This is consequential because a clinician may use a loop to remove accumulated fluid before airway liberation, but a late or poorly selected start may worsen kidney injury or merely mark impending extubation. Fluid overload itself is associated with poor outcomes; that does not prove active removal improves them.

The inspected full XML of Kuriyama et al. 2026 (PMCID `PMC13476641`, DOI `10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100120`, frozen source `[source checksum]`) reports 26 randomized trials and 1,652 participants. Most comparisons were low or very-low certainty and clinically heterogeneous, largely heart-failure and post-cardiovascular-surgery populations; it does not establish the effect of starting a loop after resolved shock. The inspected abstract/full XML of Bircher et al. 2026 (PMCID `PMC13154636`, DOI `10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100075`, source `[source checksum]`) states that optimal timing, volume, and duration of fluid removal remain limited by evidence and individualized judgment. Hansford et al. 2026 (PMCID `PMC12927355`, DOI `10.1136/bmjmed-2024-001280`, source `[source checksum]`) supports aligning eligibility, strategy assignment, and follow-up; target-trial structure does not remove confounding or measurement error. These are frozen full XML sources. The research-ambition papers are methodological demonstrations, not loop-benefit evidence; no unavailable cancer main text or STAR Methods is claimed as read.

The strongest claim already supported is therefore that fluid overload matters, diuretic-strategy evidence is uncertain, and MIMIC contains exact but imperfectly aligned recorded ventilation, extubation-task, loop-delivery, physiology, and same-admission outcome streams. The unresolved falsifiable claim is:

> In the prespecified measured-overlap discovery population, assignment-compatible follow-up under A12 increases the lower bound of five-day RHA-E48-D5 by more than 0.05 versus B12, while one-sided upper confidence limits for record-defined 48-hour creatinine rise, observed RRT7, and recorded terminal/hospice/acute-transfer harm remain below +0.03, +0.05, and +0.03. A12 can be satisfied only by a qualifying loop onset strictly before the linked recorded airway-cessation boundary (C).

This is an observational dynamic-policy association, not a pharmacologic receipt effect, proof of durable extubation or renal safety, or a treatment recommendation.
