# Does arrival measurement opportunity, rather than transfer status itself, explain early ICU mortality risk?

## Scientific deliverable and unresolved claim

This is a substantive eICU adaptation of expert seed eICU-07 and an evolution of [prior hypothesis]. It is not a reproduction of any demonstration. The future solver must newly construct one auditable adult first-ICU-stay cohort and fit a prespecified decomposition of the observed risk contrast between patients labelled `Other Hospital` and `Direct Admit`. Completion means: (1) cohort-flow and source/value/unit audit; (2) a stay-level 0–60-minute feature and measurement-opportunity ledger; (3) deterministic APACHE duplicate and timing audit; (4) fixed hospital-held-out split manifest; (5) fitted nested competing-risk models and the matched early-state model; (6) standardized 48-hour risks/contrasts with hospital-clustered uncertainty; (7) held-out calibration/proper scores and decision-curve output only when an actionable threshold is justified; (8) falsification/sensitivity tables; and (9) a conclusion-to-artifact map. Discovery has fitted no model and establishes no clinical result.

The strongest claim supported before fitting is only data availability and feasibility evidence inherited from the audited parent: the frozen eICU snapshot contains admission-source labels, ICU-relative disposition, hospital identifiers, APACHE fields, time-stamped vital/lab rows, and the stated join keys. The parent audit found 12,688 adult first ICU admissions (9,771 Direct Admit; 2,917 Other Hospital), 179 hospitals, 112 with both arms, 425 recorded unit deaths by 48 ICU-relative hours (315 and 110), and 6,473 alive exits. These are feasibility counts, not an association or treatment result. The audit also found APACHE physiologic coverage for 12,616 stays, but APACHE has no variable-level time field.

The unresolved claim is whether the higher observed 48-hour recorded ICU-unit death incidence in the Other Hospital group, if present, is materially attenuated by (a) the opportunity to record early physiology and then (b) the values/trajectories recorded, with the residual contrast localizing to early worsening or failure to recover. This is a prognostic and measurement-process question. It is not a causal effect of transfer, transport delay, referral quality, treatment, or an adjudicated organ injury.

## Clinical importance and falsifiability

Interhospital patients are often triaged with incomplete pre-arrival information. A robust arrival-risk comparison could inform whether transfer-source labels should trigger structured early reassessment, or whether apparent risk differences mostly reflect ascertainment and case mix. The clinically useful advance is a decomposition that says what information must be collected at arrival, not a leaderboard gain.

Primary hypothesis: among adult first ICU admissions with exactly one of the two observed patient-level source labels, the standardized Other Hospital–Direct Admit difference in cumulative incidence of recorded unit death by 2,880 minutes is positive in a source-only model, is attenuated by adding only early measurement opportunity, and is attenuated further by adding valid 0–60-minute physiologic values. A mechanistic alternative predicts that any residual contrast will be concentrated in prespecified early worsening/persistence states rather than a source-label main effect.

Supportive evidence requires a source-only contrast in the prespecified direction; at least 50% of its absolute contrast removed by opportunity-plus-values with a 95% interval compatible with the 50% criterion; the same direction and attenuation in held-out hospitals; and a state model in which residual risk is localized to clinically interpretable worsening/recovery states. A clinically scaled 2 percentage-point absolute contrast is the minimum interpretation margin: an interval spanning that margin is inconclusive, even if a p-value is small.

Adverse evidence is a reproducible null/reversal, no attenuation, loss after opportunity adjustment, or transport failure. It would reject the corresponding “arrival information explains the contrast” interpretation, but would not prove transfer is harmless. A large effect after within-hospital source-label permutation, or one confined to hospitals with dense measurement, falsifies the intended physiologic interpretation. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse arm overlap, missing/invalid status, unstable source labels, insufficient event/state support, APACHE timing ambiguity, wide intervals, or failure to validate analyte/unit dictionaries.

## Population, index, temporal boundaries, and outcomes

Unit is one ICU stay keyed by `patientunitstayid`; repeated stays are allowed only if every stay sharing `uniquepid` is assigned to the same split. Include patient rows with parsed age >=18 (parse `> 89` as 90 and retain an age-censored flag), `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitstaytype=admit`, nonmissing `hospitalid`, and `hospitaladmitsource` exactly `Other Hospital` or `Direct Admit`. Do not substitute `unitadmitsource` for the primary exposure; use it only for concordance sensitivity.

Time zero is ICU admission and all measured times are raw offsets from ICU admission. The primary feature window is [0,60] minutes using observation/result time, not entry/revision time. For each variable, retain first valid value, count, number of distinct timestamps, and a binary opportunity indicator. Treat negative offsets, sentinel values such as -1, nonnumeric values, and invalid units as missing. Use `labresultrevisedoffset` only in a prespecified timing sensitivity. Do not claim that a 0–60 value represents pre-transfer physiology.

The outcome ledger uses only patient disposition. Event 1 is `unitdischargestatus=Expired` with valid `0 <= unitdischargeoffset <= 2880). Event 2 is `unitdischargestatus=Alive` with the same offset range, regardless of location; it is a competing end of ICU-unit observation. A stay remaining in the unit at 2,880 is administratively censored. Missing/invalid status or offset is unknown censoring. Do not use hospital-discharge status as the primary outcome and do not extend observation beyond unit discharge. Print all disposition values before freezing maps.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

All models use the same cohort, target population, hospital split, outcome ledger, standardization and uncertainty procedure.

B0 is a transparent source-only pooled 30-minute cause-specific hazard model through 2,880 minutes, with source label, age, gender, ethnicity, unittype, hospital-level context, and time-bin indicators. B1 adds only measurement opportunity from 0–60 minutes: indicator/count/distinct-time/coverage features for each supported vital/lab domain, without any measured value. B2 adds valid measured 0–60-minute values and prespecified missingness indicators to B1. The primary decomposition reports the standardized source contrast for B0, B1, and B2; attenuation is B0 minus B2, with the opportunity-only B1 step separated. Continuous values use prespecified clinically bounded transformations and no outcome-derived cut points.

M is a mechanistic piecewise-exponential multi-state model on the same 30-minute bins and same B2 inputs. States are arrival-observed, stable/partially recovered, worsening/persistent instability, recorded unit death, and alive unit-exit/observation loss. State membership uses thresholds frozen from source units/clinical ranges in development data only; missing values remain missing states, not normal states. Transitions permit stable→worsening, worsening→stable/recovery, and either observed state→death/alive exit. Include lagged changes and persistence (not post-event values), and estimate whether source label predicts transitions after conditioning on opportunity and current state. This reveals whether a transfer contrast is a scalar level effect or an early trajectory/pathway effect; B2 loses that ordering by collapsing measurements into a landmark vector. M is not selected because it is complex: it is selected because state localization is a substantive test of the unresolved claim.

A learned sequence model (e.g., a small recurrent/transformer model) is deferred. It could model irregular measurement order, but current evidence does not establish that it would distinguish physiology from the observation process better than M; it also risks an uninterpretable site-specific leaderboard. Revisit only if M shows stable held-out state structure, analyte support is adequate, and a preregistered learned model can be compared on identical inputs/outcome/splits with calibration and feature/process ablations. This is a scientific deferral, not a neural-network or GPU ban.

## Splitting, uncertainty, and falsification

Hold out approximately 20% of hospitals using a fixed seed, stratified by hospital size and availability of both arms. Freeze dictionaries, transformations, imputation, state thresholds and calibration using development hospitals only. Keep all `uniquepid)-linked stays in one fold. Use grouped inner validation by hospital. Report held-out hospitals with one arm separately; do not extrapolate a contrast silently. Primary uncertainty is a hospital-block bootstrap or cluster-robust interval; report a `uniquepid)-clustered sensitivity.

Required falsifications and sensitivities are: source-label permutation within hospital; restriction to hospitals with >=20 patients per arm; leave-one-large-hospital-out; unit-source concordance exposure; revision-time versus result-time lab ordering; alternative first-valid tie rules; APACHE-free versus APACHE-secondary models; stratification by measurement density; exclusion of extreme `hospitaladmitoffset`; death versus alive-exit cumulative incidence; and a negative-control outcome consisting of a post-index structured documentation event unrelated to physiology. A strong result under source permutation or only in dense-measurement strata fails the intended interpretation. No “transfer treatment effect” is estimated.

## Interpretation and unavailable evidence

A supportive outcome means an observational, transportable pattern in recorded ICU-unit mortality in which early ascertainment and/or recorded physiology explain part of the source contrast. It could justify prospective collection of structured arrival reassessment data or a future triage-tool study. It does not justify a transfer policy, threshold action, causal explanation, or hospital ranking.

An adverse result rejects the corresponding portable arrival-risk hypothesis; it does not show no harm or equivalence. An inconclusive result triggers a data-quality report and no scientific direction.

Computationally checkable claims are source/schema hashes, headers, row counts, dictionaries, join integrity, cohort flow, event ordering, opportunity/value coverage, split integrity, fitted parameters, predictions, standardized contrasts, uncertainty, calibration and sensitivity outputs. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for true transfer route/timing, pre-arrival treatment, adjudicated AKI or organ injury, treatment effects, extubation/other clinical endpoints, bedside utility, harms, and overall hospital mortality. The eICU release has no images, raw waveforms, complete narrative notes, or validated clinical text extraction; `vitalPeriodic` is a five-minute summary stream. No automatic verifier can establish those unavailable clinical claims.
