# Does the ICU laboratory observation process drive mortality-model transport failure across hospitals?

## Status and deliverable

This is a computable, falsifiable 24-hour landmark study in eICU, not an executed model result. The future solver must construct the cohort, fit a matched 2×2 comparison (transparent versus learned; laboratory values alone versus values plus observation-process channels), perform whole-hospital transport evaluation and controlled missingness perturbations, and quantify which rival explanation best fits failures.

Completion requires cohort-flow/per-hospital support; laboratory name, unit, interface and availability audits; ten outer whole-hospital prediction sets from all four models; internal and target-hospital log loss, AUROC, Brier, calibration and decision curves; hospital-clustered uncertainty; observation-policy divergence, site-identifiability and perturbation results; APACHE/EOL/discharge sensitivities; and machine-readable claim-to-output links. Confirmation is not required.

## Opening, evidence, hypothesis

Existing evidence supports three narrower claims. Laboratory presence, frequency and timing can predict survival and encode healthcare processes, not physiology alone [K1]. eICU mortality discrimination and calibration vary when models move among hospitals [K2]. A recurrent model can explicitly learn from values, masks and time gaps [K3]. These do not establish that hospital-specific testing policy causes transport failure.

Untested hypothesis: among adults still in the ICU 24 hours after admission, adding first-day laboratory observation information (which analytes were measured, how often and at what intervals) will improve source-hospital prediction of death during the next 168 hours (ICU hours 24–192) but incur a larger whole-hospital transport penalty than value-only information. If hospital measurement policy materially carries that penalty, the excess should increase with source–target observation-policy divergence and respond selectively to outcome-blind perturbations of mask/count/gap channels.

The advance is attribution on identical patients, outcomes and splits—not another mortality model. Seven-day in-hospital death is consequential and close to a day-1 decision, but this study does not assert that any score threshold should direct treatment.

## Rival explanations and distinct predictions

1. Stable physiology: values retain performance across hospitals; masks/gaps add little or do not preferentially degrade with policy divergence.
2. Current severity: sicker patients both die and receive tests; observation gains attenuate after independent day-1 APACHE severity or within severity strata. Adjustment is not mechanistic proof.
3. Clinician concern: repeat testing predicts outcome within hospital and APACHE strata. This remains indistinguishable from protocols without order intent; early support and EOL proxies only bound it.
4. Discharge/death process: if the signal mainly predicts disposition, it is similarly strong for alive ICU/hospital discharge by day 7 and shifts in a competing three-state analysis.
5. Interface/timestamp artifact: effects disappear after system-unit harmonization, de-duplication and availability-time enforcement.
6. Hospital measurement policy: hospital is predictable from residualized observation signatures; excess transport gap tracks signature divergence; perturbing explicit observation channels selectively changes observation-aware models.
7. Unmeasured case mix/care: residual site differences remain observational; causal policy attribution requires order times, intent, staffing/protocol data or another design.

## Features

Create twelve 2-hour bins in minutes 0–1440. Retain the last value available in each bin after unit harmonization and prespecified physiologic-range QC; normalize and winsorize using training hospitals only. Before first observation impute the training-hospital median; afterward carry the last value forward.

Value channels are imputed trajectories plus first/last/min/max/slope summaries, without explicit masks, counts or timing. Residual observability can remain in repeated imputed values, so 'value-only' means availability-blinded, not complete erasure. Observation channels are analyte-by-bin masks, counts, first/last observation time and elapsed time since measurement. Revision delay and interface/unit strings are audit-only.

## Matched methods

1. B-V: elastic-net logistic regression on common covariates and value summaries, training-fold median imputation without missing indicators.
2. B-VO: identical regression/value inputs plus mask/count/gap summaries. This isolates information-set effects from model class.
3. L-V: one-layer GRU over the twelve imputed value bins plus common covariates, without mask/gap channels.
4. L-VO: GRU-D-style learned input/hidden decay from value, mask and elapsed-time channels plus common covariates.

Common covariates are age, gender, ethnicity, ICU type/source/stay type and training-only vocabulary for apacheadmissiondx. No hospital ID, facility descriptor, outcome-derived APACHE field, discharge field or post-landmark datum is predictive input.

The learned alternative can reveal whether the meaning of a value depends on observation recency and whether that dependence changes by hospital—information the static baseline loses. Keep L-V/L-VO parameter counts within 10%; same weighted cross-entropy, Adam, hospital-grouped early stopping and bounded tuning grid. Report logistic coefficients and learned decay/mask ablations.

## Splits, estimands and uncertainty

Assign 69 hospitals deterministically to ten outer folds using a frozen hash, stratified only by pre-outcome hospital size. Every test fold contains unseen hospitals. Tune within outer training data using five hospital-grouped folds; all admissions for a uniquepid remain together.

Primary metric is log loss. For model m and held-out hospital h, transport gap is target-hospital log loss minus source-internal validation log loss after direct standardization to target age/sex/ICU-type/admission-diagnosis strata where overlap permits. Co-primary contrasts are (a) source-internal log loss L-VO minus L-V and (b) the hospital-median transport excess (gap L-VO minus gap L-V); corresponding B-VO minus B-V contrasts are transparent information-set controls. Report hospital and pooled values without treating rows as independent hospitals.

Secondary metrics: Brier, AUROC, calibration intercept/slope, observed/expected risk, and decision-curve net benefit at 2%–20%. Paired 95% intervals resample hospitals and then people within hospitals, 2,000 replicates. Control FDR for 69 hospital-specific intervals.

Measure source–target observation-policy divergence using Jensen–Shannon distance over 14×12 mask frequencies and analyte count distributions. Relate paired excess gap to divergence with hospital-size weighting and robust uncertainty. Separately fit a 69-class hospital classifier from observation channels, then after residualizing on values/common covariates; site identifiability is not causal proof.

## Outcome-blind perturbations

1. Within target hospital, APACHE quartile, ICU type and broad diagnosis, permute mask/count/gap channels among people while fixing imputed values; 100 repeats. This tests direct reliance but creates inconsistent counterfactual records.
2. For each held-out hospital/analyte/bin, randomly thin observations to pooled source rates only when denser; recompute values/masks/gaps, 100 repeats. This cannot add unknown values to sparse hospitals.
3. Neutralize explicit observation channels to source-training medians while retaining values.
4. Repeat after exact-duplicate collapse, result-offset-only availability, and restriction to system-unit/analyte combinations present in ≥90% of training hospitals. Sensitivity implies interface/timestamp artifact.
5. Repeat contrasts for alive ICU discharge, alive hospital discharge and the three-state day-7 outcome.

## Severity, concern and selection analyses

Repeat in APACHE-linked stays adding acutephysiologyscore/apachescore/version; within APACHE quartiles and broad diagnosis/ICU strata; after adding day-1 ventilation, intubation, dialysis, urine and neurologic proxies; and excluding day-1 EOL records. Compare death with discharge endpoints. Never condition the primary analysis on remaining hospitalized through day 7. These bound measured rivals but cannot recover clinician intent.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: the 95% interval for source-internal L-VO minus L-V log loss is below zero and the interval for paired excess transport gap is above zero; directions agree in the transparent controls; excess rises with policy divergence; and observation perturbations selectively affect observation-aware predictions. Persistence through severity, EOL and artifact checks supports only that observation information measurably carries transport fragility.

Adverse: no source-internal gain, or a precise excess-gap interval at/below zero, no divergence relationship and no selective perturbation sensitivity falsify the claim for this population/panel/horizon. Observation-aware models may still predict well.

Inconclusive: wide hospital-clustered intervals, disagreement between learned and transparent contrasts, poor standardization overlap, unstable calibration or artifact dependence. Do not interpret an imprecise null as invariance.

Support cannot establish that policy causes harm, that tests are unnecessary or that suppressing testing is safe. Causal claims require order-entry times/reasons, staffing or protocol changes, and a prospective or quasi-experimental study. Deployment requires contemporary external validation, clinician review of units/thresholds/workflow/fairness and post-discharge outcomes.

## Resources and alternatives

Full patient/lab preprocessing took 59.8 and 45.8 seconds in two measured four-CPU probes. Solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 8 GPUs and 8 hours. Plan 8 CPUs/64 GiB for extraction and one A100/32 GiB for ten-fold L-V/L-VO fitting; 2–5 GPU-hours and 1–2 CPU-hours are unverified estimates. Elastic nets/metrics use CPU. CPU fallback is slower but feasible.

Deferred alternatives: boosted static trees add flexibility but not elapsed observation time; revisit if both logistic and GRU underfit values. A joint marked point-process/latent physiology model is more mechanistic but assumption-heavy; revisit after a robust observation signal. Hospital-adversarial learning asks mitigation before attribution. Interface strings remain audit variables.

Continue to mitigation only if observation dependence is supported and artifact-resistant; revise toward severity/case mix if APACHE attenuates it; abandon testing-policy attribution after precise adverse results across horizons.

## Checkable versus adjudicative

An automatic verifier can check cohort/time logic, joins, support, hospital grouping, training-only preprocessing, model inputs, metrics, perturbations, uncertainty and conclusion-rule compliance. It must reject correct computations paired with unsupported causal conclusions. It cannot establish clinician intent, causal policy, appropriateness of testing, goals-of-care completeness, unit conversion validity, decision thresholds or deployment safety.
