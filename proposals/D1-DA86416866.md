# Strict prior assay content for an all-admission documented HCC pathway resolution

## Successor decision and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. The strict prior-encounter provenance repair is preserved, but the primary endpoint is sharpened.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among all eligible adult first-observed HCC-coded inpatient admissions, strictly prior assay identity and assay-specific results/trajectories improve calibrated prediction of the first locally documented pathway resolution during the admission’s first 72 hours beyond admission/current-lab context and strictly prior capture intensity.

The primary estimand is the held-out paired proper-score contrast E minus Q for the complete pathway-resolution probability vector. Lower E minus Q means that prior assay content adds information beyond prior observation/capture intensity. This is an information hypothesis about locally recorded care, not a causal claim that laboratory values determine treatment.

The endpoint change is material. The parent’s seven-state procedure/discharge target remains a semantics-independent safety sensitivity. The new primary target distinguishes a documented family-coherent order followed by a recorded procedure, a procedure without that recorded order, an order without a subsequently recorded procedure, discharge before a pathway event, no pathway event by 72 hours, and unresolved timing. It uses the verified orders, procedures and encounters fields directly and retains early discharge rather than conditioning on continued hospitalization or procedure occurrence.

The future solver must newly produce:

1. A frozen all-admission cohort, patient partition, exact-key/multiplicity audit and strict-history provenance audit.
2. A pre-locked lexical-family and order–procedure concordance audit, including the adequacy gate.
3. Exhaustive pathway labels for every eligible admission, with early/later timing, U states and all missing-time counts.
4. Matched A/L/Q/E/H fits and the predeclared continuous-time S/S_Q alternative, all on the same split and target.
5. Locked patient-held-out probabilities, log-loss/Brier E-minus-Q contrasts, calibration, clustered intervals and state-specific results.
6. Anchor-linked, 30-day-exclusion, order-time, timestamp, observation-process, permutation, split and tie-precedence falsifications.
7. The original parent seven-state procedure/discharge E-minus-Q analysis as a semantics-independent sensitivity, and as the primary endpoint if the pathway adequacy gate fails.
8. A claim-to-output table separating computationally checkable conclusions from claims requiring clinical adjudication, linkage or another study.

Completion is established by these computed outputs, regardless of whether the hypothesis is supported.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

The inspected HCC snapshot supports native encounter admission/discharge times; native lab, procedure and order times; structured payloads; and exact joins on (Patient ID, Encounter ID). A parent full audit found 105,044 encounter rows, 43,815 distinct patients, 3,604 encounters with invalid admission or discharge timing, 1,810,646 diagnosis rows and 338,040 procedure rows. It found 7,781 distinct procedure strings and 179 blank procedure names. A repeat procedure scan found 322,166 parseable procedure starts and 243,947 midnight starts, showing that recorded procedure time is often coarse.

A bounded audit of the first 300,000 order rows found 294,319 nonblank names, populated Order entry time in all 300,000 rows, populated Start time in 281,413, populated End time in 281,051 and populated Order status in all 300,000. This is not a full-order estimate. The prefix contained many generic orders, so generic order presence is not treated as a pathway plan.

The strongest supported statement is that this source can describe recorded assays, recorded observation/capture, and recorded orders/procedures/discharge transitions using native fields and keys. The unresolved claim is whether strictly prior assay content adds reproducible calibrated information for the new pathway vector after Q, and whether that increment is robust to provenance and endpoint falsifications.

The data do not establish active HCC, stage, tumor burden, resectability, indication, intent, eligibility, recommendation, consent, scheduling, administration, completion, response, toxicity, survival, benefit, outside-care completeness or counterfactual treatment effects. The diagnosis table has no diagnosis-time field. Orders have no verified intent/completion semantics. Procedure timestamps may be convention-generated. These limitations bound every interpretation.

## Cohort and strict temporal provenance

Use HCC snapshot [source checksum] and catalog [source checksum]. All source files are ordinary read-only CSV files; no archive member is used.

Normalize Unicode and surrounding whitespace in keys and diagnosis strings. Join diagnoses to encounters exactly on (patient master index, encounter number). Define an HCC-coded encounter as a normalized diagnosis name equal to hepatocellular carcinoma or containing the case-insensitive substring hepatocellular carcinoma. Preserve all matched diagnosis strings, diagnosis type values, duplicate keys and unmatched keys. This is coding ascertainment, not clinical confirmation.

Require:

- age Age >=18;
- parseable native admission time and discharge time;
- nonnegative length of stay;
- anchor admission time in [2011-01-01, 2026-01-01);
- the earliest HCC-coded encounter by admission time, then encounter number, for each patient.

If the earliest HCC-coded record has invalid admission timing, do not replace it with a later record. Include early discharges, admissions with procedures, admissions without procedures, and admissions whose future procedure/order timing is unresolved. Do not require continued hospitalization at 72 hours.

Set t0 to the anchor Admission Time. Use half-open windows:

- strict prior history: [t0 minus 730 days, t0);
- current-lab context: [t0, t0 plus 12 hours);
- pathway horizon: [t0, t0 plus 72 hours);
- early pathway bin: [t0, t0 plus 24 hours);
- later pathway bin: [t0 plus 24 hours, t0 plus 72 hours).

A primary strict-history row must have a valid native event time in the prior window and be linked by (patient master index, encounter number) to an encounter with valid native admission time strictly earlier than t0. The primary history uses labs, procedures, orders and medications only through their native event fields and exact keys. Rows linked to the anchor encounter are excluded from primary history even if their event time is before t0. Report those rows as a labelled anchor-linked sensitivity, not as “no history.”

Audit duplicate keys, multiplicity, unmatched keys, invalid event times, invalid linked encounter times, exact boundary/tie rows, after-discharge rows, 730-day left truncation, and no-strict-history patients. The anchor-linked branch restores the event-time-only rule and labels anchor-linked rows. The 30-day negative-control branch uses [t0 minus 730 days, t0 minus 30 days), testing whether an apparent effect is concentrated in peri-admission documentation.

## Primary pathway-resolution endpoint

The outcome is a recorded workflow state, not a clinical decision. It is constructed for every eligible admission from orders, procedures and encounters. It has six event types, each split into early 0 or later 1 timing where applicable:

- C0/C1, concordant documented pathway: a nonblank order and a nonblank procedure on the same encounter key have the same exact-one family, the order’s valid order time is strictly before the procedure’s valid start time, and the procedure is recorded within the 72-hour horizon. The procedure wins a procedure/discharge exact-time tie under the incumbent rule.
- P0/P1, procedure-only pathway: a valid nonblank procedure is recorded in the horizon but no qualifying same-family order has a valid Order Time strictly before it. This includes multi-family, unmappable and other procedure strings; their family ambiguity is reported rather than silently deleted.
- O0/O1, order-only pathway: a valid nonblank order with an exact-one family is recorded before discharge in the horizon, and no corresponding same-family procedure is recorded before discharge and the horizon closes. It means “order documented without a locally recorded matching procedure,” not nonreceipt.
- D0/D1, discharge before an established pathway event: discharge occurs before 24 hours or in [24,72 hours), respectively, and no C/P/O event has been established before discharge. An order exactly tied to discharge is not treated as established without a strict earlier creation time; report a tie sensitivity.
- N72, no established pathway event before 72 hours: no C/P/O event is established in the horizon and discharge is not earlier than an established event in the horizon.
- U0/U1, unresolved: missing or invalid order/procedure timing, conflicting order times, or another unresolved row could change event classification or early/later assignment. Missing timing is unresolved, never “no event.”

For deterministic construction, use the earliest valid procedure event if any; label it C when at least one matching order was created strictly earlier, otherwise P. If no valid procedure event exists, use the earliest exact-one-family order that is strictly before discharge as O only when no matching procedure is recorded by the end of the horizon. If no C/P/O event is established, assign D when discharge is before 72 hours, otherwise N72, except that an unresolved row that could change the result assigns U. A sensitivity changes the default handling of exact discharge/procedure ties to discharge-wins. Order start-time-only records are a separate lower-confidence tier and cannot establish primary concordance because order time is unavailable.

### Frozen family map and adequacy gate

Normalize names, then apply only these frozen substring families:

- E: TACE, embolization, chemoembolization;
- R: resection, transplantation;
- A: ablation, radiofrequency, microwave, absolute alcohol;
- I: chemotherapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, perfusion;
- D: contrast imaging, puncture, biopsy, drainage, catheter placement.

A string matching zero or more than one family is not exact-one. Exact-one matching is required for C and O; generic order names do not count. Every raw string, match set, blank and multi-match is retained. The family label is an audit label, not a validated treatment classification.

Before locked labels, using only fit/selection data and never locked outcomes, require:

- at least 100 unique admissions with an exact-one-family procedure and a candidate same-key order or procedure-family resolution;
- at least 80% of timed procedures nonblank and exact-one family;
- at least 70% of candidate orders with valid order entry time;
- no family accounting for more than 90% of concordant events;
- no more than 25% of concordance candidates depending on exact-midnight ties or unresolved timing.

If the gate fails, do not claim the family-aware pathway endpoint. Run the parent’s semantics-independent seven-state endpoint as the primary E-minus-Q study. A generic any-order fallback is allowed only if it independently has at least 100 temporally resolvable admissions and passes the same timing audit; otherwise the family layer is inconclusive and the original endpoint remains primary. Gate failure is not a negative biological result.

## Matched information sets and alternatives

All models use the same cohort, endpoint, split, fit-only preprocessing and scoring.

- A, admission baseline: age, sex, clinical department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, admission missingness and strict-history availability.
- L, current-lab baseline: A plus labs in [t0,t0+12 hours), using Test, Qualitative Result, Quantitative Result, Specimen Type and Test Time. Within each assay, use first/last valid numeric value, change, elapsed time, count, density/missingness and slope only with two distinct times; preserve qualitative results assay-specifically. Do not pool numeric values across assays because units are absent.
- Q, capture-intensity comparator: L plus strictly prior encounter counts/durations/days active; days since the last strict encounter/event; source-specific row and distinct-encounter counts for labs, procedures, orders and medications; observed source-type count; missing-time counts; left-truncation and no-history indicators. Q contains no assay identity, value or result and no procedure/order/medication names, dose, route, frequency or status.
- E, primary content model: Q plus strictly prior assay identity, assay-specific numeric/qualitative result, fit-only within-assay scaling, recency, span, count, missingness, changes and slopes where supported. No cross-assay physiologic score.
- H, transparent process/content comparator: Q-like encounter/source summaries plus assay-specific prior-lab summaries and prior procedure/order/medication occurrence, recency and gap summaries. It does not interpret names as treatment. H is a comparator, not a replacement for the E-minus-Q estimand.
- S, substantive learned alternative: a low-dimensional continuous-time latent state over exactly the strict prior rows used by E/H. Each event has source/type, elapsed time and payload mask; only lab events carry assay identity and assay-specific numeric/qualitative content. Prior orders, procedures and medications are generic process tokens. Append the same L context at the landmark. Predict the same C/P/O/D/N/U early/later target.
- S_Q, process-only learned control: identical architecture, event times, occurrence information, regularization and split, but remove all lab values/results. It tests whether a learned gain is only irregular capture structure.

Fit A/L/Q/E/H with identical regularized sequential multinomial-logistic heads and fit-only preprocessing. Fit S/S_Q with fixed latent dimension, decay family, masking/reconstruction term, shrinkage and seed count chosen on selection data. No locked outcomes choose the endpoint, dictionaries, gate, hyperparameters or calibration.

The transparent models are selected for auditability and a direct assay-content estimand. S is retained because it can reveal nonlinear interactions, order-sensitive irregular trajectories and representation uncertainty lost by first/last/count summaries. The mechanistic alternative is deliberately deferred: absent lab units, reference ranges, stage, imaging and validated physiology, a liver-reserve score would invent cross-assay meaning. Revisit it only after expert review supplies those dependencies. Complexity or GPU use is not evidence of clinical value.

Use a patient-level SHA-256(normalized patient master index) modulo-100 split: buckets 0–59 fit, 60–69 selection/preprocessing/calibration, 70–79 locked evaluation and 80–99 inaccessible. All rows and admissions for a patient follow one bucket. The inaccessible bucket is not external validation.

## Analysis, uncertainty and interpretation criteria

The primary outcome is the complete pathway vector, not an R24-conditioned subset. Report paired held-out multiclass log loss and multiclass Brier for E minus Q, with Q minus L, E minus L, H minus L, S minus E and S_Q minus Q as secondary contrasts. Report state-specific calibration intercept/slope, reliability, observed versus predicted probabilities, and probability contrasts for C, P, O, D, N and U, separately by timing bin where estimable.

Use patient-clustered paired bootstrap with at least 1,000 resamples, preserving all admissions of sampled patients, for 95% intervals. Stratify descriptively by no strict history, 1–2 and at least 3 strict prior encounters; fit-only capture-intensity strata; and 30-, 180- and 730-day history windows. These are associations, not causal subgroup effects or external validation.

Supportive evidence requires:

- E minus Q below zero for log loss and/or Brier with a patient-clustered interval excluding zero;
- no material calibration deterioration;
- Q not reproducing the E/H increment;
- attenuation after within-patient/assay content permutation and time/history falsification;
- directionally stable results across capture strata, strict-history windows and the anchor-linked sensitivity;
- no forbidden-window, anchor-linked-primary, or patient-split leakage;
- if the pathway endpoint is promoted, a passed gate and non-dominant C/P/O/U states, with the increment not arising only from an artifact-prone low-confidence order-time tier.

Adverse evidence includes no strict-history E-Q improvement, Q reproducing E/H, calibration worsening, persistence after content permutation, a gain only when anchor-linked rows are admitted, concentration in the 0–30-day exclusion failure, forbidden-window leakage, split violation, or a pathway result driven by procedure-only strings after concordance disappears.

Inconclusive evidence includes a failed gate, dominant U or N states, sparse C/O states, severe left truncation, wide clustered intervals, invalid/coarse timing, dictionary ambiguity, family dominance, source-specific reversal, or a result present only when order start time substitutes for order creation time. Gate failure or inconclusive C/O evidence does not confirm or refute the assay-content hypothesis; the semantics-independent endpoint is the appropriate report.

## Required falsifications and sensitivity analyses

Run and report:

- shuffle assay values within patient and fit-only assay strata while preserving assay identity, sampling count and event time;
- shuffle strict prior event times while preserving source counts and patient membership;
- compare strict prior-encounter history with the labelled anchor-linked event-time-only sensitivity;
- exclude the final 30 days of history;
- use [t0+24 hours, t0+72 hours) rows as a forbidden-window predictor negative control, never for the primary claim;
- shuffle patient identifiers before splitting as a split-leakage detection control;
- scramble order creation/procedure times while preserving names and encounter keys; this should disrupt C/O concordance, not be mistaken for evidence about assay physiology;
- repeat with order start time in place of order placement time only as a clearly labelled lower-confidence tier;
- change procedure/discharge exact-tie precedence from procedure-wins to discharge-wins;
- report all procedure-before-admission, after-discharge, boundary, midnight, multiple-procedure, duplicate-key, unmatched-key and row-inflation audits.

The solver must state which negative controls were expected to attenuate which claim and must report them even when unfavorable.
