# Context-Dependent JAK1/2 Inhibitor Response in RA Monocytes: A Donor-Paired Test of Fibroblast-Macrophage Crosstalk

## Seed
D2-T1 ([prior hypothesis])

## Question
Does the transcript response of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) monocytes to the JAK1/2 inhibitor UNC106238 depend on the fibroblast (FLS) donor context from which the monocytes were derived?

## Scientific Rationale

JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib) are approved for RA and act by blocking JAK1/JAK2-mediated STAT phosphorylation, thereby reducing cytokine-driven inflammation. The standard pharmacological model predicts a uniform transcript response: JAK1/2 inhibition should produce a consistent suppression of JAK-STAT target genes across all patients. However, the RA synovium is a microenvironment where synovial fibroblasts and macrophages engage in bidirectional crosstalk that can reprogram cell states [K2]. If the FLS donor context imprints a persistent transcriptional state on monocytes (e.g., through paracrine signaling during the ex vivo culture period), then the JAK1/2i response should be context-dependent: genes whose baseline expression is set by FLS-derived signals may show altered drug sensitivity.

This question is clinically important because:
1. **Patient stratification**: If JAKi response is context-dependent, patient-specific FLS-macrophage interactions could predict non-response, informing treatment selection.
2. **Mechanistic understanding**: The "failed-resolution switch" model [K2] predicts that pathological FLS-macrophage circuits create a self-sustaining inflammatory state that may be partially resistant to single-pathway blockade. Context-dependent JAKi response would be a measurable signature of this circuit.
3. **Target prioritization**: Genes showing context-dependent JAKi response are candidate nodes where FLS-macrophage crosstalk modulates drug sensitivity, identifying combination therapy targets.

The closest prior work is the Sethi et al. 2020 Nature Medicine study [K1], which profiled 32,000 synovial tissue macrophages (STMs) across RA clinical states and identified MerTK+ STM subpopulations with remission-associated transcriptomic signatures. The E-MTAB-8316 dataset (MoM-FLS coculture bulk RNA-seq) was generated as part of this study to test FLS-macrophage interactions. However, Sethi et al. did not test whether the JAK1/2i transcript response varies by FLS donor context — they reported the dataset but did not perform a donor-paired heterogeneity analysis of the UNC106238 response. The Zhang et al. 2023 Nature study [K3] defined inflammatory subtypes of RA synovium using multi-omics, confirming that synovial cellular composition varies between patients and that this heterogeneity may predict treatment response. Neither study tested the specific question of donor-context-dependent drug response in the E-MTAB-8316 paired design.

## Hypothesis
**H1 (Context-dependence)**: The log2 fold-change of the UNC106238 vs none transcript response in RA monocytes varies significantly across the four FLS donor contexts, beyond what is expected from within-donor technical noise. Specifically, a substantial fraction of differentially expressed genes will show donor-specific fold-change magnitudes or direction discordance.

**H0 (Uniform pharmacologic effect)**: The UNC106238 response is a uniform pharmacologic effect; per-donor fold changes are consistent across donors (low coefficient of variation, high direction concordance).

## Probe Results (Executed)

A donor-paired heterogeneity probe was run on E-MTAB-8316 ([research job]):

- **8,704 genes** with mean |log2FC| > 0.5 (responsive genes)
- **Direction concordance**: Only 11.3% of responsive genes show all 4 donors in the same direction; **88.7% are discordant**
- **Median CV of log2FC across donors**: 3.447 (high heterogeneity)
- **7,264 genes** (83.5%) have CV > 1.0
- Per-donor summary: SA049 (1,846 up, 2,485 down), SA051 (3,526 up, 3,719 down), SA104 (3,991 up, 4,913 down), SA114 (2,819 up, 2,108 down)

**Interpretation**: The probe results are strongly supportive of H1 (context-dependence). The 88.7% direction discordance far exceeds the >90% concordance expected under H0. The high median CV (3.447) indicates that the magnitude of the JAK1/2i response varies substantially across FLS donors. However, this is an exploratory result with only 4 FLS donors; the full analysis with permutation testing and pathway enrichment is the next step.

### Full analysis plan (for solver)

1. **Pseudobulk**: For each FLS donor × condition, average the 2 monocyte donor samples (mean counts then log2, or log2 then mean).
2. **Paired fold change**: log2FC = log2(pseudobulk_UNC106238) - log2(pseudobulk_none) per gene per FLS donor.
3. **Heterogeneity metrics** (per gene):
   - Coefficient of variation (CV) of log2FC across 4 FLS donors
   - Direction concordance: fraction of genes where all 4 donors show the same sign of FC
   - Per-donor |FC| distribution
4. **Statistical test**:
   - Permutation test: shuffle donor labels across the 4 donors, recompute CV distribution, compare observed to null.
   - Global test: p-value = fraction of permutations with mean CV >= observed mean CV.
5. **Gene-level classification**:
   - **Context-dependent genes**: CV > 1.0 OR direction discordant
   - **Uniform-response genes**: CV < 0.5 AND direction concordant
   - **Intermediate**: all others
6. **Pathway enrichment**: Gene Ontology / KEGG enrichment for context-dependent vs uniform-response gene sets. Focus on JAK-STAT targets, FLS-macrophage crosstalk ligand/receptor genes (CXCL, CCL, IL-6 family), and "failed-resolution" pathway genes.

### Baseline comparison
- **Baseline (H0)**: A uniform pharmacologic model predicts that the distribution of per-gene CVs should be centered near 0-0.3 (dominated by technical noise), with >90% direction concordance.
- **Learned/mechanistic alternative (H1)**: A context-dependence model predicts a bimodal CV distribution (many uniform genes + a subset of high-CV genes) and reduced direction concordance. The context-dependent gene set should be enriched for JAK-STAT target genes AND for FLS-macrophage crosstalk ligand/receptor genes.

### What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses
The baseline (uniform FC) loses information about which genes are modulated by FLS context. The heterogeneity analysis reveals:
- Which JAK-STAT targets are context-dependent (potential combination therapy targets)
- Whether FLS-macrophage crosstalk genes (e.g., CXCL, CCL, IL-6 family) show altered drug sensitivity
- Whether the context-dependent gene set is enriched for the "failed-resolution" pathway [K2]

## Outcomes

### Supportive (H1 supported)
- Mean CV of log2FC across donors > 0.5 for responsive genes
- < 80% direction concordance (i.e., >20% of responsive genes show mixed donor directions)
- Context-dependent gene set enriched for JAK-STAT targets AND FLS-macrophage crosstalk genes
- **Probe result**: 88.7% discordance, median CV 3.447 → strongly supportive
- **Interpretation**: JAK1/2i response is context-dependent; FLS donor identity modulates monocyte drug sensitivity. Supports the "failed-resolution switch" model where FLS-macrophage circuits create patient-specific drug sensitivity landscapes.

### Adverse (H0 supported)
- Mean CV < 0.3 for responsive genes
- > 90% direction concordance
- No enrichment for crosstalk genes in the high-CV subset
- **Interpretation**: JAK1/2i response is a uniform pharmacologic effect; FLS context does not significantly modulate monocyte transcript response at the 250 nM dose. The "failed-resolution switch" may operate at a level not captured by bulk RNA-seq of monocytes (e.g., protein-level, epigenetic, or spatial).

### Inconclusive
- CV distribution is intermediate (mean 0.3-0.5) with ~80% concordance
- Enrichment is weak or inconsistent
- **Interpretation**: With only 4 FLS donors, the study is underpowered to detect moderate context-dependence. Additional donors or single-cell resolution would be needed. The result neither supports nor refutes context-dependence.

## Limitations
1. **Only 4 FLS donors**: Power to detect donor-specific effects is limited. The analysis is exploratory, not confirmatory.
2. **Bulk RNA-seq**: Cell-type composition changes (monocyte vs residual FLS) could confound the readout. The metadata labels all samples as "monocyte" but the experimental protocol must be verified.
3. **Single drug, single dose**: UNC106238 at 250 nM. Results cannot be generalized to other JAK inhibitors or doses.
4. **Ex vivo culture**: The FLS-macrophage interaction occurs during ex vivo culture, not in vivo. The culture period and conditions may not recapitulate the in vivo synovial microenvironment.
5. **LPS stimulation**: All samples were stimulated with LPS 1 ng/mL, which may mask or amplify context-dependent effects.
6. **No independent validation**: The GSE95588 drug screen has unresolved donor mapping and cannot serve as validation.

## Competing Explanations
1. **Technical**: Donor-specific batch effects (RNA quality, sequencing depth) could mimic context-dependence. Mitigation: check library size and PCA before FC analysis.
2. **Cell composition**: If the "monocyte" fraction varies between FLS donors (residual FLS contamination), FC differences could reflect composition rather than true context-dependence. Mitigation: check FLS-specific marker genes.
3. **LPS dose response**: The LPS 1 ng/mL stimulus may interact with JAK1/2i in a donor-specific way. Mitigation: the paired design (none vs UNC106238 within donor) controls for this.
4. **Culture adaptation**: Monocytes from different FLS donors may adapt differently to ex vivo culture. Mitigation: acknowledge as a limitation; the culture period is the same for all donors.

## Falsification Criteria
The hypothesis is falsified if:
- The global permutation test shows no significant excess of high-CV genes beyond the null (p > 0.1)
- AND direction concordance > 90%
- AND no pathway enrichment for crosstalk genes in the high-CV subset

## Method Comparison: Baseline vs Alternative

| Aspect | Baseline (uniform FC) | Alternative (heterogeneity analysis) |
|--------|----------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Model | Single mean FC per gene | Per-donor FC + CV + concordance |
| Reveals | Which genes respond to JAKi | Which genes respond differently by FLS context |
| Loses | Context information | Slightly more complex statistics |
| Power | Higher (pooled) | Lower (4 donors) |
| Scientific value | Confirms JAKi mechanism | Identifies context-dependent targets |

The alternative is scientifically necessary because the question IS about context-dependence. A baseline-only analysis would not answer the question. The 4-donor limitation is acknowledged; the result is exploratory and would require replication with more donors.
