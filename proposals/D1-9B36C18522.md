# Observation-robust renal and documented-nutrition signal during recorded insulin infusion

Status: episode-8 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and observation-process study, not a causal treatment, administration, feeding, AKI, or alert-benefit study.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe eICU person-period cohort, freeze the label/unit/coverage codebook before outcome analysis, quantify glucose ascertainment, and estimate whether recorded renal worsening plus a documented nutrition gap predicts a newly detected low glucose during a recorded insulin infusion.

Completion requires:

- a frozen cohort/data dictionary, flow diagram, label audit, and counts by hospital;
- the primary standardized six-hour detected-low risk difference (joint renal-worsening/documented-gap versus neither), with uncertainty, not merely an odds ratio;
- an explicit glucose-observation model, stabilized observation weights, positivity diagnostics, and sensitivity to truncation;
- a transparent primary model and a matched temporal alternative evaluated on identical periods, outcomes, covariates, hospital folds, and censoring rules;
- falsification, missingness/interface, competing-event, and leave-one-hospital-out results;
- an explicit supported, adverse, or inconclusive decision under the pre-registered support gate.

The episode-7 outcome-blind support audit is feasibility evidence, not a study result: it found 10,423 insulin-plus-recent-glucose periods across 2,798 stays and 58 hospitals, but only 783 conservative primary periods across 465 stays and 32 hospitals after requiring paired creatinine windows and prior qualifying nutrition. Of 770 periods with a subsequent glucose record, only 9 had a first detected glucose <70 mg/dL and 2 had <54 mg/dL; the renal-worsening/documented-gap cell had 33 periods and 1 detected <70 event. These counts make the gate essential.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest evidence-supported claim is only that this frozen eICU v2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative patient/stay, recorded infusion, laboratory, intake/output, nurse-charting, hospital, and EOL fields. Headers and timestamps do not establish that insulin was administered, nutrition calories were delivered, a documentation gap means feed cessation, a low glucose was symptomatic or confirmed, or creatinine change is adjudicated AKI.

The falsifiable claim is:

> Among adults in their first ICU stay with a recent valid glucose and a numeric exact-label recorded Insulin (units/hr) update, recorded creatinine worsening combined with absence of qualifying documented nutrition delivery in the preceding 12 hours is associated with a higher probability of a newly observed glucose <70 mg/dL in the next six hours, beyond insulin intensity, prior glucose trajectory, and glucose-testing history; if support permits, the contrast should remain after ascertainment adjustment and be directionally reproducible across hospitals.

This could identify a transportable trigger for prospective chart review or intensified glucose surveillance. It cannot authorize insulin dose changes, feed restart, or a monitoring interval.

## Population, index, and outcomes

Use snapshot `[source checksum]`. eICU offsets are minutes relative to ICU admission; the patient table has no `unitadmitoffset`, so offset 0 is the native ICU origin. Retain adults age >=18 (encode age >89 as 90), positive `unitdischargeoffset`, and the smallest valid `unitvisitnumber` per `uniquepid`, breaking ties by `patientunitstayid`.

Decision times are 1,440, 1,800, 2,160, 2,520, 2,880, 3,240, 3,600, and 3,960 minutes, only while before discharge and before an EOL save/discussion. Require a recent valid glucose in [t-360,t] and at least one numeric exact `Insulin (units/hr)` update in [t-360,t). Predictors end at t.

The primary outcome is the first valid glucose in (t,t+360]. A value <70 mg/dL is a detected-low event; <54 mg/dL is a secondary severe threshold. A glucose observation is not an observed negative event. Discharge, death, or EOL censoring/competing events take precedence. Results must separate no observation from observed non-low.

Recorded renal change is the latest creatinine in [t-360,t] minus the latest in [t-1800,t-1080], requiring both. The primary exposure is continuous change; >=0.3 mg/dL and >=1.5-fold are sensitivity definitions. It must be called recorded creatinine worsening, never KDIGO AKI.

Primary documented nutrition requires a positive numeric intake/output row with a frozen whitelist of oral/P.O., enteral/tube-feeding, parenteral, or TPN labels before t and no qualifying positive delivery in (t-720,t]. Exclude flushes, medication/insulin volume, dextrose-only rows, and ambiguous labels. No interface evidence, inadequate coverage, and no qualifying delivery are separate states. The codebook is frozen before looking at outcomes.

## Primary analysis and support gate

First model the probability that glucose is observed in (t,t+360] using history through t: glucose testing density/gaps, interface, severity, hospital-fold training data, discharge/EOL context, and other prespecified covariates available at t. Cross-fit by hospital so observation information cannot leak across folds. Estimate detected-low risk both unweighted and with stabilized inverse-probability-of-observation weights, reporting truncation and positivity. This does not recover unobserved hypoglycemia.

B0 is a transparent pooled-logistic cause-specific model with prespecified splines for insulin intensity, prior glucose values and gaps, demographics, weight, admission context, early severity, vasopressor context, coverage, hospital effects, and competing-event handling, excluding the renal/nutrition block. B1 adds continuous recorded renal change, strict documented-gap status, coverage categories, and their prespecified interaction. The primary estimand is the standardized marginal six-hour risk difference for joint renal worsening plus documented gap versus neither, calculated only within the common-support population and reported with patient-clustered bootstrap and hospital-aware intervals.

The analysis-readiness gate is fixed before fitting the interaction: at least 20 detected <70 events overall, at least 5 events in each prespecified renal-by-nutrition contrast cell used for standardization, at least 10 contributing hospitals with an event and non-event, effective sample size after observation weighting >=100, and no observation-weight truncation sensitivity that changes the sign. These are stability requirements, not clinical thresholds. If the gate fails, report cohort flow, event/observation rates, descriptive risk intervals, and why the estimand is unsupported; do not fit or interpret a high-variance interaction as a negative finding.

## Substantive alternative and method-selection record

The baseline is scientifically necessary because its coefficients and standardized contrasts show whether a clinically interpretable renal/nutrition signal exists after adjustment and ascertainment correction. The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over twelve one-hour bins before t, with the same predictors plus explicit missingness, measurement-time, and observation channels and separate heads for detected low glucose, glucose observation, and competing discharge/EOL. It is evaluated on the exact same periods, target, censoring, and whole-hospital development/validation/transport folds (approximately 60/20/20, with `uniquepid` kept together).

The temporal model could reveal whether ordering, persistence, and co-movement of glucose, creatinine, insulin updates, nutrition documentation, and testing gaps carry information lost by last-value summaries; ablations distinguish physiology from documentation artifact. It is not a complexity bonus and is not inferentially primary when the event gate fails. A GPU is optional and conditional: one allocated A100 using `cuda:0` only if the gate passes; B0/B1 are CPU-first. Future solver planning envelope: at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds. The measured episode-7 audit used 4 CPUs, 16 GiB, and 27.2 seconds; temporal convergence and event-fold sufficiency remain unverified.

A mechanistic latent-trajectory branch is deferred: the available snapshot lacks the genetic inputs used by the Bayesian demonstration and lacks adjudicated feeding/insulin physiology. It would be revisited only if event support improves and expert-reviewed clinical labels become available. The natural-history transformer adaptation remains a conditional temporal comparison, not a reproduction.

## Falsification and uncertainty

Prespecify:

- permuted insulin times within stay while preserving rates;
- renal/nutrition history shuffling within hospital;
- a pre-index placebo glucose window;
- strict prior-delivery, complete-observable, direct-unit, and no-carry-forward definitions;
- laboratory versus bedside glucose strata;
- insulin-free analogous periods;
- clinical versus revision timestamp sensitivity;
- leave-one-hospital-out analyses with and without observation channels; and
- newly observed non-glucose laboratory testing as a negative-control outcome.

Supportive evidence requires the gate to pass, stable observation weights, a prespecified clinically meaningful joint-versus-neither difference (2 percentage points), uncertainty excluding zero, incremental calibration/Brier/alert-burden value over B0, concordance of the temporal profile with B1, no single-hospital dominance, and failed placebo/permutation/negative-control falsifications. It supports only a prognostic monitoring signal for prospective chart-reviewed evaluation.

Adverse evidence is a null below target, reversal, disappearance after weighting or strict labels, poor held-out calibration, one-hospital/interface confinement, negative-control similarity, or reproduction in placebo/insulin-free periods. It refutes the measurable marker in this snapshot or suggests surveillance artifact; it does not prove insulin or feeding interruption harmless.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed event/cell/hospital support, sparse renal-gap cells, ambiguous labels, unstable weights, unsupported transport folds, competing-event miscalibration, or temporal nonconvergence. It should lead to data-quality work, clinical adjudication, or a prospective study, not a forced clinical conclusion.

Administration, delivered calories/feed-stop records, rescue treatment, clinician intent, symptomatic/confirmed hypoglycemia, adjudicated AKI, and alert benefit require additional data, expert review, or an emulated/prospective intervention study.

## Claims that computation can and cannot establish

The Harbor computation can check source availability, cohort construction, timestamp leakage, event and support counts, codebook application, model fitting, uncertainty calculations, calibration, transport, and whether reported numerical conclusions match generated outputs. It cannot establish that an infusion was administered, calories were delivered, a detected low glucose was symptomatic, creatinine change was AKI, a monitoring strategy changes outcomes, or a bedside intervention is safe. Those claims require adjudicated clinical data, expert review, external validation, or prospective study.
