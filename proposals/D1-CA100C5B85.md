# Proposal: selection-aware early-transition test of post-TACE laboratory pathways

## Episode, parent, and actual scientific deliverable

This is a substantive Episode-25 child of assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent's encounter-constrained first timed TACE-like index, component-resolved AFP/albumin/total-bilirubin trajectory, day-45 landmark, strict-versus-broad pathway ontology, recorded-plan `P) and reassessment-workflow `R) proxies, schedule/capture controls, temporal split, clustered uncertainty, and evidence limits.

The unresolved ambiguity is landmark selection. The parent conditions its primary day-46-to-365 comparison on having no primary-ontology liver-directed therapeutic episode through day 45, while its recorded-plan proxy is collected during days 0-45. Patients with an early recorded therapeutic event, loss of local observation, or dense measurement can therefore be selected into or out of the day-45 risk set for reasons that are related to the laboratory trajectory and care intensity. A day-45 association may be a selected-survivor/observable-cohort association rather than a pathway property that is transportable across index patients. This is distinct from the parent's schedule-only and plan-context repairs: the question is whether early competing transitions and observation loss change the target population.

The actual future deliverable is newly fitted, patient-split transition/prediction models and locked estimates, not a presumed positive result. The solver must produce: an index/episode manifest; frozen assay and trajectory definitions at days 14 and 45; early-transition and observation-state counts; full-index and day-45-landmark transition probabilities; profile contrasts; selection/observation weight diagnostics; predictions, dynamic log score/Brier score and calibration; patient-clustered uncertainty; falsification outputs; fitted parameters or a documented model deferral; and an interpretation file that links every conclusion to exact estimates and intervals.

## Evidence, hypothesis, and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is operational: the HCC snapshot contains dated procedure, encounter and laboratory records, plus dated non-drug orders and examinations that can define reproducible recorded-care and observation proxies. The direct local header audit verified the exact fields and ordinary-file paths; the prior frozen parent audit found nonzero examples for the relevant procedure/laboratory/order vocabularies. No local computation has established that any laboratory trajectory predicts a later pathway, and a candidate assessment is not clinical evidence.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among adults with an HCC diagnosis proxy and a first encounter-constrained timed TACE-like recorded episode, a favorable early component profile (AFP falling with non-worsening albumin and total bilirubin) has incremental, disease-specific recorded-pathway information beyond measurement opportunity and local-care capture. That information should be detectable in a dynamic model that includes all index patients and their early competing recorded transitions, and should be directionally compatible with the parent day-45 landmark estimate. If the apparent signal disappears when early exits and observation loss are modeled, the day-45 result is better explained by landmark selection/capture than by a stable pathway association.

The clinical importance is decision-oriented but not prescriptive. A robust result would justify a prospective, adjudicated validation study of whether early post-TACE laboratory monitoring can prioritize reassessment or surveillance. A selection-only result would warn against using a day-45 laboratory rule for the full post-TACE population without accounting for early events and local observation. Neither result establishes treatment indication, response, progression, or benefit.

## Population and time

Create one first-index episode per patient.

1. From `procedures`, form the primary 24-hour within-patient/within-encounter episode using `手术开始时间`; the earliest timed episode with literal `TACE`, `动脉化疗栓塞`, or both `肝动脉` and `栓塞` is the candidate index. Hepatic angiography alone is not therapeutic index. Tied rows use the earliest start as `t_0`; retain source labels and missing/invalid-time rows for audit.
2. Require age >=18 and nonmissing sex from a linked `encounters` row. Require a diagnosis name containing `肝细胞癌` on a linked encounter whose `就诊时间` is in [`t_0`-180 days, `t_0`+7 days]. Diagnosis has no native timestamp, so this is an encounter-time diagnosis ascertainment proxy, not onset.
3. Require `t_0` <= 2025-01-01. Do not infer death, survival, outside care, or local discharge as clinical endpoint.
4. The all-index population includes every eligible index patient, including those with an early recorded event or incomplete trajectory. For dynamic prediction, an individual contributes a landmark-specific risk set only if the required information boundary is reached; this is reported as selection, not silently excluded.
5. The day-45 continuity population is the parent's risk set: no primary-ontology liver-directed therapeutic episode in (t_0, t_0+45 days], with diagnostic contacts permitted. Report early therapeutic events and observation loss separately rather than treating them as missing.

Use two prespecified information boundaries:
- early boundary (L=14) days: last pre-index and first numeric value in days 7-14;
- parent boundary (L=45) days: last pre-index and first numeric value in days 7-45.

All predictors for a boundary are restricted to data available by that boundary. Primary outcomes are first mutually exclusive recorded states after the applicable boundary through 365 days after t_0. The full-index multi-state analysis additionally models first transitions from t_0 through day 45, so the early event/observation process is not discarded. Report CIFs at 30, 90 and 320 days after t_0, and conditional post-boundary CIFs through day 365. No post-outcome or post-day-45 variable enters a predictor set.

## Recorded outcomes and estimand

Use the parent's frozen mutually exclusive ontology. Primary recorded outcome is the first post-index liver-directed therapeutic state, with a strict TACE state separated from alternate/mixed/unclassified liver-directed records. Secondary states are diagnostic-only/technical, non-liver procedure, and administrative loss of local observation. Keep strict, broad, ambiguous/mixed, diagnostic and non-liver tiers, plus 24-hour versus encounter-only episode sensitivities.

The primary estimand is predictive and noncausal:

[
Delta^{L}_{k}(h)=
P(	ext{first recorded state }k	ext{ by }hmid A_F,L,	ext{history/capture})
-
P(	ext{first recorded state }k	ext{ by }hmid A_D,L,	ext{history/capture}),
]

where (A_F) is AFP decrease with non-worsening albumin and total bilirubin, and (A_D) is AFP decrease with worsening of either component, using separate training-only within-assay standardization and preserved missingness/measurement timing. Estimate (Delta^{14}_k(h)) and (Delta^{45}_k(h)) on their respective dynamic risk sets, standardized to the same pre-index demographic, diagnosis, prior-care and observation-opportunity distribution. Separately estimate the full-index multi-state transition probabilities from t_0, including early transitions before day 45. The selection gap, early-transition probabilities, risk-set sizes, effective sample sizes and weight diagnostics are required outputs; do not call standardization a causal recovery of outcomes for patients who had an early event.

The prespecified scientific target is that the favorable-versus-discordant contrast for later strict TACE or a liver-directed therapeutic state remains directionally compatible at L=14 and L=45, with the joint full-index model attributing a nonzero incremental component contribution beyond capture/early-transition features. A practical margin is |contrast| >= 0.03 at 320 days, with a patient-level interval excluding zero and adequate common support; the margin is a design gate, not a claimed result. If the day-45 contrast meets the margin but the early-transition-aware model does not, the selection explanation is favored.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

All methods use the same index patients, outcome ontology, information boundary, split, locked test and uncertainty procedure.

- (B_0), transparent history-only: regularized discrete-time cause-specific hazards/multi-state transitions using age, sex, index encounter timing/department, diagnosis ascertainment features, prior recorded procedure burden, and pre-index component values where available.
- (B_{mathrm{obs}}), transparent observation/capture baseline: (B_0) plus assay availability, measurement delays/counts, lab qualitative/inequality flags, encounter/admission/discharge/contact intensity, procedure-source mix, coding density and administrative observation indicators, but no post-index laboratory value.
- (B_{mathrm{traj}}), transparent trajectory model: (B_{mathrm{obs}}) plus separate AFP/albumin/bilirubin states at L=14 or L=45, within-assay changes, exact delay, missingness and uncertainty; include early transition status as a competing state rather than conditioning it away.
- (B_{mathrm{context}}), parent continuity model: (B_{mathrm{traj}}) plus recorded-plan (P), plan timing/status sensitivity and plan-by-profile contrasts for the day-45 analysis; (R) remains an observed-event context descriptor, not a pre-event predictor of that same event.

The substantive learned/mechanistic alternative is (M_{mathrm{joint-selection}}): an irregular-time multi-output state-space plus marked competing-transition model. It has separate latent AFP, albumin and bilirubin trajectories, assay/specimen-specific observation models, qualitative/missing indicators, local observation intensity, early recorded transitions, and the order-plan process through day 45. It jointly estimates transition hazards from t_0 and post-landmark CIFs, allowing asynchronous or nonlinear component changes and informative measurement/care capture. That information is lost by categorical (B_{mathrm{traj}}), which can confuse a late measurement, early care exit, and a true component trajectory. Fit to the same training period; compare locked dynamic log score/Brier score, calibration, CIF contrasts and patient-bootstrap intervals, not just discrimination.

CPU is the default because the transparent fits are tabular and the latent model is modest relative to the available envelope. A GPU is not a scientific requirement; a bounded allocated A100 probe may be used only if repeated latent fitting materially benefits, with `gpus:1` and `cuda:0`. No discovery model fit has been run. The latent model may be deferred with a checkpoint if convergence/positivity fails; deferral does not authorize replacing the scientific question with an easier model.

Deferred alternatives are a generic transformer (semantic labels and adjudicated outcomes are limiting; reconsider if validated longitudinal text/time and clinical labels become available), ALBI/MELD or a reserve mechanistic score (units, INR and validated thresholds are absent), image/multimodal modeling (HCC image files are unavailable), causal treatment-policy estimation (intent, assignment, completion and outcomes are unavailable), and mortality/survival modeling (death and outside-care ascertainment are unavailable). The research demonstrations were read as bounded inspiration: Delphi supports dated structured-sequence adaptation; the Bayesian example supports irregular-time latent-state reasoning but its genetic inputs are unavailable; the cancer demonstration's main article/full STAR Methods remain unavailable and HCC images are absent, so no reproduction claim is made.

## Split, uncertainty, falsification and interpretation gates

Freeze all lexical screens, assay rules, episode definitions, early-event/observation states, time windows, profile thresholds, common-support and sparse-cell rules before locked evaluation. Use 2010-2022 fit, 2023 tune, and 2024 through 2025-01-01 locked test when dates and event support permit; otherwise use a deterministic patient-hash split declared before fitting. No patient crosses splits. Fit all observation/transport weights on training data only. Use patient-clustered bootstrap or equivalent intervals, never row bootstrap.

Required falsifications:

1. **Parent continuity:** reproduce the parent unstratified day-45 strict contrast and its plan-positive/negative and reassessment-context outputs. Failure to reproduce is a packaging/definition problem, not evidence against the new hypothesis.
2. **Landmark selection:** compare L=14 and L=45 after common-support standardization; report early therapeutic/diagnostic/observation-loss CIFs. A signal confined to L=45 and explained by early state/observation features is adverse to transportability.
3. **Observation process:** compare (B_0), (B_{mathrm{obs}}), (B_{mathrm{traj}}), and (M_{mathrm{joint-selection}}); report positivity, effective sample size, calibration and missingness strata. Large trajectory attenuation after (B_{mathrm{obs}}), unstable weights, or incompatible latent observation models favors capture/selection.
4. **Endpoint/workflow controls:** retain strict-versus-broad, diagnostic/technical, non-liver and ambiguous/mixed outcomes; apply the same procedure episode and encounter-only sensitivity. A comparable profile effect for non-liver or diagnostic capture is adverse.
5. **Temporal falsification:** permute exact assay value-to-time within patient while preserving assay/specimen and separately permute eligible plan-order times; use a pre-index placebo trajectory with the same construction. Preserved effects favor timing/capture artifact.
6. **Plan/reassessment context:** report the parent's (P) and (R) strata, index-encounter/first-24-hour order exclusions, non-canceled sensitivity and R narrative sensitivity. A plan-positive-only effect, status/index-bundle concentration, or similar permutation effect weakens a disease-specific interpretation.
7. **Missingness and early-event sensitivity:** repeat with numeric-only complete pairs, qualitative/inequality retained as missing indicators, inverse-observation weighting, and an explicit early-event absorbing state. No version may silently discard patients with early events.
8. **Model integrity:** require convergence, held-out calibration and locked predictions for the learned model; incompatible latent estimates are adverse/inconclusive, never grounds to prefer the learned model.

Supportive results require: parent continuity; compatible L=14 and L=45 direction after capture standardization; an incremental (B_{mathrm{traj}}) contribution beyond (B_{mathrm{obs}}); nontrivial component-specific effects not confined to missingness or local-care intensity; adequate overlap/calibration; weaker non-liver/placebo/permuted effects; stable episode/endpoint tiers; and compatible converged (M_{mathrm{joint-selection}}) estimates. This supports only a robust association with a locally recorded post-TACE pathway and motivates adjudicated prospective validation.

Adverse results include null/reversal, day-45-only signal explained by early exit or observation, (B_{mathrm{obs}}) explaining the trajectory, plan/status/index-bundle concentration, comparable controls/placebo/permutations, poor overlap/calibration, endpoint instability or learned-model sensitivity. These favor selection, scheduling, coding or measurement explanations.

Inconclusive results include sparse early-event or paired-assay cells, missing/invalid timestamps, unstable vocabulary, insufficient follow-up, temporal-split failure, positivity failure, intervals crossing the margin, non-identifiable early observation states, or latent nonconvergence. None of these establishes absence of biological response or absence of treatment benefit.

The verifier can check source paths/schema IDs, headers, two-key aggregation, frozen screens, patient splits, temporal leakage, episode/state construction, transition/CIF calculations, predictions, uncertainty, calibration, overlap, permutations and conclusion-to-output links. It cannot adjudicate treatment indication/intent/completion, technical success, radiologic/pathologic response, viable tumor, recurrence/progression, liver failure, death/mortality/survival, outside care, appropriateness, benefit, utility or external validity. Those require clinical adjudication, linked mortality/outside-care data, and prospective or external study.
