# Working proposal: pre-illness creatinine–cystatin C discordance, grip strength, and hospitalized AKI

Status: working design; the full hypothesis has not been tested. Aggregate feasibility and follow-up diagnostics are not inferential results.

## Scientific opening and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest currently inspected evidence supports three bounded claims: large eGFRcys−eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have many non-GFR predictors [K1]; the difference is associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes in SPRINT [K2]; and acute muscle loss can make creatinine eGFR substantially overestimate measured GFR during prolonged critical illness [K3]. None establishes that pre-illness discordance predicts incident AKI in generally ambulatory adults, that low grip modifies that association, or that cystatin-C testing prevents harm.

Primary hypothesis: among UK Biobank participants with baseline 2021 CKD-EPI eGFRcr >=60 mL/min/1.73m2 and no prior inpatient N17 code, a 15-unit lower eGFRcys−eGFRcr predicts a higher 10-year cumulative incidence of first hospitalized coded AKI after adjustment for eGFRcr and prespecified confounders. The discordance risk contrast is larger at lower sex-specific grip strength.

Leading explanation: discordance identifies renal vulnerability missed by creatinine in people with low muscle reserve. Strongest rival: discordance chiefly reflects inflammation, adiposity, smoking, diabetes, frailty, or generalized hospitalization/coding opportunity. This study tests prognostic separation, not a biological mechanism or treatment effect.

## Estimand and analyses

Primary estimand: standardized cumulative-incidence difference and ratio at 10 years comparing delta=eGFRcys−eGFRcr of −15 versus 0 at eGFRcr 90, separately at sex-specific 25th and 75th grip percentiles. Report the cause-specific HR and delta×grip interaction with 95% confidence intervals. Never include eGFRcr, eGFRcys, and their algebraic difference in one model.

Baseline: cause-specific Cox model with splines, sequentially adding delta, grip, and delta×grip to eGFRcr plus age, sex, ethnicity, smoking, diabetes, BMI/waist, blood pressure, CRP, albuminuria, glycemia, assessment year, and center when verified. Use Aalen–Johansen/g-formula standardization, death as competing event, Fine–Gray sensitivity, bootstrap uncertainty, proportional-hazards checks, first-year exclusion, alternate discordance thresholds, and complete-case sensitivity after covariate-only imputation.

Ascertainment/frailty rival tests, treated as secondary outcomes rather than adjustment variables:

1. Repeat the standardized discordance contrast for first inpatient acute appendicitis (K35*) as a plausibly kidney-unrelated acute coded event; exclude prevalent/index-day K35.
2. Repeat it for first hip fracture (S72*) as a frailty-sensitive outcome; exclude prevalent/index-day S72. A similar association with both controls, especially without AKI specificity, weakens the renal-vulnerability interpretation; null controls strengthen specificity but cannot prove mechanism.
3. Report event counts and confidence intervals. Specificity is supportive only when AKI's lower confidence bound is >0 and each control estimate is smaller with an interval including 0. It is adverse when either control has lower bound >0 and a point estimate at least as large as AKI. It is inconclusive when control intervals cannot exclude an AKI-sized effect. Do not interpret imprecise null controls as reassuring.

Learned alternative: fit a same-input competing-risk gradient-boosted survival model on a frozen 70/15/15 participant split stratified by AKI, death, and sex. Compare held-out calibration slope/intercept, integrated Brier score, time-AUC, and decision curves with the Cox baseline; inspect stability of nonlinear delta×grip regions across bootstrap/split repetitions. The alternative is retained only if it reveals reproducible nonlinear interaction or clinically relevant calibrated net benefit, not a small AUC increase. It cannot establish mechanism. Disease-sequence learning is deferred unless prior event order materially confounds this estimand; latent trajectories lack repeated biomarker support; raw-image multimodal reproduction is unavailable and a lab-only variant does not add a distinct test.

## Falsification and interpretation

Lock thresholds before fitting. Primary support requires the lower 95% confidence bound for the adjusted 10-year risk difference (delta −15 versus 0) to exceed 0. Clinical usefulness additionally requires its lower bound to exceed 0.5 percentage points and held-out calibration/decision utility to improve. An adverse result is an upper bound <=0; adverse for utility is an upper bound <0.5 points with no incremental utility. Intervals crossing these thresholds, unstable date pairing/splits, inadequate negative-control precision, or an unverifiable 10-year censor date are inconclusive. Apply the same directional rule to the low-versus-high grip contrast.

Required newly computed deliverables: attrition and missingness; code/date-pair audit; defensible follow-up endpoint decision; event counts; eGFR distributions; baseline coefficients and standardized risks; interaction and uncertainty; negative-control estimates; held-out performance/calibration/decision metrics; sensitivity analyses; and a machine-readable conclusion linked to outputs.

Supportive results justify prospective prognostic enrichment and testing research, not causal benefit, measured GFR, or a muscle mechanism. Adverse results argue against this stratification claim but not against cystatin C assay accuracy. Inconclusive results require deferral or a study with linkage metadata. Clinical adjudication/additional data are required for KDIGO AKI/stage, measured GFR, indications for testing, treatment response, and external transportability.
