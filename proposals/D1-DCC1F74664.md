# Laboratory-observation history after recorded physiology: a fixed-burden ICU monitoring experiment

Status: Episode-20 targeted repair of `[prior hypothesis]`. This preserves the assessed parent’s adult first-stay population, ICU-relative six-hour landmark, competing recorded ICU-death estimand, whole-hospital transport, and physiologic-adjusted laboratory-process question. It makes no fitted-result, causal, or clinical-benefit claim.

## Scientific question and advance

At ICU-relative minute 360, does the patient-linked history of which laboratory results were recorded add transportable information for later recorded ICU death after accounting for the recorded physiologic state and the general intensity of recorded vital monitoring? The clinically consequential translation is deliberately narrow: at the same number of monitoring reviews, does a laboratory-process score identify more recorded deaths or achieve higher recorded-death PPV than a physiologic-state score?

The strongest available evidence is the parent’s outcome-blind feasibility audit: 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows known by minute 360. These are feasibility facts, not model results, physiologic validation, clinician intent, or evidence of benefit.

The unresolved claim is that a laboratory-observation increment is patient-linked and transportable rather than a proxy for omitted instability, generic monitoring intensity, local interface, revision practice, or hospital workflow. The substantive advance is a prespecified separation of:

1. laboratory state/availability beyond recorded vital state (`Bp-Bv`);
2. laboratory process beyond both recorded vital state and vital-observation intensity (`BpV-BvV`);
3. equal-review-burden retrospective policy consequences; and
4. temporal ordering/persistence/co-movement beyond compressed landmark summaries.

A favorable result is still only evidence for a retrospective recorded-death monitoring proxy. It is not evidence that ordering/reviewing a laboratory test improves care.

## Falsifiable hypothesis

Primary hypothesis:

> In adults reaching ICU-relative minute 360, a patient-level laboratory-observation process model (`Bp`) has a favorable, held-out-hospital incremental competing-risk score over the recorded-state baseline (`Bv`) for recorded ICU death by minute 1,800. The increment remains directionally favorable in the stricter comparison that adds general vital-observation intensity (`BpV-BvV`), and, at a prespecified per-hospital review burden, the laboratory policy captures more recorded deaths or has higher recorded-death PPV than the `Bv` policy.

This hypothesis is rejected for the patient-linked/actionable interpretation if the increment disappears or reverses after vital-state or vital-intensity adjustment, equal-burden capture/PPV does not improve, the process permutation does not attenuate the increment, or a hospital-descriptor/workflow model reproduces it. A temporal-model improvement is not counted as a substantive result unless it is stable across hospitals and depends on temporal order rather than merely a larger or differently regularized feature set.

## Population, landmark, outcome, and estimand

Use eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`.

Construct one row per person’s first valid ICU stay:

- `patient.age >= 18`; recode numeric ages greater than 89 to 90;
- select the smallest valid numeric `patient.unitvisitnumber` within `patient.uniquepid`, breaking ties by `patientunitstayid`;
- require numeric `patient.unitdischargeoffset > 360`;
- retain only stays reaching the fixed landmark at minute 360.

The predictor window is ICU-relative ([0,360]) minutes, inclusive at 0 and 360. A laboratory row is eligible only when `lab.labresult` is numeric, `lab.labresultoffset in [0,360]`, and `lab.labresultrevisedoffset <= 360` or null. A vital row is eligible when `observationoffset in [0,360]`. Result time and revision time remain separate features; a result whose revision was not known by minute 360 cannot be treated as known at the landmark.

The primary outcome is the first discharge event represented by the selected `patient` row after the landmark and no later than minute 1,800:

- event of interest: normalized `unitdischargestatus == Expired` and `unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800]`;
- competing event: normalized `unitdischargestatus == Alive` and `unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800]);
- administrative censoring: the selected stay remains without either event through minute 1,800.

Unexpected discharge-status values, duplicate selected stays, negative/invalid offsets, and impossible status/offset combinations must be audited and reported before fitting; they cannot be silently mapped to an outcome. Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, latent death, and post-ICU outcomes are outside the estimand.

Estimate cause-specific discrete-time hazards in 24 one-hour bins covering (360,1800], then derive the cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death with alive ICU discharge as a competing event. Models may use the landmark features only; no post-landmark predictor is allowed.

## Models and the substantive temporal alternative

All codebook decisions, filtering, imputation, scaling, regularization, temporal hyperparameters, and policy definitions are frozen within development hospitals. No outcome, post-landmark row, hospital ID, or hospital descriptor enters a patient predictor.

- `Bv): pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic hazards using admission context (`gender`, `ethnicity`, `admissionweight`, `hospitaladmitoffset`, `hospitaladmitsource`, `unitstaytype`) plus supported laboratory state/availability and recorded vital state/availability.
- `Bp): exactly `Bv` plus patient-level laboratory-process features listed above.
- `BvV`: `Bv` plus vital-observation-intensity features.
- `BpV`: `Bp` plus vital-observation-intensity features. The primary strict actionability contrast is `BpV-BvV`; `Bp-Bv` is the primary physiologic-adjusted process contrast.

All four transparent models use the same discrete-time competing-event likelihood, folds, eligible episodes, and feature-support rules. Report coefficient uncertainty for the primary process terms when stable, but judge transport performance by held-out predictions and hospital-clustered uncertainty rather than coefficient significance.

### Temporal alternative

Fit one prespecified compact one-layer GRU (hidden size 32, dropout 0.1, development-only early stopping; a temporal convolution is a deferred sensitivity alternative) on six fixed bins ([0,60), [60,120), [120,180), [180,240), [240,300), [300,360]). Each bin contains the supported laboratory state, laboratory availability/process channels, vital state/availability, and vital-observation-intensity channels derived from that bin; admission context is concatenated to the final representation. The GRU has competing death and alive-discharge heads for the same 24 post-landmark bins and no hospital ID.

The scientific comparison is not “deep model versus simple model.” `BpV` deliberately compresses the six-bin records to prespecified landmark summaries (first/last/median/range/slope and coverage); `T` retains bin order and joint within-window timing. Thus `T` can test whether persistence, ordering, and co-movement of laboratory and vital observations contain information lost by a transparent landmark summary. To demonstrate that any temporal result depends on time rather than extra parameterization, require a prespecified time-order permutation/shuffle ablation and compare `T` with a parameter-count-matched non-temporal model. A small AUROC gain alone is not substantive: a temporal claim requires reproducible held-out-hospital calibration or equal-burden policy improvement, with attenuation after temporal-order shuffling.

## Hospital transport, equal-burden policies, uncertainty

Use five grouped outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once. Keep all stays with the same `uniquepid` together and stratify by eligible episode/event counts when feasible. A hospital is never split across train and evaluation. All model fitting and feature transformations are development-only.

Primary performance estimand: the macro-hospital paired difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score over (360,1800] for `Bp-Bv`, with the direction predeclared as lower Brier being favorable. Secondary summaries are CIF at 1,800, time-specific Brier/calibration, `BpV-BvV`, and `T-BpV`. Report per-hospital differences, pooled summaries only alongside macro-hospital summaries, and 95% hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals with at least 1,000 resamples if feasible. Use a prespecified multiplicity report for the paired model contrasts; do not turn multiple metrics into post hoc selection.

### Corrected equal-review-burden rule

For each held-out hospital h and q in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, define the reference `Bv` policy as episodes with the held-out `Bv` cumulative-incidence score at least q. Let k(h,q) be its alert count. For each comparator (`Bp`, `BpV`, `T`), rank the same held-out episodes by its own landmark score and select exactly the top k(h,q), with deterministic prespecified tie-breaking. This uses no held-out outcome and guarantees equal review burden within hospital; it replaces a development threshold that could not guarantee equal burden after transport. If k(h,q)=0, that hospital/q cell is not assigned a PPV or capture value.

For each supported h/q/model report review fraction, recorded-death capture, recorded-death PPV, calibration among reviewed and non-reviewed episodes, and incremental alert membership relative to `Bv`. Summaries are macro-hospital and pooled, with the paired difference as the estimand. A common-threshold decision curve may be reported as a secondary label-relative diagnostic, but it cannot replace the exact top-k comparison or be interpreted as clinical utility.

The q actionability gate is fixed before evaluation: at least 20 evaluation hospitals, at least 10 alerts per evaluation hospital on average, at least 100 alerted episodes, and at least 20 recorded deaths pooled across supported held-out cells. Unsupported q values are exploratory only and cannot support an implementation-oriented conclusion.

## Support gates and readiness

Before fitting, write a frozen data dictionary, codebook, cohort flow, fold manifest, and support manifest. The primary interpretation is allowed only if all applicable gates pass:

- at least 1,000 recorded deaths and 50 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes;
- at least 20 evaluation hospitals in every outer fold and at least 20 eligible episodes per evaluation hospital;
- each retained vital field has observations in [0,360] for at least 30% of eligible episodes overall and at least 10% in at least 80% of evaluation hospitals;
- the strict `BpV-BvV` comparison has overlapping prespecified Bv-risk and vital-coverage strata, with at least 10 episodes in every populated evaluation hospital/stratum used for permutation;
- every primary model converges in all five folds, yields finite predictions for all eligible evaluation episodes, and passes timestamp and patient/hospital-leakage audits;
- every reported q passes the equal-burden support gate above.

If a gate fails, retain the computation as a support result, but label the affected contrast descriptive or inconclusive and do not upgrade it into clinical/actionability evidence.

## Falsification and robustness

Lock these before outcome evaluation:

1. Permute laboratory-process channels within hospital, held-out-independent development risk strata, and vital-coverage strata while holding laboratory/vital state fixed. The incremental process signal should attenuate.
2. Compare `Bp-Bv` with `BpV-BvV`; disappearance after general vital-intensity adjustment supports a monitoring-workflow explanation.
3. Repeat with a strict common analyte panel and revision-known-only rows. A result that vanishes under defensible codebook/revision rules is not robust.
4. Fit vital-observation-only and hospital-descriptor-only diagnostics. Descriptor reproduction or concentration in one hospital/interface/analyte family falsifies a general patient-linked interpretation.
5. Use outcome-free within-hospital percentile normalization of process features as a transport diagnostic, requiring at least 50 calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes per hospital.
6. For `T`, permute bin order and use a parameter-count-matched non-temporal comparator. A temporal claim requires attenuation under order permutation.
7. Recompute equal-burden policy metrics using the same per-hospital k(h,q); gains caused solely by more reviews cannot count.
8. Perform leave-one-hospital-out influence summaries and report fold direction, not only a pooled number.

Supportive results require support gates, a favorable paired held-out-hospital `Bp-Bv` performance result, a same-direction `BpV-BvV` result with uncertainty excluding no improvement for the declared primary metric, and an equal-burden capture/PPV improvement without unacceptable calibration loss. Process permutation must attenuate, and no descriptor/site/interface/revision artifact may explain the result. These results establish only a retrospective recorded-death review proxy.

Adverse results include null/reversed strict contrast, no equal-burden improvement, calibration harm, persistence under process permutation, descriptor/site reproduction, or loss under defensible codebook/revision controls. They support workflow, missing-physiology, interface, or revision explanations and argue against the patient-linked interpretation.

Inconclusive results include failed support/overlap gates, sparse q cells, unstable fold directions, failed convergence, leakage, wide intervals spanning clinically relevant favorable and adverse values, or an unsupported temporal fit. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.

## Evidence limits

The eICU snapshot contains no validated lab-order time, clinician reason for ordering, human review event, alert acknowledgement, treatment response, independent physiologic adjudication, bedside waveform, or prospective outcome under a review policy. Public eICU narrative sections are removed; structured chart fields do not establish intent. A vital value can be missing or wrong, and a process increment that survives recorded-vital adjustment can still encode unmeasured physiology or local workflow.

Therefore even a fully supportive computation cannot establish causal benefit, clinician actionability, physiologic truth, or superiority of a real monitoring program. Those claims require expert review of analyte/physiology validity, external-hospital validation, prospective silent-alert testing, and ultimately an impact or stepped-wedge study measuring review burden, responses, harms, and patient-centered outcomes.
