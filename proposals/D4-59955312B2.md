# Scientific Proposal: Proteomic Signatures Predict Distinct Multimorbidity Trajectories

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Hypothesis**: Among UK Biobank participants without major chronic disease at baseline, baseline proteomic signatures predict distinct long-term multimorbidity trajectories that differ fundamentally from those predicted by traditional clinical risk factors alone.

**Specific Testable Claim**: Baseline proteomic profiles (measured at UK Biobank assessment center) predict the incidence and temporal pattern of incident multimorbidity (≥2 chronic conditions) more accurately than age, sex, BMI, and traditional cardiovascular risk factors alone.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Early Risk Stratification**: Proteomic biomarkers could enable earlier identification of individuals at risk for developing multimorbidity, potentially years before traditional risk factors manifest clinically.

2. **Mechanistic Insights**: The differential prediction of proteomic signatures vs. traditional risk factors could reveal novel biological pathways driving disease progression, distinguishing between inflammatory, metabolic, and other mechanisms.

3. **Precision Prevention**: Understanding distinct proteomic trajectories could enable targeted prevention strategies for different disease progression patterns.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Existing research has identified proteomic signatures for specific diseases (e.g., cardiovascular disease, cancer) but has not characterized how proteomic profiles predict the *temporal trajectory* of multimorbidity development. This study will:

- Test whether proteomic signatures provide independent predictive value beyond traditional risk factors
- Characterize distinct proteomic subgroups associated with different multimorbidity trajectories (rapid vs. gradual accumulation)
- Identify specific proteins/pathways that uniquely predict trajectory patterns

## Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis

1. **Multimorbidity Definition**: Incident multimorbidity = ≥2 distinct chronic conditions diagnosed within 10 years of baseline assessment
   - Chronic conditions: ICD-10 codes for cardiovascular disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, respiratory disease, cancer, etc.
   - Exclude participants with baseline diagnosis of any chronic condition

2. **Proteomic Feature Engineering**:
   - Z-score normalize protein abundances within each participant group
   - Create proteomic risk score: weighted sum of top 100 proteins by univariate association
   - Identify proteins with p < 0.05 in univariate analysis

3. **Primary Comparison**:
   - Model A: Multimorbidity ~ age, sex, BMI, smoking, traditional cardiovascular risk factors
   - Model B: Multimorbidity ~ age, sex, BMI, smoking, traditional CV risk factors + proteomic score
   - Test: ΔAUC, ΔC-statistic, net reclassification improvement (NRI)

4. **Trajectory Analysis**:
   - Stratify by time to multimorbidity onset (<2 years vs. 2-5 years vs. >5 years)
   - Compare proteomic signatures across trajectory subgroups
   - Test for differential protein associations across trajectory strata

### Sensitivity Analyses

1. **Alternative multimorbidity definitions**: ≥3 conditions, ≥4 conditions
2. **Different follow-up periods**: 5 years, 10 years, 15 years
3. **Protein selection methods**: Top 100, top 500, pathway-based selection
4. **Causal inference**: Mendelian randomization using genomics data (if sufficient instruments)

## Falsification Criteria

**Hypothesis will be falsified if**:
- Proteomic score provides no independent predictive value (ΔAUC < 0.01, p > 0.05 for interaction)
- No proteins show differential association across trajectory subgroups (p > 0.05 for all trajectory × protein interactions)
- Traditional risk factors explain all variance in multimorbidity trajectory patterns

**Hypothesis will be supported if**:
- Proteomic score significantly improves prediction (ΔAUC ≥ 0.03, p < 0.001)
- At least 10-20 proteins show significant differential association across trajectory subgroups
- Proteomic signature identifies a subgroup with accelerated multimorbidity progression

## Method Comparison

### Baseline Approach
- Logistic regression with traditional risk factors only
- AUC comparison between proteomics-only vs. traditional-only models

### Substantive Alternative Approach
- Machine learning ensemble (random forest, gradient boosting)
- Feature importance analysis to identify top predictive proteins
- Non-linear interaction detection between proteomic and traditional factors

### Information Revealed
- The baseline approach tests simple additive effects
- The ML approach can detect non-linear relationships and interactions
- If ML outperforms linear models substantially, suggests complex biological mechanisms
- If linear models perform similarly, suggests proteomics act primarily through traditional pathways
