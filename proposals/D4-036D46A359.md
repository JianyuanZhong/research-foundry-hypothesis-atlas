# Episode 13 proposal — UKB17 posterior-thigh MFI and later falls report

## Question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank `ukb672073` participants who attend imaging visit 2 and provide a valid falls response at visit 3, does higher bilateral posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) at visit 2 have a positive adjusted association with later self-reported falls, beyond visceral adiposity, thigh-muscle distribution, and measured baseline frailty/function? Require visit 3 to follow visit 2 by at least 366 days; the visit-3 question recalls the preceding year. Falls can cause injury, disability and loss of independence. A robust association would motivate independent prospective validation of muscle-quality phenotyping; a precise adverse/null association would lower its priority. This is not an estimate of true fall-event risk, a causal effect, or treatment benefit.

**Evidence already supported.** The inspected abstract for UKB thigh-MFI work reports broad mortality and incident-disease associations, not this lagged falls contrast [K1]. The closest overlap reports thigh-muscle distribution associations with falls/function [K2], so broad MRI-thigh/falls novelty is not justified. A myosteatosis review leaves aging versus disease/disuse explanations unresolved [K3]. The proposed question adds a visit-2-to-visit-3 MFI association beyond measured VAT, thigh distribution and baseline function; the cited works do not demonstrate that association.

**Hypothesis and rival.** Among the selected returnees with a valid coded outcome and complete prespecified fields, 1-SD higher visit-2 bilateral posterior-thigh MFI is positively associated with any visit-3 falls report after adjustment. The strongest rival is that MFI marks pre-existing frailty, inactivity/disuse, illness or prior falls; recall/reporting error or selective MRI/follow-up may also create or distort the pattern. Covariate adjustment and prespecified shape/heterogeneity checks probe measured proxies only; they cannot eliminate unmeasured confounding, reverse causation or selection bias.

## Analysis, method comparison and resources

Baseline is adjusted logistic regression for any reported fall, with linear 1-SD MFI and a fixed nonredundant comparison/covariate set: VAT; posterior/anterior muscle distribution ratio from `22403`–`22406`; body fat, BMI, age, sex, smoking, activity, pace, grip and prior falls. Do not include algebraically redundant muscle components, totals and ratios together. Report conditional MFI OR, interval and fit diagnostics; no causal interpretation.

On the exact same cohort and outcome, fit a substantive alternative: low-degree penalized logistic GAM with prespecified smooth MFI plus hierarchical shrinkage MFI-by-prior-falls and MFI-by-grip interactions, retaining the same covariates. Fix complexity in advance and tune only within training data. The alternative can reveal curvature or concentration in measured frailty-proxy strata that the linear common slope averages away; it cannot determine whether frailty, disuse or report behavior caused that pattern. Use full-cohort participant-bootstrap uncertainty (target 1,000 resamples, report failures). A secondary fixed-seed participant-level 80/20 split evaluates both models on the same holdout; tuning only in training; report Brier score, log loss, calibration-in-the-large/slope and discrimination with uncertainty. This is a predictive diagnostic, not external validation, and small score differences do not determine clinical value. Defer GAM if support is inadequate rather than alter population/outcome.

A transformer or sequence model adds no established information for this single-MRI exposure and coarse retrospective outcome: it cannot provide event dates, validate reports or resolve selection. The matched GAM directly addresses relevant nonlinearity/heterogeneity. Future solver estimate is CPU 4, 16 GiB, <=7,200 seconds for chunked raw read, low-dimensional fits and bootstrap; this is unmeasured planning, not a discovery result. ## Falsification and claims
