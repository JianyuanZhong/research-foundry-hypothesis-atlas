# Does UNC1062-associated FLS response depend on the macrophage subset?

## Question and falsifiable hypothesis

**Question:** In the 48-hour ex-vivo E-MTAB-8873 synovial tissue macrophage–fibroblast cultures, is UNC1062-pretreated MerTK/CD206-positive macrophage coculture associated with a lower FLS matrix/TGF-associated transcript score than its same-contributor untreated Mpos comparator, and is that pattern distinguishable from a nonspecific inhibitor-associated shift observed with Mneg macrophages?

**Untested hypothesis:** Among source-verified Mpos donor-contributor blocks, UNC1062-pretreated cocultures will show a lower prespecified matrix/remodeling-associated transcript score than the corresponding untreated Mpos cocultures. The primary score is the unweighted mean of log2(1 + RSEC molecules-per-cell) for gene symbols COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, FN1, MMP1, MMP2, MMP3, MMP13, TGFB1, TGFB3, TGFBI, TGFBR1, TGFBR2, TIMP1, TIMP2 and TIMP3, averaging multiple assay targets for the same symbol before scoring. A prespecified secondary inflammatory score uses IL1B, IL6, TNF, CCL2, CXCL1, CXCL2, CXCL8 and TNFAIP3. These scores describe measured transcripts only; they do not measure matrix deposition, TGF activity, macrophage polarization, or target engagement. The targeted panel includes only 445 measured features; absent genes are not zeros.

The primary prediction is a negative paired difference (inhibitor-pretreated minus untreated) for the matrix/remodeling score in each verified Mpos contributor block. An increase in the inflammatory score is a secondary prediction, not required for support. A one-pair Mpos-versus-Mneg difference-in-differences in R1 is a mechanistic alternative/sensitivity only: it asks whether the inhibitor-associated FLS transcript contrast appears state-selective rather than generic, but cannot identify MERTK-specific causality.

## Evidence, strongest supported claim, and unresolved claim

Prior human synovial studies establish that macrophage subsets vary across inflammatory/resolution contexts and can influence FLS phenotypes. Alivernini et al. describe MerTK-positive macrophage subsets associated with remission and FLS repair responses [K1]. Kuo et al. demonstrate a distinct HBEGF-positive macrophage-to-FLS invasive pathway and show that drug-associated macrophage-state changes need not represent restored resolution [K2]. Zhang et al. document diverse inflammatory cell states in RA synovium, constraining generalization from a small targeted ex-vivo assay [K3]. These works were inspected at the abstract/metadata level only; this proposal does not claim review of their complete methods or supplementary materials.

**Strongest evidence-supported claim:** RA synovial macrophage states can be associated with, and in some studied contexts experimentally influence, fibroblast behavior; cell-state-specific response and unintended state shifts are consequential possibilities. **Unresolved claim tested here:** whether UNC1062 pretreatment in this specific Mpos coculture is associated with a coherent change in the measured FLS matrix/remodeling transcript profile beyond the paired untreated condition, and whether the limited Mneg inhibitor contrast points to generic rather than Mpos-associated change. Neither effect is established by the references or by this unrun experiment.

The substantive advance is a bound perturbation-response check of the repair-versus-inflammatory interpretation, with exact assayed features, paired donor contributions, and a nonspecific-drug rival made explicit. It is not a novel ligand-receptor discovery or a clinical treatment test. Strong rivals include compound carryover directly affecting FLS, vehicle/wash differences, viability, donor/culture/run effects, imperfect Mpos/Mneg sorting, and measurement of transcript abundance without a functional FLS endpoint.

## Computable analysis and alternatives

### Selected simple baseline

Before examining paired differences, map the 16 matrix/remodeling and 8 inflammatory gene-symbol sets above to exact assay `feature` IDs. Retain transcript/amplicon identifiers; collapse multiple targets per symbol by averaging on the log2(1+RSEC) scale so COL1A1 does not receive extra weight. Report coverage and exclude no unmeasured feature as a zero. For each source-verified Mpos block, compute the two prespecified score differences and feature-level differences. Show each block separately, alongside leave-one-block-out direction checks; no cell-level tests, ordinary donor t-test, asymptotic p-value, or bootstrap CI is justified by two or three potentially overlapping contributors. RSEC abundance is already per-cell; no bulk library-size normalization is applied. Report sensitivity to a prespecified pseudocount 0.5 versus 1 and to exclusion of the unresolved R1 block.

### Substantive alternative: state-by-inhibitor interaction

Using the R1 same-contributor Mpos and Mneg untreated/inhibitor conditions only if sample identity and treatment are source-verified, compute for each score the difference-in-differences: `(Mpos inhibitor − Mpos untreated) − (Mneg inhibitor − Mneg untreated)`. This alternative asks whether the inhibitor-associated profile change differs by macrophage subset, information the Mpos-only baseline necessarily loses. It is not a robust fitted interaction: one block cannot validate a state-specific effect, Mneg biology differs, and residual drug/culture effects remain. Do not estimate a flexible high-dimensional interaction model or claim held-out generalization. Compare the interaction sign and score coherence to the baseline; if it is absent or opposite, treat state specificity as unsupported even if the Mpos-only contrast is negative.

The baseline is selected for the primary deliverable because it directly estimates the narrow paired association with fewer parameters and preserves exact block-level transparency. The interaction is a scientifically meaningful, prespecified but low-evidence alternative; it is exploratory rather than a method-complexity bonus. GPU/neural modeling is not useful for 33 sample profiles and a 445-feature targeted panel. A future solver estimate is under two CPU-hours, <8 GiB RAM, with a bounded table read and score calculation; this is an unmeasured planning estimate, not a completed run.

## Results that would change the next decision

- **Supportive:** all source-verified Mpos pairs show a lower matrix/remodeling score, the direction survives exclusion of R1 and pseudocount sensitivity, and the Mneg interaction does not show an equally large generic reduction. This supports only a consistent inhibitor-associated transcript pattern in these measured cultures and justifies an independently donor-balanced mechanistic experiment.
- **Adverse:** verified Mpos pairs consistently show no reduction or the opposite direction with adequate panel coverage, or the Mpos-only pattern is equally present in Mneg so subset-specific response is unsupported. This weakens the narrow transcriptional hypothesis, not the broader biology of MerTK-positive macrophages.
- **Inconclusive:** sample/protocol mapping or inhibitor washout/vehicle handling remains unresolved; contributors are too few/overlapping; signs disagree; feature coverage is poor; or the interaction is unstable. A noisy null with two or three linked blocks is not evidence of no effect.

The immediate decision is whether to prioritize a donor-balanced follow-up, revise the assay hypothesis, or defer pending source adjudication—not a patient treatment choice. Before a mechanistic conclusion, assay experts must reconcile the source sample map and technical repeats, verify compound concentration/wash/vehicle and well allocation, and establish which cells were measured. Stronger evidence requires a new independent donor-balanced co-culture with vehicle and inhibitor-only/FLS controls, verified washout, macrophage/FLS viability, MERTK target engagement plus orthogonal perturbation/rescue, and protein or functional matrix/invasion outcome. Clinical efficacy requires a separate in-vivo or clinical study.

Computationally checkable claims are sample/feature joins, score calculations, paired signs, sensitivity analyses, and uncertainty boundaries. Drug-specific MERTK attribution, carryover, cell purity, functional repair, clinical relevance, and source study patient assignment require expert adjudication or additional experiments; no automated verifier can establish these conclusions.
