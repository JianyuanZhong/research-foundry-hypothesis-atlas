## Scientific question and substantive repair

The prior child asks whether prior-panel information improves pathway-specific fixed-capacity lists. Its separate cardiovascular, renal and metabolic top-10% lists do not implement one finite resource: the same participant may occupy two or three slots, and the sum of domain captures has no single review-workload or yield interpretation.

This successor tests the clinically consequential question:

> After a repeat assessment, does retaining the earlier biomarker panel improve allocation of one fixed review budget across cardiovascular, renal and metabolic domains, compared with current values plus the repeat interval alone?

The primary hypothesis is that prior history changes the optimal domain assignment and produces a clinically material increase in weighted capture of subsequently recorded target-domain events under a shared one-person/one-review budget. The hypothesis is direction-free before analysis: prior history may improve, have no material effect, or worsen the allocation.

The substantive advance is an explicit policy and utility estimand. It resolves the overlap ambiguity without treating a model score or a domain-specific Brier change as a clinical decision. It also tests whether history is useful for cross-domain prioritization, where information valuable for one pathway competes for the same finite review slot.

## Evidence-supported versus untested claims

The local export and catalog support the presence of one-row-per-`eid` structured tables, repeated field-instance columns, assessment date fields, paired diagnosis-code/date arrays, and death-date fields. A prior local header audit recorded the required columns and unique headers in the five bound files. The UKB metadata explicitly warns that field-instance indices are not elapsed time, most labels/units/missing-value meanings are unavailable, and negative numeric codes must not be globally recoded.

The evidence does not yet support claims about analyte identity, units, specimen timing, disease onset, diagnosis validity, severity, complete follow-up, or whether a review changes care. The experiment tests incremental prediction and allocation conditional on certified data semantics. It cannot establish a causal benefit of repeat testing or review, a treatment effect, biological mechanism, or a deployable clinical threshold.

## Shared finite-capacity policy and estimand

Let (D={C,R,M}), (N) be the eligible paired-cohort size, and (K=lfloor0.10N
floor), fixed from eligibility before model fitting and outcome evaluation. A policy assigns each participant at most one action (A_iin{0,C,R,M}), where 0 means no review, subject to (sum_i1(A_i
e0)=K). Thus the total unique review workload is exactly K, regardless of overlap in domain risk.

For participant (i), let (S_isubseteq D) be the set of domains represented by the primary first-event category (a singleton, a same-day joint subset, or empty for death/no record). Use primary equal domain weights (w_C=w_R=w_M=1), fixed before outcome inspection. The weighted shared-budget utility is

[
U_K(pi;w)=N^{-1}sum_{i=1}^Nsum_{din D}w_d,1{A_i^pi=d},1{din S_i}.
]

Because one participant receives only one assignment, a joint event yields at most one captured domain under this operational policy. The principal estimand is the paired policy contrast

[
Delta U_K = U_K(pi_{mathrm{prior}};w)-U_K(pi_{mathrm{current}};w),
]

with the companion per-review yield contrast (NDelta U_K/K). These are recorded-event capture estimands conditional on the selected cohort, not causal effects of review. Report domain-specific capture, assignment counts, event yield, and false-review proportion as secondary decomposition of this single utility. Sensitivity weights (for example (w=(2,1,1))) are descriptive scenarios only unless clinical experts prespecify and justify them before analysis.

The policy score is derived from held-out estimates (hat p_{id}=P(din S_imid X_i)). For each participant, choose (d_i^*=argmax_d w_dhat p_{id}), breaking ties deterministically by C then R then M, and define (s_i=max_d w_dhat p_{id}). Select the K participants with largest (s_i), assigning (d_i^*). This is equivalent to selecting participant-domain pairs under a no-duplicate-participant constraint when there are no domain quotas. The solver must freeze the policy rule and tie handling before reading held-out outcomes. The old three independent domain top-10% lists may be generated only as a diagnostic ledger showing overlap and the implied unique workload; they are not a primary result.

The primary global null is (Delta U_K=0). A planning margin of 0.01 utility units per eligible participant is not a validated clinical-benefit threshold. Supportive evidence requires all semantic/timing/ascertainment gates, a two-sided 95% interval whose lower bound is at least +0.01, no integrity or leakage failure, and no material calibration or safety deterioration. Adverse evidence is an upper 95% bound at or below -0.01, or a clear loss of utility accompanied by material calibration degradation. An estimate near zero, an interval spanning the practical margin, or a failed gate is inconclusive for clinical utility. A statistically nonzero effect smaller than the margin is reported as detectable but not clinically material under this design.

## Uncertainty and falsification

Use 2,000 paired participant-level bootstrap replicates, preserving each participant’s paired held-out predictions and outcomes, recomputing the shared-budget policy at the prespecified K within each replicate. Stratify resampling by outer fold and primary outcome category where feasible. State explicitly whether bootstrap intervals condition on fitted outer models; if so, they omit training uncertainty. Report fold-specific estimates, bootstrap distributions, event counts, assignment shares and 95% intervals. The temporal-transport estimate uses a test-cohort bootstrap and does not refit the historical training model.

Required falsification and integrity checks are:

- source/schema hashes, exact headers, row counts, unique `eid` keys, join coverage and overlap agreement;
- eligibility flow, T0/T1 parsing, code/date position matching, duplicate-date handling, death precedence and endpoint flow;
- outcome-category permutation within training/evaluation strata and prior-panel permutation within current-risk, interval and repeat-selection strata, preserving current values, missingness and selection;
- a deliberate code/date position shift and label/date permutation, both expected to fail the pairing or validity audit;
- a negative-control pre-T1 recorded endpoint frozen before examining post-T1 results;
- complete-case versus fold-local missingness sensitivity, and raw-prior versus raw-change sensitivity where permitted;
- leakage scans for dates, outcomes, imputation, calibration, selection weights and policy construction;
- fold stability, calibration stability, assignment-share stability and temporal drift diagnostics.

Feature permutations should destroy any claimed incremental history signal while leaving the current baseline’s appropriate structure intact. These tests check computation and leakage, not causality. A Brier improvement without positive shared-budget utility is adverse or null for the stated decision. A positive utility estimate that disappears under supported repeat-selection weighting, has poor positivity, or fails temporal transport is not evidence for population-wide policy benefit.

## Alternatives, deliverable and compute boundary

The selected design compares a transparent nested baseline and a same-input learned alternative on the same one-budget question. M1a/M1b is selected as the primary scientific contrast because it isolates prior values from current values and repeat spacing, has interpretable participant/domain assignments, and can expose the consequence of allocating a scarce review slot across competing domains. M2 is retained because nonlinear prior-current discordance and interval interactions may alter cross-domain assignment even when average additive effects are small. Both use the same target, split, K, utility, calibration and uncertainty evaluation.

A dated disease-history transformer is deferred: the local UKB export contains field-instance arrays but no certified dated disease sequence or complete event chronology, so it cannot establish that a learned ordering represents disease history. A mechanistic organ-reserve model is deferred because analyte identities, units, specimen dates, assay quality and organ mappings are not certified. Imaging, proteomics, CHIP, sequence/PRS and cancer-stage branches are deferred because the catalog provides no verified raw imaging, sequence-variant bundle, validated staging chronology or required modality semantics. These alternatives should be revisited only when the named dependency is supplied; complexity, GPU availability and small predictive gains are not reasons to substitute them.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted fold-safe M0, M1a, M1b, optional M1c and M2 models; pooled held-out predictions; the frozen K and assignment rule; one-budget policy ledgers for both policies; (Delta U_K) and per-review yield with uncertainty; domain and overlap diagnostics; calibration/safety metrics; repeat-selection and temporal-transport outputs; falsification logs; and an interpretation linked to those files. A complete fail-closed gate certificate is an acceptable computational deliverable if semantics or ascertainment cannot be certified; it is not a positive or negative clinical result.

The planned future solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 256 GiB RAM and 8 hours for five-fold regularized multinomial/boosted tabular fitting, calibration, policy ledgers, 2,000 bootstrap scoring and sensitivities. This estimate is unverified; no full fit is claimed here. The task is expected to be CPU-suitable because the inputs are tabular and the proposed alternatives are bounded boosted/multinomial fits. GPU use is deferred unless an allocated benchmark demonstrates material benefit; the configured A100 capacity is not treated as either required or disqualifying.

## Interpretation limits

Computationally checkable claims include source integrity, cohort flow, field presence, endpoint implementation conditional on the certified codebook, competing-event arithmetic, exact one-budget enforcement, held-out predictions, utility contrasts, uncertainty, calibration, weighting diagnostics, leakage and permutation behavior.

Clinical adjudication or additional evidence is required for the meaning of codes, disease onset, specimen timing, assay validity, complete ascertainment, event severity, whether a domain assignment merits review, domain utility weights, review harms/costs, acceptable action thresholds and whether review changes outcomes. External validation in a population with different repeat-assessment selection and a prospective randomized or carefully designed policy study are required before recommending repeat-testing schedules, pathway referral, treatment, or deployment. No conclusion about clinical benefit may be made from this UKB computation alone.
