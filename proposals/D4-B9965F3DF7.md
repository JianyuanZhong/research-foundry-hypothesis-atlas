# Prior biomarker history beyond the later panel: a selection-aware UKB risk-triage experiment

Parent: [prior hypothesis]  
Status: proposed executable child; no model has been fitted and no clinical conclusion is claimed.

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must newly estimate, with held-out predictions and participant-level uncertainty intervals, whether adding the earlier eight-field biomarker panel to the later panel improves five-year prediction of a prespecified recorded cardiorenal/metabolic outcome in a defined repeat-panel population. It must also quantify whether repeat-panel eligibility is predictable from the later panel and measured covariates, re-estimate the performance contrast with fold-local selection weights, and report whether the conclusion is conditional on repeat attendance. Completion is either these estimates with all timing/ascertainment gates documented, or a fail-closed audit showing that the relevant estimand is not estimable. No result is implied here.

## Scientific question, importance and advance

The unresolved question is:

> Among people who have a valid later panel and a valid earlier panel, does the earlier biomarker history improve prospective five-year risk triage beyond the later panel and routine covariates, particularly among people whose later-panel risk is neither low nor high; and is that incremental value robust to the fact that repeat-panel availability is selected?

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is that the history model M1 has a lower held-out Brier score and higher research net benefit than the later-panel baseline M0 in the prespecified middle (20th–80th percentile) of M0 risk, with the contrast not reversing across short, medium and long assessment intervals. The selection-weighted sensitivity tests whether that contrast changes materially after reweighting the complete-repeat cohort toward the measured covariate distribution of all eligible later-panel participants. This is a prognostic-information hypothesis, not a causal effect of repeat testing, monitoring, treatment or attendance.

This advances the inherited population-average comparison by separating two questions that are otherwise confounded: whether history is useful when available, and whether the people for whom history is available differ in observed current risk and demographics. A null result would be clinically useful because current values may suffice for the tested recorded-outcome task; an unstable or selection-sensitive result would warn against turning repeat-panel associations into a triage policy. The study cannot establish an optimal retesting interval or benefit of a monitoring program.

The strongest locally supportable fallback is prediction of a future recorded category in this export, conditional on the assessment landmark and available records. A prospective incidence interpretation is permitted only after the gates below pass.

## Population, repeat-selection estimand and timing

Construct horizontal one-to-one joins by eid from population to main, assessment, biological_samples and health_outcomes; fail closed on duplicate keys, missing join anchors or disagreement.

Define the later-panel eligibility cohort C1 as participants with valid approved age and sex, valid T1=53-1.0, T1 <= 2013-12-31, and all eight instance-1 fields numeric and valid under the supplied field dictionary. If the dictionary is unavailable, the validity gate fails; do not globally recode negative values. C1 is the population to which the measured repeat-selection audit refers.

Define S=1, the primary repeat-panel cohort, by additionally requiring valid T0=53-0.0, T0<T1, all eight instance-0 fields valid, and 0.25 < L <= 5.0; define short, medium and long intervals as (0.25,1], (1,3], and (3,5] years. Impossible or out-of-range intervals are excluded, not clipped. Keep the wider (0.25,10] interval only as a predeclared sensitivity and report dilution.

Before the outcome horizon, remove participants with a qualifying target-domain record on or before T1. Call this “no recorded prior target,” never disease-free. For every participant retain instance-1 values x1, instance-0 values x0, raw changes x1-x0, and annualized changes (x1-x0)/L; the primary history representation is raw change because specimen timing is unverified and annualization can amplify interval error.

The primary estimand is conditional incremental prediction in S=1. To expose repeat-assessment selection, report the C1-versus-S=1 flow and compare current-panel distributions, age, sex, T1 year, interval availability and prior-record prevalence. Fit a fold-local selection model Pr(S=1 | x1, age, sex, T1 year) within C1 using only variables available before the outcome. Apply stabilized inverse-probability weights to S=1 held-out metrics, with no truncation in the primary sensitivity and 1st/99th percentile truncation as a robustness sensitivity; report weight range, effective sample size and positivity. If selection positivity is poor or the weighted effective sample is below 50% of S=1, label the transport sensitivity inconclusive rather than tune weights until it passes. This weighting addresses measured selection only; it cannot correct unmeasured invitation, health-care access, illness or assay selection.

No M1 prediction is imputed for S=0. Therefore the weighted analysis is a transport sensitivity for the incremental contrast among repeat-panel participants, not evidence that a person without a prior panel can be triaged using history.

## Matched models and substantive alternative

Use the same participants, outcome, preprocessing, deterministic five-fold split and held-out evaluation for every model.

- M0 is the transparent later-panel baseline: the eight instance-1 fields, approved age, sex, T1 calendar year and L. L is included in all arms as assessment-design context, not called a biomarker history feature. If Gate A fails, rename the panel “instance-1” throughout. M0’s held-out target probability defines all risk strata.
- M1 is the primary transparent history model: M0 plus the eight raw changes. Fit multinomial logistic regression with fold-local imputation, standardization and L2 penalty; select C from {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} by inner four-fold training loss only. The annualized-change model is a fixed sensitivity, not a replacement.
- M2 is the substantive learned alternative: the same M0/M1 inputs, fitted as HistGradientBoostingClassifier for the three-category outcome, with fold-local selection from max_iter in {100,300}, learning_rate in {0.03,0.1}, max_leaf_nodes in {7,15}, and l2_regularization in {0,1} using inner folds. It can reveal thresholds, current/history discordance and interval-dependent interactions that additive M1 loses. M2 is a model-form diagnostic; a small predictive gain alone cannot establish clinical superiority.

The prospective and recorded modes use the same fixed-horizon three-category estimand. If prospective censoring with valid individual end dates is supplied instead of complete ascertainment, the solver must predeclare and fit censoring weights within training folds; otherwise it must use recorded mode. Death remains a competing category, not an IPCW censor.

## Decision-stratified analysis and uncertainty

Use participant-level folds defined by int(first_8_hex_digits(SHA256(str(eid)))) mod 5, and publish the fold manifest. For each outer fold, derive M0 20th and 80th target-risk cut points from training-only cross-fitted M0 predictions; apply them to held-out M0 predictions to form lower, middle and upper bands. The primary interaction is the M1-minus-M0 Brier contrast in the middle band, by interval regime. The principal heterogeneity contrast is middle-band minus the pooled tails; interval modification is the difference across short/medium/long regimes.

Report overall and stratified multiclass Brier score, target-specific Brier score, calibration intercept/slope or multiclass calibration curves, target/death/none counts, and discrimination secondarily. Report decision-curve net-benefit contrasts at 0.05 and 0.10 as research thresholds only, plus M0-to-M1 movement across those thresholds in the middle band. Report M2-versus-M1 only as a nonlinear-pattern check. Use 1,000 participant-level bootstrap replicates over held-out predictions with folds preserved; for weighted results bootstrap the repeat participants and recompute fixed fold-local weights. Report 95% percentile intervals, fold-specific estimates, effective sample size and missingness.

Support for the hypothesis requires: Gate C and D at minimum for a recorded-domain analysis; no integrity failure; at least 100 target events overall and prespecified minimum 50 participants in every interpreted interval-by-risk cell; middle-band M1-minus-M0 Brier 95% upper bound below zero; no reversal across interval regimes; no material calibration degradation; and positive net-benefit contrast at both thresholds. For a prospective interpretation, Gates A and B must also pass. These are evidentiary rules, not proof of clinical benefit. Failure to meet them is adverse or inconclusive as specified below, not an invitation to pool sparse cells.

## Falsification and integrity criteria

The solver must run and retain:

- source/schema hash, header, row-count, key-uniqueness, join-coverage, overlap-agreement and parse-failure audits;
- participant-level fold audit and leakage check;
- a post-T1 leakage audit proving no outcome code/date or future field enters features, selection weights or M0 cut points;
- repeat-selection model diagnostics, positivity, weight sensitivity and C1-versus-S flow;
- permutation of prior-panel histories within fixed M0-risk and interval strata, preserving current values, L, missingness and outcomes;
- permutation of outcome labels and date arrays as separate null controls;
- raw versus annualized changes, interval-bound, same-day-tie and confirmed-domain sensitivities;
- a date/code-position break test and a date reassignment test, neither of which may improve the prespecified primary contrast;
- complete-case versus fold-local imputation sensitivity and outer-fold stability;
- subgroup counts, calibration and uncertainty, not discrimination alone.

Supportive results mean only that history adds reproducible predictive information for the specified recorded or certified prospective endpoint in the analyzed repeat-panel population, with measured-selection weighting not changing the direction. Adverse results are a null or worse M1 middle-band contrast, calibration harm, reversal by interval, disappearance under history permutation or code/date integrity controls, or a materially different conclusion after measured-selection weighting. Inconclusive results include failed dictionary/timing/ascertainment gates, fewer than 100 events, sparse cells, poor positivity, low weighted effective sample, unstable folds, incompatible endpoint or missingness sensitivities, or no reliable endpoint semantics.

No result supports a causal effect of repeat testing, disease onset, disease-free status, optimal interval, monitoring benefit, treatment recommendation, biological mechanism, or performance in people without a prior panel. Clinical adjudication, verified specimen and assay metadata, complete individual follow-up, treatment/context data, external validation and a prospective policy study would be required for those stronger claims.

## Alternative-method record and resource plan

The simple scientific baseline is M0 with regularized multinomial regression; it answers whether later values and routine covariates are sufficient. M1 adds interpretable paired differences and is the primary test because its coefficients and raw/annualized sensitivity make the history claim inspectable. M2 was retained as a substantive alternative, not a leaderboard model, because nonlinear current-by-history discordance and thresholds could be clinically informative even when an additive average change is small. A mechanistic organ score was deferred: local labels, units, specimen times and validity ranges are not verified, so assigning biological directions would invent information. A transformer/longitudinal generative model was also deferred because only two unverified assessment-linked instances are available for this question; it becomes revisit-able with more reliable dated episodes or additional repeated panels. The explicit evidence for revisiting either branch is an approved dictionary plus specimen metadata and sufficient repeated measurements, not a small AUC gain.

Direct header/schema inspection and the earlier selected-column availability counts are measured. Model runtime, event support, selection positivity and bootstrap duration are unverified until fitting. The future solver envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM and 28,800 seconds; this tabular five-fold nested fitting and 1,000-replicate bootstrap is expected to fit on CPU after selected-column ingestion, but the estimate is not a measured runtime. The deployment has A100-SXM4 80-GB GPUs available through managed jobs, but this design has no GPU requirement and GPU use is not evidence of scientific value.
