clinical_question_and_importance:
  question: >-
    Among selected UK Biobank repeat attenders aged 40–75 who have paired protocolized CBCs and
    remain alive, registry-observable and malignant-cancer-free to 366 days after their second
    assessment, do annualized falls in haemoglobin and MCV and rise in platelet count add
    prognostic information for first registry-recorded colorectal cancer during days 366–1825,
    beyond age, sex and current CBC values?
  importance: >-
    If a sparse protocolized trajectory reliably enriches future CRC events at a fixed statistical
    review capacity, it would justify external validation in clinically timed CBC data and then a
    prospective FIT/referral impact study. A precise null, opposite-direction result, or general-
    cancer rather than CRC-specific signal would discourage acting on sparse UKB trends. This
    experiment cannot establish screening, referral, FIT, colonoscopy, stage, mortality, harms,
    cost-effectiveness or individual clinical benefit.
  strongest_supported_claim: >-
    The inspected cancer-demonstration supplement reports longitudinal routine-data cancer
    prediction with a 90-day washout and performance declining as more prediagnostic data are
    removed; its main article and STAR Methods were unavailable and are not claimed as inspected.
    Predecessor work inspected BLOODTRACC full text and reports external validation of clinically
    ordered longitudinal haemoglobin, MCV and platelet histories for two-year CRC risk. A frozen
    predecessor full-source scan supports feasibility in the stricter four-marker cohort: 20,343
    instance-1 dates, 18,379 paired four-marker CBCs, 1,801 prior malignant cancers, 16,578
    preliminary cancer-free landmarks, 81 CRC diagnoses in days 366–1825 and 40 in days 366–1095.
    These facts do not establish incremental value in sparse protocolized repeats.
  unresolved_hypothesis: >-
    The three prespecified direction-coded annualized CBC changes jointly add information beyond
    landmark values for first CRC after delayed entry, and the incremental contrast is greater for
    CRC than for first non-CRC malignant cancer on the same people, horizon and resamples.

  supportive: >-
    Evidence supports incremental prognosis only if the prespecified joint slope contrast is in the
    adverse direction and excludes the null with uncertainty quantified, the paired out-of-fold
    five-year prediction increment is positive without worse Brier/calibration, and estimates are
    not explained by leakage checks. CRC specificity additionally requires paired D>0 with an
    interval excluding 0 and compatible Brier findings. Capacity yields remain descriptive because
    no justified clinical minimum exists. The strongest claim is prognostic enrichment among
    selected repeat attenders.
  adverse: >-
    A sufficiently precise joint null or opposite-direction slope pattern together with no positive
    out-of-fold increment, or a paired D at or below zero with adequate precision, is adverse to the
    sparse-trajectory hypothesis. It does not refute clinically ordered longitudinal CBC models.
  inconclusive: >-
    Unverified field semantics, no export-linked coverage endpoint, fewer than 30 valid CRC events,
    broad intervals spanning consequential positive and null effects, unstable censoring weights or
    resamples, discordant discrimination/calibration/Brier results, or underpowered non-CRC contrast
    is inconclusive. A nonsignificant but imprecise estimate is never called adverse.
