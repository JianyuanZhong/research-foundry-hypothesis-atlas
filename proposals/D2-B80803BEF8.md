# Donor-aware protective-versus-pathogenic target selectivity

Seed: D2-T2. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Does an intervention-associated transcript pattern preferentially suppress a pathogenic fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state, such that a target ranked from an averaged signal is misleading? This matters because macrophage and fibroblast programs can have opposing roles in fibrotic or inflammatory disease: an intervention-associated change in one compartment may reflect a protective response, a pathogenic program, or simply differential cell composition and stimulation. Resolving the question would change whether a target is pursued as context-specific, requires a cell-type-resolved intervention, or is deprioritized.

## Strongest currently supported claim

The strongest current evidence is descriptive: the configured datasets contain macrophage/fibroblast-related conditions and intervention-associated transcript changes. The datasets are available as prepared counts and design metadata. The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that these datasets contain the necessary conditions and transcript measurements to test whether an intervention-associated transcript pattern can separate protective macrophage-linked and pathogenic fibroblast states.

## Unresolved hypothesis

The unresolved claim is whether a protective macrophage-linked state and a pathogenic fibroblast program can be separated by an intervention-associated transcript response without overinterpreting expression as direct target action. Specifically, we test whether a target ranked from an averaged signal is misleading because it does not account for the opposing roles of macrophage and fibroblast programs.

## Donor-aware uncertainty

The analysis uses a donor-aware linear mixed model with donor random effects and condition fixed effects. The uncertainty is quantified using the donor-aware model's standard errors and confidence intervals. The donor-aware model accounts for nested-donor dependence and prevents overfitting to individual donors.

## Controls

The controls include vehicle/unstimulated and condition controls where actually present. The analysis also includes a permutation test to assess the significance of the separation between protective and pathogenic states.

## Competing explanations

The competing explanations are differential cell composition, stimulation, and measurement artifact. The analysis tests whether the separation is driven by these factors by including them as covariates and by using a permutation test.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if the intervention-associated pattern does not separate protective and pathogenic states, or if the separation is driven by differential cell composition or stimulation.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive outcomes

- Supportive: the intervention-associated pattern separates protective and pathogenic states.
- Adverse: the pattern does not separate the states, or the separation is driven by artifact.
- Inconclusive: the pattern is ambiguous or the data are insufficient.

## Method comparison

The donor-aware linear mixed model baseline is simple and interpretable. The regularized multivariate alternative could reveal nonlinear or high-dimensional patterns that the baseline loses. The baseline is selected for its interpretability and the alternative is deferred for future work.
