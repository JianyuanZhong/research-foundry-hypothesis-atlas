# Hospital-transportable modification of the observed hypotension–creatinine signal by documented vasopressor timing

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct an auditable eICU cohort and interval ledger, fit matched hospital-aware competing-state models and an ordered observation/transition model, and emit uncertainty-quantified estimates of:

1. the 0–6-hour low-MAP-burden gradient for a 6–48-hour observed creatinine-rise signal within each documented vasopressor-timing category;
2. the prompt-versus-delayed/no timing difference in that gradient within hospitals and after standardization across hospitals;
3. the extent to which between-hospital variation in that difference is attenuated when creatinine test opportunity and ICU exit are represented explicitly; and
4. whole-hospital held-out transport, calibration and prespecified falsification sensitivities.

No model has been fitted and no clinical conclusion is claimed in discovery.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported before fitting is limited to data availability: the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative MAP observations, ICU-keyed laboratory results and revision offsets, named infusion rows with offsets, ICU disposition, admission descriptors and hospital identifiers that can be joined by the specified keys. A source/header audit found 200,859 patient rows and 208 hospital rows; a bounded first-200,000-row audit found all five target-agent name families and creatinine rows with mg/dL in both unit fields. These are availability checks, not evidence that a vasopressor was administered, that hypotension caused kidney injury, or that the signal transports.

The unresolved claim is whether documented rescue timing changes the prognostic interpretation of hypotension in a way that is reproducible across hospital practice contexts, or whether apparent differences are primarily artifacts of laboratory ascertainment and competing ICU exit.

> Among adult ICU stays with a creatinine measured before the first observed low MAP, does the association between 0–6-hour low-MAP burden and a 6–48-hour observed creatinine-rise signal differ between documented prompt-after-low, delayed-after-low, pre-low/concurrent and no-documented-target-pressor patterns within hospitals, and does that difference remain transportable after explicit modeling of test opportunity and death/alive ICU exit?

Primary directional hypothesis: conditional on the same minimum MAP, pre-low creatinine, MAP coverage/trajectory, measured admission risk and hospital, prompt documentation of a target vasopressor within 30 minutes after the first observed MAP <65 is associated with attenuation of the burden gradient relative to delayed/no documentation. This is a noncausal, recorded-process hypothesis. The directional claim is falsified by a reproducible null, reversal or amplification, or by loss of the interaction in held-out hospitals or after process decomposition. A reversal is not evidence that vasopressors cause renal injury.

The hospital-heterogeneity hypothesis is that the prompt-versus-delayed/no contrast is not merely a hospital label: it persists within hospitals and transports with the same sign. The observation-process alternative is that hospital variation in the contrast materially attenuates when the model separately represents the chance of a qualifying creatinine test and the competing chances of unit death or alive discharge/transfer before testing. This would support ascertainment/exit dependence of a recorded endpoint, not a biological renal null.

Clinical margin: the primary prompt-after-low versus delayed/no difference in standardized 48-hour observed-rise CIF burden gradients must be at least 2 percentage points in absolute risk. This margin is a prespecified clinical-interpretation threshold, not a treatment-effect threshold.

Clinical importance: a scalar nadir or burden may be interpreted as a patient-intrinsic shock marker even when local rescue and measurement practices differ. Showing a stable timing-context interaction could identify when early hypotension signals should be interpreted cautiously and inform design of future prospective renal-monitoring or resuscitation studies. Showing stable burden gradients across timing categories and hospitals would support a more transportable prognostic descriptor. Neither result alone justifies changing vasopressors or MAP targets.

Use the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot [source checksum]. The unit is an ICU stay keyed by patientunitstayid; repeated ICU stays are retained. All stays sharing uniquepid remain in one split, with a uniquepid-clustered sensitivity. Time zero is ICU admission; all offsets are integer minutes relative to ICU admission.

The first qualifying creatinine is the smallest labresultoffset in [0,60] satisfying the frozen label/unit rule, not the minimum value. Deduplicate exact labid rows; if multiple valid rows remain at the selected offset, use their median and report the count. Exclude urinary creatinine, invalid numeric values and unknown/incompatible units; do not convert unverified units. The broader cohort without a pre-low baseline is a mandatory sensitivity and is labelled post-exposure-baseline selection. Stays without a low MAP form a descriptive companion cohort but do not enter the timing interaction.

The 0–6-hour MAP ledger uses raw periodic observations. Collapse duplicate stay/time rows by prespecified median. For the primary burden, linearly bridge gaps <=30 minutes only, retain observed and bridged minutes, all unbridged gaps and coverage indicators, and calculate:

- primary: trapezoidal area-under-65 in mmHg-min;
- secondary: observed minutes below 65, longest low-MAP run, low-episode count, observed-coverage fraction below 65, recovery slope and minimum observed MAP;
- non-burden trajectory features: early MAP level, slope, persistence and recovery indicators.

The first-low time and treatment category always use raw observations and are unchanged by interpolation. Minimum MAP and the non-burden trajectory enter every model so the burden contrast is not a disguised nadir comparison.

## Documented timing exposure

Define the first documented target-pressor record as the smallest infusionDrug.infusionoffset in [0,360] whose case-insensitive drugname matches a frozen name map for norepinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine or epinephrine. This is a record timestamp, not verified administration onset, order time, dose, indication or treatment intent.

Mutually exclusive categories:

1. pre-low/concurrent: first matching record <= first raw low-MAP time;
2. prompt-after-low: first matching record strictly after low MAP and <=30 minutes later;
3. delayed-after-low: first matching record >30 minutes after low MAP and <=360 minutes;
4. no documented target-pressor by 360: no matching record by 360 minutes.

A same-minute MAP and infusion record is pre-low/concurrent. Report agent-family indicators and the number of distinct families descriptively. Do not infer intensity from drugrate, infusionrate, drugamount, volumeoffluid or patientweight; these fields remain available only for an explicitly labelled positive-rate-record sensitivity and are never interpreted as dose.

## Outcome, observation process and competing exit

For each eligible stay define end_i=min(2880, unitdischargeoffset_i). Follow-up starts at minute 360 and ends at 2,880 minutes or the ICU-unit boundary. A post-landmark qualifying creatinine is observable only if labresultoffset >360, labresultoffset <= end_i, and it occurs before unit exit. A same-minute result and exit are assigned to exit.

The primary endpoint is the first qualifying result at least baseline +0.3 mg/dL. The terminal outcomes in 30-minute intervals are mutually exclusive:

- observed creatinine-rise signal;
- unit death before that signal, identified by unitdischargestatus="Expired" or unitdischargelocation="Death";
- alive unit discharge/transfer before that signal, using non-death status/location;
- no terminal event by 2,880 minutes while still within the observable unit boundary.

A qualifying test without a rise is not terminal. Each interval contains only information available before that interval: qualifying-test opportunity, test result if already observed, all-numeric-lab activity, time since last qualifying creatinine, time since any lab, MAP state and exit boundary. No future result can enter a predictor.

The process decomposition is prespecified:

- test-opportunity CIF: first qualifying post-landmark creatinine observation, regardless of rise;
- rise-given-test: recorded rise conditional on a qualifying test opportunity, reported as a conditional process contrast with uncertainty;
- death CIF and alive-exit CIF before a rise/test;
- observed-rise CIF as the primary recorded endpoint.

No process component is called mediation. Unit exit is an observation boundary and competing event, not assumed noninformative.

## Primary noncausal estimands and hospital transport

Let G_h,c be the standardized 48-hour observed-rise CIF difference between area-under-65 at development-set P75 and P25 within hospital h and timing category c, with the same prespecified distribution of baseline creatinine, minimum MAP, MAP coverage/trajectory, static covariates and admission-risk descriptors. The primary timing-context contrast is:

D_h = G_h,prompt-after-low - G_h,delayed-after-low/no-record

where delayed and no-record are reported separately and jointly only when overlap and support are adequate. The population estimand is the development-population average of D_h with hospital-level standardization; the transport estimand is the pooled standardized D_h evaluated in whole hospitals held out from fitting. Report both raw within-hospital estimates and shrinkage estimates; do not rank hospitals.

The primary hospital-transport criterion is all of:

- held-out pooled D_h has the same sign as development and absolute magnitude >=2 percentage points;
- at least 70% of held-out hospitals with >=50 primary-cohort stays have the same sign of their prespecified shrinkage estimate;
- timing-category support, burden overlap and event support meet the predeclared thresholds;
- calibration intercept/slope and Brier score do not materially deteriorate beyond prespecified tolerances; AUROC/AUPRC are secondary, never the scientific objective.

The main heterogeneity parameter is the between-hospital variance of D_h from a hierarchical model, with a 95% interval and a prediction interval for a new hospital. A large variance or poor sign consistency is evidence against transport, not a quality ranking. Hospital numbedscategory, teachingstatus and region are descriptive transport context; any association with D_h is exploratory and cannot establish staffing, preference or mechanism.

## Hospital split, models and matched alternatives

Assign hospitals to development (80%) and held-out transport (20%) before any fitting using a deterministic hash of hospitalid, stratified only to avoid empty timing strata where feasible. If a uniquepid occurs in multiple hospitals, replace individual hospital assignments with connected hospital components and report affected hospitals and coverage loss. No patient, uniquepid or hospital may cross splits. If the resulting held-out set has fewer than 10 hospitals or inadequate timing/event support, transport is inconclusive, not repaired by pooling.

All models use the same cohort, 30-minute intervals, raw event times, label/unit maps, outcome ordering, features, split, development-only preprocessing and standardization.

Transparent baseline:

- B0: minimum MAP, non-burden MAP trajectory/persistence/recovery, MAP coverage, pre-low baseline creatinine, age, sex, ethnicity, admission/unit source/type, time interval, hospital context, and admission-risk descriptors; no burden or timing.
- B1: B0 + area-under-65 burden.
- B2: B1 + four timing categories, target-agent family indicators and distinct-family count.
- B3: B2 + burden-by-timing interaction.
- B4: B3 + interval test-opportunity/history and observation-boundary features.
- B5: B4 without burden-by-timing interaction (the matched no-interaction null).
- B6: B4 with hospital-specific random slopes for the burden-by-timing contrast and a random intercept for each development hospital; report D_h, its variance and prediction interval. B6 is the primary hospital-heterogeneity fit, not a post hoc model choice.

Fit regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic models for observed rise, death and alive exit; fit the first-test process in parallel. Report coefficients, standardized CIFs, calibration, design-matrix condition numbers and convergence. The primary comparison is B6 versus the matched B5/B4 interaction restriction; the scientific result is the standardized D_h, not AUROC improvement. If random-slope variance is on a boundary or category overlap is poor, use the prespecified fixed-effects stratified estimate and label heterogeneity inconclusive rather than selecting a new model.

Mechanistic alternative: on the identical interval ledger, fit a piecewise-exponential multi-state model with hospital random intercepts/slopes for the same timing-context terms where estimable. States/transitions explicitly include first raw low MAP, first documented pressor record, subsequent recorded MAP recovery, first qualifying creatinine test without rise, observed rise, unit death and alive unit exit. It has separate observation intensity, conditional recorded-rise, death and alive-exit transitions and reports hospital-specific D_h decompositions. The alternative can reveal whether hospital heterogeneity is carried by the low-MAP-to-pressor record transition, subsequent recorded MAP recovery, testing opportunity, or competing exit. The transparent model retains the same inputs but collapses ordering into marginal hazards and loses this process decomposition. No state is latent physiology; no transition is treatment mediation.

Do not add a generic neural sequence model merely for predictive gain. A learned sequence branch is deferred unless both prespecified models leave reproducible, shape-specific residual miscalibration across held-out hospitals after process accounting. Revisit requires the same observed-rise/test/exit estimands, hospital-held-out split, calibration and uncertainty; a small discrimination gain alone is insufficient. A direct causal pressor study is deferred because the infusion table lacks validated administration/start semantics, universal dose units, indication/order and intent, while treatment is confounded by evolving illness.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support for the directional claim requires the B6 interaction and mechanistic model to agree in direction; the prompt-versus-delayed/no standardized contrast to be at least 2 points with uncertainty not compatible only with a trivial contrast; the effect to persist within hospitals and in held-out hospitals; and the sign/calibration to survive explicit test-opportunity and competing-exit modeling and the prespecified sensitivities.

Support for a hospital-practice-context effect requires nontrivial within-hospital support, a stable held-out sign, and hospital-level variation in D_h that is not explained away solely by sparse strata. It does not identify a causal practice effect or justify hospital rankings.

Support for an observation/exit explanation requires measurable between-hospital variation in test opportunity or exit, plus consistent attenuation of D_h and/or its variance when those processes are represented, with no corresponding claim about true AKI biology.

Adverse results include a reversed/amplified interaction, a null interaction, loss of within-hospital or held-out transport, or a burden gradient that is explained by test opportunity/exit. These do not prove treatment equivalence, pressor harm, or a biological renal null. A stable burden gradient with no timing modification is a valuable negative result about treatment-context heterogeneity.

Inconclusive results include fewer than 10 held-out hospitals, sparse timing or event support, no overlap, near-universal/absent pressor or testing documentation, unstable connected components, boundary random-effect variance, severe collinearity, nonconvergence, extreme weights, invalid label/unit/disposition maps, or intervals spanning the 2-point margin.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/headers, joins, filters, offsets, deduplication, label and agent maps, split integrity, ledger states, model fits, standardization, intervals, calibration, transport summaries and sensitivity reproducibility. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for actual vasopressor administration/start/indication/dose/intent, completeness of infusion records, intended MAP target, pre-ICU renal reserve, urine-output criteria, KDIGO AKI and whether an observed creatinine rise reflects true kidney injury. The design cannot establish that prompt pressor use helps or harms, that hypotension causes AKI, or that clinicians should change treatment.
