# HCC proposal: dynamic post-treatment response and recurrence risk

## Scientific question and importance
Among adults with HCC who receive a first documented locoregional treatment, does an early post-treatment response trajectory add prognostic information for subsequent recurrence/progression beyond baseline tumor burden, liver reserve, treatment type, and time since diagnosis? This matters because a favorable response could identify patients for closer surveillance or reconsideration of treatment, while a null result would discourage overinterpretation of response categories. The leading explanation is residual viable tumor or treatment-resistant biology; strong rivals are baseline burden, treatment selection/intensity, liver disease, differential surveillance, and de novo carcinogenesis. The test is prognostic, not causal.

## Evidence and unresolved claim
The inspected NTAA study found tumor number, tumor size, AFP and ALBI grade prognostic after TACE in a retrospective multicenter cohort, with internal, external and non-HBV validation [K1]. A companion editorial emphasizes that treatment heterogeneity, etiology and calibration limit generalization and clinical interpretation [K2]. A separate retrospective imaging study found high early recurrence after RFA-TACE and complementary CEUS/MRI features, but used expert integrated interpretation rather than a prespecified algorithm and requires prospective validation [K3]. These works support feasibility and motivate the question, but do not establish that a dynamic EHR-derived response trajectory adds information in this local routine-care cohort. That incremental claim is unresolved.

Recurrence requires dated post-response imaging or a diagnosis linked to a valid post-response encounter; history, postoperative status and orders alone are not outcomes. Require 180 days observable follow-up for the primary analysis; report exclusions and use all-follow-up inverse-probability-of-censoring as secondary. Primary estimands are 1- and 2-year risk differences and cause-specific hazard ratios by response category, adjusted prognostically.

## Models and evaluation
Baseline: penalized Cox or pooled logistic model using only pre-T0 variables (age, sex, treatment, tumor-burden proxy, liver disease, AFP, liver function, time since diagnosis), with missingness indicators. Alternative: patient-level gradient-boosted survival model adding response category and longitudinal summaries (AFP slope/level, liver-function change, imaging count/timing, residual-disease indicators) from T0 through the response window. Fit on earlier index years, tune on a middle validation year, evaluate on held-out later years; never split by row. Report integrated Brier score, time-dependent AUC, calibration intercept/slope, risk differences, and bootstrap intervals. The alternative tests whether temporal response information adds what baseline loses; it is not chosen for complexity. A latent residual-versus-de-novo mechanistic model is deferred because local data cannot distinguish etiologies without validated imaging adjudication, pathology/genomics/ctDNA. CPU is appropriate; GPU is not needed. Planned solver envelope: 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, 2 GPUs, 28800 seconds; no model has been fitted.

## Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: response remains associated after adjustment and the response-augmented model improves held-out calibration/Brier score with stable direction across treatment strata and calendar periods. Adverse: association disappears after adjustment, is confined to one modality, reverses across periods, or the alternative is worse than baseline; this favors burden, selection or ascertainment explanations. Inconclusive: sparse adjudicated response/recurrence, wide uncertainty, poor calibration or excessive missingness; this does not refute biology. The computation can check joins, dates, leakage, model fitting and conclusion/output consistency. It cannot establish mechanism, treatment benefit, residual viable tumor, or an action threshold. Expert radiology/clinical adjudication of response, recurrence, tumor burden and treatment intent is essential; prospective validation and ideally imaging/pathology or molecular residual-disease data are needed for stronger claims.
