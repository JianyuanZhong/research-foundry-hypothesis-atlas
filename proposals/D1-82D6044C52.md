TITLE
Fresh pre-incision liver state versus TACE history and elapsed time: an identical-sample test of postoperative hepatic prognosis after HCC resection

CLINICAL QUESTION, SUBSTANTIVE ADVANCE, AND CLAIM LADDER

For adults with recorded HCC who remain without a recorded resection at a fixed 28-day landmark after their earliest qualifying TACE and subsequently undergo a recorded liver resection, does a fresh pre-incision bilirubin/INR state provide essentially all available prognostic information about absolute postoperative day-5-to-7 hepatic dysfunction, or do elapsed time since the last TACE and the magnitude of the acute post-TACE perturbation add reproducible information beyond that current state?

This crossover combines the strongest elements of the two assessed-valid parents without turning either surrogate into a treatment rule. [prior hypothesis] contributes the temporally honest day-28 eligibility ledger, continued visibility of patients who are not resected, an explicit last-TACE clock in the presence of repeat TACE, and the clinically motivated acute bilirubin/INR history. [prior hypothesis] contributes the direct nested comparison of latest preoperative state against historical information on identical patients and the use of absolute postoperative laboratory outcomes, which avoids algebraically embedding the current predictor in the endpoint. The substantive advance is a head-to-head, same-patient information test: it separates what is known immediately before incision from what the calendar and an earlier biochemical response may still contribute.

Strongest claim already supported by the available evidence: bilirubin and INR are clinically relevant components of hepatic reserve and of the laboratory description of post-hepatectomy liver dysfunction; the HCC snapshot contains dated perioperative measurements and enough selected cases to estimate a prognostic comparison. The source also supports the feasibility of a fixed day-28 ledger and demonstrates that repeat TACE is frequent, that a fresh paired current state and postoperative measurements are available for hundreds of operations, and that historical acute anchors are available for a smaller overlapping group. These are feasibility and biological-plausibility claims, not evidence that any laboratory threshold is safe or that waiting causes recovery.

Untested claim: on the same selected resection patients, after prespecified case-mix adjustment and leakage/assay gates, (i) current pre-incision bilirubin and INR substantially outperform elapsed time alone, and (ii) adding last-TACE-to-operation time and/or acute post-TACE perturbation yields no clinically material, stable improvement beyond the fresh current state—or, alternatively, yields a reproducible increment that justifies prospective validation of history-aware prognostic assessment. The estimand is incremental prognosis among observed complete cases, not the causal effect of assigning a surgery date. A positive increment cannot establish readiness, a safe delay, a benefit for unresected patients, or a scheduling policy.

The clinical importance is direct. Calendar intervals are easy to record but may be a poor proxy for the patient's current reserve; conversely, a current value may conceal whether a patient is recovering from a large insult or simply has chronically abnormal liver function. Knowing which representation is reproducibly informative would determine whether a future prospective study should prioritize fresh laboratory measurement alone or retain a pre-TACE reference, acute trajectory and time since treatment. It could reduce unsupported calendar-based inference without pretending that this retrospective source can choose the operation date.

TEMPORAL WINDOWS AND PREDICTORS

Let O be the first resection start and T be T_last. All predictors must have measurement time < O, and no post-t0 or postoperative information can enter the eligibility ledger, exposure, transformations, or covariates.

Current state C is the latest numeric bilirubin and latest numeric INR separately observed in [O-7 days,O). Retain their recencies and assay labels; require both within the same window, but not necessarily at the same timestamp. A <=24-hour-pair sensitivity tests tighter pairing. Freshness sensitivities use [O-5,O) as clinically preferred and [O-3,O) as stress test; values older than seven days cannot be carried forward as “at operation.”

Pre-TACE anchor B is the latest bilirubin and INR in [T-7 days,T). Acute history A uses the maximum bilirubin and maximum INR in [T,T+7 days), right endpoint excluded. Since the primary landmark cohort requires operation >28 days after index TACE but T may be a repeat TACE, the implementation must explicitly verify A's window ends before O; if any later TACE intervenes, do not attribute measurements after that later TACE to T. For every marker retain the exact time, assay and specimen, and report the time from anchor to T and T to O.

Derive, without using postoperative outcomes:
- current bilirubin log level log(C_B) and INR level C_I;
- residual bilirubin r_B = log(C_B/B_B) and residual INR r_I = C_I-B_I, where the denominator/anchor is the latest pre-TACE value;
- acute perturbation q_B = log(A_B/B_B) and q_I = A_I-B_I, with robust-standardized components combined only as a secondary summary score; primary models retain marker-specific terms so one marker cannot conceal discordance;
- elapsed days d = (O-T)/24 hours, modeled continuously, and the number/spacing of prior TACE episodes.

Require positive numeric denominators for bilirubin, inspect implausible values, and prespecify winsorization/robust scaling inside training folds only. If laboratory director review finds routine/urgent or calendar-era noncomparability, restrict to validated routine assays and repeat 2018–2025 or other validated-era analysis. Albumin, creatinine, platelet, ALT and hemoglobin are covariates only after assay/unit validation and must not be allowed to replace the primary two-marker state.

OUTCOMES THAT AVOID MECHANICAL COUPLING

Primary outcomes are two absolute continuous postoperative measures, reported separately:
1. log of the maximum numeric total bilirubin in [O+5 days,O+8 days);
2. maximum numeric INR in [O+5 days,O+8 days).

These values do not algebraically contain the latest preoperative predictor. Fit standardized linear/robust models or appropriate transformed regressions, with a prespecified joint multivariate test but separate effect estimates and intervals. A result is supportive of a general hepatic-state claim only when bilirubin and INR are directionally coherent; a single-component result is labeled marker-specific.

A unit/reference-range-gated binary dysfunction signal may be secondary or co-primary only if a laboratory director maps assay/era/specimen-specific ULNs before fitting. Define it as postoperative bilirubin >=2x validated ULN AND postoperative INR >=1.20 in [O+5,O+8). If ULNs cannot be recovered or validated, do not substitute a percentile or call this endpoint ISGLS PHLF; make the absolute continuous outcomes primary. The parents' relative composite (postoperative bilirubin >=1.5 times current bilirubin AND postoperative INR >= current INR+0.20) is retained only as a coupling audit/sensitivity and is prohibited for judging incremental value of current state.

Clinician-adjudicated ISGLS PHLF/grade, encephalopathy, new or worsened ascites, ICU escalation, reoperation, infection/sepsis, bile leak, transfusion and in-hospital death are secondary validation outcomes if feasible. Management evidence and exact document timestamps are incomplete, so automated analysis cannot establish a true ISGLS grade.

PRIMARY ESTIMANDS AND NESTED COMPARISONS

The principal estimand is the optimism-corrected incremental prognostic value on the identical common cohort with current state, pre-TACE anchor, acute history and absolute postoperative outcomes. Prespecify four nested representations:

M0 (fresh-state baseline): current bilirubin/INR levels plus validated ordinary preoperative case mix, age, sex, calendar year, service, admission-to-operation time, utilization/measurement intensity, prior TACE count/spacing, preoperative albumin/creatinine/platelets/ALT/hemoglobin, comorbidity candidates, preoperative imaging features and validated operation extent/approach.
M1 (add elapsed time): M0 + continuous restricted-cubic-spline d and its recency/clock terms. This tests whether the calendar retains information after current state.
M2 (add pre-TACE residual history): M1 + marker-specific r_B and r_I, and anchor recencies. This tests whether knowing the patient's pre-TACE starting point adds information beyond current state and elapsed time.
M3 (add acute perturbation): M2 + marker-specific q_B and q_I and acute measurement recencies. This is the acute-history extension and is interpreted only on the same complete patients on whom M2 is refit.

The main contrast is M0 versus M1+M2+M3 as a block only if event/measurement support permits, but the clinically interpretable prespecified contrasts are M1−M0 (elapsed time beyond current state), M2−M1 (pre-TACE residual history beyond current state and time), and M3−M2 (acute perturbation beyond current state, residual history and time). Do not infer that a nonsignificant block proves equality; report confidence intervals around incremental performance. Also report current state versus a timing-only baseline (M_time: d plus case mix) on the larger identical available cohort, because the question includes whether fresh state is sufficient relative to the calendar.

For continuous outcomes report cross-validated/bootstrapped change in scaled Brier or mean squared error as appropriate, log score where a probabilistic model is used, calibration slope/intercept, and explained variance; for the optional binary outcome report optimism-corrected Brier, log loss, calibration and AUC, never AUC alone. Use decision-curve net benefit only for a validated binary outcome and label the 10–20% range as an exploratory risk-communication analysis, not a surgical cutoff. Report standardized predicted postoperative bilirubin and INR for representative current states and histories only within empirical support. The primary interpretation is whether added variables yield reproducible, clinically material prediction, not whether any individual regression coefficient is causal.

All model preprocessing, transformations, variable selection and penalization must be learned inside training folds. Use nested patient-level bootstrap with >=500 resamples or repeated stratified 10-fold cross-validation, preserving paired observations. Ridge/elastic-net or penalized splines are mandatory if the absolute endpoint has fewer than 40 events or if interaction/separation diagnostics are unstable. Do not fit high-dimensional interactions. The absolute outcome model carries the principal inference if ULN validation succeeds; otherwise all binary claims are unavailable.

FEASIBILITY, OBSERVATION, AND SUPPORT GATES

A bounded full-source scan in the parent supports 338,040 raw procedure rows (322,166 valid start times), 28,159,928 lab rows, 825 last-TACE selected resections in a closely aligned >28–180 cohort, 578 complete preoperative/postoperative paired cases with 74 events for the relative composite, 567 acute pairs, and 411 common acute-history/outcome cases with 57 relative events. An independent scan in the measured-state parent found 846 >14–180 cases, 594 current/outcome pairs, 503 current/pre-TACE/outcome cases, 423 acute-history/outcome cases and 61 relative events. The differences arise from the day-28 restriction, exact duplicate and intraoperative-TACE rules, and clock definition; compilation must rerun one frozen algorithm and emit both ledgers rather than borrow counts. The exact absolute-outcome yield is unknown until the implementation and unit gate are run.

Before clinical interpretation require:
- Common-cohort support: >=400 patients and >=50 paired relative events for historical/acute incremental analyses where the relative endpoint is used only as sensitivity; >=400 for current-state models; and no empty timing-decile-by-history support. If absolute binary events are <40, binary incremental results are exploratory/inconclusive and continuous outcomes govern.
- Freshness: reproduce principal direction and clinically relevant uncertainty in [O-5,O); [O-3,O) is a stress test. Failure of the five-day sensitivity while the seven-day analysis is positive makes “fresh at incision” inconclusive.
- Observation: tabulate current/anchor/outcome completeness by timing decile, injury/history quantile, calendar year, service, assay and case mix. Use inverse-probability-of-outcome-observation weighting only as a sensitivity. Stop clinical interpretation if weighted ESS <100 in either half of timing support, >10% of weight is carried by <=10 patients, anchor availability differs by >15 percentage points after adjustment, or any history-by-time empirical decile is empty.
- Unit/assay: laboratory director verifies bilirubin/INR scales, ULNs if binary endpoint is attempted, routine versus urgent handling, specimen comparability and calendar discontinuities. Inspect same-time routine/urgent agreement; run validated routine-only and calendar-era sensitivities. Failure makes thresholded claims unavailable and limits continuous claims to assay-specific prognosis.
- Case mix: two blinded reviewers validate >=200 stratified preoperative imaging reports and >=200 procedure names for diameter, multiplicity, invasion, extrahepatic disease, rupture, response, resection extent and approach. Automated adjustment requires sensitivity and PPV >=0.90; otherwise fully adjudicate or state residual case-mix confounding unresolved.
- Intent/selection: hepatobiliary surgeon and interventional radiologist, blinded to outcomes, classify index/last TACE as planned preoperative, conversion/downstaging, bridge/definitive/palliative, emergency hemostatic/rupture, or indeterminate, and assess documented potential resectability at t0. If kappa <0.70 or >30% are indeterminate, prohibit readiness, scheduling and resectability interpretations. The quantitative estimand remains selected resection prognosis, not a treatment-strategy effect.

FALSIFICATION AND NEGATIVE CONTROLS

1. Leakage/coupling audit. Verify programmatically that every predictor precedes O, that the acute window is preoperative and correctly reset after repeat TACE, and that absolute outcomes contain no current-state formula. Any violation is computational failure. A signal confined to the mathematically coupled relative composite and absent from both absolute components falsifies a current-state increment.
2. Preoperative pseudo-outcome. Predict late preoperative bilirubin/INR in [O-3,O) from earlier [O-7,O-3), time and histories, using a separate no-leakage cohort. If M1/M2/M3 improves this pseudo-outcome as much as or more than postoperative outcomes, the apparent increment is compatible with monitoring or channeling rather than postoperative vulnerability and is downgraded/inconclusive.
3. Hemoglobin negative-control outcome. Use postoperative day-1-to-3 hemoglobin nadir/change, adjusted for preoperative hemoglobin and validated operative extent. A standardized history/time increment >=0.20, scaled Brier improvement >=0.02, or p<0.01 in the same direction flags generic illness or operative-complexity selection.
4. Negative-control exposure. TACE day of week, adjusted for calendar year and service, should not predict either absolute hepatic component. Omnibus p<0.01 or standardized effect >=0.20 flags scheduling/service structure.
5. Pseudo-anchor. Replace the true pre-TACE anchor with a measurement from [T-30,T-14) when available. Equal or greater improvement from this pseudo-anchor than from the true anchor falsifies a specific post-TACE recovery-history interpretation and suggests stable utilization or patient history.
6. Clock/repeat-TACE stress. Compare last-TACE primary, index-TACE sensitivity and one-TACE restriction. A result that appears only under the biologically inappropriate index clock is adverse to the acute-injury interpretation. Measurements after an intervening TACE cannot be attributed to an earlier TACE.
7. Case-mix stress. Repeat with and without operation extent, among validated minor resections, planned/conversion intent, routine assays and fresh [O-5,O). Disappearance after validated extent/tumor adjustment indicates operative channeling rather than a robust physiologic-history signal.
8. Stability/falsification of calendar form. Use continuous spline as primary; 55-, 60- and 65-day cuts are displays/placebos only. Significance isolated to one arbitrary cut, reversal across reasonable clocks, or a direction supported by only one marker falsifies the claimed stable increment.

PRESPECIFIED RESULT BRANCHES

Supportive for fresh current state being sufficient: M0 beats the timing-only baseline by a clinically material and optimism-corrected improvement, both absolute bilirubin and INR are coherent, calibration is acceptable, [O-5,O) reproduces direction, and M1−M0, M2−M1 and M3−M2 all have confidence intervals excluding a prespecified clinically meaningful improvement (for example, no >=0.005 absolute Brier or >=0.02 AUC gain where a validated binary endpoint exists), with all observation, assay, case-mix, intent and negative-control gates passing. This supports prospective validation of fresh pre-incision measurement as a prognostic representation. It does not prove that current labs are sufficient for clinical decision-making or that changing timing is harmless.

Supportive for elapsed time/history adding information: on identical patients, M1, M2 or M3 yields a reproducible incremental improvement over M0, with paired uncertainty excluding the no-material-gain region, acceptable calibration and net benefit only if the binary endpoint is validated; both absolute components are directionally coherent; the gain persists under [O-5,O), validated case mix, routine assays and appropriate last-TACE clock; and pseudo-outcome, hemoglobin, day-of-week and pseudo-anchor controls pass. Label the winning increment precisely (time, pre-TACE residual, or acute perturbation) rather than claiming all history matters. This supports a prospective measurement/validation study, not a wait-or-operate rule.

Adverse/opposite: current state performs worse than timing-only, history improves only on the coupled relative endpoint, acute history degrades calibration, one component reverses direction, or the apparent history signal strengthens only after improper adjustment or under the index clock. This rejects the proposed general physiologic-history representation and points to assay, monitoring, operative case-mix or selection artifacts. No scheduling inference follows.

Precise null: all relevant paired incremental 95% confidence intervals exclude clinically meaningful gains (continuous and validated binary outcomes), calibration is adequate, both components agree, freshness and assay sensitivities reproduce the null, and controls pass. This supports no clinically material added prognostic information from the tested history/time representation beyond fresh current state in observed complete selected resections. It does not establish equivalence for all TACE recipients or causal irrelevance of waiting.

Inconclusive/falsified: intervals include important benefit and harm; common-cohort or event thresholds fail; [O-5,O) does not reproduce [O-7,O); ULN/unit/assay validation fails; positivity, observation, intent or case-mix gates fail; absolute components conflict; any negative control or pseudo-outcome reproduces the finding; or the result depends on one clock, threshold or missingness pattern. Report the selection and availability ledgers and no timing or readiness recommendation.

LIMITS AND REQUIRED ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE

The selected-resection complete-case design cannot represent people who never undergo recorded surgery, progress, die, receive outside treatment or lack postoperative institutional measurement. The source lacks reliable outside-care/death capture, ECOG/BCLC, longitudinal resectability, scheduling rationale, standardized Child-Pugh/ALBI without units, ICG clearance, portal pressure, future-liver-remnant volume, raw imaging/mRECIST, embolization selectivity, blood loss/transfusion/anesthetic detail, and timestamped management needed for true ISGLS grade. Clinical documents have no row timestamp. Clinicians must adjudicate intent, operation validity/extent, tumor response/case mix, assay interpretation and true postoperative liver failure. A computational verifier can establish hashes, rows, joins, windows, duplicate handling, two clocks, no leakage, absolute endpoint arithmetic, identical samples, model fitting, bootstrap uncertainty, controls, gates and interpretation mapping. It cannot establish causality, safe thresholds, resectability, intent, true PHLF, benefit from waiting, or generalization beyond observed selected resections. Prospective multicenter validation with scheduled fresh labs, reference ranges, operative detail, complete outcomes and explicit decision intent is required before any decision rule is considered.

CHANGE FROM PARENTS

Relative to [prior hypothesis], this child replaces the acute-history-by-time interaction as the sole principal comparison with a clinically sharper nested question: does acute injury or elapsed time add information after the patient's fresh pre-incision state is known? It retains the fixed day-28 landmark, nonresection ledger, last-TACE clock, acute window and two-clock sensitivity, while demoting sparse stratum-specific interaction claims. Relative to [prior hypothesis], it restores the day-28 landmark and explicit repeat-TACE/acute perturbation framework, changes the principal outcome family to absolute continuous bilirubin and INR rather than a unit-dependent binary threshold, and forces all time/history comparisons onto identical complete samples. It consequently preserves the more feasible continuous inference while making a direct test of whether current state is sufficient. Remaining uncertainty is not hidden: current and acute histories are observed only among selected operations, assay comparability and absolute thresholds need laboratory review, and even a stable increment cannot establish a causal timing decision.
