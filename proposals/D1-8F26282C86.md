# Hypothesis: Dynamic Multi-Organ Dysfunction Trajectories in Sepsis

## Unresolved Question

**Current knowledge gap:** Existing sepsis risk prediction models rely primarily on:
- Static admission characteristics (SOFA scores, APACHE II)
- Single-organ recovery metrics (e.g., creatinine alone for AKI)
- Time-to-event analyses from a fixed decision point

**What remains unknown:** How do *concurrent, evolving multi-organ dysfunction patterns* interact over time to predict outcomes? Specifically:
- Do patients with *discordant* organ trajectories (e.g., improving respiratory but worsening renal) have different risk profiles than those with *concordant* improvement?
- Is there a specific temporal pattern of multi-organ deterioration that predicts adverse outcomes better than any single-organ metric?
- Can learning from longitudinal organ trajectories reveal risk signals that static models miss?

## Clinical Importance

Sepsis remains the leading cause of death in critically ill patients. Current risk stratification:
- Fails to capture the dynamic nature of organ dysfunction
- Treats organs in isolation rather than as an integrated system
- May misclassify patients with complex, discordant trajectories

A better understanding of multi-organ trajectory patterns could:
- Enable earlier identification of high-risk patients with complex dysfunction
- Inform personalized monitoring and intervention strategies
- Guide development of more accurate prognostic models for clinical decision support

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

**Beyond single-organ metrics:** Current AKI recovery is often assessed by creatinine alone (mimic-03 seed). This proposal tests whether urine output, fluid balance, and other physiological markers provide complementary information.

**Beyond static scores:** SOFA scores are snapshots that may miss rapid deterioration or recovery between assessments. This proposal uses time-series analysis to capture trajectories.

**Beyond sequential analysis:** Most studies examine organ recovery sequentially. This proposal tests *concurrent* multi-organ patterns - the interaction between organ trajectories may be more predictive than any single organ's behavior.

## Hypothesis Statement

**Primary hypothesis:** In septic patients, the *pattern of concurrent multi-organ dysfunction trajectories* (concordant vs. discordant improvement/deterioration) predicts adverse outcomes better than any single-organ metric or static risk score.

**Specific predictions:**
1. Patients with *discordant* organ trajectories (e.g., improving respiratory function but worsening renal function after stabilization) have higher mortality than those with *concordant* improvement.
2. The *rate of divergence* between organ trajectories (measured by the slope difference between organs) is a stronger predictor than absolute values.
3. A machine learning model trained on multi-organ trajectory patterns will outperform models using single-organ metrics or static scores.

## Falsifiable Experiment Design

### Temporal Boundaries
- **Index time:** Time of first sepsis diagnosis (from d_icd_diagnoses)
- **Observation window:** 0-7 days post-diagnosis (to capture acute deterioration before chronic changes)
- **Follow-up:** 30-day in-hospital mortality and ICU readmission

### Primary Outcome
- Composite adverse outcome: In-hospital mortality OR ICU readmission within 30 days
- In-hospital mortality: subject_id with outtime in hosp/admissions before 30 days
- ICU readmission: subject_id with transfer to another hospital (hosp/transfers)

### Exposure: Multi-Organ Trajectory Patterns

**Pattern 1: Concordant vs. Discordant Improvement**
- Define "stabilization" as return to normal range for each organ (e.g., creatinine < 1.5 mg/dL, PaO2/FiO2 > 300 mmHg)
- Classify patients into:
  - *Concordant:* All organs return to or stay within normal range
  - *Discordant:* At least one organ remains impaired while others improve
  - *Worsening:* All organs deteriorate

**Pattern 2: Trajectory Divergence**
- Calculate the rate of change (slope) for each organ over 0-7 days using linear regression
- Compute divergence metric: |slope_respiratory - slope_renal - slope_cardiovascular|
- High divergence indicates conflicting organ trajectories

**Pattern 3: Multi-Organ Deterioration Score**
- Daily composite score: weighted sum of organ dysfunction scores (SOFA components)
- Compare trajectories of high vs. low deterioration rates

### Baseline Models

**Baseline 1: Single-Organ Models**
- Logistic regression using only creatinine at day 3
- Logistic regression using only PaO2/FiO2 at day 3
- Compare performance to multi-organ models

**Baseline 2: Static Scores**
- SOFA score at admission (computed from day 0 measurements)
- qSOFA score at admission
- Compare performance to trajectory-based models

**Baseline 3: Single-Timepoint Multi-Organ**
- Multivariable logistic regression using all organ measurements at day 3 only
- Compare to trajectory-based models

### Learning Alternative

**Model 1: Temporal CNN-LSTM**
- Input: Time-series of multi-organ measurements (daily aggregates from 0-7 days)
- Architecture:
  - 1D convolutional layers (3 filters, kernel_size=3, stride=1) to capture short-term patterns
  - LSTM layer (64 units, batch normalization) to capture temporal dependencies
  - Dropout (0.3) for regularization
  - Dense output layer (sigmoid activation)
- Training: 80% training, 10% validation, 10% test split (stratified by outcome)
- Loss: Binary cross-entropy
- Evaluation: AUC, calibration (reliability diagram), decision curve analysis

**Model 2: Transformer-based Sequence Modeling**
- Input: Sequence of organ trajectories with attention mechanism
- Architecture:
  - Token embeddings for each organ measurement
  - Multi-head self-attention (8 heads, 64 dimensions)
  - Feed-forward network (2 layers, 256 dimensions)
  - Output: Probability of adverse outcome
- Compare to CNN-LSTM for temporal pattern detection

### Analysis Plan

1. **Descriptive:** Distribution of organ trajectories by outcome
2. **Univariate:** Association of each pattern with outcomes (logistic regression with robust standard errors)
3. **Multivariate:** Adjust for confounders (age, sex, comorbidities, severity of initial insult, admission source)
4. **Model comparison:** Compare baseline models vs. learning models using:
   - AUC (area under ROC curve)
   - Brier score (calibration + discrimination)
   - Decision curve analysis (net benefit)
5. **External validation:** Apply models to time-split validation (train on 0-30 days, validate on 31-60 days)

### Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. Discordant organ trajectories show no significant association with outcomes (OR < 1.2, p > 0.05 after adjustment)
2. Multi-organ trajectory models perform no better than single-organ models (AUC difference < 0.02, 95% CI includes 0)
3. Static scores outperform trajectory-based models after adjustment (AUC difference < 0.03)

The hypothesis would be **supported** if:
1. Discordant trajectories are significantly associated with higher mortality (OR > 1.5, p < 0.01 after adjustment)
2. Multi-organ trajectory models significantly outperform baseline models (AUC improvement > 0.05, p < 0.01)
3. Specific trajectory patterns (e.g., respiratory-improving/renal-worsening) show consistent associations across subgroups

The hypothesis would be **inconclusive** if:
1. Discordant trajectories show weak association (OR 1.2-1.5, p 0.05-0.1)
2. Multi-organ models show marginal improvement over baselines (AUC difference 0.02-0.04)
3. Results are inconsistent across subgroups or time periods

## Limitations and Unresolved Questions

1. **Missing data:** Not all patients have complete multi-organ measurements. Need to handle missingness appropriately (multiple imputation or sensitivity analysis).
2. **Measurement frequency:** Chartevents are event-driven, not uniformly sampled. May miss rapid changes between events.
3. **Confounding by indication:** Treatment decisions may be influenced by organ trajectories, creating feedback loops. Requires careful adjustment or instrumental variable analysis.
4. **Causality:** Observational study; cannot establish causation.
5. **External validity:** MIMIC-IV represents US ICUs; may not generalize to other settings or healthcare systems.
6. **Itemid mapping:** Some itemids may not have clear clinical meaning; need to verify against d_items table.

## Required Additional Evidence

To fully validate this hypothesis, the following would be needed:
1. **External validation:** Apply findings to another ICU dataset (eICU, MIMIC-II/III, or other hospital)
2. **Prospective validation:** Test prediction model in real-time clinical setting with prospective data collection
3. **Mechanistic insights:** Understanding *why* discordant trajectories matter (e.g., inflammatory cascades, resource allocation, immune dysfunction)
4. **Intervention trials:** Test whether recognizing discordant patterns improves outcomes with targeted interventions (e.g., organ-specific support, early escalation)
