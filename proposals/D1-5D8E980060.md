# Day-42 recorded-assay signal: transportability across observation and care-process strata

Status: substantive transportability child of [prior hypothesis]. No model has been fitted and no clinical result is claimed.

## Scientific deliverable and decision boundary

The new deliverable is a prespecified transportability analysis of the inherited day-42 HCC landmark experiment. It must produce a frozen cohort and timing audit; development-only stratum cutpoints; held-out hazards and patient-level losses for every baseline/learned channel model; all-source censoring and assay-observation support audits; stratum-specific and partial-pooled assay increments; horizon and calendar interactions; negative controls; uncertainty intervals; and a claim-to-output map. Completion is demonstrated by these computed outputs and their provenance, not by readiness or a favorable result.

The clinically consequential question is:

> Among patients with a first dated locally recorded exact-code TACE who are observable at day 42 and have no exact-code repeat before day 42, is the incremental information in recorded assay state at day 42 stable enough across measurement opportunity, order availability, encounter intensity, care-process intensity, calendar period, and early versus late follow-up that it could be validated as a reusable recorded-state signal for a later operational repeat TACE?

The falsifiable claim is not “assays identify tumour response” or “repeat TACE benefits patients.” It is that the held-out improvement from (S) over (M0+P_{obs}+P_{care} is present and not materially different across supported workflow strata after all-source IPCW and cross-fitted assay-observation weighting. I will call this transportability claim supported only if every prespecified supported stratum has a positive primary loss increment, the partial-pooled heterogeneity estimate is below the predeclared computational margin, and the direction survives the timing, calendar, split, and negative-control checks. The margin is an operational separation rule—not a clinical minimal important difference, which is unavailable in this snapshot.

The strongest rival is a care-process/timing explanation: high-intensity encounters, orders, assays, or planned retreatment pathways create both more recorded assay values and more exact-code repeat TACE. A second rival is incompletely measured current severity. Under these rivals, the (S) increment will be concentrated in high-opportunity/high-order/high-care strata, disappear after observation weighting or process adjustment, be strongest only in days 43–90, vary across calendar periods, or be reproduced by timing/value permutations. A stable increment would support reusable recorded information for this operational event; it would not identify a biological mechanism. A null or adverse result would be clinically useful because it would argue against spending effort on assay-only transport and redirect the next study toward imaging, intent, and workflow linkage.

### Three inspected works and the unresolved advance

[K1] Dai et al. inspected full-text passages from a selected three-center CT/radiomics/deep-learning repeat-TACE cohort. It supports the strongest existing claim available here: treatment-adjacent recorded information can predict outcomes in a selected repeat-TACE imaging cohort, while external performance fell and the authors noted small-sample, scanner, and generalization limitations. This child adds a routine longitudinal EHR test with explicit observation and care-process strata; it does not reproduce the imaging study.

[K2] Tsurusaki et al. was inspected at the abstract level. It bounds the endpoint: HCC treatment response uses necrosis-aware mRECIST/RECICL concepts, not a procedure code or an uncalibrated laboratory value. [K3] Minh et al. was also inspected at the abstract level; its documented response comparison was sensitive to undocumented imaging outcomes, ascertainment, exposure classification, and treatment-selection confounding. The advance here is a falsifiable boundary test for whether a recorded-assay signal is portable across those observable recording processes, while explicitly refusing a response or causal claim.

The proposed result cannot establish tumour response, progression, TACE benefit/harm, treatment necessity, assay causality, or mechanism. Those require expert imaging and intent adjudication, validated assay units/reference ranges, mortality/outside-care/transplant ascertainment, and an independent cohort.

## Disjoint features and prespecified transport strata

Define elapsed time from the index start without date rounding. All feature cutoffs are day 42 unless stated otherwise.

- (M0): pre-index age, sex, department, encounter/admission history, diagnosis dictionary counts through matched encounters, procedure counts, and exact-assay summaries from [-365,0). All transformations and imputations are fold-fitted.
- (S): target-assay numeric values and qualitative/inequality flags in (0,42], plus assay-specific change from a valid pre-index observation in [-365,0). (S) contains no assay-request indicator or missingness indicator. Numeric missingness is imputed fold-wise by the frozen baseline/median rule; absence of an assay is represented only in (P_{obs}.
- (P_{obs}: dated encounter, examination, medication/order, and lab-observation counts, distinct-date counts/gaps, assay-label request/result counts, and availability/missingness indicators in (0,42], with no assay values, qualitative results, or state changes.
- (P_{care}: dated non-TACE procedure tokens, non-drug-order tokens, medication starts/ends, department/visit process marks, and their timing in (0,42], with no assay values. It is a competing operational explanation, not a causally adjusted confounder.

The transport axes are computed after eligibility but before any outcome fitting. Cutpoints are determined from the development partition only and frozen before untouched test scoring:

1. Measurement opportunity (G_{meas}): number of distinct elapsed calendar dates in (0,42] containing any valid encounter, examination, lab, medication, or order timestamp. Strata are zero, low-positive, and high-positive, with low/high split at the development median among positive values.
2. Order availability (G_{order}): number of distinct dates with a valid `开立时间` in (0,42]. Strata are no order, low-positive, and high-positive, split at the development median among positive values. An order with a later start/end time does not count as available before that time.
3. Encounter intensity (G_{enc}): number of distinct composite encounter IDs with at least one valid encounter timestamp in (0,42]. Strata are zero, low-positive, and high-positive using the development median among positive values.
4. Care-process intensity (G_{care}): number of distinct dated, de-duplicated non-TACE process marks from procedures, orders, medications, and encounter department/visit process marks in (0,42]. Strata are zero, low-positive, and high-positive using the development median among positive values. Exact-code TACE rows and all assay values are excluded.
5. Calendar period (G_{cal}): three equal-frequency index-date bins from the development partition, with breakpoints computed from index timestamps only and frozen before test outcome evaluation. The exact timestamp breakpoints are an output, not a post hoc choice.
6. Horizon (G_{hor}): fixed early [43,90] versus late [91,181) risk intervals; no fitted cutpoint.

The primary analysis estimates each axis one at a time to avoid sparse five-way cells. A secondary joint analysis uses only the supported 2×2 low/high (G_{meas}) by (G_{care}) cells; zero/positive categories remain separately reported. Any stratum that fails support is not collapsed into a neighboring stratum.

## Transport estimands and analysis

Let (A=M0+P_{obs}+P_{care}, (B=A+S), and let (L_i^{m}) be a patient-level integrated, all-source IPCW loss for model (m) over the intervals during which the patient is at risk. The primary stratum-specific incremental information estimand is
[
Delta_g^{LL}=E[L^A-L^Bmid G=g],
]
with positive values meaning that recorded assay state improves held-out weighted log loss. The secondary is (Delta_g^{Br}=E[Br^A-Br^Bmid G=g]). Report each within-stratum increment, its overall test-set standardization using the frozen test stratum proportions, pairwise stratum contrasts, and the difference between ordinary and observation-weighted estimates.

All-source right-censoring weights are fitted inside development folds from information available before each risk interval, with a frozen development-fold tail rule and support/effective-sample-size audit. For models containing (S), fit a separate cross-fitted day-42 assay-observation model for the probability of at least one valid target-assay value using (M0), pre-day-42 (P_{obs}, and (P_{care}. Use stabilized observation weights within each transport axis:
[
w_{obs,g}=Pr(S observedmid G=g)/Pr(S observedmid M0,P_{obs},P_{care},G=g).
]
The numerator and denominator are fold-fitted; weights are truncated only by the frozen development-fold rule. No extrapolation is permitted. Report ordinary and observation-weighted results separately. If the (S)-observation probability has unsupported cells, effective sample size is below 100, or the maximum weight exceeds the frozen tail rule after allowed truncation, the affected stratum’s transport conclusion is inconclusive. This is a predictive transport/sensitivity analysis, not causal identification or imputation of biology.

Fit the inherited models on the same frozen data and inputs:

- Transparent baseline: elastic-net pooled-logistic discrete-time hazards for days 43–180 with fixed day bins or a low-degree time spline, one channel set at a time: (M0), (M0+S), (M0+P_{obs}, (M0+P_{care}, (M0+P_{obs}+P_{care}, and (M0+P_{obs}+P_{care}+S).
- Learned alternative: compact time-aware GRU with elapsed-time decay over the same -365 through +42 events, event category/label, numeric value only after scale audit, qualitative/inequality flags, relative time, time since prior event, and channel tag. It receives the same channel ablations, target, split, censoring weights, observation weighting, metrics, and three fixed development seeds. It is not rewarded for complexity.
- For both model families, the primary transport test evaluates the global model’s untouched-test predictions within each (G) stratum. A secondary interaction fit adds one prespecified (G) one-hot token and (S\times G) terms at the day-42 boundary, one axis at a time, with the same matched input and scoring rules. This checks whether explicit heterogeneity improves held-out calibration; it does not convert a prediction interaction into mechanism.
- Use one patient-level calendar split: earliest 80% of eligible index dates for development and latest 20% for untouched test, frozen before outcome evaluation. No patient appears in both partitions. A patient-hash random split is a prespecified transport sensitivity. Within development use five grouped folds for all dictionaries, scaling, imputation, censoring/observation weights, hyperparameters, interactions, and controls.

Report weighted log loss (primary), weighted Brier at day 180, integrated Brier, calibration slope/intercept, and secondary AUROC/AUPRC. Bootstrap untouched-test patients, preserving each patient’s person-days, censoring weights, stratum memberships, and paired model predictions. Use 95% patient-level intervals for every (Delta_g), pairwise contrast, overall standardized increment, and (	au) heterogeneity estimate. The primary increment is (B) versus (A) for the transparent model; learned results are a matched scientific replication/trajectory-sensitivity analysis, not a confirmation requirement.

For each axis, fit a normal-normal partial-pooling model to the held-out stratum loss differences using their bootstrap standard errors, reporting shrinkage estimates and (	au_{axis}). Also report prespecified interaction contrasts from the matched interaction fit. Do not pool unsupported strata or repeatedly choose strata after seeing outcomes. The operational transportability margin is 0.005 weighted log-loss units or 0.010 weighted Brier units; these are computational decision margins because no clinical MCID is available, not claims of clinical benefit.

## Falsification, controls, and ambiguity resolution

Run every control on the same split, folds, strata, weights, and outputs:

1. Permute response-window timestamps within patient while preserving assay/result multisets and process counts.
2. Shuffle same-assay numeric values across development patients within calendar block, (P_{obs} counts, and baseline-severity strata while preserving timing/count structure.
3. Permute (P_{obs} and (P_{care} marks across patients within calendar and event-count strata without moving assay values.
4. Compare capacity-matched (P_{obs}-only and (P_{care}-only models in every supported (G) stratum.
5. Permute development outcome labels and score against untouched true-label test outcomes.
6. Construct process features only through day 28 while retaining the day-42 landmark; evaluate early and late horizons separately. A gain appearing only after late care marks and only in [43,90] is compatible with planned-retreatment timing.
7. Repeat without any patient with an undated strict TACE, with alternate all-source `obs_end definitions, and with exact-code-only versus a separately frozen high-specificity alias sensitivity. Never merge aliases after results.
8. Report assay availability (S)-observation rates and weights by every axis. A signal that is present only where observation support is strong is not transported to unsupported strata.
9. Run a lead-time control that excludes process/assay records in the last prespecified 7 days before the day-42 cutoff; retain the same landmark and outcome window.

A state increment reproduced by timestamp/value permutations, matched process-only channels, or opportunity/missingness channels favors a process explanation. If (P_{care} alone matches (S), this is an operational-care result, not evidence for assay state. If the increment is stable but only in one calendar bin, documentation/coding drift remains a rival and external validation is required.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive outcomes

Supportive evidence for the transportability claim requires:

- at least 50 observed later exact-code repeat events overall;
- completed index/early-repeat, all-source `obs_end, duplicate, exact-label, and calendar-scale audits;
- at least 15 events and effective sample size at least 100 for each claimed stratum, with observation and censoring support under the frozen rules;
- a positive held-out (S) increment over (M0+P_{obs}+P_{care} in the primary weighted log loss in every claimed stratum, with 95% intervals and a partial-pooled heterogeneity result within the 0.005 log-loss margin; secondary Brier results must not show material reversal;
- stable direction under ordinary versus observation-weighted analysis, early/late horizon, calendar, random-split, undated-history, and `obs_end sensitivities; and
- no comparable increment in process-only or permutation controls, with acceptable calibration.

This supports a reusable recorded-assay signal for an operational care event and justifies a next study linking assays to adjudicated imaging/intent. It does not justify a treatment rule.

Adverse evidence is a precise loss of the (S) increment after process adjustment, a process/permutation control matching it, a meaningful stratum interaction beyond the margin, or calibration/weight failure. If high-opportunity or high-care strata alone carry the signal, the assay direction is not transportable; prioritize measurement and intent linkage. If care-process strata are predictive without (S), that may be a useful workflow finding but is adverse to the state interpretation.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 50 events, fewer than 15 events or effective sample size below 100 in a necessary stratum, unsupported observation/censoring weights, label/calendar scale drift, severe censoring, wide intervals spanning the margin, or incomplete learned fitting. An imprecise null does not refute a biological hypothesis; biology is not tested here. If the primary transparent model is complete but the GRU cannot fit within its stated envelope, report the baseline transport result and defer only the learned sensitivity, without changing the question.

## Revisit, defer, or abandon criteria

Continue to an imaging/intent linkage study if the state increment is stable across supported strata and the event is sufficiently frequent. Revise the workflow representation if the signal is concentrated in one opportunity or care stratum but remains reproducible; the next revision should measure scheduling, intent, and outside care rather than add model complexity. Defer the transport claim when required strata lack support or units/scale drift prevents comparable state representation. Abandon the assay-state validation direction when adequately powered process/permutation controls repeatedly match (S) or when the signal disappears under leakage-safe timing definitions. None of these decisions treats a failed GRU as a scientific failure of the assay question.
