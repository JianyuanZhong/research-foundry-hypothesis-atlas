# Does reduced laboratory surveillance after recorded instability predict a specific next recorded deterioration signal?

Status: Episode 48 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational, recorded-data and measurement-process experiment. It is not a trial of laboratory ordering, a test of treatment efficacy, a physiologic-recovery study, a statement about clinician intent, a causal analysis, or a hospital-quality ranking.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The parent’s strongest evidence-supported claim is a design claim, not a result: in the frozen multi-hospital eICU snapshot it is possible to define an adult first-stay landmark cohort, a revision-safe laboratory-availability process, recorded vital instability, and held-out recorded death versus alive ICU discharge. The parent’s design already compares death with alive discharge and controls end-of-life documentation, interface support, observation burden and timing clocks. No model fit or clinical conclusion from the parent is reused.

The strongest remaining ambiguity is outcome specificity. Even if a laboratory-observation reduction after recorded instability predicts recorded ICU death, a broad disposition outcome cannot tell whether it is a marker of a near-term recurrent recorded instability process or simply a residual administrative/disposition pattern. The eICU snapshot cannot supply a validated clinical deterioration label, so the narrowest testable successor is an outcome-specific recorded endpoint.

The question is:

> Among supported adult first ICU stays with recorded vital instability in (180,300] and prior revision-safe laboratory surveillance, does a fall to zero recorded laboratory availability in (300,360] predict a first post-landmark recurrence of the same recorded vital-abnormality pattern, in addition to a death-specific association, and is the increment larger in the unstable than the support-matched non-unstable group?

This matters because it separates two decisions. A signal that predicts only recorded death or alive discharge is not enough to motivate a future monitoring-review policy: it may be a disposition or documentation marker. A signal that also predicts a temporally subsequent, reproducibly defined recorded vital-abnormality recurrence would be a more specific recorded-process marker suitable for prospective silent review and clinical adjudication. A null result would be useful evidence against treating the laboratory drop as a general deterioration cue. Neither result shows that testing was missed, that physiology worsened, that treatment should have changed, or that more testing would improve outcomes.

The substantive advance over the parent is therefore a prespecified endpoint-specific decomposition, not a larger model. The parent tests whether reduced surveillance after instability is associated with recorded death versus alive discharge. This child retains that exact primary estimand and adds a temporally ordered first-event endpoint for recurrent recorded instability. It asks whether the association is proximal and process-specific rather than only disposition-specific.

## Hypothesis and evidence boundary

Primary retained hypothesis H1 is the parent’s instability interaction: in the dynamic instability risk set, D=1 (zero recorded laboratory availability in (300,360] after at least two availability times in (180,300]) has a positive, hospital-transportable association with the cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death through minute 1,800, relative to D=0 (continued availability), after adjustment for recorded state and support context; the D contrast is larger for I=1 than for support-matched I=0.

New endpoint-specific hypothesis H2 is:

- In the same I=1 risk set, D=1 has a positive standardized cumulative-incidence contrast for a first post-landmark recurrent recorded instability event (RRI), defined only from qualifying `vitalPeriodic` observations in (360,1800].
- The RRI contrast is larger in I=1 than in I=0.
- The D contrast for RRI is not explained by an equally large alive-discharge contrast, and the RRI signal is not eliminated by predeclared EOL, interface, timing-clock, observation-density, hospital-regime and process-permutation checks.

H2 is a recorded observation-pattern hypothesis. RRI is not adjudicated physiologic deterioration. The word “recurrence” means recurrence of the frozen recorded abnormality rule, not recurrence of a clinical syndrome.

The two-cause primary outcome remains unchanged: cause 1 is recorded ICU death and cause 2 is alive ICU discharge, both in (360,1800], with no event by minute 1,800 administratively censored. Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, undocumented death and a destination-imputed death are outside the estimand.

For endpoint specificity only, define a separate first-event sensitivity estimand with three mutually exclusive causes in the same (360,1800] boundary: first RRI, recorded ICU death before RRI, or alive ICU discharge before RRI. Once RRI occurs, that stay leaves this first-event risk set; this secondary estimand must not be substituted for H1. It makes the ordering explicit and avoids counting a later death as an independent “outcome” after the first RRI.

The general landmark cohort is this adult first-stay cohort. The dynamic risk set R retains the parent’s exact support gates:

1. At least two distinct valid `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset` values in (180,300], separated by at least 30 minutes.
2. At least two distinct revision-safe laboratory availability times in (180,300].
3. At least one valid `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset` in [0,120] and at least one in (120,180].
4. No `carePlanEOL` save or discussion offset in (300,360] in the primary care-eligible analysis. Retain all R stays for the EOL-inclusive sensitivity.

Define recorded instability I=1 exactly as follows. Among distinct `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset` times in (180,300], at least two times separated by >=30 minutes have any numeric value meeting one or more of: `systemicmean < 65`, `sao2 < 92`, `heartrate >= 120`, or `respiration >= 24`. Missing values do not count as normal. I=0 has the same observation-support requirements and no qualifying abnormal observation in (180,300]. A support-matched I=0 group is descriptive effect modification, not a claim that these patients were clinically stable.

Define the new RRI event from distinct valid `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset` values in (360,1800]. A qualifying post-landmark abnormal time meets the same four-field rule. RRI occurs at the second qualifying abnormal observation time for which the two qualifying times are separated by >=30 minutes. If no such pair occurs before a recorded death or alive discharge, there is no RRI in the endpoint-specific risk set. This deliberately requires temporal recurrence and does not use post-event values as predictors. The solver must also report a sensitivity requiring two qualifying observations in one of the fixed 60-minute outcome bins and a sensitivity using `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` only as an additional, separately labeled covariate; `vitalAperiodic` cannot redefine I or RRI.

For `lab`, retain a row only when numeric `labresultoffset` is in [0,360] and `labresultrevisedoffset` is missing or <=360. Set `availability_offset = max(labresultoffset, labresultrevisedoffset)` when revision is numeric, otherwise `labresultoffset`. Use distinct availability times, not row counts. D=1 requires >=2 distinct availability times in (180,300] and zero in (300,360]. D=0 requires >=2 distinct availability times in each of (180,300] and (300,360]. The primary exposure is not defined for a no-interface patient. Retain through minute 300 the prior availability count, gap/span, distinct key/interface count and revision-known/missing count. A result-time sensitivity uses distinct `labresultoffset` values on the identical retained row set. Invalid or post-360 revisions are excluded and counted in a leakage audit.

The primary outcome intervals are exactly (360,420], (420,480], ..., (1740,1800]. The first-event RRI sensitivity uses the same intervals and cause-specific discrete-time hazards. For primary H1, `unitdischargestatus = Expired` with numeric `unitdischargeoffset` in (360,1800] is death; `unitdischargestatus = Alive` in that window is alive ICU discharge. Missing/other status is reported and excluded by a frozen rule. Destination is never used to impute death.

## Matched baseline and substantive learned alternative

The scientific comparison is endpoint specificity. Both methods use exactly the same R/I/D rows, six bins, admission/state covariates, lab codebook and revision rule, missingness, outcome definitions, hospital folds and no post-landmark predictors.

Transparent baseline:

- `B_pre`: pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards. For primary H1, it has separate death and alive-discharge heads over 12 intervals with admission context, recorded vital state/trajectory through 300, I, prior lab state/process through 300, recorded support activity/transitions through 300 and EOL documentation through 300. It excludes all (300,360] laboratory-process variables.
- `B_drop`: `B_pre` plus D, final-hour distinct lab availability/result counts, key/interface count, revision-known/missing status and the prespecified I-by-D term. The primary increment is `B_drop-B_pre`, reported in I=1, I=0 and as the interaction. It is a conditional predictive association, not an effect of ordering or withholding tests.
- For H2, fit the same `B_pre/B_drop` feature matrices to the first-event three-head endpoint (RRI, death-before-RRI, alive-discharge-before-RRI). Report RRI-specific coefficients and CIF contrasts, but do not pool them with H1.

Learned/mechanistic alternative:

- `T_multi` is a one-layer six-bin GRU with shared encoder and separate hazard heads for primary death, alive discharge and secondary first RRI/death/discharge. It receives exactly the six-bin channels used by `B_drop`, with fit-fold imputation/scaling, and no `hospitalid`, APACHE fields or future variables. Its substantive target is the same interval-specific hazards, not a proxy loss.
- Fit a parameter-matched non-temporal MLP on the same patient-level channels and a within-patient bin-order shuffle of the GRU inputs. A GRU advantage is evidence about temporal ordering only if it remains over the MLP, attenuates under order shuffle, and is present for RRI or the endpoint-specific contrast rather than only a global score. A gain that survives order shuffle is adverse to a temporal interpretation.
- The baseline is primary because H1/H2 contrasts and their uncertainty are auditable. The GRU is retained because endpoint specificity may depend on persistence and cross-domain ordering—recorded instability followed by laboratory surveillance loss followed by recurrent recorded abnormality—which additive summaries can lose. This is a scientific uncertainty, not a complexity or GPU preference.

A mechanistic observation/physiology state-space model is deferred. The local data lack verified specimen/order timestamps, laboratory units and equivalence, raw waveforms, complete testing opportunities, delivered-dose intervals and expert-adjudicated physiologic states. An observation model built without these dependencies would risk labelling interface/workflow as biology. Revisit only with timestamped orders/collections, unit-aware assays, waveforms and adjudicated states.

## Split, uncertainty and support gates

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals. Keep `uniquepid` groups together. Within each development fold use a 75/25 hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Learn codebooks, eligible support, imputation, scaling, elastic-net penalty, calibration, GRU stopping and any threshold only in the fit partition. Never use outcome-derived analyte selection or endpoint-derived feature selection.

The primary uncertainty is >=1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions, or a predeclared hierarchical hospital/fold model if bootstrap is unstable. Report macro-hospital and patient-weighted estimates, fold/hospital counts, support overlap, missingness, calibration, integrated competing-risk Brier scores, and death/discharge/RRI/censor counts. Report 95% intervals for 1,440-minute standardized death CIF D=1 versus D=0 separately in I=1 and I=0 and their difference. Separately report 1,440-minute RRI CIF contrasts in the first-event sensitivity and the contrast-of-contrasts. Report 600, 1,200 and 1,800-minute CIFs and interval-specific hazards. Uncertainty must be propagated through all learned preprocessing by refitting within each bootstrap/fold as feasible.

Primary H1 support gates inherit the parent: R >=500 episodes, >=20 hospitals and >=200 recorded deaths; in I=1 each D cell >=200 episodes, >=20 hospitals and >=100 recorded deaths; for the I=0 interaction each D cell >=200 episodes, >=20 hospitals and >=100 recorded deaths. H2 is classifiable only if the RRI event has >=200 first events overall, >=20 hospitals, and each primary I=1 D cell has >=50 RRI events and >=20 hospitals; the I=0 RRI interaction additionally requires >=50 events in each D cell and >=20 hospitals. Any support category used in adjustment requires >=1% fit-fold prevalence and >=20 hospitals. If a gate fails, leave H1 classifiable if its gates pass, label H2 inconclusive, and do not shrink the population or redefine RRI to rescue it.

## Falsification and observation-process controls

1. Reassign laboratory process histories within hospital, I, prior lab-intensity and baseline-risk strata while preserving patient process distributions. D contrasts for death and RRI should attenuate.
2. Permute the (180,300] and (300,360] lab-bin labels within support-compatible patient rows while preserving total counts, key composition, revisions and missingness. A surviving timing signal is not evidence that the reduction follows instability.
3. Reassign I or vital histories within hospital and baseline-risk strata while preserving observation burden. The I-by-D contrast should attenuate.
4. Fit the analogous post-landmark outcome for a documented support-process reduction using treatment/respiratoryCare event channels. A similarly sized RRI/death signal indicates generic documentation workflow.
5. Compare H1 death and alive-discharge CIF increments. A discharge increment as large or larger, especially near minute 360, is adverse to a deterioration interpretation.
6. Compare RRI and discharge first-event CIFs. A D association confined to discharge, or RRI only after a discharge-like interval, is adverse to endpoint specificity.
7. Exclude any EOL save/discussion in [0,360], then include save/discussion timing as covariates; report differences without interpreting absence as no goals-of-care discussion.
8. Repeat result-time and conservative availability-time lab clocks on identical retained rows; revision-known-only rows; and common key/interface panels. Disagreement is inconclusive for the timing interpretation.
9. Mask (300,360] predictors and then mask earlier bins. If the claimed final-hour signal is recreated without the final hour, it is not evidence for the proposed timing mechanism.
10. Repeat with vital observation-density covariates, the `vitalAperiodic` sensitivity, and observed support/interface strata. A one-interface or one-hospital result is not transportable evidence.
11. Use outcome-free hospital surveillance fingerprints from lab/vital coverage and leave one supported regime out. Hospital descriptors are diagnostics, not patient predictors.
12. Repeat RRI with the prespecified adjacent threshold/single-field and one-bin definitions. Reversal across clinically adjacent recorded definitions is unstable/inconclusive.
13. For `T_multi`, require superiority over the matched MLP, loss under order shuffle, and endpoint-specific rather than global improvement. Otherwise do not claim that temporal order was informative.
14. Audit each predictor for no post-360 value, no `activeupondischarge` inference, no invalid revision, no hospital ID, no target-fold preprocessing, no APACHE field and no text-derived diagnosis.
15. A fixed-burden descriptive review diagnostic may compare top-k D scores with `B_pre` scores per held-out hospital, matched within I and risk strata at k = 1%, 5% and 10%. This is label-relative retrospective discrimination, not alert utility, net benefit or a recommendation.

## Interpretation rules

Supportive H1 requires its inherited gates, a positive held-out I=1 death-CIF D contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero, incremental death-specific performance for `B_drop` over `B_pre`, and an I-by-D contrast whose interval excludes zero. A clinically material H1 interaction retains the parent’s prespecified lower 95% bound >1 percentage point at 1,440 minutes; smaller effects remain exploratory.

Supportive endpoint-specific evidence additionally requires H2 RRI gates, a positive held-out I=1 RRI CIF contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero, a larger I=1 than I=0 RRI contrast, no equally large alive-discharge contrast, and attenuation under patient-link and lab-bin permutations. It must remain reasonably stable in macro-hospital estimates, leave-one-regime-out folds, EOL and clock sensitivities. The learned branch is supportive only when its endpoint-specific temporal advantage meets the MLP/order-shuffle criteria.

The bounded supportive conclusion would be: in supported held-out eICU hospitals, reduced recorded laboratory availability after recorded vital instability is associated with a subsequent, more specific recurrence of the frozen recorded vital-abnormality pattern as well as recorded death, beyond preceding recorded state and care process. This remains an association of recorded data. It does not mean under-testing caused deterioration, that a laboratory result was clinically indicated, or that physiology worsened.

Adverse evidence includes no or reversed H1/H2 contrasts; no instability interaction; a death-only signal with no RRI specificity; an equally large or larger discharge/process-documentation signal; survival under patient/time permutation; dependence on EOL, interface, one hospital or one threshold; clock disagreement; failed overlap; or a learned gain surviving order shuffle. High overall discrimination does not rescue these failures.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed dynamic, death or RRI support gates; intervals spanning zero or the materiality threshold; unstable hospital folds; sparse interface/regime cells; nonconvergence; disagreement between clocks or RRI definitions; or endpoint timing that cannot be separated from discharge workflow. Negative/inconclusive findings speak only to the defined recorded-data estimand and cannot prove adequate monitoring, absent biological deterioration or absence of clinical benefit.
