## Scientific question and advance

The parent asks whether retaining a prior eight-field biochemical panel improves prediction of a pooled subsequent recorded circulatory event beyond the current panel and repeat-assessment spacing, especially in the current-only middle-risk band, and whether that increment transports from an early assessment era to 2013. That pooled result is important, but it can conceal a clinically consequential distinction: information that helps identify a first later cardiac/heart-failure record need not help identify a first later cerebrovascular record.

This successor tests the more decision-specific question:

> Among people eligible for the parent’s paired-assessment analysis and without a prior recorded target, is the incremental predictive information in the prior panel, conditional on the current panel and assessment spacing, materially different for a first later cardiac/heart-failure event versus a first later cerebrovascular event in the parent’s current-risk ambiguity band, and is that difference stable under the parent’s early-to-2013 no-refit test?

The potential decision is pathway-specific review after a current assessment: whether historical measurements should be retained to distinguish a cardiac/heart-failure-oriented review from a cerebrovascular-oriented review when pooled current-only risk is ambiguous. A positive result would justify prospective evaluation of domain-specific risk communication or review routing. It would not justify a treatment, referral, monitoring-frequency or repeat-testing policy.

The substantive advance over the parent is an outcome/heterogeneity estimand, not another pooled prediction comparison. It asks whether the same historical information has different value for two mutually defined first-event pathways while preserving the parent’s exact pooled endpoint as an internal benchmark. It can distinguish a genuinely domain-specific increment, a common increment with no domain separation, or a pooled increment driven by one domain and diluted by the other.

## Evidence boundary and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest available evidence supports the immutable UKB snapshot, the eight ordinary read-only CSV sources, their catalog relationships and hashes, the eid join key, the existence of all 259 position-matched 41270-0.k/41280-0.k columns, the two bound death-date fields, the assessment-date columns 53-0.0 and 53-1.0, and all sixteen literal panel cells. Local approved metadata identifies age 21022 and sex 31; it does not provide complete field dictionaries for the biochemical panel, specimen timing, diagnosis-code semantics or observation end.

That evidence does not support any fitted association, biomarker-specific mechanism, biological disease onset, outpatient completeness, severity, or causal effect. The code-family interpretation below is a prespecified candidate endpoint and remains gated on an approved ICD/code-domain dictionary and clinical review.

Let C be the cardiac/heart-failure domain and S the cerebrovascular domain:

- C = {I20, I21, I22, I23, I24, I25, I50};
- S = {I60, I61, I62, I63, I64}.

These two sets exactly partition the parent’s frozen target list. The primary hypothesis is deliberately direction-agnostic because the local evidence does not certify analyte identity:

> In the parent’s central 60% current-only risk band, the held-out prior-panel increment differs between C and S by at least 0.002 multiclass one-vs-rest Brier units, with the direction of that heterogeneity retained when early-fitted models are applied without refitting to the 2013 assessment cohort.

For a negative increment, adding prior values improves prediction. Thus, a negative contrast means the prior panel helps more for C; a positive contrast means it helps more for S. The direction is determined by the prespecified contrast, not chosen after seeing results. The 0.002 value is a practical information margin, not a clinical utility threshold.

The test is not that the biochemical panel causes either event, that a recorded event marks onset, or that one pathway should be treated differently. Its unresolved claim is only conditional predictive heterogeneity for two frozen recorded-event categories.

## Baseline, domain models and learned alternative

All models use the same participants, endpoint construction, outer split, frozen band, weights and held-out evaluation.

First reproduce the parent comparator with the three-category pooled endpoint {T,D,N}:

- M0: current eight-panel values, age, sex and T1 calendar year;
- M1a: M0 plus L;
- M1b: M1a plus all eight literal prior-panel values;
- M1c: M1a plus raw current-minus-prior changes, only as a representation sensitivity after timing and units pass.

Then fit the same nested feature sets to the five-category domain endpoint {C,S,J,D,N}. The primary domain increment is M1b versus M1a. No extra predictor is allowed in the successor’s primary contrast.

The substantive learned alternative is a matched shallow gradient-boosted multinomial model M2 with exactly M1b inputs and a matched M2a with exactly M1a inputs. Use a small prespecified training-only depth/learning-rate/regularization grid, fixed tree limit and inner validation or training-only early stopping. M0/M1a/M1b/M1c are regularized multinomial probability models with fold-local preprocessing. No model uses event dates, outcome labels, late data or any future information in feature construction.

The transparent model tests whether domain-specific historical information exists after spacing is separated. The learned alternative can reveal nonlinear current/prior discordance and interactions that an additive model averages away, such as a pattern where history changes C-versus-S probabilities only in a subset of the pooled ambiguity band. It must be judged by the same domain heterogeneity estimand, calibration, fold stability and late no-refit behaviour. A small learned score gain without a stable, interpretable domain contrast is inconclusive.

## Estimands and uncertainty

For domain k in {C,S}, era e in {early, late}, and band g, let BS_k(M,g,e) be the one-vs-rest Brier score for class k computed from the five-category held-out probabilities. Define:

- Δ_k,g,e = BS_k(M1b,g,e) − BS_k(M1a,g,e);
- H_g,e = Δ_C,g,e − Δ_S,g,e;
- τ_H = H_A,late,no-refit − H_A,early.

Negative Δ means prior values improve prediction for that domain. H<0 means the improvement is larger for C; H>0 means it is larger for S.

The primary estimand is H_A,early, with τ_H as the prespecified transport estimand. Report Δ_C,A,e and Δ_S,A,e directly, the matched learned contrasts, and all L/A/U values as secondary diagnostics. Also report the parent’s pooled d_g,e = BS(M1b)-BS(M1a), its D_e = d_A,e − (d_L,e+d_U,e)/2, and τ_A exactly as defined by the parent. This makes clear whether a domain-specific result explains, refines or contradicts the pooled parent increment.

Use paired participant bootstrap intervals, prespecifying 2,000 replicates, resampling within fixed era-by-band cells and retaining fold, endpoint, band, predictions and weights. For H, the C and S scores must be computed from the same bootstrap sample. Report percentile 95% intervals, fold-specific estimates, weighted and unweighted results, and failed-support replicates. A sensitivity may refit the complete early pipeline in bootstrap samples; if not done, state that uncertainty conditions on the fitted outer models and does not include training variation. Do not use late outcomes for calibration, tuning or band construction.

Primary support requires at least 100 pooled T events overall, at least 200 eligible participants in each early/late by L/A/U cell, at least 30 C and 30 S events in both early-A and late-A for H_A and τ_H, at least 20 J events overall for the five-class construction, and late weighted ESS at least 200 and at least half the unweighted size in every band. Domain contrasts in L or U additionally require 30 events of each domain in each corresponding era-by-band cell. Require finite weights and adequate early-to-late covariate overlap. These are feasibility gates, not observed results; no band, endpoint or boundary may be changed to pass them.

## Interpretation and falsification criteria

Supportive evidence requires all semantic, code-domain, timing, ascertainment, join and endpoint-integrity gates to pass; all primary support cells to pass; no leakage or permutation failure; and a 95% interval for H_A,early entirely above +0.002 or entirely below −0.002, with the late H_A estimate retaining the same direction and the complete 95% interval for τ_H inside [−0.002,+0.002]. The domain favoured by H must also have a direct Δ_A,early and Δ_A,late with 95% upper bounds below zero, without material deterioration of D/N calibration. Selection weighting may not reverse the primary direction, and the parent’s pooled comparator must be internally reproducible. A stable M2 shape showing domain-specific prior/current interactions strengthens the explanation but cannot rescue a failed transparent contrast.

This supports conditional heterogeneity of prior-panel information for the specified recorded endpoint in the supported UKB analysis population and a calendar-era stability result. It does not support a clinical routing policy, a treatment or referral threshold, the benefit of repeat testing, a causal pathway, biological onset, severity, outpatient completeness or external validity.

Adverse evidence is H_A near zero, reversal between eras, no direct improvement for either domain, a pooled gain that is entirely due to spacing M1a, loss of the contrast after prior permutation, reversal under selection weighting, poor calibration, fold instability, or a learned-only improvement without a transparent nested contrast. A result where one domain improves and the other worsens can support pathway differentiation but is adverse to any broad claim that retaining prior values is uniformly useful; it requires clinical review before action.

Inconclusive evidence includes missing codebook or clinical review, unverified assessment/specimen timing, incomplete H ascertainment, death-date disagreement, any malformed accepted pair, sparse C/S/J cells, nonidentifiable bands, poor overlap or ESS, wide intervals spanning the practical heterogeneity margin, conflicting endpoint/missingness sensitivities, or unstable learned shapes. “No qualifying record in the available source” under incomplete follow-up is inconclusive, not a negative clinical finding.

Computationally checkable claims include source/schema/header integrity, joins, all-259 position handling, code-family construction, event ordering, parent/domain label agreement, fold manifests, model fits, held-out predictions, proper scores, calibration, bootstrap intervals, weights, support gates and permutation behaviour. ## Alternatives, preserved branches and evidence limits

The chosen alternative is the five-category outcome-specific estimand because it changes the clinical question while using the parent’s exact panel, timing, split, all-259 endpoint construction and pooled comparator.

The following alternatives were considered and deferred:

- The parent’s pooled T/D/N increment remains the required baseline comparator, not a replacement.
- A BMI/waist/grip body-shape and physical-reserve question is scientifically substantive and was represented in the available UKB context, but its field-level semantics, units, measurement protocol and clinical endpoints are not approved in the local metadata; raw imaging and verified disability outcomes are unavailable. It is deferred for evidence reasons.
- A trajectory-only current-minus-prior representation is retained as M1c sensitivity, not promoted to the primary claim because two assessment landmarks do not establish a biological turning point and specimen timing is not verified.
- A disease-history transformer or latent organ-age/mechanistic model could reveal richer temporal structure, but the local UKB catalog lacks verified disease chronology, field semantics, raw imaging, raw sequence variants and assay metadata needed for an interpretable adaptation. The shallow learner is sufficient to test nonlinear domain interaction without inventing mechanism.
- Infection, cancer, CHIP, sleep, genetic-risk and raw-imaging branches from the expert seed library require unavailable or unverified modalities/outcomes for this question and are not silently substituted.

The local research-ambition README and methods-and-compute summary were inspected. They motivate rigorous comparison of structured baselines and learned longitudinal alternatives but do not prescribe this topic. The natural-history demonstration’s accessible local material supports considering structured sequence learning; no reproduction claim is made.
