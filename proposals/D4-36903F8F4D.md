# Episode 91: durability qualification of the frozen AG1-versus-AF1 N18 experiment

## Lead decision and substantive successor

Use the assessed-valid exact-binding anchor [prior hypothesis], with [prior hypothesis] and [prior hypothesis] as direct scientific/endpoint parents. This is a substantive child, not a transcription repair: it preserves the exact population, O1_hold=2,334, n1=466, baseline-only immutable rosters, upstream H1/D1 hierarchy, five-year window, and the inherited first recorded inpatient N18-before-death endpoint, while adding one outcome-blind durability qualification.

The new hypothesis is:

> Conditional on the inherited frozen AG1-versus-AF1 experiment having a supported contrast in first recorded inpatient N18 before death, the contrast will also be present for a prespecified durable N18 phenotype: at least two distinct, valid position-paired inpatient N18 records whose dates are separated by at least 90 days, both after t1, within the same five-year window, and strictly before death.

This is clinically important because a single inpatient N18 code can represent an acute episode, a coding artifact, or an episode that never recurs in the available record. A repeated record separated by at least three months is a stronger longitudinal signal for ongoing or recurrent recorded kidney disease and would make a transient-code explanation less plausible. It still is not adjudicated CKD: the fields do not establish chronicity of kidney function, outpatient capture, measured GFR, renal attribution, or clinical benefit.

The substantive advance over the parent is therefore a falsifiable durability test of the same fixed-capacity decision. The parent asks whether AG1 and AF1 differ on the first recorded event. This child asks whether any such difference survives a stricter longitudinal qualification, without refitting or changing the policies. It does not duplicate the parent’s nonrenal record-density standardization and does not open a new policy-specific outcome while selecting the experiment.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The available UKB evidence supports outcome-blind feasibility: the frozen snapshot has the required paired diagnosis/date arrays and two death fields; the parent lineage authenticated the 2,334-person holdout, 466-person rosters, H1/D1 hierarchy, and exact five-year endpoint construction. The source audit performed for this proposal inspected headers and catalog metadata only: it found 4,896 columns in canonical health_outcomes, 30,799 in main, and all 521 endpoint/death fields in each source. No participant outcome rows, AG1/AF1 event counts, or policy-specific results were inspected.

Public evidence motivating the qualification, but not proving this hypothesis, was read from Zemplényi et al. (Int J Public Health 2023; DOI 10.3389/ijph.2023.1605635; acquired source_id [source checksum]). That different cohort compared laboratory-confirmed with diagnosis-coded CKD and reported substantial under-recording; its discussion also states the usual chronicity requirement. It does not validate UKB N18 as CKD, establish the AG1/AF1 contrast, or establish that 90 days of separated codes means persistent disease here.

The strongest available claim is thus that repeated, time-paired N18 coding can be computed as a prespecified recorded-outcome qualification in this snapshot. The unresolved claim is whether the inherited AG1-versus-AF1 first-event contrast remains at this more durable recorded-outcome level.

## New durability endpoint, outcome-blind construction

Before opening any AG1/AF1 event result, construct a participant-level list

V_i = {(j,d_j): code(41270-0.j) is a valid trimmed-uppercase string beginning exactly "N18", its paired 41280-0.j date is valid, t1 < d_j <= t5, and d_j < death}

for j=0,…,258. Preserve the array position: never sort diagnosis codes independently of their paired dates. If two positions carry the same date, they are not two temporally separated records.

Define the child endpoint:

D18_i=1 iff there exist two distinct positions j != k in V_i with d_j != d_k and |d_j-d_k| >= 90 calendar days; otherwise D18_i=0 when classifiable.

This is a repeated recorded N18 phenotype, not a diagnosis of persistent CKD. The 90-day rule is fixed before outcomes and is not tuned after seeing event counts. A participant who dies before a second qualifying date is D18=0 for the fixed five-year risk estimand, subject to the source’s incomplete capture limitation; same-day death cannot supply the second record. A blank code/date pair is no event. A nonblank N18 code with a blank or invalid paired date is an integrity failure, not a negative. Reuse the parent’s strict pair-integrity policy for other malformed endpoint pairs.
