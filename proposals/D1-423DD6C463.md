# Do early laboratory recording practices transport poorly after measured acuity is accounted for?

Status: Episode-3 Lead child of `[prior hypothesis]`; adaptation of expert seed `[starting question]`, not a reproduction of any demonstration.

## Scientific question, evidence boundary, and deliverable

The unresolved question is whether early laboratory recording process is transport-unstable across hospitals after the same measured physiologic state is available. In adults who remain observable at six ICU hours, does adding laboratory observation/missingness/timing information produce a larger development-site-to-new-hospital loss than a values-only model, and does that contrast persist after measured-acuity adjustment and a general-monitoring control?

The strongest supported claim before fitting is only that this eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative patient, hospital, laboratory, and vital observations, with heterogeneous laboratory recording and revision fields. The inspected parent audit reported 140,801 eligible adult initial stays across 207 hospitals and 2,339,035 early laboratory rows, with at least one eligible early laboratory row in 117,368 stays. Those are feasibility/audit results, not evidence of mortality prediction, transport failure, clinician workflow, or causality.

The primary scientific deliverable is newly fitted, leakage-safe paired predictions for every eligible test stay from values-only (V) and values-plus-process (V+P) models, with hospital-held-out transport contrasts, hospital-cluster uncertainty, acuity/workflow sensitivity blocks, the competing-state analysis, and falsification logs. Completion requires: a frozen cohort/state manifest; canonical laboratory name/unit coverage and revision audit; patient/hospital split assignments; fold-specific preprocessing and fitted models; out-of-fold predictions; external and internal AUROC/AUPRC/Brier/calibration summaries; process transport penalties and bootstrap intervals; residualized/vital-control results; permutation and timing falsifications; and an explicit failure or deferral record for any model branch. No clinical conclusion is claimed until these outputs exist.

## Population, time origin, and competing-event estimand

The analysis unit is one `patientunitstayid`. Use all source rows meeting the rules below—no sampling. From `patient`, include numeric `age >= 18`, `unitstaytype='admit'`, `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitdischargestatus` exactly `Alive` or `Expired`, and finite numeric `unitdischargeoffset >= 360`. If multiple qualifying stays share a `uniquepid`, retain the smallest non-missing `hospitaladmitoffset), breaking ties by smallest `patientunitstayid`; if all are missing, retain the smallest ID. Deduplicate before any split and group `uniquepid` in every fit and internal validation split.

Time zero is ICU-relative minute 360. Predictors may use only observations with clinical/result/observation offset in [0,360], and a laboratory result must be available by minute 360 under the revision rule below. The primary outcome is D: `unitdischargestatus='Expired'` and `360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240`. An alive discharge with `360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240` is C, an explicitly competing terminal state. R is alive and still in the ICU at minute 3240, including a stay whose eventual discharge/death offset is after 3240. The primary binary estimand is the marginal probability of D within (360,3240] among the landmark population, plus the paired difference in development-to-new-hospital transport performance for V+P versus V. This is not hospital mortality, a causal effect, or a claim about post-discharge outcomes.

The mandatory sensitivity is a three-state D/C/R analysis using the same folds and predictor blocks: cause-specific one-vs-rest models and a multinomial model, reporting state probabilities, state-specific Brier scores, and hospital-stratified state prevalence/cumulative incidence. If a finite discharge offset and the three mutually exclusive states cannot be constructed, stop the primary analysis and report a prerequisite failure rather than censoring discharge or silently relabeling it.

## Comparable alternatives and selection

Both alternatives test the same target D, blocks V versus V+P, and hospital-held-out estimand.

1. Primary transparent baseline: fixed L2 logistic regression (C=1, deterministic solver), one-hot categorical encoding, and numeric median/mode imputation fitted within each outer training set. V is context (`gender, ethnicity, admissionheight, admissionweight, hospitaladmitsource, unitadmitsource, unittype`) plus vital and canonical-lab value summaries. V+P adds only the fixed laboratory process features. This is auditable and appropriately tests whether recorded process adds transport-unstable conditional information, but static summaries lose order, slope, recovery/worsening shape, and irregular timing.

2. Substantive temporal alternative: one-layer width-64 GRU, dropout 0.1, Adam 1e-3, batch 256, weighted binary cross-entropy, maximum 30 epochs, patience 5, one fixed seed per outer repetition (20260913–20260917). Discretize [0,360] into twelve 30-minute bins. For each value channel/bin use mean, last value, and last-value time; V sequences have outer-training medians and no observation masks/counts, while V+P sequences add lab masks, within-bin counts, time-since-latest-result capped at 360, and fixed early process totals, with context appended to the final hidden state. The GRU can reveal whether trajectory/order and irregular observation timing carry information that the static baseline erases. It is a substantive representation of the same process question, not a search for a small predictive gain.

Fit V and V+P for both families on the same five repetitions of five whole-hospital folds (50 bounded GRU fits), with all imputation, residualization, stopping, and threshold choices isolated to the outer training hospitals. If CPU time or convergence prevents the GRU, preserve completed outputs and label only that branch deferred; never replace it or alter the estimand. The static logistic deliverable remains mandatory. Approved science budget is 7,200 seconds, concurrency 2, no mandatory GPU; use at most one 8-GiB CPU extraction/logistic job and request GPU only if the executor demonstrates that the bounded GRU requires it. Persist checkpoints, manifests, split assignments, preprocessing objects, predictions, and logs.

The selection reason is scientific: logistic is primary because its process increment is inspectable; GRU is retained because temporal order and observation timing are plausible information-bearing mechanisms. A small GRU performance gain alone cannot establish the transport hypothesis. Revisit the GRU only if the value/process sequences have sufficient support, the static comparison is complete, and temporal falsification is interpretable. A mechanistic survival or causal workflow model is deferred because orders, cancellations, turnaround, staffing/capacity, treatment intent, and reliable event-time process semantics are unavailable; inventing such a model would overstate the data.

## Transport evaluation and uncertainty

Before fitting, report counts by hospital, D/C/R prevalence, canonical coverage, value availability, process distributions, and hospitals meeting support. Retain hospitals with at least 200 eligible stays and at least 10 D events; use one fixed supported-hospital set for every block/model. If fewer than 20 hospitals qualify, report descriptive estimates and prerequisite failure, not a definitive transport conclusion.

For each repetition, assign whole hospitals to five folds with fixed seeds and approximate balance of stay count and D events. External E is all eligible stays in held-out hospitals. Internal I is a patient-grouped 20% holdout within outer-training hospitals, used only as a development-site comparator. Report hospital-macro and pooled out-of-fold metrics separately; hospitals with one outcome class contribute Brier/calibration but not AUROC.

For each fitted family define:
`T_AUROC = (I_AUROC(V+P)-I_AUROC(V)) - (E_AUROC(V+P)-E_AUROC(V))`;
`T_Brier = (I_Brier(V)-I_Brier(V+P)) - (E_Brier(V)-E_Brier(V+P))`.
Positive T means the process increment is less preserved in new hospitals. Report E(V), E(V+P), direct external increments, I values, calibration intercept/slope, Brier, AUROC, AUPRC, fixed-bin calibration, and descriptive decision curves at 5%, 10%, and 20%. Decision curves are not clinical utility claims because benefits, harms, actions, and thresholds are unavailable.

Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of complete hospital prediction sets, preserving V/V+P pairing and all repetitions, for percentile 95% intervals. Directional support requires T>0 with a 95% interval excluding zero; a clearly non-trivial signal additionally requires the lower bound to exceed 0.02 in the relevant AUROC/Brier scale. Robust support requires concordant direction in logistic and GRU, no integrity failure, and poorer/less stable external calibration or Brier for V+P despite a nonnegative internal increment. The primary interpretation remains a transport penalty, not a causal process effect. Also report GRU(V)-logistic(V) externally with the same uncertainty; temporal information is supported only by reproducible external calibration/Brier improvement, not a tiny AUROC change.

## Falsification and interpretation criteria

Run without changing the primary estimand:

- Permute D within hospital and refit: AUROC should approach 0.5 and AUPRC the permuted prevalence. Persistent process-specific signal indicates leakage or split failure.
- Compare the valid revision-safe build with a permissive diagnostic build that ignores `labresultrevisedoffset`; the permissive build is never a scientific result. A material difference triggers leakage investigation.
- Permute each patient’s P vector among patients within hospital. Collapse of the process increment toward V supports patient linkage; persistence suggests redundancy or a hospital proxy.
- Shuffle eligible lab offsets within stay while preserving values/counts. Timing-sensitive GRU effects should attenuate while static count effects may remain; failure to separate these patterns weakens the temporal interpretation.
- Compare raw P, training-only V-residualized P, V-risk tertiles, and the vital-monitoring control. Shrinkage after these checks supports measured acuity/observability as an explanation. Persistence of a lab-specific effect, with hospital shifts in P and attenuation under within-hospital rank normalization, is consistent with a workflow/documentation proxy. Neither establishes clinician intent.
- Verify no post-360/revision-after-360 row, identifier, discharge/APACHE outcome, or outer-test preprocessing object entered any predictor, and that each `uniquepid` has one split role per fit. A patient-overlapping random split may be reported only as an optimistic benchmark; an effect present there but absent in hospital holdout is site dependence, not deployment evidence.

Supportive results: positive T with an interval excluding zero, external V+P calibration/Brier worse or less stable than V despite a nonnegative internal process increment, concordance across both model families, and all integrity checks passing. If residualized/stratified results persist, the narrow conclusion is that recorded process contains transport-unstable information beyond measured state; workflow remains only a plausible explanation.

Adverse results: T at or below zero with a sufficiently narrow interval, no internal process increment, or disappearance after acuity/vital-monitoring controls. This weakens or falsifies the hypothesis for this panel/cohort; it does not prove testing practice is universally transportable or safe.

Inconclusive results: fewer than 20 supported hospitals, missing canonical combinations, inadequate D/C/R support, unstable site metrics, intervals spanning zero and 0.02, family disagreement, failed leakage/integrity checks, or GRU convergence failure. Preserve all estimates and failure reasons and do not select a favorable point estimate.

## Clinical limits and required next evidence

Computationally checkable here are cohort rules, joins, ICU-relative time, revision cutoff, feature construction, split isolation, model fitting, predictions, competing states, metrics, bootstrap intervals, and permutation behavior. Not established here are whether missingness means no order, an unrecorded order, interface loss, delayed result, or clinical intent; whether residualization removes acuity; whether process-dependent predictions improve triage/escalation/transfer/bed allocation or patient outcomes; or whether thresholds have acceptable clinical utility. Those require expert review, order/cancellation and turnaround data, interface audit, staffing/bed-capacity context, a prospective silent deployment, and an independent health system. Hospital ID is not an instrument, and this design cannot support a causal testing-policy claim. Removed calendar dates, removed narrative note sections, and absent raw waveforms prevent calendar-drift, text, and waveform conclusions.
