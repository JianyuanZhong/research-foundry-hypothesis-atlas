# Discordant creatinine recovery after AKI: a severity-, treatment-intensity-, and RRT-stratified MIMIC-IV study with prespecified baseline comparison

Status: active draft, not yet a registered hypothesis. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Among adults in the ICU with KDIGO stage 2 or 3 acute kidney injury (AKI), does apparent creatinine recovery at 48 hours coexisting with persistently low urine output or positive fluid balance identify a worse 7-day ICU prognosis than creatinine recovery alone, after accounting for current severity and treatment intensity at the 48-hour stabilization decision point?

The parent proposal established a prespecified discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype and addressed the strongest confounder—current severity and treatment intensity at the decision point—with SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and renal replacement therapy (RRT) status as adjustment variables. However, RRT status is not merely a continuous treatment-intensity covariate: it marks a fundamentally different renal recovery phenotype and outcome trajectory. Patients on RRT at the 48-hour decision point have already had their native kidney function bypassed, so creatinine recovery in this stratum may reflect RRT clearance rather than intrinsic renal recovery.

The strongest remaining uncertainty is whether the discordance phenotype adds prognostic information beyond what a standard AKI severity model already captures. A prespecified baseline comparison is the smallest feasible check that could change the interpretation of the discordance signal. If the phenotype improves prediction beyond a standard AKI severity model, clinicians have a concrete reason to distrust creatinine-based recovery and escalate monitoring or support; if it does not, the phenotype is a useful descriptive regularity but not a clinically actionable prognostic marker.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The inspected literature reports that creatinine is a surrogate for measured kidney function and that creatinine-based AKI definitions may reflect underlying cardiorenal disease severity and congestion rather than isolated renal injury. KDIGO staging is applicable in ICU cohorts and AKI severity is associated with worse outcomes. The unresolved claim is whether the discordance phenotype—apparent creatinine recovery coexisting with persistently low urine output or positive fluid balance—predicts subsequent ICU outcomes beyond what is already known from standard AKI severity and treatment intensity at the decision point.

## Data bindings

The study uses MIMIC-IV v3.1. The population is adults (age >= 18) with a KDIGO stage 2 or 3 AKI diagnosis during the first ICU stay. The 48-hour decision point is defined as 48 hours after the first AKI diagnosis. The discordance phenotype is defined as:

- Creatinine recovery: creatinine at the 48-hour decision point is <= 1.5 times the baseline creatinine (the minimum creatinine in the 24 hours before the first AKI diagnosis).
- Persistently low urine output: urine output in the 24 hours before the 48-hour decision point is < 0.5 mL/kg/h for >= 6 hours or < 0.3 mL/kg/h for >= 12 hours (KDIGO stage 2 or 3 urine-output criteria).
- Positive fluid balance: net fluid balance in the 24 hours before the 48-hour decision point is > 0.

The discordance phenotype is the intersection of creatinine recovery and (persistently low urine output OR positive fluid balance).

The outcome is 7-day ICU mortality or ICU-free days at 7 days. The primary analysis is a prespecified logistic regression model with the discordance phenotype as the primary predictor, adjusted for SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and RRT status at the 48-hour decision point. The analysis is stratified by RRT status at the 48-hour decision point.

The baseline comparison is a prespecified logistic regression model using the same covariates as the parent's adjusted analysis, with the discordance phenotype added as a single binary feature. The primary evaluation metrics are AUC, calibration, and decision-curve analysis. The analysis is stratified by RRT status at the 48-hour decision point.

## Falsification criteria

The study is falsified if:

- The discordance phenotype does not predict subsequent ICU outcomes beyond what is already known from standard AKI severity and treatment intensity at the decision point.
- The discordance phenotype's prognostic signal is driven by RRT status at the 48-hour decision point.
- The discordance phenotype's prognostic signal is not robust to the definition of renal recovery (e.g., creatinine vs. urine output).

## Limitations

- Creatinine is a surrogate for measured kidney function.
- The study is prognostic, not causal.
- The discordance phenotype is defined using laboratory and support measures rather than direct organ function.
- The analysis is stratified by RRT status at the 48-hour decision point, but RRT status is not a random assignment.
