# Episode 19 evolution: all-eligible observation-aware ICU-discharge instability with a paired temporal boundary check

Status: design-only successor of [prior hypothesis]. No cohort scan, row-count estimate, fitted model, Harbor execution, or clinical result was run in this branch.

## Decision and scientific advance

The selected parent’s sharp question is whether a persistent multi-domain abnormality in the final 24 hours before an alive ICU exit is more prognostic for the next 48 hours than the same summary 48–24 hours before exit. Its vulnerability is that the paired primary cohort is known to have adequate observations in both windows. That can condition on staffing, severity, discharge destination, documentation and an unmeasured clinical decision to measure. The prior all-eligible alternative ([prior hypothesis]) correctly retained insufficiently observed exits as an explicit unknown state, but its main contrast was not itself temporal.

This successor combines the two ideas with a changed estimand hierarchy:

1. The primary estimand is an all-eligible observed-discharge state profile. Every eligible adult alive at the index ICU exit is retained; late-window state is stable, one persistent domain, at least two persistent domains, or unknown/insufficient. Unknown is an ascertainment state, never normal and never a latent physiologic class.
2. The parent’s same-subject late-versus-early boundary estimand is retained as a complementary, explicitly conditional check among subjects known in both windows. It is no longer presented as representative of all eligible exits.
3. Observation opportunity is a primary design variable. Pre-window observation density, the unknown-state risk gradient, and a training-only observation-weighted sensitivity are required outputs.
4. The configured separate discharge-note files are available for a secondary, outcome-blinded clinical adjudication sample of discharge context/missingness reasons. Notes are not predictors, weights, or outcomes; lexical flags are not adjudication.

The clinically consequential unresolved claim is:

> Among adults reaching an observed alive first ICU exit, does late persistent multi-domain physiologic abnormality retain a reproducible 48-hour first-event association when all eligible exits, including insufficiently observed exits, are retained, and is the sharper late-versus-early contrast present within the subset observed in both windows?

The primary hypothesis is that the all-eligible late state ≥2 persistent domains has higher first ICU-readmission and/or hospital-death cumulative incidence than the observed stable state, while unknown/insufficient has a distinct risk profile. The complementary hypothesis is that, among the paired known-in-both-windows subset, the standardized late-minus-early risk difference is positive. The key rival is observation/discharge selection: if sparse measurement or a discharge process creates the apparent signal, the unknown state will carry a strong gradient, the known-only contrast will attenuate after density/severity auditing or weighting, or late and early contrasts will be similar. These are prognostic associations conditional on observed ICU exit, not effects of discharge timing, monitoring, oxygen, treatment, or destination.

A supportive result would justify prospective clinical validation of a time-local instability phenotype or discharge-monitoring hypothesis. It would not justify delaying discharge or claim mechanism. An adverse result would redirect the work toward ascertainment, residual severity, support meaning or destination context. An inconclusive result would prevent a discharge-readiness interpretation without more complete observation and clinical adjudication.

## Evidence boundary and three key works

The strongest inspected evidence supports only that short-term ICU readmission/death after ICU transition is clinically consequential and associated with severity/support features, while discharge-day scores have limited/moderate performance. Timmers et al. report 48-hour readmission importance, respiratory decompensation among readmission causes and scarce predictors in a surgical ICU cohort [K1]. Rosa et al. report a prospective 1,277-patient cohort in which SWIFT, SOFA and TISS-28 had similar moderate accuracy for a 48-hour readmission/unexpected-death composite [K2]. Kramer et al. show that poor patient-level outcomes among readmitted patients do not establish a unit-quality or discharge-policy effect after case-mix adjustment [K3].

Those works do not establish a MIMIC-IV late-versus-early boundary, independence from observation opportunity, a missingness mechanism, a discharge rationale, or causality. The present adaptation adds an all-eligible unknown-state estimand, explicit competing events and loss audit, pre-exposure observation/severity checks, and a paired temporal comparison.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The unit is one hospital admission.

- Read mimic-iv-3.1/hosp/patients.csv.gz and retain anchor_age >= 18; report gender.
- Join icu/icustays to hosp/admissions on (subject_id, hadm_id), then join patients on subject_id.
- Require finite intime < outtime. Within each (subject_id, hadm_id), select the earliest valid ICU stay by intime, breaking an exact tie with minimum stay_id. Do not replace an ineligible first stay with a later stay.
- Define t_d = icustays.outtime. Retain only an alive exit: admissions.deathtime is null or strictly later than t_d; death exactly at t_d is excluded. Do not restrict ultimate discharge location.
- The primary late exposure window is [t_d-24 hours, t_d), divided into four half-open six-hour bins. The complementary earlier window is [t_d-48 hours, t_d-24 hours), also four bins.
- A primary record requires charttime < t_d; when storetime is non-null require storetime <= t_d. Missing storetime fails closed for the primary core exposure. Charttime-only availability is a labeled sensitivity.
- The outcome clock is [t_d, t_d+48 hours). No post-t_d value, note, destination, treatment or outcome-derived field enters an exposure or primary adjustment.

The index ICU stay is excluded from later-readmission matching. All subjects remain in the all-eligible flow regardless of observation state, immediate destination, ultimate disposition or unknown values.

## Exposure dictionary and unknown state

Use the parent’s fixed item dictionary, with no outcome-tuned threshold changes:

- 220045 Heart Rate, bpm;
- 220052 Arterial Blood Pressure mean, mmHg;
- 220210 Respiratory Rate, insp/min;
- 220277 O2 saturation pulseoxymetry, %;
- 223761 Temperature Fahrenheit, converted to Celsius;
- 223762 Temperature Celsius;
- 223834 O2 Flow, L/min;
- 223835 Inspired O2 Fraction;
- 226732 O2 Delivery Device(s), text-only descriptive sensitivity, never coerced to numeric.

Resolve each item against icu/d_items and require the expected label, abbreviation, linksto, category, unitname, param_type and normal-range metadata; fail closed on absent/colliding dictionary rows. Numeric records require finite valuenum and compatible valueuom. If both temperature itemids occur in a bin, collapse to Celsius and fail closed on disagreement beyond a fixed implementation tolerance.

For each six-hour bin, take median HR/MAP/RR/SpO2/temperature and maximum O2 flow/FiO2. Preserve per-channel valid-record counts, core masks and total/core observation counts. Define fixed domain flags:

- respiratory/oxygen: SpO2 <92%, O2 flow >0 L/min, or FiO2 >0.21;
- hemodynamic: MAP <65 mmHg, HR >100 or <50 bpm;
- ventilatory pattern: RR >24 or <8/min;
- temperature: Celsius <36.0 or >38.3.

A domain is persistent if it is flagged in at least two of four bins. The primary late categorical exposure is:

- stable: no persistent domain;
- one: exactly one persistent domain;
- multi: at least two persistent domains;
- unknown/insufficient: fewer than three of four bins contain a valid primary core observation.

Missing is unknown, not normal. The primary all-eligible contrasts are each state versus stable, with multi-versus-stable the clinical contrast and unknown-versus-stable an ascertainment result. Report domain-specific, oxygen-support and density components separately. Do not interpret unknown as a physiologic phenotype.

## Outcomes and competing events

Construct first-event transitions from t_d with fixed precedence for exact timestamp ties: hospital death, ICU readmission, alive hospital discharge, then an integrity flag for unresolved invalidity. Do not let exposure state determine tie handling.

- ICU readmission (R): the earliest later icustays.intime for the same (subject_id, hadm_id) with t_d <= intime < t_d+48h, excluding the index stay_id.
- Hospital death (D): admissions.deathtime with t_d <= deathtime < t_d+48h; hospital_expire_flag is a consistency check only and cannot replace a missing time.
- Alive hospital discharge (A): valid admissions.dischtime in the window, only if it precedes R and D.
- Loss of same-hospital follow-up (L): an observability transition only when both admission-level endpoint times are absent/invalid and no later bound timestamp verifies the admission through 48 hours. Use the maximum valid pre-window-end timestamp among bound transfer, ICU stay, lab, chart, input, output and procedure times. If the admission endpoint is complete and L is empty, report zero/not estimable; never convert L to alive discharge or a clinical adverse outcome.

Report R, D and A as separate 48-hour Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidences, with L, administrative censoring, invalid-time flags and all competing transitions shown. The composite R-or-D is secondary only. For standardized risks use six-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards; standardization is descriptive, not causal.

## Primary all-eligible estimand and paired boundary estimand

Let E_L be the four-level late state for every eligible alive exit. For each k in {R,D,A}, estimate:
RD_all,k(e) = CIF_48,k(E_L=e) − CIF_48,k(E_L=stable)
using the all-eligible population, fixed outcome rules and subject-cluster bootstrap intervals. State-specific risks, denominators and unknown fractions are mandatory. A large unknown-state gradient is not evidence about latent physiology; it is evidence that observation opportunity changes the interpretation of a known-state association.

Define E_E analogously for the earlier window. The paired boundary cohort is fixed before outcomes are read as subjects with known status in both windows (O_L=O_E=1). On exactly these subjects, estimate:
Delta_RD,k = RD_pair,k(E_L=multi vs stable) − RD_pair,k(E_E=multi vs stable)
where both risk differences use the same pre-W_E/static covariate distribution, same competing-event rules and no late value to adjust the early contrast. Report the two component risks, paired transition table and cluster-bootstrap interval. The paired result is a boundary check conditional on observation; it is not the all-eligible estimand and is not a causal effect.

Prespecify the eight-bin transition cells stable→stable, stable→persistent, persistent→persistent, persistent→stable and other/unknown. The late-specific interpretation predicts higher risk in stable→persistent than stable→stable/resolving; a residual-severity or selection explanation predicts a comparable earlier contrast, similar persistent→persistent risk after earlier-state standardization, or attenuation when unknown/density is represented.

## Observation opportunity and severity audit

Set O_L=1 when at least three late bins contain a valid primary core observation; otherwise O_L=0. Define O_E identically. Before the late window begins, construct Z from only:

- age, gender and admission type;
- ICU first/last care unit, intime, outtime and LOS;
- earlier-window core medians, masks, valid counts and all-chartevent counts/coverage;
- earlier-window latest valid values and missingness for creatinine 50912, lactate 50813, glucose 50809, hemoglobin 50811 and 51222 as distinct itemids, platelet 51265 and WBC 51301;
- earlier-window counts from labevents, chartevents, inputevents, outputevents and procedureevents as opportunity descriptors.

Fit P(O_L=1 | Z) on training data only, with subject-level splitting, training-only scaling and training-only stabilized-weight truncation at the 1st/99th percentiles. Report untruncated/truncated distributions, effective sample size, overlap and positivity by prespecified severity/density/destination audit strata. The weighted known-exposure analysis is a sensitivity under the untestable assumption that latent exposure and outcomes are independent of O conditional on Z; it is not a correction that recovers unknown subjects’ latent state.

Do not put destination, ultimate discharge location, notes, death, readmission, post-landmark values or late summaries in Z. Report density quartiles and the all-eligible flow. A late signal that follows total chart density more closely than physiology, or disappears after density stratification/weighting, is adverse to a time-local physiologic interpretation.

## Fixed-summary baseline and same-question temporal alternative

Both methods use the same all-eligible subjects, four-level late exposure contract, earlier-window severity/opportunity variables, first-event outcomes, loss rules and subject-level split: SHA-256 of subject_id, 70% train/15% validation/15% test; all admissions for a subject remain together. Fit maps, scaling, density cutpoints and observation weights on training only.

B: fixed-summary baseline, primary computational estimator. Fit separate six-hour cause-specific logistic hazards for R, D, A and L. Use the four-level E_L state, domain flags/counts, bin-order-free medians/min/max/range and flagged-bin counts, masks, O_L, density/opportunity counts, static covariates and pre-W_E severity. Unknown remains a categorical state with its masks; no hidden imputation. Standardize held-out 48-hour CIFs to the all-eligible test risk set. Use B for auditable state-specific risk differences and the paired boundary table.

T: chronological GRU, same scientific question. Fit a one-layer GRU, hidden size 32, over the same late window in 12 chronological two-hour bins: seven numeric channels after temperature collapse, per-channel masks, elapsed/bin index, core and total observation counts and fixed domain flags. Static covariates and pre-W_E severity enter a static head. Output four cause-specific hazards over eight six-hour post-exit intervals. Unknown/insufficient coverage is represented by masks and coverage; a missing bin may not be encoded as normal. Use Adam 1e-3, batch size 128, dropout 0, maximum 50 epochs, early stopping after five validation epochs without improving mean cause-specific Brier score, and seeds {17,29,43}. Train/evaluate the same model contract for the all-eligible state profile and report the paired boundary evaluation separately.

B deliberately loses chronology and the order/timing of observation bursts. T can reveal whether recency, persistence, resolution or measurement timing carries information beyond fixed summaries. A substantive temporal result requires stable held-out calibration/Brier behavior across seeds, a reproducible state/transition contrast, and a difference from an order-permuted T trained with identical values, masks, counts and split. A small AUROC/AUPRC gain alone is predictive utility, not evidence for a time-local phenotype.

Held-out outputs must include six-hour and 48-hour cause-specific Brier scores for R/D/A/L, calibration slope/intercept or observed-versus-predicted risk tables, secondary AUROC/AUPRC, standardized state/transition predictions, seed-specific results, aggregate uncertainty and 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap intervals. The verifier must test unsupported positive conclusions, adverse attenuation, and appropriate interpretation of inconclusive results.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following: a clinically meaningful, sufficiently precise all-eligible multi-versus-stable contrast for R and/or D; separate component CIFs and competing events; an explicitly reported unknown-state gradient; directionally compatible known-only and observation-weighted sensitivities; a paired late-minus-early contrast larger than the earlier contrast under the fixed boundary rules; and chronology/transition information that is stable across GRU seeds and not reproduced by order permutation or one density/destination stratum.

This supports a reproducible observed-discharge prognostic association compatible with a time-local instability phenotype. It does not establish a safer discharge rule, effect of delaying discharge, monitoring benefit, mechanism, intent or transportability.

Adverse evidence includes a comparable early contrast, attenuation after unknown/density/severity representation, a large unknown gradient with weak known-state contrast, loss of direction under the observation-weighted sensitivity, destination-only or oxygen-support-only concentration, charttime-only reversal, comparable ordered and permuted T, or a temporal gain without state/transition contrast. These favor residual severity, discharge selection, support/documentation meaning, or generic prediction.

Inconclusive evidence includes dictionary/unit failure, unavailable or unmatched joins, high unknown fraction, weight nonpositivity/unstable effective sample size, fewer than 100 subjects or 20 component events in a required audit cell, incompatible event clocks, material follow-up loss, unstable seeds/calibration, or intervals spanning clinically important benefit and harm. Do not retune thresholds, redefine unknown, merge destination families or alter windows after outcomes are inspected. An imprecise null is not decisive refutation.

Computationally checkable claims are source binding, schema/item resolution, joins, filters, half-open availability, unknown construction, event precedence, risk sets, split integrity, CIFs, model metrics, calibration and uncertainty. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for discharge rationale, goals of care, treatment limitation, chronic oxygen indication, work of breathing, functional status, clinician intent, latent physiology, causal discharge/monitoring effects and external validation.

## Alternatives, selection and resources

The fixed-summary B is selected as primary because the all-eligible unknown state, competing risks, density audit and weights must be auditable. The GRU T is retained because order, persistence, resolution and observation bursts are scientifically relevant rivals that B loses; its purpose is not a small predictive contest. A transformer is deferred because the question is a small temporal-boundary test and added capacity would not distinguish a new explanation. A causal discharge-policy model is deferred because no validated intervention assignment, intent or complete treatment-limitation field is available. A mechanistic latent physiologic model is deferred because work of breathing, functional status, total-body fluid state and physiologic ground truth are unavailable. Revisit these alternatives only with reproducible chronology beyond order permutation, clinical adjudication, richer measurements or an external cohort—not an isolated AUROC increase.

Measured discovery evidence inherited from the parent: streaming the 10.55-GB archive hash/header probe on one CPU completed in 13.1 seconds; a selected dictionary probe completed in 0.54 seconds. These were binding diagnostics, not cohort scans or model fits. Future solver planning estimates (not measured): archive scans/materialization and B on 2–4 CPUs with 8–16 GiB; 1,000 subject-bootstrap resamples and three small GRU seeds fit within the inherited planning envelope of up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM and 28,800 seconds, subject to compiler recheck against inputs.json. CPU-first is appropriate for the tabular baseline and small GRU; an allocated A100 is optional only if repeated sequence fits become the bottleneck. If used, request one GPU and use visible cuda:0; ordinary-shell CUDA absence is not evidence against feasibility. No GPU job, solver training or clinical adjudication was run in discovery.

The actual deliverable is newly estimated all-eligible state-specific and unknown-state CIFs, observation/severity sensitivity outputs, the paired late-minus-early boundary contrast, transition contrasts, and held-out B/T calibration/Brier/uncertainty outputs. Completion is not a package, readiness probe, model fit alone or favorable hypothesis result.
