# Observation-adjusted renal/feed signal for safer insulin-infusion monitoring

Status: episode-4 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process study, not a causal treatment-effect or alert-benefit study.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe six-hour eICU person-period cohort and complete a two-stage analysis:

1. estimate the probability that a glucose will be observed in the next six hours, and estimate detected low-glucose risk both unweighted and with stabilized inverse-probability-of-observation weights; and
2. test whether renal worsening plus loss of qualifying documented nutrition adds a clinically meaningful, transportable monitoring signal beyond insulin intensity, recent glucose values, and recent glucose-testing history.

Completion requires a frozen cohort/data dictionary and flow diagram; raw-label/unit/interface audit; eligible-period, observation, event and competing-event counts; estimates and uncertainty for the renal/feed interaction and standardized risk contrasts; ascertainment-model diagnostics and weight summaries; held-out-hospital predictions and calibration; fixed-alert-burden decision curves for the baseline and enriched models; temporal-model ablations; and all prespecified falsifications. These outputs establish that the computation was completed, not that the hypothesis is true or that an alert improves care.

## Unresolved question, strongest evidence, and hypothesis

The strongest available evidence is limited to the existence of timestamped ICU records. The verified raw sources contain recorded insulin-infusion updates, glucose and creatinine laboratory rows, structured intake/output labels, EOL offsets, and hospital/stay identifiers. A bounded raw audit also observed the exact infusion label `Insulin (units/hr)`, bedside-glucose and glucose labels, creatinine, explicit unit/interface fields, and nutrition-related labels such as P.O., tube-feeding flush and insulin volume. These records do not prove drug administration, delivered calories, true feeding interruption, symptomatic or clinically confirmed hypoglycemia, KDIGO AKI, or clinician intent.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults in their first ICU stay with a recent valid glucose and a numeric recorded insulin-infusion update, renal worsening in the preceding 24 hours plus no qualifying documented nutrition delivery in the preceding 12 hours predicts a higher risk of a newly observed glucose <70 mg/dL in the next six hours, beyond current glucose-surveillance history; the joint risk contrast remains clinically meaningful after adjustment for the probability of glucose observation and transports to held-out hospitals.

The primary detected-event outcome is the first valid glucose <70 mg/dL in (t,t+6]. Secondary thresholds are <54 mg/dL and recurrent low-glucose burden. A window with no glucose measurement is not a negative hypoglycemia outcome. The primary estimand is the observation-adjusted detected-event risk contrast under the explicit assumption that, conditional on recorded history through t, the observation model captures the relevant selection into glucose measurement. The unweighted detected-event estimand is reported alongside it. Neither is the incidence of unobserved hypoglycemia.

The clinical advance is narrower but more decision-relevant than a static association: it tests whether a renal/feed-context marker identifies periods for prospective chart review or intensified monitoring after accounting for the fact that measurement itself is a clinical decision. The study must not recommend a dose change, feed restart, or monitoring interval from retrospective data alone.

## Population and time

Use the frozen eICU v2.0 snapshot `[source checksum]`, with offsets in minutes from ICU admission.

Primary population: age >=18 (retain documented >89 as 90), valid identifiers and discharge fields, first ICU unit stay (smallest valid `unitvisitnumber`) per `uniquepid`, and a numeric `Insulin (units/hr)` infusion update in [t-6,t) plus at least one valid glucose on or before t. Decision times are t = 24, 30, ..., 66 hours after ICU admission. Follow-up ends at the earliest of t+6, ICU discharge or death. Death and alive discharge before the six-hour endpoint are competing events; periods without adequate follow-up are censored. Exclude a care-plan EOL save or discussion at or before t in the primary analysis, then repeat with an EOL indicator. Exclude periods within 12 hours of discharge as a sensitivity analysis. A secondary analysis retains later stays with patient-clustered uncertainty.

No predictor may use a value after t. Use `labresultoffset` as event time, not `labresultrevisedoffset`; suppress duplicate/revision records rather than counting revisions as new clinical events. The primary glucose outcome is the first valid result in (t,t+6]. Define “glucose observed” before examining its value as any valid, unit-validated glucose result in that window. Pre-index glucose history ends at t and includes last value, minimum, mean, slope, count and time since last measurement.

Renal worsening is the latest creatinine in (t-6,t] minus the latest creatinine in [t-30,t-18], requiring both values. Report continuous change and >=0.3 mg/dL and >=1.5-fold sensitivity definitions; this is not adjudicated AKI. Qualifying documented nutrition is a positive numeric P.O./oral, enteral/tube-feeding, parenteral or TPN delivery label after an audited codebook. Exclude flushes, medication volume, insulin volume and dextrose-only rows. “Documented feed loss” means no qualifying delivery in (t-12,t] after at least one qualifying positive delivery earlier in the same ICU stay. Retain separate no-interface-evidence and nutrition-coverage categories; never call this true feeding cessation.

## Baseline, enriched model and substantive alternative

Freeze cohort and codebooks before outcome modeling.

The primary transparent comparison is:

- B0: pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific model with hospital fixed effects or documented random intercept; patient/stay-clustered uncertainty; insulin rate/update density, prior glucose values and measurement gaps, demographics, weight, admission source/unit, early severity/support, vasopressor context, EOL and coverage variables; no renal/feed interaction block.
- B1: B0 plus renal change, documented-feed-loss, coverage category and a prespecified renal-change × documented-feed-loss interaction.

For both B0 and B1, fit an observation model for any valid glucose in (t,t+6] using only variables known at t. Estimate detected-event models unweighted and with stabilized inverse-probability-of-observation weights, with prespecified truncation and diagnostics. Separate death and alive-discharge heads. Standardize the joint-versus-neither six-hour detected-risk contrast at the empirical covariate distribution, report risk differences/ratios and the interaction on logit and risk-difference scales. Compare sensitivity, PPV, calibration and alerts per 100 decision times at a fixed alert burden (for example, the B0 development-set alert rate, frozen before transport evaluation); any threshold is a policy-analysis parameter, not a clinical cutoff.

The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over 12 one-hour bins before t, hidden size 32 or 64 selected only in development. It uses the same insulin, glucose, creatinine/BUN/potassium, vital, nutrition/coverage, explicit missingness and static inputs, with hypoglycemia, glucose-observation, death and discharge heads. Compare full, physiology-only and observation-only ablations. Its scientific purpose is to test whether temporal ordering and observation-process information change the renal/feed contrast and transport calibration, not to reward a black-box AUROC gain. If event counts or hospital counts cannot support stable temporal folds, retain B0/B1 descriptive estimates and label the learned comparison inconclusive.

Split entire hospitals approximately 60/20/20 into development, validation and held-out transport folds, keeping all stays for a `uniquepid` together. Fit preprocessing, observation models, weights and thresholds only in development; early-stop on validation Brier score and use one validation calibration step. Use patient/stay-clustered bootstrap for coefficients and hospital-aware bootstrap for transport, recording resamples and small-hospital limitations.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required falsifications are: insulin update-time permutation within stay preserving rates; renal/nutrition-history shuffling within hospital; a pre-index placebo low-glucose window after excluding prior lows; glucose-density/gap adjustment and laboratory-only versus bedside-only strata; strict prior-delivery, complete-observable nutrition, direct-unit and no-carry-forward analyses; insulin-free analogous renal × documentation periods; future-value timestamp audit; and leave-one-hospital-out comparison with and without observation-process channels. A negative-control outcome is a newly observed non-glucose laboratory result in the same window; a strong renal/feed association with all laboratory observation, without a specific low-glucose contrast, is evidence of surveillance artifact rather than support.

Support requires adequate unit/label coverage; positive, monotone B1 joint-versus-neither contrast above a prespecified two-percentage-point six-hour risk-difference target; a nonzero interaction with 95% uncertainty excluding zero; incremental calibration/alert-burden value beyond B0; agreement of the learned profile contrast with B1; no single-hospital dominance; acceptable weights and held-out calibration; and failure of the time/placebo/negative-control falsifications. This supports a transportable prognostic monitoring signal suitable for chart-reviewed prospective evaluation.

Adverse evidence is a narrow null below the target, reversal, disappearance after observation weighting or strict labels, poor held-out calibration, an effect confined to one hospital/interface, similar prediction of the negative-control observation outcome, or reproduction in placebo/insulin-free periods. This refutes the measurable marker in this snapshot or identifies documentation/surveillance artifact; it does not prove insulin or feed interruption harmless.

Inconclusive evidence is too few paired renal/feed periods or events, unreliable labels/units, unstable weights, unsupported hospital folds, intervals spanning both null and the target, poor competing-event calibration, or temporal-model nonconvergence. It should trigger data-quality or prospective-study work, not a forced conclusion.

The data cannot establish true insulin administration, calories delivered, feed cessation, clinician intent, symptomatic/confirmed hypoglycemia, AKI, or benefit/harm from an alert or treatment change. Those claims require medication administration records, calorie/feed-stop data, point-of-care confirmation, chart adjudication, and a prospective or emulated-intervention study. A causal effect of intensified monitoring remains unavailable.
