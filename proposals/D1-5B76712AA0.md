# HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B validation with event/source-specific observability horizons

## Targeted two-parent successor and preserved scientific question

This targeted successor crosses the valid mature differential-Y design in `[prior hypothesis]` with the discharge/transfer availability repair in `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the frozen adult HCC repeat-TACE cohort, repeat-event selection, B/P/event/Y clocks, annual forward blocks, locked nested ridge models, primary observed-case estimand, assay-separated missing-Y partial-identification bounds, bootstrap target, and noncausal evidence boundary. The only substantive change is a more conservative, executable observability bridge for discharge and downstream recorded-care sources.

The primary falsifiable question remains unchanged:

> Among selected HCC repeat-TACE episodes with remote baseline assay B and pre-repeat assay P, does P improve held-out prediction of the subsequent same-assay laboratory value Y beyond B and the locked pre-event core?

The added observability question is deliberately narrower:

> Among BP-eligible episodes, how do assay-specific Y observation and later recorded system activity vary by temporally valid, outcome-blind administrative states, and which event/source windows are actually observable under a patient-event-specific horizon rule?

This can establish only recorded-data availability patterns. It cannot establish completed care, discharge disposition, death, transfer, external-care capture, assay completion, treatment delivery, patient-important outcomes, utility, causality, or clinical benefit.

## Evidence-supported versus untested claims

Existing full-source reconstruction supports feasibility of a selected short-horizon prognostic comparison and outcome-blind observation diagnostics. It does not establish that P is incrementally predictive, that missing Y is missing at random, that either assay is clinically interpretable across patients, or that a recorded downstream event represents care received. Existing local counts include 319 systemic-record and 1,491 comparator patients, 159 and 526 selected repeat events, 242 BP-eligible episodes per assay, and 2022/later calendar-block ascertainment differences; those are inherited audit facts, not new results from this successor.

The untested claim is robustness of any apparent P increment to differential assay observation and administrative recording availability. A favorable complete-case increment alone remains selection-sensitive. The observability bridge is a falsification and interpretation safeguard, not a new outcome and not an attempt to make administrative records clinically meaningful.

## Outcome-blind opportunity and discharge descriptors

For every assay-specific BP row, define `D_y = event_time + 72 hours`. Before inspecting the target Y value or any source rows after `D_y`, freeze the inherited mutually exclusive opportunity strata using only outcome-blind timing/presence:

1. `exam+followup`: valid examination `开始时间` in (event,D_y] plus an additional distinct encounter with valid `就诊时间` in the same interval;
2. `exam_only`: exam but no additional timed encounter;
3. `followup_only`: additional timed encounter but no exam;
4. `document_only`: nonempty linked document for the selected encounter, with neither timed indicator;
5. `none`: none of the preceding conditions.

Documents have no native timestamp. A document linked to an additional encounter may be called `encounter_timed_document_presence`, never a document-time event. These strata are retained unchanged from the mature differential-observation design and cannot replace discharge states.

For the selected event encounter, parse `出院时间` only if finite and not earlier than valid `入院时间` when both exist. Define an administrative descriptor with explicit equality handling:

* `< event_time`: `discharge_before_event`;
* `== event_time`: `discharge_at_event`;
* `(event_time, D_y]`: `discharge_in_Y_window`;
* `> D_y`: `discharge_after_Y_window`;
* absent, malformed, or pre-admission-invalid: `discharge_missing_or_invalid`.

A discharge timestamp is not disposition, alive status, death, transfer, complete follow-up, or proof that Y could not be measured. It never excludes, censors, reweights, imputes, or changes any BP row. There is no configured death field/table: emit `death_status=unavailable` globally and by block, and never infer death from absent encounters or document text. A nominal transfer key match is `nominal_transfer_key_present` only; its semantic value remains `transfer_payload_unavailable`, never a timed transfer.

## Event-specific, source-specific horizons and conservative completeness

The prior snapshot-maximum rule is replaced. For each BP event, assay, origin/block, source, and downstream window, construct a **source-specific patient-event horizon** from valid timestamps in that source for that patient, not from a global dataset maximum.

For each source, declare its eligible timestamp expression and semantic label before scanning outcomes:

* encounters: `就诊时间` for additional-encounter capture; selected event encounter excluded;
* labs: `检验时间`, with exact albumin and exact bilirubin kept separate; target-Y rows and all rows used to construct frozen B/P/Y clocks excluded as coverage anchors;
* examinations: `开始时间`;
* orders: first valid among `开立时间`, `开始时间`, `结束时间` under a fixed precedence;
* medications: valid start/end timestamp;
* procedures: valid start/end timestamp;
* clinical_documents: no native timestamp, so only nonempty rows linked to a valid encounter `就诊时间` may be counted as encounter-timed document presence and their horizon is inherited from encounter capture;
* transfers, vitals, and front_page: no usable temporal payload, therefore horizon unavailable and no timed care state may be generated.

For a source/window with endpoint `W_end`, define `source_horizon_reaches_window=1` only if the source has a valid timestamp field and, for that patient-event, at least one eligible source timestamp `t >= W_end`, after removing the selected event encounter where applicable and removing target-Y/clock-defining rows where applicable. This is a necessary administrative observability condition, not proof of continuous capture, patient-level complete follow-up, or absence of outside care. A source with no independent eligible anchor is `administratively_incomplete`; its missing recorded flag is not a zero. A dataset-wide maximum is never substituted for the event-specific horizon.

Use the horizon that corresponds to the window actually being reported. For D1=(event+72 hours,event+7 days), require a source-specific anchor at or beyond event+7 days. For D2=[event+7 days,event+30 days], require a source-specific anchor at or beyond event+30 days. Do not label a D1 observation complete merely because a source reaches D2 unless both the source and the relevant endpoint are explicitly reported. For Y opportunity, separately report a horizon at `D_y`; Y itself remains the frozen target and cannot be used as a source-completeness anchor.

Serialize a field-availability table before outcome interpretation with source, table, column/expression, nonempty count, valid timestamp count, min/max valid timestamp, duplicate-key count, event-specific horizon rule, and semantic status. For every source/event/window report `has_valid_time_field`, eligible-anchor count, horizon endpoint, horizon flag, and administrative status. If required fields are absent, if target rows were used as anchors, or if the parser/endpoint closure differs from this contract, fail closed.

Interpretation precedence is:

1. `unsupported` for source/schema/hash/header/clock/join failure, target leakage into horizon, an unavailable death/transfer/disposition field silently inferred, or invalid parser semantics;
2. `inconclusive` for failed BP/arm gates, sparse cells, nonfinite predictions, empty/degenerate training range, future Y outside the training range, non-estimable SD/bootstrap, or incomplete event/source horizon for the requested recording contrast;
3. `adverse_primary` for a nonpositive adequately supported primary observed gain, repeated M1 degradation, or an all-BP global bound wholly nonpositive; no downstream pattern rescues it;
4. `selection_sensitive` for favorable complete-case gain with a global bound crossing zero, material assay/opportunity/discharge gradients, source disagreement, strong calendar/arm heterogeneity, or a negative-control warning;
5. `supported_recording_description` only for a finite, reproducible, gate-passing recording vector with complete relevant administrative horizons. This label is a description of recorded data, not support for benefit, completed care, or actionability.

For the frozen primary prognostic claim, supportive language remains allowed only if the inherited observed-case estimand is estimable, both support gates pass, no material differential-observation warning exists, and the global all-BP Delta interval is strictly positive throughout the declared training-range scenario. Even then it supports only selected short-horizon prognostic association in the tested calendar mixture. A local 2022 result cannot be generalized to later blocks without adequate support and horizon/observation robustness.

A larger recorded fraction among Y-missing episodes may represent delayed testing, in-system reconciliation, or selection; a smaller fraction may represent no captured contact, outside care, or incomplete source coverage. The configured data cannot distinguish these mechanisms. A discharge contrast is not mortality, readmission, safety, or outcome evidence, and a null contrast does not prove equivalent care.
