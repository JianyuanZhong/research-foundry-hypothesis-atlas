# Provisional hypothesis: hypotension duration versus nadir for early AKI in eICU

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in eICU with a qualifying ICU stay and at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during the first 24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of new or worsening acute kidney injury during the next 48 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Clinical importance and unresolved question
A single nadir may be transient or artifact-prone, whereas sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved question is whether duration and trajectory add clinically meaningful information about subsequent AKI beyond the nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, measurement frequency, or treatment response. Resolving this could inform whether early AKI risk assessment should use duration/trajectory rather than a single minimum value.

## Falsifiable test and analysis
Define exposure onset at the first qualifying hypotensive measurement in the first 24 hours. Compare cumulative minutes below 65 mmHg through 24 hours, with nadir as the primary comparator, against AKI onset in the subsequent 48 hours. Use a prespecified time-aware split or hospital-held-out evaluation, with a simple adjusted model using nadir and baseline severity as the baseline and a duration/trajectory model as the substantive alternative. Report effect estimates and uncertainty, calibration/discrimination only as secondary descriptive outputs, and sensitivity analyses for measurement frequency, vasopressor timing, missingness, and transient single readings.

Supportive results would require a reproducible duration association and incremental information beyond nadir in the held-out evaluation, with uncertainty compatible with a clinically meaningful effect. Adverse results would be a null or reversed association, loss after artifact/severity controls, or no incremental information. Inconclusive results include wide intervals, inadequate event/measurement counts, or sensitivity to plausible exposure definitions. None establishes causality or a treatment threshold.
