# Episode 8 successor: does transfer-proximal worsening add information beyond current burden and charting intensity?

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`, assessed valid and assigned by the Lead.

This is a substantive successor, not a generic prediction contest. It preserves the parent’s clinically important ICU-transition estimand but makes the trajectory contrast exact: worsening is the change in observed physiologic abnormality from the preceding 24 hours to the final 24 hours, tested after adjustment for final burden, final last values, and measurement density in both windows. The final-day burden contrast remains a prespecified secondary benchmark.

## Unresolved question, evidence boundary, and clinical advance

An ICU transfer removes continuous bedside observation and some rescue capacity. A patient may have the same final vital sign as another patient but be improving, stable, or deteriorating over the preceding day. The unresolved question is whether deterioration that is proximal to `outtime` identifies a clinically distinct risk state beyond how abnormal the patient is at the end, what the latest recorded values are, and how intensively the patient was charted.

The expert seed “Residual instability before ICU discharge” proposes fluctuating vital signs or high oxygen requirements before ICU discharge in relation to ICU return or in-hospital death. That seed is an untested question, not evidence. The parent established the available MIMIC-IV bindings and a final-day repeated-burden experiment. This child advances it by separating:

1. final burden (`F`);
2. preceding-window burden (`P`);
3. transfer-proximal change (`Delta = F - P`);
4. final and preceding measurement intensity.

The strongest evidence-supported claim before execution is only that the frozen MIMIC-IV 3.1 snapshot contains the linked structured tables, timestamps, ICU chart events, dictionary labels, ICU stays, transfers, and in-hospital death/discharge fields needed to construct these quantities. No available evidence establishes that `Delta` is independent of ascertainment, predicts a competing-risk endpoint in this cohort, or identifies a safe or modifiable discharge policy.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among adults whose first eligible ICU stay in a hospital admission ends before the admission ends, a higher transfer-proximal change in the fraction of covered four-hour blocks with an abnormal valid vital observation (`Delta = F-P`) is associated with higher seven-day cumulative incidence of first later ICU return and death before ICU return, and adds clinically interpretable locked-test information beyond final burden, final last values, and charting density in both 24-hour windows.

This is a prognostic association and incremental-prediction hypothesis at ICU `outtime`. It is not a causal claim that discharge caused the outcome, that delaying discharge would help, or that oxygen treatment or monitoring should be changed.

The current public search context is not treated as proof of novelty or truth. The parent records a frozen Europe PMC search/acquisition concerning a 2026 MIMIC post-valve-replacement ICU-readmission benchmark (DOI `10.3390/diagnostics16030447`, source ID `[source checksum]`). That benchmark is relevant to endpoint importance and leakage control but does not establish this narrower change-beyond-current-burden question. A current Europe PMC query in this episode returned heterogeneous abstracts rather than a directly comparable, validated trajectory-versus-density result. The three research-ambition demonstrations are methodological context only; their modalities and claims are not imported, and the cancer demonstration’s unavailable main article/full STAR Methods are not claimed to have been read.

## Cohort and frozen temporal order

All timestamp parsing is timezone-naive and uses one explicit datetime convention. Cohort/index selection occurs before any chart-event feature construction or future-outcome inspection.

1. Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`. Record unmatched keys and impossible chronology.

2. Define eligible index-stay candidates as adults (`anchor_age >= 18`) with nonmissing ICU `intime` and `outtime`, nonmissing hospital `dischtime`, `outtime < dischtime`, ICU LOS at least one hour, and `deathtime` missing or strictly later than `outtime`. An index that has already died by `outtime` is not eligible.

3. Within each `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, sort candidates by `intime`, then numeric `stay_id`, and choose exactly the earliest eligible stay as the one primary index. This decision uses no chart events or future outcomes. Later ICU stays are retained only for outcome chronology and the all-eligible-stay sensitivity. Every eligible and ineligible candidate remains in an audit manifest.

4. Set `t0 = outtime`. Define two half-open, contiguous 24-hour windows, each split into six four-hour bins:
   - preceding window A: `[t0 - 48 hours, t0 - 24 hours));
   - final window B: `[t0 - 24 hours, t0)).
   A row at exactly `t0 - 24 hours` belongs to B; a row at exactly `t0` is excluded. Retain only rows with the selected index `stay_id`; matching subject/admission keys with a different stay are excluded. `storetime` never alters this order.

5. Build an all-index manifest first. The primary paired trajectory analysis requires at least four of six covered vital bins in both A and B. This is a prespecified measurement-eligibility estimand, not an event-driven filter. Indexes failing the paired gate are retained and compared by outcome, care unit, anchor-year group, total chart density, and reasons for failure. A secondary all-eligible prediction analysis uses missing indicators and development-only imputation for A/B summaries rather than silently excluding sparse records.

## Fixed feature construction and trajectory estimand

Use `valuenum` for numeric summaries. Fixed validity rules are applied before all summaries:

- HR 20–250; RR 4–80; SpO2 50–100; each MAP 20–200 mmHg.
- Temperature 30–43 °C after converting Fahrenheit as `(F - 32) / 1.8`.
- Oxygen flows are nonnegative.
- FiO2 accepts 0.21–1.00 as a fraction or values in (1,100] as percentages divided by 100; normalized values outside 0.21–1.00 are invalid.

Invalid values are counted by item and window/bin and excluded from derived features. They are never silently repaired. A covered vital bin contains at least one valid HR, MAP, RR, SpO2, or temperature row. An abnormal vital is HR <50 or >100, RR <8 or >24, SpO2 <92%, MAP <65 mmHg, temperature <36 °C or >=38 °C. Arterial and non-invasive MAP remain separate; combined MAP is the minimum valid MAP within a bin.

For each window (w \in \{A,B\}), define:

`covered_w = number of bins with at least one valid vital`

`abnormal_w = number of covered bins with at least one abnormal valid vital`

`burden_w = abnormal_w / covered_w`

Use `P = burden_A`, `F = burden_B`, and primary trajectory exposure `Delta = F - P`. Higher `Delta` means more observed abnormality in the transfer-proximal window. The primary model includes `F` and `Delta`, so the trajectory coefficient is not allowed to substitute for current burden. It is interpreted as the information in the preceding-to-final change conditional on the final state. Do not add `P` as a separate predictor in this primary nested contrast because `F` and `Delta` already define the planned change decomposition; report `P` descriptively and in the all-eligible sensitivity.

For every window save only aggregate features: covered/abnormal bin counts, `burden_w`, six-bin coverage and abnormality vectors, total valid/invalid rows, per-domain row counts, per-domain abnormal indicators, and missingness indicators. The six-bin vectors are aggregate derived data, not raw clinical rows.

For each numeric domain in B, the last-value snapshot is the median of valid observations at the latest `charttime`; if multiple rows share that latest time, use their median. Keep missing-domain indicators. Save separate arterial MAP, non-invasive MAP, and combined MAP. Oxygen summaries are maximum valid flow over the three flow IDs and maximum normalized FiO2, plus item-specific observed indicators. Device and code-status text are used only as observed/not-observed indicators and restricted aggregate frequency audits; no text value is translated into a validated support mode, treatment limitation, or goals-of-care label.

## Outcomes and competing risks

For every index define `tau = min(t0 + 7 days, dischtime)`; events at `tau` are included. A candidate ICU return is a later ICU stay with the same `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, a different `stay_id`, and nonmissing `intime >= t0). Select the earliest by `intime`, then numeric `stay_id`. A later stay whose `intime < t0` is an overlap/chronology anomaly, not a return.

- Cause 1: first qualifying ICU return by `tau), provided it is strictly earlier than qualifying death.
- Cause 2: `admissions.deathtime > t0` and `deathtime <= tau), provided it is strictly earlier than the first qualifying ICU return.
- If return and death have exactly the same timestamp, label the index tie/indeterminate and exclude it from the primary competing-risk fit; report fixed sensitivities assigning all ties to return and all ties to death.
- No event by `tau` is an observed no-cause record for this same-admission horizon. Death after a return is not cause 2.
- The primary endpoint is the composite of causes 1 and 2. Descriptive sensitivities use 12-hour and 48-hour horizons and follow-up through hospital `dischtime`.

This is explicitly a same-hospital-admission endpoint. If a patient is discharged alive before seven days without a qualifying return, follow-up ends at `dischtime`; no outpatient ICU return is observable in this design.

## Nested baselines and analysis

Use exactly the same index manifest, labels, preprocessing, subject partition and locked test subjects for every model:

- `B0`: gender, anchor_age, admission_type, admission_location, insurance, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU LOS, hours from `admittime` to `t0`, and number of earlier ICU stays in the admission ending by `t0`; missing indicators included. `anchor_year_group` is either included in every model as a categorical covariate or omitted from every model; it cannot be used as a time split.
- `B_last`: `B0) plus final-window last valid HR, separate/combined MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, oxygen flow and FiO2, their observed/missing indicators.
- `B_density2`: `B_last) plus final and preceding six-bin vital coverage counts/vectors, total valid-vital rows, per-domain valid-row counts, oxygen observation indicators, and fixed `log1p) counts in both windows. This is the primary ascertainment-aware baseline.
- `B_final`: `B_density2) plus final burden `F) and prespecified final per-domain abnormal indicators. This tests whether current residual burden adds value beyond last values and both-window density.
- `B_traj`: `B_final) plus `Delta` only. This is the primary incremental contrast. No oxygen-intensity maximum is added to `B_traj); oxygen remains a secondary bundled chart signal.
- `B_oxygen`: `B_traj) plus final and preceding maximum flow/FiO2 features. It is interpreted only as additional charted-oxygen information, not true delivered oxygen physiology.

Fit two ridge-penalized cause-specific Cox models (first ICU return; death before return). Select penalties by inner development cross-validation only. Derive competing-risk cumulative-incidence predictions from the two fitted cause-specific hazards. Report nonparametric Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence and model-based cause-specific associations, but never treat the competing cause as an ordinary noninformative censoring event in the reported cumulative-incidence target.

Use a subject-level SHA-256 partition of 60% development, 20% validation, and 20% locked test; all admissions and stays for a subject stay in one partition. Fit imputation, scaling, one-hot encoding, penalties, and any calibration mapping using development/validation rules only. The locked test is untouched until final evaluation. No post-`t0` field, future charting, discharge destination, hospital-expire flag, or outcome-derived cut point may enter a predictor.

The primary incremental estimand is the paired locked-test difference in seven-day composite competing-risk Brier score for `B_traj - B_final`, plus calibration intercept/slope and absolute predicted-risk difference between prespecified observed `Delta` percentiles. Report cause-specific coefficient estimates per development-cohort SD of `Delta` and final burden, with subject-clustered uncertainty. The final-burden contrast `B_final - B_density2` and oxygen contrast `B_oxygen - B_traj` are secondary.

Use 1,000 fixed-seed subject-bootstrap resamples of the locked test subjects for paired 95% intervals of Brier, calibration, and absolute-risk differences; use a subject-clustered bootstrap or robust estimator for coefficients. Require, before fitting:

- total-cohort gate: at least 1,000 paired-primary indexes, at least 100 composite events, and at least 60% of all eligible indexes with at least four covered bins in each window;
- locked-test gate: at least 20 composite events and at least 10 of each cause when cause-specific inference is reported.

A failed gate is reported as infeasible/sparse; do not lower thresholds, change the window, or substitute an easier model. If a cause-specific gate fails, report descriptive Aalen–Johansen estimates and uncertainty only for that cause. If paired eligibility is too sparse but all-eligible prediction is feasible, label the two estimands separately and do not present the all-eligible imputed result as equivalent to the paired trajectory association.

The reference execution is CPU-only: request up to 4 CPUs, 16 GiB memory, and a 7,200-second timeout, within `inputs.json` (`science_seconds=7200`). First read the small cohort/outcome members; then make one sequential projection pass through `icu/chartevents.csv.gz) inside the ZIP for the fixed item IDs and the fixed A/B windows. Do not materialize full `chartevents`, make one archive pass per item, or write raw rows/notes. Output aggregate manifests, model summaries, audit counts, and a machine-readable conclusion ledger only. This is an execution contract, not a computed result.

## Falsification and integrity criteria

Fail closed on: duplicate primary index; any selected feature row with `charttime >= t0) or outside A/B; use of `storetime` for eligibility; mismatched `stay_id); impossible admission/ICU/death chronology; event ties handled inconsistently; subject leakage across partitions; preprocessing fit on validation/test; prohibited post-index predictors; or conclusions not linked to output rows.

Required falsification/ascertainment checks:

1. **Earlier-window placebo.** Construct the identical two-window change contrast one day earlier: A0 = `[t0-72h,t0-48h)`, B0 = `[t0-48h,t0-24h)`, holding the primary index, outcomes, validity rules, density features, partitions, and model recipe fixed. A similar or stronger `Delta) signal there weakens the claim that worsening is transfer-proximal.

2. **Density stress test.** Compare `B_last`, `B_density2`, and `B_final`; report whether the final burden association is attenuated by density. The primary result cannot be called physiologic if `B_traj) adds no value over `B_final), or if it disappears when both-window density is included.

3. **Current-burden matching interpretation.** Report `Delta) associations and absolute-risk predictions within prespecified development-independent final-burden strata (quartile cut points learned in development only). A signal visible only because `F) is imbalanced is not evidence that worsening adds information at comparable current burden. This is an interpretation check, not a new primary estimand.

4. **Outcome-label permutation.** Permute endpoint labels within locked subject partitions while preserving exposure coverage and the observed cause proportions; repeated locked performance should return to chance within bootstrap uncertainty. Failure invalidates the computation and its conclusion, not the clinical hypothesis.

5. **Definition robustness.** Repeat with arterial and non-invasive MAP separately, abnormal valid-observation fraction rather than abnormal-bin fraction, fixed 2-hour exclusion of returns immediately after `t0`, 12/48-hour horizons, equal-time tie assignments, and the all-eligible imputed prediction sensitivity. Large directional or calibration divergence is reported as definition/selection dependence.

6. **Documentation audit.** Compare A/B burden, density, invalid-value rates, and paired-gate failure across outcome, first/last care unit, anchor-year group, and subject partition. Audit code-status and device-value frequencies without classifying them clinically. A strong association confined to highly charted records is an ascertainment warning.

7. **Chronology and permutation audit.** Confirm that a shifted earlier window never reads rows from B, that the same index and outcome manifest is reused, and that all permutation outputs are generated from the locked recipe rather than refit choices.

Supportive results require all of the following:

- `Delta) has a prespecified positive or monotonic association with the primary composite and directionally compatible cause-specific results in the locked test;
- `B_traj) improves seven-day competing-risk calibration and/or Brier score over `B_final), with a paired interval excluding no improvement and an absolute-risk separation across observed `Delta) values that is not merely a negligible discrimination change;
- the result persists when both-window density is included, is not materially explained by the earlier-window placebo, and is not erased by MAP-source, return-gap, tie, or paired/all-eligible checks;
- current-burden-stratified predictions still show a trajectory gradient, and label permutation returns to chance.

These criteria support the narrower claim that the recorded change phenotype carries reproducible prognostic information beyond the specified recorded current state and ascertainment proxies. They do not support a discharge threshold, transfer policy, causal effect, or delivered-oxygen interpretation.

Adverse results include null or reversed `Delta) association, no incremental Brier/calibration value over `B_final), a placebo signal as strong or stronger, attenuation after density adjustment, current-burden-stratum disappearance, or major instability across MAP/source/eligibility definitions. They would argue that transfer-proximal worsening adds no reliable information beyond current recorded burden and charting intensity, or that it is chiefly a general-acuity/documentation proxy. They do not establish that ICU transfer is safe.

Inconclusive results include failed cohort/coverage/event gates, sparse paired windows, unstable bootstrap intervals, inability to distinguish final burden from density, contradictory competing-risk or placebo results, or a failed permutation/integrity check. A failed permutation or integrity check makes the computation invalid; it is not evidence against the clinical hypothesis.

## What the data cannot establish

Computationally checkable claims include source/member/schema availability, fixed item labels, exact index construction, A/B timestamp eligibility, valid/invalid row counts, burden/change/density derivation, event labels and ties, competing-risk estimates, locked-test metrics, uncertainty, missingness, sensitivity outputs, and the conclusion ledger.

MIMIC cannot adjudicate true bedside instability, work of breathing, delivered FiO2, oxygen device actually in use, functional/cognitive readiness, staffing, ward capacity, clinician discharge intent, treatment limitations, or the clinical reason for ICU return. Code-status/device text is not a validated substitute. The endpoint captures only a same-admission ICU return or death through `min(t0+7 days, dischtime)); outpatient events and readmission reasons are unavailable. Deidentified timestamps do not support calendar-trend claims.

Observational associations cannot show that worsening caused return/death, that delaying transfer or changing treatment would change risk, or that the model has transportability, a safe threshold, prospective utility, or equitable clinical performance. Those stronger claims require clinician adjudication of returns and treatment limitation, richer prospective physiology and oxygen-delivery data, external validation, and a prospective surveillance or target-trial/interventional study.
