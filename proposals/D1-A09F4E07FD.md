# Episode 10 outcome-semantics branch

Status: substantive child proposal of [prior hypothesis]; no cohort count, fitted parameter, prediction, metric, or scientific result is claimed. This branch preserves the parent ancestry and all parent state/process estimands, and adds a prespecified endpoint/landmark interpretation test.

## Scientific question and hypothesis

The parent’s strongest evidence-supported claim is conditional and descriptive: after adding fixed-bin lab/vital value trajectories to the latest-value state, an observed lab/vital recording-process increment may still or may not transport across held-out eICU hospitals. The parent experiment can test measured temporal-state sufficiency, but its ICU-discharge mortality label remains potentially entangled with when the ICU stay ends. The available data have no reliable death timestamp and no adjudicated fixed-horizon vital-status field.

The unresolved clinical claim is narrower:

Does the eICU recording-process increment remain transport-sensitive for mortality defined at hospital discharge after a prespecified 720-minute ICU landmark, or is the increment confined to the ICU-discharge label and its discharge/EOL selection?

The primary parent endpoint remains ICU-discharge mortality on P12. The new semantic test is a hospital-discharge mortality endpoint on the same early-observation design, with a competing-endpoint decomposition of ICU death versus death after ICU discharge. It is not a claim that hospital-discharge status is a non-discharge-selected gold standard. It is the closest clinically coherent alternative available in this snapshot: it moves ascertainment beyond the ICU boundary without inventing a death time. If it is unavailable, sparse, or no more independent than the ICU label, the correct conclusion is that the outcome semantics remain inseparable in eICU.

A falsifiable alternative is:

- If recording density is chiefly an ICU-discharge/outcome-timing artifact, the process increment for ICU-discharge mortality will be materially larger than for hospital-discharge mortality after the 720-minute landmark, and the signal will concentrate in the ICU-expired component or in EOL/early-discharge strata.
- If the process increment is not uniquely an ICU-discharge artifact, hospital-discharge mortality will retain a non-negligible held-out-hospital process increment, including a reproducible post-ICU-death component, after the parent’s fixed-bin trajectory state and trajectory-state process null are applied.
- Either result remains descriptive. Persistence does not prove physiology, monitoring adequacy, clinician action, treatment effect, or a causal recording mechanism.

This is an outcome/selection boundary test, not a change to P12/S720/S6, the [0,360] predictor window, or the parent’s learned representation.

## Why this matters and what it adds

A process feature that predicts ICU discharge mortality may be clinically misleading if it mainly records that a stay ended, that end-of-life planning was documented, or that observation stopped before the outcome label. Such a feature should not be treated as a transportable deterioration or monitoring signal. Conversely, if the increment survives a later hospital endpoint and appears among patients who leave the ICU alive but die before hospital discharge, the next study should validate recording semantics and outcome ascertainment across sites rather than dismissing the signal as ICU discharge coding.

[K1] supports temporally structured EHR histories and multi-center prediction, but its inspected evidence does not identify whether a recording increment is state, workflow, or endpoint selection. This child adds a bounded endpoint-semantics comparison to the parent’s fixed-bin state test.

[K2] (abstract-only) uses landmark cause-specific modeling with hospital discharge and death as competing events and warns that information presence can be predictive yet transport-sensitive. This child adapts that logic to the fields that actually exist here; it does not pretend that eICU supplies event times or adjudicated death.

[K3] supports explicit external validation, calibration, and careful outcome ascertainment in fragmented EHR data. Its richer 30-/90-day follow-up and manual review are unavailable here; this child makes that absence an explicit interpretation branch rather than silently treating a discharge field as a fixed-horizon outcome.

The substantive advance over [prior hypothesis] is therefore a direct test of whether the parent’s recording-process increment generalizes from ICU-discharge mortality to a later hospital-discharge endpoint, plus an outcome-type decomposition. It preserves the parent’s temporal-state augmentation instead of using an outcome change to rescue or replace it.

  Unknown or contradictory records are a C_UNKNOWN audit category and are not forced into a competing event. Do not use actualhospitalmortality as a substitute endpoint.

Hospital-discharge mortality is clinically more patient-centered than ICU-discharge mortality, but it is still a discharge-anchored EHR status and may reflect documentation or disposition. Therefore, even a positive Y_H result does not establish a truly non-discharge-selected outcome. A true non-discharge-selected clinical endpoint would require fixed-horizon vital status, a reliable death timestamp, registry linkage, or adjudicated follow-up, none of which is available.

## Inherited state and process design

The parent’s X and T are unchanged.

- X contains age, gender, ethnicity, admissionweight, up to 30 lab analytes selected by training-fold distinct-stay coverage (>=1% coverage), and up to 20 qualifying vital numeric channels selected by training-fold coverage and deterministic name tie-breaks. Each retained channel uses its latest valid value in [0,360], without carry-forward, with training-fold median imputation.
- T is X plus, for the same fold-selected channels, median values in [0,120), [120,240), and [240,360] (the final bin includes 360). Standardize and impute empty channel/bins with training-fold parameters; do not add missingness indicators, row counts, offsets, gap lengths, observed-bin flags, revision timing, treatment, care, discharge, or EOL fields. T therefore cannot intentionally reconstruct P.
- P retains the parent’s four explicit process blocks computed independently from values in [0,360]: lab intensity and lab gap, and vital intensity and vital gap. Intensity includes log1p(valid row count), distinct exact offsets, distinct 30-minute bins with a valid row, and retained channels observed at least once. Gap includes 360 minus last valid offset, largest empty 30-minute run, and fraction of 12 bins without a valid row. Duplicate channel/offset rows count for intensity but are set-based for bins/gaps. These are recording proxies, not confirmed orders, specimens, bedside measurements, device output, interface completeness, clinician decisions, or death timing.

Both use identical fold-isolated X/T/P preprocessing, targets, model inputs, class handling, seeds, hospital folds, and evaluation. The additive model gives direction and lab/vital block attribution. The GBM can reveal nonlinear interactions between trajectory state, process intensity/gaps, and endpoint type that an additive model loses. A GBM gain alone cannot establish a state or clinical mechanism.

Sort viable hospital IDs by SHA-256("eicu-transport-v2\\0"+hospitalid) and greedily balance S720 stays into five contiguous folds. External evaluation holds out an entire hospital fold. Within training hospitals, reserve the fixed 20% patient-hash internal reference using SHA-256("eicu-internal-v2\\0"+patientunitstayid). No patient-only split is external validation. All channel selection, medians, scaling, imputation, class handling, risk cells, and weights are training-fold-only.

For every model and endpoint, report AUC, AUPRC, Brier, calibration-in-the-large, calibration intercept/slope, alert burden, and decision-curve net benefit at 5%, 10%, and 20%. With positive values oriented as improvement:

## Falsification and interpretation

The endpoint-semantic hypothesis is supported only if all of the following are met on prespecified held-out hospitals, with directionally coherent internal results and learner agreement:

1. The alternative H endpoint has adequate observed hospital-status coverage, at least five viable hospitals, and at least 100 H events for the primary landmark analysis; otherwise the semantic result is inconclusive.
2. The parent U process increment is non-negligible where the comparison is made, so a zero H increment is not being interpreted against an absent parent signal.
3. Q_P^(T,H)(external) exceeds the inherited 0.01 AUC negligible margin with a paired uncertainty interval excluding that margin (and, where supported by discrimination, the 10% net-benefit contrast exceeds 0.005), rather than appearing only in one hospital.
4. The H increment is not reproduced by the trajectory-state null at a comparable level, and the result is not explained solely by EOL360/EOL12 or an ICU-discharge timing band.
5. The post-ICU endpoint component C_POST has sufficient events and a directionally consistent held-out result, or the study explicitly reports that this decomposition is underpowered.

This result would weaken—but not eliminate—the ICU-discharge-selection explanation and support the bounded statement that the process increment transports to a later hospital-discharge endpoint after the fixed-bin state test. It would not establish that recording density causes mortality or that a monitoring intervention would help.

The outcome-selection rival is supported if U has a positive process increment but H is below the 0.01 AUC margin with an interval excluding the margin, especially when the signal is confined to C_U, EOL360/EOL12, or early unit-discharge strata. That would make ICU discharge/outcome timing a leading explanation and should stop any claim that P is a general hospital-mortality or deterioration signal. If both U and H increments persist but are reproduced by the trajectory-state null, the likely interpretation is residual measured/unmeasured state or hospital recording practice, not a validated outcome-independent process mechanism. If H persists outside the null but is highly heterogeneous across held-out hospitals, the next step is feed/measurement validation rather than clinical deployment.

Adverse evidence is a precisely estimated absence of the process increment for both U and H after technical checks, which weakens the parent’s transport claim for this endpoint family. Inconclusive evidence includes intervals crossing the margins; fewer than five viable hospitals or 100 relevant events; low hospital-status completeness; many C_UNKNOWN or contradictory statuses; unstable hospital folds; excessive T imputation/coverage loss; unstable null-cell merges; unstable S6 weighting; Apache discordance that prevents a useful audit; or failed source/join/leakage checks. An imprecise null does not refute either mechanism.

The available data cannot distinguish death before ICU discharge from death after ICU discharge in continuous time, and cannot establish whether non-Expired means alive at a fixed time beyond the recorded discharge event. They also cannot distinguish withdrawal-of-care decisions, terminal decline, transfer documentation, delayed entry, and true physiologic deterioration. The strongest permissible conclusion is therefore a comparative transport/selection statement about recorded endpoint labels.

## Method alternatives and resource decision

The simple baseline and learned alternative are deliberately matched on the same scientific question:

- Inputs: inherited X, fixed-bin T, explicit P blocks, and no discharge/EOL/Apache feature.
- Targets: U, H, C_U, and C_POST as defined above.
- Split: fixed hospital-held-out v2 folds with the parent’s internal patient-hash reference.
- Evaluation: paired AUC/AUPRC/Brier/calibration/net benefit, per-hospital estimates, trajectory-state and inherited nulls, and 2,000 paired hospital-cluster bootstrap intervals.
- Budget: the parent’s planned future solver envelope of 16 CPUs, up to 262,144 MiB RAM, and 28,800 seconds; this is a planning estimate, not a measured full-study runtime. Discovery performed source/schema checks and literature inspection, not a fitted study.

CPU is selected because the work is gzip tabular extraction, fixed-bin aggregation, five hospital folds, two tabular learners, endpoint audits, nulls, and paired bootstrap. GPU capacity exists on the deployment, but a GPU does not resolve missing death timestamps or status semantics and is not scientifically informative here. A raw-event neural sequence model is deferred: it may capture ordering, but it would not solve the endpoint-identification problem, and the parent already specifies a fixed-bin state test. Revisit it only if a bounded diagnostic shows that fixed bins lose clinically relevant ordering for the same endpoint contrasts and all nulls/uncertainty can be preserved. A survival or formal competing-risk model is also deferred because reliable death/event times and censoring times are unavailable; an outcome-type decomposition is the valid bounded substitute. A hospital random-effects model is deferred as a primary analysis because it can absorb the site behavior that held-out evaluation is designed to expose; it may be added descriptively after stable external estimates.

## Missing evidence and verifier boundary

The available records do not provide a reliable death timestamp, fixed-horizon vital status, registry linkage, adjudicated hospital mortality, order/specimen/device/interface semantics, treatment assignment, clinician rationale, or a validated goals-of-care event. carePlanEOL is a recorded care-plan table; absent rows do not prove absence of a decision. Hospital and unit discharge statuses are documentation endpoints, not independent gold labels. Apache actualicumortality is an audit field only. No result licenses a monitoring, treatment, transfer, or goals-of-care change.

The computational verifier can check exact source paths/hashes, table columns, ordinary-file archive status, joins, offset ranges, cohort flags, endpoint label construction, status completeness handling, no post-window feature use, fold-isolated channel selection/imputation, fixed bins, process definitions, hospital-held-out folds, null assignment, predictions, metrics, bootstrap intervals, and whether conclusions cite computed outputs. It cannot adjudicate whether an endpoint status is clinically correct, whether a patient died before or after a particular time, whether a process variable is physiologic or workflow-generated, or whether a clinical intervention would improve outcomes. Those claims require chart review, timestamp/measurement validation, registry or prospective follow-up, and another study.

## Alternatives not chosen and revisit evidence

- A true fixed-horizon mortality or formal time-to-event competing-risk analysis becomes appropriate only with reliable death/follow-up timestamps and censoring ascertainment.
- External vital-status or registry linkage becomes the preferred next study if H remains positive but discharge semantics remain ambiguous.
- Manual clinical adjudication and source-level audit become necessary if hospital/unit status discordance, C_UNKNOWN, or Apache disagreement is substantial.
- A raw-event sequence or latent-trajectory model is revisited only if a bounded comparison demonstrates information loss in the three fixed bins for the same endpoint-semantic contrasts; predictive gain alone is not sufficient.
- A hospital hierarchical model is revisited only as a descriptive heterogeneity analysis after held-out results and after confirming it does not replace external evaluation.
