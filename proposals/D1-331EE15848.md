# Is early ICU hypotension duration an independent prognostic signal for 72-hour AKI after accounting for vasopressors, baseline kidney function, severity, and recording density?

Status: working proposal; no cohort analysis or model fitting has been executed.

## Question, clinical importance, and unresolved claim

Among adults with at least one usable blood-pressure observation during the first 24 ICU hours, does the proportion of observed 30-minute intervals that are hypotensive retain a positive association with KDIGO creatinine AKI during the first 72 ICU hours after adjustment for early vasopressor exposure, baseline creatinine, illness severity, nadir pressure, and recording density?

The question matters because a duration summary could be clinically useful only if it conveys information beyond how low pressure fell, how sick the patient was, how much vasopressor was required, and how often the patient was monitored. If the association disappears after these adjustments, it is more consistent with a severity or monitoring artifact than with an incremental prognostic signal. If it persists, the result supports prognostic information, not a causal effect or treatment target.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

The strongest claim supported by the inspected eICU schema is that longitudinal periodic and aperiodic blood pressures, vasopressor infusions, creatinine results, APACHE severity fields, and hospital identifiers are available and can be aligned to ICU admission. The unresolved claim is whether a duration measure remains prognostic after adjustment for the strongest available confounding and measurement explanations, with stable performance across hospitals.

## Population and temporal design

- Adults (age >= 18) with at least one usable systolic blood pressure during the first 24 ICU hours.
- Exposure: proportion of observed 30-minute intervals during hours 0–24 with SBP < 90 mmHg. Intervals without a usable BP are not imputed in the primary analysis; a sensitivity analysis uses linear interpolation across gaps <= 30 minutes.
- Outcome: KDIGO creatinine AKI during hours 0–72, using the first creatinine at or after admission as baseline and requiring a creatinine at or after hour 24 for the 2x or 3x criteria. No creatinine measured before the exposure window is used to define the outcome.
- Censoring: discharge, transfer out of ICU, or death before hour 72 is recorded; the primary analysis treats these as non-AKI only when no qualifying creatinine event is observed, with a sensitivity analysis using competing-risk or inverse-probability weighting if feasible.

## Data bindings

- Source: eICU-CRD v2.0, `datasets/eicu/` archive members and Parquet derivatives as listed in `datasets/README.md`.
- Blood pressure: periodic and aperiodic vital-sign tables, columns for systolic pressure and timestamp, joined to patient/stay by the documented patient/stay identifier.
- Vasopressors: infusion table, drug name and rate columns, timestamp, joined by patient/stay identifier.
- Creatinine: laboratory table, analyte name and value columns, timestamp, joined by patient/stay identifier.
- Severity: APACHE II component fields and admission diagnosis fields from the patient/stay table.
- Hospital: hospital identifier field for whole-hospital validation.
- Time fields: ICU admission time and record timestamps; all intervals are computed relative to admission.

## Rival explanation and falsification

The strongest rival is that duration is a proxy for illness severity, recording density, or vasopressor requirement. The primary model adjusts for APACHE severity, nadir SBP, early vasopressor exposure, baseline creatinine, and the number of BP observations per hour. A negative-control outcome is creatinine change before the exposure window; a positive association there would indicate baseline severity or measurement bias rather than forward prognostic information. A recording-density falsification test compares duration estimates under different observation-density strata.

## Methods comparison

- Baseline: severity/recording-density-adjusted logistic regression with nadir SBP, APACHE severity, baseline creatinine, early vasopressor exposure, and observation density.
- Learned alternative: monotonic gradient-boosted model with time-aware features (duration, nadir, time-to-first-hypotension, vasopressor onset) and monotonic constraints on duration and severity.
- Evaluation: whole-hospital holdout validation, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, Brier score, and decision-curve net benefit at clinically relevant thresholds. AUROC is reported but not treated as the sole criterion.

## Interpretation limits

This is a prognostic association study. It does not establish that treating to a duration threshold prevents AKI, identify a mechanism, or support a causal claim. External validation, clinical adjudication, and a prospective or interventional study would be required for those stronger conclusions.
