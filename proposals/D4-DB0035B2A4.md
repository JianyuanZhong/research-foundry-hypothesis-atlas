# Hypothesis: Latent Disease Signatures Predict Incident Cardiovascular Disease Beyond Baseline Risk

## Scientific Question

**Among UK Biobank participants, do latent disease signatures (learned from longitudinal health outcomes) predict incident cardiovascular disease (CVD) more accurately than traditional baseline risk scores, and do these signatures show differential predictive value by genetic predisposition?**

## Background and Rationale

### Existing Knowledge

Three key studies demonstrate the state-of-the-art in longitudinal disease prediction:

1. **Delphi-2M (Shmatko et al., Nature 2025)**: A transformer-based model trained on 0.4 million UK Biobank participants that predicts rates of 1,000+ diseases, capturing temporal dependencies and comorbidity clusters. However, it treats diseases in isolation and doesn't explicitly integrate genetic information [K1].

2. **ALADYNOULLI (Urbut et al., Nature 2026)**: A Bayesian framework that jointly models longitudinal EHRs with germline genetics to identify 21 replicable disease signatures. These signatures show high cross-cohort stability and alignment with established disease biology (e.g., familial hypercholesterolaemia enriches in cardiovascular signature) [K2].

3. **Oncoformer (Liu et al., Cell 2026)**: A multimodal model for cancer prediction using longitudinal clinical data, demonstrating that pre-diagnostic physiological signals can predict disease onset months to years in advance, with a 90-day washout period maintaining AUROC > 0.85 [K3].

### The Unresolved Question

While these studies demonstrate that:
- Temporal disease patterns contain predictive information beyond baseline measurements (Oncoformer)
- Genetic information can be integrated into disease modeling (ALADYNOULLI)
- Transformer-based models can predict 1,000+ diseases simultaneously (Delphi-2M)

**No study has explicitly tested whether latent disease signatures provide superior prediction of specific outcomes (e.g., CVD) compared to traditional risk scores, and whether this superiority is modulated by genetic risk stratification.**

Traditional risk scores (e.g., Pooled Cohort Equations, RECK) use baseline measurements but cannot capture:
- Dynamic risk evolution over time
- Information from multiple correlated conditions
- Genetic predisposition

## Hypothesis

**H1 (Primary):** Among UK Biobank participants, latent disease signatures derived from longitudinal health outcomes predict incident cardiovascular disease more accurately than traditional baseline risk scores, as measured by time-dependent AUROC and C-index.

**H2 (Secondary):** The predictive performance of signature-based models differs by genetic risk stratification, with higher-risk individuals showing stronger signature-disease associations.

**H3 (Exploratory):** Specific latent signatures (e.g., "metabolic dysregulation," "inflammatory response") show stronger associations with CVD than others, suggesting distinct biological pathways.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Early Detection**: Identifying high-risk individuals before traditional risk thresholds are crossed, enabling earlier intervention.

2. **Precision Prevention**: Tailoring prevention strategies based on specific biological pathways rather than aggregate risk scores.

3. **Risk Re-stratification**: Updating risk assessments as new longitudinal clinical data becomes available, rather than relying on a single baseline assessment.

4. **Biological Insights**: Understanding which underlying processes (captured by signatures) drive CVD risk, potentially revealing new therapeutic targets.

## Analysis Plan

### Baseline Method
1. Cox proportional hazards model with baseline covariates
2. Time-dependent AUROC and C-index at 1, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 years

### Signature-based Alternative
1. **Signature Learning**: Use ALADYNOULLI-style approach to identify latent signatures from longitudinal health outcomes
   - Bayesian mixture model formulation
   - 21 disease categories as mixture components
   - Age and genetic effects as covariates
2. **Model**: Hierarchical Bayesian model where:
   - Disease risk = weighted combination of signature-specific probabilities
   - Signature loadings derived from genetic effects and time-varying covariates
3. **Prediction**: Use individual signature loadings at baseline to predict incident CVD
4. **Evaluation**: Time-dependent AUROC, C-index, Brier score, calibration

### Genetic Stratification Analysis
- Stratify by top quintiles of genetic risk scores (if available)
- Compare signature-CVD associations across strata
- Test interaction between genetic risk and signature loadings

## Method Comparison

### Baseline: Cox Proportional Hazards Model
- **Approach**: Traditional survival analysis with baseline covariates
- **What it captures**: Static risk factors at a single timepoint
- **What it misses**: Temporal dynamics, information from multiple conditions, genetic predisposition
- **Computational cost**: Low (CPU-only, minutes)

### Alternative: Hierarchical Bayesian Signature Model
- **Approach**: Joint modeling of longitudinal outcomes with latent signatures and genetic effects
- **What it captures**: Temporal evolution of risk, multiple correlated conditions, genetic predisposition
- **What it misses**: May overfit if signatures don't generalize; requires complex computation
- **Computational cost**: High (GPU-accelerated Bayesian inference, hours to days)

### Scientific Uncertainty Addressed
The baseline method tests whether traditional risk factors predict CVD (expected to be true). The alternative method tests whether **dynamic, multi-condition, genetically-informed risk profiles** predict CVD better than static baseline measures. This addresses the fundamental question of whether **temporal and multi-dimensional information** provides incremental predictive value beyond traditional risk assessment.

## Missing Evidence / Clinical Adjudication Required

1. **Signature interpretation**: The learned signatures need clinical validation to ensure they represent biologically meaningful processes.

2. **Causal inference**: Observational data cannot establish causation; intervention studies would be needed to determine whether modifying signature-associated processes reduces CVD risk.

3. **External validation**: Results should be validated in independent cohorts (e.g., MIMIC-IV, eICU) to assess generalizability.

4. **Clinical utility**: Decision-curve analysis would determine whether signature-based prediction improves clinical decision-making beyond risk re-stratification.

5. **Genetic data availability**: The full ALADYNOULLI approach requires genetic data which may not be available in the UK Biobank snapshot. An EHR-only adaptation would be a scientifically distinct model with changed assumptions.
