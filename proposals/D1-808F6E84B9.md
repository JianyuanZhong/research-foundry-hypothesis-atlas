# Hidden biochemical stress despite reassuring recorded vital signs

Status: Episode-32 substantive successor to \`[prior hypothesis]\`. This is an observational, prognostic, measurement-process and transport experiment. It is not a causal test of ordering, an alert-benefit study, a hospital ranking, a treatment recommendation, or proof that laboratory surveillance changes survival. No model has been fitted in discovery.

## Unresolved question and substantive advance

The parent asks whether revision-safe laboratory information-availability timing carries transportable, death-specific information beyond aggregate recorded state and process. A clinically important ambiguity remains: a late laboratory signal may be useful because it marks a patient whose biochemical information is discordant with apparently reassuring recorded vital signs, or it may merely reproduce a generic monitoring/documentation workflow.

The new falsifiable question is:

> At ICU-relative minute 360, among adults whose recent recorded vital-sign state is not itself high risk, does a revision-safe biochemical-state/availability pattern identify later recorded ICU death more accurately and with better calibration than additive vital and laboratory state/process summaries at the same review burden, and is any increment specific to death rather than alive ICU discharge?

This advances the parent from “late information is associated with outcome” to a clinically interpretable, patient-linked challenge: whether apparent stability in recorded pressure/heart-rate/oxygenation can coexist with a laboratory pattern that deserves reassessment. A positive result would justify a prospective silent-mode review of discordant information and timestamp quality. It would not show that the patient was physiologically stable, that a test was ordered because of deterioration, or that more testing or intervention improves outcomes.

The strongest available evidence is only an outcome-blind source/schema and bounded feasibility audit plus prior candidate audits. eICU records result and revision offsets, structured laboratory values, periodic/aperiodic vital observations, recorded ICU disposition and sparse end-of-life documentation. It does not provide specimen-collection time, laboratory order time, initial-posting time, laboratory units, raw waveforms, complete goals-of-care documentation, clinician intent or complete post-ICU mortality. No fitted result supports the hypothesis yet.

## Hypothesis, estimand and claim boundaries

Primary hypothesis H1: in a prespecified low-to-moderate recorded-vital-risk stratum at minute 360, adding a cross-domain discordance block—revision-safe laboratory state plus conservative information-availability timing relative to recent vital state—to an additive baseline improves held-out-hospital death-specific cumulative-incidence calibration and integrated competing-risk Brier score for recorded ICU death during (360,1800], without a material increase in fixed-burden reviews.

The primary contrast is B_discordance minus B0, estimated as the macro-hospital mean held-out difference in death-specific integrated competing-risk Brier score over 360–1800 and calibration at 600, 1200 and 1800 minutes. The same contrast is reported for all-risk adults and for prespecified vital-risk strata. Alive ICU discharge is a competing outcome, not censoring.

A supportive result requires the primary support gates, a positive uncertainty-compatible death-specific increment concentrated in the low/moderate vital-risk stratum, no material calibration harm, attenuation after patient-link and temporal-order falsifications, and a smaller/non-positive increment for alive discharge. It must also persist on the conservative revision-safe clock, common analyte panel and interface-stratified analyses.

Adverse evidence is a null or harmful increment; an increment confined to high vital-risk patients; unchanged performance after temporal or patient-link permutation; an equal/larger alive-discharge increment; a matched vital-process increment of similar size; concentration in one hospital/interface; or a gain present only when post-360 revisions are admitted. These results reject the proposed hidden-stress interpretation even if discrimination is high.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed event/overlap/support gates, wide intervals spanning zero and the meaningful-effect region, unstable fold directions, sparse low/moderate-risk cells, inability to reproduce the codebook, or an unresolved result-time versus availability-time discrepancy. A learned model winning without these checks is not supportive.

The result is label-relative prognostic evidence about recorded information patterns. It cannot establish true biochemical stress, physiologic stability, causality, clinician actionability, or clinical benefit.

Use hourly intervals (360,420] through (1740,1800] and separate cause-specific discrete-time hazards. Convert them to death and discharge cumulative-incidence predictions using the competing-risk product:
\`CIF_j(t)=sum_k h_jk(x) product_{r<k}(1-h_death,r(x)-h_discharge,r(x))\`.

Define the vital-risk stratum without outcome leakage: fit B0 in each development fold, obtain out-of-fold predicted death CIF by 1,800, and freeze fold-specific cut points at 0.01 and 0.05 (low, intermediate, high) using development hospitals only. The primary discordance claim is low/intermediate risk (predicted CIF <=0.05); all-risk and high-risk analyses are secondary. If a fold cannot support the low/intermediate cell, that stratum is inconclusive rather than redefined.

## Baseline, alternative and scientific information

B0 is pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic regression with hourly hazards. Inputs are admission context, landmark lab state, aggregate revision-safe laboratory process, landmark/recent vital state and process, and carePlanEOL presence/timing. It has no cross-domain discordance score or six-bin timing interaction. All preprocessing, codebook, scaling, imputation, calibration and cut points are learned within development hospitals.

B1 is a matched transparent discordance model using the same cohort, retained rows, 64-key codebook, vital channels, hospital folds, hourly hazard framework and carePlanEOL inputs as B0, adding only the prespecified lab-state/availability-by-vital-risk interactions. Its coefficient and standardized-risk contrasts show whether the claim is supported in an auditable way.

The substantive learned alternative T is a small one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over the six 60-minute vectors containing the same lab keys, conservative availability-time channels, vital channels, missingness and carePlanEOL timing. It has separate death/discharge heads and no hospital identifier. A parameter-matched non-temporal MLP is required as a control. T can reveal nonlinear temporal patterns—such as a late biochemical burst during stable recorded vital state, persistence versus one-off observation, and cross-domain co-movement—that B1's additive score discards. T_result repeats the same architecture on identical retained rows binned by result time.

T is retained as a substantive scientific result only if it improves held-out death calibration/Brier over B1 with uncertainty compatible with improvement, survives within-patient time-order and patient-link falsifications, and yields stable temporal-pattern summaries across folds. Otherwise it is a prespecified negative/inconclusive branch and B1 remains the primary interpretation. A mechanistic state-space model is deferred because the available data lack specimen/order timestamps, assay units, raw waveforms and adjudicated physiologic states; fitting one would encode unverifiable biological assumptions. It becomes revisit-worthy only after those dependencies are available.

## Splits, uncertainty, fixed-burden evaluation and support

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals; keep all stays for each \`uniquepid\` together. Within each development fold use a hospital-stratified 75/25 fit/validation split. No target-fold preprocessing, outcome-dependent analyte selection or hospital identifier predictor is allowed.

Primary uncertainty is 95% intervals from at least 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions, or a prespecified hierarchical hospital/fold model if bootstrap is unstable. Report macro-hospital estimates first; patient-weighted summaries are secondary. Provide CIF calibration intercept/slope, calibration tables at 600/1200/1800, integrated competing-risk Brier by cause, and fold/hospital distributions.

At minute 360, compare B0 and B1 at fixed predicted death-CIF thresholds 0.01, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10: reviews per 100 episodes, recorded-death capture, PPV, false-review burden and number needed to review. Report low/intermediate and all-risk strata. Require at least 200 held-out episodes, 20 recorded deaths, 20 alive discharges and 20 hospitals per reported threshold cell; unsupported cells are marked inconclusive. These are label-relative review diagnostics, not net benefit or clinical utility.

Primary support gates: >=100 eligible hospitals, >=50 event-bearing hospitals, >=1,000 recorded deaths and >=1,000 competing discharges; approximately 20 hospitals per fold where feasible; >=30% coverage for each retained vital field (or >=20% with at least two observations); overlapping revision-safe state/process support in >=80% of held-out hospitals; and non-sparse low/intermediate vital-risk cells and early/late lab bins. Gate failure makes the corresponding claim inconclusive and does not authorize changing definitions.

1. Permute six-bin labels within patient, separately for availability/result clocks, preserving counts, codebook state and revision/missingness.
2. Reassign lab process histories within hospital and baseline-risk strata, breaking patient linkage while preserving workflow distributions.
3. Fit matched vital-process interactions and compare lab-only, vital-only and joint increments.
4. Compare death-CIF and alive-discharge-CIF increments.

## Demonstration and seed dispositions

The three demonstrations were used as ambition/method references, not topic requirements. Delphi supports a bounded learned structured-history adaptation, but eICU lacks its disease-history/genetic/external-validation setting; the selected sequence model is a conditional adaptation, not a reproduction. ALADYNOULLI's longitudinal latent model is not reproduced because the available eICU snapshot lacks verified genetic inputs and a mechanistic latent state would overclaim; the small temporal learner is retained as a bounded EHR-only alternative. Oncoformer cannot be reproduced because images and the main article/STAR Methods are unavailable; its accessible lab-only idea is reflected only as a conditional temporal alternative, with no claim that the main paper was read.

All ten imported eICU expert seeds were inspected as cards and assessed as untested source material requiring data-bound children. Seeds 01 vasopressor strategy, 03 hypotension duration, 04 hyperoxia, 05 ventilatory burden, 06 nighttime discharge, 07 transfer risk and 10 recovery speed remain deferred because their imported versions lack complete local bindings/estimands/support audits. Seed 02 testing-practice dependence and seed 08 reduced testing are scientifically related to this parent and are deferred as alternatives because the selected child provides a more tightly bound revision-safe test; they should be revisited if a future episode supplies order/interface timestamps or a different outcome. Seed 09 hypoglycemia after insulin has valid developed descendants, but is not selected here because this episode's strongest assessed parent and available exact evidence support the lab-information timing question. Expert origin does not establish novelty, feasibility or clinical truth.

## Evidence limits and revisit criteria

Harbor can verify source paths, joins, temporal exclusion, feature arithmetic, competing-risk computation, held-out predictions, uncertainty, falsification execution and whether conclusions follow from outputs. It cannot establish true physiology, specimen timing, clinician intent, whether a review would change care, complete mortality or clinical benefit.

Clinical adjudication/source-system audit with order, collection, initial-posting and revision timestamps, assay units and clinician context is required to distinguish biochemical change from interface/revision workflow. External health-system validation is required for transportability beyond this eICU snapshot. A prospective silent-mode study is required before using the discordance score in care, and an impact/randomized study is required before claiming surveillance changes outcomes. Revisit the deferred mechanistic state-space model only if timestamped orders/collections, units, raw waveform/physiologic adjudication and longitudinal treatment context become available.
