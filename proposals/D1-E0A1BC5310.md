# Congestion-guided early deresuscitation after shock stabilization

## Clinical question, supported claim, and unresolved hypothesis

Fluid accumulation after resuscitation is associated with worse ICU outcomes, but association does not show that removing fluid improves outcomes. RADAR-2 (180 patients; DOI 10.1007/s00134-021-06596-8) showed that a conservative/active-deresuscitation strategy lowered fluid balance; clinical outcomes were underpowered and the mortality estimate could not exclude harm. A 2026 review (Orieux et al., DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100083; full text inspected from frozen source [source checksum]) concludes that ICU renal congestion lacks validated diagnostic criteria or therapeutic targets and outcome-driven interventional evidence. The strongest supported claim is therefore that active deresuscitation can lower recorded fluid balance; its patient-centered benefit, safety, timing, and target population remain unresolved.

The falsifiable hypothesis is: **among mechanically ventilated adults who at ICU hour 48 have survived initial vasopressor-treated shock, are currently off vasopressors, and have at least 5% documented fluid accumulation, initiating an intravenous loop diuretic within 12 hours increases 28-day ventilator-free days compared with withholding loop diuretics for 24 hours, without increasing 7-day death or new renal-replacement therapy (RRT). Benefit is larger when the latest pre-landmark chest report shows pulmonary vascular/interstitial congestion.**

This matters because clinicians must trade possible faster resolution of pulmonary edema against hypotension, electrolyte loss, and kidney injury. The advance is a fixed-decision target-trial emulation that begins only after observed stabilization, handles grace periods, jointly tests respiratory benefit and renal safety, and asks whether contemporaneous imaging distinguishes a responsive congestion phenotype from nonspecific positive balance.

## Target trial

### Population and time zero

Use the first ICU stay per subject_id in the discovery partition. Set t0 = icu/icustays.intime + 48 hours. Include:

1. Age at intime >=18, calculated as anchor_age + year(intime) - anchor_year.
2. Still in ICU at t0; do not condition on later survival.
3. An invasive-ventilation interval (icu/procedureevents itemid 225792) overlaps t0.
4. At least one vasopressor infusion before t0 (icu/inputevents itemids 221289 epinephrine, 221662 dopamine, 221749 phenylephrine, 221906 norepinephrine, 222315 vasopressin), but none overlaps t0.
5. Median valid MAP in (t0-3h,t0] >=65 mmHg using chartevents itemids 220052, 220181, 225312. Exclude missing eligibility MAP.
6. Documented accumulation from intime through t0 >=50 mL/kg: completed inputs minus measured outputs, with L converted to mL, divided by admission weight. Freeze and report all item/unit/status handling and missingness. Weight hierarchy: chartevents 226512, first 224639, then median inputevents.patientweight; require 30-300 kg.
7. No IV loop in (t0-6h,t0], no RRT before t0 (procedureevents itemids 225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809, 225955), urine output >0.1 mL/kg/h in (t0-6h,t0], and latest potassium in (t0-12h,t0] >=3.0 mmol/L.

Do not use discharge-assigned diagnoses for eligibility or baseline adjustment. Exclude ECMO at t0. Comfort-only status can be excluded only if an order/note timestamp proves it was known before t0; otherwise it is an explicit unmeasured limitation.

### Strategies and estimand

- A: initiate an actually administered IV loop diuretic in (t0,t0+12h].
- B: withhold all loop diuretics through (t0,t0+24h].

Detect furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide, or ethacrynic acid from hosp/emar actual administration joined to emar_detail for IV route and nonzero dose. ICU inputevents furosemide itemids 221794 and 228340 provide concordance and infusion capture. Oral treatment is not concordant.

Clone each eligible patient to both strategies at t0. Censor A at 12h if untreated and B at first loop administration through 24h; treatment during 12-24h deviates from both. The primary per-protocol estimand is the adjusted mean difference in 28-day ventilator-free days (VFD, A minus B). A day is free only when alive and without invasive ventilation; death before day 28 gives VFD=0. Secondary estimands are risk differences for 28-day death, successful extubation by day 7, ICU/hospital-free days through day 28, 24/48h net balance, and the 7-day safety composite of death or new RRT. Report safety components, creatinine change, and potassium <3 separately. Discharge alive implies alive/unventilated afterward unless patients.dod contradicts it.

The prespecified modifier is the latest chest report in (t0-12h,t0]: explicit pulmonary vascular/interstitial edema/congestion; isolated pleural effusion; neither/unknown. Freeze a section-aware, negation-aware lexicon before outcomes. Missing report is its own category.

## Analysis and baselines

Create person-hour clones through hour 24. Estimate inverse-probability-of-adherence/censoring weights with pooled logistic models using information available at each hour; truncate at 1st/99th percentiles and report weight distributions, strategy-specific effective sample sizes, and positivity.

Covariates: age, sex, race, admission year/type, first care unit/service; comorbidity only from prior completed admissions; admission weight, balance and hourly input/output; MAP, heart rate, temperature, FiO2, PEEP, oxygenation, CVP when present; creatinine itemid 50912, lactate 50813, potassium 50971, hemoglobin 51222; vasopressor duration/dose; sedation, albumin/crystalloid; and radiographic phenotype. Do not use discharge notes or post-index coding.

Use weighted outcome models with subject-clustered robust SEs and 500 subject-level bootstrap replicates for VFD and interaction intervals. Report an unadjusted landmark baseline, a conventional 12h propensity-overlap-weighted baseline, and clone-censor-weighting as primary. Impute covariates only—not eligibility MAP, exposure, imaging availability, or outcomes.

Sensitivity analyses: 10% instead of 5% accumulation; balance continuous; 6h and 24h initiation strategies; exclude cardiac surgery or prior heart failure; exclude albumin co-initiation; account for new RRT as competing strategy; require eMAR/inputevents concordance; restrict to complete accounting; index first ICU per hospitalization; calendar-period interaction; and quantitative bias analysis. Positive-control process outcomes are urine output and more negative 24h balance. A falsification check asks whether future initiation predicts the pre-t0 6h creatinine or urine-output slope after adjustment; material association indicates residual indication bias and downgrades causal language.

## Falsification and interpretation

Declare infeasible/inconclusive before outcome modeling if either strategy has <100 observed adherent patients, weighted ESS <75, or >10% of eligible patients have adherence probability <0.01.

- Supportive: VFD difference >0 with two-sided 95% CI excluding 0 and point estimate >=+1 day, while the upper 95% bound for excess 7-day death/new RRT is <+5 percentage points. Imaging-specific benefit is supported only if its interaction is directionally concordant and interval excludes no interaction.
- Adverse: VFD upper 95% bound <=0, or safety-harm lower 95% bound >=+5 points. Harm without imaging congestion would argue against universal use.
- Inconclusive: all other patterns, including wide intervals, incompatible benefit/safety, nonpositivity, exposure discordance, or failed falsification. Nonsignificance is not equivalence.

Support justifies external validation/trial enrichment, not treatment recommendation. Adverse results challenge this strategy in this state, not all fluid removal. Inconclusive results preserve equipoise.

## Availability audit and limits

Actual read-only feasibility passes found 1,122 broad landmark-eligible stays before adult/MAP/urine/RRT refinements; 116 initiated furosemide by 6h, 212 by 12h, and 348 by 24h. In a nearly identical 1,120-row pass, 599 had a radiology report in the prior 12h, 357 had a crude congestion-term match, and 53 of those initiated furosemide by 6h. These are feasibility counts, not results; final unit handling, duplicates, and attrition must be reported.

Two clinicians blinded to treatment/outcome must independently review at least 200 stratified reports; third review resolves disagreement. Without adjudication, call the modifier a text-derived radiology phenotype, not confirmed pulmonary/renal congestion. MIMIC lacks examination, VExUS, reliable treatment indication, randomization, post-discharge ventilation, and external-site validation. Prospective multicenter study with protocolized volume assessment is required for a recommendation.

## Verification contract

Computationally checkable: hashes/members/columns; discovery partition; one index per subject; attrition; no post-t0 leakage; item/unit/status mappings; clone/censor logic; exposure concordance; outcomes; missingness; weight/positivity diagnostics; intervals; falsification/sensitivity outputs; and exact conclusion-branch mapping.

Challenge cases: (1) supportive outputs plus “diuretics proven beneficial/standard care” must fail; (2) adverse VFD or safety outputs called supportive must fail; (3) wide intervals/nonpositivity called equivalence or no effect must fail; (4) bounded supportive, adverse, and inconclusive interpretations pass only when computations agree. Imaging validity and clinical eligibility need human review; exchangeability, transportability, and causal truth are not automatically verifiable.
