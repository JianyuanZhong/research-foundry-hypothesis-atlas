# Recovery Asynchrony Index (RAI) in ICU Patients

**Expert Seed:** `[starting question]` (Asynchronous organ recovery)
**Dataset:** MIMIC-IV
**Status:** Refined hypothesis with computable metric and falsifiable predictions

## Hypothesis to Test

After circulatory stabilization in the ICU, patients exhibiting greater asynchrony in multi-organ recovery trajectories (measured by the Recovery Asynchrony Index, RAI) are more likely to experience in-hospital mortality, independent of traditional severity scores (APACHE II, SOFA).

### Core Prediction

Higher RAI values (greater temporal discordance between organ system recovery trajectories) predict increased mortality risk after adjusting for baseline severity, demographics, and organ support intensity.

## Study Population and Main Data

**Population:** Adult ICU patients in MIMIC-IV with:
- Admission with infection or shock (ICD-10 codes for sepsis, septic shock, pneumonia, etc.)
- At least 3 daily laboratory measurements for at least 3 SOFA components
- At least 5 daily vital sign measurements
- ICU length of stay ≥ 3 days

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with active major bleeding
- Patients with known pre-existing organ failure at baseline
- Patients discharged alive from ICU before day 3

**Main Data Tables:**
- `hosp/admissions`: subject_id, hadm_id, admittime, dischtime, deathtime
- `icu/icustays`: stay_id, subject_id, hadm_id, admission_type

- `icu/d_items`: itemcode, itemname, itemtype
- `hosp/labevents`: labeventoffset, subject_id, hadm_id, labresultoffset

**Join Keys:** subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id
**Temporal Columns:** charttime, labresultoffset (converted to timestamps)

## Hypothesis Definition

### Recovery Asynchrony Index (RAI)

The RAI is computed as a weighted Euclidean distance between normalized trajectory vectors of four organ systems:

1. **Respiratory:** SpO2, respiratory rate (RR)
2. **Renal:** Serum creatinine, urine output
3. **Cardiovascular:** Mean arterial pressure (MAP), heart rate (HR)
4. **Hepatic:** Bilirubin, INR

**Formula:**
```
RAI = Σ_i<j w_ij * d(Z_i, Z_j)
```

Where:
- Z_i is the normalized trajectory vector for organ system i
- d(Z_i, Z_j) is the Euclidean distance between systems i and j
- w_ij is the weight for the pair (i,j), derived from clinical importance

**Normalization:** Each trajectory is normalized to Z-score relative to the admission mean and standard deviation.

**Interpretation:** Higher RAI indicates greater temporal discordance between organ systems, suggesting "fragile" multi-organ recovery.

## Outcomes

1. **Primary:** In-hospital mortality
2. **Secondary:**
   - ICU length of stay
   - Days of mechanical ventilation
   - Vasopressor-free days
   - ICU readmission within 30 days

## Baseline Models

1. **Simple baseline:** Age, sex
2. **Clinical baseline:** APACHE II score, admission SOFA score
3. **Machine learning baseline:** Random Forest on demographics, comorbidities, admission vitals
4. **Advanced baseline:** Delphi-like transformer on lab/vitals tokens predicting individual organ recovery trajectories

## Alternative Model

**RAI Transformer:** A transformer model that learns to predict the RAI directly from longitudinal tokens, computing SHAP values for organ-specific recovery tokens. This model compares the "asynchrony" signal learned by the model to the explicit RAI metric.

## Analysis Plan

1. **Descriptive statistics:** Distribution of RAI by mortality status, baseline characteristics
2. **Univariate analysis:** Association of RAI with outcomes (logistic regression, Cox regression)
3. **Multivariate analysis:**
   - Cox regression for mortality adjusting for APACHE II, baseline SOFA, organ support intensity, infection status, demographics
   - Time-dependent ROC-AUC for RAI vs. mortality
   - Calibration plots (observed vs. predicted mortality by RAI deciles)
4. **Subgroup analysis:**
   - Organ-specific RAI (focusing on respiratory, renal, cardiovascular, hepatic)
   - Time-varying RAI (calculated at different time points during ICU stay)
   - Interaction with treatments (vasopressor de-escalation, mechanical ventilation liberation)
5. **Comparison:** Compare RAI-based prediction to baseline models using AUC and Brier score

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if:
1. RAI shows no independent association with mortality after multivariable adjustment (HR ~1, 95% CI includes 1)
2. RAI has no predictive value beyond baseline models (AUC not significantly better than APACHE II or SOFA)
3. The RAI distribution is similar across mortality groups

## Limitations

1. **Missing data:** Not all patients have complete lab/vital measurements; will use MICE imputation
2. **Selection bias:** Measurement timing may be influenced by clinical decisions
3. **Causal inference:** Observational study; cannot establish causality
4. **External validation:** Requires validation in external ICU datasets
5. **Mechanistic understanding:** Does not establish biological mechanisms for asynchrony
