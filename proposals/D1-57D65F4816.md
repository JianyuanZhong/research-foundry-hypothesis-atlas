# Monitoring de-intensification while unstable predicts short-term deterioration in ICU patients: an eICU test of monitoring–clinical-state alignment

Working draft (Lead, episode 1, eICU island). Parent: expert seed eicu-08 ([prior hypothesis]), assessed valid. All data bindings below were verified by direct header inspection of the frozen eICU 2.0 CSVs on 2026-10-14 (see resume note); no analysis results are claimed.

## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICUs routinely titrate laboratory testing intensity to perceived patient stability: as patients improve, testing is de-escalated; as they worsen, testing is escalated. The unresolved question is whether the *alignment* between testing intensity and the patient's current physiologic state carries prognostic information that is not already captured by standard severity measures. Specifically: **does an abrupt reduction in laboratory testing frequency that occurs while a patient remains objectively unstable predict short-term deterioration (organ-support escalation, death, or post-discharge bounce-back) beyond APACHE severity and current physiologic state, and does this signal vary across hospitals?**

Clinical importance: (i) De-implementation programs (Choosing Wisely, WICH-ICU trial [K1]; surgical-ICU QI initiatives [K3]) are actively reducing routine testing at the *unit* level, but their safety analyses are not stratified by patient-level instability dynamics — our result would tell them whether the risky subgroup is "reduction while unstable" rather than "reduction" per se. (ii) Discharge/readiness prediction models (GRU-D++ vs SWIFT [K2]) use physiologic variables but not the monitoring-intensity trajectory; if testing de-intensification adds incremental predictive information, it is a cheap, always-available signal for ward-transfer decisions. (iii) A finding that testing tracks recovery appropriately (no signal after adjustment) would be a genuinely useful negative: it would support the safety of patient-targeted de-escalation and localize risk to physiology alone.

## 2. Advance over existing knowledge

- WICH-ICU [K1] tests unit-level testing reduction vs 30-day mortality; it does not examine whether testing reduction is differentially associated with harm when it occurs in unstable patients. Our proposal tests the misalignment signal at patient level.
- Heo et al. [K2] (MIMIC-IV + external validation) predict ICU readmission/death within 7 days of discharge with AUROC 0.802/0.756 using deep learning on physiologic time series — testing cadence is not used as a feature, and residual instability at discharge is inferred only from physiology.
- Yeo et al. [K3] report a two-phase surgical-ICU de-implementation with aggregate before/after outcomes, no patient-level instability stratification.
- Nobody (to our knowledge, based on the inspected sources above) has tested the *dynamic coupling* of testing intensity with concurrent instability as an early deterioration signal. This is the gap our hypothesis fills.

## 3. Hypothesis (falsifiable, directional)

**H1:** Among ICU patients who are objectively unstable at time t, an abrupt (≥50%) reduction in laboratory testing rate over the following 24 h (relative to the prior 24 h) is associated with higher risk of a composite deterioration outcome within 48 h, after adjustment for APACHE score, age, unit type, and concurrent physiologic state, with hospital random effects. **Rival (H0-misalignment-null):** Testing de-escalation reflects clinician knowledge of improvement not visible in structured data; conditioning on the pre-event physiologic trajectory should attenuate the association toward the null, and remaining risk is explained by severity alone.

## 4. Population, exposures, outcomes (all columns verified in source headers)

Data: eICU Collaborative Research Database v2.0 (31 CSVs, read-only, paths and SHA-256 in datasets/eicu/README.md). Join key throughout: `patientunitstayid`; hospital-level: `hospitalid` (patient.csv, hospital.csv); readmission linkage: `uniquepid` and `patienthealthsystemstayid` (patient.csv) linking multiple unit stays within one hospitalization.

- **Population:** adult (age ≥ 18, patient.csv `age`) ICU stays with `unitstaytype` in (admission, readmission); stay ≥ 48 h (`unitdischargeoffset` ≥ 2880 min); ≥ 8 laboratory results in the first 24 h (lab.csv) to establish a measurable baseline testing rate; APACHE score available (apachePatientResult.csv `apachescore`, `apacheversion` — restrict to one version, prefer `apacheversion='4'` where present, per-patient de-dup by `apachepatientresultsid`).
- **Exposure (index time t, candidate windows every 8 h from ICU hour 24 onward):** testing rate R24_before = lab.csv rows with `labresultoffset` in [t-1440, t) minutes; R24_after = rows in [t, t+1440). A *de-intensification event* requires R24_before ≥ hospital-median daily testing rate, R24_after ≤ 0.5 × R24_before, and unit discharge not within the following 24 h (`unitdischargeoffset` − t ≥ 1440). *Instability at t* (all within 4 h before t): active vasopressor (infusionDrug.csv `drugname` matched to norepinephrine/epinephrine/vasopressin/phenylephrine/dopamine with `infusionoffset` ≤ t and `drugrate` > 0), OR mean arterial pressure < 65 mmHg (vitalPeriodic.csv `systemicmean`; vitalAperiodic.csv `noninvasivemean` as fallback, by `observationoffset`), OR invasive ventilation / FiO2 ≥ 0.5 (respiratoryCharting.csv `respcharttypecat`/`respchartvaluelabel`, by `respchartoffset`). Primary contrast: unstable-with-de-intensification vs unstable-without (rate change < 20%).
- **Outcomes (within 48 h after t, adjudicated by timestamps, not notes):** composite: (a) ICU death (`unitdischargestatus`='Expired' and `unitdischargeoffset` ≤ t+2880); (b) organ-support escalation: new vasopressor start after t or ≥50% rate increase (infusionDrug), or new invasive ventilation after t (respiratoryCharting); (c) discharge-within-48 h followed by unplanned ICU readmission within 72 h of unit discharge (same `patienthealthsystemstayid`, next unit stay, `unitvisitnumber` > 1). Secondary: hospital death (`hospitaldischargestatus`='Expired').
- **Exclusions/censoring (expert seed cautions operationalized):** any carePlanEOL entry active before t (carePlanEOL.csv); hospital-day-level data outages — exclude candidate windows at hospital-days when >80% of that hospital's simultaneously present patients also show ≥50% testing drop (interface interruption artifact); discharge/transfer windows are not exposure windows (censored, not misclassified as stable).

## 5. Analysis plan

1. **Descriptive alignment check:** cross-tabulate testing-rate change vs instability status at t (is testing actually coupled to state?), with hospital-level variation (hospital.csv `region`, `numbedscategory`).
2. **Baseline model (simple, pre-specified):** logistic regression for 48-h deterioration on APACHE score, age, sex, unit type, instability indicators at t, hospital random intercept. This is the "severity alone" reference.
3. **Primary test of H1:** add the de-intensification-while-unstable indicator (and separately, the continuous testing-rate ratio) to the baseline model; report adjusted OR with 95% CI (cluster-robust by patient, random effect by hospital). Prespecified supportive threshold: adjusted OR ≥ 1.15 with CI excluding 1.0 in ≥ 20 hospitals' pooled estimate.
4. **Learned alternative (substantive, not complexity-seeking):** gradient-boosted survival model adding time-varying testing-intensity trajectory features (rate ratio, 72-h slope, interaction with instability) to the same physiologic covariates, trained on 60% of hospitals, AUROC/C-index evaluated on held-out hospitals. Scientific question: does the monitoring signal add *incremental information* (ΔAUROC ≥ 0.02 on held-out hospitals) beyond severity physiology — NOT leaderboard optimization. If ΔAUROC ≈ 0 but the adjusted OR stands, the signal is real but redundant with physiology (interpretation differs; see §7).
5. **Hospital heterogeneity:** random-slope model; report whether the de-intensification signal varies by hospital testing-intensity quartile.

CPU-only; lab.csv (517 MB gz) read in chunks with pandas (selected columns), vitals down-sampled to 4-h means by chunked aggregation; total solver compute estimated < 2 h CPU, well inside the CPU-only envelope; no GPU needed (tabular counts/logistic/GBM).

## 6. Falsification criteria and outcome interpretation

- **Supportive:** adjusted OR ≥ 1.15 (CI > 1) for de-intensification-while-unstable AND/OR held-out ΔAUROC ≥ 0.02 → monitoring–state misalignment carries early risk information; a candidate trigger for de-implementation programs to exempt unstable patients, and a cheap feature for discharge-readiness models. NOT proof of causation: confounding by clinician foresight cannot be excluded by observational data.
- **Adverse:** OR ≤ 1.0 with CI excluding 1.0, or association confined to EOL-adjacent stays → testing de-escalation is appropriate (aligned with recovery); de-implementation safety concern not supported at patient level; strengthens [K1]'s program at the dynamic level.
- **Inconclusive:** CI spanning 1.0 with width > 0.15, or < 500 exposed events after exclusions → underpowered; report event counts honestly and defer.
- **Claims NOT answerable here:** causality (would need a randomized testing-reduction stratified by instability), mechanism (why clinicians de-escalate), and ward-level outcomes (eICU ends at hospital discharge; no post-discharge follow-up).

## 7. Alternatives considered (method exploration record)

- Baseline: severity-adjusted logistic regression (chosen; transparent, directly tests H1).
- Learned alternative: GBM with time-varying monitoring trajectory (chosen as the incremental-information test; §5.4).
- Rejected: deep sequence models (GRU-D++-style [K2]) — high cost, weak interpretability for a single-question incremental-information test; the GBM with trajectory features answers the same scientific uncertainty at a fraction of the budget. Revisiting justified if GBM shows strong nonlinearity (interaction trees) or if time-to-event censoring dominates.
- Rejected direction (other seeds): hypotension–AKI (seed 03), hyperoxia (seed 04), ventilatory burden (seed 05) are well-covered in literature; seed 08's dynamic misalignment question had the clearest gap vs. inspected sources.
