## Scientific opening, strongest evidence, unresolved hypothesis, and advance

The strongest inspected evidence supports only three bounded claims. Large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, this difference was associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes, so a combined equation can discard prognostic information [K2]. Administrative AKI coding can be insensitive and subgroup-dependent relative to serum-creatinine-defined AKI [K3]. These findings do not establish that equal-combined-eGFR negative discordance predicts biological AKI, that UKB first-appearance dates measure contacts, or that a residual adjusted profile contrast is mechanistic.

The unresolved hypothesis is: among supported P− and P0 profiles at the same combined eGFR, P− has a larger 10-year contrast for first recorded N17 than for first recorded N18, and this difference remains materially positive after measured pre-index first-appearance frequency and conditional bundle density are represented by a calibrated, cross-fitted two-factor model.

The leading interpretation is bounded recorded acute-renal selectivity. The strongest rival is stable morbidity/recording propensity: participants with greater pre-index discovery-date intensity or denser same-day bundles may later receive more renal and nonrenal first-appearance codes. The rival predicts temporal persistence of these two pre-index processes and attenuation of the N17-versus-N18 contrast after their cross-fitted factors are added. The leading claim predicts a positive N17-versus-N18 difference under both the transparent scalar-history model and the calibrated two-factor model.

The advance over the inspected work is a fixed-combined-eGFR, competing-transition comparison that explicitly tests robustness to two distinct measurable recording patterns. The audit requires a narrower claim than the parent: because profile-specific broad endpoint cells are sparse, this study cannot establish that the N17 contrast exceeds B30/F15/H10 contrasts or is absent from high-density N17 dates. Even a fully supportive result means only “recorded N17 selectivity robust to measured pre-index first-appearance frequency/density.” Encounter- and laboratory-adjudicated validation remains necessary.

## Scientifically substantive alternative: cross-fitted recording factors

The scalar baseline loses persistence and the distinction between recurrent date frequency and conditional bundle density. Fit a two-part model using the frozen seed-20260924 70/15/15 participant split, stratified only by sex and aggregate renal transition:

1. A yearly piecewise-exponential recurrent discovery-date process with participant effect `u_i`.
2. Conditional on a discovery date, a zero-truncated negative-binomial bundle-size mark model with participant effect `v_i`.
3. Estimate `corr(u_i,v_i)`; treat death as absorbing.

Fit four prespecified variants at matched specificity: fixed covariates only, intensity-only, mark-only, and correlated two-factor. Use only [index−10,index) first-appearance history to infer participant factors: [−10,−5) predicts [−5,0) for transport, then all pre-index history informs factors at S. Never update `u_i/v_i` with post-S B30/F15/H10, renal outcomes, bundle sizes, or death for renal inference.

Fit population parameters in training, calibrate/select on validation, and evaluate once on test. K30/L15/H10 are pooled held-out calibration outcomes, not profile contrasts. Report early-to-late rank correlation; yearly discovery-date counts and zero counts; conditional bundle mean, variance, quantiles and tail; pooled B30/F15/H10 CIF calibration and Brier scores; pooled N17/N18/death calibration as descriptive model checks; joint deviance; factor correlation; and bootstrap stability. Retain the two-factor path only if every sufficiently supported binary target has |calibration intercept|<=0.10 and slope 0.8–1.2; observed/expected yearly counts are 0.8–1.25 in at least 8/10 years; 90th bundle-quantile error is <=1 group or <=20%; and no Brier score is worse than the transparent process model by >0.01.

After all process choices are frozen without inspecting profile effects, create five-fold cross-fitted pre-index `u_i/v_i` scores for the complete renal cohort: each participant's score uses loadings/hyperparameters fitted without that participant's fold and only that participant's pre-index history. Add these scores to the identical full-cohort renal model. Compare `theta17`, `theta18`, and D with the transparent model using joint participant-bootstrap uncertainty. Conditioning on pre-index factors changes the standardized contrast and may block pathways through baseline morbidity; call this a robustness diagnostic, not a causal direct effect.

This alternative can reveal whether persistent measured discovery frequency or density accounts for attenuation that scalar summaries miss. It cannot label `u_i` as contact, `v_i` as admission intensity, or residual D as biological AKI. Code-composition NMF is deferred because profile-specific broad effects are already infeasible and composition could absorb severity/code salience without resolving frequency versus density. Revisit NMF only in a separate study if the two-factor model fails a held-out process target and a preregistered composition model improves a failed target without worsening another by >0.01 Brier or >5% deviance. A transformer remains deferred because only first appearances are available; a latent true-GFR model remains deferred without measured GFR or repeated-marker error data.

## Falsification and interpretation

All interpretation requires verified C and field semantics, renal-code review, support/matching, adequate renal precision, convergence/calibration, >=90% bootstrap success, joint 95% intervals, and no material principal/tie or cause-specific/CIF reversal.

Supportive for bounded recorded-N17 selectivity requires:

- lower 95% bounds of theta17 and D >0 in the transparent model;
- matched and principal-position direction agreement;
- the calibrated cross-fitted two-factor model also has D lower bound >0;
- the two models' D estimates do not differ by a clinically material amount, prespecified as a ratio-of-relative-risks factor of 1.25.

This supports external adjudication only. It does not establish biological AKI, exclude unmeasured contact/coding, or show N17 superiority to broad endpoints.

Adverse to robustness under measured stable recording propensity is adequate pre-index transport and held-out process calibration plus attenuation in the two-factor model to a precise clinically small interval: D upper bound <=log(1.25), particularly when the joint transparent-minus-two-factor D difference excludes zero. This is compatible with stable morbidity, contact, or capture; it proves none.

Adverse to acute-versus-chronic recorded selectivity is D upper bound <=0 in either calibrated prespecified renal model, or precise similar theta17/theta18. Both renal theta upper bounds <=0 are adverse to both profile claims. A death-driven reversal is inconclusive for selectivity.

Inconclusive includes unverified C; support/matching failure; renal intervals spanning material alternatives; failed pre-index transport; process miscalibration/instability; <90% bootstrap success; transparent/two-factor disagreement without a calibrated pathway; or principal/tie reversal. Sparse broad profile cells and high-density N17 are known unavailable claims, not evidence against the primary hypothesis. An imprecise null does not refute the hypothesis.

Stronger conclusions require additional data. Biological AKI/CKD and code sensitivity require record-level admissions, serial creatinine/cystatin C/albuminuria, and clinician adjudication. Contact/testing and complete opportunity require provider/nation/encounter and export provenance. True filtration requires measured GFR or validated error data. Transportability and benefit require external validation and a prospective decision-impact or intervention study.
