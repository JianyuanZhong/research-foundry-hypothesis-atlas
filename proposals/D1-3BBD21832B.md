# Ventilatory burden and liberation from mechanical ventilation: construct-valid Harbor experiment

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The clinically important unresolved claim is whether a prespecified ventilatory-burden measure adds information about failure to liberate from mechanical ventilation that is not present in a simple tidal-volume model, after accounting for death and transfer/discharge. This could justify a prospective multicenter study of weaning risk and could identify whether pressure-related information is useful for an automated alert. It is not a treatment-effect study.

The strongest evidence currently supports only that eICU contains longitudinal respiratory-care and vital-sign records with timestamps and patient-level identifiers. The source audit found 865,381 respiratory-care rows. The explicit airway categories are Oral ETT, No Artificial Airway, Tracheostomy, Nasal ETT, Other, Double-Lumen Tube, and Cricothyrotomy; airwayposition contains center/right/left and numeric values plus many nonstandard values. cuffpressure has plausible values, but is not validated as cuff pressure here. The *limit fields are populated in many rows, while ventstartoffset is 0 in 685,205 of 865,381 rows and ventendoffset is 0 in 865,224. These patterns are consistent with status rows and alarm/setting fields, not validated measured physiology. Therefore the test is a construct-valid **ventilatory-burden proxy**, not a claim that the fields measure tidal volume, plateau pressure, or respiratory rate.

## Population, temporal boundaries, and outcomes

Use ICU-stay rows from patient, joined by patientunitstayid to respiratoryCare, vitalPeriodic, and hospital. Do not treat rows with the same patientunitstayid as independent observations.

Include a stay only if:

1. unitstaytype is exactly ventilator or ventilator_vent.
2. unitadmittime24 and unitdischargeoffset are valid.
3. At least 24 hours of respiratory-care rows exist after admission.
4. At least 48 hours of valid vitalPeriodic rows exist after admission.
5. At least one respiratory-care row has an explicit ventilator airway type and a valid ventstartoffset > 0.
6. The first valid ventendoffset is at least 24 hours after admission and at most 72 hours after admission.
7. There is at least one respiratory-care row after the first valid ventendoffset and before the first event.

The time origin is ICU admission (unitadmittime24). The index is the first valid ventendoffset in the 24–72-hour window, not the first ventilator start. The primary outcome window is 0–48 hours after index. A patient who dies, is transferred, or leaves the ICU before 48 hours has a competing event at that time. A patient discharged alive from the ICU with no recorded ventilator interval in the window is censored at 48 hours. Exclude stays with multiple ventilation intervals in the index window. Report a sensitivity analysis using all eligible intervals and excluding stays with any prior interval.

The primary exposure is a 15-minute interval-level proxy from explicitly labeled fields. Tidal volume, plateau pressure, and respiratory rate are used only if their source semantics are explicitly validated; otherwise the primary burden model is unavailable and the prespecified tidal-volume model is used. The primary burden proxy is a standardized weighted sum of validated tidal-volume, plateau-pressure, and respiratory-rate components divided by admission height. It is a descriptive proxy, not a treatment assignment.

The primary outcome is time from index to the first of liberation from mechanical ventilation, death, or transfer/discharge. Liberation is no recorded ventilator interval after the index and no subsequent ventilator status in the 48-hour window. Use the time to the first event. Report restricted mean time to liberation from index to 48 hours, cumulative incidence functions, cause-specific hazards for liberation, death, and transfer/discharge, and the difference in restricted mean time to liberation between the burden proxy and the tidal-volume baseline with a 95% confidence interval and two-sided test. The primary estimand is the difference in restricted mean time to liberation at 48 hours, conditional on the prespecified population and index. Report calibration intercept/slope, calibration-in-the-large, Brier score, discrimination metrics, event counts, censoring, missingness, and hospital-level estimates. Use hospital as a fixed effect because hospital characteristics are available. Do not use hospital as an instrumental variable or make a causal claim. Use a prespecified bootstrap or cluster-robust standard error with clustering at patientunitstayid and at the hospital level.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The baseline is a transparent competing-risk model with tidal volume, admission height, monitoring density, and hospital effects. The substantive alternative adds validated plateau pressure and respiratory rate to the burden proxy. Both use the same population, index, outcome window, covariates, and evaluation. The alternative can reveal whether pressure and rate add information that the tidal-volume proxy loses; a small predictive improvement is not sufficient for clinical value.

A neural sequence model is deferred because the available longitudinal rows are sparse and the primary question is a bounded physiologic comparison. Revisit it only if complete patient-level trajectories and computational resources justify it, with whole-hospital holdout and explicit calibration. A mechanistic ventilator model using plateau pressure and airway resistance is deferred because plateau pressure is not validated here. A treatment-effect study requires a randomized or adequately identified intervention, treatment adherence, and outcomes not represented in this snapshot.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires a clinically meaningful, precisely estimated improvement in the burden proxy over the tidal-volume baseline, with better discrimination/calibration and a positive difference in restricted mean time to liberation, while the improvement persists after hospital and monitoring-density adjustment. It would support the hypothesis that the proxy adds information; it would not establish that increasing tidal volume improves outcomes or that the proxy is a causal treatment target.

Adverse evidence is a null or negative difference, or a burden model that performs worse or is less well calibrated than the tidal-volume baseline. This argues against adding pressure/rate components to this risk measure and against interpreting the seed as a basis for a new alert.

Inconclusive evidence includes a wide confidence interval, unstable event counts, excessive missingness or unknown ventilation states, or a result that disappears after hospital and monitoring-density adjustment. It would justify narrowing the question or collecting more complete data rather than changing the interpretation.

Falsification checks are:

1. Exclude patients with sparse or highly irregular recording density; the burden effect should not be driven by monitoring intensity.
2. Exclude unknown ventilation states and report the proportion excluded.
3. Repeat with the alternative state definition and with 48-hour versus 72-hour index windows.
4. Repeat with death as the only competing event and with transfer/discharge as a separate event.
5. Compare the pooled estimate with hospital-level estimates and exclude the top 20% of hospitals by event count.
6. Remove the burden proxy; if the apparent burden effect disappears, the result is not robustly attributable to the added physiology.
7. Verify that no derived file is written to the configured source paths and that source rows remain read-only.

A supportive result would justify a prospective multicenter study with explicit ventilator measurements, sedation, organ-support, and treatment data. It would not justify changing bedside ventilation settings from this retrospective analysis. A causal effect, mechanism, or recommendation for a specific target requires expert review, more complete clinical data, and ideally a randomized or adequately identified interventional study.
