# Same-current HbA1c history and transition to cardiometabolic multimorbidity

Parent: [prior hypothesis]
Seed: [starting question], “Metabolic deterioration and multimorbidity”

Status: prospective observational hypothesis; no fitted result is claimed.

## Unresolved question and advance

The available evidence supports that static metabolic/atherogenic measures are associated with cardiometabolic disease, but it does not establish whether a prior HbA1c trajectory adds prognostic information once the current HbA1c is known. The clinically relevant unresolved question is whether two otherwise comparable adults at the same current HbA1c should have different risk interpretation because one reached that level from a lower prior value. This is a narrower and testable advance over a static association: it tests history dependence in the transition to a second disease family, not whether HbA1c itself is associated with disease.

The primary hypothesis is: among UK Biobank participants with a valid baseline and strictly later paired repeat HbA1c, alive and free of inpatient-coded type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease and heart failure at the repeat date, a recent rise in HbA1c is associated with a clinically material difference in 5-year risk of the second distinct disease family among those three families, even at the same current HbA1c. The expected direction is higher risk after a rise, but an opposite material contrast is also a falsifiable result. This is a noncausal prognostic hypothesis in a selected repeat-observed population, not a claim that testing or changing HbA1c improves outcomes.

## Population, exposure and time

Use one row per eid and horizontal one-to-one joins. Baseline is assessment instance 0 and the repeat landmark is assessment instance 1. Require assessment dates `53-0.0` and `53-1.0`, strictly `53-1.0 > 53-0.0`, valid HbA1c fields `30750-0.0` and `30750-1.0`, and verified evidence that biological-sample instances 0 and 1 correspond to those assessment dates. Follow-up starts at the repeat date; no pre-landmark person-time is used.

The exposure is current HbA1c C and prior HbA1c B, after field-specific unit verification and any documented conversion. The primary history contrast compares B=C-0.5 percentage points with B=C (same current level, a 0.5-point difference in prior history), standardized over the empirical distribution of C and baseline covariates and restricted to observed common support. This is deliberately called a history contrast: holding current C fixed, a larger repeat-minus-baseline change means a lower prior B. It must not be described as a causal effect of deterioration.

A secondary contrast holds B fixed and compares C=B+0.5 with C=B. It addresses ordinary deterioration at fixed baseline. Do not include unrestricted current, baseline and change terms together; current=B+change is algebraically redundant. If units cannot be verified, the 0.5-point clinical contrast is not reported and the result is inconclusive.

Exclude participants with any qualifying component diagnosis or death on/before the repeat landmark. Report baseline-to-repeat disease and death as selection losses, not as eligible follow-up. The target is therefore the repeat-observed, disease-free landmark population; comparison with people lacking a valid repeat and stabilized baseline inverse-probability-of-repeat weighting are selection diagnostics, not causal recovery of excluded people.

## Outcome and temporal rules

In health_outcomes, use aligned pairs `41270-j` (inpatient diagnosis code) and `41280-j` (its date), for every available j, retaining only valid code/date pairs from the same j. Define first post-landmark dates for ICD-10 E11-E14 (type 2 diabetes), N18.x (chronic kidney disease), and I50.x (heart failure). The primary event is the second distinct family: the later of the first two distinct post-landmark family dates; same-date distinct families count. Unresolved code/date alignment is an ascertainment failure, not absence. Follow to the second-family event, death, or administrative end 2023-03-31. Death before the event is a competing event. A one-year delayed-outcome analysis and a 1–6-year baseline-to-repeat restriction are prespecified reverse-causation checks.

Secondary outcomes are each first component, first any component, all-cause death and an expanded outcome adding I20-I25 and I60-I69. They cannot replace the primary endpoint if the primary becomes infeasible.

## Analysis and baselines

First produce a complete flow: source row counts, eid uniqueness and overlap agreement, date and biomarker validity, repeat interval, missing/negative/out-of-range counts without global negative recoding, disease/death exclusions, aligned outcome counts, family order/ties, follow-up, events and competing deaths.

The primary analysis is a cause-specific hazards model for the second-family event with death competing, using a prespecified flexible joint surface in C and B plus age, sex, BMI, systolic/diastolic BP and verified baseline renal covariates. Standardize 5-year cumulative incidence with Aalen–Johansen and report absolute risk differences, risk ratios, cause-specific hazards, curves and participant-bootstrap 95% intervals. The nested baseline is M0=current HbA1c plus baseline covariates; M1 adds baseline/history surface. The secondary deterioration parameterization uses baseline plus change. Fine–Gray is sensitivity only. Repeat-weighted, complete-case and within-resampling imputed analyses are reported with positivity/effective-sample-size diagnostics.

A bounded model-learning alternative (regularized spline surface or shallow gradient boosting) may be fit only as a prespecified sensitivity to the same landmark cohort and outcome. It must be compared with M0 using calibration, Brier score and optimism-corrected incremental performance; AUC or a small prediction gain is not evidence of clinical utility or of the hypothesis.

## Falsification and interpretation

Before fitting, define clinical materiality as an absolute 5-year risk difference of 2 percentage points, explicitly a research threshold requiring expert review rather than a treatment threshold. Support for the expected direction requires all gates to pass; at least 100 primary events overall and 10 in each displayed exposure cell; adequate common support; a positive primary history contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero and point estimate at least 2 points; consistent direction after one-year washout, interval restriction, competing-risk and repeat-weighted analyses; and no equal-or-larger association for fixed negative-control inpatient appendicitis K35.x, with its event count reported.

An opposite material contrast is adverse to the expected direction but evidence that history matters. A null with a narrow interval excluding the 2-point threshold supports current HbA1c sufficiency for this endpoint in this selected population, not universal irrelevance of trajectories. A current-level association without history increment supports static risk association but falsifies the added-history claim. Disappearance after washout or weighting suggests reverse causation or measured selection. Results are inconclusive if any hard data gate fails, event/overlap thresholds fail, weights are unstable, dates are not ordered, the endpoint is sparse, or uncertainty spans both trivial and clinically material effects.

Computation can establish availability, filtering, temporal ordering, inpatient-code-defined disease-order associations, competing-risk estimates, model calibration and gate status. It cannot establish outpatient disease, adjudicated diagnoses, disease severity, measured physiology, treatment effects, causality, representativeness, or benefit from repeat testing. Those require validated dictionaries and dates, outpatient laboratory/medication data, adjudication, external validation and a prospective testing-and-management study.
