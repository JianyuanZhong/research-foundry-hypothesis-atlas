# Outcome-ascertainment and missing-Y sensitivity for frozen repeat-TACE HCC P-beyond-B validation

## Purpose and unchanged scientific schema

This is a compiler-ready successor of `[prior hypothesis]`, combining its executed outcome-blind support/transport audit with the missing-outcome partial-identification layer of `[prior hypothesis]` and the explicit ascertainment bindings of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the frozen scientific schema exactly. It does not introduce a treatment comparison, causal estimand, safety endpoint, utility endpoint, clinical-benefit claim, or new laboratory outcome.

The unresolved falsifiable question is: among adults already selected for a recorded repeat TACE and an outcome-blind eligible baseline/pre-repeat assay pair, does contemporaneous pre-repeat value `P` improve prediction of the subsequent same-encounter assay value `Y` beyond remote baseline `B` and the locked decision-time core, and can that predictive contrast remain favorable when unobserved `Y` values are allowed to vary over a prespecified training-observed assay range? The primary claim remains a selected, short-horizon prognostic prediction claim. The added layers test whether calendar support and outcome ascertainment make the observed complete-triplet result selection-sensitive; they do not recover clinical follow-up or assume missing at random.

The strongest executed evidence currently available is narrower: the inherited reconstruction and audit support a 2022 forward block, while 2023 and 2024 are not interpretable as transport evidence because of sparse systemic arms and substantial Y-observation loss. The parent audit found 2023 `29/43` and 2024 `14/70` Y observation among BP-eligible episodes; 2024 has only one observed systemic complete outcome. The new bounds and ascertainment outputs must not be described as executed until a result artifact is produced.

## Frozen event, assay clocks, eligibility, and outcome

For each selected repeat event, retain exactly the inherited clocks:

- `event_time`: selected repeat procedure `开始时间`.
- `B`: latest valid uncensored assay on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1; `B_time` is its `检验时间`.
- `P`: latest valid assay in the selected repeat encounter during `[event_time − 72 hours, event_time)`; `P_time` is its `检验时间`.
- `Y`: valid assay in that encounter during `(event_time, event_time + 72 hours]`, selected nearest +24 hours with the inherited deterministic tie rule; `Y_time` is its `检验时间`.

Require `B_time < P_time < event_time < Y_time`, positive P lead (`event_time − P_time > 0`), one row per patient-assay event after deterministic deduplication, and no laboratory-informed event selection. Keep albumin and total bilirubin as separate assay-specific rows. Define `BP_eligible` before reading or selecting Y as the conjunction of selected event, valid B, valid P, strict clock ordering, and all inherited non-laboratory eligibility rules. Define `Y_observed` only afterward from valid Y. `complete = BP_eligible AND Y_observed` is exactly the frozen complete-triplet analysis population, not a new target population.

## Training-range partial-identification layer for missing Y

This layer is secondary and must be computed independently of the primary complete-triplet result. For each assay and origin, fit M0/M1 on the same pre-origin observed complete triplets, freeze predictions, and calculate predictions for every future BP-eligible row (all have B and P by construction). Retain only aggregate output; patient identifiers and raw values are forbidden.

Define `L_train = min(Y_train)` and `U_train = max(Y_train)` from pre-origin observed Y values using the exact assay-specific parser. Establish the range before reading future Y. If the training range is degenerate, or any later observed Y lies outside `[L_train,U_train]`, mark the bounded analysis `unsupported` and do not silently widen it. A full-source assay range may be reported only as a non-temporal descriptive check and cannot rescue a temporal bound.

For each future BP-eligible row with fixed predictions `p0`, `p1`:

- observed rows use `e0=(Y-p0)^2`, `e1=(Y-p1)^2`, and `d=e0-e1`;
- missing rows use no imputed value. For model j, the rowwise squared-error minimum over `[L_train,U_train]` is `(clip(pj,L_train,U_train)-pj)^2` and the maximum is `max((L_train-pj)^2,(U_train-pj)^2)`;
- the direct contrast `d=(Y-p0)^2-(Y-p1)^2` is affine in Y, so its rowwise extrema occur at L and U. Sum observed and missing contributions and divide by the all-BP-eligible row count to report sharp MSE intervals for M0, M1, and `D = MSE(M0)-MSE(M1)`, overall and by arm/block.

Report the unchanged observed-complete-triplet primary RMSE gain alongside missing counts/fractions and the all-BP-eligible MSE contrast interval. Do not call the latter a standardized test RMSE gain: test SD is unidentified with missing Y. An optional dimensionless display may divide MSE contrast by fixed training `SD_train(Y)^2`, labeled secondary only.

Compute three locked endpoint patterns for each assay/origin/block: all missing Y at L; all at U; and rowwise least-favorable endpoint for the direction M1 versus M0 (the endpoint minimizing D, with deterministic tie to L). These are mathematical support scenarios, not plausible imputations. Compute a deterministic common-displacement tipping point: let `m_obs` be the observed future-Y mean among rows with Y, and for `delta` in the closed interval that keeps `clip(m_obs + delta,L,U)` defined, set every missing Y to `clip(m_obs + delta,L,U)` solely for evaluating D. Find every sign-changing root of the resulting piecewise-quadratic D(delta) by evaluating all interval breakpoints and exact quadratic roots; report the root with minimum absolute displacement, or `no_root_in_support` if none exists. If the observed-case mean is unavailable, report tipping point non-estimable. Never treat this scenario as an imputation model.

The bound is `robust_favorable` only if D remains strictly positive over the entire specified range and all support/observation gates pass; if its interval contains zero or the tipping point is inside/near the range, label `selection_sensitive`; if it reverses under a supported completion, label `adverse`; if the range is unsupported, cells are too sparse, or gates fail, label `inconclusive_or_unbounded`. The bound cannot establish transport, causality, clinical validity, or patient benefit.

## Interpretation and falsification

Supportive evidence requires all reconstruction and clock assertions, outcome-blind and complete-triplet support gates, an adequately supported block, the parent's prespecified primary RMSE criterion, no material arm-specific ascertainment warning, and a bounded all-BP-eligible D that remains favorable over the supported range. This means only selected short-horizon prognostic robustness in that calendar mixture under an explicitly mathematical support scenario.

Adverse evidence is a nonpositive primary contrast in an adequately supported block, repeated M1 degradation, or a supported bounded completion that reverses D. This challenges the incremental predictive interpretation or demonstrates outcome-selection sensitivity; it does not prove B is sufficient or identify a mechanism. A favorable complete-triplet estimate with D crossing zero is selection-sensitive, not supportive. A favorable later point estimate with failed arm support remains descriptive. Failed ranges, low observation ESS, outside-calendar support, influential episodes, or non-estimable ascertainment cells are inconclusive rather than null findings.

Retain the executed parent facts without upgrading them: the full-source audit scanned all five inherited sources and found 242 BP-eligible episodes per assay; 2022 had 29 BP-eligible (10 systemic/19 comparator) and 28 observed outcomes (10/18); 2023 had 43 BP-eligible (9/34) and 29 observed (5/24); 2024 had 70 BP-eligible (9/61) and 14 observed (1/13). Only the 2022 block passed the stated support gates. The new ascertainment and bound analyses are unexecuted in this proposal.

No output may contain patient identifiers, raw laboratory values, clinical text, notes, or direct source rows. Derived outputs must be aggregate JSON/CSV containing source paths/hashes, row counts, denominator assertions, clock assertions, support and selection flags, opportunity-pattern counts, primary metrics, MSE bounds, tipping-point statuses, and interpretation labels.
