# Hypothesis: MAP Trajectory Predicts Clinical Outcomes Better Than Minimum MAP

## Scientific Question

**At comparable minimum mean arterial pressure (MAP) levels, patients whose MAP shows a more favorable trajectory (higher mean, lower variability, less time below target) during the first 24 hours of ICU admission experience improved outcomes (lower mortality and reduced acute kidney injury), after adjusting for shock etiology, vasopressor burden, and comorbidities.**

## Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

### Current Understanding
Current clinical practice and research have focused on:
- Minimum blood pressure thresholds as a proxy for hemodynamic stability
- Minimum MAP as a risk stratification marker
- Static "best" or "worst" values from admission vitals

### The Gap
These approaches have limitations:
1. **Minimum MAP is temporally arbitrary**: A single minimum value doesn't capture the dynamic nature of hemodynamic instability
2. **Ignores trajectory information**: Patients with the same minimum MAP may have vastly different MAP courses (rapid correction vs. sustained instability)
3. **May miss early warning signals**: Subtle, sustained MAP abnormalities may predict deterioration before they become apparent in minimum values

### Clinical Importance
This hypothesis addresses a critical gap in early sepsis resuscitation strategies. Current guidelines often focus on minimum blood pressure thresholds, but dynamic trajectory information may provide more actionable insights for:
- Early identification of patients at risk of deterioration
- Refinement of resuscitation targets beyond simple thresholds
- Personalized risk stratification in ICU populations

## Hypothesis

At the same minimum MAP level, patients with more favorable MAP trajectories (higher mean MAP, lower MAP variability, less time below target MAP, steeper upward slope) during the first 24 hours of ICU admission experience:
1. Lower 28-day mortality
2. Reduced incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI)

## Study Population

Patients meeting all of the following criteria:
1. Admitted to ICU with confirmed shock (treatment table indicates shock etiology)
2. Continuous blood pressure monitoring during first 24 hours of ICU admission
3. Complete laboratory data including serum creatinine
4. Vasopressor administration during admission
5. Known shock etiology (sepsis, cardiac, hemorrhagic, etc.)

### Primary Data Sources (eICU-CRD)

   - Join key: `subject_id` (patient identifier)

   - Columns: age, sex
   - Purpose: Covariate adjustment

## Trajectory Metrics

For each patient's first 24 hours of ICU admission, compute:

1. **Minimum MAP** (`min_map`)
   - Definition: Lowest MAP value during first 24 hours
   - Purpose: Baseline comparison with expert seed

2. **Mean MAP** (`mean_map_24h`)
   - Definition: Average MAP over first 24 hours
   - Purpose: Overall hemodynamic status

3. **MAP variability** (`map_cv_24h`)
   - Definition: Coefficient of variation of MAP over first 24 hours
   - Purpose: Hemodynamic stability

4. **Time below target** (`time_below_target_24h`)
   - Definition: Duration during which MAP < 65 mmHg (or patient-specific target)
   - Purpose: Burden of hypoperfusion

5. **MAP slope** (`map_slope_24h`)
   - Definition: Linear regression slope of MAP over first 24 hours
   - Purpose: Direction of hemodynamic change

## Outcomes

1. **28-day mortality**
   - Definition: Death within 28 days of ICU admission
   - Source: patient table or survival indicator

2. **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)**
   - Definition: KDIGO stage ≥1 within 24 hours of admission
   - Criteria: Increase in creatinine ≥0.3 mg/dL or ≥50% from baseline, or initiation of dialysis
   - Source: lab table

## Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis
1. Partition patients by minimum MAP quartiles
2. Within each quartile, compare trajectory metrics between outcome groups
3. Test hypothesis that more favorable trajectories predict better outcomes

### Secondary Analysis
1. Compare trajectory metrics between outcome groups across all minimum MAP levels
2. Test for interaction between minimum MAP and trajectory metrics
3. Stratify analysis by shock etiology

### Adjustment
1. Multivariable logistic regression for mortality
2. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards for AKI
3. Covariates: age, sex, shock etiology, vasopressor burden, comorbidities

### Falsification Criteria
The hypothesis would be falsified if:
1. No association between trajectory metrics and outcomes after adjustment
2. Worse trajectory metrics predict better outcomes (reverse relationship)
3. Minimum MAP alone predicts outcomes as well as or better than trajectory metrics

## Limitations

1. **Data quality**: Missing BP readings may bias trajectory estimates
2. **Artifact handling**: Automated BP monitoring may include artifacts
3. **Target definition**: Target MAP may vary by institution and protocol
4. **Causality**: Observational design cannot establish causation
5. **Generalizability**: Single-center data (if applicable) may limit external validity
