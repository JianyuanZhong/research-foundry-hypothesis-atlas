# Episode 11 successor: support-ordered, noncausal lagged discordant-AKI comparison

Status: substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. Design only: no cohort scan, fit, Harbor execution, or clinical result was run.

## Scientific question and advance

Creatinine recovery accompanied by low urine output or positive recorded balance can be a clinically useful warning pattern, but it can also be selected by the support process. A patient receiving more administered input, more intensive recording, ventilation, or renal support may have both a different measured phenotype and a different short-term outcome. The six-hour washout prevents the landmark observation from being used as a same-time outcome, but it does not establish that the pre-landmark contrast is independent of treatment selection or surveillance.

The strongest evidence supports bounded premises: creatinine can overestimate measured kidney function during critical illness and muscle loss [K1]; urine-output windows and persistence rules change AKI classification and mortality prediction [K2]; recorded fluid balance and support intensity track ICU AKI/mortality while fluid capture and kidney-support initiation are incomplete or discretionary [K3]. Those findings do not establish this MIMIC phenotype, true GFR, true volume status, treatment intent, or causality.

Untested hypothesis:

> In adults with a creatinine-defined AKI episode who reach the same prespecified 24-, 48-, or 72-hour landmark in their first eligible ICU stay, a lagged recovery/urine/recorded-balance discordance has a component-specific seven-day hospital-death or ICU-recorded-RRT risk difference versus concordant recovery that remains directionally present in adequately supported pre-landmark strata and is not confined to support-first or high-surveillance patients.

The strongest rival predicts that the contrast will attenuate or concentrate in patients with high administered-input/support intensity, support recorded before the first renal-defining observation, or dense documentation. A second rival is unmeasured biology and creatinine/muscle measurement error. This is a prognostic/measurement comparison, not a treatment-effect estimate. Persistence would establish a reproducible association within the observed risk sets; it would not establish a renal mechanism or that support caused the phenotype.

The substantive advance over the parent is an executable ordering test. It separates (i) the renal state construction, (ii) the observed support process, and (iii) surveillance opportunity; it reports support-first, renal-first, simultaneous and no-support patterns. Because administered input can itself create positive recorded balance, the primary order diagnostic is paired with an input-excluded organ-support order sensitivity. Support is never silently “controlled away” as a causal confounder.

- renal-first: `r_L < s_L-1 minute);

Also record the first support class (input-volume, RRT, ventilation, or multiple) and the exact timestamps. Because input may itself generate positive recorded balance, repeat this order diagnostic with input rows removed from `s_L`, leaving only dictionary-verified organ support. The input-excluded result is the cleaner test of whether the renal observation was preceded by other support; it does not erase the primary input-driven selection pathway. Do not call either order contrast causal.

Primary support intensity strata are fixed using positive-input values in the training partition for each landmark: no compatible input; positive input below the training-partition median; positive input at/above that median. Do not calculate cutpoints from the held-out test set or from outcomes.

Surveillance opportunity is separately summarized as primary output row count and three-bin coverage, non-creatinine `hosp/labevents` count, and non-weight `icu/chartevents` count in S_L. ## Risk sets, outcomes and informative discharge

At each L, the risk set is the retained-stay population alive at Ltime, still in the clipped index ICU interval at Ltime, without incident RRT before Ltime, and with exposure/state opportunity determined without washout data. Report losses from death, ICU exit, RRT, missing creatinine, insufficient opportunity, invalid timing and washout contamination.

For hospital death D, use the first valid `hosp/admissions.deathtime` in `[Ltime,Ltime+7d)`, no later than valid `dischtime`, with internally consistent `hospital_expire_flag` as a check rather than a substitute. Death remains observable after ICU exit while the admission is active. Alive hospital discharge is an explicit competing event; an alive in-hospital subject at day 7 is administrative end-of-window censoring. A deathtime equal to dischtime is a death only when timing/flag consistency passes; otherwise it is a timing exception.

For ICU-recorded RRT R, use the first procedureevents row in the exact RRT set with finite `starttime` in `[Ltime,Ltime+7d)`, strictly before clipped ICU exit and while alive. ICU exit alive and hospital death before RRT compete; the earliest valid time wins, with death winning an exact tie. A start exactly at ICU exit or death is not an RRT event and is a tie exception. Missing `starttime` is unobservable and counted. Do not backfill ward or post-ICU RRT.

The primary estimand is noncausal and component-specific:
`RD_D(L,s)=F_D(7d | discordant,s)-F_D(7d | concordant,s)` and
`RD_R(L,s)=F_R(7d | discordant,s)-F_R(7d | concordant,s)`,
where each F is the observed Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence in the same L risk set and support stratum s, with the stated competing events. L=48 is the primary landmark; L=24 and 72 are prespecified replication/boundary tests. Report the empirical contrasts and a fixed-summary standardized contrast, each with the complete risk-set denominator.

Discharge/exit is not assumed non-informative: it is a competing event in the primary estimand, reported with its own CIF and counts. Add an observation-complete sensitivity restricted to subjects remaining observable for the relevant endpoint through day 7; label it a selection sensitivity, not a corrected population estimate. A pre-L support/density model may produce stabilized inverse-observation weights as a secondary descriptive sensitivity, but it must use only t0 and S_L variables, truncate weights at prespecified 1st/99th percentiles, report truncation and positivity, and never replace the primary competing-event analysis. If there are no adequate support cells, do not weight or merge them to manufacture precision.

Estimate Aalen–Johansen CIFs and risk differences overall and by intensity/order stratum. Use a six-hour discrete-time cause-specific hazard implementation only as a computational route to the same competing-event estimand. Use 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap replicates, preserving all admissions for a subject in each replicate and split; report percentile intervals, bootstrap failures and the number of usable replicates. Model-based uncertainty is secondary to the empirical AJ audit.

## Sparse strata and falsification

A support/intensity/order cell is estimable only if it has at least 50 risk-set subjects and at least 10 events for the component outcome. Otherwise publish its denominator, state counts, competing-event counts and “inconclusive/not estimable”; never merge cells after viewing outcomes. The overall contrast remains reported, and a prespecified coarse diagnostic may combine support-first and renal-first only when both component cells separately satisfy the threshold. Unknown/unresolved support and opportunity are reported, not imputed.

The primary falsification set is fixed before fitting:

1. A process-only exposure replaces renal discordance with quartiles of S_L non-creatinine laboratory plus non-weight chart density. A similar component gradient, or attenuation of the renal contrast after density restriction, weakens a renal-recovery interpretation.
2. Compare support-first, renal-first and simultaneous order. A contrast confined to support-first, especially if it disappears in input-excluded organ-support order, supports support-process selection.
3. Compare the six-hour-washout exposure with the boundary-contaminated current-window sensitivity. A result present only when the washout is violated is adverse to the lagged interpretation.
4. Compare primary direct-urine items with the excluded measurement-error items and all-three versus two-of-three bin completeness. Dependence on excluded or sparse measurement is adverse.
5. Compare hospital death and ICU-RRT components separately. A union-only signal, or an ICU-RRT signal that is explained by ICU exit/recording opportunity, is not evidence for one common renal mechanism.

Supportive evidence requires a clinically nontrivial, directionally consistent component contrast at L=48 and at least one of L=24/72, adequate support cells, no concentration in support-first/high-density cells, and a process-only gradient smaller and qualitatively different from the renal contrast. Adverse evidence includes attenuation after support/order restriction, support-first-only signal, process-gradient equivalence, current-window-only signal, excluded-item dependence, or component mismatch driven by discharge/exit. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse cells, wide intervals spanning clinically meaningful benefit and harm, many unknown states, unstable bootstrap, nonpositivity, timing exceptions, or a sequence-model gain without a reproducible component/order contrast. An imprecise null is not decisive refutation.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative

The fixed-summary baseline uses exactly the same t0, S_L and A_L inputs as the sequence alternative: pre-t0 creatinine baseline/AKI stage; A_L creatinine level, peak, slope and masks; three-bin urine rates/masks and item composition; accepted input volume, recorded balance, row counts, unresolved fraction and categories; weight and exploratory CVP; pre-L RRT/organ-support indicators and durations; support intensity/order; ICU timing, admission type, age, sex; non-creatinine/non-weight density and missingness. Fit separate six-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards for hospital death versus alive discharge and ICU-RRT versus ICU exit/death, with support-by-state/order interactions. Standardize held-out CIFs and risk differences, and report calibration, time-dependent Brier score, component discrimination, event counts and cluster-bootstrap intervals. This is primary because its rows, order and estimand are auditable.

The substantive alternative is a small GRU (or temporal convolution if the runtime lacks a stable GRU implementation) with separate cause-specific heads. It receives the same six-hour bins from t0 through Ltime-6h: creatinine, urine values/masks, accepted input volume/composition/missingness, weight/CVP, organ-support indicators, non-renal density and missingness. It cannot receive washout or post-L data. Its target is the discrete-time competing state: hospital death, alive hospital discharge, ICU-RRT, alive ICU exit, or administrative continuation. Use a subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split, with all admissions for one subject in one split; fit each landmark with training-only support cutpoints and preprocessing. Evaluate on the identical held-out subjects using component CIF calibration, time-dependent Brier, cause-specific discrimination, support-stratum/order calibration and bootstrap intervals. The sequence model is scientifically useful only if it reveals timing/persistence or support-response trajectories that change component/order interpretation; an AUROC-only improvement or a small predictive gain does not justify replacing the baseline.

## Clinical limits and alternatives not chosen

The configured data do not provide cystatin C/measured GFR, muscle mass, tubular biomarkers, adjudicated total-body volume or insensible losses, treatment intent/indication, complete bedside urine/fluid collection, reliable post-ICU urine, complete ward RRT, or prospective goals of care. Recorded input is not complete treatment exposure; chart density is not a validated surveillance score; ICU-recorded RRT is not all-hospital RRT. Clinical adjudication or another prospective/external study is required for mechanism, treatment effects, true kidney function/volume, complete support capture and transportability. Automatic Harbor verification can check bindings, joins, construction, estimates, uncertainty, split integrity and interpretation-to-output linkage, but cannot establish those clinical claims.

A causal treatment analysis, post-ICU/ward RRT correction, or a single learned model as primary is deferred because intent, complete exposure and adjudicated endpoints are unavailable. A larger neural model is also deferred: the small sequence alternative already tests the substantive timing/persistence uncertainty, and additional capacity would not resolve unmeasured support selection. Revisit with an adjudicated cohort containing complete support intent, total urine/fluid capture, measured filtration, and post-ICU RRT follow-up. Abandon the direction after a well-powered observation-complete validation finds no reproducible component/order contrast.
