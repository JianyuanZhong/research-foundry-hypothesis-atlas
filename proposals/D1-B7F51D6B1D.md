# Residual instability before an ICU-to-ward transfer and 48-hour deterioration

Status: complete experiment proposal; no outcome model has been fit and no hypothesis result is claimed. Parent: `[prior hypothesis]` (expert seed `[starting question]`). Dataset: MIMIC-IV 3.1 core.

## Clinical opening, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

The strongest available evidence supports three bounded claims. A recent MIMIC-IV/external-hospital study found that a GRU-D++ model discriminated seven-day ICU readmission/death better than SWIFT, but it averaged variables at one-day intervals, explicitly noted that this can lose short-term variability, and did not fully handle operational factors or DNR status [K1]. A systematic review found that stronger ICU-readmission models commonly used clearly defined outcomes and longitudinal routinely collected predictors, while definitions and bias varied across studies [K2]. More generally, EHR measurement timing and frequency can carry more prognostic information than measured values because records reflect care processes as well as physiology [K3].

Those results do **not** establish that persistence or volatility of physiologic and oxygen abnormalities in the final 24 hours before a specific ICU-to-general-ward transfer adds clinically important 48-hour information beyond the last-measurement snapshot. They also do not establish whether any apparent trajectory signal is biological residual instability, intensified observation, or discharge selection.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis.** Among adults making their first eligible ICU-to-general-ward transfer in a hospitalization after at least 24 hours in the ICU, prespecified final-24-hour persistence and volatility features from heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, SpO2, temperature, FiO2, oxygen flow, and oxygen-device charting improve calibrated prediction of ICU return or in-hospital death within 48 hours beyond the same channels' last pre-transfer measurements, recent organ support, measured charting intensity, code status, and transfer context. “Improve” requires both a statistically supported and prespecified clinically material held-out gain; association alone is insufficient.

This is a prognostic and measurement-discrimination hypothesis, not a causal claim that delaying transfer prevents deterioration. The leading explanation is unresolved physiologic/oxygen instability. The strongest rivals are:

1. **Observation artifact:** clinicians measure unstable patients more often, so chart density, recency, missingness, and store lag—not value trajectories—carry the signal.
2. **Discharge selection:** destination, night/weekend timing, ICU/service, recent organ support, and treatment limitations jointly affect who is transferred and whether deterioration leads to readmission versus death.
3. **Endpoint sufficiency:** once the final value and support state are known, preceding persistence/volatility adds no clinically useful information.

The predictions differ. Biological residual instability predicts incremental value from measured-value trajectories after density adjustment, persistence in dense-observation and no-treatment-limitation analyses, and loss of gain when values—not masks—are permuted. Observation artifact predicts a strong measurement-only model, similar performance from masks/timing alone, and marked attenuation in dense-observation or value-permutation controls. Endpoint sufficiency predicts no material trajectory gain despite adequate coverage and event precision.

**Clinical importance and substantive advance.** The 48-hour window targets the immediate ward-monitoring and transfer decision, not the broader seven-day risk studied in [K1]. The study changes the question from “can a longitudinal model predict?” to “what information is gained over the actual endpoint snapshot, is that gain clinically material, and does it survive explicit observation/discharge-process challenges?” A supportive result would justify external validation of trajectory-aware transfer review or ward-monitoring allocation. An adverse result would discourage adding opaque trajectory burden when current state and care-process context suffice.

## Population and fixed landmark

The unit is a hospital admission's **first eligible ICU-to-general-ward transfer**. A subject may contribute more than one hospitalization, but all admissions for one `subject_id` remain in one split and bootstrap cluster.

For every row in `icu/icustays`, let `t0 = outtime`. Join a successor `hosp/transfers` row on exact `subject_id, hadm_id` and `transfers.intime = icustays.outtime`. An eligible index transfer must meet all of the following:

- age at ICU admission `anchor_age + year(icustays.intime) - anchor_year >= 18`;
- ICU length of stay `los >= 1.0` day;
- alive at `t0`: `admissions.deathtime` is null or greater than `t0`;
- exact successor destination is one of the frozen general-ward values below;
- the transfer is the earliest eligible transfer by `outtime, stay_id` within `hadm_id`.

The frozen primary general-ward set is: `Medicine`, `Neurology`, `Med/Surg`, `Medicine/Cardiology`, `Transplant`, `Vascular`, `Med/Surg/GYN`, `Surgery/Trauma`, `Med/Surg/Trauma`, `Surgery`, `Cardiac Surgery`, `Medical/Surgical (Gynecology)`, `Surgery/Pancreatic/Biliary/Bariatric`, `Cardiology`, `Thoracic Surgery`, `Oncology`, and `Hematology/Oncology`.

The primary cohort excludes any successor ICU, PACU, emergency/observation unit, discharge lounge, psychiatry, obstetric/nursery unit, `UNKNOWN`/`Unknown`, and all intermediate/step-down units. This holds the receiving monitoring environment more nearly constant. A prespecified sensitivity adds these exact intermediate/step-down destinations as separate strata: `Cardiology Surgery Intermediate`, `Hematology/Oncology Intermediate`, `Medicine/Cardiology Intermediate`, `Neuro Intermediate`, `Neuro Stepdown`, `Surgery/Vascular/Intermediate`, and `Surgical Intermediate`.

The exposure window is exactly `[t0-24h, t0)`; values at or after `t0` are forbidden. The endpoint snapshot uses the last valid observation before `t0`, with a separate indicator for whether it is within four hours. No outcome, post-transfer ward measurement, discharge summary, or future transfer enters a predictor.

## Outcome and temporal logic

The primary outcome is the first of:

- a new ICU stay in the same `hadm_id` with `icustays.intime > t0` and `<= t0 + 48h`; or
- in-hospital death with `admissions.deathtime > t0` and `<= t0 + 48h`.

If both occur, the earlier event time is retained and the composite is positive. Live hospital discharge before 48 hours is an observed competing state, not censoring silently assumed independent. The main prediction target is the three-state 48-hour outcome: deterioration, live hospital discharge before deterioration, or alive/in-hospital without deterioration at 48 hours. The reported primary probability is the deterioration class. Component ICU-return and death cumulative incidences are mandatory secondary outputs. A cause-specific sensitivity treats live discharge as competing and reports cumulative incidence; it must not reinterpret live discharge as 48 hours of observed ward stability.

Unplanned versus planned ICU return is not directly labeled. A sensitivity excludes returns preceded by a PACU or operating/procedural transfer row in `hosp/transfers`; definitive planned-return classification requires clinical adjudication.

## Measurements and prespecified feature construction

All numeric chart values first pass fixed plausibility checks, with rejected counts reported: heart rate 20–250/min; SBP 40–300 mmHg; MAP 20–200 mmHg; respiratory rate 4–80/min; SpO2 50–100%; temperature 30–43 °C after Fahrenheit conversion; oxygen flow 0–100 L/min; FiO2 0.21–1.0 after converting values >1 and <=100 by division by 100. No data-dependent winsorization is allowed.

Create twelve left-closed two-hour bins in `[t0-24h,t0)`. Use the median within a bin for continuous values. For BP, prefer invasive over noninvasive within a bin and retain source. Preserve masks, count, distinct chart hours, time since last observation, median/IQR `storetime-charttime`, and distinct `caregiver_id` count.

Prespecified abnormal states are: heart rate <50 or >110/min; SBP <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg; respiratory rate <10 or >24/min; SpO2 <92%; temperature <36.0 or >38.3 °C; and elevated oxygen support defined by FiO2 >0.30, oxygen flow >4 L/min, or an exact observed device value mapped before model fitting to high-flow, reservoir/face-mask, noninvasive positive-pressure, or invasive-airway support. Missing oxygen documentation is unknown, never room air. The solver must freeze an `oxygen_device_dictionary.csv` listing every observed item 226732 value, count, exact normalized mapping, and unmapped values before opening test outcomes; fuzzy substring expansion is forbidden.

For each physiologic/oxygen channel, prespecified trajectory features are:

- abnormal observed-bin fraction (persistence);
- longest run of consecutive abnormal observed bins, with a missing bin breaking a run;
- number of normal-to-abnormal and abnormal-to-normal transitions;
- 90th–10th percentile range and median absolute successive difference, scaled on training data (volatility);
- Theil–Sen slope and early-12-hour minus late-12-hour median;
- worst value and final four-hour value;
- observed-bin count, record count, time since last observation, and missing-bin fraction.

Features requiring fewer than four observed bins are set missing with a missingness flag. Report lower/upper persistence bounds obtained by treating missing bins as normal/abnormal; these bounds are a measurement-uncertainty diagnostic, not substituted into the main feature. The prespecified trajectory-burden score is the mean across the six domains (heart rate, BP, respiratory rate, SpO2, temperature, oxygen support) of the training-empirical percentile ranks of four equally weighted quantities: abnormal-bin fraction, longest abnormal run divided by 12, median absolute successive difference, and transition count divided by 11. It is defined when at least four domains meet coverage; quartile cut points are learned in training and frozen.

## Treatment limitations, treatment context, and discharge selection

The last `chartevents.itemid=223758` value at or before `t0` is frozen as exact text and mapped to full-code, DNR/DNI, comfort-measures-only, other, or undocumented. Primary models include this variable and its interaction with outcome component; sensitivity analyses (a) exclude DNR/DNI/comfort-measures-only and (b) restrict to explicitly documented full code. “Undocumented” is not full code. Discharge summaries are created after the index decision and may describe later events; they are not model inputs. Determining true goals of care or whether a death/readmission was expected requires pre-transfer note review by clinicians and cannot be automated from item 223758 alone.

Recent treatment context includes any interval overlap with the final 24 hours for invasive ventilation and selected vasopressors, plus hours since the last interval ended. Exclude `statusdescription='Rewritten'`; require a positive `rate` or `amount` for input events. Destination, source ICU, latest clinical service at or before `t0`, ICU length of stay, prior ICU-stay count in the admission, admission type, transfer hour, night (19:00–06:59), and weekend are included. MIMIC does not provide reliable ICU occupancy, clinician discharge intent, ward staffing, rapid-response availability, or transport delay; these remain unmeasured selection variables.

## Models on one frozen question

### S0: endpoint-snapshot comparator

A ridge-penalized multinomial logistic model uses age, sex, admission type, source/last ICU, destination ward, latest service, ICU LOS, prior ICU-stay count, transfer hour/night/weekend, last value and four-hour freshness for every trajectory channel, last GCS components, recent ventilation/vasopressor state, code-status category, and all measurement-density variables. Continuous terms use training-fitted restricted cubic splines where nonlinearity is prespecified; missing values use training medians plus indicators. This is deliberately stronger than SWIFT and ensures that trajectory gain is tested beyond endpoint severity and charting context.

### T1: prespecified trajectory-feature baseline (primary scientific comparison)

T1 adds only the persistence, run, transition, volatility, slope, early-late, and worst-value features above to S0. Penalty strength is selected on validation log loss. The primary scientific contrast is T1 versus S0 on the untouched test set. A measurement-only model M contains static transfer context plus masks/counts/recency/store lag/caregiver counts, but no physiologic values; it quantifies the artifact rival.

### L1: substantive longitudinal alternative

Fit a compact GRU-D to the same twelve two-hour bins, with value, observation-mask, and time-since-last-measurement channels plus the identical static S0 context, to predict the same three-state target. GRU-D can represent nonlinear duration, cross-channel lag, late relapse, and interactions that fixed summaries lose. It is an adaptation, not a reproduction of [K1]: the horizon, MIMIC version, cohort, bins, inputs, endpoints, and artifact tests differ.

Freeze architecture and tuning before test evaluation: one or two recurrent layers, hidden size from {32,64}, dropout from {0,0.2}, Adam learning rate from {1e-3,3e-4}, early stopping on validation multinomial log loss, and five fixed random seeds. Calibrate each model on validation data using multinomial temperature scaling. The test set is opened once.

Artifact ablations are mandatory:

- **masks-only L1:** remove measured values but retain masks/deltas/static context;
- **values-with-standard-grid L1:** retain values while replacing informative deltas with fixed two-hour spacing and excluding mask features, evaluated only in the dense stratum;
- **within-stratum value permutation:** permute complete 24-hour value sequences across patients within destination, snapshot-risk decile, and measurement-density quintile while retaining each patient's masks/timestamps;
- **dense-observation analysis:** require heart rate, BP, respiratory rate, and SpO2 in at least eight distinct hours and at least one oxygen-support observation; report channel-specific support;
- **selection sensitivities:** no treatment limitation; full-code-only; daytime weekday; general ward plus step-down with destination interaction; first eligible transfer per subject rather than per admission.

L1 is retained as scientifically useful only if it is calibrated and materially improves on T1, not merely on S0, and measured values account for the gain. Otherwise the interpretable T1 is preferred. A small predictive increment without changed risk stratification or decision-curve benefit is not a substantive advance.

## Split, evaluation, estimands, and uncertainty

Because MIMIC timestamps are subject-specifically shifted, cross-subject calendar-time splitting is invalid. Assign subjects, not rows, to 60% train, 20% validation, and 20% test using a frozen seed (`20260923`), stratified at the subject level by any qualifying outcome and dominant destination group. All admissions from one subject stay together. Fit preprocessing, splines, imputation, penalties, device mappings, model selection, and calibration without test outcomes.

Primary estimands are:

1. paired test-set reduction in composite-class Brier score, `Brier(S0)-Brier(T1)`;
2. standardized 48-hour deterioration risk difference between the top and bottom quartiles of a training-frozen equal-weight trajectory burden score, standardized to the test distribution of S0 covariates;
3. secondary paired `Brier(T1)-Brier(L1)`.

Report multinomial log loss, composite AUROC/AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope and plots, component cumulative incidences, and decision-curve net benefit at 2%, 5%, and 10% risk thresholds. Use 2,000 subject-cluster bootstrap replicates for paired 95% intervals. Report destination and outcome-component heterogeneity as prespecified secondary analyses; no univariate predictor screening is allowed. For any secondary coefficient family, control false discovery rate by Benjamini–Hochberg and retain effect sizes/CIs.

## Falsification and interpretation contract

**Supportive for clinically important trajectory information:** T1 improves test Brier over S0 by at least 0.002 with a subject-bootstrap 95% CI entirely above zero; the standardized high-versus-low trajectory-burden risk difference is at least 2 percentage points with its CI above zero; calibration slope is 0.8–1.2; net benefit is positive versus S0 at at least two prespecified thresholds; and the direction remains in dense-observation and no-treatment-limitation analyses. Values must add beyond the masks-only model, and value permutation must remove most of the gain.

**Adverse/falsifying:** the upper 95% confidence bound rules out a 0.002 Brier improvement and a 2-point standardized risk difference despite adequate events/coverage, supporting endpoint sufficiency; or apparent gain is matched by masks-only data, survives value permutation, or attenuates by more than half in dense-observation analyses, supporting the observation/discharge-process rival rather than biological residual instability.

**Inconclusive:** intervals span the clinical thresholds; the held-out set has fewer than 200 deterioration events; more than 30% of transfers lack enough core-channel bins for trajectory estimation; calibration is outside 0.8–1.2 after validation-only recalibration; treatment-limitation or destination sensitivities reverse direction; or component outcomes conflict. Inconclusive is not confirmation or refutation.

Support establishes incremental prognostic information in this selected single-center cohort. It does not establish a biological mechanism, that instability caused deterioration, that transfer delay or additional monitoring would help, or transportability. Artifact-pattern results show that documentation/discharge processes explain the computable signal better; they do not prove absence of biological instability. A calibrated L1 gain over T1 shows useful unresolved temporal structure, not mechanism. Treatment benefit requires prospective impact evaluation; biological interpretation, planned-return status, and goals-of-care classification require clinical adjudication; transportability requires an external harmonized study.

## Feasibility evidence and execution budget

A completed small-table audit against the exact archive found 94,458 ICU stays; 94,444 had an exact successor transfer beginning at `icustays.outtime`. The broad adult, at-least-24-hour, alive-at-transfer general-ward cohort contained 53,001 transfers. Selecting the first eligible transfer per hospitalization left 49,157 transfers with 2,002 ICU returns, 661 in-hospital deaths, and 2,607 unique composite events within 48 hours (5.30%); 10,327 broad-cohort transfers ended in live hospital discharge within 48 hours. These are feasibility counts, not hypothesis results. A managed full-`chartevents` support scan is recorded separately and must be used only to confirm channel coverage and code-status values.

Full execution fits within the planning envelope of 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds. Estimated, not yet measured: extraction and feature construction with 8 CPUs/64 GiB, 2–4 hours; S0/T1/bootstrap with 8 CPUs/64 GiB, 1–3 hours; L1 five-seed training with one allocated A100, 8 CPUs/64 GiB, 2–4 hours. CPU execution is possible for the compact GRU but slower. GPU availability was verified deployment-wide; no GPU probe was needed to establish this proposal's feasibility.

## Alternatives considered and revisit conditions

- **SWIFT alone:** deferred because it is an intentionally weak snapshot comparator and [K1]/[K2] show inconsistent performance. Revisit only as a secondary benchmark if the required PaO2/FiO2 and GCS components have adequate local coverage.
- **Gradient-boosted trajectory summaries:** a reasonable predictive alternative but loses the direct interpretability of the nested S0/T1 scientific test and reveals less about temporal order than L1. Revisit if T1 shows clear gain but L1 optimization is unstable.
- **Joint latent-state/observation-process model:** scientifically attractive because it could model physiology and charting as separate processes, but its latent-state assumptions are not identifiable from this single-center retrospective record without stronger observation-process validation. Revisit with external sites, staffing/monitoring metadata, or chart adjudication.
- **Seven-day all-destination replication of [K1]:** deferred because it would mostly reproduce an answered predictive comparison and blur the immediate ward-transfer decision. Revisit only for external benchmarking after this 48-hour estimand is resolved.
