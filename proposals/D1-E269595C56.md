# Discharge-landmarked timing and observation of strict-prior HCC return risk

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent established a strong binary endpoint—any subsequent local recorded admission in 30 days after index discharge—and a strict completed-prior assay-content versus capture-intensity comparison. Its consequential unresolved validity and decision-relevance limitation is that the binary label treats a return on day 1 and day 29 identically, while the no-return group may mix patients re-observed locally with patients who die, use outside care or disappear from the released system. The available tables do not resolve those clinical states.

This successor preserves the parent’s binary 30-day estimand as the primary endpoint and adds a discharge-landmarked observed-care trajectory:

1. a frozen adult first HCC-keyed anchor cohort, strict completed-prior ledger, discharge landmark, binary return label, interval return label, later re-observation label, patient split and join/time audit;
2. admission-time and discharge-time versions of matched A, Q and E feature blocks, with strict prior laboratory content contrasted against strict prior capture/process intensity;
3. locked binary predictions for the parent’s 30-day outcome;
4. locked interval-hazard predictions and cumulative probabilities for mutually exclusive first-return states [0,7), [7,14), [14,30), and no recorded return in 30 days;
5. the incremental E-versus-Q contribution to the binary score and to each interval’s cumulative-incidence score, with a pre-specified test of whether the increment is larger in [14,30) than [0,14);
6. an observation-status sensitivity among complete 90- and 180-day follow-up anchors: no 30-day return followed by a local encounter in [30,90) or [30,180), versus no such recorded encounter;
7. locked predictions, uncertainty, falsification results and a claim-to-evidence table.

No model is fitted and no clinical conclusion is claimed in discovery.

## Question and falsifiable hypothesis

Among adults at their first locally recorded HCC-keyed inpatient encounter who later discharge, does strictly attributable assay content from completed prior encounters add information about the *timing* of a subsequent local recorded admission beyond the same discharge context and prior capture intensity?

The primary binary estimand remains:

> Among complete-follow-up anchors, does E improve held-out calibrated prediction of any same-patient encounter with native admission time in [td, td+30 days), beyond Q, where td is the anchor’s native discharge time?

The new trajectory hypothesis is:

> If the parent’s assay-content increment is real, it is not time-homogeneous: after accounting for Q, E will show a larger, stable incremental contribution to the late [14,30)-day return hazard/cumulative probability than to the early [0,14)-day trajectory, consistent with the observed concentration of recorded returns late in the window. A process-only control will not reproduce that interval-specific content increment.

This is falsifiable in either direction. An equal or larger early increment, no interval-specific increment, or complete reproduction by process-only controls is adverse to this directional hypothesis. The timing is never called urgent, planned, treatment-related or disease-specific because those fields are unavailable.

The observation-policy hypothesis is secondary and explicitly noncausal:

> Among 30-day nonreturns, E-Q differences will be attenuated or change materially when the comparison is restricted to anchors with a later local encounter in [30,90) or [30,180), indicating that the binary zero mixes local re-observation with unobserved follow-up states.

A stable result across these restrictions would support robustness to this particular observation marker; it still would not establish complete follow-up.

Existing evidence supports only that native encounter times can define a reproducible local recorded-return label and that prior assay payload can be separated from capture intensity under a strict availability policy. It does not support a biologic mechanism, readmission status, mortality status or clinical utility. The experiment tests the unresolved incremental-information and timing claims.

## Information sets and matched alternatives

All models use the same people, labels, discharge landmark, strict ledger, split, preprocessing and feature manifest. Direct identifiers (`name`, `identity-card number`, `mobile phone number`, `insurance/encounter card number`, `hospitalization number`) are excluded.

A (admission context) contains age, sex, department, calendar era, native admission time-of-day and missingness. A_d adds only native length of stay and discharge calendar/time-of-day for the discharge-landmark sensitivity. Q/Q_d adds strict prior capture/process features: completed prior encounter count and duration; active days; time since the last completed encounter; source-specific row counts and distinct linked-encounter counts for labs, procedures, orders, medications and examinations; observed source-type count; missing/excluded-time counts; left-truncation and no-history indicators. It contains no assay identity, assay value/result, medication name/dose/route/frequency/status, procedure name, order name or examination text.

E/E_d adds to Q/Q_d lab `Test` identity, `Qualitative result`, valid assay-specific `Quantitative result`, `Specimen type`, recency, span, count, missingness and within-assay change/slope only when at least two distinct valid `Test time` values exist. Numeric values are never pooled across assays because the catalog has no unit column. The primary E/Q history is [t0-730 days, t0), and a row is eligible only if its two-column key links to a non-anchor encounter for the same patient, that encounter has parseable admission and discharge with linked discharge < t0, and its source-specific availability time is parseable and in the half-open history window:

- labs: `Test time`;
- procedures: `Start time`;
- orders: `order time` (never later `start time`);
- medications: `Start time`;
- examinations: `start time`.

Rows linked to the anchor are excluded even if their event timestamp precedes t0. Count missing/invalid availability, missing linked discharge, open/overlapping linked stays, anchor-linked rows and post-t0 rows. Timestamp-only and anchor-linked histories are sensitivities, not primary evidence.

Simple baseline B0/B1: fit-only standardized regularized logistic models for the binary 30-day label using Q/Q_d (B0) and E/E_d (B1). These provide the direct parent-compatible E-Q comparison. For the trajectory output, use a matched regularized multinomial-logistic version on exactly the same Q/Q_d and E/E_d rows and features, with the four first-return states; report its first-three-state sum and proper vector scores.

Substantive alternative H0/H1: fit a discrete-time piecewise-exponential hazard model on person-interval rows for [0,7), [7,14), [14,30), with interval-specific baseline hazards and pre-specified E-by-interval terms. Use the same Q/Q_d and E/E_d feature vectors as B0/B1, same patient split and same fit-only preprocessing; only the likelihood representation changes. Estimate interval hazards, cumulative recorded-return probabilities and E-Q contrasts in each interval, with a complementary-log-log link and ridge/weakly informative regularization fixed in selection data. The model’s scientific value is the time-localized hazard decomposition and coherent survival constraint, not a small AUROC increase. Aggregate H predictions to the 30-day binary probability and compare its proper scores to B. The model can reveal whether the binary baseline’s single coefficient hides a late-versus-early information pattern; it cannot reveal clinical cause or urgency.

Retain the parent’s continuous-time latent ledger model S/S_Q as a deferrable exploratory branch only if the solver budget supports it after the primary hazard experiment. It is not selected as the main alternative because the new uncertainty is outcome timing/observation policy, which H addresses transparently with the same feature inputs and lower unverified resource demand. A neural sequence model or document/pathology branch is not scientifically required here: text/pathology lacks native time and validated extraction, while the timing estimand is already identified by encounters. Revisit those alternatives only if audit-log, release-time, validated text extraction or timestamped disease-stage evidence becomes available.

## Split, estimation and uncertainty

Use the frozen participant split SHA-256(normalized `Patient Master Index`) modulo 100: buckets 0–59 fit, 60–69 preprocessing/hyperparameter/fit-only calibration selection, 70–79 locked evaluation, and 80–99 inaccessible. All rows and interval records for a patient remain in one bucket. Freeze cohort, follow-up labels, strict ledger, feature manifest, interval boundaries, model dimensions, regularization and calibration before reading locked labels/predictions.

Primary binary outcomes are paired held-out patient-clustered changes in log loss and Brier score, score(E_d)-score(Q_d), lower better. The trajectory outcomes are vector/multiclass log loss and Brier score, plus one-vs-state calibration and the aggregate first-three-state probability. Report Q-A and E-Q for the binary and multinomial models. Define H_Q as the hazard model with Q_d and H_E as the hazard model with E_d; report H_E-H_Q for vector scores and for each interval’s cumulative probability. The primary trajectory contrast is the difference in E_d versus Q_d interval-specific cumulative probability and proper score, with a pre-specified interaction contrast (late [14,30) minus early [0,14)).

Use >=1,000 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples of locked predictions, sampling patients and retaining all their interval rows/encounters. Report 95% percentile or BCa intervals, calibration intercept/slope and reliability by state, threshold summaries only as descriptive. Do not use AUROC/AUPRC as the decision criterion. Report support-stratified diagnostics by no-history, Q-intensity, assay support and calendar era; these are internal heterogeneity checks, not external validation. For the 90/180-day re-observation sensitivity, report complete-case composition, missing follow-up boundaries and the change in E-Q score/interval contrast; do not interpret restriction as eliminating informative censoring.

## Falsification and audit criteria

1. Shuffle complete strict histories across patients within each partition while preserving Q; E-Q should attenuate for both binary and trajectory outputs.
2. Permute lab payload within assay and partition while preserving assay identity, timestamps and counts; a content increment should attenuate.
3. Permute assay identity while preserving values, times and counts; assay-specific signal should attenuate.
4. Shuffle strict event times within patient/source while preserving payload/counts; timing-sensitive history signal should attenuate.
5. Fit H_Q and compare with H_E; process-only reproduction rejects the assay-content interpretation.
6. Fit the binary and trajectory models on the same feature vectors; the trajectory aggregate must equal the binary prediction within numerical tolerance after calibration. Noncoherence is a computational failure.
7. Test sentinels for any row at/after t0, anchor-linked row, current-anchor row in primary history, post-discharge row, outcome-derived feature, duplicate-patient split leakage and follow-up beyond the snapshot.
8. Repeat exact-boundary, equal-time, overlapping-stay, missing-time, earliest-anchor and return-tie rules; material endpoint changes are inconclusive.
9. Reconcile the parent’s 42,688/4,916 audit with the independent 42,681/4,913 audit before locked evaluation; unexplained differences block confirmatory interpretation.
10. Permute labels within calendar era and department; persistent performance suggests leakage or label artifact.
11. For the observation-policy sensitivity, compare the re-observed and not-re-observed strata only after publishing their counts and administrative eligibility. A large E-Q reversal is evidence of observation-policy dependence, not proof of outside-care bias.

Supportive evidence requires: (i) a negative 95% patient-clustered interval for binary E_d-Q_d log loss and/or Brier without material calibration harm; (ii) a stable, negative E_d-Q_d trajectory proper-score contrast or a prespecified nonzero late-minus-early E-Q contrast; (iii) attenuation under payload/history permutation; (iv) no future-window or split sentinel; (v) no complete reproduction by Q-only/H_Q; and (vi) no unexplained cohort-ledger discrepancy. Supportive evidence means strict prior assay content adds reproducible information about this local recorded-return distribution and, if the interaction is present, that its timing is not represented by the binary endpoint.

Adverse evidence is no E-Q increment, calibration deterioration, no late-versus-early interaction, process-only reproduction, permutation persistence, a strict-to-timestamp reversal, or any leakage. Inconclusive evidence includes unresolved cohort counts, dominant missing-time burden, sparse interval states, wide clustered intervals, unstable calendar/source strata, substantial left truncation, or observation-status reversal. A positive hazard result with a negative binary result is not a contradiction: it would mean timing information does not improve the parent’s any-return probability enough to change binary proper score, and must be reported as trajectory-only evidence.
