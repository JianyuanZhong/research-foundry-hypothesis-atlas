# Hypothesis Proposal: Temporal Discordance Between Inflammation and Functional Reserve Predicts Multi-Organ Decline

## Scientific Question

**Among adults with established multimorbidity, does the temporal discordance between the rate of inflammatory burden decline and the rate of functional reserve decline over time predict subsequent multi-organ decline better than either measure alone, and does this relationship differ by baseline multimorbidity burden?**

## Unresolved Question

Current clinical practice focuses on:
1. **Baseline inflammation** (e.g., CRP, fibrinogen, WBC) as a static risk marker
2. **Baseline function** (e.g., grip strength, gait speed) as a static reserve measure
3. **Single-organ trajectories** (e.g., kidney function, cardiac function) in isolation

However, three critical questions remain unaddressed:
1. **Temporal dynamics**: How do inflammation and function change over time, and does the *rate* of change matter?
2. **Discordance**: Does the *divergence* between inflammatory and functional trajectories predict worse outcomes better than either alone?
3. **Multimorbidity modification**: Does the burden of multimorbidity modify the inflammatory-function relationship?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Early Risk Stratification**: Identifying patients whose inflammatory and functional trajectories are discordant could flag those at highest risk of multi-organ decline before organ failures become apparent.

2. **Precision Prevention**: Understanding which discordance patterns predict which organ systems could guide targeted interventions (e.g., anti-inflammatory strategies for certain discordance profiles, physical rehabilitation for others).

3. **Beyond Single-Organ Focus**: Current risk tools (SOFA, APACHE II, KDIGO) assess individual organs but fail to capture the *temporal convergence* of organ dysfunctions driven by inflammatory-function discordance.

4. **Mechanistic Insight**: The hypothesis tests whether the biological mechanism of multi-organ decline involves both inflammatory drive AND functional capacity depletion, with discordance between these processes being particularly dangerous.

## Scientific Advance Over Existing Knowledge

| Existing Work | Limitation | How This Proposal Advances |
|--------------|------------|---------------------------|
| Inflammation-functional discordance ([prior hypothesis]) | Baseline only; cross-sectional | Tests **temporal discordance** - rate of change over time, not just baseline levels |
| MODD trajectories ([prior hypothesis]) | Single timepoint reserve | Tests **interaction between temporal inflammation decline AND temporal function decline** |
| Organ aging clocks (Wang et al. 2025) | Organ-specific clocks | Tests **systemic inflammatory-function discordance** as a unifying mechanism |
| Frailty-inflammation (Vitale et al. 2024) | Cross-sectional frailty | Tests **dynamic discordance** as a predictor, not just static frailty status |

**Novelty**: This is the first study to examine the **temporal discordance** between inflammatory and functional trajectories, not just baseline discordance. The hypothesis that *divergent rates of change* (rather than just baseline divergence) predict multi-organ decline has not been tested.

## Hypothesis Statement

**Among adults with established multimorbidity:**
1. The degree of discordance between the rate of inflammatory burden decline and the rate of functional reserve decline over 12 months predicts subsequent multi-organ decline better than either measure alone.
2. This relationship is modified by baseline multimorbidity burden (more comorbidities → stronger inflammatory-function discordance effect).
3. Specific discordance patterns predict specific organ system declines (e.g., high inflammation decline + low function decline → renal/cardiac decline; low inflammation decline + high function decline → respiratory/neurological decline).

## Study Population

**UK Biobank participants** who meet all criteria:
- Age 40-75 years at index assessment
- Baseline measurements of:
  - Inflammatory markers (CRP, fibrinogen, WBC, IL-6, TNF-α) from proteomics table (fields 630-1001)
  - Functional measures (grip strength, gait speed, physical activity) from fields 87-92
- At least one follow-up assessment within 12 months with repeat measurements
- No incident cancer, stroke, or major surgery between index and follow-up
- Follow-up for ≥1 year for incident outcomes

**Exclusion criteria**:
- Missing >20% of required inflammatory or functional measures at index
- Missing >20% of required measures at follow-up
- Index hospitalization within 6 months before assessment (reverse causation)

## Analysis Plan

### Baseline Model (Reference)
```
Multi-organ decline ~ Inflammation_baseline + Function_baseline + Age + Sex + BMI + Smoking + Alcohol
```
- Outcome: Time-to-first multi-organ decline (Cox proportional hazards)
- Hazard ratios (HR) with 95% CI

### Discordance Model (Primary)
```
Multi-organ decline ~ Inflammation_decline_rate + Function_decline_rate +
                    (Inflammation_decline_rate × Function_decline_rate) +
                    Age + Sex + BMI + Smoking + Alcohol + Multimorbidity_count
```
- Inflammation_decline_rate = (Inflammation_followup - Inflammation_index) / Δtime
- Function_decline_rate = (Function_followup - Function_index) / Δtime
- Interaction term: Inflammation_decline_rate × Function_decline_rate
- HR for discordant vs concordant trajectories (quartile-based)

### Multimorbidity Modification Model
```
Multi-organ decline ~ Inflammation_decline_rate × Function_decline_rate +
                      Multimorbidity_count × Inflammation_decline_rate × Function_decline_rate +
                      ...
```
- Test interaction between discordance and multimorbidity burden
- Effect modification: Does discordance predict worse outcomes more strongly in those with ≥5 comorbidities vs ≤3?

### Organ-Specific Models
```
Renal decline ~ Discordance × Organ_system + ...
Cardiac decline ~ Discordance × Organ_system + ...
Respiratory decline ~ Discordance × Organ_system + ...
```
- Test if specific discordance patterns predict specific organ system declines

### Alternative Model: Baseline Discordance Only
```
Multi-organ decline ~ Inflammation_index - Function_index + ...
```
- Test if baseline discordance alone predicts outcomes
- Compare with temporal discordance model

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **falsified** if:
1. **No significant interaction** between inflammation decline rate and function decline rate (p > 0.05)
2. **Baseline models outperform temporal models** (AUC difference > 0.02, p > 0.05)
3. **No organ-system specificity** (all organ-specific models have HR ~1.0, 95% CI includes 1.0)
4. **No modification by multimorbidity** (interaction p > 0.05)

## Supportive Results

**Supportive** if:
1. **Significant interaction term** (p < 0.05) indicating discordance predicts worse outcomes
2. **Temporal model outperforms baseline model** (ΔAUC > 0.02, p < 0.05)
3. **Specific discordance patterns** predict worse outcomes (e.g., high inflammation decline + low function decline HR > 2.0)
4. **Multimorbidity modification** (p < 0.05) showing stronger effect in high-burden individuals

## Inconclusive Results

**Inconclusive** if:
1. **Significant interaction** but **no clinical significance** (HR 1.1-1.3, 95% CI 1.0-1.5)
2. **Temporal and baseline models** have similar AUC (ΔAUC < 0.01)
3. **Mixed organ-specific results** (some organs show effect, others don't)
4. **No multimorbidity modification** but interaction is significant

**Next steps for inconclusive results**:
- Expand to longer follow-up (≥5 years)
- Include more functional measures (handgrip, chair stand, 6-min walk)
- Add inflammatory subtypes (acute vs chronic inflammation)
- Consider mediation analysis (does discordance operate through specific pathways?)
