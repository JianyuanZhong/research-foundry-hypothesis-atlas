# Protective versus pathogenic macrophage–fibroblast target selectivity

Seed: D2-T2. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Does an intervention-associated transcript pattern reduce a pathogenic fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state, such that a target prioritized from an averaged signal would be misleading? This matters because macrophage and fibroblast programs can have opposing roles in fibrotic or inflammatory disease: an intervention-associated change in one compartment may reflect a protective response, a pathogenic program, or simply differential cell composition and stimulation. Resolving the question would change whether a target is pursued as context-specific, requires a cell-type-resolved intervention, or is deprioritized.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The seed and domain guide identify four configured sources: GSE95588 coculture and d1d2d3d4 counts, E-MTAB-8316, E-MTAB-8873 and E-MTAB-8322. These are available as prepared counts and design metadata. The strongest current evidence is descriptive: the datasets contain macrophage/fibroblast-related conditions and intervention-associated transcript changes. The unresolved claim is whether a protective macrophage-linked state and a pathogenic fibroblast program can be separated by an intervention-associated transcript response without overinterpreting expression as direct target action.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The population is the set of samples in the four configured sources with macrophage/fibroblast-related conditions and intervention-associated transcript changes. Temporal boundaries are defined by the timepoint field where present, otherwise by condition ordering. Donor reuse and nested-donor dependence are handled explicitly.

## Variables and outcomes

- Exposure: intervention-associated transcript pattern.
- Outcome: separation of protective macrophage-linked and pathogenic fibroblast states.
- Covariates: donor_id, cell_type, condition, timepoint.
- Competing explanations: differential cell composition, stimulation, measurement artifact.

## Analysis

A donor-aware linear mixed model baseline is compared with a regularized multivariate alternative. The baseline uses donor random effects and condition fixed effects. The alternative uses donor-aware regularization and a held-out split. The outcome/uncertainty evaluation is based on the separation of protective and pathogenic states.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if the intervention-associated pattern does not separate protective and pathogenic states, or if the separation is driven by differential cell composition or stimulation.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive outcomes

- Supportive: the intervention-associated pattern separates protective and pathogenic states.
- Adverse: the pattern does not separate the states, or the separation is driven by artifact.
- Inconclusive: the pattern is ambiguous or the data are insufficient.

## Method comparison

The donor-aware linear mixed model baseline is simple and interpretable. The regularized multivariate alternative could reveal nonlinear or high-dimensional patterns that the baseline loses. The baseline is selected for its interpretability and the alternative is deferred for future work.
