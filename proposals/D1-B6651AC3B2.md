# Persistent hyperlactatemia conditional on improving versus worsening measured perfusion at live ICU exit

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly fit and audit a leakage-controlled, competing-risk prognostic study. It must determine whether the 48-hour risk associated with persistent hyperlactatemia is modified by the direction of measured perfusion immediately before an observed live ICU exit, and whether that interaction survives clinically available measurement and selection stress.

Completion requires:

1. an all-eligible first-live-exit flow and a separately reported phenotype-complete flow;
2. a first eligible ICU exit per admission with a fixed one-hour pre-exit landmark;
3. a primary interaction estimand, not an unqualified subgroup claim;
4. matched held-out predictions from a transparent latest-state baseline (B0), an order-blind generic-history comparator (B1), a process-only comparator (Bmask), an auditable phenotype model (Bdisc), and a training-only trajectory sensitivity (M2);
5. row-level source/member/item/unit/time provenance and exact coverage, indeterminate, and ascertainment counts;
6. a prespecified measurement-error and decision-threshold audit: the primary phenotype must be repeated under the fixed 2.0 mmol/L lactate threshold, a fixed 4.0 mmol/L sensitivity, continuous lactate-by-perfusion interaction, and an uncertainty/interval analysis that does not turn missing or discordant measurements into physiology;
7. selection-process, timing, component, endpoint, and outcome-permutation falsifications; and
8. a conclusion ledger mapping every statement to computed outputs and distinguishing prognostic association from treatment effect, microcirculatory diagnosis, discharge appropriateness, utility, and transportability.

No clinical result is claimed here. Readiness, a successful fit, or directional support for the hypothesis is not completion; completion is the reproducible set of fitted outputs, uncertainty, audits, and interpretation checks.

## Unresolved question and strongest supported evidence

The expert seed asks whether repeatedly high lactate has different meaning when perfusion is improving versus worsening. The local MIMIC-IV v3.1 documentation supports only that the database contains internally time-consistent, repeated structured observations and that the configured snapshot contains the required tables. The local dictionary audit verified that lactate, MAP, vasoactive and Foley item IDs exist with the stated table links and units. It does not establish that these variables measure microcirculatory perfusion, that repeated testing is nonselective, or that a phenotype is common enough for inference. The seed is explicitly untested, and no fitted result is asserted.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among first eligible live ICU exits with repeated lactate and paired pre-landmark hemodynamic measurements, is the excess 48-hour risk associated with persistent hyperlactatemia smaller when measured perfusion is improving than when it is worsening?

The directional hypothesis is that the interaction is negative: persistent lactate should have less excess R/D risk in the improving-perfusion stratum. The primary estimand at 48 hours is:

`[CIF(R/D | persistent, improving) - CIF(R/D | nonpersistent, improving)] - [CIF(R/D | persistent, worsening) - CIF(R/D | nonpersistent, worsening)]`.

The primary clinically meaningful null region is an interaction absolute-risk difference from -2 to +2 percentage points. Report the exact estimate and 95% interval as well. A negative estimate inside this region is not primary support. This null region is a decision-relevance guard, not a claim that a two-point change is universal clinical utility.

The substantive advance over the existing mature live-exit branch is to test effect modification with an explicit clinically meaningful null region while requiring strict concordance of two measurable perfusion domains and retaining an indeterminate group. A positive result would show that a lactate threshold alone is an incomplete prognostic description; a null result would argue against adding this distinction to retrospective risk stratification. Neither result identifies lactate etiology or prescribes fluids.

## Population, landmark, temporal boundaries, and outcomes

Use one index per `hadm_id): the first `icu/icustays` row with valid `intime < outtime`, an observed live exit at `t0 = outtime`, and sufficient time for the primary window. Require `intime <= t0 - 13 hours` so the 12-hour phenotype window and one-hour availability buffer are fully in the ICU. Exclude missing/reversed intervals and exits with `admissions.deathtime <= t0`. Keep, count, and separately report hospital-expire rows with missing `deathtime` as unresolved death ascertainment; exclude them from the timed primary estimand unless an adverse-bound analysis is supplied. Missing admission linkage is a counted linkage failure.

Set `tL = t0 - 1 hour`. The primary feature window is `[tL-12h,tL]`, divided into early `[tL-12h,tL-6h]` and late `(tL-6h,tL]` halves. A primary measurement is available only if its clinical time and recording time are both at or before `tL`:

- `chartevents`, `outputevents`, and `labevents`: `charttime <= tL` and `storetime <= tL`;
- `inputevents`: valid `starttime <= tL`, valid interval, `storetime <= tL`, with any interval contribution clipped at `tL`; no future `endtime` information is used;
- no feature uses `outtime`, `los`, `last_careunit`, future transfers, endpoint fields, notes, or any row after `tL`.

A chart-time-only sensitivity admits clinical-time rows despite later storage and reports the patients, rows, and conclusions that change. Missingness is retained as missing and represented separately by counts/indicators; it is never imputed to normal or zero.

The phenotype-complete primary cohort requires at least two valid lactate results with one in each half, valid paired MAP summaries in both halves, and valid paired vasoactive-support summaries in both halves. All eligible live exits and every coverage restriction are reported. A cell with fewer than 30 subjects or 10 R/D events cannot support the primary interaction. An interaction interval wider than six percentage points is inconclusive.

Define mutually exclusive first transitions in `(t0,t0+48h]`:

- R: another `icu/icustays` row for the same `hadm_id`, different `stay_id`, with `intime > t0` and `intime <= t0+48h`;
- D: `hosp/admissions.deathtime` in the interval;
- S: alive `hosp/admissions.dischtime` in the interval before a later R or D, an ascertainment-boundary transition rather than a safe outcome;
- N: no R, D, or S while the admission remains ascertainable through 48 hours;
- U: unresolved timed-death ascertainment, including hospital-expire flag 1 with missing death time.

At equal timestamps D precedes R; S is assigned only while alive and before the other event. Report R, D, S, R/D and N with cause-specific hazards and cumulative incidence. A 7-day and complete-ascertainment sensitivity, plus adverse and non-adverse U bounds, are required. R/D with S competing is primary; R and D separately are secondary.

## Phenotype and measurement-error audit

For each half, calculate the median valid lactate and median valid MAP. Define persistent hyperlactatemia as early and late lactate medians >=2.0 mmol/L after checking the recorded unit; 4.0 mmol/L is a fixed sensitivity. Reject incompatible/non-numeric lactate values and report counts. Compute pressor-support minutes as the union of valid overlap of the prespecified input intervals, clipped to each half and to `tL); preserve drug-specific units and do not pool rate or dose.

Improving perfusion requires MAP late minus early >=5 mmHg and late pressor minutes <= early pressor minutes. Worsening requires MAP change <=-5 mmHg and late pressor minutes >= early pressor minutes. All remaining cases are indeterminate and never forced into either primary group. The primary change is the interaction among persistent lactate, improving/worsening perfusion, and their product; the indeterminate group is descriptive and a sensitivity.

The measurement-error audit reports each domain's raw count, time coverage, unit coverage, test intensity, and within-half reliability. It repeats the interaction using MAP-only, arterial MAP-only (225312), pressor-only, continuous standardized changes, and a secondary third domain of normalized Foley output. It reports complete-case versus availability-indicator models, 4/6/8 valid-bin requirements, 2.0 versus 4.0 lactate cutoffs, and the difference between clinical-time-plus-storetime and chart-time-only eligibility. No sensitivity is selected after viewing held-out outcomes.

The decision-threshold layer does not claim utility. On the held-out test set, report calibration and risk-capture at fixed 5%, 10%, and 20% predicted 48-hour R/D thresholds, with the number reviewed, R/D events captured, sensitivity, PPV, and false-negative count for B0, B1, and Bdisc. Thresholds and calibration are fitted only on training/validation. A threshold result is clinically informative only as an operating characteristic; net benefit, staffing effect, patient preference, and actionability require another prospective study.

## Models, fitting, split, and uncertainty

Freeze a participant-level 70/15/15 split before outcome-based thresholding, stratified only by endpoint and anchor-year group; no subject crosses partitions. For the primary phenotype comparison, every model uses the same phenotype-complete cohort and split.

B0 is a penalized discrete-time cause-specific complementary-log-log model for R, D, and S using admission/demographic/current-ICU context, terminal-bin physiology, terminal missingness, and observation-process variables. It is the transparent latest-state baseline.

B1 is B0 plus order-blind summaries in twelve one-hour bins across the same physiology, lactate, organ-marker, output, pressor-presence, and observation-process fields. It tests whether an interaction is more than generic late history and measurement intensity.

Bmask has the same context, counts, masks, test indicators, Foley-charting, and pressor-presence history with physiologic values removed. If Bdisc does not exceed Bmask, a physiology-specific interpretation fails.

Bdisc adds persistent-lactate status, strict improving/indeterminate/worsening perfusion, early and late lactate, MAP/pressor changes, their interaction, organ-marker values, and separate availability indicators to B1. It is the primary clinically auditable alternative; the primary scientific output is the interaction and standardized cumulative-incidence contrast, not a black-box score.

M2 is a training-only diagonal-covariance Gaussian mixture on standardized early-to-late changes in lactate, MAP, pressor minutes, and secondarily output/organ markers. Use posterior component probabilities in the same hazard model. Label a component improving/worsening only if its training means meet the fixed direction rules; otherwise leave it unlabeled. M2 may reveal nonlinear coupled trajectories, but is sensitivity only because selective measurement can destabilize mixture labels.

A chronological masked GRU is deferred: the parent branch already tests chronology versus order-blind late history, while this child tests cross-domain effect modification. A causal fluid-response model is also deferred because the available records do not provide reliable fluid responsiveness, clinician intent, counterfactual dose-response, treatment limitations, or adequate time-varying confounding control. These deferrals are scientific/dependency decisions, not bans on neural networks or GPUs.

Report held-out 48-hour CIF and transition-aware Brier scores for R, D, S, R/D, and N, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC/AUPRC for components, paired Bdisc-B1 and B1-B0 differences, interaction estimates, fixed-threshold operating tables, and subject-bootstrap 95% intervals. The proposed full solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds. Discovery measured only the dictionary/header audit; archive scan, phenotype prevalence, model fits, bootstrap, and selection audit remain unverified planning estimates. Small tabular fits are expected to run on CPU; if repeated fitting requires acceleration, request an allocated A100 and use `cuda:0). Do not shrink the cohort, bootstrap, or estimand to fit an unmeasured estimate.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required audits include dictionary labels/linkage/units, numeric ranges, dual timestamps, interval clipping, joins, duplicate handling, first-exit selection, prohibited fields, split integrity, and row-level provenance.

Permute outcomes within partitions and refit: held-out gains should disappear. Permute early/late assignments within subject while preserving counts and availability: a true trajectory interaction should attenuate. Compare Bdisc with Bmask and a process-only phenotype model: similar gains without physiologic values support documentation/selection rather than physiology. Compare available-by-landmark with chart-time-only data: a gain requiring later-stored rows is adverse to real-time use. Repeat components and cutoffs as fixed sensitivities. Report R, D, and S separately: a gain confined to S does not support adverse physiology. Fit the outcome-free live-exit negative-control model for a six-hour exit process; a much stronger signal there than for R/D supports selection/process explanation. Audit current unit using only transfer intervals covering tL, anchor-year strata, q-overlap/ESS selection stress, phenotype prevalence, indeterminate fraction, and measurement intensity.

Supportive evidence requires adequate events and coverage; an interaction below -2 points with a 95% interval entirely below the null region; a corresponding lower R/D CIF for persistent lactate in improving than worsening perfusion; calibrated held-out Bdisc improvement over B1; Bdisc exceeding Bmask; and stability across component, timing, unit, coverage, endpoint, threshold, and selection sensitivities. M2 can corroborate but cannot validate the proxy alone.

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversed interaction, Bdisc matching B1 or Bmask, process-only or chart-time-only explanation, instability across measurement definitions, gains confined to S/N, outcome-permutation resistance, or threshold performance that is not calibrated. The result is inconclusive when phenotype cells/events are sparse, indeterminate/U fractions are high, units are incompatible, selection overlap/ESS is poor, calibration is unstable, or the interaction interval remains wide.

Computationally checkable claims are source provenance, cohort flow, timing, units, item support, joins, phenotype prevalence, split integrity, model fitting, predictions, CIF/Brier/calibration, interaction contrasts, threshold tables, uncertainty, selection audit, and falsifications. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for microcirculatory dysfunction, lactate etiology, fluid responsiveness, clinician intent, discharge appropriateness, preventability, causal fluid/discharge effects, monitoring benefit, net utility, staffing recommendations, external transportability, and bedside policy.
