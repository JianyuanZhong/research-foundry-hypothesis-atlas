## Persistent respiratory endpoint

The primary endpoint is care-plan-blinded and uses no `carePlanGeneral` values, care-plan counts, care-plan groups, notes, treatment strings, or post-D exposure features. It is assembled from `respiratoryCharting` and `vitalPeriodic`; `respiratoryCare` is used for a prespecified corroboration sensitivity and for observation/airway context, not as proof of bedside delivery.

For each frozen row:

- baseline state window `B=(D-720,D]`;
- pre-D placebo trajectory `P=(D-1440,D-720]` versus B;
- post-label window `O=(D+360,D+1800]`;
- horizon sensitivity `O12=(D+360,D+1080]`.

All windows use `respchartoffset`, `respcarestatusoffset`, or `observationoffset` as appropriate. Endpoint windows are half-open exactly as written; boundary tests must be included in `verification.json`.

For each mapped respiratory support family, a window is observed only when at least two valid observations have distinct clinical offsets separated by at least 30 minutes. Summarize all valid observations by median and retain count, unique timestamp count, first/last offset, median, maximum gap, parse failures, and conflicts. A support reduction is one of:

- FiO2 median decreases by at least 0.05;
- PEEP/PEEP-CPAP decreases by at least 2 cm H2O;
- pressure support decreases by at least 2 cm H2O;
- set respiratory rate decreases by at least 2 breaths/min.

Peak inspiratory pressure is excluded from the primary and reported only as an ablation because the inherited audit found it dominant and its structured semantics are not clinically adjudicated.

Safety is based on `vitalPeriodic.sao2` and `vitalPeriodic.respiration`, using the same two-observation and 30-minute separation rule and medians in B and O. Among signals observed in both windows, safety passes if median `sao2` does not fall by more than 2 percentage points and median respiration does not rise by more than 4 breaths/min. If neither safety signal is available in both windows, the primary endpoint is missing; a signal observed in both that violates its threshold fails safety. A sensitivity requires both signals; a second requires at least one.

The mutually auditable endpoint states are:

1. `persistent_improvement`: support reduction, safety pass, and complete required component observation;
2. `observed_non_improvement`: complete endpoint observation but no qualifying support reduction, including safety failure;
3. `endpoint_missing`: insufficient repeated measurements, parsing/conflict failure, or unavailable safety measurement without a competing exit;
4. `competing_exit`: death, discharge/transfer, or source truncation before the end of O, with exit type retained.

For the primary endpoint, a respiratory-care corroboration requirement is a declared sensitivity, not silently mixed into the state definition. The sensitivity requires a post-window `respiratoryCare` record with nonmissing `airwaytype` or a nonmissing ventilator interval (`ventstartoffset`, `ventendoffset`, `priorventstartoffset`, `priorventendoffset`). Additional fixed sensitivities use an interval overlapping O and pre-L airway recency windows of 6, 24, and 72 hours. Treatment strings are validation context only; an exact, pre-frozen ETT-removal candidate followed by no invasive-airway respiratory-care record for 48 hours may be reported only as an unavailable/inconclusive sensitivity when support is below 10% in either arm. No treatment string proves extubation.

Competing-exit precedence is deterministic. Use `patient.unitdischargeoffset`, `unitdischargestatus`, and `unitdischargelocation`; discharge at or before `D+360` is additionally flagged as no post-washout opportunity. Do not recode exit as improvement or ordinary non-improvement. If source coverage cannot establish the endpoint window and no explicit exit exists, classify endpoint missing and report truncation/source coverage separately.

## Pre-D predictors and documentation opportunity

The baseline and prediction models may use only information available by D. State variables include fixed summaries of pre-D respiratory values, trends in P versus B, source-presence indicators, and counts/timestamps from the pre-D windows. Opportunity variables are outcome-blind counts, distinct clinical times, distinct labels, respiratory-care rows/airway types, vital observations, treatment-row availability, and entry-minus-clinical lag distributions. The label is the only added exposure feature in the state-plus-label model.

No post-D respiratory value, endpoint state, post-D source density, care-plan row, treatment string, or outcome-derived feature may enter baseline. An automated column-level leakage audit must fail if any such field appears. A separate opportunity-only model excludes respiratory values and label strings. Hospital and landmark may stratify training or be covariates, but held-out hospital identity cannot be encoded through outcome-derived target rates.

## Analysis and hospital-held-out feasibility

The descriptive complete-observation contrast reports evaluation and no-trial counts, endpoint event rates, risk difference, and uncertainty, alongside the four-state distribution at the full 540-row denominator. It must never present a complete-case contrast without observation and competing-exit denominators.

The primary transport analysis is leave-one-hospital-out. For each held-out hospital, fit all preprocessing, imputation, standardization, regularization, and optional observation weights using training hospitals only. Report row-weighted micro and equal-hospital macro log loss/Brier score, calibration slope/intercept where estimable, AUROC only when both classes occur, observed and predicted arm contrasts, endpoint availability, competing-exit rates, and label-arm counts. Stay, not row, is the uncertainty cluster; repeated landmarks from a stay remain together in cluster bootstrap resampling.

Before fitting, publish `site_support.csv` for every hospital and landmark with arm counts, complete endpoint counts, missingness, competing exits, and four-state counts. The label-contrast transport gate requires at least five hospitals with at least 10 complete endpoint-observed rows under each label and at least 50% of complete rows in those hospitals. If this fails, label-contrast transport is inconclusive; the inherited adverse label-concentration finding is not converted into a new endpoint result. Folds with one arm absent cannot produce a within-hospital arm contrast and are not forced by relabeling.

Use fixed seed `61061`, stay-cluster bootstrap with at least 500 refitted replicates (target 1,000), and report all failures before interpreting results. A label permutation sensitivity shuffles within hospital-by-landmark strata only where both labels exist, preserving arm counts; if fewer than 20 valid strata exist, mark permutation inference unavailable rather than manufacturing a null distribution. Observation weights, if used, are a secondary sensitivity estimated from pre-D information only, with fixed positivity and 1st/99th-percentile cap rules; failure of positivity or extreme arm-specific exits makes the weighted result inconclusive, not a reason to redefine the endpoint.

## Falsification and robustness criteria

1. Apply the identical endpoint algorithm to P versus B. A comparable pre-D label contrast is adverse to a newly emerging post-label interpretation.
2. Use a lead-time placebo at `D-720` while retaining the fixed post window. Similar predictive gain indicates pre-existing trajectory or timing leakage.
3. Ablate each support and safety component, and separately remove airway corroboration. A result surviving only one field, peak pressure, one source, or one hospital is not supportive.
4. Predict post-window documentation density, timestamp count, and entry lag from label and pre-D variables. Opportunity change without value-based trajectory gain supports documentation opportunity, not patient-state information.
5. Compare the held-out increment with the valid within-hospital-by-landmark permutation distribution.
6. Repeat fixed 6-, 24-, and 72-hour pre-L airway recency sensitivities without changing the frozen primary denominator unless explicitly declared as a separate feasibility variant.
7. Repeat two-observation separation at 60 minutes and first/last summaries. A large change is evidence of copied-chart/persistence sensitivity.
8. Verify disjoint care-plan, post-D, boundary, duplicate, parser, and source-hash audits mechanically.

## Fail-closed gates and interpretation

Call the computation **inconclusive feasibility** if parent reconstruction, source hash/header/schema checks, row-level digest, exact labels/D, parser freeze, or window audit fails; if fewer than five supported hospitals exist for the held-out label contrast; if endpoint observation is too sparse or nonpositive; if no valid held-out contrast exists; or if primary and declared sensitivity definitions cannot be executed reproducibly. Do not relax thresholds, expand labels, impute missing endpoint states as non-events, pool unsupported folds, or substitute a nearby artifact.

Call the computational result **supportive of incremental structured-chart information** only if the support gate passes, the evaluation-minus-no-trial endpoint contrast is positive with at least 4/5 estimable hospital directions positive, state-plus-label improves both micro and equal-hospital macro held-out log loss by at least 0.01 over the identical state-plus-opportunity model with a stay-cluster interval excluding zero, the gain exceeds pre-D/lead-time placebo and valid permutation controls, survives declared ablations without hospital reversal, and is not explained by missingness/competing exits. If independent airway corroboration is below 10% in either arm, the result is at most partially supportive/inconclusive. Even all gates support only incremental information in the eICU chart construct.

Call it **adverse computational evidence** only when endpoint support is adequate and a prespecified failure is reproduced: no label increment over state/opportunity, hospital reversal, placebo/permutation equivalence, component/source dependence, or opportunity-only change. This does not prove absence of bedside evaluation or a documentation artifact.

A stronger clinical conclusion requires local interface dictionaries, clinician/abstractor adjudication, device-linked delivered settings, validated SBT/extubation/reintubation outcomes, notes and workflow context, and external or prospective replication. The current files lack those modalities, raw waveforms, complete notes, and post-ICU follow-up.
