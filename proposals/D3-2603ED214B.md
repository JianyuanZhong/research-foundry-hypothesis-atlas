# Provisional hypothesis: donor-aware macrophage context in E-MTAB-8873

## Proposed relationship

In the frozen E-MTAB-8873 targeted human RA synovial type B synovial cell (FLS) assay, FLS samples exposed to MerTK/CD206-positive macrophages have a reproducible, donor-set-aware difference in measured transcript state from FLS samples exposed to MerTK/CD206-negative macrophages, beyond the recorded contributing-donor composition and cell-count differences. This is a provisional context-association hypothesis, not a causal macrophage-mechanism claim.

The primary planned contrast is the none-treatment MerTK/CD206-positive versus MerTK/CD206-negative paired/co-culture comparison. The complete contributing-donor set, not an individual donor row alone, is the relevant analysis unit because samples can contain mixed donor composition. A malformed donor assignment identified in prior inspection is excluded from the primary set and retained as a sensitivity check.

## What is and is not established

The available evidence supports that the frozen design and matrix contain these fields and contrasts. It does not establish that the macrophage phenotype causes the FLS response. No expression contrast, model fit, or biological result has been computed in this provisional draft. The strongest supported claim is only that a bounded, donor-aware test is feasible in principle using the verified files.

The unresolved claim is whether any apparent context association survives donor-set and cell-count adjustment and is reproducible across donor sets. A stable signal would justify independent donor-aware validation or perturbation; it would not establish a pathway, direct target action, treatment effect, or clinical benefit.

## Falsifiable test and interpretation

The planned test compares the none-treatment Mpos/Mneg contrast after accounting for the complete contributing-donor set and recorded cell count. A simple donor-aware paired contrast is the baseline. A scientifically substantive alternative is a hierarchical model that borrows information across feature instances and donor sets while retaining the same estimand; it could reveal whether an apparent signal is stable across donors or driven by one donor/composition, information the baseline loses.

Supportive results require a prespecified direction and uncertainty interval for the context contrast that survives the donor-set adjustment, with sensitivity to the malformed-record exclusion and feature-instance handling. Adverse results are a null or reversed contrast after adjustment, or loss of the signal when donor composition is represented correctly. Inconclusive results include insufficient donor replication, unstable uncertainty, or inability to separate context from donor composition.

The experiment must report the fitted contrast, uncertainty, donor-level contributions, excluded records, and sensitivity analyses. It must not claim clinical or mechanistic truth from these outputs.
