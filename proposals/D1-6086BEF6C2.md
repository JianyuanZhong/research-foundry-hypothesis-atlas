# HCC method exploration: dynamic response versus care-process explanation

Status: working design, verified schema bindings; no scientific result has been computed in this discovery episode.

## Scientific question and deliverable

Among HCC patients receiving a first recorded transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does the early post-TACE trajectory of measurable disease/liver-state signals add information about near-term repeat intervention beyond baseline patient severity, or is the apparent signal largely a consequence of observation intensity and clinical workflow?

The falsifiable primary hypothesis is:

> Conditional on pre-TACE severity and treatment history, an early post-TACE state trajectory (paired/serial laboratory change, when observed) predicts 180-day repeat TACE; its incremental association should persist after accounting for observation intensity, whereas a process-only explanation predicts that the association attenuates substantially when laboratory/imaging/encounter frequency is included or time/order is destroyed.

This is not a causal claim that TACE response causes repeat TACE. Repeat TACE is a consequential but imperfect operational endpoint: it may represent residual/recurring disease, planned retreatment, clinician preference, access, or follow-up opportunity. Mortality, progression-free survival, and validated radiologic response are not released as explicit adjudicated outcomes.

The new scientific deliverable is a held-out, patient-level comparison of (i) a pre-specified interpretable severity/process model and (ii) a time-aware learned state/process model, plus calibration, uncertainty, ablations and an observation-process falsification test. Completion requires newly fitted out-of-fold risks and uncertainty intervals, not a proposed architecture alone.

## Evidence opening and clinical advance

The available evidence supports two narrower claims. A recent retrospective TACE cohort reports prognostic value for baseline and post-treatment AFP-related measures, but it is single-institution and observational ([K1]). HCC response assessment is not reducible to size: mRECIST incorporates viable enhancement/necrosis, and response criteria and imaging appearances have practical limitations ([K2]). A current surveillance review emphasizes that relapse biology is heterogeneous, radiologic relapse can lag behind biology, and biomarkers should be interpreted with imaging and clinical context rather than used as standalone treatment triggers ([K3]).

The unresolved claim is whether a dynamic signal in this local HCC care record reflects patient-state information beyond baseline severity, or instead reflects who gets repeat labs/imaging and therefore has more opportunities both to show a signal and to receive repeat TACE. Resolving this matters because a state signal could justify a risk-adapted early review or trial of intensified surveillance; a process signal would caution against using the apparent biomarker trajectory as a treatment trigger and would instead identify a measurement/workflow problem. The result would advance beyond existing retrospective prognostic associations by explicitly testing the competing observation-process explanation with time-preserving and process-permuted comparisons.

The primary analysis must show both a complete-window cohort and an all-index cohort with censoring/observation sensitivity. No administrative death assumption is permitted.

## Two comparable methods on the same estimand

### Baseline: interpretable severity plus observation-process model

Fit a prespecified regularized logistic model for repeat TACE by day 180 (and a complementary discrete-time pooled-logistic model for days 43--180 if enough events).

Model B0 (patient-state baseline) uses only index/baseline features: age/sex, prior encounter/procedure counts, diagnosis dictionary counts, pre-index liver-state assay summaries/directions, baseline AFP if verified, index department, and index admission duration. Model B1 adds response-window observation-process features: number and timing of encounters, labs by assay, examinations by modality/type, orders, medications and number of observed days. Do not put post-index repeat TACE rows into predictors.

Report odds/hazard ratios with bootstrap 95% intervals, calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, AUROC and AUPRC with patient-level five-fold cross-validation. Compare B0 versus B1 by pre-registered likelihood-ratio/deviance change and held-out calibration, not by a small AUC gain alone. Standardize numeric features using training folds only, impute within training folds with missingness indicators, and use class weights only within training folds.

This baseline is intentionally transparent: it estimates whether an apparent response signal survives explicit adjustment for baseline severity and observation intensity. Its limitation is that it compresses the order, timing, and joint pattern of events and cannot model a transient response followed by rebound without hand-crafted summaries.

### Substantive learned alternative: time-aware state/process sequence model

Fit a compact time-aware GRU (or equivalent gated sequence model) on the same index cohort, with one event token per dated encounter/lab/examination/procedure/medication/order in the −365 to +42-day input horizon. Each token carries event type, assay/procedure/modality category, numeric value only when assay identity and unit-safe representation are verified, qualitative result, time since prior event, time since index TACE, and missingness. Use two explicit channels: state channel (baseline/dynamic labs, diagnoses, procedures) and process channel (encounter/test/order/medication observation marks). The target is the same repeat-TACE outcome and the same optional discrete-time hazard.

Pre-register these fits and ablations:
1. state-only sequence;
2. process-only sequence;
3. combined state+process sequence;
4. combined sequence with response-window event times permuted within patient or process marks permuted across patients, preserving counts;
5. a time-shuffled baseline-feature control.

Evaluate on an untouched patient-level 20% test split; choose hyperparameters only in the remaining 80% with five-fold grouped validation. Use three fixed random seeds and report mean plus seed range; bootstrap patients for 95% intervals on Brier, calibration, AUROC/AUPRC and incremental held-out log loss. Compare learned models to B0/B1 using paired patient-level bootstrap differences and calibration plots. The key scientific test is not whether the GRU wins a tiny predictive contest: a state signal is supported only if state-only improves held-out log loss/calibration over B0 and remains similar after adding process information, while process-only or process permutation does not explain the gain. If combined performance improves but state-only does not, that supports an observation/care-process explanation.

The sequence model could reveal information lost by B0/B1: event order, time gaps, transient post-TACE laboratory change, and interactions between a state trajectory and the opportunity to measure it. Its explicit process channel makes informative observation testable rather than hidden. It still does not establish mechanism or treatment causality.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive result: paired dynamic features are available in a prespecified fraction sufficient for precision; state-only and combined models improve held-out calibration/log loss beyond B0; the state contribution remains after B1 adjustment; process-only and process-permuted models do not reproduce it; and the direction is stable across assay-complete and observation-complete sensitivity cohorts. Interpretation: evidence that dynamic recorded state contains incremental prognostic information, not proof that the biological response causes repeat TACE or that changing treatment improves outcomes.

Adverse result: B0 performs comparably, or the state increment disappears after B1, or process-only/permuted observation marks reproduce the gain. Interpretation: the apparent signal is more consistent with baseline severity or care-process/measurement artifact; do not recommend response-triggered management.

Inconclusive result: too few paired AFP/liver-state observations, too few repeat TACE events, unstable alias mapping, wide intervals, large missingness, or nontransportable local procedure coding. Interpretation: not a refutation; defer clinical use and specify the missing data/adjudication needed.

Essential clinical adjudication/additional evidence: expert review of TACE intent (planned versus rescue), actual HCC progression and viable tumor, mRECIST/LR-TR imaging labels, treatment toxicity, transplant status, death, outside-hospital care, laboratory units/reference ranges, and whether a repeat procedure was clinically indicated. These cannot be established automatically from this snapshot.

## Resource plan and method decision

Discovery evidence is schema inspection plus bounded row/sample inspection; no GPU result is claimed. Future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 8 GPUs, 262,144 MiB and 28,800 seconds. Proposed execution: 8--16 CPUs and 64--128 GiB for one-pass chunked CSV extraction/join and fold materialization (estimated 1--2.5 hours, unverified on the mounted 2.2 GB labs file); one allocated A100, 32--64 GiB for the compact sequence model, three seeds and inference (estimated 2--4 hours, unverified); logistic/CV bootstrap fits on CPU (estimated <1 hour after materialization). Keep total under 7.5 hours with checkpoints after cohort construction and fold outputs; if the full sequence fit exceeds the envelope, preserve the precomputed cohort and run one seed plus the complete baseline/negative-control analysis, labelling the learned comparison incomplete rather than changing the estimand.

A multimodal image/text transformer was deferred: no image payload exists, clinical documents lack a time field, and narrative lexical extraction is explicitly unvalidated beyond narrow cirrhosis axes. A mechanistic joint liver-tumor state-space model was also deferred because lab units/reference ranges, adjudicated tumor burden and treatment intent are unavailable; its assumptions would dominate the answer. Revisit either alternative only after image files, temporally anchored expert labels, unit metadata and treatment-intent adjudication are supplied. The selected learned alternative is retained because it tests the same question while directly representing timing and observation process and remains within the planning envelope.
