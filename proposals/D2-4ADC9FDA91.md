# Context-specific MerTK target prioritization in rheumatoid-arthritis synovial coculture

Status: Lead design, planned experiment; no scientific screening result has been executed. Episode 1, crossover of D2-T1 and D2-T2.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The available evidence supports two facts but leaves an actionable boundary unresolved. MerTK-positive synovial macrophage states are associated with remission and can induce a fibroblast repair response; the inspected source also reports that MerTK inhibition increases FLS IL6 and MMP1/3/14 [K1]. Separately, HBEGF-positive macrophages can drive an invasive FLS program [K2], and fibroblast pathology is heterogeneous across immune-interacting and vascular-interacting states [K3]. These findings do not establish that MerTK perturbation has the same consequence in every macrophage context, nor that a MerTK-directed intervention should be selected without stratifying protective versus inflammatory macrophage states.

Falsifiable hypothesis: in rheumatoid-arthritis synovial FLS coculture, MerTK inhibition produces a larger inflammatory FLS response when the macrophage context is MerTK/CD206-positive (resolution-like) than when it is MerTK/CD206-negative, after accounting for contributing FLS/macrophage donors and generic LPS stimulation. The clinically consequential extension is that a MerTK-preservation or activation strategy should be considered state-stratified: patients or cocultures enriched for the MerTK-positive/CD206-positive resolution context should show the greatest avoidable fibroblast harm from MerTK loss. This is a hypothesis about a perturbation-by-context interaction, not a claim that MerTK activation is already an effective treatment.

The strongest available claim is a broad protective association/perturbational effect, not clinical efficacy or direct target causality. The strongest rival is that the observed FLS changes are generic inflammatory or stress responses, cell-composition/sorting proxies, donor/reuse artifacts, or assay timing effects; under that rival, inhibitor effects should not show a reproducible CD206-context interaction after donor-aware adjustment. [K2] makes the macrophage-state alternative consequential, while [K3] motivates separating FLS state readouts from mere bulk expression changes. The proposed advance over [K1] is a state-by-perturbation safety/selection test, not another broad MerTK expression association.

## Computable Harbor experiment

1. Freeze the eligible E-MTAB-8873 rows and an analysis manifest recording the exact filtering above, sample IDs, donor memberships, and all excluded/ambiguous rows. Normalize each sample's measured feature counts to log2(CPM + pseudocount), using the matrix's feature and sample columns; do not merge duplicate identifiers or infer absent genes.
2. Prespecify two outcomes. The primary harm score is the mean of z-scored IL6, MMP1, MMP3 and MMP14 across measured features; secondary harm components are IL1B, MMP12 and MMP13. The context/state panel is MERTK, MRC1, TREM2 and SPP1. If a planned repair marker is not present in the exact header, it is unavailable rather than imputed.
3. Fit the simple baseline: feature-wise and harm-score linear models with inhibitor, macrophage context (CD206pos/neg), inhibitor-by-context interaction, LPS indicator, FLS-only indicator, age, and assay batch/round when identifiable. Use cross-classified donor-cluster random intercepts or cluster-robust inference for every contributing donor; average repeated context measurements only when the documented donor/condition definition warrants it. Generate donor-cluster bootstrap intervals and leave-one-donor-cluster-out sensitivity, not ordinary donor t-tests. The estimand is the inhibitor contrast within context and their difference, on the FLS harm score.
4. Fit a substantive mechanistic alternative on exactly the same inputs: a hierarchical multivariate latent-state model in which a macrophage-resolution score is a weighted latent factor of MERTK/MRC1/TREM2 (with SPP1 and IL1B as an inflammatory contrast) and FLS harm is a latent factor of IL6/MMP1/MMP3/MMP14. Estimate the inhibitor-by-context interaction jointly with crossed FLS and macrophage donor effects, allowing feature-specific residual covariance. The model's target is not a predictive label; it is the posterior distribution of the context-specific perturbation effect and the probability that the interaction exceeds a prespecified scientifically meaningful direction. Fit by CPU using a reproducible Bayesian or penalized-likelihood implementation; no GPU is needed for this small table. If the donor design is too sparse to identify the latent model, defer it and report the baseline only.
5. Compare the baseline and mechanistic alternative on scientific information: sign and uncertainty of the same interaction estimand, leave-one-donor-cluster-out stability, posterior/cluster-bootstrap predictive checks, and whether the alternative changes interpretation by separating a correlated macrophage state from the FLS harm outcome. Do not rank by small predictive improvement. No random train/test split is appropriate for this finite hypothesis test; the held-out unit is a donor cluster. The future solver must report coefficient/effect tables, donor membership and exclusions, uncertainty intervals or posterior intervals, feature-level and score-level outputs, sensitivity analyses, and diagnostic plots/data.
6. Compute a bounded negative control: the LPS-treated versus untreated contrast within the same macrophage context where present. A selective MerTK interaction should not be reproduced identically by the LPS contrast. FLS-only samples assess whether an apparent response is present without macrophages; this is a control, not evidence of direct MerTK action.
7. Contextualize, do not pool, E-MTAB-8316 paired UNC106238 versus none at the verified donor/other-donor unit and E-MTAB-8322 condition proportions/state metadata. A consistent direction can motivate a balanced follow-up, but it cannot be called external validation because the datasets derive from the same literature program and have different biological units.

Supportive result: the inhibitor-by-context interaction is positive and materially larger in CD206pos coculture for the prespecified harm score, stable in donor-cluster sensitivity, while LPS and FLS-only controls do not reproduce it. This supports state-stratified MerTK preservation/activation as a next target-development experiment, not a clinical treatment claim.

Adverse result: no interaction, a larger CD206neg effect, the same signal under LPS, or a signal confined to FLS-only samples. This refutes the proposed selectivity claim and favors generic inflammatory/cell-composition explanations; do not prioritize MerTK state targeting on this evidence.

Inconclusive result: sparse inhibitor cells, unresolved contributing-donor mappings, nonidentifiable latent model, contradictory donor clusters, or missing annotation/feature coverage. Acquire balanced paired Mpos/Mneg cocultures with matched FLS and macrophage donors, inhibitor dose/viability data, MerTK protein/activation and efferocytosis readouts, secreted mediators, and direct FLS invasion/matrix degradation. Clinical treatment benefit, patient selection, and mechanism require expert review, orthogonal perturbation (e.g., agonism or genetic perturbation), and an independent cohort; Harbor QA can check computation and whether conclusions follow from outputs but cannot establish those claims.

## Alternative methods and selection

The pathway/paired-score baseline is transparent and appropriate to the sparse, repeated-donor table. The hierarchical multivariate latent-state alternative could reveal whether the apparent interaction is a shared macrophage-to-FLS program rather than a single-gene artifact, and it propagates uncertainty across correlated markers; that information is lost by the baseline. It is deferred as secondary because five inhibitor-treated samples and unresolved contributing-donor structure may not identify crossed latent effects. Both methods use the same exact inputs, estimand, donor-cluster held-out sensitivity and uncertainty outputs. A CPU future solver envelope of 16 CPUs/262144 MiB and 8 GPUs is available in inputs.json, but this study is small and does not justify GPU; the discovery diagnostic jobs completed in under a second, while full solver fit time is unmeasured and should be budgeted as a bounded CPU job. No neural model is required because the scientific uncertainty is mechanistic interaction/selectivity, not prediction.

## What must be newly fitted and completion deliverable

The solver must newly fit the context-by-MerTK-inhibitor effect and its uncertainty from E-MTAB-8873, fit or explicitly defer the hierarchical latent alternative, and produce the frozen analysis manifest, donor-aware effect/uncertainty tables, prespecified harm/state scores, LPS/FLS-only controls, leave-one-donor-cluster-out results, and a conclusion linked to those computed outputs. Completion is not a polished hypothesis or a readiness probe: it requires these computed outputs and an interpretation that distinguishes supportive, adverse and inconclusive cases.
