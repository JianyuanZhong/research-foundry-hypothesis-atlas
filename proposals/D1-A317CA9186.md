# Microbiology-contextualized antimicrobial contraction at first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and fit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study of whether a **recorded antimicrobial regimen contraction** in the last 24 hours of ICU care identifies a clinically different early post-ICU pathway from continued broad recorded coverage, and whether that contrast is different when a microbiology result-bearing record is present. This is a prognostic and care-process/measurement question about recorded treatment. It is not a causal estimate of stopping antibiotics and it is not a bedside de-escalation rule.

Completion requires locked held-out outputs, not readiness alone:

1. `run_manifest.json` with the catalog and source hashes, archive members, schema hashes, exact drug-class dictionary, item labels, joins, time windows, split seeds, outcome-topology rules, software versions and output hashes.
2. `cohort_audit.parquet`, one row per subject's earliest eligible ICU stay, with every join and exclusion count, culture-window and antibiotic-window eligibility, observation coverage, missing-detail flags and prespecified audit strata.
3. `antibiotic_ledger.parquet`, retaining every qualifying and non-qualifying `emar` medication row in the analysis windows, its normalized ingredient/class mapping, `event_txt`, corresponding `emar_detail` fields, administration-evidence tier, duplicate/contradiction status and exact event time.
4. `microbiology_ledger.parquet`, retaining every eligible microbiology row and its specimen/result-bearing fields, with specimen `charttime`, `storetime), organism and susceptibility presence, and an explicit flag that result availability is not observed.
5. `hourly_features.parquet`, 48 one-hour rows in `[outtime-48h,outtime)` with administered antibiotic class sets, administration counts, prescription/order/process masks, microbiology-record masks, physiology, selected laboratory values, missingness and measurement-density variables. No post-`outtime` field may enter predictors.
6. `outcome_topology.parquet`, all later ICU candidates, bridge candidates, event times, death/discharge ordering, ties and lower/upper escalation endpoints through 48 hours.
7. `cif_contraction_contrasts.parquet`, cause-specific and Aalen-Johansen competing-event estimates at 6, 24 and 48 hours, standardized to the eligible cohort, with lower/upper topology-compatible contrast intervals and uncertainty.
8. `test_predictions.parquet`, `calibration_uncertainty.parquet` and `latent_regimen_diagnostics.parquet` for the locked patient-level test sets. The latter must show inferred-state occupancy, duration, posterior entropy and sensitivity to missing administration detail; it is not a claim that a latent state is a clinical diagnosis.
9. `falsification.parquet` and `interpretation.md`, linking every conclusion to computed rows, uncertainty, overlap, topology-bound width and prespecified supportive, adverse or inconclusive criteria.

The scientific completion criterion is a reproducible estimate of the contraction-versus-continuation contrasts for bridge-confirmed escalation, any ICU return, death and alive discharge, with the result classified as supportive, adverse or inconclusive under the gates below. AUC or a small score improvement alone does not complete the study.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The imported expert seed `[starting question]` asks whether antimicrobial de-escalation after results is safer when infection evidence is clear and organ function is improving. That seed is a useful clinical direction, not evidence or an executable definition. The strongest claim supported by the local audit is narrower: the source contains dated medication administration records, medication orders, microbiology records, ICU outtimes, physiology and laboratory measurements. It does not establish that an antibiotic was clinically de-escalated, that a microbiology result was available to the treating team at a particular time, that infection was present, or that any treatment change caused an outcome.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults leaving a first eligible ICU stay with a recent infection-compatible microbiology record and repeated recorded antibiotic administrations, does a recorded contraction of antibiotic ingredient classes in the final 24 ICU hours, after at least two classes were recorded in the preceding 24 hours, identify a different early competing-outcome pathway than continued broad recorded coverage after standardizing for measured illness, support, observation and discharge-process features?

The primary directional hypothesis is that, if contraction is mostly a marker of clinical recovery or appropriate stewardship, contraction-compatible patients will have a lower 48-hour death cumulative incidence and no higher bridge-confirmed ICU-escalation cumulative incidence than patients with stable broad coverage. The adverse alternative is a higher escalation/death contrast, consistent with contraction being a marker of premature transition or unresolved illness. The primary test is two-sided: a null or reversal is allowed and does not get rescued by the directional prior.

A secondary, explicitly weaker hypothesis asks whether the contraction contrast differs between patients with and without a **microbiology-result-recorded** signal by the landmark. This does not use the word “culture available” because the local microbiology table has `charttime` and `storetime`, but no validated result-availability timestamp. A row with an organism or susceptibility field is called result-bearing in the data, not known to have been available at that time.

This advances the parent in a clinically distinct way. The parent studies the order of recorded respiratory and vasoactive withdrawal. This successor studies a stewardship and ICU-to-ward transition decision with a different data-generating process, while retaining the parent's safeguards around partial identification, observation and competing outcomes. It is not a new antibiotic risk score: the main deliverable is an interpretable contrast about recorded regimen transition and its uncertainty.

## Antibiotic exposure and contraction definition

The primary exposure uses administration evidence from `hosp/emar`, not a prescription order alone. Normalize case, punctuation and salt/formulation suffixes in `emar.medication` to ingredient names using a versioned, hand-audited dictionary locked before test evaluation. The initial dictionary must include, at minimum, these ingredients observed in the local susceptibility vocabulary or their exact eMAR generic equivalents:

- vancomycin;
- piperacillin/tazobactam and piperacillin;
- cefepime, ceftazidime, ceftriaxone, cefazolin and cefuroxime;
- meropenem, imipenem and ertapenem;
- aztreonam;
- ampicillin, ampicillin/sulbactam and penicillin G;
- ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin and moxifloxacin;
- gentamicin, tobramycin and amikacin;
- metronidazole, clindamycin, doxycycline, tetracycline, linezolid, daptomycin, tigecycline and fluconazole only if the dictionary explicitly marks antifungal exposure as a separate non-antibacterial class.

The solver must publish every matched string, unmatched string count and class mapping. Antifungals are not silently mixed with antibacterials. The primary antibacterial analysis uses a prespecified ingredient-class set; an ingredient-level sensitivity and a restricted antibacterial-only analysis are required. A medication row that cannot be mapped is retained as unknown, not dropped from the ledger.

A primary administration is an `emar` row with `event_txt` exactly equal to `Administered` and non-null `charttime`. Join `hosp/emar_detail` by `subject_id, emar_id, emar_seq` after carrying `hadm_id` from the parent `emar` row. Retain `administration_type`, `dose_given`, `dose_given_unit`, `product_description`, `route`, `infusion_complete`, `continued_infusion_in_other_location` and `reason_for_no_barcode` as evidence and missingness fields; do not infer a dose from a prescription. Duplicate parent rows, multiple detail rows and contradictory administration fields remain visible. `event_txt` values such as Held, Not Given, Refused or Missing Dose are non-administration observations and are used for observation-process sensitivity, not counted as delivered therapy.

Use `hosp/prescriptions` (`starttime,stoptime,drug,route,dose_val_rx,dose_unit_rx`) and `hosp/pharmacy` (`starttime,stoptime,medication,status,route`) only as order/process corroboration and negative-control features. An order is never an administered dose. Invalid or reversed order intervals are retained as contradictions. `hosp/poe` (`ordertime,transaction_type,discontinue_of_poe_id,order_status`) and `hosp/poe_detail` (`field_name,field_value`) may supply order/discontinuation process features but cannot define exposure.

For each patient, form class sets in:

- W1 = `[outtime-48h,outtime-24h)`;
- W2 = `[outtime-24h,outtime)`.

Require at least two qualifying administered rows with distinct timestamps in each half for the primary exposure. Let C1 and C2 be the normalized administered antibacterial class sets. Define:

- **stable broad**: `|C1| >= 2` and `C2 = C1`;
- **contraction-compatible**: `|C1| >= 2`, `1 <= |C2| < |C1|` and `C2` is a proper subset of C1;
- **broad switch/discordant**: `|C1| >= 2`, `C2` is not a proper subset and differs from C1;
- **single-class continuation**: `|C1|=|C2|=1`, an audit/secondary group;
- **full recorded stop**: `|C1|>=1` and `C2` is empty, a secondary group only;
- **indeterminate**: insufficient administration timestamps, unmapped drug evidence, contradictory detail, or no support for distinguishing the class set.

The primary estimand compares contraction-compatible with stable broad among patients meeting the `|C1|>=2` and repeated-observation criteria. Full stop, switching and single-class continuation are never collapsed into contraction to manufacture power. A sensitivity replaces class-set contraction with a simple count contraction, and another uses exact ingredient sets; disagreement is a scientific result about the phenotype definition.

The exposure is called “recorded contraction-compatible,” never “successful de-escalation,” “appropriate de-escalation,” “antibiotic cessation,” or “coverage narrowing” without the qualifier that these are unobserved clinical concepts.

## Outcomes and partial identification

For h in {6, 24, 48} hours after `outtime`, define:

- U_h: any later `icu/icustays` row for the same `subject_id,hadm_id` with `intime > outtime` and `intime <= outtime+h`;
- L_h: a U_h later ICU stay preceded by a documented non-ICU transfer bridge in `hosp/transfers`: non-null `careunit`, a valid interval beginning after `outtime) and ending before later ICU `intime`, duration at least 30 minutes, and a care-unit comparison to the later ICU stay when defined;
- T_h: the latent clinical escalation event, not observed in this data.

Retain every candidate transfer and topology alternative. The defensible relation is `L_h <= T_h <= U_h). A later ICU row is not called unplanned, preventable, deterioration or readmission for clinical deterioration.

The first competing event is one of bridge-confirmed escalation L, topology-unconfirmed ICU return U-only, in-hospital death before a return, or alive hospital discharge before a return. Apply a one-minute tie rule and repeat with 0 and 5 minutes. Use `admissions.deathtime` only for death timing and outcome definition, never as a predictor. Censor at the earlier of the horizon and `admissions.dischtime) when no event occurs.

For contraction group A and stable-broad group B, report group-specific CIFs and risk differences for L and U, plus the compatible contrast interval `[L_A-U_B, U_A-L_B]` for latent escalation. Report the interval width itself. A result only in U is evidence about recorded transfer topology; it is not strong evidence of clinical escalation. Standardize contrasts to the eligible primary population, with overlap and effective sample size reported.

## Baseline and scientifically informative alternative

Use fixed subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits with seeds 17, 29 and 43. Split by `subject_id` before any mapping thresholds, imputation, dictionary extension, scaling or model fitting. No subject contributes to multiple splits. The primary comparison uses exactly the same rows, derived inputs, outcome definitions and split assignments.

### Auditable discrete-time baseline

Fit pooled one-hour cause-specific discrete hazards for L, U-only, death and alive discharge, with a separate aggregate-U analysis. Use penalized logistic regression with prespecified linear/spline terms and calendar-free time-since-landmark bins. The full feature set contains the contraction class, class counts and timestamps; physiology/laboratory trajectories; antibiotic administration and order-process masks; microbiology context; discharge-process covariates; demographic/admission fields; and ICU LOS. Report nested models:

1. physiology/support and baseline context only;
2. plus antibiotic administration and contraction fields;
3. plus microbiology fields;
4. plus observation/order-process fields;
5. full model.

Compute standardized 6/24/48-hour CIFs and contraction contrasts from held-out predictions, not just odds ratios. The baseline is the primary estimand anchor because each component is inspectable and its contrast can be audited row by row.

### Observation-aware duration model

Fit a factorized hidden semi-Markov regimen model on the same one-hour inputs. Its latent states are not clinical truth and must be named `broad-recorded`, `contracted-recorded`, `switched/discordant`, `no-administration-observed` and `unknown` only as model states. Emissions include administered class indicators and counts from `emar`, detail completeness and non-administration events, prescription/pharmacy/POE order masks, microbiology-record masks, and the same physiology/laboratory covariates. The model has explicit state-duration distributions and a competing-hazard head for L, U-only, death and discharge. Fit state parameters and hazard parameters only on training data, select duration complexity and regularization in validation, and freeze before test evaluation.

The alternative can reveal information the baseline loses: whether a contraction is a sustained regimen state or a one-hour documentation gap, whether a switch and a true subset contraction have different dwell times, and whether posterior uncertainty or observation density accounts for the apparent outcome contrast. It is scientifically useful only if these duration/observation distinctions alter the contraction estimand or expose instability; it is not selected for a small predictive gain. Compare baseline and alternative on the same standardized CIF contrasts, calibration, IPCW Brier score, split stability and uncertainty. High posterior entropy or disagreement is inconclusive, not confirmation.

The baseline is expected to fit on CPU. The hidden semi-Markov model should begin with one CPU allocation and bounded profiling; use an allocated A100 only if actual sequence fitting or bootstrap repetition is matrix-limited. The runtime has ordinary-shell CUDA masking, so a shell CUDA failure would not establish GPU infeasibility. The discovery audit measured source/header/schema access and a 7,200-second discovery allowance; full extraction, repeated bootstrap and hidden-state fitting remain unmeasured. The separate solver planning envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB RAM, 8 allocated GPUs and 28800 seconds. No GPU is mandatory.

## Uncertainty, falsification and interpretation

Use subject bootstrap 95% intervals within the locked test set, all three split seeds, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier scores, event counts, exposure overlap/effective sample size, unknown and contradiction rates, missing-detail rates, topology-bound width and hidden-state posterior entropy. Censoring weights must be fitted without post-index information. Report absolute CIFs and contrasts, not only relative scores.

Prespecified falsifications and sensitivity analyses are:

- permute contraction/stable labels within strata of W1 class count, total administrations, physiology, ICU LOS, microbiology-record status and observation density, preserving missingness and outcome data;
- shuffle administration order within each patient's two windows while preserving class counts, timestamps' marginal distribution and observation masks;
- replace eMAR administration with prescriptions/pharmacy orders as a negative-control exposure; a similar primary signal would implicate ordering/documentation rather than administration;
- remove `emar_detail` dose fields, then remove all order-process fields, then remove all physiology/lab fields, and report which component carries the contrast;
- substitute `storetime` for `charttime` in eMAR and microbiology as timestamp negative controls;
- shift the exposure frontier by 6 hours and repeat W1/W2 windows using 12/24 and 24/48 hours;
- vary exact class dictionary, ingredient-level versus class-level sets, the requirement for two administrations per half, and exclusion of full stop;
- compare chart-time and store-time microbiology strata without calling either the true availability time;
- vary bridge duration 0/15/30/60 minutes, tie tolerance 0/1/5 minutes, and transfer-careunit comparison;
- use pseudo-landmarks 6 and 12 hours before outtime with outcomes shifted consistently;
- stratify by first/last ICU careunit, admission type and anchor-year group, without cross-subject calendar comparisons.

Supportive evidence for the directional hypothesis requires adequate event support and positivity; a clinically meaningful contraction-versus-stable contrast with uncertainty excluding the locked null for the prespecified outcome; no higher L contrast for contraction; consistency in direction between L and U with a non-wide topology interval; attenuation under order-label permutation; and agreement of baseline and duration model on standardized CIF contrasts. The evidence may support only the recorded-data association, and it must say whether support is present for L, only U, or neither. A microbiology interaction is supportive only if it survives the chart-time/store-time and observation-mask sensitivities.

Adverse evidence includes higher L or death with contraction, reversal across seeds, disappearance after observation/process adjustment, dependence on one drug string or one eMAR evidence tier, persistence under order permutation, a similar prescription-only signal, or a result confined to U. A U-only signal is adverse to the stronger clinical-escalation interpretation but may document transfer topology.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse contraction cells, high indeterminate/unknown medication prevalence, poor overlap, broad topology-compatible intervals, unstable culture-context strata, unmeasured-result-time sensitivity, calibration failure, high latent entropy, split instability, or disagreement between models. Do not merge full stop, switch, unknown and stable groups to create power. A null is a valid scientific result.

## Evidence limitations and claims requiring another study

The computation can establish the recorded regimen phenotype, exact temporal construction, held-out prognostic CIFs, calibration, observation-process sensitivity, competing risks and topology bounds. It cannot establish:

- that a drug was actually delivered whenever an eMAR row says administered, or that no drug was delivered when no row exists;
- that a regimen was broad, narrow, de-escalated, appropriate or clinically indicated;
- when a microbiology result became available to a clinician;
- true infection, source control, pathogen-directed appropriateness, treatment limitation or clinician intent;
- physiologic recovery, ward monitoring intensity, unrecorded outside-hospital treatment or preventability of ICU return;
- a causal effect of changing antibiotics, a safe stopping rule, antibiotic resistance consequences, clinical utility or transportability.

These stronger claims require expert chart adjudication of infection source, source control, culture finalization/availability, treatment rationale, antibiotic appropriateness and deterioration; ward administration and monitoring data; external validation; and ultimately a prospective or causal study. MIMIC's subject-specific date shifting also prevents cross-subject calendar-time comparisons. Discharge notes, radiology text, CXR images and raw waveforms are not primary inputs; the relevant external clinical evidence is unavailable here.

## Demonstration, seed and deferred-branch record

The three research demonstrations were used as methodological context only. The natural-history demonstration supports the general idea that dated structured histories can be learned, but it does not establish this MIMIC question or provide its missing labels. The Bayesian longitudinal demonstration supports duration/latent-trajectory reasoning, but an EHR-only adaptation is not a reproduction and no genetic inputs are assumed. The cancer demonstration's full article and STAR Methods remain unavailable in the local bundle; only its accessible supplement was inspected as described in the research-ambition README, and no multimodal claim is imported.

The MIMIC antimicrobial seed `[starting question]` is the selected direction, but its original after-result hypothesis is deferred as written because result availability is not represented by a validated timestamp and source control is incomplete. This proposal tests the computable recorded-contraction successor and reports that limitation explicitly.

The following alternatives are retained but not selected:

- **Target-trial emulation of antibiotic de-escalation versus continuation:** clinically attractive, but not identified without a reliable result-availability time, indication/source-control variables, complete ward treatment and adequate adjustment for time-varying confounding. Revisit only after those dependencies are obtained.
- **The parent’s respiratory/vasoactive temporal order:** already the selected parent and a valid question; not duplicated here because a new stewardship transition supplies a distinct clinical decision.
- **Residual instability or renal-respiratory discordance at ICU discharge:** feasible and clinically relevant, but too close to the parent’s support/physiology object for this successor; revisit for a different landmark or adjudicated renal phenotype.
- **Persistent lactate as an occult-hypoperfusion phenotype:** exact blood lactate itemids are available, but it would need a prespecified repeated-measurement cohort and careful treatment/measurement confounding work. It is deferred rather than added as a cosmetic covariate interaction.
- **GRU/TCN/transformer sequence model:** deferred because the HSMM directly tests the substantive duration and documentation-gap uncertainty. Revisit only if contraction cells are adequately populated, the HSMM leaves reproducible duration structure unexplained, and a preregistered sequence ablation has a defined scientific target and measured budget.
- **Narrative NLP of discharge rationale and full multimodal modeling:** requires validated clinical NLP labels or unavailable images/waveforms, so it cannot be a primary branch in this snapshot.
