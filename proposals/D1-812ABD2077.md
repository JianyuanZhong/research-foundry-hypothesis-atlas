# Remote hepatic state and a jointly adverse next panel after repeat TACE

## Substantive synthesis and clinical question

This child evolves `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]` into one clinically sharper, evidence-bounded monitoring experiment. It preserves the reverse-nested question from the latter—whether information remote from the repeat event adds information beyond the contemporaneous pre-repeat state—and the event-aligned trajectory, selection, timing, and noncausal safeguards from the former.

The monitoring question is:

> Among adults with a frozen, independently selected first repeat TACE and a complete, same-event albumin/bilirubin panel, does remote pre-TACE2 hepatic-state information add reproducible out-of-fold prediction beyond the contemporaneous pre-repeat panel for the next event-anchored panel, with particular attention to the adverse pattern of albumin falling and total bilirubin rising?

This matters because a clinician monitoring a repeat-TACE episode may care about a coordinated deterioration signal, not an isolated assay association. The proposal does not claim that TACE, a systemic medication record, or either pathway caused the laboratory pattern. It asks whether a remote state marker adds reproducible monitoring information once the current pre-event state is known. A positive result supports only a future study with units, adjudicated decompensation/complication outcomes, and prospective validation; it does not justify a treatment or surveillance policy.

### Evidence-supported versus untested claims

The complete-source, direction-blinded evidence already supports a reproducible frozen construction: 319 systemic-record and 1,491 comparator patients; 159 and 526 first strict repeat-TACE events; and primary assay-specific triplets of 40/118 albumin and 41/119 total bilirubin (systemic/comparator). Strict common-timestamp triplets are 40/118. The source contains no laboratory units or reference ranges, and medication rows do not verify administration.

The unresolved claim is predictive, not causal: remote pre-TACE2 albumin/bilirubin information improves reproducible prediction of the next observed same-event panel beyond the contemporaneous pre-repeat panel. No outcome direction, model association, or clinical benefit is claimed by the audit.

## Assay parser, selectors, and the joint endpoint

Parse `Quantitative Result` only as a signed decimal/scientific number with optional leading `<`, `>`, `≤`, or `≥`, retaining the inequality flag. Primary numeric inference uses exact uncensored numeric rows; censored rows are audited and excluded from primary selectors. Do not infer units from `Specimen Type`. For duplicate same-assay patient/encounter/timestamp groups, retain the final source-order row only when numeric values are concordant and exclude/report discordant groups. Audit invalid times, parser attrition, duplicates, ties, encounter multiplicity, and event-to-panel hours.

For each assay and selected event, define the inherited clocks:

- `B_a`: latest valid uncensored result on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1.
- `P_a`: latest valid uncensored result in the selected repeat encounter during `[event_time−72h,event_time)`.
- `Y_a`: valid uncensored result in that encounter during `(event_time,event_time+72h]`, nearest nominal event +24h, earlier on an exact tie. Event timestamp is excluded.

The primary joint cohort requires both assays to have their selected `B`, `P`, and `Y` under the same patient/event. To make “panel” literal, the primary version additionally requires the albumin and bilirubin values at each stage to share the selected stage timestamp (baseline, pre-repeat, and post-repeat); the complete-source audit expects 40 systemic and 118 comparator observations. If common timestamps are unavailable due to an exact, predeclared implementation distinction, stop the joint primary rather than silently substituting assay-specific timing; assay-specific joint completeness is a locked sensitivity and is not used to rescue a failed primary.

The binary monitoring pattern is prespecified as `J=1` when `Y_albumin < P_albumin` and `Y_bilirubin > P_bilirubin`; equality is not adverse. This is a direction-only descriptive endpoint, not a clinical toxicity definition. The direction-blinded audit was extended without inspecting predictors or fitting associations. It found, under the assay-specific primary selectors, joint complete/adverse counts of 40/26 systemic and 118/71 comparator. Fixed five-fold feasibility counts for systemic had one fold with zero non-events (fold sizes 6,5,14,12,3; adverse 5,4,7,7,3), so a five-fold binary classifier or arm-interaction claim is not supportable. This is a negative feasibility result, not evidence for or against deterioration biology.

Because units are unavailable and the binary pattern is sparse in the systemic arm, the primary analysis is a continuous multivariate/rank-based fallback rather than an indefensible laboratory-threshold dichotomy. For each assay define the event-proximal change `C_albumin=Y_albumin−P_albumin` and `C_bilirubin=Y_bilirubin−P_bilirubin`; do not pool native assay values. Within each outer training fold, transform the two changes to midranks among training patients, orient them adversely (`R_A=rank(-C_albumin)` and `R_B=rank(C_bilirubin)`), and map held-out changes through the training empirical CDF with deterministic endpoint clipping. Define the joint adverse severity score `S=(R_A+R_B)/2`. This score preserves direction and graded movement without asserting unit-based thresholds. Report the two assay-specific continuous outcomes separately as well as `S`; the binary `J` is a descriptive feasibility/interpretation endpoint only.

1. Reconstruction mismatch, parser/duplicate/timestamp mismatch, or failure of expected 319/1,491, 159/526, or common-panel 40/118 counts is reconstruction-inconclusive.
2. Failure of common-panel completeness, fold support, rank transformation, training-only preprocessing, nonzero variance, or 95% bootstrap estimability is computationally inconclusive. The sparse binary endpoint remains non-estimable regardless of any point estimate.

4. If M1 worsens the primary continuous score prediction with its interval wholly below zero, report adverse degradation of the remote-history monitoring hypothesis.

Supportive evidence requires a positive, precisely estimated incremental gain, predeclared design margin `delta=0.05` standardized RMSE (a design convention, not clinical importance), interval excluding zero, non-adverse MAE/calibration, no linkage-placebo reproduction, stable locked timing/duplicate/partition/influence results, and no selection gate failure. 7. If M1 adds no improvement while a current-state-only benchmark performs similarly, report no demonstrated remote increment/ordinary persistence. If the binary J is sparse, state explicitly that its feasibility failed and that it cannot be used to support a joint-adverse clinical claim.

## Clinical limits and verifier boundary

The source cannot establish laboratory units/reference ranges, cirrhosis severity, tumor burden, transfusion/albumin infusion, infection, bleeding, renal or hepatic decompensation, technical TACE success or intent, medication administration/adherence/dose, outside care, or mortality. Diagnoses are lexical encounter proxies without native time. Conditioning on repeat TACE and complete observed panels is post-baseline selection and may induce collider/observation bias; day-14 observation is not survival. Albumin down plus bilirubin up is a laboratory pattern, not adjudicated hepatic failure or toxicity.

A verifier can check source hashes, full scans, joins, exact selectors, common timestamps, parser and censoring rules, counts, fold construction, rank transformation, training-only preprocessing, nested predictions, bootstrap arithmetic, permutations, and branch precedence. It cannot establish whether the pattern is clinically important, caused by TACE or medication, or actionable. Those claims require unit-validated labs, expert-adjudicated decompensation/complication endpoints, verified exposure and procedure details, complete follow-up, and prospective/external calibration and utility validation.
