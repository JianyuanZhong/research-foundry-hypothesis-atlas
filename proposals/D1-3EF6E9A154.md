# Decision-bounded risk prioritization of lactate-by-perfusion at a fixed ICU landmark

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` (episode-25 targeted repair). It preserves the parent’s exact source bindings, fixed-landmark timing, phenotype, endpoint rules, dual clinical/store-time gate, subject split, and noncausal boundaries. The repair narrows the decision interpretation from monitoring utility to an estimand identifiable with these records: retrospective prioritization of a fixed review queue. It preserves the parent’s primary live-exit estimand, the fixed-landmark 24-hour R/D/S/U endpoint, the dual clinical/recording-time coverage gate, the event-support rules, the uncertainty plan, and the noncausal claim boundaries. It adds a clinically consequential but bounded question:

> At a prespecified 12-hour ICU landmark, does the lactate-by-measured-perfusion phenotype improve held-out prioritization of patients who will experience the parent’s record-defined ICU-level transition or timed death within 24 hours, at a fixed review-queue capacity?

The estimand is a policy-independent, retrospective risk-prioritization estimand—not treatment effect, monitoring utility, benefit of rescue, or proof that a review changes care. Define E as the strict-gate fixed-landmark cohort with complete eligibility at t_F, and E* = E minus U cases whose first transition cannot be ordered. Partition E* once by subject into E*_train, E*_validation and E*_test using the frozen 70/15/15 split. Models are fit on E*_train/E*_validation and scored only on E*_test; the queue contains exactly the same integer k_w subjects for every model at workload w∈{0.05,0.10,0.20}, with k_w=max(1, round-half-up(w|E*_test|)) and deterministic subject/stay tie-breaking frozen before test scoring. Capture is TP_A/#A_test, where A=R-or-D and #A_test is counted only in E*_test; report the corresponding E* ascertainable count separately. Thus the primary claim is conditional on observed ascertainment and strict measured-pressor coverage, not a claim about all ICU patients. The same R, D, S, N and U labels are retained, with S an observation-boundary competing transition and U unresolved ascertainment. The all-E analysis retains U in the queue and reports adverse-U and non-adverse-U bounds using k^E_w=max(1, round-half-up(w|E_test|)); it is a nominal-w sensitivity, not a way to relabel U.

The primary decision contrast is fixed-capacity incremental capture of the phenotype over a matched history baseline:

**Rule:** G_10 = TP_A(Bdisc,k_10)/#A_test − TP_A(B1,k_10)/#A_test,

where both queues use the same held-out E*_test queue size k_10 and all rankings are frozen before test outcomes are read. Report the random-allocation benchmark G^rand_10 = k_10/|E*_test| for capture proportion (and its expected PPV), so a model is not described as useful merely because it beats an uninformative rule. Use the exact same rule for 5% and 20% secondary workloads. A secondary threshold-policy table may use a CIF threshold chosen only from training/validation to target 10%; report its realized test workload separately and never substitute that workload for the fixed-k estimand. No test outcome selects a threshold, workload, feature, phenotype, or model.

The preregistered prioritization hypothesis is that G_10 is at least 5 percentage points and its subject-bootstrap interval excludes zero, with Bdisc’s A-capture proportion exceeding the random benchmark at the same k and its primary Bdisc−B1 contrast meeting the criterion, while the gain is not reproduced by Bmask. Bdisc−B0 is a secondary question about total value beyond latest-state/context information, not the phenotype-specific estimand. A capture proportion is not utility: the data contain no intervention, response, burden, harm, preference or cost. The 5-point criterion concerns incremental capture only; it does not establish monitoring benefit. Calibration is a separately prespecified risk-estimation check: report intercept, slope, observed/expected ratio, Brier score and reliability plots with uncertainty. Failure of calibration prevents any absolute-CIF threshold claim and makes the threshold-policy analysis inconclusive, but does not silently turn a ranking result into a utility claim. The strongest evidence currently available is design-level and source-level: the local MIMIC-IV 3.1 snapshot contains ICU boundaries, repeated lactate and MAP records, vasoactive intervals, organ laboratories, ICU support/procedure records, and admission death/discharge fields from which the parent’s operational trajectory and competing states can be constructed. This establishes data availability, not prevalence, physiological validity, or clinical benefit. No fitted result is claimed.

The parent established a design-level prognostic hypothesis: persistent lactate may have different 48-hour risk associations when measured perfusion is improving versus worsening, with a fixed early-landmark robustness analysis to reduce live-exit selection concerns; no fitted result is treated as established evidence here. What remains unresolved is whether the phenotype contributes held-out prioritization information beyond matched longitudinal history. A statistically detectable interaction can still be too poorly calibrated, too sparse, too dependent on observation intensity, or too weak at a realistic alert capacity to inform such a decision. Conversely, useful event capture at fixed review capacity would be a stronger prioritization result than an isolated hazard ratio, while still remaining observational.

The substantive advance over the parent is therefore an estimand change from “is there prognostic effect modification?” to “does the prespecified phenotype add reliable held-out event capture at a constrained review capacity?” The phenotype-specific comparison is now nested: B1 contains the same order-blind history, process and organ domains, while Bdisc adds the frozen lactate-by-perfusion terms. This prevents a gain from generic longitudinal history from being misattributed to the phenotype. It also repairs a concrete fixed-landmark leakage threat: `icustays.last_careunit` is a stay-level terminal field and cannot be used as a feature at (t_F). The fixed model instead uses the `transfers` interval covering (t_F), plus `icustays.first_careunit`, both available by the landmark. For the live-exit analysis, care-unit features likewise come from the transfer interval covering t_L = t_0-1 hour; terminal `last_careunit` is prohibited from the primary feature matrix. This repair does not alter an endpoint or a coverage rule.

A supportive result would justify only a prospective silent-mode or externally validated review-prioritization study in a similarly selected population, carrying forward the fixed queue capacity and ascertainment limitations. It would not show that review prevents R/D, that a record-defined transition was clinically necessary or preventable, that escalation is indicated, or that treating lactate or perfusion improves outcome. An adverse result would be clinically useful: if Bdisc does not beat B1 at fixed workload, if Bmask matches it, or if gains disappear under timing and process falsifications, the phenotype should not be promoted as an escalation or monitoring-utility signal from these data. An inconclusive result would mean that the data cannot resolve the prioritization question because of sparse events, coverage, U, calibration, overlap or uncertainty, not that the phenotype is absent.

## Models, decision analysis and matched learned alternative

All models use the same deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 split, with all admissions from a subject in one partition. The split is frozen before fitting. B0, B1, Bmask, Bdisc and J1 use the same strict fixed-landmark subjects, endpoint labels, source whitelist and cutoff. For the live-exit parent estimand, the same source and split discipline applies.

- **B0 transparent competing-risk baseline.** Penalized discrete-time cause-specific complementary-log-log hazards for R, D and S, using admission context, age/gender/year-group, first-careunit and landmark transfer careunit, elapsed ICU time, terminal valid lactate/MAP/organ values, active support status, terminal masks and measurement-process summaries. It is the latest-state/context comparator for whether the phenotype adds value.
- **B1 matched order-blind history comparator.** It uses fixed twelve one-hour-bin summaries over the 12-hour fixed window (medians, minima, maxima, SD/IQR, abnormal fractions, valid-bin and distinct-time counts, pressor presence/minutes, Foley charting and lab-test indicators), with the same context, organ, process and missingness domains as Bdisc but without early/late order or phenotype terms. Bdisc is nested in B1: it adds only the frozen persistent-lactate, improving/indeterminate/worsening and lactate-by-perfusion interaction terms, with no additional non-phenotype variables.
- **Bmask process-only falsification.** Uses context, masks, counts, time-since-observation, test intensity, Foley charting, pressor-presence and support-process history, but no physiologic values. It tests whether alert capture is driven by documentation intensity.
- **Bdisc phenotype-aware baseline.** B1 plus only the fixed persistent-lactate indicator, early/late lactate medians, fixed improving/indeterminate/worsening direction, early/late MAP and pressor changes, and their lactate-by-perfusion interaction; all organ values and availability indicators are already matched in B1. The interaction is the scientific feature; indeterminate remains a labeled category and is not forced into improving/worsening. Bdisc is the primary transparent model for (G_{10}).
- **J1 learned alternative.** A small masked chronological GRU with six two-hour bins, the same raw physiologic, mask, process, support and context domains and exactly the same subjects, labels, split and held-out test partition as Bdisc. It has a three-cause R/D/S head and predicts discrete-time hazards/CIFs. Hidden size 32 or 64, dropout, early stopping and five fixed seeds are selected only on training/validation. J1 can reveal persistence, reversal, time-local lactate/MAP coupling and nonlinear trajectories discarded by B0/B1/Bdisc; a gain without calibration, Bdisc/Bmask separation or attenuation after chronology shuffling is not evidence for the phenotype.

## Method alternatives, selection and compute record

The simple baseline is B0: a transparent discrete-time competing-risk model that a clinician or analyst can audit and recalibrate. Bdisc is the selected transparent scientific comparator because its terms expose the fixed lactate-by-perfusion question; B0 supplies the latest-state/context decision reference, and Bmask tests observation-process substitution. All use the same endpoint, subjects, split and outcome-independent preprocessing.

J1 is the selected learned alternative, not because complexity is intrinsically valuable, but because six chronological bins can reveal persistence, reversal and time-local cross-domain coupling that terminal and order-blind summaries lose. Its target is the same three-cause discrete-time R/D/S hazard and its evaluation is the same held-out CIF, calibration, Brier and fixed-workload capture. Five seeds and any hyperparameter choice are training/validation-only. J1 is deliberately small; it does not expand the clinical question or claim mechanistic discovery.

A compact two-component latent mixture on early-to-late lactate/MAP/pressor changes was considered and deferred: without adjudicated stabilization, dose-quality evidence or validated physiological labels, latent components could mostly encode selective treatment recording. A larger transformer and a treatment-effect model are also deferred. The transformer would add scale without resolving the fixed alert-capture uncertainty; treatment effect would require treatment intent, responsiveness, counterfactual outcomes and adequate time-varying confounding control, which these tables do not supply. These are evidence-bound choices, not blanket exclusions of neural methods, GPU use or structured data.

Measured discovery facts are the source/archive hashes, catalog schemas, exact archive members, and dictionary labels/links/units verified by read-only inspection. Unmeasured are strict-gate prevalence, event counts, overlap/ESS, calibration, alert capture, bootstrap duration and J1 convergence. The future solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, up to 8 A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs and 28,800 seconds; discovery science budget is separate (7,200 seconds). These are planning limits, not measured runtimes; the local archive-member listing was measured, while strict-gate prevalence, event support, model convergence and bootstrap duration remain unmeasured. B0/B1/Bmask/Bdisc and CIFs are expected to be CPU-first but runtime is unmeasured. J1 is expected to fit on CPU at this small size; if repeated seeds/refits threaten the envelope, an allocated GPU may be requested and the model/tensors must use `cuda:0` inside the allocation. No GPU is required, and no proposer/solver training is authorized.
