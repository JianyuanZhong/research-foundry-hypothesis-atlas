# Forward-calendar transport of contemporaneous P beyond remote B in frozen repeat-TACE triplets

## Clinically stronger unresolved question and evidentiary boundary

This two-parent successor combines `[prior hypothesis]`'s exact prognostic nesting with `[prior hypothesis]`'s outcome-blind rolling-calendar framework and sparse-era lesson. In each frozen assay-specific complete-triplet cohort, the unresolved question is whether a model trained only on earlier repeat-TACE episodes continues to predict the next observed same-assay value better after adding the contemporaneous pre-repeat value `P` to remote baseline `B` and the identical compact decision-time core.

The strongest evidence already supported is internal rather than temporal: bounded pooled five-fold analysis gave positive standardized RMSE gains for adding `P` for albumin (0.1637) and total bilirubin (0.2070), but its uncertainty and inherited linkage permutation did not establish robust incremental information. The temporal parent separately showed that later systemic-record support becomes sparse. Neither result establishes forward transport of the corrected P-beyond-B nesting.

The experiment tests that forward prognostic transport claim. It does not test a treatment-arm contrast, treatment response, toxicity, safety, benefit, causal effect, clinical threshold, decision utility, or clinical outcome. The systemic-record/comparator label remains one prespecified covariate and a composition diagnostic only; no exchangeability between labels is assumed.

## Locked forward-calendar experiment

The five origins are fixed from calendar time without viewing future `Y`: 2022-01-01, 2022-07-01, 2023-01-01, 2023-07-01, and 2024-01-01. For each origin, train on all assay-specific complete-triplet episodes whose repeat-event date is strictly before the origin and test on the next 365 calendar days `[origin, origin+365 days)`. Thus every test outcome is later than all training outcomes. Overlapping 365-day tests are robustness views, not independent replications.

For assay `a`, compare the exact nested ridge models:

```
M0: Y_a ~ B_a + systemic-record indicator + age + sex indicators
          + TACE2-to-repeat days + P-lead hours
M1: Y_a ~ B_a + P_a + systemic-record indicator + age + sex indicators
          + TACE2-to-repeat days + P-lead hours
```

The core, coding, and temporal availability are identical. Within each origin and model, age median imputation plus a missingness indicator, variance filtering, scaling, and ridge fitting are learned only in the pre-origin training set. Select the ridge penalty by training-only leave-one-patient-out error over `{0.01,0.1,1,10,100}`, with smaller-alpha deterministic ties. No test `Y` participates in preprocessing, tuning, feature selection, or fitting.

The primary estimand is pooled within assay and origin:

`[RMSE_test(M0) − RMSE_test(M1)] / SD_test(Y)`.

Positive values favor incremental future-era prediction from `P`. Standardized MAE gain and prediction bias are secondary. Arm-specific errors and arm counts are diagnostics of changing mixture/support, never treatment-arm effects. Assays are never numerically pooled because units/reference ranges are unavailable.

Use paired patient-frequency bootstrap resampling within observed arm strata, holding the two pre-origin fitted prediction vectors paired, for a conditional 95% interval. This quantifies test-sample uncertainty conditional on the trained pipelines; it is not uncertainty over all calendar eras, model development, or institutions. The prespecified interpretation gate requires at least 20 total, 10 systemic-record, and 10 comparator episodes in both training and test. Ungated estimates remain descriptive even if their conditional intervals exclude zero.

This is not an arm-effect finding; it demonstrates why later comparator-dominated pooled point estimates cannot justify an exchangeable or arm-balanced transport claim.

## Falsification and interpretation

A single-origin result is **supportive for that cutoff only** when the gate passes, pooled gain is positive with its paired conditional interval above zero, neither arm diagnostic reverses direction, and no implementation/denominator/clock gate fails. Both assays met those conditions at 2022-01-01, supporting forward prediction in that selected 2022 test mixture.

A **reproducible future-era transport** conclusion requires at least two well-supported, preferably non-overlapping later test eras with positive adequately precise pooled gains, non-dominant influence, acceptable calibration, and no material adverse arm-composition diagnostic. That stronger criterion was not met: only one origin was gated, the rolling windows overlap, and later systemic support was 6/5/2/1–2. The overall result is therefore inconclusive for reproducible calendar transport rather than proof of stable monitoring value.

An **adverse** result would be a gated interval at or below zero, repeated gated degradation of M1, severe calibration failure, or directional reversal under non-sparse composition. That would falsify stable incremental `P` prediction for the tested era/population, but would not establish absence of biological change or adequacy of `B` alone. An **inconclusive** result includes sparse arms, fewer than 20 test episodes, wide intervals, influential outliers, denominator drift, missing units, or only one gated origin. More years or an independent external cohort are required to distinguish general transport from a cutoff-specific pattern.
