# eICU lab-testing signals and hospital transport of ICU mortality prediction

**Status:** Proposed and not executed. Source files are read-only; all four configured datasets remain accessible, but this study uses only eICU. The candidate’s three frozen UTF-8 key-source excerpts and `key-references.json` are attached.

## Question and claims

Among adults still in ICU at hour 24, does adding first-day lab-testing volume/timing to measured physiology improve prediction of later in-hospital death, and is that increment smaller in hospitals held out from training?

**Evidence already supported:** eICU is a multicenter critical-care database with patient/stay and hospital joins [K1]. Prior ICU prediction research reports performance drops at unseen hospitals [K2]. A 15-site EHR study argues laboratory missingness can reflect both disease course/clinician concern and mapping differences [K3]. These findings motivate the test but do not prove the hypothesis. The leading account is that test-process features partly reflect site/workflow-sensitive practice. The strongest rival is that they capture genuine, incompletely measured severity or clinician concern and retain predictive value at unseen hospitals. Case mix, treatment pathways, assay mapping, outcome coding, and selection at the 24-hour landmark also rival workflow explanations.

The experiment estimates a predictive feature increment and its transport across EICU hospitals. It cannot identify a causal mechanism or show that changing testing practice improves care. A supportive result is a positive internal increment that is smaller/absent at held-out hospitals; this is consistent with site-sensitive prediction but not proof of workflow artifact. A stable held-out increment weakens fragility for these data, not universal transportability. No model result is claimed.

## Estimand and analyses

Primary estimand is paired out-of-sample change in Brier score and calibration from adding the lab-process block to the same physiology-only feature set, comparing patient-grouped internal validation with whole-hospital holdout. Hypothesis: a positive internal increment attenuates in held-out hospitals.

Simple baseline: regularized logistic regression, physiology-only versus physiology-plus-lab-process, same cohort/splits; train-only preprocessing and calibration. Numeric lab physiology requires a prespecified harmonization dictionary. Substantive learned alternative: a small one-layer GRU on the same 24-hour observations in 24 hourly bins, with matched physiology-only and +process arms, explicit missingness masks, binary cross-entropy, training-only normalization, development-hospital early stopping, hidden width 32–64, at most 30 epochs and three prespecified seeds. GRU captures order, recency, clusters and physiology/testing interactions that aggregate linear terms lose. It may test reproducibility of temporal predictive information; it cannot identify clinician intent or mechanism. Keep regression primary; fit GRU only if data harmonization/event support permit, and defer it if support is weak or its additional temporal signal is not stable. This model comparison tests an information loss, not a small AUROC contest.

Split by `hospitalid` for entire-site holdout; within development sites use patient-grouped internal split. No `uniquepid` may appear across training/evaluation, including cross-hospital repeated stays. Fit all preprocessing in training folds. EICU removes calendar dates, so calendar-forward drift validation is unavailable; enforce the past 0–24h feature/future outcome boundary without claiming calendar-time validation. Report held-out site patient/event support, paired Brier differences and 95% uncertainty intervals, calibration-in-the-large/slope, and secondary AUROC/PR-AUC. Estimate the paired difference between internal and held-out increments directly; do not rely on significance in one split but not another. Show site-level estimates and resample hospitals for transport uncertainty, accounting for repeated identities as prespecified.

**Supportive:** positive internal increment with a smaller/absent held-out increment supported by paired uncertainty and calibration. **Adverse:** no useful internal increment, or comparably useful, calibrated held-out increment; call evidence adverse to fragility only if uncertainty excludes a meaningful decrement. **Inconclusive:** wide intervals, sparse/unbalanced sites, conflicting score/calibration evidence, or unresolved labels/harmonization. An imprecise null is not falsification. No post-hoc threshold or subgroup rescue.

## Dependencies and deliverables

Clinical adjudication is essential for lab name/unit equivalence, site-specific mappings, ambiguous age/status and hospital-death coding. Tables alone cannot establish unmeasured severity, clinician indication/concern, outages, protocols, treatment limitations, actionable bedside utility or causality. These stronger claims need chart review, linked operations/indication data, or another prospective/causally identified study.

Solver must newly fit both model families and feature arms; submit executable cohort flow, exclusions, per-site support, feature/cutoff/leakage checks, identity-safe splits, model/preprocessing specifications, held-out predictions, paired Brier/calibration/uncertainty files, site summaries, and conclusions linked to computed outputs and justified uncertainty. QA can check computation, joins, offsets, leakage guards and whether conclusions fit supportive/adverse/inconclusive outputs. It cannot adjudicate clinical assay equivalence, label validity, mechanism or generalization beyond EICU; readiness is not completion.
