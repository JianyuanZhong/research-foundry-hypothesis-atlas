# Threshold-nearest, N18-specific test of raw-grip cystatin-C allocation

## Scientific decision, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

A service that can order cystatin C for only 20% of adults with creatinine eGFR (eGFRcr) 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m2 needs evidence that collecting grip strength improves whom it tests, not merely that grip predicts a laboratory difference. [prior hypothesis] established a locked, capacity-matched UK Biobank experiment, but left two clinically consequential gaps. First, grip could beat fitted B/F comparators while losing to the transparent rule of testing those nearest the eGFRcr 60 threshold. Second, its N17/N18 downstream composite could be driven by acute kidney failure or generic hospitalization vulnerability rather than chronic-kidney relevance.

The strongest evidence available before this experiment is limited. Chen et al. (Kidney Medicine 2024; DOI 10.1016/j.xkme.2024.100796; PMCID PMC10986041) reported in 468,969 UK Biobank participants that demographic, clinical, laboratory and lifestyle characteristics predict creatinine–cystatin eGFR differences; the indexed abstract and inherited full-text evidence support association and internal prediction, not fixed-budget policy superiority, measured-GFR validity, or benefit. Yim et al. (Front Med 2022; DOI 10.3389/fmed.2022.1021936; PMCID PMC9747759; frozen full text inspected) studied 138 Korean adults using BIA skeletal muscle index and calf circumference—not handgrip or measured GFR—and supports plausibility only. No inspected evidence establishes the best 20% allocation rule or that a cystatin-selected result changes care.

The unresolved, falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In each of two untouched UKB partitions, the inherited raw-grip policy G will identify at least 2 more combined-equation eGFR<60 reclassifications per 1,000 assays, with a yield ratio at least 1.10, than a threshold-nearest policy T that assays the same 20% with the lowest full-precision baseline eGFRcr. G must also pass all inherited B/F, placebo, discordance, missingness, calibration and sex-stability gates. If N18 event sufficiency passes, G will identify at least 1 additional baseline-reclassified participant with a subsequent 10-year inpatient N18 diagnosis per 1,000 assays versus B and will be noninferior to both F and T at a margin of -1 per 1,000. N17 or N17/N18 composite enrichment cannot satisfy the downstream claim.

This tests comparative biochemical allocation and N18-specific prognostic enrichment. It does not test that grip measures muscle mass, that creatinine is the biased marker, that combined eGFR is measured GFR, that an inpatient N18 code is persistent/adjudicated CKD, or that testing improves outcomes.

## Falsification and result meaning

Mandatory checks include source hashes/headers; unique one-to-one eid joins; no lock overlap; exact code/date index pairing; formula fixtures; no future/outcome predictor; identical B matrices; G=B+only Z_R; development-only transforms/F; exact n for all policies/placebos; T equal to full-precision ascending eGFRcr plus tie hash; no T fitting/outcome access; participant-level bounds; N18-only downstream classifier; N17 not censoring N18; same-day death first; and exact reproduction of output-linked conclusions.

Fully supportive requires every inherited B/F biochemical, placebo, D, calibration, missing-bound and sex-stability gate; both locks pass all T H/ratio/D gates; N18 sufficiency passes; G beats B and is noninferior to both F and T for H×K18. This supports prospective comparison only.

Biochemical allocation supportive, chronic consequence unresolved means all B/F/T biochemical and attribution gates pass but N18 is sparse or its intervals cross decision margins. Operationally adverse means G fails a T H materiality/ratio/interval or unique-set gate—even if it beats B/F—so the transparent T rule is preferred on available evidence. N18-adverse means sufficiently powered H×K18 misses the B materiality gate or is inferior to F/T; positive N17/composite cannot reverse it. Inconclusive includes partition disagreement, crossed thresholds, adverse missing bounds, >1% missing cystatin, zero feasible denominator, sparse swap sets, chronology/integrity failure, leakage, calibration failure or sex instability. Negative findings are valuable and must not be disguised by sensitivity success.

Verifier fixtures must include: G passing B/F but failing T; G beating T biochemically but failing N18 noninferiority; positive N17/composite with nonpositive N18; sparse N18 requiring biochemical-only/inconclusive; shared missing labels whose joint bounds differ from marginal subtraction; rounded-eGFR T ordering failure; N17 incorrectly censoring N18; same-day death; supportive calculations followed by unsupported claims of CKD, guideline status, muscle mechanism or prevented events; and correct supportive/adverse/inconclusive narratives.
