# Evolution episode 4: separating early hypotension duration from observation, treatment, and severity artifacts

Status: proposed substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. No full cohort, label audit, fit, or result has been computed.

## Scientific bottleneck and revised question

The parent asks whether cumulative systemic MAP time below 65 mmHg in ICU minutes 0–360 adds transportable prognostic information for a new creatinine-defined event in minutes 360–2880, beyond nadir and early severity. That question remains clinically useful, but the parent’s process and treatment checks are primarily ablations. A duration variable can still look incremental if the same unmeasured deterioration causes more BP checks, more creatinine orders, pressor treatment, and AKI ascertainment. Conversely, recorded treatment can be a response/mediator rather than a nuisance confounder. The eICU fields cannot identify either mechanism or a treatment effect.

The revised falsifiable question is:

> Within adult first eICU stays with common support on early severity, BP observation, treatment recording, and creatinine ascertainment, does early cumulative hypotension duration provide reproducible held-out prognostic information for subsequent creatinine rise that is not mirrored by the observation process and does not disappear after cross-fitted adjustment for recorded treatment/process variables?

This is a prognostic/descriptive estimand, not a causal effect of hypotension or a treatment recommendation. The strongest evidence currently supports only that temporal hypotension summaries can be prognostic in a prior cohort [K1], that a 0.3-mg/dL creatinine rise is part of a KDIGO-style AKI definition [K2], and that randomized BP-target evidence does not license interpreting an observational MAP association as a pressor benefit or harm [K3]. The unresolved claim is narrower: an incremental, hospital-transportable duration signal beyond the recorded observation and treatment process. A positive result would justify external validation and clinical adjudication; it would not establish renal hypoperfusion, a MAP threshold, mechanism, or benefit from raising BP.

## New artifact-separation design

The key change is to make process diagnostics co-primary design checks rather than optional ablations.

1. Observation-process endpoint. From lab, using the same frozen creatinine vocabulary but without requiring a rise, define (a) any valid creatinine result in 360–2880 and (b) minutes from 360 to the first such result. Fit the same B0/B1 feature blocks to this endpoint using an observation-process loss and censoring rule. This endpoint is not a clinical outcome and is not assumed independent of hypotension: a strong duration association is evidence that clinical concern/measurement intensity may create ascertainment. Report the joint pattern rather than treating it as proof of bias. Use inverse-probability-of-observation weights estimated in training hospitals from baseline severity, early BP coverage/modality, labs, recorded treatment, and early discharge/EOL process features; never use post-index creatinine values or post-index EOL fields to construct predictors.

2. Pre-index negative-control process check. Preserve the parent’s frozen pre-index check: among stays with a valid creatinine in -48–-24 and a distinct result in -24–0, use the same 0.3-mg/dL rise rule. A stronger association than the post-index association, or a comparable association among an outcome window that precedes the exposure window, is adverse evidence for baseline severity/measurement process. It is not a causal negative-control proof.

3. Common-support residualized duration. Define nuisance blocks before fitting:
   - S: age, sex, ethnicity, hospital/ICU type, baseline creatinine, APACHE admission meanbp/creatinine where available, initial MAP/HR/respiration, and pre-index lab coverage.
   - O: covered-bin count, median gap, modality/missingness, BP measurement timestamps, and the observation-process model outputs.
   - T: conservative early infusionDrug indicators/timing and recorded rate/amount summaries only after a frozen drug-name vocabulary and field-unit audit. If that audit fails, mark treatment unknown and do not invent dose conversions.
   - E: the duration/nadir/episode features.

   In each training fold, fit a nuisance model for E from S+O and another from S+O+T; apply it once to held-out hospitals to obtain residual duration. Restrict comparisons to prespecified common-support strata defined by nadir band, covered count, baseline-creatinine band, initial vital bands, modality/missingness, and S/O/T propensity overlap. Do not tune strata, thresholds, or outcome windows after looking at held-out results. Compare B0 against (i) raw E, (ii) E residualized on S+O, and (iii) E residualized on S+O+T. This is an incremental prognostic comparison, not a causal effect. A gain that survives S+O but disappears only after T is compatible with treatment coupling or recorded severity; a gain that survives S+O+T is stronger evidence for an independently useful temporal summary, while still not mechanistic.

4. EOL leakage guard. carePlanEOL.cpleolsaveoffset and cpleoldiscussionoffset are observational process markers. Only events with offset <=360 may enter index predictors, and activeupondischarge is an end-of-stay sensitivity/process descriptor, never a primary predictor. Post-index EOL rows may be reported to characterize censoring but cannot be used to predict the creatinine outcome. This explicitly corrects a possible future-information leak in the parent’s broad EOL sensitivity wording.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive pattern: the creatinine vocabulary/scale audit passes; B1 improves B0 on held-out hospitals with uncertainty excluding no improvement; calibration is stable; duration-risk is monotone within fixed-nadir/common-support strata; the gain persists after S+O and S+O+T residualization, common-density thinning, and observation weighting; duration does not show a comparable pre-index negative-control association; and M reproduces trajectory information across hospital holdout. This supports an independently useful prognostic duration summary. It does not establish mechanism, causality, a BP target, or treatment benefit.

Adverse pattern: label readiness fails; the observation endpoint is strongly predicted by duration and the AKI gain vanishes after observation weighting; the pre-index control is as strong as the primary association; the gain is confined to low-density or one hospital; common support is poor; duration disappears after recorded-treatment adjustment; or M learns only masks/treatment and not reproducible BP trajectory. These favor ascertainment, treatment-response, selection, or severity explanations and argue against a robust duration signal.

Inconclusive pattern: wide intervals, few qualifying creatinine events, unresolved laboratory scale, incomplete follow-up, exposure-dependent censoring, too few hospitals, unstable support strata, or ambiguous pressor vocabulary. These do not refute H1. They require clinical laboratory adjudication, better urine/dialysis and treatment semantics, or external validation.

An automatic verifier can check source hashes, exact joins, time-window separation, EOL leakage guard, feature construction, split integrity, nuisance fitting without leakage, held-out metrics, uncertainty, and whether reported claims match outputs. It cannot adjudicate creatinine units/vocabulary, BP modality semantics, urine/dialysis meaning, EOL intent, residual unmeasured severity, mechanism, causal treatment effects, or clinical benefit. Those require expert review or another study.

## Limits

The dataset lacks clinician-adjudicated AKI, reliable pre-ICU kidney function, verified laboratory units, complete fluid balance, calibrated raw BP waveforms, validated renal-replacement intent, staffing/bed context, and independent external validation. Even a positive, process-robust result remains a transportable prognostic association requiring clinical adjudication and external validation.
