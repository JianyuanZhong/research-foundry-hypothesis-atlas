# Successor proposal: calibrated late-lactate review value at the 24-hour pressor landmark

## Hypothesis and clinical decision

In adults with a valid early lactate and a valid repeat lactate at a prespecified 24-hour landmark after early norepinephrine/Levophed exposure, adding the repeat lactate to a time-indexed laboratory and cardiorespiratory model will yield at least **+1 net true-positive-equivalent mortality-review decision per 100 people** over fixed review-risk thresholds from 0.30 through 0.50, **after honest probability recalibration**, while both the comparator and augmented models satisfy a prespecified calibration guardrail in hospitals not used for their prediction.

The clinical question is not whether lactate is associated with death. It is whether a repeat lactate can change a mortality-review prioritization decision enough to matter when the displayed probabilities are reliable. A score with a positive net-benefit point estimate but a calibration slope of 0.589 cannot safely be interpreted at probability thresholds. This successor therefore makes calibration a deployment requirement rather than a favorable secondary diagnostic.

The parent established that the uncalibrated R1 point increment was +1.0097/100, but its copied-hospital refit 95% interval was -0.2906 to +2.0575/100 and its held-out calibration slope was 0.58921. Thus the strongest supported claim is only that the locked uncalibrated model had favorable point discrimination/proper-score diagnostics and a positive point decision-curve contrast in this EICU cohort. The unresolved claim is conditional and falsifiable: **does the incremental review value remain materially positive when R0 and R1 probabilities are generated and calibrated as they would be for a new hospital?** This is a substantive advance over repeating the favorable point estimate or expanding the model after seeing the failed guardrail.

Recent public work has explored dynamic lactate-derived prognostic associations and external validation, but prognostic association or an AUC gain does not establish calibrated incremental utility at a locked action threshold. This experiment targets that missing decision-facing claim. It remains observational and cannot establish that ordering lactate, reviewing a patient, or acting on a score improves survival.

## Estimand, population, and temporal rules

The primary estimand is the hospital-population-average incremental integrated net benefit:

`Delta-INB = 100 * integral_{p=0.30}^{0.50} [NB(p; calibrated R1) - NB(p; calibrated R0)] dp`

where the integral is the trapezoidal average over the prespecified grid p = 0.30, 0.31, ..., 0.50, and

`NB(p) = TP/N - FP/N * p/(1-p)`.

The unit is one selected person; N is the complete eligible evaluation population. The outcome is hospital mortality, defined from `patient.hospitaldischargestatus` as the frozen parent mapping of known death/expired versus alive outcomes. The estimand is predictive decision-curve utility under this explicit false-positive-to-true-positive exchange rate, not clinical utility measured from clinicians or patients.

Use the complete EICU snapshot `[source checksum]`, without source-row sampling. Include age >=18, mapping literal `> 89` and numeric ages >89 to 90. Define t0 as the earliest qualifying norepinephrine/Levophed infusion entry in ICU minutes [0, 1440]. Select one stay per `uniquepid` using lexicographically smallest (t0, `patientunitstayid`). Require known hospital discharge status and `hospitaldischargeoffset >= 1440`.

Define L24 = t0 + 1,440 minutes. Require a valid baseline lactate B in [t0-360, t0], with `labresultrevisedoffset <= t0+60`, and choose the latest valid collection; require B >= 2 mmol/L. Require a valid follow-up lactate F in [t0+720, L24], with `labresultrevisedoffset <= L24`; choose the collection nearest t0+1080, then the earlier collection, then smallest `labid`. For both lactates, validity is numeric 0.2-30 mmol/L, the frozen lactate name/system mapping, and the parent's revision/conflict rule: at a patient/marker/collection offset retain only the greatest revision; conflicting values at tied greatest revision invalidate that collection; identical ties collapse to smallest `labid`.

Keep the parent's outcome-blind hospital eligibility rule: a hospital is eligible only when it has at least five people in each historical X category, X = 1[(B-F)/B < 0.20]; X is a selection audit variable, not a predictor, stratifier, subgroup, or reported treatment effect. The intended complete flow is the audited parent flow (1,229 people, 507 deaths, 722 survivors, 33 hospitals); recompute it and fail as Not computable rather than silently changing the cohort if it differs.

- t0/1,440 and its square;
- baseline lactate lag, ln(B), and ln(B) squared;

- last, median, minimum, maximum, resolved-timestamp count, and missingness for heart rate, respiration, and SpO2 in [L24-360, L24].

R1 contains all R0 predictors and adds only ln(F) and ln(F) squared. Do not add clearance, F/B, X, hospital, diagnoses, treatments, notes, post-L24 data, or any new laboratory/vital marker. Do not optimize thresholds, feature transformations, regularization, or model complexity after looking at results.

Resolve laboratory rows collection-bounded to [t0, L24] and revision-bounded to <=L24 using the frozen resolution rules. Resolve VitalPeriodic independently by stream at patient/timestamp and retain only heart rate 20-250/min, respiration 2-80/min, and SpO2 40-100%, with observations collection-bounded to [L24-360, L24]. The vital source is five-minute summary observations; it has no entry, validation, or display timestamp.

Fit each base model as unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1, `lbfgs`, max_iter=10,000, tol=1e-8, no class weighting, seed 20260961. Within every fit, winsorize observed continuous features at the training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize using training mean and population SD, and mean-impute to zero after scaling. Held-out hospitals use only constants learned in the corresponding training fit.

## Honest calibration and hospital-held-out evaluation

For each outer hospital h:

1. Exclude all rows from h. On the remaining hospitals, fit the exact R0 and R1 base models under the rules above.
2. Within this outer training set, produce inner cross-fitted logits separately for R0 and R1: for each inner hospital g, fit the relevant base model on outer-training hospitals excluding g, using only that fit's preprocessing constants, and predict g. Every outer-training person therefore receives a prediction from a model that did not use that person's outcome or hospital.
3. Fit a two-parameter logistic recalibration separately for R0 and R1 using only those inner cross-fitted predictions: logit(Pcal) = a + b * logit(Pbase). Use ordinary logistic likelihood with intercept and slope, no additional covariates or regularization; if numerical separation occurs, mark the replicate Not computable rather than alter the estimator.
4. Refit the exact R0 and R1 base models on all outer-training hospitals, apply the corresponding learned (a,b) to h's base logits, and retain one calibrated probability per person in h. Never estimate calibration parameters using h.
5. Pool the 33 outer-hospital predictions for the primary contrast. The same procedure, including inner calibration and preprocessing, is repeated in every copied-hospital bootstrap replicate.

Calibration is assessed on the pooled outer-held-out probabilities, not on the inner calibration predictions. The prespecified guardrail for each model is calibration slope 0.80-1.25 and calibration-in-the-large (intercept) -0.05 to +0.05 when regressing outcome on the held-out model logit. Report the point estimates and 95% copied-hospital intervals for both; the primary supportive label requires both R0 and R1 point estimates to satisfy the guardrail and no guardrail's 95% interval to be wholly outside its acceptable range. This makes a calibration result compatible with uncertainty rather than treating recalibration as proof of calibration.

Report, but do not use as a replacement primary endpoint, AUROC, Brier score, log loss, calibration plots/tables by hospital, calibration slope/intercept, mean absolute probability change, threshold-specific Delta-NB, and all 33 leave-one-hospital-out Delta-NB values. Maintain the parent's requirement that every outer hospital has both outcome classes and the largest hospital contributes <15%; report exact coverage and discarded counts.

## Ordered interpretation and falsification criteria

Apply this hierarchy without rescue by AUROC, proper scores, point estimates, or site deletions:

1. **Not computable:** any source/hash mismatch; altered cohort flow; missing required columns/keys; unresolved duplicate/conflict behavior; a time-boundary violation; outcome-class/site-size failure; incomplete held-out predictions; a nonfinite/nonconverged required fit; or fewer than 2,000 valid bootstrap replicates. Do not downgrade this to an apparent scientific result.
2. **Supportive:** Not computable is false; both calibrated models pass the calibration guardrail; all 33 site-deletion Delta-INB values are finite and >= -0.50/100; and the Delta-INB 95% lower bound is >= +1.00/100. This supports the narrow, calibrated incremental review-value hypothesis in this EICU snapshot.
3. **Opposite:** Not computable is false and the Delta-INB 95% upper bound is < 0/100. This falsifies incremental value in the direction of the hypothesis, even if point diagnostics elsewhere look favorable.
4. **Adverse to materiality:** Not computable is false, the Delta-INB interval is wholly nonnegative but its upper bound is < +1.00/100, and both models pass calibration. This supports at most a nonnegative but clinically submaterial increment under the locked utility.
5. **Inconclusive:** all other computable cases, including an interval spanning zero or +1, a calibration guardrail failure, a calibration interval that is wholly outside the acceptable range, discordant stability, or a calibration-validity/value mismatch. A favorable point estimate cannot override this label.

The interval and calibration outcomes are the falsification targets. A negative upper-bounded increment falsifies the material-value direction; a positive point estimate with a lower bound below +1 fails to establish materiality; and a failed held-out calibration guardrail prevents a deployment-value claim. Calibration itself is not evidence of causal benefit.

## Clinical evidence limits and required next study

The source supports structured measurements, ICU offsets, discharge status, and hospital clustering. It does not establish that a pressor row proves administration or indication, that a lab revision offset equals specimen/display availability, that VitalPeriodic values were available to a clinician at L24, or that the mortality label has an adjudicated cause/time. It contains no observed review action, clinician utility weights, harms, treatment assignment, counterfactual outcome, or patient-centered benefit. Public narrative note sections are removed; retained structured note fields are not assumed genuine narrative, and no text is used here.

Accordingly, a supportive result means only that a time-safe, honestly calibrated prediction rule has a robust modeled review-value increment under the explicit NB utility in this EICU snapshot. It does not mean ordering lactate helps, that review decisions change care, or that mortality falls. Before clinical use, require expert adjudication of the pressor/lactate availability and intended decision, prospective synchronized timestamps, validation in a genuinely independent cohort, and an impact study with clinician/patient elicitation of false-positive and false-negative costs. The configured reserved partition is not accessible and is explicitly not an independent external validation set.
