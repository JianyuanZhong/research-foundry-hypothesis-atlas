# When does laboratory observation history support a transportable ICU alert?

Status: substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prediction, measurement-process, and decision-analytic interpretation study. It is not a causal effect of laboratory ordering, a hospital quality ranking, a clinical utility trial, or evidence that changing testing improves outcomes.

## Real weakness repaired

The parent rigorously estimates incremental whole-hospital predictive information and uses conditional process permutation to distinguish patient-linked process information from workflow fingerprinting. Its fixed-alert-burden table, however, does not state what pattern would make the process signal clinically interpretable: a small Brier improvement can be irrelevant at any plausible alert threshold, can be confined to a low-risk stratum, or can reflect a site workflow that will not transport. The repair adds one prespecified, support-gated decision-analytic interpretation at the same fixed ICU landmark. It does not change the population, temporal boundaries, outcome, transport estimand, or primary model comparison.

## Scientific question and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported before fitting is feasibility only. An outcome-blind audit attached to the parent found 135,680 eligible adult first stays, 2,470 recorded ICU-death events by 1,800 minutes, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows, and 111,655 episodes with at least one eligible landmark-known laboratory row. These counts support a whole-hospital experiment; they do not show that testing history predicts death, improves an alert decision, represents clinician intent, or benefits patients.

The unresolved claim is whether a patient’s laboratory observation process adds patient-specific information that is both transportable and potentially useful for prioritizing a risk alert after measured state and simple availability have been accounted for. The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In adult first ICU stays with a fixed six-hour ICU-relative landmark, patient-level laboratory timing and intensity will improve zero-shot prediction of recorded ICU death by 1,800 minutes beyond state and simple availability in held-out hospitals, and the improvement will yield positive incremental decision-curve net benefit at prespecified alert thresholds in at least one adequately supported baseline-risk stratum. That incremental net benefit should attenuate toward zero under within-hospital, baseline-risk-stratified process permutation and should not be reproduced by site descriptors alone. If it persists after permutation or occurs only through site descriptors, the signal is better interpreted as a workflow fingerprint than patient-specific actionable information.

“Potentially useful” here means favorable expected utility under an explicitly stated false-alert cost ratio. It does not mean that an intervention exists, that the alert changes treatment, or that clinical benefit has been established.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly freeze and report:

1. A leakage-safe first-stay cohort, analyte/unit/interface/revision audit, feature dictionary, outcome flow, and five-fold whole-hospital fold manifest.
2. Matched state-plus-availability (B0) and state-plus-availability-plus-process (B1) feature matrices, with every imputation, scaling, categorical mapping, and spline decision fit within development hospitals.
3. Out-of-fold competing-risk predictions for recorded ICU death and alive ICU discharge, zero-shot for held-out hospitals.
4. The primary paired macro-hospital difference in integrated Brier score, calibration-in-the-large/slope, and uncertainty; patient-weighted results are secondary.
5. A prespecified decision-analytic output: per-hospital and macro-hospital net benefit for B0 and B1 at risk thresholds 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, and 0.10, plus alerts per 100 stays and incremental net benefit, overall and within fixed B0-risk strata.
6. A process-permutation null, common-panel and revision/unit sensitivity results, and a site-descriptor diagnostic.
7. An interpretation trace linking every supportive, adverse, or inconclusive statement to computed output files.

Completion is a paired transportability and decision-interpretation report, not a fitted clinical conclusion. A solver must not silently replace the decision analysis with AUROC optimization.

## Population, landmark, and outcome

Use the frozen eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`.

Include the first adult ICU stay per person:

- `age >= 18`, treating values greater than 89 as 90;
- numeric `unitdischargeoffset > 360`;
- smallest valid `unitvisitnumber` per `uniquepid`, ties broken by `patientunitstayid`;
- retain only stays with enough observation time for the six-hour landmark.

The primary predictor window is ICU-relative `[0,360]` minutes. This is the fixed landmark and the only primary window. A separately labeled sensitivity may use `[max(hospitaladmitoffset,-360),360]` with pre-ICU features kept distinct; it must not be pooled with the primary analysis.

A lab row is eligible for landmark-known numeric features when `labresultoffset` is in `[0,360]`, `labresult` parses as numeric, and `labresultrevisedoffset <= 360` or is null under a prespecified, reported null-revision convention. Conflicting units and unresolved system/interface mappings are excluded from numeric state features but retained in the audit. No result or revision after 360 minutes may enter a primary feature.

The primary event is the first `patient.unitdischargestatus` normalized exactly to `Expired` with `unitdischargeoffset` in `(360,1800]`. The estimand is the cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death by 1,800 ICU-relative minutes. Alive ICU discharge before 1,800 is a competing event; an ICU stay still present at 1,800 without recorded death is administratively censored at that horizon. A secondary cause-specific hourly analysis must preserve the competing discharge risk. This is not all-hospital mortality, post-ICU mortality, latent death, or a causal testing outcome.

## Baseline, alternative, and equal-input comparison

Freeze cohort, codebook, windows, event definitions, transforms, folds, and support gates before fitting.

B0 is a transparent elastic-net pooled discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly rows from 360 through 1,800 and two event heads for recorded death and alive discharge. It uses the patient-table demographics/admission context, the eligible laboratory value summaries and threshold flags, and simple analyte availability. A value-only model without availability is a lower-bound sensitivity.

B1 is the matched transparent process-aware model on exactly the same episode-hour rows, outcomes, folds, and state/availability inputs, adding only patient-level laboratory timing and intensity. Prespecified nonlinear splines for counts/recency are allowed only when the codebook and support audit pass.

The substantive temporal alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins spanning `[0,360]`, using exactly the same retained lab value, availability, count, elapsed-time, interface, and revision channels available to B1. It has competing outcome heads for recorded death and alive discharge, with an optional next-bin-observation auxiliary head used only to characterize temporal information. Its scientific role is to test whether ordering/co-movement/abrupt change carries information that B1’s summaries lose; it is not justified by model complexity or small discrimination gains. Report it only if feature support, convergence, calibration, and the same held-out-hospital evaluation pass. If it is deferred, preserve that decision and the failed readiness criterion.

All models use five grouped outer folds, each holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once. Within each outer training partition, split hospitals approximately 75%/25% for fitting, regularization, codebook decisions, and temporal early stopping. Stratify hospitals by eligible and event counts where possible; keep all stays of a `uniquepid` together. A patient-random split is secondary only.

## Decision-analytic and state-stratified interpretation

The primary clinical-interpretation output is a hypothetical alert policy, not a treatment policy. For each held-out episode, use the model’s cumulative incidence at 1,800 minutes. At threshold `q`, alert when predicted risk is at least `q`. Use `q in {0.01,0.02,0.05,0.10}`; each threshold corresponds to an explicit false-alert/false-negative exchange `q/(1-q)). Report net benefit per 100 stays as:

`100 * [TP/N - FP/N * q/(1-q)]`

where an event is recorded ICU death by 1,800 minutes and alive ICU discharge before 1,800 is treated as a non-event for this hypothetical alert utility. Also report observed alerts per 100 stays, event capture, and calibration within the same horizon. The competing-risk cumulative-incidence and Brier analyses remain primary safeguards; net benefit must never be computed from a cause-specific hazard as if discharge were independent censoring.

Define three fixed baseline-risk strata using out-of-fold B0 risk: low `<0.01`, intermediate `0.01-<0.05`, and high `>=0.05`. The thresholds and strata are fixed before reading B1 performance. For each threshold and stratum, estimate the paired B1-minus-B0 net-benefit difference within each held-out hospital, then summarize macro-hospital and patient-weighted effects with hospital-bootstrap or hierarchical intervals. Use a simultaneous 95% hospital-clustered interval across the prespecified 12 stratum-threshold cells, rather than selecting a favorable cell after fitting. A stratum-threshold cell is interpretable only if it has at least 200 held-out stays, 30 recorded-death events in the aggregate, and at least 10 stays in at least 80% of contributing hospitals; otherwise mark it unsupported, not negative.

The decision layer is supportive only when: (a) B1 has a directionally favorable, uncertainty-quantified macro-hospital Brier/calibration result; (b) at least one supported prespecified stratum-threshold cell has positive incremental net benefit with its interval excluding zero; (c) the process-permutation decrement is present in the same cell or cells; and (d) the result is not reproduced by the site-only diagnostic, and B1 does not materially worsen calibration relative to B0 (report calibration-in-the-large, slope, and their hospital-clustered uncertainty). Apply the same prespecified threshold grid and simultaneous interval rule to the decision outputs. This means the process signal is a candidate for prospective alert evaluation in a defined state range, not that alerting or testing improves care.

Interpretation is adverse to patient-specific actionability when B1 has no transportable calibration/Brier improvement, incremental net benefit is consistently non-positive in supported cells, or the apparent benefit survives process permutation and is reproduced by hospital descriptors (with raw `hospitalid` restricted to the secondary patient-random diagnostic). A gain limited to low-risk patients, a single hospital, or an unsupported cell is a workflow or sampling signal, not an actionable clinical finding. Results are inconclusive when support gates fail, intervals span a clinically meaningful utility range, or B1 and the temporal alternative disagree without adequate calibration.

The alert analysis must include sensitivity to the threshold grid and to treating alive discharge as a competing non-event; no utility number is presented as a clinical recommendation. Because eICU lacks intervention assignment, clinician intent, alert burden harms, treatment response, patient preferences, and prospective outcomes, even a supportive result cannot establish net clinical benefit. Those claims require expert review, an intervention-defined target, external validation, and a prospective impact study.

## Falsification and uncertainty

1. Within each held-out hospital and B0-risk stratum, permute the complete patient-level process vector across episodes while preserving the hospital and stratum distribution. Refit or score according to a predeclared computationally valid null procedure. The observed B1 increment and net-benefit increment should attenuate. A surviving increment indicates residual state/site structure or leakage, not evidence for actionability.
2. Restrict to a common analyte/unit/interface panel and repeat the primary and decision outputs.
3. Exclude revision-sensitive rows and repeat the revision-known convention.
4. Compare primary patient-level models with a site-descriptor-only diagnostic using only `numbedscategory`, `teachingstatus`, and `region` from the hospital table; do not fit raw `hospitalid` as a predictor in a held-out-hospital fold because the held-out category is unseen. A raw-hospital-ID model is permitted only as a clearly labeled patient-random-split optimism diagnostic. These are diagnostics, not competitors for clinical deployment. A site-descriptor signal that matches B1 is evidence of workflow fingerprinting.
5. Report revision/unit/interface support, missingness, hospital-level event support, calibration-in-the-large and slope, macro-hospital and patient-weighted integrated Brier, cause-specific Brier, discrimination only secondarily, fixed-threshold alerts, net benefit, and hospital-clustered uncertainty. All reported conclusions must point to computed output files.

A correct computation with no supported incremental net benefit is a valuable negative result. No computed clinical conclusion is claimed here.

## Compute, method selection, and limits

The source/header/schema audit and the parent’s cohort-support counts are measured. Full model runtime, convergence, and temporal-model calibration on this snapshot are unverified. B0/B1 are expected to be CPU-feasible with approximately 4-8 CPUs and 16-32 GiB RAM; these are planning estimates, not measurements. The temporal alternative may request one allocated A100 with approximately 16-32 GiB host RAM, but GPU necessity and runtime are unverified. Ordinary shell CUDA absence is not evidence against feasibility; any GPU probe or fitting must use `job_submit` with an explicit allocation and `cuda:0` inside it. The configured discovery budget is separate from the future solver envelope.

The transparent B1 is selected as the primary alternative because it directly answers whether patient-linked process features add transportable and potentially useful information while retaining auditability. The GRU/temporal-convolution alternative is retained because it can reveal order, co-movement, and abrupt temporal transitions lost by summaries; it is deferred if support or calibration fails. A full causal model of test ordering, target-trial emulation, natural-language processing, waveform modeling, external validation, and prospective utility analysis are deferred because the local files do not identify treatment intent, intervention effects, or external clinical benefit. These alternatives should be revisited only with the missing evidence or a new study.
