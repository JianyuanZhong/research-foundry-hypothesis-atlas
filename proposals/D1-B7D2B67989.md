# Strict prior assay content and 30-day recorded return admission after an HCC anchor

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. The incumbent established a careful question about strictly prior laboratory content versus capture intensity for an immediate seven-state procedure/discharge transition. This child preserves that question and its strict temporal policy, but asks whether the information survives at a later, clinically consequential care-continuity endpoint: a subsequent local admission within 30 days after the index admission's discharge.

The newly fitted/estimated deliverable is:

1. a frozen adult first-observed record-coded HCC cohort with an administrative follow-up ledger, strict prior-event ledger, patient split, multiplicity/key/time audit and exact return-admission labels;
2. locked predictions for admission-only A, capture/process-only Q, assay-content E, and matched continuous-time S and S_Q models;
3. the paired held-out E-minus-Q increment in calibrated binary log loss and Brier score for 30-day return admission, with patient-clustered uncertainty;
4. S-minus-E and assay-free S_Q-minus-Q contrasts, calibration and decision-threshold summaries;
5. the incumbent 72-hour native procedure/discharge transition as a prespecified secondary endpoint;
6. strict-availability, timestamp-only, anchor-linked, calendar-era, source-inclusion and planned-event-unresolvable sensitivities, plus permutation falsifications and a claim-to-evidence table.

No model has been fitted here, and no clinical conclusion is claimed.

### Hypothesis

> Among eligible adults at their first locally observed HCC-coded inpatient encounter, strictly attributable prior laboratory assay content (identity, qualitative/numeric result and within-assay trajectory) improves held-out calibrated prediction of a subsequent local recorded admission within 30 days after index discharge beyond the same admission context and strictly measured prior capture/process intensity.

The incumbent’s evidence supports only that native encounter/event times and source payload can define a reproducible local recorded-care history and immediate recorded transition. The unresolved claim is that lab payload contains information about later recorded return admission that is not explained by how intensively the patient was captured and documented. A supportive result would identify a potentially actionable measurement gap for post-discharge risk stratification; it would not establish physiology, causality or utility.

This endpoint is deliberately called “subsequent recorded admission,” not unplanned readmission. The data do not provide admission type, emergency status, planned treatment scheduling, death, outside-hospital care or a validated readmission-cause field.

## Population, temporal boundaries and labels

Four otherwise eligible anchors were administratively excluded in the bounded audit because their discharge was too late. Define the primary outcome on the half-open window [td, td + 30 days): 1 if any other encounter for the same patient has parseable native `Admission Time` in that window; 0 if none. Require the candidate return encounter’s admission to be >= td, so overlapping index rows are not called returns. Use the earliest qualifying return for time-to-return summaries. If multiple encounters share the same admission timestamp, retain a deterministic `Visit Number` tie order and report the ambiguity. There is no death censoring or outside-system follow-up; a zero means no qualifying local row in the observable window.

## Strict prior history and feature information sets

Primary strict history is [t0 - 730 days, t0). A row is eligible only when its normalized two-column key matches an encounter for the same patient, the linked encounter is not the anchor, that encounter has parseable admission and discharge with linked discharge < t0, and the source-specific availability time is parseable and in the half-open history window. Use:

- labs: `Test time`;
- procedures: `Start time`;
- orders: `Order Time`, never a later start time to move a row into history;
- medications: `Start time`;
- examinations: `start time`.

For orders/medications, start/end are only secondary duration descriptors after the strict availability test. Missing or invalid availability excludes a row from primary features but is counted by source and linked encounter. Rows linked to the anchor are excluded even if their event time precedes t0. Record anchor-linked, open/overlapping, missing-discharge, missing-event-time and post-t0 exclusions. This is “strictly attributable to a completed prior encounter,” not proof that a result was visible at the time; the files have no audit-log or result-release timestamps. Timestamp-only and anchor-linked history are prespecified sensitivities.

All models use identical people, labels, strict ledger, patient split and fit-only preprocessing:

- A: age, sex, department, calendar era, admission time-of-day and admission missingness.
- Q: A plus prior completed-encounter count/duration, active days, time since last completed encounter, source-specific row counts and distinct linked-encounter counts for labs, procedures, orders, medications and examinations, observed source-type count, missing/excluded-time counts, left-truncation and no-history indicators. Q contains no assay identity/value/result and no names, doses, routes, frequency or status.
- E: Q plus lab `Test` identity, `Qualitative Result`, valid assay-specific `Quantitative Result`, `Specimen Type`, recency, span, count, missingness and within-assay change/slope only when at least two distinct valid `Test Time` values exist. The catalog has no lab unit column; do not pool numeric values across assays or construct ALBI/MELD-like scores.
- H: transparent all-history sensitivity adding assay-specific summaries and prior occurrence/recency/gap summaries for named source types. Names are descriptive tokens, not treatment meanings.
- S: a continuous-time latent event model over exactly the E ledger. Event inputs are coarse source/type, relative availability time, payload mask, and for labs assay identity plus assay-specific payload; non-lab names are excluded. Latent coordinates decay with elapsed time; assay emissions and missingness are modeled; a supervised binary head predicts the return label. Fix latent dimension, decay family, masking, regularization and seed count before locked evaluation and tune only in selection data.
- S_Q: identical architecture and rows with lab assay identity and payload removed, retaining source/time/masks. It is the process-only control.

The simple baseline is regularized logistic regression for Q and E, with the same fit-only preprocessing and nested feature blocks. The substantive alternative is S, not because complexity is intrinsically valuable, but because it can expose irregular timing, nonlinear assay interactions, informative missingness and trajectories discarded by counts/first-last summaries. Keep S only if its calibrated increment is stable and clinically interpretable at the level of recorded return risk; a small discrimination gain alone is not a scientific advance.

## Split, analysis and falsification

Use the frozen participant split SHA-256(normalized `patient master index`) modulo 100: buckets 0–59 fit, 60–69 preprocessing/hyperparameter/fit-only calibration selection, 70–79 locked evaluation, 80–99 inaccessible. All rows for a patient remain in one bucket. Freeze cohort, follow-up label, strict ledger, feature manifest, model dimensions and calibration before locked labels/predictions.

Primary held-out outcomes are paired patient-clustered changes in binary log loss and Brier score, score(E) - score(Q), lower better. Also report Q-A, H-A, S-E and S_Q-Q; calibration intercept/slope, reliability, observed/predicted risk, threshold-specific sensitivity/PPV/NPV with confidence intervals, and time-to-first-return distributions. The primary uncertainty is >=1,000 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples of locked predictions, preserving all admissions per sampled patient. AUROC/ AUPRC are secondary descriptive measures, not the decision criterion. Report results by no-history, Q-intensity, assay-support, 30/180/730-day history and calendar-era strata; these are internal heterogeneity diagnostics, not external validation.

Prespecified falsifications and audits:

1. shuffle complete strict histories across patients within each partition, preserving Q; E-Q should attenuate;
2. permute lab payload within assay and partition, preserving identity, times and counts; a content increment should attenuate;
3. permute assay identity while preserving values, times and counts; assay-specific signal should attenuate;
4. shuffle event times within patient/source while preserving payload/counts; time-order signal should attenuate;
5. compare strict, timestamp-only and anchor-linked ledgers; a large reversal makes the availability interpretation inconclusive;
6. fit S_Q and Q; process-only reproduction of E-Q or S-E rejects the content interpretation;
7. test sentinels for any row at/after t0, anchor-linked row, post-discharge row, outcome-derived feature, duplicate-patient split leakage and follow-up beyond the snapshot;
8. repeat exact-boundary, same-time, overlap, missing-time and return-tie rules;
9. permute labels within calendar era and department; persistent performance suggests leakage or label artifact;
10. for the secondary incumbent endpoint, audit procedure times before admission/after discharge, minute-00 concentration, missing timing and discharge/procedure tie precedence.

Supportive evidence requires a negative 95% patient-clustered interval for E-Q log loss and/or Brier without material calibration deterioration, attenuation under payload/history permutations, no time/follow-up sentinel, and no complete reproduction by Q or S_Q. It supports incremental information in strictly attributable prior assay content for this local 30-day recorded-return distribution. A stable S-E gain may support information in irregular trajectories beyond transparent summaries, but not a biological mechanism.

Adverse evidence is no E-Q improvement, calibration harm, process-only reproduction, persistence after payload permutation, strict-to-timestamp reversal, or split/label leakage. Inconclusive evidence includes a dominant unresolved/missing-time burden, sparse assay families, wide intervals, unstable calendar/source strata, substantial left truncation, ambiguous overlapping returns, or insufficient support after administrative censoring. If return admissions are mostly demonstrably scheduled after expert review (not available here), the endpoint’s care-continuity interpretation must be narrowed.

## Clinical evidence limits and alternative branches

A Europe PMC abstract search returned Yang et al. (2025), DOI `10.1186/s13040-025-00479-0`, an analogous two-center decompensated-cirrhosis early-readmission prediction study. The abstract supports that early return admission is a clinically studied care-continuity problem and that external validation matters; it does not establish this HCC cohort’s mechanism, prevalence or transportability. Only the search abstract was inspected, not the full paper. The local HCC snapshot cannot establish active HCC timing, cirrhosis, liver reserve, stage, tumor burden, treatment indication, planned versus unplanned status, admission cause, death, outside care, treatment receipt/completion, benefit, safety, clinical utility or external validity. The research-ambition cancer demonstration’s main article and complete STAR Methods remain unavailable locally; no reproduction claim is made. The available demonstrations motivate dated-history and longitudinal modeling, not a fixed method choice.

The actual scientific decision is whether assay payload adds reproducible information beyond a more complete capture-intensity comparator for a later recorded return event. A negative result is scientifically useful: it would indicate that the incumbent’s assay increment does not extend to this later endpoint or is indistinguishable from observation intensity. A positive result is still only a measurement/information finding. Clinical deployment, causal interpretation, planned/unplanned adjudication, disease-specific attribution, prospective validation and intervention require expert review, additional fields or another study.
