# Is early ICU hypotension duration an independent prognostic signal for 72-hour AKI after accounting for vasopressors, baseline kidney function, severity, and recording density?

Status: working proposal; no cohort analysis or model fitting has been executed.

## Question, clinical importance, and unresolved claim

Among adults with at least one usable blood-pressure observation during the first 24 ICU hours, does the proportion of observed 30-minute intervals that are hypotensive retain a positive association with KDIGO creatinine AKI during the first 72 ICU hours after adjustment for early vasopressor exposure, baseline creatinine, illness severity, nadir pressure, and recording density?

The question matters because a duration summary could be clinically useful only if it conveys information beyond how low pressure fell, how sick the patient was, how much vasopressor was required, and how often the patient was monitored. If the association disappears after these adjustments, it is more consistent with a severity or monitoring artifact than with an incremental prognostic signal. If it persists, the result supports prognostic information, not a causal effect or treatment target.

The strongest claim supported by the inspected eICU schema is that longitudinal periodic and aperiodic blood pressures, vasopressor infusion times and rates, creatinine results, APACHE severity fields, and hospital identifiers are available and can be aligned to ICU admission using `patientunitstayid` and offset fields. The unresolved claim is whether the 24-hour duration estimand remains prognostic after conditioning on the strongest available proxies for severity, treatment intensity, baseline kidney function, and recording density.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use one ICU admission per patient: select the first `patientunitstayid` in `patient.csv.gz` for each `uniquepid`, ordered by `unitadmittime24` where available. Require an adult stay, at least one physiologically plausible systolic or MAP observation in the first 24 hours, and at least one creatinine result by hour 24.

Define ICU admission as the time origin. Use `observationoffset` and `labresultoffset` as minutes from admission. Exclude stays with no usable pressure in hours 0–24, no creatinine by hour 24, or creatinine AKI already established before hour 24. Report all exclusion counts and sensitivity analyses for the first-admission rule.

## Exposure and outcome

Use a fixed 24-hour exposure window, hours 0–24. Hypotension is systolic pressure <90 mmHg or mean arterial pressure (MAP) <65 mmHg. Use `vitalPeriodic.systemicsystolic` and `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` together with `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivesystolic` and `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean`; retain only values within prespecified physiologic ranges and do not silently impute missing monitor values.

The primary estimand is the fraction of observed 30-minute intervals in hours 0–24 with at least one hypotensive pressure. A 30-minute interval is counted as hypotensive only when an observed value is hypotensive; intervals without a pressure observation are not counted as normotensive. Report observed interval count, coverage, and the fraction of the 24-hour window with any pressure observation. A secondary estimand is the time-weighted mean pressure deficit below 90 mmHg using the same interval convention. Minimum systolic and MAP are calculated over the same window and serve as baseline exposures.

The primary outcome is KDIGO stage 1–3 creatinine AKI during hours 0–72. Use creatinine from `lab.csv.gz`, mapped by `labname`, `labresult`, `labresultoffset`, `labmeasurenamesystem`, and `labmeasurenameinterface`. Define baseline creatinine as the lowest creatinine observed before hour 24, or admission creatinine when no earlier value exists. AKI is creatinine at least 2 times baseline with absolute creatinine at least 1.2 mg/dL; report stage 2 and stage 3 separately.

Censor at death, ICU discharge/transfer, or 72 hours, whichever occurs first. Death and discharge are secondary outcomes. Missing creatinine after hour 24 is not treated as AKI or non-AKI; report censoring and missingness by hospital. Use a sensitivity analysis requiring a creatinine result after hour 24 for the primary outcome, and report the primary result with explicit uncertainty.

## Rival explanations and adjustment strategy

The strongest rival is that longer hypotension is a proxy for greater illness severity, more intensive vasopressor support, or more frequent monitoring. A sicker patient may have both a lower nadir and longer low-pressure periods; a more monitored patient has more opportunities for a low pressure to be recorded.

The primary adjusted analysis conditions on:

- age, gender, ethnicity, APACHE II components available in `apacheApsVar`, APACHE score and predicted mortality in `apachePatientResult`, and admission diagnoses in `admissionDx`;
- early vasopressor exposure, defined from `infusionDrug.csv.gz` using `patientunitstayid`, `drugname`, `startoffset`, `endoffset`, and `rate`; restrict to prespecified vasopressor drug names and summarize as hours infused, cumulative rate-time exposure, and maximum rate during hours 0–24;
- baseline creatinine and baseline creatinine trajectory, using the lowest creatinine before hour 24 and the change from admission creatinine to that baseline when both exist;
- nadir systolic and MAP; and
- a prespecified recording-density measure: number of pressure observations and coverage in the first 24 hours.

The primary comparison is within similar nadir-pressure strata, with prespecified interactions for duration by nadir and duration by recording density. The analysis reports the unadjusted association, the association after severity adjustment, and the association after the full adjustment set. The key clinical interpretation is the change in the duration effect across these stages, not a claim that adjustment removes all confounding.

## Falsification and negative-control tests

The primary falsification test is a negative-control outcome measured before the exposure window: the change in creatinine from admission to the first creatinine result before hour 24, or, when that is unavailable, the admission-to-hour-24 creatinine change. Later hypotension should not cause an earlier creatinine change. A strong association between duration and this pre-exposure outcome indicates residual severity or recording-density confounding and weakens the prognostic interpretation.

A second falsification test uses a prespecified pressure-derived negative-control exposure that should not be a direct proxy for renal risk: the number of non-hypotensive pressure observations in hours 0–24, after conditioning on total pressure-observation count and coverage. If duration appears associated with AKI only when the total number of observations is high, or if the association is explained by observation count rather than hypotensive interval count, the result is more consistent with recording density.

A third sensitivity analysis restricts to patients with at least one vasopressor infusion in hours 0–24 and repeats the full adjustment. If the duration association is present only in the vasopressor-exposed subgroup and is explained by vasopressor rate-time exposure, the claim of an independent duration effect is weakened. Conversely, a stable association in both exposed and unexposed strata would strengthen, but still not prove, prognostic independence.

The null hypothesis is that the adjusted association between duration and 72-hour AKI is null or reverses, and that any apparent association is attenuated by recording density or explained by the pre-exposure negative-control outcome. The hypothesis is adverse if the association disappears after adjustment, reverses, or is explained by recording density. It is inconclusive if confidence intervals are wide, coverage is too sparse, or hospital performance is unstable.

## Baseline versus learned alternative

Baseline: a prespecified severity-adjusted logistic model for AKI using age, gender, ethnicity, APACHE II components, admission diagnosis, vasopressor use, baseline creatinine, nadir systolic/MAP, and recording density. This model is transparent and establishes whether duration has any incremental signal.

Alternative: a time-aware gradient-boosted model fitted on the same 24-hour exposure and covariates, with a monotonic constraint on the duration variable and hospital fixed effects or hospital-stratified fitting. The learned model can reveal nonlinear duration-nadir interactions and patient-specific patterns that a linear baseline loses, while the monotonic constraint prevents an implausible protective interpretation of longer hypotension. It is not a mechanistic causal model.

Both models use the same whole-hospital holdout, bootstrap confidence intervals, calibration-in-the-large/separation, AUROC, and decision-curve net benefit at clinically relevant thresholds. The baseline is preferred if it captures the duration signal adequately and is more interpretable; the alternative is preferred only if it materially improves held-out calibration or reveals a clinically plausible nonlinear interaction, not merely a small AUROC gain.

## Validation and interpretation

Use whole-hospital holdout: fit on all but one hospital, predict the held-out hospital, repeat across prespecified hospitals, and report calibration and uncertainty rather than treating one split as definitive. Hospital identifiers come from `hospital.csv.gz` and `patient.hospitalid`.

The hypothesis is supported if duration remains associated with AKI after nadir, vasopressor, baseline-creatinine, severity, and recording-density adjustment, with a positive monotonic trend and stable held-out-hospital calibration. It is adverse if the association disappears after adjustment, reverses, or is explained by recording density. It is inconclusive if confidence intervals are wide, coverage is too sparse, or hospital performance is unstable. A positive result supports prognostic information, not a treatment target or causal effect.
