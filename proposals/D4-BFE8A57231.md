# UKB 40 adaptation: glycemic worsening with weight loss as an early pancreatic-cancer signal

## Scientific opening

The UKB 40 seed asks whether similar HbA1c worsening accompanied by weight loss predicts later pancreatic cancer more strongly than weight gain. This is clinically important because pancreatic cancer is often diagnosed late, and a reproducible pre-diagnostic metabolic pattern could justify earlier imaging or specialist review. However, the seed's claim is not yet identifiable: weight loss may reflect intentional dieting, diabetes treatment, another malignancy, or early pancreatic cancer itself. The unresolved question is whether the excess risk is concentrated in a prespecified short window and persists after accounting for medication and pre-existing cancer, rather than merely describing people who were already ill.

## Hypothesis and strongest rival

**Hypothesis:** Among UKB participants with valid HbA1c and weight at two assessment visits and no prior pancreatic cancer, a prespecified increase in HbA1c accompanied by weight loss (rather than weight gain) is associated with a higher hazard of pancreatic cancer diagnosed within 2 years after the second visit, compared with similar glycemic worsening accompanied by weight gain.

**Strongest rival:** The apparent association is driven by treatment or pre-existing illness. Diabetes treatment, intentional weight loss, another malignancy, or pancreatic cancer already present at the second visit could alter both metabolic measurements and cancer diagnosis. The design therefore uses a prespecified origin at the second visit, excludes prior pancreatic cancer, and performs treatment and prior-nonpancreatic-cancer sensitivity analyses.

## Evidence and claim scope

The inspected literature supports the clinical plausibility of pancreatic cancer-associated weight loss and glycemic changes [K1], but does not establish a causal or pre-diagnostic signal in this population [K2]. The UKB catalog provides exact repeated metabolic measurements, medication codes, cancer diagnosis dates, and assessment dates [K3]. The current evidence supports a hypothesis worth testing, not a confirmed clinical biomarker.

## Data bindings

The analysis uses the UKB/ukb672073 split tables from the completed subset. Required fields include:

- 30750 Glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c)
- 30740 Glucose
- 21002 Weight

- 40005 Date of cancer diagnosis
- 40006 Type of cancer: ICD10
- 41270 Diagnoses - ICD10
- 41280 Date of first in-patient diagnosis - ICD10
- 53 Date of attending assessment centre

The primary population requires two valid HbA1c and weight measurements, a prespecified minimum interval between visits, and no pancreatic cancer diagnosis before the second visit. The primary endpoint is pancreatic cancer diagnosed within 2 years after the second visit. Follow-up begins at the second visit.

## Analysis and falsification

The baseline analysis is a Cox proportional hazards model with an interaction term for glycemic worsening by weight change. The learned alternative is a survival model trained on the same covariates and evaluated with time-dependent AUC and calibration. The primary comparison is the Cox interaction estimate and its confidence interval.

The hypothesis is supported if the interaction is positive, the confidence interval excludes the null, and the association is stronger in the early window. It is falsified if the association is absent, reversed, or disappears after treatment and prior-cancer sensitivity analyses. An inconclusive result is declared if event support is insufficient or the confidence interval is too wide.

## Method comparison

The Cox model is the baseline because it is interpretable and directly estimates the interaction. The learned survival alternative is included to test whether nonlinear or high-dimensional patterns improve prediction, but it is not a substitute for the prespecified interaction test. If the learned model improves prediction without changing the direction of the interaction, the result is descriptive rather than mechanistic.
