# Does remote pre-TACE2 history add prediction beyond the current pre-repeat state?

## Substantive child and decision-time advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves that parent's frozen HCC population, pathway arms, repeat-event clock, assay selectors, exact source bindings, audited complete-triplet support, and noncausal evidence limits. It reverses the parent's nesting.

The parent asked whether the contemporaneous pre-repeat assay value `P` improves prediction of the next observed same-assay value `Y` beyond the remote pre-TACE2 baseline `B`. That comparison will often be won by `P` through ordinary serial autocorrelation and therefore has limited incremental value at the repeat-TACE decision, when `P` is already available. The clinically sharper unresolved question is:

> Among patients with the frozen complete repeat-TACE triplet, does remote pre-TACE2 assay history add reproducible prediction of the next observed same-assay value beyond the contemporaneous pre-repeat value and covariates already available immediately before the repeat procedure?

The primary nested comparison is therefore `current state` versus `current state + remote history`. A positive result would support retaining longitudinal assay history in a future decision-time prognostic tool; a precise null would support the narrower conclusion that, for this observed short-horizon laboratory target, the latest assay state captures essentially all reproducible information available from the single remote anchor. Neither result establishes toxicity, recovery, procedural effect, safety, treatment benefit, or clinical utility.

No outcome value, sign, model result, or pairwise candidate comparison was inspected or used in this design. Feasibility is inherited from the parent's direction-blinded complete-source audit and was checked against the current HCC catalog/schema. The inherited aggregate audit found approximately 158 triplets per assay: albumin 40 systemic-record plus 118 comparator (`n=158`), total bilirubin 41 plus 119 (`n=160`), and a strict common-timestamp joint sensitivity of 40 plus 118 (`n=158`). These are availability facts, not outcome evidence.

## Claim ladder

### Strongest claim already supported

In HCC snapshot `[source checksum]`, an exhaustive, value-blinded reconstruction supports all of the following frozen facts:

- 319 systemic-record and 1,491 comparator patients satisfy the inherited common-TACE2 pathway construction.
- The first strict repeat TACE on TACE2 calendar days 15–90 occurs in 159 and 526 patients, respectively.
- Under the locked primary assay-specific selectors, complete `B,P,Y` triplets exist for albumin in 40/118 and for total bilirubin in 41/119 systemic/comparator patients.
- The strict common-timestamp joint triplet contains 40/118 patients.
- The seven inherited timing selectors have already been audited without using laboratory values to choose a favorable selector.

These facts establish computational feasibility only. They do not show that `B`, `P`, a trajectory, a pathway record, or repeat TACE predicts or causes any subsequent laboratory value.

### Unresolved falsifiable claim

For each assay separately, adding the remote value `B` to a model that already contains the current value `P` and locked pre-event covariates improves genuinely out-of-sample prediction of `Y` by at least the prespecified minimum predictive-relevance benchmark and survives timing, linkage-placebo, selection, influence, and optimism diagnostics.

The benchmark is a cross-validated `R²` increment of 0.02, equivalently a 2% reduction in the base model's out-of-fold mean squared prediction error. This is a statistical design convention, not a validated clinical minimum important difference. Because the source has no laboratory unit column and no adjudicated decision threshold, even a result above 0.02 cannot by itself be called clinically important.

### Claims not tested here

This experiment does not test whether repeat TACE caused `Y`, whether a medication was administered, whether the systemic-record pathway modifies an effect, whether a patient recovered or experienced toxicity, or whether adding history improves a clinical decision or outcome. Those stronger claims require verified units/exposures, adjudication, and another study.

## Decision-time predictors and the reversed nested models

Construct predictor values strictly before the repeat-event time. The same predictor matrix, preprocessing, folds, and held-out patients are used for both models.

The locked compact covariate vector `Z` contains only variables available before the repeat event and derivable from the verified bindings:

- frozen pathway indicator `A` (systemic record=1, comparator=0), used as context rather than as a treatment effect;
- age and sex at TACE2;
- TACE2 calendar year plus sine and cosine of calendar month;
- elapsed TACE1-to-TACE2 calendar days;
- elapsed TACE2-to-repeat-event calendar days;
- counts of recorded encounters, procedures, and strict TACE patient-days strictly before the repeat-event time;
- repeat-event encounter department, with category levels learned in each training fold and levels represented by fewer than 10 training patients collapsed to `other`;
- hours from `P` to the repeat-event clock;
- days from `B` to TACE2, included in both models as an observation-recency covariate without revealing the `B` result.

No outcome-dependent SMD screen or feature selection is allowed. No post-event laboratory, medication, procedure, discharge, complication, or outcome-timing variable enters either model. Missing continuous covariates are median-imputed within the training fold and accompanied by a missingness indicator; categorical missingness is an explicit level. Continuous centering/scaling and department-level collapsing are learned in the training fold and applied unchanged to its holdout fold. If age, sex, procedure clocks, or either assay value required by the triplet is unavailable, the row is not repaired from notes; reconstruction/count failure is reported under the relevant gate.

For each assay `a`, fit the following genuinely nested models:

```text
M_current: Y_a = f(P_a, Z) + error
M_history: Y_a = f(P_a, B_a, Z) + error
```

The primary `f` is a linear ridge regression with an unpenalized intercept. All non-intercept columns are standardized within training data. Select ridge `lambda` separately for each model, using only the outer-training fold, by stratified four-fold inner cross-validation over the fixed grid `{0, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100}`; ties choose the larger `lambda`. The loss is mean squared error. This supplies stable prediction with approximately 158 observations without permitting a flexible learner to substitute for the scientific question.

A locked ordinary least-squares compact sensitivity omits department indicators and missingness indicators that are constant in the training set and reports the partial `B` coefficient with HC3 uncertainty. A locked nonlinear sensitivity adds `P²` to both models and `B²` only to `M_history`, after training-fold standardization. Neither sensitivity can replace the ridge primary result.

`D=P−B` is a trajectory display and an exactly equivalent linear reparameterization of the history addition when `P` is already in the model: `Y~P+B+Z` and `Y~P+D+Z` span the same linear predictor space. Therefore this proposal does not conduct or count `B` and `D` as two independent tests, compare them pairwise, or claim that one is a separate trajectory discovery. Report `D`, the elapsed `B→P` time, and the per-day slope `D/elapsed_days` descriptively. The only primary hypothesis is the incremental predictive contribution of the remote value/history block beyond current `P`.

The pathway-by-history interaction is not a primary or confirmatory target. It may be emitted descriptively with a wide interval, but the approximately 40 systemic triplets are not used to support a treatment-pathway modification claim.

## Cross-fitting and performance estimand

Use 20 deterministic repetitions of five-fold outer cross-fitting, stratified by frozen arm. Generate folds from a documented SHA-256 hash of the internal patient key, assay, repetition number 0–19, and fixed string `remote-history-v1`; patient identifiers never leave the computation. Each patient appears in exactly one holdout fold per repetition. All preprocessing, category collapsing, and ridge selection occur inside each training fold.

For each patient, average the 20 held-out predictions from a model before calculating aggregate performance. Report arm-specific fold counts but pool arms for the primary prediction estimand. The principal metric is

```text
Delta_R2_CV = (MSE_current − MSE_history) / MSE_current,
```

where both MSEs use the same patient-level averaged out-of-fold predictions. Positive values favor remote history. Also report paired changes in standardized RMSE (`RMSE/SD(Y)`), standardized MAE (`MAE/SD(Y)`), median absolute error, and calibration intercept/slope for each model. Report every repetition's performance and the number/proportion favoring history, so an average cannot conceal fold instability. Native-database-scale errors are shown with the explicit missing-unit caveat; assays are never pooled on raw scales.

### Deterministic uncertainty

Use a stratified patient-level nonparametric bootstrap with exactly 1,000 replicates and seed derived from SHA-256(`remote-history-bootstrap-v1`, assay). Resample patients with replacement within arm, keep all records from a sampled patient together, reconstruct 10 deterministic repeated five-fold outer splits within each bootstrap replicate, and rerun all training-fold preprocessing and inner ridge selection. Bootstrap duplicates must remain in one fold to prevent train/test leakage. Use percentile 2.5th and 97.5th quantiles for the 95% interval of `Delta_R2_CV` and paired error changes; report the bootstrap median and failure count. The point estimate remains the original 20-repeat estimate. If fewer than 950 replicates complete, uncertainty fails and the result is inconclusive. This interval quantifies sampling/model-fitting uncertainty in the selected observed cohort, not uncertainty due to unmeasured selection, missing units, or outside care.

As an analytic check, report a corrected repeated-cross-validation standard error across the 20 repetition-level paired loss differences. Disagreement in sign between the bootstrap and analytic intervals is an instability flag, not an invitation to select the preferred interval.

## Minimum-support and computation gates

All gates are fixed before outcome inspection.

1. **Reconstruction gate:** exact frozen arm/event/triplet counts, clocks, assay labels, selectors, and joins must reproduce. Any unexplained discrepancy blocks primary inference; do not widen a window or choose another event.
2. **Outer-fold support:** in every primary five-fold split, each held-out fold must contain at least 8 systemic and 20 comparator patients, each corresponding training fold at least 30 systemic and 90 comparator patients, and both training and held-out `Y` variance must be nonzero. For locked timing variants, each held-out fold must contain at least 5 systemic and 15 comparator observations, total support at least 25 systemic and 100 combined, and nonzero `B/P/Y` variance. Failure makes that analysis non-estimable.
3. **Predictor support:** no nonconstant training-fold column may have fewer than 10 observations in its less frequent binary/category level; rare department levels are collapsed by the locked rule. A ridge fit, prediction vector, and selected `lambda` must exist for every fold. OLS sensitivity requires training observations per estimated coefficient of at least 8 and full rank; otherwise OLS is marked unavailable without affecting the ridge computation.
4. **Bootstrap support:** at least 950/1,000 full-pipeline bootstrap replicates must complete, and no bootstrap estimate may be substituted after inspecting its value.
5. **Influence:** report leave-one-patient-out changes in `Delta_R2_CV` using deterministic five-fold predictions. A supportive claim requires no deletion to change the sign and no single deletion to change `Delta_R2_CV` by more than 0.01 or 50% of its full magnitude, whichever is larger. Also report the five largest changes. Failure is inconclusive/influence-dependent.
6. **Optimism/stability:** a supportive claim requires at least 16/20 repetition-level `Delta_R2_CV` estimates to be positive and the interquartile range of those estimates not to cross zero. Calibration slope for `M_history` must lie in `[0.75,1.25]` or be closer to 1 than `M_current` without its 95% interval indicating gross miscalibration.

These gates are feasibility conventions, not guarantees of external validity or clinical usefulness.

## Timing, placebo, linkage, and selection diagnostics

### 1. Locked selector stability

Run the identical nested analysis under all seven inherited timing selectors and the strict common-timestamp sample. At least six of seven timing variants must be estimable. For supportive primary interpretation, at least `ceil(0.75m)` of the `m` estimable variants must have positive `Delta_R2_CV`; each of those concordant variants must be within 0.03 absolute `Delta_R2_CV` of the primary estimate and, when the primary estimate is at least 0.01, within a magnitude ratio of 0.5–2.0. Reversal, dependence on one selector, or failure of the common-timestamp sensitivity is timing instability. No variant may be promoted.

Report performance by quartiles of `P` lead time, `B` recency, event day, and actual event-to-`Y` lag. These are diagnostics only; actual `Y` lag is not a decision-time predictor.

### 2. Patient-linkage permutation placebo

Within each assay and arm, permute `B` across patients 1,000 times with a fixed SHA-256-derived seed, while keeping `P`, `Y`, `Z`, folds, and the marginal `B` distribution fixed. Rerun the complete nested prediction comparison. This destroys person-specific historical linkage while retaining arm and measured current context. A supportive result requires the observed `Delta_R2_CV` to exceed the 95th percentile of permutation values. Failure is adverse to the claim that a patient's own remote history, rather than model flexibility or chance, adds prediction. This diagnostic is not treated as a causal randomization test.

### 3. Pre-event pseudo-next placebo

When the repeat-event encounter has an additional same-assay value strictly before `P`, choose the latest such value at least 6 hours before `P` and within 168 hours before the true event as pseudo-current `R`; set the true `P` as pseudo-next outcome. Compare `P~R+Z` with `P~R+B+Z` using the same pipeline, replacing event-lead timing appropriately. Do not use a post-event value. Run this only if at least 100 total and 25 systemic pseudo-triplets satisfy all fold gates; otherwise label it not estimable.

If the pre-event placebo gain is within 0.01 of, or larger than, the true post-event history gain, the result may still demonstrate generic serial-history prediction but cannot be described as timing-specific to the next post-repeat panel. A comparable placebo gain is adverse to any event-specific interpretation, not proof that the predictive computation is wrong.

### 4. Regression-to-the-mean and misspecification checks

Because current `P` remains in both models, the primary increment does not ask whether a raw change score predicts another coupled change score. Report `B`, `P`, and `Y` distributions; correlations; `D=P−B`; and performance by quintiles of `P` and `B`. If apparent gain is confined to extreme `P` or disappears in the locked quadratic sensitivity, classify it as model-form/regression-to-the-mean sensitive. Do not infer deterioration or recovery from the sign of `D`.

### 5. Repeat-event and triplet-observation selection

Using all 1,810 frozen-arm patients, fit outcome-blinded, five-fold cross-fitted models for (a) having the first strict repeat event and (b) among repeat events, having the assay-specific complete triplet. Predictors are restricted to variables fixed by TACE2 or recorded before the repeat-event/panel selection, never `Y` or a post-event variable. Report calibration, arm-specific inclusion, probability distributions, covariate balance, and reasons for deterministic exclusion.

Unweighted complete-triplet prediction is primary because it directly matches the observed target population. As a diagnostic sensitivity only, construct the inherited repeat-event/observation product weights, normalize them to mean 1, and repeat cross-fitted prediction. Weighted interpretation requires systemic ESS at least 30, combined ESS at least 120, at least 95% of component probabilities in `[0.05,0.95]`, none outside `[0.01,0.99]`, maximum normalized weight at most 10, top 5% carrying at most 25% of total weight, and no patient carrying more than 5%. A supportive result additionally requires weighted and unweighted `Delta_R2_CV` to share sign and differ by no more than 0.02. Failure means selection-sensitive/inconclusive.

Weighting cannot recover unobserved outcomes or identify all-frozen-cohort performance without an unverifiable missing-at-random assumption. Passing diagnostics does not remove collider bias from conditioning on repeat TACE and panel observation.

### 6. Post-panel context

Describe later medication and procedure records only as context. Never adjust for a post-`Y` record. Preserve the parent's locked sensitivity excluding repeat events with a matched non-bevacizumab systemic record between TACE2 and repeat TACE. It cannot redefine the primary population.

## Prespecified interpretations

Interpret each assay separately. Albumin and bilirubin may disagree; do not pool raw values, require a common biological direction, or select the favorable assay.

### Supportive remote-history prediction

Call the result supportive only if all of the following hold:

- primary `Delta_R2_CV` point estimate is at least 0.02;
- its 95% full-pipeline bootstrap interval excludes 0 on the positive side;
- at least 16/20 repeated-cross-validation estimates are positive;
- calibration, fold support, bootstrap, influence, timing-selector, linkage-permutation, and weighted/unweighted stability gates pass;
- no locked nonlinear sensitivity reverses the result.

Conclude only: in this assay's selected, observed repeat-TACE triplets, the person's remote pre-TACE2 value adds reproducible short-horizon prediction of the next observed same-assay value beyond the current pre-repeat value and available pre-event context. This supports further evaluation of longitudinal history as a prognostic feature. It does not establish an acute procedure effect, a mechanism, clinical usefulness, or a treatment recommendation. If the pre-event pseudo-next gain is comparable, explicitly narrow the conclusion to generic serial-history prediction rather than repeat-event timing specificity.

### Adverse to the hypothesis

Classify the result as adverse when the upper 95% interval is below 0 (history reproducibly worsens out-of-sample prediction), the patient-linkage placebo is not beaten, the apparent gain reverses under most locked selectors or the quadratic current-state model, or performance is driven by a few patients. Conclude that the data do not support stable incremental prediction from the person's remote anchor and that an apparent in-sample history association is compatible with chance, overfitting, misspecification, regression to the mean, or observation structure. Do not conclude that history is biologically irrelevant.

### Precise null for a material predictive increment

Classify as a precise null when every support, calibration, selection, timing, bootstrap, and influence gate passes but the upper 95% interval for `Delta_R2_CV` is below 0.02 and the lower bound is not below −0.02. Conclude that this design excludes a predictive gain as large as the prespecified 2% benchmark in the selected observed cohort. This is a null for a statistical predictive benchmark, not clinical equivalence and not absence of hepatic change. If the interval lies entirely below zero, use the adverse branch instead.

### Small but precise positive gain

If the interval excludes zero but the point estimate is below 0.02 or its upper bound is below 0.02, report a reproducible but smaller-than-benchmark increment. Do not relabel it supportive or clinically useful.

### Selection- or timing-sensitive

If weighting changes sign or magnitude beyond the locked bound, ESS/positivity/weight gates fail, only one selector supports gain, or the pseudo-next placebo is comparable, state exactly which component is sensitive. A generic pre-event history signal may remain, but an event-specific or transportable interpretation is unsupported.

### Inconclusive

Classify as inconclusive if reconstruction counts fail, fewer triplets than audited are available without explanation, folds or variants fail support, outcome/predictor variance is zero, fewer than 950 bootstraps complete, intervals span both meaningful gain and harm, weights are unstable, influence is concentrated, unit/duplicate handling cannot be validated, or required diagnostics are non-estimable in a way that prevents the relevant claim. Preserve the negative or unavailable result. Do not widen windows, substitute a later event, add assays, or choose a favorable model.

## Evidence limits and additional data required

The target is the next *observed* same-assay result in a highly selected set of recorded repeat-TACE encounters, not a latent physiologic state. The HCC source lacks a laboratory unit field, analyzer/reference-range metadata, validated inpatient/outpatient specimen context, adjudicated acute illness, exogenous albumin/transfusion/fluid data, imaging and tumor burden/response, performance status, complete Child-Pugh components, repeat-TACE rationale and technical details, verified medication administration/adherence/dose, procedural complications, outside-care capture, and reliable mortality. Diagnoses have no native timestamp. The day-14 observation rule is not proof of survival. Conditioning on repeat TACE and complete panels can induce collider bias through disease status, clinician decisions, access, surveillance, and survival.

Clinical experts must adjudicate units, assay comparability, technical success, concomitant care, retreatment appropriateness, complications, and whether any prediction difference is actionable. A future clinical utility study would need verified decision-time covariates and exposures, prospectively fixed prediction outputs, clinically meaningful outcomes, thresholds and decision consequences, external validation, and comparison with clinician judgment. Causal treatment or policy claims require a separate longitudinal target-trial design with time-varying severity and decision covariates and clinically adjudicated outcomes.

An automatic verifier can check source hashes, exact bindings, frozen counts, clocks, selectors, parsing/duplicates, fold construction, leakage prevention, nested model fitting, ridge tuning, OOF predictions, bootstrap/permutation calculations, support/selection/timing gates, and whether the reported branch follows computed outputs. It cannot establish clinical importance, assay units, administration, toxicity, recovery, safety, mechanism, benefit, or why repeat TACE occurred.
