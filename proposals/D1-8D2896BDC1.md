# Episode 4 child: day-42 repeat-TACE risk, separated assay state from observation and retreatment process

Status: proposed delivery child of [prior hypothesis]. No model has been fitted and no clinical result is claimed.

## Scientific deliverable and decision boundary

The deliverable is a newly fitted, leakage-audited comparison of (1) transparent pooled-logistic discrete-time hazards and (2) a compact time-aware sequence model, each evaluated on the same day-42 landmark estimand with four pre-specified feature channels. Completion requires frozen cohort and timing audits, patient-level temporal split, fold-fitted censoring and assay-observation weights, held-out hazards for every model/channel combination and control, uncertainty intervals, calibration, falsification outputs, and a conclusion table that links each claim to an output. Readiness or a planned model is not a result.

Among patients with a first dated locally recorded exact-code TACE who are observable at day 42 and have no exact-code repeat before the landmark, does the recorded assay trajectory available by day 42 add held-out information about a later operational exact-code repeat TACE beyond pre-index history and two explicitly separated post-index processes: measurement opportunity and care/retreatment proxies?

This is a prediction and boundary-testing question, not a treatment-effect or mechanism question. The operational repeat-TACE endpoint may reflect planned retreatment, clinician selection, access, current severity, or measurement opportunity. A state increment that survives process adjustment is evidence for incremental recorded-assay information only; it is not evidence of tumour response, progression, TACE benefit, or a biological pathway.

## Evidence, opening, and unresolved rival

The closest inspected predictive work is a retrospective three-centre CT/radiomics/deep-learning study of sequential TACE, with internal and external testing but a small selected cohort and lower external AUCs [K1]. It establishes that richer, treatment-adjacent information can be predictive in a selected repeat-TACE setting; it does not establish that routine longitudinal assays add value in an EHR population or that a repeat TACE is radiologic response.

Radiologic treatment response in HCC requires imaging concepts such as viable enhancement/necrosis and criteria including mRECIST or RECICL [K2]. The HCC sources here do not provide adjudicated imaging response, treatment intent, assay units, or reference ranges. A real-world TACE imaging study demonstrates how recorded response can remain sensitive to missing outcome ascertainment and treatment-selection confounding [K3]. Those works bound the claim: this study tests an operational care event and observation/process explanations, not response.

The leading explanation is that assay trajectories contain information about a patient's recorded state that remains useful after measured care opportunity is accounted for. The strongest rival is that the apparent state signal is a timing/selection artifact: patients selected for assays, examinations, orders, or a planned retreatment pathway both have more measured values and are more likely to receive an exact-code repeat. A second rival is current severity that is incompletely represented by the baseline history. These explanations differ in whether an assay increment remains after disjoint measurement and care-process channels, after cross-fitted assay-observation transport weighting, and in lead-time/horizon controls. None of those comparisons identifies biology.

## Assays and disjoint state/process channels

The labs source is `[internal dataset path]`, table `labs`, with exact columns `患者主索引`, `就诊号`, `检验`, `定性结果`, `定量结果`, `标本类型`, `检验时间`. The audited target labels and row/patient counts are: 甲胎蛋白 (190,022/42,304), 白蛋白 (238,293/42,490), 前白蛋白 (184,917/38,444), 总胆红素 (238,553/42,490), 直接胆红素 (238,334/42,491), 丙氨酸氨基转移酶 (308,671/43,271), 血小板 (74,121/19,152), and 血小板计数 (253,588/42,932). There is no units or reference-range column and `标本类型` is blank in nearly all audited target rows. Keep exact labels separate, use only ordinary numeric `定量结果` in the numeric state channel, preserve inequality/censoring and qualitative flags separately, and never impose clinical thresholds or call these values liver function/tumour burden. Audit label-by-calendar distributions before fitting; material scale drift makes the state comparison inconclusive. All scaling and imputation are fold-fitted.

Define four disjoint feature sets, with every feature available by the stated cutoff and no `obs_end`, future event, future count, or file-order field:

- M0: pre-index age, sex, department, encounter/admission history, diagnosis dictionary counts via matched encounter, procedure counts, and exact-assay summaries from [-365,0). All longitudinal counts use valid timestamps before index and patient-level joins use `患者主索引`; visit-level assignments additionally use `就诊号`.
- S (recorded state): target-assay numeric values and qualitative/inequality result flags in (0,42] plus assay-specific changes from a valid pre-index observation in [-365,0). S has no missingness or assay-request indicator. Missing numeric state is imputed within fold by a pre-registered baseline/median rule, so missingness cannot masquerade as physiology.
- P_obs (measurement opportunity): dated encounter, examination, medication/order and lab-observation counts, gaps, assay-label request/result counts, and availability/missingness indicators in (0,42]. It has no assay values, qualitative results, or state changes. An assay row contributes only its existence/timing here.
- P_care (care/retreatment process): non-TACE procedure tokens, non-drug-order tokens, medication starts/ends, department/visit process marks and their times in (0,42], excluding exact TACE rows and all assay values. This channel is deliberately retained as a competing explanation because it can encode planned retreatment, scheduling, access, or clinician selection; it is not interpreted as a confounder that has been causally removed.

For orders, use `开立时间` as the earliest documented availability of an order; a `开始时间` or `结束时间` contributes only when that timestamp itself is no later than the feature cutoff. For encounters, exams, medications, labs, and procedures use their documented event timestamp. This prevents a later effective order time from being treated as knowledge available at day 42. Do not include exact-code TACE after the index in any predictor; early repeat is an eligibility exclusion.

The primary comparison is M0, M0+S, M0+P_obs, M0+P_care, M0+P_obs+P_care, M0+P_obs+P_care+S. A recorded-state increment is evaluated both over M0 and over the complete process channels. The latter is the decisive descriptive comparison, while the former shows total incremental utility. If a state increment disappears only after P_care, planned care-process explanation is favored.

To address assay opportunity rather than merely adjust for it, fit a cross-fitted observation model for the probability of at least one valid target-assay value by day 42 using M0 and P_obs/P_care summaries available before that time. Apply stabilized, fold-fitted observation weights to the S-containing analyses, with a pre-registered positivity/weight-tail audit and no extrapolation when support is absent. This is a transport/sensitivity analysis, not an identification of biology. If the effective sample size collapses or positivity is unsupported, the state conclusion is inconclusive.

## Models, fitting target, split, and uncertainty

The baseline is a transparent elastic-net pooled-logistic discrete-time hazard. Create person-day rows for days 43 through 180, with an event indicator at the first exact repeat and an at-risk/censoring mask. Include fixed day-bin indicators or a low-degree time spline and exactly one feature set at a time. Fit all transformations, label dictionaries, imputation, elastic-net penalty, censoring model, and observation model inside five grouped development folds. The primary estimand is held-out IPCW prediction of the exact operational repeat hazard under recorded follow-up. IPCW censoring weights are estimated fold-wise from information available before each risk interval, truncated only at a frozen development-fold tail rule; report the rule, support, effective sample size, weighted log loss, weighted Brier at day 180, integrated Brier, calibration slope/intercept, and secondary AUROC/AUPRC.

The learned alternative is a compact time-aware gated recurrent model (GRU with elapsed-time decay, or an equivalent gated sequence implementation fixed before test scoring) over the same patient history from -365 through +42. Each event carries event category, exact assay label where applicable, numeric value only after the label/unit audit, qualitative/inequality flags, relative time, time since prior event, and an explicit channel tag S/P_obs/P_care/M0. It produces the same day-specific hazard and uses the same IPCW target, split, channel ablations, metrics, and uncertainty procedure. Freeze maximum events per patient, truncation rule, hidden size, dropout, optimizer, stopping rule, and three seeds in development; no test-based seed selection. The sequence model may reveal transient trajectories, irregular timing, and state-process interactions that window summaries in the baseline lose. It does not make those interactions causal.

Use one frozen patient-level calendar split: earliest 80% of eligible index dates for development and latest 20% for untouched test, with boundary frozen before outcome evaluation. No patient may occur in both partitions. Within development, use five grouped folds for every preprocessing, dictionary decision, weight, hyperparameter, and control. A patient-hash random split is a prespecified transport sensitivity only. Bootstrap the untouched test predictions at the patient level, preserving each patient's person-days and censoring weights; report 95% intervals and paired differences for S over M0 and over full process, separately for baseline and learned model. Do not repeatedly peek at the test set.

## Falsification and ambiguity checks

Run all controls with the same frozen split and folds:

1. Permute response-window timestamps within patient while preserving assay/result multisets and process counts.
2. Shuffle same-assay numeric values across patients within development-fold strata of calendar block, P_obs counts, and baseline severity, preserving timing/count structure.
3. Permute P_obs and P_care marks across development patients within calendar and event-count strata without moving assay values.
4. Fit capacity-matched P_obs-only and P_care-only models; compare their held-out increments and calibration with S.
5. Permute outcome labels in development and score against untouched true-label test outcomes as a leakage sanity check.
6. Repeat the full channel comparison using only process records available by day 28, while retaining the day-42 landmark, and report prespecified horizons 43-90 and 91-180. A state increment that appears only after late P_care and only in the immediate 43-90 horizon is considered compatible with planned-retreatment timing; persistence into 91-180 after early-process adjustment is more consistent with durable recorded information but still not biology.
7. Re-run without patients with any undated strict TACE history, with alternate all-source observation definitions, and with exact-code-only versus a separately frozen high-specificity alias sensitivity. Do not merge aliases after seeing results.

A state increment that is reproduced by timestamp/value permutations, matched process controls, or missingness/opportunity features is a process explanation. A process increment with no state increment is an adverse result for the state-triggered direction but a useful care-process finding. A large difference between ordinary and observation-weighted S is evidence of assay-selection sensitivity, not proof of a mechanism.

## Results criteria and evidence limits

Supportive results require at least 50 observed later repeat events as a precision screen; completed timing, all-source observation, exact-label/calendar-scale, and leakage audits; supported censoring and assay-observation weights; a held-out S increment over M0 and over P_obs+P_care in weighted log loss or Brier with an uncertainty interval excluding no improvement; acceptable calibration; stable direction in temporal and random-split checks, day-28/late-process sensitivity, undated-history sensitivity, and observation definitions; and no comparable gain in the permutations or capacity-matched process controls. This supports only incremental recorded-assay information for an operational repeat-TACE event. The next study is expert imaging/intent linkage, not a treatment rule.

Adverse results include no S increment after complete process adjustment, an increment matched by P_care/P_obs or permutations, poor calibration, unsupported weights, or instability under timing/coding sensitivities. With adequate precision, abandon a state-triggered validation direction and prioritize treatment-intent and care-process measurement. Inconclusive results include fewer than 50 events, too few day-42 landmarks, assay label/scale drift, severe censoring or observation-weight instability, wide uncertainty, or an incomplete learned fit. An imprecise null is not biological refutation.

No output establishes response to TACE, progression, benefit or harm from TACE, treatment necessity, causal effects of assays, or a mechanism. Expert adjudication requires imaging with viable enhancement/necrosis using mRECIST/RECICL or related standards, treatment intent and scheduling, clinically validated assay units/reference ranges, death/outside-care/transplant ascertainment, and preferably an independent temporal or external cohort. The closest inspected learned/radiomics work itself shows external performance degradation and small-cohort robustness limits [K1]; this proposal therefore treats the learned model as a secondary scientific comparison, not a merit bonus.

## Alternatives and selection rationale

The baseline and learned alternative are deliberately comparable: same M0/S/P_obs/P_care inputs, same day-42 cutoff, same target, split, IPCW, observation weights, metrics, bootstrap, and controls. The baseline is selected as the primary report because its hazard, censoring, channel ablations, and calibration are inspectable. The time-aware GRU is retained because irregular timing and transient state/process interactions are scientifically relevant to the rival explanation and may be lost by summaries. It is deferred if the approved solver envelope cannot support a pre-specified three-seed fit; transparent models and all controls remain mandatory and no question is changed.

Approximate future-solver budget: baseline and audits use 8-16 CPUs and 64-128 GiB, usually CPU-suitable; chunked source materialization is checkpointed. The learned fit requests one allocated A100 (use `cuda:0` inside the allocation), 8 CPUs, 64 GiB, and at most 28,800 seconds for three fixed seeds and checkpoints. These are planning estimates, not observed runtimes; a readiness job may measure imports and a tiny tensor/sequence probe, but no fitted result is claimed here. A mechanistic state-space model is not chosen because the available HCC data lack assay units/reference ranges, imaging, treatment intent, and validated tumour-burden observations; extra complexity would not resolve those missing dependencies.
