# Literal RT Vent Off versus Suspended: within-stay sequence, hospital transportability, and fixed-event-time disposition audit

## Substantive child, unresolved question, and clinical importance

This is a substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` and assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the first parent's literal RT-event extraction, exact-record and anchored 30-minute deduplication, fixed 6/24/48-hour observation partitions, setting-family definition, controls, and prohibition against interpreting chart values as bedside ventilation states. It imports the second parent's hospital-concentration, common-support, site-contrast, heterogeneity, leave-one-hospital-out, and within-stay persistence framework. The core experiment asks whether the inherited Off-versus-Suspended documentation difference is reproducible across hospitals and first episodes, or instead arises from repeated within-stay workflow sequences concentrated at a small number of hospitals. A secondary, strictly descriptive experiment enumerates recorded ICU death, live ICU discharge, hospital death, live hospital discharge, and continued-observation states at the same fixed event times, without treating discharge as success or status as a causal outcome.

This measurement question is clinically consequential because repeated `Suspended` records could inflate episode-level contrasts and because a pooled or unqualified multicenter endpoint could appear precise while primarily describing one hospital's charting workflow. The strongest inherited evidence is that literal `Off` and `Suspended` should not be pooled: among fully observed episodes, late setting-family positivity was 11.0% versus 51.8% at 6-24 hours and 15.1% versus 51.7% at 24-48 hours, while later literal Start recurrence differences had clustered intervals crossing zero. The unresolved hypothesis tested here was that the setting-trajectory contrast would remain positive under common hospital support, first-episode restriction, hospital leave-one-out analysis, and stay-clustered sequence modeling, whereas the smaller recurrence contrast might be site- or episode-order-dependent. `Off`, `Suspended`, and `Start` remain literal structured chart values; they are not called ventilator discontinuation, extubation, liberation, reintubation, or clinical failure.

A mixed Start/off-direction cluster is a conflict; a mixed Off+Suspended cluster is semantically ambiguous. The index population requires a patient row, nonmissing ICU boundary, `T >= 360` minutes for the inherited six-hour pre-window, and `T <= unitdischargeoffset`. A later Start is not excluded.

## New computable sequence and transportability experiment

Within each stay, index Off/Suspended clusters are ordered by `(respchartoffset, cluster identity)`. New variables are episode ordinal, first versus later episode, previous index label and elapsed minutes, immediate next clean decisive label before/on ICU discharge, first later Start, and the next Off/Suspended index after that Start. These support three descriptive sequence summaries:

1. adjacent Off/Suspended index pairs, including persistence and alternation;
2. immediate Off/Suspended -> Start transitions in the clean decisive stream;
3. index -> Start -> later index cycles within each fixed horizon.

Repeated episodes are correlated records. First-versus-later recurrence is reported by label and horizon with Wilson intervals; stay-resampled 3,000-replicate percentile intervals quantify later-minus-first differences. The main label contrasts use both stay- and hospital-clustered 3,000-replicate bootstrap intervals.

Hospital concentration is reported as HHI, inverse-HHI effective hospital count, top-one share, and top-three share. A common-support hospital has at least five complete episodes under each label at the relevant horizon. Within this set, the audit reports site-specific Suspended-minus-Off risk differences, episode-weighted and equal-hospital-weight contrasts, and equal-hospital-weight leave-one-hospital-out estimates. Cochran Q, Q p-value, descriptive I-squared, DerSimonian-Laird tau-squared, range, and direction diagnose rather than erase heterogeneity; zero-variance all-zero sites remain visible but cannot contribute inverse-variance weight.

A descriptive linear probability model within common-support hospitals includes `Suspended`, `later_episode`, their interaction, and hospital fixed effects, with stay-clustered sandwich covariance. It is not causal and does not remove patient-state or workflow confounding. For each horizon, the same machinery is applied to recurrence and to the fixed corresponding setting partition: 0-6, 6-24, or 24-48 hours.

## Executed sequence results

The 1,063 episodes comprise 649 single-episode stays and 100 repeated-episode stays containing 414 episodes; the maximum was 32 episodes in one stay. Episode order was highly label-imbalanced: Off had 639 first and 74 later episodes, whereas Suspended had 110 first and 240 later episodes. This makes first-episode and clustered analyses essential.

Among adjacent Off/Suspended index pairs, counts were Off->Off 40, Off->Suspended 29, Suspended->Off 34, and Suspended->Suspended 211. Thus Suspended persistence dominated its 245 outgoing adjacent pairs (86.1%), while 63 pairs alternated labels. In the complete clean decisive stream before ICU boundary, the immediate next state after 713 Off episodes was Start for 55, Off for 25, Suspended for 14, and no later clean state for 619. After 350 Suspended episodes it was Start for 41, Suspended for 191, Off for 29, and none for 89. These are chart-sequence facts, not bedside state transitions.

Start-cycle return was especially concentrated among Suspended episodes. By 24 hours, 25 Suspended episodes had a recurrent Start and 13 then had another index within the horizon (12 Suspended, 1 Off), versus 23 recurrent Starts and 1 later Off after Off. By 48 hours, the corresponding counts were 40 Starts and 23 later indices after Suspended (21 Suspended, 2 Off), versus 33 Starts and 3 later Off indices after Off. This pattern supports recurrent workflow sequencing but cannot identify clinical causes.

First-versus-later complete-observation recurrence showed episode-order dependence primarily for Off:

- 6 hours: first Off 8/579 = 1.38%, later Off 4/68 = 5.88%, later-minus-first +4.50 points (stay-bootstrap 95% -0.41 to +10.62); first Suspended 2/107 = 1.87%, later Suspended 7/237 = 2.95%, +1.08 points (-2.01 to +4.23).
- 24 hours: first Off 16/521 = 3.07%, later Off 7/62 = 11.29%, +8.22 points (+0.73 to +16.60); first Suspended 7/101 = 6.93%, later Suspended 18/229 = 7.86%, +0.93 points (-5.65 to +7.49).
- 48 hours: first Off 23/348 = 6.61%, later Off 9/55 = 16.36%, +9.75 points (+0.29 to +20.50); first Suspended 9/86 = 10.47%, later Suspended 25/212 = 11.79%, +1.33 points (-8.35 to +10.90).

Therefore episode-level recurrence is not exchangeable across order, especially for Off. The label contrast was positive among first episodes but negative among later episodes at all horizons; however, common-support hospital-fixed interaction intervals included zero for recurrence at 6, 24, and 48 hours. The data do not resolve whether the reversal is label-by-order biology, patient selection, or workflow.

## Hospital concentration and common-support results

Index episodes were strongly concentrated. For Off, HHI was 0.450 (effective hospitals 2.22), hospital 73 supplied 467/713 = 65.5%, and the top three supplied 83.2%. For Suspended, HHI was 0.675 (effective hospitals 1.48), hospital 73 supplied 286/350 = 81.7%, and the top three supplied 92.0%. At 48 hours, top-hospital shares rose to 72.7% and 84.9%. Five hospitals had >=5 complete episodes per label at 6 and 24 hours; four did at 48 hours. Thus broad hospital transportability is adverse even though within-overlap directional replication can still be assessed.

### Fixed setting partitions

The Off-versus-Suspended setting contrast reproduced directionally in every common-support hospital:

- 0-6 hours: pooled complete rates 7.73% versus 29.94%, RD +22.21 points; stay-bootstrap 95% +13.58 to +30.94 and hospital-bootstrap +19.74 to +32.20. All five site RDs were positive; equal-hospital leave-one-out RDs ranged +21.63 to +29.03 points.
- 6-24 hours: 10.98% versus 51.82%, RD +40.84 points; stay-bootstrap +30.12 to +51.70 and hospital-bootstrap +37.29 to +67.11. All five common-support site RDs were positive (+21.44 to +70.83), and leave-one-out estimates were +44.62 to +56.97 points. The first-episode common-support RD was +45.73 points. Site magnitude heterogeneity was moderate (I-squared 55.6%) but did not reverse direction.
- 24-48 hours: 15.14% versus 51.68%, RD +36.54 points; stay-bootstrap +25.90 to +47.41 and hospital-bootstrap +27.20 to +59.82. All four common-support site RDs were positive (+20.37 to +75.00), and leave-one-out estimates were +38.04 to +56.25 points. The first-episode common-support RD was +40.32 points. Magnitudes were heterogeneous (Q p=0.0068, I-squared 75.3%) despite shared direction.

Hospital-fixed, stay-clustered models retained a positive Suspended coefficient among first episodes: +35.0 points at 0-6 hours, +45.1 at 6-24, and +40.1 at 24-48, with all cluster intervals excluding zero. Negative Suspended-by-later interactions (-27.6, -24.0, and -23.3 points) also excluded zero. This shows that repeated Suspended episodes attenuate, rather than manufacture, the first-episode setting difference; it also warns that a single common episode-level effect is inadequate.

### Literal Start recurrence

The all-episode Suspended-minus-Off recurrence RDs were +0.76 points at 6 hours, +3.63 at 24 hours, and +3.47 at 48 hours. Stay- and hospital-clustered intervals crossed zero at every horizon. At 24 hours, common-support site RDs were positive at hospitals 63, 71, and 73 and zero at 66 and 68; leave-one-out equal-hospital estimates remained positive. At 48 hours, site RDs ranged from -11.1 to +37.6 points, Q p=0.0074 and I-squared 75.0%, and omitting hospital 71 reversed the equal-hospital contrast to -0.44 points. First-episode common-support RDs remained positive (+4.21 points at 24 hours and +4.00 at 48 hours), but hospital-fixed, stay-clustered Suspended and interaction intervals included zero. Recurrence transportability is therefore inconclusive and becomes clearly site-sensitive by 48 hours.

## Secondary fixed-event-time disposition audit

Using the unchanged 1,063 episode population, all 200,859 `patient` rows were scanned and all events joined exactly on `patientunitstayid`. At `h = 360, 1440, 2880` minutes, ICU states use `unitdischargeoffset` and `unitdischargestatus`: still in the index ICU, recorded ICU death by h, recorded live ICU discharge by h, or unknown boundary status. Hospital states separately use `hospitaldischargeoffset` and `hospitaldischargestatus`: recorded hospital death by h, recorded live hospital discharge by h, no recorded hospital boundary by h, or unknown status. A joint accounting distinguishes hospital boundary, ICU death without a hospital boundary by h, live ICU departure with hospital outcome after h, and continued index-ICU observation. Future hospital status is never projected backward to ICU discharge. Wilson intervals and Suspended-minus-Off stay-, person-, and hospital-clustered 4,000-replicate bootstrap intervals are descriptive only; common-support equal-hospital, leave-one-hospital-out, first-index-per-stay, and six-hour-spaced-event analyses probe concentration and repetition.

At 48 hours after Off, recorded states were ICU death 29/713 (4.1%), live ICU discharge 281/713 (39.4%), and still in index ICU 403/713; hospital death 57/713 (8.0%), live hospital discharge 37/713 (5.2%), and no hospital boundary yet 619/713. After Suspended, the corresponding counts were ICU death 1/350 (0.3%), live ICU discharge 51/350 (14.6%), still in ICU 298/350; hospital death 6/350 (1.7%), live hospital discharge 22/350 (6.3%), and no hospital boundary yet 322/350.

Crude 48-hour Suspended-minus-Off differences were -3.78 percentage points for ICU death, -24.84 for live ICU discharge, -6.28 for hospital death, and +1.10 for live hospital discharge. Hospital-clustered 95% intervals were -4.55 to +0.23, -40.80 to -11.13, -7.21 to -0.87, and -11.87 to +12.49 points, respectively. First-index-per-stay estimates were -3.0, -20.7, -4.3, and -1.7 points; estimates after greedily spacing events by at least six hours were -3.8, -24.5, -5.9, and +1.2. Removing dominant hospital 73 retained negative ICU-death, live-ICU-discharge, and hospital-death differences, but live-hospital-discharge remained unstable. Site-specific discharge differences reversed in some sparse hospitals.

This outcome branch is adverse to a one-dimensional clinical-success interpretation: fewer recorded deaths after Suspended coexist with substantially fewer live ICU departures, and 941/1,063 episodes still lacked a hospital boundary by 48 hours. ICU discharge is a location transition, not success; hospital follow-up is predominantly unresolved at these horizons. Case mix, illness trajectory, goals of care, repeated documentation, and local workflow remain uncontrolled. These findings do not authorize an exposure, treatment-effect, safety, extubation, liberation, reintubation, or benefit claim.

## Prespecified gates and interpretation

1. **Site overlap: adverse.** Support required at least five common-support hospitals at both 24 and 48 hours and neither label top-site share above 60%. There were only four at 48 hours, and Suspended top-site share exceeded 75% overall and at every horizon. This cohort cannot establish broad multicenter representativeness.

2. **Late setting reproducibility: supportive within observed common-support hospitals.** At both 6-24 and 24-48 hours, at least 75% of site RDs exceeded +20 points, all equal-hospital leave-one-out RDs remained above +10 points, and first-episode RDs remained above +20 points. The supported claim is directional reproducibility of a structured documentation difference in the observed overlap sites, not a common magnitude or a random-hospital generalization.

3. **Recurrence transportability: inconclusive.** Point estimates were often positive, but clustered intervals included zero; 24-hour sites included zeros, and a 48-hour leave-one-hospital-out estimate reversed. This did not meet the supportive gate and did not meet the prespecified adverse requirement of reversal at both 24 and 48 hours.

4. **Sequence dependence: inconclusive for recurrence and adverse to exchangeability.** First-only contrasts did not reverse, and recurrence interaction magnitudes were under 10 points with intervals including zero, so the formal adverse label-interaction gate was not met. Nevertheless, later Off recurrence was substantially higher than first Off recurrence, and first/later label contrasts changed sign. Repeated episodes must not be treated as independent exchangeable events.

5. **Fixed-time disposition: adverse to success semantics and partly inconclusive.** Support was limited to reproducible descriptive state occupancy under clustered, common-support, dominant-site, and repeated-event analyses. Suspended had fewer recorded ICU and hospital deaths but also far fewer live ICU departures; hospital-clustered uncertainty crossed zero for ICU death and live hospital discharge, site discharge directions varied, and most episodes lacked a hospital boundary at 48 hours. These competing observations falsify any simple favorable-outcome reading, while leaving clinical causes unresolved.

6. **Clinical semantics: inconclusive.** Independent timestamped device/airway and adjudicated extubation evidence are unavailable. Nothing computed here validates a clinical state.

## Strongest supported claim, falsification, and remaining evidence needs

The strongest evidence-supported claim is that literal `RT Vent Off` and `Suspended` encode reproducibly different structured respiratory-charting regimes within the small set of hospitals that document both labels often enough for comparison. Suspended's higher fixed-bin setting-family positivity persists at every common-support hospital, in first episodes, under leave-one-hospital-out analysis, and in hospital-fixed/stay-clustered models. The experiment simultaneously falsifies three stronger shortcuts: episodes are not exchangeable across order, recurrence does not supply a stable quantitative multicenter distinction, and downstream death or discharge cannot substitute for missing ventilation-state validation. High HHI and dominant hospital 73 make any broad hospital-general claim unsupported.

The unresolved clinical hypothesis is whether either literal label corresponds to actual ventilator/device status, airway removal, extubation, invasive-ventilation liberation, clinical deterioration, or benefit. Exact additional evidence required is independently timestamped ventilator connection, invasive versus noninvasive mode, airway insertion/removal, extubation/reintubation, post-event oxygen device, RT and nursing narrative, post-ICU support, local workflow metadata, and blinded clinician adjudication sampled by hospital, label, episode order, and discordant sequence. A separate validation study would need sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, inter-rater reliability, and hospital-level transportability.

Computationally checkable claims are source and artifact hashes, full-source row counts, event normalization, exact and anchored 30-minute deduplication, conflict exclusions, patient join and ICU/hospital boundaries, all index and later-Start identities, fixed partitions and fixed-time competing states, episode order, adjacent transitions, Start cycles, complete-observation denominators, setting-family outcomes, site counts, HHI, Wilson intervals, bootstrap resampling, common-support and leave-one-out calculations, heterogeneity diagnostics, model coefficients, and repeated-event sensitivities. Automated verification cannot establish bedside state, intent, causal effects, appropriateness, safety, extubation, liberation, cause of death, adequacy of case-mix control, or generalization to unobserved hospitals.
