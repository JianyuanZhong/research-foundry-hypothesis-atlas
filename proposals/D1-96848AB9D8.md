# Discordant creatinine recovery after AKI: a severity-, treatment-intensity-, and RRT-stratified MIMIC-IV study with KDIGO urine-output sensitivity analysis

Status: active draft, not yet a registered hypothesis. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Among adults in the ICU with KDIGO stage 2 or 3 acute kidney injury (AKI), does apparent creatinine recovery at 48 hours coexisting with persistently low urine output or positive fluid balance identify a worse 7-day ICU prognosis than creatinine recovery alone, after accounting for current severity and treatment intensity at the 48-hour stabilization decision point?

The parent proposal established a prespecified discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype and addressed the strongest confounder—current severity and treatment intensity at the decision point—with SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and renal replacement therapy (RRT) status as adjustment variables. However, RRT status is not merely a continuous treatment-intensity covariate: it marks a fundamentally different renal recovery phenotype and outcome trajectory. Patients on RRT at the 48-hour decision point have already had their native kidney function bypassed, and creatinine-based recovery may reflect dialytic clearance rather than intrinsic renal recovery.

The strongest remaining interpretive limitation is that creatinine is a surrogate for measured kidney function and its trajectory can be affected by muscle mass, fluid balance, and measurement timing. A urine-output-based sensitivity analysis directly tests whether the discordance signal is robust to the definition of recovery or is an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The inspected literature reports that AKI is associated with increased ICU mortality and that creatinine-based AKI definitions may miss patients with urine-output-based AKI. The KDIGO guidelines recommend using both creatinine and urine-output criteria for AKI staging [K1]. However, no study has tested whether a discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype (creatinine recovery coexisting with low urine output) is prognostic beyond current severity and treatment intensity, or whether the signal persists when renal recovery is defined by urine-output criteria rather than creatinine.

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that longitudinal laboratory and support patterns can distinguish ICU patients with different prognoses. The unresolved claim is that the discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype identifies a worse prognosis than creatinine recovery alone, after accounting for current severity and treatment intensity at the 48-hour decision point, and that this signal is robust to the definition of renal recovery (creatinine vs. urine output).

## Population and temporal design

- **Source:** MIMIC-IV v3.0, ICU stays only.
- **Inclusion:** Adults (age ≥ 18) with at least one ICU stay and KDIGO stage 2 or 3 AKI during the first 48 hours of the ICU stay.
- **AKI definition:** KDIGO creatinine criteria (SCr ≥ 2× baseline or ≥ 3× baseline) or urine-output criteria (UO < 0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥ 12h or < 0.3 mL/kg/h for ≥ 24h). Baseline SCr is the minimum SCr in the 7 days before ICU admission, or the first SCr in the ICU stay if no prior value is available.
- **Decision point:** 48 hours after ICU admission.
- **Recovery definition (primary):** Creatinine recovery = SCr at 48h ≤ 1.5× baseline SCr.
- **Discordant phenotype (primary):** Creatinine recovery at 48h coexisting with persistently low urine output (UO < 0.5 mL/kg/h in the 24h before the decision point) or positive fluid balance (fluid balance > 0 in the 24h before the decision point).
- **Outcome:** 7-day ICU mortality (death before ICU discharge or within 7 days of ICU admission).
- **Follow-up:** 7 days from ICU admission.

## KDIGO urine-output sensitivity analysis

### Purpose
The primary analysis defines renal recovery using creatinine criteria. The UO sensitivity analysis redefines renal recovery using KDIGO urine-output criteria and tests whether the discordance signal persists. This directly tests whether the discordance signal is robust to the definition of recovery or is an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.

### UO recovery definition
- **UO recovery (KDIGO):** UO ≥ 0.5 mL/kg/h in the 24h before the decision point.
- **UO discordance:** Creatinine recovery at 48h coexisting with UO < 0.5 mL/kg/h in the 24h before the decision point.
- **UO agreement:** Creatinine recovery and UO recovery both present, or both absent.
- **UO disagreement:** Creatinine recovery present but UO recovery absent (UO discordance), or creatinine recovery absent but UO recovery present.

### UO extraction logic
- **Item mapping:** `chartevents.item_id` = 221289 (Urine Output, mL/h) or 221289 (Urine Output, mL).
- **Time window:** 24 hours before the 48-hour decision point (i.e., 24h to 48h after ICU admission).
- **Calculation:** Mean UO (mL/h) in the time window, converted to mL/kg/h using a standard weight assumption (70 kg) if actual weight is unavailable.
- **Fallback:** If UO data are sparse (fewer than 3 measurements in the time window), exclude the patient from the UO sensitivity analysis and report the number of excluded patients.

### Analysis
1. **Primary UO analysis:** Compare 7-day ICU mortality between UO discordance and UO agreement, adjusted for SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and RRT status at the 48-hour decision point.
2. **UO disagreement analysis:** Identify patients where creatinine-based and urine-output-based recovery definitions disagree. Compare 7-day ICU mortality between the disagreement subgroups.
3. **Sparse data handling:** If fewer than 50% of the primary cohort has adequate UO data, interpret the UO sensitivity analysis with caution and report the number of patients with adequate UO data.

### Interpretation
- **Supportive:** The UO discordance phenotype is associated with worse 7-day ICU prognosis than UO agreement, after adjustment for severity and treatment intensity. This suggests the discordance signal is robust to the definition of recovery.
- **Adverse:** The UO discordance phenotype is not associated with worse 7-day ICU prognosis, or the association is attenuated after adjustment. This suggests the discordance signal may be an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.
- **Inconclusive:** UO data are sparse (fewer than 50% of the primary cohort has adequate UO data), or the association is not statistically significant. This does not refute the discordance signal but limits the strength of the claim.

## Method comparison

- **Baseline:** Unadjusted comparison of 7-day ICU mortality between discordant and concordant recovery groups.
- **Adjusted model:** Cox proportional hazards model with SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and RRT status at the 48-hour decision point as covariates.
- **Propensity-score analysis:** Propensity-score matching or overlap-weighted secondary analysis to address residual confounding.
- **RRT stratification:** Stratified analysis by RRT status at the 48-hour decision point, with stratum-specific estimands and an interaction test.
- **UO sensitivity analysis:** KDIGO urine-output criteria as described above.

## Falsification criteria

- **Negative control outcome:** If the discordance phenotype is associated with a negative control outcome (e.g., 7-day ICU length of stay) that is not plausibly related to renal recovery, this suggests the signal may be driven by confounding or measurement artifacts.
- **Temporal falsification:** If the discordance phenotype is not associated with 7-day ICU mortality but is associated with 24-hour ICU mortality, this suggests the signal may be driven by current severity rather than renal recovery.
- **UO falsification:** If the discordance phenotype is not associated with 7-day ICU mortality when renal recovery is defined by KDIGO urine-output criteria, this suggests the signal may be an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.

## Limitations

- Creatinine is a surrogate for measured kidney function and its trajectory can be affected by muscle mass, fluid balance, and measurement timing.
- Urine output data may be sparse or selectively obtained, limiting the UO sensitivity analysis.
- The study is prognostic, not causal. The discordance phenotype may reflect current severity, treatment selection, or observation intensity rather than a causal mechanism.
- MIMIC-IV is a single-center dataset, and generalizability to other populations is uncertain.
- The 7-day ICU mortality outcome may not capture longer-term renal outcomes or quality of life.
