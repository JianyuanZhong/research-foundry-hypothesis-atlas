# Branch B — vital-trajectory state versus laboratory-process transport in eICU

Status: proposed child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`; no fitted study result is claimed. This branch preserves that parent’s ICU-discharge mortality estimand, hospital-held-out split, selection/EOL audits, state-matched process null, exact laboratory bindings, and uncertainty plan. It adds a verified compact vital-trajectory state representation and makes the process comparison conditional on that richer state.

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence supports only bounded premises. Temporally represented EHR data can predict mortality across centers [K1]. In a different longitudinal EHR setting, information presence/missingness can be predictive and changing missingness patterns can threaten transportability [K2, abstract-only inspection]. External mortality validation can retain discrimination while local EHR patterns improve calibration or performance [K3]. The inherited eICU proposal therefore supports testing whether laboratory-result presence, frequency, and timing transport differently between hospitals, but does not establish that the signal is a monitoring gap, a physiologic trajectory, or a causal effect of testing.

The unresolved question is whether a laboratory-process increment remains when “patient state” includes compact, temporally ordered vital trajectories rather than only six-hour laboratory values. The clinically consequential distinction is:

- Patient-state explanation: recent level and change in vital physiology explain much of the apparent process signal. A process increment should attenuate after adding within-patient vital trajectories and should be reduced by a process permutation matched on temporal-state risk.
- Workflow/selection explanation: process fields encode observation density, local documentation, discharge/EOL selection, or timing artifacts. A process increment should persist after temporal-state adjustment and/or after state-matched permutation, or disappear at the parent’s >720-minute/no-EOL/eligibility boundaries.

This remains a descriptive transportability question, not a causal claim that orders, recording, testing, or monitoring cause death.

### Falsifiable hypothesis

Among adult first ICU admissions observed beyond six hours, the process-aware model will retain a non-zero hospital-transport contribution beyond a compact temporal vital-state model only if the process vector carries patient-linked information not explained by the observed trajectory. Operationally, the primary process increment is

`I_process^T(e) = performance(TP,e) - performance(T,e)`

where T is temporal vital/laboratory state without process variables and TP adds laboratory/vital recording-process summaries. The primary transport contrast is

`D_process^T = [I_process^T(internal) - I_process^T(external)]`.

A process increment that is attenuated by a within-hospital temporal-state-matched process permutation, and remains directionally similar under >720-minute, no-EOL, and measured-eligibility boundaries, is compatible with a patient-linked observation/state signal. A process increment that survives the matched permutation or concentrates near discharge/EOL boundaries is more compatible with stable case mix or workflow/documentation. Neither pattern proves mechanism.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

If the process increment vanishes after temporal state is represented, the clinically useful advance is a boundary against interpreting lab-result density as an actionable monitoring signal: current and changing physiology, not recording frequency, is the more defensible descriptive carrier. If it persists and is patient-linked across held-out hospitals, it motivates clinician review of whether observation-process features add information beyond state, but not a change in testing or treatment. If it is site-specific or selection-bound, the next decision is feed harmonization, recalibration, or prospective adjudication—not deployment.

Relative to candidate-064, this child tests the weakest remaining state assumption directly. The parent’s latest laboratory values and lab-process summaries cannot tell whether a transient heart-rate, oxygenation, respiratory, or blood-pressure pattern is being misclassified as “process.” The new temporal representation can reveal level-plus-change, within-window instability, and ordered vital information that a latest-value summary loses. The matched process model then asks whether process features still matter after those state summaries are available. A small predictive gain alone is not evidence of clinical value; coherence across state matching, transport, calibration, utility, and selection controls is required.

## Feature construction and models

All transformations are fit within each outer training partition. Select the 30 laboratory analytes by distinct eligible-patient coverage among analytes with >=1% training-hospital coverage. Retain each analyte’s latest valid value in [0,360] and original offset, with no carry-forward; impute values using training-fold medians. V includes age, gender, ethnicity, admissionweight and those lab values.

For vitals, retain finite numeric values at offsets [0,360]. The candidate payload list above is fixed before scoring. A field enters the model only if its training-hospital patient coverage is >=1%; omitted fields and coverage are reported, not rescued by test-fold inspection. Within each field and each of three fixed bins [0,120), [120,240), [240,360], compute the median and the last finite value. For each field over W0 also compute first finite value, last finite value, last-minus-first change, and an OLS slope against observationoffset when at least two distinct offsets exist. There is no carry-forward. Training-fold robust clipping, if used, is fixed at training 0.5th/99.5th percentiles and reported; a no-clipping sensitivity is required if clipping affects field inclusion. Values and trajectory summaries are median-imputed from training data without missingness indicators, so recording availability remains in the separate process channel. Report per-field row count, distinct-offset count, first/last offset and observed-bin count as audit variables.

Models use identical preprocessing, hyperparameters, seeds, folds and learner capacity:

- V-Logit: elastic-net logistic regression on V; the simple interpretable baseline.
- V-GBM: parent capacity-matched value-only histogram GBM on V.
- VS-GBM: V-GBM plus latest values from eligible vital fields; this tests whether current vital level, not temporal shape, explains the process increment.
- T-GBM: VS-GBM plus the fixed compact vital trajectory summaries above; no process counts/timing variables.
- TP-GBM: T-GBM plus process features: per selected lab, per eligible vital table/field, and overall, include finite-row count, distinct-offset count, first/last observation/result offset, observed-bin count and presence. These are recording proxies, not orders.
- TP-StateNull: within each hospital, cyclically assign the complete process vector to another stay within a training-defined temporal-state stratum. The stratum is quintiles of out-of-fold T-GBM risk with admission age/gender/ethnicity/weight bins as tie-breakers; fewer-than-10 cells are merged with the adjacent quintile before assignment. For held-out hospitals, use the fold-fitted T model. Keep labels, hospital, V/VS/T values and outcomes fixed; use SHA-256(`"eicu-process-state-null-v2\\0"+patientunitstayid`) for deterministic ordering and never use outcome/process in assignment.
- TP-TimeNull: move only process offsets by +180 minutes modulo W0 (t+180 if t<=180 else t-180), preserving process counts/presence and all patient state. This tests coarse timing alignment, not causality. Retain parent’s unrestricted process permutation as a secondary control.

The primary scientific comparison is TP versus T, not a search over model classes. T reveals ordered level/change information that the parent value baseline loses; TP tests whether process adds beyond it. The simple V-Logit provides direction/calibration interpretability. The capacity-matched GBM controls for nonlinear interactions. A neural raw-event sequence model is deferred: current uncertainty is state versus process and the sources lack order/specimen/interface semantics; revisit only if a bounded readiness check shows the fixed summaries discard important temporal information. A causal testing/monitoring intervention is unavailable from these data.

## Selection, EOL and falsification controls

Preserve the parent’s boundaries:

- `S6=1(unitdischargeoffset>360)` is the primary landmark risk set. On the full adult first-admission denominator report S6, early discharge/death status and vital/lab coverage; do not define post-360 Y for S6=0.
- `S720=1(unitdischargeoffset>720)` repeats the same [0,360] feature window and label, and is a selection-boundary sensitivity, not a causal estimand.
- `EOL6=1` if a finite cpleol save/discussion offset lies in [0,360]. Report no-EOL and EOL strata only when event/hospital counts support intervals; EOL is documentation, not a clinical decision timestamp.
- Fit training-only stabilized inverse-probability weights for S6 using age, gender, ethnicity and admissionweight, truncate propensity at [0.05,0.95], report truncation and effective sample size, and cluster-bootstrap. This standardizes only measured admission selection; it cannot recover early outcomes or unmeasured severity.
- Report the vital trajectory-observed/coverage gate as an audit and sensitivity. Do not condition the primary cohort on a post hoc vital-completeness rule. If a field’s coverage or a later-landmark risk set collapses, label the result infeasible/inconclusive rather than substitute a new population.

Supportive results require: TP’s held-out process increment beyond T is directionally positive with uncertainty excluding clinically negligible harm; it attenuates in TP-StateNull relative to TP; and the pattern is broadly coherent across S720, no-EOL and measured-IPW analyses, with calibration/net-benefit support. This is evidence of patient-linked recorded information, not physiology, actionable monitoring adequacy, or causality.

Results adverse to the process interpretation are an upper interval below zero for TP-T, no held-out calibration/utility improvement, or process advantage that survives TP-StateNull while being concentrated in one hospital or documentation boundary. Support for a workflow/selection explanation is attenuation at S720/no-EOL/IPW, concentration near discharge/EOL, or little attenuation after temporal-state matching. Mixed results (e.g., trajectory helps but process remains site-specific) should narrow the claim. Inconclusive results include intervals spanning clinically meaningful values, fewer than five viable hospitals, unstable IPW/effective sample size, sparse EOL strata, substantial vital coverage collapse, material feature-selection instability, or failed leakage/row-count audits. Do not treat an imprecise null as refutation.

The verifier can check exact rows/joins/offsets, fold isolation, feature inclusion, model parity, state-null assignment, timing displacement, predictions, metrics, bootstrap intervals and conclusion-to-output linkage. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a row represents an order, specimen collection, bedside observation, clinician rationale, interface outage, a monitoring gap, or an EOL decision. eICU provides no order status, specimen time, interface-outage indicator, clinician rationale, or death time. A prospective silent deployment with those timestamps, clinician adjudication, and outcome follow-up is required before a claim of clinical utility or changed practice; an independent health system or later release is required for replication beyond this hospital-held-out estimand.

## Alternatives, compute and revisit criteria

The selected alternative is T/TP-GBM because compact temporal summaries answer a substantive uncertainty—the state-versus-process boundary—while preserving a matched learner and exact held-out evaluation. V-Logit is the low-cost interpretable baseline, and V/VS-GBM are capacity-matched controls. A raw sequence neural encoder could model irregular order more richly, but is deferred for scientific focus and semantic limitations, not because neural models or GPUs are forbidden. If the bounded readiness check shows the fixed summaries lose meaningful within-window ordering, revisit a compact GRU/temporal-convolution child with the same target, folds, uncertainty and state/process null. If extraction or T fitting exceeds the future solver envelope, retain V/VS/TP and report T as deferred; do not shrink the cohort or change Y.

Discovery feasibility measured by this episode: one CPU read-only vital coverage scan, 8 CPUs/32 GiB, two configured gzip sources, with output in `vital_coverage_probe.out`; its wall time and counts are recorded in the job receipt.
