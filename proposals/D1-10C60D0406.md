## 1. Unresolved question, clinical importance, advance
ICU early-warning/deterioration models increasingly ingest routinely charted labs. Laboratory
*ordering* is a clinician decision shaped by workflow, so testing frequency, panel choice and
measurement missingness may encode **hospital practice** rather than patient state. [K1] Agniel 2018
showed across 272 tests / 669,452 patients that the *presence and timing* of a test order predicts
3-year survival independently of the result value — process information is predictive but is a
property of care, not physiology. [K2] Vallée 2026 argues models degrade outside their training
environment because they rely on context-specific correlations; this remains a hypothesis with
"empirical validation … limited". [K3] Larsson 2026 (WICH-ICU) treats ICU lab overuse as a
*modifiable unit-level practice* worth a trial, i.e. testing intensity varies by unit practice, not
only by illness.

Decision-facing gap: **if a model's internal performance comes partly from practice-derived features,
its bedside predictions will be systematically miscalibrated after transport to a new hospital** — a
consequential failure for escalation/discharge decisions. The advance over [K1–K3] is the first
*transportability-decomposition* test in a large multi-hospital ICU database: does adding
testing-process features to a physiology-anchored model increase the performance loss from in-sample
to leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO), assessed by calibration/O:E/net-benefit, not AUROC alone?

- **H1 (practice-leakage):** a model with testing-process features loses *more* AUROC/calibration
  under LOHO than a physiology-only model on the same cohort/outcome.
- **R1 (severity confounding, strongest rival):** practice features are proxies for severity (sicker
  patients are tested more), so their LOHO loss is ordinary overfitting/miscalibration, not
  "practice leakage." Distinguished by: severity-adjusted incremental value, and the *practice-only*
  model's performance profile (collapses under LOHO if it is a hospital fingerprint).
- **R2 (hospital structure):** teaching status / size drive both testing and outcome (adjust).
- **R3 (outcome/timing leakage):** mortality ascertainment tied to discharge timing (use fixed
  24h landmark + hospital-discharge status).
- **R4 (measurement missingness = care-limiting):** absence of a panel reflects lower acuity/discharge.

## 2. Design (frozen)
**Data:** eICU 2.0 (snapshot per datasets/eicu/README.md). Access read-only; derived files in workspace.
**Population:** adult ICU stays, `age>=16`, `unitdischargeoffset>=1440` (survive to a 24h landmark),
≥1 creatinine and ≥1 initial-panel lab in (0,24]h. Join key `patientunitstayid`.
**Landmark / outcome window:** features from (0,24]h; outcome = `hospitaldischargestatus` (died vs
alive) restricted to `hospitaldischargeoffset` valid; index-stay only (`unitvisitnumber==1`).
**Feature families:**
- `F_phys` (physiology anchor): demographics + baseline severity `apachePatientResult.apachescore`,
  `acutephysiologyscore`, `predictedhospitalmortality` + fixed-window worst/mean lab VALUES
  (`lab.labname` in {creatinine, WBC x 1000, sodium, glucose/bedside glucose, Hgb, Hct, platelets})
  and `apacheApsVar` first-24h vitals.
- `F_proc` (testing process): number of lab rows in (0,24]h, number of distinct `labtypeid` panels,
  number of distinct `labname`s ordered, count of standard-panel draws, presence/absence indicators
  for expected daily panels, ordering time-of-day structure (night fraction, hourly dispersion of
  `labresultoffset`).
- `F_both`.
**Models:** baseline = L2-penalized logistic regression (linear). Substantive alternative =
gradient-boosted trees with a hospital random effect / hospital-ID held out from features; plus a
`F_proc`-only model to quantify how much a *hospital fingerprint* predicts outcome. (Method choice
recorded: the tree model can capture practice×severity interactions a single linear term cannot;
compared by calibration/O:E/Brier/net benefit under LOHO, not AUROC.)
**Primary estimand:** Δ_deg = [AUROC_random − AUROC_LOHO]_F_both − [AUROC_random − AUROC_LOHO]_F_phys.
**Secondary:** calibration slope & ICI, O:E ratio, Brier, decision-curve net benefit at prespecified
thresholds, under both random 5-fold and LOHO over 208 hospitals.
**Supportive:** Δ_deg ≥ +0.02 AUROC AND F_proc-only LOHO calibration poor AND F_both net benefit
drops under LOHO. **Adverse:** Δ_deg ≈ 0 with CIs excluding +0.02 (physiology explains practice
signal; practice features transport as well as physiology). **Inconclusive:** wide CIs / sparse
hospitals / unstable calibration.
**Negative controls:** hour-12 placebo landmark; shuffled-hospital LOHO control; repeat on a
non-mortality negative-control outcome (e.g., long LOS) to separate generic degradation.
**Falsification:** H1 is refuted if the physiology-only model shows equal-or-greater LOHO degradation.

## 4. Honest limits / required evidence
- No order-set, staffing, or ICU-capacity tables → organizational mechanism cannot be established;
  the claim is about *transport of predictions*, not quality of care.
- `labname`/`labtypeid` strings not harmonized across sites (documented as a measurement limit).
- eICU-only internal LOHO transport, not external validation; association of process features with
  practice is inferred, not observed directly.
- Discovery = design + verified bindings only. The fitted Δ_deg, calibration, O:E, net-benefit and
  any causal reading require the solver execution + clinical adjudication.
