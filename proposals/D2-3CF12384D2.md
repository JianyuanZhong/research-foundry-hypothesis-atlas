# Donor-aware identifiability of context-selective MerTK harm in RA synovial coculture

Status: substantive child of [prior hypothesis]; planned Harbor experiment only. No molecular screening or result has been executed.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The inspected evidence supports a broad claim: MerTK-positive synovial macrophage states are associated with remission/repair, and MerTK inhibition has been reported with increased FLS inflammatory transcripts [K1]. It does not establish that the perturbation is more harmful in the MerTK/CD206-positive context than in the CD206-negative context. That unresolved boundary matters for target prioritization: a MerTK-preservation or activation strategy could be selectively attractive only if loss of the resolution-like macrophage context produces a materially larger fibroblast harm program, rather than a generic response to perturbation or assay stress.

The falsifiable hypothesis is unchanged: in RA type-B synovial FLS coculture, macrophage MerTK inhibition produces a larger FLS inflammatory harm response in the MerTK/CD206-positive context than in the CD206-negative context, after accounting for contributing FLS and macrophage memberships. The clinical-research implication is a context-stratified next-step decision, not a claim of clinical efficacy or that MerTK activation is already a treatment.

The strongest rival explanations are generic LPS/stress signaling, altered cell composition or capture, donor and assay-round confounding, and timing or preparation-label artifacts. They predict either a similar effect under LPS, a signal in FLS-only samples, instability after donor-aware mapping, or no coherent marker-level interaction. [K2] makes an inflammatory macrophage-state explanation consequential; [K3] cautions that FLS inflammatory programs are heterogeneous and niche-linked. [K1] supports the starting perturbational observation, but does not identify this state-by-perturbation interaction. The substantive advance is an explicit identifiability and decision-usefulness test under the actual sparse design.

## Estimand and identifiability gate

Normalize the measured feature rows per sample to log2(CPM + pseudocount), preserving transcript identifiers and without imputing unmeasured genes. Before modeling, emit a manifest with every included row, exposure tier, round, FLS membership, macrophage membership, context, cells, exclusions and mapping scenario.

The primary outcome is a prespecified FLS harm score H: the mean of feature-standardized IL6, MMP1, MMP3 and MMP14. Standardization parameters are frozen from the eligible untreated coculture reference rows; if a required feature is absent from the exact header, the score is unavailable rather than repaired. Secondary outcomes are IL1B, MMP12, MMP13 and each primary component. The context panel is MERTK, MRC1, TREM2 and SPP1; it is descriptive of the state and cannot prove direct target engagement.

The primary estimand is

  Delta = [E(H | exact inhibitor, Mpos) - E(H | untreated, Mpos)]
          - [E(H | exact inhibitor, Mneg) - E(H | untreated, Mneg)]

with donor membership treated as crossed dependence, not as independent observations. Because the prepared design contains only one exact-inhibitor Mneg row and two unambiguous exact-inhibitor Mpos rows, only the R1 SA256A/SA259A pair supplies a complete same-FLS/macrophage-pair context comparison. Therefore the interaction is not automatically identifiable as a population-level causal effect. The solver must first report a rank/overlap gate: treatment-context cells, exact matched counterpart availability, donor membership overlap, and whether Delta can be estimated without using the ambiguous row or the preInhibitor tier. A rank-deficient or overlap-failing primary model returns inconclusive, not a zero effect.

The primary baseline is a transparent paired-score/interaction analysis. Construct within-round matched differences wherever the design explicitly supports the same FLS/macrophage membership, then estimate exact-exposure context contrasts with a small fixed-effects model including context, exposure, their interaction and assay round only when estimable. Represent every contributing FLS and macrophage donor with a membership incidence matrix; use a cross-classified random-intercept or penalized fixed-effect formulation only if its rank is documented. Do not include age or sex as if independently varying when age is round-confounded and all rows are female. Report feature-level effects, H effects, donor-cluster bootstrap intervals, leave-one-round-out and leave-one-membership-out sensitivity, and exact mapping scenarios. Ordinary donor t-tests, cell-level replication and random row splits are prohibited.

The LPS rows are a bounded generic-inflammatory control: compare preLPS with untreated within context where available and ask whether the marker pattern and context contrast are reproduced. FLS-only rows test whether a response appears without macrophages. Neither control establishes MerTK specificity; both can falsify a selective interpretation. Mixed-composition rows test whether changing Mpos:Mneg mixture alone tracks H and are not used as clean context labels.

## Substantive alternative on the same inputs

The alternative is a cross-classified multivariate partial-pooling model, not a different question. On the same log-CPM outcome matrix and the same frozen manifest, model the primary and secondary FLS features jointly with feature-specific intercepts, exposure-tier effect, context effect, exposure-by-context effect, assay-round effect, and crossed FLS/macrophage membership effects. A shared latent FLS-harm factor loads on IL6, MMP1, MMP3 and MMP14; a separate macrophage-context factor uses MERTK, MRC1 and TREM2, with SPP1 and IL1B retained as an inflammatory contrast rather than forced into the resolution factor. Feature-specific residual covariance allows correlated markers to contribute uncertainty jointly.

Its fitting target is the posterior or penalized-likelihood distribution of the same Delta, the two context-specific exposure effects, the shared-harm loading coherence and the probability that Delta exceeds a material threshold. It is not trained to predict a label. Fit only after design-rank checks; if the one-Mneg treated cluster or the ambiguous memberships make the latent model non-identifiable, record the attempted model and defer it rather than replacing it with a simpler unannounced fit. Compare the alternative with the baseline by sign, interval width, donor/mapping stability and marker coherence. The information gained is whether a seemingly single-marker interaction is a correlated FLS program and whether partial pooling makes uncertainty widen or shrink appropriately; the score baseline loses that structure but is more transparent under sparsity.

The held-out unit is an assay round or donor-membership cluster for leave-one-cluster-out sensitivity. With only R1, R2 and R5 and unbalanced treatment, this is a stability diagnostic, not external validation. Use posterior predictive or residual checks for the fitted model, not predictive accuracy as the scientific criterion. The estimated future solver cost is 4–8 CPU cores, less than 16 GiB memory and minutes for this 33-by-11 feature table; full runtime is unmeasured and must be bounded in the solver job. GPU is deferred because the matrix is small and the scientific uncertainty is design/identifiability, not computational scale.

Deferred alternatives are: a neural expression predictor, because predictive improvement cannot answer whether the interaction is causal or decision-useful; a pooled analysis that merges exact inhibitor and preInhibitor, because protocol equivalence is unavailable; ordinary differential expression t-tests, because donor dependence is explicitly unresolved; E-MTAB-8322 target scoring, because the feature namespace lacks a configured map; and GSE95588 validation, because donor-to-GSM mapping is unresolved. These alternatives can be revisited only after the named missing dependency is supplied or the question changes.

## Supportive, adverse and inconclusive rule

Define a material harm threshold delta = 0.5 SD of H, fixed before fitting. A result is supportive only if the exact-exposure Delta is positive and its uncertainty supports Delta > delta (or the equivalent prespecified posterior probability is at least 0.90), the sign is unchanged across admissible donor mappings and round/membership holdouts, the primary marker components are directionally coherent, and neither LPS nor FLS-only controls reproduces the same selective pattern. This supports a balanced orthogonal follow-up to test state-stratified MerTK preservation/activation; it does not justify a clinical treatment or patient-selection claim.

A result is adverse if Delta is nonpositive or Mneg harm is at least as large, or if generic LPS/stress or FLS-only controls reproduce the apparent response. This weakens or refutes the context-selective prioritization rationale and favors generic inflammation, composition or timing explanations; do not advance the state-selective MerTK direction on this evidence.

A result is inconclusive if the rank/overlap gate fails, the mapping scenario changes the sign, uncertainty spans zero or delta, only one donor cluster drives the result, exact and preInhibitor tiers disagree without protocol resolution, or the partial-pooling alternative is non-identifiable. The next decision is to obtain balanced matched Mpos and Mneg perturbations across FLS/macrophage donor pairs, with dose, exposure time, viability, protein/phospho-MerTK, efferocytosis, secreted mediators and direct FLS invasion or matrix-degradation readouts.

Harbor can check source joins, feature selection, estimand computation, uncertainty outputs and whether the conclusion obeys these rules. It cannot establish clinical benefit, direct molecular mechanism, cell viability, spatial localization, therapeutic window or independent biological validity. Those require expert adjudication, orthogonal perturbation such as agonism or genetic loss-of-function, and an independent cohort.
