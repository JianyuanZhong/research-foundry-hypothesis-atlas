# Provisional eICU hypothesis: laboratory-monitoring reduction during persistent instability

Status: provisional and untested. This is a design checkpoint, not a result.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a continuous eICU stay who remain physiologically unstable at a prespecified landmark, a reduction in laboratory-testing intensity during the preceding 12 hours is associated with higher risk of a time-local respiratory or vasopressor/infusion deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond contemporaneously measured severity and recent support. The association should attenuate when end-of-life documentation, discharge/transfer context, and support de-escalation are handled explicitly. A residual association in held-out hospitals would be a monitoring-process signal, not proof that changing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

The configured eICU catalog and table metadata support that the dataset contains longitudinal, timestamped laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, patient, hospital, care-plan, and mortality records. The within-dataset join key is `patientunitstayid`, and `hospitalid` enables hospital-level stratification. The expert seed supplies the opening that testing frequency and missingness may encode hospital practice and may transport poorly. It does not establish that reduced testing predicts deterioration.

The unresolved claim is whether a dynamic laboratory-monitoring reduction, conditional on current instability, predicts near-term deterioration beyond current state. This is a falsifiable observational association, not a causal or mechanistic claim.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use adult `patient` stays with a valid `unitdischargeoffset` greater than 18 hours and a single unit interval. Restrict the primary analysis to continuous ICU stays with no transfer or early discharge before the outcome window. Index at hourly times from 6 through `unitdischargeoffset - 12` hours. Require at least one valid vital observation and one laboratory observation in the preceding 24 hours; label missingness separately from zero testing.

## Exposure

At index time t, count distinct `labname` values and distinct laboratory episodes in (t-12,t], using `labresultoffset`; do not count a revised result as a new test unless its result time is distinct. Compare the prior interval (t-24,t-12] with the current interval. Primary reduction: current distinct-analyte count is zero after a positive prior count, or falls by at least 50% when the prior interval has at least two episodes. Secondary analyses use continuous change and episode count.

## Outcome and competing events

Primary deterioration is a prespecified binary event in (t,t+12]: new or escalated respiratory support from `respiratoryCare` (`respcarestatusoffset`, `airwaytype`, `ventstartoffset`, `ventendoffset`, `highironoxlimit`) or new/escalated vasopressor/infusion from `infusionDrug` (`infusionoffset`, `drugname`, `drugrate`, `infusionrate`, `patientweight`). Use the first qualifying event and do not use post-index laboratory values.

Death before the event is a competing event. Discharge/transfer before the event is also a competing event, with a sensitivity analysis excluding those stays. `apachePatientResult` mortality fields (`actualicumortality`, `actualhospitalmortality`) are secondary outcomes, not substitutes for time-local deterioration.

## Contemporaneous severity and rival explanations

Adjust only for measurements available at or before t: `vitalPeriodic` (`observationoffset`, temperature, SaO2, heart rate, respiration, systemic pressures), respiratory state from `respiratoryCharting` (`respchartoffset`, `respchartentryoffset`, type/value labels), recent infusion/support, age, sex, unit type, and hospital. Do not use future values or post-index labs.

End-of-life is a rival, not a nuisance to be hidden. Use `carePlanEOL` (`cpleolsaveoffset`, `cpleoldiscussionoffset`, `activeupondischarge`) to define prespecified EOL-context strata and a sensitivity analysis excluding documented EOL plans before t. Also model discharge/transfer status/location and recent support de-escalation. These fields are sparse and documentation-dependent; missing EOL documentation is not equivalent to no EOL care.

## Analysis, alternatives, and falsification

Fit a prespecified discrete-time logistic baseline with the same index times, competing-event censoring, hospital fixed/clustered effects, and clustered uncertainty. Compare it with a longitudinal recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard model with time-varying testing intensity and physiologic trajectories. The latter can reveal whether the signal is transient, persistent, or preceded by a trajectory; it is not justified merely for predictive gain.

Use hospital-held-out validation, calibration, discrimination, absolute risk difference, and uncertainty intervals. Negative controls are: (1) testing intervals permuted within hospital while preserving stay length and outcome; (2) a post-index testing exposure, which should not predict the prior deterioration; and (3) a documentation-timestamp control from non-laboratory charting where available. A result that disappears with EOL/discharge adjustment or negative controls supports a documentation/selection explanation.

Supportive: reproducible held-out-hospital association after state and EOL/discharge handling, with negative controls null. Adverse: null, instability, or attenuation after explicit rival adjustment. Inconclusive: wide intervals, sparse events, or inability to distinguish interface interruption from clinical intent. None establishes causality or a recommendation to increase testing.

## Exact bindings and limitations

- `lab.csv.gz`: labid, patientunitstayid, labresultoffset, labname, labresult, labresulttext, labresultrevisedoffset.

- `carePlanEOL.csv.gz`: patientunitstayid, cpleolsaveoffset, cpleoldiscussionoffset, activeupondischarge.

The full source headers and a bounded row sample still need to be inspected before execution; the catalog metadata confirms the listed columns. No full cohort analysis has been run. Missing evidence includes clinical adjudication of deterioration, direct interface-interruption flags, staffing/bed capacity, complete pre-ICU history, and reliable EOL intent. A future solver may fit the baseline and longitudinal alternative on CPU; GPU is not required.

## Method comparison and deliverable

The simple baseline is an adjusted discrete-time logistic model for each 12-hour landmark, with competing-event censoring and hospital clustering. It estimates an interpretable association and uncertainty but loses the ordering and persistence of trajectories. The substantive alternative is a longitudinal recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard model using the same exposure, covariates, and outcome definitions; it can distinguish a transient gap from a persistent monitoring trajectory and reveal whether the association depends on the preceding physiologic path. Both use the same split (hospital-held-out validation), outcome, and uncertainty evaluation. The future deliverable is a fitted baseline, fitted longitudinal alternative, held-out-hospital performance/association estimates with intervals, negative-control results, and a conclusion classified as supportive, adverse, or inconclusive. No result is claimed here.
