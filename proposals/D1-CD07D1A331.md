# Discordant AKI recovery: a concordant decision rule versus creatinine-only recovery

## Question

Among ICU patients with KDIGO stage 2-3 acute kidney injury, does a recovery decision rule that requires concordant improvement in creatinine, urine output and fluid balance identify a lower-risk group than a creatinine-only rule, with better prediction of 7-day AKI recurrence and 28-day mortality?

## Why it matters

Creatinine is the standard marker for AKI recovery, but it can be affected by muscle mass, fluid shifts and measurement timing. Urine output and fluid balance provide complementary information about renal perfusion and function. If a concordant recovery rule improves risk stratification, clinicians should not rely on creatinine alone to declare recovery. This could change when supportive care is escalated and when patients are considered for discharge from the ICU.

## Evidence base

[K1] Haines et al. (2023) demonstrated that eGFR creatinine systematically overestimated kidney function after prolonged critical illness, with a posterior mean difference of 33 ml/min/1.73m² at ICU discharge. Cystatin C better estimated true kidney function because it was unaffected by muscle loss. This supports the biological plausibility that creatinine alone may misclassify recovery.

[K2] KDIGO clinical practice guideline for acute kidney injury (2012) defines AKI stages using creatinine and urine output, and emphasizes that both markers should be considered. This provides the clinical framework for a concordant recovery rule.

[K3] Johnson et al. (2023) described MIMIC-IV, the dataset used here, and documented the availability of creatinine, urine output and fluid balance measurements. This supports the feasibility of the proposed analysis.

## Design

### Population

Adult ICU patients (age >= 18) in MIMIC-IV with at least one KDIGO stage 2-3 AKI episode. AKI is defined per KDIGO: stage 2 if creatinine >= 2x baseline or creatinine >= 1.5x baseline with urine output < 0.5 ml/kg/h for >= 12 h; stage 3 if creatinine >= 3x baseline or creatinine >= 2x baseline with urine output < 0.5 ml/kg/h for >= 12 h, or new RRT.

### Recovery decision rule

At a fixed time point (e.g., 24 h after the AKI nadir), classify recovery as:
- Creatinine-only: creatinine has fallen to < 1.5x baseline.
- Concordant: creatinine has fallen to < 1.5x baseline AND urine output has been >= 0.5 ml/kg/h for >= 12 h AND net fluid balance over the preceding 24 h is <= 0.

### Outcomes

- 7-day AKI recurrence: creatinine rises again to >= 1.5x baseline within 7 days of the recovery time point.
- 28-day mortality: death within 28 days of the recovery time point.

### Analysis

Time-ordered split: first 70% of patients for training, last 30% for testing. Compare the two rules using time-dependent ROC/AUC and calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow test, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope). Report discrimination and calibration for both outcomes.

### Falsification

If the concordant rule does not improve discrimination or calibration over creatinine-only, or if it worsens calibration in the high-risk stratum, the hypothesis is not supported. A null result would suggest that creatinine-only recovery is sufficient for risk stratification in this population.

## Data bindings

- MIMIC-IV: patients, admissions, labevents (creatinine), chartevents (urine output), outputevents (fluid balance), mortality.

## Limitations

- MIMIC-IV does not include cystatin C, so true kidney function cannot be directly measured.
- Urine output and fluid balance are selectively measured and may be missing.
- The analysis is observational and cannot establish causality.
- The concordant rule is a prespecified decision rule, not a learned model; its performance may be limited by the arbitrary thresholds.
