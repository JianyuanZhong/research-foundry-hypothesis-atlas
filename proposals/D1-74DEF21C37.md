# Outcome-blind assay-sequence and negative-control validation for HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B prognosis

## Successor, clinical question, and substantive repair

This is a targeted child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent's frozen adult HCC repeat-TACE population, selected event, B/P/Y clocks, annual forward validation, locked nested-ridge models, and exact noncausal estimand. The primary question remains:

> Among selected repeat-TACE events, does the contemporaneous pre-repeat assay P add short-horizon prognostic information for the subsequent assay Y beyond remote baseline assay B?

The estimand remains assay-specific observed complete-triplet held-out standardized RMSE gain,
`G = (RMSE_0 - RMSE_1) / SD_test(Y)`, with M0 and M1 exactly as frozen below. This is prognosis only; it is not a treatment effect, treatment response, safety, utility, action rule, or clinical benefit analysis.

The substantive repair is a prespecified, outcome-blind evidence-validity layer that separates a genuine pre-outcome prognostic signal from an encounter/recording artifact. The parent already reports broad examination/follow-up/document opportunity proxies, but those proxies can coexist with duplicated or administratively generated assay rows and do not test whether the apparent P increment is specifically dependent on a post-outcome record or on patient-level join leakage. The child adds two independent negative controls and a clinical-event ascertainment tier:

1. **Post-Y temporal-leakage negative control (NC-post).** After the primary Y has been selected, identify the first *distinct* same-assay lab result strictly after Y. Add it only in a secondary control model; it is forbidden from the primary fit. A post-Y value cannot be a legitimate pre-Y predictor. Any reproducible incremental improvement from this variable is evidence of timestamp leakage, encounter-level proxying, duplicate-result contamination, or outcome-driven documentation, not support for P.
2. **Patient-preserving P permutation negative control (NC-perm).** Within each calendar block, arm, and assay, permute P values across different patients while preserving each row's P lead-time and missingness pattern; retain B, Y, event selection, and all denominators. Refit the identical training-only pipeline under 1,000 declared permutations. A materially positive permuted gain indicates join leakage, patient duplication, target leakage, or an overly permissive evaluation implementation. The permutation is a computational null, not a clinical null and cannot replace the primary estimate.
3. **Outcome-blind clinical-event ascertainment tier.** Before reading Y, classify each BP-eligible selected event using only encounter, examination, and document linkage into (A) independently timed follow-up, (B) encounter/document-only follow-up, or (C) no independent follow-up evidence. The primary complete-triplet estimand is unchanged. Report the primary result within tiers and require that any apparently favorable result not be confined solely to a low-specificity administrative tier. These are ascertainment strata, not adjudicated clinical outcomes.

The repair narrows the strongest claim available: even if the primary gain is favorable, it is supportive only when NC-post is null, NC-perm is null, and the signal is not entirely attributable to low-specificity recording. A failure is useful: it would identify an assay-recording or implementation artifact and prevent a clinically misleading prognostic interpretation. No result establishes mortality, hepatic failure, radiographic response, treatment benefit, or transport.

## Outcome-blind ascertainment tier

Before reading any Y values, create the following indicators for every BP-eligible event using only rows and timestamps outside the Y assay outcome itself:

- `timed_exam`: at least one examinations row with valid `开始时间` in (event_time, event_time+72 hours].
- `timed_followup`: at least one additional distinct encounters row with valid `就诊时间` in that interval; exclude the selected repeat encounter itself and deduplicate by composite key.
- `document_presence`: a nonempty clinical_documents row linked to the selected encounter, without treating document text as a clinical event or assigning it a timestamp.
- `post_event_linkage`: existence of either timed_exam or timed_followup, with document_presence recorded separately.

Assign mutually exclusive strata by precedence: `A_timed_exam_and_followup`, `B_timed_exam_only`, `C_timed_followup_only`, `D_document_only`, `E_none`. Do not call any stratum completed follow-up or a clinical outcome. Keep a separate cross-tab for document presence.

Report the funnel selected event -> has_B -> has_P -> BP_eligible -> Y_observed -> complete triplet, overall and by assay, arm, calendar block, and ascertainment stratum. Report counts and Wilson 95% intervals for observation fractions; suppress or mark cells with fewer than five BP-eligible events. Also report pre-Y covariate SMDs across strata. The primary estimand remains pooled and unchanged; tiered analyses are descriptive robustness analyses. The most specific ascertainment interpretation permitted is that the assay was recorded in an encounter with the stated data opportunity.

## Negative-control NC-post: post-outcome timestamp leakage

For each assay-specific BP-eligible row with an observed Y, search the same patient's assay rows in `labs` and identify the earliest finite result strictly after `Y_time` and no later than `Y_time +72 hours`, excluding the selected Y row by source-row identity or deterministic timestamp/value tie key. Call it `Z_post`; do not use a row at or before Y, and do not use a different assay. If multiple same-time rows remain, apply the inherited source-row deterministic tie rule after duplicate audit. Record `Z_post_observed`, its lead after Y, and whether it lies in the same encounter or a distinct encounter.

This control is evaluated only as follows:

1. Define a secondary model `Mpost = M1 + Z_post` with exactly the primary covariates and preprocessing, but fit it only in training complete-triplet rows where Z_post is observed and Y is observed. Evaluate on future rows with both Y and Z_post observed. Do not substitute missing Z_post, and do not alter the primary complete-triplet denominator.
2. Use the same annual origins, patient-disjoint forward blocks, alpha grid, ridge implementation, and patient-cluster paired bootstrap. Report incremental held-out RMSE gain of `Z_post` over M1 and its 95% interval, separately by assay and block; require at least 20 training and 10 future rows with Z_post, otherwise label non-estimable.
3. A **null NC-post** means the point estimate is not materially positive and the interval does not exclude zero in adequately supported blocks, subject to small-sample uncertainty. A positive NC-post is not evidence for the hypothesis. It is a validity warning, and if repeated it invalidates interpretation of the corresponding primary gain until linkage/timestamp adjudication resolves it.
4. Compare same-encounter versus distinct-encounter Z_post descriptively. Same-encounter positivity is particularly concerning for duplicate-result or encounter-level recording leakage, but no clinical causation is inferred.

Because Z_post is defined after reading Y, it is strictly a secondary control and cannot be used for population selection, primary model training, primary support gates, missing-Y bounds, or ascertainment strata. The control's missingness is reported, not imputed.

## Negative-control NC-perm: patient-preserving computational null

For each assay, origin, and future block, use the exact primary design matrix and observed complete-triplet rows, but permute P across patients within the same training calendar block and arm. A P value may be assigned only to a different patient; preserve its observed finite/missing status, standardized value, P-lead hours, and event row. If a patient has multiple events, permute at the patient level so all of that patient's P values remain together only when the permutation unit is the patient; never split a patient's event history. The simplest declared implementation is a random permutation of patient labels within strata `(calendar_year, arm, assay)` followed by a deterministic within-patient event-order matching; if unequal event counts prevent an exact match, stop that stratum rather than reuse or duplicate values.

For each of 1,000 permutations with a recorded seed:

- reconstruct M0 unchanged;
- replace only P in M1, retaining all non-P covariates, training/future membership, Y, B, and the original complete-triplet eligibility;
- repeat the exact training-only preprocessing, inner patient-level alpha selection, forward prediction, RMSE, and standardized gain calculation;
- record permutation gain, the fraction >= the unpermuted primary gain, and any failed replicate.

This is a null for patient linkage and implementation, not a null for clinical prognostic information. It is valid only if the observed P rows are not duplicated across patients, if the permutation preserves the declared strata, and if no outcome or Y-derived field enters the permutation. A 95th-percentile permutation gain above zero, a permutation distribution centered materially above zero, or a large fraction of failed permutations is a computational/selection warning. A nominally favorable primary gain with a similarly favorable permutation distribution is not supportive evidence. Do not use the permutation p-value as a clinical hypothesis-test claim; report it as a diagnostic.

For small strata, exact patient counts and event-count matching are required. If fewer than 10 eligible patients exist in a stratum, report NC-perm non-estimable rather than permuting events. Use the same fixed original test SD denominator for each permutation's standardized gain. The permutation layer does not modify deterministic missing-Y bounds.

## Falsification and interpretation contract

### Supportive pattern

A locally supportive prognostic result requires all of the following in an adequately supported future block: denominator and clock assertions pass; complete-triplet and BP arm gates pass; primary G is estimable; deterministic missing-Y Delta bounds remain strictly positive over the declared training-range scenario; ascertainment is not confined to `D_document_only`/`E_none`; NC-post is nonpositive/non-warning; NC-perm is centered near zero and does not reproduce the observed gain; and assay-semantic diagnostics show no unexplained recording break. Even then, the claim is only that P is associated with better held-out prediction of Y in this selected calendar mixture. It does not establish transport, treatment response, or benefit.

### Adverse or invalidating pattern

A nonpositive primary gain, repeated M1 degradation, or a bounded Delta sign reversal is adverse for the prognostic claim. A positive NC-post, positive NC-perm distribution, material gain confined to low-specificity ascertainment tiers, patient duplicate leakage, future Y outside the training range, nonfinite predictions, or failed exact permutation matching is a validity warning or computationally inconclusive result, not a positive clinical finding. A favorable observed-case result with a strong observation gradient or Delta crossing zero remains selection-sensitive.

### Inconclusive pattern

Failed arm/event gates, sparse post-Y controls, fewer than 10 permutation patients, non-estimable SD/bootstrap, unsupported or degenerate training range, low transport ESS, marked specimen-type/value-scale drift, or missing unit/reference metadata yield inconclusive evidence. Do not silently pool blocks, assays, strata, or control denominators.

## Compiler and verifier requirements

The compiler must implement and emit machine-checkable artifacts for: source and schema hashes; duplicate-safe joins; exact denominator/event assertions; outcome-blind ascertainment labels; primary frozen predictions and fixed-denominator bootstrap; deterministic missing-Y bounds; Z_post exclusion from all primary paths; NC-post support counts and held-out results; patient-level NC-perm seeds, strata, exact matching, failures, and gain distribution; and assay-semantic audit tables.

The verifier must test at least these adversarial cases:

1. A favorable primary G with a positive NC-post is labeled leakage-warning, not supportive.
2. A favorable primary G reproduced by NC-perm is labeled computationally invalid/selection-sensitive, not supportive.
3. Z_post cannot affect BP eligibility, missing-Y bounds, support gates, or primary training.
4. A document without a native timestamp cannot be placed in a Y or Z_post window.
5. A same-assay row at or before Y cannot be called Z_post; the selected Y row cannot be reused as Z_post.
6. Permutation never crosses assay, arm, calendar stratum, or patient-count mismatch; failed matching is non-estimable, not silently repaired.
7. The primary SD denominator is the original observed test SD for bootstrap and permutation, and zero/nonfinite SD yields non-estimable G.
8. Negative controls cannot be described as clinical null tests or causal tests.
9. Missing Y is never recoded as a negative event, and ascertainment opportunity is never called completed clinical follow-up.
10. All conclusions preserve adverse, selection-sensitive, unsupported, and inconclusive branches and never upgrade prognostic association to causality, utility, safety, transport, or patient benefit.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes, schema/column presence, duplicate-safe linkage, timestamps, event reconstruction, outcome-blind strata, finite parsing, negative-control isolation, permutation bookkeeping, and arithmetic. Clinical claims about assay units, specimen adequacy, disease severity, imaging response, treatment intent/administration, mortality, hepatic failure, external-care capture, completed follow-up, and patient benefit require laboratory metadata, expert adjudication, or an independent temporal/prospective cohort.
