# Testing-process signals and hospital transportability of early-ICU mortality models

Status: complete design draft; no study result is claimed here. This branch evolves the assessed expert seed parent `[prior hypothesis]`.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The unresolved question is whether laboratory-result presence and frequency improve ICU-mortality prediction because they reveal patient state, or because they encode a hospital's testing and documentation practice. Missing indicators can carry risk information in EHR prediction, but shifts in those patterns may damage transportability [K2]. Rich, time-structured EHR representations can predict mortality at more than one center [K1], while external deployment can still encounter major data-availability differences and require local adaptation [K3]. None of those inspected works isolates a laboratory-process contribution under a hospital-held-out evaluation in a common ICU database.

Leading explanation: process-aware models exploit hospital-specific monitoring intensity, analyte availability, and result timing. Their apparent internal advantage will therefore shrink more on unseen hospitals, with greater calibration and decision-curve deterioration, than a value-only model.

Strong rival: process signals are a stable proxy for latent acuity. If patients needing more tests are consistently sicker for reasons not captured by the observed values, process features may retain or improve performance and calibration on held-out hospitals. Other plausible artifacts are result-interface loss, delayed revision, unit mix, and conditioning on patients still present at 6 hours. The proposed design can test descriptive prediction and transportability, not which mechanism caused a result or whether changing testing would change mortality.

Falsifiable hypothesis: among adult first ICU admissions alive and under observation at 6 hours, the process-aware model (same learner and value inputs plus prespecified testing-process features) has a larger internal-to-hospital-held-out degradation in discrimination and/or calibration than the capacity-matched value-only model. A stronger supportive result requires both (i) a positive paired degradation contrast and (ii) worse held-out decision-curve net benefit for the process-aware model at prespecified thresholds. The hypothesis is falsified if the process-aware model is no less transportable and has no adverse calibration or utility pattern. A noisy or discordant result is inconclusive.

Clinical importance: a mortality score that performs well only when a target hospital shares the development site's testing workflow can mis-rank patients or produce unsafe absolute risks. Evidence of this failure would support excluding process features, restricting deployment to harmonized feeds, or requiring site validation and recalibration. Evidence of stable process-aware performance would justify further prospective workflow auditing, not an assumption that testing causes or prevents death.

## Actual scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct leakage-safe early-ICU features, fit three prespecified models on hospital-held-out folds, and output patient-level predictions plus per-hospital metrics and uncertainty. Completion requires:

1. a frozen cohort manifest with the exact inclusion/exclusion counts and hospital-fold assignment;
2. model predictions for every eligible held-out stay from V-Logit, V-GBM, and VP-GBM;
3. paired internal-versus-hospital-held-out AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, and decision-curve net benefit;
4. cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals for the degradation contrasts;
5. an interpretation report that labels results supportive, adverse, or inconclusive without converting them into causal or clinical-benefit claims.

No fitted result is present in this proposal.

## Population, time zero, horizon, and outcome

Population: all eICU patient rows with `unitvisitnumber=1` and `unitstaytype=admit`, age at least 18 (numeric ages; `> 89` is retained as an adult category), and a nonmissing `hospitalid`, `patientunitstayid`, `unitdischargestatus`, and `unitdischargeoffset`. Exclude ICU stays with `unitdischargeoffset < 360` minutes because a full 6-hour feature window is unavailable, duplicate patient-unit rows, and rows with an unparseable age. Preserve unknown gender/ethnicity as explicit categories rather than complete-case filtering. The primary estimand is conditional on being alive and under ICU observation at 6 hours; it is not an estimate for patients who die or leave the ICU before 6 hours.

Time zero is ICU admission. Use eICU offsets in minutes relative to ICU admission, as documented in the eICU metadata. The prediction landmark is 360 minutes after ICU admission. The horizon is the remainder of that ICU stay, ending at `unitdischargeoffset`. Outcome `Y=1` is `patient.unitdischargestatus == "Expired"`; `Y=0` is an ICU discharge with a non-Expired status. This is all-cause ICU mortality, not 24-hour mortality, because the configured `patient` table has discharge status and offset but no death timestamp. Audit, never predictor: compare the primary label with `apachePatientResult.actualicumortality` by `patientunitstayid`; resolve discordances descriptively and do not silently replace the primary label.

## Feature construction

Derivation must be performed separately inside each outer training fold, then frozen for its held-out hospitals.

1. Define an analyte key as normalized `labname` plus `labmeasurenamesystem`; if the system is blank, use `labmeasurenameinterface`. Parse finite numeric `labresult`. On the outer-training hospitals only, retain the 30 analyte keys with the largest number of distinct eligible patientunitstayids, subject to at least 1% patient coverage. If fewer than 30 meet the rule, retain all and report the count. This is a prespecified, reproducible coverage rule, not test-set feature selection.
2. For each selected analyte at the 6-hour landmark, calculate the last valid numeric value and the time of that value. Values are not carried forward from outside the 0--360-minute window. If absent, V-Logit and V-GBM use a training-fold median imputation learned from the analyte values; missing values do not receive missing indicators in either value-only model.
3. Static value-only covariates are age, gender, ethnicity, and admissionweight. Keep the category levels and imputation rules learned in training. Do not use hospitalid, hospital metadata, unitdischarge fields, Apache scores, diagnosis text, notes, therapies, or any post-landmark event.
4. Process features for VP-GBM are added to the same value inputs: per-analyte result-present indicator; number of valid results; number of distinct result offsets; minutes from ICU admission to first and last result; and overall valid-lab row count and distinct-offset count. Counts are based only on rows passing the revision rule. These are proxies for observed measurement and recording intensity, not confirmed clinician orders.
5. Use no hospital identifier or site-level process summary as a feature. A process-aware model may therefore use patient-level process patterns but cannot trivially memorize a hospital label.

## Models and method alternatives

Primary nested comparison:

- V-GBM: histogram gradient boosting classifier with fixed, prespecified hyperparameters and only value features (static covariates plus 30 imputed latest lab values).
- VP-GBM: the identical histogram gradient boosting implementation, preprocessing, class handling, and hyperparameters, with the process features above added. The paired V-GBM versus VP-GBM contrast isolates the scientific contribution of process signals from the learner family.
- V-Logit: elastic-net logistic regression with standardized imputed values and fixed regularization, included as the transparent simple clinical baseline. It asks how the process comparison relates to a familiar additive model, but it is not the primary attribution contrast.

Do not select a model by held-out hospital performance. Fit each model on the outer-training hospitals; use a fixed class-weight policy derived from training prevalence only (or no class weighting if the implementation's probabilistic loss is used; record the chosen policy before execution). Any preprocessing, analyte selection, imputation, and category handling is fit within training data. Use the same random seed and fixed parameters for paired models. The learned alternative can reveal nonlinear value-process interactions and whether process signals are useful only when the local workflow is represented; the simple baseline loses those interactions but is easier to inspect and less exposed to shortcut learning.

## Split, analysis, uncertainty, and utility

Primary split: include only hospitals with at least 100 eligible primary-cohort stays after the 6-hour rule. Sort hospital IDs by SHA-256 of the string `"eicu-transport-v1\0"+hospitalid`, then assign contiguous hospitals to five folds by greedy balancing of eligible-stay counts, without using outcomes or features. Each fold is held out once; all stays from a hospital remain in one fold. If fewer than five eligible hospitals remain, stop as infeasible rather than substitute patient-level splitting. A patient-level 80/20 split within each outer training set is an internal reference only; it must not be presented as external validation.

For each outer fold, fit on four hospital folds and produce predictions for (a) the held-out hospitals and (b) an internal 20% patient-stratified sample drawn from the four training folds, with the internal split fixed by patientunitstayid hash and no reuse in fitting. Compute:

- discrimination: AUROC primary; AUPRC secondary;
- overall accuracy of probabilities: Brier score primary calibration metric;
- calibration: intercept, slope, calibration-in-the-large, and reliability plots, all without site-specific updating;
- clinical utility: decision-curve net benefit at fixed risk thresholds 5%, 10%, and 20%, compared with treat-none and treat-all. The modeled action is “trigger senior review / goals-of-care and monitoring review,” not a treatment recommendation.

The main estimands are paired contrasts aggregated over outer folds:
`D_AUC = (AUC_VP,internal - AUC_VP,external) - (AUC_V,internal - AUC_V,external)`.
For Brier score and absolute calibration-slope error, define the analogous external-minus-internal deterioration contrast so positive means greater deterioration for VP. Also report direct held-out VP-minus-V differences and per-hospital paired values; the degradation contrast is not a causal effect.

Use a hospital-cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples of eligible hospitals with replacement, preserving all patient predictions within each sampled hospital) for 95% confidence intervals of contrasts, direct metric differences, and net-benefit differences. Report fold and hospital counts, event prevalence, and effective sample sizes. Do not use a patient bootstrap as the sole uncertainty estimate. Because hospital folds are the transportability units, interpret wide intervals as limited evidence rather than a null finding.

## Falsification, interpretation, and limits

Supportive: the lower 95% CI for `D_AUC` is above zero and the process model shows either greater Brier deterioration / calibration-slope error or lower held-out net benefit at at least two of the three thresholds. This supports a descriptive workflow-dependence claim.

Adverse to the hypothesis: the upper 95% CI for `D_AUC` is below zero, with no worse held-out calibration or net benefit for VP-GBM; or the process model is consistently at least as transportable and clinically useful. This would support revising the claim that process signals are a transportability liability, not prove they are biologically stable.

Inconclusive: the interval spans zero, there are too few eligible hospitals/events, calibration and discrimination disagree without a prespecified decision interpretation, or revision filtering makes the analyte set unstable. The result should not be converted into evidence for or against an intervention.

Sensitivity/falsification checks, each planned before seeing outcomes: (1) use 3-hour and 12-hour windows only as secondary checks, preserving the same leakage rules; (2) repeat with no revision-time filter and label it a leakage-sensitivity analysis; (3) stratify results by hospital teaching status and region without fitting site features; (4) compare a process-only model to test whether process signals alone predict mortality; (5) report results after removing stays with end-of-life limitation indicators only if a validated timing field is available, otherwise mark this unavailable. A process-only model that transports poorly while value-only remains stable is a particularly direct shortcut falsification. A process-only model that transports well would challenge the workflow-shortcut explanation but still would not identify a mechanism.

This is descriptive transportability, not causal inference. Hospital-held-out evaluation does not randomize testing practice; residual differences can reflect case mix, severity, care pathways, interface completeness, discharge selection, or label differences. The source cannot distinguish “not ordered” from “ordered but not recorded,” does not provide clinician rationale, and lacks a death timestamp. Clinical adjudication would be needed to interpret individual test omissions, end-of-life decisions, interface outages, and whether an alert changes care or outcomes. Prospective silent deployment and an impact study would be required before clinical use.

## Feasibility and compute

The configured eICU source is read-only. A bounded 4-CPU/16-GiB diagnostic was submitted to scan the lab gzip and summarize first-window support; its status and output must be preserved with the candidate. The full solver should use CPU: this is a tabular feature-extraction and three-model fitting study, not a sequence neural-network workload. Planning estimate (not measured): 16 CPUs, 64--128 GiB, and 2--6 hours for one complete five-fold run including the lab scan and 2,000-replicate cluster bootstrap; reruns and robustness checks fit within the configured 8-hour / 16-CPU / 256-GiB solver-planning envelope. No GPU is required. The estimate is unverified until the readiness job measures feature extraction and one fold; it must not be silently replaced by a smaller scientific question.

Alternative considered and deferred: a temporal neural model over every lab event could retain ordering and nonlinear value-process interactions, but it would make process attribution less transparent and is unnecessary until the paired tabular comparison establishes a transportability signal. The learned VP-GBM is the chosen substantive alternative because it is nonlinear, capacity-matched to V-GBM, interpretable enough for process ablation, and feasible on CPU. Revisit the temporal model only if the top-30 summary discards clinically relevant ordering and a bounded sequence diagnostic shows adequate events per hospital; current data alone do not establish that need.

## Closest inspected works and bibliography

[K1] Rajkomar et al. supports multi-center EHR mortality prediction with rich temporally structured records, but does not isolate testing-process features or prove clinical benefit. The present design adds a prespecified lab-process ablation and hospital-held-out calibration/utility analysis.

[K2] Gao et al. supports that missing indicators can be predictive and warns that shifts in EHR missingness may damage transportability. It studies CLABSI and imputation in one institution; the present design tests ICU mortality across held-out hospitals and separates value-only from process-aware features.

[K3] Hadler et al. shows that external deployment can preserve discrimination while local adaptation improves calibration in the presence of missing data. It concerns interhospital transfer and a different model/outcome; the present design tests within-eICU hospital transport without site-specific recalibration.
