## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The parent resolved one ambiguity—whether its recorded-vital process increment persists across an absolute threshold/persistence label and a source-scale trajectory label—but both labels still come from `vitalPeriodic`/`vitalAperiodic`. A common monitor/interface, observation schedule, or workflow could therefore create agreement without a patient-linked transition. eICU has a separate respiratory-care stream with its own patient key and respiratory time fields. It can test whether the paired vital labels coincide with a separately recorded support-state change.

The strongest claim currently supported by the parent is only that early recorded state/process information may be associated with later recorded vital transitions under two constructions. It does not establish physiologic deterioration, respiratory failure, a treatment effect, or a useful alert. The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable:

**Hypothesis.** Among the parent’s S6 ICU stays, the early process increment and both paired recorded-vital labels will show convergent, hospital-transportable support for a later *recorded invasive respiratory-support initiation* in the independent respiratory-care stream, after fixed state T, competing ICU exits, future-observation audits, and inherited process/state nulls. Convergence requires that the vital labels’ time-local agreement with respiratory-support initiation and the P increment for the respiratory endpoint are directionally coherent in both the elastic-net and capacity-matched learned model, survive observation and discharge-time controls, and are not confined to one hospital. If the vital labels predict only respiratory documentation availability, or if convergence disappears when respiratory rows are audited by stratum and source time, the apparent same-stream signal is more plausibly threshold, observation, interface, or workflow artifact.

This is descriptive prognostic/measurement research. A respiratory-support initiation is a care-state event, not a physiologic deterioration time and not an intervention effect. The experiment cannot determine whether support was clinically indicated or beneficial.

### Why this is a substantive advance

A respiratory-care transition is not another threshold or another learner. It is a separately recorded operational state change with different fields, timestamp names, and likely workflow. Its possible outcomes change the next scientific decision:

- convergence across vital and respiratory streams supports a reproducible cross-stream operational event and justifies clinical adjudication of a subset;
- vital-only convergence, respiratory-only prediction, or dependence on missingness points to recording/observation mechanisms;
- no convergence despite adequate support weakens the interpretation of the parent vital labels as a general transition construct, while leaving a bounded recorded-vital association possible.

The experiment therefore separates cross-stream reproducibility from same-stream semantic and observation artifacts. It does not convert an observational respiratory record into a treatment recommendation.

## Respiratory-care state and endpoint definitions

The primary endpoint is deliberately named `R_inv_start_recorded`, not respiratory failure, deterioration, intubation benefit, or treatment need.

### Auditable interval construction

From `respiratoryCare`, retain raw rows and create a row-level valid interval only when `ventstartoffset` is finite and, if `ventendoffset` is finite, `ventendoffset > ventstartoffset`. The interval is [`ventstartoffset`, `ventendoffset`) when the end is valid. If the end is missing, the interval is right-censored at ICU exit/720 for state-audit purposes, never treated as a verified stop. Preserve invalid, duplicate, same-offset, and contradictory rows in an audit rather than repairing them into outcomes.

`R_inv_start_recorded` is the earliest finite `ventstartoffset` strictly in (360,720] and before recorded ICU exit for which no valid invasive interval from the same stay covers the immediately preceding minute. If multiple respiratoryCare rows have the same start, retain one event time and all row IDs. An event at exactly 360 is excluded from follow-up. A start copied across duplicate rows is one event, not multiple transitions.

A secondary `R_inv_stop_recorded` is the earliest valid `ventendoffset` in (360,720] whose interval was active immediately before the end. It is analyzed as a separate weaning/stop recording and is not combined with initiation in the primary endpoint. No respiratory event is constructed from `ventstartoffset` or `ventendoffset` outside the stated boundaries, from a discharge row, or from a missing end.

### Source-state strata

At the 360-minute landmark, assign mutually exclusive strata using only pre-landmark respiratory records:

1. `S_inv_active`: a valid respiratoryCare interval covers 360.
2. `S_none_support_observed`: at least one respiratoryCare or respiratoryCharting row is present in [0,360], no valid invasive interval covers 360, and any source-coded airway state is either an explicitly audited no-support state or absent without an unresolved contradictory field. This means no auditable invasive support at 360, not physiologic normality.
3. `S_nonrespiratory_care_record`: neither respiratoryCare nor respiratoryCharting has a row in [0,360]. This is “no respiratory-care record,” not a clinical claim that the patient is non-respiratory; it remains an observation-incomplete stratum.
4. `S_unknown_support`: respiratory rows exist but interval fields are invalid/contradictory, or `airwaytype` contains an unrecognized or conflicting state that prevents a defensible classification.

An optional `S_noninvasive_or_oxygen` sub-stratum is created only if a pre-outcome source dictionary or frozen raw-value audit provides unambiguous `airwaytype` categories. The mapping must be fixed before looking at endpoint or model results, list every raw category and its frequency, and send unrecognized values to `S_unknown_support`. Do not infer oxygen, CPAP, BiPAP, high-flow, airway, or invasive status from an abbreviation or from `airwaysize`, `airwayposition`, `cuffpressure`, or an apparently meaningful string alone. If the available dictionary cannot establish semantics, this sub-stratum and all airwaytype-derived transitions are deferred; the numeric vent-interval endpoint remains the primary test.

The primary respiratory analysis includes all S6 stays with the four strata reported. A pre-specified sensitivity restricts to `S_none_support_observed` and `S_inv_active`, where the starting state is auditable. `S_nonrespiratory_care_record` and `S_unknown_support` are never silently pooled with no-support and never recoded as event-free.

- **support-state permutation:** within hospital, starting stratum, T-risk cells, and exit-time bands (360–480, 480–720, >720), permute complete P vectors while retaining D labels, respiratory states, and exits.
- **discharge-time selection null:** preserve R and D endpoints but permute P within hospital/T-risk/exit-time cells, as in the parent.
- **cross-stream schedule null:** preserve each stay’s vital and respiratory observation schedules, hospital, starting stratum, and exit time, then permute D labels within training-fold state/coverage strata. This tests whether apparent D/R timing is explained by schedule alignment; it does not prove exchangeability.
- **source-time null:** repeat cross-stream concordance using respiratory clinical-entry and documentation-entry offsets separately. A large difference between `respchartoffset` and `respchartentryoffset` is a documentation-lag warning, not evidence for patient physiology.
- **interval-integrity null:** remove invalid/duplicate/contradictory respiratoryCare intervals and report how many events are lost; do not repair them toward support.
- **stratum/source stability:** report R and D results separately in `S_inv_active`, `S_none_support_observed`, `S_nonrespiratory_care_record`, and `S_unknown_support`, and by hospital. Never pool periodic and aperiodic vital fields into one channel.

Leading explanations and predictions are explicit:

- **Cross-stream operational event:** D_thr and D_traj each precede or co-occur with R_inv_start, P adds a stable increment for R after T, and the pattern survives schedule/discharge nulls across hospitals.
- **Same-stream vital threshold/trajectory artifact:** D labels agree with one another but have weak or absent R convergence; D prediction collapses under offset or measurement nulls; or only the threshold label converges.
- **Common observation/interface workflow:** P predicts M_R or M_D flags and apparent D/R timing, but endpoint increments disappear after observability restriction/weighting or are concentrated in one hospital or documentation-time convention.
- **Severity without mechanism:** T explains R and D while P adds little; this is a descriptive state association, not evidence that process observations caused support or that support changed outcomes.
- **Care action is not physiology:** R convergence is positive, but it cannot distinguish respiratory need from clinician decision, protocol, availability, or end-of-life practice.

## Interpretation and pre-specified decision criteria

Supportive results require all gates:

1. Source audit verifies numeric respiratory interval fields, offset ordering, duplicate semantics, and enough valid event support; any airwaytype/chart vocabulary used is frozen and auditable. At least five viable hospitals remain, with per-hospital event/exit and positivity reporting.
2. In both D_thr and D_traj, (C_k(120)) exceeds the schedule-stratified cross-stream null with a paired 95% interval excluding zero in the estimable analysis, is not driven by one hospital, and remains directionally coherent in the ±120-minute sensitivity.
3. The P increment for R after T is directionally coherent in elastic-net and learned models, with a paired interval excluding the parent’s 0.01 AUC negligible margin where AUC is estimable and a coherent calibration or 10% net-benefit contrast. The D endpoint increments remain interpretable under the same folds.
4. The pattern is not explained by M_R/M_D availability, observation weighting, the trajectory-state null, the discharge-time null, documentation-entry timing, or invalid-interval removal.
5. Respiratory charting, where a valid support dictionary exists, corroborates a meaningful fraction of R starts with stable hospital-specific behavior; if charting semantics are unavailable, the claim is limited to respiratoryCare interval records.

Support would mean: “the parent’s early process/state association and both paired recorded-vital labels are convergent with a later independently recorded respiratory-care support initiation across held-out eICU hospitals.” It would not mean the patient physiologically deteriorated, that respiratory support was indicated, that monitoring caused treatment, that treatment improved mortality, or that an alert should be deployed.

Adverse results include adequate respiratory support and observation with no D/R convergence after T; convergence only for D_thr with absent or unstable D_traj support; P predicting only availability; or apparent convergence collapsing under source-time, schedule, discharge-time, or hospital-held-out nulls. These weaken the parent’s vital-transition interpretation but do not erase a valid descriptive recording association.

Results are inconclusive/deferral rather than negative when valid support events are too rare, fewer than five hospitals remain, intervals are semantically unresolved, chart vocabulary cannot be audited, observation positivity is poor, event time is documentation-lag dominated, uncertainty spans meaningful and negligible margins, or the respiratory endpoint is only available in a selected stratum. No clinical endpoint adjudication follows an inconclusive computation.

The smallest next decision is:

- revise toward source/interface and observation-workflow validation if availability or documentation timing dominates;
- defer or abandon a physiologic-transition interpretation if respiratory interval or category semantics fail;
