# Recorded-support evidence versus unobserved support at first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly fit an auditable, leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study. It must produce:

1. `cohort_audit.parquet`, one row per eligible `subject_id,hadm_id,stay_id`, with first-stay selection, inclusion/exclusion flags, temporal-integrity checks, and event counts;
2. `hourly_features.parquet`, twelve one-hour rows in `[outtime-12 hours,outtime)`, with chart-time physiology, interval support evidence, source-specific masks, and no post-landmark fields;
3. `support_observation_audit.parquet`, which distinguishes positive support evidence, observed non-positive values, uninformative absence of a row, contradictory evidence, and invalid intervals in each domain and half-window;
4. locked test-set predictions and 6-, 24-, and 48-hour standardized cumulative-incidence functions (CIFs) for mutually exclusive first ICU return, in-hospital death, and alive hospital discharge;
5. the primary recorded-evidence contrast, the observation/process-adjusted contrast, and a prespecified sensitivity curve over unrecorded-support misclassification parameters;
6. comparison of a transparent cause-specific discrete-time baseline with a same-input, two-domain observation-aware hidden semi-Markov alternative, including calibration, IPCW Brier scores, bootstrap uncertainty, overlap, and latent-state diagnostics;
7. all predeclared falsifications and an interpretation file mapping each conclusion to an output row, estimate, interval, event count, and sensitivity result.

Completion means these estimates, uncertainty intervals, diagnostics, and interpretation mapping have been newly fitted/estimated on a locked patient-level test set. It does not mean that a clinical policy, bedside readiness label, or causal effect has been established. No fitted result is asserted in this proposal.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent correctly separates physiology, support documentation, and observation intensity, but its phrase “no support record in the final half” can still be read as support cessation. In MIMIC, a missing respiratory chart row does not prove that oxygen/ventilation was absent, and absence of an inputevents interval does not prove that vasoactive support was absent. Measurement intensity and discharge workflow can be caused by the same unobserved clinical state; conditioning on their observed proxies cannot identify that state. Thus, an association that remains after observation adjustment need not be physiologic, and an association that disappears need not be an artifact.

The strongest supported claim before fitting is narrower: the available tables contain dated, linked observations and support-related records from which a *recorded-support evidence* prognostic estimand can be constructed. The local archive/header and dictionary inspection establishes availability of the required fields and item IDs, not an association or clinical truth. The demonstrations and public literature motivate the importance of longitudinal ordering and EHR observation processes, but are not reproduction targets; no claim is made about unavailable full methods of the cancer demonstration.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults at the first ICU outtime, does a recent *recorded* withdrawal pattern, compared with durable *recorded-off evidence*, predict the competing first transition to ICU return, death, or alive hospital discharge, and does that contrast remain materially different from zero under prespecified plausible levels of unrecorded support?

The primary claim is deliberately about recorded evidence. A physiology-like interpretation is only considered supported if the result persists across observation/process adjustment, source-channel definitions, and a transparent range of missing-support assumptions. This is prognostic, not causal: stopping support is not an intervention in this analysis.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

Early ICU return and death after an ICU-to-ward transition are consequential outcomes. A reliable record-based signal could identify which transitions merit prospective reassessment; a null or sensitivity-wide result would prevent clinicians from treating sparse “off-support” documentation as evidence of recovery. The advance is not a small predictive gain. It separates three claims that the parent leaves partially entangled:

- what the record positively documents;
- what is observed but non-positive versus simply not observed; and
- what, if anything, can be inferred about an unrecorded support state.

The main scientific result is a calibrated competing-event contrast plus its robustness frontier. If the sign or clinical interpretation changes across plausible missingness values, the correct conclusion is non-identification/inconclusive evidence, not a physiology claim.

## Exposure and observation ledger

Create separate source-specific masks. Physiology is a recorded value, summarized per hour by median, last value, range, slope, and final-minus-preceding-half change when present. Keep arterial and NIBP MAP separate, retain source channels, and convert Fahrenheit only with an auditable rule.

A positive respiratory-support evidence flag is present when a valid charted value documents O2 flow >2 L/min, FiO2 >0.21, PEEP >0, or a valid procedure interval corroborates intubation/NIV. A positive vasoactive flag requires a valid overlap interval for a listed inputevent. Procedure rows corroborate transitions and do not establish continuous support.

For every domain and half-window, classify each hour as:

- positive recorded support evidence;
- observed non-positive value (respiratory channel observed at/below its operational threshold);
- no qualifying row/interval (uninformative absence);
- contradictory source evidence; or
- invalid/out-of-window timing.

The primary four-category exposure is restricted to adequate coverage and remains explicitly record-based:

- durable recorded-off evidence: no positive evidence in either half, with at least two respiratory observation bins per half and at least two MAP observation bins per half;
- recent respiratory withdrawal evidence: positive respiratory evidence in the preceding half, none in the final half, with the same domain coverage;
- recent vasoactive cessation evidence: a valid vasoactive interval in the preceding half, none in the final half, with MAP coverage;
- both recent withdrawal evidence: both domain-specific definitions.

“None in a half” means no *positive recorded evidence*, not continuous absence. Retain active/residual, uninformative, discordant, and inadequate-coverage strata in counts and secondary analyses; do not force them into durable recovery. Record the last positive-evidence time as a capped recorded buffer, but do not call it support-free time.

Observation/process covariates include per-domain observed-bin fraction, maximum gap, row counts, distinct source channels, final/preceding coverage, support interval-record counts, contradictory-time indicators, pre-index transfer count/last careunit, admission type/location, first careunit, ICU LOS, age, gender, and anchor-year group. These are adjustment variables or process descriptors, not latent physiology.

## Misclassification sensitivity that repairs interpretation

The observed ledger cannot identify the true support state. The solver must therefore report, not hide, a missing-support sensitivity.

For each domain (d) and half (h), define (q_{dh}) as the probability that an uninformative no-row hour is actually positive support. The primary recorded-evidence analysis sets (q=0) only as a label for the observed-data estimand, not as a belief that support was absent. Sensitivity analyses use a locked grid (q_{dh}in{0,0.10,0.25,0.50,0.75,1.00}), with a parsimonious shared-(q) curve and a conservative domain/half-specific grid. Observed non-positive respiratory values are not randomized into support; the solver must report this asymmetry. Because vasoactive “no interval” is especially weak evidence, include a prespecified higher-(q) vasoactive scenario. Report how many exposure labels change, effective sample size, overlap, and the standardized CIF contrast at every grid point.

The primary interpretation gate is a robustness frontier: report the smallest (max q_{dh}) at which the sign, interval compatibility with zero, or clinical interpretation changes. Do not choose (q) after seeing outcomes. A pattern that is robust only at (q=0), or whose frontier lies inside a clinically plausible range justified from coverage strata, is adverse/inconclusive for a physiology interpretation. This is a sensitivity analysis under assumptions, not an identified correction. No local data establish the true (q); expert review of support documentation or prospective device-level data would be required.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods use the same cohort, static covariates, twelve-hour channel matrix, support-evidence flags, and observation masks. The baseline receives a fixed summary map; the alternative receives the hourly ordering. Thresholds, item mapping, imputation, exposure recoding, and fitting are training/validation-only.

Simple baseline: pooled cause-specific discrete-time hazards at 1, 6, 24, and 48 hours for ICU return, death, and alive discharge. Predictors are the four recorded-evidence categories, final/preceding physiology summaries, source-specific positive/non-positive/uninformative masks, observation/process fields, and admission/context covariates. Fit nested models for (a) recorded physiology/support evidence, (b) observation masks only, (c) process/context only, and (d) the full observed-data model. Standardize predictions to the common eligible population to estimate CIFs and contrasts. Refit the full baseline across the locked (q) recodings. Report hazard ratios only as secondary descriptors; the clinical estimand is absolute competing-event CIF.

Scientifically informative alternative: a two-domain factorized hidden semi-Markov model over the twelve hourly bins. Separate respiratory and hemodynamic latent chains have duration distributions and constrained transitions representing persistence and withdrawal ordering. Emissions use the same physiologic values and support evidence. An explicit observation model uses channel masks, row counts, and history to model whether evidence is recorded. The event head estimates post-outtime cause-specific hazards conditional on posterior state uncertainty and context. Fit emissions, transitions, observation model, and event head on training data; choose state number, duration restrictions, regularization, and any sensitivity-grid implementation on validation; lock test posteriors and CIFs. Refit/evaluate the same (q) grid, or marginalize latent states under each fixed (q) assumption.

The alternative can reveal ordered duration and uncertainty about an unobserved state that fixed summaries lose. It is scientifically useful only if state assignments have reproducible domain/order meaning, observation-model diagnostics are acceptable, and the sensitivity frontier is not an artifact of high posterior entropy. If state entropy is high, split stability fails, or the model changes only discrimination without changing the recorded-versus-unobserved interpretation, the baseline is the reportable result. A flexible GRU/TCN is deferred because it would add sequence capacity without solving the missing-support identifiability problem; revisit only after the HSMM demonstrates stable order information. A causal discharge-policy model, note-language readiness model, renal endpoint branch, waveform model, and imaging model are deferred because intent/readiness labels, validated text labels, adjudicated renal recovery, raw waveforms, and MIMIC-CXR images are unavailable or outside this estimand.

## Evaluation, uncertainty, and falsification

Primary outputs are standardized 6-, 24-, and 48-hour CIF contrasts for each event, with patient-bootstrap 95% intervals, event counts, positivity/effective sample size, and calibration intercept/slope. Use IPCW Brier scores and calibration plots as prediction diagnostics; AUROC is secondary. Bootstrap subjects, not rows or repeated seeds. The interpretation table must distinguish the marginal recorded-evidence contrast from the observation-adjusted and (q)-sensitivity contrasts.

Predeclare:

- shuffle hourly time labels while preserving values, masks, and support totals;
- remove masks, use masks only, remove support evidence, and use physiology/support only;
- replace `charttime` with `storetime` as a negative timing control;
- replace interval-overlap coding with last-value coding;
- remove procedure corroboration;
- separate arterial from NIBP MAP and FiO2/O2-flow/PEEP channels;
- vary window length, support thresholds, coverage thresholds, and the one-minute event-tie tolerance;
- use a pseudo-landmark six hours before outtime with a newly defined leakage-safe outcome window;
- stratify by first careunit and admission type;
- compare aggregate return with transfer-topology-confirmed/unconfirmed return;
- compare competing-discharge analysis with discharge-as-censoring only as sensitivity;
- report complete-case, coverage-restricted, and missing-support sensitivity results; and
- audit all excluded/contradictory intervals and prove no post-outtime predictor leakage.

Supportive evidence requires an interpretable prespecified recorded-evidence contrast, adequate overlap, calibrated predictions, stable bootstrap intervals, and persistence across mask/source/time falsifications and a declared (q) range. It supports retrospective prognostic association with recorded evidence only. It does not support continuous support cessation or bedside recovery.

Adverse evidence includes reversal/collapse under observation adjustment, dependence on `storetime`, last-value coding, a single source channel, topology, or a narrow (q) assumption; non-positivity; or an alternative with unidentifiable states. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse strata/events, wide intervals, high state entropy, unstable splits, contradictory event timing, or a robustness frontier inside the prespecified plausible range. Neither adverse nor inconclusive results are evidence that discharge is safe or unsafe.

Even supportive results cannot establish causal effects of stopping support, appropriateness of ICU discharge, clinician intent, goals of care, treatment limitation, ward surveillance, preventability, planned/unplanned return, or transportability to another hospital. Expert chart/device adjudication, ward-level monitoring, external validation, and prospective or causal studies would be needed for those claims.
