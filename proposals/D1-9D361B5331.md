# Hypothesis Proposal: Liver Functional Reserve Predicts TACE-Induced Decompensation

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question:** Can pre-treatment liver functional reserve, as captured by Child-Pugh score and metabolic markers, predict the development of acute decompensation after TACE therapy?

**Secondary Question:** Do patients with preserved liver reserve (Child-Pugh A) develop different complications compared to those with marginal reserve (Child-Pugh B) following TACE, even when both groups are treated similarly?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Patient Safety**: TACE can cause hepatic decompensation (jaundice, ascites, coagulopathy) even in patients considered "fit" for treatment. Identifying patients at risk could prevent severe complications requiring hospitalization or transplant listing.

2. **Treatment Selection**: Understanding which patients develop decompensation could inform shared decision-making between treating hepatologists and patients about TACE risks vs. benefits.

3. **Procedural Optimization**: If certain metabolic markers predict decompensation, clinicians could consider alternative therapies (e.g., radiofrequency ablation, systemic therapy) for high-risk patients.

4. **Post-TACE Monitoring**: Identifying high-risk patients could justify more intensive monitoring and earlier intervention for decompensation.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Current literature focuses on:
- **Tumor response** to TACE (reduction in tumor size/volume)
- **Survival outcomes** based on stage and Child-Pugh at baseline
- **Treatment timing** relative to diagnosis

This proposal addresses:
- **Treatment complications** (decompensation) as a distinct outcome
- **Dynamic liver function** during and after TACE
- **Metabolic biomarkers** beyond standard Child-Pugh components

The key insight: **Baseline liver reserve predicts not just survival, but acute treatment complications**. This is distinct from the TACE timing question explored in Episode 1.

## Data Requirements and Bindings

### Primary Dataset: HCC Clinical Registry

**Source Files:**
- `HCC/data_基本信息_2500296761891079109.csv` - Basic patient demographics (patient_id, encounter_id, age, sex)
- `HCC/data_诊断_7504718184492840569.csv` - HCC diagnoses with timing (patient_id, encounter_id, diagnosis_name, diagnosis_type)
- `HCC/data_手术_8024330590283626027.csv` - TACE procedures (patient_id, encounter_id, 手术, 开始时间，结束时间，手术来源)
- `HCC/data_检验_609065997844652188.csv` - Laboratory results (patient_id, encounter_id, 检查，定性结果，定量结果，目标类型，检查时间)
- `HCC/data_出入转_7369490831683459252.csv` - Admission/discharge/transfer events (patient_id, encounter_id)
- `HCC/data_病历文_8434587325530196878.csv` - Clinical documents (patient_id, encounter_id) for additional context

**Key Variables:**

1. **Primary Exposure**: Pre-TACE liver functional reserve
   - Child-Pugh score at baseline (computed from: 白蛋白/albumin, 总胆红素/total bilirubin, 凝血酶原时间/PTT, 前白蛋白/pre-albumin)
   - Subcategories: Child-Pugh A (5-6), B (7-9), C (10-15)
   - Metabolic reserve score: Composite of albumin, bilirubin, pre-albumin

2. **Outcome**: TACE-Induced Decompensation
   - **Definition**: Development of jaundice (总胆红素 > baseline + 50%), ascites requiring intervention, or coagulopathy (INR > 1.5) within 30 days post-TACE
   - **Categorical**: No decompensation, mild decompensation (jaundice only), severe decompensation (ascites/coagulopathy requiring hospitalization)
   - **Time-to-event**: Days from TACE to first decompensation event

3. **Covariates:**
   - Tumor characteristics: Number of tumors, largest tumor size (from diagnosis records)
   - Concomitant medications: Sorafenib, lenvatinib, immunotherapy (from 用药 table)
   - Performance status indicators: From clinical documents or encounter notes
   - Baseline liver function: Pre-TACE albumin, bilirubin, creatinine, PT/INR
   - Demographics: Age, sex

4. **Time Fields**: All timestamps in format `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`

### Population Definition

- **Inclusion**:
  - Patients with confirmed HCC diagnosis (诊断名称 contains "肝癌" or "肝细胞癌")
  - At least one TACE procedure performed
  - Pre-TACE laboratory values available (albumin, bilirubin, creatinine)
  - Post-TACE follow-up within 30 days

- **Exclusion**:
  - Pre-existing liver decompensation before TACE
  - Multiple TACE procedures (focus on first-line)
  - Missing critical laboratory values

- **Temporal Boundaries**:
  - Study period: 2000-2015 (based on dataset range)
  - TACE index date: First TACE procedure
  - Follow-up window: 0-30 days post-TACE

## Analysis Plan

### Baseline Approach
**Logistic regression** (or Cox proportional hazards if time-to-event):
```
decompensation ~ child_pugh_score + tumor_burden + age + sex + concomitant_therapy
```

### Learned Alternative
**Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (XGBoost/LightGBM)**:
- Advantages: Handles non-linear relationships, provides feature importance, works with mixed data types
- Scientific information gained: May reveal complex interactions (e.g., albumin × tumor burden) that linear models miss
- **Mechanistic insight**: Could identify specific metabolic thresholds (e.g., albumin < 3.0 g/dL) that predict decompensation

### Key Comparisons
1. Child-Pugh A vs. B vs. C decompensation rates
2. Continuous metabolic reserve score (per 1 g/dL albumin decrease)
3. Pre-TACE bilirubin as continuous predictor vs. categorical Child-Pugh

## Falsification Criteria

**Hypothesis will be falsified if:**
- No association between baseline liver reserve and decompensation (p > 0.05)
- Decompensation rates equal across Child-Pugh groups after adjustment
- Metabolic markers show no predictive value beyond standard Child-Pugh

**Supportive Evidence:**
- Strong association between lower Child-Pugh score and decompensation (OR > 2.0)
- Metabolic markers (albumin, bilirubin) independently predict decompensation
- Specific thresholds identified (e.g., albumin < 3.2 g/dL = high risk)

**Inconclusive:**
- Weak association with wide confidence intervals
- Effect modified by subgroup (e.g., only in patients without concomitant therapy)

## Missing Evidence and Clinical Adjudication

1. **Precise Decompensation Definition**: Need to verify that "jaundice" in clinical documents truly represents clinically significant decompensation, not just mild lab elevation.

2. **Child-Pugh Calculation**: The dataset provides component measurements; Child-Pugh score must be computed following standard algorithm (weighting of albumin, bilirubin, INR, ascites, encephalopathy).

3. **Concomitant Therapy Confounding**: Patients receiving systemic therapy may have different decompensation patterns; need to stratify or adjust for this.

4. **External Validation**: This analysis is internal; external validation would require another HCC cohort.

5. **Clinical Document Review**: May need manual review of 病历文 to confirm decompensation events not captured in structured data.

## Expected Impact

If supported, this hypothesis could:
- **Identify high-risk patients** who should receive alternative therapies or intensive monitoring
- **Guide treatment selection** between TACE and other modalities
- **Inform shared decision-making** about TACE risks
- **Trigger earlier intervention** for decompensation in at-risk patients

## Method Comparison Rationale

**Baseline (Logistic/Cox model)**: Provides interpretable odds/hazard ratios, handles censoring, establishes clear null hypothesis. Directly tests main effect of liver reserve.

**Learned Alternative (Gradient Boosting)**:
- Could reveal non-linear reserve effects (e.g., U-shaped relationship)
- May identify important interactions (reserve × tumor burden, reserve × age)
- Provides feature importance for clinical prioritization
- Could handle missing data more flexibly

**Scientific Information Gained**: The learned model may reveal that the relationship between liver reserve and decompensation is threshold-based rather than linear, or that specific combinations of markers (e.g., low albumin + high bilirubin) predict risk better than either alone. This would fundamentally change clinical guidance from "Child-Pugh score" to "metabolic reserve profile."
