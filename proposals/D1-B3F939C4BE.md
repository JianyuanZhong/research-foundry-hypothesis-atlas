# Day-42 HCC assay transportability: frozen support and workflow/state interpretation child

Status: proposed substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. No clinical result is claimed. The inherited scientific question, day-42 landmark, endpoint, and source bindings are unchanged.

## Scientific question and claim boundary

Among patients with a first locally recorded exact-code TACE who are observable at day 42 and have no exact-code repeat before day 42, does recorded assay state at day 42 add held-out information for a later operational exact-code repeat TACE, and is that increment stable across prespecified measurement-opportunity, ordering, encounter, care-process, calendar, and early/late follow-up conditions?

The untested claim remains that the assay-state increment is reusable recorded information rather than a signal confined to the workflow that records it. Existing evidence supports only narrower claims: [K1] reports treatment-adjacent imaging/radiomics prediction in a selected repeat-TACE cohort, with generalization limitations; [K2] establishes that treatment response is an imaging/necrosis-aware construct rather than a procedure code; and [K3] shows why documented response comparisons are vulnerable to ascertainment, exposure-classification, and treatment-selection limitations. This experiment adds a bounded EHR transportability test. It does not reproduce [K1], and cannot establish response, tumor biology, TACE benefit, treatment necessity, causality, or mechanism.

The strongest workflow rival is that encounter intensity, order availability, assay opportunity, or planned retreatment pathways cause both recording and repeat-code probability. A severity/selection rival is incompletely measured current disease state. A stable state increment after process adjustment and observation weighting would support portable recorded information for this operational event only. A process-only or permutation-matched increment would favor workflow/timing. The comparisons remain descriptive/predictive; neither establishes a causal workflow effect.

## Channels and transport axes

Use the inherited disjoint channels:

- `M0): pre-index age, sex, department, encounter/admission history, matched-encounter diagnosis/procedure counts, and pre-index exact-assay summaries.
- `S): eight target-assay numeric values and qualitative/inequality flags in (0,42], plus assay-specific pre-index change where valid; no assay-request or missingness indicators.
- `P_obs): dated observation/order/encounter/lab opportunity and missingness counts, without assay values or state.
- `P_care): dated non-TACE procedures, non-drug orders, medication starts/ends, and department/visit process marks, without assay values.

Define (A=M0+P_obs+P_care) and (B=A+S). The primary incremental information is the paired held-out weighted log-loss difference (Delta_g = E[L^A-L^B|G=g]), positive when S improves prediction. The overall (Delta) is primary; Brier and calibration are secondary. This is not a causal or biological estimand.

Axes are fixed before outcome scoring: measurement opportunity (zero/low/high distinct dates), order availability (no/low/high valid `开立时间` dates), encounter intensity (zero/low/high distinct composite encounter IDs), care-process intensity (zero/low/high de-duplicated dated process marks), calendar period (three development-defined equal-frequency index-date bins), and fixed early/late horizon. Positive cutpoints and calendar breakpoints are development-only and frozen. The five non-horizon axes form the primary transport family; horizon is a prespecified temporal sensitivity, not another license to select favorable strata. The secondary joint analysis is only the predeclared 2x2 low/high measurement-by-care table and is not used to rescue a failed one-axis claim.

## Support gate, effective sample size, and multiplicity

Support is audited once, before any outcome-driven model or interaction choice, and frozen in `transport_support_freeze.json`. The audit reports index, early-repeat, duplicate, observation-through-day-42, exact-label, timestamp/scale, censoring, and calendar counts, separately for development and untouched test. It also reports assay-observation and all-source censoring effective sample size (ESS), weight tails, and event counts in every prespecified category and the predeclared joint cells.

A stratum is *claimable* only if it was predeclared from development and has, in both development and untouched test, at least 15 later exact-code events, at least 100 effective patient-level observations for the relevant weighted loss, nondegenerate assay-observation and censoring support, and no weight-tail failure under the frozen rule. The overall cohort additionally requires at least 50 later events. These are feasibility gates, not clinical power or a minimal important difference. A test stratum failing a gate stays in the table as `unsupported/inconclusive`; it is never collapsed into a neighbor, omitted from the family, or called adverse. If development support fails, no confirmatory stratum claim is attempted for that category. Counts are audited before fitting, and no re-binning is allowed after seeing outcomes.

The primary error-control family is predeclared:

1. One overall transparent-model state increment (Delta) uses the primary weighted log loss and a two-sided 95% patient-bootstrap interval; the transport claim is not made unless its interval is strictly above zero.
2. For each of the five non-horizon axes, calculate the predeclared maximum absolute pairwise contrast in (Delta_g) and the partial-pooled heterogeneity (	au). Use a single simultaneous max-t patient-bootstrap interval over all supported axis contrasts and the five (	au) estimates (the bootstrap preserves patients, person-days, strata, weights, and paired predictions). This controls the reported family at 95% without post-hoc stratum selection. The operational heterogeneity margin remains 0.005 weighted log-loss units (and 0.010 weighted Brier units); it is computational, not a clinical MCID.
3. Call transport supported only under an intersection rule: the overall increment is positive, every one of the five axes passes its frozen support gate, and the simultaneous interval for each axis's heterogeneity quantity lies within the margin with no direction reversal in the prespecified sensitivities. A failure to establish equivalence is not evidence of heterogeneity when precision is inadequate.
4. Report the fixed-horizon early/late contrasts with their own labelled sensitivity intervals, not as additional opportunities for a favorable primary claim. Calendar- and random-split results are sensitivity analyses with labels retained.
5. No nominal p-value or interval is reported for an unsupported stratum. This makes an unsupported axis inconclusive rather than favorable and prevents multiplicity from being reduced by deleting sparse cells.

The final table must distinguish `supported`, `adverse`, `unsupported/inconclusive`, and `not estimable`, and link each label to the exact row of counts, ESS/weights, loss differences, and simultaneous interval.

## Falsifying workflow versus state interpretations

Fit all channel ablations on the same temporal split and folds: (M0), (M0+S), (M0+P_obs), (M0+P_care), (M0+P_obs+P_care=A), and (B=A+S). The key state contrast is (B-A); the key workflow contrasts are (A-M0), (M0+P_obs-M0), and (M0+P_care-M0). Use matched-capacity process-only models and the inherited timestamp, within-calendar value, process-mark, outcome-label, day-28 feature-cutoff, undated-TACE, alternate-`obs_end`, and last-seven-day lead-time controls. Observation weighting is reported beside ordinary analysis, never substituted for it.

Interpretation is constrained:

- Stable (B-A) across supported axes, with process-only and permutation controls not comparable, supports a reusable recorded-assay signal for the operational repeat code.
- A gain that disappears after observation weighting, is reproduced by timestamp/value permutation, or is matched by (P_obs/P_care) is adverse to a state interpretation and compatible with workflow/timing.
- A gain confined to high-opportunity/high-care or one calendar period is not transportable; it motivates measurement/intent linkage rather than a more complex model.
- Sparse support, scale drift, weight failure, a wide simultaneous interval, or an incomplete learned fit is inconclusive. It does not refute biology because biology is not measured here.

Imaging response, intent to retreat, outside-care events, death/transplant, assay units/reference ranges, and expert adjudication are unavailable or not established by this snapshot. A consequential follow-up study must link adjudicated imaging/response and treatment intent, validate assay measurement scales, and externally validate the operational signal.

## Comparable method alternatives and selection

Simple auditable baseline (confirmatory): elastic-net pooled logistic discrete-time hazards for days 43–180 with fixed day bins or low-degree time spline; fold-fitted imputation/scaling/dictionaries; the six channel ablations above; target is the paired patient-level all-source IPCW weighted log loss over at-risk intervals. Development is the earliest 80% of index dates, untouched test the latest 20%, with five grouped development folds. Metrics are weighted log loss (primary), weighted Brier/integrated Brier, calibration slope/intercept, and AUROC/AUPRC (secondary). Uncertainty is a patient-level bootstrap preserving all person-days and weights, with max-t adjustment for the predeclared transport family. Approximate future budget: CPU, 4 cores/16 GB, about 10–30 minutes after source materialization plus bootstrap time; measured only by the bounded source census, not by a model fit. It is selected as the scientific decision model because its channel separation, leakage audit, and stratum losses are inspectable.

Substantive learned alternative (deferred sensitivity, not excluded): a compact time-aware GRU with elapsed-time decay over the same -365 through +42 event stream, event category/label, audited numeric value, qualitative/inequality flag, relative time, time-since-prior-event, and channel tag. It receives the same ablations, target, temporal split, IPCW/observation weights, metrics, support gates, and three fixed development seeds. The target is identical: held-out weighted patient-level hazard loss; uncertainty is the same paired patient bootstrap. It could reveal whether ordered assay trajectories and event timing carry information lost by baseline summaries, but it cannot repair an unsupported stratum or justify a clinical mechanism. Approximate future budget is 1 allocated A100, 4 CPUs, 16 GB, roughly 1–4 hours including three seeds and scoring; this is an unverified planning estimate, not a discovery result. It is deferred from the confirmatory conclusion because per-stratum GRU gains are harder to audit and the event/support census must first establish estimability. Revisit it if the baseline has complete support and the learned implementation is available within the approved solver envelope; abandon it as a scientific requirement if it adds only a small predictive change without changing workflow-versus-state interpretation.

Completion is the newly fitted baseline estimates, simultaneous uncertainty, support/weight audit, falsification outputs, and claim-output map. A completed GRU is optional secondary evidence; a favorable model score alone is not completion.
