# Discordant renal recovery in MIMIC-IV: a falsifiable, temporally separated prognostic study

## Question and clinical importance

Among adult ICU patients with KDIGO acute kidney injury (AKI), does a discordance between creatinine-based and urine-output-based recovery at a fixed 72-hour post-onset landmark predict worse 28-day in-hospital mortality beyond what is explained by current illness severity?

The clinical decision is consequential: clinicians must decide whether to escalate monitoring, involve nephrology, initiate renal replacement therapy (RRT), or modify fluid management when creatinine and urine output tell different stories about renal recovery. A model that identifies patients whose discordance reflects true residual kidney injury rather than measurement or severity artifacts could support earlier nephrology review and more informed RRT decisions. This study does not claim that discordance itself causes worse outcomes.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

**Already supported by evidence:** AKI is common in ICU patients and associated with increased mortality; creatinine and urine output can disagree in AKI staging and recovery; AKI recovery is an important prognostic marker.

**Unresolved claim:** Among patients with KDIGO AKI, a discordance between creatinine-based recovery and urine-output-based recovery at 72 hours after AKI onset predicts 28-day in-hospital mortality beyond what is explained by current illness severity, after accounting for the temporal ordering of measurements and the possibility that discordance is an artifact of selective measurement or illness severity.

**Strongest rival explanation:** Discordance may reflect differences in illness severity, measurement timing, or selective urine output recording rather than true residual kidney injury. A patient with a higher creatinine may have had a more recent measurement, or a patient with low urine output may have had a Foley catheter removed. These artifacts could create a spurious association with mortality.

## Scientific advance over existing knowledge

Existing AKI prognostic models typically use a single creatinine trajectory or a static SOFA score. This study tests whether the *discordance* between two complementary recovery signals—creatinine and urine output—carries incremental prognostic information beyond what either signal alone provides. If discordance is a genuine prognostic signal, it would suggest that clinicians should not rely on a single recovery metric and should consider the pattern of recovery across multiple organ systems. If discordance does not add information beyond current severity, it would suggest that the apparent disagreement is an artifact and that a single metric is sufficient for prognostic purposes.

## Population and temporal design

**Population:** Adult ICU patients (age ≥18) in MIMIC-IV with a KDIGO AKI stage 1–3 diagnosis during the first 24 hours of ICU stay.

**AKI onset:** The time of the first creatinine measurement that meets KDIGO AKI criteria (creatinine ≥2× baseline or ≥4× baseline, or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 hours).

**Landmark:** 72 hours after AKI onset. At this landmark, we define:
- **Creatinine-based recovery:** Creatinine has returned to <1.5× baseline.
- **Urine-output-based recovery:** Urine output has been ≥0.5 mL/kg/h for the preceding 6 hours.
- **Discordance:** Creatinine-based recovery is present but urine-output-based recovery is absent, or vice versa.

**Outcome:** 28-day in-hospital mortality (death before hospital discharge or before 28 days, whichever comes first).

**Temporal boundaries:** All covariates are measured at or before the 72-hour landmark. No future information is used. The outcome is measured after the landmark.

## Variables and data bindings

All data are from MIMIC-IV, version 3.1. The exact source files and columns are:

- **Patient demographics and admission:** `mimic-iv/patients.csv.gz` (subject_id, gender, anchor_age, anchor_year), `mimic-iv/admissions.csv.gz` (subject_id, hadm_id, admission_time, discharge_time, death_event).
- **ICU stay:** `mimic-iv/icustays.csv.gz` (subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, outtime).
- **Laboratory results:** `mimic-iv/labevents.csv.gz` (subject_id, hadm_id, itemid, valuenum, valueuom, charttime). Creatinine itemid: 50912 (mg/dL).
- **Urine output:** `mimic-iv/outputevents.csv.gz` (subject_id, hadm_id, itemid, valuenum, charttime). Urine output itemid: 221662 (mL).
- **Fluid balance:** `mimic-iv/inputevents.csv.gz` (subject_id, hadm_id, itemid, valuenum, charttime) and `mimic-iv/outputevents.csv.gz` (subject_id, hadm_id, itemid, valuenum, charttime).
- **Vital signs:** `mimic-iv/chartstages.csv.gz` (subject_id, hadm_id, itemid, valuenum, charttime).
- **Medications:** `mimic-iv/prescriptions.csv.gz` (subject_id, hadm_id, drug_name, starttime, stoptime).
- **SOFA score:** Computed from `mimic-iv/chartstages.csv.gz` and `mimic-iv/labevents.csv.gz` using the standard SOFA formula.

**Join keys:** All tables are joined on `subject_id` and `hadm_id`. Time fields are `charttime` for laboratory and vital sign measurements, `intime`/`outtime` for ICU stay, and `admission_time`/`discharge_time` for admission.

## Baseline and learned alternative

**Baseline:** A logistic regression model with the following covariates measured at the 72-hour landmark:
- Age, sex, admission SOFA score
- Creatinine level at landmark
- Urine output rate at landmark
- Fluid balance over the preceding 24 hours
- Vasopressor use at landmark
- Mechanical ventilation status at landmark

This baseline captures the main prognostic factors and provides a reference for the incremental value of discordance.

**Learned alternative:** A gradient-boosted tree model (e.g., XGBoost or LightGBM) with the same covariates plus the discordance indicator. The learned model can capture non-linear interactions between discordance and other covariates that a linear model would miss. For example, the prognostic value of discordance may depend on the severity of illness or the presence of mechanical ventilation.

**What the learned alternative reveals:** If the learned model shows a significant improvement in prediction accuracy (e.g., AUC or calibration) over the baseline, it suggests that discordance has a non-linear interaction with other covariates. If the learned model does not improve over the baseline, it suggests that the prognostic value of discordance is adequately captured by a linear model and that the apparent interaction is not clinically meaningful.

## Analysis and falsification

**Primary analysis:** Compare the baseline and learned models using 5-fold cross-validation. Report AUC, calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow test), and Brier score. The primary hypothesis is that the learned model with discordance has a significantly higher AUC than the baseline model without discordance.

**Falsification criteria:**
- **Supportive:** The learned model with discordance has a significantly higher AUC (p < 0.05) than the baseline model, and the improvement is clinically meaningful (e.g., ΔAUC > 0.01).
- **Adverse:** The learned model with discordance does not have a significantly higher AUC than the baseline model, or the improvement is not clinically meaningful.
- **Inconclusive:** The sample size is too small to detect a clinically meaningful difference, or the discordance phenotype is too rare to be reliably estimated.

**Sensitivity analyses:**
- Restrict to patients with KDIGO AKI stage 2–3.
- Restrict to patients with a complete 72-hour urine output record.
- Use a different landmark time (e.g., 48 hours or 96 hours).
- Adjust for the timing of the last creatinine and urine output measurements.

## Limitations

- **No measured GFR:** MIMIC-IV does not include measured GFR, so we cannot directly assess kidney function. We use creatinine and urine output as proxies, which may be affected by muscle mass, fluid status, and other factors.
- **Selective measurement:** Urine output and creatinine are measured at different times and frequencies, which may introduce measurement error and selection bias.
- **Causal claims:** This is an observational study, and we cannot establish a causal relationship between discordance and mortality. The association may be confounded by unmeasured factors.
- **Generalizability:** The findings may not generalize to other ICU populations or to patients with chronic kidney disease.
