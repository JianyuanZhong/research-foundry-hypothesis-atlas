# Residual instability and nighttime ICU discharge: an eICU hospital-outcome experiment

## Scientific question, strongest supported claim, and hypothesis

The highest-mu valid parent tests whether the temporal shape of low MAP (late-persistent versus early-recovered) predicts a subsequent creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal. This proposal develops the deferred eICU seed 06 into a different, clinically consequential decision question:

> Among adults discharged alive from the ICU, is nighttime ICU discharge associated with a higher short-horizon risk of in-hospital death specifically when measurable physiologic instability persists in the final six hours before ICU discharge?

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that the frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative vital observations, ICU and hospital discharge offsets/statuses, hospital identifiers, and structured respiratory/infusion records. The seed, parent and public documentation do not establish that nighttime discharge causes harm, that residual instability is adjudicated clinical instability, or that staffing/bed capacity explains any association.

The unresolved claim is an observational effect-modification claim: the risk contrast between nighttime and daytime ICU discharge differs by pre-discharge instability after adjustment for admission severity, ICU length of stay, hospital and measured care-process variables. The primary falsifiable hypothesis is that the standardized 48-hour cumulative-incidence contrast for nighttime versus daytime discharge is positive in the prespecified residual-instability stratum and materially smaller in the stable stratum, with the interaction direction reproducing in held-out hospitals.

A positive result could identify a subgroup for prospective discharge-readiness and handoff research. It cannot establish a causal nighttime-discharge effect or justify hospital rankings. The substantive advance over the parent is that it tests whether recovery status changes the safety context of a discrete transition decision, with an observable hospital-level outcome, rather than another refinement of hypotension exposure or a non-adjudicated renal proxy.

## Population, exposure and time boundaries

Retain the first ICU stay per person: adult age (map `> 89` to 90 and record nonnumeric ages), `unitvisitnumber=1`, nonmissing hospital and numeric `unitdischargeoffset`. Retain only ICU discharges with `unitdischargestatus=Alive`, a valid `unitdischargetime24`, at least six hours of ICU time before discharge, and at least 48 minutes of usable pre-discharge vital coverage in the final six-hour window. If multiple valid first-visit rows exist per `uniquepid`, retain the earliest valid admission and report the conflict.

Define daytime/nighttime from the recorded ICU discharge clock `unitdischargetime24`, not from an inferred calendar date: nighttime is 23:00:00 through 06:59:59 and daytime is 07:00:00 through 22:59:59. Repeat with 22:00–06:59 and 00:00–07:59 as prespecified boundary sensitivities. Patients with impossible or missing clock values are excluded from the primary exposure analysis, never assigned to a class.

Primary residual instability is constructed without post-discharge information from the final six hours [D-360,D), where D is `unitdischargeoffset`. Using valid periodic `systemicmean` in [-50,300], use no carry-forward across gaps and the metadata-consistent next-observation coverage rule. Define a prespecified binary unstable state as either (a) at least 30 covered minutes with MAP <65 mmHg, or (b) final-hour MAP <65 for at least 15 covered minutes; require at least 80% covered minutes in the window. The stable state is neither condition. The continuous secondary score is standardized low-MAP minutes, low-MAP depth area, minimum MAP, final-hour MAP, final-hour heart rate/respiration/SpO2 and their slopes. Repeat at 70% and 90% coverage and with aperiodic noninvasive MAP.

Support context includes a conservative ventilation-active indicator when a respiratoryCare interval covers the window endpoint and a qualifying documented infusion row in [D-360,D); these are covariates/sensitivities, not causal treatment variables. ICU length of stay is `D`. All predictors are timestamped before D. Discharge time itself defines the exposure and is not used as a physiologic predictor.

## Outcome and estimand

The primary outcome is a 48-hour post-ICU hospital outcome. Convert the ICU discharge time to hospital-relative time as H = `hospitaladmitoffset + unitdischargeoffset`. Define hospital death as `hospitaldischargestatus=Expired` with numeric `hospitaldischargeoffset` in (H,H+2880] (minutes), and define alive hospital discharge within 48 hours as `hospitaldischargestatus` indicating alive/non-expired with numeric discharge offset in (H,H+2880]. Do not count a discharge before H as a post-ICU event; report inconsistent records. If final hospital disposition is not available or the offset is not interpretable, censor rather than assume survival. Death and alive hospital discharge are competing events; patients with neither observed by 48 hours are administratively censored. A secondary endpoint is eventual in-hospital death when the final disposition and temporal ordering are valid, explicitly labeled less time-specific.

The primary estimand is the standardized 48-hour cumulative-incidence risk difference for nighttime versus daytime ICU discharge, separately in stable and unstable strata, plus the predeclared interaction difference-in-risk-differences. Fit a cause-specific competing-risk model with patient-clustered robust inference (one discharge per person) and standardize over the eligible-landmark covariate distribution. Bootstrap patients within hospital for 95% intervals. Evaluate each hospital with at least 200 eligible discharges and both exposure/outcome levels for leave-one-hospital-out transport; do not rank hospitals.

The compiled audit must report the number of eligible discharges, missing clock/disposition/coverage, instability support by hospital and exposure, event/censoring/competing-event counts, and all exclusions. If either exposure stratum, either instability stratum, or the primary outcome has inadequate support, return an inconclusive feasibility result rather than altering cutoffs post hoc.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

The transparent baseline is a regularized pooled cause-specific model with daytime/nighttime exposure, instability state, their interaction, ICU length of stay, admission demographics, admission Apache variables/scores, admission support indicators, final-window MAP minimum/mean/SD/last value, low-MAP minutes/depth, coverage and gap features, final-window heart-rate/respiration/SpO2 summaries/slopes, conservative ventilation and infusion row-presence indicators, and missingness flags. Hospital is a stratification variable, not a feature. The nested baseline without the interaction is the direct test comparator.

The substantive learned alternative is a small order-aware temporal-convolution model over six one-hour bins in [D-360,D), using the same physiologic/support channels, explicit masks and gap/coverage channels, plus admission covariates, to estimate the same competing risks. Fit a matched no-interaction and interaction model with the same target and patient-contained hospital split: training hospitals, fixed patient-level validation split for tuning/early stopping, and untouched held-out hospitals for evaluation. Use three fixed seeds and patient/hospital-stratified bootstrap uncertainty; never use hospital ID as a feature.

The temporal model can reveal whether instability immediately before discharge, gradual recovery, or irregular observation order modifies the nighttime contrast in ways lost by a binary instability rule. Compare standardized risk differences, interaction estimates, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, cause-specific calibration and prespecified decision curves; AUROC is secondary. A small CPU implementation is preferred (two CPUs, <=8 GiB, expected 30–90 minutes including extraction/fitting, within the configured 7,200-second science budget); GPU is deferred because it is not necessary for this scientific comparison. If the learned model fails computationally, the baseline estimate remains the primary deliverable and the failure is reported, not replaced with an audit-only result.

## Falsification and interpretation

Before fitting, audit source hashes, exact headers, cohort counts, clock parsing, ICU-to-hospital offset ordering, vital coverage, instability support, support-record semantics and outcome support.

Falsification tests:

1. Within hospital, permute discharge clock labels among eligible patients while preserving instability and outcome; the nighttime interaction should attenuate. A persistent interaction suggests hospital/time composition or labeling artifacts.
2. Repeat with the final six-hour window shifted earlier by 12 hours. A large association in the placebo window but not the true pre-discharge window indicates nonspecific severity or selection.
3. Repeat MAP source, coverage, night boundaries and instability thresholds; exclude EOL-proxy records, open ventilation intervals and discharges within 12 hours of ICU admission.
4. Restrict to hospitals with both night/day and stable/unstable support; leave one hospital out. A result confined to one site is not transportable evidence.
5. Repeat with eventual hospital death and with the 48-hour competing-event outcome; do not convert missing disposition into survival.
6. Test whether adjustment for documented infusion/ventilation records changes estimates, explicitly not as causal treatment adjustment.

Supportive results require a nonzero unstable-stratum nighttime risk difference and positive interaction with a 95% interval excluding zero, consistent direction across boundary/source/coverage sensitivities, attenuation under clock permutation and earlier-window placebo, and calibration/transport evidence in held-out hospitals. This supports an observational signal warranting prospective study of recovery-aware ICU discharge and handoff, not a causal policy.

Adverse results include a precise null/negative interaction, disappearance under falsification or missingness controls, or failure to reproduce in held-out hospitals. This indicates that residual measured instability does not identify a distinct nighttime-discharge risk context in this snapshot, or that apparent differences are site/process confounding.

Inconclusive results include inadequate clock/disposition support, inconsistent ICU-to-hospital offset ordering, sparse unstable or nighttime strata, insufficient vital coverage, missing outcome ascertainment, unresolved EOL/transfer selection, or wide intervals. No clinical recommendation follows.

Computationally checkable claims include source hashes and schema, filters/joins, clock and offset arithmetic, exposure construction, competing-event labels, split integrity, fitted estimates, calibration, bootstrap intervals and falsification outputs. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to establish true physiologic instability, whether discharge was clinically indicated, the role of staffing/bed capacity/handoff quality, post-ICU care, causality, and whether changing discharge timing improves outcomes. eICU has no staffing/bed-occupancy data, no reliable narrative notes, no raw waveforms and no adjudicated post-ICU complications.

## Demonstrations, seeds, alternatives and deliverable

The three demonstrations were inspected as references, not templates. Delphi supports an ordered structured-trajectory adaptation, which is used here only as the small temporal model; the original UK Biobank training/external validation is unavailable and is not reproduced. ALADYNOULLI motivates latent longitudinal structure but its genetic inputs and full reproduction dependencies are absent; a genetic adaptation is deferred, and a simple temporal model is sufficient for this question. Oncoformer’s main paper and complete STAR Methods remain unavailable and local eICU images are absent; its multimodal experiment is not pursued. A lab-only adaptation is not needed for this discharge question.

All ten eICU seeds were retrieved and assessed before parent choice. Seed 06 (nighttime ICU discharge) supplies this new question. Seed 10 (hospital recovery speed) is a related deferred alternative because treatment-practice semantics and quality interpretation remain confounded. Seed 03 (hypotension duration) is the parent lineage; seeds 02 and 08 remain useful testing-process comparators. Seeds 01 (vasopressor strategy), 04 (hyperoxia), 05 (ventilatory burden), 07 (transfer risk), and 09 (insulin/hypoglycemia) remain deferred because exact treatment semantics, required measures, outcome support, or unavailable clinical evidence were not sufficient to displace this design. In particular, `infusionDrug` lacks stop times; absent intake/output cannot establish nutrition interruption; SpO2 is not a hyperoxemia measure; and plateau pressure/mechanics, staffing and adjudicated outcomes are unavailable.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitting the interaction baseline and the matched temporal interaction model, not merely auditing inputs. Completion outputs are:

1. machine-readable provenance with snapshot/hash, exact paths/tables/columns, filters, clock/offset rules, seeds, joins and resources;
2. pre-fit cohort, coverage, instability, support, outcome and hospital audit;
3. standardized 48-hour competing-risk risk differences for night versus day in stable/unstable strata and their interaction with patient/hospital bootstrap intervals;
4. baseline versus temporal-model calibration, Brier, decision and held-out-hospital outputs;
5. clock-permutation, earlier-window placebo, source/coverage/boundary/EOL/ventilation sensitivities and missing-outcome accounting; and
6. a conclusion labeled supportive, adverse or inconclusive that links each claim to computed outputs and separates computation from clinical adjudication.

A reference execution or feasibility-only audit does not complete the experiment.
