# Proposal: Preoperative laboratory prediction of 1-year TACE treatment-uptake
# after hepatic resection for HCC — a treatment-uptake outcome with a concrete
# clinical decision

## 1. Clinical question and why it matters
After curative-intent hepatic resection for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), ~20% of
patients receive transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) within 1 year (measured:
1,839/8,950 = 20.5% in this cohort). TACE in this window is overwhelmingly tied to
documented recurrence (99.5% of TACE procedures have an HCC diagnosis within 14 days),
making it a treatment-uptake outcome that directly informs surveillance intensity,
early TACE referral, and adjuvant therapy eligibility.

Published recurrence models (nomograms [K1,K2], MERP) rely on variables that are NOT
available as structured data in this EHR: microvascular invasion (MVI), tumor
size/number, resection margin, histologic grade, and imaging features. The unresolved
question is whether **routinely drawn preoperative laboratory values alone** — the only
variables with dense, time-stamped availability in this cohort — carry enough
independent prognostic signal to identify patients who will need TACE within 1 year,
and whether a simple, deployable lab-only rule can approach the discrimination
reported for richer models.

A validated lab-only score would be computable at the preoperative lab draw in any
hospital, before MVI and tumor size/number are known from pathology. This is a
consequential clinical decision: patients predicted to need TACE can be enrolled in
intensified surveillance, referred early for TACE planning, and considered for
adjuvant therapy trials.

## 2. Evidence inspected (see key-references.json and attached abstracts)
- [K1] Wang et al. 2018 (PMID 29420221, Clin Cancer Res): adjuvant TACE RCT in
  HBV-related HCC after resection (n=280). RFS 56.0% vs 42.1% (P=0.01); OS HR 0.59.
  Establishes that post-resection TACE can reduce recurrence in selected high-risk
  patients, but the trial enrolled by predefined clinical/pathological risk features
  (tumor >5cm, MVI, multiple tumors), not by a lab-based prediction model.
- [K2] Jiang et al. 2015 (PMID 25914472, World J Gastroenterol): propensity-matched
  adjuvant TACE after curative resection (n=229, 61 pairs). No significant OS or
  recurrence benefit. AFP >=400 and tumor >5cm were independent predictors of
  mortality. Suggests adjuvant TACE benefit is not universal and depends on risk
  features — supporting the need for a prediction model.
- [K3] Zhao et al. 2025 (PMID 41200181, Front Immunol): multicenter real-world
  (n=300, 4 centers, 2015-2024). Adjuvant therapy (TACE/TKI) improved OS (matched
  HR 0.47) and DFS (HR 0.43) in high-risk HCC. TACE showed greatest efficacy (OS
  HR 0.54, DFS HR 0.55). Supports the clinical relevance of post-resection therapy
  allocation.

## 3. Hypothesis (falsifiable)
H1: A model using only preoperative (0-30d before index resection) laboratory values
predicts TACE treatment within 1 year after hepatic resection with discrimination
meaningfully above a clinical baseline (age, sex, calendar year): held-out C-index
>=0.62, and top-vs-bottom tercile TACE<=365d rates differing by >=10 percentage points.
Falsification: if the best lab-only model's held-out C-index <=0.58 or the tercile risk
gap is <=5pp, routine labs alone are not supported for TACE-uptake triage in this cohort.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Primary analysis
- Estimand: 1-year TACE treatment-uptake in the full 8,950-patient resection cohort,
  with administrative censoring at last encounter.
- Method: Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model (subdistribution HR with 95% CI) on
  the full cohort, with the lab feature set (B1/B2 scores as covariates in separate
  fits) and B0 (age, sex, year) as the baseline. Cause-specific Cox is reported as a
  robustness check (equivalent here since there is no death competing event — censoring
  only).
- Censoring time = last encounter date (基本信息.就诊时间); event time = first TACE date;
  event indicator = 1 if TACE date <= 365d.
- Cumulative incidence at 1 year (Aalen-Johansen) reported for the full cohort and by
  model terciles.
- Model evaluation: for B0, B1, B2 —
  (a) time-dependent C-index at 1 year with DeLong CI on the held-out test set (2017-2018);
  (b) top-vs-bottom tercile TACE<=365d cumulative-incidence gap;
  (c) calibration (slope, intercept, calibration plot);
  (d) NRI/IDI of B1/B2 vs B0.
- Decision thresholds: supportive if best model C-index >=0.62 AND tercile gap >=10pp;
  adverse if C-index <=0.58 OR gap <=5pp; inconclusive in between.
- Split: temporal — train 2013-2016 (n≈6,079), test 2017-2018 (n≈2,871); no patient
  overlap. Secondary: 5-fold CV within 2013-2018.
- Models:
  - B0: logistic regression on age, sex, resection year (clinical baseline).
  - B1: penalized logistic regression (L1/L2, 5-fold CV for penalty selection) on the
    16-lab set + derived ALBI/PLR/NLR/GNRI.
  - B2: gradient-boosted trees (e.g. LightGBM) on the same features — the substantive
    learned alternative; tests whether non-linear interactions (AFP×ALBI, GGT×PLT) and
    threshold effects (e.g. AFP >400 as in [K2]) add information the linear model loses.
    A Delphi-style sequence model remains not justified at n≈9,000 with ~20 features.
  For the Fine-Gray primary, each model's predicted probability (from the temporal-split
  fit) is entered as a single covariate (score test + subdistribution HR per 0.1
  probability increment); terciles are cut on the test-set predicted probability.

### 5.2 Sensitivity analyses (outcome specificity)
All three reuse the identical features, split, models, and decision thresholds; only the
outcome definition changes. Each is reported as a separate 1-year cumulative incidence +
C-index + tercile gap on the full cohort (Fine-Gray), so the estimand stays cohort-level.
- Sensitivity A (exclude multi-visit resection patients): patients with >=2 distinct
  resection 就诊号 are excluded (n≈7,584; 15.3% excluded). TACE<=365d = 1,588 (17.7%).
  Rationale: multi-visit resection may indicate complex disease or re-resection for
  recurrence, which could confound the TACE-uptake outcome.
- Sensitivity B (TACE with HCC diagnosis >90d after index): event = first TACE <=365d
  AND first HCC diagnosis encounter >90d after resection (excludes same-admission TACE).
  Measured: 664 (7.4%). Rationale: most specific for true recurrence-driven TACE;
  if the lab signal persists on this outcome, it is robust to the same-admission
  TACE concern.
- Sensitivity C (TACE with no HCC diagnosis in (90,365]): event = first TACE <=365d
  AND no HCC diagnosis encounter in (90d, 365d] after resection. Measured: 1,063 (11.9%).
  Of these, 750 (70.6%) have HCC diagnosis only <=90d (same-admission), 313 (29.4%)
  have HCC diagnosis >365d. Rationale: captures same-admission TACE and later-diagnosis
  TACE; directional robustness check.
- Interpretation grid (pre-specified):
  - Base supportive AND B supportive → strong: lab signal robust to same-admission TACE.
  - Base supportive, B adverse/inconclusive → the base signal is substantially driven by
    same-admission TACE; the lab-only claim is weakened to "predicts early post-op TACE
    uptake, not necessarily true recurrence-driven TACE."
  - B supportive (despite n≈664) → strongest specificity evidence; B adverse with base
    supportive → outcome-proxy concern dominates; do not claim recurrence-driven TACE
    prediction.
  - All adverse → lab-only triage not supported (negative result).

### 5.3 Secondary analyses
- Systemic therapy within 365d (n=32, 0.4%): descriptive only, too rare for modeling.
  Systemic therapy ever after resection (n=396, 4.4%): descriptive.
- TACE <=730d (n=2,074, 23.2%): 2-year treatment-uptake; Fine-Gray on the full cohort.
- MVI mediation: add text-adjudicated MVI to the lab model; measure attenuation of the
  lab score's subdistribution HR. Underpowered for precision (sparse text MVI);
  directional only.
- AFP-L3 exploratory 2019-2025 sub-cohort: assay introduced 2019; shorter follow-up;
  descriptive only.

## 6. Interpretation plan
- Supportive: best model (B1 or B2) held-out 1-year C-index >=0.62 AND tercile
  cumulative-incidence gap >=10pp on the FULL cohort (Fine-Gray), with sensitivity B
  concordant → routine labs carry actionable TACE-uptake signal; next steps: external
  validation (not available here) and decision-curve analysis.
- Adverse: C-index <=0.58 OR gap <=5pp → lab-only triage not supported; record as a
  negative result; the field should rely on postoperative MVI/imaging.
- Inconclusive: 0.58 < C-index < 0.62 → weak signal; report with CIs; do not claim
  utility.
- Outcome-proxy adverse (base supportive, B adverse): the lab signal predicts early
  TACE uptake but the TACE may be same-admission rather than recurrence-driven; the
  clinical claim is downgraded accordingly and chart review (clinical adjudication) is
  required before a recurrence-specific claim.
- NOT established by this study: causality, external validity (single center), or that
  the lab signal is not a proxy for unmeasured MVI/tumor burden (partially addressed by
  MVI mediation). Death-unknown censoring means the 1-year cumulative incidence is a
  lower bound if lost patients died before 1y with unobserved TACE.

## 8. Limitations and essential adjudication
- TACE outcome is tied to documented recurrence (99.5% have HCC diagnosis within 14d of
  TACE); it is a treatment-uptake outcome, not a pure adjuvant-therapy outcome.
  Sensitivity B (TACE with HCC diagnosis >90d) addresses this.
- 71.7% of the cohort is censored before 1y in this EHR (loss to facility or
  death-unknown). The Fine-Gray design handles this by censoring rather than exclusion,
  but the 1-year cumulative incidence is a lower bound under differential loss.
- Single center; no external validation.
- No mortality data; death is unobserved.
- MVI is sparse in pathology text; mediation is directional only.
- Clinical adjudication (chart review) remains essential for a recurrence-specific
  claim and is not computable here.
