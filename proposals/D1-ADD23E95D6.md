# Paired respiratory-hemodynamic recovery at ICU discharge and early failure

## Scientific deliverable and unresolved claim

The future solver must construct the leakage-safe first-ICU-discharge cohort, estimate a prespecified paired recovery phenotype from the final six hours and preceding six hours, fit cause-specific baseline and temporal models, and produce locked held-out predictions, phenotype-specific cumulative-incidence estimates, calibration/uncertainty, and falsification diagnostics. No readiness result is a clinical conclusion.

The parent’s broad residual-instability construct leaves an important unresolved question: does residual respiratory support mean the same thing as unresolved hemodynamic instability, and does either phenotype preferentially identify ICU return versus death? The primary falsifiable hypothesis is that, among adults at a valid first ICU-discharge landmark, patients with isolated respiratory residual burden, isolated hemodynamic residual burden, or dual residual burden in the final six hours have different and higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU return or in-hospital death than patients with low/resolved burden in both domains, after adjustment for discharge context. The direction and magnitude may differ by event type.

A secondary information hypothesis is that a masked temporal model using the same 24 hourly physiologic streams improves cause-specific calibration and cumulative-incidence estimation over a fixed-feature baseline because it retains persistence, recovery, cross-domain order and observation masks. This tests a substantive recovery interpretation, not a small predictive-score contest.

Supported before fitting: the frozen MIMIC-IV 3.1 archive contains the relevant timestamps, repeated charted support/physiology and ICU-return/death fields; the parent scan measured 546,028 admissions, 94,458 ICU stays, 70,138 eligible first-hospital-ICU landmarks, 3,872 48-hour ICU returns, 856 exact-timestamp deaths and 4,630 composite events. These are feasibility facts, not phenotype or association results. Coverage, phenotype prevalence, model convergence and uncertainty remain to be computed.

## Clinical importance and advance

A respiratory-residual patient and a hemodynamic-residual patient may require different monitoring after ICU transfer. A return/death composite also hides whether a phenotype predicts revisible ICU escalation or death before return. Separating the domains and competing first events could inform what a prospective discharge-readiness study should adjudicate. A supportive result would justify prospective review and external validation, not delaying discharge or changing oxygen/vasopressor treatment. A null or context-sensitive result would be equally important evidence against using a generic residual-risk score.

## Baseline, alternative and uncertainty

The simple baseline is a pair of cause-specific Cox models for first ICU return and first death using the four-level phenotype, early-to-late burden, final values/masks, age, gender, ICU LOS, first/last careunit, admission_type and a small prespecified admission covariate set. A nested snapshot-only version removes early trajectory/phenotype features. Estimate Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence, cause-specific associations, calibration and bootstrap confidence intervals.

The substantive alternative is a small masked GRU or temporal-convolution model with two discrete-time cause-specific hazard heads over eight six-hour post-index bins. Inputs are exactly the 24 hourly physiologic bins, source/observation masks, phenotype channels and baseline covariates; target is first ICU return, first death, or censoring. It can reveal whether transient excursions, persistence, cross-domain order and recovery interactions contain information beyond fixed phenotype summaries. Evaluate IPCW cause-specific and integrated Brier scores, cumulative-incidence calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, secondary AUROC/AUPRC, phenotype risk separation and bootstrap/subject-level uncertainty. Fit normalization, phenotype checks, model and calibration using training/validation only; patient-level stratified train/validation/test split; lock test predictions. Use three split seeds as sensitivity.

Falsification ablations: within-stay hourly-order permutation; mask-only and mask-removed versions; respiratory-only and hemodynamic-only channels; fixed-phenotype versus full-sequence compression; threshold/source sensitivity; and careunit/admission-type stratification. Any gain that vanishes after mask or context adjustment is evidence for a documentation/disposition process, not a physiologic recovery signal.

## Interpretation and missing evidence

Supportive: phenotype-specific cumulative incidence is directionally and clinically meaningfully higher for residual groups, event-type differences are reproducible with confidence intervals excluding the prespecified null/margin, and the temporal model improves cause-specific calibration beyond the baseline without the improvement being explained by masks, order artifacts or care context. This supports a prognostic phenotype and prospective adjudication only.

Adverse: no separation, reversed associations, unstable phenotype definitions, no calibrated temporal improvement, or disappearance after mask/context/order falsification. This falsifies the paired recovery hypothesis as operationalized and argues against using it for discharge decisions.

Inconclusive: inadequate domain coverage, rare phenotype/events, unstable competing-risk fits, wide intervals crossing clinically important margins, missing death timing, or non-positivity. Report feasibility and uncertainty without selecting a clinical rule.

The data cannot adjudicate bedside work of breathing, goals of care, treatment limitation, discharge rationale, staffing, planned versus unplanned return, or whether intervention changes outcome. Local notes are not a validated primary label; radiology images and raw waveforms are unavailable. External validation in eICU or another hospital, clinician adjudication and prospective/causal study are required before clinical adoption.
