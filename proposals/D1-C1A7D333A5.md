# Is the hypotension-burden → AKI association an exposure effect, or a monitoring/detection-intensity fingerprint?

Lead working proposal — eICU 2.0 island, **episode 23**. Operation: **targeted repair** of true parent
expert seed `[starting question]` (`[prior hypothesis]`, assessed *repairable*),
"Duration of hypotension". Status: **design owned by the Lead for compilation**. No reference/solver
solution was executed at discovery — this is design + grounding (readiness), not a solved result.

## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

The seed asks whether, *at the same minimum blood pressure*, longer-lasting hypotension predicts more
subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI). AKI is one of the most consequential ICU morbidities and is the
outcome most often used to justify MAP targets (e.g. MAP ≥ 65 mmHg), yet retrospective MAP–AKI
associations are observational and may not transport or may not be causal. K1 confirms the premise at
the level the seed states it: in a VitalDB surgical cohort, time-weighted MAP<65 burden is associated
with creatinine-based (KDIGO) AKI in a base logistic model (AUC 0.725). The clinically consequential,
still-unresolved question is **what that association means**: an exposure effect of sustained low MAP,
or an artifact of *how much the patient is measured*.

The **substantive advance** over K1/K3 and the seed is a **positive falsification test of the
monitoring/detection-intensity rival**, built on this island's own observation-process insight (K2):
patients sampled more frequently for blood pressure have a *longer recorded* hypotensive burden (the
numerator is a sampled fraction), and patients sampled more frequently for creatinine have *more
detected* AKI (an outcome defined by a lab rise). If the crude association is largely explained by
measurement intensity, then "longer hypotension predicts AKI" is partly a statement about monitoring,
not physiology — a materially different interpretation for MAP-target decisions.

## 2. Hypotheses and discriminating predictions

- **H1 (exposure / seed).** Greater time-weighted hypotension burden (fraction of observed time with
  MAP<65 mmHg) in the pre-landmark window predicts AKI after adjusting for nadir MAP and severity, and
  the effect **survives** adjustment for both BP sampling density and creatinine sampling density.
- **R1 (monitoring/detection-intensity artifact — strongest rival).** The H1 effect is explained by
  measurement intensity: it attenuates to a null/CI-including-1 estimate once BP observations/hour and
  creatinine assays/24h are in the model, or its within-hospital transport collapses.
- **R2 (confounding by indication/severity).** Sicker patients have more hypotension recorded and more
  AKI; adjust for APACHE IVa acuity and admission diagnosis.
- **R3 (recovery/reverse).** Longest hypotensive episodes mark patients already recovering/being
  de-escalated; pre-specify a non-monotone/reverse shape check.

**Separating observation.** H1 and R1 make different predictions on the *same* coefficient: the
hypoburden effect after adding sampling-density covariates. H2/R1 also differ on a pre-specified
**negative control**: the burden→AKI slope should persist in the subgroup with dense, uniform sampling
(R1 predicts it shrinks toward null as sampling density variance is removed), while in a
sparse/irregular-sampling subgroup the *crude* slope is inflated under R1 but not under H1.

Pre-specified decision rules (numeric):
- **Supportive (H1):** adjusted OR per +0.10 hypotensive-time fraction > 1 with 95% CI excluding 1;
  effect retains ≥70% of its crude magnitude after adding both sampling-density terms; leave-one-
  hospital-out (LOHO) calibration slope c_slope ∈ [0.7, 1.4]; negative-control subgroup slope ≥ 0.7×
  dense-sampling slope.
- **Adverse (R1):** adjusted OR CI includes 1 (or ≥30% attenuation to null) once sampling densities are
  modelled, OR crude slope is concentrated in the sparse-sampling subgroup and near-null in the dense
  subgroup, OR c_slope < 0.5.
- **Inconclusive:** point estimate between thresholds; < 15 hospitals; or < 200 AKI events.

## 3. Population, temporal boundaries, variables, outcome

- **Population.** Adult eICU 2.0 stays, `patient.age >= 16`, with ≥ 6h of pre-landmark `vitalPeriodic`
  coverage and a baseline creatinine available; one row per `patientunitstayid`. (Exact eligibility
  counts to be produced by the solver; a bounded Lead probe is planned, not asserted here.)
- **Temporal boundaries.** Landmark = hour 24 after ICU admission (`vitalPeriodic.observationoffset`
  and `lab.labresultoffset` are in **MINUTES**, verified from source headers). Exposure window
  `(0, 1440]` min; outcome window `(1440, 2880]` min; baseline creatinine from `(-inf, 0]`
  (pre-ICU/admission) or the earliest in `(0,1440]`.
- **Exposure.** `hypoburden = (time with systemicmean < 65)/(observed time)` in `(0,1440]`; also
  `nadir_map = min(systemicmean)`; `bp_density = #vitalPeriodic obs / observed hours`.
- **Outcome.** AKI in `(1440, 2880]` by a pre-specified creatinine-rise rule vs baseline: rise ≥ 0.3
  mg/dL or ≥ 1.5×baseline (KDIGO Stage-1 analogue), from `lab.labname` creatinine strings (exact
  strings to be confirmed by the solver recount — see §6); `cr_assays = #creatinine labs / 24h` as the
  detection-intensity covariate.
- **Severity.** `apachePatientResult` acuity columns (exact columns confirmed from source header by the
  solver) + admission diagnosis stratum.

## 4. Two methods on the same question (method exploration)

- **Baseline (simple).** Fixed-effect logistic regression: AKI ~ hypoburden + nadir_map + severity +
  bp_density + cr_assays. Yields an adjusted OR per +0.10 burden; the H1/R1 discriminating coefficient.
- **Substantive alternative (mechanistic/time-varying).** A **joint/marked point-process or
  multivariable time-varying survival model** of the MAP trajectory and creatinine observation process:
  model MAP as a latent trajectory with an observation (sampling) sub-model, and creatinine as
  irregularly observed, so the *duration effect* is estimated from modeled physiology rather than from
  the sampled fraction. This is the method that could separate "true exposure time below 65" from
  "sampled time below 65" — information the baseline logistic (which treats hypoburden as an observed
  scalar and assumes sampling is ignorable) cannot recover.
- **Evaluation.** Discrimination (ΔAUROC vs baseline), calibration (slope/intercept), and LOHO
  transport of both. Deliverable outputs: adjusted OR per +0.10 burden (baseline and alternative);
  attenuation fraction on adding sampling density; subgroup (dense vs sparse) slopes; LOHO c_slope.

## 5. Falsification / interpretation

- Supportive H1 changes the interpretation toward a physiology-linked duration signal worth a prospective
  MAP-target test. Adverse (R1) result means the retrospective association is largely a monitoring
  fingerprint and should **not** be used to justify a MAP threshold — a valuable negative finding.
  Inconclusive keeps the question open and specifies the missing data (uniform sampling / external
  registry). An association surviving adjustment is **not** causal identification; a true causal MAP
  target claim requires a prospective randomized design (K3 explicitly warns adjusted burden does not
  establish a threshold).
