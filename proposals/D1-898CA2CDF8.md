# Support-withdrawal context at ICU discharge and early failure

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe first-ICU-discharge cohort, derive pre-index physiologic and support-transition features, fit a prespecified baseline and a joint latent recovery-state model, and produce locked held-out estimates. Completion requires an auditable cohort/exclusion table; one-hour physiologic/support feature and coverage tables; counts and uncertainty for prespecified support-context strata; cause-specific predictions and cumulative-incidence/association/calibration tables for first ICU return and first death; latent-state transition/emission diagnostics; order, mask and support-channel falsification ablations; and a conclusion-to-output map. No discovery probe or readiness check is a clinical conclusion.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The selected parent established that paired respiratory and hemodynamic burden near ICU outtime may distinguish early ICU return from death. The remaining clinical ambiguity is whether an apparently acceptable final MAP or oxygen burden means stable recovery, or merely transient normalization while vasoactive/ventilatory support has just been withdrawn. This matters because a discharge-readiness assessment could reasonably monitor these states differently, but the available retrospective data cannot establish that changing support or delaying discharge would improve outcomes.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among adults at a valid first ICU-discharge landmark with comparable final-six-hour respiratory and hemodynamic burden, patients whose normalized physiology occurs while vasoactive or ventilatory support remains active, or within a prespecified recent-withdrawal interval, have higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU return than patients with comparable physiology that is stable off those supports. The direction may differ for first death. A secondary hypothesis is that support-physiology order and persistence improve estimation of the two competing outcomes over the parent-style fixed phenotype and snapshot summaries.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is data feasibility: MIMIC-IV contains dated ICU stays, charted MAP/respiratory measurements, ICU support procedures, infusion intervals, admission death times and later ICU stays. The parent’s measured feasibility counts (70,138 eligible first-hospital-ICU landmarks, 3,872 48-hour ICU returns, 856 exact-timestamp deaths and 4,630 composite events) are inherited feasibility facts, not evidence for this new association. Support-state coverage, prevalence, event counts, convergence and effect estimates must be computed afresh.

## Population, index and outcomes

Use MIMIC-IV adults with patients.anchor_age >=18, retaining the >89 top-code limitation. Within subject_id, select the first ICU stay in the hospitalization by icu/icustays.intime. Require linked admissions, non-null intime/outtime, outtime > intime, and admissions.dischtime > outtime; report every exclusion. Exclude impossible joins/times. Split by subject_id.

The index is icu/icustays.outtime. All predictors use only records strictly before outtime. Charted physiology uses icu/chartevents.charttime in [outtime-24 hours, outtime), with one-hour bins and no value at outtime. The support-transition windows are [outtime-12 hours,outtime-6 hours) and [outtime-6 hours,outtime). An infusion/procedure is active in a bin only when its starttime is before bin end and its endtime is after bin start; null/invalid end times are not silently treated as active and are reported. No post-index order, medication or note is used.

Follow to min(outtime+48 hours, admissions.dischtime). The first event is ICU return when a later icu/icustays.intime for the same subject_id and hadm_id is >outtime and within the horizon, or death when admissions.deathtime is non-null, after outtime, within the horizon and before return; tied times classify death first. Censor at the earlier administrative boundary otherwise. Primary estimands are 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU return and first death as competing events. Cause-specific hazard ratios are secondary associations. Retain the parent composite and seven-day ICU return as sensitivity outcomes. Call the endpoint ICU return, not unplanned readmission, because intent is unavailable. Never infer an event time from hospital_expire_flag.

## Feature construction and strata

Resample chart streams to one-hour medians and observation masks; never impute missing as normal. Define hemodynamic burden as MAP <65 mmHg, preserving arterial and non-invasive sources. Define respiratory support burden separately as FiO2 >=0.40 or O2 flow >=4 L/min; keep these on their native scales. For each domain, require at least two observed bins in each six-hour half-window for a primary phenotype; otherwise mark unavailable and report coverage.

For each six-hour half-window derive fraction of observed MAP bins <65, fraction of observed respiratory bins above threshold, median values, number of observations, and observation masks. Derive support exposure per hour from infusion/procedure interval overlap. A vasoactive-support flag is positive if any prespecified vasoactive infusion overlaps the bin; a ventilatory-support flag is positive if intubation/NIV procedure coverage overlaps the bin, with extubation ending the recorded episode. Preserve procedure/support source and item IDs.

The primary support-context contrast is prespecified before outcome inspection:
1. normalized-off-support: final-six-hour MAP burden <1/3 and respiratory burden <1/3, with no vasoactive infusion and no active intubation/NIV procedure in the final six hours;
2. normalized-on-support or recent-withdrawal: the same low final burden, but support overlaps the final six hours or was active in the preceding [outtime-12h,outtime-6h) window and ended before outtime;
3. residual support/physiology: final respiratory or hemodynamic burden >=1/3, with support status retained rather than collapsed;
4. mixed/unavailable: discordant or insufficiently observed domains, reported rather than discarded.

The key estimand is the adjusted difference in 48-hour competing cumulative incidence between groups 1 and 2 among patients with adequate final-window coverage; groups 3 and 4 are descriptive/secondary. Thresholds, item sets, windows and source-specific sensitivities are locked before fitting. The support flag is a context marker, not an assigned treatment, and the result cannot establish that stopping support caused failure.

## Baseline, mechanistic alternative and method choice

The simple baseline is two cause-specific Cox models for first ICU return and first death using the four-level support-context variable, parent respiratory/hemodynamic phenotype, early-to-late burden, final values/masks, age, gender, ICU LOS, first/last careunit, admission_type and a small prespecified admission covariate set. A nested snapshot baseline removes early-window and support-transition features. Estimate Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence, cause-specific associations, calibration, bootstrap confidence intervals and absolute risk differences.

The substantive alternative is a constrained, four-state hourly latent recovery model fitted only on the training split: emissions are the observed MAP/respiratory streams and support indicators; states are initialized/interpreted as stable-off-support, normalized-on-support, residual respiratory, and residual hemodynamic/dual burden, with a sparse transition matrix and state-specific cause-specific discrete hazards for eight six-hour post-index bins. Fit emissions/transitions by EM (or a documented Bayesian equivalent), propagate posterior state probabilities rather than hard labels, and evaluate held-out competing-event risk. This alternative can reveal persistence, support withdrawal followed by destabilization, and cross-domain order that the fixed-feature Cox baseline loses; its scientific value is mechanistic state characterization, not a claim that the latent states are bedside diagnoses. Compare IPCW cause-specific and integrated Brier scores, cumulative-incidence calibration intercept/slope, phenotype/state separation and bootstrap/subject-level uncertainty. Use patient-level stratified train/validation/test splits and three split seeds; lock test predictions and fit all normalization/state definitions on training data.

A masked GRU/temporal-convolution model was considered because the demonstrations show that dated order and observation masks can carry information. It is deferred as a secondary sensitivity rather than selected as the principal alternative: the parent already contains a masked sequence comparison, while this episode’s new uncertainty is support-dependent recovery and state persistence. Revisit it if the latent model fails convergence or if a held-out comparison shows state compression discards clinically relevant order. No GPU is mandatory. Streaming extraction and Cox/HMM fitting are expected to be CPU-feasible; approximate solver costs (1–4 hours on up to 16 CPUs and under 64 GB for extraction/fits, with optional one A100 only for a later GRU sensitivity) are unverified planning estimates, not measured execution.

## Falsification and interpretation

Falsification checks include permutation of hourly support/physiology order within stay; mask-only and mask-removed models; support-channel removal; physiology-only and support-only models; replacing interval support with storetime or last-value coding; arterial versus non-invasive MAP and FiO2 versus O2-flow sensitivity; threshold/window sensitivity; careunit/admission-type stratification; and a negative-control horizon before the index where no post-index event can be caused by the recorded transition. If the contrast disappears after removing masks, storetime artifacts or care context, it supports documentation/disposition bias rather than a recovery mechanism.

Supportive results require adequate coverage and reproducible, clinically meaningful separation of normalized-on/recent-withdrawal versus normalized-off-support for first ICU return, with uncertainty excluding the prespecified null/margin; a latent model should improve competing-event calibration over the snapshot baseline and its state/transition signal should survive support/order/context falsifications. This supports a prognostic hypothesis and a prospective adjudication/validation study only.

Adverse results are null or reversed contrasts, support-state instability, no calibrated improvement, convergence failure, or disappearance after documentation/context/order controls. That falsifies the operationalized support-withdrawal hypothesis and argues against using these retrospective markers for discharge decisions.

Inconclusive results include inadequate support coverage, rare strata/events, non-positivity, wide intervals crossing the clinical margin, unstable competing-risk models, unreliable interval endpoints, or missing death timing. Report feasibility and uncertainty without selecting a clinical rule.

The data cannot adjudicate bedside work of breathing, actual ventilator settings between chart events, clinician intent, planned versus unplanned ICU return, goals of care, treatment limitations, staffing, discharge rationale, or whether delaying discharge or continuing support changes outcomes. Notes are not a validated substitute for those labels; radiology images and raw waveforms are unavailable. External validation in eICU/another hospital, clinician adjudication of support and discharge readiness, and a prospective or causal study are required before clinical adoption.
