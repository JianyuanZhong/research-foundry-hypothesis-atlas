# Time-weighted hypotension and incident kidney injury after ICU admission

## Scientific question and hypothesis

The unresolved claim is not that hypotension causes AKI or that a MAP threshold is a treatment target. It is whether, among adult ICU stays with a recorded early MAP and no early creatinine-defined AKI, the *duration/intensity* of low MAP during the first 6 ICU hours is associated with incident kidney injury over the subsequent 6–48 hours after conditioning on the same minimum MAP and measured baseline risk.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: conditional on minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, demographics, admission context, hospital and measured severity, greater time-weighted MAP burden below 65 mmHg during ICU hours 0–6 is associated with higher risk of a new creatinine rise during hours 6–48. A null result, reversal, or failure to transport across hospitals falsifies the proposed incremental claim.

Clinical importance: minimum blood pressure is easy to record but discards duration and recovery. If burden adds stable, calibrated information, it could improve early AKI surveillance and help design trials that stratify shock exposure. It cannot by itself establish a vasopressor target, causal effect, or benefit of prolonging MAP.

The strongest available evidence supports only that the snapshot contains temporally indexed blood-pressure, creatinine, vasopressor, severity and disposition data, and that these signals can be aligned. The expert seed and related references do not establish this eICU association. The proposed study tests the unresolved incremental association.

## Population, time boundaries and estimand

Use the eICU 2.0 snapshot (snapshot ID `[source checksum]`) at the ICU-stay level. Include one stay per `patientunitstayid), age >=18 (including the eICU older-age category if encoded as a nonnumeric >89 value), with at least two valid `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` observations in hours 0–6, a creatinine result in hours 0–6, and sufficient follow-up to hours 6–48 unless death or ICU/hospital discharge occurs first. Exposure (primary): reconstruct MAP from `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` in [0,360] minutes. Remove missing/non-numeric values and implausible values using a prespecified physiologic range; linearly interpolate only across gaps <=30 minutes and leave longer gaps unobserved. Compute (i) time-weighted minutes below 65 mmHg, (ii) area-under-65 (mmHg-min), and (iii) minimum observed MAP. Report monitoring density and repeat the analysis with `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` as an independent measurement source. ## Analysis and baselines

Primary estimand: adjusted risk ratio/odds ratio and absolute-risk contrast per prespecified increase in area-under-65, conditional on minimum MAP, with 95% confidence intervals and hospital-clustered or robust uncertainty. Fit a transparent penalized logistic model for incident AKI using minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, monitoring density, prespecified patient/admission covariates and hospital effects; add burden terms in a nested model. Report calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC, observed-versus-predicted plots, partial effect with confidence band, and likelihood-ratio/incremental explained variation. Use bootstrap resampling by hospital and patient-level split by `uniquepid`.

Transport test: hold out entire hospitals, preferably repeated grouped folds with a final locked hospital set selected before fitting. Require minimum hospital size and report the number excluded. Fit on training hospitals, tune only within training folds, and report discrimination, calibration and absolute risk on held-out hospitals. A clinically useful claim requires consistent direction and calibration, not a small AUROC gain.

Mechanistic alternative on the same question: a cause-specific piecewise-exponential multi-state model with 5-minute or 30-minute MAP segments, time-varying MAP burden, recovery slope after each low-MAP episode, early vasopressor exposure, and random hospital intercepts. States are AKI-free ICU stay, creatinine-defined AKI, death/discharge before AKI; fit only on pre-outcome rows. This alternative can reveal whether risk is tied to persistent exposure, failure to recover, or a transient nadir—information the scalar minimum-MAP baseline loses. Compare prespecified contrasts at the same minimum MAP, cause-specific hazards, cumulative incidence, calibration and bootstrap uncertainty.

Learned sequence alternative (deferred unless the mechanistic model leaves substantial trajectory residuals): a small GRU/temporal convolution model on 5-minute bins over hours 0–6 containing MAP, heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO2 where available, creatinine/glucose/labs available before 6 hours, vasopressor rates, observedness indicators and time-since-last-measurement. Target is the same AKI endpoint; patient-level `uniquepid` split and hospital-held-out test are identical. Compare against the transparent baseline and mechanistic model using Brier/calibration/AUPRC and prespecified subgroup transport, not only AUROC. The sequence model is scientifically justified only if it identifies reproducible recovery/trajectory phenotypes or improves calibrated risk across hospitals; it is deferred in the primary design because the mechanistic model directly tests the duration-versus-nadir uncertainty with less opaque missingness behavior. Future solver estimate: baseline/mechanistic fits are CPU-feasible under 16 CPU/256 GiB and likely hours, but unmeasured; the sequence sensitivity is approximately one allocated A100, 4–8 CPU, 32–64 GiB and 2–6 hours for a small set of seeds, also unverified. Discovery used only a small read-only probe, not the solver study.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence is a prespecified positive burden coefficient after minimum-MAP adjustment, clinically nontrivial absolute-risk gradient with calibrated uncertainty, directionally consistent hospital-held-out results, and mechanistic evidence that duration/recovery—not merely missingness or vasopressor recording—explains the increment.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed burden contrast, loss of calibration/direction in held-out hospitals, or disappearance after monitoring-density/artifact sensitivity. That would support minimum MAP alone, reveal site-dependent measurement bias, or refute general transport—not prove absence of physiological harm.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few qualifying creatinine follow-ups, sparse MAP coverage, unstable estimates across lab naming/measurement windows, or wide intervals spanning clinically meaningful effects. It should lead to a narrower descriptive study or additional data, not a forced conclusion.

Computationally checkable claims are row counts, eligibility flow, variable construction, leakage-free splits, fitted coefficients/hazards, uncertainty, calibration, transport metrics and sensitivity analyses. Clinical adjudication is required for true AKI diagnosis, pre-ICU renal reserve, reasons for monitoring gaps, treatment intent, and whether a burden threshold should alter care. The dataset cannot establish those claims, and observational associations cannot establish that changing vasopressors or MAP would change AKI.

## Demonstration and alternative dispositions

The natural-history demonstration was inspected as a full readable article: its learned dated health-trajectory approach motivates a possible sequence sensitivity, but eICU lacks its population-scale longitudinal diagnosis design and external Danish validation; it is an adaptation, not a reproduction. The Bayesian demonstration was inspected as a full readable article: its latent longitudinal structure is informative conceptually, but eICU lacks genetic inputs, so an EHR-only latent model would change the question and is deferred. The cancer demonstration's main article/STAR Methods remain unavailable; only its supplement was inspected, which documents multimodal/missingness-aware learning and washout ideas. eICU has no images or genuine narrative notes, so full multimodal reproduction is unavailable; its missingness sensitivity is retained conceptually, not cited as a reproduced method.
