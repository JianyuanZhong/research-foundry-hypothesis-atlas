## Scientific opening, claim boundary, and hypothesis

The parent establishes a feasible and clinically important question, but its primary repeated-CBC cohort is conditioned on returning for a second research assessment, surviving, remaining cancer-free, and having complete repeat measurements. Its Cox specification also risks linear dependence when baseline CBC, second CBC, and their change are entered together. The remaining consequential question is whether a cancer-type contrast survives an unconditional first-CBC design and a same-table source/timing audit.

Prior inspected work supports that prediagnostic CBC abnormalities or trajectories can associate with CRC, but does not establish CRC specificity against haematologic cancer, nor whether registry timing and observation selection create the contrast [K1]–[K3]. The strongest evidence-supported claim is therefore a descriptive CBC–cancer association in selected populations. The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults with a complete first research-assessment CBC and no registry-coded malignant cancer at that assessment, a platelet-high/haemoglobin-low profile is more associated with CRC than haematologic cancer during years 2–5, is more CRC-specific than the analogous contrast for other primary cancers, and is not reversed or materially displaced when the same event families are derived from paired inpatient diagnosis/date fields.

This is a descriptive, temporally ordered association. It is not a causal effect of changing CBC, a biological mechanism, a symptomatic-population diagnostic rule, or clinical utility.

The clinically consequential boundary is whether a CBC pattern remains cancer-type-specific after the near-diagnostic interval and alternate ascertainment source. A supportive result would justify an indication-rich external validation; a near-diagnostic-only, generic-cancer, era-dependent, or source-discordant result would argue against interpreting the pattern as a stable CRC-specific precursor.

## Population, outcomes, and temporal estimands

Primary population: one row per unique `eid) with valid T0 and complete first CBC. Exclude participants with any valid registry C00–C97 primary malignant code/date on or before T0, including C77–C79 only in the broad prior-cancer screen; do not impute missing CBC. Record flow, missingness, prior-cancer exclusion, and first-assessment calendar-era counts.

Primary follow-up begins at T0. Define mutually exclusive event classes from each source separately:

- CRC: ICD-10 C18–C20, including subcodes.
- Haematologic: C81–C96.
- Other primary malignant cancer: C00–C97 excluding CRC and haematologic; do not treat this as a true negative control.
- Non-cancer death: `40000-0.0`.
- Same-day multi-class cancer ties are a separately reported tie outcome and are excluded from the mutually exclusive type contrast; a sensitivity assigns by a predeclared array-order rule. Other cancer and death are competing events.

The primary estimand is the delayed 2–5-year contrast under a common registry-clean risk set. Enter the risk set at T0+2 only for people alive, event-free and administratively observable then; follow to the first event, T0+5, or T_admin=min(source maximum valid registry date, date of analysis), expected 2022-06-01. The primary cause-specific coefficient contrast is:

`D_H,2-5 = (beta_P,CRC - beta_Hb,CRC) - (beta_P,HEME - beta_Hb,HEME)`

where P and Hb are standardized first-CBC platelet and haemoglobin values and each beta is from a target-specific cause-specific model. Report 95% intervals and a clinically interpretable +1 SD platelet/−1 SD haemoglobin finite contrast. The secondary specificity estimand is:

`Q_2-5 = D_H,2-5 - D_O,2-5`

where D_O replaces HEME with other primary cancer. Standardize to the development covariate distribution. Neither contrast is a causal effect or an individual probability.

The 0–2-year analysis uses the identical first-CBC population and competing-event rules, with follow-up from T0 to T0+2. It is a prespecified near-diagnostic context comparison, not a replacement primary. Report the 0–5 piecewise model and 2–5 delayed-entry model together so attenuation or confinement to the early window is visible. Report cumulative incidence at 2 and 5 years and absolute risk differences for fixed development-defined CBC profiles, without calling them diagnostic risks.

Ascertainment bridge: rerun the complete locked population, temporal windows, code families, competing events and covariates separately using source A registry pairs and source B inpatient pairs. Never pool the sources, use one source to fill missing dates in the other, or let source performance tune the scientific hypothesis. Report source-specific D/Q, event counts, registry-only/inpatient-only/both capture, and same-class date displacement among participants with valid events in both sources. This measures source/timing sensitivity, not clinical adjudication.

Observation-selection stress test: define R1 as parseable `53-1.0>T0` plus complete instance-1 CBC, and R2 analogously for instance 2. Report R1/R2 prevalence, gap distribution, T0 calendar era, and baseline covariate balance. Repeat-CBC analyses start at T1 only among those cancer/death-free at T1 and use four first-to-second changes plus the gap; they are secondary and explicitly conditional on survival, cancer-free status, repeat attendance and complete measurement. Compare unweighted and baseline-only inverse-probability-of-R1 weighted estimates, with fixed 1st/99th percentile truncation and positivity diagnostics. This cannot recover unmeasured clinical indication.

## Competing methods, split, uncertainty, and compute

Baseline (primary explanatory model): separate cause-specific piecewise Cox models for CRC, haematologic cancer, other primary cancer and death, using only first-CBC WBC, haemoglobin, RDW, platelets, age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP, T0 calendar era, and a 0–2/2–5 interval indicator. Do not put second CBC levels and their changes into this model. Use robust participant-level standard errors, predeclared spline terms only for age/interval if required, and Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence. Report PH/time-varying diagnostics and linear contrasts, not coefficient stories detached from D/Q.

Learned alternative: a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multitask competing-risk model with the exact same first-CBC inputs, population, outcome source, windows, competing events and calendar split. It outputs cause-specific hazards/probabilities for CRC, haematologic, other cancer and death at 2 and 5 years. Tune only in development. Its scientific role is to reveal nonlinear platelet–haemoglobin interactions, threshold-free profiles, or era-dependent type-specific hazards that an additive Cox contrast can average away; a small AUC gain without a stable D/Q contrast and calibration is not a scientific advance.

Use an earliest-70% T0 calendar split for development and latest-30% temporal holdout, preserving participants and fitting all scaling, imputation, tuning, weighting and calibration in development only. Report held-out target-specific calibration slope/intercept, cumulative-incidence Brier/log scores at 2 and 5 years, and time-dependent discrimination secondarily. Use 500 participant bootstrap replicates for D/Q, source differences, weighted estimates and holdout predictions, preserving convergence failures. If the held-out haematologic count is below 50, the type-specific learned comparison is inconclusive regardless of point estimate.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Supportive requires: (1) D_H,2-5 is positive with a 95% interval excluding zero and point estimate at least 0.20 log-hazard units; (2) Q_2-5 is positive with an informative interval; (3) the delayed direction is not a supported reversal across calendar eras or R1/gap strata; (4) the inpatient-source D has compatible direction and no source reversal or absolute discrepancy >0.20 log-hazard units, with adequate paired support; (5) the learned model reproduces the prespecified type contrast on the temporal holdout with acceptable calibration. This supports a reproducible descriptive cancer-type signal compatible with, but not proving, biology.

Adverse includes a precise delayed reversal, a stronger haematologic direction, Q at or below zero with useful precision, a positive 0–2 signal that attenuates/reverses in 2–5, registry/inpatient reversal or >0.20 source discrepancy, generic similarity to other cancer, era/R1/gap dependence, or learned calibration failure. These findings support ascertainment/proximity/selection rivals; they do not prove unmeasured indication bias or refute all CBC–cancer associations.

Inconclusive includes fewer than 50 held-out haematologic events, sparse paired source overlap, invalid pair/date support, positivity failure, nonconvergence, or an interval spanning both no meaningful effect and the 0.20 boundary. Do not search alternative thresholds after seeing results.

Computationally checkable: schema/namespace binding, cohort flow, pair integrity, event timing, D/Q, source displacement, repeat-observation strata/weights, temporal holdout calibration, uncertainty and the final classification. Unavailable without additional data: symptoms, reason for test, ordering channel, primary-care contact intensity, ferritin/iron/FIT, medication and infection timing, pathology, stage, morphology, molecular subtype, treatment, adjudicated cancer onset, symptomatic-population diagnostic accuracy, causality, mechanism, clinical utility, and prospective impact.

## Alternatives and decision record

Selected: first-CBC delayed source-robust contrast, because it removes the parent’s repeat-visit conditioning from the primary estimand and directly tests an observable ascertainment implication using only same-namespace fields.

Retained secondary: the parent’s repeated-CBC change analysis, corrected so a model never includes baseline level, second level and change simultaneously. It remains useful for within-person trajectory description but cannot repair missing clinical indication.

Deferred/rejected alternatives:

- Parent primary repeat-CBC trajectory: deferred as primary because repeat attendance is a collider/selection gate and its original level/change specification is non-identifiable; retained as a secondary sensitivity.
- A neural sequence/state-space model: deferred; the source has only two broadly complete repeat CBC visits, sparse haematologic events, and no indication process. Extra capacity would not resolve the ascertainment uncertainty.
- Cross-namespace `ukb672073` HRO/Online Follow-up/Assessment Center joins: rejected for this design; matching `eid` values without a documented crosswalk is invalid.
- Expert seed 40 (HbA1c/weight change → pancreatic cancer): deferred after its bounded repeat-landmark audit found zero events in both proposed 0–2 and 2–5-year windows; not refuted, but not an answerable successor here.
- Expert seed 39 (ApoB/albumin → HCC): deferred; its same-table restart has only sparse paired HCC support (reported prior audit: 20 early and 40 delayed events), so it does not provide a stronger ascertainment test.
- Expert seed 03 (MRI muscle quantity/MFI/grip): scientifically useful and separately developed under ukb672073, but it answers a different muscle-function question and cannot be used to improve this CBC ascertainment estimand. It also reinforces the namespace rule rather than supplying a same-table observation field.
