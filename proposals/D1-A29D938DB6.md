# Duration of Hypotension Beyond Minimum MAP for New AKI in Vasopressor-Treated ICU Patients: A Bounded eICU Study

## 1. Unresolved Clinical Question

In ICU patients receiving vasopressor infusion in the first 24 hours of admission, does the **duration** of hypotension (time-weighted burden of MAP < 65 mmHg in the first 24 h) add independent prognostic information for **new acute kidney injury (AKI)** beyond the **depth** of hypotension (minimum MAP in the same window), and is this association **consistent across hospitals**?

This question is clinically consequential because:
- The MAP 65 mmHg threshold is the operative boundary from the 65 trial [K3], which established that permissive hypotension (target 60–65 mmHg) did not reduce 90-day mortality but was associated with more acute renal failure as the most common serious adverse event.
- Small single-center and multicenter cohorts suggest duration matters [K1, K2], but no large heterogeneous multicenter study has tested whether duration adds information *beyond* minimum MAP, or whether the association is stable across hospital practice variation.
- If duration adds independent information, it could be used as a bedside monitoring metric (cumulative hours below threshold) to trigger renal-protective interventions, distinct from the instantaneous minimum MAP.

## 2. Leading Claim vs. Strongest Rival

**Leading claim (duration hypothesis):** Sustained hypoperfusion causes cumulative renal ischemic injury; the time-weighted burden of MAP < 65 mmHg adds prognostic information for AKI beyond the minimum MAP and standard severity covariates.

**Strongest rival (severity-confounding hypothesis):** Minimum MAP is a proxy for overall shock severity; once severity (APACHE score), age, sepsis status, and vasopressor dose are adjusted, duration adds nothing because it merely reflects the same underlying severity that drives both low MAP and AKI.

**Distinguishing observation:** In a multivariable model containing minimum MAP and covariates, the coefficient of hypotension duration (hours MAP < 65 in 0–24 h) and its cross-hospital consistency. If the rival is correct, the duration coefficient will be near zero with a wide CI after adjustment, and the effect will be inconsistent across hospitals (driven by a few high-severity centers). If the leading claim is correct, duration will retain a significant positive coefficient and show consistent direction across hospitals.

**What each outcome would and would not establish:**
- A significant duration coefficient supports an association but does not establish causation (observational data, residual confounding within hospitals).
- A null duration coefficient does not refute the mechanistic hypothesis (measurement granularity of 5-min medians, threshold choice, or outcome definition could attenuate the effect).
- Cross-hospital consistency (or lack thereof) addresses generalizability but not within-hospital treatment-selection bias.

## 6. Outcome

**Primary: New AKI (KDIGO creatinine-based) within 72 h of ICU admission.**

- Baseline creatinine = first creatinine with `labresultoffset` in [−1440, 0]; if none, first in (0, 720].
- AKI Stage 1: peak creatinine ≥ 1.5× baseline OR ≥ 0.3 mg/dL absolute increase within 72 h of baseline time.
- AKI Stage 2: ≥ 2.0× baseline OR ≥ 1.0 mg/dL absolute increase.
- AKI Stage 3: ≥ 3.0× baseline OR ≥ 4.0 mg/dL OR initiation of renal replacement therapy (not available in eICU; use creatinine criteria only).
- **Primary outcome:** any new AKI (stage ≥ 1), binary.
- **Secondary outcome:** AKI stage ≥ 2, binary.

## 8. Analysis Plan

### Primary analysis

1. **Model A (baseline):** Multivariable logistic regression, outcome = any new AKI. Predictors: covariates + `minMAP`.
2. **Model B (extended):** Same covariates + `minMAP` + `durHypot`.
3. **Tests:**
   - (a) Wald z-test for `durHypot` coefficient in Model B.
   - (b) Likelihood-ratio test (LRT) comparing Model A vs. Model B.
   - (c) Change in AUROC (Model B − Model A).

### Cross-hospital consistency

4. **GLMM:** Hospital random intercept, fixed effects as Model B. Report the `durHypot` coefficient with 95% CI, and the between-hospital variance component (I² or proportion of variance explained).
5. **Leave-one-hospital-out:** Forest plot of the `durHypot` OR for the top 20 hospitals by n (excluding each hospital in turn).

### Sensitivity analyses

6. MAP threshold 60 mmHg (instead of 65).
7. Exposure window 0–12 h (instead of 0–24 h).
8. Relax baseline creatinine exclusion (allow 3.0–4.0 mg/dL).
9. Restrict to septic shock (if `admissionDx` sepsis codes available and n permits).

## 9. Method Comparison: Baseline vs. Deferred Learned Alternative

### Baseline (primary, required)

Multivariable logistic regression / GLMM as specified above. This is the scientifically adequate primary analysis because:
- The primary estimand is the **independent contribution of duration beyond depth**, which is a linear contrast directly answered by a regression coefficient.
- The binary outcome at ~20–30% prevalence with n ~ 10k is well-suited to logistic regression.
- The GLMM directly addresses the cross-hospital consistency question with interpretable variance components.
- Coefficients are clinically interpretable (OR per hour of hypotension).

### Learned alternative (deferred, documented)

Gradient-boosted trees (e.g., LightGBM) with SHAP values to test whether `durHypot` contributes non-linearly or in interaction with `minMAP`/vasopressor dose beyond the linear model.

**Deferred because:**
- (a) The primary question is whether duration adds information *at all* — a linear model answers this directly with a testable coefficient.
- (b) With expected n ~ 10k and a binary outcome at ~20–30% prevalence, GBM adds complexity without a clear scientific advantage for the primary estimand.
- (c) SHAP values would not resolve the cross-hospital consistency question that the GLMM addresses directly.

**Revisit criteria:** If the linear model shows a significant but non-monotonic duration effect (e.g., U-shaped), or if residual confounding is suspected that a flexible model could address (e.g., interaction between duration and vasopressor dose), the GBM analysis becomes scientifically motivated.

**GPU:** Not needed. All analyses are tabular fits on ≤ 20k rows; CPU is appropriate.

## 10. Falsification Criteria

| Outcome | Criteria | Interpretation |
|---------|----------|---------------|
| **SUPPORTIVE** | `durHypot` OR per hour > 1.05 with 95% CI excluding 1.0 in Model B; LRT p < 0.05; consistent direction in ≥ 75% of hospitals (GLMM I² < 50%) | Duration adds independent prognostic information beyond depth; association is generalizable across hospitals. Supports using cumulative hypotension time as a bedside monitoring metric. |
| **ADVERSE** | `durHypot` OR 95% CI includes 1.0, OR LRT non-significant (p ≥ 0.05), OR direction inconsistent across > 50% of hospitals | Duration does not add information beyond depth + covariates in this population. The small-cohort findings [K1, K2] may not generalize, or may be driven by severity confounding. |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | n < 3,000 after all exclusions, OR creatinine missingness > 50% in the exposure population, OR AUROC delta < 0.01 with non-significant LRT | The study is underpowered or data quality is insufficient to draw conclusions. A larger or differently-defined cohort is needed. |

## 11. What Supportive, Adverse, and Inconclusive Results Would Mean

**Supportive result:**
- Would establish that time-weighted hypotension duration is an independent prognostic factor for AKI in a large heterogeneous ICU population, beyond what minimum MAP captures.
- Would support clinical monitoring of cumulative hypotension time (e.g., "hours below 65") as a trigger for renal-protective interventions (fluid optimization, vasopressor titration, avoidance of nephrotoxins).
- Would NOT establish causation (observational design) or that a specific intervention targeting duration would prevent AKI.
- Cross-hospital consistency would support generalizability but not within-hospital causal inference.

**Adverse result:**
- Would suggest that the small-cohort findings [K1, K2] are not generalizable to a large heterogeneous population, or that the duration effect is fully explained by severity and depth.
- Would redirect clinical attention to minimum MAP (or other depth metrics) as the operative prognostic variable.
- Would NOT refute the mechanistic hypothesis of cumulative ischemic injury (measurement granularity, threshold choice, or outcome definition could attenuate the effect).
- Would motivate a mechanistic or interventional study (e.g., RCT of duration-targeted interventions).

**Inconclusive result:**
- Would indicate that the eICU data (creatinine availability, MAP granularity) are insufficient for this specific question.
- Would motivate a different dataset (e.g., MIMIC-IV with more frequent labs), a different exposure window, or a different outcome definition.
- Would NOT support or refute the duration hypothesis.

## 13. Key References

### [K1] Janssen van Doorn K, et al. (2014)

**Title:** The duration of hypotension determines the evolution of bacteremia-induced acute kidney injury in the intensive care unit.
**Journal:** PLoS ONE, 9(12):e114312. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0114312. PMID: 25504214.
**Claim supported:** In 137 adults with septic shock and proven bloodstream infection, duration of MAP < 65 mmHg was associated with evolution to RIFLE Failure (OR 1.02 per 10 min, p = 0.0472); a cut-off of ≥ 51 min of severe hypotension identified higher risk.
**What this proposal adds:** Tests whether duration adds information beyond minimum MAP in a multicenter cohort (208 hospitals, ~200k stays) and whether the association is consistent across hospitals, rather than in a single-center n = 137 septic-shock cohort.
**Limitation:** Single center, n = 137, septic shock with proven bacteremia only; RIFLE endpoint; no cross-hospital generalizability; does not establish that duration beats minimum MAP in broader shock populations.
**Inspection:** Abstract only (Europe PMC metadata + abstract).

### [K2] Panwar R, et al. (2020)

**Title:** Relative Hypotension and Adverse Kidney-related Outcomes among Critically Ill Patients with Shock. A Multicenter, Prospective Cohort Study.
**Journal:** Am J Respir Crit Care Med, 202(11):1342–1351. DOI: 10.1164/rccm.201912-2316oc. PMID: 32614244.
**Claim supported:** In 302 shock patients on ≥ 4 h vasopressors at 7 ICUs, time-weighted average MPP deficit and % time with MPP deficit > 20% were independently associated with new significant AKI and MAKE within 14 days (adjusted odds +5.6% per 1% MPP deficit, p = 0.001).
**What this proposal adds:** Bounded prior evidence that time-weighted hypotension burden predicts kidney injury in shock; this proposal uses eICU's 5-minute periodic MAP to test the same duration-vs-depth contrast in a much larger, heterogeneous multicenter population and quantifies cross-hospital consistency.
**Limitation:** Prospective 7-ICU cohort (n = 302) with preillness basal MPP, which eICU does not provide; uses relative (basal-relative) hypotension, not absolute MAP < 65; does not address whether duration adds beyond minimum MAP within the same patient.
**Inspection:** Abstract only (Europe PMC metadata + abstract).

### [K3] Lamontagne F, et al. (2020)

**Title:** Effect of Reduced Exposure to Vasopressors on 90-Day Mortality in Older Critically Ill Patients With Vasodilatory Hypotension: A Randomized Clinical Trial.
**Journal:** JAMA, 323(6):539–548. DOI: 10.1001/jama.2020.0930. PMID: 32049269.
**Claim supported:** In 2463 randomized patients ≥ 65 with vasodilatory hypotension, a permissive-hypotension MAP target (60–65 mmHg) did not significantly reduce 90-day mortality (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.85–0.98 after adjustment OR 0.82); acute renal failure was the most common serious adverse event (3.2% vs 2.5%).
**What this proposal adds:** Establishes that the MAP 60–65 target is the clinically operative boundary used in practice; the proposal's exposure window (MAP < 65) is anchored to this trial-defined threshold, and the trial's null mortality result motivates focusing on AKI, a measurable and mechanistically plausible intermediate outcome.
**Limitation:** RCT of MAP targets in older patients; does not measure time-weighted hypotension burden or its independent contribution beyond minimum MAP; 90-day mortality endpoint, not AKI.
**Inspection:** Abstract only (Europe PMC metadata + abstract).

## 16. Limitations and Unresolved Issues

- **MAP granularity:** eICU `vitalPeriodic` is 5-min medians of 1-min monitor averages, not raw waveform; MAP < 65 classification is approximate.
- **No preillness basal MPP:** K2's exposure (basal-relative hypotension) is unavailable in eICU; this proposal uses absolute MAP < 65, which is a different (but clinically standard) exposure.
- **Vasopressor unit harmonization:** `drugrate` units vary (ml/hr, mcg/min, mcg/kg/min); norepinephrine-equivalent dose requires unit harmonization — solver must document this.
- **Creatinine frequency:** Varies by hospital; missingness is a known limitation, addressed by the inconclusive criterion (n < 3,000 or missingness > 50%).
- **No causal claim:** This is an observational association; hospital random effects address between-hospital confounding but not within-hospital treatment-selection bias.
- **AKI definition:** Creatinine-only (no RRT data in eICU); may undercount stage 3 AKI.
