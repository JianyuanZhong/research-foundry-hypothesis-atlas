# Directional cell communication: macrophage-to-fibroblast versus fibroblast-to-macrophage signaling in RA

Seed: D3-M2
Domain: Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses

## Unresolved question

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), do macrophages drive fibroblast activation (macrophage-to-fibroblast direction), or do fibroblasts drive macrophage polarization (fibroblast-to-macrophage direction), or both? This question is consequential because the dominant signaling direction determines whether therapeutic intervention should target the immune compartment or the stromal compartment.

## Evidence for the opening

- E-MTAB-8316 contains four FLS donor clusters with six nested monocyte donors and twelve condition samples. The nested design allows donor-aware paired comparisons of FLS and monocyte/macrophone expression under the same donor context.
- E-MTAB-8873 provides a targeted assay (445 measured features) with FLS/coculture pseudobulk samples, enabling measurement of specific ligand/receptor pairs in the same experimental system.
- GSE95588 coculture data provide additional paired condition comparisons, though r1/r2 includes earlier GSE57723 data and cannot count as independent validation.

The key observation is that in RA synovium, both macrophages and fibroblasts show activated states, but the direction of causal influence is not established by cross-sectional co-expression. The leading claim is that macrophage-derived signals (e.g., TNF, IL-6, IL-1) drive fibroblast activation. The strongest rival is that fibroblast-derived signals (e.g., CXCL12, CCL2, TSLP) drive macrophage polarization toward a pro-inflammatory state, creating a feed-forward loop where the initial trigger is stromal.

## Hypothesis

In RA synovial cocultures, macrophage-derived pro-inflammatory signals (TNF, IL-6, IL-1β) are the primary drivers of fibroblast activation, as evidenced by:
1. Donor-aware paired comparison showing macrophage activation markers precede or co-occur with fibroblast activation markers in the same donor.
2. Ligand/receptor pair analysis showing macrophage ligands (TNF, IL6, IL1B) are upregulated in macrophages while their fibroblast receptors (TNFRSF1A, IL6R, IL1R1) are upregulated in fibroblasts, in a donor-matched manner.
3. The pattern is not explained by a reverse-direction model where fibroblast ligands (CXCL12, CCL2, TSLP) and macrophage receptors (CXCR4, CCR2, CCR5) show the same donor-matched co-upregulation.

## Falsifiable test

**Primary test (donor-aware paired comparison):**
Using E-MTAB-8316, average repeated context measurements within each donor/condition, then perform donor-aware paired comparisons (not ordinary t-tests) of:
- Macrophage activation markers (TNF, IL6, IL1B, CD68, CD163)
- Fibroblast activation markers (COL1A1, MMP1, MMP3, TGFBR1, THY1)

If macrophage-to-fibroblast direction is correct, we expect macrophage activation to be more strongly associated with fibroblast activation than vice versa, after accounting for donor.

**Secondary test (ligand/receptor pair analysis):**
Using E-MTAB-8873 (445 measured features), identify ligand/receptor pairs where:
- Ligand is upregulated in macrophages and receptor in fibroblasts (forward direction)
- Ligand is upregulated in fibroblasts and receptor in macrophages (reverse direction)

Compare the number and magnitude of forward vs. reverse pairs in a donor-aware manner.

**Tertiary test (coculture validation):**
Using GSE95588 coculture data, test whether the direction of signaling is consistent in the coculture system. Note: r1/r2 includes GSE57723 data and cannot count as independent validation.

## Expected outcomes

- **Supportive:** Macrophage activation markers are more strongly associated with fibroblast activation than vice versa; forward-direction ligand/receptor pairs are more numerous and larger in magnitude than reverse-direction pairs.
- **Adverse:** Fibroblast activation markers are more strongly associated with macrophage activation than vice versa; reverse-direction ligand/receptor pairs are more numerous and larger in magnitude.
- **Inconclusive:** No clear directionality; both forward and reverse pairs show similar magnitudes; or donor-aware analysis cannot resolve the direction due to limited donor number.

## Competing explanations

1. **Composition artifact:** Differences in cell composition between conditions could explain apparent directionality. Mitigation: use donor-aware paired analysis and control for cell type proportions.
2. **Treatment artifact:** If conditions include treatment, treatment effects could confound the directionality. Mitigation: explicitly model treatment as a covariate.
3. **Measurement artifact:** The targeted assay (E-MTAB-8873) only measures 445 features; unmeasured ligands/receptors could change the conclusion. Mitigation: acknowledge this limitation and use the broader E-MTAB-8316 dataset for primary analysis.
4. **Selection artifact:** Donor selection could bias the result. Mitigation: use all available donors and report donor-level results.

## Next test

If the primary test supports macrophage-to-fibroblast direction, the next test would be a perturbation experiment: inhibit macrophage-derived TNF/IL-6/IL-1β in the coculture system and measure whether fibroblast activation is reduced. This would require wet-lab validation and is beyond the scope of the current computational analysis.
