# HCC early liver-directed treatment and 1-year mortality: working proposal

## Scientific opening and unresolved question
The configured HCC data contain many patients with liver-cancer diagnoses and repeated encounters, procedures, medications, examinations, and clinical documents. A recorded liver-directed intervention may be associated with survival, but the clinically important uncertainty is whether that association reflects treatment benefit or competing explanations: patients selected for treatment may have different tumor burden/stage, liver reserve, performance status, access, or documentation intensity; and survival follow-up may be incomplete or informative.

The provisional hypothesis is: among adults with a first documented HCC diagnosis in the HCC dataset, receiving a recorded liver-directed treatment during the early post-index interval is associated with lower 1-year all-cause mortality than no recorded treatment, after adjustment for baseline age, sex, comorbidity burden, disease stage/extent when documented, and treatment-selection proxies. This is an observational treatment-association hypothesis, not a causal treatment-benefit claim.

The leading rival explanations are (1) stage and tumor burden: earlier or less extensive disease is more likely to receive treatment and has better prognosis; (2) selection/healthcare access: treatment receipt reflects availability, performance status, and clinician judgment; (3) documentation intensity: more observed encounters produce more recorded procedures and more complete follow-up; and (4) informative censoring: patients with sparse follow-up may appear to survive or die differently. A treatment association that disappears after stage/selection adjustment, or is concentrated among patients with dense documentation, supports a rival explanation rather than treatment benefit.

## Index population and time zero
Construct the index cohort from the first qualifying HCC diagnosis for each `患者主索引`, using `diagnoses.诊断名称` and `diagnoses.诊断类型`, with a prespecified rule: include current/primary HCC labels such as `肝细胞癌` or `肝恶性肿瘤` and exclude explicit personal-history labels (`肝恶性肿瘤个人史`) and, in a sensitivity analysis, explicit postoperative/recurrent labels. Join the qualifying diagnosis to `encounters` on `(患者主索引, 就诊号)` and use the earliest `就诊时间` (with `入院时间` as a documented tie-breaker) as index time. Require a valid encounter row and index date. If the diagnosis table has no timestamp, the encounter time is the operational index time; this limitation must be reported.

Primary treatment definition: a procedure row in `procedures` with `开始时间` from index time through index time + 30 days and a prespecified liver-directed label set. The label set is to be frozen after a bounded review of the observed procedure vocabulary; initial candidates are TACE/embolization (`TACE`, `经导管肝动脉栓塞术`, `动脉化疗栓塞`, `经动脉造影化疗栓塞术（TACE）`), hepatic resection (`肝叶切除术`, `肝叶部分切除术`, `腹腔镜下肝部分切除术`, `部分肝切除术`, `特殊肝段切除术`), and ablation (`微波消融`, `射频消融`). `TACE` and its variants are not to be double-counted as separate modalities. A secondary definition adds systemic therapy from `medications` only after a prespecified drug-name mapping is reviewed; it is not mixed into the primary definition.

Patients with a qualifying procedure before index time are excluded from the primary incident-treatment cohort, with a sensitivity analysis retaining them and labeling prior treatment. Patients with no qualifying procedure through 30 days are the untreated comparator. Treatment is time-zero exposure, not a time-varying exposure, to avoid post-index outcome conditioning.

## Covariates, outcome, and follow-up
Baseline covariates are measured or derived from records dated at or before index time: age and sex from `encounters`; department from `encounters.就诊科室`; comorbidity indicators from pre-index `diagnoses`; liver disease indicators from pre-index diagnoses (`肝硬化`, `乙肝后肝硬化`, `门静脉高压`, `门静脉瘤栓`, etc.); baseline laboratory values from `labs` using the last valid result in a prespecified 30-day pre-index window, with missingness indicators; imaging/examination count and stage-related text features from `examinations`; and documentation density from the number of encounters, diagnoses, labs, examinations, and orders before index. Stage is a primary covariate only when extractable from diagnosis labels or adjudicated text; otherwise it is an uncertainty, not silently imputed as known.

The primary outcome is all-cause mortality within 365 days of index time. Because the catalog has no explicit death table, mortality must be operationalized as an in-hospital death outcome only if a death field is found and validated in the source rows; otherwise the primary outcome is unavailable and the study must not claim 1-year mortality. The fallback computable endpoint is 1-year observed survival/contact status using the last documented HCC encounter (`出院时间`/`就诊时间`) as administrative censoring, explicitly labeled as informative and not equivalent to mortality. A second analysis may use death recorded in clinical documents or diagnoses only after prespecified text adjudication; this requires expert review.

Follow-up is from index time to the earliest of 365 days, validated death, or last documented contact. Patients with no later encounter are censored at last contact, not assumed alive. Report the proportion with follow-up beyond 30, 90, and 365 days, and perform inverse-probability-of-censoring sensitivity analysis. The key limitation is that this dataset may not contain reliable external mortality capture; any mortality conclusion is conditional on the operational endpoint and must state that limitation.

## Baseline and substantive alternative
The simple baseline is a prespecified cause-specific or fine-Gray model for the operational 1-year endpoint, with treatment as the exposure and a transparent adjustment set: age, sex, index department, comorbidity/liver-disease indicators, documented stage, baseline laboratory values, and documentation density. Report standardized mortality/contact risk differences and 95% confidence intervals, with missing-stage and missing-lab sensitivity analyses. This baseline tests the adjusted association but loses nonlinear interactions, time-varying observation processes, and the distinction between treatment assignment and treatment effect.

The substantive alternative is a two-part selection/observation model: (i) estimate treatment propensity from baseline covariates and documentation intensity, and (ii) estimate the outcome and censoring models, then compare standardized risks under the treated and untreated exposure distributions using inverse-probability weighting or g-computation, with overlap diagnostics and a prespecified positivity threshold. A secondary learned model (gradient-boosted survival model or regularized cause-specific model) can use the same baseline inputs and predict the endpoint, but it is evaluated for calibration, discrimination, and treatment-effect stability—not rewarded for a small predictive gain. The alternative reveals whether the association is concentrated in a selected treatment region, whether documentation intensity explains it, and whether nonlinear stage/liver-reserve interactions change the adjusted estimate. It does not identify a causal mechanism.

Both methods use the same patient-level split (e.g., 70/30 by `患者主索引`, with no patient appearing in both train and test), the same endpoint, and the same uncertainty evaluation (bootstrap or cluster-robust confidence intervals; calibration plots; standardized risk differences). The baseline is selected as the primary interpretable analysis; the learned/selection model is retained because it tests the clinically important selection and documentation rivals. A GPU is not required for these tabular analyses; CPU is adequate unless the learned model is expanded, in which case a bounded allocated probe should verify runtime.

## Falsification and interpretation
Pre-specify these checks before fitting: (1) treatment labels recorded after index but before the 30-day window are excluded from exposure and used as a timing/negative-control check; (2) a pseudo-treatment date shifted outside the early window should not reproduce the association; (3) exclude patients with no post-index contact and compare with censoring-weighted results; (4) stratify by documented stage and documentation density; (5) compare procedure-only and procedure-plus-medication definitions; (6) assess positivity, missingness, and calibration. A supportive result requires a reproducible adjusted risk difference with adequate overlap and robustness to stage/missingness and censoring checks. An adverse result is no association, reversal, or disappearance after stage/documentation adjustment, which supports selection or documentation explanations. An inconclusive result is sparse treatment, poor stage coverage, inadequate follow-up, or wide intervals; it does not refute benefit.

No result from these observational analyses establishes that treatment causes survival benefit. Establishing causal benefit would require a validated mortality registry, reliable treatment assignment/intent, clinical adjudication of stage and performance status, and preferably a prospective or quasi-experimental design. The actual deliverable is a newly fitted, uncertainty-quantified treatment-association analysis with exact cohort construction, endpoint limitations, and rival-explanation diagnostics.

## Key evidence and claim scope [K1][K2][K3]

[K1] Yen et al. (2026) inspected a retrospective BCLC stage B cohort and used tumor number and MELD-defined liver reserve to compare resection with TACE. This supports treating stage, tumor burden, and liver reserve as central selection variables, but does not establish a causal treatment effect or resolve the present broader HCC question. [K2] Meriggi et al. (2026) describe the stage- and sequence-dependent HCC treatment paradigm and argue that timing may matter, while emphasizing that earlier treatment does not automatically improve outcomes and prospective evidence is needed. This bounds the present timing hypothesis; it does not validate an early-treatment survival benefit. [K3] Bardak et al. (2026) provide a real-world target-trial emulation precedent using landmarking, propensity matching, and sensitivity analyses, while acknowledging residual confounding. This motivates the present selection/observation-aware design but does not establish effects for HCC treatment.

All three works were inspected at the abstract/metadata level only; no unavailable full text is claimed. The proposal therefore treats them as contextual evidence, not proof of the hypothesis.
