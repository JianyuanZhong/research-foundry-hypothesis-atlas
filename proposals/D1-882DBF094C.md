# Do hospital differences in recorded ventilator liberation persist after physiologic and documentation adjustment?

## Question, importance, and evidence boundary

Among adult ICU-unit stays with a documented ventilator start by minute 360 and still in the unit at that landmark, do hospitals differ in time to a first durable *recorded ventilator-off* transition after adjustment for pre-landmark patient state, and is the residual spread attenuated by respiratory-charting opportunity? This could identify a practice-review target because delayed support liberation carries sedation, immobility and ventilator-associated risks, while premature liberation can lead to restart. It does not rank hospitals or identify a causal treatment effect.

Before fitting, the strongest supported claim is only data availability. The frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative unit boundaries, respiratory-care episode fields, respiratory charting and hospital IDs. My bounded audit measured 200,859 patient rows, 44,772 stays with respiratoryCare rows, 180,176 nonzero ventilator starts, 157 nonzero ventilator ends, and 1,016 respiratory-charting records with `RT Vent On/Off=Off` versus 2,734 `Start` records. No hospital association or liberation result has been established.

The falsifiable hypothesis is that the standardized between-hospital median time to durable recorded Off differs by at least six hours, with at least half the contrast retained in held-out hospitals after physiologic adjustment, but is materially attenuated when charting opportunity is added. The endpoint is documentation-bounded, not extubation.

## Population and estimand

Construct a cohort-flow ledger. Include age >=18, valid unique `patientunitstayid`, `hospitalid`, known `unitdischargestatus`, and `unitdischargeoffset > 360`. Require either respiratoryCare `ventstartoffset>0` at or before minute 360 or respiratoryCharting `respchartvaluelabel=RT Vent On/Off`, value `Start` (case/whitespace normalized), with `respchartoffset<=360`. Preserve the two source arms and overlap. Only patients alive and in the ICU at minute 360 enter the primary risk set; report pre-landmark deaths and alive exits separately to prevent immortal-time bias.

Primary index is minute 360. Primary event is the first respiratoryCharting Off after minute 360 and before `min(unitdischargeoffset,2880)`, followed by at least 360 minutes with no later Start and no active respiratoryCare interval. This is “durable recorded Off.” Secondary event is first respiratoryCare `ventendoffset>360), analyzed separately. Report exact Off, care-end, and concordant Off+care-end definitions.

Competing events are unit death (`unitdischargestatus=Expired`), alive unit discharge/transfer before Off, and unknown/invalid observation boundary. Otherwise censor at unit discharge or 2,880 minutes. Estimate hospital-specific standardized cumulative incidence and median/time contrasts at 6, 12, 24 and 48 post-landmark hours, with death and alive exit competing. Do not use later hospital death as a substitute.

## Matched methods and deliverable

The transparent baseline is a pooled cause-specific Cox model (or discrete-time complementary-log-log if ties dominate) for durable Off, with death and alive exit competing, prespecified admission/severity and pre-landmark physiology summaries, hospital shrinkage/random effects, and patient/hospital-clustered uncertainty. Report standardized cumulative incidence, calibration/Brier score and discrimination secondarily.

The substantive alternative is a piecewise-exponential multi-state model with `vent-on`, `documented-off`, `vent-restarted`, `unit-death`, `alive-exit`, and `observation-loss`. It models On->Off, Off->restart and documentation/exit transitions separately using physiologic trajectory, support intensity and charting counts/gaps/entry delay, with hospital effects by transition. It uses the identical cohort, windows, split and endpoints. It can reveal whether variation is recovery, restart/persistence, or recording opportunity—information the scalar baseline loses.

A GRU/transformer event-token alternative is deferred: Off events are sparse/heterogeneous, extubation adjudication and raw waveforms are absent, and the first question requires transition interpretability. Revisit only if event support and held-out calibration justify a substantive transition hypothesis; GPU use is optional, not a merit criterion. The actual deliverable is newly fitted uncertainty-quantified estimates: source/header/unit audit, cohort/event-discordance tables, out-of-fold and hospital-held-out predictions, standardized contrasts with intervals, transition-specific variance, calibration/Brier, and falsification outputs. Discovery has not fitted the study.

Use uniquepid patient grouping, development-only standardization, patient-grouped internal validation and whole-hospital holdout transport. Use clustered bootstrap/influence-function intervals and prespecified multiplicity across horizons.

## Falsification and interpretation

Repeat using chart entry offset; require 6/12/24-hour no-Start persistence; compare Off, care-end and concordant definitions; shift landmarks to 240/480 minutes; audit starts/deaths/exits before 360; vary charting-coverage thresholds; exclude exits near Off; audit duplicates, impossible intervals, blanks/zeros, case and whitespace; and test FiO2-only changes as a placebo endpoint. Compare physiology-only, physiology-plus-support, and charting-intensity models. Material endpoint-source or placebo instability falsifies a liberation interpretation.

Supportive evidence is a lower 95% bound above zero for hospital variance, >=6-hour standardized contrast, at least half retained in held-out hospitals after state adjustment, and no major placebo contradiction. This supports hospital-associated variation in recorded liberation and a practice-review hypothesis only. Adverse evidence is null/sub-material heterogeneity, disappearance after charting adjustment, transport failure, or placebo replication; this supports documentation-driven variation or refutes the stated persistence. Inconclusive evidence is sparse Off events, unstable effects, inadequate coverage, nonconvergence, or intervals spanning null and margin; it requires another study, not a forced conclusion.

Clinical adjudication is essential for extubation/reintubation status, spontaneous-breathing tests, sedation, bedside settings, staffing, bed availability, goals of care, treatment indication and intent. Linkage to ventilator logs and expert review would be required before protocol change, quality ranking, or causal claims.
