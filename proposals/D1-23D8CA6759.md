# Does arrival organ injury explain most of the post-arrival risk difference between transferred and directly admitted eICU patients?

## Scientific opening

Interhospital transfer is often associated with worse ICU outcomes, but the available eICU data do not capture the pre-arrival clinical course. The unresolved question is whether the crude excess risk among transferred patients is primarily explained by greater organ injury already present at arrival, or whether a post-arrival risk difference persists after accounting for that arrival state. This matters because it changes whether the target of improvement is arrival severity assessment, transfer selection, or post-arrival care.

## Hypothesis

Among adult eICU ICU stays with a prespecified arrival-time window, the crude post-arrival risk difference between interhospital-transferred and directly admitted patients is substantially attenuated after adjustment for organ injury and illness severity at arrival. A residual difference may remain, but it cannot be attributed to transfer itself or pre-arrival delay.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use only eICU CRD files. Define the cohort as adult ICU stays (`patient.csv.gz`, `patientId`, `stayId`, `age`, `gender`) with a valid `patientStayId` in `hospital.csv.gz` and a first available post-ICU-record timestamp. Define arrival as the first timestamp in the ICU stay, using `patientStayId` and available timestamps in `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`, `lab.csv.gz`, `nurseCharting.csv.gz`, and `respiratoryCharting.csv.gz`. Exclude stays without an arrival timestamp, with an impossible timestamp, or with fewer than two distinct post-arrival observations.

Use a fixed 24-hour post-arrival window. If 24 hours yields sparse outcomes, prespecify a 12-hour window as a sensitivity analysis, not a post hoc replacement. The pre-arrival course is unavailable and must not be inferred.

## Exposure and outcome

Classify exposure from admission source fields in `patient.csv.gz` and `hospital.csv.gz` where available: interhospital transfer versus direct admission. If source labels are incomplete, restrict to stays with an explicit source field and report label coverage; do not infer transfer from diagnosis or notes.

Primary outcome is in-ICU death during the fixed window, using `patient.csv.gz`/`hospital.csv.gz` disposition and `patientStayId`. Secondary outcomes are new or worsening organ injury within 24 hours, defined from creatinine, lactate, platelets, bilirubin, respiratory support, vasopressor infusion, and urine output where available in `lab.csv.gz`, `intakeOutput.csv.gz`, `infusionDrug.csv.gz`, `respiratoryCare.csv.gz`, and `respiratoryCharting.csv.gz`.

## Arrival-state covariates

Use only values observed before or at the arrival window. Join `patient.csv.gz`, `hospital.csv.gz`, `admissionDx.csv.gz`, `apacheApsVar.csv.gz`, `apachePredVar.csv.gz`, `apachePatientResult.csv.gz`, `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`, `vitalAperiodic.csv.gz`, `lab.csv.gz`, `nurseCharting.csv.gz`, `respiratoryCharting.csv.gz`, `infusionDrug.csv.gz`, `intakeOutput.csv.gz`, and `diagnosis.csv.gz` on `patientStayId`/`patientId` as documented in the eICU metadata. Include age, sex, race, admission source, APACHE variables/results where available, GCS, temperature, respiratory rate, oxygen support, FiO2, blood pressure, heart rate, creatinine, lactate, platelets, bilirubin, vasopressor exposure, and acute diagnoses. Preserve hospital identifiers for fixed effects and whole-hospital validation.

## Analysis and validation

Primary analysis: compare crude 24-hour outcomes by admission source, then estimate adjusted risk differences with a logistic regression or standardized-mean-difference-weighted model using arrival covariates and hospital fixed effects. Report standardized mean differences before and after adjustment, adjusted risk difference with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, and calibration by hospital.

Validation: use whole-hospital holdout. Train within hospitals, then evaluate on hospitals not represented in the training split. Report performance degradation and adjusted risk estimates by hospital. Do not pool patients across hospitals for a causal claim.

## Baseline and learned alternative

Baseline: a prespecified logistic model using a small set of arrival organ-injury variables (creatinine, lactate, platelets, bilirubin, respiratory support, vasopressor exposure, APACHE variables where available) and hospital fixed effects.

Alternative: a regularized gradient-boosting model using the same arrival-state inputs, with hospital fixed effects or hospital-level calibration. This can reveal whether nonlinear interactions or high-dimensional arrival-state information improve prediction beyond the interpretable baseline. It does not identify transfer harm.

Fitting target: 24-hour in-ICU death. Split: whole-hospital train/test split, with repeated hospital-level cross-validation if feasible. Evaluation: AUROC, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, and adjusted risk difference. Resource estimate: CPU-only, 8–16 CPUs, 16–32 GB RAM, under 1 hour for a bounded tabular fit; no GPU required. Select the baseline if its adjusted risk estimate is stable and interpretable; revisit the learned model only if it materially improves calibration or reveals a clinically meaningful subgroup without overfitting.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: the crude risk difference is large, standardized mean differences for arrival organ injury are large, and adjustment materially attenuates the risk difference while calibration remains acceptable.

Adverse: adjustment does not attenuate the risk difference, or the residual difference is large and stable across hospitals, suggesting arrival-state adjustment is insufficient to explain the observed difference. This would motivate further work on post-arrival care or missing pre-arrival data, not a causal transfer claim.

Inconclusive: sparse transfer labels, poor covariate overlap, unstable hospital-level estimates, or wide confidence intervals crossing both null and clinically important attenuation.

## Limitations

The pre-arrival course, transfer timing, reason for transfer, and source-hospital treatment are unavailable. The design tests whether arrival organ injury explains the crude difference, not whether transfer causes harm. Missingness, selection into transfer, and post-arrival treatment differences remain important confounders.

## Key evidence context

[K1] The eICU-CRD documentation describes the available multi-hospital ICU data and the limitations of using retrospective admission records. [K2] Prior ICU transfer literature reports associations between transfer and outcomes but often cannot separate arrival severity from transfer-related effects. [K3] Propensity/standardization methods provide a framework for estimating adjusted associations while explicitly acknowledging residual confounding and transportability limits.
