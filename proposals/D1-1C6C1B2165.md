# Ordered final-day recovery versus charting process after ICU discharge

Parent: [prior hypothesis]
Dataset: MIMIC-IV v3.1, snapshot [source checksum]

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

When an adult leaves the ICU alive, does the ordered trajectory of physiologic recovery during the preceding 24 hours predict a failed transition beyond the most recent state and the fact that clinicians measured the patient? A positive answer could support prospective validation of a targeted monitoring-risk stratum after ICU transfer, which is consequential for ward surveillance and ICU bed review. It would not show that delaying discharge or adding monitoring prevents an event.

The strongest available support is only plausibility: residual oxygen requirement and fluctuating vital signs are clinically recognizable warning signs, and MIMIC-IV records enough repeated structured observations to test whether they are associated with subsequent recorded events. The seed and literature do not establish the association, an effect size, plannedness of ICU returns, or benefit from changing discharge policy.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among adults discharged alive from an index ICU stay, an ordered, irregular final-day sequence of vital signs and respiratory support will improve locked patient-held-out 48-hour cause-specific risk estimation for first ICU readmission, without materially worsening calibration for death, compared with a prespecified last-state-plus-observation-process baseline. The stronger mechanistic interpretation—that measured physiologic trajectory rather than charting intensity carries the increment—is supported only if the full sequence beats a process-only model and loses performance when values are permuted while observation masks are preserved.

This is a substantive advance over a conventional last-value score: it separates state, persistence/recovery/order, and the measurement process in one same-cohort comparison. It is not a reproduction of Delphi-2M, ALADYNOULLI, or Oncoformer.

## Outcomes and estimand

Start follow-up at outtime. Primary horizon is 48 hours; secondary horizon is 7 days. End at the earliest horizon, hospital dischtime, a first event, or unresolved event timing.

Cause 1 is the first later icu/icustays row for the same (subject_id, hadm_id) with intime > index outtime and intime <= outtime+horizon; event time is that intime. The tables do not reliably identify planned versus unplanned returns, so the estimand is any subsequent ICU admission.

Cause 2 is death before readmission: hosp/admissions.deathtime > outtime, within horizon, and earlier than the first later ICU intime. A death after readmission is reported descriptively, not as the competing first cause. Censor at alive hospital dischtime before the horizon. If hospital_expire_flag=1 but deathtime is missing, mark ordering unresolved and exclude from the primary timed analysis; report its count and a sensitivity treating death as occurring at dischtime only when no later ICU admission is recorded. Show death-time and expire-flag ascertainment separately.

The estimand is observed-process cause-specific cumulative incidence for first ICU readmission and first death by 48 hours/7 days in the defined index population. The sum is a descriptive failed-transition probability; no causal treatment or discharge-policy estimand is claimed.

## Baseline, process null, learned alternative and analysis

Fit the baseline as regularized pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific hazards over the four six-hour outcome bins. Include interval indicators, anchor_age, gender, admission_type, admission_location, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU duration, destination, last valid physiologic values, support at outtime, recency, counts and missingness. Prespecify only SpO2×FiO2 and support×device interactions. Convert hazards to cause-specific CIFs.

Fit the process-only null with the same non-measurement covariates and bin-level masks, observation counts, gaps and timing, but no physiologic values. Fit the learned one-layer 64-unit GRU-D-style encoder with dropout 0.1 over the four ordered bins, using the physiologic values, support, mask, count and delta-time channels plus the same baseline covariates, with two cause-specific hazard heads and competing-risk negative log likelihood. Early-stop on validation loss; no test tuning. The alternative is scientifically substantive because it tests persistence, recovery, oscillation and nonlinear interactions, not just a different classifier.

Split by subject_id using deterministic 70/15/15 train/validation/test partitions, with five fixed hash seeds as robustness checks. All preprocessing, standardization, device mapping, and imputation parameters are training-only. Save internal-ID split manifests; do not publish source rows.

Primary comparison is paired test-set 48-hour cause-specific Brier score and CIF calibration for readmission and death. A prespecified interpretation benchmark is at least 0.005 absolute readmission Brier reduction without material death-calibration degradation; it is not proof of clinical utility. Secondary outputs are 7-day CIF calibration, integrated Brier, AUROC/AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope, and decision-curve net benefit over thresholds fixed before test evaluation. Use 2,000 subject-level paired bootstrap replicates and report point estimates, intervals, event counts and all seeds.

## Required scientific deliverable and falsification

The completion deliverable is newly extracted one-row-per-index-stay data, fitted baseline, process-only and GRU-D models, locked test predictions, cause-specific CIFs, paired metrics and uncertainty, calibration tables/plots, split and exclusion manifests, and ablation results. A schema audit or reference execution alone is not completion.

Required ablations are last-bin-only, order permutation, value permutation preserving masks/counts, and removal of mask/delta channels. A physiologic trajectory claim requires full-sequence improvement over the baseline, improvement over process-only, and degradation under value/order permutation. If process-only matches it or order permutation does not hurt, interpret any gain as observation-process or aggregate information, not trajectory physiology. Label permutation within outcome-rate strata must eliminate apparent performance; otherwise investigate leakage. A pre-outtime-only audit must show zero predictor timestamps at or after outtime.

Supportive results are reproducible held-out readmission improvement, acceptable death calibration, and evidence that measured values/order—not only charting process—contribute. This supports prognostic information value and prospective monitoring-risk validation, not a safer discharge policy.

Adverse results are no incremental improvement, worse calibration, instability across subject splits, no value/order-ablation loss, or label-permutation performance that remains above chance. These refute the specified incremental ordered-trajectory claim in this snapshot, not the existence of residual instability.

Inconclusive results are too few events, broad intervals crossing no effect, high missingness, poor transfer linkage, unresolved death timing, convergence failure, or collapse in a robustness split. These mean the data cannot resolve the claim.

Automatically checkable claims are cohort flow, joins, temporal exclusion, one-row-per-stay construction, first-event ordering, model fitting, locked metrics, calibration, bootstrap intervals and ablations. Clinical adjudication is required for whether ICU returns were planned, whether a device field reflects actual support, whether treatment limitations or clinician intent caused discharge, and whether monitoring or delayed discharge changes outcomes. The snapshot lacks reliable goals-of-care/treatment-intent labels, functional readiness, bedside assessments, raw waveforms and MIMIC-CXR images. Clinical reviewers and external/prospective data are required before deployment or causal interpretation.

## Demonstration and seed dispositions

- Delphi-2M: concrete bounded adaptation is this same-input ordered EHR trajectory versus last-state baseline. The local MIMIC snapshot lacks the UK Biobank/Danish cohorts and broad lifetime follow-up, so no reproduction or external-validation claim is made.
- ALADYNOULLI: not reproduced; germline/genetic inputs and its multi-biobank setting are unavailable, and the short ICU-transition question does not require latent genetic signatures. A small EHR-only latent model is deferred until the primary signal is stable.
- Oncoformer: structured longitudinal adaptation is retained, but chest-X-ray images and the main article/complete STAR Methods are unavailable; no multimodal or paper-reproduction claim is made.
- MIMIC expert seeds 1–9 were retrieved and assessed as repairable but were not selected: they require stronger causal assumptions or incompletely observed phenotypes (fluid-response timing, AKI recovery, lactate mechanism, HbA1c, sedation depth, antimicrobial de-escalation, congestion, transfusion indication). Seed 10 is the parent and is retained with the new measurement-process safeguards.
- UKB/eICU seeds cannot be bound to this MIMIC episode and are not imported into the analysis. They remain available for later dataset episodes; numbering is not ranking.

The source catalogue confirms all four configured dataset islands remain available read-only. This episode uses only MIMIC because cross-dataset joins would invent a population and violate the configured dataset-specific design.
