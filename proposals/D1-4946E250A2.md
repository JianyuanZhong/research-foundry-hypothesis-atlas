# Candidate: Observed hyperoxemia burden during the first day of invasive ventilation

## Hypothesis and unresolved claim

In adults who are invasively ventilated at ICU entry, a greater burden of arterial hyperoxemia (PaO2 >120 mmHg) during the first 24 ICU hours is associated with higher hospital mortality and longer ventilation than predominantly normoxemic management (PaO2 60–120), after accounting for pre-exposure severity, respiratory support intensity, hospital, and the probability of obtaining an arterial blood gas. The clinically useful unresolved claim is not that oxygen causes death in this retrospective dataset; it is whether a reproducible, treatment-relevant threshold signal remains after time ordering and measurement-process adjustment, and whether it is concentrated in patients with acute respiratory failure/sepsis.

This could inform a trial or protocol comparing oxygen targets. A null result with a narrow interval would also be useful because the current target range is unsettled. The hypothesis is falsifiable: pre-specify a clinically meaningful mortality contrast and direction before computation.

## What is already supported versus what this tests

A current review of ICU oxygen therapy (Wang et al., DOI 10.4103/mgr.medgasres-d-24-00143; retrieved public Europe PMC search and acquired source [source checksum]) describes uncertainty about optimal conservative-oxygen parameters and populations, and mixed mortality/ventilation findings. That evidence supports equipoise, not this EICU association or a causal effect. This experiment tests the narrower claim that early hyperoxemia burden, measured from observed arterial gases and adjusted for informative observation, is associated with outcomes in a large ICU cohort.

## Population and temporal design

- Source population: all EICU ICU-stay rows in patient; choose the first ICU admission per uniquepid (sort by unitadmitoffset, then patientunitstayid) to avoid treating repeat stays as independent. Keep adults with numeric age >=18; exclude missing/invalid age and stays with unit discharge before 24 hours for the primary landmark analysis.
- Time zero is ICU admission (patient.unitadmitoffset, conventionally minutes relative to ICU admission). Include patients with a respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset in [-60, 360] and either a respiratory-care interval that overlaps time zero or apacheApsVar.vent=1/the exact mechanical-ventilation treatment string recorded by offset <=360. Report the inclusive rule and counts for each ascertainment route; do not infer intubation from a diagnosis alone.
- Exposure window is [0, 1,440] minutes after ICU admission. For each arterial paO2 result in lab in this window, pair the closest respiratory FiO2 observation from respiratoryCharting within 30 minutes, retaining the pair only when both numeric values are valid. Use the paired PaO2 for oxygenation category: hypoxemia <60, normoxemia 60–120 (reference), hyperoxemia >120 mmHg. Treat thresholds as protocol values fixed before looking at outcomes.
- Make hourly bins, retaining the last valid paired observation in each bin. Carry a category forward for at most 6 hours only as a sensitivity analysis; the primary metric is observed-bin burden: proportion of valid bins classified hyperoxemic. Use categories 0%, >0–25%, >25–50%, and >50%; require at least two valid bins and report the number/spacing of valid observations. A secondary continuous metric is time-weighted hyperoxemia using linear interpolation between paired PaO2 observations, capped at the observed interval and not extrapolated beyond 6 hours.
- Record oxygen-delivery context from respiratoryCharting labels FiO2, PEEP, Plateau Pressure, Tidal Volume (set), TV/kg IBW, RT Vent On/Off, and LPM O2; do not conflate FiO2 or PaO2 with a randomized oxygen policy.

## Outcomes

Primary: hospital mortality, apachePatientResult.actualhospitalmortality (EXPIRED versus ALIVE), joined on patientunitstayid. Secondary: ICU mortality actualicumortality; actual ventilation days actualventdays/unabridgedactualventdays among survivors with a nonmissing value; ICU and hospital length of stay (unabridgedunitlos, unabridgedhosplos); and a composite of death or >7 actual ventilation days, with the cutoff prespecified and reported as a sensitivity outcome. Do not call actualventdays a 28-day ventilator-free-day outcome: EICU has no post-discharge 28-day follow-up.

## Analysis and baselines

Estimate adjusted risk differences and odds ratios for hospital mortality using a prespecified logistic model or standardization, with nonlinear restricted splines for hyperoxemia burden, hospital fixed effects or a hierarchical hospital effect, and robust standard errors clustered by hospital. Adjust only for variables available by time zero: age, gender, ethnicity, hospital/ward/unit type, admission source, unit-stay type, admission diagnosis fields entered by <=0, and pre-zero physiologic summaries from vital/lab data. Add apacheApsVar and apachePredVar severity variables only in a clearly labeled post-baseline/APACHE sensitivity model, since their construction can include early physiology. Do not adjust for post-exposure lab values, respiratory settings, vasopressors, or organ outcomes in the primary causal-style model.

The main measurement-process adjustment models whether a paired PaO2-FiO2 observation occurs in each hour from prior observed history and pre-exposure severity, uses inverse-probability-of-observation weights stabilized and truncated at prespecified bounds, and compares this with complete-case and simple observed-bin analyses. Use multiple imputation only for baseline covariates, not fabricated PaO2 values. For time-to-discharge analyses use competing-risk methods with death as a competing event; for ventilation days use a two-part model (death/being ventilated and positive days among survivors), not a naive comparison among survivors alone.

Baselines are: crude category contrasts; baseline-adjusted standardization without observation weights; the observation-weighted model; a model using mean FiO2 and PaO2 burden separately; and a severity-only model using APACHE variables. Prespecify interactions by admission acute respiratory failure and sepsis/septic shock labels recorded at or before zero, but treat these structured labels as phenotyping proxies.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support requires an adjusted hyperoxemia >50% versus 0–25% risk difference of at least +2 percentage points for hospital mortality, two-sided 95% CI excluding 0, monotonic dose response, and directionally consistent complete-case and observation-weighted estimates; a similar signal for ICU mortality or the death/long-ventilation composite strengthens transportability. It still supports only an association and trial-prioritization, not oxygen toxicity or a recommendation.

Adverse-to-the-hypothesis results are a risk difference <= -2 points with the same prespecified robustness, or a coherent protective dose response. An inconclusive result is a 95% CI spanning both -2 and +2 points, unstable estimates across measurement strategies, inadequate overlap, or too few valid paired observations. A falsifying pattern is no dose response plus a strong association of the exposure with recording density, or disappearance after observation-process adjustment.

Additional data required for a stronger clinical conclusion include clinician oxygen targets, continuously delivered FiO2/PaO2 measurements, ventilator mode and alarm data, arterial sampling indications, treatment limitations, post-ICU/28-day outcomes, and expert adjudication of ARDS/sepsis and cause of death. A randomized oxygen-target trial or a validated external cohort would be required to claim that changing oxygen targets changes mortality.
