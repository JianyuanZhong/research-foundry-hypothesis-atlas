# Does posterior-thigh MFI add to thigh-muscle distribution for later falls?

## Question, clinical value, and claim boundary

Among UK Biobank participants with visit-2 body MRI and a valid visit-3 falls response at least 366 days later, is higher visit-2 bilateral posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) associated with any fall reported for the year before visit 3, conditional on visceral adipose tissue (VAT), total thigh fat-free volume, posterior/anterior (P/A) muscle-volume ratio, and measured baseline falls/function and covariates?

The hypothesis is a positive conditional prognostic association per 1-SD higher MFI. If present beyond measured regional muscle distribution and baseline proxies, this would distinguish a muscle-composition signal from a mass-distribution phenotype; if precisely near-null, it would lower the priority of MFI as an additional MRI phenotype for falls-risk research. Falls can cause injury, fracture, disability, and loss of independence. This study does not test an intervention, identify a mechanism, validate screening thresholds, or establish clinical utility.

Strongest plausible rival: MFI is a marker of frailty, disuse/inactivity, adiposity, illness, or prior falls; these factors and selection into MRI/return visits can also influence later reports. Recall and measurement error could attenuate or distort associations. A >=366-day gap keeps the recalled year before visit 3 after index, but cannot remove residual confounding, selection, differential reporting, or reverse causation. The claim under test is limited to conditional association among selected respondents, not an effect of changing MFI.

**Evidence already supported:** three inspected works establish broad relevance of thigh MRI/MFI and underscore interpretive limitations; a previous aggregate-only UKB overlap screen found 4,962 participants with >=366-day date separation and a nonempty follow-up response before response-code classification. This is not an eligible complete-case N, event count, or fitted result. UKB source schemas expose the prespecified fields. **Unresolved:** whether posterior-thigh MFI retains a positive, sufficiently precise conditional association beyond the closest P/A volume comparator and measured frailty proxies. No outcome model has been fitted for this proposal.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: positive adjusted MFI estimate beyond VAT, total volume and P/A ratio, with uncertainty excluding the null and a stable exposure-response over data-supported values; report as conditional prognostic association in selected respondents only. Consistency across linear/GAM summaries and modest measured-proxy attenuation would strengthen that limited interpretation.

Adverse: a sufficiently precise estimate centered near or below null, or a well-supported opposite direction, argues against a distinct positive conditional MFI signal for this endpoint/cohort. Strong attenuation after measured-frailty adjustment supports that rival. Do not call a result clinically negligible/equivalent unless a material-effect margin is justified before fitting from external evidence; none is asserted here.

Inconclusive: wide intervals, sparse events, severe missingness/selection, unstable ratio support, model separation/nonconvergence, or discordant unstable shape estimates. An imprecise null is not falsification. Every conclusion must cite the computed output, sample denominator, effect estimate, and interval. A prediction metric alone is not a mechanism or clinical-utility result.

The UKB fields cannot determine whether falls reports are accurate or severity-adjudicated, validate MRI MFI against muscle histology, identify unmeasured frailty, or reveal outcomes among nonresponders. Clinical adjudication, independent population validation, and decision-utility/intervention work are required for stronger clinical conclusions. Verifier can check code, filters, source paths/columns, joins, calculations, reported denominators, uncertainty, and whether interpretations match supportive/adverse/inconclusive outputs; it cannot establish clinical truth or resolve missing evidence.

## Deliverable and deferred alternatives

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted estimates: primary adjusted MFI OR/95% CI; matched GAM exposure-response/uncertainty; paired nested-frailty coefficient contrast/CI; and response-status flow/summary outputs. Completion requires those computed artifacts and conclusions linked to them, not packaging readiness. A result that is supportive justifies external/adjudicated validation; adverse results deprioritize MFI as a distinct conditional signal; inconclusive results defer interpretation pending more events/measurement or another cohort.

A sequence model (Delphi-like) was not chosen: the outcome here is a single coded retrospective response and the intended contrast is an adjusted MRI composition association, not disease-history ordering. A high-dimensional learned predictor would not resolve selection, missing outcomes, or the muscle-quality-versus-distribution rival. The GAM is the scientifically relevant flexible alternative. Revisit sequence modeling only if a future question has dated recurrent falls/disease trajectories and patient-level split opportunity. The prior discovery demonstrations are not proposed as default methods: Delphi's structured disease sequence is not needed for this MRI-indexed binary question; an EHR-only ALADYNOULLI latent trajectory would change the target and lacks a stated advantage for this outcome; Oncoformer image modality is unavailable and its lab-only adaptation does not answer this MRI phenotype question. These are adaptation-fit decisions, not blanket method exclusions.
