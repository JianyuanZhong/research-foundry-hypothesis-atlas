# Episode 92 successor: can a label-free cross-practice correction replace local threshold recalibration?

## Unresolved clinical question and substantive advance

A receiving ICU may inherit a mortality-risk score from another ICU or practice environment. It then faces a consequential choice: display the score as a probability threshold for triggering review, or obtain local outcome labels and recalibrate first. The recent EICU lineage separates fixed-capacity rank enrichment from absolute-risk calibration, and it has proposed both a source-stratum intercept and a target-label, hospital-held-out intercept. Neither alone answers the practical replacement question.

The unresolved claim tested here is:

> Can an intercept correction learned only from source-stratum outcomes transport to the opposite repeat-lactate-practice stratum, meeting an absolute calibration bound and being no more than 0.02 absolute-risk units worse than a leave-one-target-hospital-out local-label recalibration, in both directions?

This is a clinically meaningful advance over the parents. A source-only correction that is merely better than an uncorrected score may still be clinically inferior to waiting for local labels. Conversely, a local recalibration result cannot establish that a label-free receiving ICU can deploy the correction. The proposed experiment makes portability versus local validation the primary decision and keeps fixed-capacity ranking as a secondary, separate question. It does not claim that a 10% threshold is a validated clinical action threshold; it tests a prespecified analytic screen for whether such a display would be transportable.

The strongest available evidence supports only narrower claims. Baysan et al. (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750, PMCID PMC9444407; acquired full-text XML, SHA-256 [source checksum]) found that adding 24-hour lactate to APACHE IV modestly improved discrimination in a derivation/validation sepsis setting (recalibrated C-statistic 0.62 to 0.64), but did not establish a portable 10% threshold or a benefit from acting on it. Sisk et al. (2021, DOI 10.1093/jamia/ocaa242, PMCID PMC7810439; acquired full-text XML, [source checksum]) reviewed informative presence/observation and concluded that measurement-process variables can be useful predictors, while guidance on when they should be used remains incomplete. Those sources motivate representing repeat availability and observation process; they do not support cross-practice intercept transport, the chosen margin, review utility, or causal benefit.

No experiment result is assumed. The cancer demonstration's main article and STAR Methods remain unavailable according to the ambition README; only its supplement/metadata availability limitation is known and it is not used as evidence here.

## Primary hypothesis and estimands

Let hospitals be assigned before any outcome analysis to low versus high repeat-lactate practice using the inherited hospital-level repeat proportion. Let d denote either low-to-high or high-to-low transfer, with source stratum A and target stratum B.

For each direction, fit the frozen source-only R_full model on all source-stratum hospitals and obtain the target raw linear predictor eta_0. Let p_0=expit(eta_0). Estimate a single source-only portable offset delta_A from nested source-hospital-held-out predictions: each source hospital's labels are scored out of fold, then the unique penalized intercept root is fit across the pooled held-out source predictions. Apply that delta_A to every target patient to obtain p_tr=expit(eta_0+delta_A). No target outcome enters p_tr.

For the local-label comparator, use the same source-trained eta_0 and, for each target hospital h, estimate delta_{B,-h} from all other target hospitals in B using their D7 labels and the same penalized offset equation. Score h only with delta_{B,-h}, yielding p_loc. No outcome from h enters its prediction. This is a historical validation reference, not a claim that labels would be available at deployment.

For p in {p_tr,p_loc}, define z(p)=1[p>=0.10], including equality. For each direction d, F_available status s in {0,1}, and threshold side z in {0,1}, define

E_d(p;s,z) = mean(D7-p | target direction d, F_available=s, z(p)=z)

and

q_d(p) = max_{s,z} |E_d(p;s,z)|.

The primary contrasts are:

- absolute portable error q_tr,d = q_d(p_tr);
- local-label reference error q_loc,d = q_d(p_loc);
- local-reference gap G_d = q_tr,d - q_loc,d;
- worst-direction portable error q_tr,* = max_d q_tr,d.

The fixed primary hypothesis is a two-part non-inferiority-and-validity claim:

1. In each direction, portable recalibration is no more than 0.02 absolute-risk units worse than local held-out recalibration: upper 95% bound for G_d <= 0.02 for both d.
2. Portable calibration meets the absolute screen: upper 95% bound for q_tr,* <= 0.05.

The 0.02 non-inferiority margin and 0.05 absolute-error bound are operational, prespecified analytic margins, not guideline thresholds or validated utility values. Support also requires at least 30 original target people in each of the eight p_tr threshold cells per direction and at least 1,900 finite joint copied-hospital replicates. Because q is a maximum across cells, no pooled or averaged cell error can substitute.

This primary formulation prevents two invalid conclusions. A portable correction that beats p_0 but is materially worse than p_loc fails the replacement claim. A portable correction that is close to p_loc but has q_tr>0.05 is not threshold-valid under this screen. A local comparator that is itself poor cannot rescue p_tr because q_tr,* must independently pass.

The primary result states are deterministic:

- Supportive portable replacement: both directions satisfy the non-inferiority upper bound, q_tr,* upper bound <=0.05, all cell-size and bootstrap gates pass, and deletion stability passes.
- Adverse non-portability: either direction has lower 95% bound for G_d >0.02, or q_tr,* lower 95% bound >0.05.
- Inconclusive: all other computable cases, including an interval crossing either margin, sparse cells, or deletion sensitivity. A point estimate near or below zero is not support.
- Not computable: any source/schema/cohort/clock/feature/fold/leakage/fit/replicate failure described below.

Secondary, non-rescuing outputs report q_d(p_0), q_d(p_tr), q_d(p_loc), the eight component errors, the source and receiving-stratum prevalence baselines, the portable-versus-local offset values, and the threshold capacity induced by p_tr in each hospital. A pattern in which p_loc passes but p_tr fails supports only the need for local labels; it does not prove that the score is clinically useful. A pattern in which p_tr passes but rank enrichment fails does not support a queue.

## Portable and local offsets

Portable delta_A is estimated only from source-stratum D7 labels. Fit source-hospital-held-out R_full models and preprocessing: for each source hospital g, train on all other source hospitals and score g. Pool these out-of-fold eta values and solve by deterministic bisection on [-20,20], tolerance 1e-12, for

sum_i [D7_i-expit(eta_i+delta_A)] - delta_A = 0.

The penalty makes the root unique under separation. Refit R_full on all source hospitals with source-only preprocessing, score the target stratum, and apply delta_A. No target label may enter p_tr.

For each target hospital h, use the full source-trained eta_0 and solve the identical equation on target-stratum people in hospitals other than h, then score h. This produces p_loc and excludes all labels from h. A local root is a validation reference, not a prospective deployment method. Empty calibration sets, nonfinite roots, or wrong hospital exclusions are not-computable.
