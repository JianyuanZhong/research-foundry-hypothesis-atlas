# Independent nursing/device/respiratory evidence does not validate literal RT Vent Off versus Suspended as bedside states

## Substantive child and clinically consequential unresolved question

This substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]` preserves their literal `RT Vent On/Off` event extraction, exact-tuple and earliest-anchored 30-minute deduplication, 1,063-episode population, fixed windows, discharge-bounded observation rules, first-episode and hospital/common-support safeguards, recurrence and setting controls, and strict semantic limits. The new experiment asks whether the large same-table Off-versus-Suspended respiratory-setting trajectory distinction has convergent validity in independently charted nursing, airway, or device domains.

The inherited evidence supports only a structured-charting claim: 713 literal Off and 350 literal Suspended episodes have directionally different subsequent respiratoryCharting setting-family activity within observed common-support hospitals, while later literal Start recurrence is less transportable. The untested clinical claim was whether independently entered records corroborate different contemporaneous respiratory support or airway states. This matters because an analyst might otherwise convert `Off` into an extubation or ventilator-liberation endpoint, or treat `Suspended` as ongoing support. This child never calls either value extubation, ventilator discontinuation, liberation, reintubation, success, failure, or a verified bedside state.

The falsifiable audit hypothesis was: if the literal labels encode different bedside respiratory-support states rather than only respiratoryCharting workflow regimes, at least one independently charted domain should be sufficiently populated around both labels and show a temporally aligned, common-site and first-episode-stable distinction not already present in the six-hour pre-event sham window. Explicit airway/device records were prioritized over nonspecific physiology. Absence or discordance is an informative adverse result.

## Bounded full-source audit and exact temporal rules

All source rows were scanned; no source-row sample was used. To avoid an unfocused export, rows were retained only for the 749 inherited stays and the union across their episodes of `[T-360, min(T+2880, unitdischargeoffset)]`. The retention scan used inclusive endpoints to avoid losing boundary rows. Episode-level analyses then used exact half-open bins:

- pre-event sham: `[T-360,T)`;
- post-event: `[T,T+360)`;
- intermediate: `[T+360,T+1440)`;
- late: `[T+1440,T+2880)`.

Post-event bins were eligible only when `unitdischargeoffset >= T + bin_end`; live ICU discharge truncates observation and is not a favorable outcome. Pre-event eligibility is inherited through `T >= 360`. Rows may contribute to more than one episode when fixed windows overlap; uncertainty therefore resamples stays, and first-episode results address repeated episode nonexchangeability.

Actual scan counts were:

- nurseAssessment: 15,602,498 total rows, zero rows in any of the 749 target stays, and zero retained event-window rows;
- nurseCare: 8,311,132 total rows, zero target-stay rows, and zero retained event-window rows;
- respiratoryCare: 865,381 total rows, 1,867 target-stay rows, 845 retained event-window rows covering 643 target stays;
- nurseCharting: 151,604,232 total rows, 3,226,798 target-stay rows, 740,139 retained event-window rows, and 156,517 rows matching the bounded respiratory/oxygen keyword audit.

Zero table overlap is a database-coverage result, not evidence that clinical assessment or care did not occur.

## Independent evidence availability results

The respiratoryCare rows did not supply contemporaneous airway/device validation. Among all 845 bounded rows, `airwaytype`, `airwaysize`, `airwayposition`, and `cuffpressure` were each nonmissing in 0 rows. The interval/history fields were populated in all 845 rows; `ventendoffset` was uniformly 0. These history fields reproduce the inherited workflow concern and are not reinterpreted as current support or airway state.

NurseCharting supplied abundant monitoring but almost no explicit support/device semantics. Exact bounded row counts were:

- `O2 Saturation`: 79,364;
- `Respiratory Rate`: 65,286;
- `Respiratory Assessment`: 11,854;
- `O2 L/%`: 7 rows in 6 stays;
- `O2 Admin Device`: 6 rows in 5 stays.

The 11,854 exact Respiratory Assessment values were `X` (11,448), `WDL` (405), and `.X` (1), which are generic status/completion values rather than a specific airway or respiratory-support device. Exact O2 Admin Device values were `ventilator` (5) and `nasal cannula` (1). O2 L/% values were only 7 records and mixed values such as 30, 35, 40, 44, 90, and 100, without enough records or unambiguous units for a label comparison. Explicit device/amount evidence is therefore unavailable for convergent validation, not negative for device use.

Generic nursing observation remained available under both labels and is a documentation-loss control. In the six-hour pre-event sham window, saturation was present in 709/713 Off and 349/350 Suspended episodes; respiratory rate in 678/713 and 335/350; and generic respiratory assessment in 698/713 and 345/350. In eligible post-event 0-6-hour windows, corresponding counts were saturation 646/647 versus 342/344, respiratory rate 620/647 versus 331/344, and respiratory assessment 641/647 versus 341/344. Availability remained approximately 99-100% for saturation and generic respiratory assessment in later complete-observation windows. Thus the failure to recover device semantics is not explained by wholesale disappearance of nursing charting.

## Descriptive physiologic audit, controls, and uncertainty

Because no explicit device/airway endpoint was analyzable, a narrow secondary audit used `any numeric O2 Saturation <90` in each structurally eligible bin. This outcome describes at least one charted low saturation, not hypoxemia adjudication, ventilation state, device state, support intensity, liberation, safety, or a causal effect. It depends on measurement count and observation opportunity. Per-episode median saturation and number of records were retained as sensitivity variables; the binary result is reported chiefly as a discordance/baseline-control test rather than a clinical endpoint.

The contrast is Suspended minus Off risk. Three thousand cluster bootstrap replicates were computed at both stay and hospital levels. First-episode results use `episode_ordinal == 1`. Site summaries require at least five eligible Off and five eligible Suspended episodes at that hospital. The six-hour pre-event bin is the required sham; post-minus-pre difference-in-differences is descriptive and does not create causal exchangeability.

Executed results were:

- Pre-event sham: Off 46/709 = 6.49%, Suspended 9/349 = 2.58%, RD -3.91 percentage points; stay-bootstrap 95% interval -6.43 to -1.24, hospital-bootstrap -8.43 to +1.26. First episodes: RD -3.09 points.
- Post-event 0-6 hours: Off 76/646 = 11.76%, Suspended 28/342 = 8.19%, RD -3.58 points; stay interval -7.57 to +0.37, hospital interval -11.06 to +1.95. Post-minus-pre RD change was +0.33 points, with stay interval -4.45 to +5.17 and hospital interval -9.50 to +6.98. First-episode RD was -1.66 points. There is no event-specific distinction in this window.
- 6-24 hours: Off 128/583 = 21.96%, Suspended 28/330 = 8.48%, RD -13.47 points; stay interval -17.96 to -8.34 and hospital interval -17.85 to -6.37. All five common-support site RDs were negative, but magnitudes varied. The post-minus-pre RD change was -9.56 points; stay interval -15.03 to -3.40 and hospital interval -14.68 to -1.42. The first-episode RD was attenuated to -7.41 points.
- 24-48 hours: Off 98/403 = 24.32%, Suspended 48/297 = 16.16%, RD -8.16 points; stay interval -14.46 to -1.18, but hospital interval -11.20 to +7.26. Common-support site directions included one positive and three negative RDs. The post-minus-pre RD change was -4.25 points; both stay (-11.13 to +2.76) and hospital (-7.99 to +9.91) intervals crossed zero. First-episode RD was -5.53 points.

The intermediate-window low-saturation contrast survives the baseline subtraction and both cluster schemes, so it is not merely a total charting-availability artifact. It nevertheless does not converge with a simple interpretation that Off is a verified favorable bedside off-support state: Off had more episodes with a charted low value, a difference was already present before the event, later results attenuated under first-episode restriction, 48-hour hospital uncertainty crossed zero, and device/support state remains unknown. Candidate mechanisms include baseline case mix, episode order, site workflow, oxygen/device differences, measurement frequency, or genuine physiology; the available data do not adjudicate among them.

## Evidence gates, falsification, and interpretation

A supportive convergent bedside-state result would require (i) an independently entered explicit airway/device field sufficiently populated under both labels for common-support comparisons, (ii) a post-event label difference aligned with the inherited setting trajectory, (iii) no comparable pre-event sham imbalance or a stable post-minus-pre change, and (iv) persistence under first-episode, stay-clustered, hospital-clustered, and common-site analyses. These requirements were not met because explicit airway/device evidence was nearly or entirely absent.

An adverse workflow-only result is supported when generic nursing observation continues but explicit independent device/airway evidence is unavailable, or when an independent physiologic difference is discordant with a straightforward favorable Off-state reading. Both occurred. This falsifies the proposed convergent validation with the configured fields; it does not prove that Off and Suspended have identical bedside states.

An inconclusive result applies to actual clinical semantics whenever independent device/airway state cannot be reconstructed, physiologic measures have pre-event imbalance or measurement-exposure dependence, or hospital/episode-order support is unstable. All apply. The strongest supported conclusion is therefore negative and measurement-focused: Off and Suspended remain distinguishable structured respiratoryCharting regimes, and nursing surveillance is abundant, but the audited independent tables do not validate either literal label as a specific bedside ventilation, airway, oxygen-device, liberation, or outcome state.

The result is clinically useful because it blocks a consequential endpoint shortcut. Off may remain a candidate label for future adjudication, but it must not be deployed as a clinical liberation phenotype based on this database evidence alone. Suspended must not be pooled with Off, as established by the parents, yet this audit cannot assign either label a verified clinical meaning.

## Exact additional data and adjudication required

Resolving the bedside claim requires independently timestamped ventilator connection/disconnection, invasive versus noninvasive mode, airway insertion/removal, extubation/reintubation, oxygen device and flow/FIO2, RT and nursing narrative, sedation and procedure context, measurement provenance, post-ICU respiratory support, local workflow metadata, and blinded clinician adjudication sampled by hospital, literal label, episode ordinal, and discordant low-saturation trajectory. A separate validation study should estimate sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, inter-rater reliability, and hospital transportability. Causal safety or benefit would require a different design and additional confounder/outcome evidence.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes and schemas; inherited episode artifact identity; full-source row counts; target-stay and bounded-window filters; half-open temporal bins; discharge eligibility; exact label/value counts; device/airway nonmissingness; nursing availability; numeric parsing; low-saturation counts; first-episode filtering; common-support site thresholds; and cluster-bootstrap calculations. An automatic verifier cannot establish bedside state, whether a low value was clinically real, extubation, liberation, reintubation, clinical intent, appropriateness, benefit, causality, or transportability beyond observed hospitals.
