# Ischemia-Modified Effect of PRBC Transfusion on In-Hospital Mortality: A Falsifiable MIMIC-IV Experiment

**Parent:** [prior hypothesis] (expert seed 9: "At the same hemoglobin level, the effects of transfusion differ between patients with ischemia or an oxygen supply-demand imbalance and stable patients.")
**Dataset:** MIMIC-IV 3.1 (frozen archive)
**Status:** Data-bound proposal with verified itemids, prespecified landmark + phenotype, executed bounded feasibility probe, and three inspected key references. No full analysis was run.

---

## 1. Scientific Question

**Unresolved claim (what this experiment tests):**
> In adult ICU patients who receive PRBC transfusion in the first 24h of ICU stay, the association between hemoglobin level at transfusion and in-hospital mortality differs by myocardial ischemia status (troponin-T elevation or EKG within 24h of transfusion) — i.e., ischemia status is an effect modifier of the hemoglobin–mortality relationship in the context of transfusion.

**Why this matters:**
The transfusion threshold is one of the most consequential ICU decisions. The MINT trial [K1] randomized MI patients with Hb <10 g/dL to restrictive (target 7-8) vs liberal (target 10) transfusion and found no significant difference in 30-day mortality (15.2% vs 14.3%), but a trend toward higher cardiac death in the restrictive arm. The patient-level meta-analysis [K2] confirmed a significant increase in cardiac death with restrictive strategy (RR 1.47, 95% CI 1.11-1.94) and higher 6-month all-cause mortality (HR 1.08). However, these trials enrolled only patients with confirmed acute MI. The critical unresolved question is whether the ischemia–transfusion interaction generalizes to the broader ICU population, where ischemia is incompletely measured and confounding by indication is far stronger.

If ischemia status modifies the hemoglobin–mortality relationship, this would:
1. Support a higher transfusion threshold for ischemic ICU patients
2. Inform population stratification for future transfusion trials
3. Change bedside decision-making: a patient with Hb 7.5 g/dL and troponin elevation would be managed differently from one without

**Strongest rival (confounding by indication):**
The leading rival explanation for any observed interaction is confounding by indication: clinicians transfuse ischemic patients at higher hemoglobin levels (lower threshold), so the "ischemic + low Hb" group is enriched for patients who were *not* transfused aggressively despite ischemia — a group that may be sicker or have different pathophysiology. The landmark design (first transfusion in first 24h) + severity adjustment + interaction test partially addresses this, but residual confounding is acknowledged as a fundamental limitation.

**Second rival (measurement artifact):**
Troponin-T and EKG are imperfect proxies for myocardial ischemia. Troponin is elevated in many non-ischemic conditions (sepsis, renal failure, heart failure). EKG is performed for many reasons. The "ischemia" phenotype is therefore a composite of true ischemia and non-specific markers. This limits the interpretability of a positive interaction.

## 2. Supported Claims (from prior literature, not re-tested here)

- [K1] MINT trial (Carson et al. 2023, NEJM): In MI patients with Hb <10 g/dL, restrictive (7-8) vs liberal (10) transfusion showed no significant difference in 30-day mortality (15.2% vs 14.3%, p=0.61), but cardiac death trended higher in restrictive (8.3% vs 5.7%). The trial was underpowered for the primary outcome.
- [K2] Patient-level meta-analysis (Carson et al. 2025, NEJM Evid): Pooling 4311 patients from 4 trials, restrictive vs liberal: 30-day composite (death/MI) RR 1.13 (0.97-1.30); cardiac death RR 1.47 (1.11-1.94); 6-month all-cause mortality HR 1.08 (1.05-1.11). The cardiac death signal is the most consistent finding.
- [K3] MIMIC-IV causal forest study (Kang et al. 2026, Sci Rep): In 3,710 MIMIC-IV ICU admissions with pre-transfusion Hb ≤8.0 g/dL, a double ML causal forest estimated optimal post-transfusion Hb ~11.0 g/dL with U-shaped dose-response. The MI subgroup showed similar optimal levels, but the study did not test for interaction between ischemia status and Hb effect.

**What this proposal adds beyond [K1]-[K3]:**
- [K1] and [K2] are RCTs in confirmed MI only; they cannot answer whether the interaction generalizes to undifferentiated ICU
- [K3] used MIMIC-IV but tested Hb dose-response, not ischemia × Hb interaction
- This proposal tests the specific interaction (ischemia × hemoglobin) in a large, undifferentiated ICU cohort with a landmark design to partially address confounding by indication

## 3. Study Design

### 3.1 Population
- **Inclusion:** Adult ICU patients (age ≥18) with at least one PRBC transfusion in the first 24h of ICU stay
- **Exclusion:**
  - No hemoglobin measurement within ±12h of first PRBC transfusion
  - Active major bleeding (ICD-9/ICD-10 codes for GI bleed, trauma with hemodynamic instability) — *sensitivity analysis only, as ICD coding for "active bleeding" is imprecise in MIMIC-IV*
- **Unit of analysis:** First ICU stay per admission

### 3.2 Landmark Design
- **Landmark:** First PRBC transfusion in the first 24h of ICU stay
- **Hemoglobin at transfusion:** Closest hemoglobin measurement within ±12h of first PRBC (from labevents itemid 51222, 50811, or 51640)
- **Ischemia status (prespecified, measured within 24h of first PRBC):**
  - Troponin-T > 0.1 ng/mL (chartevents itemid 227429, value > 0.1)
  - OR EKG performed (procedureevents itemid 225402)
  - Ischemia = 1 if either criterion met; Ischemia = 0 if neither
  - *Note: This is a broad "ischemia signal" phenotype, not a specific diagnosis of acute coronary syndrome. It captures patients for whom ischemia is a clinical concern.*

### 3.3 Exposure and Comparison
The primary comparison is **not** transfused vs non-transfused (which would be heavily confounded by indication). Instead:

**Primary analysis:** Among all eligible patients (transfused in first 24h), test whether the association between hemoglobin level at transfusion and in-hospital mortality differs by ischemia status.

This is an **effect modification** analysis:
- Model: mortality ~ Hb + ischemia + Hb × ischemia + covariates
- The Hb × ischemia interaction term tests whether the slope of the Hb–mortality relationship differs between ischemic and non-ischemic patients

**Secondary comparison:** Among patients with Hb 7-8 g/dL at transfusion (the MINT trial range), compare mortality between ischemic and non-ischemic patients. This is a descriptive comparison, not a causal contrast.

### 3.4 Outcomes
- **Primary:** In-hospital mortality (admissions.hospital_expire_flag = 1)
- **Secondary:** ICU mortality (icustays.outtime < admissions.discharge_time)

### 3.5 Covariates
- Age (patients.anchor_age)
- Gender (patients.gender)
- APACHE IV score (if available; otherwise use: admission type, first ICU stay indicator)
- MAP at landmark (chartevents itemid 220181, mean in 2h before first PRBC)
- Heart rate at landmark (chartevents itemid 220045, mean in 2h before first PRBC)
- Time from ICU admission to first PRBC (hours)
- Total PRBC volume in first 24h (inputevents itemid 225168, sum of amounts)

## 4. Analysis Plan

### 4.1 Baseline Model
Logistic regression:
```
mortality ~ Hb + age + gender + MAP + HR + time_to_prbc + prbc_volume
```
- Hb is centered at 7.5 g/dL (midpoint of the 7-8 range)
- Report OR per 1 g/dL increase in Hb

### 4.2 Extended Model (Primary)
```
mortality ~ Hb + ischemia + Hb × ischemia + age + gender + MAP + HR + time_to_prbc + prbc_volume
```
- **Primary test:** p-value for Hb × ischemia interaction
- **Secondary:** Difference in predicted mortality at Hb=7.0 vs Hb=8.0, separately for ischemic and non-ischemic patients (marginal effects)
- **Tertiary:** OR for mortality at Hb 7-8, ischemic vs non-ischemic (descriptive)

### 4.3 Alternative: XGBoost with Interaction Detection
- Fit XGBoost (1000 trees, max_depth=6, learning_rate=0.05, 5-fold CV) with Hb, ischemia, and all covariates
- Extract SHAP values for Hb and ischemia
- Compute SHAP interaction values (Hb × ischemia)
- Compare: Does the XGBoost model detect a significant Hb × ischemia interaction (mean |SHAP interaction| > 0) that the logistic model misses?
- **What the alternative could reveal that the baseline loses:**
  - Non-linear Hb effect (e.g., U-shaped dose-response as in [K3])
  - Higher-order interactions (e.g., Hb × ischemia × severity)
  - The logistic model assumes a linear Hb effect and a single 2-way interaction; XGBoost + SHAP can detect arbitrary non-linearities
- **Caveat:** SHAP interactions are model-dependent and not a formal hypothesis test. The logistic interaction p-value is the primary inferential statistic.

### 4.4 Sensitivity Analyses
1. **PSM on Hb + severity:** Propensity score match ischemic vs non-ischemic patients on Hb, age, gender, MAP, HR. Compare mortality in the matched cohort.
2. **Restrict to Hb 7-8 g/dL:** Re-run primary analysis in the MINT trial Hb range.
3. **Exclude patients with ICD codes for active bleeding:** GI bleed (K92.2, I85.9), trauma (S00-T88 with hemodynamic instability).
4. **Troponin-only ischemia definition:** Repeat with troponin > 0.1 only (no EKG).
5. **EKG-only ischemia definition:** Repeat with EKG only (no troponin).

### 4.5 Falsification Criteria
- **Supportive:** Significant Hb × ischemia interaction (p < 0.05) with larger mortality benefit from higher Hb in ischemic patients at Hb 7-8 (i.e., the Hb slope is more negative in ischemic patients)
- **Adverse:** No interaction (p > 0.2), or larger benefit in non-ischemic patients (interaction in opposite direction)
- **Inconclusive:** Interaction p between 0.05-0.2, or < 100 ischemic patients in the final cohort, or Hb coverage < 50%

### 4.6 What Each Outcome Would Mean
- **Supportive result:** Would suggest that ischemia status modifies the Hb–mortality relationship in ICU, supporting a higher transfusion threshold for ischemic patients. Would NOT establish causality (residual confounding by indication). Would justify a larger, better-powered study or a pragmatic trial stratified by ischemia status.
- **Adverse result (no interaction):** Would suggest that the MINT trial's cardiac death signal does not generalize to undifferentiated ICU, or that the ischemia phenotype (troponin/EKG) is too imprecise to capture the relevant biology. Would NOT refute the MINT trial finding (different population, different ischemia definition).
- **Inconclusive result:** Would indicate insufficient power or data quality to resolve the question. Would justify a larger dataset or a different ischemia definition.

## 7. Limitations

1. **Confounding by indication:** The landmark design + severity adjustment partially addresses this, but clinicians who transfuse ischemic patients at higher Hb are making a clinical judgment that is not fully captured by measured covariates. Residual confounding is a fundamental limitation of any observational transfusion study.
2. **Ischemia phenotype imprecision:** Troponin-T > 0.1 ng/mL is elevated in many non-ischemic conditions (sepsis, renal failure, heart failure, PE). EKG is performed for many reasons. The "ischemia" group is a heterogeneous mix of true ACS and non-specific markers. This limits the interpretability of a positive interaction.
3. **Single-center, single-dataset:** MIMIC-IV is a single academic medical center. Generalizability to other settings is uncertain.
4. **No external validation:** The analysis is exploratory; findings require validation in an independent cohort or a pragmatic trial.
5. **Hemoglobin measurement timing:** The ±12h window for Hb measurement may not capture the true Hb at the moment of transfusion decision.
6. **No APACHE IV score:** MIMIC-IV does not include APACHE IV scores directly. Severity is approximated by MAP, HR, and admission type.

## 8. Method Comparison: Baseline vs Alternative

| Aspect | Baseline (Logistic) | Alternative (XGBoost + SHAP) |
|---|---|---|
| Hb effect | Linear (assumed) | Non-parametric (can detect U-shape) |
| Interaction | Single 2-way (Hb × ischemia) | Arbitrary order (SHAP interaction) |
| Inference | Formal p-value for interaction | Model-dependent, no formal test |
| Interpretability | Direct OR interpretation | SHAP values require expertise |
| What it could reveal | Clear interaction estimate | Non-linear Hb effect, higher-order interactions |
| Risk | Misses non-linear effects | Overfitting, model-dependent |

**Why both are needed:** The logistic model provides a formal hypothesis test for the interaction. The XGBoost model tests whether the interaction is robust to model misspecification (non-linear Hb effect). If the logistic model finds a significant interaction but XGBoost does not, this suggests the interaction is driven by a linear approximation that doesn't hold. If both agree, confidence in the interaction increases.
