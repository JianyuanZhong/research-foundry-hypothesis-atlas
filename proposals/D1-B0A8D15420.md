## Unresolved question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

After vasopressor-treated shock has resolved, should a markedly fluid-positive, invasively ventilated adult with adequate blood pressure, urine output, and potassium start or restart an ICU-input-system recorded furosemide or bumetanide delivery during the next 24 hours? The possible benefit is earlier durable liberation from an endotracheal airway; countervailing harms are early recorded creatinine rise, renal replacement therapy (RRT), or death.

The inherited reviewed literature supports narrower claims only: heterogeneous conservative-fluid/deresuscitation strategies can lower fluid balance and may improve ventilator-free days, while outcome-guided loop initiation and renal safety remain uncertain. The inspected full XML of Chanda et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13223704; DOI 10.1016/j.xkme.2026.101374; frozen source [source checksum]) supports separating record-defined creatinine change from clinical AKI; it supplies no loop-effect evidence. The ambition papers are methodological demonstrations, not clinical evidence here; the cancer main article remains unavailable locally. The strongest supported claim is that active fluid-removal strategies can change recorded balance. The unresolved claim is whether this precisely timed recorded-loop strategy is associated with a clinically material airway benefit and a prespecified renal-harm signal.

The respiratory hypothesis is that, in the frozen measured-overlap population, the 24-hour recorded-loop strategy increases the lower bound of five-day, airway-anchored 48-hour extubation success by more than 5 percentage points versus recorded avoidance. Separate adverse hypotheses are increases of at least 3 points in all-eligible record-defined 48-hour creatinine rise or at least 5 points in observed RRT7. These are associational, record-defined hypotheses. Failure to cross a harm threshold is not renal safety.
