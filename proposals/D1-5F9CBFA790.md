# Hospital transportability of measurement-process signals in early ICU risk prediction

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

The unresolved question is whether laboratory testing frequency and missingness encode transportable patient state or primarily encode a hospital's observation process. The strongest inspected evidence supports only that informative missingness can improve prediction within a setting [K1], and that a single-setting deterioration study benefited from longitudinal and missingness-aware laboratory representations [K2]. Multisite external evaluation is feasible and clinically consequential [K3], but those works do not establish whether process features are the cause of transport degradation.

Hypothesis: among adult eICU ICU stays with a valid first-six-hour window, a process-aware model that uses laboratory values plus measurement counts, intervals, and missingness will show a larger loss in discrimination, calibration, and decision-curve net benefit on untouched hospitals than a matched physiology-only model using the same early physiologic values but no observation-process features, after case-mix and outcome-prevalence standardization.

This is an explanatory transportability hypothesis, not a causal claim that clinicians or hospitals cause mortality. The substantive advance is a direct hospital-held-out decomposition of transport failure: stable physiology, local observation practice, case mix, prevalence, and calibration can produce different predictions under the same eICU snapshot.

## Estimand and analysis

The primary estimand is the paired difference in performance between process-aware and physiology-only models on untouched target hospitals, averaged across prespecified target-hospital splits. Report AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, and decision-curve net benefit over a prespecified clinically reviewed risk range. Give hospital-cluster bootstrap intervals and the distribution/prediction interval of effects across target hospitals; do not report only a pooled patient-level confidence interval.

Use identical patient-level inclusion and hospital splits for both models:

1. Baseline: penalized logistic regression, CPU-feasible, with prespecified early vital/lab values and simple slopes or last-minus-first summaries, age, sex/gender, admission source, and admission severity. Impute physiologic values using training-hospital information and do not add missingness indicators. A gradient-boosted tabular sensitivity model may test nonlinearity but is not allowed to replace the primary baseline.
2. Substantive alternative: a modest time-aware sequence model fitted to 0-6-hour lab/vital observations, retaining last values, trajectories, measurement counts, inter-measurement intervals, and missingness indicators. Use a small LSTM, temporal convolution, or attention model only if readiness supports enough events and sites; otherwise fit a hierarchical discrete-time logistic model with splines for time since measurement. Its scientific purpose is to represent trajectory and observation process jointly, not to chase a small score gain.

Partition by hospital into training, validation, and untouched target hospitals; use prespecified repeated hospital partitions or leave-one-hospital-group-out when support permits. Fit preprocessing, imputation, lab-name mapping, and thresholds on training hospitals only. Primary comparisons are process-aware minus physiology-only performance on each target hospital. Secondary decompositions are (a) process-feature permutation within target hospital, (b) removal of process features, (c) target-hospital intercept recalibration, and (d) case-mix standardization/reweighting using age, sex, admission source, baseline severity, and available hospital descriptors. No post-six-hour treatment or discharge-derived feature may enter the primary model.

## Falsification, interpretation, and limits

The workflow-dependence explanation is weakened or falsified if the process-aware model does not show preferential transport degradation, if within-hospital random splits show the same degradation as hospital holdouts, or if case-mix/prevalence adjustment removes the difference while process-feature importance is not site-specific. A negative-control analysis using an admission-stable outcome may test whether the pattern is specific to a dynamic task, when a valid negative control is available.

Supportive results are a larger target-hospital loss for the process-aware model, with comparable physiology-only performance and attenuation after process ablation or target recalibration. This supports workflow dependence as a transportability explanation. Adverse results are equal/smaller degradation for the process-aware model or degradation explained by case mix/prevalence; they would bound or reject the proposed explanation, not prove universal transportability. Inconclusive results are insufficient events/sites, unstable laboratory identity mapping/timestamps, wide intervals, or inconsistent effects across target hospitals.

Computationally checkable outputs are cohort and site counts, feature-window and split integrity, fitted predictions, metrics, calibration, net-benefit, ablation/recalibration differences, and uncertainty. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for any deterioration label, clinical threshold choice, claim that workflow modification improves care, or causal attribution to hospital practice. This retrospective experiment cannot establish deployment safety, a biologic mechanism of missingness, or a treatment effect.

## Baseline versus alternative and compute

The transparent penalized-logistic baseline answers whether a small set of early physiologic summaries transports. It loses irregular timing, trajectory shape, and the distinction between a stable value and a changing value. The learned/process-aware alternative can reveal whether those temporal and observation-process structures explain cross-hospital performance changes, even if it does not improve pooled discrimination. A hierarchical discrete-time model is the scientifically substantive CPU fallback if a neural sequence model is unsupported; complexity is not a merit criterion.
