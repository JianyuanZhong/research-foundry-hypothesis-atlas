# TACE2-to-repeat-TACE biochemical persistence as a secondary vulnerability diagnostic

## Decision and substantive advance

This proposal is a substantive, deliberately narrowed child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent's frozen 2018–2024 common-TACE2 cohort, strict TACE ontology, earliest adjacent qualifying TACE pair, fixed days-1–14 systemic-record pathway, ambiguity exclusions, first strict repeat-TACE event on TACE2 days 15–90, exact assay labels, and admission-linked acute repeat-TACE perturbation as the primary inferential component.

The new audit tested whether a within-patient change-of-change analysis is feasible. It scanned every row of the relevant HCC sources without sampling and found exact-clock dual-session albumin pairs in 36 systemic-record versus 109 comparator patients, total-bilirubin pairs in 37 versus 109, and joint albumin-plus-bilirubin pairs in 36 versus 109. INR-plus-core support is 30 versus 92, and all seven-assay support is also 30 versus 92. These counts are sufficient for a prespecified exploratory persistence diagnostic, but not for making change-of-change the primary arm contrast or for a seven-assay claim. Recovery remains unavailable and is not reintroduced: the present design uses only the two procedure-proximal sessions.

The clinical advance is to ask a falsifiable and clinically interpretable marker question rather than claiming cumulative TACE injury: among patients selected for a repeat procedure, does the immediate biochemical response observed at TACE2 predict the immediate response at the first repeat TACE, and does that persistence differ by the frozen pathway? If supported, this could motivate intensified laboratory surveillance around future procedures. It cannot establish that TACE2 caused later vulnerability, that systemic treatment caused a response, or that either procedure caused clinically important liver injury.

## Strongest evidence-supported claim and unresolved claim

The complete source supports reconstruction of 6,589 earliest adjacent qualifying pairs, 2,992 era/observation-qualified patients, frozen arms of 319 systemic-record and 1,491 comparator patients, and 159 versus 526 selected first repeat-TACE events. Selection is differential (49.84% versus 35.28%). At the selected event, all 685 procedure encounters link to admission/discharge records; the procedure occurs about 0.6 days after admission and about 2.5 days before discharge by the audit's medians. Exact-label source-wide availability is high, but the laboratory schema has no unit field.

The unresolved hypothesis is conditional and noncausal:

> In patients with a selected first strict repeat TACE on days 15–90 and valid exact-clock pairs at both procedures, a larger TACE2 immediate change in a given exact assay is associated with a larger (or otherwise systematically different) immediate change at repeat TACE; the strength or direction of this response persistence may differ between the frozen systemic-record and TACE2-only pathways.

The primary clinical interpretation is response persistence or vulnerability marking, not cumulative damage. A null is informative: it would argue that the first observed acute response is not a useful marker of the later observed acute response in this selected population. A positive association could reflect stable susceptibility, evolving liver state, procedure similarity, or care-selection processes and requires external validation before guiding care.

## Exact dual-session observation rules

For each exact assay and each procedure separately:

1. Preprocedure is the latest valid numeric result in `[procedure timestamp - 7 days, procedure timestamp)`.
2. Acute postprocedure is the valid result in `(procedure timestamp, procedure timestamp + 72 hours]` minimizing absolute distance to procedure time plus 24 hours, with earlier timestamp as deterministic tie-breaker.
3. Same-timestamp values are neither pre nor post. Preserve inequality markers and specimen types. Parse only a complete signed decimal/scientific numeric string with optional leading `<`, `>`, `≤`, or `≥`, after removing commas; do not convert inequalities into exact clinical values.
4. A dual-session pair requires a valid pre and acute value at both TACE2 and repeat TACE for the same patient and exact assay. The primary joint profile requires both albumin and total bilirubin; assays are never pooled across labels, and acute-suffixed or generic bilirubin/albumin labels are excluded from the primary analysis.
5. Define `delta2 = post2 - pre2`, `delta3 = post3 - pre3`, and `change_of_change = delta3 - delta2` only as a descriptive sensitivity. For positive values, report within-assay `log(post/pre)` differences as a scale sensitivity. Ratios do not solve missing units or establish clinical margins.

The audit found the following available dual-session denominators: albumin 36/109, total bilirubin 37/109, joint albumin-plus-bilirubin 36/109, albumin-plus-bilirubin-plus-INR 30/92, and all seven exact assays 30/92 (systemic versus comparator). These are observation-selected subsets of the 685 selected repeat events, not population counts.

## Estimands and analysis hierarchy

### Primary inherited repeat-event estimand

Retain the parent's primary event-pair analysis: within the selected first repeat-TACE population, compare exact-clock repeat-TACE albumin and bilirubin changes between frozen pathways, with baseline-overlap weighting and explicit event/pair selection diagnostics. Its interpretation remains a selected-event association, not a treatment effect or TACE toxicity estimate.

### New secondary persistence estimand

For each assay separately, among patients with valid dual-session pairs, estimate the association between TACE2 response and repeat-TACE response. Use a pre-specified parsimonious model of repeat postprocedure value (or repeat change in a sensitivity) with:

- TACE2 postprocedure value or TACE2 change as the focal predictor;
- TACE2 preprocedure value and repeat-TACE preprocedure value as level-adjustment covariates;
- frozen pathway arm and a TACE2-response-by-arm interaction as exploratory terms;
- time from TACE2 to repeat TACE, repeat event day, and exact postprocedure timing as limited timing covariates.

Report arm-specific Spearman correlations and robust regression slopes before adjustment, then the parsimonious adjusted association with 95% intervals. Do not fit high-dimensional models, use all seven assays as a composite, or call a small interaction clinically meaningful. The joint albumin/bilirubin analysis is a concordance description with separate assay estimates, not a unitless liver-injury score. If sample size permits, use five-fold cross-fitting only for baseline overlap and dual-observation selection probabilities; otherwise report the persistence analysis as descriptive/exploratory with no causal weighting.

The change-of-change estimate is mandatory as a transparent sensitivity, but it is not primary because it is vulnerable to regression to the mean, mathematical coupling, assay measurement error, and unequal baseline levels. Compare results with the level-adjusted model, and report whether the direction survives. A TACE2 response may be a post-baseline mediator or consequence of the first procedure; it must not be treated as a baseline confounder for the pathway effect.

### Selection and missingness diagnostics

Retain all frozen-arm patients in denominators for repeat-event selection, and all selected-event patients in denominators for dual-assay selection. Report arm-specific event fractions, exact dual-pair fractions, timing distributions, missingness patterns, and whether dual-pair availability differs by admission department, event timing, calendar year, or preprocedure level. Fit observation models using only pre-TACE2 covariates and do not use postprocedure values to predict their own observation. Extreme or poorly calibrated weights make the persistence analysis descriptive only; weighting cannot repair unmeasured repeat-event rationale or laboratory observation selection.

## Falsification, robustness, and result branches

Required computational checks are: frozen reconstruction; first-event ordering; same-timestamp exclusion; exact 72-hour boundary; deterministic tie-breaks; assay-label fidelity; no unit inference; and aggregate dual-pair counts matching the audit. Repeat the persistence diagnostic under locked alternatives: pre windows of 3 and 14 days; nearest +48-hour and first-post selectors; calendar days +1–3; strict exact labels versus a separately labeled broad ontology sensitivity; exclusion of repeat events after 2024; and exclusion of later ontology records between TACE2 and repeat TACE. No favorable selector may be chosen after seeing results.

Include a pre-event negative-control check using the same assay from an earlier pre-TACE2 window to the TACE2 preprocedure value where available. A comparable pre-event arm contrast or a strong apparent persistence that disappears when adjusting for both session-specific prelevels supports regression-to-mean or secular-care explanations rather than repeat-procedure vulnerability. Report baseline shifts (`pre3 - pre2`) alongside every change-of-change result.

- Supportive persistence: a reproducible, directionally coherent association between TACE2 and repeat perturbations, with reasonable observation support, stable timing sensitivities, and no comparable pre-event signal. This supports only a prognostic response-persistence marker in selected repeat-TACE patients; it does not support cumulative injury or treatment causality.
- Adverse or vulnerability signal: repeat perturbation is systematically larger after a larger TACE2 perturbation, especially if clinically reviewed units later validate the magnitude. This warrants external validation and intensified monitoring research, not a claim that the first procedure caused the second response.
- Precise null: narrow assay-specific intervals around no persistence. Report no detectable reproducible marker relationship, not equivalence or absence of risk; current missing units prevent automated clinical equivalence.
- Selection-sensitive: dual-pair fractions, weights, or estimates differ materially by arm or selector. Downgrade to descriptive evidence and state that repeat-event and assay-observation selection govern inference.
- Regression-to-mean/mathematical-coupling signal: change-of-change differs from the level-adjusted model, or baseline shifts explain the result. Reject a cumulative-stress interpretation.
- Inconclusive: systemic dual-pair support, effective sample size, overlap, or interval precision is inadequate; the result remains feasibility evidence only. Do not promote INR/all-seven assays to primary outcomes.
- Recovery unavailable: no days-8–21 recovery endpoint is estimated. The preceding audit's recovery triplets were only 2 systemic versus 5 comparator and remain outside this child's estimand.

## Unavailable evidence and limits on conclusions

The data do not provide laboratory units, analyzer/reference-range validation, imaging response or tumor burden, repeat-TACE rationale, technical selectivity or embolized territory, drug/contrast dose, verified medication administration, albumin infusion/fluid balance, outside-care capture, performance status, adjudicated complications, or reliable mortality. Procedure/admission linkage does not prove that a laboratory change was caused by TACE. A stable response relationship could represent patient susceptibility, disease trajectory, or care selection. Stronger clinical conclusions require laboratory metadata and unit validation, interventional-radiology details, medication administration and fluid records, imaging and retreatment rationale, serial outpatient laboratories, external-care capture, and prospective or externally validated longitudinal analysis.

An automatic verifier can check all population reconstruction, joins, clocks, exact labels, dual-session counts, model inputs, selection diagnostics, sensitivity branches, and whether reported conclusions follow computed outputs. It cannot adjudicate whether a biochemical change is clinically important, whether a procedure caused it, whether drugs were taken, or whether the marker should change patient management.
