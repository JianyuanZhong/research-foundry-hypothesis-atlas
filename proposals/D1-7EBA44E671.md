# Strictly prior-encounter assay content beyond capture intensity for the next recorded HCC transition

## Successor decision and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the incumbent's primary scientific question and endpoint:

> Among all eligible adult first-observed HCC-coded inpatient admissions, does prior assay content (assay identity, result/value and dated trajectory) improve calibrated prediction of the complete all-admission recorded procedure/discharge transition beyond admission/current-lab context and a capture-intensity-only comparator?

The repair changes the identification of “prior” information. A prior event must now satisfy both:

1. its native event time is valid and lies in `[t0 - 730 days, t0)); and
2. its linked encounter's native `admission time` is valid and strictly earlier than the anchor `t0`.

Rows linked to the anchor encounter are excluded from the primary history models even when their event timestamp is earlier than `t0`; they are retained in a frozen secondary sensitivity branch. This matters because an earlier event timestamp on the anchor encounter may be retrospective entry, registration/peri-admission workflow, or a genuinely available result, and this snapshot has no entry/order-record timestamp that resolves that ambiguity.

The new, falsifiable identification claim is:

> The E-minus-Q assay-content increment remains after restricting history to rows linked to encounters that were already admitted before `t0`, and is materially attenuated when anchor-linked rows are the only added history.

Here E and Q retain their incumbent meaning, but are evaluated under the stricter, availability-proximal history rule. The primary outcome remains the unchanged exhaustive all-admission seven-state recorded transition. The order-before-procedure concordance layer remains a gated secondary decision-relevance analysis; it is not allowed to redefine or rescue the primary estimand.

The actual deliverable is a newly frozen cohort and row/time audit; strict-history and anchor-linked sensitivity feature manifests; exhaustive primary labels; matched fitted A/L/Q/E/H models and irregular S/S_Q models; locked patient-held-out probabilities; paired log-loss and Brier contrasts; calibration and clustered uncertainty; strict-history versus anchor-linked comparison; falsifications; and a claim-to-evidence table. Completion is established by these computed outputs, not by a positive result.

## Primary endpoint, preserved exactly

Using only procedure presence and native `procedures.Start Time`, construct an exhaustive admission-anchored terminal state for every eligible admission:

- `P0`: a valid procedure starts in `[t0,t0+24h)) and before discharge;
- `D0`: discharge is at or before 24 hours before any established valid early procedure;
- `U0`: missing/invalid procedure timing could change procedure/discharge ordering or window assignment;
- `R24`: none of those early states is established;
- among `R24`, `P1`, `D1), `U1` and `N72) are assigned analogously in `[t0+24h,t0+72h)`.

Use the incumbent's deterministic precedence, including procedure-wins for exact discharge/procedure ties, and report a discharge-wins sensitivity. Procedures before admission, after discharge, at exact window boundaries, multiple procedures and midnight starts receive explicit audit counts. Missing timing is unresolved, never “no procedure.” Report the complete seven-state terminal probability vector on all admissions; any R24-conditional analysis is secondary.

The primary estimand is the paired held-out score contrast
`score(E) - score(Q)` for this complete seven-state vector, where lower is better, using multiclass log loss and multiclass Brier score. Restricting the history provenance changes the identification of the assay-content contrast, not the target population, outcome, landmark or all-admission estimand.

## Decision-relevance secondary endpoint

Retain the incumbent's deterministic, noncausal order-procedure audit. Apply only frozen substring maps to normalized `procedures.Surgery` and `orders.Non-drug Order`:

- embolization/TACE: `TACE`, `embolization`, `chemoembolization`;
- resection/transplant: `resection`, `transplant`;
- ablation: `ablation`, `radiofrequency`, `microwave`, `absolute ethanol`;
- systemic/infusion: `chemotherapy`, `targeted therapy`, `immunotherapy`, `perfusion`;
- diagnostic/access: `Angiography`, `Puncture`, `Biopsy`, `Drainage`, `Catheter Placement`;
- otherwise `other`.

Preserve every multi-match, blank and ambiguous string. For each procedure eligible for P0/P1, join orders on `(patient master index, visit number)). Primary concordance requires nonblank `non-drug medical order`, a valid `order time` strictly before procedure `start time`, and a shared deterministic family label. Valid `start time` without valid `order time` is a separately reported lower-confidence tier, never silently pooled. Exact ties, orders after procedures, missing/conflicting times and ambiguous family assignments are not concordant.

Freeze the gate before locked labels: at least 100 uniquely family-assigned, temporally resolvable procedure admissions; >=80% of timed procedures nonblank and exactly one family; >=70% of candidate orders with resolvable `order time`; no family >90% of concordant events; and <=25% of concordant candidates dependent on exact-midnight ties or unresolved timing. If the family gate fails, run the generic any-non-drug-order-before-any-procedure fallback only if it has >=100 temporally resolvable events; otherwise declare the secondary endpoint inconclusive and retain the primary study.

For a passing secondary layer, emit mutually exclusive early and later states with fixed precedence: concordant order-procedure (`C0/C1`), procedure-only (`P0only/P1only`), order-only (`O0only/O1only`), discharge before an established event (`D0/D1`), unresolved/ambiguous timing (`U0/U1`), and no established event (`R24/N72`). Report strict-history E-Q on this endpoint only after the gate. It is a recorded temporal concordance, not a decision, intent, recommendation, appropriateness, administration, completion or benefit. Its clinical meaning requires chart review of order semantics, scheduling and intent.

## Matched information sets and alternatives

All models use exactly the same cohort, endpoint, patient split, fit-only preprocessing and scoring. The strict-history provenance rule applies to Q, E, H and S; the anchor-linked sensitivity reruns all applicable models with the same frozen hyperparameters and no retuning.

- **A (admission baseline):** age, sex, admitting department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, admission missingness and a strict-history availability indicator.
- **L (current-lab baseline):** A plus labs in `[t0,t0+12h)`, using `test`, `qualitative result`, `quantitative result`, `specimen type`, `test time`. For each assay, use first/last valid numeric value, change, elapsed time, count, density/missingness and slope only with two distinct times; preserve qualitative results assay-specifically. No cross-assay numeric pooling because units are absent.
- **Q (capture-intensity comparator):** L plus prior strict-history process intensity only: prior encounter counts/durations/days active; days since last strict prior event/encounter; source-specific row and distinct-encounter counts for labs/procedures/orders/medications; observed source-type count; missing-time counts; left-truncation and no-strict-history indicators. Q contains no assay identity, value or result and no procedure/order/medication names, dose, route, frequency or status.
- **E (primary content model):** Q plus prior strict-history lab content and dated trajectory from the exact lab fields: assay identity, assay-specific numeric/qualitative result, fit-only within-assay scaling, recency, span, count, missingness, change and slope where supported. Do not create a physiologic score or pool assays.
- **H (transparent all-history comparator):** Q-like encounter/source summaries, assay-specific prior-lab summaries, prior procedure occurrence/recency/gaps and order/medication process counts/recency/gaps, with no name interpretation. H is rerun with strict history and is a comparator for the incumbent's broader summary.
- **S (irregular learned alternative):** a low-dimensional continuous-time latent state over exactly the strict prior rows used by E/H. Each event has source/type, elapsed time, payload mask, and only lab events carry assay-specific numeric/qualitative content. Use elapsed-time decay, assay-specific emissions, and a supervised early/later competing-state head. Generic order/procedure/medication tokens represent process, not treatment.
- **S_Q (process-only learned control):** identical S architecture, split, regularization and event times, but remove all lab values/results while retaining assay/event occurrence, source/type and capture timing. It tests whether S's gain is merely irregular observation structure.

A/L/Q/E/H are the transparent auditable comparison. S can reveal order-sensitive trajectories, nonlinear interactions, irregular spacing and representation uncertainty that first/last/count summaries lose. S_Q distinguishes content from timing/capture complexity. A mechanistic alternative is deliberately limited to assay-specific first-to-last change/slope and recency terms already in E: the local data lack lab units and validated reference ranges, so a cross-assay organ-reserve score would add unsupported physiology rather than scientific information. If S is unstable or adds no calibrated information beyond E/H, retain the transparent estimand and defer richer sequence models. If expert adjudication validates clinically meaningful assay units/semantics, revisit a mechanistic organ-reserve model in a new study.

Fit A/L/Q/E/H with identical regularized sequential multinomial-logistic heads for early and R24-conditional later distributions, then marginalize to the complete target. Fit S/S_Q with supervised terminal-state negative log likelihood; fix latent dimension, decay family, masking/reconstruction term, shrinkage and seed count before locked evaluation, tune only on the selection split. The primary comparison remains E-Q; secondary contrasts are Q-L, E-L, H-L, S-E and S_Q-Q. No model's complexity or GPU use is evidence of clinical value.

## Split, estimand analysis and uncertainty

Assign patients by SHA-256(normalized `Patient Master Index`) modulo 100: buckets 0-59 fit, 60-69 selection/preprocessing/calibration, 70-79 locked evaluation and 80-99 inaccessible. All rows and admissions for a patient follow one bucket. Never use locked labels to choose history rules, dictionaries, concordance gates, assay dictionaries, hyperparameters, calibration or sensitivity interpretation.

Report paired held-out multiclass log loss and Brier for the complete primary seven-state vector and, if gated, the secondary vector. Use patient-clustered paired bootstrap with at least 1,000 resamples, preserving all admissions for each sampled patient, for 95% intervals. Report state-specific calibration intercept/slope, reliability curves, observed versus predicted probabilities and per-state probability contrasts. Stratify by no strict history, 1-2 and >=3 strict prior encounters; by capture-intensity strata defined on fit-only quantiles; and by 30/180/730-day strict history windows. These are descriptive associations, not causal subgroups or external validation.

Supportive primary evidence requires a strict-history E-Q interval below zero for log loss and/or Brier without material calibration deterioration; Q not reproducing the E/H increment; attenuation after assay identity/value/history permutation; stability across capture strata and prespecified history windows; and no forbidden-window, anchor-link or patient-split leakage. Strong identification support additionally requires the strict-history result to agree in direction with the anchor-linked sensitivity while not depending on anchor-linked rows, or a clear attenuation pattern consistent with those rows being documentation artifacts.

Adverse evidence is no strict-history E-Q improvement, worsening calibration, Q reproducing E/H, persistence after content permutation, a gain only in the anchor-linked branch, a gain concentrated in the 0-30-day exclusion failure, forbidden-window leakage or patient-level split violation. For the concordance layer, adverse evidence includes disappearance under order-time scrambling, procedure-only/order-only dominance, or instability across the `Order Time` versus `Start Time` tiers.

Inconclusive evidence includes dominant U states, wide patient-cluster intervals, sparse assays, severe left truncation, invalid/coarse timestamps, too few strict-history patients, dictionary ambiguity, failed concordance coverage, source-specific reversal or a secondary finding present only in a low-confidence timing tier. Inconclusive does not confirm the hypothesis.

Falsifications must include: shuffled assay values within patient and within fit-only assay strata; shuffled prior-history times preserving capture counts; replacing strict event provenance with anchor-linked rows; the `t0-30d` exclusion; a forbidden-window model using [24,72h) rows as predictors (expected to expose leakage and never used for the primary claim); shuffled patient IDs before splitting; and discharge/procedure tie precedence sensitivity. The solver must label which falsifications are expected negative controls and report them even when unfavorable.
