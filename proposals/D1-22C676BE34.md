# Nighttime ICU discharge: is the excess risk concentrated in patients with residual pre-discharge instability?

Branch: `work/[producer attribution withheld]/` — Lead generation, eICU island episode 36 (expert seed `eicu-06`, true parent `[prior hypothesis]`).
Status: **DESIGN + VERIFIED BINDINGS ONLY — not executed. No cohort was built, no model fitted, no outcome computed.** All numbers below that are not marked "verified" are planned specifications, not results.

## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICU discharge "out of hours" (evening/night) is associated with higher in-hospital mortality and ICU readmission [K1]. The published meta-analysis of 18 cohorts (1,191,178 patients) reports RR 1.39 (95% CI 1.24-1.57) for mortality and 1.30 (1.19-1.42) for readmission, and states plainly that "whether these increases result from patient differences, differences in care, or a combination remains unclear" [K1]. Two materially different explanations remain live:

- **Leading claim (rival we want to isolate): selection-by-instability.** Nighttime discharges are a *selected* sicker/less-stable subgroup; the timing variable is a marker of residual instability (a triage artefact), not a cause.
- **Competing claim: discharge-timing / monitoring practice effect.** Holding pre-discharge stability fixed, nighttime discharge is itself harmful (thin overnight ward staffing, slowed response, rushed handoff, reduced monitoring).

This matters because the two imply different decisions. If the excess is concentrated in unstable patients, the actionable intervention is to **target monitoring/discharge-readiness at high-instability patients regardless of hour** and nighttime timing per se is not the lever. If the excess persists among *stable* patients discharged at night, then **avoiding/delaying nighttime discharge or boosting overnight ward coverage** is the lever. Refining this decision is the seed's stated significance.

## 2. Evidence vs untested claim

- **Supported by evidence:** an association between out-of-hours discharge and worse outcome, robust across definitions/systems [K1]. A measurable pre-discharge instability signal exists and predicts 48h readmission (AUROC 0.77 internal; 0.725 on eICU-CRD) [K2].
- **Untested claim (this experiment):** whether the nighttime association is *concentrated among* (i.e., moderated by) residual instability, on an interaction scale, within one harmonized multi-hospital dataset after severity and hospital adjustment. K1 could not test it (unadjusted pooled cohorts); K2 does not adjust for discharge hour and is predictive, not causal; K3 shows readmission endpoints are inconsistently defined and pooling is unreliable, so a pre-specified temporal outcome is required. No inspected work delivers the instability × discharge-time interaction estimate.

## 3. Estimand (explicit)

Primary estimand: **the multiplicative interaction** between nighttime discharge (exposure) and a pre-discharge residual-instability index (categorical tertile or continuous) on the odds of the primary outcome, adjusted for measured severity and hospital fixed effects, in adult eICU patients who survived an ICU stay of ≥24 h. Formally: RD of interaction on the risk-difference scale (and OR-interaction / ratio-of-ORs on the logit scale) of `NighttimeDischarge:Instability`. The sign and magnitude of this interaction — not the marginal nighttime main effect — is the target. If the practice effect story holds, the interaction is negative on the additive scale (nighttime harm larger/only present in low-instability strata) is NOT expected; instead: **selection** ⇒ nighttime excess concentrated in high-instability (positive interaction on the additive scale); **practice** ⇒ nighttime excess present across strata including stable patients (interaction ≈ 0 with a nonzero main effect among stable patients). This directional mapping is the discriminating observation.

## 4. Population, temporal boundaries, exposure, outcome

### 4.1 Population (planned)
- Unit: eICU **patientunitstayid** (single ICU stay). Adult: `age >= 16` (records are age-in-years; administrative values like "> 89" and blanks exist and must be code-mapped — see §7 open items).
- Survived ICU discharge: `unitdischargestatus == 'Alive'` (verified values: Alive/Expired, plus 34 NaN in a 200k-row partial scan).
- Landmark to permit a pre-discharge instability window: `unitdischargeoffset >= 1440` min (≥24 h ICU LOS) as the primary spec; sensitivity at `>= 4320` (72 h).
- Non-specialist-ICU restriction is *not* enforced (eICU `unittype` mixes MICU/SICU/CTICU) — retained as a covariate instead; a sensitivity analysis excludes CTICU/Cardiac.
- Plausible-offset guard: drop `unitdischargeoffset <= 0` (ICU-to-ICU transfers) and cap extreme values.

### 4.2 Exposure: nighttime discharge (planned)
- Parse `unitdischargetime24` (`HH:MM:SS` **verified** format, e.g. "03:50:00") → hour = int of the first field.
- Primary `night_discharge = (hour >= 20) | (hour < 7)` (a symmetric 2000-0700 window). Sensitivity windows: 1900-0700, 2200-0600, and a continuous circular hour term. This range brackets the definition spread reported in [K1] (16:00-22:00 start, 05:59-09:00 end).
- Alternative exposure for robustness: `unitdischargetime24` is a wall-clock field; cross-check against `unitadmittime24` + `unitdischargeoffset` (min) modulo 1440 to detect discordance.

### 4.3 Pre-discharge instability index (planned)
- Window: observations with `observationoffset` (vitalPeriodic) / `labresultoffset` (lab) in `[unitdischargeoffset - 1440, unitdischargeoffset)` — **last 24 h before discharge**.
- Vitals from `vitalPeriodic` (verified columns: `temperature`, `sao2`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, `systemicsystolic`, `systemicdiastolic`, `systemicmean`): per-stay min/max/last/mean and **last-6h slope** of `systemicmean`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, `sao2`, plus count of hypotensive readings (`systemicmean < 65`).
- Labs from `lab` (verified `labname` values include `lactate`, `pH`, `BUN`, `HCO3`, `creatinine`, `chloride`, `magnesium`): last value in window for `lactate`, `pH`, `BUN`, `creatinine`, `HCO3`.
- Aggregation: z-score each component on the analysis cohort; instability index = mean of standardized adverse direction (higher = more unstable). Report index as continuous and as tertiles (low/mid/high) — tertiles define the primary stratified/interaction test.
- Guards: if a component is missing, index = available-component mean (require ≥3 components); report a missingness indicator.

### 4.4 Outcomes (planned)
- **Primary (secondary-ICU endpoint to avoid the practice-vs-ward conflation):** ICU readmission within 48 h, built by ordering ICU stays within the same patient via `uniquepid` and `unitvisitnumber`, using the next stay's `unitadmitoffset` on the same `patienthealthsystemstayid`. 48h is chosen to match [K2] and is pre-specified per [K3]'s warning about inconsistent readmission timeframes.
- **Secondary:** in-hospital death after ICU discharge (`hospitaldischargestatus == 'Expired'` while `unitdischargestatus == 'Alive'`); and a 7-day ward-deterioration composite if derivable.
- Event ascertainment must respect the same-hospital stay boundary (`patienthealthsystemstayid`); readmission requires the next `unitvisitnumber` to be within the same health-system stay to avoid cross-hospital identity leakage.

## 5. Confounding adjustment and rival separation

- Severity: `apachescore`, `predictedicumortality`, `predictedhospitalmortality` (verified columns in `apachePatientResult`; `apachescore` observed range -1..211 in partial scan — negatives are sentinel/administrative and must be set missing), plus `unitadmitsource`, `unittype`, `unitvisitnumber`.
- **Hospital fixed effects:** `hospitalid` (from `patient`); eICU `hospitalid` is a distinct namespace — no cross-dataset join.
- Rival separation: (i) **stratified** nighttime effect within instability tertiles; (ii) **discharge-time × instability interaction** term with its CI; (iii) a negative-control-ish check: nighttime discharge **among the most stable tertile** — the practice claim predicts a nonzero effect there, selection predicts ≈null.

## 6. Baselines and substantive alternatives (with method comparison)

- **B1 — discharge-time only:** logistic regression `outcome ~ night_discharge + severity + hospital FE`. Reproduces the marginal nighttime association; loses all information about who was discharged.
- **B2 — instability only:** `outcome ~ instability_index(+tertiles) + severity + hospital FE`. Captures selection-by-instability; loses the timing channel.
- **A1 — interaction / depth-conditioned (primary substantive alternative):** `outcome ~ night_discharge * instability_tertile + severity + hospital FE`. This is the discriminating model: it can reveal *where* the nighttime excess sits. **What A1 reveals that B1/B2 lose:** B1 cannot distinguish "harm from timing" from "harm from who was discharged"; B2 cannot detect that timing adds harm *only* in stable patients. A1's interaction is the scientific deliverable.
- **A2 — hospital-hierarchical / GBT alternative:** (a) mixed-effects logistic with random hospital intercept and random nighttime slope, to check whether the interaction is a hospital-composition artefact; (b) a gradient-boosted tree on the same features with SHAP-based partial dependence for night vs instability, to test whether a flexible learner finds a nighttime×instability structure that the linear A1 misses. Selection rule: choose A1 as primary unless A2 materially changes the interaction sign/CI; report both.

## 7. Falsification criteria (pre-specified)

- **SUPPORTIVE of the interaction/selection-concentration hypothesis:** A1 interaction term negative on the additive scale with 95% CI excluding 0, i.e. the nighttime risk difference is **larger in the high-instability tertile** and ≈null in the lowest tertile; and the nighttime effect among the lowest-instability tertile is not distinguishable from 0.
- **SUPPORTIVE of a practice/timing effect:** a nighttime main effect present **within the most stable tertile** (CI excluding 0) with A1 interaction CI including 0. This would challenge the "pure selection" story and support timing/monitoring as a lever.
- **ADVERSE / refuting the seed's concentration claim:** stable-tertile nighttime effect ≥ high-instability effect, or an interaction in the opposite direction (nighttime harm larger among the *stable*). This would mean residual instability does NOT explain the nighttime excess.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** wide CIs including 0 for both the interaction and the stable-tertile effect; or an index with severe missingness; or a readmission event rate too low after 48h/landmark restrictions to power the interaction. An imprecise null does not refute either claim — it defers.

## 8. What each outcome would / would not establish

- Supportive results would establish a **moderation pattern** (descriptive/associational). They would **not** establish that delaying nighttime discharge helps, nor root-cause the mechanism (bed pressure, staffing, handoff).
- A stable-tertile effect would **not** by itself prove a causal practice effect; unmeasured case-mix (e.g., family/transport pressure) remains.
- **Essential clinical adjudication / unavailable evidence (explicit):** eICU has **no bed-capacity, staffing, nurse-ratio, or handoff-quality fields**; the seed card itself flags this. Therefore organizational mechanism is **not identifiable here** and must not be claimed. A causal practice-effect claim would require an external natural-experiment or staffing/capacity dataset plus expert review — a **different study**, not this one. Identity/version hazards: `uniquepid` reuse across `unitvisitnumber` and health-system stays needs expert-validated ordering rules; the readmission endpoint needs clinical review of transfer-vs-readmission semantics.

## 11. Single most important unresolved objection

**Measure/estimand risk:** eICU lacks organizational variables (bed capacity, staffing, handoff), so even a clean instability × timing interaction identifies only *where the excess sits*, not *why*. A reviewer will also press on whether the "last-24h instability index" is a proxy for the same selection that generates nighttime discharge (endogenous), which can bias the interaction toward the selection story. This is the highest-risk item and is not resolvable with eICU alone.
