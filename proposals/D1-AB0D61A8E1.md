# HCC longitudinal recurrence/progression proposal (deliverable child)

## Status
Lead working draft evolved from [prior hypothesis]. This is a proposal, not a completed scientific result. The child resolves the cohort-feasibility blocker by binding a narrower, data-supported first-HCC index event and pre-index window, and attaches three inspected key references.

## Question
Among patients with a dated first recorded HCC-directed treatment event (resection, TACE, or ablation) during a 180-day pre-index observation window, does a longitudinal pattern in routine liver-related laboratory values and HCC-directed treatment events during that window predict a subsequent recorded HCC-related event within 365 days, beyond what is captured by the latest available lab values and encounter intensity?

## Why it matters
HCC recurrence and progression can change surveillance and treatment decisions. The primary scientific value is testing whether temporal routine-data patterns contain information lost by current-state summaries. A negative result would argue against adding a temporal model for this endpoint in this dataset.

## Evidence inspected
- HCC catalog and table metadata confirm compatible `患者主索引` and `就诊号` identifiers, 105,044 encounters, 43,815 patients, and explicit encounter timestamps.
- Labs have `检验时间`, procedures have `开单时间`, diagnoses have `诊断时间`, and encounters have `入院时间`/`出院时间`.
- A bounded feasibility scan found that broad HCC-related diagnosis/procedure definitions do not yield a stable eligible cohort, motivating the narrower dated treatment-event index.
- Three inspected public works bound the question to prior longitudinal-clinical-data research: Delphi for learned disease-history sequences, ALADYNOULLI for longitudinal latent trajectories, and the Oncoformer supplement for lab-only versus multimodal cancer prediction.

## Population and temporal design
- Population: patients in the HCC snapshot with a dated first recorded HCC-directed treatment event (resection, TACE, or ablation) in `data_手术_8024330590283626027.csv` or a corresponding dated treatment order/procedure field.
- Index date: the date of that first treatment event.
- Pre-index observation window: 180 days before the index date, requiring at least two dated lab encounters and at least one liver-related laboratory measurement.
- Endpoint: any recorded HCC-related event within 365 days after the index date, using dated diagnoses, procedures, or treatment orders consistent with the index definition.
- Exclude patients whose first treatment event cannot be dated or whose pre-index window is empty.

## Variables and data bindings
- Patient identifier: `患者主索引` in all HCC tables.
- Encounter identifier: `就诊号` in encounters and related tables.
- Encounter times: `入院时间`, `出院时间` in `data_基本信息_2500296761891079109.csv`.
- Lab values and times: `检验时间`, item names and values in `data_检验_609065997844652188.csv`; focus on liver-related tests such as albumin, bilirubin, transaminases and AFP where present.
- Treatment/procedure events: `开单时间`, procedure names and order fields in `data_手术_8024330590283626027.csv` and `data_医嘱(非药品)_2062526727266216118.csv`.
- Diagnoses: `诊断时间` and diagnosis text/codes in `data_诊断_7504718184492840569.csv`.
- Clinical context: age, sex and relevant clinical-document fields where available, without using post-index information.

## Baseline versus alternative
- Baseline: a transparent logistic model using the latest available liver-related lab values, encounter intensity, and treatment-event count in the pre-index window.
- Alternative: a temporal model using dated lab and treatment-event sequences over the 180-day window, with patient-level splitting and held-out evaluation.
- The alternative can reveal whether ordering, trends, or repeated treatment episodes add information beyond the latest state; the baseline establishes whether such added information is needed.

## Analysis and falsification
- Use patient-level train/validation/test splits, with no leakage across patients.
- Compare discrimination (AUC) and calibration for the 365-day recorded-event endpoint, with uncertainty intervals.
- The claim is supported only if the temporal model improves discrimination or calibration beyond the baseline after accounting for uncertainty.
- An adverse result means the current-state summary is sufficient for this endpoint in this dataset.
- An inconclusive result means the cohort, outcome support, or uncertainty is too limited to distinguish the models.

## Key references
[K1] Delphi: learned disease-history sequences from structured, dated clinical data; supports the value of temporal disease histories but does not establish that HCC recurrence in this dataset requires a learned sequence model.
[K2] ALADYNOULLI: Bayesian longitudinal latent trajectories; supports modeling longitudinal clinical structure but does not establish HCC-specific recurrence prediction or validate the proposed endpoint.
[K3] Oncoformer supplement: cancer prediction using longitudinal routine data and modality ablations; supports the relevance of longitudinal EHR features for cancer outcomes but does not establish that a lab-only HCC adaptation outperforms current-state summaries.

## Limitations
- The endpoint is a recorded HCC-related event, not adjudicated recurrence or progression.
- Treatment-event coding and HCC-specific diagnosis coverage must be validated before full analysis.
- The feasibility scan found broad definitions insufficient; the narrower index must be checked for cohort size and outcome support.
- No causal or mechanistic claim is made from this observational comparison.
