# UKB 16: Glycemic trajectories with discordant liver fat and pancreatic fat

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

At similar BMI and liver fat, those with higher pancreatic fat are more likely to develop subsequent deterioration in glucose metabolism, whereas lipoprotein abnormalities are more prominent in those with high liver fat.

## Study population and key data

Abdominal MRI population; liver and pancreatic PDFF, metabolomics, HbA1c, and diabetes outcomes.

## Basic Approach

Compare the conditional associations of liver fat and pancreatic fat separately; analyze repeated biochemistry independently from the second assessment onward, and do not use concurrent data as follow-up.

## Significance of the Research

Distinguish the organ-function pathways associated with ectopic fat distribution.

## Main Challenges

PDFF is not equivalent to cellular function; measurement error and selection into the imaging population need to be addressed.

## References

Relevant background or data description, not proof that the hypothesis is valid or novel: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-022-01199-5; field definitions: https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=21088; https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=21090; https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=21085

## Life Sciences

Cross-organ metabolism and disease divergence

## Competing Explanations

Overall visceral fat and potential insulin resistance explain the shared association with both organs.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

First check whether sufficient PDFF variation remains when visceral fat and BMI are similar; if there is insufficient overlap, do not make conditional comparisons.
