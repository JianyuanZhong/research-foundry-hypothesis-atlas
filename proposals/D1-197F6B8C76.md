# Evolution audit: landmark selection and residual severity in discordant-AKI recovery

Status: proposed successor design; no cohort scan, model fit, or scientific result was run.

Parent audited: [prior hypothesis].  
Purpose: resolve the remaining uncertainty that the 48-hour discordant-versus-concordant contrast may be created by conditioning on survival/continued ICU observation, residual pre-landmark severity, or unequal recording opportunity.

## 1. Scientific opening and substantive change

The parent asks whether a fall in creatinine after ICU AKI is prognostically misleading when urine output or recorded net volume does not improve. Its primary 48-hour post-onset landmark is leakage-safe and clinically interpretable, but it conditions on being alive, still in the ICU, and sufficiently observed at 48 hours. That conditioning can select a different mixture of severity than the original AKI-onset population. A second concern is that “low urine” can mean either genuine low output or sparse charting, while recorded balance is incomplete and treatment-dependent.

The strongest available evidence supports only bounded background claims:

- [K1] reports, in a small ICU study, that creatinine-based eGFR can overestimate measured kidney function with prolonged critical illness and muscle loss. It does not show that a MIMIC creatinine fall identifies persistent dysfunction.
- [K2] shows that urine-output thresholds and time windows change AKI mortality prediction in MIMIC-IV/eICU. It does not establish the correct threshold for recovery discordance, nor a mechanism or treatment effect.
- [K3] associates positive fluid balance with ICU AKI and mortality in a prospective cohort, while defining balance as intake minus output without insensible losses and leaving KRT initiation to clinicians. It does not study this recovery phenotype.

The unresolved claim remains prognostic, not causal: among patients at a specified post-AKI landmark, does discordant recovery have a reproducible excess 7-day hospital-death/ICU-recorded-RRT risk over concordant recovery after accounting for measured pre-landmark severity and recording opportunity? The new claim is narrower and testable: if the contrast is clinically meaningful, it should not be confined to one survivor landmark or to patients with unusually dense renal documentation; persistent discordance should be more informative than a transient or newly observed discordance.

This successor changes the information obtained in three ways:

1. It estimates a prespecified landmark-selection curve at 24, 48, and 72 hours rather than treating the 48-hour risk set as the whole target population.
2. It distinguishes persistent, resolving, and newly discordant trajectories between adjacent landmarks.
3. It makes “insufficient opportunity to observe” an explicit state and tests a process-only recording signal, rather than silently dropping those patients or coding absent urine rows as zero.

It does not identify true GFR, total-body volume, tubular injury, clinician intent, or a causal treatment effect.

## 2. Actual scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly construct the same index-ICU-stay AKI cohort and produce:

1. A flow table from AKI onset through each landmark, including death, ICU discharge, pre-landmark RRT, missing creatinine, insufficient urine/input opportunity, and each classifiable exposure state.
2. The parent primary 48-hour standardized risk difference:
   `RD_48 = P(Y_48,7d=1 | discordant) - P(Y_48,7d=1 | concordant)`
   among eligible, classifiable creatinine-recovery patients in the 48-hour risk set.
3. Component-specific 48-hour contrasts for low-urine-only, positive-recorded-balance-only, and both, against concordant recovery.
4. The same state-specific risk estimates at L=24, 48, and 72 hours among the corresponding risk sets, with the number and outcome rate of the explicit unknown/insufficient-recording state.
5. A persistence table for patients classifiable at both 24 and 48 hours: concordant-to-concordant, discordant-to-discordant, discordant-to-concordant, concordant-to-discordant, and other/unknown transitions, with the 48-hour forward outcome.
6. A severity/observation-process sensitivity envelope and a prespecified process-only falsification check.
7. The parent baseline-versus-learned comparison, extended only to ask whether ordered trajectories and persistence add information beyond fixed summaries.

Completion requires saved computed outputs linked to all estimates, uncertainty intervals, cell counts, missingness/selection flows, model specifications, and adverse/inconclusive interpretations. Readiness or a favorable coefficient is not completion.

## 3. Population, temporal boundaries, and estimands

Use the first eligible ICU stay per hospital admission for adults, joined on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`. Retain the parent time origin:

- Adults: `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`.
- First ICU stay per admission, with valid `icustays.intime < icustays.outtime`.
- AKI onset t0: earliest valid creatinine observation within 72 hours after ICU `intime` satisfying the parent KDIGO-like rule: at least 0.3 mg/dL above a creatinine value in the preceding 48 hours or at least 1.5 times the minimum valid value in the preceding 7 days.
- If no prior value supports onset, retain a separately labeled admission-baseline sensitivity; never silently invent baseline.
- Exclude established RRT before t0 using the parent procedure-event set.
- Patient-relative times only; no cross-patient calendar ordering.

For each L in {24, 48, 72} hours after t0, define a risk set R_L containing patients alive, in the index ICU, and not already experiencing the outcome-defining incident RRT at L. Report separately those who die, leave the ICU, or receive RRT before L. The primary 48-hour estimate remains a survivor/ICU-landmark prognostic estimand; it must not be relabeled as an onset-cohort or causal estimand.

At L, exposure uses only clinical times in [L-24 h, L]. No exposure or adjustment variable may use a clinical time after L. For creatinine, identify the most recent valid value in [L-12 h, L] and the peak valid value in [t0, L]. Define creatinine recovery at L as peak minus landmark creatinine >=0.3 mg/dL; report the landmark/peak <=0.8 ratio sensitivity. Neither definition means normal kidney function.

For each L, define:

- concordant: creatinine recovery, urine recovery, and recorded net volume <=0;
- low-urine-only: creatinine recovery, low observed urine, and recorded net volume <=0;
- positive-balance-only: creatinine recovery, urine recovery, and positive recorded net volume;
- both: creatinine recovery, low observed urine, and positive recorded net volume;
- unknown/insufficient: creatinine recovery but inadequate opportunity to classify urine or recorded balance;
- no-creatinine-recovery: descriptive state, not part of the primary discordant-versus-concordant contrast.

The parent binary `discordant = low urine OR positive recorded balance` remains the primary 48-hour contrast for comparability. The four classifiable states and the unknown state are mandatory secondary outputs; if the binary contrast is stable but one component is not, the conclusion must say so.

Primary outcomes remain:

- `Y_L,7d`: hospital death or first ICU-recorded incident RRT in [L, L+7 days), with follow-up not truncated at ICU outtime.
- Hospital death uses `admissions.deathtime` and `hospital_expire_flag`; alive hospital discharge is a competing/censoring event as specified by the parent outcome plan.
- RRT uses only the audited ICU `procedureevents` items and is labeled ICU-recorded RRT; ward-only RRT is unavailable.
- Secondary outcomes: death alone, ICU-recorded RRT alone, same-admission ICU readmission, and hospital death by hospital discharge.

For the dynamic landmark estimates, use cluster-robust inference or a patient-level bootstrap because one patient can contribute to more than one risk set. Do not pool the three risk sets as independent observations.

## 4. Landmark-selection audit

The solver must report, before comparing exposure groups, a risk-set flow with denominators at t0, 24, 48, and 72 hours:

- alive and still in the index ICU;
- hospital/ICU death before L;
- ICU discharge before L;
- incident RRT before L;
- no recent valid creatinine;
- insufficient primary urine observation;
- insufficient balance composition;
- classifiable concordant/discordant component states.

The principal selection diagnostic is the curve of (a) risk-set retention, (b) outcome-before-landmark, (c) unknown/insufficient recording, and (d) the discordant-minus-concordant estimate at each L. A 48-hour contrast that is large only after a sharp loss of high-risk patients, or only when unknown patients are discarded, is not evidence of a stable recovery phenotype.

The parent 48-hour analysis remains the main clinical comparison, but the dynamic estimates answer a different question: the prognosis of observed recovery states among those who reach each landmark. They do not recover a causal effect or a unique onset-cohort effect. A true onset-cohort effect would require data and assumptions sufficient to handle death, discharge, and treatment-dependent observation before L; those are not available here.

For the 24-to-48-hour trajectory analysis, use only states computed from pre-24 and pre-48 windows and outcome time after 48 hours. A patient who has an outcome before 48 is reported in the selection flow and is not assigned a 48-hour trajectory state.

## 5. Residual severity and observation-intensity rivals

### Residual severity

The leading rival is that the discordant group is simply more severely ill at or before the landmark. For each L, fit and report:

- an unadjusted standardized state-specific risk;
- a prespecified severity-adjusted model using onset AKI stage, age, sex, admission type, pre-exposure baseline creatinine, creatinine immediately before the [L-24,L] window, peak creatinine through L, pre-window urine/output and recorded-volume summaries, pre-window RRT/support indicators, and ICU timing;
- stratum-specific estimates across bounded categories of pre-window creatinine, AKI stage, and support intensity, with no estimate reported for empty or sparse cells;
- a sensitivity excluding patients with RRT in (t0,L) and a sensitivity that treats pre-L RRT/death as selection events rather than silently including them in a post-L risk contrast.

All such adjustment is prognostic standardization, not causal control. Variables measured in the exposure window are not claimed to be pre-exposure confounders; their use is a residual-severity sensitivity, and the exact timing must be reported. Do not adjust for post-L data, future outcome proxies, or the learned model’s predictions.

A contrast that vanishes in every well-supported severity stratum supports the residual-severity explanation. A contrast that survives some strata is still only an association; it does not prove intrinsic kidney injury, dilution, congestion, or muscle loss.

### Observation and measurement process

Use exact numeric observations, not imputed zeros:

- For primary urine, numeric mL values in `icu/outputevents` from itemids 226559 Foley, 226560 Void, 226561 Condom Cath, 226563 Suprapubic, and 226567 Straight Cath.
- Count zero-valued numeric output as an observed zero. A missing six-hour bin is unknown, not zero.
- Define urine observation opportunity as the number of distinct six-hour bins in [L-24,L] containing at least one valid primary-set output record. The primary classifiable urine definition requires at least 3 of 4 bins; sensitivities require at least 2 and all 4. Report the exact chosen rule and the other two sensitivities.
- Convert urine to mL/kg/h using the nearest valid weight at or before L, within 48 hours. Weights are `chartevents` itemids 224639, 226512, and 226531 with unit conversion verified from `icu/d_items`.
- Exclude nephrostomy 226564/226565, OR/PACU 226627/226631, mixed GU-irrigant 226566/227489, text estimate 226713, and Apache-derived 227519 from the primary phenotype; report them only in sensitivity/measurement-error outputs.
- For recorded balance in [L-24,L], use one mutually exclusive administered-volume rule from `inputevents`: never sum `amount` and `totalamount` from one row. Retain item-level composition, unresolved/non-mL fractions, and missingness. Recorded balance is input proxy minus direct urine output, not true fluid balance.
- Count output, creatinine, input, weight/CVP, and non-renal chart/lab observations in four six-hour bins. Use `charttime`/clinical time for the primary process counts; `storetime` ordering is a labeled sensitivity because documentation time is not an availability truth.

The process-only falsification check is prespecified: replace kidney discordance by quartiles of pre-landmark non-renal documentation density (non-creatinine `labevents` count plus non-weight/CVP `chartevents` count, with input/output event opportunity counts reported separately), and estimate its association with the same Y outcome. This is a diagnostic for acuity/recording intensity, not a valid negative-control outcome and not evidence that documentation causes death. If the process-only gradient is as large as the kidney contrast and the kidney contrast attenuates with process strata, the clinical interpretation is weakened. A post-L non-renal documentation count may be reported only as a descriptive downstream process endpoint among subjects event-free at L; it cannot be used as a primary adjustment variable.

The explicit unknown state is essential. Compare its size and outcome rate with classifiable states. If unknown patients are high risk, complete-case discordant-versus-concordant results are selection-sensitive. Do not impute unknown urine as low or normal in the primary analysis.

## 6. Dynamic trajectory estimand

Among patients classifiable at both 24 and 48 hours, estimate the 48-hour forward risk for:

- concordant -> concordant;
- discordant -> discordant (persistent);
- discordant -> concordant (resolving);
- concordant -> discordant (new);
- component-specific transitions when cell support permits;
- any transition involving unknown, reported separately.

The baseline comparison is the parent 48-hour binary discordant-versus-concordant estimate. The trajectory analysis asks whether persistence, resolution, or new discordance is more informative than the current state alone. Standardize transition risks over the observed transition population using pre-24 onset severity and pre-24 observation variables. Do not interpret a transition estimate as the effect of making a patient change state: transition selection, treatment, death, and discharge remain unresolved.

If a transition has sparse events, report counts and refrain from fitting an unstable contrast. That is an inconclusive feasibility result, not permission to merge states after seeing outcomes.

## 8. Baseline versus learned alternative

The transparent baseline remains primary:

- Inputs: fixed L-specific summaries of creatinine peak and pre-window level/slope, AKI stage, urine rate and observed-bin count, item-level recorded input/output composition, weight, exploratory CVP, RRT/support summaries, ICU timing, non-renal observation density, and missingness/unknown indicators.
- Target: Y_L,7d at each landmark; for the trajectory branch, the 48-hour forward outcome by the predeclared transition state.
- Fit: standardized logistic risk with bounded restricted cubic splines or prespecified linear terms; patient-level cluster bootstrap; report risk differences, calibration, Brier score and uncertainty.
- Split: for any predictive fit, subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test, with every stay of a subject in one split. The primary descriptive/standardized contrast must not be selected on the test set.
- Budget: CPU tabular fit within the parent solver envelope (16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, 28,800 seconds maximum); the exact runtime is unmeasured.

The substantive learned alternative uses the same question and exact pre-landmark sources:

- Input sequence: 6-hour bins from t0 through each L, containing creatinine, primary-set urine amount/rate and observation mask, input/output recorded-volume components, weight, CVP, RRT/support indicators, non-renal observation counts, and missingness channels.
- Target: Y_L,7d and, in a separate prespecified head, the state/transition risk at L. The model must not see post-L values or future outcome proxies.
- Evaluation: held-out calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, AUROC, and held-out standardized discordant-versus-concordant and persistent-versus-resolving risk contrasts, all with bootstrap uncertainty. Compare against the fixed-summary baseline on the same test patients.
- Information gained: a sequence can reveal whether ordering, persistence, and recovery timing matter beyond a fixed 24-hour summary. It cannot separate residual severity from mechanism and must not replace the interpretable state analysis.
- Resource estimate: parent planning estimate was 4–16 CPU cores, 16–64 GB RAM, 1–6 hours for a larger CPU sequence fit, or one allocated A100 for 1–3 hours if batching is the bottleneck. These are unverified estimates; GPU allocation is optional and no GPU was used for this discovery audit.

Selection: retain the baseline as the primary scientific deliverable. Fit the learned alternative only if the dynamic states have adequate support and repeated measurements; defer it if not. Revisit the learned branch only for a replicated trajectory interaction or improved calibration that changes interpretation, not a small AUROC gain. This is a scientific adequacy decision, not a CPU/GPU preference.

## 9. Falsification and interpretation

Supportive result:

- the 48-hour parent contrast is clinically nontrivial and directionally consistent at 24/48/72 hours;
- the contrast is not confined to a single narrow severity stratum or dense-recording stratum;
- persistent discordance has greater risk than resolving discordance with adequate cells;
- the unknown state is not so large or high-risk that complete-case interpretation is untenable; and
- component-specific estimates do not reveal that the binary result is only an artifact of one contaminated or poorly observed component.

This would justify prospective validation of enhanced renal monitoring and an adjudicated study with measured GFR, cystatin C, biomarkers, complete urine collection, total-body fluid measures, treatment intent, and ward-RRT ascertainment. It would remain a prognostic association.

Adverse result:

- the contrast disappears after the landmark curve, severity strata, or recording-opportunity/process checks;
- it reverses across landmarks or is driven by unknown/missing patients;
- a documentation-density process signal is comparable to the kidney contrast; or
- only a mixed/contaminated urine set produces the signal.

This would weaken the claim that creatinine-only recovery identifies a reproducible clinical discordance and redirect work toward measurement validation or other recovery markers. It would not prove creatinine is valid, that fluid management is harmless, or that treatment has no effect.

Inconclusive result:

- sparse transition cells, wide intervals spanning clinically meaningful harm, unstable estimates across reasonable windows, large unknown states, or incomplete ascertainment of hospital outcomes.

This would mean MIMIC cannot resolve the question. It would justify a measurement/adjudication or external-validation study, not a favorable conclusion. A small predictive improvement from the learned model is also inconclusive for clinical value unless it reveals a reproducible trajectory distinction.

Computationally checkable claims are: exact cohort construction, temporal exclusion, joins, item filters, state counts, missingness/opportunity counts, risk estimates, uncertainty, held-out predictive metrics, and whether conclusions match outputs. Clinical adjudication is required for true kidney function, complete urine collection, fluid status, treatment intent, mechanism, causal effects, and ward-only RRT. Automatic verification cannot establish those claims.

## 10. Unavailable evidence and non-reproduction boundaries

This is a bounded MIMIC-IV adaptation, not a reproduction of any cited paper. The configured snapshot lacks cystatin C, measured GFR/iohexol, muscle mass, tubular-injury biomarkers, true total-body volume, insensible losses, medication/fluid intent, complete bedside collection adjudication, clinician goals of care, and reliable ward-only RRT capture. `CVP` is exploratory and not a fluid-status gold standard. The dynamic landmark design makes selection visible; it does not solve informative discharge/death or treatment-dependent observation.

The parent’s three demonstrations and other seed directions are not used as topic constraints. The structured sequence idea is used only as a baseline comparison for ordered trajectories; no unavailable modality or cohort is claimed.

## 12. Alternatives not chosen and revision decision

- **Unchanged fixed 48-hour analysis only:** rejected as the sole design because it cannot tell whether a result is specific to the survivor/ICU selection or to dense recording. It remains the primary comparator.
- **Single complex sequence model:** deferred because better prediction alone would not resolve residual severity or measurement error. The sequence is retained only as a secondary, same-question test of ordered/persistent information.
- **Causal treatment-effect or inverse-probability intervention analysis:** not selected because treatment intent, complete fluid exposure, ward RRT, and informative discharge/death are unavailable. A causal interpretation would require additional data and expert review.
- **A hard negative-control clinical outcome:** not selected because no MIMIC outcome here is credibly independent of acute severity and surveillance. The process-only documentation analysis is explicitly diagnostic, not a validated negative control.
- **Changing the primary population to all AKI-onset patients with imputation of later state:** rejected because death/discharge before the landmark makes a later recovery state undefined; imputation would hide the very selection uncertainty under study.

Evidence to revisit the deferred branches: a validated measurement/adjudication subset with complete urine and fluid collection, cystatin C/measured GFR or biomarkers, treatment intent and ward RRT; or an external cohort with prospective landmark follow-up. Evidence to abandon the direction: a well-powered, observation-complete validation showing no reproducible component or trajectory contrast across landmarks and severity strata.
