# HCC post-TACE trajectory: episode-3 transportability and observation-process child

Status: proposed Harbor experiment; no full study or model fit has been executed. This is a substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. It preserves the parent's population, time zero, outcome window, and primary incremental-prognostic estimand, while resolving the remaining uncertainty about who has an observed day-0–7 trajectory and whether an assay/name signal transports across calendar and department regimes.

## Scientific opening, hypothesis, and advance

The established opening is that a short-lived post-TACE aminotransferase injury may differ from a coordinated loss of hepatic synthetic/renal reserve. The parent already made the signal testable with exact HCC bindings, assay-aware transformations, utilization controls, endpoint concordance and a regularized-versus-state-space comparison. The unresolved rival is now explicit: requiring baseline plus a day-3–7 laboratory measurement may select people who remain in care, are readmitted early, or are managed by departments/eras with denser testing. A second rival is transport failure: names, panels and local assay conventions can change over calendar time or department, making a learned trajectory appear portable only inside its source regime.

Primary hypothesis (unchanged): among adult HCC patients with a first observed TACE and no documented pre/index decompensation, a 0–7-day trajectory dominated by worsening synthetic/renal reserve predicts a new post-discharge decompensation-coded admission during days 8–90 beyond baseline risk. An isolated AST/ALT flare is the leading substantive rival. The primary estimand remains the paired chronological-test-set incremental prognostic performance of the dynamic model versus the same baseline-only model for that outcome; it is not a treatment effect, causal effect, mechanism, or action threshold.

The new episode asks whether that same incremental signal is transportable and selection-robust. A supportive result would show that the signal is present in the full eligible population through a prespecified missingness-aware model, remains after observation-process adjustment, and has compatible performance/calibration in held-out calendar and department regimes. This would advance the parent from “a potentially useful trajectory in observed patients” toward evidence that the result is not merely a local documentation practice. It would still not establish external validity, causal TACE harm, biological mechanism, or clinical utility without adjudication and prospective validation. An adverse result would localize the claim to an observed-care regime or identify trajectory availability as the dominant explanation; that is a clinically useful boundary, not a failed computation.

## Population, time zero, temporal boundaries, and outcomes

Use only HCC. Identify each patient's first procedure row whose procedure contains TACE, transarterial chemoembolization, transcatheter hepatic artery embolization, or arterial chemoembolization, with a parseable start time and a matching encounter. Require age >=18 and an HCC diagnosis (at minimum hepatocellular carcinoma or malignant neoplasm of the liver) before/on the index encounter. Exclude prior/index high-specificity decompensation terms: ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, gastrointestinal bleeding, esophageal and gastric fundal varices, hepatorenal syndrome, acute liver failure, liver failure, spontaneous peritonitis, or jaundice; report broader cirrhosis-decompensation flags separately. Missing labs, missing discharge, or absent day-3–7 measurements do not exclude an index patient from the primary analysis.

Time zero is the earliest qualifying surgery start time. Preserve the parent's complete-follow-up rule: include only index dates with a verifiable 90-day source observation horizon, using 2025-10-03 or a recomputed equivalent cutoff after checking source maxima. Baseline is the closest assay-specific quantitative result in [-14,0] days; [-30,0] is a sensitivity window. Dynamic information is restricted to [0,7] days and raw timestamps are retained. The primary event is the first distinct encounter after index discharge time whose admission time is in [8,90] days and whose diagnosis contains a prespecified high-specificity component. Missing discharge is reported and separately analyzed; it is never silently changed into an admission or used to redefine time zero.

Report the composite plus ascites, encephalopathy, bleeding, renal/hepatic failure and jaundice components; report diagnosis-only, clinical-document-confirmed, discordant and missing-document strata. Any admission and repeat TACE are non-equivalent secondary outcomes. Do not infer planned/unplanned status.

The primary estimand is unchanged: on the chronological test set, compare baseline-only and dynamic predictions for the primary composite using paired differences in Brier score, calibration, discrimination and decision-curve net benefit. The target population is all eligible index patients, not only those with a complete trajectory. The dynamic prediction is therefore defined under observed-data/missingness handling; the complete-trajectory subgroup is a prespecified transportability/selection estimand, not the primary population.

## Measurement and endpoint audit

Labs have no unit column. Before outcome analysis, freeze a training-only assay dictionary from laboratory-test values, including raw-name variants, qualitative values, specimen type, duplicate rate, timestamps, calendar era and department distribution. Parse only numeric quantitative results. Candidate assay concepts are total/direct bilirubin, albumin, platelets, PT/INR or PT ratio, creatinine, sodium, AST, ALT and AFP; presence and comparability must be demonstrated. Within each assay/name regime, use training-set standardization, patient-centered changes or ranks; do not pool raw thresholds or claim valid ALBI/MELD/Child-Pugh without expert unit verification. Map observations to [0,1–2,3–4,5–7] day bins with a deterministic nearest-time tie rule, but retain event-level timestamps and name/regime labels.

Create a data-availability record for every eligible index: baseline availability, any day-0–7 lab, at least one day-3–7 lab, assay-specific bin coverage, number of lab timestamps, distinct assays, first-lab lag, last observed day, encounters with labs, and whether the index admission or a day-8–90 admission generated the observations. The last item is descriptive only: all features must stop at day 7 and later outcome-related observation is never used. Also record encounter, admission, department, procedure, medication, order and examination counts in pre-index and day-0–7 windows. Define availability strata before outcome inspection: A0 no baseline; A1 baseline but no day-3–7 lab; A2 baseline plus day-3–7 lab; and a finer predeclared quartile/low-medium-high observation-density partition within calendar/departments when counts permit. Never condition the primary result on A2 alone.

The primary endpoint is a transparent diagnosis-name lexical rule. The document sensitivity requires the same component in the listed clinical_documents fields. Produce a blinded clinician adjudication packet sampled across availability, calendar and department strata to label true new/worsening decompensation, planned status, competing illness/HCC progression, TACE injury and text validity. Adjudication is essential for clinical truth and cannot be replaced by model performance.

## Baseline comparator and substantive alternative

B0 is the parent's L2-regularized logistic regression using time-zero age, sex, department, HCC/cirrhosis/portal-vein-thrombus/prior-decompensation flags, procedure context, pre-index utilization, baseline assay-specific values and missingness. A no-lab B0 is a sensitivity comparator. B1 is the same L2 model plus prespecified [0,7] assay deltas, robust slopes, extrema, last values, observation counts, first-lab lag and separate AST/ALT-flare blocks. B0 and B1 are fit and scored on the same all-eligible index population through the missingness strategy below.

The substantive alternative is a missingness-aware joint outcome/observation model, not a complexity upgrade for its own sake. Let R_{ijb} indicate whether assay j is observed in bin b for patient i, and Y_i the days-8–90 composite. Model the latent standardized reserve and transient-injury factors with the same two-factor irregular-time measurement model as the parent, while jointly modeling the observation intensity R and its bin-level count using pre-index severity, department, calendar era, discharge/encounter context and the latent current state. Include department- and calendar-era random intercepts/slopes for the observation and outcome heads, with partial pooling; assay/name-regime-specific loadings and noise are estimated in training data. The outcome head predicts Y from the day-7 posterior state plus the same baseline covariates. Use a prespecified pattern-mixture sensitivity parameter for nonrandom missingness, varying the association between latent state and unobserved measurement probability over a clinically stated range. Do not use day-8–90 observations.

This alternative adds information B1 loses: it estimates uncertainty about the latent trajectory when measurements are irregular, separates reserve from transient enzyme injury, and estimates how much of apparent performance is explained by who gets measured. Partial pooling can reveal whether loadings and calibration are stable across departments/eras rather than silently averaging incompatible regimes. Risks are stronger assumptions (state evolution, missingness model and exchangeability within department/era), weak identifiability in sparse strata, convergence failure, and sensitivity to unresolved units/name mapping. Latent factors remain representations, not biological mechanisms. A neural sequence model remains deferred because it would add capacity without directly identifying selection or assay transportability.

Alternatives were specified at comparable detail before selection:

1. L2 B0/B1: exact inputs are baseline plus fixed-bin trajectory summaries, missingness and utilization controls; fit target is Y_i; train/validation/test is chronological; paired Brier, calibration, discrimination, net benefit and bootstrap uncertainty are required. It is the transparent primary comparator and may be adequate if the signal is robust.
2. Joint hierarchical observation/outcome state-space model: exact inputs are the same labs/timestamps plus R/count process, department, calendar era and baseline utilization; fit targets are Y_i and the observation process; the same chronological split plus held-out department/era evaluations are required; report predictive metrics, calibration, state/loadings stability, observation-process contribution and sensitivity analysis. It is selected for this episode because the unresolved scientific question is selection and transport, which B1 cannot represent.
3. Neural sequence model: exact inputs would be irregular assay/name/time tokens and utilization context, target Y_i, with temporal and department holdouts; it is deferred because it cannot by itself distinguish missing-not-at-random observation from learned proxy behavior, and its added capacity is not justified before the hierarchical model establishes whether a substantive residual transport problem remains.

Tune penalties, state dimension, random-effect shrinkage and sensitivity-grid presentation on train/validation only. If a department is too sparse for a standalone holdout, retain it in pooled testing and report that transport contrast as inconclusive rather than merging after seeing results.

Primary analysis: all eligible patients, with B0 and the joint model producing a risk for each patient. Missing baseline or trajectory values are represented by the declared observation model and uncertainty; no complete-case primary analysis. Compare paired test-set B0 versus dynamic predictions in Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, ROC-AUC/PR-AUC, decision-curve net benefit and clinically prespecified high-sensitivity operating point. Use patient-cluster bootstrap 2,000 replicates or the maximum permitted bounded equivalent, stratified only as a sensitivity analysis, and report event count, availability strata, effective sample size and 95% intervals. Report model convergence and state/loadings uncertainty.

Selection diagnostics: describe A0/A1/A2 flow and compare baseline covariates, outcome rate, departments, calendar eras, early readmission and observation density across strata. Estimate an inverse-observation-probability weighted complete-trajectory contrast only as a secondary sensitivity analysis, with weights fitted in train/validation from variables available by day 0 (and a prespecified stabilized truncation); do not present it as a cure for unmeasured MNAR. The primary joint model’s observation head is the main missingness-aware estimand. If A2 is rare or positivity fails, report this as a transportability limitation.

## Falsification and interpretation

Mandatory negative controls are: within-patient timestamp permutation preserving values and counts; a matched pre-index pseudo-window; measurement-only prediction from counts/lag/department/era without lab values; AST/ALT-only versus reserve-panel-only blocks; any-admission and repeat-TACE outcomes; and a “future observation” audit that proves no post-day-7 feature is present. Add a regime-shuffle control that permutes department/era labels within the training design: if apparent transport heterogeneity disappears under the shuffle, it may be a regime-label artifact; if the primary signal survives timestamp permutation or is reproduced in the pre-index window, ordering/timing or utilization is the stronger explanation.

Support requires: (i) a non-negligible paired dynamic improvement on the untouched chronological test in the all-eligible analysis; (ii) calibrated performance and uncertainty that are not confined to A2 complete-trajectory patients; (iii) persistence after observation controls and the MNAR sensitivity range; (iv) no material timestamp-permutation or pre-index reproduction; (v) assay/name mappings and loadings that are stable enough across held-out eras/departments, with no severe calibration drift; and (vi) at least directionally concordant document/component results. This supports transportable incremental prognostic value, not mechanism or causality.

Adverse evidence is a dynamic gain that disappears after observation-process adjustment; large A2-versus-non-A2 selection differences with no positivity; a gain confined to one department/era or to one unmapped/local assay; calibration failure in held-out regimes; timestamp-permutation or pre-index reproduction; count-only dominance; AST/ALT-only dominance; or endpoint/document discordance. These results would revise the claim to “local observed-care association,” prioritize standardized assay capture or adjudication, or abandon the trajectory direction if no regime has adequate support.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few events in availability/department/era cells, non-identifiable observation parameters, unstable state-space convergence, wide paired intervals, unresolved units, high unmapped assay names, missing discharge, or insufficient common support. Do not interpret an imprecise null as refutation. Continue only if the primary computation and enough regime support are complete; revise to a narrower regime if only a prespecified regime is estimable; defer if adjudication or assay verification is missing; abandon only if a well-powered, adequately observed test repeatedly fails after the negative controls.

Computationally checkable outputs are source hashes/headers, join and duplicate audits, cohort flow, timestamp bounds, assay dictionary and unmapped rates, availability/selection tables, split integrity, predictions, metrics, calibration, paired intervals, regime contrasts, convergence, negative-control behavior and absence of post-day-7 leakage. Clinical adjudication is required for true/worsening decompensation, planned status, competing illness and text validity. Expert assay/unit review, imaging/tumor burden, procedural intensity, death linkage, external validation and prospective surveillance are required for stronger clinical or causal conclusions.
