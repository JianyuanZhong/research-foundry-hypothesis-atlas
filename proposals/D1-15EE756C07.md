# Channel-disjoint recognition: does a within-patient ICU lab-draw-intensity drop mark unmet monitoring need, or is the parent's recognition-mode test circular?

Lead working proposal — eICU 2.0 island, **episode 21**. Operation: **evolve** of true parent
`[prior hypothesis]` (valid, delivery-complete, mu 32.06; episode-20 evolve of
`[prior hypothesis]`; seed origin `[starting question]` = "Reduced testing and
subsequent deterioration"). Status: **design owned and frozen by the Lead for compilation**.
No reference/solver solution was executed at discovery — this is design work, not a solved result.

## 1. Unresolved question and why it matters

The branch (episodes 12–20, seed eicu-08) established a *severity-adjusted, within-patient* relation
between the change in clinical-panel lab-draw intensity and subsequent organ-support escalation /
failure-to-rescue, with a non-monotone (inverted-U) exposure–response and hospital random-slope +
leave-one-hospital-out transport as the between-hospital rival test. The episode-20 leader
(`854bad5e`) converted the branch's #1 limit into a directional test by stratifying the outcome into
**recognition modes** — escalation that was *triggered* by an earlier charted abnormality vs
*unheralded* escalation.

**The unresolved threat (this episode's target).** In the parent, "unheralded" is operationalised
from the SAME measurement channel that defines the exposure (lab draws / charted labs). A patient
whose lab-draw rate dropped is, by construction, more likely to have no charted lab "trigger" before
escalation. H1 ("harmful effect of a within-patient draw-rate drop concentrates in unheralded
escalation") can therefore be partly true *by construction* — the recognition label is entangled
with the exposure. This is a **mechanism-defining circularity**, not a cosmetic flaw: it makes the
parent's most novel statistic non-falsifiable in the direction it claims. Clinically the question is
whether under-monitoring (fewer draws while the patient is still unstable) leaves deterioration
unnoticed until organ-support escalation is required — a decision-relevant monitoring question.

## 2. Hypothesis being tested vs evidence already supported

- **Already supported (do not re-claim as novel):** order presence/timing carries survival signal
  ([K1] Agniel 2018, 272 tests / 669,452 patients); within-patient panel draw-rate change is
  associated with later deterioration after severity adjustment, non-monotone (branch probe).
- **Unresolved claim the child tests (falsifiable):** the *association* between a within-patient
  clinical-panel draw-rate drop and escalation survives when recognition is labelled on a
  **channel-disjoint** modality (vital-sign/nurse-charting surveillance), and is concentrated where
  the vital channel did NOT flag instability ("silent" deterioration). If the parent effect was
  circular, the channel-disjoint differential is null or reverses.

- **Leading claim (H1):** a within-patient drop in lab-draw intensity marks unmet monitoring need →
  harm concentrates in escalations with *adequate vital-sign surveillance but no lab confirmation*
  (the channel H1 says was missing).
- **Strongest rival (R2, practice/measurement fingerprint):** the association is a draw-practice /
  record-density artifact → the channel-disjoint recognition differential is null, and the effect
  vanishes under record-density adjustment and hospital random slopes.
- **Additional rivals:** R1 residual severity/indication (adjust hour-24 APACHE-IVa + trajectory);
  R3 disposition/EOL artifacts (exclude active `carePlanEOL`, maximal support); R4 reverse ordering
  (deterioration causes the draw drop within the window → enforce strict temporal ordering + washout
  + hour-12 placebo landmark).

## 3. Design (frozen)

- **Population / landmark:** adult ICU stays alive at hour 24 with ≥1 clinical-panel lab in (0,12]
  and (12,24], no active `carePlanEOL`, not on maximal support at hour 24 (rescue-eligible). Keep the
  parent population verbatim.
- **Exposure:** within-patient clinical-panel lab-draw-rate ratio `logR` between (0,12] and (12,24]
  (pre-specified panel labname strings; total-draw contrast as secondary).
- **Outcome:** organ-support escalation in (24,48]h (new vasopressor infusion or mechanical
  ventilation), stratified by **channel-disjoint recognition**:
  - `VITALS_FLAG` = a pre-specified abnormal vital sign (e.g. MAP/HR/SpO2 threshold) charted within
    4h before escalation on the periodic-vital channel;
  - `SILENT` = no such vital-channel flag (escalation neither lab- nor vital-heralded on the primary
    vital channel).
- **Primary statistic:** recognition-mode differential of the logR odds ratio,
  `OR(logR | SILENT) / OR(logR | VITALS_FLAG) = exp(-delta)`, inside a hospital random-intercept
  model, adjusted for hour-24 APACHE-IVa severity and RCS(logR).
- **Baseline vs alternative (method comparison):** baseline = fixed-effect cause-specific logistic
  with linear `logR`; substantive alternative = within-patient marked point-process **draw-deficit**
  model (physiology-adjusted expected-vs-observed draw hazard), because a single linear `logR` cannot
  separate a within-patient deficit from a between-hospital level shift. Compare by dAUROC,
  calibration slope, O:E ratio, Brier, net benefit, under leave-one-hospital-out transport.
- **Falsification / decision rules (pre-specified):**
  - **Supportive (H1):** delta>0 with 95% CI excluding 0 AND the VITALS_FLAG stratum null
    (CI includes 1); alternative ≥ baseline on net benefit under LOHO.
  - **Adverse/refuting (R2):** delta ≈ 0 (CI includes 0) AND effect attenuates to null after
    record-density adjustment / hospital random slope.
  - **Inconclusive:** CI too wide / strata too sparse → report as unresolved, not as support.
- **Negative controls:** hour-12 placebo landmark; a "stable-subgroup" negative control where no
  deterioration is plausible.
