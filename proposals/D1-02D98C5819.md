# Can observed laboratory metadata explain hospital heterogeneity in the documented hypotension–creatinine confirmation process?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The parent established whether a later recorded creatinine confirmation is portable across ICUs. Its remaining limitation is endpoint validity: a hospital spread after repeat-test opportunity is modeled may still combine patient biology, assay/interface differences, timestamp/revision behavior, and missing testing. eICU cannot identify true assay error or biological kidney injury, but it can support a bounded, falsifiable separation test.

The future solver must newly produce:

1. The parent’s standardized hospital 90th-minus-10th percentile spread in the 48-hour cumulative incidence (CIF) of a distinct later qualifying recorded creatinine confirmation, H_all.
2. The same spread after standardizing on observable laboratory metadata at the first-rise landmark and prior-test history, H_M.
3. Attenuation A_M = H_all - H_M, with joint uncertainty, and H_M/H_all only when the denominator interval excludes zero.
4. A secondary metadata-complete confirmation endpoint and an ordered decomposition into repeat-test opportunity, metadata availability, confirmation after repeat, and unit exit.

Completion requires fitted estimates, 95% hospital-cluster intervals, supported-hospital counts, missingness/category audits, convergence and shrinkage diagnostics, held-out-hospital transport, and all prespecified endpoint, timestamp and boundary sensitivities. Discovery has fitted nothing and claims no result.

This is a substantive change: it tests whether the hospital signal survives adjustment for the only available measurement-process proxies. Large attenuation would make the result actionable as an interface/metadata harmonization or endpoint-restriction problem; a residual spread would show that repeat-testing and observed laboratory metadata do not make the recorded endpoint site-neutral. Neither identifies assay bias, true AKI, or treatment effect.

## Evidence-supported and unresolved claims

The inspected evidence establishes only that the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative observations, creatinine laboratory rows, laboratory measurement-system/interface fields, revision offsets, patient disposition, hospital identifiers, named infusion rows and Apache descriptors. The linked table schemas and a bounded read-only field probe support availability; the probe was not a prevalence estimate and did not fit a model.

The strongest supported claim is that eICU can represent a documented creatinine-record process with ICU-relative time and a strict unit-discharge boundary. The unresolved claim is:

> After standardizing case mix, hypotension history, prior testing opportunity and observable laboratory metadata, the hospital spread in later recorded creatinine confirmation remains clinically material.

Primary hypothesis H_sep: among supported development hospitals, H_M is at least 5 percentage points, its two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval excludes zero, and H_M is at least 50% of H_all when the parent spread has a nonzero interval. These are endpoint-portability thresholds, not claims about benefit.

The competing adverse hypothesis is that the spread attenuates after repeat-test and metadata standardization, or appears only in the metadata-complete restriction. That supports a surveillance/interface explanation. Any conclusion remains about heterogeneity in an observed documentation process; it cannot distinguish renal injury from hemoconcentration, assay variation, revision artifact, undocumented testing or case mix.

The first rise is the first post-landmark row at least 0.3 mg/dL above baseline; early qualifying rises through 360 are excluded.

A confirmed recorded rise is a distinct labid result at s>=r+360, value at least baseline+0.3, and s<=2880 and s<U when applicable. Same-minute rows, exact duplicates, revisions of the same labid, and results before the six-hour delay cannot confirm. The primary clock is labresultoffset; repeat with labresultrevisedoffset where present. This is a persistence-of-record state, not KDIGO AKI.

The observable measurement regime M, frozen at the first-rise transition, comprises lab.labtypeid category for baseline/first-rise and prior tests; normalized labmeasurenamesystem and labmeasurenameinterface category; metadata-complete/conflicting indicators; presence of labresultrevisedoffset and numeric revision lag when available; and prior test count and time since the previous qualifying creatinine. M is an interface/revision proxy, not an assay or specimen identity. Confirmation-row metadata are not predictors of confirmation; they define a secondary endpoint/process state.

For the secondary metadata-complete endpoint, both first-rise and repeat rows must have nonblank, nonconflicting metadata in supported frozen categories. A stricter sensitivity requires the first-rise and repeat rows to share normalized system/interface and labtypeid when support permits. Sparse restrictions are inconclusive, not forced into a result.

Represent no qualifying test, qualifying test without rise, first rise, repeat qualifying test, metadata-complete repeat, confirmed rise, recorded unit death, alive unit exit and unknown-status censoring. The six-hour delay and strict t<U rule are structural.

## Primary estimands

For each supported development hospital h, let q_all,h be the 48-hour CIF of confirmed recorded rise after first rise, with recorded death and alive exit competing and unknown-status exit censored. Let q_M,h be the same endpoint standardized to the development first-rise distribution including M and the parent covariates: age, sex, ethnicity, admission source/unit type, baseline and first-rise creatinine, low-MAP features, documented pressor timing, Apache descriptors, and hospital bed/teaching/region context without hospital identity.

For hospitals with at least 50 first-rise stays and 10 confirmed events under the applicable endpoint, use the same supported-hospital rule for both:
H_all = Q90(q_all,h) - Q10(q_all,h)
H_M = Q90(q_M,h) - Q10(q_M,h).
Report A_M = H_all-H_M, joint bootstrap intervals, and H_M/H_all only when interpretable. Also report spreads for repeat-test opportunity, metadata-complete repeat and confirmation conditional on a repeat, without multiplying unstable ratios. Fewer than 10 supported hospitals makes the separation claim inconclusive.

Retain the parent’s standardized burden P75-versus-P25 CIF contrast by documented pressor timing for any-rise and confirmed-rise endpoints as secondary. Infusion timing is a record offset, not validated administration, dose, intent or causal exposure.

## Matched methods

The transparent additive baseline fits regularized cause-specific pooled-logistic models on identical 30-minute intervals, risk sets, covariates, metadata inputs, hospital split, standardization distribution and uncertainty procedure. It has separate repeat, metadata-complete-repeat, confirmation, death, alive-exit and unknown-status processes, linear/spline elapsed time, common slopes and transition-specific hospital random intercepts. Nested fits are B0 case mix/common time with no hospital heterogeneity; B1 adds hospital effects and produces H_all; B2 adds prior-test opportunity and repeat-process terms; B3 adds prespecified M terms and produces H_M; and B4 removes hospital-by-time slopes as a stability sensitivity. No post-rise value or confirmation-row metadata is a predictor.

The substantive alternative is an ordered piecewise-exponential multi-state model on the identical inputs, split, standardization distribution and bootstrap. It separates first-rise to repeat-test opportunity, metadata-complete repeat availability, repeat-to-confirmation, unit death, alive exit and unknown-status censoring, with transition-specific shrunken hospital effects. It can show whether attenuation occurs at testing opportunity, metadata availability, or confirmation after a metadata-observed repeat. Agreement on H_M and A_M is robustness; disagreement is model uncertainty and must be reported.

A learned longitudinal model is deferred, not prohibited. Revisit only if both models show reproducible shape-specific held-out miscalibration and a learned model can estimate the identical spreads, attenuation and process states without post-outcome metadata.

## Split, uncertainty, transport and falsification

Hash hospitalid deterministically into 80% development and 20% held-out hospitals before cohort summaries, metadata pooling, imputation, fitting or thresholds. Keep any uniquepid connected hospital component together and report coverage loss. Fit and standardize only in development hospitals. Apply frozen models to held-out hospitals without refitting, local recalibration or tuning; report observed-versus-predicted confirmation, repeat and metadata-complete curves, calibration and Brier scores. Transport is inconclusive with fewer than 10 hospitals, inadequate risk-set/event support, missing category overlap or calibration failure.

Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates for H_all, H_M, A_M, process spreads, model differences and transport summaries, with a uniquepid-clustered sensitivity. Report category support, missingness, effective sample size, convergence, shrinkage, overlap and observed/predicted event counts. Repeat with revised lab clock, 3-hour/12-hour gaps, +0.3 versus 1.5-times-baseline where calculable, raw versus bridged MAP, non-invasive MAP, unit-status/location, boundary and agent-map sensitivities.

Supportive evidence requires H_M>=5 percentage points, its interval excluding zero, H_M/H_all>=0.5 when valid, and qualitative replication by both models. This supports residual heterogeneity in a documented process after observed metadata adjustment—not assay heterogeneity, biological AKI or a treatment recommendation.

Adverse evidence is a null/reversed H_M, attenuation below 50%, disappearance after repeat opportunity or metadata-complete restriction, or instability across clocks, gap, boundary, MAP channel, status and support sensitivities. A large H_all with small H_M supports interface or ascertainment calibration as the actionable next step. A large spread only in the metadata-complete endpoint indicates selection by observability, not assay validity.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse metadata categories/events, extreme weights, failed convergence, unstable intervals, poor held-out transport, strong missing-status dependence, or model disagreement. Clinical adjudication is required before calling the endpoint KDIGO AKI or changing testing/treatment policy.
