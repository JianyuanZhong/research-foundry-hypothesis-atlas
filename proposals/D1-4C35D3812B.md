## Assay-specific sensitivity audits without invented units

For sensitivity only, and separately within each exact assay and each pre-origin training fold, create two dimensionless transformations. Never pool albumin and bilirubin and never use future rows, missing-Y rows as pseudo-outcomes, or external clinical constants.

* Rank-normal audit: form the empirical CDF from valid training values, use deterministic midranks for ties, and map to `z = Phi^-1(clip(F(value), 0.5/n, 1 - 0.5/n))`, where n is the training count and Phi is the standard normal CDF. Use one fixed finite-tail convention. If support is too small under a declared threshold, retain raw scale and mark this audit `inconclusive`.
* Robust within-assay audit: use the training median and IQR and transform `asinh((value - median)/IQR)`. If IQR is zero or undefined, mark this audit unavailable/inconclusive; do not substitute a different scale after seeing results.

Fit all maps and preprocessing within the training fold only and freeze them before applying to future events. Report raw, rank-normal, and robust-audit P increment direction and declared uncertainty side by side. Do not select the most favorable scale, average scales, or describe a transformed value as normal/abnormal, hepatic function, or clinical severity. A material direction disagreement, or an audit that cannot be estimated, makes the relevant block `selection_sensitive` or `inconclusive`, not supportive.

The transformed analyses are robustness diagnostics and do not replace the primary raw-scale estimand. They must not alter the all-BP missing-Y bounds, whose range is defined on the raw assay-specific recorded scale.


The observation layer must remain outcome-blind: no Y value, Y label, post-event lab content, examination narrative, or document narrative may be used to define an opportunity category, support gate, transformation, or observation model. The code must materialize BP rows and the opportunity ledger before joining post-event lab values.

Fit two diagnostics, never primary weights or imputations.

* Calendar-membership diagnostic: on pre-Y variables B, P, age, sex, P-lead hours, TACE2-to-repeat days, and arm, fit training-only prediction of future calendar membership. Report future propensity range, fraction outside training range, normalized training transport ESS overall/by arm, maximum normalized weight, and same-arm nearest-neighbor distance relative to the training 95th percentile.
* Observation diagnostic: on the same pre-Y variables plus the ascertainment indicators and calendar block, fit training-only prediction of `Y_observed`. Report future observed fraction and Wilson interval, predicted probability range, Brier score, inverse-observation-probability ESS among observed rows, and pre-Y observed-versus-missing SMDs. Do not call this MAR evidence, imputation, or a primary-analysis weight.

Differential observation bounds must be reported for every assay, origin, block, and arm on all BP-eligible rows. The following are bounds under a declared recorded-scale training-range scenario, not a recovery of missing outcomes.

1. Fit M0/M1 only on observed finite complete pre-origin triplets. Freeze finite held-out predictions `p0_i` and `p1_i` for every future BP row. If a required prediction is nonfinite, stop the bound for that block as computationally invalid; never drop that row.
2. Let `N` be all BP-eligible rows in the block and `N_a` all BP-eligible rows in arm a. Use exactly N or N_a as the denominator, including missing-Y rows. Never use complete-triplet counts for these bounds.
3. Define `L_train = min(Y_train)` and `U_train = max(Y_train)` over the exact finite training parser before inspecting future Y. If the training range is empty or degenerate, mark the temporal bound `unsupported`. If any observed future Y lies outside `[L_train,U_train]`, mark it `unsupported` and do not widen the range with future outcomes. A full-source range can be printed only as a separately labeled descriptive check. If no future Y is observed, the training observed-Y mean can be a training-derived tipping coordinate only.
4. For observed rows use `e0_i=(Y_i-p0_i)^2`, `e1_i=(Y_i-p1_i)^2`, and `d_i=e0_i-e1_i`. For missing row i and model j use

```
e_j,min = (clip(p_j,L_train,U_train)-p_j)^2
e_j,max = max((L_train-p_j)^2,(U_train-p_j)^2)
```

5. Compute M0-minus-M1 contrast using the affine identity

```
d_i(y) = (y-p0_i)^2 - (y-p1_i)^2
       = 2*y*(p1_i-p0_i) + p0_i^2-p1_i^2.
```

For each missing row, contrast endpoints are `min(d_i(L_train),d_i(U_train))` and `max(d_i(L_train),d_i(U_train))`. Thus

```
Delta_lower = (sum_observed d_i + sum_missing min{d_i(L),d_i(U)}) / N
Delta_upper = (sum_observed d_i + sum_missing max{d_i(L),d_i(U)}) / N
```

and arm-specific versions use only arm-a rows and denominator N_a. Report N, observed, missing, missing fraction, observed contribution, missing endpoint contribution, lower/upper bounds, and three deterministic scenarios: all missing Y=L, all missing Y=U, and each missing row at its least-favorable endpoint for the lower M0-minus-M1 contrast (`L` when `2(p1_i-p0_i) >= 0`, otherwise `U`).

For each model separately, divide its SSE endpoints by N or N_a to obtain MSE endpoints. The conservative unstandardized RMSE-difference enclosure is

```
[sqrt(MSE0_lower) - sqrt(MSE1_upper),
 sqrt(MSE0_upper) - sqrt(MSE1_lower)]
```

Never call this enclosure sharp and never standardize it by an unidentified test SD. If the observed test-Y SD is nonfinite or zero, the standardized gain and its CI are non-estimable while valid unstandardized bounds may still be emitted.

6. Run the deterministic common-displacement stress test on every declared bound. Set all missing outcomes simultaneously to

```
y_i(delta) = clip(m_ref + delta, L_train, U_train)
```

where m_ref is the observed future-Y mean if any future Y exists, otherwise the training observed-Y mean explicitly labeled training-derived. Evaluate

```
Delta(delta) = (sum_observed d_i + sum_missing d_i(y_i(delta))) / N
```

or N_a for arm-specific results. This is continuous piecewise linear, not quadratic. Breakpoints are `L_train-m_ref` and `U_train-m_ref`; on each segment use slope `sum_missing,unclipped 2(p1_i-p0_i)/N` (or N_a), solve the linear zero equation, retain roots only within the segment, and verify every root by deterministic endpoint evaluation. Report all roots in `[L_train-m_ref,U_train-m_ref]`, choose the smallest absolute displacement with ties by smallest delta, and report zero if Delta(0)=0. If no root exists, label the common-displacement scenario sign-robust. Also report whether the endpoint interval crosses zero. This is a sensitivity coordinate, not an imputation, estimate, or clinical plausibility statement.

7. Optional uncertainty is separate from deterministic identification regions. If run, use a declared-seed paired arm-stratified patient-frequency bootstrap with B=2000, or record a smaller B only with a resource-failure reason. Resample patients with replacement within arm, retain all selected events, keep pre-origin fits and predictions fixed, do not impute missing Y, and calculate the observed complete-triplet RMSE difference divided by the original point-estimate SD_test(Y), not replicate SD. Report percentile 95% interval, failures, and effective observed-patient clusters. Zero/undefined SD, no observed triplets, or fewer than two observed patient clusters is non-estimable, not zero. The CI does not repair observation-selection bias and never merges with Delta bounds.

## Falsification, interpretation, and limits

Supportive recorded-scale evidence would require all hash/reconstruction/clock and duplicate-join assertions, both support gates, estimable primary analysis, no material observation or assay-validity warning, concordant assay-specific audits, and an all-BP Delta interval strictly above zero over the declared training-range scenario. Even then the conclusion is limited to selected short-horizon prognostic association in that calendar mixture on a recorded-value scale. It does not establish biomarker harmonization, physiologic meaning, causality, treatment response, safety, utility, benefit, or transport.

Adverse evidence is a nonpositive primary contrast in an adequately supported block, repeated M1 degradation, or a wholly nonpositive valid all-BP envelope. It challenges incremental prediction in the selected population; it does not prove B alone is sufficient or identify mechanism.

Selection-sensitive evidence is favorable complete-case performance with a zero-crossing or wide bound, a near-boundary tipping displacement, strong pre-Y observation gradients, specimen/qualitative/calendar/department/encounter discrepancies, or raw/transformed direction disagreement. It means the available records cannot distinguish prognostic information from observation or assay-scale selection.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed support gates, degenerate/unsupported training range, future Y outside the range, nonfinite predictions, zero/undefined test SD, failed bootstrap, too few observed patient clusters, low diagnostic ESS, sparse strata, unavailable transformations, or reliance on a full-source range. The inherited executed audit found a locally supported 2022 block but marked later-calendar observation loss as a major limitation; those inherited facts do not become new results here, and no downstream bound or model finding is asserted.

Computational verification can establish hashes, headers, full scans, duplicate-safe joins, exact event clocks, row validity flags, outcome-blind funnel arithmetic, differential observation summaries, train-only transformations, prediction finiteness, bound endpoint arithmetic, roots, and bootstrap bookkeeping. It cannot establish laboratory units, analyzer harmonization, collection-time validity, specimen adequacy, final-result status, disease severity, tumor burden, imaging response, TACE intent or administration, transfusion/fluid exposure, mortality, hepatic failure, completed follow-up, external-care capture, causal effect, clinical utility, or patient benefit. Those require laboratory metadata, expert chart/laboratory adjudication, imaging and patient-important outcomes, linked mortality/claims, or prospective independent validation.
