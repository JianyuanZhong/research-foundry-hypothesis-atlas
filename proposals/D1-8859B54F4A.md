The unresolved clinical-anchoring question is narrower than “was follow-up completed?”: among parent-defined opportunities, does the stored discharge recommendation have subsequent **target-compatible positive documentation** in the same MIMIC patient record during its due interval, and how often is that positive signal accompanied or pre-empted by observable death, readmission, or alternate target-linked documentation? This is useful because a routing construct that has no relationship to any later target-compatible record deserves less priority for a silent workflow bridge, while a positive relationship can motivate a governed bridge. It still cannot establish that a recommendation was delivered, accepted, scheduled, completed, appropriate, safe, beneficial, or causal.

## Evidence already supported, hypothesis, and evidence boundary

The parent establishes only that its stored-text construct can be measured under a frozen, outcome-blind, two-phase design if its gates pass. The configured MIMIC snapshot demonstrably contains the required admissions, patient, service, ICU, transfer, discharge-note, radiology-note, and radiology-detail sources. A direct read-only profile of the supplied snapshot found 546,028 admissions, 2,321,355 radiology rows, 331,794 DS discharge rows (all observed discharge-note rows had `note_type=DS`), 100,163 subjects with multiple admissions, and only 1,406 radiology reports linked by `(subject_id,hadm_id)` with `charttime>D` (983 admissions, 965 subjects). Thus longitudinal documentation is feasible but sparse and incompletely linked; it cannot be treated as a complete follow-up registry.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Within the locked parent phase-1 master admissions and the exact recommendation units, at least one prespecified supported atom-route stratum will show a reproducible, nonzero rate of positive target-compatible post-discharge radiology documentation in its due interval, and that rate will remain distinguishable from its prespecified shifted-time and wrong-target negative controls after explicitly reporting death, readmission, alternate-documentation, and right-censoring states.

This is a hypothesis about **observable evidence**, not completion or benefit. The primary estimand is the finite, source-observable probability of a positive target-linked radiology record during a due window among recommendation-bearing parent admissions/units, with the unit-level all-unit routing state retained descriptively. It is not a causal effect of recommending CT, nor a completion proportion.

Supportive evidence is a prespecified positive-evidence rate above the negative-control rate with a simultaneous interval excluding the registered null margin, adequate uncensored person-opportunities, and no material target leakage. Adverse evidence is zero/near-zero positive evidence, a control-equivalent or control-exceeding signal, predominant censoring/competing events, target-matching instability, or evidence concentrated only after an observed competing event. Inconclusive evidence is `NO_SELECTION`, sparse target cells, undefined due windows, no uncensored opportunities, interval overlap, or failure to distinguish outside care from non-observation. No result may be converted into a claim of noncompletion, safety, benefit, responsibility, clinical appropriateness, or causal effect.

## Post-discharge evidence definition

### Due windows and observation clock

For each eligible recommendation unit with due interval `m` in `{3,6,12}`, set the due anchor as the subject-relative discharge time `D` plus exactly `m` calendar months (or the parent’s already frozen due date rule). Define a primary tolerance window `[due_date - 30 days, due_date + 30 days]`, and report the exact untoleranced window separately. A report qualifies temporally only by `charttime`, not `storetime`, and must have `charttime>D`; this avoids counting the index report or a late-stored pre-discharge report. All boundaries are deterministic and inclusive as registered.

### Positive target-linked evidence hierarchy

Create three mutually exclusive evidence grades; never collapse them into completion.

1. **Grade A, encounter-linked evidence:** a radiology row has the same `subject_id` and a nonmissing `hadm_id` that matches a later admission for that subject, its `charttime` lies in the due window, and locked text matching identifies the recommended target cluster and modality/action. This is a positive record in the MIMIC hospital system, not proof that it was the recommended study or that the patient complied.
2. **Grade B, patient-time evidence:** `hadm_id` is missing or does not identify a later admission, but the same subject has a radiology report with `charttime` in the due window and locked text matching for target cluster and modality/action. Grade B is retained as positive patient-level documentation with uncertain encounter attribution; it is not used to claim index-admission completion.
3. **Grade C, alternate target-linked evidence:** a report in the due window has locked target-cluster evidence but a modality/action different from the recommendation, or target-compatible language that cannot uniquely establish the recommended action. It is positive alternate documentation, not “replacement” and not completion.

Deduplicate by `(subject_id,note_id)`; retain the earliest qualifying `charttime`, all note IDs, `note_seq`, and addendum ordinals. Require `charttime<=storetime` when both are present and retain violations as an audit/adverse data-quality state, not as evidence. Target matching is a locked, outcome-blind, report-text rule derived only from the recommendation ontology and predeclared synonyms/modality terms; because the configured text extraction is explicitly unvalidated for general clinical diagnosis, two independent blinded human readings of a fixed audit sample may only characterize algorithmic matching error. If that audit is unavailable, report matching uncertainty and do not call the result clinically adjudicated. Images are absent, so there is no imaging-level confirmation.

### Conservative attribution and competing documentation

For every unit, scan all later `hosp/admissions` rows with `admittime>D` and all radiology rows by the same subject. Mark the first observed event time among positive Grade A/B/C evidence, readmission, and observable death. A report occurring after a readmission is labeled `post-readmission documentation` and is never interpreted as an index-discharge follow-up signal. If a radiology row's `hadm_id` matches an admission that starts after `D`, it is encounter-linked to that later admission only; it is not linked back to the index recommendation.

Death is an observable competing event if `admissions.deathtime`/`hospital_expire_flag` for the index admission or `patients.dod` occurs before the end of the due window, with source and time recorded. Conflicting death fields produce `death-date-conflict`, not an invented event time. Readmission is an observable competing event using the earliest later `admittime>D`; it may be an opportunity for alternate care and does not imply cancellation, replacement, or failure. Alternate Grade C evidence is a competing documentation state to the recommended action, not a replacement event. There is **no cancellation field, order/referral field, scheduling field, report-view field, acknowledgement field, or outside-care feed** in the bound sources. Cancellation is therefore `unobserved/unknown` for every record; absence of a radiology row is never coded cancellation or noncompletion.

### Censoring and incomplete capture

Define an administrative observation horizon `L_i` for each subject as the latest nonmissing event time available from the bound admissions (`admittime`, `dischtime`, `deathtime`), `patients.dod`, and radiology `charttime`/`storetime`, after source validity checks. The primary follow-up opportunity is **fully observed only** when `L_i` reaches the due-window end and no death/readmission has already been observed. If `L_i` falls before the due-window end, assign right-censored/incomplete-capture (`K_C`) and do not impute a negative. Death and readmission before due-window end are competing events, not censoring. A sensitivity analysis uses inverse-probability-of-censoring weights only under a prespecified model using baseline variables available at `D`, but the principal result is the directly observed positive-evidence bound; if model support is inadequate, the sensitivity analysis is omitted and marked inconclusive. Because source completeness and outside care are unknown, all bounds are source-observable bounds, not population completion bounds.

For clinically transparent partial identification, report for each stratum:

* lower bound `LB = weighted count of qualifying Grade A/B evidence before a competing event or censoring / weighted count of all eligible units`;
* an upper bound that treats every `K_C`, outside-care-possible, and unobserved-cancellation unit as potentially positive, while keeping observed death/readmission before the due window as competing events rather than positives;
* a strict in-source estimand restricted to fully observed, non-competing opportunities, with its denominator and selection shown;
* Grade A-only, Grade A+B, and Grade A+B+C results separately.

Do not present the upper bound as likely completion. It is a mathematical identification region under explicit unknowns. If the upper and lower bounds overlap the prespecified decision margin, conclude that MIMIC cannot resolve the clinical anchoring question.

## Negative controls and falsification

Pre-register two negative controls, preserving the parent’s control-roster firewall.

1. **Shifted-time control:** for each recommendation unit, apply the identical matcher to a subject-relative window shifted 90 days earlier than the primary due window, requiring `charttime>D` and excluding any primary-window report. This tests whether the apparent target signal is merely a patient’s general radiology density or a discharge-adjacent temporal artifact. If the due window has no valid earlier window, mark the control unavailable rather than substitute a different shift.
2. **Wrong-target/action control:** apply the same locked matcher to a prespecified anatomically unrelated target or nonrecommended modality/action from the same report ontology, with identical timing, linkage, and censoring rules. The control is an information-process control, not a claim that the alternate study is clinically inappropriate.

A positive result requires the primary Grade A+B signal to exceed both controls by the registered margin with a simultaneous interval; a control-equal or control-greater result falsifies the target-specific anchoring claim. Report raw counts and bounds before any adjustment. A report cannot serve as both primary evidence and a wrong-target control for the same unit; assign control roles using a sealed deterministic rule.

## Prespecified interpretation

* **Supportive:** a primary Grade A+B positive-evidence rate exceeds both controls by the registered margin; the lower bound is nonzero; censoring and competing-event fractions are reported and do not account for the entire contrast; target matching passes the audit threshold; and intervals do not rely on undocumented imputation. This supports only that the discharge construct has source-observable longitudinal anchoring sufficient to prioritize a separately approved, prospective silent actual-workflow bridge.
* **Adverse:** the primary rate is zero/near-zero, the lower and upper bounds remain clinically unhelpful, the shifted/wrong-target control is comparable or larger, evidence occurs only after readmission, or matcher/audit failure dominates. Do not interpret this as noncompletion, ineffectiveness, or clinical invalidity; it lowers priority for the silent bridge or demands a different data source.
* **Inconclusive:** `NO_SELECTION`, sparse cells, no fully observed opportunities, wide identification regions, death/readmission/censoring overwhelming the opportunity set, incompatible clocks, target leakage, source linkage failure, or unavailable human audit. The remedy is additional linked data, a prospective workflow log, expert adjudication, or a different study—not favorable selection or a stronger retrospective claim.

The strongest permissible child conclusion remains: “the stored-text construct is or is not accompanied by a bounded, target-specific pattern of positive MIMIC documentation under explicit competing-risk and incomplete-capture assumptions.” It cannot say the recommended imaging was completed, cancelled, replaced, delivered, viewed, acknowledged, clinically indicated, safe, beneficial, or caused by the routing construct. A definitive clinical anchoring result requires a consecutive prospective cohort with immutable order/scheduling/report-view/acknowledgement and outside-care ascertainment, expert target matching (preferably image review), adjudicated cancellation/replacement reasons, patient preference and burden, and later comparative safety/diagnostic/causal outcomes.
