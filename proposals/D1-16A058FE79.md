# Prevalence-aware, fixed-lock multicenter validation of EHR restart against intent-qualified pump rescue

## Decision question, frozen evidence, and substantive repair

The clinical decision is not which vasopressor should be stopped first. It is whether a binary “stopped-agent restart within six hours” reconstructed from EHR infusion transactions is sufficiently accurate, complete, and symmetric to be a defensible endpoint component in a later discontinuation-order trial.

All inherited adverse findings are frozen. Among 6,040 MAP-eligible repeated landmarks, assigned actions were rare: 211 norepinephrine-first (NE-first; 3.49%) and 167 vasopressin-first (VP-first; 2.76%), both below the 5% positivity gate; the 5,638 no-action landmarks are not controls. The inherited observed-stop cohort has 84 NE-first and 53 VP-first episodes, with 49 and 24 determinate composite failures. Those arms are neither randomized nor exchangeable. Of 73 raw failures among 136 determinate episodes, 59 include a charted stopped-agent restart and 36 are restart-only. Mechanical circulatory support (MCS) is unknown in 137/137. Compact temporal models had negligible Brier improvement (M1 about 0.000174 over M0; M2 about 0.000111 over M1, both below 0.01) and adverse later transport; no score or subgroup advances.

The complete inherited audit used all 137 episodes without episode or row sampling after declared stay/item/time filters and reproduced all 137 boundaries and 59 raw restarts. Symmetric transaction-continuity rules invalidated 0, 1, 13, 16, or 23 original stop indices. The inherited chain-plus-dose rule invalidated 13/137 stop indices and 13/59 restart indices (10 NE-first, 3 VP-first), all at `Paused` boundaries. These data establish endpoint-label instability only. They do not establish pump delivery, genuine cessation, rescue intent, MCS absence, false-positive rate, or treatment effect.

The unresolved prospective hypothesis remains clinically consequential:

> For both NE-first and VP-first episodes, the episode-level EHR restart label has PPV and sensitivity greater than 0.80 for intent-qualified pump-native rescue, and the absolute NE-versus-VP difference is less than 0.10 for each metric.

This child preserves full consecutive verification and co-primary PPV/sensitivity but repairs the parent's operating-characteristic problem. The parent's 220 metric-event targets are statistically coherent for fixed inference, but its caps are not prevalence-coherent: under the frozen arm mix (84/137 NE-first, 53/137 VP-first) and frozen EHR-positive proportions (46/84 and 13/53), simulation reached neither VP-first metric denominator before the 1,000-NE-arm cap in 10,000/10,000 runs, despite true PPV and sensitivity of 0.90. The median lock was 1,631 total episodes with only 155 VP-first EHR-positive and reference-positive episodes. Therefore the repair separates a blinded prevalence pilot from a fixed confirmatory lock, removes outcome-dependent denominator stopping, makes the first qualifying episode per patient the primary independent unit, and defines cluster/site transport inference explicitly.

## Estimand hierarchy and prospective population

### Primary patient-level estimands

A person contributes only the first qualifying first-stop episode across all participating ICUs during validation. Let `E=1` denote the frozen episode-level EHR restart and `R=1` the intent-qualified pump-native rescue. In arm `a` (NE-first or VP-first):

* `PPV_a = P(R=1 | E=1, arm=a)`;
* `Sensitivity_a = P(E=1 | R=1, arm=a)`;
* `Delta_PPV = PPV_NE - PPV_VP`;
* `Delta_Sens = Sensitivity_NE - Sensitivity_VP`.

The primary scientific claim is the conjunction of four accuracy claims and two symmetry claims: all `PPV_a` and `Sensitivity_a` exceed 0.80 and both deltas lie strictly within `(-0.10, 0.10)`. PPV and sensitivity are co-primary because EHR-positive-only verification could conceal pump rescues missed by EHR. The population-weighted, patient-level metrics are primary because they correspond to the binary endpoint in a future patient-randomized study.

### Secondary recurrent-episode estimands

All later qualifying episodes are retained in a secondary recurrent-episode cohort rather than discarded. Their PPV, sensitivity, and arm differences use patient-cluster bootstrap intervals stratified by site and arm, and a marginal logistic GEE with patient and site clustering as a sensitivity analysis. They cannot rescue a failed primary result. Report episodes per patient, time between episodes, arm switching, and the effective number of independent patient clusters. This prevents repeated episodes from being silently treated as independent while preserving a clinically relevant robustness analysis.

## Locked eligibility, index, and six-hour window

Enroll every consecutive adult who reaches a qualifying first stop, regardless of whether EHR later records a restart. Eligibility uses only data known at the initial cessation:

1. age at least 18, alive and in an adult ICU;
2. pump-supported concurrent NE and VP delivery for at least six continuous hours immediately before cessation;
3. at the beginning of the final 30 pre-index minutes, NE is greater than 0 and no more than 0.20 mcg/kg/min and VP is greater than 0 and no more than 0.04 U/min, with neither increased during those 30 minutes;
4. no pump-delivered epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine in the prior two hours;
5. at least three valid MAP values spanning at least three of four prior 30-minute bins, including the final bin; median MAP at least 65 mmHg and minimum at least 55 mmHg; and
6. one study drug crosses below its pump delivery threshold while the comparator continues above threshold.

The reference index `t0` is the first pump-native time the first-stopped drug falls below 0.005 mcg/kg/min for NE or 0.005 U/min for VP and remains below for at least five continuous minutes while the comparator remains above threshold. Drug-library identity, concentration, patient, pump, and channel must be linked. Both drugs crossing below threshold within five minutes is a simultaneous stop and is excluded and counted. A procedure/transport pause known by `t0` is excluded and counted. Actual MCS device/type/state is captured and reported as a prespecified stratum, never inferred absent from silence.

The target window is `(t0, min[t0+6 hours, death, ICU exit]]`. Death or ICU exit closes the window rather than erasing it. Capture/linkage failure in the observable window yields an indeterminate reference, never a negative. Eligibility never depends on future restart, rescue, physiology, death, exit, or capture.

## Frozen index test and reference standard

### EHR index test

The automatic index test is generated and escrowed before reference adjudication. Apply the MIMIC-compatible normalization symmetrically:

* NE item 221906: retain mcg/kg/min; divide mcg/min by contemporaneous valid weight 30–300 kg; reject normalized rates above 5 mcg/kg/min.
* VP item 222315: divide units/hour by 60; retain units/min; reject values above 0.2 units/min.

`E=1` if a new positive normalized interval for the stopped drug has `starttime` inside the target window and `endtime > starttime`. A row already spanning `t0` is not a new restart. If genuine pump rescue occurs inside one EHR interval spanning stop and rescue, `E=0`, permitting a false negative. `orderid`, `linkorderid`, `statusdescription`, `storetime`, caregiver, `isopenbag`, and `continueinnextdept` may characterize discrepancies but cannot alter the primary label. Boundary-near events within the measured 99th-percentile cross-clock error are reported without moving them across six hours.

### Intent-qualified pump-native reference

`R=1` requires both:

1. after the qualifying off interval, pump-native delivery of the stopped agent again reaches at least 0.005 in its agent unit for at least five continuous minutes, with resolved drug/concentration/patient/channel linkage and no relevant export gap; and
2. two independent critical-care/pharmacy adjudicators determine from contemporaneous, timestamped evidence that resumption deliberately treated or sought to prevent worsening hypotension or impaired perfusion after cessation.

Routine bag/syringe replacement, pump/channel exchange, transfer continuation, reconciliation, technical/procedural pause, mistaken-stop correction, or uninterrupted delivery segmented only in documentation is reference-negative. The pump algorithm runs before adjudication. Adjudicators see pump traces, monitor physiology, cointerventions, workflow and contemporaneous intent sources, but not the EHR index label, transaction-chain/status diagnostics, arm accuracy, or each other's label. Drug identity cannot be hidden. Categories are rescue-positive; pump resumption without rescue intent; uninterrupted/technical/workflow transition; no resumption; clock/export failure; or intent-indeterminate. A blinded third adjudicator resolves disagreement.

A secondary pump-only reference removes intent. It diagnoses interface validity but cannot replace the clinical rescue target.

## Indeterminates, event matching, and analysis populations

Every screened episode receives one disposition: eligible/determinate, eligible/reference-indeterminate, eligible/index-indeterminate, ineligible with prespecified reason, or duplicate patient for the primary cohort. No enrolled episode is deleted after observing `E` or `R`.

Primary conservative bounds are metric-specific. For PPV, an indeterminate EHR-positive episode remains in the denominator and contributes zero to the true-positive numerator. For sensitivity, an indeterminate EHR-negative episode is treated as a possible reference-positive false negative and added to the denominator. If `E` itself cannot be computed, report both extreme assignments. For each metric and arm, report determinate estimate, conservative estimate, and full identification interval over all admissible indeterminate assignments. Global support requires the conservative gate to pass and indeterminacy no greater than 5% overall and 7.5% in every arm-by-site cell contributing at least 20 primary patients. Differential indeterminacy greater than 5 percentage points between arms is adverse even if conservative accuracy passes. An identification interval crossing any threshold is inconclusive, not supportive.

Episode labels are primary. Secondary event matching uses all pump-native resumptions and all new EHR intervals in the window. First, collapse within-source fragments separated by at most 30 seconds when drug/channel continuity is established. Then perform maximum-cardinality, minimum-total-absolute-lag bipartite matching under a locked ±10-minute caliper, with deterministic tie-breaking by earliest pump event then earliest EHR event. Report unmatched pump events, unmatched EHR events, duplicates, signed lag, absolute median/95th percentile lag, and boundary-near events. Matching cannot relabel an episode.

## Fixed confirmatory design, prevalence pilot, and operating characteristics

### Infrastructure run-in

At least six adult ICUs with synchronized smart-pump, monitor and EHR exports each process 50 fresh consecutive run-in episodes. Definitions are already locked; only clock mapping/export/linkage infrastructure may be repaired. A site enters the prevalence pilot only if at least 95% of windows have unique patient-channel-drug linkage, measured offsets, and complete pump export from two hours before through the observable target window, and at least 90% have a contemporaneous intent source. A failed site repeats with 50 new episodes after repair; a second failure makes it feasibility-only. Run-in episodes never enter validation.

### Blinded prevalence pilot and sample-size lock

The first 400 consecutive validation-eligible primary patients across passing sites form an internal blinded pilot and remain in final analysis. The unblinded data center may count only arm, `E`, pump-algorithm resumption before intent adjudication, capture/indeterminate status, patient cluster, and site; no PPV, sensitivity, cross-tabulation, or accuracy estimate is released. Define planning prevalences conservatively as the lower simultaneous 95% score bounds for the arm-specific E-positive proportion and pump-positive proportion, taking their minimum within each arm. The intent-qualified reference-positive prevalence cannot exceed pump-positive prevalence, so this is optimistic for sensitivity accrual; apply an additional prespecified 20% attrition factor. If either arm has fewer than 20 E-positive or 20 pump-positive pilot patients, do not extrapolate: declare accrual feasibility failed unless sites can document additional capacity and repeat the pilot in 400 new consecutive patients without changing definitions.

Lock the total primary-patient maximum `Nmax` as the smallest integer for which each of the four arm-by-metric denominators has at least 97.5% binomial probability of reaching 220 under the blinded planning prevalence and observed arm fraction, divided by 0.80 for intent/indeterminate attrition, then inflate by the prespecified design effect `1+(m-1)rho` using pilot mean primary patients per site `m` and `rho=0.01`. Cap `Nmax` at 4,000 primary patients and 48 months. If the formula exceeds either cap, the confirmatory claim is prospectively infeasible under current prevalence; the study may complete a descriptive cohort but cannot claim endpoint suitability. No arm-specific stopping cap is used. No accuracy-based sample-size re-estimation is allowed.

After the pilot, accrue all consecutive eligible primary patients through the fixed common database-lock date corresponding to `Nmax` or 48 months, whichever occurs first. Do not stop when 220 denominators are reached: because denominator attainment depends jointly on `E` and adjudicated `R`, such stopping is outcome-dependent and can complicate fixed-sample inference. All sites continue through the common lock. If any denominator is below 220 at lock, the result is inconclusive and attained exact precision is reported. A masked independent statistician may stop only for infrastructure failure, adjudication failure, or mathematical impossibility of denominator attainment given remaining `Nmax`; no efficacy, accuracy, or equivalence look occurs. Thus final intervals are ordinary fixed-lock intervals, not sequential intervals.

The bounded design audit (`[research job]`; 10,000 simulations, seed 20260909) confirmed that at denominator `n=220`, 186 correct classifications are required for a one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound above 0.80; power is 0.619 at true accuracy 0.85, 0.949 at 0.88, and 0.996 at 0.90. Exact score-equivalence power at 220 per arm is 0.916 for 0.90 versus 0.90, 0.869 for 0.88 versus 0.88, 0.141 for 0.90 versus 0.82, and 0.011 for 0.92 versus 0.80. Under equal 0.90 accuracy and 50% arm-specific metric prevalence, the full six-gate simulated success probability was 0.868, not the parent's component-level 0.996. At the 0.80 boundary it was 0/10,000; under PPV 0.92 versus 0.80 it was 0.0078. These are planning operating characteristics, not clinical evidence.

## Confidence, multiplicity, and symmetry

For each arm-specific PPV and sensitivity, success requires a one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound strictly greater than 0.80. For each difference, use the 90% Miettinen-Newcombe score interval; equivalence requires the entire interval strictly within `(-0.10,0.10)`. The global result is an intersection-union test: every one of the six gates must pass. No multiplicity correction is needed for that conjunctive claim; if any component null is true, global false support is bounded by 0.05. This statement does not mean each component has 95% simultaneous coverage. Report every interval and avoid declaring individual metrics “validated” outside the global hierarchy.

Use unstratified Wilson/Newcombe analysis for the primary patient-population claim and site-stratified bootstrap as a robustness check. If the two disagree at a decision boundary, classify transport as inconclusive. Secondary specificity, NPV, agreement, pump-only accuracy, prevalence discrepancy, timing, recurrent episodes, and documentation strata are descriptive with 95% intervals. Holm adjustment applies only to four prespecified arm-by-site interaction tests (PPV and sensitivity).

## Adjudication and multicenter transport gates

After the first 100 adjudicated pilot episodes, report raw agreement, Gwet AC1 and kappa without accuracy. If raw agreement is below 85% or both corrected coefficients are below 0.70, suspend, clarify the manual without viewing accuracy, and restart primary validation using 100 new episodes; prior episodes remain feasibility data. A second failure makes the study inconclusive.

A transportable claim requires at least six sites at final lock; both arms at at least four sites; at least three sites contributing at least 30 E-positive and 30 reference-positive primary patients in each arm across the set of sites; and no site contributing more than 30% of any metric denominator. These constraints are checked at the pilot projection and final lock. Site-specific estimates are reported only with denominator at least 20. Hierarchical binomial models estimate interface/site variance, and leave-one-site-out estimates and 95% site prediction intervals are reported. Transport is adverse if any adequately supported site estimate is below 0.70, the adjusted site accuracy range exceeds 0.15, or either arm's 95% prediction interval lower bound is below 0.70. It is inconclusive if prediction intervals or site support cannot classify those rules. Passing pooled gates with material heterogeneity supports at most the identified interfaces, not a transportable raw-EHR endpoint.

Stop a site's accrual for unresolved linkage, clock failure, or pump export failure in more than 5% of its latest 50 episodes; resume only after a new passing run-in. The whole study is operationally inconclusive after two reliability failures, fewer than four active validation sites, or the fixed administrative lock. No rule permits changing windows, thresholds, labels, margin, continuity rule, or verification fraction.

## Required prospective fields absent from MIMIC

For each episode acquire synchronized source-identifiable fields: patient/stay/site identifiers; pump/channel and drug-library identifiers; concentration; patient weight and source/time; programmed and delivered rate; stop/pause/resume/bolus/alarm events; bag/syringe identity; channel association; export-gap/downtime flags; pump, monitor, EHR and adjudication timestamps plus cross-clock offsets/time-zone/DST mappings; technical, procedure, transport, transfer, reconciliation and container-change markers; timestamped clinician intention for cessation, permanent stop versus temporary pause, restart decision/reason and hypotension/perfusion concern; continuous/high-frequency MAP with source/quality; all vasoactive agents, fluids and cointerventions; actual MCS device/type, insertion/removal, active/standby state and support setting; ICU exit and death; adjudicator identifiers, independent labels, reasons and resolution.

MIMIC supplies none of the pump-native delivery truth, validated intent, reliable MCS absence, or prospective clock mapping. It supplies only the retrospective eligibility/audit frame and EHR-compatible index-test specification.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive:** all four conservative arm-specific lower bounds exceed 0.80; both 90% difference intervals lie inside ±0.10; all fixed-lock denominator, capture, reliability, indeterminate and site-distribution requirements are met; there is no material site/interface heterogeneity; and clock/off-duration sensitivity analyses do not reverse conclusions. This supports only the intent-qualified restart component on represented interfaces.

**Adverse:** any completed accuracy gate fails; either arm difference is non-equivalent; indeterminacy is excessive or differential; paired errors are directional; event matching shows systematic missed/spurious events; or adequately supported interface heterogeneity violates transport gates. High PPV cannot compensate for low sensitivity, and pooled accuracy cannot compensate for material arm or site asymmetry.

**Inconclusive:** a denominator is below 220 at fixed lock; intervals cross thresholds; pilot projection exceeds caps; capture, linkage, clocks, adjudication, site representation, or prediction intervals prevent classification. Inconclusive does not authorize margin relaxation, denominator-conditioned extension, favorable rule selection, or positive-only verification.

An automatic verifier can reproduce the MIMIC filters/audit, prospective dispositions, confusion matrices, missingness bounds, fixed-lock intervals, denominator/accrual rules, matching and decision logic. It cannot establish delivered drug, rescue intent, MCS absence, clinical importance, safety, equipoise, or general transport. Even fully supportive validation cannot show that rescue causes harm, that stopping order causes rescue, that either order is preferable, or that randomization is safe. Those stronger claims require expert review and a separate prospective interventional study.
