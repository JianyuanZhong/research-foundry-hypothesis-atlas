# Do laboratory workflow features make early ICU death alerts unsafe to transport?

Status: Episode-5 substantive successor of `[prior hypothesis]`; adaptation of expert seed `[starting question]`, not a reproduction of any demonstration paper.

## Unresolved clinical question and advance

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that the frozen eICU snapshot contains multi-hospital ICU stays linked to ICU-relative vital and laboratory observations, including laboratory revision times, and that the source has heterogeneous observation processes. The parent feasibility work reported source-availability counts, not a fitted model, transport failure, workflow causation, or clinical utility.

The unresolved claim is decision-facing:

> Among adults still observable six ICU hours after an initial ICU admission, does adding early laboratory observation-process information to measured clinical values improve an early 48-hour ICU-death alert at a development-site operating point, yet lose sensitivity, positive predictive value, calibration, or alert-rate stability when applied to entirely held-out hospitals?

A score that looks accurate in its development ICUs can produce missed high-risk patients, excessive alerts, or poorly calibrated escalation lists in a new ICU if it exploits local test-ordering, interface, or documentation practices. The substantive advance over the parent is to make the primary output an operating-characteristic transport contrast for a realistically constrained alert, rather than treating AUROC alone as the clinical result. The experiment still estimates association and portability, not the effect of changing testing, an optimal alert threshold, clinical utility, or hospital quality.

## Scientific deliverable

The new scientific deliverable is a leakage-safe fit and evaluation of paired values-only (V) and values-plus-laboratory-process (V+P) alert models, with:

1. a frozen cohort/state, canonical-lab coverage, revision-safe availability, feature, split, and threshold manifest;
2. fitted logistic models and, if the bounded branch converges, fitted temporal GRU models for V and V+P;
3. held-out predictions for every eligible stay, with each outer fold’s threshold chosen using only its development hospitals;
4. the primary alert transport table: internal and external sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, alert fraction, Brier score, calibration slope/intercept, and the paired external-minus-internal loss for V+P versus V;
5. AUROC/AUPRC and the three-state D/C/R sensitivity as secondary diagnostics;
6. hospital-cluster uncertainty, permutation/timing/revision/split falsifications, and a conclusion tied to those files.

## Population, temporal boundary, and outcome

The analysis unit is one eICU `patientunitstayid`. From `patient`, retain numeric age >=18, `unitstaytype='admit'`, `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitdischargestatus` exactly `Alive` or `Expired`, and finite `unitdischargeoffset > 360`. If multiple qualifying stays share non-missing `uniquepid`, retain the smallest non-missing `hospitaladmitoffset`, breaking ties by smallest `patientunitstayid`; if all offsets are missing, retain the smallest ID. Deduplicate before splitting. Report retained stays with missing `uniquepid`; every non-missing `uniquepid` must have one split role within each fit.

Time zero is ICU-relative minute 360. Predictors may use only records whose clinical/result/observation offset is in [0,360], and laboratory results must also be revision-available by minute 360. The strict discharge-offset inequality excludes a stay discharged exactly at minute 360, so the outcome states are exhaustive for the landmark population.

The primary binary endpoint is ICU death in (360,3240], defined by `unitdischargestatus='Expired'` and `360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240`. The alert is intended to identify this endpoint, not post-discharge mortality. The mandatory competing-state sensitivity uses D = death in that interval, C = alive ICU discharge in that interval, and R = alive with finite `unitdischargeoffset > 3240` and still in the ICU at minute 3240. If finite offsets or mutually exclusive D/C/R states cannot be constructed, stop the definitive analysis and report prerequisite failure.

The primary transport estimand is defined at an operating point, separately for V and V+P. For each outer repetition/fold, choose the lowest risk threshold on the development internal holdout that achieves at least 80% sensitivity for D; if no threshold achieves it, record threshold failure and do not substitute a favorable threshold. Apply that development-only threshold unchanged to the held-out hospitals. Estimate the difference-in-differences in external versus internal performance for V+P relative to V for sensitivity, PPV, and alert fraction, with calibration/Brier as co-primary safety diagnostics. The 80% target is an analysis operating point, not a recommendation for bedside care. Also report fixed 5%, 10%, and 20% predicted-risk summaries descriptively, without treating them as validated clinical thresholds.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The baseline and alternative answer the same question, use identical folds and target, and differ in the information representation.

The primary transparent baseline is paired L2 logistic regression (C=1, deterministic solver) for V and V+P. Numeric imputation/scaling, categorical encoding, threshold selection, and any residualization are fit within the relevant development data only. This is the preferred primary method because the incremental P block is auditable and its threshold behavior is easy to inspect. Its scientific limitation is that static summaries lose temporal order, trajectory, recovery/worsening shape, and irregular timing.

The substantive learned alternative is a one-layer width-64 GRU with dropout 0.1, Adam 1e-3, batch size 256, weighted binary cross-entropy, at most 30 epochs and patience 5, using twelve fixed 30-minute bins over [0,360]. V sequences contain only within-bin mean and last observed value for each measured channel; empty values use outer-training medians, with no observation mask, count, timestamp, or time-since-last feature. V+P adds laboratory observation masks, within-bin counts, capped time-since-latest-result, and fixed early process totals, with context appended to the final hidden state. This tests whether temporal values reveal information static summaries lose, while preserving the substantive contrast between measured state and documentation process.

Use five whole-hospital outer folds across supported hospitals and five fixed repetition seeds 20260913–20260917. Within each outer training set, use a patient-grouped 20% internal holdout for threshold selection and development-site comparison. A hospital must have >=200 eligible stays and >=10 D events to enter the supported set; use one fixed set for all models. If fewer than 20 hospitals qualify, report descriptive estimates and prerequisite failure rather than a definitive transport claim. If CPU time or convergence prevents the GRU, preserve the checkpoint/log and label that branch deferred; do not alter the cohort or estimand. The science budget is 7,200 seconds with concurrency 2; request at most one GPU only if a bounded diagnostic demonstrates the GRU cannot fit on CPU. Expected completion files are the manifest, fold assignments, preprocessing objects, model/checkpoint files, all predictions, threshold table, alert metrics, competing-state metrics, bootstrap table, falsification outputs, and interpretation log.

A mechanistic workflow model is deferred: the available bindings do not reliably identify order intent, cancellations, turnaround, staffing/capacity, interface failures, or treatment indications. ## Analysis, uncertainty, and falsification

For each model family report internal and external pooled and hospital-macro metrics, with hospitals having one outcome class omitted only from AUROC and retained for Brier/calibration/alert metrics. The primary alert table reports sensitivity, PPV, alert fraction, specificity and NPV at the development-selected 80%-sensitivity operating point. The primary contrasts are the V+P minus V difference in external-minus-internal change for sensitivity, PPV and alert fraction; calibration intercept/slope and Brier are safety diagnostics. Report AUROC/AUPRC and the parent’s paired T_AUROC/T_Brier as secondary information-retention measures.

Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of complete paired hospital prediction sets, preserving folds and repetitions, for percentile 95% intervals. Directional support for a transport hazard requires a positive external loss in at least one primary alert metric with an interval excluding zero, no compensating improvement in calibration/Brier, and a nonnegative internal P increment. A lower interval bound >0.02 is a non-triviality check, not a clinical-benefit threshold. Robust support requires concordant direction in logistic and GRU and passing integrity checks.

Run: (1) within-hospital D permutation and refitting; AUROC should approach 0.5 and alert metrics should follow permuted prevalence; (2) permissive revision-ignorant lab availability versus the valid build, diagnostic only; (3) within-hospital permutation of each patient's P vector; (4) within-stay offset shuffling preserving values/counts; (5) raw P versus training-only V-risk residualized P, V-risk strata, and vital-monitoring process controls; (6) split-integrity, patient-overlap, post-360, revision-after-360, identifier, and outer-test preprocessing checks. An effect only in a random patient split but absent in hospital holdout is site dependence, not deployment evidence.

Supportive results are a positive transport loss in alert PPV/sensitivity/alert fraction or calibration for V+P, with a nonnegative internal increment, reproducibility across model families, and all leakage checks passing. They support the narrow conclusion that recorded laboratory process can make early alerts less portable beyond measured state; they do not show that testing practice causes harm or that a hospital should change testing.

Adverse results are no transport loss, stable/improved external operating characteristics for V+P, or disappearance after acuity/monitoring controls. That falsifies or weakens the hypothesis for this panel/cohort; it does not prove universal portability. Inconclusive results include missing canonical fields, <20 supported hospitals, threshold failure, unstable site metrics, intervals spanning zero and the 0.02 check, family disagreement, GRU deferral when temporal claims are made, or failed integrity checks. Preserve adverse and inconclusive results rather than selecting favorable point estimates.

Computationally checkable are cohort rules, joins, time/revision cutoffs, feature construction, threshold isolation, fits, predictions, state labels, metrics, intervals, and falsification behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a missing row reflects no order, an unrecorded order, interface loss, delayed result, or de-escalation; whether residualization removes acuity; whether an alert changes care or outcomes; and whether any policy is beneficial. The dataset lacks reliable clinician intent, staffing, interface logs, turnaround, post-discharge mortality, prospective alert actions, and external validation outside this eICU snapshot.
