# Provisional hypothesis: context-specific MerTK-inhibitor response in E-MTAB-8873

## Status

This is a provisional, design-level hypothesis for episode 13. It is not an executed result, a claim of direct MerTK action, causality, protection, clinical efficacy, or patient benefit.

## Proposed relationship

In the available E-MTAB-8873 RA macrophage–fibroblast coculture assay, the transcriptional response associated with a MerTK inhibitor will differ between macrophage contexts defined by MerTK/CD206 positivity and MerTK/CD206 negativity. The primary estimand is a donor-aware, condition-verified context-by-inhibitor interaction in the targeted expression matrix. A secondary descriptive question is whether any interaction is enriched for a macrophage-linked resolving/repair-associated program rather than a generic inflammatory or fibroblast-linked program.

The clinically consequential question is whether MerTK modulation has a context-dependent transcriptional signature that would justify targeted mechanistic follow-up, rather than merely producing a generic treatment response. This would not by itself establish that the compound acts directly on MerTK or that the response is therapeutic in patients.

## Evidence supporting the opening

The recovered episode state and prior candidate work report biological plausibility from inspected literature: MerTK-positive synovial macrophage subsets have been associated with remission and repair-related responses; macrophage functional reprogramming can alter inflammatory mediator production; and macrophage–fibroblast repair circuits are context-, timing-, and niche-dependent. These are supporting observations, not evidence that this assay's inhibitor-associated response is context-specific.

The strongest claim currently supported is only that the proposed context-dependent comparison is biologically plausible and that the available assay contains the required sample-level design structure. The unresolved claim is whether the inhibitor-associated response is genuinely context-specific after accounting for donor reuse/mixing, pseudobulk composition, treatment imbalance, and condition/technical artifacts.

## Falsifiable test and baselines

The baseline is a donor-aware descriptive comparison of inhibitor-associated expression changes within each verified macrophage context, with uncertainty from the observed sample/donor dependence. The substantive alternative is a prespecified interaction model or hierarchical comparison that estimates whether the context-by-inhibitor contrast differs, while retaining donor and condition structure. The alternative can reveal whether the apparent response is context-specific rather than a generic treatment shift; the baseline alone loses that distinction.

The experiment should include:
1. condition-verified context labels and treatment labels;
2. donor-aware contrasts or a model that respects repeated/mixed-donor dependence;
3. a composition negative control using a comparable non-targeted or negative-control feature set if available;
4. a within-context treatment negative control or placebo/untreated comparison if available;
5. uncertainty intervals and a prespecified rule for distinguishing a reproducible interaction from a generic response; and
6. a held-out or independent confirmation strategy if the assay permits it.

Supportive results would be a reproducible context-by-inhibitor interaction with uncertainty excluding the prespecified null, robust to donor/composition controls, and not reproduced by the negative controls. Adverse results would be a generic treatment effect, no context difference, or a pattern explained by composition/donor imbalance. Inconclusive results would include insufficient independent donor replication, unstable feature definitions, missing condition verification, or wide uncertainty. None of these outcomes establishes direct target action or clinical benefit.
