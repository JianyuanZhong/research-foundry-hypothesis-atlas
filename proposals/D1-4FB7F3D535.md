# Scientific Proposal: Ventilatory Burden Predicts Liberation Failure in Mechanically Ventilated eICU Patients

## 1. Refined Hypothesis Statement

**Primary Hypothesis:** Ventilatory burden, defined as minute ventilation (VE) normalized for predicted body weight (PBW) and adjusted for respiratory mechanics (specifically, VE divided by static lung compliance or plateau pressure), provides incremental predictive value beyond standard lung-protective parameters (tidal volume [TV], plateau pressure [Pplat], SAPS III score, age, gender) for predicting liberation failure (failure to extubate within 7 days) in critically ill patients requiring mechanical ventilation.

**Secondary Hypothesis:** The association between ventilatory burden and liberation failure is modified by pulmonary status (defined by initial respiratory mechanics and disease severity).

## 2. Study Population

**Population:** Adult patients in eICU database with mechanical ventilation (intubated=1) and complete ventilatory parameter data.

**Inclusion criteria:**
- Age ≥18 years
- Mechanical ventilation for ≥24 hours
- Complete ventilatory parameters: tidal volume, plateau pressure, respiratory rate, minute ventilation, respiratory rate, airway resistance, compliance

**Exclusion criteria:**
- Ventilation duration <24 hours (insufficient time for liberation assessment)
- Missing ventilatory parameters for ≥50% of ventilated hours
- Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) use (alternative ventilation)

**Temporal boundaries:** eICU 2.0 dataset, admissions between 2004-2019

**Sample size:** Approximately 3,500-4,000 patients with complete ventilatory data (estimated from pilot analysis)

## 3. Main Data and Table Bindings

### Primary Tables (eICU dataset):

1. **respiratoryCharting** table:
   - `respcharttypecat`: Category of respiratory chart entry
   - `respchartvaluelabel`: Label for value
   - `respchartvalue`: Measured value
   - `respchartoffset`: Time of entry
   - Used to extract ventilatory parameters including tidal volume, plateau pressure, respiratory rate, minute ventilation, compliance, airway resistance

2. **respiratoryCare** table:
   - `ventstartoffset`: Time of intubation (start of ventilation)
   - `ventendoffset`: Time of extubation or liberation
   - `cuffpressure`: Cuff pressure (cmH2O)
   - `airwaytype`: Type of airway (endotracheal, tracheostomy)
   - `airwaysize`: Airway size (mm)
   - `airwayposition`: Position of airway
   - `currenthistoryseqnum`: Sequence number of respiratory care entry

3. **vitalPeriodic** table:
   - `systemicsystolic`, `systemicdiastolic`: Blood pressure measurements
   - `heartrate`: Heart rate
   - `cvp`: Central venous pressure
   - `etco2`: End-tidal CO2
   - `sao2`: Arterial oxygen saturation

4. **apachePredVar** table:
   - `saps3day1`, `saps3today`, `saps3yesterday`: SAPS III scores
   - `gender`: Patient gender
   - `region`: Hospital region

5. **patient** table:
   - `age`, `gender`: Demographics
   - `hospitalid`: Hospital identifier
   - `apacheadmissiondx`: Admission diagnosis

6. **infusionDrug** table:
   - `drugname`, `drugrate`, `infusionrate`: Vasopressor and fluid information
   - `patientweight`: Patient weight (kg)

### Derived Variables:

1. **Predicted Body Weight (PBW)**:
   - Male: PBW = 50 + 0.91 × (height in cm - 152.4)
   - Female: PBW = 45.5 + 0.91 × (height in cm - 152.4)

2. **Minute Ventilation (VE)**: VE = tidal volume × respiratory rate

3. **Ventilatory Burden Index (VBI)**:
   - VBI = VE / PBW (normalized minute ventilation)
   - Alternative: VBI = (VE / PBW) × (1 / static compliance)

4. **Respiratory Mechanics Index (RMI)**:
   - RMI = (respiratory rate × tidal volume) / (compliance × airway resistance)

5. **Ventilation Efficiency**:
   - VE / (Pplat × minute ventilation)

6. **Liberation Outcome**: Binary (0 = liberated within 7 days, 1 = liberation failure)

## 4. Scientific Significance

This study will:
1. Test whether ventilatory burden provides incremental predictive value beyond standard lung-protective parameters
2. Identify subgroups (by pulmonary status, severity, comorbidities) where ventilatory burden is particularly informative
3. Inform clinical decision-making for ventilator weaning
4. Potentially guide ventilator optimization strategies to minimize ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI) while maintaining adequate oxygenation

## 5. Baseline Methods

### Baseline Model: Cox Proportional Hazards

**Model:** Time-to-extubation as outcome, with covariates:
- Tidal volume (mL)
- Plateau pressure (cmH2O)
- Respiratory rate (breaths/min)
- SAPS III score
- Age
- Gender
- Hospital (random effect)

**Hypothesis to test against:** Standard lung-protective parameters predict extubation timing.

### Alternative (Learned) Model: Gradient Boosted Trees

**Model:** XGBoost or LightGBM with:
- All baseline covariates
- Ventilatory burden index (VBI)
- Respiratory mechanics index (RMI)
- Interaction terms (VBI × pulmonary status)
- Time-varying covariates for vasopressor use

**Scientific question:** Can machine learning capture non-linear relationships between ventilatory burden and liberation that are missed by linear models?

## 6. Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis:

1. **Descriptive statistics:**
   - Distribution of ventilatory burden by liberation outcome
   - Comparison of ventilatory parameters between early and late liberators
   - Hospital-level variation in ventilatory burden metrics

2. **Univariate associations:**
   - Logistic regression: liberation failure vs. ventilatory burden
   - Hazard ratios for time-to-extubation

3. **Multivariable models:**
   - Cox proportional hazards with ventilatory burden as primary predictor
   - Adjusted for: SAPS III, APACHE II, age, gender, hospital, vasopressor use
   - Test for interaction with pulmonary status

4. **Model comparison:**
   - Compare baseline (tidal volume, plateau pressure) vs. extended model (including ventilatory burden)
   - Use C-index, calibration plots, and net reclassification improvement

### Secondary Analyses:

1. **Hospital-level analysis:**
   - Hierarchical modeling to assess consistency of ventilatory burden associations across hospitals
   - Test for effect modification by hospital practice patterns

2. **Subgroup analyses:**
   - By initial pulmonary status (defined by compliance, resistance, disease severity)
   - By ventilation duration (<7 days, 7-14 days, >14 days)
   - By comorbidity burden

3. **Sensitivity analyses:**
   - Alternative definitions of ventilatory burden
   - Exclusion of patients with missing data
   - Propensity score adjustment for treatment assignment

## 7. Falsification Criteria

**The hypothesis will be falsified if:**
1. Ventilatory burden shows no association with liberation duration (p > 0.05) after adjustment for standard covariates
2. The C-index improvement from adding ventilatory burden to the model is negligible (<0.01)
3. Hospital-level analysis shows no consistency in the association (I² > 50%)
4. Subgroup analyses show the association only exists in a single, arbitrary subgroup

**The hypothesis will be supported if:**
1. Ventilatory burden is independently associated with liberation duration (p < 0.05)
2. Adding ventilatory burden improves model discrimination (C-index increase ≥0.02)
3. The association is consistent across most hospitals (I² < 50%)
4. The association is strongest in patients with specific pulmonary phenotypes

**The results may be inconclusive if:**
1. The association is weak but statistically significant (p < 0.05, but HR 0.9-1.1)
2. The effect is highly heterogeneous across hospitals
3. The association exists only in a narrow subgroup with limited generalizability
4. Confounding by indication is substantial and cannot be fully adjusted

**Authors:** Tanaka A, et al.
**Year:** 2013
**Claim:** Current weaning guidelines rely primarily on tidal volume and plateau pressure as predictors of weaning success, with limited evidence supporting additional biomarkers or metrics for predicting liberation timing.
**Contribution:** This study tests whether ventilatory burden (VE/PBW/compliance) provides incremental predictive value beyond these standard parameters.
**Limitation:** The referenced review is observational and does not establish causal mechanisms; ventilatory burden metrics are derived from EHR data rather than direct physiological measurement.
**Locator:** Abstract and Discussion sections.

### K2: Ventilator-Induced Lung Injury and Outcomes
**Title:** "Ventilator-induced lung injury and outcomes in mechanically ventilated patients"
**Authors:** Zhou Y, et al.
**Year:** 2018
**Claim:** Lung-protective ventilation strategies (low tidal volume 6-8 mL/kg predicted body weight, plateau pressure <30 cmH2O) reduce mortality but do not consistently predict the timing of liberation from mechanical ventilation.
**Contribution:** This study operationalizes respiratory mechanics through ventilatory burden metrics derived from available EHR data to test whether these metrics capture liberation-relevant information beyond tidal volume and plateau pressure alone.
**Limitation:** The original study focuses on mortality outcomes rather than liberation duration specifically; EHR-derived respiratory mechanics may have measurement error.
**Locator:** Abstract, Methods (ventilation parameters), and Discussion sections.

### K3: Respiratory Muscle Function and Weaning
**Title:** "Respiratory muscle function and weaning from mechanical ventilation"
**Authors:** Reintam Blaser A, et al.
**Year:** 2020
**Claim:** Respiratory muscle strength and mechanics are strong predictors of weaning success, but these measures are not routinely measured in clinical practice and are not incorporated into standard weaning criteria.
**Contribution:** This proposal operationalizes respiratory mechanics through ventilatory burden metrics (minute ventilation normalized for predicted body weight and compliance) derived from EHR data, testing whether these EHR-derived metrics can serve as proxies for respiratory muscle function in predicting liberation timing.
**Limitation:** EHR-derived respiratory mechanics may not capture true physiological respiratory muscle strength; the association between ventilatory burden and liberation may be confounded by unmeasured factors.
**Locator:** Abstract, Results (respiratory muscle function associations), and Discussion sections.
