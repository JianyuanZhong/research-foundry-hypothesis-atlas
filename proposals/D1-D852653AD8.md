# Proposal (strengthened child): Preoperative laboratory prediction of early (≤1-year) HCC recurrence after hepatic resection — full-cohort censoring-aware estimand and outcome-specificity sensitivity analyses

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (valid, selected episode 2). This child keeps the
hypothesis, thresholds, temporal split, and models (B0/B1/B2) unchanged and resolves the two
Lead-identified unresolved design issues: (1) the primary analysis no longer conditions on
≥1-year follow-up — it is a competing-risk / censoring-aware analysis on the FULL resection
cohort; (2) the diagnosis-text outcome proxy is stress-tested with two pre-specified
specificity restrictions (≥90 d post-resection; contrast-enhanced imaging confirmation).

## 1. Clinical question and why it matters (unchanged)
After curative-intent hepatic resection for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), roughly a quarter to
half of patients recur within one year. Early recurrence drives consequential decisions:
intensified surveillance, adjuvant/systemic therapy (lenvatinib, IO combinations), and transplant
listing. Published recurrence models (nomograms [K1], MERP [K2]) rely on variables that are NOT
available as structured data in this EHR: microvascular invasion (MVI), tumor size/number,
resection margin, histologic grade, and imaging features. The unresolved question is whether
**routinely drawn preoperative laboratory values alone** — the only variables with dense,
time-stamped availability in this cohort — carry enough independent prognostic signal to stratify
1-year recurrence risk, and whether a simple, deployable lab-only rule can approach the
discrimination reported for richer models (C-index ~0.68–0.74 [K1,K2]).

A validated lab-only score would be computable at the preoperative lab draw in any hospital,
whereas MVI is only known after surgery and imaging-feature models require radiology pipelines.

## 2. Evidence inspected (unchanged; see key-references.json and attached abstracts)
- [K1] Zeng et al. 2020 (PMID 33145280, Ann Transl Med): inflammation-based nomograms for OS/RFS
  after radical resection (n=3,071); RFS C-index 0.71/0.74 (train/val). Uses AFP, ALBI, PLR,
  NrLR, GPR, tumor size/number, MVI, margin, grade — most unavailable here. Bounds the
  discrimination achievable with "accessible clinical parameters" in a comparable Chinese cohort.
- [K2] Wei et al. 2026 (PMID 42323021, J Hepatol): MERP MRI-based 1-year RFS prediction in 1,811
  single BCLC 0/A resections; C-index 0.685–0.790 vs staging systems 0.524–0.682. State of the
  art for 1-year recurrence prediction; requires imaging features + MVI unavailable here.
- [K3] Chen et al. 2024 (PMID 39336451, Medicina): preoperative nomogram for MVI before
  hepatectomy (n=489) using AFP, platelets, GOT/GPT ratio, AAR, ALBI, GNRI — evidence that
  preop labs partially capture the biology (MVI) that dominates recurrence risk. Strongest rival
  explanation: any lab signal may proxy MVI rather than independent biology.

## 3. Hypothesis (unchanged, falsifiable)
H1: A model using only preoperative (0–30 d before index resection) laboratory values predicts
1-year recurrence after hepatic resection with discrimination meaningfully above a clinical
baseline (age, sex, calendar year): held-out C-index ≥ 0.62, and top-vs-bottom tercile 1-year
recurrence rates differing by ≥ 10 percentage points.
Falsification: if the best lab-only model's held-out C-index ≤ 0.58 or the tercile risk gap is
≤ 5 pp, routine labs alone are not supported for early-recurrence triage in this cohort.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Primary analysis (STRENGTHENED — full-cohort, censoring-aware)
- Estimand: 1-year cumulative incidence of HCC re-diagnosis (base outcome definition) in the
  FULL 7,701-patient resection cohort, accounting for administrative censoring at last encounter.
  This is a recurrence-free-survival estimand in the absence of death data; it is NOT overall
  survival, and patients lost before 1 y are censored, not excluded.
- Method: Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model (subdistribution HR with 95% CI) on the full
  cohort, with the lab feature set (B1/B2 scores as covariates in separate fits) and B0
  (age, sex, year) as the baseline. Cause-specific Cox is reported as a robustness check
  (equivalent here since there is no death competing event — censoring only).
  Censoring time = last encounter date (基本信息.就诊时间); event time = first post-op HCC
  diagnosis encounter date; event indicator = 1 if that date ≤ 365 d.
- Cumulative incidence at 1 year (Aalen–Johansen) reported for the full cohort and by model
  terciles.
- Model evaluation (unchanged decision criteria): for B0, B1, B2 —
  (a) time-dependent C-index at 1 year with DeLong CI on the held-out test set (2017–2018);
  (b) top-vs-bottom tercile 1-year cumulative-incidence gap;
  (c) calibration (slope, intercept, calibration plot);
  (d) NRI/IDI of B1/B2 vs B0.
  Decision thresholds UNCHANGED: supportive if best model C-index ≥ 0.62 AND tercile gap ≥ 10 pp;
  adverse if C-index ≤ 0.58 OR gap ≤ 5 pp; inconclusive in between.
- Why Fine-Gray over the parent's conditioned logistic: conditioning on ≥1 y follow-up (n≈2,191,
  28.4% of the cohort) changes the estimand to "1-year recurrence among follow-up-complete
  patients" — a selected subset enriched for patients who remained in facility care (and possibly
  for sicker patients who return for surveillance). The subdistribution hazard uses all 7,701
  patients, treats <1-y loss as censoring, and yields a cohort-level cumulative incidence that is
  directly comparable to published 1-year RFS rates [K1,K2].
- Split (unchanged): temporal — train 2013–2016 (n≈5,303), test 2017–2018 (n≈2,398); no patient
  overlap. Secondary: 5-fold CV within 2013–2018.
- Models (unchanged):
  - B0: logistic regression on age, sex, resection year (clinical baseline).
  - B1: penalized logistic regression (L1/L2, 5-fold CV for penalty selection) on the lab set.
  - B2: gradient-boosted trees (e.g. LightGBM) on the same features — the substantive learned
    alternative; tests whether non-linear interactions (AFP×ALBI, GGT×PLT) add information the
    linear model loses. A Delphi-style sequence model remains not justified at n≈5,000–8,000 with
    ~20 features.
  For the Fine-Gray primary, each model's predicted probability (from the temporal-split fit) is
  entered as a single covariate (score test + subdistribution HR per 0.1 probability increment);
  terciles are cut on the test-set predicted probability.

### 5.2 Sensitivity analyses (NEW — outcome specificity)
All three reuse the identical features, split, models, and decision thresholds; only the outcome
definition changes. Each is reported as a separate 1-year cumulative incidence + C-index +
tercile gap on the full cohort (Fine-Gray), so the estimand stays cohort-level.
- Sensitivity A (≥90 d post-resection): event = first HCC-diagnosis encounter with
  encounter date > 90 d after resection (excludes postoperative diagnostic workup).
  Measured: 1-year event patients 958 (vs 1,947 base). Rationale: 49% of base 1-year events occur
  in encounters ≤90 d post-resection, consistent with the original tumor diagnosis being re-stated
  during postoperative workup.
- Sensitivity B (imaging confirmation): event = first HCC-diagnosis encounter ≤365 d that is
  imaging-confirmed per the 检查 rule above (same 就诊号). Measured: 1-year event patients 382
  (19.6% of base). Rationale: high-specificity / low-sensitivity check — if the lab signal
  persists on imaging-confirmed events, it is robust to the re-diagnosis concern; if it vanishes,
  the base outcome was driven by non-imaging re-diagnosis.
- Sensitivity C (A and B): event = first HCC-diagnosis encounter >90 d AND imaging-confirmed.
  Measured: 1-year event patients 228. Most specific; underpowered for C-index precision —
  directional robustness check only.
- Interpretation grid (pre-specified):
  - Base supportive AND A supportive → strong: lab signal robust to workup-window re-diagnosis.
  - Base supportive, A adverse/inconclusive → the base signal is substantially driven by
    ≤90-d re-diagnosis; the lab-only claim is weakened to "predicts early post-op HCC
    re-diagnosis, not necessarily true recurrence".
  - B supportive (despite n≈382) → strongest specificity evidence; B adverse with base
    supportive → outcome-proxy concern dominates; do not claim recurrence prediction.
  - All adverse → lab-only triage not supported (negative result, as in parent).

## 6. Interpretation plan (updated)
- Supportive: best model (B1 or B2) held-out 1-year C-index ≥ 0.62 AND tercile cumulative-
  incidence gap ≥ 10 pp on the FULL cohort (Fine-Gray), with sensitivity A concordant →
  routine labs carry actionable early-recurrence signal; next steps: external validation
  (not available here) and decision-curve analysis.
- Adverse: C-index ≤ 0.58 OR gap ≤ 5 pp → lab-only triage not supported; record as a negative
  result; the field should rely on postoperative MVI/imaging.
- Inconclusive: 0.58 < C-index < 0.62 → weak signal; report with CIs; do not claim utility.
- Outcome-proxy adverse (base supportive, A or B adverse): the lab signal predicts early HCC
  re-diagnosis but the re-diagnosis may include restatement of the original tumor; the clinical
  claim is downgraded accordingly and chart review (clinical adjudication) is required before any
  recurrence-specific claim.
- NOT established by this study: causality, external validity (single center), or that the lab
  signal is not a proxy for unmeasured MVI/tumor burden (partially addressed by MVI mediation).
  Death-unknown censoring means the 1-year cumulative incidence is a lower bound if lost patients
  died before 1 y with unobserved recurrence.

## 8. Limitations and essential adjudication
- Outcome is a diagnosis-text proxy (unstructured 诊断名称; 诊断类型 often 入院/主治医师诊断),
  not imaging/pathology-confirmed recurrence. Sensitivity B adds an imaging-confirmation layer
  but it is an administrative proxy (exam ordered in the encounter), not radiologist-confirmed
  recurrence. Clinical adjudication (chart review) remains essential for a recurrence-specific
  claim and is not computable here.
- ~72% of the cohort is censored before 1 y in this EHR (loss to facility or death-unknown).
  The Fine-Gray design handles this by censoring rather than exclusion, but the 1-year cumulative
  incidence is a lower bound under differential loss (e.g. if sicker patients leave care).
- No death data → the estimand is 1-year recurrence-free survival, not OS; recurrence and death
  are not separated.
- MVI is sparsely and textually recorded; the mediation analysis is underpowered for precision.
- Single center; no external validation cohort in the configured datasets.
- Assay drift 2013–2018 handled by temporal split + year covariate.
- 检查 exam names are free text; the 增强 rule is lexical and may miss atypically named
  contrast studies (e.g. 普美显 variants are captured; purely English-named studies are not
  observed in the top-40 exam list).

## 9. Alternatives considered (recorded, with deferral reasons)
- MVI-prediction model (as in K3): deferred as primary — MVI labels sparse (5,604 pos / 585 neg /
  rest silent) and text-adjudicated; kept as secondary mediation analysis.
- Imaging-text tumor size extraction: deferred — adds a second unvalidated NLP extraction layer;
  revisit if the lab-only signal is weak and size is the suspected missing variable.
- AFP-L3% model: assay unavailable for the primary cohort (introduced 2019); kept as a
  2019–2025 exploratory sub-cohort.
- Neural/sequence models (Delphi-style): not justified at n≈5,000–8,000 with ~20 features;
  gradient boosting is the appropriate learned alternative.
- Cause-specific Cox as the SOLE primary: not chosen as primary because with no observed
  competing event (death) it is numerically close to Fine-Gray here but the subdistribution
  hazard is the estimand-appropriate model when loss-to-follow-up is informative; cause-specific
  Cox is retained as the robustness check.
- 复发-keyword outcome: rejected as primary or sensitivity — only 60 cohort HCC rows contain
  复发 (measured); far too sparse to define an outcome.
- Systemic-therapy timing questions (lenvatinib/IO era): deferred — treatment-course timestamps
  unreliable (median course duration 0 d) and heavily confounded by era.
