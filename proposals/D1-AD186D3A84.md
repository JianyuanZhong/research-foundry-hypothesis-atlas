# Final episode-1 hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery in MIMIC-IV

Status: research task, not a completed result. This is a repair of expert seed `[starting question]`.

## Hypothesis and clinical importance
Among adults with an ICU stay and a prespecified creatinine-defined AKI episode, creatinine improvement without concurrent urine-output improvement at a fixed landmark will identify higher subsequent risk of renal deterioration or death than concordant recovery, after accounting for the same landmark severity and treatment state. Resolving this matters because a creatinine-only “recovery” label could misclassify vulnerable patients and delay monitoring or escalation. The unresolved claim is incremental prognostic information, not a mechanism or treatment effect.

## Evidence boundary
The strongest inspected evidence supports the measurement concern, not the proposed association. Haines et al. found that eGFR creatinine systematically overestimated kidney function after prolonged critical illness, while cystatin C better estimated measured GFR in 38 ventilated patients [K1]. A systematic review identifies the 2012 KDIGO framework as standardizing AKI diagnosis using creatinine and urine output, while noting implementation and population-applicability gaps [K2]. A recent MIMIC-IV/eICU landmark study used creatinine, AKI stage, urine output, and severity to predict observed CRRT initiation, but explicitly treated CRRT as a clinician-driven observed outcome rather than objective necessity [K3]. None establishes that discordant recovery predicts subsequent outcomes.

## Analysis and falsification
The simple baseline is a landmark logistic or Cox model with current creatinine, creatinine slope, urine-output summary, AKI stage, age/sex, and severity/treatment context. The substantive alternative is a compact trajectory model using pre-landmark creatinine and urine-output sequences (slopes, nadir, persistence, and co-movement), with identical patient splits, held-out temporal evaluation, calibration, discrimination, and uncertainty. The alternative is retained because persistence and ordering can distinguish true discordance from a noisy single measurement; it is deferred if sequence support is inadequate. CPU is adequate for both; no GPU is required.

Support requires a prespecified directionally consistent association and reproducible incremental calibration/discrimination in held-out data, with uncertainty and sensitivity analyses showing it is not explained solely by missing urine measurements, diuretic/RRT exposure, or current severity. Adverse evidence is no incremental signal, reversal, or instability after these adjustments. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse discordant groups, inadequate follow-up, or wide uncertainty. None supports mechanism or treatment benefit.
