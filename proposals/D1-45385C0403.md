# Baseline-severity-conditioned robustness of time-weighted hypotension burden for early AKI

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP), after controlling for measurement density and after showing that the signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined)?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density. The strongest rival is that the apparent burden signal is an artifact of recording density, measurement timing, or baseline illness severity, or that it is specific to one arbitrary choice of blood-pressure summary. A second, clinically important rival is that the burden signal is driven by baseline illness severity: sicker patients may have both more recorded hypotension and worse kidney outcomes, so an unadjusted burden signal could reflect severity rather than an independent prognostic effect.

## Clinical importance

Early AKI is a common and consequential complication of ICU care. If a time-weighted hypotension burden measure adds prognostic information beyond MAP after controlling for severity and measurement density, it could inform early risk stratification and monitoring decisions. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful and would support continued use of simpler MAP-based summaries.

## Population and temporal boundaries

- Inclusion: adult patients (age >= 18) with at least one systolic and one diastolic blood-pressure measurement in the first 24 hours after ICU admission.
- Exclusion: patients with fewer than 5 blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours (measurement-density floor); patients with no creatinine measurement in the first 24 hours or within 48 hours after admission (outcome floor).
- Exposure window: first 24 hours after ICU admission.
- Outcome window: AKI within 48 hours after admission (KDIGO stage 1 or higher: creatinine >= 2x baseline or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for >= 6 hours; eICU has creatinine but not continuous urine output, so the creatinine criterion is the primary outcome).

## Exposure and outcome definitions

- Time-weighted hypotension burden: for each patient, compute the fraction of the first 24 hours during which MAP < 65 mmHg, using linear interpolation between recorded blood-pressure points. This is the primary exposure.
- 24-hour MAP: the time-weighted mean of MAP over the first 24 hours, using the same interpolation. This is the primary comparator.
- Robustness summaries: repeat the analysis using systolic BP < 90 mmHg, diastolic BP < 60 mmHg, and a combined criterion (systolic < 90 OR diastolic < 60) as alternative definitions of hypotension.
- AKI outcome: KDIGO stage 1 or higher creatinine criterion within 48 hours after admission. Baseline creatinine is the first creatinine measurement in the first 24 hours.

## Controls and confounding

- Measurement density: number of blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours (continuous and categorical: <10, 10-20, >20).
- Baseline illness severity: APACHE IV score computed from available eICU fields (age, gender, temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, blood pressure, GCS, lab values) with SOFA score as a fallback. Severity strata: low, medium, high (terciles).
- Vasopressor exposure: any vasopressor infusion in the first 24 hours (from `infusion.csv.gz`).
- Baseline kidney function: baseline creatinine (first measurement in the first 24 hours).
- Hospital: `hospital_id` as a fixed effect or stratification variable.

## Analysis plan

1. Primary analysis: logistic regression of AKI within 48 hours on time-weighted hypotension burden, 24-hour MAP, measurement density, baseline severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline creatinine, and hospital. Compare the burden model to the MAP-only model using likelihood-ratio test, AUC, and calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow).
2. Robustness to summary choice: repeat the primary analysis using systolic, diastolic, and combined hypotension definitions. The claim is supported only if the burden signal is present in all four summaries.
3. Severity stratum analysis: repeat the primary analysis within each severity tercile. The claim is supported only if the burden signal is present in at least two of three strata.
4. Missing-severity sensitivity analysis: repeat the primary analysis excluding patients with missing APACHE IV and SOFA scores. The claim is supported only if the burden signal is present in this subset.
5. Hospital-level analysis: report the burden signal within each hospital (or hospital stratum) to assess transportability.

## Falsification criteria

- If the burden measure adds no significant incremental prediction beyond MAP after adjustment for measurement density and baseline severity, the claim is falsified.
- If the burden signal is absent in at least one blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined), the claim is falsified.
- If the burden signal is absent in at least two of three severity strata, the claim is falsified.
- If the burden signal is absent in the missing-severity sensitivity analysis, the claim is falsified.

## Solver deliverable

The solver must fit and report:
- The primary logistic regression model (burden + MAP + controls) and the comparator model (MAP + controls).
- Likelihood-ratio test p-value, AUC, and Hosmer-Lemeshow calibration for both models.
- Robustness results for all four blood-pressure summaries.
- Severity stratum results and missing-severity sensitivity analysis.
- Hospital-level analysis.
- Confidence intervals for all key estimates.

## Limitations

- eICU does not have continuous urine output, so the KDIGO creatinine criterion is the primary outcome.
- APACHE IV and SOFA scores are computed from available eICU fields and may not be identical to the original scoring systems.
- The study is observational and cannot establish causality.
- Hospital-level analysis is limited by the number of hospitals and the heterogeneity of ICU practices.
