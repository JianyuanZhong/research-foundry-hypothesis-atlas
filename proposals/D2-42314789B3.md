# Donor-aware protective-versus-pathogenic target selectivity

Seed: D2-T2. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Does an intervention-associated transcript pattern preferentially suppress a pathogenic fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state, such that a target ranked from an averaged signal is misleading? This matters because macrophage and fibroblast programs can have opposing roles in fibrotic or inflammatory disease: an intervention-associated change in one compartment may reflect a protective response, a pathogenic program, or simply differential cell composition and stimulation. Resolving the question would change whether a target is pursued as context-specific, requires a cell-type-resolved intervention, or is deprioritized.

## Strongest currently supported claim

The strongest current evidence is descriptive: the configured datasets contain macrophage/fibroblast-related conditions and intervention-associated transcript changes. The datasets are available as prepared counts and design metadata. The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that these datasets contain the necessary conditions and transcript changes. This is not evidence that a specific transcriptomic signature is protective or pathogenic.

## Unresolved claim

Whether the intervention-associated transcript pattern selectively suppresses a pathogenic fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state, in a way that is not explained by donor composition or stimulation.

## Analysis

A donor-aware linear mixed model on the four configured sources tests whether the intervention-associated pattern separates protective and pathogenic states. Donor random effects and condition fixed effects are used. A negative-control gene set and a stimulation control model composition and stimulation artifacts.

## Competing explanations

- Donor-specific baseline differences
- Differential cell composition
- General stimulation response
- Targeted assay limits (unmeasured genes unavailable)
- Cross-sectional disease/treatment groups do not establish transitions
- No wet-lab validation available

## Falsification

Supportive: the selectivity signal persists after controlling for donor composition and stimulation.
Adverse: the signal disappears or reverses under donor-aware analysis.
Inconclusive: the signal is weak, inconsistent across sources, or confounded by donor structure.

## Method comparison

A simple baseline is a donor-averaged expression comparison. A learned alternative is a donor-aware linear mixed model. The mixed model could reveal donor-specific patterns that the baseline loses. The mixed model is selected because donor structure is a known confounder in these datasets.
