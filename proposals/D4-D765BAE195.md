# Sleep regularity beyond sleep duration: a UK Biobank incremental-risk experiment

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The unresolved clinical question is whether sleep-wake timing consistency adds information beyond total sleep duration for the risk of a consequential metabolic outcome. The seed hypothesis is that, at the same sleep duration, irregular sleep-wake schedules increase diabetes risk among people with metabolic abnormalities. This is an association hypothesis, not a claim that irregular sleep causes diabetes.

The strongest evidence currently supports a plausible but non-identifying clinical opening: prior work found a non-linear association between a sleep regularity index and mortality in UK Biobank, and a recent Chinese prospective cohort found an association between accelerometry-derived sleep regularity and incident type 2 diabetes after adjustment for sleep duration. These findings motivate testing whether sleep regularity is an incremental risk marker in a large UKB cohort. They do not establish a mechanism, and the UKB data do not contain the same accelerometry-derived regularity index or the same outcome ascertainment as those studies.

The experiment tests the falsifiable incremental-risk hypothesis:

> Among UK Biobank participants with at least five valid assessment dates, the 10-year cumulative incidence of incident diabetes is greater for the lowest versus highest sleep regularity category after adjustment for sleep duration and measured clinical covariates, with the incremental association persisting after adjustment for baseline metabolic status.

The primary estimand is the difference in 10-year cumulative incidence of incident diabetes between the lowest and highest sleep-regularity categories, conditional on the assessment date. The comparison is observational and conditional on the assessment date; it does not identify the causal effect of changing sleep regularity.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

Diabetes and cardiovascular risk are consequential for prevention and follow-up decisions. Total sleep duration is a common public-health target, but sleep regularity is a distinct sleep-health dimension. A positive result would show that timing consistency is an incremental risk marker beyond duration and could justify including timing in risk communication or stratification. A null result would show that, in this UKB data structure, duration alone is sufficient for this incremental association. Either result would change the next decision: whether to pursue timing-specific interventions or to retain duration as the primary metric.

The advance over the prior UKB evidence is a temporally explicit incremental-risk experiment. The prior UKB sleep-regularity study did not provide a complete local source binding or a prespecified test of whether its association remains after adjustment for baseline metabolic status and the exact outcome fields. This proposal binds the available UKB tables and explicitly tests the strongest rival explanations: reverse causation, severity/health-selection, and measurement artifact.

## Baseline, alternative, and analysis

The simple baseline is a Cox proportional-hazards model (or equivalent time-to-event model) with sleep regularity, sleep duration, age, sex, smoking status, alcohol use, physical activity, and a prespecified set of baseline metabolic covariates. The primary model includes sleep regularity, sleep duration, and the prespecified covariates; the incremental estimand is the adjusted 10-year risk difference between the lowest and highest regularity categories. The model should report the adjusted hazard ratio, 95% confidence interval, Schoenfeld residual test for proportional hazards, calibration, and event counts.

The substantive alternative is a matched, regularized spline or penalized regression model that jointly models sleep regularity and sleep duration with flexible non-linear terms. It should be fitted on the same complete-case population and use the same outcome and time scale. This alternative can reveal whether the apparent association is linear or whether an interaction between regularity and duration is important, information that a simple additive baseline loses. It is not selected for predictive gain alone.

The solver must also fit an interaction model testing whether the regularity association differs by baseline metabolic status, and a sensitivity analysis excluding participants with a baseline diagnosis or outcome before the 10-year window. A placebo or negative-control analysis is useful: repeat the regularity association with an outcome that should not be plausibly related to sleep regularity, or test a clinically implausible timing contrast, to check for residual severity/selection artifacts.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence is an adjusted, positive incremental association between sleep regularity and incident diabetes with a confidence interval excluding zero, stable across the prespecified sensitivity analyses, and not explained by an interaction with sleep duration or baseline metabolic status. It would support the incremental-risk hypothesis, not a mechanism.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed association, a confidence interval excluding the prespecified clinically meaningful effect size, or a result explained by duration, baseline severity, or measurement artifact. That would argue against the proposed incremental association in this data structure.

Inconclusive evidence is a wide confidence interval, sparse events, poor calibration, or inability to verify the required sleep-field meaning/outcome time. The solver must report these limitations and not claim clinical truth.

The strongest claim the experiment can establish is an adjusted association conditional on the assessment date. A causal or mechanistic claim would require longitudinal intervention data, repeated measurements, better measurement of sleep, and clinical adjudication of outcomes. The local data cannot establish that sleep regularity causes diabetes or that changing sleep regularity would change risk.

The planned solver envelope is CPU-only for this tabular cohort; no GPU is required. A bounded discovery probe is not needed because the main uncertainty is data-field availability and temporal validity, not model fitting. The future solver must newly fit the baseline and alternative models, estimate the 10-year cumulative-incidence risk difference, uncertainty, calibration, and sensitivity analyses, and write the required outputs. The outputs must include the population flow, exact source paths and field mappings, model coefficients/HRs, confidence intervals, event counts, calibration, sensitivity results, and a conclusion linked to computed outputs.

The method comparison is retained: the simple baseline is selected as the primary analysis because it directly tests the incremental question with transparent uncertainty; the learned alternative is retained because it can reveal non-linear regularity-duration interactions that the baseline loses. If the required sleep field or outcome time cannot be verified, the alternative is deferred and the study is blocked rather than replaced with a different scientific question.
