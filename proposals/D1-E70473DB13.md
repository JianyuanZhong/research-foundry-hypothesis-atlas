# Support-dependent normalization at ICU discharge with informative hospital discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe adult first-ICU-discharge cohort; derive pre-index physiology, observation masks, support intervals and support-transition features; fit a prespecified cause-specific baseline and a constrained latent recovery-state alternative; and produce locked held-out estimates.

Completion requires:

1. an auditable cohort/exclusion and temporal-integrity table;
2. feature, observation-coverage and support-evidence tables;
3. counts and uncertainty for prespecified support-context strata;
4. held-out risks for first subsequent ICU return, in-hospital death and alive hospital discharge;
5. Aalen–Johansen cumulative-incidence functions and cause-specific associations at 6, 24 and 48 hours;
6. calibration, IPCW Brier scores and bootstrap/subject-level uncertainty;
7. latent-state emission/transition diagnostics;
8. order, observation-mask, support-channel and discharge-process falsification analyses; and
9. a conclusion-to-output map that prevents unsupported clinical or causal claims.

No discovery probe, readiness result or model fit in this proposal is a clinical conclusion.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent established a clinically useful but potentially biased question: whether apparently normalized physiology at ICU outtime is support-dependent or recently withdrawn, and whether that context distinguishes early ICU return from death. Its unresolved limitation is that patients discharged alive from hospital before 48 hours were effectively censored even though discharge is related to recovery, disposition and support decisions. Consequently, a conventional 48-hour ICU-return estimate can conflate lower observed opportunity with lower risk.

Primary hypothesis: among adults discharged alive from a first ICU stay, patients with low final-window physiologic burden but active or recently withdrawn vasoactive/respiratory support have a higher cause-specific hazard and 24/48-hour cumulative incidence of first subsequent ICU return or in-hospital death, and a lower or delayed probability of alive hospital discharge, than patients with similarly low burden who are stably off support. The support-dependent versus stable-off-support contrast is prognostic and not a treatment effect.

The primary estimand is the between-stratum difference in Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence for each mutually exclusive first event—ICU return, death, or alive hospital discharge—by 48 hours after outtime, with discharge treated as a competing event. The observed-hospital estimand is deliberately limited: after alive discharge, post-discharge death and ICU care are not observed in this MIMIC hospitalization. Cause-specific hazard ratios are secondary associations, not causal effects. The parent composite and a seven-day ICU-return sensitivity analysis are retained, with the same observability caveat.

What is already supported is narrower: MIMIC-IV contains dated ICU discharge, hospital disposition/death, physiologic observations, vasoactive infusion intervals and recorded respiratory procedures; these permit an auditable prognostic comparison. Neither the expert seed, the parent proposal, nor the available local data establishes that support withdrawal causes failure, that a support-context marker improves bedside discharge decisions, or that ICU return is unplanned.

## Population, landmark and temporal boundaries

Use MIMIC-IV 3.1 adults with `patients.anchor_age >= 18`, a valid first ICU stay within each hospitalization, and alive discharge from that ICU stay:

- Select the earliest `icu/icustays.intime` for each `subject_id,hAdm_id` (actual field spelling is `hadm_id`) after joining to `hosp/admissions`. Require non-null and ordered `intime < outtime`, `outtime < dischtime`, and no `deathtime <= outtime`.
- Landmark is ICU `outtime`; index is the first eligible ICU discharge per hospitalization. Use no event, order, medication, support or note whose source time is after outtime.
- The feature window is `[outtime-12 hours, outtime)`, divided into preceding `[outtime-12h,outtime-6h)` and final `[outtime-6h,outtime)` six-hour windows. Resample chart observations into one-hour bins by chart time, not store time.
- Follow until `min(outtime+48 hours, dischtime)`. Define the first event as: (a) ICU return, a later `icustays.intime > outtime` for the same `subject_id,hadm_id` within the 48-hour window; (b) death, `admissions.deathtime > outtime` within the window and before ICU return; or (c) alive hospital discharge, `admissions.dischtime > outtime` before the other two events and before 48 hours. If death and discharge share a timestamp, classify death first. If ICU return and death tie, classify death first. If none occurs by 48 hours, censor administratively at 48 hours. Require `dischtime` for the competing-discharge analysis; report the excluded/missing-disposition count.
- Do not infer death timing from `hospital_expire_flag`, and do not call ICU return “unplanned readmission.” Report the proportion discharged before 48 hours and the identifiable follow-up time.

## Feature construction and exposure strata

Use one-hour medians for numeric chart streams and one-hour observation masks. Never impute missing as normal. Define hemodynamic burden as the fraction of observed MAP bins below 65 mmHg; preserve arterial and non-invasive MAP source. Define respiratory burden as the fraction of observed bins with FiO2 >=0.40 or O2 flow >=4 L/min, preserving native channels. PEEP, RR and SpO2 are secondary physiology, not substitutes for unobserved ventilator settings.

Require at least two observed MAP bins and two observed respiratory bins in each six-hour half-window for the primary phenotype; otherwise assign mixed/unavailable and report coverage. For each half-window derive domain burden fractions, median values, counts and masks. Derive support per hour by interval overlap:

- vasoactive support: any valid prespecified vasoactive `inputevents` interval overlapping the bin;
- recorded respiratory-procedure support: an intubation/NIV interval overlapping the bin, terminated by an extubation interval when an ordered, non-negative interval permits it;
- charted respiratory support: FiO2/O2-flow/PEEP evidence in `chartevents`.

The primary support-context variable is locked before outcome inspection:

1. **stable normalized off support:** final MAP and respiratory burden each <1/3, no vasoactive interval, no recorded respiratory-procedure overlap, and no charted respiratory-support threshold in the final six hours;
2. **normalized on support or recent withdrawal:** the same low final burdens, but valid vasoactive/procedure/chart support in the final window, or support in the preceding window that ends before outtime;
3. **residual support/physiology:** final MAP or respiratory burden >=1/3, retaining support source and domain;
4. **mixed/unavailable:** insufficient coverage, unresolved interval endpoints, discordant domains or support evidence that cannot be ordered.

The primary contrast is stratum 2 versus stratum 1 among adequately observed patients. Strata 3 and 4 are descriptive/secondary, not silently excluded. Thresholds, windows, item sets, invalid-endpoint rules and source-specific sensitivities are fixed before fitting. The support marker is not an assigned intervention.

## Baseline, substantive alternative and method choice

The simple baseline is two cause-specific Cox models—one for ICU return, one for death—with alive discharge as a competing event and the four-level context variable as the main exposure. Covariates are the parent paired respiratory/hemodynamic phenotype, early-to-final burden change, final values and masks, age, gender, ICU LOS, first/last careunit, admission type and a small prespecified admission covariate set. A nested snapshot baseline uses only final-window values, masks, context and case mix. Estimate cause-specific hazards, Aalen–Johansen CIFs, absolute CIF differences, calibration intercept/slope and IPCW Brier scores. Do not interpret a discharge coefficient as a patient benefit.

The substantive alternative is a constrained four-state hourly latent recovery model, fitted only on the training split. Its observed emissions are the same MAP/respiratory streams, masks and support indicators. States are initialized and interpreted as stable-off-support, normalized-on-support/recent-withdrawal, residual respiratory, and residual hemodynamic/dual burden; a sparse transition structure permits persistence and withdrawal-associated transitions. Each state has cause-specific discrete hazards for eight six-hour post-index bins, with competing alive discharge included. Fit emissions/transitions by EM (or a documented Bayesian equivalent), propagate posterior state probabilities rather than hard labels, and evaluate held-out competing-event risks. This model can reveal persistence, support withdrawal followed by destabilization, and cross-domain order that fixed summary features lose; state labels remain analytic constructs, not bedside diagnoses.

Use patient-level stratified train/validation/test splits (70/15/15), with three fixed split seeds. Fit thresholds, scaling, state parameters and all imputation/normalization rules on training data only; lock test predictions. Evaluate return, death and discharge jointly with cause-specific and integrated Brier scores, time-dependent calibration, CIF separation, state persistence/transition diagnostics and bootstrap confidence intervals or subject-level bootstrap across split seeds. The minimum clinically meaningful margin for the primary CIF contrast must be declared before outcome inspection; if no defensible margin is approved, report estimates and 95% intervals without a binary claim.

Compute planning: streaming extraction from the compressed archive and Cox/Aalen–Johansen fits are expected CPU-feasible; approximate future solver cost is 1–4 hours on up to 16 CPUs and <64 GB RAM, unverified in this episode. The latent model is expected to fit within the same envelope if features are materialized in a compact hourly table. A single optional A100 job may accelerate a later masked-sequence sensitivity, but no GPU is required. These are planning estimates, not measured execution, and discovery compute is separate from the solver envelope.

A masked GRU or temporal-convolution model remains deferred. It could better capture irregular order and mask patterns, but the parent already considered a masked sequence comparison and this successor’s scientific uncertainty is informative discharge plus support-state persistence. Revisit it if the latent model fails convergence or held-out diagnostics show that state compression loses reproducible order. The deferred branch is not rejected on CPU/GPU grounds.

## Falsification and interpretation

Prespecified falsifications include:

- permuting hourly physiology/support order within stay;
- mask-only versus mask-removed models;
- removing support channels and removing physiology channels;
- replacing interval overlap with storetime or last-value support coding;
- arterial versus non-invasive MAP and FiO2 versus O2-flow sensitivities;
- threshold/window perturbations;
- careunit and admission-type stratification;
- a negative-control pre-index horizon in which no post-index event can be caused by the recorded transition;
- treating alive discharge as censoring versus a competing event, with the estimand difference reported rather than selected post hoc; and
- excluding procedure-derived respiratory support to test whether the signal is driven solely by unreliable procedure intervals.

Supportive results require adequate coverage/positivity, reproducible separation of normalized-on/recent-withdrawal from stable-off-support for ICU return and/or death, a stable discharge CIF estimate, uncertainty excluding the prespecified null/margin, and latent-state/calibration improvement that survives order, mask, coding and context falsifications. This supports a prognostic hypothesis and motivates clinician-adjudicated prospective validation; it does not support withholding discharge or continuing support.

Adverse results are null/reversed contrasts, strong dependence on discharge censoring choice, unstable support strata, no calibrated improvement, convergence failure, or disappearance after mask/documentation/context controls. These falsify the operationalized support-dependent-normalization hypothesis as a useful retrospective marker.

Inconclusive results include rare strata/events, inadequate six-hour coverage, non-positivity, unreliable interval endpoints, missing/contradictory death or discharge times, wide intervals crossing the clinical margin, or large disagreement between observed and latent support definitions. Report feasibility and uncertainty without a clinical rule.

MIMIC-IV cannot adjudicate bedside work of breathing, actual ventilator settings between observations, clinician intent, planned versus unplanned ICU return, goals of care, treatment limitations, staffing, discharge rationale, or post-discharge outcomes. The note text is not a validated readiness label; radiology images and raw waveforms are unavailable. External validation in eICU or another hospital, clinician adjudication of support/discharge readiness, and a prospective or causal study are required before adoption.
