# Does interhospital-transfer status retain a clinically meaningful post-landmark deterioration association after arrival state, trajectory, hospital, and observation-process adjustment?

## Scientific opening, evidence, and hypothesis

Interhospital transfer is clinically consequential, but observed outcome differences combine at least four processes: illness severe enough to prompt referral, selection of patients judged transportable, receiving-hospital case mix, and delayed or denser observation after arrival. In severe AKI, transfer was not associated with higher adjusted mortality and the authors specifically identified healthy-enough-to-transfer selection, immortal time, unknown transfer rationale, and unavailable travel time as threats [K1]. A nationwide matched study of ventilated sepsis likewise found similar mortality but longer stay after transfer, while lacking time-stamped physiology, transfer timing/reason, and sending/receiving hospital detail [K2]. Conversely, a transferred-only tertiary-MICU cohort found that transfer-day organ dysfunction and arrival oxygen requirement predicted early death, showing why arrival physiology matters without identifying a transfer effect [K3].

The strongest claim currently supported is therefore: transferred patients can differ materially in acute physiology, selection, and length of stay, and adjusted mortality evidence is mixed and context dependent. Existing evidence does **not** establish that transfer itself harms or benefits patients.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adult first ICU stays that remain alive and in the receiving ICU at hour 24, in receiving hospitals with adequate transferred/direct support, concordant interhospital-transfer status retains at least a 2-percentage-point incremental association with new organ support or death during hours 24–72 after transparent arrival-organ-injury adjustment; this association is not reduced below that threshold by an ordered multivariate trajectory/observation model, hospital control, or end-of-life and competing-discharge checks.

The leading explanation is a residual transferred-patient penalty not represented by measured arrival injury—potentially pre-transfer course, transport stress, delayed definitive care, or unrecorded referral complexity. The strongest rival is that early multivariate trajectory, transportability selection, hospital referral patterns, denser surveillance, and slower discharge fully account for the apparent penalty. These explanations differ observably: a transfer contrast that remains clinically meaningful after an ordered, observation-aware model and survives death-only, support-label, hospital, and discharge checks favors a residual risk marker; marked attenuation below 2 points favors the rival. Neither result identifies a causal transport mechanism.

Clinical value is risk-assessment and triage calibration at receiving ICUs. A persistent residual marker would justify retaining transfer provenance in early deterioration assessment and motivate prospective collection of sending-hospital/transport data. Complete attenuation would argue against treating transfer status itself as a risk penalty once early physiology and trajectory are available.

## Deliverable and smallest decisive experiment

The deliverable is newly fitted, cross-validated evidence on one frozen cohort:

1. an exact cohort/support/clock audit;
2. a transparent baseline estimate of the transfer-standardized 72-hour deterioration contrast;
3. the same estimate from a substantively ordered, observation-aware alternative using identical source rows, feature families, outcomes, and person folds;
4. held-out calibration and competing-risk performance;
5. bootstrap uncertainty and predeclared supportive/adverse/inconclusive classification;
6. observation-intensity, end-of-life, disposition, repeat-person, and transfer-semantics falsification reports.

This is an associational boundary test, not an estimate of the effect of transferring a patient.

## Population, exposure, clocks, and estimand

Index rows are `patient.patientunitstayid`. Map `age='> 89'` to 90 for adjustment. Keep adults age at least 18, `unitstaytype='admit'`, and the first ICU stay in each `patienthealthsystemstayid`, ordered by valid `unitvisitnumber` then `patientunitstayid`. Require `hospitaladmitoffset<=0`, `unitdischargeoffset>1440`, `hospitaldischargeoffset>=unitdischargeoffset`, and alive/in the ICU at minute 1440. Exclude exact `carePlanGeneral.cplitemvalue='Comfort measures only'` recorded by minute 1440. Do not exclude DNR in the primary cohort; it is adjusted and then excluded in a sensitivity. Impossible negative event chronology is excluded stream-wise and reported.

The exposure is deliberately strict:

- transferred: both `hospitaladmitsource='Other Hospital'` and `unitadmitsource='Other Hospital'`;
- direct: concordant `Emergency Department/ Emergency Department` or `Direct Admit/Direct Admit`;
- all discordant source combinations are excluded from the primary contrast and retained in a semantic audit.

This defines arrival to the current hospitalization and immediate ICU source concordantly. It does not distinguish referring ED from referring ICU, identify transfer indication, or establish transfer time.

The primary analysis is restricted before outcome inspection to hospitals with at least 20 transferred and 100 direct eligible stays. The bounded audit found 19 such hospitals, 1,608 transferred and 14,986 direct stays before the comfort-only exclusion. Across the broader concordant cohort it found 2,045 transferred and 47,068 direct stays; 2,030/2,045 transferred stays had at least one direct comparator at their hospital. These are feasibility counts, not outcome-model results.

Temporal boundaries are fixed:

- arrival snapshot: minutes 0–360;
- pre-landmark trajectory: minutes 0–1440, represented in six four-hour bins;
- landmark: minute 1440;
- post-landmark deterioration window: `(1440,4320]` (hours 24–72);
- seven-day sensitivity: `(1440,11520]`;
- event clocks, entry/revision clocks, and discharge clocks remain distinct. Values are eligible only if their event and, when available, entry/revision offsets are no later than the prediction landmark.

The primary estimand is the transferred-population standardized **associational** risk difference at 72 hours: for held-out transferred stays, the mean predicted cumulative incidence of deterioration when only the transfer indicator is set to 1 minus that when it is set to 0, with hospital and all pre-landmark features held fixed. Report this from both models with a 95% person-and-hospital clustered bootstrap interval. This model-based toggle is not a counterfactual treatment effect because exchangeability and intervention consistency are not credible.

## Variables and primary outcome

The common raw input is the exact pre-minute-1440 panel used by both models. Plausible ranges/units are frozen before fitting; values outside them become missing with an audit flag. Each four-hour bin contains count, missingness, time since last value, median, minimum, maximum, and last value for:

- cardiovascular: heart rate, invasive/noninvasive systolic/diastolic/mean pressure; vasoactive infusion presence;
- respiratory: respiratory rate, SpO2, PaO2, PaCO2, FiO2, pH, PEEP, and ventilation/oxygen-support indicators;
- renal/metabolic: creatinine, BUN, sodium, potassium, bicarbonate/HCO3, lactate, glucose, and urine/output where a clinically adjudicated intake/output mapping is available;
- hematologic/hepatic: WBC, hemoglobin/hematocrit, platelets, bilirubin, albumin;
- neurologic: available GCS eye/verbal/motor components; sedative exposure is an observation/confounding flag, not proof of coma;
- process: number of lab, periodic-vital, aperiodic-vital, respiratory-chart, treatment, and nurse-chart rows; unique observed hours; longest gap; entry-minus-event delay where available.

Static inputs are age, sex, ethnicity, admission diagnosis category, unit type, hospital, admission hour, `hospitaladmitoffset`, admission weight, first-day APACHE physiology components (with `-1` treated as missing), ventilation/dialysis indicators, comorbidity fields, and hospital bed/teaching/region. No APACHE predicted or actual outcome, discharge field, or post-landmark value enters either model.

Primary deterioration is the first of:

- hospital death;
- new invasive mechanical ventilation;
- new vasoactive infusion;
- new renal replacement therapy,

during `(1440,4320]`, among patients without that same support documented during `[0,1440]`. Mechanical ventilation and RRT require a prespecified treatment-string family plus corroboration from respiratory-care/charting or dialysis intake/output/lab evidence where available. Vasoactive support uses a clinician-approved normalized drug-name list in `infusionDrug`; a positive numeric rate is required. Exact source-specific mappings are frozen outcome-blind and attached to output.

Alive ICU/unit discharge before 72 hours is a competing event, not “no deterioration.” Emit a four-state 72-hour endpoint: deterioration first, alive ICU discharge first, alive without either at 72 hours, and indeterminate chronology. Hospital live discharge is separately retained. A death-only seven-day cumulative-incidence endpoint and each support component are mandatory sensitivities. A bounded extraction found 137 primary composite events among 2,043 transferred stays and 2,807 among 46,919 direct stays after comfort-only exclusion in the broad cohort; these unadjusted counts are feasibility checks, not evidence for the hypothesis.

## Comparable models on identical data and splits

All rows belonging to one `uniquepid` stay in one of five folds, stratified within hospital and transfer group. The split manifest is generated once and used by every model, imputation, tuning step, bootstrap, and sensitivity. The audit found 3,549 repeated persons and 717 cross-hospital persons in the broad analytic cohort, so stay-level random splitting is forbidden. Primary folds retain all 19 hospitals for within-hospital estimation; leave-one-hospital-out is a transportability sensitivity, not a replacement estimand.

Missing values are represented by missingness and elapsed-time features; no future value is carried backward. Training-fold medians/ranges and categorical levels are applied to held-out folds. Hyperparameters are selected only inside training folds by log loss/calibration, never by the transfer coefficient.

### B0: transparent arrival/trajectory baseline

Fit a multinomial elastic-net model for the four-state outcome. For each raw feature family, B0 receives the same panel but reduces it transparently to first-six-hour worst/last value, 24-hour worst/last value, ordinary least-squares slope across the six bins, observed-bin count, and longest gap. Include hospital fixed effects and prespecified transfer interactions only with invasive ventilation at the landmark and broad admission category. Fit paired versions without and with the transfer indicator. Estimate the transfer-standardized deterioration risk difference, calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, log loss, and cumulative-incidence calibration in held-out folds.

B0 is adequate if ordering beyond a slope and missingness summaries adds no consequential information. It remains interpretable enough to identify which measured organ domains attenuate the crude contrast.

### M1: ordered latent organ-state and observation alternative

Fit a six-step hierarchical state-space model (a constrained GRU implementation is acceptable) to the identical raw panel and folds. At each four-hour step, a 16–32-dimensional latent state updates from values, masks, elapsed times, and process counts. Separate heads predict the next-bin observed values and observation masks; a competing-risk head predicts the four-state outcome. Hospital random intercepts are regularized; transfer is entered only in the final outcome head, not the state transition. Train paired versions without and with transfer.

M1 can reveal worsening/recovery order, asynchronous organ changes, and informative observation timing that B0’s extrema and slopes lose. It does not reveal a biological mechanism. M1 is scientifically preferred only if held-out calibration is noninferior to B0 (absolute calibration-slope deviation no worse by >0.10 and Brier score no worse by >0.005), next-bin mask calibration is adequate by transfer group/hospital, and its conclusion is stable across three seeds. Otherwise B0 governs and M1 is reported as failed/inconclusive, not used to manufacture attenuation.

Estimated solver resources: extraction and B0, 8–16 CPUs/64–96 GiB for 1–3 hours; 2,000 clustered bootstraps, 16 CPUs for 2–4 hours; M1, one allocated A100-80GB plus 8 CPUs/64 GiB for approximately 1–3 hours per outer fold, with checkpoints. These are planning estimates. The completed census/observation audit ran in about 49 seconds on 8 CPUs/32 GiB after a CSV-quoting repair. GPU use is conditional on M1 and is not scientific merit.

## Support, artifact, and falsification gates

Before interpreting a transfer contrast, all must hold:

1. **Semantics/support:** at least 90% of rows with `hospitaladmitsource='Other Hospital'` are concordant at unit source; at least 15 hospitals, 1,200 transferred stays, and 100 transferred primary events remain. Report every discordant source combination.
2. **Overlap:** within each hospital, transferred and direct groups remain represented; at least 80% of transferred stays lie inside the direct group’s 1st–99th percentile multivariate support, weighted effective sample size is at least 50% of transferred N, and weighted absolute standardized differences are below 0.10 for prespecified static/arrival features. No extrapolative trimming may be tuned by outcome.
3. **Observation process:** held-out next-bin observation calibration error is below 0.05 overall and by transfer group; transfer-group differences in support-label PPV and missing-window coverage are at most 0.10. Refit after matching/downsampling direct observation opportunities to the transferred distribution and after excluding the sparsest quartile.
4. **Outcome validity:** a blinded clinician audit of 240 packets, balanced by transfer, outcome component, and hospital, must show weighted PPV at least 0.80 (lower 95% bound at least 0.70) for new support and between-group PPV difference at most 0.10. Packets hide transfer source and post-event disposition.
5. **EOL/disposition:** estimates must be directionally stable after excluding any pre-landmark DNR, after excluding death within six hours of the landmark, and in the four-state competing-outcome model. Death-only and support-only results must not point in materially contradictory directions. Report ICU and hospital live-discharge cumulative incidence.
6. **Hospital/person robustness:** no hospital supplies more than 25% of transferred information; leave-one-hospital-out estimates do not reverse direction in more than 20% of supported sites; the cross-hospital repeated-person exclusion changes the risk difference by less than 1 point.
7. **Clock/label robustness:** conservative entry/revision-time availability, landmark shifts to hours 18 and 30, exact duplicate collapse, and source-family removal do not move either model’s risk difference by more than 1 point or across both 0 and 2 points.

The smallest continue/revise/stop decision is made without changing the estimand, threshold, outcome, or subgroup after viewing results.

- **Supportive:** all gates pass; B0 and admissible M1 each estimate a risk difference of at least 2 points; M1’s lower 95% bound is above 0; and M1 attenuation from B0 is less than 50%. This supports transfer provenance as a residual receiving-ICU risk marker. It does not establish harm from transfer or a transport mechanism.
- **Adverse to the hypothesis:** all gates pass but B0’s upper bound is below 2 points, or admissible M1’s upper bound is below 2 points with at least 50% attenuation from B0. This supports the rival that measured trajectory/hospital/observation processes account for any clinically meaningful penalty. It does not establish equivalence or benefit unless precision also excludes the prespecified margin.
- **Inconclusive:** an interval spans both 0 and 2 points; B0 and M1 disagree without M1 meeting admissibility; event/support/PPV/overlap gates fail; hospital effects are unstable; or observation/downsampling and disposition checks materially change the result. Defer the transfer-penalty claim and obtain adjudication or richer transfer data.

A positive transfer coefficient paired with no held-out calibration or no incremental risk separation is not supportive. A precise null can still be clinically informative.

## Audits already completed and limits

The outcome-blind structural audit verified 200,859 patient rows, no duplicate `patientunitstayid`, distinct hospital- versus unit-source fields, and substantial repeated-person structure. In the broad concordant hour-24 cohort, transferred patients had denser early observation: median 57 versus 41 lab rows, 6 versus 5 lab-observed hours, and 4 versus 1 respiratory-chart hours. Seven-day live discharge was 977/2,045 versus 30,299/47,068. Thus surveillance and competing disposition are empirically strong rivals, not decorative sensitivities. Crude event counts were inspected only for feasibility; no adjusted hypothesis result has been computed.

The audit script initially failed because auto-detection did not recognize quoted commas in `respiratoryCharting`; forcing standard CSV quoting repaired ingestion. This is an execution detail to retain in the compiler.

## Clinical-adjudication and claims boundary

Computationally checkable claims include source semantics/counts, clocks, cohort flow, joins, support/overlap, feature provenance, identical splits, model fitting, calibration, risk differences, uncertainty, observation/downsampling, competing-event estimates, and whether the reported conclusion follows the gates.

Clinical review is essential for vasoactive normalization, whether treatment rows represent genuinely new support rather than delayed documentation, urine/RRT mapping, invasive ventilation onset, and goals-of-care interpretation. eICU lacks referring-hospital trajectory, transfer request/acceptance and indication, transport duration/events, clinician intent, bed availability, and patients considered but not transferred. Its public notes are predominantly structured and cannot reconstruct these. Therefore neither an automatic verifier nor this study can establish a causal transfer effect, appropriateness of transfer, preventability, or a transport mechanism. Those require clinician adjudication, linked sending/transport records, and preferably a prospective or quasi-experimental study.

## Alternatives not selected

A propensity-matched mortality-only study is simpler but loses early organ-support deterioration and treats discharge poorly; it remains a mandatory sensitivity. A fully causal emulation was rejected because treatment versions, referral eligibility, transfer time zero, and sending-hospital confounders are unavailable. A large transformer was deferred: six ordered bins and roughly 16,000 supported stays do not justify it before the compact state-space model demonstrates stable additional information. Revisit richer sequence learning only if M1 is admissible, ordering materially changes the conclusion, and more temporal resolution is shown to be supported.
