# Provisional hypothesis: discordant HbA1c rise and weight trajectory predict incident diabetes

## Status

This is a provisional, evidence-bounded draft registered for episode 8. It is not a result, not a selection, and not evidence that the hypothesis is true. The prior board contains a repairable UK Biobank restart on this question. Exact source/table bindings, repeat-measurement overlap, incident-diabetes ascertainment, event support, and the three inspected key references remain to be verified.

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with two valid assessment-center visits, no diabetes or cancer before the second visit, a comparable rise in HbA1c is associated with higher subsequent incident-diabetes risk when accompanied by weight loss than when accompanied by weight gain or stability.

The clinically relevant question is whether the same worsening glycemic state should be interpreted differently when body weight is falling. This could identify people who need earlier metabolic follow-up, but the association would not establish that weight loss causes diabetes, that it identifies occult cancer, or that treatment should change without clinical review.

## Uncertainties and missing evidence

- The exact assessment-center table, columns, visit dates, and participant key for two valid HbA1c and weight measurements are not yet verified in this episode.
- The incident-diabetes outcome definition, diagnosis source, date field, prevalent-diabetes exclusion, and cancer exclusion are not yet verified.
- Repeat-measurement overlap, missingness, event support, and follow-up time are not yet quantified.
- Weight loss may be intentional, treatment-related, or a consequence of illness; available data may not distinguish these mechanisms.
- The strongest plausible rival is that apparent discordance reflects current severity, ascertainment, or selection into repeat visits rather than a distinct biological state.
- Three inspected key works with frozen receipts are absent and are required before final selection.

## Falsifiable test

Define index as the second valid assessment-center visit. Include participants with valid HbA1c and weight at both visits, no diabetes or cancer before index, and adequate follow-up. Compare subsequent incident-diabetes incidence among participants with a prespecified HbA1c rise and weight loss versus weight gain/stability, adjusting for baseline HbA1c, weight, age, sex, BMI, and relevant available covariates. Use a simple adjusted baseline/trajectory model and a prespecified interaction or trajectory model as the substantive alternative. The interaction/trajectory model can reveal whether the weight trajectory modifies the HbA1c-rise association, information a single baseline adjustment loses.

Supportive evidence would be a reproducible, uncertainty-quantified higher risk for the discordant loss group, robust to prespecified exclusions and sensitivity analyses. Adverse evidence would be no interaction, reversal, or instability across measurement periods and adjustment sets. Inconclusive evidence would be insufficient events, poor overlap, wide uncertainty, or inability to define the endpoint; it would not refute the hypothesis. A positive association remains prognostic and does not establish causality, occult cancer, or a treatment rule.
