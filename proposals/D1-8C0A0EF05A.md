# Candidate: Does acidemia-associated occult arterial hyperoxemia at SpO2 96–97% transport across EICU hospitals?

## Clinical question and advance

When a mechanically ventilated adult has an apparently acceptable monitor SpO2 of 96–97%, is the probability of arterial PaO2 >120 mm Hg materially higher during acidemia, and is that measurement relationship reproducible across hospitals?

A reproducible relationship would identify when an SpO2-only protocol is physiologically unreliable and justify prospective testing of selective ABG reassessment. Failure to transport would argue against a universal pH rule. This study does not test oxygen toxicity or benefit from lowering FiO2.

The strongest available evidence supports only this: a 2017–2022 single-center Zurich study aligned ABGs with preceding five-minute SpO2/FiO2 medians and found SpO2 >=98% often accompanied PaO2 >120/>150, while 96–97% was generally physiologic and pH/FiO2 modified the relationship (Delgado et al., DOI 10.3390/antiox15020235; full XML source [source checksum], [source checksum], inspected). A 2026 meta-analysis of 11 randomized trials/20,786 ventilated adults found no overall mortality difference between conservative and liberal oxygen strategies (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.95–1.10), so a blood-gas association is not treatment evidence (DOI 10.3389/fmed.2026.1697749; full XML source [source checksum], inspected).

**Untested primary hypothesis:** among eligible EICU observations with preceding monitor SpO2 96–97%, same-panel acidemia (pH <7.35) versus normal pH (7.35–7.45) has a FiO2- and case-mix-standardized risk difference of at least +10 percentage points for PaO2 >120 mm Hg. Support also requires a positive adjusted contrast in at least four of five or 70% (whichever is larger) adequately sized hospitals. This is a noncausal measurement/transportability estimand.

The advance is a prespecified multi-hospital test under a coarser monitoring system with explicit specimen-revision, pulse-oximetry, ventilator, and FiO2 semantics. The inherited 24-hour design was infeasible for its hospital gate; extending observation to 72 hours preserves the estimand and yields a computable transport stratum.

## Variables and estimands

Primary outcome is observed PaO2 >120; secondary outcomes are PaO2 >150, continuous PaO2, and PaO2 <60 safety description. These are arterial blood-gas states, not tissue exposure or injury.

Primary exposure is same-panel pH <7.35 versus 7.35–7.45 within SpO2 96–97. Alkalemia and continuous spline pH are secondary. FiO2 is continuous with cohort-percentile spline knots and summaries at 30%, 40%, and 60%. Prespecified adjustment variables are age, gender, ethnicity, unit type, admission source, six-hour block, FiO2, and same-panel PaCO2. Missing PaCO2 gets a missing indicator plus median imputation; outcome, pH, SpO2, and FiO2 are not imputed. APACHE variables are sensitivity-only.

The primary estimand is the marginal standardized acidemia-minus-normal risk difference among observed 96–97 measurements, standardized to that stratum's covariate distribution. It is descriptive and noncausal.

A separate exploratory decision estimand asks whether most recent prior pH 30–360 minutes before the current draw improves prediction of current PaO2 >120 beyond current SpO2/FiO2. Same-panel pH is forbidden. This could inform a future selective-repeat-ABG study but cannot carry the primary claim.

## Analysis and baselines

- Report complete attrition, revision exclusions, units/ranges, documentation lags, observations/stays/persons/hospitals, and raw PaO2 >120/>150 prevalence by integer SpO2, pH group, FiO2 band, block, and hospital.
- For the primary 96–97 stratum, exclude alkalemia and fit a prespecified logistic GEE for PaO2 >120 clustered by uniquepid, with a binary acidemia indicator, hospital intercepts, a restricted spline for FiO2, age, gender, ethnicity, unit type, admission source, PaCO2 plus its missing indicator, and six-hour block. Standardize by switching the binary acidemia indicator in every analysis row. Use 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples for the primary 95% CI and report refit failures. A separate secondary model over SpO2 92–99 uses continuous spline pH plus pH×SpO2 and FiO2×SpO2 interactions.
- An adequate hospital has >=30 band observations, >=5 acidemic, >=10 normal-pH, and >=5 PaO2 >120 events. Estimate a reduced hospital-specific adjusted contrast (FiO2 spline, age, PaCO2, block), using penalized logistic regression for separation. Pool log-odds contrasts with REML random effects; retain risk differences and exact denominators. Report tau-squared, I-squared, and prediction interval.
- Portable measurement baselines over SpO2 92–99 are M0 SpO2; M1 SpO2+FiO2; M2 M1+same-panel pH/interactions. Leave one adequate hospital out, never include hospital ID, and report calibration-in-the-large, slope, Brier, log loss, AUROC/AUPRC, and bootstrap M2−M1 differences. This is not a deployable pH rule.
- Exploratory lagged models are D0 current SpO2; D1 +current FiO2; D2 +prior pH, prior PaCO2, and elapsed time. All chart and entry times must be <= current draw. Evaluate held-out-hospital calibration and decision-curve net benefit for “obtain ABG now” at 10%, 20%, 30%; never interpret this as evidence to reduce FiO2.
- Sensitivities: PaO2 >150; first 24 hours; one observation/stay; first six-hour block; FiO2 lookback 60; future-vulnerable nearest FiO2 ±30 and SpO2 ±5; alternate FiO2 labels; broader/narrower ventilation definitions; complete PaCO2; APACHE adjustment; omit hospitals 458/420/443 individually; exclude neuro/cardiothoracic units and ECMO-coded stays only after exact mapping validation.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive:** standardized RD point >=+10, 95% CI excludes 0; positive adjusted contrast in at least 4/5 or 70% of adequate hospitals; random-effects prediction interval excludes clinically important opposite effect below −5; and M2 improves held-out Brier or log loss over M1 with 95% CI excluding zero and calibration slope 0.8–1.2. This supports a transportable measurement warning and prospective selective-ABG protocol study only.

**Adverse:** upper 95% RD <+10 falsifies the prespecified material effect; upper limit <0 indicates opposite association. Nonpositive contrast in most adequate hospitals or worse calibration with no M2 gain is adverse to transport. This rejects a universal pH warning, not the possibility of oxygen harm or benefit.

**Inconclusive:** CI spans 0 and +10; fewer than five hospitals satisfy frozen adequacy; prediction interval spans below −5 and above +10; <80% retain preceding SpO2 among otherwise eligible gases; results reverse under 24/72-hour, FiO2-window, or ventilation sensitivities; or model/bootstrap fails. Report inconclusive, not “no association.” Exploratory D2 can never upgrade primary support.

## Verification and adjudication boundary

The verifier can check source hashes/headers, latest revisions, type 7, exact-offset pivot, ranges, joins, first-unit/discharge/72-hour bounds, ventilation predicates, FiO2 conversion/label/window, last nonfuture SpO2, thinning, counts, formulas, bootstraps, calibration, and conclusion gates. Fixtures must reject correct computations paired with toxicity/treatment-benefit claims; accept correct supportive, adverse, and inconclusive interpretations; and reject future FiO2/SpO2 or same-panel pH in D2.

Automation cannot establish one arterial specimen, whether zero vent ends are missing/open, airway persistence, artifact-free SpO2, or delivered FiO2. Site informatics and clinical review must adjudicate these. Raw monitor/device data are needed for timing/delivered FiO2; prospective external validation is required for lagged prediction; a randomized protocol with hypoxemia and patient-centered outcomes is required before changing oxygen targets or claiming benefit.
