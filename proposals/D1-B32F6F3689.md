# Residual, repeatedly observed physiologic instability in the final 24 hours before ICU discharge

## Clinical question and hypothesis

After an ICU stay has ended, does repeatedly charted residual physiologic instability in
the preceding 24 hours identify patients at increased risk of a subsequent ICU return or
in-hospital death during the same admission?

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among patients alive at ICU discharge and still
hospitalized, greater final-24-hour instability burden—defined before looking at outcomes
as the fraction of observed domain-hours with abnormal oxygenation/support, hemodynamics,
respiration, or temperature—is associated with a higher 48-hour risk of the composite
of subsequent ICU stay or in-hospital death. A prespecified secondary hypothesis is that
instability burden is associated with distinct cumulative incidences of a subsequent ICU
stay and death before a subsequent ICU stay.

This is a predictive association hypothesis, not a claim that instability causes
readmission, that discharge caused harm, or that changing a physiologic variable would
improve outcomes.

## What is known versus unresolved

The inspected expert card supports only the importance of testing persistent oxygen
requirements and fluctuating vital signs before ICU discharge; it supplies no cutoff,
effect estimate, or validated clinical label
([references/expert-seeds/cards/mimic-10.md](../references/expert-seeds/cards/mimic-10.md)).
The local MIMIC guide and schemas establish that ICU stays, timestamped chart events,
the item dictionary, transfers, and admission outcomes are available. They do not
establish that any particular instability score predicts outcomes, nor that charting is
complete or clinically equivalent across patients.

The unresolved claim is therefore narrower and testable: does a prespecified,
repeated-measure summary add reproducible prognostic information beyond simple
discharge-time and admission context, and does it discriminate a return-to-ICU signal
from death before return? The substantive advance over a one-time last vital or generic
ICU severity score is an auditable, domain-specific measure of residual instability that
retains observation coverage and missingness, explicitly handles competing first events,
and tests simple transportable baselines. It remains an association study.

## Population and index event

The unit is an ICU stay (`stay_id`), with clustering and sensitivity analyses at
`subject_id`.

Include an index stay from `icu/icustays` when:

1. `intime` and `outtime` are present and `intime < outtime`;
2. the corresponding `hosp/admissions` row by `subject_id, hadm_id` is present and
   has `dischtime > outtime`, so there is positive post-ICU in-hospital follow-up;
3. the patient is alive at `outtime`: `deathtime` is null or
   `deathtime > outtime`;
4. the stay is not excluded because of impossible chronology (admission after
   discharge, negative interval, or duplicate `stay_id`).

This includes adults and older adults as represented by MIMIC; no age cutoff is imposed
because the local patient schema uses `anchor_age` and represents age >89 as 91.
The primary analysis includes every qualifying stay, including multiple stays in one
hospitalization, with subject-clustered uncertainty. A sensitivity analysis uses only the
first qualifying ICU discharge per `hadm_id`; another uses only the final qualifying
ICU discharge per `hadm_id`. These analyses distinguish repeated index opportunities
from a single-hospitalization estimand.

The index time is the canonical `icu/icustays.outtime`. The feature window is the
half-open interval [`outtime - 24 hours`, `outtime`), and no chart event at or after
`outtime` enters a feature. The outcome clock starts at `outtime`.

## Item-level oxygen and vital definitions

Only the following `chartevents` item IDs are eligible. The item dictionary, not a
free-text label search, defines membership.

- Heart rate: `220045`, Heart Rate, bpm.
- Mean arterial pressure: arterial `220052`, Arterial Blood Pressure mean, mmHg;
  non-invasive `220181`, Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean, mmHg. Systolic/diastolic
  items `220050/220051` and `220179/220180` are retained as secondary descriptive
  variables, not substituted into the primary MAP definition.
- Respiratory rate: `220210`, Respiratory Rate, insp/min. Ventilator set/spontaneous/
  total rates `224688/224689/224690` are not silently mixed into the primary RR.
- Pulse oxygen saturation: `220277`, O2 saturation pulseoxymetry, %.
- Temperature: `223762`, Temperature Celsius, °C; `223761`, Temperature Fahrenheit,
  °F. Fahrenheit is converted by (F - 32) / 1.8. If both are present in the same hour,
  Celsius is preferred.
- Inspired oxygen fraction: `223835`, Inspired O2 Fraction, unit `None`. After
  numeric parsing, values in (1.5, 100] are divided by 100; values in [0, 1.5] are
  interpreted as fractions. Retain only normalized values in [0.21, 1.00].
- Oxygen flow: `223834`, O2 Flow, L/min; `227287`, O2 Flow (additional cannula),
  L/min; `227582`, BiPap O2 Flow, L/min. These are retained separately and an
  auxiliary flow summary is the maximum available among them at a given hourly bin;
  they are not added, because the records may represent alternative or partial
  channels.
- Oxygen delivery device: `226732`, O2 Delivery Device(s), text. Preserve the raw
  observed category for descriptive tables and a regularized top-category/other
  sensitivity feature only after deriving the category list from the development set.
- Ventilator context: `223848`, Ventilator Type, and `223849`, Ventilator Mode, text,
  are descriptive sensitivity variables only. They are not treated as oxygen dose.

Alarm items, Apache-derived scores, arterial oxygen saturation `220227`, ECMO oxygen
variables, and unrelated flow/oxygenator items are excluded from the primary exposure.
This prevents alarm settings, derived severity scores, blood-gas variables, and ECMO
device parameters from being misrepresented as bedside oxygen requirement.

For every numeric item, discard rows with missing/non-numeric `valuenum`, nonfinite
values, or `warning`-flagged values if the warning is explicitly a data-quality flag.
Apply item-specific data-quality bounds before aggregation: HR 20–250 bpm; MAP 30–250
mmHg; RR 4–80 insp/min; SpO₂ 50–100%; temperature 25–45 °C; FiO₂ normalized
0.21–1.00; flow 0–100 L/min. These bounds are plausibility filters, not diagnoses or
clinical normal ranges. Do not clip retained values.

## Repeated measurement construction and exposure

Use fixed one-hour bins aligned backward from `outtime`: bin k is
[`outtime-(k+1)h, outtime-kh`) for k=0,...,23. For each item and bin, use the median
of valid numeric rows. Text device/mode values use the last charted value in the bin.
There is no forward fill across an unobserved hour and no imputation before the
observation audit.

Define four domains per hourly bin:

- Oxygen/support is observed if at least one valid normalized FiO₂ or flow measure is
  present; it is abnormal if FiO₂ ≥0.40 or auxiliary flow ≥4 L/min.
- Hemodynamics is observed if a valid arterial or non-invasive MAP, or valid HR, is
  present; it is abnormal if MAP <65 mmHg, HR <50 bpm, or HR >110 bpm.
- Respiration is observed if valid RR or SpO₂ is present; it is abnormal if RR <8 or
  >24 insp/min, or SpO₂ <90%.
- Temperature is observed if valid Celsius-equivalent temperature is present; it is
  abnormal if <36.0 °C or ≥38.5 °C.

The thresholds are fixed clinical screening thresholds selected before outcome analysis;
they are not claims of disease or treatment indication. When a domain has multiple
measurements, the domain is abnormal if any of its valid component medians crosses its
threshold. For each stay, the primary exposure is:

`instability_burden = abnormal observed domain-hours / all observed domain-hours`.

Also prespecify:

- `instability_domain_count`: number of domains with burden ≥0.25;
- `persistent_instability`: at least two of the last three non-empty hourly bins have
  one or more abnormal domains, provided those bins are within the final six hours;
- `oxygen_burden`, `hemodynamic_burden`, `respiratory_burden`, and
  `temperature_burden` separately;
- `instability_transitions`: count of normal-to-abnormal or abnormal-to-normal changes
  between adjacent observed bins, with the number of intervening hours retained so
  widely separated observations are not treated as adjacent;
- coverage: number of observed domain-hours, number of observed hourly bins, and
  domain-specific missingness indicators.

The primary exposure analysis requires at least six observed domain-hours spanning at
least three distinct hourly bins. The prediction analysis retains all eligible stays:
development-set medians are used for imputation, each feature has a missingness indicator,
and no future information is used. A complete-case/minimum-coverage sensitivity analysis
and a missingness-only sensitivity analysis are mandatory. Missingness is not assumed to
mean physiologic stability; it is an observation-process signal.

## Outcomes and competing risks

The primary horizon is 48 hours after `outtime`, with a secondary 7-day horizon. All
outcomes are restricted to the same `hadm_id` and are censored at
`min(dischtime, outtime + horizon)`.

1. Primary composite: first occurrence of either a subsequent ICU stay or death in the
   hospital during the horizon.
2. Operational ICU-return event: the earliest later `icu/icustays` row for the same
   `subject_id, hadm_id` with `intime > outtime` and `intime ≤ horizon end`.
   This is the reproducible primary proxy for “ICU readmission.”
3. Strict ICU-readmission sensitivity event: the operational ICU-return event must also
   have at least one `hosp/transfers` row between index `outtime` and next ICU
   `intime` whose `careunit` is not one of the ICU/stepdown care units observed in
   `icu/icustays.first_careunit/last_careunit`. The exact careunit list is frozen from
   the source rather than hand-invented. If transfer chronology is ambiguous, report the
   broad subsequent-ICU outcome and the strict outcome separately.
4. Death: `admissions.deathtime` after `outtime` and on or before the horizon end and
   `dischtime). `hospital_expire_flag` is a consistency check, not a substitute time
   stamp. Death after an ICU return is counted in the composite and reported as a
   secondary post-return death outcome.
5. Administrative censoring: discharge before either event, end of horizon, or missing
   outcome chronology.

For first-event competing-risk analysis, event type 1 is operational ICU return and event
type 2 is death before any ICU return; the other event is a competing event, and
administrative discharge/horizon is censoring. Report cause-specific Cox hazards and
Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence with 95% confidence intervals, clustered by subject
where supported. Report the 48-hour composite risk ratio/risk difference with robust
confidence intervals as the primary estimand. Do not interpret cause-specific hazard
ratios as causal effects or as probabilities for a specific patient.

## Covariates and baselines

Prespecify three nested baselines.

- Null baseline: development-set event prevalence, with no patient-level prediction.
- Context baseline: penalized logistic regression for the 48-hour composite with age,
  gender, admission type/location, index ICU first/last careunit, ICU LOS, time from
  admission to index outtime, number of earlier ICU stays in the admission, and
  post-index observation opportunity. The same model is evaluated for the competing
  event causes using cause-specific regressions.
- Last-value baseline: context baseline plus the last valid value in the 24-hour window
  for each primary numeric domain and coverage/missingness indicators, without
  burden, persistence, or transition features.
- Instability model: last-value baseline plus the prespecified burden, persistence,
  domain count, transitions, and oxygen/device summaries.

Use L2-penalized logistic regression with regularization selected only in grouped
development/validation data. Use cause-specific Fine–Gray or cause-specific Cox as a
secondary competing-risk model; if the Harbor implementation lacks Fine–Gray, retain
cause-specific Cox plus nonparametric Aalen–Johansen estimates rather than changing the
estimand. Do not use a neural network or unrestricted feature search. Device categories
and any cutpoints beyond those fixed above are frozen from development data and cannot
be outcome-selected.

Split by `subject_id`, never by row or stay, using a fixed reproducible seed. Use 60%
development, 20% validation, and 20% held-out test partitions, with all stays from a
subject in one partition. If a chronological split is attempted, it must be labeled
exploratory because subject-specific timestamp shifts invalidate cross-subject calendar
interpretation. Report AUROC only with precision-recall AUC, Brier score, calibration
intercept/slope, calibration plot, and decision-curve net benefit at explicitly stated
thresholds. Compare the instability model with the last-value/context baselines on the
same test subjects and report paired bootstrap or subject-clustered uncertainty. A small
AUC gain alone is not a clinically meaningful advance.

## Missingness, multiplicity, and uncertainty

Report a cohort flow diagram, per-item row counts, distinct-stay counts, hourly coverage,
and distribution of missingness by outcome and care unit before fitting. Do not infer
normality from absence of a chart entry. Median aggregation, QC ranges, FiO₂
normalization, item inclusion, thresholds, exposure minimum, horizons, and primary
estimand are frozen before test evaluation.

The primary hypothesis has one exposure and one 48-hour composite endpoint. Domain
analyses, 7-day results, strict readmission, subgroups, and feature ablations are
secondary and confidence intervals are interpreted descriptively with false-discovery
control or an explicitly labeled exploratory status. Use bootstrap confidence intervals
resampled by subject, and propagate uncertainty for competing risks through the same
resampling. Quantify sensitivity to: (a) excluding flagged rows, (b) alternate MAP source
preference, (c) complete-case versus missingness indicators, (d) first/final ICU stay
selection, and (e) broad versus strict ICU-return outcome.

Minimum feasibility gates are decided before outcome interpretation: at least 1,000
eligible index stays, at least 100 primary composite events, and at least 50 events of
each first-event type in the full eligible cohort. If these are not met, do not fit the
full instability model; report a descriptive data-support audit and an inconclusive
proposal. Event counts are not to be manufactured by changing windows or cutoffs.

## Falsification and negative controls

The hypothesis is falsified if, on held-out subjects, higher prespecified burden has no
positive association and the confidence interval excludes the prespecified clinically
relevant risk threshold, or if it fails to outperform the last-value baseline on
calibration/Brier score and the direction is not consistent across the 48-hour and
7-day horizons. A null result is scientifically useful if uncertainty is sufficiently
narrow; a wide interval is inconclusive, not evidence of no association.

Adverse/contradictory patterns include instability predicting only the broad technical
subsequent-ICU proxy but not the strict ward-separated return, or an apparent signal
vanishing after coverage and care-context adjustment. These would suggest that transfer
coding or observation intensity, not residual physiology, explains the association.

Prespecified checks:

- Negative-control exposure: measurement density in the earlier 24–48 hours before the
  final window, adjusted for ICU LOS and care unit. A strong isolated association here
  would indicate charting intensity/confounding.
- Negative-control outcome: a subsequent ICU row occurring before the index `outtime`
  is impossible by construction; any nonzero result is a join/time bug and invalidates
  the pipeline.
- Temporal placebo: shift each feature window 24 hours earlier while keeping the same
  index and outcome. A similar or stronger signal requires explanation as baseline
  severity rather than residual discharge instability.
- Permutation check: permute subject-level outcomes within the held-out partition; the
  full pipeline must return null discrimination and calibration consistent with the
  null.
- Leakage audit: no `deathtime`, `dischtime`, later transfers, later ICU rows, or
  `hospital_expire_flag` may enter features. Final hospital `discharge_location` is
  not a primary predictor because it is recorded after the index; an explicitly labeled
  post-index destination sensitivity may be reported only as an operational benchmark.

Supportive results are a reproducible burden gradient or prespecified persistent-
instability contrast, improved calibration/Brier score versus context and last-value
baselines, and similar direction in the strict outcome, with uncertainty and coverage
reported. This supports prognostic transport within this MIMIC snapshot only. It does
not show that discharge was premature or that monitoring or treatment changes improve
outcomes.

Adverse results support no useful incremental prognostic value for this operational
measure, or show that the signal is explained by transfer/measurement patterns.
Inconclusive results arise from insufficient events, unstable estimates, wide intervals,
poor coverage, or failure of permutation/leakage checks; they require another design or
data source, not a forced conclusion.

## Clinical limits and required additional evidence

MIMIC charting is selective and treatment-dependent. The proposed measures cannot
adjudicate work of breathing, true oxygen delivery, patient comfort, clinician intent,
stability during uncharted intervals, or whether a discharge decision was appropriate.
FiO₂ and flow are not interchangeable across devices, and the text device field is not
a validated oxygen-delivery taxonomy. The operational ICU-return event is a database
proxy, not an expert-adjudicated readmission reason.

The local metadata states that narrative text extraction is an unvalidated, limited
lexical-context detector and does not support comprehensive diagnosis or implicit
temporality. Although `note/discharge` is available, it must not be used to claim
reliable goals-of-care classification. Goals of care, treatment limitations, DNR/DNI
status, palliative intent, discharge readiness, and clinician rationale require expert
chart review or a validated abstraction protocol. The available radiology files are
reports, not images; raw waveforms are unavailable according to
`datasets/mimic/metadata.json`. Therefore the study cannot adjudicate imaging-defined
lung pathology, waveform instability, or oxygen-device performance.

A positive result warrants external validation, calibration assessment, and clinician
review of false positives/negatives before any monitoring policy. A causal claim about
delaying discharge, changing oxygen, or preventing readmission requires a target-trial
or prospective intervention with time-varying treatment and discharge-confounding
measurement. A clinical decision rule requires transport validation, prospective impact
evaluation, and adjudicated outcomes.

## Three research-ambition demonstrations: disposition

1. **Learning the natural history of human disease with generative transformers.** I
   inspected the local full article text extraction
   ([references/research-ambition/natural-history/article.readable.txt](../references/research-ambition/natural-history/article.readable.txt)).
   It demonstrates longitudinal temporal modeling, competing future events, external
   validation, calibration concerns, and explicit bias discussion in large disease
   trajectories. MIMIC-IV lacks the UK Biobank/Danish population structure and the
   article's broad longitudinal disease vocabulary, so reproducing a generative
   transformer is not needed for this focused ICU question. I adapt the ambition of
   temporal, competing-event evaluation into a transparent bounded model with
   outcome-linked uncertainty; no claim from that paper establishes this MIMIC
   hypothesis.

2. **Advancing cancer detection and treatment using longitudinal routine clinical data.**
   I inspected only the local supplementary PDF text extraction
   ([references/research-ambition/cancer/supplement.readable.txt](../references/research-ambition/cancer/supplement.readable.txt)).
   The local research-ambition README explicitly says the main article and full STAR
   Methods were unavailable, so I do not claim to have inspected them. The supplement
   documents a large multi-cohort, multimodal longitudinal cohort and missing-data/
   cohort-invariance architecture. MIMIC's configured snapshot has structured ICU
   charting and radiology reports but not the cited cancer cohorts or radiology images;
   I retain the principle of explicit cohort construction and missingness auditing but
   do not reproduce the cancer task or adversarial multimodal model.

3. **A Bayesian framework for longitudinal EHR and genetic discovery.** I inspected the
   local full article text extraction
   ([references/research-ambition/bayesian/article.readable.txt](../references/research-ambition/bayesian/article.readable.txt)).
   It demonstrates explicit longitudinal modeling, patient-level trajectories,
   selection-bias awareness, and uncertainty in EHR prediction, but it depends on
   biobank genetics and long follow-up unavailable here. I retain the separation of
   longitudinal state from prediction and explicit uncertainty, while using a bounded
   penalized regression and competing-risk estimator appropriate to the available
   MIMIC tables. None of these demonstrations is evidence for the proposed ICU
   association.
