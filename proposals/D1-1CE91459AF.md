# Episode 29: renal-injury-aware post-shock recorded-loop initiation

**Parents:** [prior hypothesis], [prior hypothesis]

## Scientific question and substantive advance

After vasopressor-treated shock has resolved, an invasively ventilated adult remains markedly fluid positive but has adequate blood pressure, urine output and potassium. Should clinicians initiate a furosemide or bumetanide loop during the next 24 hours? This is consequential because decongestion might facilitate liberation from the airway, while excessive diuresis or hemodynamic stress might produce early kidney injury before dialysis is required.

The available evidence supports narrower claims only. The inspected 2024 ISCCM weaning position statement (Clerk et al., PMCID PMC11369923, DOI 10.5005/jp-journals-10071-24716) describes a conventional 48-hour period without reinstituted ventilatory support after extubation and notes that avoiding positive balance is relevant, while diuretic use to achieve negative balance remains controversial. A current Europe PMC search retrieved a 2026 review on renal congestion syndrome (PMCID PMC13218125; frozen source_id [source checksum]) which explicitly describes the mechanism as hypothesis-generating and says interventional evidence and clinical utility of congestion targeting remain to be established. These sources do not establish that loop initiation causes respiratory liberation or is renally safe. The three expert-demonstration papers were read only for their available local material and methodological ambition: Delphi-2M warns that temporally directed EHR patterns are not necessarily causal; ALADYNOULLI uses explicit selection-bias correction but lists EHR capture and date uncertainty limitations; the cancer main article was unavailable and only its supplement was inspected, so it is not evidence for this clinical question.

The selected parent makes the airway endpoint computable but leaves renal harm mainly to observed RRT7, which can miss non-dialysis creatinine injury and is affected by clinical thresholds. This child adds a source-bound early renal injury endpoint and makes the benefit-harm claim more clinically decision-relevant without changing the parent’s primary respiratory outcome.

Primary hypothesis: in the frozen eligible population and prespecified measured-overlap population, the ICU-input-system recorded-loop initiation strategy increases the risk of the parent’s airway-anchored item-227194 48-hour success by more than 5 percentage points versus avoiding the same recorded event, while the conservative upper risk difference for an observation-qualified record-defined acute creatinine injury through 48 hours is below 3 percentage points and the conservative upper observed-RRT7 risk difference is below 5 points.

This is a hypothesis about a recorded strategy and record-defined outcomes. It does not test IV treatment, receipt through every route, clinician intent, physiologic congestion, clinical readiness, causal efficacy, or renal safety in the clinical sense.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive recorded evidence requires the parent airway lower one-sided 95% limit for DeltaL > +0.05; OCI48 conservative upper RD < +0.03 with at most 40% unknown in either arm and no >5-point difference in renal-observation coverage; RRT7 conservative upper RD < +0.05 under both date brackets; no adverse ALDL7/destination reversal; compatible unanchored/link/NIV/72-hour/seven-day directions; source, partition, clock, unit, state, overlap, calibration, negative-control, balance and bound checks pass; B eMAR contamination <=5%; ambiguity/key/time/link contradictions <=1%; each arm ESS >=75; and <=25% compatible decisions close by ICU exit before G.

Adverse recorded evidence is declared if the upper one-sided limit for the primary airway DeltaU <=0, OCI48 lower RD >=+0.03 with its observation contract and both missingness analyses pointing the same way, RRT7 lower RD >=+0.05 under both brackets, or ALDL7 DeltaU <=-0.05. These are record-defined concerns, not proof of toxicity. Margin-falsified means airway DeltaU upper one-sided limit <=+0.05; it rejects the prespecified meaningful recorded respiratory association, not every biologic benefit.

Inconclusive/infeasible includes eligible n<200; fewer than 100 A satisfactions or B compatible closures; fewer than 100 definite airway events; fewer than 100 subjects with decision-time C0 and valid post-t0 renal opportunity; ESS<75; >10% person-hours with treatment probability outside [.01,.99]; >1% ambiguity; >5% B eMAR contamination; >25% E-first closures; >40% unknown OCI48 bounds; >5-point renal-observation imbalance; material sensitivity reversal; failed negative controls; or unreconciled state/bound arithmetic. A narrow observed-lab subset cannot be generalized to all eligible patients. Never infer no AKI from no lab, no RRT, survival, discharge or urine output.

Supportive language is limited to: “In the measured-overlap discovery cohort, the ICU-input-system recorded-loop strategy was associated with at least a five-point increase in the record-defined airway-anchored 48-hour extubation outcome, without the prespecified record-defined early creatinine-injury or RRT signals.” Adverse language must say record-defined concern. Margin-falsified language rejects the prespecified recorded margin. Inconclusive language says uncertainty, observation, overlap or feasibility prevented resolution and does not establish equivalence. No result warrants a treatment recommendation.
