# Overlap- and trajectory-restricted target trial of delivered IV-loop initiation after post-shock fluid accumulation

Parent: [prior hypothesis]

## Successor repair

This is a targeted successor, not a new clinical topic. It preserves the parent’s corrected discovery partition, two-clock temporal contract, unit-safe cumulative fluid phenotype and weight hierarchy, hour-48 post-shock landmark, delivered 24-hour loop strategies, clone-censoring, observation-bounded day-7 respiratory endpoint, mortality/RRT safety gates, exact MIMIC snapshot and evidence limits.

The remaining validity threat is confounding by indication coupled to treatment positivity. A patient who receives a loop during the next day may have a different unrecorded trajectory, bedside examination, treatment intent, or prognosis than a patient who does not. A conventional propensity model, overlap weighting, or a large flexible model cannot prove exchangeability when both treatment options are not observed in comparable patients. This successor therefore changes the primary estimand to a prespecified empirical-overlap target population and makes poor support, trajectory imbalance, and unmeasured-indication sensitivity explicit reasons to withhold causal interpretation.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence supports only that positive fluid accumulation is associated with poor critical-illness outcomes and that conservative fluid management/active deresuscitation can reduce fluid exposure in randomized or feasibility settings without detectable worsening of lactate or kidney-injury markers. The inspected RADAR-2 secondary analysis was modest and imprecise (frozen source [source checksum]). It does not establish that a delivered IV loop started at this post-shock landmark causes durable ventilator liberation or is renally safe.

An inspected 2025 MIMIC-IV observational timing study (Wang et al., DOI 10.21037/tau-24-620, frozen source [source checksum]) compared early versus delayed loop use and reported different mortality/AKI outcomes while acknowledging that complete adjustment was impossible. Its exposure timing and population differ from this target trial; its findings motivate, but do not resolve, the present question.

The unresolved falsifiable claim is:

> Among adult, invasively ventilated ICU patients at ICU hour 48 who have recovered from vasopressor-treated shock, have a unit-safe cumulative balance of at least +50 mL/kg, retained urine output and potassium at least 3.0 mmol/L, and for whom both delivered loop initiation and withholding have observed empirical support, does initiating an actually delivered ICU loop (furosemide or bumetanide) during the next 24 hours increase the probability of being alive and durably liberated from invasive ventilation at day 7 by more than 5 percentage points, without excess recorded 28-day death or observed new RRT?

This could identify a clinically plausible subgroup for a pragmatic randomized trial. It cannot establish that the effect applies outside the overlap population, cannot identify bedside congestion or clinician intent, and cannot justify a treatment recommendation.

### Clinical overlap and estimands

At each hour j=0,...,23 while a clone remains eligible and untreated, define history H_j using only lagged decision-available variables and prior delivered treatment:

- baseline: age/sex/race/admission type/year/unit/service, prior completed-admission diagnoses, weight, balance, prior loop exposure and baseline KDIGO;
- trajectory: prior six-hour slopes and levels of MAP, vasopressor dose/rate and cessation, urine/output and balance, creatinine/lactate/potassium, FiO2/PEEP/oxygenation and respiratory support, sedation, crystalloid/albumin and imaging;
- care intensity: ICU location and observed measurement density, as a proxy for trajectory/attention rather than a clinical indication.

Fit the treatment hazard p_j = P(delivered qualifying loop in hour j | H_j, still untreated) using cross-fitted logistic GAM/gradient-boosted nuisance models, with simple logistic regression as baseline. Fit a separate censoring model for discharge/observation loss. All nuisance training is within bucket 0–79 and cross-fitted by subject; no outcome is used in H_j.

Define the primary supported overlap set as records whose estimated initiation probability p_j is in [0.10,0.90] at every risk-set hour, and whose stabilized adherence/censoring weights remain in the 1st–99th percentile. Report the fraction excluded and clinical strata excluded. Do not silently trim: if fewer than 500 eligible subjects or fewer than 150 subjects per observed arm remain, or either arm ESS is <75, the primary causal contrast is infeasible/inconclusive.

The primary estimand is the day-7 ALDL7 risk difference under adherent A versus adherent B in this overlap population, estimated using pooled clone-censor inverse-probability weights and a doubly robust outcome model. Report unadjusted, baseline-overlap-weighted and full hourly clone-censor estimates with subject-bootstrap 95% intervals. Report standardized mean differences before/after weighting, weight distributions, arm ESS and positivity by balance, baseline KDIGO, MAP/pressor trajectory, oxygenation, age, admission year and unit.

As a positivity-robust secondary estimand, estimate stochastic initiation policies that multiply each untreated hour’s observed odds of delivered initiation by delta=2 versus delta=0.5, truncated only by the predeclared support bounds. Use the same H_j, censoring model and cross-fitted sequential doubly robust estimator. This asks how outcomes change under a feasible shift in delivery propensity, not what would happen if every patient were forcibly treated or untreated. It does not replace the delivered A/B question. A large difference between deterministic-overlap and stochastic estimates is a transportability/positivity warning.

## Falsification and sensitivity plan

The design fails its causal interpretation gate if any of the following occurs:

- positivity: any clinically important stratum has no observed A or B support, more than 10% of risk sets have p_j<0.01 or >0.99, more than 10% of subjects have adherence probability <0.01, overlap trimming removes >50%, or estimates depend materially on the [0.10,0.90] bound;
- trajectory: after adjustment, A and B differ by >0.10 standardized units in pre-t0 six-hour MAP, urine, creatinine or oxygenation slopes; a negative-control outcome measured before t0 is predicted by future strategy; or results change materially when measurement density, trajectory splines, treatment-by-trajectory interactions and unit/era effects are added;
- treatment measurement: ICU-only, eMAR-concordant and explicit-IV-eMAR exposure definitions disagree materially; route-unknown administrations change the arm; or initiation is based on order rather than delivery;
- endpoint/fluid measurement: ALDL7 bound width precludes a useful decision, observation gaps are bridged, fluid unit/status sensitivity changes direction or safety gates, or arm-differential missingness is material.

Required sensitivities include 6/12-hour washouts; 6/12/24-hour initiation windows; balance +30/+100 mL/kg and continuous balance; complete-hour input/output capture; never-treated since ICU admission; no albumin co-initiation; cardiac-surgery/heart-failure exclusions; KDIGO strata; 24/48/72-hour durability; planned-only extubation; charttime-only measurements; first ICU per admission; and calendar/unit/era interaction.

Quantify unmeasured-indication bias rather than declaring it absent. For plausible prevalence of an unrecorded clinician-judged congestion/diuretic-readiness signal, vary its treatment-risk and ALDL7-risk associations over relative-risk pairs 1.25, 1.5, 2 and 3 and report the corrected RD interval. If a +5-point benefit disappears under a clinically modest bias scenario or the sign changes across plausible scenarios, the claim is not robust.

## Interpretation branches

Supportive means: in the overlap population, RD lower 95% limit >0 and point estimate >+0.05; upper 95% excess 28-day mortality <+0.03; upper 95% excess observed day-7 RRT <+0.05 under both date brackets; process outcomes move as expected; positivity, pre-t0 trajectory, exposure, fluid and ALDL7 checks pass; and benefit remains directionally compatible across the stochastic policy, simpler model, adherence and unmeasured-indication analyses. This supports trial prioritization and estimation of a restricted clinically supported effect only—not observational proof, generalization to excluded patients, renal safety or a treatment recommendation.

Adverse means: the upper 95% limit of RD_U <=0, or mortality-harm lower bound >=+0.03, or observed-RRT-harm lower bound >=+0.05, with adequate overlap and valid endpoint capture. Better urine or more negative balance cannot override respiratory failure, mortality or RRT harm.

Inconclusive means: RD_L does not support benefit while RD_U permits it; ALDL7 bounds are wide; either safety gate is sparse or bracket-sensitive; overlap/ESS thresholds fail; treatment probability is near deterministic; trajectories remain imbalanced; exposure definitions conflict; results are sensitive to fluid or observation definitions; or plausible unmeasured-indication bias erases the effect. Nonsignificance is neither equivalence nor safety.

## Unavailable evidence and verifier limits

MIMIC lacks randomization, explicit diuretic indication or intent, bedside congestion examination, reliable medication refusal/adherence beyond recorded administration, clinician awareness at each clock time, complete ward medication/ventilation/RRT capture, post-discharge events, robust goals-of-care state, cardiac/echo/hemodynamic congestion measures and the RADAR-2 biomarker panel. The downloaded radiology files are text reports, not images; MIMIC-CXR images and raw waveforms are unavailable. A lexical note feature is not a validated diagnosis.

The verifier can check archive/hash/header bindings, partition, joins, two clocks, fluid units, exposure delivery and eMAR linkage, overlap definitions, cloning/censoring, trajectory lags, weights/ESS, stochastic-policy computation, ALDL7 bounds, RRT brackets, falsification tests, uncertainty and whether a reported branch follows computed outputs. It must reject correct computations paired with unsupported causal or safety claims.

The verifier cannot establish exchangeability, bedside treatment intent, actual fluid completeness, clinical extubation truth, renal safety, transportability or a recommendation. Those require expert clinical adjudication, missing evidence, external validation and preferably randomization.
