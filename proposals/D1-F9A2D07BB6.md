# Patient state versus laboratory observation process in transportable ICU mortality prediction

Status: episode-13 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prediction and measurement-process study, not a causal effect of testing, a hospital quality ranking, or evidence of clinical benefit.

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must newly freeze a leakage-safe first-stay cohort, a revision- and unit-aware laboratory codebook, fixed ICU-relative windows, and whole-hospital outer folds. It must fit paired competing-risk models on identical episodes and estimate the out-of-hospital transport value of patient-level laboratory observation timing and intensity beyond measured patient state and simple availability.

Completion requires:

1. a cohort flow, source/data dictionary, analyte/unit/interface/revision audit, and fold manifest;
2. patient-level state, availability, and process feature matrices with development-fold normalization;
3. out-of-fold cumulative-incidence predictions for recorded ICU death and alive ICU discharge, zero-shot on held-out hospitals;
4. macro-hospital and patient-weighted integrated Brier scores, calibration-in-the-large/slope, fixed-alert-burden summaries, uncertainty and ablations;
5. a conditional within-hospital process-permutation falsification and a common-panel sensitivity analysis;
6. a predeclared comparison with a compact temporal model only if event and feature support pass; and
7. an interpretation trace linking every reported claim to computed output files.

The scientific output is an estimate of incremental, transportable predictive information and its failure modes—not the highest AUROC and not a clinical decision rule.

## Strongest supported claim and unresolved claim

The available eICU evidence supports feasibility, not the hypothesis. The prior outcome-blind audit found 135,680 eligible adult first stays, 2,470 recorded ICU-death events by 1,800 minutes, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows, and 111,655 episodes with at least one eligible landmark-known laboratory row. These counts support a whole-hospital experiment; they do not show that testing history predicts death, improves transport, or represents clinician intent.

The eICU expert seed `[starting question]` proposes that models using testing frequency and missingness lose performance when moved to another hospital. Existing clinical prediction practice supports the importance of leakage control, calibration and external transport assessment, while the demonstrations show that dated structured histories and learned representations can be useful. What remains unresolved in this dataset is whether a patient’s own laboratory observation history carries reproducible information beyond the patient’s measured state and basic availability, and whether any apparent gain is merely a hospital fingerprint.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among adult first ICU stays, patient-level laboratory observation timing and intensity during a fixed six-hour ICU window improve zero-shot prediction of 24-hour recorded ICU death beyond measured patient state and simple analyte availability in genuinely held-out hospitals; the improvement should disappear when process vectors are conditionally permuted within hospital and state-risk strata, whereas a gain that survives that permutation or is obtained only from hospital/site features is a workflow fingerprint rather than patient-level transportable information.

This can inform whether a deployment should audit observation-process shift and retain process features, use a state/availability model with recalibration, or avoid treating apparent testing-based accuracy as robust. It does not justify changing test ordering.

## Population, temporal boundaries and outcome

Use the first adult ICU stay per person from the frozen eICU snapshot:

- age at least 18, treating age greater than 89 as 90;
- positive numeric `unitdischargeoffset`;
- smallest valid `unitvisitnumber` per `uniquepid`, ties broken by `patientunitstayid`;
- `unitdischargeoffset > 360`, so the 360-minute landmark is observable.

The primary predictor window is ICU-relative minutes [0, 360]. This fixed ICU window prevents variable pre-ICU hospital length from being silently encoded as observation history. A secondary sensitivity window is [max(`hospitaladmitoffset`, -360), 360], with pre-ICU features labeled separately; it is not pooled with the primary analysis.

For a laboratory result to be known at the landmark, require `labresultoffset` in [0, 360] and `labresultrevisedoffset <= 360` or null, with explicit reporting of the null-revision convention. Results with nonnumeric values, conflicting units, or unresolved interface/system mappings are excluded from numeric state features but retained in the audit. No post-landmark revision or result is used.

The primary outcome is the first `patient.unitdischargestatus` normalized exactly to `Expired` with `unitdischargeoffset` in (360, 1,800]. The estimand is cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death by 1,800 minutes. Alive ICU discharge before 1,800 is a competing event; stays remaining in the ICU at 1,800 without recorded death are censored administratively at that horizon. A secondary cause-specific hourly analysis uses the same landmark-known features and preserves the competing discharge risk. This is not all-hospital mortality, post-ICU mortality, latent death, or a causal testing outcome.

## Variables and estimands

For each retained analyte, derive from the primary [0, 360] window:

- first and last valid value, last-minus-first slope when at least two valid values exist, range when at least two values exist, and prespecified threshold flags;
- an availability indicator (any valid value);
- number of distinct measurement times and result rows;
- time from landmark to last result, time from first to last result, inter-test interval summaries and total distinct analytes;
- stable system/interface strata only after the audit, plus revision-known indicators.

The state block contains demographics, admission context and patient physiology/value summaries. The availability block contains only the prespecified analyte availability indicators needed to make imputation explicit. The process block adds timing, count, interval, interface and revision-documentation features. Training-fold medians, scaling and categorical mappings are fit in development hospitals only. No hospital ID or site summary enters the primary patient model.

The primary estimand is the paired outer-test difference in macro-hospital integrated Brier score for the 24-hour recorded-death cumulative incidence between:

- B0, state plus simple availability; and
- B1, the identical state/availability model plus patient-level observation timing and intensity.

Report the patient-weighted difference as secondary. Evaluate calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope/error and integrated cause-specific Brier score before discrimination. A fixed top-5% alert-burden table is descriptive and cannot establish clinical utility without a prospective utility analysis.

## Baseline, substantive alternative and split

Freeze eligibility, analyte codebook, windows, outcomes, competing events, feature transforms and folds before fitting.

B0 is an elastic-net pooled discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly rows from 360 through 1,800, two heads for recorded death and alive discharge, and identical regularization selected only within development hospitals. It uses demographics, admission context, last/first physiologic values, early slopes/ranges and threshold flags, plus simple analyte availability. A pure value-only model without availability is a lower-bound sensitivity, not the main comparison.

B1 is the matched transparent process-aware model on exactly the same episode-hour rows, outcome definitions, folds, and state/availability inputs, adding only the patient-level process block. It estimates standardized cumulative-incidence risks and allows prespecified nonlinear splines for counts/recency if the codebook supports them.

The substantive learned alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins covering [0, 360], with the same analyte values, availability, counts, elapsed-time, interface and revision channels used by B1. Its heads predict recorded death, alive discharge and next-bin observation; the observation head is an auxiliary characterization of temporal information, not a clinical endpoint. It is scientifically useful because B1 summarizes order and persistence, while the temporal model can reveal whether co-movement, abrupt changes, and observation timing carry information that summaries lose. The temporal model is deferred if event support, common feature support, convergence, or calibration is inadequate. It is not selected because it is complex or uses a GPU.

Use five grouped outer folds, each holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once. Within each outer training partition, split hospitals into development and validation subsets (approximately 75%/25%) for fitting, regularization, early stopping and codebook choices.
