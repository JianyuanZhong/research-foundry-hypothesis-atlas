# Provisional hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery is prognostic, not causal

## Proposed relationship
Among critically ill adults with acute kidney injury, a discordant recovery pattern—improvement in one renal/organ signal while another remains impaired—may identify a distinct short-term prognosis from a simple severity or single-lab trajectory. The test is prognostic: whether the pattern predicts subsequent clinical deterioration or death after accounting for measured illness severity and treatment context. It is not a claim that the discordance causes deterioration or that intervening on one signal improves outcomes.

## Uncertainties and missing evidence
- The key unresolved issue is whether discordant recovery reflects a real prognostic state rather than severity, measurement timing, treatment response, or documentation intensity.
- A bounded audit must verify that the proposed variables exist in MIMIC-IV with usable units and adequate coverage, and must define the observation window, index event, outcome window, exclusions, and missingness handling.
- Clinical adjudication of AKI stage, recovery, and deterioration is not automatically available from item-level data; any such labels require explicit rules and sensitivity analysis.
- The causal interpretation is unavailable without treatment assignment, confounding control, and ideally external validation or a prospective study.

## Falsifiable test
Compare a prespecified baseline model (severity and available treatment/context covariates) with a time-aware model adding longitudinal renal/organ trajectories and discordance indicators. Evaluate discrimination, calibration, and decision-curve utility in a temporally ordered or patient-held-out split, with uncertainty intervals and prespecified performance thresholds. The time-aware model must improve clinically meaningful prognostic performance and show a stable direction of association; failure to do so, or instability under missingness/unit/coverage sensitivity checks, would falsify or narrow the hypothesis.
