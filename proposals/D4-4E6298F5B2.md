## Supported evidence, unresolved claim, and advance

The strongest evidence currently supports only three bounded claims. Large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. Such differences carry frailty and broad adverse-outcome information in SPRINT, and a combined equation may lose some prognostic information [K2]. Administrative AKI coding can have incomplete and subgroup-dependent recognition relative to serum-creatinine KDIGO AKI [K3]. The parent’s outcome-only audit additionally observed different coding contexts for recorded N17-first and N18-first (12.0% versus 1.39% same-day principal-family confirmation and median prior-year nonrenal first-code counts 3 versus 0). That observation neither establishes a profile association nor identifies its cause.

Unresolved hypothesis: among supported UKB adults aged 40–69 in the common day-30 N17/N18-free state, the profile P− (2021 combined eGFR G=90; eGFRcys−eGFRcr=−15), versus P0 (G=90; delta=0), has a larger adjusted 10-year relative-risk contrast for first recorded inpatient N17 than for first recorded inpatient N18, and this N17 selectivity is not merely a manifestation of pre-index coding/frailty burden or a similarly broad association with subsequent nonrenal code emission.

Let theta17=log RR10(N17_first; P−/P0), theta18=log RR10(N18_first; P−/P0), and D=theta17−theta18. D remains the primary estimand. The child improves discrimination between two explanations without conditioning on an exposure-, prodrome-, or outcome-affected process:

- renal-selective profile information predicts N17 more than N18 even after measured pre-index history, without a comparably strong broad nonrenal-code pattern;
- broad morbidity/observation explains the apparent selectivity if the profile tracks pre-index code-history structure and subsequent nonrenal first-code emission as strongly as N17.

This is a descriptive standardized joint-marker profile contrast. “Robust to measured baseline history” is not “deconfounded,” and no result identifies an intervention, true GFR, renal reserve, biological AKI/CKD, treatment effect, or mechanism.

## Substantive learned alternative: baseline-history phenotypes

The scalar block loses diagnosis composition, recency-by-chapter structure, and principal/all-position mix. Learn an outcome-blind nonnegative matrix factorization (NMF) from pre-index history only.

Freeze the inherited participant-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split, stratified by sex and aggregate first-transition class, before profile associations are inspected. Construct participant rows and columns for normalized 3-character ICD-10 group × recency bin ([5,2), [2,0.5), [0.5,0) years) × position (all-position; same-code/same-date principal-confirmed). Entries are binary first appearance plus a separate count channel. Drop no rare column using outcome information; prespecify training-frequency >=50. Fit rank 8, 16, and 32 NMFs in training participants only, seeds 20260923–20260925. Select rank by validation Poisson reconstruction deviance plus stability (matched-component cosine >=0.80 across seeds), not renal prediction. Apply frozen loadings to validation/test and bootstrap refits; name components only after listing highest-loading codes, and never label them admissions or mechanisms.

Use the learned component scores, rather than post-baseline states, in the identical cause-specific/discrete-time competing-risk model. Compare: inherited clinical covariates; transparent scalar-history block; NMF history; and scalar+NMF. Hold the P−/P0 estimand, population, horizon and endpoint definitions fixed. Evaluate held-out calibration-in-the-large/slope, endpoint/multiclass IPCW Brier, time-dependent AUC, and exact theta17/theta18/D across seeds. Scientific value comes from whether composition/recency reveals a shared morbidity/coding axis that the scalar count hides, not a small AUC gain.

As a multi-outcome stress test, use frozen NMF scores with the broad comparison outcomes. A component that predicts N17, injury/infection, remote-code families and total nonrenal emission similarly favors a shared history/observation interpretation; an N17 association orthogonal to broad components is compatible with, but does not prove, renal selectivity.

A sequence transformer is deferred because each code has only a first-appearance date and NMF directly targets the unresolved baseline composition/recency question. Revisit only if NMF reconstruction/stability fails and a preregistered sequence model improves held-out baseline-history reconstruction—not merely renal AUC. A latent true-GFR model remains deferred absent measured GFR or validated repeat-assay errors.

## Interpretation and falsification

All interpretations require verified censoring, units/code semantics, exact-risk-set support, convergence, calibrated held-out predictions and justified uncertainty.

Supportive for *recorded* N17 selectivity robust to measured baseline history requires: theta17 lower 95% bound >0; D lower bound >0; matched D agrees; transparent and stable NMF baseline-only models preserve positive compatible D; principal-position estimates do not reverse; and broad/nonrenal Q comparisons do not show equally strong profile associations with adequate precision. This supports prioritizing encounter/laboratory-validated AKI study only.

Adverse for the broad coding/frailty rival: D is precisely null/reversed after baseline-history adjustment; P− predicts broad nonrenal emission or multiple comparison families comparably to N17; NMF shows the apparent N17 signal concentrated on a shared high-burden component; or principal-position D reverses with incompatibility. Do not rescue by changing profiles, windows, code families, NMF rank, or outcomes.

Adverse for acute selectivity but supportive of chronic/shared renal information: theta18>0 with D<=0, or both renal contrasts are similar with adequate precision. Adverse to both profile claims: both endpoint upper bounds <=0 with adequate precision.

Inconclusive: export provenance remains unverified; negative controls or principal N18 are too sparse; comparison estimates are imprecise; support/matching/calibration/stability fails; methods disagree without adjudication; ties alter direction; or intervals cannot distinguish patterns. An imprecise null does not refute the hypothesis.

Even fully supportive results do not establish KDIGO AKI, biochemical CKD onset, complete contact opportunity, true GFR, renal reserve, mechanism, transportability, or benefit from marker-guided care. Those require record-level encounters, longitudinal creatinine/cystatin C/albuminuria, provider/nation/test data, renal clinician adjudication, external validation and ultimately an intervention study.
