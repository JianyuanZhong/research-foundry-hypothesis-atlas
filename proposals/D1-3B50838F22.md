# Episode 24: durable respiratory recovery with an observable-discharge guard after first post-shock opportunity

**Parent:** [prior hypothesis]

## Substantive repair

The parent correctly repairs the sampling and exposure problems: it freezes each subject's earliest broad hour-48 ventilated ICU opportunity, uses a source-symmetric ICU `inputevents` recorded-delivery contrast, treats eMAR as a coverage diagnostic rather than proof of non-use, and retains death/ICU exit as shared competing closures. I retain all of those rules.

The remaining clinical-design problem is the parent's primary ANHATD28 endpoint. `ANHATD28` is computable, but it is a composite of a hospital's discharge decision and destination (`dischtime`, `hospital_expire_flag`, `discharge_location`), not a measure of recovery. A loop could change discharge timing, hospice referral, or destination without improving respiratory function or survival. The parent's ALDL7 secondary is closer to the mechanism, but it is bounded and can be unknown after ICU exit. Treating “no ALDL7 adverse signal” as sufficient for supportive results leaves a clinically important respiratory harm/benefit question underweighted.

I therefore make the primary endpoint a conjunctive, explicitly observation-aware patient-centered endpoint:

**Definite recorded respiratory recovery by day 28 (DRR28)** requires (i) an accepted extubation by (t0+5) days, (ii) no later accepted invasive-ventilation episode through (t0+7) days, with continuous linked-ICU observation from extubation to day 7, (iii) no recorded index-hospital death or exact hospice/acute-hospital discharge by (t0+28) days, and (iv) a recorded live index-hospital discharge by day 28 to a non-hospice, non-acute destination. Patients with an ICU observation gap, an early nonterminal ICU exit before day 7, or otherwise insufficient respiratory ascertainment are not silently counted as failures or successes; their DRR28 value is [0,1]. This is a partially identified endpoint by design.

The falsifiable hypothesis is: among adults meeting the parent's first-opportunity, hour-48 post-shock, positive-fluid and measured-overlap criteria, initiating a qualifying ICU-input-system recorded furosemide/bumetanide delivery before the 24-hour decision closure increases the DRR28 risk difference by >5 percentage points versus avoiding that same recorded event, while the conservative upper risk-difference limits for recorded death/hospice/acute discharge and observed RRT7 remain below +3 and +5 percentage points, respectively.

This advances the parent by requiring respiratory recovery and a favorable recorded disposition jointly, rather than using discharge alone as benefit or using respiratory ascertainment only as a one-sided safety screen. It may be inconclusive because the intersection has wider bounds; that is a clinically informative feasibility result, not a reason to weaken the endpoint.

## What evidence supports, and what remains unresolved

The inspected Europe PMC record for Orieux et al., “Renal congestion syndrome in critically ill patients: a neglected mechanism of acute kidney injury” (2026; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100083; frozen search response source_id [source checksum]) describes renal congestion as a hypothesis-generating construct and states that interventional evidence and clinical utility of targeting congestion remain to be established. Its indexed evidence supports physiologic plausibility and the need for outcome studies, not a loop-specific benefit or safety effect. The local research-ambition README was inspected; its cancer paper is not cited because the main text and STAR Methods were unavailable.

Thus the strongest supported claim is only that fluid accumulation/renal-congestion mechanisms are clinically plausible and that conservative-fluid/deresuscitation interventions can motivate outcome research; the unresolved claim is whether a specific, recorded loop-initiation decision after shock stabilization improves a respiratory-and-disposition endpoint without recorded harm. DRR28 is not true functional recovery, post-discharge survival, or a causal treatment effect.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive requires: lower one-sided 95% DRR28 RD limit >+0.05; conservative upper recorded death/hospice/acute-discharge effect <+0.03; conservative upper observed-RRT7 effect <+0.05; no adverse ALDL7 bound; no destination reversal; all source, partition, clock, unit, transition, overlap, calibration, balance, negative-control, weight and bound diagnostics pass; no material reversal under 6/12-hour grace, Paused, treatment-first ties or contemporaneous-store sensitivity; B eMAR loop contamination <=5%; late candidate confirmation <=1% and not materially differential; ambiguity/key/time/state contradictions <=1%; each arm ESS >=75; and no more than 25% of compatible decisions closed by ICU exit before G.

Adverse if DRR28 DeltaU upper limit <=0, recorded death/hospice/acute-discharge DeltaL lower limit >=+0.03, observed-RRT7 DeltaL lower limit >=+0.05 under both chartdate brackets, or ALDL7 DeltaU upper limit <=−0.05. These are adverse record outcomes, not proof of toxicity.

Margin-falsified if DRR28 upper one-sided limit <=+0.05. This rejects the prespecified clinically meaningful margin for the record-defined composite, not all biological benefit.

Inconclusive includes all other estimable results; wide DRR28 bounds; precise effects between 0 and +0.05; eligible n<200; A qualifying n<100; B completions/compatible closures<100; ESS<75; >10% person-hours with treatment probability outside [.01,.99]; >1% ambiguity/contradiction; >5% B eMAR contamination; >1% late confirmation or >5 percentage-point differential lag; >25% E-first closures; material sensitivity/destination reversal; failed negative controls; or unreconciled bound/state arithmetic.
