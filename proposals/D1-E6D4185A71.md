# Component-Specific Residual Instability Before ICU Discharge: Does the Prognostic Value Differ by Dominant Component? (MIMIC-IV)

**Parent:** [prior hypothesis] (board leader, valid, compile-ready; mu=31.00)
**Dataset:** MIMIC-IV 3.1 (frozen archive, [source checksum])
**Status:** Scientific evolution of the parent. Population, temporal boundaries, outcomes, and data bindings are unchanged from the parent (with one FiO2 unit-normalization correction). The **new scientific content** is the component-specific 4-group phenotype, the component × outcome interaction test, and the baseline (composite OR) vs extended (4-group) model comparison that directly answers whether the TYPE of residual instability (oxygen-requirement vs hemodynamic-variability) has different prognostic value.

---

## 1. Hypothesis

### Supported claim (established by prior literature, not re-tested here)
Vital-sign variability and oxygen requirement during ICU stay are associated with adverse outcomes. [K1] Wang et al. [K1] demonstrated that heart-rate-derived variability measures (HR SD) calculated from routine ICU charted data in the first 24 hours are independently associated with in-hospital mortality (HR SD OR 1.855, 95% CI 1.211–2.841). [K2] Kalinoski et al. [K2] showed that persistent critical illness (ICU LOS ≥ 10 days) is associated with a threefold increase in mortality risk (HR 3.01, 95% CI 2.72–3.33). [K3] Orosz et al. [K3] demonstrated that post-ICU clinical deterioration (captured by MET calls) is strongly associated with ICU readmission (27.5% vs 2.2%, p < 0.001), with hypoxia (49.5%) and tachycardia (28.5%) as the most common triggers.

### Unresolved claim (this evolution tests)
**In adult ICU patients with residual physiological instability in the last 24 hours before ICU discharge, the association between instability and ICU readmission / in-hospital death differs by the dominant instability component: oxygen-requirement-driven instability (FiO2 ≥ 0.4 or SpO2 < 94%) has a different prognostic association than hemodynamic-variability-driven instability (MAP SD > 10 or HR SD > 20), independent of baseline severity (ICU LOS) and discharge destination.**

This claim is unresolved because:
1. The parent candidate (and all prior work) treats residual instability as a **single composite OR phenotype** (FiO2 ≥ 0.4 OR SpO2 < 94% OR MAP SD > 10 OR HR SD > 20). The composite collapses two mechanistically distinct physiological processes — respiratory oxygenation and circulatory stability — into one binary flag.
2. Prior studies have examined variability at ICU *admission* (first 24 h) [K1], not at ICU *discharge* (last 24 h), and none has decomposed the discharge-time instability signal by component.
3. The clinical consequence of the decomposition is direct: if the components differ, the **TYPE** of post-ICU monitoring (respiratory: pulse oximetry/ABG vs hemodynamic: BP/HR trending) can be prioritized for at-risk patients; if they are similar, the composite phenotype is clinically sufficient and no component-specific monitoring protocol is warranted.

### What answering this question would change
- **Supportive (components differ, interaction significant):** component-specific post-ICU monitoring protocols — e.g., prioritize respiratory monitoring for oxygen-driven instability, hemodynamic trending for variability-driven instability. Would justify prospective validation of component-specific monitoring intensity.
- **Adverse (components similar, interaction null):** the composite OR phenotype is clinically sufficient; a single "residual instability" flag without component decomposition is adequate for risk stratification. No component-specific monitoring protocol needed.
- **Inconclusive (insufficient component-specific events):** the question cannot be resolved in MIMIC-IV; would require a larger multi-center dataset or a different time window.

## 2. Study Population (unchanged from parent)

### Leading explanation and strongest rival
- **Leading claim (to be tested):** the two components have *different* prognostic weights because they reflect different pathophysiology — hemodynamic variability at discharge reflects unresolved circulatory compromise, while residual oxygen requirement may reflect either unresolved respiratory failure OR successful weaning (down-titrated FiO2).
- **Strongest rival:** the apparent component-specific difference is a **confounding-by-indication artifact** — patients with residual high FiO2/low SpO2 at discharge are often those *improving* (extubated, O2 weaned), so the oxygen flag marks recovery, not risk. The 4-group design with LOS and discharge-destination adjustment is specifically constructed to separate these explanations: if the oxygen component's OR rises above 1 after adjustment, the rival is weakened; if it stays ≤ 1, the rival is supported and the composite phenotype's oxygen arm is not prognostic.
- **Third, weaker rival:** the difference is driven by **case-mix** (the "both" group is simply sicker/longer-stay). Adjusting for LOS and discharge destination addresses this; a PerCI (LOS > 14 d) exclusion sensitivity further tests it.

### Inclusion criteria
- Adult patients (`anchor_age` ≥ 18 at first encounter; `hosp/patients`)
- ICU length of stay ≥ 1.0 day (`icu/icustays.los`)
- At least one chartevent in the last 24 h of the ICU stay (data availability)
- Discharged alive from the ICU (`outtime` IS NOT NULL)
- **First ICU stay of the hospitalization only** (min `stay_id` per `subject_id`+`hadm_id`)

### Exclusion criteria
- ICU stay < 1.0 day
- No chartevent data in the last 24 h for HR, MAP or SpO2
- Non-first ICU stay of the hospitalization

### Temporal boundaries (unchanged)
- **Exposure window:** [outtime − 24 h, outtime] of the index ICU stay
- **Primary outcome (ICU readmission, Definition A):** any subsequent `icustay` with the same `subject_id` + `hadm_id` and `intime` > index `outtime`
- **Sensitivity outcome (Definition B):** any subsequent `icustay` with the same `subject_id`, different `hadm_id`, `intime` within 72 h of index `outtime`
- **Secondary outcome (in-hospital death):** `deathtime` IS NOT NULL OR `hospital_expire_flag` = 1 for the index `hadm_id`

## 4. Component-Specific Residual Instability Phenotype (NEW in this evolution)

In the **last 24 hours before ICU discharge** (`charttime` ∈ [outtime − 24 h, outtime]):

### Component 1: Oxygen-Requirement-Driven Instability
- **Primary (FiO2 available, ≥ 1 measurement):** FiO2 (itemid 223835, normalized to 0–1 fraction) max ≥ 0.4 OR mean ≥ 0.4
- **Fallback (no FiO2 in window):** SpO2 (itemid 220277) min < 94%
- **Oxygen flag = 1** if either the primary or fallback criterion is met

### Component 2: Hemodynamic-Variability-Driven Instability
- **MAP variability:** SD of MAP (itemid 220181, fallback 220052) > 10 mmHg, with ≥ 5 measurements in the window
- **HR variability:** SD of HR (itemid 220045) > 20 bpm, with ≥ 5 measurements in the window
- **Hemodynamic flag = 1** if either MAP SD > 10 OR HR SD > 20

### 4-Group Component-Specific Phenotype (NEW)
| Group | Definition |
|-------|------------|
| **oxygen_only** | Oxygen flag = 1 AND hemodynamic flag = 0 |
| **hemo_only** | Oxygen flag = 0 AND hemodynamic flag = 1 |
| **both** | Oxygen flag = 1 AND hemodynamic flag = 1 |
| **neither** | Oxygen flag = 0 AND hemodynamic flag = 0 (reference) |

### Composite (parent phenotype, retained for comparison)
**Residual instability (composite OR) = oxygen flag OR hemodynamic flag** (i.e., oxygen_only + hemo_only + both)

### Missing-data rule
- A component is "missing" if its defining item has 0 rows in the window.
- The oxygen flag uses FiO2 when available (≥ 1 measurement), else SpO2 (≥ 1 measurement). If neither FiO2 nor SpO2 is available, the oxygen flag is set to 0 and the stay is retained (SpO2 coverage is 99.4%, so this is rare).
- The hemodynamic flag requires ≥ 5 measurements of at least one of MAP/HR (to make SD meaningful). If neither has ≥ 5 measurements, the hemodynamic flag is set to 0.
- A stay with < 2 available components (i.e., both flags uncomputable) is excluded from the primary analysis (expected < 1% given 99%+ HR/MAP/SpO2 coverage).

### Sensitivity analyses
- FiO2 threshold: 0.30, 0.40, 0.50; MAP SD: 5, 10, 15 mmHg; HR SD: 10, 20, 30 bpm; window: 12 h, 24 h, 48 h
- PerCI exclusion: exclude ICU LOS > 14 days (tests the case-mix rival)
- Discharge-destination stratification: home vs non-home

## 6. Analysis Plan (NEW: 4-group + interaction)

### 6.1 Primary analysis: 4-group comparison with interaction test

**Model (readmission, primary):** Multivariable logistic regression with:
- Covariates: age (per 10 years), sex (male), ICU LOS (per day), discharge destination (home vs non-home)
- Exposure: 3 dummy variables for the 4-group phenotype (oxygen_only, hemo_only, both; reference = neither)
- **Interaction term:** oxygen_flag × hemo_flag (equivalently, the "both" group's OR relative to the multiplicative product of the two single-component ORs)

**Model (death, secondary):** Cox PH with the same covariates and 4-group exposure; interaction via the "both" coefficient.

**Key estimates:**
1. Adjusted OR (95% CI) for each component group vs neither (readmission and death)
2. **Interaction OR** (95% CI, p-value): does the "both" group's effect differ from the product of the two single-component effects? A significant interaction (p < 0.05) or a large non-additive difference (|OR_both − OR_oxy × OR_hemo| ≥ 0.5) indicates component-specific prognostic value.
3. **Component-specific contrast:** OR for oxygen_only vs hemo_only (direct comparison of the two components, adjusting for covariates). This is the cleanest test of "which type of instability is more prognostic."

### 6.2 Baseline vs extended model comparison (method comparison)

- **Baseline model (parent):** logistic regression with the **composite OR indicator** (binary 0/1) + covariates.
- **Extended model (this evolution):** logistic regression with the **4-group component-specific exposure** (3 dummies) + covariates.
- **Comparison:**
  - Likelihood ratio test (extended vs baseline): does the 4-group decomposition improve fit over the composite?
  - Harrell C-index (baseline vs extended)
  - NRI and IDI (category-free and category-based)
  - If the extended model significantly improves discrimination (ΔC ≥ 0.01 or NRI > 0, p < 0.05), the component decomposition adds prognostic information beyond the composite.

### 6.3 Substantive alternative: XGBoost with continuous component features

- **Features:** FiO2 mean, FiO2 max (normalized), MAP SD, HR SD, HR mean, SpO2 min, LOS, age, sex, discharge destination
- **Model:** gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost), `n_estimators=300`, `max_depth=4`, `learning_rate=0.05`, `subsample=0.8`, `colsample_bytree=0.8`, `n_jobs=4`, 5-fold stratified CV
- **What the alternative reveals:**
  - **Nonlinearity/threshold dependence:** whether the relationship between each component and outcome is monotonic or has a threshold (e.g., HR SD effect only above 25 bpm). The binary 4-group design cannot detect this.
  - **Component contribution to discrimination:** feature importances show which continuous component (FiO2, MAP SD, HR SD, SpO2 min) contributes most to predicting readmission/death — a direct, quantitative answer to "which type of instability matters more."
  - **Component interactions:** tree splits that condition on both FiO2 and HR SD reveal whether the combination is stronger than either alone (beyond the logistic interaction term).
  - **Why this alternative is scientifically necessary (not just a predictive exercise):** the 4-group logistic design answers *whether* the components differ (interaction test) but cannot answer *how* they differ — i.e., whether the relationship is threshold-dependent, nonlinear, or driven by a specific continuous feature (e.g., HR SD effect only above 25 bpm, or FiO2 effect only above 0.5). The XGBoost feature importances and tree splits reveal the *shape* of the component-outcome relationship and identify which continuous component contributes most to discrimination. This is a mechanistic question (what drives the signal?) that the binary 4-group design cannot address. If XGBoost substantially outperforms the 4-group logistic model (ΔC ≥ 0.02), the binary component thresholds lose information and the continuous component features are the clinically relevant signal; if similar, the simple 4-group phenotype is clinically sufficient.

### 6.4 Falsification criteria (NEW, component-specific)

- **Supportive (components differ):**
  - Interaction p < 0.05 (logistic or Cox), OR
  - |adjusted OR_oxygen_only − adjusted OR_hemo_only| ≥ 0.5 for readmission OR death (95% CIs non-overlapping), OR
  - Extended (4-group) model significantly improves discrimination over baseline (composite) (ΔC ≥ 0.01 or NRI > 0, p < 0.05)
- **Adverse (components similar; composite sufficient):**
  - Interaction p ≥ 0.20, AND
  - |adjusted OR_oxygen_only − adjusted OR_hemo_only| < 0.3 (95% CIs overlapping), AND
  - No incremental discrimination (ΔC < 0.005, NRI not significant)
- **Inconclusive:**
  - < 50 outcome events in any of the three non-reference component groups, OR
  - < 100 patients in any component group, OR
  - > 50% missing data in the exposure window for the defining component

### 6.5 Interpretation
- **Supportive:** the TYPE of residual instability matters. Post-ICU monitoring should be component-specific: respiratory monitoring (pulse oximetry, ABG) for oxygen-driven instability; hemodynamic monitoring (BP/HR trending) for variability-driven instability. Would justify prospective validation of component-specific monitoring protocols.
- **Adverse:** the composite OR phenotype is clinically sufficient. A single "residual instability" flag without component decomposition is adequate; no component-specific monitoring protocol is warranted. The parent's design is validated as the right level of granularity.
- **Inconclusive:** insufficient component-specific events; would require a larger multi-center dataset (e.g., eICU) or a different time window.

## 8. Limitations and Alternatives

- **FiO2 unit duality:** raw values are 0–1 or 0–100; normalization is mandatory. Documented in Step 4.
- **Selection bias:** the last-24h window conditions on survival to that point; ICU LOS is a covariate; PerCI exclusion sensitivity (LOS > 14 d).
- **Confounding by indication (oxygen component):** residual high FiO2/low SpO2 may mark weaning success, not risk. The 4-group design + LOS/destination adjustment is the primary defense; the PerCI sensitivity is a secondary check. **This is the key scientific uncertainty the experiment resolves.**
- **Discharge destination confounding:** included as a covariate; stratified sensitivity.
- **Competing risk of death:** Fine-Gray primary; Cox sensitivity.
- **No external validation:** single-center; internal bootstrap/CV only.
- **HR SD from irregularly spaced charted values**, not continuous ECG.
- **Alternatives not chosen:** SOFA/APACHE scores (deferred; not computable for all patients in the last 24 h); eICU external validation (different itemid namespace; requires a separate crosswalk study); continuous-time survival with component-specific hazard functions (deferred; the 4-group logistic/Cox design is the minimal feasible check).

---

### Alternatives not chosen (recorded for transparency)
- **SOFA/APACHE severity scores:** deferred; not computable for all patients in the last 24 h (require admission-time labs and vitals that may be stale at discharge). The 4-group design uses only last-24h charted data.
- **eICU external validation:** deferred; different itemid namespace requires a separate crosswalk study. MIMIC-IV single-center results are hypothesis-generating, not externally validated.
- **Continuous-time survival with component-specific hazard functions:** deferred; the 4-group logistic/Cox design is the minimal feasible check. A component-specific hazard model would require a larger dataset and is a natural next step if the 4-group design is supportive.
- **GPU-accelerated deep learning (LSTM/Transformer on vital-sign sequences):** not scientifically justified for this question. The 4-group design tests a categorical (component) effect, not a temporal sequence pattern. XGBoost on aggregated features is the appropriate learned alternative; a sequence model would add complexity without addressing the component-decomposition question.
- **Mediation analysis (does hemodynamic variability mediate the oxygen→outcome pathway?):** deferred; requires a causal framework (e.g., g-computation) that is beyond the scope of this observational association study. The 4-group design is the minimal check; mediation is a follow-up if the interaction is significant.
