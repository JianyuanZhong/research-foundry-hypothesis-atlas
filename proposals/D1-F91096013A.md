# Strictly lagged MIMIC AKI discordance: severity, support, exit and ascertainment audit

Status: design-only substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. No cohort scan, model fit, Harbor execution, or clinical result was run.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The selected parent asks whether a creatinine-defined recovery pattern that is discordant with direct urine output and/or positive recorded input-minus-urine predicts short-term outcomes. The unresolved scientific problem is discrimination: the same pattern could reflect residual physiologic severity, muscle-related creatinine measurement error, treatment/support response, differential recording opportunity, survivor selection at an ICU landmark, or endpoint ascertainment rather than a renal-specific signal.

[K1] supports the bounded premise that creatinine-related measures can track muscle mass and mortality, so creatinine alone is not a complete physiologic reference when muscle changes. [K2] supports that fluid accumulation associations are heterogeneous and low-certainty, so recorded input must not be called true total-body volume or a treatment. [K3] shows, in an abstract-only MIMIC/eICU prediction study, that measurement-process features can carry workflow signal while contributing little incremental predictive value beyond creatinine trajectories; this motivates a process-only falsification rather than assuming documentation density is a confounder or a mechanism. These works do not establish the proposed MIMIC phenotype, its outcome contrasts, a causal effect, true GFR, true volume status or complete RRT ascertainment.

The falsifiable hypothesis is unchanged from the parent:

> Among adults with a first creatinine-defined AKI episode who reach a prespecified 24-, 48-, or 72-hour landmark in their first eligible ICU stay, does strictly lagged creatinine-recovery discordance with low direct urine output and/or positive recorded input-minus-urine have a component-specific seven-day observed risk difference versus concordant recovery for hospital death and ICU-recorded RRT, with the association remaining directionally present in adequately observed/support-stratified risk sets and not confined to support-first, high-surveillance or ICU-exit-selected patients?

This is a prognostic/measurement comparison, not a treatment-effect or mechanistic claim. The strongest rival is that the contrast will attenuate or concentrate among patients with abnormal pre-exposure context, dense recording, high administered-input/support intensity, support preceding renal observation, or early ICU exit. Persistence would establish a reproducible association within these observed MIMIC risk sets only.

The substantive repair is to make each rival testable without changing the parent’s primary exposure, six-hour washout, landmarks or component estimands:

- residual severity: add a fixed context window strictly before the lagged exposure, using a literal dictionary-checked six-vital/seven-nonrenal-lab panel and opportunity masks;
- surveillance/recording opportunity: report pre-exposure and exposure-window density separately, including a process-only density exposure;
- treatment/support response: distinguish support-before-exposure, support-first, renal-first and simultaneous ordering, and repeat ordering after excluding input rows;
- survivor/ICU-exit selection: publish all landmark losses and treat alive ICU exit as a competing transition for ICU-recorded RRT;
- endpoint ascertainment: keep hospital death and ICU-recorded RRT separate, use distinct clocks, and never backfill ward/post-ICU RRT.

## Risk sets, outcomes and competing events

At each L the risk set consists of retained-stay subjects alive at Ltime, still in the clipped index ICU interval at Ltime, without incident RRT before Ltime, with exposure/state opportunity determined without W_L, and with non-recovery/unknown status handled as specified. Publish losses from death, alive ICU exit, pre-L RRT, missing creatinine, missing weight/urine opportunity, unresolved input, invalid timing and washout contamination.

For hospital death D, use the first valid hosp/admissions.deathtime in [Ltime,Ltime+7d), no later than valid dischtime, with hospital_expire_flag as a consistency check rather than a substitute. Death remains observable after ICU exit while the admission is active. Alive in-hospital discharge is an explicit competing event; alive in hospital at day 7 is administrative end-of-window censoring. A deathtime equal to dischtime is a death only when time/flag consistency passes; otherwise count a timing exception.

For ICU-recorded RRT R, use the first icu/procedureevents row in the exact RRT set with finite starttime in [Ltime,Ltime+7d), strictly before clipped ICU exit and while alive. The RRT clock has causes RRT, hospital death before RRT, and alive ICU exit. The earliest valid event wins; death wins an exact death/RRT tie. A start exactly at death or ICU exit is not an RRT event and is counted as a tie exception. Missing starttime is unobservable and counted. Do not backfill ward or post-ICU RRT. ICU exit is therefore a competing transition, not ordinary censoring.

For prespecified support/order stratum s, the primary noncausal component-specific estimands are:
RD_D(L,s) = F_D(7d | discordant,s) - F_D(7d | concordant,s)
RD_R(L,s) = F_R(7d | discordant,s) - F_R(7d | concordant,s),
where F_D and F_R are Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidences in the same L risk set with the component-specific competing events above. L=48 is primary; L=24 and L=72 are replication/boundary tests. Report complete denominators, state counts, competing transitions, administrative censoring and timing exceptions. The observation-complete restriction through day 7 is a selection sensitivity, not a corrected population estimate. A pre-L descriptive observation-weight sensitivity may use only t0, B_L and S_L variables, prespecified 1st/99th percentile truncation and positivity reporting; it never replaces the primary AJ estimate.

A support/order cell is estimable only with at least 50 risk-set subjects and at least 10 events for that component. Otherwise publish denominator and event counts as inconclusive/not estimable and never merge cells after viewing outcomes. Use 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap replicates, keeping all admissions for a subject together; report failed/usable replicates and percentile intervals.

## Falsification and decision rules

The falsification set is frozen before fitting:

1. Process-only exposure: quartiles of non-creatinine lab plus non-weight chart density in S_L, and separately B_L. A comparable component gradient or strong attenuation of the renal contrast after density restriction weakens a renal-recovery interpretation.
2. Temporal order: compare support-before-A, support-first, renal-first, simultaneous and input-excluded order. Concentration in support-first/support-before-A or disappearance after input exclusion supports support-process selection.
3. Severity audit: compare primary contrasts across B-context strata and a B-only standardized sensitivity. Attenuation/reversal after B context is adverse to an interpretation beyond residual severity; persistence is only evidence of robustness to measured context.
4. Boundary and measurement: compare six-hour-washout A_L with current-window sensitivity; compare primary urine items with excluded items, two-of-three with all-three coverage, and clipped versus unclip-able timing. A signal present only when washout is violated or only with excluded/sparse measures is adverse.
5. Components and exits: report hospital death and ICU-RRT separately. A union-only result, an RRT contrast explained by ICU exit, or a death contrast with inconsistent ascertainment is not evidence of one common renal mechanism.

Supportive evidence requires a clinically nontrivial, directionally consistent RD_D and/or RD_R at L=48 and at least one of L=24/72, adequate strata, no concentration in support-first/high-density/B-severe or exit-selected cells, process-only gradient smaller/different, stable dictionary/measurement sensitivities, and calibrated held-out estimates. It still establishes only an observed association.

Adverse evidence is attenuation or reversal after the pre-exposure audit; concentration in support-first, support-before-A, dense-recording or early-exit cells; process-only equivalence; current-window-only dependence; excluded-item dependence; unstable endpoint precedence; or component-specific signal explained by discharge/exit. It favors the corresponding rival but does not prove normal GFR, benign volume status, or no treatment effect.

Inconclusive evidence is failed dictionary/unit binding, high unknown opportunity, sparse cells, fewer than 10 component events, nonpositivity, wide intervals spanning clinically important benefit and harm, unstable bootstrap/calibration, large timing exceptions, or sequence improvement without reproducible component contrasts. An imprecise null is not refutation. A result cannot establish mechanism, causality, true GFR, total-body balance, treatment intent, complete collection, complete ward/post-ICU RRT, goals of care or transportability; those require clinical adjudication or another study.

## Matched baseline versus learned alternative

Both methods use the identical eligible rows, t0, A_L/W_L/S_L/B_L boundaries, outcomes, missingness, support/order features and deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 split seeded 20261002; all admissions of a subject stay together. Neither receives W_L, post-L measurements or future ICU movement.

M0, the primary fixed-summary baseline, uses prespecified summaries of pre-t0/t0 context, B_L vitals/labs/opportunity, A_L creatinine peak/level/slope and masks, three-bin urine values/masks/item composition/coverage, canonical accepted input and representation/missingness, recorded balance, support/order/intensity, weight, organ-support indicators, age/sex/admission/ICU timing and B/S density. Fit separate six-hour pooled-logistic cause-specific hazards for hospital death versus alive discharge and ICU-RRT versus alive ICU exit/death, then recursively obtain seven-day CIFs. Standardize held-out component CIFs and RD_D/RD_R; report calibration, Brier, secondary discrimination, predicted/observed counts, support/order/B-context calibration and cluster-bootstrap intervals.

M1 is a small two-stream temporal competing-risk model, implemented as a GRU or temporal convolution only if the configured runtime lacks a stable GRU. Its renal stream receives the three A_L bins (creatinine, direct urine, masks, coverage and item composition). Its support/observation stream receives accepted input volume/composition/representation/missingness, organ support, order, density, weight and masks from t0 through Ltime-6h; B_L summaries and fixed demographics/ICU context are static inputs. It has separate discrete-time heads for hospital death/alive discharge and ICU-RRT/alive ICU exit/death. Fixed ablations are M0, B-context-only, renal-only, support/observation-only and full two-stream. Evaluate only on the same held-out subjects and require component CIF calibration/Brier and replicated component/order contrasts; AUROC gain alone is not a scientific success.

The alternative is scientifically informative because M0 retains interpretable levels, peaks, slopes, counts and strata but loses within-window persistence, timing and missingness trajectories. M1 can reveal whether temporal order/persistence adds information beyond summaries and whether it is renal-stream or support-stream information. The alternative is deferred if sparse/unstable or if it improves prediction without changing component-specific calibration/contrast interpretation. A larger transformer is not chosen because capacity cannot identify missing physiology or intent and would reduce attribution. A latent physiologic state-space model is deferred because MIMIC lacks measured GFR, total-body volume and adjudicated validation targets. A causal treatment analysis is deferred because treatment intent and complete exposure/follow-up are unavailable.

## Alternatives not selected and revisit criteria

Revisit M1 only if held-out calibration and component/order behavior are stable. Revisit a latent physiology model only if measured GFR, muscle or volume markers become available.
