# Rolling-origin temporal robustness of calibrated persistence in frozen repeat-TACE HCC episodes

## Substantive evolution from [prior hypothesis]

The parent asked whether an affine calibration learned before 2023-01-01 transported better than raw persistence and inherited M1 in one later calendar block. This child replaces that single-cutoff transport claim with a prespecified, outcome-blind rolling-origin robustness experiment. It retains the parent population, source snapshot, arm definition, assay-specific complete-triplet denominators, exact clocks, inherited M1 feature set and ridge procedure, and prognostic/noncausal limits. The substantive advance is to distinguish a stable temporal transport pattern from a cutoff-specific result, while explicitly allowing recent-window recalibration to reveal drift without claiming that drift has a mechanism.

## Question and hypothesis

The evidence-supported claim from the parent is limited: in the 2023-and-later block, an affine forecast `Yhat = a + bP`, fitted on earlier episodes, had lower test RMSE than raw `Yhat=P` for albumin (3.566 versus 4.332) and total bilirubin (13.519 versus 16.016), while inherited M1 was not better than affine persistence. That single block does not establish temporal stability.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is that affine persistence remains transportable across multiple future calendar origins, and that an expanding-window fit is not materially inferior to a fixed recent-window fit. The primary estimand is descriptive out-of-sample prognostic error in the selected complete-triplet population: paired test-period RMSE difference, with positive `RMSE(raw) - RMSE(affine)` favoring affine calibration. Secondary estimands compare M1 with affine and recent-window affine with expanding-window affine. No estimate is a treatment effect, causal effect, treatment-response effect, or bedside utility measure.

Interpretation is gated rather than forced. A split is transport-interpretable only if each training and test set has at least 20 total episodes and at least 10 systemic and 10 comparator episodes. A supportive pattern requires affine to beat raw with a paired bootstrap interval excluding zero in more than one gated origin and without material calibration failure; stable expanding versus recent performance would support transportability of the simple calibration, whereas repeated recent-window improvement would support temporal drift and adaptation rather than universal transport. An adverse pattern is repeated loss of affine to raw, or deterioration under later origins. Results with sparse systemic support or broad intervals are inconclusive, not negative evidence of transport.

## Population, temporal boundaries, and outcome-blind procedure

The frozen HCC repeat-TACE population is unchanged. From every row of the five required source CSVs, the reconstruction identifies adult HCC patients with a first later TACE 15--90 days after TACE2, excludes the inherited treatment-arm exclusions, and keeps one deterministic event per patient. The two assay-specific complete-triplet populations are 158 albumin episodes (40 systemic, 118 comparator) and 160 total-bilirubin episodes (41 systemic, 119 comparator). Source audit is unchanged: encounters 105,044; diagnoses 1,810,646; procedures 338,040; medications 4,097,517; labs 28,159,928; exact-assay lab rows 476,846; valid uncensored numeric/time assay rows 476,820; seven duplicate groups and zero discordant groups.

Five origins were fixed from calendar time before any Y-dependent metrics were examined: 2022-01-01, 2022-07-01, 2023-01-01, 2023-07-01, and 2024-01-01. Each test window is the following 365 days; the expanding training window is all earlier event dates. The alternate recent training window is the preceding 365 days, ending at the origin. Thus no later Y is used to select origins, window length, model, or hyperparameters. The latest available event date is 2025-02-25, so later origins have fewer observed test episodes and are expected to trigger sparse-arm gates.

For each assay and origin, raw persistence uses `P` unchanged. Expanding and recent affine fits regress `Y` on an intercept and `P` using only their respective pre-origin training episodes. The inherited M1 is fit only in the expanding training window, using the parent decision-time predictors `B`, `P`, age, sex indicators, event timing (`days`), systemic-arm indicator, and P-lead hours; age imputation, scaling, variance filtering, and the locked alpha grid/inner leave-one-out selection are learned inside training only. No test Y is used for fitting, tuning, or selection. Metrics are RMSE, MAE, prediction bias, calibration error summaries, and paired RMSE differences. Uncertainty is a 1,000-replicate patient-frequency bootstrap stratified by systemic/comparator arm, conditional on fitted predictions; it is not universal transport uncertainty.

## Computed results

Managed full-source execution succeeded in 93.8 seconds with no sampling. Results are in `rolling_temporal_robustness_results.json`; the job was `[research job]`. All five sources were scanned and the inherited cohort/event/triplet gates passed.

Only the first origin is fully gated for both assays. At origin 2022-01-01, the 365-day test has 28 episodes (10 systemic, 18 comparator), expanding training has 78 (22, 56), and recent training has 45 (14, 31). Albumin expanding affine RMSE is 2.554 versus raw 2.619, gain 0.065; its bootstrap 95% interval is [-0.692, 0.809], so it is inconclusive. Recent affine RMSE is 2.519, gain versus raw 0.100, and gain versus expanding 0.035; both intervals include zero. Total bilirubin expanding affine RMSE is 4.734 versus raw 7.542, gain 2.808, bootstrap interval [1.070, 4.245]. Recent affine RMSE is 4.809, gain versus raw 2.734, and is not distinguishable from expanding (gain -0.075; interval [-0.183, 0.047]). In this gated origin, affine improves bilirubin over raw, but albumin and recent-versus-expanding comparisons are inconclusive.

The apparent 2023 cutoff result is not contradicted, but subsequent origins cannot meet the prespecified systemic-arm gate. For example, origin 2022-07-01 has only 6 systemic test episodes; origin 2023-01-01 has 5; origin 2023-07-01 has 2; and origin 2024-01-01 has 1. The code reports their metrics descriptively but marks their transport interpretation as ungated. This is an important adverse feasibility finding: the available HCC snapshot cannot establish multi-origin arm-balanced temporal robustness or distinguish expanding from recent recalibration in later eras. Ungated point estimates show affine still beats raw in all later blocks, but they must not be promoted to confirmatory transport claims. M1 is worse than affine at the fully gated 2022 origin for both assays (albumin RMSE 2.708 versus 2.554; bilirubin 8.285 versus 4.734); at ungated later origins its direction varies by assay and origin, reinforcing that incremental M1 value is not established.

## Falsification and meaning of outcomes

- **Supportive:** across at least two independently gated origins, affine beats raw with uncertainty excluding zero, calibration bias remains small relative to the assay-specific observed scale, and recent-window affine does not consistently outperform expanding. This would support reproducible within-institution prognostic calibration transport, not clinical benefit or causal inference.
- **Recent-window advantage:** repeated gated improvement of recent over expanding would indicate temporal drift or stale-history harm and motivate prospective recalibration. It would not identify whether drift reflects assay practice, case mix, treatment, measurement, or biology.
- **Adverse:** affine loses to raw or has worsening bias/error across gated origins. This would falsify stable affine transport in this selected population, not prove raw persistence is clinically adequate or identify a mechanism.
- **Inconclusive:** sparse systemic-arm support, broad intervals, missing assay units, or too few later events. This is the present overall conclusion for multi-origin robustness: one origin is gated and favorable for bilirubin but inconclusive for albumin; all later origins fail the arm-support gate. More data are required rather than a positive or negative bedside recommendation.

## Boundaries and required follow-up

The experiment is prognostic and descriptive only. It cannot adjudicate hepatic deterioration, tumor response, treatment toxicity, or benefit. The sources lack validated assay units/reference ranges, imaging or tumor burden, adjudicated outcomes, reliable outside-care capture, a clinical action threshold, and a prespecified clinical loss function. Repeated patients were reduced under the inherited construction, both eras are from one institution/snapshot, and temporal nonexchangeability remains possible. Stronger conclusions require an independently collected temporal or external cohort, unit harmonization, clinically adjudicated outcomes, sufficient systemic-arm events, and expert review of whether a prediction difference is clinically meaningful. The computed outputs establish execution and quantify uncertainty; they do not establish universal transport, mechanism, or clinical utility.
