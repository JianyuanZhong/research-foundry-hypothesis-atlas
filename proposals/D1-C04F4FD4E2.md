# Admission-anchored laboratory information for a recorded liver-directed HCC pathway

## Scientific deliverable and advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent corrected the major estimand error of conditioning on survival to hour 12, but its primary endpoint still treats every procedure as equivalent. The successor makes the clinical consequence sharper while preserving that admission-anchored design.

The future solver must newly freeze and estimate, for every eligible admission:

1. a clinically narrower, auditable terminal pathway: first recorded liver-directed intervention (L), discharge before L (D), unresolved liver-intervention timing (U_L), or no L/D/U_L by 72 hours (N72);
2. the incremental value of native-time laboratory history over admission and pre-admission information;
3. whether an irregular latent laboratory trajectory adds information beyond transparent laboratory summaries; and
4. whether the laboratory increment and pathway distribution differ by documentary context, with lexical robustness.

This is a recorded-care information study. L is not asserted to be indicated, completed, appropriate, or beneficial treatment.

## Evidence, unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest available evidence is structural and descriptive: the HCC snapshot contains encounter, diagnosis, procedure and time-stamped laboratory tables; the procedure file has 338,040 rows and 7,780 distinct procedure strings in the full source audit, with 322,166 parseable starts and 15,874 missing starts. Frequent strings include transarterial embolization/TACE, ablation, hepatic resection and liver transplantation. A prior parent audit found 1,810,646 diagnosis rows and HCC-core text on 105,044 encounter keys, but its A/P/T/U counts were provisional for the exact conflict rule.

These facts do not establish active HCC, stage, tumor burden, imaging/pathology confirmation, treatment intent, receipt or completion. Diagnosis rows have no native time; pathology has no native time; procedures can lack start times; numeric laboratory results have no unit/reference-range column.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults with a first observed eligible inpatient HCC-coded admission, does assay-specific laboratory history observed strictly before each boundary improve calibrated prediction of the first recorded liver-directed pathway through 72 hours beyond admission/pre-admission information, and is that incremental information stable across documentary contexts?

The directional expectation is that laboratory information will improve the held-out proper score for L versus D/N72, with a larger increment in encounter-context documentation (A) than pooled non-A documentation, because documentary context may reflect different recorded-care pathways. This is falsifiable and is not a claim that A denotes active disease or that laboratories cause intervention.

A supportive result would identify a transportable recorded-care signal worth testing prospectively with adjudicated active-HCC and indication data. An adverse result would discourage overinterpreting laboratory/documentation associations. Either result is useful; it does not establish clinical utility or a treatment recommendation.

## Models and split

Use one deterministic patient split: participant-hash buckets 0–59 fit, 60–69 frozen preprocessing/regularization selection, 70–79 locked evaluation. All records for a patient stay in one bucket; 80–99 are reserved/inaccessible and are not external validation. Freeze cohort, dictionary, assay set, preprocessing, state dimension and hyperparameters before test predictions.

B0 is a transparent multinomial cause-specific hazard model at boundaries 0, 6, 12 and 24 hours. Inputs are age, sex, admitting department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, prior encounter count, and pre-admission assay-specific nearest/count/recency/missingness summaries. It excludes Q labels, diagnosis/document text, orders, medications, procedure text, discharge after the boundary and future labs.

B1 uses exactly B0's target, split, preprocessing and regularization, adding assay-specific time-safe lab summaries: first/last valid value, change, elapsed time, count, missingness/density and within-assay slope when at least two valid times support it. It is the transparent laboratory-increment comparator.

B2 is the substantive alternative: an assay-specific irregular state-space model with latent smooth multivariate laboratory state, assay-specific offsets/noise, irregular observation intervals, shrinkage, posterior state uncertainty and coupled competing-hazard heads for L/D/U_L/N72. It is fitted to the same rows, target and split. B2 may reveal nonlinear joint trajectories and informative timing that B1's summaries discard; it is not a validated physiologic score. A density/count-only model is required to determine whether gains are merely testing intensity.

For each model, integrate interval hazards to an all-admission terminal distribution. Primary held-out comparisons are multiclass log score and Brier score, with interval-specific calibration and 72-hour calibration. Define the sign before evaluation so positive `delta_i` means B1 improves over B0; report `delta_all^q` as the observed-population weighted patient-level paired contrast and `H^q=E(delta_i|A)-E(delta_i|not-A)` under each Q encoding. Report B2-minus-B1, modality composition, absolute L probability, and the parent's semantics-agnostic sensitivity separately. Do not call predictions counterfactual treatment probabilities.

Use patient-clustered bootstrap (>=500, preferably 1,000) preserving each patient's complete risk-set sequence; report uncertainty for score differences, calibration, terminal probabilities and H. Partial pooling is a sensitivity for documentary groups, never a substitute for support counts.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required falsifications:

1. Shuffle lab times within patient/assay and, separately, lab values within assay; a trajectory-linked gain should attenuate.
2. Compare value/trajectory models with count/density/missingness-only features; equivalence indicates observation-process information, not laboratory physiology.
3. Permute terminal labels within calendar-era/admitting-department cells; surviving gains indicate leakage or documentation artifact.
4. Apply a forbidden-window sentinel: future [12,24) labs must not improve prediction of an earlier interval.
5. Re-run exact-boundary, tie, discharge-before-procedure, and conservative/optimistic U_L rules; material changes make the primary result inconclusive.
6. Permute Q labels within supported era/department cells; heterogeneity should attenuate.
7. Recompute Q encodings without raw untimed diagnosis text as prediction features; sign changes are lexical fragility.
8. Vary the frozen L dictionary only as a blinded sensitivity (core strict dictionary versus prespecified expanded dictionary); do not select on test performance.
9. Report the parent's all-procedure P/D/Uproc/N72 endpoint and the R12-only analysis as sensitivities, never as the primary L estimand.
10. Use orders, examinations, documents and pathology only for gated concordance audits. No order establishes intent or receipt; no text establishes time-linked imaging/pathology confirmation.

Supportive evidence requires correct joins and time flow; an uncertainty-supported positive B1-minus-B0 score contrast for the primary L terminal distribution; stable A-versus-not-A H across Q encodings; calibration improvement; attenuation under time, value, label and Q permutations; no dominance by density or U_L handling; and reproducible B2 improvement over B1 if the learned alternative is claimed informative.

Adverse evidence includes null/opposite contrasts, permutation-surviving gains, density-only equivalence, large dictionary/U_L sensitivity, or a result driven by one procedure string. This rejects the directional information/heterogeneity claim or identifies process artifact; it does not prove laboratories have no clinical value.

Inconclusive evidence includes high U_L mass, sparse L modality or documentary strata, unstable precedence, wide clustered intervals, insufficient repeated assays, nonconvergence, or disagreement across dictionaries/Q encodings. Inconclusive must remain the conclusion.

## Selection and deferred alternatives

The transparent B0/B1 pair is required to identify the incremental contribution of laboratory values beyond baseline and observation intensity. B2 is selected because irregular, coupled assay trajectories could reveal clinically relevant transition information lost by first/last/change summaries; its latent states are treated as statistical representations, not physiology.

Deferred alternatives are raw diagnosis/document sequence learning (no native times or validated scope/temporality), imaging/pathology confirmation (no images and no time-linked adjudicated truth), causal treatment-effect analysis (no indication, receipt, eligibility, counterfactual or outcomes), and a generic neural sequence model (revisit only with a prespecified target, same split, calibration/uncertainty and measured resource need). A liver-directed dictionary is not evidence of treatment intent; revisit its clinical interpretation only after chart adjudication. No complexity or GPU bonus is assumed.
