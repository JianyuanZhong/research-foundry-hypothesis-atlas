## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim currently supported by the parent evidence is methodological: recorded support, observation intensity and transfer topology can be separated, and documented non-ICU bridging is a lower-bound marker while any later ICU return is an upper-bound marker. The prior broad audit is feasibility evidence only: under its adult first-stay/12-hour rule there were 52,591 eligible first outtimes, 2,661 later ICU returns within 48 hours, 2,060 with a qualifying bridge and 601 without one; 294 returns had multiple bridge candidates. It did not estimate exposure associations.

The unresolved clinical claim is whether different recent support transitions carry different *competing* early-failure signatures. Among adults at a valid first ICU outtime with adequate recorded respiratory and MAP observation, compare:

- durable recorded-off: no positive respiratory or vasoactive evidence in either six-hour half;
- recent respiratory withdrawal: respiratory-positive in the preceding half and no positive respiratory evidence in the final half;
- recent vasoactive cessation: vasoactive-positive in the preceding half and no positive vasoactive evidence in the final half;
- joint recent withdrawal: both domain definitions.

The primary hypothesis is an interaction/contrast hypothesis, not a prediction-ranking claim:

1. relative to durable recorded-off, recent respiratory withdrawal has a larger 48-hour contrast for the latent transition-compatible ICU-escalation event than recent vasoactive cessation;
2. recent vasoactive cessation has a larger 48-hour in-hospital-death contrast than recent respiratory withdrawal;
3. joint withdrawal has the largest total early-failure burden, but its return and death components may differ.

For each exposure group, let L_h be the 6/24/48-hour CIF of a documented non-ICU bridge followed by the first later ICU return, and U_h the CIF of any later ICU return. The latent transition CIF T_h is only known nonparametrically to satisfy L_h <= T_h <= U_h. For each pairwise exposure contrast A versus B, report the compatible interval [L_A-U_B, U_A-L_B], not a midpoint. Death and alive discharge remain competing first events. The primary directional claims must be evaluated at all three horizons and across the locked missing-support q grid and topology variants.

The domain hierarchy is falsified if the relevant worst-case contrast intervals include zero or reverse direction, if the hierarchy is not stable at 6/24/48 hours, if adjustment for observation/process variables collapses it, if the result is dependent on one support channel or bridge rule, or if transfer-topology permutation preserves it. A positive aggregate-return contrast with a null or negative lower-bound contrast is adverse for the stronger transition interpretation, not supportive. Wide overlapping intervals, sparse domain strata, poor positivity or unstable q/topology sensitivity are inconclusive.

This is not a claim that support withdrawal causes failure, that ICU discharge was inappropriate, or that any return was unplanned or preventable. Respiratory and vasoactive recorded evidence are proxies for treatment/documentation states.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

ICU discharge decisions require distinguishing patients likely to return to intensive care from patients likely to die before return; those pathways imply different monitoring and escalation needs. Existing broad “residual instability” signals do not resolve whether a respiratory transition and a hemodynamic transition have different endpoint profiles, and an aggregate return can conceal missing or immediate transfer paths. The substantive advance is a prespecified competing-pathway estimand with partial identification: it can show a robust domain-specific association, show that only aggregate return is associated, or demonstrate that topology/documentation uncertainty prevents the clinical interpretation. It does not convert a retrospective signal into a bedside policy.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods use identical data, exposure ledger, q scenarios, topology outcomes, covariates and subject splits.

The transparent baseline is pooled one-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards for confirmed return, unconfirmed return, death and alive discharge, plus an aggregate-return model. Include the four exposure categories, preceding/final physiology summaries, support evidence, masks, observation/process variables, admission context, age, gender, anchor-year group, ICU LOS and prespecified domain interactions. Standardize held-out predictions to the common primary population and calculate L, U, their contrast bounds, death contrasts and joint-vs-domain contrasts. Fit nested physiology/support-only, mask/process-only and full models; use a landmark coverage model only for descriptive standardization, not causal correction.

The alternative is an observation-aware two-domain factorized hidden semi-Markov model with respiratory and hemodynamic duration states, constrained persistence/withdrawal transitions, emissions from the same physiology/support ledger, and an observation model for masks, row counts and source channels. Its post-landmark state component distinguishes ICU, documented non-ICU and unknown location and predicts the same observable hazards plus a model-based latent transition hazard. Fit state/duration/regularization on training/validation; evaluate the test latent output over declared transfer-capture sensitivity/false-bridge grids, and reject any output outside [L,U]. It can reveal ordered duration and domain discordance lost by fixed summaries, but its latent point estimate is never identified truth.

A flexible GRU/TCN is deferred because it adds sequence capacity without resolving missing-path endpoint semantics. Causal discharge-policy, treatment-limitation, validated intent/readiness, ward-monitoring, waveform/image, external-validation and renal-ground-truth analyses are deferred because those evidence are unavailable.

## Uncertainty, falsification and interpretation

Use subject-level bootstrap 95% intervals, preserving lower/upper bound logic, and repeat seeds 17/29/43. Report calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier, event counts, overlap, positivity, effective sample size and bound widths; AUROC is secondary. Predeclare no-leakage timestamp audits, within-window time shuffling, storetime negative control, mask/support/physiology/process ablations, procedure-removal and channel-specific analyses, q/window/coverage/topology/tie sensitivities, transfer-careunit permutation, +6-hour transfer shift, pre-landmark pseudo-return negative control, discharge-as-censoring sensitivity, first-careunit/admission-type/anchor-year/transfer-density strata and latent-state stability.

Supportive evidence requires the respiratory-versus-vasoactive hierarchy to be directionally stable in the worst-case bounds with uncertainty excluding the null for the prespecified contrast, adequate overlap/events, baseline/alternative agreement, loss of topology signal after transfer permutation and no dependence on storetime or one channel. It supports only retrospective record-compatible associations. Adverse evidence includes reversal/collapse after q/observation adjustment, a signal only for U but not L, dependence on one topology rule, persistence after permutation, non-positivity or latent estimates outside bounds. Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals, sparse domain strata, unstable q/topology frontiers, high latent entropy or poor transport overlap.

No result establishes causality, discharge appropriateness, intent, preventability, surveillance adequacy, treatment limitation, or a monitoring/discharge policy. Those require clinician adjudication of intent and deterioration, validated transfer semantics, treatment-limitation and ward-monitoring data, external validation and preferably a prospective/causal study.
