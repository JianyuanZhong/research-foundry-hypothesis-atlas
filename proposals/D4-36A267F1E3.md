## Opening, supported claim, unresolved hypothesis, and clinical advance

The strongest inspected evidence supports only three bounded claims. Large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, that difference was associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes, and a combined equation may discard prognostic information [K2]. Administrative AKI coding can be insensitive and subgroup-dependent relative to creatinine-defined AKI [K3]. None establishes biological AKI selectivity at fixed combined eGFR, the meaning of UKB first appearances as encounters or onset, or a clinical action.

The unresolved hypothesis is: among supported P− and P0 profiles at the same combined eGFR, P− has a larger ten-year contrast for first recorded N17 than for first recorded N18 after measured baseline characteristics and pre-index first-appearance history are represented. Define `theta17=log RR10(N17-first; P−/P0)`, `theta18=log RR10(N18-first; P−/P0)`, and the frozen renal-selectivity estimand `D=theta17−theta18`, standardized over the same eligible cohort.

The leading bounded explanation is recorded acute-renal selectivity. The strongest rival is stable generalized morbidity/recording propensity: persistently greater opportunity for discovery or denser same-day coding may increase both later N17 and N18 recording. They differ observably if a calibrated pre-index process predicts broad recording yet the N17-over-N18 contrast persists; attenuation under that process favors the rival. Neither comparison identifies healthcare contact, testing, admission severity, coding intent, morbidity, or biological onset.

The advance is a same-question comparison: a transparent joint competing-risk baseline versus a sparse-history-aware hurdle/shared-frailty process, selected by held-out calibration and recovery rather than complexity. It can reveal whether the timing and conditional density of pre-index first appearances carry stable information that scalar counts discard.

## Chosen substantive alternative: support-aware hurdle plus one shared frailty

The alternative uses the same population, transitions, covariates, estimands, split, standardization, and bootstrap. Define `H_i=1` when at least one valid nonrenal discovery date occurs in `[index−10y,index)`. A fixed-effects Bernoulli hurdle models H using age, sex, assessment year/centre, and frozen clinical covariates. It does not label H=0 as healthy or estimate a person score from absence alone.

Conditional on H=1, fit yearly piecewise-exponential recurrent discovery-date intensity with one shared `b_i~N(0,sigma_b^2)`, calendar/age/sex/clinical terms, and the observed ten-year window as exposure. Conditional bundle size on each date follows a zero-truncated negative binomial with loading `lambda_mark*b_i`. For H=0, retain the covariate-conditioned population distribution of b and an H=0 indicator; never substitute a fitted zero or empirical-Bayes point. Integrate `p(b_i|pre-index history)` by adaptive quadrature or >=50 deterministic posterior draws. No post-S event updates b.

Three variants are frozen: V0 fixed hurdle/date/mark effects without frailty; V1 hurdle plus intensity frailty, with bundle summaries retained as fixed covariates; V2 hurdle plus one frailty shared by intensity and marks. Choose the simplest variant meeting all gates; V2 may replace V1 only if the mark loading contributes identifiable frequency-density information, not a small predictive gain.

Before any profile effect is opened, require on held-out data:

- hurdle calibration within 5 percentage points overall and within sex × age-band and assessment-period cells with >=100 participants;
- yearly date-count observed/expected 0.8–1.25 in at least 8/10 years and no systematic early/late sign reversal;
- conditional bundle mean error <=20% and 90th-quantile error <=20% or one group;
- converged quadrature and <=0.01 integrated-Brier degradation from the transparent baseline for sufficiently supported pooled N17, N18, and death;
- >=90% convergence in 200 parametric recovery datasets preserving the observed no/one/two/three-plus-history pattern, median relative bias <=20% for sigma_b and lambda_mark when nonzero, and empirical 95% coverage 85–98%;
- stability to deleting the early or late five-year half. V2 additionally requires lambda_mark's 95% interval to exclude both zero and effects smaller than a 10% bundle-mean change per frailty SD; if its interval also spans a >=25% change, it is too unstable and V1 is selected.

If V1 fails frailty recovery, V0 is reported but cannot satisfy the recording-process robustness gate; the renal result is then inconclusive for the stated hypothesis. Never revive two person factors. After selection, generate fold-external posterior nodes, estimate standardized pre-index P−/P0 contrasts in hurdle probability, annual date intensity, and conditional bundle mean as falsifiers, and fit the renal model with population integration. Bootstrap process fitting, cross-fitting, matching, renal fitting, and standardization together.

This alternative retains event times, separates any-history support from recurrence, represents bundle marks only where observed, and propagates uncertainty for thin histories. The baseline loses that timing/mark covariance; the alternative loses transparency and still cannot identify contact, morbidity, severity, or coding cause. Conditioning may block morbidity pathways, so baseline-versus-alternative attenuation is a robustness contrast, never a causal direct effect.

Deferred: the two-factor correlated process requires denser repeat histories plus successful recovery; code-composition NMF answers a different salience/comorbidity question; a transformer is deferred because UKB provides sparse first-appearance summaries rather than encounter sequences; true-GFR latent modeling needs measured GFR or repeated-marker error data.
