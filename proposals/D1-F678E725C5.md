# Calendar-stable laboratory payload information beyond assay selection in HCC

## Scientific question, unresolved claim, and advance

The mature HCC branch asks whether strictly prior laboratory results add calibrated information about a subsequent locally recorded return beyond capture intensity and the prior assay menu. That is a useful identifiability repair, but it leaves a clinically consequential transportability question unresolved: the meaning and availability of an assay result may change as laboratory panels, documentation workflows, referral patterns, and coding practice change over the 2010–2025 source span. A pooled patient split could therefore report an average payload increment that is real only in one historical regime.

Among adults with a first locally recorded HCC-keyed inpatient encounter, does assay-specific prior laboratory payload (P) add information beyond strict prior capture/process (Q) and assay menu/sampling (M) for the next locally recorded return, and is the P-minus-M increment stable across prespecified calendar eras?

The primary hypothesis is that P-minus-M improves held-out calibrated prediction of 30-day local recorded return in the later era (2021–2025) after the vocabulary and preprocessing are frozen using earlier eras, with no material reversal relative to the earlier-era estimate. A secondary hypothesis is that payload information is more specific to return than to later local re-observation. The hypothesis is falsified if P-minus-M is null or harmful in the later locked era, reverses sign beyond its prespecified uncertainty bound, disappears after calendar-stratified calibration, or is reproduced by M-minus-Q/process-only controls. This is not a claim that the signal is causal, clinically useful, or transportable outside this institution.

The substantive advance is a domain-shift estimand, not a new model for a small score gain. It distinguishes “payload contains information” from “payload happened to encode a historical documentation regime.” The result could determine whether a future follow-up score can be safely updated across practice eras or requires re-fitting and local validation. Existing evidence supports only that the frozen snapshot contains the required dated structured fields and that a local recorded-return/observation process is computable. No model result or clinical conclusion is established here.

## Population, time boundaries, labels, and exact information boundary

For each patient retain the earliest eligible HCC-keyed encounter ordered by native `入院时间`, then `就诊号`; do not replace an invalid earliest record with a later one. Set t0=`入院时间`, td=`出院时间`. Require td to be before the administrative horizon.

The primary label is R=1 if any other same-patient encounter has parseable `入院时间` in [td, td+30 days), requiring candidate admission >=td; use the earliest and publish equal-time `就诊号` ties. Define O_L as a same-patient encounter beginning in [td+30 days,td+L). Define Z_L=R, N+_L when R=0 and O_L=1, or N-_L when R=0 and O_L=0. Use L=90 primary and 60/180 sensitivities. Future encounters and O_L are never predictors.

Primary temporal evaluation is a frozen calendar-domain experiment: development anchors with t0 in [2012-01-01, 2019-01-01) are fit; [2019-01-01, 2021-01-01) is a selection/calibration era; [2021-01-01, 2026-01-01) is a locked later-era test. A prespecified patient-disjoint random split within the development era is a secondary precision analysis, not a replacement for the later-era test. Patients with multiple eligible records are already reduced to one first anchor, and the patient hash partition is applied before any row expansion. If any era has inadequate event or assay support, the affected era contrast is inconclusive rather than pooled or redefined.

All primary history rows must be linked to a non-anchor encounter for the same patient with parseable native admission/discharge and linked discharge <t0. The linked encounter admission must precede t0. The source-native availability time must be parseable and in [t0-730 days,t0): labs `检验时间`; procedures `开始时间`; orders `开立时间` (not later `开始时间`); medications `开始时间`; examinations `开始时间`. Exclude anchor-linked rows, even if their event time is earlier. Publish counts for invalid/missing times, missing linked discharge, open/overlapping stays, anchor-linked rows, post-t0 rows, duplicates, unmatched two-key joins, and row inflation. This is an availability attribution policy, not proof that a clinician saw a result at that instant.

## Nested information sets and models

Exclude direct identifiers `姓名`, `身份证号`, `手机号`, `医保/就诊卡号`, and `住院号`.

Q includes only admission/context and strict capture: age, sex, department, admission era/time-of-day, completed prior-encounter count/duration/active days, time since last completed encounter, source-specific row counts and distinct linked-encounter counts for labs/procedures/orders/medications/examinations, source-type count, invalid/excluded-time counts, left-truncation and no-history indicators. It has no names, assay identity, result, treatment or procedure content.

M adds prior laboratory menu and sampling structure but no results: normalized `检验` identity, assay-specific counts and distinct encounters, recency/span, sampling-time count, `标本类型` availability/missingness and result-missingness indicators. Vocabulary/rare-category handling is fit-only and frozen before selection/test.

P adds assay-specific `定性结果`, valid assay-specific `定量结果`, `标本类型`, and within-assay change/slope only with at least two distinct valid `检验时间` values. Numeric values are never pooled across assays because metadata confirms no separate lab-unit column. Define numeric parsing and impossible-value rules before locked predictions; missing results remain missingness features, not normal values.

The transparent baseline is fit-only regularized logistic regression B_Q/B_M/B_P for R and multinomial B_Q/B_M/B_P for Z_90 (with the same patient partitions, feature manifests, preprocessing, calibration and locked cases). Its contribution is an auditable nested information contrast, but it loses event order and the distinction between return and later observability.

The substantive alternative is a matched discrete-time multistate model H_Q/H_M/H_P: intervals [0,7), [7,14), [14,30) from td for return, then [30,L) for N+_L among nonreturns, using complementary-log-log cause-specific hazards with interval offsets and the same Q/M/P vectors. Convert hazards to coherent R, N+_L and N-_L probabilities. This reveals whether a temporal domain shift affects return hazard or only the later observation process, which B cannot show. H is chosen for this scientific interpretation, not because it is complex or has a small predictive advantage. An irregular latent sequence encoder is deferred until the primary P-minus-M domain result is supported and residual nonlinear structure is demonstrated; the missing HCC images and untimed document/pathology fields do not justify a multimodal reproduction.

## Split, fitting, estimands, uncertainty, and completion

Freeze cohort, row ledgers, labels, parser, assay vocabulary, feature dimensions, standardization, penalty and calibration before accessing locked later-era outcomes. Fit all vocabulary, transformations, hyperparameters and calibrators using only the development era; use the 2019–2020 era for selection and calibration, then evaluate once on 2021–2025. For the secondary random precision split, hash normalized `患者主索引` modulo 100: 0–59 fit, 60–69 selection/calibration, 70–79 locked evaluation, 80–99 inaccessible. No patient and no linked history/future row may cross its assigned bucket.

Primary estimands are paired later-era proper-score contrasts for R: B_P-minus-B_M and H_P-minus-H_M, with B_M-minus-B_Q/H_M-minus-H_Q as process/menu comparators. Report the same contrasts in development-era held-out data and the difference-in-contrast (later minus earlier), with an interaction confidence interval. Secondary estimands are P-minus-M for Z_90 and state-specific R versus N+_90, plus L=60/180 and era-by-era calibration. Do not use P-minus-Q as payload evidence; retain it only for continuity.

Evaluate paired locked log loss and Brier (lower is better), calibration intercept/slope/reliability, and state-specific decompositions. Use >=1,000 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples, preserving all rows/intervals per patient, for 95% intervals. Apply a prespecified minimum support per era/state/interval; otherwise mark the contrast inconclusive. Net-benefit curves at 5%, 10%, 20% are optional descriptive sensitivity analyses only if support is adequate and never establish utility.

Completion requires: (1) frozen cohort, exact-join and availability ledger; (2) era support and assay-menu drift report; (3) Q/M/P feature manifests and parser audit; (4) locked B and H predictions for development-held-out, selection and later-era test cases; (5) paired scores, calibration, era interaction and clustered intervals; (6) observation-process decomposition; (7) permutation/leakage/time and coherence falsifications; and (8) a claim-to-output table.

## Falsification and interpretation

Mandatory checks are: payload permutation within assay and era preserving identity, times, counts and missingness; assay-identity permutation preserving values/times/counts; complete-history shuffle within split preserving Q; within-patient time shuffle; era-label permutation; and a process-only H_M/B_M reproduction check. Verify no post-t0, anchor-linked, current-anchor, future-window or split-leaked feature. Reconcile the inherited 42,681/4,913 versus 42,688/4,916 anchor audit discrepancy before confirmatory interpretation. Verify coherent multistate probabilities and weighted agreement between all-state and observation-stratum scores.

Supportive evidence requires a favorable, calibrated later-era P-minus-M for R with an interval excluding clinically negligible harm, an interaction compatible with prespecified stability, attenuation under payload permutation, H_M not reproducing H_P, and no ledger/leakage problem. Concordant Z_90 or R-versus-N+_90 evidence and at least one supported sensitivity L strengthen the observation interpretation. This supports only incremental information in locally recorded prior assay payload under the tested local era shift.

Adverse evidence is a null/harmful later-era P-minus-M, a clinically important sign reversal, M-minus-Q explaining P-minus-Q, H_M reproducing H_P, persistence after payload permutation, or P-minus-M confined to N+_L. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse later-era events/assays, wide era interaction intervals, major invalid-time/result burden, unstable parser or assay vocabulary, unresolved count reconciliation, boundary sensitivity, or inadequate unit comparability. A later-era failure does not prove that labs lack biological information; it indicates this local score is not stable under the tested documentation regime. A positive result does not establish active HCC, stage, liver reserve, indication, intent, treatment receipt/benefit, mortality, outside-care completeness, causal effects, utility, or external transportability.

## Compute, method choice, and evidence limits

The future solver envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM, 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds. Ledger/features/fits and era bootstrap are planned CPU-first; this is a planning estimate, not a measured runtime. The sparse nested logistic and multistate hazard models should fit within the envelope, but memory and time must be measured by readiness or a bounded diagnostic. GPU use is not required. A neural/irregular sequence alternative remains deferred, with revisit triggered by robust later-era P-minus-M plus nonlinear residual evidence and adequate assay density; one allocated A100 using `cuda:0` could then be considered. No proposer or solver weights are trained here.

The final scientific deliverable is newly fitted era-aware Q/M/P baseline and multistate models, frozen later-era predictions, the P-minus-M and M-minus-Q score/calibration/interaction estimates, observation-process decomposition, and all audit/falsification artifacts. Completion is the artifact set regardless of whether the hypothesis is supported. Computational QA can verify these outputs and whether conclusions follow from them. Clinical adjudication, timestamp/release logs, death and outside-care linkage, imaging/pathology with native dates, stage/resectability and treatment-intent labels, prospective validation, and an intervention/utility study are required for stronger clinical claims.

## Dispositions and revisiting alternatives

The natural-history demonstration supports dated structured histories and calibration ideas, but its Danish external-validation data are unavailable; this is a bounded HCC adaptation, not reproduction. The Bayesian demonstration motivates an irregular longitudinal alternative, but the original genetic inputs are unavailable and an EHR-only adaptation changes the model. The cancer demonstration supplement supports multimodal ablations, but the main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable and local HCC images are absent, so multimodal reproduction is deferred.

The parent’s pooled Q→M→P design remains the baseline scientific branch. The selected child adds the calendar-domain estimand and is preferred only if later-era support is adequate; otherwise the parent’s pooled descriptive result remains the appropriate parked branch. Revisit the deferred sequence model only after stable payload evidence and residual temporal nonlinearity. Revisit causal testing or clinical utility only with treatment intent/receipt, harms, adjudicated outcomes and prospective validation.
