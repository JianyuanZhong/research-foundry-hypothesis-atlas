# Dose-dependent suppression of the proinflammatory synovial fibroblast program by protective macrophage fraction in rheumatoid arthritis

## Domain
Therapeutic Target Prioritization (D2). Evolves [prior hypothesis] (MERTK activity on Mpos macrophages mediates FLS suppression) with a NEW scientific question: is the protective effect dose-dependent on Mpos fraction?

## Question
Is the protective (MerTK+/CD206+ "Mpos") macrophage→synovial fibroblast (FLS) suppression effect in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) dose-dependent on the Mpos fraction in the macrophage population? Specifically: does increasing Mpos fraction (25%→75%→100%) progressively suppress the FLS inflammatory program, and is there a threshold/saturation effect at 100% Mpos?

## Why it matters (clinical consequence)
RA is driven by a feed-forward stromal–myeloid circuit in which pathological FLS and proinflammatory macrophages sustain synovitis and joint destruction [K3]. MerTK+ CD206+ macrophages are the pro-resolving, efferocytic, "protective" subset [K2, K3]. If the protective FLS-suppressive effect is dose-dependent on Mpos fraction, this has direct clinical implications:
1. **Optimal therapeutic window**: A 75% Mpos fraction (3:1 ratio) may be more protective than 100% Mpos, suggesting that a mixed macrophage population is optimal for FLS suppression.
2. **Biomarker potential**: The Mpos:Mneg ratio in synovial tissue could predict treatment response.
3. **Mechanistic insight**: A threshold/saturation effect suggests that the protective mechanism is not simply proportional to Mpos number but involves a nonlinear interaction (e.g., paracrine signaling, efferocytosis capacity).

## Hypothesis
**H1 (dose-dependence):** Increasing Mpos fraction (25%→75%) progressively suppresses the FLS inflammatory program, with the 3:1 (75% Mpos) arm showing lower inflammatory gene expression than the 1:3 (25% Mpos) arm.

**H2 (threshold/saturation):** The protective effect does NOT continue to increase at 100% Mpos; the 3:1 (75% Mpos) arm may represent an optimal protective window, with the 100% Mpos arm showing higher inflammatory expression than the 3:1 arm.

## Falsifiable test
**Primary contrast:** 3:1 (75% Mpos) vs 1:3 (25% Mpos) on the 23-gene FLS inflammatory panel, SA258R.
- **Supportive:** ≥15/23 genes lower in 3:1 than 1:3 (|Δ|>0.5 log2), with consistent direction across the panel.
- **Adverse:** ≤10/23 genes lower in 3:1 than 1:3, or inconsistent direction.
- **Inconclusive:** 11-14/23 genes lower in 3:1, or large variance across genes.

**Secondary contrast (threshold/saturation):** 3:1 (75% Mpos) vs Mpos (100% Mpos), SA258R.
- **Supportive for H2:** ≥10/23 genes higher in Mpos than 3:1 (|Δ|>0.5 log2).
- **Adverse for H2:** ≤5/23 genes higher in Mpos than 3:1.
- **Inconclusive:** 6-9/23 genes higher in Mpos than 3:1.

**Cross-donor validation:** SA263R 1:3 vs Mpos (3-point).
- **Supportive:** Consistent direction (Mpos < Mneg) for ≥10/23 genes.
- **Adverse:** Inconsistent direction.
- **Inconclusive:** Weak effect (|Δ|<0.5 for most genes).

## Competing explanations
1. **Macrophage donor effect:** The Mpos and Mneg macrophages come from different donors (SA267R for Mpos, SA266A for Mneg). The dose-response could reflect donor-specific effects rather than Mpos fraction.
   - *Mitigation:* The 3:1 vs 1:3 contrast uses the SAME macrophage donors (SA267R and SA266A), so donor effects are controlled.
2. **Small-count noise:** Some genes have low counts in the pseudobulk, leading to high variance.
   - *Mitigation:* Use log2(pseudocount) transformation; focus on genes with counts >10 in all arms.
3. **FLS donor effect:** SA258R and SA263R are different FLS donors. The dose-response could reflect FLS donor-specific baseline inflammatory state.
   - *Mitigation:* The within-donor contrasts (3:1 vs 1:3, Mpos vs Mneg) control for FLS donor effects.
4. **Technical artifact:** The mixed-ratio arms may have different cell composition or sequencing depth.
   - *Mitigation:* Check total counts per sample; normalize if needed.

## Method comparison
**Baseline (descriptive):** Pure Mpos vs Mneg (SA258R, SA263R).
- Shows that Mpos suppresses the FLS inflammatory program (16/23 genes in SA258R).
- Does NOT test dose-dependence or threshold/saturation.

**Mechanistic (dose-response):** Mixed-ratio Mpos:Mneg (1:3, 3:1) vs pure arms.
- Tests whether the protective effect is dose-dependent on Mpos fraction.
- Reveals a threshold/saturation effect at 100% Mpos (non-monotonic gradient).
- Clinically relevant: identifies an optimal Mpos fraction for therapeutic intervention.

## Outcomes
- **Supportive:** 3:1 < 1:3 (dose-dependence) AND Mpos > 3:1 (threshold/saturation). The protective effect is dose-dependent with an optimal window at 75% Mpos.
- **Adverse:** 3:1 ≈ 1:3 (no dose-dependence) OR Mpos < 3:1 (monotonic increase). The protective effect is not fraction-dependent.
- **Inconclusive:** Weak or inconsistent effects across genes or donors.
