# Episode 49: does continuous lactate trajectory provide material 24-hour decision value beyond the current lactate?

## Why this successor is needed

The selected result-bearing parent, [prior hypothesis], established computational feasibility and narrowed—but did not resolve—the incremental prognostic question. In its age-inclusive 24-hour EICU risk set (1,229 people, 33 hospitals, 507 eventual hospital deaths), adding continuous trajectory T=ln(F/B) and T² to a model containing the absolute late lactate changed out-of-hospital AUROC from 0.72139 to 0.72619: delta AUROC=+0.00479 with a 2,000-replicate copied-hospital refit-bootstrap 95% interval of -0.00849 to +0.02286. Brier improvement was +0.000756 (-0.00266 to +0.00435), calibration was acceptable, and all 33 fixed-prediction site deletions favored the trajectory model. The strongest evidence-supported claim is therefore only that this locked continuous trajectory produced a small, site-stable point improvement and that clustered uncertainty did not exclude either no gain or the parent's arbitrary +0.02 AUROC margin. It does not establish decision value, causal effects, treatment response, or usefulness of binary nonclearance.

Episode 48 and the parent also show why the successor must not return to a binary nonclearance contrast. Within quintiles of the late lactate F, clearance/nonclearance support was 233/0, 229/30, 156/79, 133/120, and 37/212; the positivity gate failed structurally. Episode 49 therefore contains no binary X predictor, exposure contrast, overlap claim, subgroup, or hypothesis. The 33-hospital list is carried forward as a frozen population identifier ([source checksum]) rather than regenerated from or interpreted through X. This preserves the parent's exact population while preventing the failed binary construct from entering inference.

The unresolved clinical question is more direct than whether AUROC rises by 0.02: at the 24-hour landmark, when the late lactate is already available, does retaining the earlier lactate and expressing its continuous trajectory improve mortality-risk decisions enough to matter under explicit high-risk review thresholds? A decision-support display, escalation review, or goals-of-care prompt can impose workload and downstream harms. A small change in rank discrimination is not itself evidence that those decisions improve. Conversely, an increment smaller than 0.02 AUROC could still improve decisions near relevant thresholds. The proposed estimand puts true- and false-positive classifications on the same explicit utility scale while retaining the parent's leakage-safe, out-of-hospital prediction procedure.

The literature supports the premise, not the new claim. The inspected full-text XML of Baysan et al. (2022; DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; frozen source [source checksum]) reports that in critically ill adults with sepsis, adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV increased C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64 and that admission plus 24-hour lactate had the best fit. It does not test trajectory beyond a model already containing late lactate in this norepinephrine-entry phenotype, nor decision value. The inspected full-text XML review by Levy et al. (2026; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100061; frozen source [source checksum]) supports serial interpretation but documents adrenergic, inflammatory, ischemia-reperfusion, hepatic, and renal influences; it cannot identify a mechanism here. The inspected abstract—not article body—of Vickers and Elkin (2006; DOI 10.1177/0272989X06295361; Europe PMC search source [source checksum]) describes decision curves as weighting false positives against false negatives through threshold probability. Direct article acquisition returned only a web-application-firewall page, so no claim relies on uninspected full text.

## Falsifiable primary hypothesis and estimand

Let the fixed threshold grid be P={0.30,0.35,0.40,0.45,0.50}. It brackets high-risk decisions around the parent's observed 41.25% mortality prevalence. The grid is an explicit utility scenario, not an empirically measured clinician or patient preference. Each threshold p implies false-positive weight w(p)=p/(1-p) relative to one true positive. For model m in the held-out predictions:

NB_m(p) = TP_m(p)/N - FP_m(p)/N × p/(1-p).

Treat-none has net benefit zero; treat-all has prevalence - (1-prevalence) × p/(1-p).

The primary estimand is integrated incremental net benefit over the five prespecified thresholds:

iINB = mean over p in P of [NB_M1(p)-NB_M0(p)].

100×iINB is the average number of additional net true-positive equivalents per 100 landmark-eligible people, under equal weight on the five threshold utilities. The materiality margin is iINB=0.005, or 0.5 net true-positive equivalents per 100 people. The hypothesis is:

> In the frozen age-inclusive EICU 24-hour norepinephrine-entry cohort, continuous lactate trajectory added to an otherwise identical late-lactate-conditioned model provides iINB at least 0.005 for eventual hospital mortality under thresholds 0.30–0.50, with acceptable calibration, no material proper-score deterioration, and cross-hospital stability.

This is an incremental prognostic/decision-analytic hypothesis, not a hypothesis that an alert, review, treatment, or lactate-directed resuscitation improves outcomes. Equal weighting of the threshold grid is part of the estimand; changing thresholds or weights after seeing Episode 49 results is a new study.

The existing frozen held-out predictions were re-expressed only to verify computability and set expectations, not to claim an independent result. They yield threshold-specific delta NB values +0.004882, +0.011892, +0.004068, -0.001479, and 0.000000 and an unbootstrapped iINB=0.003873 (0.387 net true-positive equivalents per 100); all 33 fixed-prediction site deletions remain positive (range 0.002660–0.005168). These are outcome-informed, same-snapshot exploratory quantities with no refit uncertainty. Episode 49 tests whether the locked material margin is supported after the full model-refit, hospital-clustered uncertainty procedure; it is not independent external confirmation.

## Models, validation, uncertainty, and reporting

Within each of the 33 leave-one-hospital-out folds, every continuous design column is winsorized at the training 1st/99th percentiles and standardized with the training mean and population SD; constants are applied unchanged to the held-out hospital. Gender indicators are not standardized. M0 and M1 are unweighted L2 logistic regressions with intercept, C=1.0, lbfgs, max_iter=10000, tol=1e-8, and no class weights. The sanity model assigns each held-out person the training-hospital mortality prevalence. No held-out outcome may affect preprocessing, fitting, tuning, threshold selection, or calibration.

Pool one prediction per person from the 33 held-out hospitals. For each model and threshold report TP, FP, FN, TN, alerts per 100, deaths captured per 100, sensitivity, positive predictive value, net benefit, treat-all net benefit, and treat-none net benefit. Report iINB, AUROC, Brier score, log loss, calibration-in-the-large, and calibration slope. AUROC is secondary context only and cannot determine the primary label. Report the five threshold-specific incremental net benefits with a simultaneous bootstrap band, so a favorable average cannot conceal a threshold-local decrement.

Uncertainty uses seed 20260924 and 2,000 copied-hospital refit bootstraps, exactly preserving the parent's cluster procedure. Sample 33 hospitals with replacement. When an original hospital is held out, exclude all its sampled copies from training; other hospitals enter training with sampled multiplicity. Recompute fold-only preprocessing and refit M0/M1. Weight held-out predictions by that hospital's sampled multiplicity. Compute every threshold metric and iINB from the weighted sample. The primary 95% interval is the percentile interval of 2,000 finite iINB estimates. Threshold-specific simultaneous bands use the 95th percentile across replicates of the maximum absolute centered threshold deviation, applied symmetrically around each original threshold estimate. Also recompute fixed-prediction iINB after deleting each hospital.

Computability gates are: exact source hashes and headers; exact 1,506 measured/1,229 retained/33-hospital population; unique uniquepid; at least 800 people, 25 hospitals, 200 deaths, and 200 survivors; every site has both outcomes; maximum site share at most 15%; finite M0/M1 probabilities and convergence in all original folds; at least 95% finite converged refit bootstraps; and finite site-deletion iINB for at least 80% of hospitals. Failed gates force not estimable/inconclusive and prohibit a scientific direction label.

Supportive guardrails are: M1 pooled calibration slope in [0.80,1.20]; absolute calibration-in-the-large at most 0.05; lower 95% bound for delta Brier=Brier(M0)-Brier(M1) at least -0.002 (no more than 0.2 percentage-point plausible deterioration); M1 point net benefit exceeds both treat-all and treat-none at all five thresholds; at least 80% of site-deletion iINB values are positive; and no threshold's simultaneous-band upper bound is below -0.005. These guards do not replace the primary interval.

Classification is locked:

- Supportive of material incremental decision value: all computability and supportive guards pass and the primary iINB 95% lower bound is at least +0.005.
- Adverse to the material-gain hypothesis: gates pass, the iINB 95% upper bound is below +0.005, and the upper bound is not below zero. This may still represent a small positive increment; it only excludes the prespecified material gain.
- Opposite under the specified utilities: gates pass and the iINB 95% upper bound is below zero. This means M1 has lower decision-analytic value over this grid, not that serial lactate harms patients.
- Inconclusive: gates pass but the interval spans the material boundary, or statistical evidence is favorable but any supportive guard fails. Report which boundary or guard caused the label.
- Not estimable: a computability gate fails. No supportive/adverse/opposite label is permitted.

## What each result would—and would not—mean

A supportive result would show that, under the explicit 30%–50% threshold utilities and this constrained late-lactate baseline, retaining the earlier lactate adds at least 0.5 net true-positive equivalents per 100 selected people on average, with transport and calibration guards. It would justify prospective external validation, elicitation of real decision thresholds, and workflow evaluation. It would not show that clinicians should escalate treatment, that an alert improves survival, or that lactate trajectory is causal.

An adverse result would rule out this material decision-scale increment for the locked procedure more directly than another AUROC margin. It would not prove that serial lactate is useless for monitoring, diagnosis, other outcomes, other populations, richer clinical models, or thresholds outside 0.30–0.50. An opposite result would show lower modeled net benefit under these utilities and warrant abandoning this display formulation pending independent evidence; it would not establish patient harm. An inconclusive result would preserve uncertainty and must not be narrated as confirmation. A threshold-heterogeneous result must report the full curve and simultaneous bands rather than averaging away adverse regions.

The strongest computationally checkable claims are source identity; cohort and clock reconstruction; B/F/T arithmetic; leakage-safe fold preprocessing; exact model fits and held-out predictions; confusion matrices; net-benefit formulas; copied-hospital refits; intervals/bands; calibration/proper scores; site deletions; gates; and whether the label follows from outputs. Verifier fixtures must (1) reject supportive language when the lower iINB bound is below +0.005 or a guard fails, (2) reject adverse language while the upper bound includes +0.005, (3) reject opposite language unless the upper bound is below zero, (4) reject any directional label after a computability failure, (5) catch threshold denominator/weight reversal, > versus >=, unweighted copied clusters, in-sample predictions, global preprocessing, post-landmark revisions, or use of binary X, and (6) accept appropriately bounded supportive, adverse, opposite, inconclusive, and not-estimable interpretations. A special fixture must pair correct computed outputs with an unsupported claim that the alert saves lives; the verifier must fail the conclusion despite correct computation.

Clinical adjudication is required to establish actual norepinephrine administration, dose and indication; arterial versus venous lactate; assay indication; fluids, beta-agonists, source control, hepatic/renal dysfunction, perfusion, treatment limitations, clinician knowledge, and whether a review would alter care. EICU lacks exact death time/cause and does not contain elicited threshold preferences or intervention costs. A richer clinical baseline or clinician prediction study is required before claiming increment over actual bedside judgment. External temporal/geographic validation is required for transport beyond these selected measured EICU patients. A prospective impact study—randomized if a trajectory-triggered action is proposed—is required to claim improved decisions, treatment, or patient outcomes.
