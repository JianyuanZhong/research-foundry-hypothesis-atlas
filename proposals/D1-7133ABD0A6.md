# Provisional HCC trajectory-versus-care-process hypothesis (episode 15)

## Proposed relationship
Among adults admitted with an exact documented HCC diagnosis, an ordered 7-day laboratory trajectory of liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker measurements may add held-out information about a subsequent inpatient procedure-based escalation proxy beyond an admission snapshot and observed treatment/documentation intensity. The primary estimand is predictive incremental value, not a causal effect.

The strongest plausible rival is a care-process artifact: procedures and documentation reflect routine HCC treatment, severity selection, or observation intensity rather than evolving patient state. The hypothesis is therefore discriminated by (i) a non-routine procedure endpoint, (ii) routine-procedure and timing-shifted negative controls, and (iii) a held-out comparison against a snapshot baseline.

The prior episode-14 candidate reports a source-bound HCC design with exact HCC source paths, table/column bindings, joins, temporal rules, and a bounded audit of HCC keys and post-window procedure rows. It also reports that the non-routine procedure dictionary, clinical endpoint meaning, units, and all fitted results remain unresolved. No effect estimate or causal conclusion is asserted here.

This provisional draft preserves that opening and does not add unverified evidence. The substantive advance over the inspected prior work is to make the next decision explicit: whether the apparent temporal signal survives a prespecified separation of routine treatment procedures from non-routine escalation and whether it disappears under a timing-shifted control.

## Falsifiable test
The solver must construct the exact HCC cohort and admission-to-day-7 window, freeze the procedure dictionary before outcome inspection, and compare a simple admission-snapshot model with a time-aware trajectory model on a prespecified held-out split. Outcomes are a non-routine post-window procedure proxy and routine-procedure and timing-shifted negative-control endpoints. Report uncertainty for the incremental predictive margin, calibration, and subgroup/timing sensitivity.

Supportive results require a non-trivial, uncertainty-supported incremental margin for the non-routine endpoint, a smaller or null routine-procedure increment, and a null timing-shifted control. Adverse results include no incremental value, equal or larger routine-procedure value, or a positive timing-shifted signal, which would favor care-process or documentation explanations. Inconclusive results include insufficient endpoint support, wide uncertainty, unstable dictionary definitions, or failure of leakage/unit checks.

The required inputs are HCC encounter/admission data, laboratory measurements with assay and timestamp fields, procedure records with timestamp and procedure identifiers, and available treatment/documentation-intensity fields. Essential missing evidence includes clinical adjudication of whether a procedure represents true escalation or harm, validated procedure semantics, laboratory units and missingness handling, and external validation. A positive predictive result would justify expert review or a confirmatory study, not a treatment recommendation or mechanistic claim.

## Method comparison
The simple baseline is a regularized admission-snapshot model using the same covariates and outcome. The substantive alternative is a time-aware model of within-assay trajectories and timing, fitted to the same target and split. The alternative can reveal persistence, slope, and cross-marker ordering that a snapshot discards. If the alternative fails to improve held-out prediction or its gain is explained by routine-care controls, the simpler baseline is scientifically adequate. No neural model or GPU is required for this decision; CPU feasibility is plausible but the full solver budget and resource estimate remain to be checked.
