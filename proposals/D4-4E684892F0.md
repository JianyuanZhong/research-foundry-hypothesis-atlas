# UKB metabolic mismatch and pancreatic-cancer timing

Status: planned Harbor experiment; no hypothesis-test result has been fitted. This is a substantive, delivery-complete alternative after an explicit deferral of expert seed `[starting question]`.

## Decision and scientific opening

The seed proposes that, when HbA1c rises by a similar amount, people whose weight falls are more likely to receive a near-term pancreatic-cancer diagnosis than people whose weight is stable or rises. The clinical value is clear: the result could determine whether a commonly observable metabolic combination deserves external validation as a trigger for more careful pancreatic-cancer assessment.

The seed is not adequately supported for its paired-change estimand in this snapshot. In a bounded audit of the exact source below, 8,944 participants had valid first/second assessment dates and complete glucose, HbA1c and weight at both assessments; after excluding prior inpatient primary cancer and death at the second date, 8,720 remained. There was one later first inpatient C25 diagnosis at any time and zero in both 0–2 and 2–5 years. The repeat-measurement direction is therefore deferred, not refuted.

The closest defensible alternative asks:

> Among adults free of recorded inpatient primary cancer and death at a first UKB assessment, does a baseline metabolic mismatch—higher HbA1c with lower body weight—associate more strongly with first inpatient pancreatic-cancer diagnosis in the next 0–2 years than in years 2–5, after accounting for glucose, age and sex?

This is a deliberately narrower descriptive/predictive question. It does not claim new-onset diabetes, actual weight loss, pancreatic mechanism, or clinical utility. It tests whether the available single-assessment phenotype has a time-localized signal that is stronger near diagnosis than after a washout-like delay. A near-term-only pattern would support a preclinical/ascertainment hypothesis; persistence in years 2–5 would support a longer-latency association, but neither identifies causality.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The inspected evidence supports only bounded claims:

- [K1] Yuan et al. reported that recent-onset diabetes accompanied by weight loss was associated with substantially higher subsequent pancreatic-cancer risk in two US cohorts with repeated questionnaire assessments. This supports the clinical premise, not the UKB estimand; the inspected source was abstract/metadata only.
- [K2] Amadou et al. reported baseline adiposity associations with pancreatic cancer and mediation involving glucose/HbA1c in UK Biobank. This supports a baseline metabolic association, not a longitudinal change signal or a near-diagnostic timing test.
- [K3] Lemanska et al. found that new-onset diabetes plus weight loss is a clinically recognized high-risk group but referral and imaging were uncommon in primary care. This establishes consequential clinical context and recording/ascertainment concerns, not predictive validity in UKB.

The unresolved claim tested here is that the joint baseline pattern itself—not simply generic obesity, glycaemic abnormality, older age, death risk, or diagnostic intensity—has a larger pancreatic-cancer association in 0–2 years than in 2–5 years. The strongest rival is reverse causation: occult pancreatic disease may cause dysglycaemia and weight loss shortly before an inpatient diagnosis. Other rivals are chronic metabolic risk, intentional weight change or treatment selection, measurement error/regression to the mean, and inpatient ascertainment. The available data cannot separate these completely.

## Population, time and estimand

Include participants with nonmissing `eid`, parseable T0=`53-0.0`, complete baseline HbA1c, glucose and weight, age 40–69 at recruitment by `21022-0.0`, and no valid inpatient C00–C97 diagnosis on or before T0 and no death on or before T0. Do not require a second assessment. Exclude impossible numeric values using a frozen range audit before fitting; do not impute missing biomarkers as normal.

Follow from T0 to the earliest of first valid inpatient pancreatic cancer, other primary cancer, death, administrative censoring at 2022-12-31, or the horizon. Primary windows are (T0,T0+2 years] and (T0+2,T0+5 years]. A participant with another primary cancer or death before pancreatic cancer has the competing event and is not later counted as a first pancreatic event.

Within each development fold, z-standardize HbA1c, glucose and weight using development-only means and standard deviations. The primary exposure is the prespecified continuous interaction z_HbA1c × z_weight, with main effects. The clinically readable contrast is the cause-specific pancreatic hazard ratio for HbA1c +1 SD and weight −1 SD versus both at their development means, reported separately in 0–2 and 2–5 years. Adjustment covariates are age, sex and baseline glucose; BMI and the baseline medication indicator are sensitivity covariates, never substituted into the primary estimand. Because weight is not repeated in the primary population, call this a baseline metabolic mismatch, not weight loss.

The exact scientific deliverable is newly fitted evidence for: (1) the interaction and joint contrast in each time window; (2) cause-specific cumulative-incidence curves for pancreatic cancer, other cancer and death; (3) the competing-risk and calibration outputs from the same-input model comparison; and (4) uncertainty and falsification reports. A readiness check or source audit does not complete this deliverable.

## Methods compared before selection

A. Transparent baseline. Fit cause-specific Cox models for pancreatic cancer, other primary cancer and death with the same baseline feature set, using robust standard errors. Estimate the primary interaction and joint contrast with 95% profile or robust intervals; use Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence at 2 and 5 years. Use penalization only if separation/convergence fails, record the failure, and do not silently change the estimand.

B. Substantive learned alternative. Fit a small CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model on exactly the same participants, features, event definitions and horizons. Use one-year intervals, an interval indicator and separate hazards for pancreatic cancer, other cancer and death. Tune only in rolling development folds. This can reveal nonlinear HbA1c/weight thresholds, interactions with age or glucose, and time-varying hazard shapes that the Cox model loses; it cannot establish a metabolic mechanism.

For both methods, sort participants by T0 date: earliest 70% for development and latest 30% for final temporal holdout, keeping participants intact. Fit standardization, imputation decisions (if any), tuning and calibration only in development. Evaluate the final holdout using pancreatic-specific 2- and 5-year Brier/log scores, calibration intercept/slope and cumulative-incidence calibration; AUC is secondary. Use 500 participant-bootstrap replicates for contrasts, curves and held-out metrics, preserving convergence failures. If the final holdout has fewer than 10 pancreatic events, report the learned comparison and its uncertainty as not estimable rather than using a random split or claiming model superiority. Given the audit total of 23 pancreatic events, this stop rule is likely and is scientifically informative.

Approximate future solver budget: 8–16 CPU cores, 32–64 GiB RAM, 1–3 hours for selected-column reading, event derivation and sparse Cox/boosted fits plus bootstrap; this estimate is unverified. ## Falsification and interpretation

Prespecified checks:

1. Recompute code/date pairing, date validity, duplicate `eid`, implausible values, death ties and administrative censoring. Report every exclusion and missingness denominator.
2. Report the 0–2 and 2–5 windows separately. A stronger 0–2 signal with attenuation after two years is consistent with reverse causation/diagnostic proximity; it is not proof of a preclinical mechanism.
3. Compare the pancreatic joint contrast with the same contrast for other primary cancers. A similar pattern across cancers falsifies pancreatic specificity and favors generic illness or ascertainment.
4. Repeat with baseline medication nonmissingness, BMI and a one-year exclusion as sensitivities. These cannot repair missing indication, intentional weight change, symptoms or healthcare-contact information.
5. Permute pancreatic versus other-primary labels among eligible event dates within development data; the type-specific interaction should collapse toward zero. Preserve timing and censoring.
6. Repeat the event derivation after dropping any code/date mismatch and after a stricter C25.0–C25.9 parser. If results depend on an unpaired or non-C25 code, reject them.
7. Do not use the repeat-change seed analysis as a favorable subgroup: its zero-window-event audit is a recorded deferral.

Supportive results require at least 20 pancreatic events for the analysis, a positive 0–2 joint contrast with a 95% interval excluding the null, no stronger adverse reversal in 2–5, and pancreatic specificity relative to other primary cancer; any learned improvement must also be stable on the temporal holdout and include calibration, not just AUC. This would justify external validation in clinically characterized diabetes/weight-loss cohorts, not a referral threshold.

Adverse results are a reasonably precise null or reversal of the joint contrast, an equally strong pattern for other cancers, or a signal that is explained by the medication/BMI/early-window sensitivity. That would weaken the baseline metabolic-mismatch direction while leaving general metabolic associations possible.

Inconclusive results include the prespecified sparse-event stop, no pancreatic events in the held-out set, unstable code/date derivation, wide intervals, nonconvergence or calibration failure. The observed zero-event repeat analysis is already inconclusive for the original paired-change claim. An imprecise null does not refute the biology.

The source lacks cancer-registry dates in 40005, symptoms, clinical test indication, diabetes onset date, medication start/stop and indication, intentionality of weight change, abdominal imaging, pathology/stage, outpatient diagnoses, laboratory ordering context, diet/activity context and complete healthcare-contact history. Clinical adjudication, registry linkage, an external clinical cohort and a decision-linked prospective study are required for diagnostic accuracy, mechanism, causal or treatment claims.

## Alternatives and revisit rule

- Deferred: the exact seed-40 repeated-change hypothesis. Revisit only if a source with timed pancreatic outcomes or a larger repeated-measurement cohort is added, or if expert review supplies outcome adjudication; do not rescue it by pooling all cancers.
- Rejected: joining `ukb671626` exposures to `ukb672073` assessment or outcome tables without an authorized crosswalk; this would violate identifier namespace constraints.
- Deferred: a transformer or latent state-space model. Two broadly complete metabolic assessments are rare, and the baseline outcome has only 23 first pancreatic events; greater complexity cannot resolve missing clinical indication or sparse event support.
- Retained but secondary: nonlinear boosting, because it tests whether the baseline Cox interaction discards clinically relevant threshold/time-shape information. It is not selected for a small predictive gain.

The future solver is complete only when it emits the cohort/missingness/event audit, split manifest, fitted baseline and learned outputs, window-specific contrasts, competing-risk curves, held-out calibration/Brier/log scores, bootstrap uncertainty, permutation and other-cancer falsification results, and a conclusion explicitly labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive. No conclusion may exceed what the inpatient ascertainment and missing clinical evidence permit.
