## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

Available evidence supports that baseline metabolic indices and single biomarkers are associated with later cardiometabolic disease, and recent UKB analyses use Cox and multistate models for cardiometabolic progression (for example, Xue et al., DOI 10.1186/s12933-026-03255-4, PMCID PMC13491610, and Wu et al., DOI 10.1186/s12937-026-01324-8, PMCID PMC13285204). Those studies do not establish whether an assessment-linked within-person change in HbA1c, triglycerides and HDL, after conditioning on the latest values and an already established domain, improves prediction of the *route* to a second domain in this repeat-tested subset. The local source also cannot establish exact specimen dates or clinical onset.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 at recruitment who have exactly one of diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or chronic kidney disease coded by paired administrative code/date fields at both assessment-linked biomarker panels, remain in the same one-domain state at panel 1, and have complete paired HbA1c, triglyceride and HDL values, worsening assessment-linked change predicts the first subsequent transition to a second domain beyond panel-1 levels, existing-domain identity, disease duration and prespecified covariates; its route-specific incremental value is greater than that of a fixed coordinated-deterioration score, while acute appendicitis should not show a comparable association.

This is a conditional internal prognostic estimand for coded outcomes among repeat-tested participants. It is not a causal effect of changing a biomarker, an exact biological slope, evidence of clinical onset, a treatment recommendation, or proof that monitoring improves outcomes.

## Matched models and substantive scientific comparison

All primary models share population, route-specific state space, death hazard, time zero, censoring, split, preprocessing, uncertainty and locked evaluation.

B0 is the simple current-level baseline: route-specific cause-specific Cox hazards for each existing-domain to each new-domain route plus explicit death. Inputs are panel-1 HbA1c, triglycerides, HDL, existing domain, duration, age, sex, gap, BMI, systolic/diastolic BP, smoking and center, with fit-frozen restricted cubic splines; no changes enter B0.

M1 is a marker-specific temporal alternative with the identical hazards and inputs, adding all three assessment-linked annualized changes and only prespecified, support-gated change-by-domain interactions. M1 can reveal which marker's dated history matters and route heterogeneity that current levels alone lose.

M2 is a mechanistic common-axis alternative. Within fit data, standardize HbA1c, triglycerides and reverse-HDL and define S_t=mean(z_HbA1c,t,z_TG,t,z_reverseHDL,t), deltaS=S1-S0. Add deltaS and prespecified deltaS-by-domain terms to the identical B0 route/death architecture. M2 tests whether coordinated deterioration, rather than a freely estimated marker-specific vector, explains progression. Compare M1 and M2 directly: M1 may detect marker-specific discordance that M2 averages away; M2 may be more stable and reveal a common metabolic burden. This is a scientific contrast, not a small-score contest.

A two-panel neural/recurrent attention model is deferred because only two verified assay panels provide little sequence structure, and extra capacity cannot identify specimen timing, repeat-testing indication or coded-onset uncertainty. Revisit after at least three verified dated assay panels or a bounded locked diagnostic shows stable value beyond M1/M2. A four-instance slope/mixed-effects model is not feasible because main has no verified assay instances 2/3. This is a question-specific adequacy decision, not a ban on neural methods or GPU use.
