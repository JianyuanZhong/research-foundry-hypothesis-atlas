# Provisional hypothesis: context-dependent intervention response

## Status
Provisional, not selected and not validated. This is a draft checkpoint, not an executed result. It preserves the leading D2-T1 question from the existing board while explicitly marking the exact source audit and literature receipt work as unresolved.

## Proposed relationship
In the available macrophage–fibroblast intervention data, the intervention-associated transcriptional response will differ between macrophage and fibroblast cellular contexts. The primary estimand is a donor-aware, condition-verified context-by-intervention interaction, not a generic mean-expression change.

This is a hypothesis, not an observed result. No dataset-specific effect has been computed in this episode. The biological opening is that MerTK-associated resolving macrophages and macrophage–stromal crosstalk may make the same intervention beneficial in one context and adverse in another; the seed and prior literature do not establish this for the available assay.

## Evidence and unresolved claim
The strongest evidence currently available from the board’s inspected works supports only that MerTK-positive synovial macrophage subsets are associated with remission and can induce repair responses in synovial fibroblasts, and that resolving macrophage–stromal circuits are context-dependent. It does not establish that UNC106238 acts directly on MerTK, that the response is causal, protective, or clinically beneficial.

The unresolved claim is whether an intervention-associated response is genuinely context-specific after accounting for donor, condition, timing, composition, and measurement artifacts. A robust context-by-intervention interaction would support the proposed specificity; a null or reversed interaction would be adverse to it; missing controls, insufficient independent donor units, or unstable estimates would be inconclusive.

## Method comparison
The simple baseline is a donor-aware paired log-ratio contrast between treatment and control, averaged within each verified donor/condition and tested by permutation. A substantive alternative is a mechanistic context-by-intervention model estimating separate macrophage-linked and fibroblast-linked program scores and their interaction, with donor-clustered uncertainty. The alternative can reveal a coordinated state change that a mean expression shift loses; it is deferred if the feature map or independent donor units cannot support the program definition. Both use the same inputs and outcomes.

Otherwise, narrow the claim to exploratory description or record the dependency as blocked. Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive results must be reported against the computed outputs, with no causal or clinical claim beyond the assay.
