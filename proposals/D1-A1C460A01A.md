Title: How much of an incidental pulmonary-nodule handoff is actually observable in MIMIC? A temporally gated propagation-state validation study

Clinical decision, supported evidence, and unresolved hypothesis

A radiology recommendation repeated in a discharge summary is not necessarily a closed care loop. A real tracking pathway normally includes assignment, acknowledgement, outreach, scheduling, exception handling, and completion monitoring. Aunger et al.'s 31-study systematic review (Chest 2025; DOI 10.1016/j.chest.2025.02.031; PMID 40081655; PMCID PMC12264345; full-text XML inspected) found that tracking systems act on more pathway stages than reporting templates, while most nonrandomized evidence had serious or critical risk of bias. The FIND program combined structured reporting with electronic tracking and alerts, increasing recorded completion from 16/52 to 34/67 in a pre/post comparison (J Digit Imaging 2023; DOI 10.1007/s10278-023-00780-6; PMID 36759382; PMCID PMC10287591; full-text XML inspected). These data support a clinically important distinction between documentation and active tracking; they do not show that a copied discharge recommendation is a closed loop or that any MIMIC text marker improves patient outcomes.

Strongest evidence-supported claim: incidental-finding follow-up is a multistage process, and interventions that track outstanding work are substantively different from templates that only repeat recommendations.

Unresolved claim tested here: among expert-adjudicated incidental pulmonary-nodule reports with an unconditional 3-, 6-, or 12-month chest-CT recommendation, MIMIC artifacts can reliably distinguish (0) no discharge propagation, (1) passive mention/carry-forward, (2) a patient-specific interval-compatible plan without observable ownership, and (3) an ownership-bearing action claim. The prespecified measurement hypothesis is that this four-state scheme is reproducible (quadratic-weighted kappa at least 0.80, with state-specific lower 95% confidence bound for PPV at least 0.80 after adjudication) and that the ownership-bearing state contributes information beyond literal copying. A secondary construct-validity hypothesis is that, among patients demonstrably observed in-system, state 3 has at least a 10-percentage-point higher standardized probability of recommendation-timed, nodule-focused same-system CT than state 1, without a comparable association with pre-exposure CT or non-chest imaging.

This does not hypothesize that MIMIC can verify a genuinely closed loop. Even state 3 is only an observable ownership/action claim. MIMIC lacks acknowledgement, patient notification, task-queue status, referral acceptance, requested outpatient execution date, appointment fulfillment, and outside-system completion. If the measurement and negative-control gates fail, the result is that MIMIC cannot validly distinguish active workflow from passive documentation—not evidence that clinicians failed to act.

Clinical advance over the parent

The parent made adjusted timely-surveillance association primary and called interval-bearing discharge text "closed-loop." This successor moves the consequential upstream measurement problem to primary status, reserves surveillance for observation-aware construct validation, and forbids any text or broad order proxy from being labeled true closure. It also drops a recommendation-heading effect as an inferential target: the complete feasibility scan found only 10 dedicated-heading reports among 751 candidates with discharge summaries, too few for a credible heading comparison. The study instead delivers an auditable propagation-state instrument that can determine whether a future MIMIC outcome study is interpretable at all.

Population and frozen temporal rules

Unit: first qualifying recommendation admission per adult subject.

Include a CT/CTA report joined by (`subject_id`,`hadm_id`) with `admittime - 12 hours <= radiology.charttime <= dischtime`; repeat with a zero-hour lower bound. Expert adjudicators, blinded to discharge state and future imaging, must confirm an incidental pulmonary/lung nodule or indeterminate nodular pulmonary opacity and an unconditional recommendation for chest CT at exactly 3, 6, or 12 calendar months. Exclude lung-cancer screening; risk-conditional, optional, or no-follow-up recommendations; nonpulmonary nodules; known/suspected lung-cancer staging or surveillance; hospice/palliative discharge; in-hospital death before the discharge artifact; and prior imaging available by index `storetime` that establishes guideline-complete stability. No future note may define eligibility. Missing two-year local imaging history is marked unknown, not stable; a high-specificity sensitivity requires at least two years of prior local radiology history.

Primary temporal eligibility requires `radiology.storetime <= final discharge.storetime`: the recommendation artifact existed by the time the final discharge artifact was stored. The stricter sensitivity requires `radiology.storetime <= discharge.charttime`. Neither rule proves when a discharge sentence was drafted or whether anyone read the report. Cases with the recommendation stored after discharge `storetime` are a temporal-impossibility control, not part of the primary propagation denominator.

Propagation-state instrument

Two clinicians/radiology-trained reviewers independently annotate all eligible paired artifacts, with a third resolving disagreements. Future radiology, diagnoses after discharge, and POE are hidden. Annotation occurs in two passes:

Pass 1, discharge-only: identify affirmed patient-specific nodule mention, plan location (problem list/hospital course/results/follow-up/instructions), chest-CT action language, compatible interval/date, named recipient or responsible service, asserted referral/order/appointment, and patient-instruction language.

Pass 2, paired with the index recommendation: adjudicate interval compatibility, whether the discharge language is a literal/near-literal carry-forward, and whether it adds a patient-specific action or responsibility not present in the radiology sentence. Automated normalized exact overlap and token similarity are frozen only as sampling/quality-assurance aids and are never clinical labels.

Mutually exclusive primary states are:

0. No propagation: no affirmed patient-specific nodule follow-up content.
1. Passive propagation: mention or recommendation carry-forward without an independently stated discharge action; this includes copied radiology text in results/hospital-course sections and generic guideline boilerplate.
2. Actionable but unowned plan: a patient-specific chest-CT action with an interval/date compatible with the index recommendation, but no named recipient/responsible service and no concrete transition step.
3. Ownership-bearing action claim: state 2 content plus at least one explicit, patient-specific responsibility/transition assertion, such as "PCP to order," named pulmonary follow-up, referral placed, appointment scheduled, or patient instructed whom to contact. The reviewer records the exact type, but the state remains a documentation claim, not verified acknowledgement or completion.

Ambiguous or discordant cases remain an explicit `uncertain` category and are excluded from primary state contrasts, with best/worst-case bounds that assign them to adjacent states. A plan with wrong modality or incompatible interval is recorded separately as discordant propagation, not collapsed into omission.

Primary estimands and analysis

1. State prevalence among all adjudicated, temporally eligible discharges, with simultaneous 95% multinomial intervals and stratification by 3/6/12-month interval, CT type, final service, and discharge destination.
2. Reproducibility: state-specific positive agreement, confusion matrix, quadratic-weighted kappa with subject-level bootstrap confidence interval, and pass-1 versus pass-2 changes. The scheme passes only if weighted kappa is at least 0.80 and each analytically contrasted state has at least 30 adjudicated subjects; otherwise MIMIC is declared unable to support the proposed distinction at adequate reliability/precision.
3. Incremental distinction from copying: cross-tabulate state by exact/near-exact recommendation reuse and estimate how often state 3 adds independently authored responsibility. State 3 is not considered distinct if at least 80% of its cases are merely copied recommendation text with no reviewer-confirmed added action/responsibility, or if its state-specific adjudicated PPV lower 95% bound is below 0.80.
4. Secondary observation-aware construct validation: among subjects with any later MIMIC admission or radiology report after recommendation due date +90 days, or recorded death before window end, estimate standardized risks of nodule-focused same-system chest CT within +/-42 days of the due date for states 1, 2, and 3. Unknown observation is never outcome=0. Standardization uses only pre-discharge age category, sex, recommendation interval, CT type, nodule features, final service, discharge destination, ICU/transfer burden, comorbidity, prior admissions/chest CT, and cancer/smoking-code availability. Use exact/parsimonious stratification if fewer than 15 timely outcomes per compared state or positivity is poor; do not fit a high-dimensional causal model. Report risk differences and ratios with bootstrap uncertainty. This is construct validity and association, not proof that state 3 caused imaging.

Nodule-focused follow-up requires blinded outcome adjudication of subsequent report indication/comparison/impression. The due date is index `charttime` plus 3, 6, or 12 calendar months, not 30-day approximations. Follow-up starts after `max(dischtime, discharge.storetime)`.

Complete-source feasibility and what it changes

Managed job `[research job]` scanned all 2,321,355 radiology rows, 1,136,797 within the stated admission/ED window, all 331,794 discharge rows, radiology detail, admissions, and candidate-admission POE. Its high-specificity lexical screen (not adjudicated truth) produced 873 first-subject candidate reports and 751 with a usable discharge artifact. Of those 751, 746 had radiology `storetime` no later than discharge `storetime`, and 716 also preceded discharge `charttime`. Proxy discharge rules found 498 action mentions, 350 exact interval matches, and 369 verbatim recommendation matches. A naive "active owner" regex labeled 496, nearly identical to action mentions, demonstrating that lexical ownership is not discriminative and must not be used without blinded annotation. Only 10 reports used a dedicated recommendation heading, so the parent's heading mechanism is descriptive only. Five temporal-impossibility controls are too few for inferential comparison; they are retained as case-level error checks.

Negative controls and falsification

- Temporal impossibility: any algorithm claiming direct propagation when `radiology.storetime > discharge.storetime` is an extraction/construct error. The five screened cases are descriptive sentinels; no power claim is made.
- Stricter timestamp sensitivity: repeat on `radiology.storetime <= discharge.charttime`. Discordance is interpreted cautiously because discharge `charttime` and `storetime` do not reveal sentence-level authorship.
- Pre-exposure chest CT: a state association with CT before the recommendation or before discharge cannot be caused by the handoff and indicates utilization confounding or leakage.
- Generic utilization: compare state associations with non-chest imaging in the due window and with same-admission broad POE burden. A similar association undermines nodule-pathway specificity.
- Copying control: exact/near-exact recommendation text in a results-only section without a discharge action must remain state 1. If reviewers or the detector systematically elevate it, the instrument fails.
- Vary the due tolerance from +/-42 to +/-90 days, require post-window local observation, exclude later acute-admission-only CT, and repeat in CTPA and non-chest CT strata. These are secondary robustness analyses, not opportunities to select favorable results.

Supportive result: reliable separation passes all annotation and arm-size gates; ownership-bearing claims demonstrably add responsibility beyond copied text; and, if powered, state 3 shows at least a 10-point higher standardized nodule-focused CT probability than state 1 without similar pre-exposure or generic-imaging associations. Interpretation: MIMIC supports a graded propagation/ownership measurement and identifies a candidate workflow target for prospective tracking evaluation. It still does not show closed-loop completion or patient benefit.

Adverse result: the states are reliably measured but ownership-bearing claims show no incremental same-system CT association with adequate precision, or show more premature/cascade imaging without more recommendation-timed imaging. Interpretation: richer discharge documentation alone may not operationalize tracking; work queues, acknowledgement, and outreach should be evaluated prospectively.

Inconclusive/measurement-failure result: weighted kappa below 0.80; fewer than 30 subjects in a key state; lower confidence bound for state-specific PPV below 0.80; fewer than 15 timely events per downstream comparison; poor overlap; wide intervals spanning both +/-10 percentage points; temporal or generic-utilization controls as large as the target association; or materially discordant observation analyses. Interpretation: MIMIC cannot resolve passive versus active pathway status. Missing external evidence must not be converted into clinician or patient failure.

Computationally checkable versus unavailable conclusions

An automatic verifier can check source hashes, complete-row scan counts, joins, first-subject selection, timestamp inequalities, lexical sampling, state-count arithmetic after supplied adjudications, agreement statistics, observation flags, due-date calculations, standardization, controls, uncertainty, and whether reported language follows the prespecified result category. It cannot automatically establish true nodule status, incidental intent, guideline appropriateness, prior stability, whether copied text is clinically meaningful, whether a named clinician accepted responsibility, all imaging received, malignancy, stage, delayed diagnosis, survival, or patient benefit.

Essential unavailable evidence includes sentence-level authoring/read times, radiology acknowledgement, secure-message or inbox delivery, task-list assignment and closure, nodule-registry state, requested outpatient execution date, referral acceptance, scheduling contacts, appointment attendance, patient notification/preferences/refusal, primary-care/pulmonary visit content, outside-hospital imaging, reliable pack-years, pixels, complete pathology linkage, and complete postdischarge mortality. Establishing a true closed loop therefore requires prospective workflow logs or another linked study, plus expert clinical review.
