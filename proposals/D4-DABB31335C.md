## Clinical question and evidence boundary

The clinically relevant narrow question is whether an already observed baseline cystatin-C result improves prioritization of review for a protocol-defined future creatinine threshold crossing among people enrolled in, and observed to return to, a repeat-measurement program. The falsifiable primary claim is:

> Among UK Biobank participants who pass certified baseline criteria and have a certified qualifying repeat visit with complete repeat cystatin-C and creatinine measurements, adding a binary baseline cystatin-C-derived threshold indicator to a flexible baseline creatinine/age/sex model yields reproducible out-of-sample improvement for the protocol-defined repeat creatinine threshold outcome, without using future interval or repeat measurements as predictors.

The parent’s evidence supports at most an association in a selected cohort between baseline cystatin-C-derived kidney-function category and later creatinine-derived threshold status. It does not establish incremental predictive information, CKD, chronicity, measured GFR, progression, causality, clinical utility, benefit, or transportability. The secondary sensitivity asks whether the predictive contrast and its calibration are materially altered by conditioning on repeat-cystatin completeness within repeat-creatinine attenders. It remains an observed-data sensitivity analysis, not a missing-at-random identification claim.
