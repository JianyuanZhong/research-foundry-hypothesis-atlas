# Day-60 HCC marker–injury identifiability and ascertainment after resection

Status: feasibility-resolved crossover proposal. The bounded audit is observed; no biomarker model has been fitted. The biological recurrence question is preserved but formally deferred. The computable deliverable is a measurement/ascertainment study because no defensible widening produces enough untreated, classifiable surveillance reports among patients with an observed day-60 marker/injury trajectory.

## Scientific opening, evidence boundary, and advance

The strongest evidence-supported claim is that AFP response measured 4–6 weeks after resection is prognostic in a selected cohort with preoperative AFP >400 ng/mL [K1]. That association does not establish that persistent AFP is residual tumor. Persistent AFP without detected recurrence has been documented after apparently curative HCC resection [K3]. Treatment is also not a recurrence diagnosis: postoperative TACE can be assigned as adjuvant management in patients treated as recurrence-free, and a current randomized protocol contrasts adjuvant TACE with intensive follow-up [K2].

The unresolved biological claim is whether a day-60 AFP/PIVKA-II persistence component can be separated from postoperative liver injury and then shown to predict independently ascertained tumor-bearing imaging. The leading explanation is a tumor-associated marker component; the strongest rival is postoperative injury/regeneration plus assay-era, medication, treatment-selection, and observation-intensity effects.

The bounded source audit shows that this snapshot cannot resolve the biological claim. The substantive advance is therefore to answer the necessary prior question: whether the available day-0–60 measurements support a reproducible marker-persistence state distinct from an injury-recovery state, and whether that estimated state is entangled with subsequent testing, imaging, and treatment processes. This determines whether a future biological study can sensibly use the local longitudinal measurements and prevents a sparse, selectively observed report endpoint from being presented as recurrence.

## Falsifiable hypothesis and exact deliverable

Primary computable hypothesis: among treatment-naive adults at a fixed day-60 landmark after first recorded HCC resection, a constrained marker-persistence state estimated from AFP and optional PIVKA-II is empirically separable from a surgery-linked injury-recovery state, improves held-out reconstruction/forecasting of marker and injury measurements over a transparent static trajectory baseline, and is not merely a surrogate for calendar era, specimen type, or generic observation intensity.

This is a measurement-identifiability hypothesis, not a tumor-recurrence hypothesis. The biological hypothesis remains unresolved and is not tested by S/T transition counts.

The solver must newly produce:

1. a reproducible index, eligibility, assay, and transition flow;
2. simple-baseline and constrained longitudinal fits on the same 224-person frozen cohort and participant split;
3. held-out measurement predictions, uncertainty, loading/state stability, simulated recovery, calendar/specimen and medication sensitivities;
4. day-61–365 measurement/contact outcomes and descriptive S/T/D/C transitions, including treatment-intent and report-content marks;
5. a gate-based conclusion labeled supportive, adverse, or inconclusive for measurement separability;
6. an explicit statement that no biological recurrence association was estimated.

The study is complete when these outputs and their uncertainty are generated. Readiness checks or the existing audit alone do not complete it.

## Observed bounded audit and narrowing decision

The audit script and aggregate result are attached. It reads the frozen source files in chunks and emits no identifiers or clinical text.

The corrected adult, same-encounter HCC/resection frame contains 19,208 index patients. After excluding recorded prior directed treatment and separate HCC-directed treatment through day 60, 15,121 remain. In that broad frame, days 61–365 contain 297 first untreated liver/upper-abdominal CT/MRI reports, 1,243 treatment-first transitions, 25 same-day image/treatment transitions, and 13,556 without either recorded transition. The 297 reports have provisional lexical marks: 60 affirmative, 105 explicit-negative, 22 uncertain, 78 conflicting, and 32 unclassified. This frame can characterize observation processes but cannot answer the day-60 marker question because most patients lack the required marker/injury trajectory.

The longitudinal measurement cohort requires preoperative AFP, recovery AFP, a recovery injury measurement, at least two postoperative marker dates and two postoperative injury dates, and no prior or day-0–60 directed treatment. It contains 224 patients: 10 S, 36 T, 2 same-day, and 176 C. S marks are 2 affirmative, 4 explicit-negative, 1 uncertain, and 3 conflicting. Treatment/same-day intent marks are 20 T-R, 0 T-A, 5 T-B, and 13 T-U. The frozen train/validation/test/reserve split has N=144/23/20/37, S=6/0/1/3, and T=21/5/6/4. Adding a specific systemic-drug sensitivity changes only one C to T.

One-axis checks resolve the proposed relaxations:

- Requiring acute and recovery injury but not acute AFP yields 241 patients and still only 10 S.
- Requiring any same-encounter pathology in the longitudinal cohort yields 212 patients and 10 S; lexical HCC pathology yields 168 patients and 9 S. Pathology availability is therefore a sensitivity, not a source of endpoint support.
- Allowing prior treatment yields 232 longitudinal patients and still 10 S. It changes the estimand to previously treated disease and adds selection/confounding without endpoint gain.
- Omitting preoperative AFP yields 302 patients and 14 S, but eliminates the persistence/clearance estimand.
- Removing all lab requirements yields 15,121 patients and 297 S. Allowing prior treatment raises this to 15,546 and 322 S, but only 65 are provisionally affirmative, and these patients do not have an observed day-60 state. This is not a defensible biological cohort.
- A corrected negation parser and expanded CT/MRI title rule recovered one additional longitudinal S relative to the inherited audit. The report problem remains decisive.

Accordingly, no report-content model is allowed in this child. The S/T/D/C structure is retained as an ascertainment description and future gate.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The index frame requires:

- age ≥18;
- earliest definitive `Diagnosis Name` containing `hepatocellular carcinoma` or `primary liver cancer`, without uncertainty language, and a same-encounter liver-resection procedure excluding transplant, donor, biopsy/puncture, cyst surgery, and explicitly recurrent surgery;
- index from 2012-01-01 through 2023-12-31, nonmissing procedure start, within 30 days of the linked encounter date;
- no recorded TACE/embolization, ablation, transplant, liver resection, or systemic-treatment proxy in days -180:-1, and no separate directed treatment in days 0:60.

The primary longitudinal measurement cohort additionally requires numeric exact-label AFP in days -90:-1 and 15:60, at least two distinct postoperative dates among AFP/PIVKA-II/AFP-L3 in days 0:60, at least two distinct injury-marker dates in days 0:60, and at least one injury measurement in days 15:60. PIVKA-II is not required. Same-encounter pathology is a sensitivity because it has no native timestamp and its availability is selective.

Predictors and measurement targets use days -90 through +60. The landmark is exactly day 60. Follow-up begins day 61 and ends at day 365, administrative source end, or last observed dated contact. Record absence is censoring, never nonrecurrence.

## Detection-time and treatment-intent states

These states describe ascertainment; none is biological onset.

- **S:** first qualifying liver/upper-abdominal CT/MRI report in days 61–365 strictly before recorded treatment. The computable report mark is affirmative, explicit-negative, uncertain, conflicting, or unclassified.
- **T:** first recorded TACE/embolization, ablation, repeat resection, transplant, or systemic-treatment proxy before S. Mark as **T-R** for explicit recurrence/residual/progression/metastasis, **T-A** for explicit adjuvant/preventive postoperative intent, **T-B** for both, and **T-U** otherwise.
- **ST:** image and treatment on the same calendar day; ordering is unresolved and the image is not called untreated.
- **D:** explicit in-hospital death language dated by linked discharge time, used only as an unvalidated competing-transition sensitivity.
- **C:** earliest of day 365, source end, or last dated contact. No post-day-60 record censors at day 60.

For an affirmative S, report the descriptive detection interval `(latest prior explicit-negative scan, first affirmative scan]`, or `(day 60, first affirmative scan]` when no prior negative exists. Do not call this recurrence time.

## Matched methods

All participants are assigned by `int(SHA256(patient master index)[:8],16) mod 100`: 0–59 train, 60–69 validation, 70–79 locked test, 80–99 untouched reserve. The observed longitudinal split is 144/23/20/37. All preprocessing, censor parsing, label rules, and hyperparameters freeze before locked-test access.

### Transparent baseline

For each analyte, fit a robust linear mixed-effects trajectory on log-transformed within-person ratios using time since surgery, calendar era, specimen type, and patient random intercept/slope. The marker summary is preoperative AFP plus latest and slope of AFP/PIVKA-II through day 60; the injury summary is acute peak, latest value, and slope for ALT/AST/bilirubin/INR/albumin/platelets. Missing PIVKA-II is an explicit indicator, not imputed as normal.

Evaluation uses patient-level masked observations: hold out one eligible postoperative measurement per analyte family in validation/test where possible, then report standardized log-scale MAE, interval coverage, calibration, and participant-bootstrap uncertainty. Fit a separate penalized Cox model for time from day 60 to next AFP/PIVKA-II measurement, with generic encounter/lab contact as a negative-control observation outcome, only if its event gate passes.

This baseline is interpretable but loses irregular nonlinear recovery, cross-marker covariance, assay-specific observation intensity, and uncertainty in a shared state.

### Substantive longitudinal alternative

Fit a constrained hierarchical continuous-time two-state model:

- injury state (I_i(t)), initialized by surgery, with recovery dynamics and loadings expected positive for ALT/AST/bilirubin/INR and negative for albumin/platelets;
- marker-persistence state (M_i(t)), allowed to clear, plateau, or rise, loading on AFP and optional PIVKA-II/AFP-L3;
- AFP may cross-load on injury; PIVKA-II observation parameters include recorded vitamin-K, warfarin, and antibiotic flags;
- calendar-era and specimen offsets, censoring indicators, irregular times, missingness masks, and assay-observation intensity enter explicitly;
- shared random effects can link (M_i(60)), (I_i(60)), baseline covariates, and prior contact to subsequent marker-testing and generic-contact intensity when the follow-up event gate passes.

Fit constrained and unconstrained variants with three seeds; align factors before comparison. Perform posterior predictive checks, leave-one-marker-out fits, and simulated recovery of known latent states. This alternative can show whether a reproducible dynamic component exists and whether it is entangled with its own measurement process; the static baseline cannot. It cannot establish that (M) is tumor.

A multivariate functional-PCA model with the same masking and observation outcomes is the CPU fallback if the state-space model is non-identifiable. A transformer is deferred because 224 trajectories and the measurement question do not justify its flexibility.

## Prespecified gates and evaluation

Measurement-model fitting proceeds only if the frozen extraction retains ≥200 longitudinal patients, ≥120 train, ≥20 validation, and ≥20 locked test, with every participant meeting the two-date marker/injury rule. The observed audit passes narrowly (224; 144/23/20). If exclusions for timestamp discordance, censor parsing, or duplicate IDs reduce any split below threshold, stop at the descriptive measurement audit.

A post-landmark measurement-intensity model requires ≥100 next-marker-test events overall and ≥15 in validation and locked test; otherwise report counts and cumulative incidence only. A transition model for S/T requires ≥150 transitions and ≥30 in train, validation, and locked test; the observed longitudinal cohort fails (48 S/T/ST; 28/6/7/7 by split including same-day as a separate state), so S/T remains descriptive.

The original report-content question requires ≥300 S overall, ≥100 adjudicated affirmative and ≥100 adjudicated explicit-negative reports, ≥20 of each in the locked test, and blinded hepatobiliary/radiology review with PPV and NPV ≥0.85. The longitudinal cohort has 10 S, two provisional affirmative and four provisional explicit-negative reports, with no S in validation and one in test. This gate is failed before modeling.

Primary measurement evaluation comprises participant-level masked MAE and log predictive density by analyte family, 90% interval coverage, state/loadings stability across seeds and bootstrap samples, simulation recovery, calendar-era transport, AFP-only and PIVKA-medication-restricted sensitivity, and comparison with generic contact/measurement intensity. Report all denominators, missingness, weight tails, and uncertainty.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive measurement evidence requires all of:

- simulated state recovery correlation ≥0.80 without label switching after factor alignment;
- expected injury-loading signs in ≥90% of bootstrap fits and marker-state loading on AFP in ≥90%, without AFP loading predominantly on injury;
- at least 10% lower participant-level masked standardized MAE than the baseline for both marker and injury families, with a paired 95% interval excluding no improvement;
- factor/state correlations ≥0.70 across calendar-era and AFP-only fits after alignment;
- no evidence that the apparent marker state is explained entirely by specimen/calendar offsets or generic contact intensity.

This would support using the measurement representation in a future prospectively ascertained study. It would not support occult-tumor diagnosis, recurrence prediction, or treatment benefit.

Adverse evidence is stable failure to recover two states, predominant AFP loading on injury, no material held-out improvement, collapse under calendar/specimen or PIVKA-medication restrictions, or a marker state whose strongest association is measurement/contact intensity. This would favor injury/measurement explanations and argue against using these routine trajectories as a tumor-specific representation.

Inconclusive evidence includes a failed measurement gate, unstable factors, incompatible assay eras, weak simulation recovery, or intervals spanning the prespecified improvement. Sparse S/T/report counts are already conclusive about endpoint infeasibility but do not refute biological marker decoupling.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/schemas, joins, temporal windows, cohort flow, deduplication, split assignment, assay parsing, state construction, masked predictions, metrics, uncertainty, S/T/ST/C ordering, and lexical marks. Not automatically checkable are curative intent, true recurrence, biological state identity, treatment intent, outside surveillance/treatment, assay equivalence, or clinical benefit. Those require hepatobiliary/radiology review, assay metadata, external linkage, or a prospective scheduled-imaging study.

## Compute and alternatives

The final full-source audit ran in 73.3 seconds on four CPUs. This measures extraction only. Planned extraction, baseline, functional-PCA fallback, and bootstrap evaluation: 8–16 CPUs, 64 GiB, 1–2 hours. The three-seed hierarchical state model is expected to fit on 8 CPUs/64 GiB in 2–4 hours; one allocated A100 may be used for throughput after a bounded probe, but this estimate is unmeasured and GPU use is not required for validity. The plan fits the 16-CPU, 8-GPU, 256-GiB, 8-hour solver envelope.

Alternatives not selected:

- Biological affirmative-versus-negative report modeling: deferred because the relevant longitudinal cohort has 10 S and only six binary-classifiable reports.
- Composite “recurrence-management” outcome: rejected because treatment may be adjuvant and absence of a report is not nonrecurrence.
- Broad 15,121-person imaging cohort: retained for ascertainment denominators only; most lack an observed day-60 marker/injury state.
- Allowing prior treatment: sensitivity only; it adds no longitudinal S and changes the estimand.
- Dropping preoperative AFP: rejected for the main question because it removes persistence/clearance.
- Pathology-required primary: deferred because it selects local pathology availability and reduces support; use as sensitivity.
- Prospective standardized AFP/PIVKA-II plus scheduled CT/MRI with blinded adjudication: required to revisit the biological hypothesis.
