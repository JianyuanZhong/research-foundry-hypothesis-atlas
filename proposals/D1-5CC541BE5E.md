## Question and claim boundaries

**Question:** In adults still in the ICU at 24 hours, do counts and timing of laboratory testing add mortality-prediction information beyond the measured physiology available by that landmark, and is that increment less stable in hospitals withheld from model development?

**Evidence already established:** EICU is a multicenter critical-care database with linked patient, lab, and hospital tables [K1]. Prior work documents that ICU prediction performance/generalizability depends on institutional data composition and validation design [K2]. Across 15 health-system sites, laboratory missingness patterns were analyzed as disease-course and clinician-concern signals, while the paper also reports site mapping/data-definition issues [K3]. These motivate—but do not prove—the present question.

**Hypothesis to test:** Adding early lab-test volume/repetition/timing to a prespecified physiologic feature set yields better patient-random internal Brier score but a smaller or absent improvement under hospital holdout; the test-process feature block therefore predicts in part through site/workflow-sensitive signals. The principal rival is that testing intensity is a valid proxy for unmeasured severity or clinician concern; if so, its incremental risk information should remain useful in hospitals not seen during training after comparable physiologic adjustment. Severity mix, treatment pathways, lab availability/mapping, outcome coding, and case-selection differences are additional rivals. Observational prediction cannot distinguish them completely.

**Strongest supportable claim if positive:** Within this EICU release and this fixed 24-hour prediction task, explicitly represented testing intensity has incremental predictive information whose performance/calibration changes across hospitals. This would not establish that testing causes death, that the signal is merely documentation artifact, or that removing it improves care. A stable held-out benefit would instead argue against strong transport fragility in this experiment, not establish universal transportability.

## Population, landmark, endpoint

One row per first ICU visit in each hospital-system admission: adult (`age` parses as >=18; EICU's deidentified `>89`/90+ code remains an older-adult category, not an exact age), `unitvisitnumber == 1`, known hospital, and ICU stay still ongoing beyond 1,440 minutes. Exclude unknown/nonadult age, missing hospital, and admissions discharged from ICU by the landmark. State number of excluded/retained stays and hospitals in the required flow output. Do not filter on later outcomes or feature completeness.

Landmark is exactly 24 hours after ICU admission. Inputs are only records with offset `0 <= offset < 1440`; no post-landmark lab/vital or discharge-derived information is allowed. Primary outcome is later in-hospital death, `patient.hospitaldischargestatus == 'Expired'`, among those still in ICU at the landmark. Report outcome missing/unknown counts and do not silently recode them as survivors. `hospitaldischargeoffset`, `unitdischargeoffset`, and status fields only define eligibility/outcome; do not enter predictor matrix. This predicts hospital death conditional on remaining in ICU at 24 hours—not admission-time mortality or treatment benefit.

## Feature sets and alternatives

Define a versioned clinically harmonized common-lab dictionary before model fitting using `labname`, `labmeasurenamesystem`, and `labmeasurenameinterface`; report included mappings/units and unmatched fraction. A clinician/laboratory-data reviewer must sign off analyte and unit equivalence; if that review cannot be obtained, run the physiological-vitals-only version as clearly narrowed exploratory analysis and do not claim adjustment for lab physiology. No mapping may be chosen by its association with the outcome. Winsorization/scaling/imputation parameters and any vocabularies are fitted on training hospitals only.

- **Physiology baseline block:** age, gender; each mapped common numeric lab's last/median/min/max in the window; each listed vital's median/min/max and first-to-last change where >=2 readings exist. Include simple measurement-availability flags for those prespecified physiologic variables and median imputation fitted on train only, so the baseline does not depend on a hidden assumption of complete measurement.
- **Testing-process block:** from valid lab rows in the same window: total result rows (`labid`-deduplicated), number of unique `labname`s, count of distinct analytes, repeat-result count beyond first per analyte, and counts of lab result rows in 0–6, 6–12, 12–18, 18–24h. Exclude names/categories used only to encode institution if they cannot be harmonized. No post-landmark or revised-after-landmark results. This block captures intensity/repetition/timing, not order intent (orders are not available in `lab`).
- **Simple baseline:** ridge logistic regression for outcome with physiology block only, compared to the identical logistic model with the process block added. Tune regularization using training-hospital-only inner folds.
- **Substantive learned alternative:** gradient-boosted trees (e.g. HistGradientBoosting or LightGBM if present) fit to the same binary endpoint and same two nested feature sets. This can learn nonlinear threshold effects and physiology-by-testing-intensity interactions that a linear logit misses; examine held-out partial dependence/SHAP only as descriptive model diagnostics, not causal explanations. Tune only within training-hospital grouped folds. Cross feature-set comparison within both model families separates process-block contribution from model-family gains. The learned model is selected only if it improves out-of-hospital performance over the corresponding logistic fit with uncertainty; complexity itself is not an objective.

**Alternative reasoned and bounded:** do not use a deep sequence network here: the decision target is whether simple explicit test-volume signals travel beyond physiology, not whether temporal representation learning wins; summaries retain counts and time bins needed to test this. Gradient boosting can expose plausible nonlinear interaction structure without requiring GPU. Approximate workload is four model/feature-set fits plus training-hospital grouped inner-CV tuning and one fixed hospital test, CPU, <=8 cores/32 GiB RAM, expected tens of minutes to a few hours after feature extraction (unmeasured estimate; full `lab.csv.gz` is ~517 MB compressed). No GPU requirement or discovery-time full-cohort fit. Revisit sequence modeling only if the coarse process features show reproducible signal and irregular event order is itself the remaining question. No alternative model result has been observed.

## Validation and estimands

Freeze a deterministic 20% hospital holdout (hospital-level split, seed recorded in code); no patient stays from a test hospital enter preprocessing, feature selection, imputation, tuning, or fitting. Remaining hospitals form development data. Inner model selection uses grouped CV by hospital, never random-row CV. For a patient-random internal comparator, generate a fixed random split within development hospitals and report it explicitly as optimistic/in-site, not external validation. Test both nested models on the same held-out stays.

Primary metric is Brier score; report macro-average by held-out hospital and pooled test cohort, with calibration-in-the-large and calibration slope. Key contrast is the change in process-block gain from within-hospital random validation to hospital-held-out validation: `G = Brier(physiology-only) - Brier(physiology+process)`; `fragility = G_internal - G_heldout`. Positive fragility means process-feature advantage attenuates out of hospital. AUROC and AUPRC are secondary; report per-hospital results and avoid AUROC for a site with one outcome class. Include confusion/calibration summaries only at a prespecified operating point if an intended workflow threshold is supplied by clinicians; without such a decision threshold, no claim of net benefit/actionability. Report prevalence, event counts, hospital counts, and missing outcomes by split.

Estimate uncertainty with paired hospital-cluster bootstrap on held-out hospitals (resample hospitals, then optionally patients within hospital); report 95% intervals for `G_heldout`, fragility, macro Brier, AUROC, calibration slope. Avoid treating patient rows as independent evidence for hospital transport. For the small number of hospitals/events, report support per site and bootstrap failure/instability; an interval spanning material benefit and harm is inconclusive. No posthoc split switching. The exact meaningful calibration/Brier margin should be elicited from the intended deployment decision before viewing the held-out results; absent such margin, only directional/effect estimates are reported, not clinical acceptability.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

- **Support for the predicted fragility pattern:** internal process-feature Brier gain is positive, held-out gain is materially smaller or adverse, paired hospital-bootstrap interval for `fragility` excludes zero in the positive direction, and calibration degradation is coherent rather than one small site's artifact. This supports a dataset-specific transportability concern and motivates external workflow/measurement audit—not the claim that process is nonphysiologic or causal.
- **Adverse to hypothesis:** process-feature gain is similar or larger out of hospital, and the interval excludes an attenuation large enough to matter under the prespecified margin; this weakens the claim that this explicit process block is transport-fragile for this population. A broad interval is not evidence of stability.
- **Inconclusive:** sparse events/sites, unstable calibration/bootstraps, mapping failures, or intervals covering both worthwhile benefit and harm. Do not convert a null p-value into falsification.
- **Model-family falsification/diagnostic:** if the boosted physiology-only model is superior to the logit but adding the process block does not improve within-site or held-out evaluation, this fails the proposed predictive role of these process summaries, regardless of model complexity. If only one hospital drives gains, report heterogeneity and do not claim a general site-level pattern.

The leading workflow-signal account and severity/clinician-concern rival remain partly observationally indistinguishable because EICU does not supply the clinician's reason for ordering, counterfactual unmeasured physiology, or a randomized practice change. Exact assay mapping, units, offset semantics, label coding, and expert confirmation of the feature dictionary are essential validation steps. Performance alone cannot adjudicate appropriateness of testing, treatment effects, mechanism, fairness, bedside utility, or transport beyond EICU; those require clinical review and prospective/external-site evaluation.
