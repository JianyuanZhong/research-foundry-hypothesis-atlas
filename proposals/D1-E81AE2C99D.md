# When does laboratory surveillance add information after recorded organ-support transitions?

Status: Episode 45 substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal treatment-effect study, a test-ordering intervention, a physiologic-truth study, a hospital-quality ranking, or evidence that changing organ support or laboratory surveillance improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest evidence available before fitting is that the frozen eICU snapshot contains patient-linked laboratory result/revision times, recorded vital trajectories, treatment events, respiratory-care start/end fields, recorded discharge outcomes, and hospital identifiers. The parent question established a fixed six-hour, revision-safe laboratory-process comparison after recorded vital state and trajectory. It did not resolve whether an apparent laboratory-observation increment is largely a proxy for the intensity and transition of documented organ support.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adult first ICU stays at minute 360, does revision-safe laboratory-observation history add a transportable, death-specific signal after the model includes recorded vital state/trajectory and outcome-free documented organ-support activity and transitions—and does the increment remain when the last 60 minutes are masked?

This matters clinically because laboratory observation often rises when ventilation, vasoactive support, dialysis, or other organ-support care is being started, escalated, or weaned. If the laboratory signal disappears after those care-context variables are measured, it should be interpreted as a severity/workflow marker rather than an independent review cue. If it persists in supported held-out hospitals, the result would identify information not redundant with the recorded vital and support history, which is a more credible basis for a future silent-review study. A persistent association still would not show occult biochemical injury, clinician intent, benefit from more testing, or benefit from changing support.

The substantive advance over the parent is a direct support-context challenge. It preserves the parent’s exact adult first-stay, [0,360] predictor, and (360,1800] competing-risk bindings, but introduces a prespecified, outcome-free phenotype of documented organ-support activity and transitions from `treatment` and `respiratoryCare`. It tests whether the laboratory-process interpretation survives a clinically meaningful care-state adjustment, rather than adding another generic transport score or cosmetic model complexity.

## Evidence boundary and hypothesis

The source and bounded support audits establish availability only. They do not establish that a treatment row means administration, that a respiratory-care row means continuous support, that a laboratory result time means specimen collection or order time, that a missing observation means absence of physiology or treatment, or that recorded ICU discharge captures all mortality. The parent’s fitted result, if any, is not reused as evidence; every solver conclusion must come from the newly fitted outputs.

Primary hypothesis H1 is conditional and prognostic:

1. Adding the revision-safe laboratory-process channels to a vital-plus-documented-support baseline improves held-out-hospital death-specific integrated competing-risk Brier score and calibration at 600, 1,200, and 1,800 minutes.
2. The improvement is not eliminated by patient-link permutation, six-bin laboratory-order permutation, or masking the final 60 minutes.
3. The improvement is smaller than or distinct from the increment obtained by adding documented support activity to the vital baseline, and is more specific to recorded ICU death than to alive ICU discharge.

The null is no incremental laboratory information after support context, or an increment that is fully reproduced by process permutations, discharge prediction, one interface/site, or near-landmark data. This is not a hypothesis about treatment efficacy, organ recovery, biological mediation, or causal effects.

## Population, landmark, and estimand

Use eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`.

Include records from `patient` with numeric age >=18, recoding eICU age `> 89` to 90. Select the first ICU stay per `uniquepid` by smallest valid numeric `unitvisitnumber`, breaking ties by numeric `patientunitstayid`. Require numeric `unitdischargeoffset > 360`. Keep all stays with the same `uniquepid` in one outer fold. No later stay data are used to construct a first-stay row.

Time zero is ICU admission as represented by eICU-relative offsets. Predictors are inclusive [0,360] minutes only. The outcome window is (360,1800]. Cause 1 is recorded ICU death: `unitdischargestatus = Expired` and numeric `unitdischargeoffset` in (360,1800]. Cause 2 is alive ICU discharge: `unitdischargestatus = Alive` and offset in (360,1800]. A stay with neither event by minute 1,800 is administratively censored. Missing or other disposition values are reported and excluded under a frozen rule; destination fields do not impute death. Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, and undocumented death are outside this estimand.

Fit separate cause-specific discrete-time hazards for 12 intervals (360,420], (420,480], ..., (1740,1800]. Convert both cause-specific hazards to death and discharge cumulative-incidence functions using the competing-risk product. The primary estimand is the macro-hospital mean, over held-out predictions, of:

- Brier-score difference `B_vS - B_vSL` integrated over 360--1,800 minutes, where `B_vS` is the vital-plus-support baseline and `B_vSL` adds laboratory process;
- calibration difference at 600, 1,200, and 1,800 minutes for death CIF;
- the corresponding alive-discharge differences as a specificity/competing-event check.

A positive value means lower error after adding laboratory process. Report macro-hospital and patient-weighted secondary estimates, fold-specific estimates, and 95% uncertainty intervals. A retrospective equal-review-burden diagnostic at fixed death-CIF thresholds (0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10) reports capture, PPV, false-review burden, and number needed to review; it is label-relative and is not clinical utility or net benefit.

## Models and substantive alternative

All models use identical rows, first-stay selection, folds, outcome intervals, missingness handling, fit-only scaling, laboratory key codebook, trajectory fields, EOL channels, and support codebook. No model uses `hospitalid`, post-360 values, Apache mortality/APACHE APS fields, or future outcome-derived variables.

Transparent baseline:

- `B_v`: admission context, vital state/observation intensity, recorded trajectory channels, landmark laboratory state, EOL channels; no organ-support channels and no laboratory-process timing channels.
- `B_vS`: `B_v` plus the outcome-blind documented support activity/transition channels from `treatment` and `respiratoryCare`, with `infusionDrug` only in sensitivity.
- `B_vSL`: `B_vS` plus conservative revision-safe laboratory-process channels and the prespecified lab-process-by-recorded-trajectory interaction inherited from the parent. `B_vSL` versus `B_vS` is the primary laboratory increment; `B_vS` versus `B_v` quantifies how much the support-context challenge explains.
- `B_resultS`: paired clock sensitivity replacing availability timing with result timing on identical laboratory rows.
- A matched support-process-only model and lab-process-only model are required to show whether a similar increment is generic observation workflow.

Each transparent model is a pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazard model with 12 intervals. Regularization, imputation, standardization, calibration, and thresholds are learned only in the development fit portion.

Scientifically informative learned alternative:

- `T_vSL` is a one-layer six-bin GRU with separate death and discharge hazard heads, no hospital identifier, and exactly the same admission, vital, EOL, support, laboratory state, laboratory process, trajectory, and missingness channels as `B_vSL`. The six bins preserve within-landmark ordering and co-occurrence of support events and laboratory observations.
- A parameter-matched non-temporal MLP using the same patient-level channels is mandatory. A within-patient six-bin order shuffle is mandatory.
- The alternative is scientifically useful only if it tests whether persistence, late support transitions, and cross-domain temporal co-movement contain information lost by additive summaries. It is not selected because it is complex or uses a GPU. If it does not improve held-out death calibration/Brier with uncertainty compatible with improvement, or if its advantage survives order shuffling, `B_vSL` remains the primary inference and the learned branch is negative/inconclusive.

A mechanistic state-space model is deferred. The eICU sources have no verified specimen/order timestamps, laboratory units, continuous raw waveforms, delivered-dose administration record, or expert-adjudicated physiologic recovery state. A future mechanistic study requires those dependencies plus expert-defined organ-support states. This is a feasibility/identification limitation, not a blanket rejection of learned models.

## Splits, uncertainty, and support gates

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals. Keep `uniquepid` groups together. Within each development fold use a 75/25 hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Learn all codebooks, support eligibility, trajectory field support, scaling, imputation, regularization, calibration, GRU stopping, and thresholds only in the fit portion. No outcome-derived support category or analyte selection is permitted.

Primary uncertainty is 95% intervals from >=1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions, or a prespecified hierarchical hospital/fold model if bootstrap is unstable. Report fold and hospital episode/event counts, support-category support, overlap, missingness, calibration, death/discharge/censor counts, and macro-hospital and patient-weighted estimates.

The equal-review threshold cell is unsupported unless it contains >=200 held-out episodes, >=20 recorded deaths, >=20 alive discharges, and >=20 hospitals. The primary laboratory increment is unsupported if the support-adjusted baseline or extension fails global row overlap, event, codebook, or category gates. Failed support is reported as inconclusive, never silently replaced by a smaller or easier population.

## Falsification and stress tests

1. Reassign laboratory-process histories within hospital and baseline-risk strata, preserving process distributions but breaking patient linkage.
2. Permute the six laboratory-bin labels within patient, preserving total count, key composition, revisions, and missingness.
3. Reassign support histories within hospital and baseline-risk strata; a laboratory increment that remains unchanged under both patient-linked process permutations is not patient-specific evidence.
4. Fit the support-only increment `B_vS-B_v` and compare it with `B_vSL-B_vS`. A support-only or matched vital-process increment as large as the laboratory increment weakens a lab-specific interpretation.
5. Compare recorded-death and alive-discharge increments. A comparable or larger discharge increment suggests length-of-stay, discharge workflow, or observation-process information rather than death-specific signal.
6. Mask (300,360] laboratory, support, and vital bins and refit; then mask early bins. A result dependent only on the final unmasked bin is flagged as near-landmark leakage or workflow artifact.
7. Run respiratory-care-only, treatment-only, and infusion corroboration analyses. Strong discordance or dependence on a single unvalidated textual category is adverse or inconclusive.
8. Repeat on common analyte/interface subsets, revision-known-only rows, and result-time versus conservative availability clocks on identical rows. No unit conversion is permitted.
9. Repeat across outcome-free hospital surveillance regimes and leave one supported regime out. A one-hospital/interface effect fails transport support.
10. Run GRU order shuffle and parameter-matched MLP controls. A temporal claim requires loss under order shuffle and stable advantage across held-out hospitals.
11. Audit all rows for no post-360 predictors, no `activeupondischarge` state inference, no invalid revision use, no hospital ID predictor, no target-fold preprocessing, and no Apache fields.

## Interpretation gates

Supportive evidence requires all data/support gates; a positive, uncertainty-compatible death-specific `B_vSL-B_vS` improvement in integrated Brier/calibration; smaller or null discharge increment; attenuation after lab-link and temporal permutation; no comparable support-only/vital-process replacement; stability after final-bin masking, revision/interface/common-panel checks, and leave-one-regime-out evaluation; and, for the learned branch, an order-sensitive improvement over the matched MLP. The conclusion is limited to a held-out, recorded-data prognostic association: laboratory observation history contains information not redundant with the recorded vital and documented-support histories.

Adverse evidence includes a null or reversed laboratory increment, unchanged performance after laboratory permutation, a support-only or discharge increment as large or larger, concentration in one hospital/interface, reliance on post-landmark revisions, dependence on an ambiguous treatment-string category, or a learned advantage surviving order shuffle. These findings reject the proposed interpretation even if overall discrimination is high.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed event/overlap/support gates; intervals spanning zero and the prespecified meaningful effect; unstable hospital folds; sparse or nontransportable support categories; unresolved lab unit/interface incompatibility; excessive revision missingness; disagreement between respiratory and treatment support channels; or nonconvergence. A complex model winning without the falsification pattern is not supportive.

## Unavailable evidence and claim limits

Harbor can verify source bindings, joins, offsets, cohort arithmetic, support codebooks, fitted predictions, uncertainty, falsification execution, and whether submitted conclusions follow from computed outputs. It cannot establish:

- specimen collection/order time, laboratory unit equivalence, assay validity, or unrecorded tests;

## Alternatives considered and revisit evidence

The parent’s recorded-trajectory effect modification is retained as a covariate and sensitivity, but support-context adjustment is selected because the new tables have explicit ICU-relative treatment/ventilation fields and the scientific ambiguity is clinically consequential. Dynamic landmarking was deferred: changing time zero would alter the assigned [0,360] and (360,1800] estimands and would mix selection with a new target population; revisit it only with a prespecified dynamic-regime estimand and adequate risk-set support.

A mechanistic organ-recovery state-space model was deferred because specimen/order times, units, continuous waveform, delivered-dose intervals, and adjudicated states are unavailable. Revisit requires those dependencies and expert state definitions.

A larger transformer or multimodal adaptation was deferred. The research-ambition files support learned dated-sequence adaptations, but the local eICU lacks the UKB lifetime/genetic scope needed to reproduce the natural-history demonstration, local images and the main article/full STAR Methods needed for full Oncoformer reproduction, and verified genetics/biological latent inputs needed for a faithful Bayesian longitudinal-genetic reproduction. The proposed compact GRU is a bounded EHR-only adaptation on verified inputs, with a transparent baseline and falsifications.
