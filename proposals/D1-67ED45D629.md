# Working proposal: post-discharge laboratory trajectories after acute coronary syndrome (broadened)

## Question
Among adults with an acute or subacute coronary syndrome diagnosis during an index hospitalization, does the direction and rate of change in early post-discharge biomarkers predict 60-day all-cause readmission beyond what is captured by discharge laboratory values alone?

## Clinical importance
ACS patients are at high risk of readmission, and post-discharge monitoring decisions often rely on a single discharge laboratory snapshot. If early post-discharge trajectories (e.g., troponin, creatinine, hemoglobin, platelet count) carry incremental information, this could inform follow-up intensity and readmission prevention. The original 30-day window with a narrow ACS definition yielded only 22 adult index patients and 1 readmission, making the question underpowered. Broadening the ACS definition to include subacute and old MI terms, and extending the follow-up window to 60 days, should increase the sample size and the number of readmission events.

## Evidence base
- [K1] Liu et al. 2026: In-hospital inflammatory-nutritional-renal trajectory phenotypes are associated with graded 90-day risks after ADHF discharge and provide limited additional prognostic information beyond baseline clinical and biomarker data.
- [K2] Marcus et al. 2026: In patients hospitalized with ADHF, a decline in platelet count from normal at admission to thrombocytopenia at discharge was associated with excess 30-day readmission and mortality.
- [K3] Research Square et al. 2026: Creatinine and urine-output AKI criteria at a 24-hour landmark are associated with subsequent severe kidney progression in critically ill adults with cirrhosis.

## Population and temporal boundaries
- Index: first adult (age >= 18) encounter with an ACS diagnosis in `诊断名称` (broadened Chinese and English variants: acute MI, subacute MI, old MI, unstable angina, NSTEMI, STEMI, ACS).
- Discharge: `出院时间` from the index encounter.
- Post-discharge window: labs with `检验时间` within 1–14 days after discharge.
- Outcome: all-cause readmission within 60 days after discharge, defined by a subsequent encounter with `入院时间` within 1–60 days after index `出院时间`.

## Baseline versus trajectory comparison
- Baseline: logistic regression or gradient-boosted model using discharge lab values and static demographics.
- Trajectory alternative: model using early post-discharge lab values and their direction/rate of change, with the same static covariates.
- Comparison: incremental AUC and calibration, with temporal split to avoid leakage.

## Falsification criteria
- Supportive: trajectory model improves AUC and calibration over discharge-only baseline.
- Adverse: no incremental improvement, or improvement is confined to a single analyte.
- Inconclusive: insufficient post-discharge lab coverage or unstable estimates.

## Alternative explanations and sensitivity analyses
- Post-discharge lab ordering intensity may be a proxy for clinician concern rather than a causal biomarker trajectory. A comparable-coverage sensitivity analysis will restrict to patients with at least one post-discharge lab and compare models with and without lab-ordering intensity as a covariate.
- Medication exposure may confound lab trajectories. A medication-exposure sensitivity analysis will adjust for post-discharge medication use where available.

## Observed data availability (bounded diagnostic)
- Original narrow ACS definition: 22 adult index patients, 1 with 30-day readmission.
- Broadened ACS definition (including subacute and old MI terms) and 60-day follow-up: bounded diagnostic in progress.

## Unresolved issues
1. The broadened ACS definition and 60-day follow-up window need verification with a bounded diagnostic to confirm adequate sample size and readmission events.
2. Post-discharge lab coverage and 60-day readmission feasibility need further verification with the broadened population.
