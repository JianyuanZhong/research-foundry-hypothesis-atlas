# Early Post-TACE Hepatic Deterioration as a Prognostic Marker in HCC

## Question
In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who undergo transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does a composite measure of early hepatic deterioration during the first 7 days after the procedure predict subsequent hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days, after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and post-procedure observation intensity?

## Clinical importance
TACE is a common locoregional treatment for HCC, but a subset of patients experience early hepatic deterioration that may signal a need for escalation to systemic therapy or closer surveillance. Identifying these patients could inform treatment-selection decisions and resource allocation. The unresolved question is whether the early laboratory trajectory itself carries prognostic information beyond baseline liver function, or whether it merely reflects more intensive post-procedure monitoring.

## Evidence and closest prior work
- [K1] Decraecker et al. (2026) review the limitations of Child-Pugh scoring and the potential value of dynamic liver function monitoring and ALBI grade in HCC patients.
- [K2] Yang et al. (2026) demonstrate that longitudinal EHR data can capture disease trajectories and predict adverse outcomes in HCC.
- [K3] Tarbaghia et al. (2026) show that time-aware survival models can improve prediction of hepatic decompensation in HCC patients.

## Temporal ordering and prognostic (not causal) design
The early hepatic deterioration composite is a prognostic marker, not a causal mediator. The design avoids post-treatment bias by specifying exact temporal boundaries:
- TACE procedure date: the date of the TACE procedure (from the procedure table).
- Early lab window: days 0–7 after TACE (from the laboratory table, filtered to labs drawn within 7 days of the procedure date).
- Outcome window: days 0–90 after TACE (from the outcome table, filtered to outcomes occurring within 90 days of the procedure date).

The early hepatic deterioration composite is measured after the TACE procedure but before the outcome window. It is a prognostic marker because it is associated with the outcome but not necessarily a causal mediator. The design avoids post-treatment bias by not conditioning on post-treatment variables (e.g., post-TACE liver function) and by using a temporal ordering that ensures the early lab window precedes the outcome window.

## Method comparison
### Baseline: Logistic regression
The baseline is a logistic regression model that predicts the 90-day outcome (hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death) from the early hepatic deterioration composite and baseline covariates (baseline liver function, tumor burden, post-procedure observation intensity). The model is fit on the HCC dataset and evaluated using concordance index, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, and decision-curve analysis.

### Learned alternative: Time-aware survival model
The learned alternative is a time-aware survival model (e.g., Cox proportional hazards with time-varying coefficients or a neural survival model) that predicts the time-to-event outcome (hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death) from the early hepatic deterioration composite and baseline covariates. The model is fit on the HCC dataset and evaluated using concordance index, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, and decision-curve analysis.

The learned alternative could reveal information that the baseline loses:
- Time-varying hazard: the learned alternative can model the hazard of the outcome as a function of time, which the baseline logistic regression cannot.
- Non-proportional hazards: the learned alternative can model non-proportional hazards, which the baseline logistic regression assumes are proportional.
- Interaction with baseline liver function: the learned alternative can model the interaction between the early hepatic deterioration composite and baseline liver function, which the baseline logistic regression may not capture.

### Selection or deferral reason
The time-aware survival model is deferred as a secondary analysis; the primary analysis remains the logistic regression baseline. The reason for deferral is that the logistic regression baseline is simpler, more interpretable, and more robust to small sample sizes. The time-aware survival model is a substantive learned alternative that could reveal additional information, but it is more complex and may be less robust to small sample sizes.

The solver must:
1. Fit the baseline logistic regression model on the HCC dataset.

3. Report hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals for the early hepatic deterioration composite and baseline covariates.

6. Report concordance index, calibration-in-the-large, and calibration slope for both models.

The outputs that establish completion are:

## Clinically actionable decision-curve analysis threshold
A concrete decision-curve analysis with a clinically actionable threshold is specified: a net benefit above the treat-all strategy at a 10% threshold probability of 90-day hepatic decompensation would support closer surveillance or escalation to systemic therapy. The actual net benefit at this threshold must be computed by the solver.

## Falsification criteria
The hypothesis is falsified if:
- The early hepatic deterioration composite is not significantly associated with the 90-day outcome after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and post-procedure observation intensity.
- The concordance index for the baseline logistic regression model is not significantly better than a null model.
- The decision-curve analysis does not show a net benefit above the treat-all strategy at a 10% threshold probability of 90-day hepatic decompensation.

## Remaining limitations
- The time-aware survival model is deferred as a secondary analysis; the primary analysis remains the logistic regression baseline.
- The decision-curve analysis threshold (10%) is a proposed clinically actionable threshold; the actual net benefit at this threshold must be computed by the solver.
