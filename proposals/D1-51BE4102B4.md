# Candidate: Does pH-conditioned “hidden hyperoxemia” transport across EICU hospitals?

## Unresolved question, importance, and advance

Oxygen protocols commonly use peripheral saturation as the bedside target, yet the same SpO2 can correspond to materially different PaO2 values as pH and FiO2 change. A 2026 single-tertiary-center study reported that SpO2 96-97% was generally associated with physiologic PaO2, whereas acid-base state and FiO2 modified hyperoxemia risk. The unresolved claim is whether this relationship is stable enough across heterogeneous US ICUs to support a simple saturation target, or whether clinically important hidden hyperoxemia persists in acidemic/high-FiO2 patients even at SpO2 96-97%.

**Falsifiable primary hypothesis:** among eligible invasive-ventilation blood-gas observations with preceding five-minute median monitor SpO2 of 96-97%, acidemia (pH <7.35) versus pH 7.35-7.45 is associated, after standardization to the same FiO2 and prespecified case mix, with at least a 10-percentage-point higher probability of PaO2 >120 mm Hg. Support additionally requires the contrast to be positive in at least 70% of adequately sized held-out hospitals and a pH/FiO2-augmented rule to improve clinically weighted detection over SpO2 alone.

This is a multi-hospital transportability and measurement study, not a test that oxygen causes injury. Its clinical advance is to determine whether a widely implementable SpO2-only protocol is physiologically reliable or whether pH and FiO2 should trigger a lower target/reassessment between ABGs. A precise null would argue against adding pH complexity. A positive heterogeneous result would define where prospective protocol testing is needed.

## What evidence already supports, and what this tests

Delgado et al. (DOI 10.3390/antiox15020235; full XML source `[source checksum]` inspected) supports a single-center same-time association among 21,406 adults and 717,064 paired measurements: SpO2 >=98% frequently signaled PaO2 >120/150, and pH and FiO2 modified the SpO2-PaO2 relationship. Diop and Mounier (DOI 10.4103/mgr.MEDGASRES-D-25-00028; full XML source `[source checksum]` inspected) supports conflicting oxygen-target evidence and warns that PaO2 is not tissue hyperoxia.

Neither source establishes transport across EICU hospitals, the 10-point acidemia contrast, calibration of a portable rule, oxygen toxicity, or benefit from changing oxygen. Those are kept distinct.

## Variables and outcomes

Primary outcome: observed arterial hyperoxemia, PaO2 >120 mm Hg at the index panel. Secondary: PaO2 >150; continuous PaO2; and hypoxemia PaO2 <60 as a safety description. These are blood-gas states, not tissue hyperoxia.

Primary exposure/stratum: pH <7.35 versus 7.35-7.45 among SpO2 96-97%. Alkalemia >7.45 is a secondary stratum. FiO2 is continuous (restricted spline) with prespecified summaries at 30%, 40%, 60%; SpO2 is continuous outside the primary stratum. Covariates fixed before analysis: age, gender, ethnicity, hospital, unit type, admission source, admission weight, six-hour block, same-panel paCO2, and pre-index PEEP (last `respiratoryCharting` label `PEEP` in the preceding 30 minutes). Admission diagnosis is descriptive only; no text-derived ARDS adjudication is assumed. APACHE variables are sensitivity covariates because they can incorporate first-day data.

## Analysis, baselines, and transportability

- Tabulate raw PaO2>120/>150 prevalence by integer SpO2, pH category, and FiO2 band. Report patient- and hospital-level denominators.
- Estimate standardized risk differences with a logistic GEE clustered by `uniquepid`, hospital fixed effects, restricted splines for pH/FiO2/SpO2, and prespecified pH×SpO2 and FiO2×SpO2 interactions. Cluster bootstrap hospitals (1,000 resamples) for the primary 95% interval. The primary estimand is the marginal acidemia contrast standardized to the observed covariate distribution among SpO2 96-97%, not a causal pH effect.
- Baselines are (B0) the published-style heuristic SpO2 >=98%; (B1) SpO2 alone; (B2) SpO2+FiO2; and (B3) SpO2+FiO2+pH+prespecified interactions. Compare B2 versus B3 using held-out-hospital calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, Brier score, and decision-curve net benefit at hyperoxemia-risk thresholds 10%, 20%, and 30%. AUROC/AUPRC are secondary, not the clinical objective.
- Perform internal-external validation by leaving out each hospital with >=100 eligible observations; train on all other hospitals without tuning the specification, then pool hospital-specific acidemia contrasts with random effects and report prediction intervals and I-squared. Hospitals below 100 contribute to the overall model but not per-hospital sign consistency.
- Sensitivities: PaO2 >150; FiO2 lookback 60 minutes; nearest FiO2 ±30 minutes (explicitly vulnerable to future-information bias); one observation per stay; first six-hour block only; same-offset paCO2 complete cases; APACHE adjustment; exclude cardiothoracic/neuro units and diagnoses where liberal oxygen may be intentional. Missing pH/PaO2 outcomes are never imputed; missing baseline covariates may use multiple imputation.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive:** the standardized acidemia risk difference is >=+10 points, its two-sided 95% CI excludes 0, >=70% of adequately sized held-out hospitals have a positive contrast, and B3 improves net benefit over B2 at both 20% and at least one adjacent threshold without materially worsening calibration (held-out calibration slope 0.8-1.2). Concordant PaO2>150 results strengthen support. This supports transportable risk stratification and prospective protocol design only.

**Adverse:** the acidemia contrast is <=0 with its 95% CI excluding 0, the prespecified interaction is opposite in most hospitals, or B3 has no net-benefit gain and worse calibration. This would reject the proposed pH-conditioned target rule and favor the simpler SpO2/FiO2 approach.

**Inconclusive:** the CI includes both +5 and +10 points; fewer than five hospitals meet validation size; held-out effects are strongly heterogeneous with a prediction interval spanning clinically important benefit and harm; joint pairing retains <20% of otherwise eligible invasive-ventilation ABGs; or conclusions change under the 30/60-minute FiO2 windows. The result must be reported as inconclusive, not “no effect.”

## Verification boundary and stronger claims

The verifier can check headers, joins, units/ranges, no future FiO2/SpO2 use in the primary analysis, cohort attrition, deterministic six-hour selection, model formulas, resampling by hospital, contrasts/CIs, calibration, decision curves, and whether the submitted conclusion follows the supportive/adverse/inconclusive rules. It must include tests in which computations are correct but a claim of oxygen toxicity or treatment benefit is rejected, and tests accepting appropriate interpretations of all three result states.

Automatic verification cannot establish that same-offset lab rows are the same arterial specimen, that `sao2` is artifact-free pulse oximetry in every hospital, that FiO2 charting equals delivered oxygen, or that a risk threshold should change care. Clinical informatics review must adjudicate those mappings; prospective external validation with raw monitor/device data must verify the rule; and a randomized protocol study with hypoxemia, organ injury, and patient-centered outcomes is required before recommending a lower oxygen target or claiming benefit.
