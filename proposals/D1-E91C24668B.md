# Provisional successor: timing-anchored eICU hypotension burden and subsequent AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with a qualifying eICU ICU stay and at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone. This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Why this is a substantive refinement
The parent question is preserved, but the most consequential unresolved issue is endpoint timing and ascertainment. The successor makes the primary estimand a time-to-first creatinine event after the exposure window, anchored to a pre-exposure baseline creatinine when available, rather than allowing a post-exposure “baseline” or an unanchored rise to define the outcome. It also separates creatinine ascertainment from urine-output ascertainment: urine output is not silently substituted for creatinine and is only a secondary endpoint if its row semantics and timing are validated. This directly addresses whether the proposed clinical signal is interpretable rather than merely a measurement artifact.

## Evidence and uncertainty
The parent’s inspected evidence supports the relevance of hypotension burden, the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of AKI, and the importance of EHR observation processes, but it does not establish this eICU association or validate the local urine-output fields. The exact eICU catalog confirms that `intakeOutput` has `patientunitstayid`, `intakeoutputoffset`, `intakeoutputentryoffset`, `outputtotal`, `celllabel`, `cellvaluenumeric`, and `cellvaluetext`; `lab` has `patientunitstayid`, `labresultoffset`, `labresultrevisedoffset`, `labname`, `labresult`, and `labmeasurenamesystem`. These fields establish availability, not clinical validity. The remaining uncertainty is whether urine rows represent point measurements, interval totals, duplicate entries, or retrospective corrections, and whether creatinine timestamps are sufficiently complete to support the 24–72-hour endpoint. No computed eICU effect estimate is claimed.

## Population, exposure, and primary outcome
Qualifying population: adults (`age >=18` at admission) in a single qualifying ICU admission with valid ICU start time and at least one valid systolic BP <65 mmHg in ICU time 0–24 hours. Exclude records lacking the required admission identifier or with impossible/ambiguous timestamps.

Exposure: derive valid systolic BP from both vital tables, cumulative minutes below 65 mmHg, nadir, and measurement counts in 0–24 hours. Use piecewise-linear interpolation between valid measurements, no extrapolation beyond first/last observed measurement, and prespecified physiologic-range filtering. Sensitivity analyses use no interpolation, shorter interpolation gaps, and measurement-count/observation-intensity adjustment.

Primary outcome: a creatinine-defined event in 24–72 hours. Define baseline as the last valid creatinine at or before ICU time 0 when available; if unavailable, use the first valid creatinine in a prespecified pre-exposure window and report missingness rather than imputing normal. Define the event as the earliest creatinine increase >=0.3 mg/dL from that baseline within 48 hours, or >=1.5 times baseline if the rise is documented within the outcome window, with time to event retained. Do not use a post-exposure creatinine as baseline. If the required pre-exposure baseline or outcome-window measurements are absent, classify the endpoint as unavailable/indeterminate rather than silently treating it as negative.

Secondary urine-output endpoint: retain only after a bounded semantic check of `intakeOutput` labels, numeric/text values, entry versus event offset, duplicate/cumulative behavior, and weight/availability. If semantics cannot be established, report urine-output AKI as unavailable rather than substitute a proxy.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative
The simple baseline is a prespecified hospital-stratified logistic model using nadir systolic BP in 0–24 hours, age, sex, baseline creatinine, and available baseline severity/vasopressor context. The substantive time-aware alternative adds cumulative hypotension minutes, time below threshold in prespecified intervals, last BP before window end, and a simple recovery/trajectory feature while retaining nadir. The alternative can reveal whether sustained exposure, recovery after the nadir, or observation density carries information that a single minimum discards. It is scientifically useful only if those features are stable and not merely proxies for measurement intensity or treatment; otherwise the baseline remains preferred.

Evaluate both models on the same rows, with a hospital-held-out split and the same outcome definition and uncertainty procedure. Report duration effect estimates and confidence intervals, calibration, and hospital-held-out discrimination as secondary descriptive outputs. Compare direction and uncertainty across hospitals. A complex sequence model is deferred because available data cannot distinguish hypotension-mediated kidney injury from severity, treatment, and observation artifacts; revisit only if a bounded diagnostic shows trajectory information is materially unavailable to the simpler representation.

## Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: duration adds reproducible information beyond nadir with a prespecified direction and uncertainty, and the result is not explained solely by observation density or a single hospital. Adverse: duration adds no information, reverses after prespecified adjustment, or is confined to artifact-prone measurements. Inconclusive: creatinine ascertainment, timestamp validity, or sampling is too sparse to estimate the comparison reliably; an imprecise null is not decisive refutation. Any causal interpretation, treatment threshold, or mechanism requires additional clinical adjudication, richer treatment/etiology data, and prospective or quasi-experimental evidence.
