# Does duration of early hypotension add AKI risk information beyond nadir?

## Scientific opening

Clinicians often summarize an ICU hypotensive episode by its lowest blood pressure. However, two patients with the same nadir may have very different total exposure to low pressure. The unresolved question is whether the duration of that exposure independently predicts subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI), beyond the nadir and baseline kidney function.

## Hypothesis

Among eICU adults with a prespecified early hypotensive episode, a longer duration at or below 65 mmHg systolic BP (or MAP < 65 mmHg) is associated with higher risk of subsequent creatinine-defined AKI, beyond what is explained by the episode nadir and baseline kidney function.

## Evidence context

[K1] Ren et al. 2025 show that intraoperative hypotension trajectories, not only minimum values, can carry prognostic information, supporting the relevance of exposure duration. [K2] Wang et al. 2026 provide eICU-CRD context for creatinine/urine-output AKI criteria and the importance of specifying which criteria are available. [K3] Ensarioğlu & Yıldırım 2026 distinguish hypotension burden from waveform features, motivating a clinically interpretable duration measure rather than a black-box representation.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use the eICU CRD 2.0 source files listed in `datasets/eicu/README.md`, with patient-level joins only within eICU. Define the cohort as adult ICU stays with at least one early blood-pressure record and a creatinine value available before or at the episode. Define the exposure episode as the first 24 hours after the first BP record, or a prespecified alternative 12-hour window if the 24-hour definition produces sparse outcomes.

Within the episode, calculate the proportion of recorded time at or below the threshold using the available BP timestamps. This is an association study, not causal evidence.

## Data bindings

- `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz` and `vitalAperiodic.csv.gz`: BP values and timestamps; join on patient/stay identifiers and time fields.
- `lab.csv.gz`: creatinine values and timestamps; use the latest creatinine before/at the episode as baseline and subsequent creatinine for AKI.
- `patient.csv.gz`: age, sex, and stay identifiers.
- `hospital.csv.gz`: hospital identifiers for whole-hospital validation.
- `admissionDx.csv.gz` and `pastHistory.csv.gz`: admission diagnoses and relevant past history for adjustment.

## Outcome

Creatinine-defined AKI: a prespecified increase in creatinine from baseline, following standard creatinine criteria. Urine-output criteria are not used because eICU urine-output data are incomplete and cannot be assumed reliable.

## Baseline and learned alternative

Baseline: logistic regression with episode nadir and baseline creatinine. Learned alternative: a sequence model over BP timestamps, if duration adds signal. The learned model would reveal whether the shape of the hypotensive episode, not just duration, carries additional information.

## Validation

Whole-hospital held-out validation: split by hospital identifier, train on a subset of hospitals, and evaluate on held-out hospitals. Report discrimination, calibration, and the duration coefficient with confidence intervals.

## Falsification criteria

Support: positive duration coefficient and improved held-out-hospital discrimination/calibration. Adverse: no independent association or worse performance. Inconclusive: sparse outcomes, unstable estimates, or inconsistent hospital validation.

## Limitations

eICU measurement density varies by hospital; duration is measured from recorded BP, not continuous monitoring. Creatinine-only AKI may miss urine-output AKI. This is an association study, not causal evidence.
