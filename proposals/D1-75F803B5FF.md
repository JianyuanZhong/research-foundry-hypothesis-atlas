# Is reduced laboratory surveillance specific to a recorded-care transition context?

Status: Episode 48 substantive child of assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational, ICU-relative, recorded-process prognostic experiment. It is not a test of withholding tests, a causal treatment study, a physiology study, a clinician-intent study, a hospital ranking, or evidence that a future review or more testing improves outcomes.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence is source-level and outcome-blind: the frozen eICU snapshot contains adult ICU-stay records with ICU-relative laboratory result/revision offsets, periodic vital observations, treatment and respiratory-care events, infusion rows, intake/output rows, EOL documentation, and recorded ICU discharge status. The parent has already established a falsifiable instability-specific laboratory-reduction design, but has no fitted result and cannot establish that a missing record means a missed test.

The remaining unresolved claim is more specific:

> Among adult first ICU stays with prior revision-safe laboratory surveillance and repeated recorded vital instability, does a final-hour fall to zero recorded laboratory availability predict subsequent recorded ICU death only when other timestamped care streams remain recorded in the same final hour, or is it mainly a generic discharge/interface/documentation transition?

This matters because a future silent review policy would have different justification in the two cases. A signal that persists when respiratory-care, treatment/vasoactive, and intake/output documentation continues is more specific to a patient-linked recorded-care pattern than a signal occurring when all recorded activity disappears. A signal confined to the latter, or shared by alive discharge, would argue against interpreting reduced laboratory records as an instability review cue. Either result is useful, but neither establishes that testing was clinically indicated or that changing surveillance would benefit a patient.

The advance over the parent is a prespecified context-specificity estimand. The parent tests an instability-by-lab-drop association and includes prior support adjustment. This child tests whether the final-hour lab-drop contrast differs by a *new, outcome-blind, non-laboratory recorded-care continuity phenotype* constructed from respiratoryCare, treatment, infusionDrug, and intakeOutput in the same pre-outcome hour. It therefore addresses the residual discharge-transition/interface explanation with an auditable process interaction, rather than adding model complexity or treating a better score as a clinical result.

## Evidence boundary and hypothesis

No model has been fitted in this episode. Parent coefficients, effect sizes, and feasibility counts are not results for this child.

Let (I) be the parent's recorded-instability indicator, (D) its revision-safe laboratory reduction indicator, and (K) the new recorded-care context defined below. The primary hypothesis is:

- Within (I=1), the standardized recorded-death CIF contrast for (D=1) versus (D=0) through 1,440 minutes is larger when (K=1) than when (K=0):
  [
  Delta_{death,K=1}-Delta_{death,K=0}>0.
  ]
- The corresponding alive-discharge contrast is not larger in (K=1), and a near-landmark discharge increment concentrated in (K=0) is treated as evidence of transition workflow.
- The joint context extension improves held-out-hospital death-specific competing-risk prediction over the prior-state model, with the increment attributable to the prespecified (D\times K) term rather than to an unconstrained high-capacity score.

This is a conditional predictive association of recorded variables. It is not a causal effect of ordering, withholding, support, or K. (K=0) means no qualifying records in the selected fields, not no care, no support, or a complete interface.

The strongest supportive pattern is a positive, hospital-held-out (Delta_{death,K=1}-Delta_{death,K=0}), smaller/null discharge contrast in (K=1), and attenuation under process-link/time and context-label falsifications. A null interaction is informative against this recorded-context specificity claim. A positive result confined to (K=0), or a discharge result at least as large as the death result, supports a transition/documentation interpretation instead.

### New recorded-care context K

Construct (K) only from fields in (300,360], before the outcome window. First create four binary domain indicators per stay; each is a record-occurrence indicator, not a clinical state:

- (R_{resp}=1) if a `respiratoryCare` row has numeric `respcarestatusoffset` in (300,360]. Do not infer ventilator occupancy from `airwaytype` or interval continuity.
- (R_{tx}=1) if a `treatment` row has numeric `treatmentoffset` in (300,360] and its lower-cased `treatmentstring` contains one of the frozen literal families: `mechanical ventilation`, `non-invasive ventilation`, `CPAP/PEEP therapy`, `oxygen therapy`, `vasopressors`, `inotropic agent`, `dialysis`, `ultrafiltration`, or `C V V H`. Never use `activeupondischarge`.
- (R_{vaso}=1) if an `infusionDrug` row has numeric `infusionoffset` in (300,360] and lower-cased `drugname` contains a predeclared vasoactive family: norepinephrine/noradrenaline, epinephrine/adrenaline, phenylephrine, vasopressin, dopamine, dobutamine, or milrinone. This records a literal row only; it does not establish administration, dose, rate, duration, or delivery.
- (R_{io}=1) if an `intakeOutput` row has numeric `intakeoutputoffset` or `intakeoutputentryoffset` in (300,360] and at least one numeric `intaketotal`, `outputtotal`, `dialysistotal`, `nettotal`, or `cellvaluenumeric`. This records a timestamped I/O entry; it does not establish fluid balance, renal function, or treatment effect.

Define (K=1) when at least two of the four domain indicators are one, and (K=0) otherwise. The two-domain threshold is frozen before outcome inspection and is intended to reduce dependence on one interface or one row type. Report the four domain indicators and a prespecified one-domain sensitivity; the one-domain sensitivity cannot replace the primary K definition. Because absent documentation is not absent care, interpret K only as a recorded-context phenotype.

The primary design includes the final-hour (K), final-hour periodic-vital observation count (V_{300,360}), and final-hour non-laboratory domain indicators in the extension. (V_{300,360}) is a count of distinct `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset` values and is not used to redefine (I). The primary lab process exposure remains (D); no post-360 value is used.

Outcome rules are unchanged from the parent. Start follow-up after minute 360, in 12 bins (360,420], (420,480], ..., (1740,1800]. Cause 1 is recorded ICU death when `unitdischargestatus = Expired` and numeric `unitdischargeoffset` is in (360,1800]. Cause 2 is alive ICU discharge when `unitdischargestatus = Alive` and the offset is in that window. No event by 1,800 is administrative censoring. Missing/other status is reported and excluded by a frozen rule. Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, undocumented death, and destination-based death imputation are outside the estimand.

## Comparable baseline and substantive learned alternative

Both methods use exactly the same selected episodes, (I,D,K,V_{300,360}), six fixed 60-minute bins [0,60], (60,120], (120,180], (180,240], (240,300], (300,360], same admission fields, same pre-landmark state/process summaries, same missingness, same lab codebook, same outcome bins, and same hospital folds. Transparent primary baseline:

- (B_0): pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards for recorded death and alive discharge, using admission context, recorded vital trajectories/burden through 300, (I), revision-safe lab state/process through 300, support/documentation activity through 300, EOL documentation through 300, and final-hour non-lab context (K,V_{300,360}), domain flags and missingness. It excludes final-hour lab process.
- (B_1): (B_0) plus (D), final-hour distinct availability/result-time counts, revision status, and prespecified (D\times K) and (D\times I) terms. The primary increment is (B_1-B_0); the primary scientific output is the held-out standardized recorded-death (D) contrast within (I=1,K=1) versus (I=1,K=0), plus its analogous discharge contrast. The nested model comparison isolates the lab-drop/context interaction; it is not a causal effect estimate.

Scientifically substantive alternative:

- (T_1): one-layer six-bin GRU with separate death and discharge hazard heads, trained on the same six-bin channels and final-hour fields as (B_1), with no hospital ID. It can represent persistence and ordering of prior instability, lab availability, respiratory/treatment/infusion/I/O events, and their co-occurrence; additive summaries in (B_0/B_1) can lose these temporal configurations. Its target is the same 12 cause-specific hazards, not an embedding or proxy.
- Fit a parameter-matched non-temporal MLP on the same patient-level channels. Run an in-patient six-bin order-shuffle control that preserves each patient’s binwise values and missingness while breaking temporal order. A GRU advantage is scientifically relevant only if it remains over the matched MLP, attenuates under order shuffle, and does not replace the auditable (D\times K) inference.

B1 is preferred for the main scientific interpretation because K, D and their interaction are transparent. T1 is retained because temporal ordering/co-occurrence is a substantive uncertainty: it could reveal whether the context-specific pattern is a sequence (instability followed by lab loss while other records continue) rather than an additive count fingerprint. A GRU gain alone is not clinical value.

## Falsification and robustness tests

1. Reassign lab process histories within hospital, I, prior lab-intensity, K and baseline-risk strata, preserving per-patient process distributions while breaking the patient linkage. The (D\times K) death interaction should attenuate.
2. Permute the two lab time-bin labels (180,300] and (300,360] within patient among support-compatible records, preserving total counts, key composition, revisions and missingness. Persistence rejects the “after instability” timing interpretation.
3. Reassign K labels within hospital and baseline-risk strata, preserving K prevalence and domain composition. A large unchanged interaction indicates a generic subgroup artifact.
4. Reassign instability labels/vital histories within hospital and baseline-risk strata while preserving observation burden. The instability-specific interaction should attenuate.
5. Fit a non-laboratory analog: replace D with a prespecified final-hour drop/no-drop indicator for documented care domains or final-hour vital observation burden, with the same K and prior-state adjustment. A comparable death interaction indicates generic documentation loss, not laboratory specificity.
6. Compare cause-specific death and alive-discharge increments by K. A discharge increment as large or larger, especially in K=0 and concentrated near (360,420], is adverse to a deterioration-oriented interpretation.
7. Exclude any EOL save/discussion in [0,360], then include save/discussion timing as covariates; report differences. Never call undocumented EOL absence.
8. Fit the parent’s lab-drop model without K and compare its held-out contrast with B1. A disappearing increment after K/domain adjustment is evidence that the parent signal was not specific beyond this recorded context.
9. Repeat with result-time versus conservative availability-time clocks on identical retained rows, revision-known-only rows, and common key/interface panels. Disagreement is inconclusive; no unit conversion or cross-interface pooling.
10. Repeat K using each individual domain, one-domain threshold, and respiratory/treatment/infusion/I/O subsets. These are labeled sensitivities, not flexible replacements. An effect confined to one interface/domain is adverse or inconclusive for the primary multi-domain claim.
11. Repeat in outcome-free hospital surveillance regimes and leave one supported regime out. A one-interface/one-hospital effect or failed overlap is not transportable.
12. Mask final-hour non-lab context and then mask earlier bins. This determines whether the interaction is genuinely attributable to pre-outcome context or merely to leakage/earlier process; no post-360 field is allowed.
13. For T1, require matched MLP and order-shuffle controls. A gain that survives order shuffling is not evidence for temporal ordering.
14. Audit all predictors for no post-360 values, no hospital ID, no APACHE mortality/APS fields, no `activeupondischarge`, no text-derived diagnosis, no target-fold preprocessing, no invalid revisions, and no many-to-many joins.

## Interpretation criteria

Supportive primary evidence requires all relevant gates; a positive held-out (I=1) death interaction (Delta_{death,K=1}-Delta_{death,K=0}) with a 95% interval excluding zero and exceeding a prespecified 1 percentage point lower-bound materiality threshold; smaller positive effects are exploratory. The death contrast in (K=1) must be larger than the discharge contrast in (K=1), the K=0 result should be smaller or show the expected transition/discharge pattern, and the effect must attenuate under lab-link, time-bin, K-label and instability falsifications. It must be stable in macro-hospital estimates, leave-one-regime-out folds, result/availability sensitivities, and EOL-inclusive versus care-eligible analyses. B1 must add held-out competing-risk information over B0 without a calibration/Brier failure. T1 is supportive only for the temporal-ordering subclaim if it improves over the matched MLP and passes order-shuffle and hospital-held-out checks.

A bounded supportive conclusion would be: in supported held-out eICU hospitals, among stays meeting the recorded-instability and prior-surveillance gates, the lab-drop association with subsequent recorded ICU death is more pronounced when multiple non-laboratory care streams also contain final-hour records, and is not explained by the tested generic documentation, discharge, interface, EOL, or timing controls. This remains a recorded-data prognostic/process statement. It does not mean patients remained physiologically unstable, clinicians should have ordered tests, tests were withheld, care was delivered, or more testing would improve survival.

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversed interaction, a larger or equally large discharge increment, a large effect in K=0 only, unchanged interaction after K/lab/time permutation, an equally large non-lab documentation analog, dependence on one hospital/interface/domain, disappearance after EOL/disposition adjustment, disagreement across clocks, or a GRU gain surviving order shuffle. Overall discrimination cannot rescue these failures.

Inconclusive evidence includes any failed dynamic or K support gate, sparse death cells, unstable folds, intervals spanning zero/materiality, inadequate hospital overlap, K domain prevalence below gate, nonconvergence, revision ambiguity, result/availability disagreement, inconsistent instability definitions, or inability to validate exact joins and timestamps. Negative/inconclusive evidence addresses only the recorded-data estimand; it cannot establish adequate monitoring, absence of biological deterioration, complete interface capture, clinician intent, causality, or clinical benefit.
