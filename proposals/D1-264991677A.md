# Evolution audit: lagged temporal validation of discordant-AKI recovery

Status: proposed child revision; no cohort scan, model fit, or scientific result was run.

Parent: [prior hypothesis]. This child preserves its MIMIC-IV question, source archive, joins, item sets, 24/48/72-hour landmarks, and prognostic (not causal) interpretation. It adds one substantive estimand/falsification repair: a strictly lagged recovery-discordance replication with a 24-hour washout before outcome follow-up.

## 1. Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The parent’s strongest evidence-supported background claims are narrower than its hypothesis. [K1] shows in a small prolonged-ICU cohort that creatinine-based eGFR can overestimate measured kidney function as muscle mass falls; it does not show that an observed MIMIC creatinine fall is a recovery phenotype. [K2] shows in MIMIC/eICU that urine-output thresholds, windows, and average-versus-persistent definitions change mortality prediction; it does not establish the parent’s discordance contrast or solve observation opportunity. [K3] associates positive recorded fluid balance with ICU AKI and mortality, but defines balance as intake minus output without insensible losses and leaves KRT to clinicians; it does not establish true volume status or the parent phenotype.

The unresolved clinical question remains: among adult ICU patients with incident AKI who reach a defined ICU landmark, does apparent creatinine recovery accompanied by low observed urine output and/or positive recorded balance identify a reproducible near-term high-risk state beyond measured severity and recording opportunity? If true, the state could identify patients for enhanced renal monitoring and prospective measurement validation. The result would remain prognostic; it would not establish persistent intrinsic kidney injury, dilution, congestion, treatment harm, or a causal effect.

The parent’s important remaining weakness is co-temporality. At L, the recent creatinine and the urine/balance exposure are measured in overlapping windows, while the creatinine peak is allowed to extend through L. A same-window contrast can therefore reflect documentation timing, treatment response, or a transient event that is nearly contemporaneous with the outcome risk rather than a clinically persistent discordant recovery state. A larger model does not solve this estimand problem.

This child tests the falsifiable refinement: if discordant recovery is a clinically meaningful prognostic state rather than a same-window recording/treatment artifact, a state defined in the preceding 24 hours and separated from outcome follow-up by a 24-hour washout should retain a directionally compatible association, and persistence across adjacent windows should be more informative than a state appearing only at the outcome-adjacent window. If the parent contrast exists only contemporaneously and not with the lag, interpretation as a reproducible recovery phenotype is weakened.

## 3. Population, time origin, and outcomes

Use the first eligible ICU stay per hospital admission for adults, joined on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, with valid `intime < outtime`.

- Adults: `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`.
- Index ICU: first eligible ICU stay per admission; retain valid `stay_id`.
- AKI onset t0: earliest valid creatinine observation within 72 hours after ICU `intime` satisfying the parent KDIGO-like rule: at least 0.3 mg/dL above a creatinine value in the preceding 48 hours or at least 1.5 times the minimum valid value in the preceding 7 days.
- If no prior value supports onset, retain a separately labeled admission-baseline sensitivity; never invent a baseline.
- Exclude established RRT before t0 using the audited procedure-event set.
- Use patient-relative times only. MIMIC deidentification preserves within-subject intervals but invalidates cross-subject calendar ordering.

For each current landmark L in `{24,48,72}` hours after t0, define `R_L` as alive, still in the index ICU, and free of incident outcome-defining ICU-recorded RRT at L. Report separately those who die, leave the ICU, or receive RRT before L. The primary 48-hour estimand is explicitly a survivor/ICU-landmark prognostic estimand, not an onset-cohort or causal estimand.

The current-window exposure uses only clinical times in `[L-24h,L]`. Use the most recent valid creatinine in `[L-12h,L]` and the peak valid creatinine in `[t0,L]`. Current-window creatinine recovery is peak minus landmark creatinine >=0.3 mg/dL; report the landmark/peak <=0.8 ratio sensitivity. Neither definition means normal kidney function.

For the lagged analysis at L in `{48,72}`, define `R^-_L` as subjects alive, still in the index ICU, free of incident ICU-recorded RRT, and without the outcome before L. The prior state uses only `[L-48h,L-24h]`:

- landmark creatinine for `A^-_L`: most recent valid value in `[L-36h,L-24h]`;
- peak creatinine: peak valid value in `[t0,L-24h]`;
- urine and recorded balance: clinical observations in `[L-48h,L-24h]`;
- weight: nearest valid weight at or before L-24h within 48 hours;
- no values after L-24h may define `A^-_L`.

The lagged outcome is `Y_L,7d`: hospital death or first ICU-recorded incident RRT in `[L,L+7d)`. Thus the lagged state is frozen before a 24-hour washout and cannot use values from the outcome interval. Subjects with death, ICU discharge, or RRT in the washout are counted in the lagged selection flow, not assigned a lagged state. This is a deliberately different estimand, not a claim to recover an onset-cohort effect.

Current and lagged states use the same definitions:

- concordant: creatinine recovery, urine recovery, and recorded net volume <=0;
- low-urine-only: creatinine recovery, low observed urine, recorded net volume <=0;
- positive-balance-only: creatinine recovery, urine recovery, positive recorded net volume;
- both: creatinine recovery, low observed urine, positive recorded net volume;
- unknown/insufficient: creatinine recovery but inadequate urine or recorded-balance opportunity;
- no-creatinine-recovery: descriptive only, not in the primary binary contrast.

The parent binary `discordant = low urine OR positive recorded balance` remains the primary 48-hour current-window comparison for comparability. The lagged binary contrast is a prespecified secondary falsification/replication estimand and must not replace the parent estimate.

Primary `Y_L,7d` uses `admissions.deathtime` and `hospital_expire_flag`, plus the audited ICU-recorded RRT items. Follow-up is not truncated at ICU outtime, but ward-only RRT is unavailable. Secondary outcomes are death alone, ICU-recorded RRT alone, same-admission ICU readmission, and hospital death by discharge. Alive hospital discharge and incomplete post-discharge RRT capture must be reported as outcome-ascertainment limitations; no causal or complete-KRT claim is permitted.

## 5. Residual severity and competing explanations

The leading rival is residual severity: discordant patients may simply be sicker at or before the landmark. For each current and lagged estimand, report unadjusted standardized risks and a prespecified prognostic adjustment using onset AKI stage, age, sex, admission type, pre-exposure creatinine, creatinine immediately before the relevant window, peak creatinine through the state endpoint, pre-window urine/output and recorded-volume summaries, pre-window RRT/support indicators, and ICU timing. Report strata across bounded categories of AKI stage, pre-window creatinine, and support intensity, suppressing empty/sparse cells. Repeat excluding patients with RRT in `(t0,L)` and separately treat pre-L death/discharge/RRT as selection events in the flow.

Variables in the exposure window are not claimed to be pre-exposure confounders. Adjustment is a residual-severity sensitivity, not causal control. Do not use post-L data, future outcome proxies, or learned predictions.

The observation-process rival is sparse or treatment-dependent documentation. Report primary-set output opportunity, input composition, creatinine count, weight/CVP count, and non-renal documentation density in each six-hour bin. Replace kidney discordance with quartiles of pre-landmark non-renal density (non-creatinine `labevents` plus non-weight/CVP `chartevents`, with input/output opportunity separate) and estimate its association with the same Y. This is a diagnostic for acuity/recording intensity, not a validated negative-control outcome. If the process-only gradient is as large as the kidney contrast and the kidney contrast attenuates in process strata, clinical interpretation is weakened.

The measurement rival is that the binary state is driven by one contaminated component. Mandatory component-specific estimates, zero-vs-missing checks, primary-only vs sensitivity item sets, and current-vs-lagged comparison must be reported. No claim of true GFR, complete urine collection, true volume, tubular injury, clinician intent, or muscle mass is allowed.

## 6. Temporal falsification and interpretation

The central prespecified comparison is:

- same-window current state at L: observations ending at L, outcome immediately after L;
- lagged state `A^-_L): observations ending at L-24h, a 24-hour washout, outcome beginning at L;
- transition `A^-_L -> A^+_L`: whether persistence, resolution, or new discordance adds information beyond current state.

Support for a clinically meaningful phenotype requires a nontrivial 48-hour current-window contrast, compatible direction in at least one lagged L with adequate support, and no complete dependence on one severity or recording stratum. Persistent discordance should not be less prognostic than a transient state solely because the current state is adjacent to the outcome window, although this is an empirical prediction, not an assumed truth. Unknown-state size and outcome rate must not make complete-case interpretation untenable.

An adverse result is a current-window contrast that disappears after the washout, reverses across lagged landmarks, is driven by unknown/missing subjects, or is matched by the process-only documentation gradient. That weakens the claim that the phenotype is a reproducible recovery state and redirects the next study toward prospective measurement adjudication. It does not prove creatinine is valid or fluid management harmless.

An inconclusive result is sparse lagged/transition cells, wide intervals spanning clinically meaningful harm, large unknown states, unstable estimates across reasonable window rules, or incomplete outcome ascertainment. It justifies deferral to a cohort with adjudicated urine collection, cystatin C/measured GFR, biomarkers, true volume measures, treatment intent, and ward-RRT capture. A small predictive gain alone is not a supportive scientific result.

Cluster-robust inference or a patient-level bootstrap must account for repeated contribution to 24/48/72 risk sets and current/lagged tables. Do not treat landmarks as independent. Standardization models must be prespecified; uncertainty must include sparse-cell limitations and not hide selection.

## 8. Baseline and substantive temporal alternative

Transparent baseline, primary for scientific interpretation:

- Inputs: current or lagged fixed summaries of creatinine peak/endpoint/slope, AKI stage, urine rate and observed-bin count, item-level recorded input/output composition, weight, exploratory CVP, RRT/support summaries, ICU timing, non-renal observation density, and explicit missingness/unknown indicators.
- Target: current `Y_L,7d`, lagged `Y_L,7d`, and transition-state forward risk.
- Fit: prespecified standardized logistic risk with linear terms or restricted cubic splines chosen before test inspection; patient-level cluster bootstrap; report state-specific risks, risk differences, calibration, Brier score, and intervals.
- Split: subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test; all stays of one subject remain in one split. The descriptive 48-hour state contrast is not selected on the test set.
- Budget estimate: CPU, 16 cores and up to 256 GiB RAM, within the parent’s future solver envelope; expected 1–4 hours for a full tabular fit is unmeasured and must be benchmarked by the solver.

Substantive temporal alternative, matched inputs and splits:

- Input sequence: six-hour bins from t0 to the relevant state endpoint, with creatinine, primary urine amount/rate and mask, input/output composition, weight, CVP, RRT/support indicators, non-renal counts, and missingness channels. For lagged predictions, truncate at L-24h; no current or post-washout values enter the lagged model.
- Target: jointly predict Y and the next six-hour state/transition, with a separate head for current-versus-lagged state risk. The model is a masked temporal convolution or gated recurrent model with prespecified regularization; it is not a mechanistic or causal model.
- Evaluation: held-out calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, AUROC as descriptive prediction metrics, and held-out current/lagged discordant-versus-concordant and persistent-versus-resolving risk contrasts with bootstrap uncertainty. Compare with the fixed-summary baseline on exactly the same test subjects and outcome definitions.
- Information gained: the temporal model can reveal whether ordering, persistence, and recovery timing add information beyond fixed summaries; the lagged head can show whether the signal survives removal of outcome-adjacent measurements. It cannot identify why the ordering occurs or distinguish severity, treatment, and mechanism.
- Resource estimate: CPU 4–16 cores, 16–64 GiB RAM, approximately 1–6 hours for a bounded fit, or one allocated A100 for approximately 1–3 hours if sequence batching is the bottleneck. These are planning estimates, not measured runtime; GPU is optional and no discovery fit was run.

Selection: the fixed-summary baseline remains primary because its state contrast is auditable. Fit the temporal alternative only if repeated six-hour support and transition cells are adequate. A small AUROC gain does not justify changing the scientific interpretation. Revisit a richer sequence/mechanistic model only if the lagged transition pattern is reproducible, clinically material, and not explained by severity or recording density. This is a scientific adequacy decision, not a CPU/GPU preference.

## 9. Interpretation boundaries and next decisions

Computationally checkable: source/hash and archive-member binding, joins, temporal exclusion, cohort flow, item filters, state counts, observation opportunity, current/lagged risk estimates, uncertainty, transition cells, held-out prediction metrics, and whether narrative conclusions match computed outputs.

Requires clinical adjudication or another study: true GFR, muscle loss, complete urine collection, total-body volume, insensible loss, tubular injury, treatment intent, goals of care, causal treatment effects, and ward-only RRT. Automatic verification cannot establish these claims.

Supportive results justify prospective validation with cystatin C/measured GFR, biomarkers, adjudicated urine collection and fluid measurement, treatment-intent capture, and complete KRT follow-up. Adverse results redirect to measurement-process work. Inconclusive results do not refute the biological idea; they show this MIMIC snapshot cannot distinguish it.

Alternatives not chosen:

- Unchanged fixed 48-hour analysis only: insufficient because it cannot test survivor selection, observation opportunity, or co-temporal measurement; retained as the primary comparator.
- Single black-box sequence model as the main deliverable: deferred because prediction alone cannot resolve severity or measurement error; retained as a matched secondary temporal test.
- Causal treatment-effect or inverse-probability intervention analysis: not selected because treatment intent, complete fluid exposure, ward RRT, and informative discharge/death are unavailable.
- Imputing later states for patients who die or leave ICU before a landmark: rejected because the state is undefined and imputation would hide selection.
- Hard clinical negative control: rejected because available outcomes are not credibly independent of acute severity and surveillance.

Evidence to revisit: an observation-complete prospective cohort or adjudication subset with cystatin C/measured GFR, muscle/biomarker measures, complete fluid collection, treatment intent and ward RRT. Evidence to abandon: a well-powered, observation-complete validation showing no reproducible current or lagged component/transition contrast.
