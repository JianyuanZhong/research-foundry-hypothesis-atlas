## Audit finding and scientific opening

The strongest claim currently supported by the evidence is only methodological: in a local HCC record, an assay trajectory could be evaluated for held-out prediction of a later literal-code TACE record, but that code is not validated as treatment completion, treatment intent, radiographic progression, treatment failure, response, benefit, or utility. The inspected literature supports using temporal structure while warning that longitudinal records learn collection and selection processes [K1], that selection weighting requires explicit assumptions [K2], and that recorded time and decision time are not biological time and do not establish clinical action [K3]. None validates the local HCC code or supplies missing intent/response evidence.

The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable:

> After the day-42 assay trajectory is compared with the day-42 snapshot under the fixed observation/workflow adjustment, does the trajectory add held-out information for a later TACE record with downstream same-visit source evidence, beyond information for a later TACE record carrying a pre-opened order signal and beyond the amount of downstream documentation?

The leading explanation is that ordered assay information captures a clinically relevant state associated with a later documented procedure episode. The strongest rival is a care-intensity/documentation explanation: patients with more observed care generate both the assay trajectory and more post-procedure examination/order/medication rows; the code and downstream rows need not identify a completed or response-driven procedure. A second rival is missingness: an absent post-source row may reflect incomplete capture, so it cannot be safely treated as evidence of planned-only care.

The clinical advance is a bounded endpoint-interpretation decision. A favorable result could justify a subsequent blinded chart/intent/imaging validation study of a precisely defined operational record. A result reproduced by the pre-order comparator or by source-density gradients would redirect the question toward workflow and endpoint acquisition, avoiding an unjustified assay-response claim. The design cannot decide whether TACE was intended, completed, appropriate, effective, harmful, or beneficial.

## Fixed population, timing, labels, and estimand

Use the first row in `procedures` whose Unicode-normalized, trimmed, case-folded `手术` equals `TACE`, with nonmissing `患者主索引`, `就诊号`, and `开始时间`; call its time `t0`. Exclude malformed dates, deterministic duplicate/alias failures, early-repeat cases as inherited, and patients without all-source observation through `t0+42 days` for R42. Preserve the inherited R28 pre-day-42 arm. Patient-held-out grouping uses only `患者主索引`; every visit join uses exactly (`患者主索引`, `就诊号`). No name, identity-card, phone, insurance-card, or file-order field is used.

All later candidates have a valid procedure `开始时间 tp) in `[t0+43,t0+181)`. The clinical window and first-event estimand do not change.

- **O0**: first later unique literal-code TACE.
- **O1**: inherited semantic hepatic-arterial procedure plus valid same-patient/same-visit timed examination or order corroboration.
- **O2**: O1 plus inherited same-visit medication corroboration.
- **O3-1**: first later candidate satisfying O2 plus at least one qualifying post-procedure source family with event/start time `u` strictly after `tp` and no later than `tp+72 hours`.
- **O3-2**: first later candidate satisfying O2 plus at least two distinct qualifying post-procedure source families, each with `tp < u <= tp+72 hours`.

For O3-1/O3-2, qualifying sources are exactly: `examinations.开始时间); `orders.开始时间) with nonmissing `订单状态) that is neither blank nor containing normalized cancellation tokens `取消`, `作废`, `撤销`, or `停用`; and `medications.开始时间). Source-family membership, all times, statuses, and row identifiers are retained in the audit. Same-timestamp duplicates use the inherited deterministic rule. O3-2 requires two different families, not two rows from one family. The 72-hour interval is a frozen provenance window, not a claim of procedure completion.

### Repair to the planned comparator

Retire the parent’s `O_preplanned` definition that required both a pre-opened order and *absence* of post-procedure corroboration. That absence is not observed capture completeness and is asymmetrically vulnerable to missing downstream rows.

Replace it with an observed process comparator `P_pre`:

> the first later literal-code TACE candidate with a same-patient/same-visit order whose nonmissing `orders.开立时间` lies in `[tp-7 days,tp)), regardless of whether post-procedure examination, order, or medication rows are present.

Retain order status, `开立时间`, `开始时间), `结束时间), and all source-row identifiers for audit; do not call `P_pre` a clinical endpoint or a negative-control outcome. A candidate may satisfy both P_pre and O3-1/O3-2; report the overlap and score each observed process label separately. No candidate is classified as planned-only merely because a downstream row is absent. Candidates with missing order time are `unresolved`, not P_pre-negative.

P_pre tests whether the assay increment is equally present for an observed pre-procedure documentation signal. O3-1 versus O3-2 tests whether the increment grows with the number of distinct downstream source families. These are discriminating process comparisons, not proof of intent or completion. The expected pattern is:

- a trajectory increment in O3-1/O3-2 but not P_pre, with no monotone strengthening from O3-1 to O3-2, is more compatible with a downstream-record association than with the specific pre-order or documentation-density alternatives, but remains nonclinical;
- a comparable P_pre increment, or a monotone stronger increment for O3-2, supports workflow/documentation selection;
- any result is compatible with unmeasured severity and missing outside care unless adjudicated.

## Fixed baseline-versus-learned comparison

The confirmatory baseline is the inherited elastic-net pooled-logistic discrete-time hazard with intercept, 14-day risk-bin indicators, M0/B0/observation/care/assay-opportunity blocks, and the nested constructions:
`A0=M0+B0+P_obs+P_care`,
`A_snap42=A0+S42`, and `A_traj42=A0+T42).
The primary contrast remains `D42-vs-snap_j=L_w(A_snap42,j)-L_w(A_traj42,j)), with O0 primary, O1 boundary, O2 secondary, and O3-1/O3-2/P_pre provenance comparisons. Elastic-net alpha is 0.5; lambda and all preprocessing/weights are fit only inside grouped development folds.

The learned alternative is the matched compact time-aware GRU: the same eight assay labels, numeric/qualitative/inequality content, sample type, relative time, inter-event time, and opportunity tag from `t0-365` through `t0+42), with M0/workflow covariates at the hazard head. One GRU layer, hidden size 32, dropout 0.10, head 32/16, Adam 1e-3, weight decay 1e-4, at most 50 epochs, eight-epoch early stopping, and fixed seeds 17/29/41 remain unchanged. Both methods receive identical O0/O1/O2/O3-1/O3-2/P_pre labels, right-censoring masks, folds, weights, and contrasts.

The baseline makes source-family counts, overlap, weighting, and coefficient blocks auditable. The GRU tests whether ordered irregular assay patterns contribute beyond deterministic summaries and may reveal interactions the baseline loses. A GRU gain without O3-versus-P_pre/source-density separation is representation-sensitive workflow prediction, not evidence of liver biology. The learned method is retained for this substantive representation uncertainty, not because complexity or GPU use is intrinsically valuable.

## Split, uncertainty, and output gates

Use earliest 80% of eligible patients by t0 for development and latest 20% as untouched test; five grouped development folds; no patient overlap. Use 1,000 patient-level paired bootstrap resamples of the untouched test set with max-|t| adjustment across O0/O1/O2/O3-1/O3-2/P_pre, A_snap42-versus-A_traj42 and A0-versus-A_traj42, ordinary and R42-weighted estimates, R28, and the fixed falsifiers. Report simultaneous 95% intervals, event/risk-set counts, calibration, Brier/integrated Brier, paired availability, missing/tied times, overlap, source-family counts, unresolved order-time/status counts, R42/R28 support, ESS, and weight tails.

Supportive means all are met:

1. O0 and O1 retain positive, simultaneously bounded trajectory-versus-snapshot contrasts after R42 weighting, with ordinary estimates directionally consistent.
2. O3-1 has event/ESS support, and O3-2 is not support-collapsed; both methods agree in direction without unstable seed behavior.
3. The O3 contrasts survive late-window removal, the R28 arm, workflow-only/time-only comparisons, and within-patient content permutation.
4. P_pre does not show a comparable trajectory increment, and O3-2 does not show a predeclared monotone strengthening over O3-1 that tracks source-family density.
5. Endpoint class counts and overlaps are stable in the untouched temporal test set and are not driven by a favorable subgroup or method.

Supportive output establishes only that trajectory content adds held-out information for a local operational TACE-record subtype with downstream source evidence and is less compatible with the observed pre-order comparator and the tested source-density pattern. It does not establish a completed procedure, intent, response, progression, failure, causality, mechanism, benefit, or clinical utility.

Adverse output includes a comparable P_pre increment; monotone O3-2 strengthening; O3-1/O3-2 loss of the increment while O0 remains positive; matching workflow/time/permutation controls; weighting or late-window failure; or elastic-net/GRU direction instability. It supports at most an association with documented care intensity and redirects toward workflow/endpoint capture rather than extra model complexity.

Inconclusive output includes inadequate O3-2 events or ESS, high missing order times/statuses, unstable source-family overlap, wide simultaneous intervals, R42/R28 incompatibility, or test-set endpoint-class instability. Do not alter windows, labels, subgroup, or model after test results. The next step is expert adjudication or better longitudinal capture.

## Three inspected works

[K1] Shmatko et al. reports that Delphi-2M learns temporal dependencies from large health-record trajectories and also reveals biases learned from training data; it supports the use of ordered history while bounding interpretation of record-derived patterns. It does not validate HCC TACE codes or clinical response. The present design adds an observed pre-order comparator and a source-family-density falsifier.

[K2] Urbut et al. presents a longitudinal EHR framework with an explicit likelihood and inverse-probability weighting for selection bias. It supports retaining an observation-selection analysis, but does not make R42 causal, nor does it supply HCC intent/response labels. The present design uses the same idea only as a transport/observation adjustment and adds endpoint-provenance comparisons.

[K3] Liu et al. distinguishes recorded/calendar time, biological time, and decision time, and notes that decision-linked temporal models require prespecified action thresholds, calibration, false-positive burden, clinician override, and prospective outcome evidence. It bounds the present study: O3-1/O3-2 are process-record endpoints, not biological or decision-linked evidence.
