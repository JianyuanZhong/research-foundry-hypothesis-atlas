# Leakage-safe decision-relevance and heterogeneity for the frozen HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B experiment

## Successor scope and unresolved clinical question

This is a targeted child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves, without alteration, the parent's selected adult HCC repeat-TACE population, recorded systemic-record/comparator ontology, event reconstruction, assay-specific clocks, annual forward blocks, nested prognostic estimand, training-range partial-identification contract, and strict noncausal limits.

The parent answers a narrow prognostic question: among selected repeat-TACE events with a remote baseline laboratory value `B` and a contemporaneous pre-repeat value `P`, does `P` improve prediction of the subsequent same-encounter laboratory value `Y` beyond `B` and the locked pre-event core? The remaining clinically consequential gap is whether any incremental signal is concentrated in particular pre-event subgroups and is large enough to change a forecast in a reproducible, prespecified way. A global RMSE gain alone does not show who experiences a meaningful forecast change, and it cannot establish that a clinician should act, that a treatment would help, or that an observed subgroup difference is causal.

The falsifiable successor question is:

> In the frozen, selected repeat-TACE risk set, is the incremental forecast from `P` beyond `B` heterogeneous across prespecified pre-event strata, and does it produce reproducible, outcome-robust forecast movement in any stratum rather than merely a statistically favorable aggregate observed-case RMSE?

The added estimand is therefore a **decision-time forecast-relevance proxy**, not clinical utility: the magnitude and direction of the fixed held-out change `p1-p0`, its movement across prespecified forecast bands, and the observed-case and missing-`Y`-bounded prediction-error contrast within each stratum. Because the laboratory files contain no assay units or reference ranges and no patient-important endpoint, the experiment must label clinical actionability, threshold meaning, utility, benefit, treatment response, safety, and causal effect as unavailable/not estimable.

No new HCC result is claimed here. The retained executed audit supports at most a local 2022 association; later calendar blocks remain sparse and observation-selected. All added analyses are downstream compiler work.

## Decision-time forecast-relevance estimands

For every future BP-eligible row with finite frozen predictions, calculate `q_i=(p1_i-p0_i)/SD_train(Y)`, using the assay-specific finite training SD. If SD_train is zero/nonfinite, all q-based outputs are non-estimable; this does not change the primary unstandardized or standardized stop rules. `q` is a forecast-scale movement, not a laboratory-unit or clinical-severity scale.

Report, overall and by each prespecified stratum, (a) median and interquartile range of q, (b) mean signed q, (c) proportion with `|q| >= 0.10`, `>=0.25`, and `>=0.50`, and (d) the proportion whose M0 and M1 predictions cross the frozen **training-derived** forecast tercile cutpoints of the assay-specific training predicted distribution. These thresholds are sensitivity coordinates, not clinical thresholds. The tercile crossing output is a ranking/reclassification diagnostic and must not be called a risk category or treatment recommendation.

For each row also retain the pre-event prediction rank among the same future block's BP-eligible rows. Report rank displacement and the fraction crossing a fixed decile boundary only as exploratory descriptive output; do not use future Y to define boundaries, and do not interpret rank movement as patient benefit. If predictions are nonfinite, stop the affected block rather than dropping rows. The primary model fit and primary G are unchanged by these summaries.

The outcome-linked within-stratum estimand is the same signed squared-error contrast used by the parent, `d_i=(Y_i-p0_i)^2-(Y_i-p1_i)^2`, divided by the fixed stratum denominator `N_s` for all BP-eligible rows in that stratum. Positive values favor M1's lower squared error. For observed complete triplets report the prespecified `G_s` only when its stratum SD and gates are estimable. Report heterogeneity as `Delta_s - Delta_ref` (and, separately, `G_s-G_ref` when both are estimable), where the reference is the complementary category within the same partition, same assay, origin, and block. This is prognostic heterogeneity, not causal effect modification.

## Missing-Y partial identification and selection-sensitive heterogeneity

Apply the parent's deterministic training-range contract without modification, now within each supported stratum as well as globally. Fit M0/M1 only on pre-origin observed complete triplets, freeze predictions for every BP row, and use `L_train=min(Y_train)`, `U_train=max(Y_train)` before inspecting future Y. If the range is empty/degenerate, a future observed Y is outside it, or any prediction is nonfinite, mark that stratum's bound unsupported or computationally invalid; do not widen the range or drop rows.

For observed rows use `d_i=(Y_i-p0_i)^2-(Y_i-p1_i)^2`. For missing rows use the exact affine endpoint values

```
d_i(y)=2*y*(p1_i-p0_i)+p0_i^2-p1_i^2
lower_i=min(d_i(L_train), d_i(U_train))
upper_i=max(d_i(L_train), d_i(U_train)).
```

Sum observed and missing endpoints and divide by fixed `N_s` to obtain deterministic `Delta_s_lower` and `Delta_s_upper`; also report all-missing-at-L, all-missing-at-U, and least-favorable-row endpoint scenarios. These are conditional identification regions, never confidence intervals. The global parent bound remains primary; stratum bounds are a predeclared heterogeneity stress analysis and must not be averaged with inverse-observation weights.

For a partition, compute the interval for the heterogeneity contrast by the conservative endpoint arithmetic `[Delta_s_lower-Delta_ref_upper, Delta_s_upper-Delta_ref_lower]`; label it an enclosure, not a sharp joint region, because strata share training fits and range information. A stratum can be called **robustly favorable** only if its Delta interval is strictly positive, its complementary reference is supported, and the heterogeneity interval excludes zero in the predeclared direction. Any endpoint crossing zero is selection-sensitive, even when observed-case G_s is favorable. Missingness fractions and the inherited outcome-blind opportunity strata must be reported by the same partition to show whether apparent heterogeneity tracks recording opportunity.

Retain the parent's common-displacement tipping analysis globally. For each supported stratum additionally evaluate the same common displacement only as a descriptive sensitivity coordinate, with shared breakpoints `L_train-m_ref` and `U_train-m_ref`, affine slopes, segment-valid roots, and direct endpoint checks. It is piecewise linear and is not an imputation, plausibility judgment, clinical threshold, or utility calculation. Do not claim that a stratum with a shorter tipping displacement is a clinically actionable subgroup.

## Outcome-blind decision-relevance and falsification controls

Before reading Y, carry forward the parent's selected-event -> B -> P -> BP funnel and mutually exclusive recording-opportunity classes: exam+follow-up, exam only, follow-up only, document-only, none. Use `examinations.开始时间` in (event_time,event_time+72h], an additional distinct encounter's `就诊时间` in that interval, and nonempty linked `clinical_documents` only as recording/opportunity proxies. Do not call an examination, encounter, order, medication, procedure, or document completed care, assay completion, review, administration, intent, or clinical need.

For each stratum report Y-observation fraction and Wilson interval, plus pre-Y observed-versus-missing SMDs for B, age, P lead, TACE2-to-repeat days, and arm. Flag absolute SMD >=0.5, observation below 80% of the pre-origin rate, low calendar ESS, or arm support failure. A favorable q pattern with a strong observation gradient is selection-sensitive, not supportive.

The compiler/verifier must execute these falsification checks.

* **No-leakage placebo:** recompute all q, crossing, and stratum labels after replacing P by a training-fold-frozen P permutation within broad event-era and arm cells while holding B, core variables, Y, row membership, and patient folds fixed. This is a restricted linkage placebo, not an exact conditional-randomization test. A similar placebo-level movement or heterogeneity pattern weakens attribution to P.
* **Timing placebo:** rerun only the descriptive forecast-movement summaries with P shifted to the prior eligible assay (or, if unavailable, an explicitly missing P), never using a post-event value. It must not alter the primary model. A comparable pattern suggests temporal/linkage artifact; failure to define a valid prior assay is inconclusive, not negative.
* **Stratum-boundary placebo:** shift each prespecified numeric boundary to the adjacent predeclared sensitivity boundary (age 60/70, lead 12/36 hours, interval 30/90 days) without selecting the best result. Concordant qualitative direction is stability only; discordance is heterogeneity instability, not evidence of a subgroup benefit.
* **Opportunity negative control:** compare forecast movement and Y observation by recording-opportunity class, outcome-blind. A large q difference confined to a class with differential observation is a selection warning. The result cannot establish completed follow-up or clinical quality.
* **Leave-one-source-family-out ascertainment check:** recompute opportunity classes omitting examinations, encounters, and documents one family at a time. This checks recording dependence only; it cannot validate assay meaning.

Placebo and boundary diagnostics are not primary outcomes and cannot rescue a failed gate, unsupported range, nonfinite prediction, or nonestimable G.

## Interpretation precedence and falsification criteria

Apply this precedence in the verifier: (1) computationally invalid or unsupported, (2) inconclusive/nonestimable, (3) adverse, (4) selection-sensitive/heterogeneity-unstable, (5) supportive surrogate-level forecast relevance. A result that meets more than one label receives the highest-priority adverse/unsupported/inconclusive label.

* **Supportive, narrowly:** reconstruction and clocks pass; the relevant future block and stratum meet outcome-blind and observed-triplet gates; finite predictions and training range pass; primary G is estimable; the stratum's deterministic Delta interval is strictly positive; its heterogeneity envelope is directionally stable under the declared boundary sensitivity and not reproduced by the P/timing placebo; and no material observation-gradient warning is present. This supports only a selected short-horizon prognostic association and reproducible forecast movement in that calendar mixture. It does not support a subgroup treatment recommendation.
* **Adverse:** M1 is no better or repeatedly worse, the relevant Delta interval is nonpositive, or a placebo reproduces the claimed P-specific pattern under adequate support. This argues against the proposed incremental prognostic signal for that scope, while not proving no clinical value.
* **Selection-sensitive:** observed-case improvement or forecast crossings coexist with a Delta interval crossing zero, strong observed/missing gradients, opportunity-class dependence, or subgroup direction changes under the predeclared boundary sensitivity. This is the expected label for a signal plausibly driven by who receives/has a recorded Y.
* **Heterogeneity-inconclusive:** sparse stratum, failed arm gate, no observed patient clusters, low ESS, unsupported range, outside-range Y, nonfinite predictions, zero SD, or unstable partition/placebo result. Do not pool or relabel it as homogeneous.

## What is and is not identifiable

Computationally checkable outputs are source and schema hashes, duplicate-safe joins, frozen denominators, event and assay clocks, pre-Y stratum membership, model fits, fixed predictions, forecast movement, rank/tercile crossing, observed-case G_s, deterministic Delta envelopes, tipping roots, placebo bookkeeping, observation fractions, and sparse/unsupported statuses.

The records cannot identify laboratory units, reference ranges, clinical severity, imaging response, assay interpretation, completed follow-up, medication administration or treatment intent, external-care capture, mortality, hepatic failure, patient-important outcomes, clinician utility, acceptable trade-offs, treatment effects, safety, benefit, transport, or an action policy. A recorded arm is not a randomized exposure. A subgroup association is not causal effect modification. Forecast movement is not clinical benefit. Establishing actual decision relevance requires unit-validated assays, adjudicated patient-important outcomes and decisions, expert review, and an independent temporally external/prospective cohort.
