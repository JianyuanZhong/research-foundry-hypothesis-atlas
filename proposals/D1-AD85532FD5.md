# Within-patient draw-rate drop as unmet monitoring need, or a between-hospital draw-practice fingerprint? A computable within-vs-between decomposition

Evolution working proposal — eICU 2.0 island, **episode 22**. Operation: **evolve** of true parent
`[prior hypothesis]` (valid, delivery-complete, mu 32.07; episode-21 evolve of
`[prior hypothesis]`; seed origin `[starting question]` = "Reduced testing and
subsequent deterioration"). This is a **bounded evolution, not a rewrite**: population, exposure,
outcome, landmark and severity adjustment are frozen from the parent. Status: **design owned by the
Lead for compilation**. No reference/solver solution was executed at discovery — this is design and
grounding work, not a solved result.

Two parent-acknowledged gaps are closed: (1) the closest prior observation-process work was not
inspected; (2) H1 (within-patient unmet need) vs R2 (between-hospital draw-practice fingerprint)
were only partially distinguishable. The advance is a **computable within-vs-between variance and
transport decomposition** with pre-specified numeric decision rules, plus freshly inspected grounding.

## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

Episodes 12–21 established a severity-adjusted, *within-patient* association between a change in
clinical-panel lab-draw intensity and subsequent organ-support escalation / failure-to-rescue, with a
non-monotone exposure–response and hospital random-slope + leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO) transport as
the between-hospital rival test. The parent (`92d62c51`) repaired a mechanism-defining circularity by
labelling escalation "unheralded" vs "triggered" on a **channel-disjoint** modality (periodic vital
signs / nurseCharting), not on the lab-draw channel that defines the exposure.

The unresolved scientific question is whether a within-patient drop in lab-draw intensity marks
**unmet monitoring need** — fewer draws while the patient is still unstable, so deterioration is not
recognised until organ support is required — or whether the association is a **draw-practice
fingerprint**: hospitals/units that draw less frequently differ in case-mix and monitoring culture, so
the apparent "within-patient" signal is actually a **between-hospital level shift** leaking into a
pooled estimate. Clinically this is the decision "is less ICU laboratory testing harmful here, or is
it low-value testing we can safely reduce?" It is the same decision now under randomized test in
Swedish ICUs ([K2] WICH-ICU: reduced ABG sampling and reduced routine laboratory testing, 30-day
mortality primary outcome). Resolving the within-vs-between question determines whether a draw-rate
drop should trigger a monitoring review or be treated as normal practice variation.

## 2. Leading claim (H1) vs strongest rival (R2), and the observation that separates them

- **Already supported (not re-claimed):** laboratory order presence/timing carries survival signal
  independent of the value ([K1] Agniel 2018, 233/272 tests for order presence; 118/174 for timing);
  a within-patient panel draw-rate change is associated with later escalation after severity
  adjustment, non-monotone (branch probe); poor vital-sign-protocol compliance before a MET response
  is associated with higher mortality ([K2]-adjacent evidence).
- **Leading claim H1 (falsifiable):** *within-patient* reductions in clinical-panel draw intensity
  increase subsequent organ-support escalation because monitoring is not aligned to need; the effect
  is carried by the **within-hospital** (hospital-mean-centered) exposure component, is largest where
  the channel-disjoint vital channel did **not** flag instability ("SILENT"), and transports across
  hospitals of different draw practice.
- **Strongest rival R2 (practice/measurement fingerprint):** the association is distributed as a
  **between-hospital** level shift and record-density artifact; the within-hospital centered
  coefficient is null while the hospital-mean draw-rate coefficient dominates, and the effect does
  not transport to held-out hospitals.
- **Exactly which observation separates them.** Write the exposure as
  `logR_ih = (logR_ih − μ_h) + μ_h`, where `μ_h` is the hospital-h mean of `logR`. A single model that
  enters **both** the hospital-mean-centered component (within, coefficient `β_W`) and the
  hospital-mean component (between, coefficient `β_B`) plus a hospital random slope on `logR` makes
  the two predictions non-equivalent:
  - H1 predicts `β_W` clearly negative (draw-rate drop ⇒ higher escalation odds), the within-hospital
    share `S_W` of the explained exposure variance high, small random-slope ICC, and stable LOHO
    transport.
  - R2 predicts `β_W` ≈ 0 with `β_B` ≠ 0, `S_W` low, and large between-hospital slope heterogeneity
    (ICC high) with poor LOHO transport.
  This is the observation that a pooled, uncentered `logR` coefficient **cannot** make, because it
  confounds exactly these two quantities (the parent's acknowledged identifiability limit).
- **Additional rivals (kept):** R1 residual severity/indication (hour-24 APACHE-IVa + trajectory);
  R3 disposition/EOL artifacts (exclude active `carePlanEOL`, maximal support); R4 reverse ordering
  (deterioration causes the draw drop within the window → strict temporal ordering + washout +
  hour-12 placebo landmark, grounded by [K3] Shintani 2009 time-varying-exposure bias).

## 3. Baseline vs substantive alternative

- **Simple baseline (what a naive design uses):** fixed-effect cause-specific logistic model for
  escalation with a **single uncentered linear `logR`**, adjusted for hour-24 APACHE-IVa, with a
  recognition-mode interaction; primary statistic `exp(-δ) = OR(logR | SILENT)/OR(logR | VITALS_FLAG)`.
  This baseline **loses** the within/between distinction: one coefficient mixes a within-patient
  deficit with a between-hospital level shift, and a single linear term cannot represent the
  non-monotone (inverted-U) exposure–response.
- **Substantive alternative (marker):** a **within-patient marked point-process physiology-adjusted
  draw-deficit model**. Fit a within-patient draw hazard `λ_i(t)` for clinical-panel labs as a marked
  point process whose intensity depends on the patient's time-varying physiology (vital-channel state
  and prior lab values), producing an **expected** draw count in (12,24]h; define the draw deficit
  `D_ih = observed − expected`. Then model escalation on `D_ih` with a hospital random intercept and a
  hospital random slope, and a spline in the deficit. **What the alternative reveals that the
  baseline loses:** (a) it isolates the *within-patient* physiology-adjusted deviation from a
  *between-hospital* level shift by construction (expected counts are anchored to each patient's own
  physiology and hospital practice is a modeled random effect); (b) it represents a non-monotone
  deficit–harm relation that a single linear `logR` cannot; (c) it yields a per-patient interpretable
  "how many fewer draws than warranted" quantity rather than a ratio.
- **Comparison/decision target:** `dAUROC`, calibration slope, O:E ratio, Brier score and net benefit,
  evaluated under **LOHO transport** (fit on all hospitals but one, predict the held-out hospital) and
  reported with the decomposition of §5. The alternative is preferred only if it improves transport
  calibration/decision value **and** yields a within-hospital effect that is not reproduced by
  between-hospital variation. **Approximate resource budget:** CPU-feasible; point-process fits and
  repeated LOHO folds are embarrassingly parallel over hospitals; fits on the frozen cohort are small
  tabular/GLMM workloads (est. < 1 GPU-hour; CPU is adequate — GPU only if the joint marked-process +
  multilevel fit is vectorized). Device choice is not the scientific question.
- **Alternatives not chosen / deferral record:** a deep sequence model (e.g. transformer disease-
  trajectory style) was **not** selected — it would predict escalation but would not directly identify
  the within-vs-between exposure decomposition that is the question, and the cohort/temporal support
  is small; would be revisited only if the multilevel decomposition is underpowered and richer
  temporal state is the binding constraint.

## 4. Population, temporal boundaries, variables, outcomes (frozen)

- **Population / unit:** adult eICU ICU stays (`age` ≥ 16) alive at hour 24, rescue-eligible; exclude
  active `carePlanEOL` and maximal-support stays at hour 24; require ≥1 clinical-panel lab in (0,12]h
  **and** (12,24]h. Unit/key: `patientunitstayid`. Offsets in **MINUTES** relative to ICU admission.
- **Landmark:** hour 24. **Exposure:** within-patient `logR = log( draw-rate in (12,24]h / draw-rate in
  (0,12]h )` on a pre-specified clinical monitoring panel (total-draw `logR_all` as secondary).
- **Outcome:** organ-support escalation in (24,48]h (new vasopressor infusion or mechanical
  ventilation), stratified by **channel-disjoint recognition**: `VITALS_FLAG` = a pre-specified
  abnormal vital sign charted within 4h before escalation on the periodic-vital / nurseCharting
  channel; `SILENT` = no such vital-channel flag.
- **Adjustment:** hour-24 APACHE-IVa severity; hospital random intercept. Recognition label never uses
  lab draws.

## 5. Computable within-vs-between decomposition (issue 2) and falsification criteria

**Estimator (primary).** Cause-specific logistic model with hospital random intercept `u_{0h}` and
hospital random slope `u_{1h}` on `logR`:

```
logit P(Y=1 | logR, X) = α + u_{0h} + β_W·(logR_ih − μ_h) + β_B·μ_h
                        + (u_{1h}·(logR_ih − μ_h)) + g(APACHE_IVa_h24) + h(logR_ih)
```

where `μ_h` is the hospital-h mean exposure, `g(·)` an hour-24 APACHE-IVa adjustment, and `h(·)` a
restricted cubic spline in `logR` (parent's non-monotone correction). `β_W` is the within-hospital
(hospital-mean-centered) log-odds of escalation per unit exposure; `β_B` is the between-hospital
component. Derived outputs:

- Between/within ratio `R = β_B / β_W`.
- Within-hospital share of explained exposure variance
  `S_W = β_W²·τ_W² / (β_W²·τ_W² + β_B²·τ_B²)`, with `τ_W² = Var(logR_ih − μ_h)`,
  `τ_B² = Var(μ_h)`.
- Hospital random-slope ICC `ICC_slope = τ_{u1}² / (τ_{u1}² + π²/3)`.
- Channel-disjoint differential `Δ = exp(-δ) = OR(logR | SILENT)/OR(logR | VITALS_FLAG)`.
- **LOHO transport contrast:** for each held-out hospital, the calibration slope `c_slope` of observed
  on predicted log-odds for the exposure component (and net benefit), summarized across the holdouts.

**Pre-specified numeric decision rules.**

- **Supportive (H1 — within-patient unmet need):** `β_W < 0` with 95% CI excluding 0 **AND**
  `S_W ≥ 0.60` **AND** `ICC_slope ≤ 0.10` **AND** `Δ > 1` with 95% CI excluding 1 **AND** LOHO
  `c_slope ∈ [0.7, 1.4]`.
- **Adverse/refuting (R2 — draw-practice fingerprint):** `β_W` 95% CI includes 0 while `β_B` 95% CI
  excludes 0, **OR** `S_W ≤ 0.40`, **OR** `ICC_slope ≥ 0.20`, **OR** the effect attenuates to a null
  OR (CI includes 1) after adding hospital-mean draw rate + charting-density, **OR** LOHO
  `c_slope < 0.5`.
- **Inconclusive:** the estimates are compatible with both rules — e.g. `S_W ∈ (0.40, 0.60)` with CIs
  spanning the thresholds, **or** `< 15` hospitals with ≥ 20 eligible stays, **or** the `SILENT`
  stratum has `< 100` escalation events (underpowered). Report as **unresolved**, not as support.

**Meaning of outcomes.** Supportive ⇒ a within-patient draw deficit after hour 24 predicts escalation
beyond severity and beyond hospital practice, informing monitoring-review triggers. Adverse ⇒ the
signal is a between-hospital ordering fingerprint, so a draw-rate drop is not by itself a clinical
warning; the branch's prior association is reinterpreted as practice variation. Inconclusive ⇒ the
identifiability question is not resolvable in eICU at the available event support; requires pooling or
a design with within-hospital randomization or order rationale. **Negative controls:** hour-12 placebo
landmark; a "stable-subgroup" negative control where no deterioration is plausible.

## 7. Computationally checkable vs needs clinical adjudication

- **Computationally checkable:** cohort counts and eligibility filters; exposure distribution;
  `β_W`, `β_B`, `R`, `S_W`, `ICC_slope`, `Δ`; LOHO `c_slope`/net benefit; event counts per recognition
  stratum and per hospital; wiring of the decision rules; placebo-landmark and stable-subgroup
  negative controls. Independent fixtures can confirm the decomposition arithmetic on synthetic data.
- **Needs clinical adjudication / missing evidence:** whether a draw-rate drop is a *deficit* or
  appropriate de-escalation (no order rationale in eICU); whether vasopressor/ventilation strings and
  `carePlanEOL` timing capture clinically intended escalation/goals of care; the causal claim
  that monitoring change *causes* deterioration (the design is associational — R2 is bounded, not
  eliminated); external validity beyond eICU hospitals (LOHO within one database is not external
  validation). A **stewardship/pharmacy or quality-registry review** is required before any
  policy-grade claim.

## 8. Honest limitations (not hidden)

- eICU has no order-set, staffing or bed-capacity fields → no organisational mechanism; only
  within-patient + transport tests.
- Recognition channels remain imperfect (charting density is itself a process).
- Reverse ordering (R4) is bounded by ordering/washout/placebo controls, not eliminated ([K3]).
- Discovery = readiness and design only; the decomposition has **not** been executed.
- **Grounding gap handling:** a directed search of Europe PMC and OpenAlex did not retrieve a
  verifiable record for the previously named "Yamamoto 2026" observation-process preprint; it is
  **not cited** (no fabrication). Grounding was substituted with inspected, verifiable works: [K1]
  (observation-process premise) and [K2] (the decision-relevant randomized de-implementation trial).
  Abstract-only inspection is labelled per work in `key-references.json`.
