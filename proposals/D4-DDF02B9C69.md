# Provisional hypothesis: fat distribution separates metabolic and functional trajectories

Status: provisional hypothesis for episode 2; not a result and not selected.

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with baseline body-composition measurements, at similar total adiposity, higher visceral adiposity will predict subsequent metabolic disease more specifically, whereas higher lower-limb muscle fat infiltration will predict subsequent functional decline or falls more specifically. The substantive test is whether fat distribution identifies divergent downstream clinical trajectories rather than merely adding a generic adiposity predictor.

## Grounding and evidence boundary

This direction is grounded in the existing UKB-17 seed and the prior episode’s data-bound draft: the seed identifies candidate UKB fields for body composition, functional assessment, falls, and diagnoses, while explicitly requiring overlap, measurement-date, missingness, and event-support checks. The prior candidate is a design artifact, not evidence that the association exists. No association, interaction, or clinical outcome has been computed in this episode.

## Falsifiable experiment

The planned Harbor experiment will use the same UKB participant namespace and documented table joins. It will define a baseline cohort with available body-composition and functional measurements, exclude prevalent outcomes at baseline, and follow from the documented assessment date to the available outcome ascertainment date. The primary comparison will test visceral fat versus posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration after prespecified adjustment for total body fat, age, sex, and measured confounders available in the bound tables. Outcomes will be subsequent metabolic disease and subsequent functional decline/falls, with temporal ordering enforced. A simple total-adiposity baseline and a prespecified composition model will be compared on held-out temporal splits, with uncertainty, calibration/discrimination, and interaction estimates. Supportive evidence requires distinct, reproducible component-specific associations; adverse evidence is a null or reversed divergence; inconclusive evidence includes inadequate event support, unstable estimates, or unresolved measurement timing.

## Required data and unresolved evidence

Exact source paths, table names, columns, joins, and time fields must be verified against the dataset catalog and source headers before compilation. Essential missing evidence includes verified measurement dates, outcome dates and event counts, treatment/medication context, and clinical adjudication of fall and functional outcomes. ## Method alternatives

The baseline is a parsimonious total-adiposity model. A substantive alternative is a prespecified composition model separating visceral fat from muscle fat infiltration and testing component-specific effects; it can reveal whether apparent risk is distributed phenotype-specific rather than generic adiposity. A more flexible learned model is deferred unless the composition model leaves a scientifically meaningful nonlinear interaction unresolved. The alternative is not justified by predictive gain alone.

## Interpretation limits

A positive association would support phenotype-specific risk stratification, not causality or a mechanism. Treatment, reverse causation, measurement timing, selection, and incomplete outcome capture require explicit analysis or additional data. Full study execution and clinical adjudication are future requirements.
