# Provisional hypothesis: hypotension duration versus nadir for subsequent AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU stay with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of new or worsening acute kidney injury during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Why this is worth testing
A single nadir can be transient or artifact-prone, while sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved question is whether duration/trajectory adds information beyond nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, treatment response, or measurement intensity. Resolving this could inform whether early AKI risk assessment should use duration/trajectory rather than a single minimum.

## Falsifiable test and interpretation
Compare a nadir-only baseline with a duration/trajectory model on the same cohort and temporal split, with hospital clustered uncertainty. Support would require reproducible incremental discrimination/calibration and a prespecified clinically meaningful effect for duration beyond nadir, not merely a small predictive gain. Adverse evidence would be no incremental signal, instability across hospitals, or reversal after measurement-intensity/severity adjustment. Inconclusive evidence would be sparse creatinine/urine observations, wide uncertainty, or inability to distinguish artifact from physiology. None of these outcomes establishes causality or a treatment threshold.
