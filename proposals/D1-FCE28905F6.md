# Held-hospital conditional transportability audit of eICU ventilation care-plan labels

## Parent, substantive evolution, and clinical advance

This is a substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent’s exact adverse and action findings rather than re-estimating or reinterpreting them:

- the durable-liberation endpoint remains failed because independent markers and strict 48-hour observation are sparse; no liberation association is retried;
- the completed 12-hour chart-proxy analysis remains unchanged: the primary setting contrast is inverse but dominated by peak-pressure charting, the no-peak contrast is sparse and directionally unstable, and sedation-rate mappings are imprecise and hospital-dependent;
- all conclusions remain about chart proxies and label use, not SBT delivery, extubation, bedside action, quality, benefit, or causality.

The parent’s key denominator limitation was explicit: its 540 rows are conditional on receiving one of the two target labels and therefore cannot estimate the hospital prevalence of either label among all otherwise eligible ventilated stays. This child repairs that limitation by making the principal unresolved question denominator-aware:

> At each of the same five landmarks, among **all adult ventilated stays that satisfy the same pre-landmark eligibility and exclusion rules whether or not either target label is present**, do hospital characteristics and measurable workflow/documentation coverage explain the hospital dependence of target-label assignment and the target-label-to-12-hour chart-proxy mappings?

The clinical advance is not a claim that a larger denominator rescues the labels. It is a falsifiable test of whether the adverse conditional transportability result is merely a consequence of conditioning on label receipt and documentation, or persists when the risk set includes eligible stays with no target label. This distinction matters before using the fields for multicenter benchmarking or decision-support feature construction.

## Evidence boundary and hypotheses

The parent supports the narrower claim that, within the selected target-labelled cohort, label use and computable mappings fail unqualified transportability gates. It does **not** support the stronger claim that hospitals differ in label prevalence among all ventilated patients, nor that the labels are local documentation conventions.

### Primary hypothesis under test

Let `R_hL` be the hospital-by-landmark denominator of all eligible adult ventilated stays at landmark `L`, with no requirement for either target label. Let `A_hL` classify each row as:

1. exact daily-evaluation label;
2. exact no-daily-trial label;
3. another care-plan ventilation value;
4. care-plan documentation present but neither target label; or
5. no target-label observation in the prespecified label ascertainment window.

The primary conditional-transportability hypothesis is that, after standardizing to common support of prespecified hospital characteristics and pre-landmark documentation/workflow coverage, (a) target-label assignment rates and (b) the parent’s 12-hour proxy mappings are sufficiently similar across hospitals. The adverse alternative is that hospital dependence, label non-observation, or proxy heterogeneity persists within comparable coverage strata or is revealed to be concentrated in hospitals with sparse documentation.

This is a descriptive measurement/transportability hypothesis. Coverage is not a treatment, hospital is not randomized, and conditioning on documentation cannot identify a causal workflow effect.

## Denominator and classification algorithm

For each first qualifying ICU stay and each `L` in the five frozen landmarks:

1. Parse adult eligibility from `patient.age`; retain age >=18 and report numeric/unknown/excluded counts. Require the same first-stay and in-unit-at-landmark rules as the inherited cohort. A stay discharged at or before `L` is not in `R_hL`; a discharge after `L` remains eligible even if it has no target label.
2. Reapply the exact inherited pre-L invasive-evidence algorithm using `respiratoryCare` and its specified offsets/values, and the exact strict physiology screen using `respiratoryCharting`, `vitalPeriodic`, and `vitalAperiodic`. Apply the same pre-L EOL, prior spontaneous-adequate, contradiction, and other exclusions. The only removed condition is receipt of a target label.
3. At `[L,L+360]`, classify the care-plan observation into target evaluation, target no-trial, other/ambiguous care-plan, or no target-label observation. A missing target label is missing exposure information, not a no-trial exposure.
4. Keep the inherited target-labelled rows as a row-identity positive control. The reconstructed denominator must reproduce 540 rows exactly when restricted to the two target classes, with identical `sid`, `uniquepid`, `hospitalid`, `landmark`, `strategy`, and `D`; otherwise stop and label the denominator reconstruction infeasible.
5. Define a pre-landmark coverage vector, calculated before `L` and independent of target-label receipt: number of distinct event offsets and number of nonmissing records in `respiratoryCharting`, `respiratoryCare`, `carePlanGeneral`, `note`, `nurseAssessment`, `nurseCare`, and `carePlanCareProvider` in `[L-720,L)`, plus binary indicators for any record in each source. Keep source-specific availability separate; do not collapse absence into clinical absence.
6. Define a landmark-window workflow vector in `[L,L+360]` only for descriptive ascertainment: any care-plan write, any provider record, any note/structured nursing record, and latency from `L` to first record. This is a documentation outcome, not a treatment or workflow-performance measure. A sensitivity using only pre-L coverage is primary to avoid conditioning on post-landmark recording that may be related to label assignment.

The full denominator table must include one row per eligible stay-landmark and at least: `sid`, `uniquepid`, `hospitalid`, `landmark`, `age`, unit-discharge fields, target class, target event time if any, all pre-L coverage fields, all hospital descriptors, and inherited screen audit fields. It must include zero-record rows by left joining event summaries to the denominator frame.

## Estimands and analysis

### A. Denominator-aware label assignment

For each hospital and landmark report counts and rates using the full `R_hL` denominator:

- evaluation / all eligible;
- no-trial / all eligible;
- either target / all eligible;
- no target / all eligible;
- other/ambiguous care-plan / all eligible;
- target mix, evaluation / either target, only as a secondary conditional quantity.

Report Wilson or exact intervals descriptively, raw numerators/denominators, age-unknown attrition, and the fraction of eligible rows with each coverage source. Do not rank hospitals on raw rates when denominators are sparse. Preserve the parent’s conditional HHI, eta-squared, 3/74 overlap result, and single-label result as a separate target-labelled analysis.

### B. Coverage-aware transportability calibration

Pre-specify a small, auditable coverage state rather than a high-dimensional model: for example, source-presence indicators plus quantile-binned total distinct pre-L event offsets, with bins defined from the pooled denominator and required common support. Report hospital-by-coverage-stratum target-label rates and standardized rates over the intersection of observed hospital/coverage/landmark cells. A descriptive logistic or binomial model with hospital, landmark, hospital descriptors, coverage bins, and prespecified clinical screen summaries may be used only as a calibration display; no coefficient is a causal effect and no model is fit if positivity/common support fails.

The primary calibration estimand is the change from the unadjusted hospital dispersion of `P(target label | all eligible)` to the coverage-standardized dispersion over common support. Secondary estimands are target-label mix and the parent’s proxy risk differences within the target-labelled subset, stratified by coverage. The action estimands retain exactly the parent rows, D, `(D,D+720]` windows, setting components, peak-only definition, sedation-rate definition, pre-D diagnostic, landmark strata, and leave-one-hospital-out analyses. Adding denominator rows must never recode an unavailable proxy as zero.

### C. Workflow/documentation coverage test

Test whether target-label assignment and target-labelled proxy mappings differ by pre-L coverage strata within hospitals. The key falsification is that a documentation artifact should be accompanied by strong dependence on source availability or record density, whereas a clinically portable semantic phenotype would show similar assignment/mapping after common-support standardization. This is only a discriminating pattern, not proof of a documentation convention: illness severity, care intensity, and data capture may all affect coverage.

## Prespecified gates and interpretation

These gates are measurement gates, not significance tests.

1. **Denominator reconstruction gate**
   - Pass only if the target-labelled restriction reproduces the parent’s exact 540 row identities, 443 unique stays, 74 hospitals, five landmark counts, 309/231 label counts, and exact D values.
   - Fail/stop if any inherited rule, missingness convention, age parsing rule, or row identity cannot be reproduced. No denominator-aware conclusion is then permitted.

2. **Denominator completeness gate**
   - Supportive: every eligible stay-landmark has a retained zero-record state and all source scans are full-source or source-indexed without sampling; unknown age and excluded rows are tabulated.
   - Adverse: any event table is sampled, target-label receipt is required, or missing target labels are dropped.
   - Inconclusive: full denominator is reconstructed but substantial source-specific coverage remains unmeasured.

3. **Common-support gate**
   - Supportive: at least ten hospitals have >=10 eligible rows at a landmark/pooled landmark and at least five hospitals have both target labels with >=10 rows each after the denominator extension, with overlap across pre-L coverage strata.
   - Adverse: denominator reveals that target assignment remains concentrated or either target has no common support across coverage strata; this strengthens rather than weakens the parent’s warning.
   - Inconclusive: denominators exist but sparse cells prevent coverage standardization.

4. **Conditional-calibration gate**
   - Supportive: coverage/hospital-descriptor standardization reduces hospital label-assignment dispersion by a prespecified relative amount (50%) without changing the target-labelled action direction, component interpretation, or leave-one-out reversals, and there is no residual adverse interaction in cells with adequate support.
   - Adverse: large hospital dependence persists after standardization, missing-target rates differ materially by hospital/coverage, or the parent’s no-peak/sedation instability remains or worsens.
   - Inconclusive: estimates move but common support or precision is inadequate. A reduction in dispersion alone is not evidence that the label is clinically meaningful.

5. **Documentation-coverage falsification gate**
   - Supportive for a portable documentation phenotype only if source availability/density is broad and balanced, target assignment is not largely predicted by coverage, and parent proxy mappings are stable across prespecified coverage strata.
   - Adverse to portability if target assignment or proxy associations are present only in high-coverage cells, reverse across coverage strata, or disappear when all eligible no-target rows define the denominator.
   - Inconclusive if missingness cannot distinguish absent documentation from absent care-plan state.

The overall conclusion may be only one of: denominator-aware transportability supported for this eICU snapshot; adverse to conditional transportability even after denominator and coverage calibration; or not identifiable because reconstruction/common support failed. None authorizes bedside action, quality ranking, causal workflow claims, or external transportability.

## Expected results and meaning

- **Supportive:** the parent’s high hospital dependence is substantially attenuated when all eligible ventilated stays and pre-L coverage are included, target-label rates have broad common support, and the parent’s proxy findings are stable across coverage strata. This would justify only a prospective replication/semantic adjudication study, not a bedside or quality claim.
- **Adverse:** all-eligible denominators show highly concentrated target-label assignment, large hospital/landmark/coverage dependence, or persistent action reversals. This would strengthen the conclusion that unqualified pooling is unsafe, while still not proving local documentation conventions.
- **Inconclusive:** exact denominator reconstruction fails, age/ventilation rules cannot be harmonized, source coverage is missing, or sparse overlap prevents standardization. The correct result is an information limitation, not a claim that hospitals are equivalent or different.

The inherited adverse endpoint and action findings are preserved under every branch. In particular, denominator extension cannot convert a sparse durable-liberation proxy into validation and cannot convert a peak-pressure-dominated chart association into delivered ventilator action.

## What remains unavailable

The eICU snapshot does not provide validated hospital workflow logs, template/version history, clinician intent, bedside SBT delivery, ventilator-device command/measurement semantics, standardized infusion units, or adjudicated extubation/reintubation. `carePlanCareProvider`, notes, and nursing fields measure recording activity, not staffing or clinical action. Hospital descriptors are coarse and observational. An external multicenter source with the same literal labels, site dictionaries, and clinician/abstractor adjudication is required for stronger semantic or clinical conclusions.
