## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest relevant evidence inspected is Haines et al., CJASN 2023, DOI `10.2215/CJN.0000000000000203`, from the local readable article. In a small prospective cohort of prolonged critical illness, paired cystatin-C/creatinine and iohexol measurements showed clinically important disagreement, with creatinine-based estimates overestimating measured kidney function; muscle loss was a plausible contributor. That evidence does not establish that a falling creatinine with persistently low urine output is prevalent in MIMIC, that Foley output is a valid measure of renal recovery, or that the discordance predicts mortality.

The repaired hypothesis is:

> Among adults with an observed early creatinine rise and observed early oliguria, who remain alive in their first ICU stay at a fixed 72-hour landmark and have adequate paired creatinine/Foley observations, creatinine recovery by 72 hours without late Foley-output recovery is associated with higher subsequent in-hospital mortality than recovery of both observed measures.

This is an association, not a treatment effect. “Observed creatinine rise,” “oliguria,” and “recovery” are measurement-defined phenotypes, not KDIGO AKI, true GFR, Foley patency, fluid status, muscle loss or renal mechanism. A positive result could justify confirmatory cystatin-C/measured-GFR and clinical review when creatinine appears to recover. It cannot justify dialysis, diuresis, fluid or medication changes.

The substantive advance is a fixed-time, paired trajectory test that asks whether creatinine-only recovery is discordant with a separately observed urine-output trajectory, while making selective Foley documentation and measurement density part of the estimand. It does not claim to reproduce the Haines study: MIMIC lacks its cystatin-C, iohexol and muscle-ultrasound evidence.

## Population and fixed temporal design

Use the earliest valid `icu/icustays.intime` per `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, one row per admission-first ICU stay. Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `subject_id, hadm_id`, and to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`. Include `anchor_age >= 18`, retaining and reporting MIMIC’s age-91 representation. Require valid `intime < outtime` and

`t0 = intime + 72 hours < outtime`.

Exclude `admissions.deathtime <= t0`. Repeated admissions may remain, but all fitting, validation, bootstrap and permutation units are `subject_id`; no subject may occur in both training and test.

All predictor observations must have `intime <= charttime < t0` (or the relevant start/end interval entirely before the boundary) and a parsed `storetime < t0`. Missing or late storage time is excluded from the primary feature set and counted. No death, discharge, later ICU stay, post-t0 treatment, or post-t0 clinical measurement is a predictor.

The primary renal analytic population additionally requires:

- at least one accepted creatinine in each of ([0,24h)), ([24,48h)), and ([48,72h));
- at least 12 observed Foley hours in both ([0,24h)) and ([48,72h)); an observed hour has at least one valid Foley row, and a missing hour is not zero;
- a qualifying weight measured by `charttime <= intime+48h`; and
- an observed creatinine rise and early Foley oliguria as defined below.

The 12-hour rule is an estimand restriction, not evidence that the other hours had zero urine. Report 18-hour and continuous-coverage sensitivity strata.

## Exact renal measurements and phenotype

Creatinine is the union of:

- `icu/chartevents.csv.gz`, archive member `mimic-iv-3.1/icu/chartevents.csv.gz`, item `220615`, using `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, charttime, storetime, itemid, value, valuenum, valueuom`;
- `hosp/labevents.csv.gz`, archive member `mimic-iv-3.1/hosp/labevents.csv.gz`, item `50912`, using `labevent_id, subject_id, hadm_id, itemid, charttime, storetime, value, valuenum, valueuom`, joined to `hosp/d_labitems.csv.gz` by `itemid` and to the index stay by `hadm_id` plus the index `intime/outtime` interval.

The frozen dictionary audit verified in the source that `d_items.itemid=220615` is “Creatinine (serum)” and `d_labitems.itemid=50912` is “Creatinine.” The audit must still report source units and invalid/non-numeric/nonpositive/out-of-range rows. Retain finite values in 0.1–30 mg/dL-equivalent range; use the item dictionary and observed `valueuom` to verify compatibility, with a source-specific unit sensitivity. At an identical `charttime`, ICU 220615 has priority over laboratory 50912; after that priority, collapse same-source duplicates by the median. Preserve source-specific counts and an ICU-only, hospital-only and combined sensitivity.

For each stay, within each 24-hour window use the minimum first-window creatinine (`early`), maximum second-window creatinine (`rise`), maximum value in the first 48 hours (`peak`), and minimum third-window value (`late`). Define an observed early creatinine rise as:

`rise >= early + 0.3 mg/dL OR rise/early >= 1.5`.

Define observed creatinine recovery as:

`late <= peak - 0.3 mg/dL OR late/peak <= 0.75`.

These are deliberately not KDIGO labels and do not use an unobserved pre-admission baseline.

Foley output is only `icu/outputevents.csv.gz`, archive member `mimic-iv-3.1/icu/outputevents.csv.gz`, item `226559`, using `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, charttime, storetime, itemid, value, valueuom`. The frozen dictionary audit verified `d_items.itemid=226559` as label “Foley,” `linksto=outputevents`, `unitname=mL`. Accept finite nonnegative values with `valueuom=mL), apply the same clinical/storage-time boundaries, assign `floor((charttime-intime)/1h)`, and sum rows within hour. Do not infer zero from missingness.

Weights are `icu/chartevents` items `226512) “Admission Weight (Kg)” and `224639) “Daily Weight,” with `charttime <= intime+48h`, finite 20–300 kg and a dictionary-compatible kg unit. The primary weight is the latest qualifying value by chart time; the audit reports blank/nonblank unit handling and an unweighted sensitivity.

For each supported stay, calculate:

`early_rate = early-window Foley mL / observed early hours / weight`

and

`late_rate = late-window Foley mL / observed late hours / weight`

in mL/kg/observed hour. Define early Foley oliguria as `early_rate < 0.5), and late Foley recovery as `late_rate >= 0.5). The primary phenotype comparison is restricted to observed creatinine rise + early Foley oliguria + observed creatinine recovery, then compares late Foley recovery with late Foley non-recovery. Keep the other creatinine/output cells visible in the flow table; they are descriptive and cannot be silently merged into the primary contrast.

This early-oliguria repair is substantive: without it, “Foley recovery” is an absolute late threshold and the word recovery is not anchored to a documented low-output state.

## Outcomes and estimand

The primary outcome is post-landmark in-hospital death:

`Y=1` if `admissions.deathtime > t0` and `admissions.deathtime <= admissions.dischtime); otherwise `Y=0) only when discharge/death fields support that determination. Missing or internally inconsistent outcome fields are reported and are not silently imputed. `hospital_expire_flag` is a consistency check and sensitivity label, never a predictor.

The primary estimand is the standardized associational risk difference:

`RD = P(Y=1 | late Foley non-recovery, primary renal population) - P(Y=1 | late Foley recovery, primary renal population)`.

Fit a prespecified logistic standardization model using admission/context variables and measurements available through 48 hours, before the late-output exposure: age, gender, admission type/location/insurance, first care unit, observed creatinine history through 48h, pre-48h MAP/HR/respiratory rate/SpO2, lactate, vasopressor support, ventilation, pre-48h RRT and CKD/ESRD diagnosis proxy. Do not adjust for late Foley, late creatinine, post-t0 variables or outcomes. Report standardized risks, RD, risk ratio and 95% subject-cluster bootstrap intervals (at least 500 resamples). Use robust Poisson only as a convergence sensitivity. This is not a causal effect of urine output.

Secondary outcomes are first new RRT after t0 and before discharge, using `icu/procedureevents.csv.gz` items 225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809 and 225955 after verifying their labels in `icu/procedureevents` metadata; and first later ICU readmission in the same admission among index-stay survivors. Treat new RRT as charted support, not proof of renal indication. Report death/new-RRT descriptively and use a competing-risk sensitivity with alive discharge. These outcomes do not replace the primary death estimand.

## Baseline versus substantive temporal alternative

The actual model deliverable is newly fitted prediction of the same post-t0 death outcome in the same paired-support cohort, not a claim that a better score proves renal mechanism.

The transparent baseline is ridge-penalized logistic regression, fit within training subjects only. Inputs are admission/context variables, age/sex, pre-72-hour creatinine summaries and counts, pre-48-hour nonrenal severity summaries (vital signs, lactate, vasopressor/ventilation/RRT indicators), CKD proxy, and explicit measurement/missingness indicators. It excludes Foley output, weight-standardized output, input-minus-output and fluid-trajectory features.

The substantive alternative is CPU `HistGradientBoostingClassifier) (or a fixed equivalent) on six-hour bins over ([0,72h)). For creatinine, Foley output, weight-standardized output, measured input-minus-output proxy, MAP, HR, respiratory rate, SpO2, lactate, vasopressor exposure, ventilation and RRT, compute last/mean/min/max/slope/variability/count/missingness features. The full temporal model includes renal output/fluid channels; the prespecified ablation removes those channels while retaining the same subjects, split, target and tuning procedure.

Use an 80/20 subject-held-out split fixed before fitting, grouped inner validation for tuning and training-fold-only calibration. Evaluate once on held-out subjects with AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope and curves, with bootstrap uncertainty. Report grouped feature-family permutation summaries as descriptive, not causal importance.

This comparison answers a clinical uncertainty: whether the timing and discordance of paired measurements add calibrated post-72-hour risk information beyond a transparent creatinine-centered severity summary. A temporal gain only counts as clinically informative if it is reproducible in calibration/Brier or clinically selected risk strata and is absent from the output/fluid ablation; a tiny discrimination gain alone does not establish a useful renal phenotype. The alternative may reveal nonlinear timing and interactions that an additive snapshot loses. It is bounded at roughly 2–4 CPU cores, <=8 GB RAM and hours including streaming extraction/fitting. A neural sequence model and latent renal-state model are deferred because their additional assumptions are not justified before paired support is established; GPU use is neither required nor a merit criterion. They become revisitable only with stable paired trajectories and a clinically adjudicated target.

## Falsification and no-go rules

Before fitting, the verifier must check that all reported counts and estimates come from the declared output files and the declared archive hash. The renal analysis is a no-go for adjusted claims if any of the following holds:

- the dictionary/link/unit checks fail for 220615, 50912 or 226559;
- either primary Foley-recovery cell is empty, has fewer than 50 stays, or the primary population has fewer than 20 post-t0 deaths;
- any phenotype or outcome is derived using post-t0 information or rows with `storetime >= t0);
- the primary label cannot be resolved for at least 95% of the eligible primary rows;
- source/coverage exclusions change either primary cell by more than 20% under the stated 12-versus-18-hour and source-specific checks, or the sign/direction is entirely driven by one source with no stable combined estimate; or
- the within-stay Foley-time permutation, preserving each stay’s total valid volume and observed-hour count, leaves the putative discordance association unchanged, indicating that timing-specific discordance is not identified.

If support is adequate, results are classified prospectively:

- Supportive: positive adjusted RD with a 95% interval excluding zero, direction retained in the 18-hour and at least two source/threshold sensitivities, and full temporal model improvement over both baseline and output/fluid ablation in prespecified calibration/Brier or risk-stratification metrics.
- Adverse: adjusted RD near/below zero, reverses under coverage/source/threshold checks, or is preserved by within-stay time permutation. Temporal prediction gain alone cannot rescue the renal interpretation.
- Inconclusive: sparse events, unstable estimates, failed calibration, strong observation-process dependence, or failure of a prespecified sensitivity without a definitive adverse direction.

Computationally checkable claims include row filtering, joins, cell counts, estimates, predictions, uncertainty, split integrity and permutation implementation. Clinical adjudication or another study is still required for KDIGO AKI, true GFR, Foley patency, fluid status, muscle loss, mechanism, treatment utility and external validity. The available data lack cystatin C, iohexol/measured GFR, muscle imaging and prospective renal adjudication.

## Distinct fallback if paired renal support fails

A failed paired audit does not authorize replacing the question with creatinine-only “recovery.” The executable fallback is a separate MIMIC question:

> Among adults alive at 72 hours with serial elevated lactate, does persistent lactate despite improving observed perfusion/support predict post-72-hour in-hospital death beyond a terminal lactate snapshot?

Use the same first-admission-first-ICU, adult, 72-hour landmark and leakage rules. Use `hosp/labevents` items 50813, 52442 and 53154 joined through `hosp/d_labitems` labels “Lactate”; require observed unit compatibility (primary threshold 2 mmol/L, with unit/threshold sensitivity). Use `icu/chartevents` MAP 220181 and heart rate 220045, plus `icu/inputevents` norepinephrine 221906 and dopamine 221662, and procedureevents ventilation/RRT as available. Define serial lactate elevation by at least one valid lactate in [0,24h) and [48,72h), and define improving perfusion as a rise in median six-hour MAP to >=65 mmHg with a nonincreasing vasopressor-support indicator, all determined by 72 hours. Compare persistent elevation versus lactate resolution, with the same post-t0 death outcome. Baseline is terminal lactate plus context; the substantive alternative is six-hour lactate/MAP/vasopressor trajectory with an ablation of support channels.

The fallback itself is no-go if lactate units are not supportable, either phenotype cell has fewer than 50 stays, or there are fewer than 20 deaths. Its conclusion would concern lactate/perfusion prognostic phenotypes only; it would not support any claim about renal recovery, Foley output or creatinine misclassification. This branch is documented so that failure of paired renal support produces a genuinely distinct, data-bound question.

## Alternative dispositions and provenance

The expert MIMIC seed 03 proposed discordant AKI recovery; seed 01 proposed asynchronous organ recovery. They are hypotheses, not evidence. The inspected Haines paper supports the concern about creatinine-based inference but does not validate the MIMIC phenotype. The three research demonstrations were treated as method examples: Delphi supports considering dated structured trajectories but its UKB cohort/validation are unavailable; ALADYNOULLI’s genetic inputs are unavailable and an EHR latent adaptation is deferred; Oncoformer’s full article/STAR Methods and images are unavailable, so no multimodal claim is made. The accessible Oncoformer supplement was not treated as the main paper.

The selected temporal booster is retained because it tests a substantive timing/discordance uncertainty against a comparable baseline at bounded CPU cost. A neural sequence/latent-state model is explicitly not selected before support audit; revisit only if paired support and calibration are adequate. A causal diuretic/RRT study is deferred because indication, volume status and treatment-time confounding are not adequately observed. A creatinine-only fallback is rejected as a silent weakening of the assigned question; the separately bound lactate/perfusion fallback is the no-go branch.

## What changed from the parent

This child keeps the fixed 72-hour noncausal estimand and exact MIMIC source bindings, but repairs four validity risks: it makes the audit’s storage-time and dictionary checks executable; anchors Foley “recovery” to observed early oliguria; prevents late exposure variables from entering the association adjustment; and makes the model comparison operate on one explicitly paired-support population. It adds numeric no-go gates for cells/events and a distinct lactate/perfusion fallback. No scientific result is claimed until the audit and fitting outputs exist.
