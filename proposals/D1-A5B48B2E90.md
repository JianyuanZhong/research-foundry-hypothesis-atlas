# Latent Disease Signatures in Critical Illness: Outperforming Traditional Severity Scores and Revealing Novel Patient Subgroups

**Dataset:** MIMIC-IV
**Expert Seed Inspiration:** None (new direction, inspired by ALADYNOULLI and Delphi-2M methodologies)
**Status:** Initial proposal - requires data exploration and method refinement

## Hypothesis to Test

Traditional ICU severity scoring systems (SOFA, APACHE II) use single-timepoint assessments that aggregate multiple organ systems into a composite score. However, these static scores may miss important temporal dynamics and co-morbidity patterns that drive outcomes. The question is: **Can unsupervised learning-based latent disease signatures identified from longitudinal ICU trajectories predict clinical deterioration in critically ill patients better than baseline SOFA scores, and do these signatures reveal novel patient subgroups with distinct clinical phenotypes?**

Formally: In critically ill patients (sepsis, shock, or other critical conditions), latent disease signatures (S_t) extracted via unsupervised learning from longitudinal ICU vitals, labs, and medications predict 7-day mortality and organ support escalation better than baseline SOFA scores (AUC_S > AUC_SOF + 0.03, p < 0.01), and these signatures identify patient subgroups with distinct risk profiles and clinical trajectories.

## Study Population and Main Data

**Population:** Critically ill patients in MIMIC-IV (ICU admissions with high-acuity conditions: sepsis, septic shock, trauma, cardiac arrest, respiratory failure, or other conditions requiring organ support)

**Inclusion criteria:**
- ICU admission with at least one organ support (vasopressors, mechanical ventilation, renal replacement therapy, or hemodialysis)
- At least 24 hours of continuous ICU monitoring
- Age ≥ 18 years

**Main data tables:**
- `hosp/d_icd_diagnoses`: ICU diagnoses (ICD-10 codes) for condition classification
- `icu/icustays`: ICU stays with SOFA scores, admission/discharge times, LOS
- `icu/chartevents`: Vital signs (SpO2, NIBP, IBP, HR, RR, EtCO2)
- `hosp/labevents`: Laboratory measurements (creatinine, lactate, WBC, platelets, sodium, bicarbonate)
- `icu/ingredientevents`: Medication administration (vasopressors, vasopressin, fluids, vasopressin)
- `hosp/patients`: Patient demographics, mortality status (dod)

**Key variables:**
- **Latent signature S_t**: Low-dimensional representation (e.g., 50-100 dimensions) extracted via autoencoder/VAE from longitudinal vitals, labs, and medications
- **Baseline SOFA**: Single-timepoint SOFA score at ICU admission
- **Confounders**: Age, gender, comorbidities (Charlson comorbidity index), admission source
- **Outcomes**: 7-day mortality, escalation of organ support, readmission within 7 days

## Scientific Significance

1. **Beyond static severity scores**: Unlike SOFA/APACHE II which use single-timepoint measurements, latent signatures capture temporal dynamics and evolving risk patterns
2. **Beyond single-disease models**: Traditional models predict one disease at a time; latent signatures capture co-morbidity patterns and disease co-evolution
3. **Unsupervised discovery**: Unlike supervised models trained on outcomes, unsupervised learning discovers patterns without outcome labels, potentially revealing novel phenotypes
4. **Patient subgroup identification**: Latent signatures may identify patient subgroups with distinct risk profiles, treatment responses, and clinical trajectories not captured by traditional scoring
5. **Methodological advancement**: Adapts the ALADYNOULLI framework (Bayesian latent signature discovery) to the critical illness domain, building on Delphi-2M's demonstration of transformer-based disease progression modeling

## Baseline Approach

**Model:** Cox proportional hazards regression with baseline SOFA score as primary covariate

**Alternative baseline:** Logistic regression with baseline SOFA

**Hypothesis (baseline):** Baseline SOFA score predicts outcomes in critically ill patients, as expected.

**Expected result:** Baseline SOFA shows moderate-to-good discrimination (AUC ≈ 0.65-0.75 for 7-day mortality).

## Alternative Approach

**Model:** Unsupervised latent signature learning + supervised outcome prediction

**Step 1: Latent signature extraction (unsupervised)**
- Use autoencoder or variational autoencoder (VAE) to learn low-dimensional representations from longitudinal ICU data
- Input: Time-series of vitals (chartevents), labs (labevents), and medications (ingredientevents)
- Output: Latent signature S_t at each timepoint (e.g., 50-dimensional vector)
- Training: Unsupervised reconstruction loss (minimize reconstruction error)
- No outcome labels used in this step

**Step 2: Outcome prediction (supervised)**
- Use latent signatures S_t as features for outcome prediction
- Models: Random forest, gradient boosting (XGBoost/LightGBM), or deep neural network
- Outcomes: 7-day mortality, escalation of organ support
- Training: Supervised learning with outcome labels

**Step 3: Subgroup identification**
- Use clustering on latent signatures (e.g., k-means, hierarchical clustering)
- Identify patient subgroups with similar latent signature trajectories
- Characterize subgroups by clinical phenotypes, risk profiles, and outcomes

**Hypothesis (alternative):**
1. Latent signatures predict outcomes better than baseline SOFA (AUC_S > AUC_SOF + 0.03, p < 0.01)
2. Latent signatures identify patient subgroups with distinct risk profiles and trajectories
3. Subgroups show heterogeneous treatment responses or risk patterns

## Data Bindings

**MIMIC-IV table mappings:**
- Condition classification: `hosp/d_icd_diagnoses` with ICD-10 codes (e.g., sepsis: A40-A47, R65.20; respiratory failure: J96.00; cardiac arrest: I46.9)
- ICU stays: `icu/icustays` with stay_id, patient_id, SOFA scores, admission/discharge times
- Vital signs: `icu/chartevents` with itemid (vital sign codes), charttime, value (numerical)
- Labs: `hosp/labevents` with itemid (lab codes), charttime, value (numerical)
- Medications: `icu/ingredientevents` with itemid (drug codes), starttime, amount
- Demographics: `hosp/patients` with subject_id, gender, anchor_age, anchor_year, dod

**Join keys:**
- `hosp/patients` (subject_id)
- `icu/icustays` (hadm_id, linked to subject_id via `hosp/patients`)
- `hosp/labevents` (hadm_id, charttime)
- `icu/chartevents` (hadm_id, charttime)
- `icu/ingredientevents` (hadm_id, starttime)

**Latent signature construction:**
- Resample time-series to fixed frequency (e.g., every 6 hours)
- Align vitals, labs, and medications by time
- Stack features into matrix (patients × timepoints)
- Train autoencoder/VAE to learn low-dimensional representation
- Extract latent signature S_t for each patient at each timepoint

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis is **falsified** if:
1. Latent signatures do not outperform baseline SOFA in discrimination (AUC_S - AUC_SOF < 0.03, p > 0.05)
2. Latent signatures do not identify novel patient subgroups with distinct clinical phenotypes
3. The association between latent signatures and outcomes disappears after adjusting for baseline SOFA
4. No improvement in calibration or dynamic prediction

The hypothesis is **supported** if:
1. Latent signatures significantly outperform baseline SOFA (AUC_S - AUC_SOF > 0.03, p < 0.01)
2. Latent signatures identify patient subgroups with distinct risk profiles and trajectories
3. Subgroups show heterogeneous treatment responses or risk patterns
4. Latent signatures improve dynamic prediction (time-dependent AUC)

The hypothesis is **inconclusive** if:
1. Results are inconsistent across different timepoints or patient subgroups
2. Sample size is insufficient for definitive conclusions
3. Latent signatures show some predictive value but not clinically meaningful improvement

## Novelty and Advance Over Existing Knowledge

This proposal advances beyond existing work in three key ways:

1. **Beyond Delphi-2M**: While Delphi-2M demonstrates transformer-based disease progression modeling, this proposal uses unsupervised latent signature learning specifically for critical illness, adapting the methodology to the high-acuity setting where temporal dynamics are most critical.

2. **Beyond ALADYNOULLI**: While ALADYNOULLI jointly models EHR and genetics to identify latent signatures, this proposal applies the framework to the critical illness domain where genetic data may be limited or unavailable, focusing on clinical data alone.

3. **Beyond traditional severity scores**: Unlike SOFA/APACHE II which use single-timepoint measurements, this approach uses longitudinal trajectories to capture evolving risk patterns and identify patient subgroups.

## Limitations

1. **Unsupervised learning interpretability**: Latent signatures are data-driven representations without explicit biological or clinical interpretation; requires clinical expert validation
2. **Data quality**: Missing or noisy longitudinal data may affect latent signature quality
3. **Temporal resolution**: ICU data granularity may limit precise timing of events
4. **Generalizability**: Results may not generalize to other ICU populations or settings
5. **Computational complexity**: Latent signature learning requires more computational resources than traditional scoring
