# Observation-controlled discharge recovery after TACE and early return in HCC

## Candidate status, clinical question, and bounded repair

This is a substantive episode-8 child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It remains an observational prognostic experiment, not a treatment-effect, discharge-policy, mortality, or clinical-utility study.

The parent’s primary early post-TACE estimand is preserved exactly:

> Among adults with HCC who have a first observed TACE episode and are discharged, does the within-person laboratory trajectory from the 14 days before TACE to the first 72 hours after TACE add reproducible held-out information about a subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days after discharge beyond baseline hepatic reserve, prior utilization, demographics, and recorded disease history?

The repaired, discharge-time claim is:

> In the prespecified nested population with at least 96 hours from observed TACE start to discharge and complete core laboratory windows, does the discharge-proximal change from the first 72 hours to the final 24 hours before discharge add held-out prognostic information beyond baseline/early laboratory information **and a prespecified measurement-opportunity block**?

This is a conditional incremental-prognostic estimand. It does not estimate the causal effect of TACE, discharge timing, laboratory ordering, monitoring, or a laboratory-triggered intervention. It does not identify a safe discharge threshold.

The substantive repair has two linked parts. First, the discharge comparison controls for how intensively the record was observed before discharge, while keeping that block in both D0 and D1; this tests whether discharge laboratory change contributes information beyond recorded opportunity to measure it. Second, a non-overlapping pre-TACE placebo trajectory and timestamp falsifications test whether the result is specific to the TACE-to-discharge temporal structure rather than a generic trend, timestamp artifact, or care-intensity marker. These are interpretation and falsification controls, not new primary outcomes.

Because the source lacks a mortality table, death cannot be treated as an observed competing event. The primary endpoint remains a fixed-window binary outcome. The first qualifying post-discharge admission is additionally classified descriptively as liver-coded or non-liver-coded; that is an operational decomposition, not adjudicated decompensation or a competing-risk mortality analysis.

## Evidence-supported claim versus unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported by the external evidence actually inspected is narrower. Sanai et al., *Scientific Reports* 2026 (DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-54466-4; PMCID PMC13457603; frozen full-text source ID `[source checksum]`) studied TACE/TARE at four tertiary centers. Its inspected abstract used ascites, variceal bleeding, or portosystemic encephalopathy in a hepatic-decompensation definition and reported baseline ALBI and treatment type as predictors of post-locoregional-treatment decompensation. This supports clinical plausibility that hepatic reserve and post-locoregional-treatment complications matter.

It does not establish that routinely recorded, time-ordered laboratory change after an observed TACE episode adds prognostic information beyond baseline values in this HCC snapshot. It does not establish that persistence of an abnormal trajectory at discharge identifies people who benefit from delayed discharge or intensified monitoring. The HCC source has no laboratory units and cannot produce a validated ALBI score.

The unresolved claim tested here is therefore precise: conditional on baseline reserve, early post-TACE values, prior utilization, recorded disease history, and the available observation-process markers, does a discharge-proximal recovery signal improve held-out prediction of a subsequent inpatient admission? A further unresolved timing claim is whether the result is stronger for the TACE-anchored trajectory than for an otherwise analogous, non-overlapping pre-TACE placebo trajectory.

This matters because discharge planning occurs at the end of the index stay, when a persistent abnormal record may influence follow-up or monitoring decisions. A reproducible incremental signal would justify a prospective study with laboratory units, clinically adjudicated outcomes, treatment/scheduling data, and a defined monitoring pathway. A null or timing-falsified result would caution against interpreting routine discharge trajectories as a TACE-specific escalation signal.

## Baselines and estimands

### Preserved primary early estimand

Fit patient-level baseline-only and trajectory models for the primary 30-day all-cause admission outcome.

Baseline-only covariates are age, sex, index department, the three baseline core assay medians, number of encounters in the preceding 365 days, and prior recorded indicators for cirrhosis, portal-vein thrombosis, ascites, diabetes, and prior liver surgery. Build prior indicators only with date-aligned joins to `encounters` plus `diagnoses` or `procedures`; diagnosis rows themselves have no time. These are recorded-code/utilization markers, not adjudicated comorbidities. Use the trajectory model’s three early-minus-baseline core changes as the incremental block. The primary estimand is the global likelihood-ratio and held-out incremental prognostic information of that block, complemented by adjusted odds ratios per training-fold IQR, absolute risk differences at representative covariate patterns, AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, and calibration intercept/slope.

Use patient-level five-fold cross-validation within discovery buckets 0–79 and a chronological split with 2010–2022 derivation and 2023–2025-12-02 testing. Keep all imputation, robust scaling, feature selection, and tuning inside training folds. Use a prespecified regularized logistic model as primary; a shallow gradient-boosted model is a bounded functional-form sensitivity, not evidence of clinical value. No GPU is needed.

### Repaired discharge estimand

In the nested cohort, Model D0 contains baseline covariates and early core assay values (retain one fixed baseline-plus-early parameterization as primary). Model D1 adds the three discharge-minus-early core changes. Neither primary D0 nor D1 may use post-discharge data, post-index medications, post-index orders, later laboratory values, or length of stay as a covariate; these can mediate recovery or leak information.

The repaired observation-controlled sensitivity uses the same measurement-opportunity vector P in both models:

- per-assay valid-row count, distinct-timestamp count, span, and maximum timestamp gap in the early and discharge windows;
- total valid core-lab row count and distinct core-lab timestamp count from TACE start to discharge;
- distinct pre-discharge encounter-key count represented by joined core lab rows.

Compare D1+P against D0+P out of fold and by likelihood ratio. The primary nested claim remains D1 versus D0; D1+P versus D0+P is the prespecified interpretation check. A surviving incremental result supports only added prognostic information conditional on recorded observation opportunity. It does not prove a biological recovery mechanism, eliminate informative measurement, or support an intervention.

Require at least 20 all-cause events in the nested analysis before inferential modeling. Require at least 20 liver-coded first-event signals before any liver-coded model; otherwise give counts and component frequencies only and label the result inconclusive regardless of p-value. Penalization cannot rescue an inadequate event gate. Use patient-level bootstrap intervals for incremental metrics and coefficient uncertainty, with the number of bootstrap resamples recorded.

### Operational event handling

The primary binary endpoint uses any first qualifying admission within 30 days. For interpretation, report first liver-coded versus first non-liver-coded admission as mutually exclusive operational categories and report subsequent admission multiplicity. If both a liver and non-liver diagnosis appear on the same first admission, classify it liver-coded and disclose the rule. Do not fit a competing mortality-risk model: mortality is unavailable. If both operational categories pass their event gate, a cause-specific descriptive model may be reported, but neither category is a clinical diagnosis and no competing-risk causal interpretation is permitted.

## Falsification and adequacy criteria

Run all checks after the discovery partition and with fold-contained preprocessing.

1. **Non-overlapping pre-TACE placebo.** Set a placebo anchor at TACE start minus 28 days. Construct placebo baseline [TACE start − 42 days, TACE start − 28 days) and placebo early [TACE start − 28 days, TACE start − 25 days], using the same assay medians and paired-window rule. These windows do not overlap the actual baseline, early, or discharge windows. Predict the same post-discharge outcome with the same baseline covariates and observation-process controls. This is a negative temporal control: a comparable predictive gain of the placebo trajectory, especially one as large as the TACE-anchored gain within uncertainty, weakens a TACE-timed interpretation. If too sparse, record the missingness/event gate and call timing evidence inconclusive.

2. **Timestamp falsification.** Within participant and assay, randomly permute valid `Laboratory Time` assignments with a recorded seed while retaining values, labels, and participant/encounter joins; rederive windows and repeat for a prespecified number of permutations. Also run a time-reversal variant. A genuinely time-ordered signal should attenuate. Do not present the permutation as a clinical null test.

3. **Observation-process challenge.** Compare complete-case, stricter two-timestamp, missingness-indicator, inverse-observation-weighted, and D0+P/D1+P results. Report selection by calendar period and department. If the signal disappears after P or is explained by measurement opportunity, the biological/recovery interpretation is not supported, although a care-record utilization marker may remain prognostic.

4. **Density and gap sensitivity.** Repeat with minimum-observation and maximum-gap requirements specified before looking at outcomes. Do not silently change the primary window or impute unobserved values.

5. **Procedure and assay audit.** Repeat using the exact frozen TACE dictionary and a narrower label subset only as a sensitivity. Preserve all label classifications and assay availability. If procedure classification or assay meaning is not reproducible, the corresponding claim is inconclusive.

6. **Leakage and joins.** Assert no post-discharge lab/order/medication row enters features; assert exact two-key joins, no multiplication, valid timestamp ordering, and no index encounter counted as a return. The `orders` table, if audited for leakage, is `[internal dataset path]`, table `orders`, catalog table `table-6b93dcf0ea823702.json`, ordinary file. It has the same two-key relationship but is not a primary predictor. The same exclusion applies to `medications` at `[internal dataset path]`, table `medications`, catalog table `table-4f6ecaeb6e8f69c2.json`.

## Prespecified interpretation

Call the primary early-trajectory hypothesis supportive only when:

- the trajectory block has global LR p < 0.05;
- optimism-corrected out-of-fold Brier improvement over baseline has a 95% bootstrap interval excluding zero;
- direction and calibration are not reversed in the chronological split;
- the gain is not solely in-sample/high-capacity and survives key density/gap and timestamp checks.

Call it adverse/falsified when there is no incremental held-out evidence, or when the apparent gain disappears/reverses chronologically, after observation-density handling, or under timestamp falsification. An in-sample AUROC gain with worse held-out Brier or calibration is not supportive.

Call the nested discharge non-recovery hypothesis supportive only when the all-cause nested event gate passes, D1 improves over D0 on held-out metrics with uncertainty excluding no useful increment, the observation-controlled D1+P versus D0+P result is directionally consistent, and the chronological, density/gap, and timestamp checks do not reverse the interpretation. A D1 gain that disappears after P is evidence that recorded measurement opportunity explains the apparent discharge signal, not evidence of discharge-time hepatic non-recovery. A placebo gain comparable to the TACE gain weakens the TACE-timed claim.

If the early hypothesis supports but the nested hypothesis does not, retain only the narrower early prognostic conclusion. If the all-cause nested event gate fails, the discharge claim is inconclusive even with a favorable p-value. A liver-coded result with fewer than 20 events is descriptive/inconclusive even if statistically suggestive.

Supportive results justify a prospective validation study with laboratory units/provenance, adjudicated decompensation outcomes, reliable treatment and scheduling information, mortality ascertainment, tumor stage, outpatient capture, and a prespecified monitoring/discharge pathway. Adverse results argue against using these routine trajectories as a stand-alone escalation rule. Inconclusive results specify the missing data or expert work needed; they are not evidence of no biological association.
