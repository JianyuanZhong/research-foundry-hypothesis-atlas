# Provisional HCC hypothesis: post-hepatectomy laboratory trajectory and 30-day deterioration

Status: provisional, episode 2; this is not a completed analysis or clinical conclusion.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with a recorded liver resection in the HCC snapshot, the early post-operative trajectory of liver-related laboratory abnormalities will be associated with a clinically important deterioration signal during the subsequent 30 days, beyond baseline disease severity and operative context. The unresolved claim is whether a reproducible trajectory (rather than a single post-operative value) identifies a distinct recovery/deterioration pattern that could inform surveillance or escalation research.

## What is observed versus what is unproven

The HCC source catalog and dataset README establish that the snapshot contains longitudinal clinical data, including procedures, laboratory results, encounters, diagnoses, medications, examinations, pathology, and clinical documents. The prior episode audit recorded 48,925 resection rows and 24,712 patients, with bilirubin/INR/ALT/albumin available, while also recording 3,135 missing procedure start times and unavailable laboratory units. These are source/availability observations, not evidence that the proposed association exists.

The hypothesis is not supported as a demonstrated effect. The available evidence does not establish a causal mechanism, a validated deterioration endpoint, or that the trajectory is more informative than baseline severity and operative context.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

If supported, the result could distinguish an early-warning recovery pattern from the simpler explanation that a single late laboratory value or current severity is sufficient. It could motivate a bounded prospective validation of post-hepatectomy monitoring. If adverse, it would argue against treating this laboratory trajectory as a distinct signal and redirect work toward validated endpoint adjudication or a different post-operative question. If inconclusive, it would identify the measurement and timing dependencies that must be resolved before clinical interpretation.

## Concrete data binding and planned test

Use the read-only HCC sources listed in `datasets/hcc/README.md`, with the exact source paths and hashes recorded there. The planned cohort is adults with a liver-resection procedure in `procedures` (`HCC/data_手术_8024330590283626027.csv`), joined by the documented patient/encounter keys to `encounters` (`HCC/data_基本信息_2500296761891079109.csv`) and longitudinal `labs` (`HCC/data_检验_609065997844652188.csv`). The procedure table and lab table schemas must be checked for exact key and timestamp columns before execution; do not assume that a matching identifier is valid across incompatible namespaces. Use pre-operative and early post-operative bilirubin, INR, ALT, and albumin only if their assay labels and timing can be frozen from the available fields.

The temporal boundary is the procedure start (or a prespecified alternative if start is missing, with missingness reported) through 30 days afterward. The outcome must be frozen before fitting: a clinically interpretable post-operative deterioration signal constructed from available encounters/clinical documents/procedures, with a prespecified adjudication rule. Because the source lacks validated diagnosis extraction, images, waveforms, complete front-page/transfer content, and separate laboratory units, this outcome and any clinical interpretation remain provisional.

The baseline comparison will be a simple prespecified model using baseline severity, operative context, and a single early post-operative laboratory value. The substantive alternative will fit a trajectory model using repeated laboratory values over the early post-operative window, with patient-level temporal split and uncertainty estimates. The alternative could reveal whether the shape and persistence of change carry information lost by a single value; it cannot by itself establish mechanism or causality. A held-out or temporally separated evaluation is required, with supportive, adverse, and inconclusive criteria defined before inspection of results.

## Missing evidence and limitations

- Laboratory units are not separately available, so cross-assay comparability and biological interpretation are limited.
- Procedure start times are incomplete; the exact cohort and temporal alignment require validation.
- The deterioration endpoint is not yet clinically adjudicated, and the source lacks validated diagnosis extraction, images, waveforms, and complete front-page/transfer content.
- The three required inspected key-reference works and frozen source receipts are not yet attached; this draft is therefore not selection-ready.
- No causal, mechanistic, or treatment recommendation is claimed.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence would require a prespecified trajectory signal to improve held-out/temporal evaluation over the baseline while satisfying uncertainty and calibration checks and remaining robust to missingness and timing sensitivity. Adverse evidence would be no reproducible incremental signal, deterioration concentrated in missingness or selection artifacts, or failure under prespecified sensitivity analyses. Inconclusive evidence would include insufficient assay/timing coverage, unstable endpoint adjudication, or wide uncertainty; it would justify repair or deferral rather than a clinical conclusion.

## Method comparison and actual deliverable

The actual scientific deliverable is a fitted comparison of the baseline and trajectory models on the same frozen cohort and outcome, with estimated incremental predictive information, uncertainty, calibration, and sensitivity analyses. A CPU tabular fit is a reasonable initial choice; GPU use is not required for this bounded question. The learned trajectory alternative is retained because it tests whether repeated measurement shape adds clinically meaningful information, not merely because it is more complex. If the missing assay units or endpoint adjudication prevent a defensible comparison, the correct outcome is an explicit repair/defer decision, not a substituted easier claim.

## Demonstration dispositions

The three expert demonstrations are not being reproduced. The natural-history transformer paper motivates longitudinal modeling but uses unavailable modalities and a different question; the cancer longitudinal-data demonstration is not a reproduction because the main article is unavailable and its full methods are not inspected; the Bayesian longitudinal EHR/genetic framework is not directly applicable because the HCC snapshot lacks the required genetic and validated longitudinal evidence structure. A bounded HCC adaptation is scientifically possible only for the explicitly available laboratory/encounter question above.
