# Working proposal: discordant kidney recovery after ICU AKI

Status: Lead design for episode 2 repair; proposed Harbor experiment, not executed results.

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly construct an index-ICU-stay cohort and fit two prespecified analyses on the same eligible population:

1. A transparent, standardized logistic-risk analysis estimating the 7-day risk difference between discordant and concordant recovery, with uncertainty and sensitivity analyses.
2. A substantive temporal alternative that fits a patient-level-split masked sequence model on the same pre-landmark data, then reports held-out calibration, Brier/AUROC, and the same discordant-versus-concordant risk contrast.

Completion requires a cohort flow and cell counts, phenotype definitions, adjusted risk difference with confidence interval, secondary outcome estimates, sensitivity/negative-control results, and model-comparison outputs linked to saved computed files. A readiness probe or a favorable-looking coefficient is not completion.

## Unresolved clinical question and hypothesis

After an ICU patient develops creatinine-defined AKI, clinicians may infer recovery when serum creatinine falls. The unresolved question is whether that inference is unsafe when urine output and recorded fluid balance do not improve at the same time.

Hypothesis: among adults with a first eligible ICU stay who develop creatinine-defined AKI, survive and remain in the ICU to a fixed 48-hour post-onset landmark, and show a prespecified creatinine fall, patients with low primary-set urine output or positive recorded-net-volume proxy at that landmark have a higher 7-day risk of hospital death or ICU-recorded incident kidney-replacement therapy than patients with both urine recovery and non-positive fluid balance. The primary estimand is the standardized prognostic risk difference, discordant minus concordant recovery.

This tests a falsifiable association. It does not claim that discordance causes death, that positive balance proves hemodilution or congestion, or that the patient has measured persistent renal dysfunction. The clinically consequential decision it could inform is whether a falling creatinine alone should be sufficient for de-escalating renal monitoring or transition planning after AKI. The result would be hypothesis-generating for a monitoring/intervention study, not a bedside rule.

## What is supported versus unresolved

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded propositions. Creatinine can overestimate measured kidney function after prolonged critical illness, in association with muscle loss [K1]. Urine output thresholds and their time windows materially alter prognostic discrimination when combined with creatinine, including in MIMIC-IV, but that work studies AKI staging rather than recovery discordance [K2]. Positive fluid balance around ICU AKI is associated with AKI and mortality in a prospective external cohort, although it is confounded by illness and treatment severity [K3].

The unresolved claim is narrower and new for this design: within a fixed post-AKI survivor landmark, does a creatinine fall accompanied by low charted urine output or positive charted net balance identify a reproducibly higher-risk recovery phenotype than creatinine plus physiologic recovery? Existing evidence does not establish that such a phenotype is present in this MIMIC snapshot, that its mechanism is dilution, muscle loss, congestion or tubular injury, or that changing treatment improves outcomes. [K1] is small and abstract-only here; [K2] does not establish mechanism or a treatment threshold; [K3] does not study this recovery comparison.

## Exposure phenotypes

Find the peak valid creatinine in [t0,tL] and define creatinine recovery as peak minus landmark creatinine >=0.3 mg/dL. A ratio sensitivity is landmark/peak <=0.8. Neither means normal kidney function.

Define urine recovery as >=0.5 mL/kg/h during [tL-24h,tL]. Among patients with creatinine recovery and complete primary physiology data:

- concordant recovery: creatinine recovery + urine recovery + net balance <=0;
- discordant creatinine-only recovery: creatinine recovery + low urine output or positive net balance;
- exploratory three-level phenotype: low urine only, positive balance only, both.

Patients without creatinine recovery are a descriptive reference group, not part of the primary discordant-versus-concordant contrast. Report all exclusions and missingness before outcome comparisons.

## Outcomes and analysis

Primary outcome Y7d is hospital death or first ICU-recorded incident RRT in [tL,tL+7d), using admissions.deathtime/hospital_expire_flag and procedureevents RRT items; do not count RRT before tL. Do not truncate hospital follow-up at ICU outtime; alive hospital discharge is censoring/competing event. Because procedureevents does not cover ward-only RRT, label the component ICU-recorded RRT and report a no-pre-tL-RRT sensitivity. Secondary outcomes are death alone, ICU-recorded RRT alone, ICU readmission during the same admission, and hospital death by dischtime. Report component-specific cumulative incidence and handle discharge/competing events descriptively or with cause-specific models; the composite is primary to avoid selecting a favorable competing-risk convention.

Primary estimand:
RD = P(Y7d=1 | discordant) - P(Y7d=1 | concordant)
among eligible creatinine-recovery patients. Fit a prespecified logistic model with restricted cubic splines or carefully bounded linear terms for age, baseline creatinine, AKI stage at t0, peak creatinine, recorded CVP, observation density and recorded-volume proxy, plus sex, admission type, and pre-landmark treatment/support summaries and missingness indicators. All adjustment is pre-tL; do not adjust for post-tL mediators or outcome proxies. Standardize predictions over the observed eligible population to obtain RD and percentile/bootstrap confidence intervals. Do not interpret adjustment as causal identification. Sensitivity analyses vary creatinine item (50912 versus 50912+52546), baseline window, urine item set, weight age, urine threshold (including K2-informed lower thresholds), balance scale, exclusion of early dialysis, and complete-case versus explicit missingness handling.

Falsification and integrity checks are prespecified:

- The direction and clinically relevant magnitude must persist across assay, urine-source, baseline, weight, recorded-volume, discharge/competing-event and missingness sensitivities to support a reproducible phenotype.
- A null with a confidence interval spanning clinically meaningful harm is inconclusive, not proof of no misclassification.
- If the association disappears after observation-density adjustment or is driven by missing weights/recording intensity, the leading clinical interpretation is not supported.
- A model using post-tL measurements, outcome proxies, or cross-patient leakage fails.
- A positive adjusted association does not establish hemodilution, congestion, intrinsic tubular injury, treatment effect or mechanism.

## Baseline versus substantive learned alternative

The simple baseline is the primary scientific analysis: fixed tL summaries of creatinine peak/slope, urine rate, net balance, weight, CVP, support/RRT, observation counts and missingness, fit with standardized logistic risk. It preserves interpretability and directly estimates the phenotype contrast.

The learned alternative is a masked temporal model over 6-hour bins from t0-24h through tL. Each bin includes creatinine, urine volume/rate, input/output volume, weight, CVP, RRT/support indicators and missingness/measurement-count channels. It targets the same Y7d outcome and uses patient-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits; all stays from a subject remain in one split. Compare calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, AUROC and held-out standardized phenotype contrast, with bootstrap uncertainty. A sequence can reveal whether the order and joint trajectory of creatinine, urine and balance contain information lost by the fixed-window baseline. It cannot identify mechanism.

Selection/deferral: retain the baseline as primary even if the learned model predicts better. Fit the learned model only if both phenotype cells have adequate event support and repeated measurements are sufficient; otherwise defer it. Revisit the learned branch only if it improves calibration or reveals a robust trajectory interaction replicated in held-out data, not for a small AUROC gain. GPU use is optional. A CPU tabular/sequence fit is expected to be feasible; the larger learned run is an unverified estimate of 4-16 CPU cores, 16-64 GB RAM, and 1-6 hours, or one allocated A100 for 1-3 hours if sequence batching is the bottleneck. These are planning estimates, not measured episode results. The solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds; discovery itself used no GPU and did not train a model.

## Interpretation

Supportive result: a stable, clinically nontrivial excess 7-day risk in discordant recovery, with adequate cell support and calibration, would justify prospective validation of enhanced renal monitoring and an adjudicated study with measured GFR/biomarkers. It would still be a prognostic association.

Adverse result: no excess risk, reversal, or disappearance after adjusting observation intensity would weaken the claim that creatinine-only recovery identifies a clinically important discordant state and would redirect work toward other recovery measures. It would not prove creatinine is valid or that fluid/urine management is harmless.

Inconclusive result: sparse cells, wide intervals, unstable thresholds, or substantial unmeasured missingness would mean the available MIMIC data cannot resolve the question. It would justify a measurement study, not a favorable conclusion.

The configured data cannot adjudicate true measured GFR, cystatin-C discordance, tubular injury, true total-body volume, medication-versus-fluid intent, ward-only RRT, clinician intent, goals of care, complete urine collection, reliable pre-ICU baseline kidney function, or causal treatment effects. Those require additional biomarker/physiology data, chart adjudication, prospective monitoring, or external validation.

## Changed scientific claim and deliverable

The parent’s 48-hour RD remains the primary observed-survivor estimand. This child adds a prespecified test of whether it is an artifact of conditioning on survival, ICU presence and observation through tL, or residual acute severity. It does not claim a causal principal stratum: counterfactual recovery and outcomes among patients who die/leave ICU before tL are unavailable. “Principal-stratum sensitivity” here means observed-landmark restriction plus eligibility weighting, explicitly interpreted as transported prognostic association.

## Eligibility weighting and residual severity

At 48 hours define S48=1 only if alive, in the index ICU, latest creatinine is present, and primary phenotype inputs are complete. Starting from every t0-eligible AKI patient, fit a prespecified logistic model for S48 using only data at or before t0: age, sex, admission type, elapsed ICU time, onset/baseline creatinine, AKI stage, pre-t0 creatinine slope/count, pre-t0 lab/chart density and missingness, pre-t0 primary urine/input availability, pre-t0 CVP/weight availability, and pre-t0 ICU-RRT status. Use stabilized inverse-probability-of-eligibility weights, trim the 1st/99th percentiles, and report overlap, maximum weight, coefficient of variation and ESS. The weighted RD targets the t0 cohort’s measured covariate distribution among observed eligible survivors; it cannot recover the dead, discharged or unobserved counterfactual phenotype. If ESS <200 or <20% of the unweighted eligible sample, call it inconclusive/non-identifying.

Fit transparent logistic models in nested sets: (0) phenotype plus age, sex, admission type, baseline creatinine, t0 AKI stage and pre-t0 observation density; (1) add non-phenotype pre-tL residual-severity summaries (pre-t0 creatinine trajectory/count, ICU-RRT before tL, CVP/weight availability, input/output recording density and volume availability); (2) add missingness and storetime-charttime ordering indicators. Do not silently condition on post-tL data, and do not adjust peak creatinine, urine or balance as if they were confounders: they define D. Set 1 is a severity/selection sensitivity, not causal adjustment.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Integrity failure occurs for post-landmark exposure rows, cross-patient leakage, duplicate input/totalamount, incompatible joins or unreported excluded urine/non-mL fractions. Supportive evidence requires a positive 48-hour RD, the same direction at at least one boundary landmark, an adequately overlapping weighted estimate with the same direction, and Set 1/2 retaining at least half the primary point estimate without sign reversal. A sign reversal, >=50% attenuation toward zero with adequate precision after residual-severity/observation adjustment, or a signal confined to 48 hours while both boundary landmarks and weighting are near null favors landmark selection/recording/severity explanations. Sparse cells, extreme weights, wide intervals spanning zero and clinically meaningful harm are inconclusive, not refutation. No result establishes dilution, congestion, muscle loss, tubular injury, measured-GFR discordance, treatment effect or causality.

The transparent standardized logistic analysis is primary because the scientific uncertainty is estimand stability and residual severity; it exposes selection, overlap and attenuation. The learned alternative uses six-hour bins from t0-24h through tL on the same 48-hour eligible population, with creatinine, urine, input/output, weight, CVP, RRT/support and measurement/missingness channels; patient-level 70/15/15 splits keep each subject in one split. It can reveal temporal ordering information lost by fixed summaries, but cannot resolve survivor selection or mechanism. Fit only with adequate repeated measurements and event support; defer if not.
