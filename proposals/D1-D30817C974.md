# Hypothesis Proposal: Pre-TACE Serum Biomarkers Predict TACE Response

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question:** Do pre-TACE serum biomarkers beyond standard Child-Pugh components (albumin, bilirubin, INR, ascites, encephalopathy) predict TACE treatment response and progression-free survival?

**Secondary Question:** Can a multivariate biomarker panel improve prediction of TACE outcomes beyond baseline Child-Pugh score alone?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Prognostic Refinement**: Current prognostic models rely heavily on Child-Pugh score, which captures only 5 components. Additional biomarkers might provide more nuanced risk stratification.

2. **Treatment Selection**: Identifying patients with unfavorable biomarker profiles might benefit from alternative therapies (systemic therapy, transplant evaluation) rather than TACE.

3. **Early Warning System**: Biomarkers that predict treatment response could help clinicians anticipate complications and optimize monitoring.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Current literature focuses on:
- **Child-Pugh score** as the primary prognostic tool for HCC treatment
- **Tumor characteristics** (size, number) as prognostic factors
- **Treatment timing** relative to diagnosis

This proposal addresses:
- **Additional serum biomarkers** beyond Child-Pugh components
- **Multivariate biomarker panels** for improved prediction
- **Biomarker trajectories** over time

The key insight: **Beyond albumin and bilirubin, other serum biomarkers might independently predict TACE outcomes.**

## Data Requirements and Bindings

### Primary Dataset: HCC Clinical Registry

**Source Files:**
- `HCC/data_基本信息_2500296761891079109.csv` - Basic patient demographics (patient_id, encounter_id, age, sex)
- `HCC/data_诊断_7504718184492840569.csv` - HCC diagnoses with timing (patient_id, encounter_id, diagnosis_name, diagnosis_type, diagnosis_date)
- `HCC/data_手术_8024330590283626027.csv` - TACE procedures (patient_id, encounter_id, 手术，开始时间，结束时间，手术来源)
- `HCC/data_检验_609065997844652188.csv` - Laboratory results (patient_id, encounter_id, 检查，定性结果，定量结果，目标类型，检查时间)
- `HCC/data_出入转_7369490831683459252.csv` - Admission/discharge/transfer events (patient_id, encounter_id)
- `HCC/data_病历文_8434587325530196878.csv` - Clinical documents (patient_id, encounter_id)

**Key Variables:**

1. **Primary Exposure**: Pre-TACE serum biomarkers beyond Child-Pugh
   - **Additional biomarkers**: Cystatin C, creatinine, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), CA19-9, D-dimer, etc. (from lab results)
   - **Biomarker panel**: Composite score of multiple biomarkers
   - **Individual biomarkers**: Each biomarker analyzed separately

2. **Outcome**: TACE Treatment Response
   - **Primary**: Progression-free survival (PFS) - time to tumor progression or death
   - **Secondary**: Decompensation within 30 days post-TACE
   - **Tumor response**: Reduction in tumor size/volume

3. **Covariates:**
   - Tumor characteristics: Number of tumors, largest tumor size
   - Baseline Child-Pugh score
   - Demographics: Age, sex
   - Concomitant therapy: Sorafenib, lenvatinib, immunotherapy

4. **Time Fields**: All timestamps in format `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`

### Population Definition

- **Inclusion**:
  - Patients with confirmed HCC diagnosis
  - At least one TACE procedure performed
  - Pre-TACE laboratory values available (including additional biomarkers)
  - Post-TACE follow-up data available

- **Exclusion**:
  - Pre-existing liver decompensation before TACE
  - Multiple TACE procedures (focus on first-line)
  - Missing critical laboratory values

- **Temporal Boundaries**:
  - Study period: Based on dataset range (likely 2000-2015)
  - Biomarker window: Pre-TACE laboratory values
  - Follow-up: Until tumor progression, death, or 12 months post-TACE

## Analysis Plan

### Baseline Approach
**Logistic regression / Cox proportional hazards** using Child-Pugh score:
```
outcome ~ child_pugh_score + tumor_burden + age + sex
```

### Learned Alternative
**Multivariate biomarker panel**:
- **Approach**: Fit a multivariate model with additional biomarkers
- **Features**: Individual biomarkers and their interactions
- **Model**: Logistic regression or machine learning model
- **Advantages**: Captures additional predictive information beyond Child-Pugh

### Key Comparisons
1. Child-Pugh score vs. biomarker panel for prediction accuracy
2. Individual biomarkers beyond albumin/bilirubin
3. Model performance comparison (C-index, AUC, calibration)
4. Net reclassification index: How many patients would be reclassified as high/low risk using biomarkers vs. Child-Pugh

## Falsification Criteria

**Hypothesis will be falsified if:**
- No association between additional biomarkers and outcome
- Child-Pugh score remains the dominant predictor
- Biomarker panel shows no improvement in prediction accuracy over Child-Pugh alone

**Supportive Evidence:**
- At least one additional biomarker significantly predicts outcome (p < 0.05)
- Biomarker panel shows improved discrimination (ΔC-index > 0.05) over Child-Pugh
- Specific biomarker thresholds identify high-risk patients

**Inconclusive:**
- Weak association with wide confidence intervals
- Effect modified by subgroup

## Missing Evidence and Clinical Adjudication

1. **Biomarker Availability**: Need to verify which additional biomarkers are available in the lab results.

2. **Outcome Definition**: PFS requires tumor progression assessment; need to verify how progression is defined.

3. **Biomarker Standardization**: Need to verify that biomarker measurements are standardized across encounters.

4. **External Validation**: This analysis is internal; external validation would require another HCC cohort.

## Expected Impact

If supported, this hypothesis could:
- **Improve prognostic accuracy** beyond Child-Pugh score
- **Guide treatment selection** by incorporating additional biomarkers
- **Enable personalized monitoring** for at-risk patients

## Method Comparison Rationale

**Baseline (Child-Pugh score)**: Provides interpretable prognostic tool, widely used in guidelines, captures 5 key components of liver function.

**Learned Alternative (Multivariate biomarker panel)**: Can capture additional predictive information beyond Child-Pugh, might reveal novel biomarkers, provides more nuanced risk stratification.

**Scientific Information Gained**: The learned model may reveal that additional serum biomarkers independently predict TACE outcomes, which would change clinical guidance from "Child-Pugh score" to "Child-Pugh + biomarker panel."
