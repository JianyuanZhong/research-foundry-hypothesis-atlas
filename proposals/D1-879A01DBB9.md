# Durable recovery after support cessation at ICU discharge: a child repair

## Reviewer diagnosis and substantive advance

The parent asks an important question but has one major scientific weakness: it collapses “support is still active” and “support was recently withdrawn” into one normalized-on/recent-withdrawal stratum, while treating intubation/NIV `procedureevents` intervals as if they were continuous ventilation episodes. Those are different clinical states and different data-quality problems. The pooled contrast can therefore be clinically uninterpretable: a signal may reflect active treatment, a fragile post-liberation period, or documentation/disposition practice. This is also partly redundant with the parent’s broad support-context prediction.

This child tests a narrower, more decision-relevant claim: among patients whose final six-hour physiology appears acceptable, does the amount and type of recovery buffer before ICU outtime distinguish durable recovery from a recently completed support transition? The substantive advance is an estimand about transition timing and persistence, not merely another risk model. It preserves the parent’s prognostic boundary: no retrospective MIMIC analysis can show that delaying discharge, continuing a vasopressor, or prolonging ventilation would improve outcomes.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly:

1. construct and audit the leakage-safe first-ICU-discharge cohort;
2. construct event-time and interval-overlap support-transition variables, retaining separate respiratory and vasoactive channels;
3. estimate 48-hour competing cumulative incidence for first ICU return and first death for each prespecified transition state;
4. fit and compare a transparent cause-specific baseline with a duration-aware hidden semi-Markov recovery model;
5. produce held-out predictions, calibration and uncertainty, coverage/positivity diagnostics, sensitivity/falsification results, and a conclusion-to-output map.

Completion means a frozen cohort/exclusion table, exposure coverage and event-count table, locked train/validation/test predictions, Aalen–Johansen estimates with confidence intervals, cause-specific association/calibration tables, state-duration/transition diagnostics, all prespecified falsification results, and an explicit list of claims that remain clinically unadjudicated. No computed result is asserted here.

## Unresolved question and hypotheses

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only feasibility: MIMIC-IV records ICU outtimes, charted physiology, vasoactive infusion intervals, procedure timestamps, admission death times and later ICU stays. The inherited parent counts (including 70,138 eligible landmarks, 3,872 48-hour returns and 856 exact-timestamp deaths) are feasibility facts from a prior analysis, not evidence for this child’s exposure definition or association. Fresh counts, support coverage, event support, positivity, convergence and effect estimates are required.

Primary hypothesis:

> At a valid first ICU-outtime landmark with low final-window respiratory burden and no vasoactive infusion overlapping the final six hours, patients with a recent extubation marker or recent vasoactive-infusion cessation have higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU return than patients with the same final-window burden and a sustained support-free buffer.

The two transition states are separate primary contrasts, not pooled. A secondary hypothesis is that recovery persistence and dwell time in a support-dependent/unstable state improve competing-event calibration beyond fixed last-window summaries.

The direction for death is not assumed. Death is a competing outcome, not folded into return as though it represented the same failure process.

## Population, landmark and temporal estimand

Use adults with `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`, retaining and reporting the >89 top-code limitation. Within each `subject_id`, select the first ICU stay in that hospitalization by `icu/icustays.intime`. Require a linked admission, non-null `intime/outtime`, `outtime > intime`, and `hosp/admissions.dischtime > outtime`. Exclude impossible keys/times and report every exclusion. Split by `subject_id`, never by row or stay.

The index is `icu/icustays.outtime`. All predictors must be available strictly before outtime. Physiology uses `icu/chartevents.charttime` in `[outtime-24 hours, outtime)), resampled into one-hour bins; no value at or after outtime is permitted. Support-transition windows are `[outtime-12h,outtime)` and the more specific recent-transition window `[outtime-12h,outtime-2h)`. The two-hour buffer prevents an event timestamp immediately adjacent to outtime from being treated as meaningful recovery time; this is a prespecified design choice and is varied only in sensitivity analysis.

Follow until `min(outtime+48h, admissions.dischtime)). The first event is first later ICU entry for the same `subject_id,hadm_id` with `icustays.intime > outtime` within the horizon, or death when non-null `admissions.deathtime > outtime` within the horizon and before return. Tied times classify death first. Otherwise censor at the administrative boundary. Call the endpoint “ICU return,” not unplanned readmission: intent is unavailable. Do not infer event times from `hospital_expire_flag`. Retain seven-day ICU return and the parent composite as secondary sensitivity outcomes.

Primary estimands are the two 48-hour cumulative-incidence differences (recent extubation versus durable support-free; recent vasoactive cessation versus durable support-free), standardized over the prespecified adjustment set. Cause-specific hazard ratios and absolute risk differences are secondary associations. The support-transition variables are context markers, not assigned treatments.

## Exposure construction

Resample each chart stream to hourly medians and retain observation masks. Never equate missingness with normality.

Respiratory burden is a proxy: in each hour, FiO2 >= 0.40 or O2 flow >= 4 L/min, with PEEP retained separately when charted. Hemodynamic burden is MAP <65 mmHg, preserving arterial and non-invasive sources. For each domain require at least two observed bins in each six-hour half-window for the primary phenotype; otherwise classify coverage as unavailable and report it.

A vasoactive interval is active in a bin only if valid `starttime < bin_end` and `endtime > bin_start`; null or reversed endpoints are not silently active. Define the last valid vasoactive cessation as the latest qualifying endtime before outtime. The primary recent-cessation marker is a cessation in `[outtime-12h,outtime-2h)`; a pressor active in the final six hours is not in the primary contrast and is separately reported.

An extubation marker is the latest qualifying 227194 `starttime` in `[outtime-12h,outtime-2h)`. A patient with such a marker is “recent extubation-marker” only if the final six-hour respiratory burden is below one-third of observed bins and no vasoactive interval overlaps the final six hours. This does not claim ventilator-free status.

The primary mutually exclusive states are:

1. **Durable support-free proxy:** final-six-hour respiratory burden <1/3; no vasoactive overlap in the final 12 hours; no extubation marker in the preceding 12 hours; adequate final-window coverage.
2. **Recent extubation-marker recovery:** same low final respiratory burden and adequate coverage; extubation marker in ([outtime-12h,outtime-2h)); no vasoactive overlap in the final six hours.
3. **Recent vasoactive cessation:** same low final respiratory burden and adequate coverage; valid vasoactive cessation in ([outtime-12h,outtime-2h)); no extubation marker in the preceding 12 hours; no vasoactive overlap in the final six hours.
4. **Residual or ambiguous:** final burden >=1/3, support active at the landmark, overlapping/discordant transitions, invalid or inadequate coverage, or other unclassifiable status.

Groups 2 and 3 are each compared with group 1. Group 4 is described and used in prespecified secondary analyses, not discarded to manufacture clean separation. The exact cutoffs, windows, item sets and source-specific rules are locked before outcome inspection. Report hours since the relevant event, number of observed bins, MAP/respiratory source, and procedure/infusion identity.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative

### Simple baseline

Fit two cause-specific Cox models, one for first ICU return and one for first death, with the three primary states and a small prespecified adjustment set: age, gender, ICU length of stay, first/last careunit, admission_type, final and early-to-late MAP/respiratory burden, observation counts/masks, and a limited admission covariate set. A nested snapshot baseline removes early-to-late trajectory and transition-time features, retaining final physiology and coverage. Estimate Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence, absolute risk differences, cause-specific associations, calibration at 48 hours, IPCW Brier score, bootstrap confidence intervals and subject-level uncertainty. Check non-positivity before standardization.

The baseline is intentionally auditable and answers whether transition labels add value beyond the final snapshot. It loses the duration and ordering of intermediate recovery: two patients with identical final values and the same binary transition state are treated alike even if one had hours of stable recovery and the other oscillated between support-dependent and unstable states.

### Substantive alternative: duration-aware hidden semi-Markov recovery model

Fit only on the training split a constrained hidden semi-Markov competing-risk model with hourly observations from ([outtime-24h,outtime)). Emissions include MAP (arterial and non-invasive separately), FiO2, O2 flow, PEEP, HR, RR, creatinine/hemoglobin when available, observation masks, vasoactive-overlap indicators and the extubation event marker. Use interpretable recovery states such as stable support-free, post-extubation recovery, post-vasoactive recovery, residual respiratory, residual hemodynamic/dual burden, and observation-limited. State-specific emission distributions, transition probabilities and dwell-time distributions are fitted on the training data; state-specific discrete hazards for return and death cover eight six-hour post-index bins. Posterior state probabilities, not hard assignments, generate held-out risks.

This alternative reveals recovery velocity, persistence and dwell-time differences that the fixed-feature baseline loses, and explicitly tests whether the clinical meaning of “recently withdrawn” depends on the duration of stability afterward. The model is prognostic state representation, not a bedside diagnosis or a causal model. To prevent outcome leakage, all state definitions, normalization and hyperparameters are locked from training data; use patient-level stratified train/validation/test splits and three split seeds.

Evaluate held-out IPCW cause-specific and integrated Brier scores, 48-hour cumulative-incidence calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, discrimination only as secondary, posterior-state separation, and bootstrap/subject-level uncertainty. The primary scientific comparison is calibrated competing-risk estimation and interpretable recovery persistence, not a small predictive score gain. A state model is selected over a masked GRU/temporal convolution as the principal alternative because the new uncertainty is duration and recovery persistence, and a semi-Markov model makes that information auditable. Revisit a masked sequence model only if the duration-aware model fails convergence or loses clearly relevant order in prespecified ablations.

Compute estimate for the future solver: streaming extraction and feature tables, 2–4 CPU-hours; repeated Cox/Aalen–Johansen and bootstrap fits, 1–3 CPU-hours; semi-Markov fitting and three split seeds, 2–6 CPU-hours, all on up to 16 CPUs and <64 GB RAM. These are unverified planning estimates, not measured results; no GPU is required. The 8-hour/16-CPU/256-GB solver planning envelope is sufficient in principle, subject to actual archive I/O and convergence. Discovery feasibility checks do not constitute a solved study.

## Falsification, uncertainty and interpretation

Prespecified falsification checks:

- permute the order of hourly physiology while preserving marginal values and masks;
- remove all support-transition channels, then remove all physiology channels;
- compare event-time extubation coding with the invalid alternative of using `storetime`;
- compare interval overlap with a last-observation coding;
- separate arterial versus non-invasive MAP and FiO2 versus O2-flow definitions;
- vary the two-hour buffer and 6/12-hour windows;
- stratify by first/last careunit and admission type;
- run a negative-control pre-index horizon using an outcome window before the index, where the purported post-transition cannot cause the event;
- repeat after excluding explicit cancellation/discontinuation procedure rows and after excluding invalid interval endpoints;
- test whether transition labels merely reproduce observation intensity by reporting mask-only performance and adding care-context controls.

Use bootstrap resampling by subject, not row. Report confidence/credible intervals, event counts, coverage, positivity, calibration uncertainty, and sensitivity to missing/invalid endpoint handling. Do not declare support from a nominal p-value alone.

Supportive results require all of the following: adequate and reproducible state coverage; clinically meaningful, directionally consistent excess return incidence for each relevant transition contrast versus durable support-free, with uncertainty excluding the prespecified null/clinical margin; and held-out calibrated improvement or interpretable duration signal over the snapshot baseline that persists after mask, storetime, care-context and source-specific falsifications. This supports a prognostic recovery-buffer hypothesis and justifies clinician adjudication or prospective validation. It does not support a rule to delay discharge or continue support.

Adverse results include null or reversed transition contrasts, no duration signal, poor calibration, non-positivity, state instability, or disappearance after documentation/order controls. These findings weaken or falsify the operationalized recovery-buffer claim and argue against using these retrospective markers for discharge decisions.

Inconclusive results include rare recent-transition strata, inadequate procedure/infusion coverage, unreliable endpoint status, wide intervals crossing the clinical margin, failed convergence, unstable competing-risk estimates, or event timing too coarse to distinguish transitions. Report these as evidence limits, not as negative clinical findings.

MIMIC cannot adjudicate actual ventilator/NIV duration between chart events, work of breathing, clinician intent, planned versus unplanned ICU return, goals of care, treatment limitations, discharge rationale, ward staffing, or whether changing the transition-to-discharge interval changes outcomes. It lacks raw waveforms and a validated ICU discharge-readiness label. External validation, clinician review of support transitions, and a prospective or causal study are required before clinical adoption.

## Alternatives not chosen and revisit criteria

- The parent’s pooled “normalized on support or recent withdrawal” state is retained as a sensitivity analysis, but not the primary question, because it conflates the two transition states.
- A masked GRU/temporal-convolution sequence model is deferred: it could reveal nonlinear order and long-range interactions, but the duration/persistence uncertainty is more directly testable and interpretable with a semi-Markov model. Revisit if semi-Markov convergence fails or order-preserving ablation shows unexplained information loss.
- Notes are deferred because available discharge text is not a validated ICU-outtime decision label and risks post-index leakage.
- Continuous ventilation reconstruction is deferred because procedureevent rows do not establish continuous duration; a future study with validated ventilator settings/raw waveforms should revisit this dependency.
- A treatment-effect or discharge-delay analysis is not attempted: clinician intent, treatment limitations, discharge rationale and time-varying confounding are unavailable.
