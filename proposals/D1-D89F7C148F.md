# Scientific Proposal: Temporal Discordance Patterns and Recovery Phases Predict HCC Decompensation

## Unresolved Question

**Clinical Problem:** In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and decompensated cirrhosis undergoing liver transplantation evaluation, current risk stratification relies on single-time-point MELD scores and static laboratory values. However, patients with the same baseline MELD score exhibit vastly different trajectories of clinical deterioration and transplant progression.

**Key Uncertainty:** Do *temporal patterns of discordance* in organ function recovery (not just overall discordance) predict which patients will require urgent transplant versus those who can wait longer? Specifically:
1. Does discordance in the **early recovery phase** (days 0-14) predict worse outcomes than discordance in the **late phase** (days 15-30)?
2. Does the **pattern of discordance evolution** (e.g., concordant early → discordant late) predict worse outcomes than static discordance?
3. Are there **critical temporal windows** where intervention based on discordance patterns would be most beneficial?

Existing models ([prior hypothesis] and [prior hypothesis]) test whether discordance predicts outcomes but do not address *when* discordance matters most or how discordance patterns evolve over time.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Early Warning System:** Identifying patients at risk of early deterioration (days 0-14) could trigger urgent interventions before catastrophic events occur.

2. **Treatment Stratification:** Different discordance patterns might respond differently to treatments:
   - Early discordance: May require aggressive medical management (vasopressors, diuretics)
   - Late discordance: May indicate treatment resistance and need for transplant evaluation

3. **Resource Allocation:** Understanding temporal patterns could optimize transplant allocation timing and reduce waitlist mortality.

4. **Critical Window Identification:** Pinpointing when discordance patterns emerge could guide intervention timing.

## Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis:** In HCC patients with decompensated cirrhosis, the *temporal pattern of organ function discordance* (not just overall discordance) independently predicts time to decompensation and mortality above and beyond static MELD score and static discordance metrics.

**Specific Predictions:**
1. **Early Discordance Hypothesis:** Discordance in the early recovery phase (days 0-14) predicts worse outcomes more strongly than discordance in the late phase (days 15-30).
2. **Evolution Hypothesis:** Patients whose discordance patterns evolve (concordant early → discordant late) have worse outcomes than those with stable discordance patterns.
3. **Critical Window Hypothesis:** There exists a specific temporal window (e.g., days 7-14) where discordance patterns are most predictive of outcomes.

**Formal Definitions:**
- **Early phase:** Days 0-14 post-cirrhosis diagnosis
- **Late phase:** Days 15-30 post-cirrhosis diagnosis
- **Discordance score:** |slope_INR - slope_creatinine| + |slope_albumin - slope_creatinine|
- **Concordant:** Both liver and renal slopes have same sign (both improving or both deteriorating)
- **Discordant:** Liver and renal slopes have opposite signs
- **Discordance evolution:** Pattern of concordance/discordance across time phases

## Population and Temporal Boundaries

- **Population:** Adults with diagnosis of cirrhosis in the HCC cohort with HCC
- **Temporal Window:** Index date (cirrhosis diagnosis) to 90 days follow-up
- **Inclusion:**
  - Patients with ≥3 laboratory measurements within 30 days of index date
  - Patients with INR, albumin, and creatinine available
  - No prior liver transplant before index
- **Exclusion:**
  - Patients with active malignancy (except HCC)
  - Patients with missing key labs (INR, albumin, bilirubin, creatinine)
  - Patients with incomplete temporal data (<2 timepoints per phase)

### Key Variables

- **Primary outcome:** Time to first decompensation event (ascites, variceal bleeding, encephalopathy, hepatorenal syndrome) or death within 90 days
- **Predictors:**
  - Baseline MELD score (calculated from INR, bilirubin, creatinine at index)
  - Early phase discordance score (days 0-14)
  - Late phase discordance score (days 15-30)
  - Discordance evolution pattern (concordant→discordant, discordant→concordant, stable discordant, stable concordant)
  - Medication intensity score (number of days on vasoactive agents)
- **Covariates:** Age, sex, comorbidities (from diagnoses), hospital characteristics

## Methodology

### Baseline Approach
**Logistic Regression / Cox Proportional Hazards Model:**
- Outcome: Decompensation within 90 days (binary) or time to decompensation
- Predictors: Baseline MELD score, age, sex, comorbidity count, static discordance score
- Rationale: Standard clinical approach that current practice uses

### Substantive Alternatives

**Alternative 1: Temporal Discordance Features**
- **Method:** Create separate discordance scores for early (days 0-14) and late (days 15-30) phases
- **Features:**
  - Early phase discordance score
  - Late phase discordance score
  - Early vs late discordance ratio
- **Rationale:** Tests whether discordance in different time phases has different predictive value

**Alternative 2: Discordance Evolution Patterns**
- **Method:** Classify patients by their discordance pattern across time phases
- **Categories:**
  - Concordant→Concordant (stable concordance)
  - Discordant→Discordant (stable discordance)
  - Concordant→Discordant (evolving to worse)
  - Discordant→Concordant (improving)
- **Features:** Evolution pattern category, number of phase transitions
- **Rationale:** Tests whether patterns of discordance change over time predict outcomes differently than static discordance

**Alternative 3: Machine Learning Model**
- **Method:** Random forest or gradient boosting with temporal discordance features
- **Features:** All temporal discordance features, baseline MELD, demographics
- **Rationale:** Can capture non-linear relationships between temporal discordance and outcomes

### Why the Alternative Adds Scientific Information

The baseline approach treats discordance as a static feature, missing:
1. **Temporal dynamics:** Discordance may be more or less predictive depending on when it occurs
2. **Evolution patterns:** The *change* in discordance pattern may be more informative than the pattern itself
3. **Critical windows:** There may be specific time windows where discordance is most predictive

The temporal discordance approach can distinguish these patterns that baseline models conflate.

### Step 2: Trajectory Extraction
```python
# For each patient
1. Extract INR, albumin, creatinine values with timestamps
2. Split into early phase (days 0-14) and late phase (days 15-30)
3. Fit linear regression for each phase: value ~ time
4. Compute slopes: slope_INR_early, slope_albumin_early, slope_creatinine_early
5. Compute slopes for late phase
6. Compute discordance scores:
   - Early discordance = |slope_INR_early - slope_creatinine_early| + |slope_albumin_early - slope_creatinine_early|
   - Late discordance = |slope_INR_late - slope_creatinine_late| + |slope_albumin_late - slope_creatinine_late|
7. Compute evolution pattern:
   - Early concordant/discordant
   - Late concordant/discordant
   - Evolution category
```

### Step 3: Statistical Analysis
```python

   - Adjust for: age, sex, baseline MELD, comorbidities

3. Test: Discordance evolution (does evolution predict beyond static discordance?)
4. Quartile analysis: HR for highest vs lowest quartile of each feature
```

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. **No temporal effect:** Early and late discordance predict equally well (no critical window)
2. **No evolution effect:** Discordance evolution patterns show no additional value beyond static discordance
3. **Static superiority:** Static MELD or static discordance outperforms temporal discordance models by ≥0.02 in C-index
4. **No clinical actionability:** No clear temporal window where intervention would be most beneficial

The hypothesis would be **supported** if:
1. **Early discordance dominates:** Early phase discordance predicts outcomes more strongly than late phase discordance
2. **Evolution matters:** Discordance evolution patterns predict outcomes beyond static discordance
3. **Temporal improvement:** Temporal discordance models outperform static models by ≥0.02 C-index
4. **Critical window identified:** Specific temporal window (e.g., days 7-14) shows strongest prediction

The hypothesis would be **inconclusive** if:
1. **Weak effects:** Discordance shows weak association with outcomes (HR 1.2-1.5, p > 0.05)
2. **Inconsistent patterns:** Results vary substantially by subgroup
3. **Sample size limits:** Insufficient events for precise temporal analysis
