# Metabolic trajectory discordance and incident cardiometabolic multimorbidity

## Scientific question and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by existing evidence is that baseline obesity, glycaemia, lipids and kidney measures are associated with later cardiometabolic disease, and that UK Biobank studies can estimate these associations prospectively. The local snapshot and the retrieved literature do not establish whether *change* in a metabolic marker, after accounting for its current level and the time-varying measurement process, identifies a clinically distinct transition toward multimorbidity.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among UK Biobank participants free of the relevant disease at the latest valid biomarker assessment, a worsening trajectory in HbA1c and triglycerides, or a falling HDL trajectory, predicts a higher hazard of incident cardiometabolic multimorbidity than the latest levels alone, after adjustment for baseline levels, age, sex, BMI, blood pressure, smoking, medication proxies where available, and assessment-center/measurement availability. The incremental effect will be stronger for the first cardiometabolic event than for a non-cardiometabolic negative-control outcome.

The primary estimand is the adjusted hazard ratio per prespecified within-person SD worsening in a trajectory slope/change score, comparing a model with current levels only against a model adding change/trajectory information. This is an association estimand, not a causal effect of changing a biomarker.

## Why it matters and substantive advance

Clinicians repeatedly decide whether a patient with an apparently modest current HbA1c, triglyceride or HDL value needs intensified prevention and surveillance. If recent deterioration adds reproducible information beyond the current value, the result would support monitoring strategies and a clinically interpretable “trajectory alert”; if it does not, it would argue against adding noisy change scores to risk assessment. The advance is a direct test of information in *within-person change* rather than another cross-sectional association or another black-box risk score.

This is an adaptation of the expert seed “Metabolic deterioration and multimorbidity” ([prior hypothesis]), repaired to use verified local fields and to treat field-instance order and date availability explicitly. It is not a reproduction of any demonstration paper.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is a Cox model with restricted cubic splines for the latest HbA1c, triglycerides, HDL, creatinine, cystatin C, BMI, grip strength, age, sex and prespecified confounders, plus missingness indicators and assessment count. It is fit on the same eligible population, same split, same outcome horizon and same censoring rules.

The substantive learned alternative is a discrete-time longitudinal state-space model: a small recurrent/temporal-attention model receives the ordered biomarker vectors, exact assessment-date gaps, and observation/missingness indicators, and estimates the hazard of each disease domain and the transition to multimorbidity. It must be trained only on the training portion of the participant-level split, early-stopped on a validation portion, and evaluated once on a locked held-out discovery bucket split. A mechanistic alternative may be substituted if the solver cannot justify the learned model: a multistate illness-deepening model with states healthy, one disease domain, two domains and death, with biomarker slopes as time-varying covariates.

The alternative is scientifically useful only if it reveals whether the same observed change has different implications depending on prior metabolic state, measurement spacing, and co-occurring kidney/physical-reserve signals—information the baseline spline model compresses into the latest snapshot. It is not selected to chase a small AUROC increase. Compare hazard-ratio calibration, time-dependent Brier score, integrated calibration error, restricted mean event-free survival, and decision-curve net benefit at prespecified surveillance thresholds, with bootstrap or repeated participant-level resampling confidence intervals. Report calibration by age, sex and number of assessments.

Approximate future solver envelope: 16 CPUs, up to 64 GB RAM, CPU-first for Cox/multistate models; one allocated A100 is optional for the temporal model if a bounded probe shows a material runtime benefit. Discovery did not measure full fitting time. The Delphi report supports feasibility of sequence learning at UKB scale but its author-reported V100-hour is not a runtime guarantee here; the ALADYNOULLI report supports longitudinal latent modeling but its exact hardware is not verified. A future solver must record actual wall time, memory, model parameters, seeds, checkpoints and whether GPU was used.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence: the prespecified change/trajectory term has a clinically nontrivial, directionally consistent association with the primary and relevant secondary outcomes; its confidence interval excludes the null; calibration or decision-curve performance improves on the locked test set without degradation in key subgroups; and the result persists across washout, complete-case/missingness analyses, and competing-death sensitivity analyses.

Adverse evidence: trajectory coefficients are null or reverse direction, the effect disappears after current levels and measurement intensity are included, or the learned model’s apparent gain vanishes under participant-level split and calibration checks. This would falsify the proposed incremental-information claim, not prove that metabolic change is biologically harmless.

Inconclusive evidence: too few participants have two valid dated panels, coding/date correspondence is insufficient, confidence intervals are wide, or outcome ascertainment is dominated by one assessment subgroup. In that case report feasibility and uncertainty only; do not claim clinical utility.

Computationally checkable claims include cohort counts, missingness, date ordering, code extraction, fitted coefficients, uncertainty, calibration, held-out performance and robustness. The study cannot establish that changing HbA1c or lipids causes disease, that a monitoring intervention improves outcomes, or that the coded events are clinically adjudicated. Those claims require validated UKB phenotype algorithms, medication and treatment context, chart review or external validation. Exact assay units, field-specific coding dictionaries, treatment changes, socioeconomic confounding, and the reason for repeat assessment are incompletely available locally and must be stated.

## Alternatives and deferred branches

- A proteomic organ-age or infection-protein hypothesis was deferred because the local catalog does not verify the needed organ-age indicators or proteomic assay labels as a ready-to-use feature set.
- The fat-distribution seed was deferred as the primary question because raw imaging is unavailable; waist/BMI/grip proxies are available but do not identify visceral fat. It remains a useful secondary sensitivity direction.
- The CHIP seed was deferred because `genomics` is documented as phenotype/derived metadata rather than a raw sequence/variant bundle; gene-specific clone size cannot be honestly claimed without validating those fields.
- The cardiovascular-after-infection seed was deferred because infection hospitalization exposure and precise pre/post infection timing are not established in the UKB catalog.
- The sleep-regularity seed was deferred because accelerometry-derived regularity and its temporal relation to metabolic measurements are not verified in the available schema.
- The physiological-reserve-before-cancer seed was deferred because staging and treatment fields are not verified; cancer diagnosis and post-diagnosis complications can be studied only as less-specific associations.
- The protective-factor/PRS seed was deferred because exact disease-specific PRS fields and an independent validation cohort are not established locally.
- The infection-onset/severity seed was deferred because diagnostic codes do not reliably adjudicate infection severity or organ damage.
- The expert demonstration adaptations were considered: Delphi-style disease-history modeling is a valid alternative above; ALADYNOULLI-style genetic latent modeling is deferred with missing genetic inputs; Oncoformer full multimodality is unavailable because images and the main paper methods are unavailable, while a lab-only adaptation is not the most direct test of this metabolic-change question.
