# Provisional hypothesis: discordant HbA1c rise and weight trajectory predict incident diabetes

## Status

This is an early, explicitly provisional hypothesis for episode 6. It is not a result and is not selected. It inherits the prior episode's feasibility finding: the pancreatic-cancer direction failed a zero-event feasibility gate, while an incident-diabetes endpoint is common and clinically interpretable. Exact catalog/table/archive bindings, repeat-measurement overlap, event support, ascertainment rules, and the three inspected key references remain to be verified.

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with two valid assessment-center visits, no diabetes or cancer before the second visit, a comparable rise in HbA1c is associated with higher subsequent incident-diabetes risk when accompanied by weight loss than when accompanied by weight gain or stability.

The clinically consequential question is whether the same worsening glycemic state should be interpreted differently when body weight is falling, because that joint pattern may identify people needing earlier metabolic follow-up. This is a prognostic association, not a claim that weight loss causes diabetes, that it identifies occult cancer, or that treatment should be changed without clinical review.

## Evidence and uncertainty

The supporting observation motivating this branch is the prior feasibility finding recorded in the episode state, not a completed analysis: the pancreatic endpoint had no usable events under the prespecified gate, whereas the metabolic endpoint is expected to be more common. That expectation is not yet verified here. No claim is made about the effect size, prevalence, or causal mechanism.

Key uncertainties are: whether two valid visits and both biomarker/body-composition measurements have sufficient overlap; whether diabetes is reliably ascertained and prevalent cases are excluded; whether weight loss is measured as an objective trajectory rather than self-report; whether cancer, frailty, medication, and observation intensity create competing explanations; and whether the association persists after separating biochemical worsening from current severity.

## Falsifiable test

Use the second valid assessment visit as the index date. Define incident diabetes from a prespecified diagnosis source and date after index, with prevalent diabetes and cancer excluded. Compare participants with similar HbA1c rise and weight trajectory (loss versus gain/stability), using a baseline model with HbA1c level/rise, weight, age, sex, and measured confounders, then a trajectory model that estimates the joint HbA1c-by-weight interaction. The primary estimand is the difference in risk or hazard ratio for incident diabetes across trajectory strata, with uncertainty intervals and prespecified sensitivity analyses for missingness, timing, cancer, and competing events.

The hypothesis is supported if the interaction is directionally and precisely consistent across prespecified analyses, with the weight-loss-plus-HbA1c-rise group having higher subsequent diabetes risk than the comparable gain/stability group. It is adverse if the interaction is null or reversed, or if the apparent contrast disappears after excluding prevalent disease, aligning measurement dates, or accounting for competing events. It is inconclusive if event counts, overlap, or ascertainment are too sparse or too uncertain to distinguish these patterns.

## Exact data and method comparison

The intended source is the UK Biobank population-multiomics source group, but the exact source path, table names, archive members, field IDs, join keys, and time fields must be verified against the full catalog before compilation. Candidate fields include repeated HbA1c, body weight, assessment-center date, diagnosis/date fields, cancer exclusions, age, sex, and available medication/frailty covariates; these are not yet asserted as available in this draft.

A simple baseline is a prespecified Cox or discrete-time model with HbA1c rise, weight change, and measured confounders, plus an interaction term. A substantive alternative is a joint longitudinal trajectory model (for example, a two-part growth model or regularized multi-state model) that estimates HbA1c and weight trajectories and their interaction, with a held-out time split and bootstrap or cross-fitted uncertainty. The alternative can reveal whether the association depends on the shape and ordering of trajectories rather than a single change score, which the baseline loses. If the required repeated measurements or event support are unavailable, the alternative is deferred and the baseline is not silently promoted to a mechanistic claim.
