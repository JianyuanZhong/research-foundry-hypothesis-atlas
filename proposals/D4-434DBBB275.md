# Dated two-panel metabolic change and transition to cardiometabolic multimorbidity

## Successor purpose and scientific deliverable

This targeted successor preserves the verified UK Biobank two-panel design and the primary 1-to-2-domain prognostic question. It strengthens the parents by making the outcome crosswalk, code/date parsing, repeat-panel selection estimand, temporal leakage controls, and decision thresholds executable without changing the scientific target.

The future solver must newly construct and freeze the cohort and phenotype codebook, audit all joins and dates, fit the nested baseline and transition-specific model, and produce locked predictions with uncertainty, calibration, decision-curve and falsification outputs. Completion is computed artifacts and a conclusion classified as supportive, adverse, or inconclusive under the prespecified criteria; readiness, a successful fit, or a positive coefficient alone is not completion.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that baseline glycaemic, lipid, anthropometric and related kidney measures are associated with later cardiometabolic disease, and that a UKB cohort can estimate prospective associations. The available evidence does not establish whether observed within-person change in HbA1c, triglycerides or HDL, after conditioning on the latest level and the repeat-measurement process, adds information about the clinically important transition from one cardiometabolic disease domain to a second.

The falsifiable primary hypothesis is:

> Among UKB participants with exactly one coded diabetes, cardiovascular, or chronic-kidney disease domain at a prespecified post-repeat-panel risk origin, annualized worsening in HbA1c, triglycerides, or HDL predicts the first transition to a second domain beyond latest levels and baseline covariates, and a transition-specific model improves calibrated five-year risk stratification at a prespecified review threshold.

This is a prognostic association hypothesis. It is not a causal claim about changing a biomarker, a treatment effect, or a validated clinical policy.

## Cohort, temporal boundaries, and leakage prevention

Define `d0 = main.53-0.0` and `d1 = main.53-1.0`, cross-checked against the same assessment fields. Eligibility is age 40–69 inclusive at recruitment, valid sex, finite field-valid primary biomarkers at both panels, valid dates with d1>d0 and a gap of 365–3650 days, and no available assessment-date ordering contradiction among `53-0.0/1.0/2.0/3.0`. The index is the actual date d1, never the instance number.

Before the risk origin, derive earliest valid onset for each domain from the frozen code/date algorithm below. Exclude anyone with two or more domains on or before d1. Define the primary risk origin as o=d1+365 days. Exclude anyone who dies before o or has a new second-domain onset in (d1,o]; count these washout losses in the flow diagram. The primary population is exactly one domain at o; the zero-domain population is retained only for the secondary 0-to-1 transition and joint multistate fitting. This washout prevents outcome information close to the second measurement from leaking into the post-landmark endpoint. Sensitivities use 0-, 2-, and 5-year washouts and a 182–5475-day panel gap.

Follow from o to the first eligible second-domain onset, death, the last trusted date in the frozen outcome snapshot, or five years after o, whichever occurs first. Death is an absorbing competing event. Report the number with a full five-year opportunity and the number censored by the snapshot boundary. Outcome fields are never used to choose covariates, repeat availability, split buckets, transformations, thresholds, or model form.

## Executable outcome-domain phenotype

Freeze this crosswalk and parser before reading outcomes for model development. For each suffix, pair the code and date at the identical suffix; a code without its paired parseable date, a date without a code, an impossible date, a blank/nonfinite value, or an unrecognized code is audit-only and cannot create an event. Normalize codes to uppercase, trim whitespace, and retain decimal detail for ICD-9 matching. Use the earliest valid date in the domain.

The three domains are:

- diabetes: ICD-10 `E10`–`E14` by three-character prefix; ICD-9 `250` by three-character prefix;
- cardiovascular disease: ICD-10 `I20`–`I25`, `I50`, or `I60`–`I69`; ICD-9 `410`–`414`, `428`, or `430`–`438`;
- chronic kidney disease: ICD-10 `N18` or `N19`; ICD-9 `585` or `586`.

No claim is made that this administrative phenotype is chart-adjudicated disease. The primary event is the earliest post-origin date on which either domain absent at o is first observed. Death `40000-0.0/1.0` is parsed as a date-only competing event, not a disease domain. A negative-control appendix uses ICD-10 `K35` and ICD-9 `540`; if the local representation or codebook cannot validate that mapping, the negative-control result is inconclusive rather than null.

## Exposure and estimand

For marker j, calculate annualized change `gj=(xj,1-xj,0)/((d1-d0)/365.25)`; reverse HDL so a higher value means worsening. Do not globally recode negative numeric values: field-specific units and negative-code meanings are incomplete. Blank/nonfinite values are missing, and no unit conversion or clinical biomarker cutoff may be invented. Standardize changes using training-partition means and standard deviations only.

The primary association estimand is the vector of transition-specific log-hazard coefficients for the three changes in the 1-to-2 cause-specific hazard, conditional on latest marker levels, existing domain identity, age, sex, latest pre-origin assessment covariates, panel gap, and explicitly represented missingness. The joint null is `H0: beta_change,HbA1c = beta_change,TG = beta_change,HDL = 0`. Report marker-specific estimates and intervals; an equal-weight worsening score is descriptive only.

The primary missingness/selection estimand is conditional: association and prediction among participants with a valid two-panel primary biomarker set and eligible landmark. Separately estimate repeat-panel availability among participants with a valid panel-0 primary set, using only panel-0 biomarkers, age, sex, BMI, smoking and BP. Report availability by age, sex, and panel-0 risk strata and assess positivity. If positivity is adequate, fit stabilized inverse-probability-of-repeat weights from that panel-0 model, truncate at the 1st and 99th percentiles, and report a sensitivity targeting the panel-0-eligible population under the stated measured-covariate selection model. This weighting does not identify the full UKB population or repair unmeasured testing indication, medications, adherence, socioeconomic factors, assay dictionaries, or outcome misclassification. For covariates, use training-partition median imputation for continuous BMI and BP and training-partition modal imputation for smoking and center, with a missingness indicator for every imputed field; age and sex remain required. Freeze these values before validation and testing; no outcome-informed imputation is allowed.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

Both models use the same primary one-domain cohort, origin, endpoint, competing-death handling, covariate preprocessing, participant split, and locked test set.

M0 is a simple current-level prognostic baseline: a cause-specific Cox model for 1-to-2 transition with training-frozen restricted cubic splines for latest `30750-1.0`, `30870-1.0`, and `30760-1.0`, existing-domain identity, `21022-0.0`, `31-0.0`, latest eligible assessment BMI/BP/smoking/center, panel gap, and missingness indicators. A secondary M0 extension adds valid latest creatinine/cystatin C but does not replace the primary comparison.

M1 is a transition-specific multistate illness-deepening model with states 0 domains, one domain identified as diabetes/CVD/CKD, two domains, and death. Fit cause-specific transitions for 0-to-1, 1-to-2, and death; include direct 0-to-2 only if dates support an identifiable transition, otherwise prespecify an interval-censoring sensitivity. M1 uses every M0 input plus the three dated changes, permits change coefficients to differ between 0-to-1 and 1-to-2, and permits existing-domain interactions only if event support and the locked validation rule allow them. Its primary 1-to-2 predictions and metrics are calculated on exactly the M0 participants. M1 is substantive because it tests illness deepening and route-specific transitions; M0 loses the distinction between first onset and progression from one domain to two. M1 remains an association model, not a mechanistic or causal model.

A compact recurrent/temporal-attention alternative was developed at matched specificity: the same three two-panel marker values and dates, latest covariates, exact gap, one-domain 1-to-2 target, participant split, competing-death rules, metrics, and bootstrap uncertainty. Its possible scientific gain is nonlinear direction-by-domain interaction and a direct learned representation of ordered measurements. With only two verified biomarker panels it has little additional sequence information, has greater overfit and selection-amplification risk, and has no measured evidence of value beyond M1. It is deferred, not prohibited, until at least three verified dated assay panels or a bounded preregistered diagnostic shows stable locked incremental value over M1. The four-instance slope/mixed-effects branch is rejected as a data claim because the exact main header lacks biomarker instances 2/3.

The selected M1 is CPU-first and fits the tabular/state structure. The solver planning envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB RAM, and 28800 seconds; full runtime and memory are unmeasured. The decision analysis is risk review, not a treatment recommendation. Define the action as “refer for additional metabolic/cardiovascular/kidney risk review,” with no claim that review improves outcomes. The principal operating threshold is 10% predicted five-year risk of a second domain; 5% and 20% are prespecified sensitivity thresholds spanning more versus less intensive review. ## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following where estimable: the joint change test and marker-specific estimates are directionally consistent with worsening for 1-to-2 after latest levels and gap; M1 improves locked Brier score by at least 0.01 with a 95% interval wholly favoring improvement; net-benefit difference at 10% has a 95% interval above zero without material calibration harm by sex, age, or existing-domain identity; and direction/performance persist across washout, gap, repeat-availability weighting, missingness, and competing-death sensitivities. The 1-to-2 versus 0-to-1 change interaction is supportive only if event support permits a 95% interval excluding zero; otherwise it is inconclusive and cannot be used to claim transition specificity. The appendicitis negative control is null or materially weaker, and within-state/sex/gap-bin permutations of change vectors do not reproduce the association or gain.

Adverse evidence includes null or opposite change after latest levels, failed or worsened locked calibration/Brier/10% net benefit, disappearance under participant splitting or washout, a comparable appendicitis signal, or a permutation signal. This falsifies the incremental transition-specific prognostic claim, not biological harmlessness.

Inconclusive evidence includes date disagreement, inadequate code/date validation, too few complete panels or events, poor repeat-availability positivity, severe subgroup calibration failure, or intervals spanning meaningful benefit and no benefit. Do not silently replace this with a single-panel study.

Computationally checkable claims are hashes, headers, join cardinality, filter counts, date/gap distributions, phenotype extraction, split integrity, fitted models, uncertainty, predictions, calibration, Brier, decision curves, permutations, and sensitivity analyses. Clinical adjudication is required for confirmed disease status and phenotype validity; causal treatment effects, measurement indication, medication effects, policy value, threshold transport, and external validity require additional data, expert review, or another study.

## Claim limits

The study can establish whether the prespecified two-panel change variables add prognostic association and locked internal predictive information in this selected UKB repeat-panel cohort. It cannot establish causality, clinical benefit from acting at 5/10/20%, adjudicated disease, absence of measurement bias, transport to non-repeat-tested populations, or external validity. Those stronger claims require chart adjudication, richer medication and testing-indication data, a prospective or external validation cohort, and clinical review.
