# Scientific Proposal: Protein Signatures Distinguishing Infection Onset from Infection-Associated Organ Damage

## Refined Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis**: Among UK Biobank participants without prior infection at baseline, pre-illness proteomic profiles that predict infection onset differ fundamentally from those that predict infection-associated organ damage. We test whether:
1. The set of proteins significantly associated with infection onset (any severity) has **less than 50% overlap** with proteins significantly associated with infection-associated organ damage (severe outcomes with organ involvement)
2. Proteins uniquely associated with infection onset represent pathways involved in pathogen detection, initial immune activation, and early inflammatory responses
3. Proteins uniquely associated with organ damage represent pathways involved in tissue injury, immune dysregulation, and host vulnerability to severe disease

**Formal Statistical Statement**: Let P_onset be the set of proteins with p < 0.05 in a multivariate logistic regression predicting infection hospitalization (any severity), and P_damage be the set of proteins with p < 0.05 in a multivariate logistic regression predicting infection hospitalization with organ damage. We test H₀: |P_onset ∩ P_damage| / min(|P_onset|, |P_damage|) ≥ 0.50 versus H₁: |P_onset ∩ P_damage| / min(|P_onset|, |P_damage|) < 0.50.

**Key Innovation**: Unlike the original expert seed which suggested comparing the two outcomes without specifying overlap testing, this proposal explicitly:
- Tests the proportion of overlapping proteins with a formal hypothesis (≤50% overlap)
- Uses multivariate methods to account for protein correlations
- Identifies specific protein pathways and biological mechanisms
- Incorporates sensitivity analyses for robustness
- Provides a clinical interpretation framework

## Clinical Importance

### 1. Prevention vs. Treatment Target Identification
- **Infection onset proteins**: Potential targets for prophylactic interventions (vaccines, antimicrobials, immune modulators) to prevent infection initiation
- **Organ damage proteins**: Potential targets for severe infection treatment (anti-inflammatories, organ-protective therapies) to prevent progression
- **Shared proteins**: Potential targets for broad-spectrum interventions addressing both prevention and severity

### 2. Risk Stratification and Early Warning
- Identify high-risk individuals who may benefit from:

## Falsification Criteria

### Hypothesis Supported
- **Overlap ≥ 50%**: Proteins associated with infection onset are largely the same as those associated with organ damage, suggesting shared biological pathways and mechanisms. This would suggest that interventions targeting these proteins could potentially prevent both infection onset and severe outcomes.

### Hypothesis Refuted
- **Overlap < 50%**: Proteins associated with infection onset are largely distinct from those associated with organ damage, suggesting distinct biological pathways. This would support the hypothesis that:
  - Infection onset and organ damage represent different biological processes
  - Prevention strategies and treatment targets may need to be distinct
  - Early intervention may be more effective for onset, while treatment may be more effective for damage

### Adverse Results
- **No significant proteins**: If no proteins show significant associations with either outcome, this would suggest:
  - The data may not support this research question
  - The proteomic measurements may not capture relevant biological signals
  - Alternative approaches (e.g., different outcomes, different populations) may be needed

### Inconclusive Results
- **Intermediate overlap (50-75%)**: May suggest partial overlap with both shared and distinct pathways, requiring further investigation:
  - Subgroup analyses (e.g., by age, sex, comorbidities)
  - Additional sensitivity analyses
  - Integration with other data types (genomics, clinical data)
