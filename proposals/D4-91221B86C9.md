# Targeted apoB testing when LDL-C looks reassuring

## One-sentence hypothesis and clinical decision

**Hypothesis.** Among UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 years who are free of *recorded* atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), report no cholesterol-lowering medication, have direct LDL-C <2.6 mmol/L, and have at least one metabolic-risk feature (triglycerides >=1.7 mmol/L, HbA1c >=42 mmol/mol or doctor-diagnosed diabetes, or BMI >=30 kg/m2), baseline apoB >=0.90 g/L identifies a clinically meaningful excess of first 10-year myocardial infarction (MI) that is missed by conventional lipids. The prespecified quantitative claim is an adjusted hazard ratio >=1.50 for high versus lower apoB and positive out-of-sample decision-curve net benefit from adding continuous apoB to a conventional risk model at a 5% 10-year MI-risk threshold, with both 95% confidence intervals excluding their null values.

The decision is whether apoB should be ordered selectively when LDL-C appears below a common clinical goal but metabolic features make cholesterol mass an unreliable proxy for atherogenic particle number. This is a prognostic testing-policy experiment, not an emulation of apoB-lowering treatment.

## What is known, what is not, and why it matters

The strongest available evidence supports a narrower statement than the hypothesis. In a fully inspected 2019 UK Biobank article, Welsh et al. studied 346,686 participants without baseline CVD and not taking statins. ApoB, LDL-C, and non-HDL-C were highly correlated; their adjusted associations with broad CVD were similar. Once total and HDL cholesterol were in the model, apoB changed the C-index by only 0.0004 (95% CI 0.0000–0.0008), and a broad discordance analysis did not materially improve prediction (Circulation 2019; DOI 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.119.041149; PMCID PMC6693929; frozen source id [source checksum]). That supports “routine apoB for everyone did not improve broad CVD prediction in that analysis,” not “apoB can never be useful.”

A 2024 Circulation review’s bibliographic record and abstract (not its full article text) state that apoB may outperform LDL-C when the measures are discordant, but that inconsistent guidance and thresholds impede clinical use (DOI 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.124.068885; PMID 38950110; PMCID PMC11219008). Direct DOI acquisition was blocked and the acquired Europe PMC landing page did not yield article text, so no supplementary or full-method claims are made from it.

The unresolved question is therefore deliberately targeted: does apoB have actionable incremental value in the low-LDL subgroup in whom metabolic biology predicts discordance? Resolving this could either support a selective lab-testing rule—far more practical than universal testing—or provide strong evidence against it. The advance over the 2019 broad-cohort comparison is the prespecified clinical gate (low LDL plus metabolic risk), a specific first-MI horizon, contemporary outcome follow-up, absolute-risk calibration, and out-of-sample net benefit rather than association or C-index alone.

## Design and estimand

### Population and time zero

Create a baseline landmark cohort using only instance 0:

1. Age 40–69 at initial assessment.
2. Nonmissing assessment date, apoB, direct LDL-C, total cholesterol, HDL-C, and triglycerides.
3. Direct LDL-C <2.6 mmol/L.
4. At least one metabolic-risk feature: triglycerides >=1.7 mmol/L; HbA1c >=42 mmol/mol or doctor-diagnosed diabetes; or BMI >=30 kg/m2.
5. An observed response in the sex-appropriate baseline medication array and no code 1 (“cholesterol lowering medication”): for field 31 code 0 (female), require at least one nonmissing value across 6153-0.0–6153-0.3 and none equal to 1; for field 31 code 1 (male), apply the same rule to 6177-0.0–6177-0.2. Exclude wholly missing arrays, “do not know” (-1), and “prefer not to answer” (-3); code -7 (“none of the above”) and selections not including code 1 establish no *reported cholesterol-lowering medication*, not proven statin nonuse.
6. No recorded ASCVD before or on time zero. Exclude an algorithmic MI date (42000) or stroke date (42006) on/before baseline, and any paired inpatient ICD-10 code/date before/on baseline for I20–I25, I60–I69, or I70–I74. Also exclude sentinel date 1900-01-01. The accurate label remains “free of recorded ASCVD” because pre-1992, primary-care-only, and unrecorded disease may be missed.

Time zero is field 53-0.0, the initial assessment date (2006–2010). Begin risk time the following day and end at first qualifying MI, death, or exactly 10 years. Every 10-year horizon ends by October 2020, before the documented inpatient-diagnosis coverage endpoint (March 2023) and algorithmic MI dates in this snapshot (through November 2024). No per-person loss-to-follow-up or emigration field is bound or assumed; unobserved emigration is a stated source of outcome misclassification. The fixed horizon avoids treating instance number as elapsed time or inventing an exact current administrative censor date.

### Exposure, comparator, and estimands

Primary exposed group: apoB >=0.90 g/L. Comparator: apoB <0.90 g/L, within the same gated cohort. The threshold is fixed before outcome analysis and is not estimated from this sample.

Primary association estimand: cause-specific hazard ratio for first externally recorded MI over 10 years, high versus lower apoB, adjusted for age, sex, ethnicity, assessment centre, smoking, mean systolic blood pressure, blood-pressure medication, BMI, diabetes/HbA1c, total cholesterol, HDL-C, log triglycerides, and LDL-C. Report standardized 10-year cumulative-incidence difference as the clinically interpretable companion estimand, treating non-MI death as competing.

Primary incremental-utility estimand: optimism-corrected change in decision-curve net benefit at a 5% 10-year MI-risk threshold when continuous apoB (restricted cubic spline, knots fixed at training-fold quantiles) is added to a conventional model. Also report thresholds 2%, 7.5%, and 10% as a curve, without claiming any is identical to the NICE QRISK treatment threshold; this outcome is MI, not QRISK’s broader CVD endpoint.

Secondary characterization: define “excess apoB” as the residual from apoB regressed on flexible LDL-C, non-HDL-C (total minus HDL), and log triglycerides in each training fold. Estimate risk per residual SD and by residual quintile. This avoids selecting an apparently favorable residual definition on the full cohort.

### Outcome

Primary outcome is the earliest algorithmically derived MI date in 42000-0.0 after baseline and within 10 years, requiring source field 42001-0.0 not equal to 0 (“self-reported only”). Thus hospital and/or death-registry evidence is required. Death date 40000-0.0 or 40000-1.0 is the competing event; use the earliest valid death record.

Sensitivity outcomes:

- All algorithmic MI, including self-report-only source 0.
- High-specificity MI restricted to hospital-primary or death-primary source codes 11 or 12.
- First MI or stroke composite using 42000/42001 and 42006/42007.
- Two-year landmark lag, excluding events in the first two years, to reduce reverse causation from occult disease.
- Negative-control outcome: first inpatient accidental injury (paired ICD-10 S00–T88 in 41270/41280) after baseline. A similarly strong apoB association would argue for residual confounding or pipeline error, although this negative control is not proof of no confounding.

### Analysis

Before fitting models, publish the complete attrition table, missingness by field, biomarker distributions, assay-range flags if available, discordant-group size, event counts, and event counts by sex and broad ethnic group. Stop and label the primary result inconclusive if there are <300 qualifying MIs overall or <100 among participants with apoB >=0.90 g/L; do not relax the population after seeing outcomes.

Fit a cause-specific Cox model with robust standard errors and a Fine–Gray/Aalen–Johansen analysis for 10-year cumulative incidence. Check proportional hazards with Schoenfeld residuals; if violated, report restricted mean event-free time and time-varying effects rather than a single misleading HR. Model continuous predictors flexibly with prespecified restricted cubic splines. Do not dichotomize covariates.

For prediction, compare:

- M0: age, sex, ethnicity, centre, smoking, systolic BP, BP medication, BMI, diabetes/HbA1c, total cholesterol, HDL-C, log triglycerides, and direct LDL-C.
- M1: M0 plus continuous apoB spline.

Use nested 10-fold cross-validation, stratified by outcome and grouped by assessment centre where feasible. Imputation parameters, spline knots, the excess-apoB regression, and model fitting must be learned inside training folds. Report time-dependent C-index, 10-year Brier score, calibration intercept/slope and plot, and decision-curve net benefit with 500 participant-level bootstrap confidence intervals. A complete-case analysis and 20-fold multiple-imputation analysis are sensitivity checks.

Prespecified robustness checks are sex interaction, broad ethnicity interaction (only if event support is adequate), exclusion of diabetes, adjustment for baseline Lp(a) and CRP, alternate LDL-C gates of 2.0 and 3.0 mmol/L, alternate apoB threshold 0.80 g/L, inverse-probability weighting for having all required biomarkers, and permutation of apoB within age/sex/LDL/TG strata. The permutation result must center on null; failure is a pipeline falsification.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive:** both primary criteria hold—high apoB HR >=1.50 with 95% CI lower bound >1.0, and M1–M0 decision-curve net-benefit difference at 5% is >0 with its bootstrap 95% CI excluding 0—while calibration slope is 0.9–1.1 and direction survives the two-year lag, complete-case/imputed analyses, and adjustment for Lp(a). This would support prospective evaluation of selective apoB testing in this phenotype. It would not prove that treating to apoB improves outcomes.

**Adverse:** the HR point estimate is <1.50, its CI includes 1, or incremental net benefit is nonpositive. Especially decisive evidence against the proposed testing policy would be an HR upper confidence bound <1.20 and an M1–M0 net-benefit upper bound <=0. A strong association without calibration/net benefit supports prognosis but falsifies clinical utility. Disappearance after Lp(a) adjustment suggests the apparent apoB signal may be Lp(a)-related and changes the next test to compare.

**Inconclusive:** inadequate events, poor calibration, unresolved follow-up coverage, incompatible results across outcome-source definitions, or CIs spanning clinically important benefit and no benefit. “Not significant” alone is not evidence of equivalence.

An automatic verifier can check joins, array pairing, dates, exclusions, event counts, coefficients, confidence intervals, calibration, and net-benefit calculations, and can verify that narrative claims point to computed outputs. It must reject conclusions recommending apoB-guided treatment, claiming causality, or claiming no benefit from a wide null interval. Clinical validation of endpoint definitions, the appropriateness of 0.90 g/L and decision thresholds, and interpretation across ethnic groups require lipidology/cardiology review. Demonstrating that testing changes prescribing or that apoB-targeted intensification prevents MI requires an external prospective impact study or randomized strategy trial.

## Availability and evidence limits

Medication data provide self-reported classes at the visit, not prescriptions, doses, adherence, duration, or post-baseline changes. The export has derived dates and summarized inpatient diagnoses, not a complete raw longitudinal primary-care record; hospital coverage shown for field 41280 begins in 1992. MI dates are algorithmically derived reports, not bespoke adjudication performed here. Exact per-participant linkage end dates are not documented locally. UKB’s healthy-volunteer selection and sparse events in some ancestry groups limit transportability. Raw sequence variants, narrative notes, and raw imaging are not present in this UKB export and are not needed for the hypothesis.

The configured HCC, MIMIC, and eICU datasets remain directly accessible and unmodified, but they are not valid substitutes for this community baseline-to-10-year UKB question. No private row or note was used in public searches.
