# Component-specific competing-risk successor: hour-12 urine trajectory and observed ICU events

## Scientific opening and substantive change

The parent asks whether a prespecified 12-hour urine-output trajectory adds held-out information for a first observed ICU deterioration event beyond current state, treatment exposure, and documentation intensity. The parent’s endpoint repair correctly separates death from administrative ICU censoring and excludes procedures already ongoing at the landmark. Its remaining consequential weakness is that a single composite can still conceal whether any signal is specific to renal support, respiratory support, or death, and can make the clinical interpretation depend on which component dominates.

This child makes the endpoint component-specific. The primary scientific question is whether the hour-12 urine trajectory adds information for each of three prespecified observed events—new renal-replacement initiation, new invasive-ventilation initiation, and death—when the other events are treated as competing events. The child retains the parent’s hour-12 landmark, treatment/documentation-adjusted baseline, subject-level split, and noncausal interpretation. It does not relabel an observed initiation as physiologic deterioration or treatment benefit.

The evidence supports only that urine output is an imperfect, treatment- and measurement-sensitive observable. Creatinine-based kidney-function estimates can be misleading during critical illness [K1], a strict-landmark model can predict subsequent observed CRRT initiation without establishing indication or benefit [K2], and AKI classification can depend on measurement strategy and observation duration [K3]. The unresolved claim is whether the ordered hour-12 trajectory contains incremental information for these observed event risks beyond current state, explicit treatment, and documentation.

## Hypothesis and rival explanations

Among adults alive and event-free at ICU hour 12, the prespecified urine-output trajectory from the preceding 12 hours improves held-out prediction of each component-specific 12-hour observed event risk beyond current state, explicit treatment exposure, and documentation intensity.

The leading rival is that the apparent trajectory signal is not physiologic deterioration but a proxy for diuretic/fluid/vasopressor response, selective charting, procedure timing, or the fact that sicker patients are more likely to be observed and documented. The experiment is predictive association, not a causal treatment effect or mechanism. A trajectory signal that disappears after treatment/documentation adjustment, is confined to low-coverage records, or is reproduced by charting-gap controls supports the rival. A signal that persists across components and coverage strata supports incremental predictive information, but still does not identify the underlying mechanism.

## Population, landmark, and temporal boundaries

Use MIMIC-IV 3.1 adult ICU stays with:

- valid age >=18, computed from patients.anchor_age and anchor_year_group using the documented age-91 convention;
- ICU duration >=24 hours;
- no qualifying event at or before the landmark L = icustays.intime + 12 hours;
- a valid stay interval and a valid hospital admission join.

Predictors use only information available at or before L. The outcome window is (L, L+12 hours]. A stay ending before L+12 hours is administratively censored at icustays.outtime only when no qualifying event occurs first. Death is retained as a competing event when admissions.deathtime is known in the outcome window; it is never treated as discharge/transfer censoring.

For procedure initiation, use procedureevents.starttime as the event time and require a valid stay/event interval. Count only a row whose starttime is strictly after L and no later than L+12 hours. If starttime <= L and endtime > L, the procedure is ongoing at the landmark and is a baseline state feature, not a new initiation. Repeated rows for the same procedure are collapsed to the earliest qualifying starttime per stay and event type. Exclude rows with invalid or contradictory intervals and report the exclusion rule and count.

Use RRT procedure itemids 225441, 225802, 225803, 225809, and 225955, and invasive ventilation itemid 225792. Exclude non-invasive ventilation itemid 225794. Use admissions.deathtime and hospital_expire_flag for death; do not infer death from a missing procedure row. If multiple event types occur at the same timestamp, retain the first by the prespecified order RRT, invasive ventilation, death, and report the tie count.

## Component-specific estimands and outcomes

For each event k in {new RRT initiation, new invasive-ventilation initiation, death}, define the cause-specific cumulative incidence function (CIF) from L to L+12 hours:

CIF_k(t) = P(T <= t, K=k | event-free at L),

where T is the first event time and K its component. Estimate CIF_RRT, CIF_IV, and CIF_death separately, with the other event types as competing events. Also report the total first-event CIF and the event-free survival probability. Do not interpret a cause-specific hazard ratio as a causal effect, and do not treat repeated procedure rows as independent observations.

The clinically interpretable deliverable is the set of component-specific CIFs and their uncertainty, not a single composite risk. A composite may be reported only as a secondary descriptive summary with its component contributions and timing; it cannot replace the component-specific results.

## Baseline and substantive trajectory alternative

B0 is regularized logistic prediction using current/last urine value, current vital signs, demographics, admission type, care unit, and preceding treatment summaries. B1 adds documentation intensity. B2 adds explicit fluid, diuretic, and vasopressor exposure summaries and timing. T1 is a prespecified hourly urine trajectory/persistence model with slope, recovery, and explicit missingness, compared with B2 on the same landmark, split, endpoint, and uncertainty procedure.

T1 can reveal ordered decline/recovery and persistence that latest-value summaries lose. This is the substantive scientific contrast, not a small predictive-score contest. A neural sequence encoder is deferred unless T1 is feasible and the artifact-adjusted contrast remains unresolved. The alternative is not selected for complexity; it is selected because it tests whether temporal ordering contains information that the baseline discards.

## Analysis, split, uncertainty, and falsification

Use a subject-level train/validation/test split, with no subject appearing in more than one split. Fit preprocessing, imputation, regularization, and any model selection inside training folds only. Use repeated subject-level bootstrap or repeated splits for uncertainty. Report AUROC, AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, and decision-curve/net benefit at prespecified alert thresholds for each component-specific event and for the total first-event endpoint. Report component-specific CIFs and competing-risk cumulative incidence, not only binary discrimination.

Run trajectory-shuffling and pre-landmark charting-gap negative controls. Supportive results require held-out incremental value beyond B2, stable calibration, persistence in high-coverage strata, and no negative-control reproduction. Adverse results include no incremental gain, gain confined to low coverage, disappearance after treatment/documentation adjustment, or negative-control reproduction. Inconclusive results include inadequate eligible landmarks, sparse urine coverage, unstable mapping, or wide uncertainty; they do not refute the hypothesis.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted B0/B1/B2 and T1 models, component-specific CIFs with uncertainty, negative-control outputs, and held-out calibration/net-benefit outputs. No cohort, endpoint adjudication, fluid-balance validity, model result, or causal claim is asserted here.
