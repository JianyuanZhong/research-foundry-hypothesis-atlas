## Supported evidence, unresolved claim, and advance

The strongest claim supported by inspected evidence is that observable characteristics can internally predict creatinine–cystatin eGFR discordance. Chen et al. (Kidney Medicine 2024; DOI 10.1016/j.xkme.2024.100796; PMCID PMC10986041) was inspected from full-text XML. It used UK Biobank, internally validated prediction models, and reported multiple predictors of lower eGFRcys; it did not test the frozen AG1/G1 equal-capacity rules, assessment-index transport, subsequent N18 enrichment, a nine-year horizon, or benefit from ordering cystatin C.

The current Europe PMC core abstract for Estrella et al. (JAMA 2025; DOI 10.1001/jama.2025.17578; PMID 41202182; PMCID PMC12595547) was inspected. It reports an individual-level meta-analysis in which a large negative eGFR difference was associated with kidney failure with replacement therapy over mean outpatient follow-up of about 11 years (HR 1.29, 95% CI 1.13–1.47), as well as larger associations with mortality and cardiovascular outcomes. Acquisition of the article body returned HTTP 404, so no full methods, tables, figures, supplement, or article-body result is claimed as read. This supports long-horizon prognostic relevance of discordance in general, not the allocation contrast or this recorded inpatient N18 endpoint.

The unresolved claim is whether any direct N18 yield difference between the fixed AG1 and G1 lists remains detectable by a common source-supported nine-year horizon. This matters because chronic kidney disease can evolve slowly, while the parent has only 40 five-year events in all O1_hold. A durable signal would strengthen the case for prospective adjudicated comparison; disappearance, reversal, or continued imprecision would materially weaken or qualify that case. The advance is temporal maturation of an unchanged allocation experiment, not a newly optimized prognosis model and not a causal testing-strategy trial.

N18 is kidney-relevant rather than a generic mortality or cardiovascular endpoint, but it remains an inpatient administrative code. It does not establish incident, persistent, staged, or clinically adjudicated CKD, kidney failure, cystatin-target-specific benefit, or mechanism.

## Interpretation and stronger evidence required

A durable_supportive result means only that the frozen AG1 list has higher recorded inpatient N18 yield than G1 at both prespecified horizons under the fixed competing-death convention. It justifies prospective, clinically adjudicated evaluation. It does not prove complete event capture, true CKD, cystatin-specific attribution, mechanism, improved decisions, prevention, benefit, cost-effectiveness, or fairness.

A nine-year adverse result argues against the +2/1,000 nine-year enrichment claim. It does not prove G1 improves kidney health. Horizon discordance may reflect delayed disease evolution, differential competing death, coding/capture, chance, or nonrenal components of the scores; it requires clinical review and cannot be converted into a preferred workflow automatically.

An inconclusive result leaves the relevant horizon unresolved because of sparse events, uncertainty, or integrity failure. It is not no difference or equivalence. A longer horizon cannot be searched post hoc.

The exact additional evidence required for stronger claims includes outpatient and primary-care kidney diagnoses, laboratory surveillance and repeated eGFR/UACR for persistence and stage, kidney replacement therapy dates, individual emigration and linkage-censoring dates, measured GFR, chart and nephrologist adjudication, assay ordering/result timestamps, downstream actions, harms, workload and costs. External transport requires another linked cohort. Benefit requires a prospective implementation or randomized testing-strategy study with patient-centered kidney outcomes.
