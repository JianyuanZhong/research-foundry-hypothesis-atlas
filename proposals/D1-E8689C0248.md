# Episode-2 hypothesis: does persistent discordant AKI recovery add information beyond current severity?

Status: research task, not a completed result. No cohort count or effect estimate is claimed.

## Proposed relationship and clinical importance

Among adults with an ICU stay and a prespecified creatinine-defined AKI episode who survive to a fixed 48-hour landmark, a persistent pattern of creatinine improvement without concurrent urine-output improvement will identify higher subsequent risk of renal deterioration or in-hospital death than concordant recovery, after adjustment for the same landmark severity, treatment state, and observation intensity. Resolving this matters because a creatinine-only recovery label could misclassify vulnerable patients and delay monitoring or escalation. The unresolved claim is incremental prognostic information, not a mechanism, causal treatment effect, or proof of true renal recovery.

## Evidence boundary

The inspected evidence supports the measurement concern, not the proposed association. Haines et al. found that eGFR creatinine systematically overestimated kidney function after prolonged critical illness, while cystatin C better estimated measured GFR in 38 ventilated patients [K1]. Li et al. describe the 2012 KDIGO framework as standardizing AKI diagnosis using creatinine and urine output, while identifying implementation and population-applicability gaps [K2]. Tang et al. used a strict 24-hour MIMIC-IV/eICU landmark with creatinine, AKI stage, urine output, and severity to predict observed CRRT initiation, but explicitly treated CRRT as clinician-driven rather than objective necessity [K3]. None establishes that discordant recovery predicts subsequent outcomes.

The strongest rival explanation is that apparent discordance is a proxy for current illness, diuretic/vasopressor exposure, differential urine measurement intensity, or noise in a single creatinine/urine summary. The proposed trajectory comparison is therefore designed to distinguish persistent ordering from a noisy cross-sectional mismatch.

## Analysis, falsification, and deliverable

The simple baseline is a landmark logistic or Cox model with current creatinine, creatinine slope, urine-output summary, AKI stage, age/sex, severity, treatment, and measurement intensity. The substantive alternative is a compact trajectory model using pre-landmark creatinine and urine-output sequences (slopes, nadir, persistence, and co-movement), with identical patient splits, held-out temporal evaluation, calibration, discrimination, uncertainty, and measurement-intensity sensitivity analyses. The alternative is retained because persistence and ordering can distinguish true discordance from a noisy single measurement; it is deferred if sequence support is inadequate. CPU is adequate for both; GPU use is not required unless the trajectory implementation becomes matrix-intensive.

Support requires a prespecified directionally consistent association and reproducible incremental calibration/discrimination in held-out data, with uncertainty and sensitivity analyses showing it is not explained solely by missing urine measurements, diuretic/RRT exposure, or current severity. Adverse results are no incremental signal, reversal, or instability after these adjustments. Inconclusive results include sparse discordant cells, inadequate follow-up, or wide uncertainty. None supports mechanism or treatment benefit.

The solver must newly construct the landmark cohort, fit both models, estimate the discordance association and incremental prediction, and report held-out calibration/discrimination with uncertainty and sensitivity analyses. It must first report cohort, coverage, discordant-cell, and event counts. MIMIC lacks measured GFR, standardized cystatin C for all patients, reliable fluid-status adjudication, and complete urine measurement; clinical adjudication of AKI/recovery and RRT indication is required before treatment claims. Cross-subject calendar alignment is invalid because timestamps are subject-specific shifts. No numerical result is claimed.
