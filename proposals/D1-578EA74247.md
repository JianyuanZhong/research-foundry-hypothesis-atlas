# Discordant recovery after AKI: does creatinine recovery overstate renal recovery?

## Status

Working proposal for MIMIC-IV v3.1 (episode 12 repair). This is a scientific design, not a completed analysis. No patient-level results have been estimated yet.

## Clinical question

Among ICU patients with AKI, does apparent recovery defined by creatinine improvement identify patients whose urine output remains abnormal, and are those patients at higher risk of subsequent renal escalation, ICU death, or in-hospital death?

The clinically consequential unresolved claim is narrower than "creatinine is invalid." The claim is that a creatinine-based recovery definition can misclassify a subgroup whose urine output remains abnormal, and that this subgroup has worse subsequent outcomes than either patients with concordant recovery or patients who have not recovered.

## Why this matters

AKI recovery is often inferred from creatinine because it is repeatedly measured and familiar. However, creatinine generation and clearance can be affected by muscle mass, nutrition, fluid balance, and sampling practices. Urine output is a direct but noisy renal output measure. If creatinine recovery is discordant from urine-output recovery, the creatinine-based definition may overstate renal recovery and misguide clinical decisions about renal support, fluid management, and prognosis.

## Evidence and design

MIMIC-IV v3.1 provides repeated creatinine, urine output, fluid balance, renal support, vasopressors, and mortality. The prior literature supports the concern that creatinine-based estimates can diverge from measured kidney function in critical illness. The experiment prespecifies AKI episode and index time, compares discordant, concordant, and non-recovered groups, and tests renal escalation, ICU death, and in-hospital death with severity-adjusted and negative-control analyses.

## Unresolved issues

The main unresolved issue is whether discordance is clinically consequential after accounting for illness severity, treatment intensity, and measurement practices, or whether it is explained by sampling and fluid-status artifacts. ## Phenotype definitions

- AKI episode: KDIGO stage 1–3 based on creatinine and urine output criteria.
- Index time: first creatinine measurement meeting AKI criteria.
- Recovery: creatinine return to baseline or improvement by ≥25% from peak.
- Discordance: creatinine recovery without urine-output recovery (urine output remains <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 hours).
- Concordance: creatinine recovery with urine-output recovery.
- Non-recovery: no creatinine recovery.

## Competing explanations

1. Discordance reflects true renal dysfunction not captured by creatinine.
2. Discordance reflects sampling and fluid-status artifacts.
3. Discordance reflects treatment intensity and severity of illness.

## Baseline versus learned alternative

- Baseline: severity-adjusted logistic regression with prespecified covariates.
- Learned alternative: gradient-boosted trees with the same covariates, evaluated on a temporal split.

## Falsification criteria

- If discordance is not associated with worse outcomes after severity adjustment, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If discordance is explained by sampling and fluid-status artifacts, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If discordance is associated with worse outcomes only in a subgroup with high treatment intensity, the hypothesis is partially supported.

## Measured-GFR limitation

The experiment does not have measured GFR. The conclusion is limited to the relationship between creatinine-based recovery, urine-output recovery, and subsequent outcomes.
