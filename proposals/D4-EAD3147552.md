## Clinical question and importance

Among selected UK Biobank repeat attenders aged 40–75 with paired protocolized CBC measurements, who are alive, observable and free of any malignant cancer through day 365 after their second assessment, do annualized falls in haemoglobin and MCV and an annualized rise in platelet count add prognostic information for CRC recorded during days 366–1825 beyond age, sex and current CBC values?

A positive result would justify external validation in clinically timed CBC data and, only afterward, a prospective impact study. A precise null, adverse direction, or signal that is no more CRC-specific than general malignant cancer would discourage acting on sparse CBC trends as CRC prioritisation evidence. This experiment does not establish screening, FIT use, colonoscopy, referral, stage, mortality, treatment, benefit–harm, cost-effectiveness, or individual clinical benefit.

The strongest available evidence supports feasibility and the plausibility of longitudinal CBC prognosis, not this incremental claim. The predecessor feasibility artifact reported approximately 81 nominal CRC diagnoses in a stricter four-marker cohort, but did not establish the present three-marker day-366 delayed-entry estimand. The cancer demonstration supplement inspected in this episode supports general descriptions of longitudinal routine-data cancer prediction, washout and calibration; its unavailable main article and STAR Methods are not claimed as inspected. The unresolved claim is that the three prespecified trajectory terms jointly add information in this exact selected risk set and do so more for CRC than for non-CRC malignant cancer.

## Populations, estimands and temporal rules

Let `L` be valid `53-1.0`, `E=L+366` calendar days, and `H=L+1825` calendar days. The washout interval is `[L,E)`, equivalently days 0–365. Follow-up includes `E` and ends at the earliest of `H`, a defensible registry-coverage endpoint, death, or loss of observable follow-up.

The primary population requires age 40–75 at `L`, valid paired finite primary CBC values at instances 0 and 1, visit interval 365–2,922 days, no malignant diagnosis before `L`, alive at `L`, and no CRC, non-CRC malignant cancer or death in `[L,E)`. Entry is conditional on `E` being within authoritative cancer/death coverage. A cancer diagnosis or death exactly on `E` is not washout; it is a follow-up endpoint or competing event.

The alternate population is a prespecified sensitivity population for CRC prognosis only. It has the same base, prior-history and survival requirements, excludes washout CRC and death, but retains washout non-CRC malignancy. This is not a harmless coding sensitivity: it targets cancer-experienced survivors and may alter CBC trajectories, surveillance and ascertainment. It must not be used for the primary paired CRC-versus-first-non-CRC specificity contrast, because non-CRC cancer has already occurred for retained participants. That specificity contrast remains in the all-malignant-free primary cohort. The alternate population can report CRC prognosis and descriptive follow-up, with prior/washout non-CRC history explicitly adjusted or stratified only if prespecified and supported.

Cancer coding accepts a verified syntax equivalent to `^[A-Z][0-9]{2}([0-9A-Z]|\.[0-9A-Z]+)?$`. Malignancy is C00–C97; CRC is C18, C19 or C20 including child codes; non-CRC is malignant excluding C18–C20. Exact duplicate `eid`/date/code pairs are collapsed. For date-level classification, CRC takes precedence if both CRC and non-CRC codes occur on a date. Diagnoses after the earliest death date are audited and never counted as observable endpoints.

## Variables and analysis

For each primary marker `m`, define
` slope_m = (m_1-m_0) / ((L-53-0.0)/365.2425) `.
The prespecified adverse directions are decreasing haemoglobin, decreasing MCV and increasing platelets. Keep three continuous one-degree-of-freedom slope terms; do not select markers, transformations or windows using outcomes. The comparator contains age at `L`, sex, and instance-1 haemoglobin, MCV and platelets. The trajectory model adds the three slopes. RDW and its slope are a separately labelled secondary extension restricted to four-marker completeness and cannot replace the primary analysis.

Use a locked low-dimensional ridge-penalized cause-specific Cox model with left truncation at `E`. For cumulative incidence, use Aalen–Johansen with death as a competing event. For cause-specific hazards, death removes individuals from the mathematical risk set. Fine–Gray is a sensitivity analysis only and cannot change the estimand.

For prediction, use identical participant-level repeated outer splits for comparator and trajectory models, with scaling, imputation, censoring estimation and penalty fitting entirely inside training data. Report paired out-of-fold time-dependent AUC/Uno C-index, IPCW integrated Brier change, and horizon-specific calibration only when event and effective-sample support is adequate. For CRC specificity, refit the identical locked pipeline for first non-CRC malignant cancer on identical eligible people and resamples and estimate
`D = incremental CRC AUC - incremental non-CRC AUC`,
with participant bootstrap intervals. Raw AUC or PPV comparisons across outcomes are descriptive because prevalence differs.

At fixed review capacities of 1%, 2% and 5%, rank all eligible participants by strictly out-of-fold predicted five-year cumulative incidence. Use deterministic tie handling and report flagged N, observed CRC events, competing deaths, administrative censoring, effective sample size, sensitivity, PPV, events per 1,000 flagged and fraction captured. Estimate capacity quantities with IPCW/Aalen–Johansen methods accounting for competing death and censoring; estimate/truncate weights within training folds. Complete-horizon proportions are sensitivity analyses only.

## Event gates and interpretation

* At least 60 coverage-valid CRC events permits the locked association, prediction and descriptive capacity analyses.
* 30–59 permits parsimonious association, cumulative incidence and descriptive censoring-aware capacity only; no flexible model or strong calibration-slope claim.
* Fewer than 30 permits flow, event audits, cumulative incidence and at most the simplest prespecified penalized association; incremental prediction is inconclusive.
* Failure of field metadata or export-linked coverage is a hard stop; markers, entry rules and horizon cannot be changed to rescue inference.

Supportive evidence requires an adverse-direction joint slope association with uncertainty excluding the null, a positive paired out-of-fold prediction increment without deterioration in Brier or calibration, and clean leakage/falsification checks. CRC specificity additionally requires paired `D>0` with an interval excluding zero and compatible Brier findings. Capacity yields remain descriptive because no clinical minimum has been established. Adverse evidence is a precise null/opposite-direction association together with no positive prediction increment, or a precise paired `D<=0`; it does not refute clinically timed CBC models. Broad intervals, inadequate event support, unstable weights/resamples, discordant metrics, underpowered non-CRC analyses, or unresolved coverage are inconclusive.
