# Testing gaps during observable instability and acute ICU deterioration: corrected eICU transport experiment

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

When an adult ICU patient is observably unstable, an abrupt reduction in core-laboratory testing is associated with greater risk of a newly documented acute deterioration event in the following 24 hours, and testing-process information is less transportable across hospitals than clinical-state information.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is methodological: EHR observation patterns can encode care processes, and process shifts can impair transportability. The eICU data and expert seeds do not establish a positive association, an instability interaction, or a causal effect. The unresolved claim is whether a reproducible, directionally positive instability-by-testing-reduction association remains after adjustment for measured physiology and support, and whether it reproduces in hospital-held-out data. This is an observational marker hypothesis, not a claim that reducing tests causes deterioration or that a quiet chart reflects clinician intent.

This matters because a stable association could justify prospective evaluation of monitoring adequacy in unstable ICU patients; a null result would discourage universal quiet-chart warnings; and a site-specific result would identify a workflow shortcut that should not be transported. The substantive advance over the expert seeds and parent is a corrected source binding, non-overlapping exposure windows, documented event proxies, explicit transport estimands, and predeclared adverse/inconclusive interpretations.

## Exposure and instability

From `lab.csv.gz`, require numeric `labresult` and deduplicate within `patientunitstayid, labname, labresultoffset`, retaining the row with the earliest nonmissing `labresultrevisedoffset`. Define a case-normalized, source-audited core panel from creatinine, BUN, glucose, sodium, potassium, WBC/white blood cell count, platelets and lactate using `labname`, `labmeasurenamesystem` and `labmeasurenameinterface`. Record all exact retained labels and counts. Drop and report an analyte with fewer than 1,000 observed stays; never substitute silently.

At landmark t, count distinct core analytes in non-overlapping windows:
- prior: [t-18h, t-6h);
- recent: [t-6h, t);
- require prior count >=4;
- primary abrupt reduction: recent count <= 50% of prior count and no core-panel observation in the separate quiet tail [t-4h,t). This permits observations in [t-6h,t-4h) and does not force recent count to zero.
Retain recent-minus-prior count and longest gap as continuous secondary exposures; prespecify 2-, 6- and 8-hour quiet-tail sensitivity thresholds.

Define instability using only [t-6h,t]: any valid `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean <65` or `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean <65`, or a documented pressor record in `infusionDrug.csv.gz`. Normalize `drugname` using a versioned list for norepinephrine/noradrenaline, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine and dopamine. Because `infusionDrug` has no end-time field, a pressor is a documented-record proxy, not proof of continuous infusion or treatment initiation. Report MAP source, invalid-value rules, pressor labels and support by hospital.

## Outcome and estimand

Within (t,t+1,440] define the first documented deterioration event as:
1. ICU death: `patient.unitdischargestatus` indicates expired and `unitdischargeoffset` is in the window;
2. a first normalized pressor record after t when none occurred in [t-6h,t]; or
3. a first invasive-ventilation start after t from `respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset` when no ventilation interval from `ventstartoffset` through `ventendoffset` crosses t.

Open/ambiguous ventilation intervals, including the source’s zero/open conventions, must be retained under a documented conservative rule and repeated under an alternate rule. The pressor component is “new documented pressor record,” not initiation. Secondary outcomes are each component and ICU death alone. The primary risk set already requires 24-hour observation; report any alive discharge/transfer handling and a cause-specific competing-risk analysis separately rather than treating discharge as deterioration.

Primary estimand: standardized 24-hour risk difference for abrupt reduction versus no reduction in unstable and stable strata, and the interaction (difference in risk differences), from B1 predictions with hospital-stratified patient-bootstrap 95% intervals. Transport estimand: changes in calibration intercept/slope, Brier score and standardized risk-difference reproducibility when process features are removed (B0/A0) and when each hospital with at least 200 eligible landmarks is held out. Hospital ID is never a predictor.

## Models, split and evaluation

B0 is a regularized pooled-logistic state model using demographics, time since admission, admission-only `apacheApsVar` fields and `apachePatientResult.acutephysiologyscore`/`apachescore`, recent/last 6-hour summaries and slopes of MAP, heart rate, respiration and SpO2, recent core-lab values/slopes, current recorded ventilation/pressor indicators, and missingness masks. Exclude all `actual*`, future LOS, future ventilation, and post-landmark fields.

B1 adds prior/recent testing counts, longest gap, analyte-specific observation indicators and the abrupt-reduction flag. A0/A1 is a small causal GRU or temporal-convolution model over four preceding 6-hour bins with exactly the same state/process inputs and explicit masks. Fit models only on training patients; use a fixed patient-level validation split within training hospitals for regularization/early stopping; evaluate untouched hospitals. Use risk-based metrics (standardized risks, risk differences, calibration, Brier and prespecified decision curves); AUROC is secondary. Use patient bootstrap stratified by hospital and repeated learned-model seeds. If the learned branch cannot fit within the declared budget, report it as a failed/inconclusive alternative and do not replace it with an unplanned model.

## Falsification and support gates

Before fitting, report eligible stays/landmarks, exact label map, analyte observed-stay counts, exposure contrast, outcome counts and timing by hospital. If exposure or event support is sparse, labels unstable, or held-out hospitals lack both exposure levels and events, the result is inconclusive.

Falsification checks:
- repeat 2-, 6- and 8-hour quiet-tail thresholds and continuous exposure forms;
- permute lab event times within stay while preserving lab counts, hospital and stay duration;
- use stable landmarks as a negative-control context;
- exclude landmarks within 12 hours of discharge/transfer, with EOL proxy records, or with abrupt loss of all source-table coverage;
- vary MAP-source priority, pressor normalization and conservative/alternate ventilation rules;
- verify every clinical and revised timestamp is <=t for predictors and that no exposure label uses post-t data.

## Interpretation and evidence limits

Supportive requires a positive unstable-stratum interaction with a 95% interval excluding zero, persistence across timestamp/threshold/censoring checks, and directionally reproducible process-feature results in held-out hospitals without calibration collapse. This supports an observational monitoring-risk marker and a prospective study, not a treatment recommendation, causal effect, or hospital-quality ranking.

Adverse is a precisely near-zero/negative interaction, disappearance under time shuffling/censoring, or material held-out degradation of process features. It argues against a universal reduced-testing warning and may still show that site-specific workflow shortcuts are unsafe to transport.

Inconclusive is inadequate source/event support, unstable label mapping, no exposure contrast, severe heterogeneity, or uncertainty too wide to distinguish support from adverse. Do not choose thresholds or claim clinical utility in that case.

Computationally checkable: cohort filters, exact joins/timestamps, fitted parameters, standardized risks, uncertainty, calibration/Brier, hospital holdout and falsification outputs. Clinical adjudication or additional data are needed to determine whether pressor/ventilation records were clinically meaningful, whether missing labs reflect interface failure, intentional monitoring or undocumented care, and whether a prospective monitoring intervention is safe. Staffing, bed capacity, clinician intent, complete treatment-limitation status, pre-ICU trajectory and prospective utility are unavailable.
