# Synovial macrophage remission signal: composition or within-state regulation?

## Question, hypothesis, importance

**Falsifiable hypothesis:** Among the captured synovial tissue macrophages in the E-MTAB-8322 rheumatoid arthritis remission-versus-treatment-resistant comparison, most of the aggregate transcriptomic difference is explained by differences in the proportions of stable macrophage expression states, rather than by expression changes within states shared across groups. This is a hypothesis about the measured cell compartment and a single biopsy time, not proof of cell-state transition or treatment causality.

This distinction changes the next scientific decision. If composition dominates, future work should examine recruitment, retention or survival of macrophage states; if a robust within-state component dominates, prioritize a reprogramming/perturbation experiment. Either result helps avoid treating one average-tissue signature as a unique mechanism. The precise clinical remission/resistance labels and therapy history need adjudication; no immediate treatment choice follows.

## Evidence, unresolved claim, closest prior work

Prior knowledge supports macrophage heterogeneity in RA and reports different cluster frequencies plus remission-associated macrophage programs [K1]. A second macrophage subset, HBEGF-positive and pro-invasive to fibroblasts, illustrates a plausible inflammatory rival rather than a one-dimensional pro-resolution/anti-resolution model [K2]. Synovial matrix and fibroblast niches can spatially pattern immune entry and disease activity [K3], a tissue-level rival that this macrophage-only dataset cannot resolve.

**Strongest evidence-supported claim:** prior work has observed remission-associated macrophage subsets and functional macrophage–fibroblast effects; these data are not evidence that a remission subset causes remission. **Untested claim:** whether, within E-MTAB-8322, the total remission-versus-resistant expression difference is quantitatively dominated by captured-macrophage state proportions or remains after standardizing state proportions. The advance is a donor-aware decomposition of the clinical-state contrast to distinguish state representation from within-state regulation, not an independent replication or a new cell marker. K1 is closely related and may use this dataset; its abstract/data availability were inspected, not all methods or supplements. The proposal does not imply the decomposition has not appeared in detailed source results.

Strongest rival is within-state transcriptional reprogramming; both can coexist. Other rivals include current disease activity, treatment class/history, age, biopsy selection, sequencing run/library differences, dissociation/capture bias and spatial stromal context. Existing prepared metadata cannot adjust for most of these. Failure to resolve these confounders narrows interpretation, rather than warranting a mechanistic conclusion.

## Analysis and substantive alternative

**Simple baseline:** construct donor-level pseudobulk expression from the prepared feature-by-sample matrix, check its lineage and count semantics, and estimate a remission-versus-resistant contrast using a prespecified count model with donor as unit. Report per-gene effects/FDR and a sample-level total-expression contrast using a fixed, reproducible representation; show each donor, age sensitivity and sequencing-run/library sensitivity. Do not treat cells, genes or SDRF lanes as independent replicates. Maximum effective n is 13, so donor bootstrap intervals and leave-one-donor-out influence are essential; narrow covariate models are not credible at this n.

**Mechanistic alternative on the same question:** fit an unsupervised, donor-aware macrophage-state mixture model to the 13 original sparse cell matrices, learning a shared set of states without using remission/resistance labels. In leave-one-donor-out folds, learn state representations on other donors and project the held-out donor; keep all cells from one donor together. Estimate donor-level state fractions and state-specific expression, then decompose total group contrast into captured-state proportion and within-shared-state components using a common reference state distribution. Evaluate cluster/program stability across donor-held-out fits and donor-cluster bootstrap uncertainty. This can reveal a compositional explanation masked by sample averages, which the baseline cannot separate from within-state change. It is exploratory and requires common states and crossed processing/run structure; if states are unstable or group and technical batch are aliased, stop and mark inconclusive. No clinical prediction model or random-cell split is warranted.

## Falsification and outcome interpretation

Support for the directional composition hypothesis requires (a) donor-level remission/resistant total expression contrast, (b) reproducibly different proportions of shared, stable macrophage states, and (c) the within-state standardized contrast materially attenuated relative to the total contrast, without a single donor or observed run/lane pattern accounting for the result. This is cross-sectional association only.

- **Supportive:** the total contrast is mainly accounted for by state proportions; standardized within-state effects are smaller and uncertain; learned states are stable in donor-held-out fits. Prioritize orthogonal validation of the captured-state composition explanation, not whole-tissue abundance or causality.
- **Adverse:** stable shared states show a precise within-state contrast that is not attenuated by standardization, while state fractions are similar; this weakens the composition-dominant hypothesis and prioritizes independent perturbation of state regulation.
- **Inconclusive:** wide donor-level uncertainty, unstable clusters, no common states, sensitivity to a single donor, unmeasured group/run alias, poor gene/barcode alignment, or unresolved labels. This is not evidence that states are equivalent.

Falsification requires adequate cell/gene coverage, stable common-state assignment and a precise within-state component not materially attenuated by proportion standardization; otherwise claim only failure to resolve. Computationally checkable: sample/donor flow, joins, matrix alignment, state-fit stability, group contrasts, decomposition, uncertainty and whether conclusions match outputs. Expert adjudication needed for remission/resistance definitions, medications, severity, biopsy context, sequencing/capture confounding, and biological relevance of inferred states. Longitudinal persistence/transition requires prospective repeated samples; whole-tissue abundance/spatial effects require unsorted or spatial data; causality and intervention relevance require perturbation and functional outcomes; clinical treatment selection requires prospective external validation.
