# HCC repeat-TACE: pre-index-to-day-42 assay trajectory versus snapshot and workflow

Status: substantive trajectory successor to [prior hypothesis]. Design only. No cohort fit, prevalence, model result, endpoint adjudication, or clinical conclusion is claimed.

## Scientific opening, unresolved claim and advance

The parent established a decision-facing endpoint hierarchy: O0 is a literal later TACE record; O1 requires a semantically matching procedure plus timed same-visit encounter and examination/order corroboration; O2 additionally requires same-visit medication evidence. It also established that a day-42 recorded assay channel must be separated from measurement opportunity and care workflow. The remaining scientific uncertainty is temporal and clinically consequential:

> Does a pre-index-to-day-42 change or ordered trajectory in the eight inherited assay labels carry reproducible information for a later operational repeat-TACE record beyond baseline severity, baseline assay state, observation opportunity and care workflow, or is the apparent signal merely the latest assay snapshot or a late documentation marker?

[K1] supports treatment-adjacent repeat-TACE prediction in a selected CT/radiomics cohort but does not answer whether a routine longitudinal laboratory trajectory is useful in this HCC boundary. [K2] bounds treatment response as imaging- and necrosis-aware, so neither an assay change nor a procedure row is a response label. [K3] highlights ascertainment, undocumented outcomes and treatment-selection limits in recorded post-TACE comparisons. These works support the opening and its limits; they do not establish this hypothesis or validate this endpoint.

The substantive advance is a nested, falsifiable representation comparison on the same landmark population. It separates:

1. baseline severity/history and baseline assay state;
2. post-index observation opportunity and care workflow;
3. the latest available day-42 assay snapshot; and
4. the change and intermediate ordering from the pre-index assay to day 42.

A positive trajectory increment is therefore not accepted merely because it predicts. It must survive the parent workflow, observation-weighting, lead-time and timestamp/value controls and must improve on a single late snapshot for O1. If it only matches the snapshot, the next decision is snapshot validation, not trajectory modeling. If it tracks workflow or timing permutations, the trajectory interpretation is rejected while prognostic value remains open.

The hypothesis is predictive/descriptive for a recorded operational event. It does not test TACE intent, tumor response, treatment efficacy, benefit, toxicity, survival, mechanism, or a treatment policy.

## What is supported versus what will be tested

The strongest evidence-supported claim available before fitting is only that locally recorded assay information may predict a later locally recorded operational repeat-TACE event. It is not established that:

- a change in an assay value represents biologic tumor or liver-state change;
- the change adds information beyond baseline severity and baseline assay state;
- a longitudinal pattern adds information beyond a single latest value;
- the signal is independent of who was measured, when they were measured, or how intensively care was documented; or
- O0/O1/O2 represent intended treatment, imaging response or clinical benefit.

The leading trajectory explanation predicts that, conditional on baseline state and workflow, a held-out change/intermediate-pattern channel improves calibrated risk for O0 and remains directionally present for the more specific O1. The strongest rivals are:

- snapshot rival: only the latest day-42 value matters; change and ordering add no information once that snapshot is present;
- severity/selection rival: baseline disease severity or clinician selection is incompletely measured, so apparent change adds no information after a sufficiently explicit baseline block;
- observation/workflow rival: measurement opportunity, encounter intensity, orders, medication documentation or lead time generate both assay trajectories and the repeat-TACE record; and
- timing/documentation rival: values near day 42 or records near the procedure are documentation markers rather than a durable state.

The experiment can distinguish predictive/descriptive patterns among these rivals, not causal mechanisms. Residual association after adjustment is not proof of biology.

## Frozen population and endpoint hierarchy

All parent population and endpoint rules are retained.

- Index is the first procedures row with Unicode-normalized, trimmed, case-folded `手术` equal to `TACE`, nonmissing `患者主索引`, `就诊号` and `开始时间`.
- Exact duplicate patient/start rows are collapsed for event counting, retaining duplicate counts and raw-label hashes.
- Require all-source observation through day 42 after index start. The inherited `obs_end` construction and censoring rules are unchanged.
- Exclude a later literal-code TACE with 0 < elapsed days < 43 from the later-event risk set. Untimed rows are flagged, never imputed or counted as timed negatives.
- O0 is the first later unique patient/start literal-code TACE with 43 <= elapsed days < 181.
- Follow-up ends at the event, inherited all-source `obs_end`, or day 181. [43,90] and [91,181) remain fixed sensitivities.
- O1 is the first later procedure with the frozen semantic hepatic-arterial embolization predicate, valid procedure start, same-patient/same-visit encounter link, and at least one timed same-visit examination/order corroborator in [t-42,t+7]. The event time is the procedure `开始时间`, not the earlier corroborator.
- O2 is O1 plus the frozen same-visit medication predicate in [t-1,t+1]. Dose, unit, route and drug class are audited but do not establish intent.
- A qualifying semantic procedure without corroboration is an O1/O2 near-miss and is negative only when its follow-up window is observed. Early semantic procedures in (0,43) remain an alias-history sensitivity.

O0 remains the primary operational outcome, O1 is the endpoint-validity boundary outcome, and O2 is secondary. O2 cannot rescue an unsupported O1, and no composite is called response or intent.

## Exact trajectory estimand

Let t0 be the index procedure `开始时间` and c=t0+42 days. For each of the exact inherited labels

`甲胎蛋白`, `白蛋白`, `前白蛋白`, `总胆红素`, `直接胆红素`, `丙氨酸氨基转移酶`, `血小板`, and `血小板计数`,

use only lab rows with a valid `检验时间`. The pre-index window is [t0-365 days,t0), and the post-index window is (t0,c]. The latest nonduplicate row per label in the pre-index window is the baseline anchor B0; the latest nonduplicate row per label in the post-index window is the day-42 snapshot S42. Exact duplicate rows with the same patient, visit, label, timestamp, result fields and specimen type are collapsed with a duplicate audit. Same-timestamp nonduplicates are summarized deterministically within label.

The trajectory block T is constructed without complete-case exclusion:

- For each label with parseable, uncensored numeric baseline and S42 values, retain a within-label change S42-B0 and its development-fold standardized version. Scaling is label-specific and fitted only in development; values are never pooled across assays and no clinical threshold is imposed.
- Preserve `定性结果`, `定量结果` inequality/censoring tokens and `标本类型` as separate availability/content fields. A censored or qualitative observation is not converted into an exact number.
- Retain post-index ordered intermediate knots from (0,14], (14,28] and (28,42], using the latest valid row per label in each knot. T includes the ordered within-label changes and a development-fitted piecewise slope only when timing and numeric values support it; missing knots are represented by their P_obs availability indicators and fold-fitted neutral imputation, not treated as normal values.
- T includes per-label transition categories (increase/decrease/no exact change, or qualitative/inequality transition) only where the source fields support that distinction. These are recording transitions, not biologic classifications.
- The latest snapshot S42 contains the same endpoint values/content fields but no intermediate knots or change features.
- All lab timing, count, gap and missingness indicators belong to P_obs, not T. Thus a trajectory model cannot claim assay content while silently using measurement opportunity.

The baseline severity block M0 contains only pre-index information available in the source: encounter age, sex and department; dated encounter/admission history; pre-index dated procedure counts; matched-encounter diagnosis counts where the encounter is dated before t0; and assay-agnostic pre-index utilization/history summaries. Baseline assay content B0 is a separate block. Pre-index assay observation counts and missingness are in P_obs. P_care contains dated non-TACE procedure, non-drug order, medication and encounter-process marks, without assay values. Free-text findings and diagnoses are not treated as response labels.

Define the feature sets:

- A0 = M0 + B0 + P_obs + P_care;
- A_snap = A0 + S42;
- A_change = A0 + T_terminal, where T_terminal is only the baseline-to-S42 change/transition block;
- A_traj = A0 + T, including terminal change and pre-index-to-day-42 intermediate ordering.

For endpoint j in {O0,O1,O2}, the primary trajectory estimand is the paired held-out, observation/censoring-weighted log-loss increment

`D_traj,j = L(A0,j) - L(A_traj,j)`.

Positive values mean T improves prediction after baseline state, severity, opportunity and workflow. The direct snapshot comparison is

`D_traj-vs-snap,j = L(A_snap,j) - L(A_traj,j)`.

The terminal-change comparison `L(A_snap)-L(A_change)` tests whether any advantage is specifically change rather than a representation difference. These are predictive increments, not causal effects or clinical utility. Brier/integrated Brier, calibration and discrimination are secondary; no small loss difference is called a clinical MCID.

The primary decision is an overall patient-held-out estimate. Availability strata are audited and may be reported only if they satisfy the inherited support and uncertainty rules; no favorable subgroup replaces the overall estimand.

## Analysis, split, weighting and uncertainty

Use the parent’s deterministic earliest-80%/latest-20% eligible-index-date patient-disjoint split, with five grouped development folds. Freeze in each development fold the label dictionary, duplicate rules, numeric parser, qualitative/inequality encoding, label-specific scaling, imputation, hazard discretization, observation/censoring models and permutation scheme. The untouched test set is used once for final paired outputs.

Fit pooled-logistic discrete-time hazards for O0/O1/O2 for A0, A_snap, A_change and A_traj. Use the inherited all-source observation/censoring weights and report ordinary and weighted metrics, ESS, risk-set counts, event counts and weight tails. Primary uncertainty is paired patient-bootstrap with one simultaneous max-|t| family covering the overall O0/O1/O2 trajectory, snapshot-versus-trajectory and fixed falsification contrasts. O0 is primary, O1 boundary validation, O2 secondary. A model/channel with unsupported paired data or endpoint support is INCONCLUSIVE, not dropped or pooled.

Do not use post-cutoff endpoint evidence in predictors. O0/O1/O2 records after day 42 are outcome evidence only. The baseline and trajectory construction is frozen before outcome scoring. Report the number of patients with baseline, post-index and paired values per exact label and per development/test split; this is an availability result, not a prevalence claim unless explicitly estimated with uncertainty.

## Learned alternative and why it is scientifically useful

The transparent elastic-net hazard is the primary decision model because its nested blocks expose whether terminal change, intermediate trajectory and workflow each alter the same estimand, and its coefficients/ablation losses can be audited. It is not assumed to capture all useful trajectories: a summary can lose irregular measurement order, elapsed time, nonlinear cross-assay combinations, recovery-then-worsening patterns and qualitative transitions.

The matched learned alternative is a compact time-aware GRU over the identical allowed event stream from t0-365 through t0+42. Each assay event carries label identity, numeric/qualitative/inequality content, relative time, time since the prior event, and a channel tag. Separate O0/O1/O2 hazard heads and the same M0/B0/P_obs/P_care versus snapshot versus trajectory ablations are used. The GRU has three fixed development seeds, identical patient split, weights, held-out outcomes, metrics and max-|t| uncertainty. It can reveal nonlinear ordered trajectory information that the transparent summaries lose; it cannot create intent, imaging response, assay units, outside-care events or causal separation from workflow.

Selection: elastic-net is confirmatory and the GRU is a bounded sensitivity, not a merit bonus. If the GRU improves but the transparent trajectory contrast does not, label the evidence representation-sensitive and do not claim a trajectory state. If both agree, the result is more reproducible but still only recorded predictive information. A planned future fit is not an observed result.

Planning estimates, not measurements: transparent materialization, fold fits, weights, bootstrap and four channel sets require approximately 1–4 hours on 4 CPU and 16 GiB; the compact GRU requires approximately 2–6 hours on 4 CPU and 16 GiB with one allocated A100 (80 GiB), using `cuda:0` inside the allocation. The solver planning envelope is 16 CPU, 8 GPUs, 256 GiB and 28,800 seconds; discovery time is separate. The primary does not require a GPU. The GRU estimate is unverified and depends on the configured CUDA/PyTorch image; a non-allocated shell CUDA check is not evidence of absence.

Alternatives not chosen are a larger transformer, automated NLP of examination/pathology/document text, a latent causal/intent model and a mechanistic liver-response model. They would add assumptions unsupported by this snapshot: pathology/diagnosis/documents lack event time, narrative temporality/coreference is unvalidated, intent/scheduling and outside care are absent, images are absent, and lab units/reference ranges are absent. Revisit them only with a timed expert-labeled intent/response corpus, actual images/reports with timestamps, validated assay units/ranges, or external care capture. Complexity or a small predictive gain alone is not a reason to switch.

## Frozen falsification and interpretation criteria

The controls are pre-specified and evaluated once on the frozen held-out outputs.

1. **Workflow/process accounting.** Compare A0, P_obs/P_care-only, A_snap and A_traj. If T adds no increment after A0, or a process-only block matches it within the predeclared resolution/uncertainty rule, the workflow rival is favored.
2. **Snapshot rival.** If A_traj improves over A0 but `D_traj-vs-snap` is nonpositive or imprecise for O1, call the result snapshot-equivalent/inconclusive for a trajectory-specific claim; do not report trajectory superiority.
3. **Late-measurement lead time.** Refit the identical analysis after removing lab/process records in (t0+35,t0+42] and with the fixed day-28 cutoff. Erasure is adverse to a durable pre-index-to-day-42 trajectory interpretation and compatible with late review/documentation.
4. **Within-patient timestamp/value permutation.** Preserve patient, label, count and measurement times but permute result content across that patient-label’s admissible time points in development-frozen strata. A matched increment indicates a timing/documentation artifact. A label-permutation control tests whether generic assay volume drives the result.
5. **Pre-procedure and horizon controls.** Preserve the parent’s [t-42,t-1], [t-28,t-1] and [t-7,t-1] corroborator restrictions, [43,90]/[91,181) sensitivities, early-alias exclusion and ordinary-versus-development-fold observation weighting. These controls do not establish causality; they test timing dependence.
6. **Availability and support.** Report paired-label availability, event/risk-set counts, censoring, missing times, near misses, ESS and weight tails before interpreting loss. Require inherited O1/O2 support gates (at least 50 overall events for O1/O2, at least 15 development and 15 test events in any reported stratum, ESS at least 100, nondegenerate observation/censoring support and no weight-tail failure) and a paired trajectory support audit. Failure is INCONCLUSIVE, not a negative finding.

The frozen three-way decision is:

- **SUPPORTIVE trajectory:** O0 and O1 pass support gates; O0 and O1 have positive simultaneous lower bounds for D_traj; O1 also has positive lower bound for D_traj-vs-snap; direction survives late-measurement, pre-procedure and permutation controls; and no process-only match explains it. This supports reproducible recorded trajectory information for an operational endpoint and justifies a blinded episode/intent/imaging-data acquisition study. It does not support a treatment rule.
- **ADVERSE to trajectory interpretation:** D_traj is absent after A0, D_traj-vs-snap is absent while the snapshot is supported, a timing/value permutation matches, or the signal disappears when late records are removed. If the snapshot remains supported, retain only the narrower snapshot estimand and validate it; if workflow dominates, obtain scheduling/intent, standardized imaging/report timestamps, outside-care capture and validated medication indication data. This does not show that assays lack prognostic value.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** O1/O2 support fails, paired trajectory availability or overlap is inadequate, weights fail, uncertainty is wide, or representation results are unstable without a clear falsifier. Freeze the direction and perform a bounded data-completeness or independent-validation study; do not select a favorable assay, subgroup or model.

A supportive computation means only that recorded assay change/order carries held-out information beyond the measured covariates. It does not mean that values reflect tumor burden, liver function, treatment response or a biologic mechanism. An adverse computation rejects only this narrow recorded-trajectory interpretation under the measured snapshot. An imprecise null is not proof of no signal.

## Clinical adjudication and unavailable evidence

The HCC files contain CSV examination reports but no image files. `检查所见` and `检查诊断` are not auto-scored as response. Pathology, diagnoses and clinical documents lack event-time fields and cannot establish temporal order by same-visit linkage. The snapshot lacks reliable treatment intent/scheduling fields, outside-care capture, validated medication indications, laboratory units/reference ranges, adjudicated imaging response, toxicity, survival, costs and utility. These claims require expert review, linkage or another study. A verifier can check computed cohort membership, feature timing, model outputs, uncertainty and whether a conclusion follows the output map; it cannot establish clinical intent, response, mechanism or benefit.
