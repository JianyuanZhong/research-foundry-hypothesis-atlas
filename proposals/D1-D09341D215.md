# Provisional hypothesis: reduced laboratory monitoring during persistent instability

## Status

This is a provisional, data-grounded adaptation of expert seed `[starting question]` (candidate `[prior hypothesis]`). It is not a completed study and no effect has been estimated. The seed's proposed relationship is retained, but its population, timing, outcome, and falsification criteria are being made explicit from the configured eICU snapshot.

## Proposed relationship

Among adult eICU stays that remain hemodynamically or physiologically unstable, an abrupt reduction in the rate of laboratory testing over a prespecified 6-hour window is associated with a higher probability of deterioration in the following 6 hours, compared with continued testing at a similar rate, after adjustment for the immediately preceding clinical state and hospital.

The primary estimand is a descriptive/adjusted association, not a causal effect of withholding tests. The clinically important unresolved claim is whether the association reflects information loss during an unstable period rather than simply being a marker of current severity, discharge/transfer, or end-of-life care.

## Proposed computable experiment

Population: one index window per eICU stay, using `patientunitstayid` as the join key across the tables above. Restrict to adult stays with a valid `unitdischargeoffset`, at least one prior 6-hour laboratory window, and at least one subsequent 6-hour observation before discharge/transfer. Exclude windows after an EOL plan is recorded and windows immediately before discharge/transfer in a prespecified sensitivity analysis.

Exposure: for each candidate window, calculate the number of distinct laboratory tests (and, secondarily, distinct `labname` values) in the preceding 6 hours and the change from the prior 6 hours. Define an abrupt reduction as a prespecified contrast such as a decrease of at least 50% or at least two tests, chosen after a single descriptive offset/missingness audit and frozen before outcome modeling. Do not interpret a zero test count as proof that testing stopped; label it missing observation.

Outcome: a binary deterioration event in the next 6 hours, defined prospectively from available vitals and laboratory values (for example, new/worsening hypotension, tachycardia, hypoxemia, or a prespecified abnormal laboratory trajectory). The exact thresholds must be fixed after the audit and reported; no threshold is assumed here. Secondary outcomes are ICU/hospital mortality from `apachePatientResult.csv.gz` and discharge status from `patient.csv.gz`.

Analysis: compare the abrupt-reduction group with continued-testing patients using a prespecified baseline model of prior 6-hour physiology, laboratory values, time since admission, hospital, and available severity measures. A hierarchical or hospital-held-out analysis should test whether the association is consistent across hospitals. Report uncertainty intervals and calibration, not only point estimates.

Falsification and interpretation:

- Supportive: the adjusted association persists across hospitals and remains after excluding EOL/discharge windows, with uncertainty excluding a clinically negligible association. This would support a monitoring-alignment signal worth prospective validation, not causality.
- Adverse: the association disappears after adjustment for current state, is confined to end-of-life/discharge windows, or reverses across hospitals. This would weaken the proposed interpretation and favor severity or observation-practice explanations.
- Inconclusive: sparse testing, wide intervals, or unstable outcome definitions would make the result non-informative; this would justify a measurement audit or another study rather than a clinical claim.

## Missing evidence and limitations

The available data do not establish whether a laboratory was unavailable, whether a test was ordered but not recorded, whether interface downtime occurred, or whether a clinician intentionally reduced monitoring. There is no direct bedside decision, staffing, bed-capacity, or treatment-limitation adjudication. Mortality and discharge fields are administrative/derived outcomes and do not validate deterioration. The exact clinical thresholds, missingness, and event rates remain to be audited.

## Method comparison to develop before selection

The simple baseline is a prespecified adjusted logistic model using counts and prior physiology. A substantive alternative is a time-aware survival/sequence model or recurrent model using the ordered laboratory and vital observations, which can retain timing, test identity, and evolving state that the count baseline loses. The alternative is scientifically useful only if it improves discrimination/calibration for the same deterioration estimand and reveals whether the signal is concentrated in particular trajectories; a small predictive gain alone is not a scientific advance. CPU is appropriate for the baseline and likely sufficient for a bounded sequence model; GPU use is not required at this stage.
