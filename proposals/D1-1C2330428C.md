# Duration of hypotension and subsequent AKI: an eICU hypothesis

Status: working draft; no analysis has been executed.

## Question and clinical importance

At a similar nadir blood pressure, does a longer post-arrival hypotension burden predict acute kidney injury (AKI) during the first 72 ICU hours? The question matters because minimum blood pressure is widely used, but it discards duration and can be driven by one abnormal reading. If duration adds clinically meaningful information after adjustment for severity and recording density, it could improve early bedside risk communication and motivate closer monitoring or earlier nephrotoxicity precautions. It would not establish that treating to a duration threshold prevents AKI.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that eICU contains longitudinal blood pressure, vasopressor, and creatinine records that can be aligned to ICU admission and used to construct a duration measure. The unresolved claim is whether this duration measure retains prognostic information beyond the minimum pressure and established severity/recording-density explanations, with stable performance in held-out hospitals.

## Population and time origin

Use one ICU admission per patient, selected as the first `patientunitstayid` in the eICU `patient` table for each `uniquepid`. Require the stay to have at least one `vitalPeriodic` or `vitalAperiodic` systolic pressure and at least one creatinine result within the first 72 hours. Define arrival as ICU admission, the origin for `labresultoffset` and vital offsets. Exclude stays with no usable pressure in the first 24 hours, no creatinine available by hour 24, or an outcome already present before the exposure window. Exact exclusions and counts must be reported.

## Exposure window and definitions

Use the fixed first 24 ICU hours after admission. Hypotension is systolic pressure <90 mmHg or mean arterial pressure <65 mmHg, using `vitalPeriodic` columns `systolicbloodpressure` and `meanarterialpressure` and the corresponding aperiodic fields. Combine periodic and aperiodic observations, retain only physiologically plausible values after a prespecified range check, and do not silently impute missing monitor values.

The primary duration estimand is the fraction of the first 24 hours with at least one observed hypotensive pressure, using a conservative 30-minute observation interval: a 30-minute interval is counted as hypotensive if its observed value is hypotensive, and intervals without a pressure observation are not counted as normotensive. Report the number of observed intervals and the observation coverage separately. A secondary estimand is the time-weighted mean pressure deficit below 90 mmHg, using the same interval convention. Minimum systolic and MAP are calculated over the same 24-hour window and serve as the baseline exposure.

## Outcome, censoring, and validation

AKI is the KDIGO stage 1–3 creatinine criterion during the first 72 hours: creatinine >=2 times the lowest creatinine observed before hour 24, or >=2 times an admission creatinine when no earlier value exists, with absolute creatinine >=1.2 mg/dL; stage 2/3 thresholds are also reported. Use `lab.csv.gz`, `labname`, `labresult`, `labresultoffset`, and `labmeasurenamesystem/interface` after mapping creatinine field names. Censor at death, ICU discharge/transfer, or 72 hours, whichever occurs first; death and discharge are secondary outcomes. Hospital-level validation uses whole-hospital holdout: fit on all but one hospital, predict the held-out hospital, repeat across prespecified hospitals, and report calibration and uncertainty rather than treating one split as definitive.

## Rival explanation and discrimination

The strongest rival is that longer hypotension is a proxy for greater illness severity or more frequent monitoring. A more monitored patient has more opportunities to have a low pressure recorded, and a sicker patient may have both a lower nadir and longer low-pressure periods. The design therefore adjusts for age, sex, race, APACHE II components available in `apacheApsVar`/`apachePatientResult`, admission diagnoses, vasopressor exposure, and a prespecified recording-density measure (number of pressure observations and coverage in the first 24 hours). The primary comparison is within similar nadir-pressure strata, with interaction tests for duration by nadir and duration by recording density. A prespecified negative-control comparison asks whether duration predicts a pre-exposure laboratory change that should not be caused by later hypotension; a strong association there indicates residual confounding or measurement bias.

## Methods compared on the same question

Baseline: a prespecified severity-adjusted logistic model for AKI using age, sex, race, APACHE II components, admission diagnosis, vasopressor use, nadir systolic/MAP, and recording density. This is transparent, clinically interpretable, and establishes whether duration has any incremental signal.

Alternative: a time-aware gradient-boosted model fitted on the same 24-hour exposure and covariates, with a monotonic constraint on the duration variable and hospital fixed effects or hospital-stratified fitting. The learned model can reveal nonlinear duration-nadir interactions and patient-specific patterns that a linear baseline loses, while the monotonic constraint prevents an implausible protective interpretation of longer hypotension. It is not a mechanistic causal model.

Both models use the same whole-hospital holdout, bootstrap confidence intervals, calibration-in-the-large/separation, AUROC, and decision-curve net benefit at clinically relevant thresholds. The baseline is preferred if it captures the duration signal adequately and is more interpretable; the alternative is preferred only if it materially improves held-out calibration or reveals a clinically plausible nonlinear interaction, not merely a small AUROC gain. A small bounded CPU diagnostic on a subsample can estimate cohort size and fitting time before the full experiment.

## Falsification and interpretation

The hypothesis is supported if duration remains associated with AKI after nadir and severity/recording-density adjustment, with a positive monotonic trend and stable held-out-hospital calibration. It is adverse if the association disappears after adjustment, reverses, or is explained by recording density. It is inconclusive if confidence intervals are wide, coverage is too sparse, or hospital performance is unstable. A positive result supports prognostic information, not a treatment target or causal effect.
