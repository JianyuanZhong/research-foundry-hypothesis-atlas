# Can a measurement-valid all-hour excess-FiO2 signal be separated from clinician response, ventilation, and observation in acute pulmonary injury?

## Status, opening, and scientific deliverable

This crossover integrates `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It is a planned two-stage eICU experiment, not an executed clinical result. It preserves the fixed hour-24 phenotype and standardization boundary, all-clock-hour 24–36 excess-FiO2 exposure, hour-36 outcome origin, seven-day in-hospital death with live discharge competing, ordered sequential alternative, and noncausal attribution ladder. It adds an outcome-blind measurement stage whose failure is itself a reportable scientific result.

ICU-ROX shows that changing oxygen targets can reduce oxygen exposure without a significant change in ventilator-free days in a broad mechanically ventilated ICU population [K1]. A mechanistic review distinguishes inspired hyperoxia from arterial hyperoxaemia and describes experimental synergy between oxygen and mechanical stress, while emphasizing inconsistent clinical mortality evidence [K2]. LUNG SAFE found incomplete, severity-dependent clinician recognition of ARDS even with prospectively collected imaging, PEEP, timing, and cardiac-failure information [K3]. These works establish the opening, not this proposal's result.

The strongest claim currently supported is limited: oxygen targets alter exposure; inspired oxygen and arterial hyperoxaemia are not interchangeable; and pulmonary-injury recognition can depend on severity. The executed outcome-blind eICU audit additionally supports feasibility and a real observability problem: among 1,869 anchored stays, only 52.2% of proxy-positive and 47.0% of no-term stays had at least six FiO2 hours; among 47 hospitals with at least ten anchored stays, proxy prevalence ranged 0.17–0.94 and six-hour FiO2 coverage 0–1.00. These are support counts, not validation, an outcome association, or evidence of toxicity.

The unresolved clinical hypothesis is:

**Among adult first ICU stays alive and still in the ICU at hour 36, with a PaO2 of 80–120 mm Hg paired to FiO2 during hours 18–24, the pre-hour-24 acute-pulmonary-injury proxy modifies the association between mean all-hour excess FiO2 during hours 24–36 and seven-day in-hospital death after hour 36; if measurement is adequate, a pulmonary-specific delayed pattern will remain distinguishable from clinician response to worsening, ventilation co-treatment, hospital documentation, and observation intensity.**

The prerequisite measurement hypothesis is:

**After outcome-blind clinical review and cross-source FiO2 validation, the simultaneous 95% upper bound on an interaction generated solely by joint pulmonary-class and FiO2 measurement error is below 1.5 absolute risk points per +0.10 mean excess FiO2, half the prespecified clinically consequential three-point interaction, overall and across frozen hospital-documentation strata.**

The primary clinical estimand is descriptive: the pulmonary-proxy-minus-comparator difference in the pre-minute-1,440 covariate-standardized seven-day death cumulative-incidence risk difference for a +0.10 contrast in mean all-hour `(FiO2-0.40)+`, over shared observed support. It is conditional on survival and ICU presence through hour 36. It is neither oxygen initiation nor a total causal effect.

The future solver must newly deliver a frozen cohort and hourly panel; outcome-blind coverage/concordance and review artifacts; clinician-label import and error envelope; a transparent landmark estimate; an outcome-blind trained sequential model plus post-freeze competing-event head; and a gate-linked supportive, adverse, or inconclusive report. Completion means those outputs and justified uncertainty exist, not that the hypothesis is confirmed.

## Why resolution matters and what explanations predict

The clinical decision is whether a future oxygen-titration study should enrich or stratify by pre-exposure pulmonary injury. A robust pulmonary-specific delayed association would justify prospective validation and trial stratification. A precise absence of a clinically consequential interaction would argue against this enrichment rule. If error, deterioration, ventilation, or charting explains the signal, the consequential next step is measurement or treatment-process validation, not oxygen restriction.

The leading explanation is delayed oxygen–mechanical-stress injury in an already injured lung. Its strongest rivals have different observable predictions:

- **Clinician response/severity:** hypoxaemia, haemodynamic worsening, or increasing support precedes FiO2 escalation; histories predict the next setting, and the interaction attenuates once ordering is represented.
- **Ventilation co-treatment:** PEEP, plateau/peak pressure, tidal volume, respiratory rate, or invasive-support changes accompany or precede both FiO2 and deterioration; lagged mechanical burden, rather than oxygen burden, carries the pulmonary-specific pattern.
- **Observation/documentation:** proxy positivity and measured excess track diagnosis entry, ABG/FiO2 charting, source, delay, and hospital; source-balanced or high-coverage results shrink, and observation heads are poorly calibrated.
- **Pulmonary-class error:** no-term patients contain review-supported injury, or proxy accuracy varies materially by ventilation or documentation stratum.
- **Toxicity-compatible timing:** after measurement gates pass, elevated FiO2 precedes delayed deterioration; no pre-escalation negative-control association appears; a lagged oxygen × prior mechanical-stress term is stronger in the pulmonary stratum; hospital/source restrictions retain direction.

Even the last pattern is compatibility evidence. Unmeasured intent, true bedside setting-change times, prior oxygen exposure, and incomplete procedures can leave explanations observationally indistinguishable.

## Exact all-hour FiO2 measurement estimand

For each fixed clock hour `t=0,...,11` in `[1440,2160)`, define `F*t` as the latent chartable bedside inspired-oxygen setting that would be represented by an error-free respiratory setting record. It is not arterial oxygen, delivered airway concentration, or “oxygen while adequate.” The target exposure is

`X* = (1/12) Σ_t max(F*t - 0.40, 0)`.

Thus all 12 hours remain in the denominator. Same-hour or subsequent SpO2/PaO2 never selects, zeros, excludes, or weights an hour. Unrecorded time is unknown, never room air and never silently removed. The observed primary source is respiratory charting: normalize values 0.21–1.00 directly and 21–100 by division by 100; reject the rest. Within each hour use the median event-time value; carry the last prior setting forward for at most 120 minutes, never backward. Lab FiO2 and `respiratoryCare.setapneafio2` are validation emissions, not automatic replacements. Multiple sources within 30 minutes form pairs and are not averaged before disagreement is measured.

The transparent complete-record display requires at least six directly or forward-supported hourly values, reports supported-hour count and gap pattern, and uses `1/12` with missing hours explicitly unresolved. M0 reports an identification/error interval for `X*` by propagating missing hours, rather than presenting the covered-time mean as the target. A covered-time mean is a diagnostic only. Display categories—FiO2 >0.40 in >=75% versus <=25% of supported hours—and thresholds 0.30/0.50/0.60 are secondary.

For sequential ordering only, pre-decision adequacy is the last oxygenation strictly before that hour's FiO2 record: SpO2/SaO2 >=94% within 30 minutes or PaO2 >=80 within 120 minutes. Pre-decision hypoxaemia is SpO2 <92% or PaO2 <60; otherwise status is unknown. This tag is used only in temporal diagnostics and the bounded scenario; it does not redefine primary `X*`. SpO2 never quantifies arterial hyperoxaemia.

## Outcome and competing event

Primary outcome is in-hospital death after minute 2,160 through 12,240 from `patient.hospitaldischargestatus='Expired'` and `hospitaldischargeoffset`. Live hospital discharge in the interval is a competing event. Remaining hospitalized at day seven is administrative censoring. Report Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence. Secondary outcomes are ICU death, death by hospital discharge, and hour-36–72 respiratory deterioration only if timestamp coverage passes. `actualventdays` is not timestamped and cannot define an endpoint.

## Stage 1: outcome-blind measurement experiment

Before mortality access, freeze P0/P1, hospital folds, source normalization, duplicate/revision rules, and documentation strata. Publish by stratum, hospital, and source: cohort flow; term/source overlap; P/F distribution; hourly FiO2, saturation, ABG, PEEP, pressure, tidal volume, support, note, and physical-exam coverage; invalid/duplicate values; event-to-entry/revision delays; and time since last observation.

Cross-source FiO2 validation reports paired counts, median absolute difference, 90th percentile, Bland–Altman summaries, disagreement >0.05, and differential disagreement by P1, support, and documentation quartile. Source downsampling asks whether equalizing chart density changes measured burden. Event time and entry/revision time stay separate.

M0 is the transparent, clinician-anchored baseline. In 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap draws it:

1. samples phenotype sensitivity/specificity from simultaneous Phase-II confidence sets, with indeterminate first false and then true;
2. samples source-specific FiO2 residuals by support, P0/P1, and hospital-coverage quartile;
3. assigns each missing hour a conservative support-compatible interval from zero excess to FiO2 1.0, tightened only by preceding/following setting evidence without backward carry;
4. recomputes the P-by-X interaction at conservative error-set vertices; and
5. simulates an error-only world where death depends on baseline severity and pulmonary state but not excess FiO2.

M0 outputs the simultaneous 95% interval for maximum absolute spurious interaction and the identification interval for the observed interaction. It is primary for measurement validity because assumptions are inspectable. It cannot recover ordering or separate latent physiological change from observation.

### Hard Stage-1 gate

No Stage-2 result may be called pulmonary-specific or toxicity-compatible unless all pass:

1. Phase-II weighted P1 PPV and sensitivity >=0.80, each simultaneous lower 95% bound >=0.70; indeterminate <=15% overall and <=25% in every documentation quartile.
2. Simultaneous upper bounds for absolute PPV/sensitivity differences across documentation quartiles and ventilation classes <=0.15; at least 30 hospitals contribute review-supported or hierarchically estimable members of both primary strata.
3. At least 150 paired FiO2 observations per primary stratum from at least 20 hospitals; paired median absolute difference <=0.05, 90th percentile <=0.10, and upper 95% bound for stratum/documentation-quartile difference in disagreement probability <=0.10.
4. At least 150 stays with >=6 supported hours and at least 40 post-hour-36 seven-day deaths in each stratum. For the +0.10 contrast, generalized-propensity effective sample size >=100 per stratum; each of ten prespecified propensity-score deciles contains >=5% of each stratum, and the common-support region contains >=80% of each stratum.
5. M0's simultaneous 95% upper bound on maximum absolute error-only interaction <1.5 risk points per +0.10 `X*`.
6. No source family or five hospitals supply >50% of weighted exposure information or determine interaction direction; event-versus-entry/revision-clock, high-coverage, and source-downsampled analyses agree in direction.

A failed phenotype condition is a positive proxy-measurement finding; a failed FiO2 condition is a positive exposure-measurement finding; a failed event/overlap condition makes the clinical contrast underpowered or unsupported. Thresholds cannot be relaxed after outcomes.

## Stage 2 baseline and noncausal attribution ladder

B0 is a cause-specific pooled logistic landmark analysis for death and live discharge. Predictors are restricted cubic spline of `X*`, P1 pulmonary probability/stratum, their interaction, and only `C0` fixed by minute 1,440: age, sex, ethnicity, admission source/type, unit type; hospital region/size/teaching and partial-pooled hospital intercept; pre-hour-24 APACHE IV score/predicted mortality; anchor PaO2, FiO2, P/F; baseline pH, PaCO2, lactate, creatinine, bilirubin, platelets, WBC; vasopressor and invasive-support status; PEEP, pressure, tidal volume, rate; diagnoses/comorbidities; and treatment limitations. Untimed APACHE “day 1” fields are sensitivity-only.

Standardize cause-specific hazards to each stratum, derive seven-day cumulative incidence, and report the pulmonary-minus-comparator difference in risk-difference slopes per +0.10 `X*` over common support with hospital-cluster bootstrap 95% intervals. Report unadjusted Aalen–Johansen risks, calibration, support, and a `C0`-only generalized-propensity sensitivity with frozen 1st/99th percentile truncation.

The ordered attribution ladder adds separately: A, hours 18–24 trends; B, hour-24–36 severity/oxygenation summaries; C, ventilation/co-treatment summaries; D, observation density/source/delay. These are residual conditional associations with changed estimands. Exposure-window physiology may be treatment-confounder feedback or response; ventilation may be confounder, co-treatment, effect modifier, or mediator; observation is a measurement process. Attenuation is not proof of mediation or confounding control, and persistence is not proof of toxicity.

## S1: learned sequential treatment–state–observation alternative

S1 uses the identical cohort, raw hourly fields, P1 uncertainty, endpoint, and five hospital-held-out folds. Hospitals and `uniquepid` never cross folds. Fit outcome-blind treatment/state/observation components on training hospitals before fitting event heads.

At hour `t`:

1. infer respiratory/severity state `R_t` only from information timestamped strictly before the FiO2 decision;
2. predict the FiO2 setting distribution `A_t | H_t` (clinician-response head);
3. represent ventilation co-treatment `V_t` with a pre-decision component and an after-decision transition, never using same-hour unordered values to justify `A_t`;
4. after `A_t`, predict next-hour physiology/support `R_{t+1}`;
5. separately predict each source/variable observation mask and entry delay `O_{t+1}`.

Emissions include respiratory/lab FiO2; PaO2/SaO2, pH/PaCO2; haemodynamics/vasopressors; PEEP, plateau/peak pressure, tidal volume, rate, invasive support; and their masks. Hospital gets hierarchical treatment and observation effects but no unrestricted hospital-to-outcome shortcut. Pre-hour-24 pulmonary state is anchored by Phase II labels. A cumulative lagged excess-FiO2 term and prespecified excess-FiO2 × lagged standardized PEEP/plateau burden enter future-state and event hazards only after treatment. M1/S1 integrates pulmonary and FiO2 uncertainty rather than assigning one truth.

Held-hospital checks are FiO2 density/calibration; clinician-label and paired-source calibration; observation probability and entry-delay calibration; next-hour PaO2/SpO2/PEEP/support errors and posterior predictive coverage; death/discharge calibration, Brier score, and integrated calibration index. S1 is inadmissible if it only improves AUROC, violates ordering, extrapolates outside support, fails any measurement calibration, or produces an error-only interaction outside M0 bounds.

S1 must first reproduce B0's observed-data interaction on shared support within a prespecified tolerance of 1.5 risk points or overlapping 95% intervals. It then reports a model-based scenario comparing the observed path with `A_t=min(A_t,0.40)` only when oxygenation was adequate strictly before the decision; hypoxaemic and unknown hours remain observed. This scenario is secondary and does not alter primary all-hour exposure. It is labeled g-computation under sequential exchangeability, consistency, positivity, correct clocks, and correct treatment/state/observation models—not an identified trial effect.

Scientific separation comes from three contrasts: clinician response is supported if deterioration predicts subsequent FiO2 and pre-escalation negative controls appear; ventilation co-treatment is supported if lagged ventilation burden, not oxygen, carries the delayed interaction; observation is supported if masks/delays or source-balanced simulation reproduce it. Toxicity compatibility requires excess FiO2 to precede delayed change after those rivals fail. These remain observational distinctions, not mechanisms proven.

## Falsification, result meanings, and stopping

**Supportive for prospective trial enrichment:** every measurement/event/overlap gate passes; B0 and admissible S1 estimate a positive pulmonary interaction whose 95% interval excludes zero, lower bound exceeds the error-only envelope, and interval includes or exceeds three risk points per +0.10; delayed rather than pre-escalation patterns predominate; ventilation and observation rivals do not reproduce it; and hospital/source restrictions retain direction. This supports prospective phenotype validation and stratification, not oxygen toxicity or treatment change.

**Adverse to the enrichment hypothesis:** measurement passes but a precise interaction interval excludes +3 points; direction reverses or is equal/larger outside pulmonary injury; the association starts before escalation; ventilation burden carries it; or a calibrated observation process reproduces it. This argues against this proxy-based enrichment hypothesis. Separately, failed phenotype/FiO2 gates are supportive evidence for a measurement-failure hypothesis and stop biological interpretation.

**Inconclusive:** review unavailable; measurement intervals too wide; overlap/events fail; intervals include both zero and +3; B0 and calibrated S1 disagree beyond tolerance; or a few hospitals determine results. An imprecise null does not refute toxicity.

Fixed negative controls are hour-24–36 burden predicting deterioration wholly in hours 12–18 after baseline adjustment; documentation entry delay and litres-per-minute oxygen conditional on FiO2 reproducing the interaction; and diagnosis event-versus-entry cutoff creating it. Report term-family removal, strict ARDS-only, P/F 200/300, invasive-support-only, high-coverage hospitals, source-specific FiO2, and downsampling jointly without outcome-driven promotion.

Continue toward a prospective multicentre target trial only after supportive results plus expert review. Revise to measurement research if ascertainment dominates. Defer when review, events, or overlap fail. Abandon this enrichment rule after a precise adverse result replicated across B0/S1 and hospitals.

## Checkable claims, unavailable evidence, and resources

Computationally checkable: cohort flow; clocks; range/normalization rules; all-hour denominator; source agreement; coverage; review sampling weights and imported labels; confusion tables; error propagation; folds; calibration; overlap/ESS; standardized competing-risk estimates; uncertainty; negative controls; and whether the submitted conclusion obeys gates.

Not automatically checkable: true acute lung injury/ARDS, bilateral opacity, hydrostatic contribution, appropriateness of FiO2, clinician intent, delivered airway oxygen, or oxidative injury. These require clinicians, source imaging/full charts, device validation, or another study. Causal oxygen recommendations require stronger exchangeability evidence and preferably randomization; transport requires external validation.

## Alternatives retained and revisit conditions

Diagnosis-only and P/F-only phenotypes were rejected as primary because one amplifies documentation and the other cannot distinguish acute pulmonary injury. Same-hour saturation-conditioned exposure was rejected because saturation may be treatment response and selection can induce post-treatment bias. Covered-time mean remains diagnostic but cannot replace the all-hour estimand. Complete-case analysis is sensitivity because dense charting is selective. Capture–recapture without clinician anchors was deferred because sources are dependent. A hospital-preference instrument was deferred because exclusion/monotonicity are implausible and documentation violates the instrument story. M0 is primary for auditability; S1 is retained because ordering and separate treatment, ventilation, and observation processes answer a substantive uncertainty M0 loses. Revisit Berlin-ARDS claims only with images/reports, PEEP/timing adjudication, and cardiac-failure assessment.
