# Is laboratory-observation risk specific to impending ICU death after documented care-limitation workflow?

Status: Episode 29 substantive successor to [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test of ordering tests, a hospital-quality ranking, a clinician-intent study, or evidence that extra monitoring improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence is an outcome-blind feasibility record and exact local schema/source inspection. The parent line has supported a feasible adult first-stay, six-hour landmark, whole-hospital comparison of laboratory observation history against recorded vital state and vital-monitoring intensity. It has not established that a laboratory-process increment is patient-linked rather than a marker of a documented decision to limit escalation, generic discharge/workflow intensity, or an interface/revision practice. It also has not established that the increment is specific to recorded death rather than any disposition process.

The unresolved claim is:

> At ICU-relative minute 360, does patient-linked laboratory observation history add transportable information for recorded ICU death after recorded vital state, general vital-observation intensity, and documented care-limitation workflow are challenged, and is that increment smaller or absent for the competing recorded alive-discharge endpoint?

This matters because a laboratory-process score that survives these challenges could prioritize retrospective review of patients whose recorded state and monitoring process disagree. A score that disappears after the care-limitation challenge would instead identify documentation or non-escalation workflow. A score that predicts alive discharge similarly to death would be a generic length-of-stay/disposition marker, not a death-specific safety signal. Both adverse findings could prevent an inappropriate monitoring intervention.

The substantive advance over the parent is a predeclared specificity test: add the exact carePlanEOL documentation timing available in this snapshot, evaluate the laboratory increment in documented-EOL and no-recorded-EOL challenge strata without treating missing documentation as absence, and compare the death cumulative-incidence increment with the alive-discharge competing endpoint. The result is still a retrospective recorded-data finding, not physiology or clinical utility.

## Falsifiable hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: in eligible adult first ICU stays at minute 360, the patient-level laboratory observation process increment over a recorded-vital-state/intensity baseline remains favorable and transportable for recorded ICU death through minute 1,800 after adding documented care-limitation workflow, and the increment is larger for recorded death than for alive ICU discharge.

Operationally, define the primary contrast as the macro-hospital difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score for BpVE minus BvVE (lower is favorable), where BvVE and BpVE differ only by patient-level laboratory process features; E is documented care-limitation timing. Report the death-CIF and exact equal-review-burden differences as co-primary interpretation outputs, with death-versus-discharge specificity as a prespecified challenge rather than a post-hoc subgroup.

Reject the patient-linked death-specific interpretation if the process increment disappears/reverses after E, is no better than the alive-discharge increment, is reproduced by process permutation or hospital descriptors, depends on one site/interface/revision family, loses support under a defensible common analyte/revision codebook, or fails the equal-burden calibration/capture gate. A temporal result is not substantive unless it survives temporal-order shuffling and a parameter-matched non-temporal control.

## Population, temporal boundaries and outcomes

Use eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`.

Construct one row per person’s first valid ICU stay:

- numeric age >=18, recoding numeric ages >89 to 90;
- smallest valid numeric `unitvisitnumber` within `uniquepid`, tie-broken by numeric `patientunitstayid`;
- numeric `unitdischargeoffset > 360`;
- retain the selected stay only if its status/offset fields pass the pre-fit validity audit.

Predictors are known by ICU-relative minutes [0,360], inclusive at the landmark. The laboratory primary row set requires numeric `labresult`, `labresultoffset in [0,360]`, and `labresultrevisedoffset <=360` or null. A revision not known by minute 360 cannot be used as known information. Vital rows use `observationoffset in [0,360]`. No post-landmark predictor is allowed.

The primary outcome starts after minute 360 and ends at minute 1,800:

- event of interest: normalized `unitdischargestatus == Expired` and `unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800]`;
- competing event: normalized `unitdischargestatus == Alive` and `unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800]);
- administrative censoring: neither recorded event by minute 1,800.

Estimate discrete-time cause-specific hazards in 24 one-hour bins and derive the cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death with alive ICU discharge competing. Unexpected statuses, duplicates, negative/invalid offsets and impossible combinations are reported and excluded rather than silently recoded. Death after ICU discharge, hospital death, latent death and post-ICU outcomes are outside this estimand. The parallel alive-discharge CIF is required for endpoint specificity.

Care-limitation workflow E is outcome-free: link the first numeric `cpleoldiscussionoffset` and `cpleolsaveoffset` at or before 360. A stay is “documented EOL by landmark” if either is in [0,360]. `activeupondischarge` is not used to infer landmark documentation. An absent row is unknown, not no care limitation. E is a challenge covariate and stratifier, not proof of patient preference, treatment limitation, or clinician intent.

## Baseline and learned alternative

Fit the following under identical rows, labels, folds and information boundaries:

- BvV: pooled elastic-net cause-specific logistic hazards for 24 post-landmark bins using admission context, supported lab state/availability, vital state/availability, vital-observation intensity and no EOL/process features.
- BpV: BvV plus patient-level laboratory process features.
- BpVE: BpV plus the outcome-free E indicator and a prespecified process-by-E challenge term only if development support is adequate. The main scientific contrast is BpVE versus BvVE, where BvVE is BvV plus E. This isolates the process increment after EOL documentation challenge.
- E-only and hospital-descriptor-only diagnostics are not replacements for the baseline; they test workflow explanations.

The primary alternative is therefore scientifically substantive: it can reveal whether observation history carries information beyond recorded state/intensity and documented care-limitation workflow, which an ordinary state-only model loses. Report standardized death-CIF and alive-discharge-CIF contrasts, integrated/time-specific Brier, calibration, and uncertainty, not merely AUROC.

Conditional temporal alternative T is a compact one-layer GRU (hidden size 32, dropout 0.1) over six bins [0,60), [60,120), [120,180), [180,240), [240,300), [300,360], containing the same supported lab/vital state, availability, process and intensity channels, with E and admission context concatenated at the final representation. It has competing death/discharge heads over the same 24 bins and no hospital ID. Compare with a parameter-count-matched non-temporal model. Require held-out calibration/Brier improvement, temporal-order shuffle attenuation and stable hospital direction before treating persistence/order/co-movement as a finding. If support, convergence or calibration fails, T is deferred and B models remain primary. CPU tabular fits are reasonable; one allocated A100 may be used for T under the future solver envelope, but GPU use is optional and not scientific evidence.

## Splits, uncertainty and fixed-review decision diagnostic

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once, keeping all stays for a `uniquepid` together. Within each development fold, use a hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Freeze codebook, ranges, imputation, scaling, regularization, E definition, temporal hyperparameters and calibration in development data only. Never use hospital ID as a patient feature.

The primary performance estimand is the macro-hospital paired difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score for BpVE-BvVE, with 95% hospital-clustered bootstrap intervals (at least 1,000 resamples if feasible) or a predeclared hierarchical equivalent. Report every hospital and fold, pooled values only alongside macro-hospital values, and a multiplicity table for BpV-BvV, BpVE-BvVE and T-BpVE.

For q in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, use the held-out BvVE score to define the reference set within each evaluation hospital. Let k(h,q) be its count; select exactly the top k episodes by each comparator’s held-out score with deterministic ties. Report recorded-death capture, PPV, review fraction, calibration and incremental membership. This tests equal burden without choosing thresholds from held-out outcomes. Do not call it clinical utility.

Interpret q only when at least 20 evaluation hospitals, at least 10 alerts per evaluation hospital on average, at least 100 alerted episodes and at least 20 recorded deaths are supported. A failure is exploratory/inconclusive.

## Readiness gates and falsification

Before looking at outcomes, save the frozen dictionary, codebook, cohort flow, fold manifest, support manifest and EOL linkage audit. Require:

- at least 1,000 recorded deaths and 50 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes;
- at least 20 evaluation hospitals in every fold and at least 20 eligible episodes per evaluation hospital;
- each retained vital field observed in [0,360] for at least 30% overall and at least 10% in 80% of evaluation hospitals;
- overlapping BvVE/BpVE support in prespecified Bv risk and vital-coverage strata, with at least 10 episodes per populated evaluation hospital/stratum used for permutation;
- convergence and finite held-out predictions for all primary models, plus timestamp/patient/hospital leakage audits;
- E documented and unknown strata sufficient for prespecified challenges; otherwise E-adjusted claims are inconclusive;
- equal-burden q support as specified above.

Lock and report:

1. Permute lab-process channels within evaluation hospital, stratified by held-out BvVE risk quintile, vital-coverage stratum and E category while holding state fixed. Attenuation supports patient-linked information; persistence suggests workflow or leakage.
2. Compare BpV-BvV with BpVE-BvVE. A large attenuation after E is evidence for care-limitation/documentation explanation; persistence is supportive only if E coverage is adequate.
3. Compare the laboratory increment for recorded death with the increment for alive discharge using identical rows and folds. Similar discharge gain is adverse to death specificity.
4. Fit vital-observation-only, E-only, hospital-descriptor-only and process-permuted diagnostics. A descriptor or E-only model reproducing the increment weakens patient-level interpretation.
5. Repeat strict common-analyte and revision-known-only codebooks; never use the absent unit column to claim unit equivalence.
6. Use outcome-free within-hospital percentile normalization of process features, requiring at least 50 calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes per hospital.
7. For T, shuffle bin order, use the matched non-temporal control, and test lab-vital interface/common-panel sensitivity.
8. Recompute exact top-k metrics with the same k(h,q); gains from more reviews do not count.
9. Leave out each hospital in influence summaries and report fold direction.
10. Audit duplicate first stays, impossible offsets/statuses, EOL timing, missingness, revisions and all exclusions before fitting.

Supportive results require all applicable gates, a favorable paired held-out-hospital process increment after E challenge, better or non-inferior calibration, death-specificity relative to alive discharge, process-permutation attenuation, and no single-site/interface/revision explanation. They establish only a transportable retrospective recorded-death proxy.

Adverse results include a null/reversed E-adjusted increment, similar alive-discharge increment, persistence under permutation, descriptor/E-only reproduction, calibration harm, or loss under strict codebook/revision rules. These reject the patient-linked death-specific interpretation even if an unadjusted association exists.

Inconclusive results include failed E support, sparse death/discharge cells, unstable fold directions, wide intervals spanning meaningful favorable/adverse values, leakage, failed convergence, or a temporal gain without order/falsification support. They are neither confirmation nor refutation.

## Evidence limits and revisit conditions

The eICU snapshot records tests, vital summaries, documentation events, discharge times/statuses and site descriptors. It does not provide specimen collection/order timestamps, laboratory units in a separate field, raw waveforms, complete EOL documentation, staffing, bed capacity, handoff quality, clinician intent, treatment administration fidelity, adjudicated physiology or complete mortality. Therefore no result can establish that a patient was physiologically unstable, that a clinician intended limitation, that test ordering caused risk reduction, or that a monitoring policy improves survival. Those claims require expert adjudication, source-system data, external validation and prospective silent-alert/impact or randomized study.
