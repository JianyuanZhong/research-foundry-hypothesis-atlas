# Is late laboratory information patient-linked after broader documentation control?

Status: Episode-43 substantive successor to [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic, transportability and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that changing surveillance improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence is an outcome-blind feasibility audit and exact local schema/header inspection, not a fitted model result. The inherited audit reports 135,680 eligible adult first ICU-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by ICU minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows known by the six-hour landmark. These counts establish a feasible retrospective experiment; they do not establish a patient-state signal, clinician intent, or benefit.

The unresolved claim is whether a late, revision-safe laboratory information-availability pattern at ICU minute 360 carries patient-linked information about later recorded ICU death after controlling for recorded physiologic state, vital-observation intensity, and broader nursing/respiratory documentation workflow—and whether that increment transports across hospitals with different surveillance regimes. This matters because a retrospective alert based on laboratory timing could be interpreted as actionable deterioration when it is actually a generic documentation or interface fingerprint. Resolving that distinction can determine whether a silent-mode monitoring review is warranted and what evidence a prospective system must collect.

The substantive advance over the parent is a prespecified cross-modality control and transport test: laboratory timing is compared with matched nursing and respiratory charting process features, then evaluated within and across outcome-blind hospital surveillance regimes. A positive result would make the recorded information-timing association more credible; it still would not show that specimens were collected late, that biology deteriorated, or that more testing improves outcome.

## Hypothesis, nulls and evidence boundary

Primary hypothesis H1: replacing aggregate revision-safe laboratory-process summaries with six bins of conservative laboratory information-availability timing improves held-out-hospital prediction of recorded ICU death in (360,1800]—measured by death-specific cumulative-incidence calibration and integrated competing-risk Brier score—after adjustment for the same recorded laboratory state, vital state/process, admission context, EOL documentation and general bedside documentation process.

Transport hypothesis H2: the laboratory-process increment is stable across hospital surveillance regimes defined without outcomes from laboratory/vital/nursing/respiratory observation fingerprints, and remains when process variables are normalized within hospital. The primary contrast is B_avail minus B_doc, where B_doc includes state plus general documentation controls but not lab timing; a secondary contrast compares B_avail with the matched vital-process increment.

Nulls/falsifiers are: no positive B_avail increment; no regime-transportability; unchanged performance after within-stay temporal-order permutation; an equal or larger documentation/vital increment; a gain confined to one hospital/interface or to post-landmark revisions; or disappearance after revision-safe/common-analyte restrictions. These adverse results reject the patient-linked timing interpretation even if a flexible model has high discrimination.

No fitted result, effect estimate, causal claim, physiologic-truth claim, external-validation claim or clinical-utility claim is made here.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

B0 is a pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with admission context, landmark laboratory state from the frozen 64-key codebook, aggregate revision-safe lab process, landmark/aggregate vital state and process, carePlanEOL channels, and no six-bin laboratory timing. B_doc adds the matched nursing/respiratory documentation-process controls. B_avail replaces aggregate lab-process timing with six-bin conservative availability channels while retaining the shared state and documentation inputs. B_result repeats B_avail on identical retained lab rows using result-time bins. A matched six-bin vital-process model tests whether any improvement is generic observation intensity.

The substantive learned alternative T is a small one-layer GRU (or documented temporal convolution fallback) over the six bin vectors containing shared lab state/process, availability channels, vital channels, nursing/respiratory documentation channels, EOL channels and missingness indicators. It has separate death/discharge heads and no hospital ID. Fit a parameter-matched non-temporal MLP control. T can reveal persistent, burst-like and nonlinear cross-modality patterns that additive B_avail loses; the alternative is not selected for complexity, GPU use or a small gain. Retain T as a substantive result only if its held-out death calibration/Brier improvement over B_avail is uncertainty-compatible, survives order controls, and yields stable interpretable pattern summaries across folds. Otherwise report it as negative/inconclusive and use B_avail for the clock inference.

A mechanistic state-space model was considered and deferred: no verified specimen-collection/order timestamps, assay units, continuous waveforms or adjudicated physiologic states are available, so biological-transition assumptions would not be testable. Revisit only if those dependencies are acquired.

## Regime definition, split and uncertainty

Assign approximately five outer folds by whole hospital, with all stays for each uniquepid kept together. Within each development fold, use a 75/25 hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Learn codebooks, scaling, imputation, calibration, thresholds and regime cut points only from fit hospitals. Define surveillance regimes before outcomes using fit-fold distributions of laboratory, vital, nursing and respiratory observation density and entry-lag summaries; use either prespecified quantile bands or a documented clustering rule, then apply fixed cut points to held-out hospitals. Also report within-hospital percentile-normalized process features and a leave-one-regime-out transport analysis when support permits.

The primary estimand is the macro-hospital mean held-out difference B_avail minus B_doc in death-specific integrated competing-risk Brier score from 360 to 1800 and calibration at 600, 1200 and 1800 minutes, with discharge CIF evaluated in parallel. Secondary estimands are B_doc minus B0, B_avail minus B_result, the death-versus-discharge increment difference, T minus B_avail, and regime interaction/heterogeneity. Report patient-weighted results only secondarily.

Use 95% intervals from at least 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions, or a prespecified hierarchical hospital/fold model if bootstrap is unstable. Save fold-level event counts, calibration intercept/slope, CIF plots/tables, integrated Brier and all regime support diagnostics.

At minute 360 provide paired fixed-burden review diagnostics at death-CIF thresholds 0.01, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10: reviews per 100 episodes, recorded-death capture, PPV, false-review burden and number needed to review. A cell requires at least 200 episodes, 20 recorded deaths, 20 alive discharges and 20 hospitals; otherwise it is unsupported. These are retrospective label-relative diagnostics, not net benefit or clinical utility.

## Support gates and falsification

Primary timing/transport claims require at least 100 eligible hospitals, 50 event-bearing hospitals, 1,000 recorded deaths, 1,000 competing discharges, approximately 20 hospitals per fold where feasible, vital-field coverage thresholds documented before fitting, overlapping revision-safe state/process support in at least 80% of held-out hospitals, and non-sparse early and late lab-bin support. Regime interaction requires at least three supported regimes and prespecified minimum hospitals/episodes per regime. Failed gates make the corresponding claim inconclusive, not a reason to alter the cohort or endpoint.

Save:

1. Within-patient permutation of lab timing-bin labels preserving counts, state, revisions and missingness.
2. Hospital- and baseline-risk-stratified reassignment of process histories across patients to break patient linkage.
3. Matched vital-only and documentation-only increments.
4. Death-CIF versus alive-discharge-CIF increment contrast.
5. Revision-known-only, strict common-analyte, interface-stratified and within-hospital-normalized analyses.
6. Mask late (300,360] and early bins separately.
7. Availability versus result clock on identical retained rows; rows with revision >360 are leakage diagnostics only.
8. T order-shuffle, MLP and parameter-matched controls.
9. CarePlanEOL presence/timing restriction/challenge, with absent documentation unknown.
10. Regime-label permutation, no post-360 rows, no hospital-ID predictors, no Apache mortality/APACHE APS fields, and no target-fold preprocessing.

Supportive evidence requires gates, a positive and uncertainty-compatible death-specific B_avail minus B_doc improvement without material calibration harm, a smaller/non-positive discharge increment, attenuation under temporal and patient-link permutation, no comparable vital/documentation-only gain, and stability across regimes, interfaces, revision and common-analyte restrictions. Adverse evidence includes a null/negative increment, permutation persistence, equal/larger discharge or documentation/vital increment, post-360-revision dependence, one-site/interface concentration, or reversal under conservative availability. Inconclusive evidence includes failed support/overlap gates, intervals spanning zero and the prespecified meaningful effect, fold instability, excessive missing revisions, or inability to reproduce the frozen codebook.

## Evidence limits and required follow-up

Available fields are recorded results and documentation, not verified orders, specimen collection, initial posting, raw waveforms, assay units, complete goals-of-care documentation, clinician review or intent. Recorded ICU disposition is not complete mortality. Harbor can check joins, offset logic, leakage, model arithmetic, uncertainty, falsifications and whether conclusions follow from outputs. It cannot establish why a test was ordered, whether a late value reflects biological decline, whether clinicians would act on a review, or whether changing surveillance improves survival.

Clinical adjudication/source-system audit with order, collection, initial-posting and revision timestamps is required to identify the clinical meaning of timing. External health-system validation is required beyond this eICU snapshot. A prospective silent-mode study and ultimately an impact or randomized study are required for clinical benefit or surveillance-policy claims.
