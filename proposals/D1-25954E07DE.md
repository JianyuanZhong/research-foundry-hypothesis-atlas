# Episode-5 candidate: time-safe, multi-hospital pH-conditioned hidden hyperoxemia

## Clinical question, evidence boundary, and advance

At a monitor SpO2 of 96–97%, clinicians may infer that arterial oxygen tension is not markedly excessive. Acidemia shifts the oxygen–hemoglobin dissociation relation, but it is unresolved whether this creates a clinically material blind spot across heterogeneous ICUs rather than in one high-resolution center.

**Primary hypothesis:** among the eligible EICU concurrent observations defined below, the observation-standardized probability of observed PaO2 >120 mm Hg is at least 10 percentage points higher when same-panel pH is <7.35 than when pH is 7.35–7.45. A separate transportability component requires a positive empirical acidemic-minus-normal contrast in at least six of eight exposure-adequate hospitals. This is a concurrent measurement association, not an effect of changing pH, FiO2, SpO2, or oxygen treatment.

The strongest existing evidence supports a narrower claim. The inspected full-text XML of Delgado et al. (Antioxidants 2026; DOI 10.3390/antiox15020235; PMCID PMC12938712) reports one tertiary center in 2017–2022, 21,406 adults and 717,064 paired measurements, with continuous SpO2 aligned to arterial gases. It found higher PaO2 in acidotic states at comparable SpO2, generally physiologic PaO2 at SpO2 96–97%, and modification by FiO2. It does not establish the prespecified +10-point EICU contrast, multi-hospital consistency, deployable prediction, causality, oxygen toxicity, or treatment benefit.

The advance is therefore a falsifiable, time-safe external replication of a clinically consequential measurement phenomenon across EICU hospitals. It uses only information charted no later than each draw, quantifies the cost of that restriction on the complete files, and separates the repeated-observation physiologic estimand from patient-level and hospital-level transportability claims.

## Variables, estimand, models, and repeated observations

Exposure is same-panel acidemia (pH <7.35) versus normal pH (7.35–7.45). Primary outcome is observed PaO2 >120 mm Hg. Secondary outcomes are PaO2 >150, continuous PaO2, and PaO2 <60; the last is a safety description, not tissue hypoxia.

The primary estimand is the **observation-weighted marginal standardized risk difference** among all 705 linked observations. Patients with more qualifying draws intentionally contribute more to this concurrent-measurement estimand. Fit B2, set acidemia to 1 and 0 for every analytic row while leaving its observed FiO2, SpO2, PaCO2, age, sex, unit type, admission source, and six-hour time block unchanged, average each prediction over all rows, and subtract normal from acidemic. This g-computation is descriptive standardization, not a causal intervention on pH.

Fixed models are:

- B0: intercept plus median SpO2, centered at 96.5.
- B1: B0 plus FiO2 per 10 percentage points, age per decade, sex, unit type, unit admission source, six-hour block 0–11, PaCO2 per 10 mm Hg, and PaCO2 missing indicator.
- B2: B1 plus acidemia and acidemia × linear FiO2.

Categorical missingness is an explicit level. Collapse a categorical predictor level to `Other` only if it has fewer than 10 primary rows, using predictor counts before inspecting PaO2. No hospital indicator enters B0–B2. The only FiO2 functional-form sensitivity replaces the linear term and interaction with fixed 21–39, 40–59, and 60–100 bands. No outcome-selected spline or category is allowed.

Fit population-average logistic GEE with exchangeable within-`uniquepid` correlation. Estimate the standardized RD and two-sided 95% interval with 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples; carry all patients and rows with a resampled hospital and relabel duplicate hospital copies before refitting. Report convergence and failed fits. This bootstrap addresses hospital sampling; GEE addresses repeated observations.

Repeated-observation sensitivities are distinct and cannot redefine the primary result: (S1) earliest eligible observation per `patientunitstayid`; (S2) equal-stay weighting of all observations; and (S3) earliest eligible draw in each stay-specific six-hour block, which the audit counts as 669 acidemic-or-normal observations. Other prespecified sensitivities are first 24 hours; last rather than median SpO2; 60-minute FiO2 lookback; chart-offset FiO2 without entry restriction; exact alternate FiO2 labels reported separately; complete PaCO2; fixed FiO2 bands; closed nonzero respiratory-care intervals; broader airway proxy; and deletion of each adequate hospital. APACHE variables cannot enter the primary analysis because they lack event timestamps.

As a missing-link sensitivity, among the 20,946 valid-panel/probable-airway observations, model retention of both strict FiO2 and SpO2 using only patient descriptors, hospital descriptors, draw block, and pre-draw respiratory-chart availability—never PaO2 or post-draw data. Refit B2 with stabilized inverse-linkage weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles. This relies on an unverifiable missing-at-random assumption and cannot repair informative ABG or monitor charting.

## Hospital consistency and measurement-model portability

The eight hospitals above are frozen by exposure/documentation counts before PaO2 inspection. For each, report acidemic and normal denominators, PaO2 >120 events, and the unadjusted within-hospital risk difference with a `uniquepid` cluster bootstrap interval. The transportability sign criterion is positive point estimates in at least six of all eight hospitals (ceiling of 70%); no hospital disappears for zero events, separation, or an unfavorable outcome. Pool the eight empirical RDs by random-effects meta-analysis and report tau-squared, I-squared, and a 95% prediction interval. A positive sign is consistency evidence, not proof of exchangeability or causal transport.

Separately, perform leave-one-hospital-out evaluation on these same eight sites, with no hospital ID or hospital-derived feature in B1/B2. Each held-out prediction must come from a model trained without that hospital. Pool the eight sets of held-out predictions and report calibration intercept and slope, Brier score, log loss, AUROC/AUPRC, and B2-B1 differences; also report site metrics when defined. B2 is “not materially worse” only if pooled held-out Brier increase is <=0.01, log-loss increase is <=0.02, and calibration slope is 0.8–1.2. This assesses portability of a same-panel measurement model. Because pH is measured in the index blood gas, it is not a pre-ABG bedside alert or deployable decision rule.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive for the pooled +10-point hypothesis** requires the B2 standardized RD lower 95% bound to be >=+10 percentage points. A point estimate >=+10 with a lower bound below +10 is not sufficient. **Full supportive evidence including transportability** additionally requires positive hospital RDs in at least six of eight sites, no adequate-hospital deletion reversing the pooled direction, a heterogeneity prediction interval not extending to <=-5 points, and B2 meeting the fixed held-out non-inferiority/calibration limits. The strongest permitted conclusion is that a clinically material concurrent pH-conditioned measurement blind spot is reproducible enough to justify prospective synchronized validation and study of a selective-ABG protocol.

**Adverse to the material-magnitude hypothesis** means the upper 95% bound is <+10 points, even if a smaller positive association remains. An upper bound <0 supports an opposite association. Fewer than six positive hospitals is adverse to the transportability component but does not erase a pooled association. Materially worse held-out B2 performance is adverse to same-panel model portability, not proof that pH has no physiologic association.

**Inconclusive for magnitude** means the 95% interval includes +10 points. Transportability is separately inconclusive if an eight-site sign cannot be computed, the prediction interval spans <=-5 and >=+10, more than 10% of primary bootstrap fits fail, strict linkage retains <20% of probable-airway panels with both monitors, or the direction reverses under entry timing, ventilation, repeated-observation, weighting, or leave-one-site-out sensitivity. If the pooled magnitude is supported but transport criteria fail, report “pooled concurrent association supported; transportability inconclusive/adverse,” never full support.

Secondary thresholds and continuous outcomes cannot rescue a failed primary hypothesis. A precise +6-point RD is adverse to the +10-point claim, not evidence of no association; a wide -2 to +22 interval is inconclusive, not supportive.

- RD +11, CI -2 to +22: accept “inconclusive”; reject support.
- A pooled-supportive result with only five positive hospitals: accept pooled support plus adverse/inconclusive transportability; reject full support.
- Any correctly computed result paired with claims of oxygen toxicity, causal benefit, a deployable pH alert, or a treatment recommendation: reject.
