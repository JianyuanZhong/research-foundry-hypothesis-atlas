# Does a plasma protein–disease association survive removal of a data-driven shared systemic axis?

Episode 8. Parent: [prior hypothesis] (episode 6, valid, mu 31.64). Operation: evolve.
Status: design + verified bindings; no association result is computed or claimed in this episode.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance
Plasma-proteomic screens repeatedly nominate a single circulating protein (GDF15, FABP1, CHI3L1,
IL-6/CRP family) as a **preclinical, disease-specific** biomarker of one diagnosis [K2]. It is
unresolved whether such a protein–diagnosis association marks a disease-specific process (claim A)
or mainly a **shared systemic morbidity/inflammation/aging axis** that is elevated across many
diagnoses and tracks cumulative disease burden [K3]. The distinction is consequential: it decides
whether a protein is a plausible disease-specific early-detection target or only a generic
risk/severity correlate — a decisive question for biomarker programmes. [K1] establishes that the
UKB-PPP Olink panel detects many protein–disease associations, so the open question is not
"is there an association" but "**how much of it is disease-specific rather than shared-axis**".

## Leading claim vs strongest rival
- Claim A (disease-specific): the case–control difference in protein p for disease d largely
  survives removal of a leave-one-protein-out shared axis and a flexible age model.
- Claim B (shared axis): most of the p–d association is explained by the shared cross-disease
  axis and/or flexible age, leaving little disease-specific residual.
The decisive observation is the **per-protein attenuation fraction** of the disease contrast after
these adjustments, with paired control fixtures — not the raw association.

## Population and temporal boundaries
- Population: the 53,014 Olink-measured participants present in the dta table with non-missing
  `n_31_0_0` and `n_21003_0_0`.
- Exposure/prevalent-state definition: disease group d = participant has ≥1 `s_41270_0_*` code in
  the pre-specified ICD10 block(s) for d. Controls = no code in that block.
- **Temporal boundary (hard limit, carried honestly)**: there is NO attendance/blood-draw date
  (`53-0.0`) and no death/cancer date (`40000`/`40005`) in this `n_eid` table (verified: absent).
  `s_41280_0_*` gives first-occurrence dates but there is no per-participant draw date to anchor
  them. Therefore diagnoses cannot be ordered relative to the blood draw, incidence cannot be
  computed, and no washout/reverse-causation timing claim is made. The test is explicitly
  **cross-sectional (prevalent-state) association**. This is the parent's identified weakness and
  is retained rather than papered over.

## Estimand and analysis (to be executed by the solver, not this episode)
For each protein p ∈ panel and each pre-specified disease group d:
1. Baseline contrast Δ_base(p,d): standardized mean difference (or logistic β) of p between cases
   and controls, adjusted for age (`n_21003_0_0`) + sex (`n_31_0_0`), with proteins
   rank-inverse-normal transformed (UKB-PPP standard).
2. Shared-axis score S_{-p}: first principal component of the 2,922 remaining proteins
   (p held out), computed on standardized NPX within the analysis sample only (no leakage).
3. Adjusted contrast Δ_adj(p,d): as (1) plus linear S_{-p}.
4. Flexible-age rival Δ_ageflex(p,d): as (1) plus a natural spline in age (4 df) instead of linear.
5. Attenuation fraction A(p,d) = (|Δ_base| − |Δ_adj|) / |Δ_base|.

Outputs that establish completion: a table of Δ_base, Δ_adj, Δ_ageflex, A(p,d) with bootstrap CIs
for every (p,d) cell above a pre-set baseline-signal threshold, plus the fixture results below.

## Baselines and the substantive alternative (method exploration)
- Simple baseline: age+sex-adjusted per-protein disease contrast (1) — interpretable, no latent factors.
- Rival mechanistic/statistical alternative: leave-one-protein-out shared-axis adjustment (2–3) —
  tests whether a single latent cross-disease direction absorbs the signal.
- Learned alternative (same question, substantive): an elastic-net / ridge cross-disease risk score
  trained on the *other* 2,922 proteins with participant-level cross-validation, then ask whether p
  adds incremental out-of-sample discrimination beyond that learned multi-protein factor. This
  reveals what the single-PC baseline loses: whether the shared structure is one linear direction or
  a multi-dimensional, non-linear predictor, and whether p carries disease-specific information that
  no combination of other proteins carries.
- Selection/deferral reason: the single-PC baseline is chosen as primary because it is directly
  interpretable as "the shared axis"; the learned score is the pre-registered secondary because a
  richer factor could absorb signal for reasons unrelated to a systemic axis, so a positive learned
  result alone would not settle claim A vs B.

## Falsification criteria (pre-specified)
- Supportive of a disease-specific component (A): a non-trivial, pre-specified set of strong
  protein–disease pairs retains ≥50% of |Δ_base| after shared-axis adjustment (median A < 50%),
  and the flexible-age rival does not remove it.
- Adverse for A (favours B): median A ≥ 70% across strong pairs, and residual signal largely
  absorbed by the flexible-age spline — i.e. p–d associations are mostly shared-axis/age.
- Inconclusive: wide bootstrap CIs / strong sensitivity of A to the latent-dimension choice, so the
  direction cannot be determined with this cross-sectional data.
- Positive control fixture: a case label constructed from the shared-axis score itself must yield
  large, near-complete attenuation (pipeline sanity for the shared-axis estimator).
- Negative control fixture: permuted case labels must yield A near 0 with null Δ_base.

## What each outcome would and would not establish
- Supportive → some plasma protein–disease signals are not reproduced by a single shared axis;
  motivates disease-specific follow-up. It would NOT establish causation, mechanism, or incidence.
- Adverse → the disease-specific reading of single proteins is largely a shared-burden artefact;
  revises biomarker expectations. It would NOT prove the shared axis is causal or that all proteins
  lack specificity.
- Inconclusive → the cross-sectional design is insufficient; the missing draw date (temporal
  anchoring) is the decisive missing evidence.

## Essential missing evidence / adjudication
- No per-participant blood-draw date and no death/cancer dates in the bound `n_eid` table ⇒ no
  incidence or washout claim. Requires a linked table with `53-0.0` in the **same** eid namespace.
- UKB ICD10 fields (`41270/41280`) capture first-occurrence **inpatient** diagnoses only:
  under-ascertainment, so disease groups require clinical adjudication and a documented
  code-list before any interpretation.
- No BMI, smoking, ethnicity or Townsend in the bound table.
- The `ukb672073` split tables use a **disjoint eid namespace** and the encrypted file is
  unavailable; they are not joined and are not used as a source of draw date.
