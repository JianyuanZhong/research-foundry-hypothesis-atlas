# Provisional repair: discordant AKI recovery after an ICU landmark

## Proposed relationship

Among ICU patients with KDIGO stage 2/3 AKI who have reached a prespecified circulatory-stabilization landmark, a patient with creatinine-defined recovery but not urine-output-defined recovery will have a higher 7-day risk of renal replacement therapy escalation or death than a patient with concordant recovery of both criteria. This discordance is proposed to represent residual renal reserve/functional recovery information that a single creatinine value loses.

## Evidence status

This is an untested hypothesis. The expert parent supplies the clinical motivation (asynchronous organ recovery may predict deterioration) but does not establish the AKI-specific relationship, cutoffs, cohort size, or data availability. No numerical result has been computed yet. Exact MIMIC-IV item IDs, units, coverage, and the three inspected literature receipts remain to be verified.

## Falsifiable test

Use a leakage-safe landmark: after an ICU admission and after a prespecified period of circulatory stabilization, define recovery over a fixed prior window using creatinine and urine output. Compare discordant creatinine-only recovery with concordant recovery for a prespecified 7-day outcome (RRT escalation or death). Fit a simple prespecified baseline (e.g., logistic regression with age, sex, baseline AKI stage, creatinine, urine-output status, comorbidity/severity proxies, and fluid balance) and a time-aware alternative using the same inputs but retaining the ordered recovery trajectory and landmark timing (for example, a penalized discrete-time model or gradient-boosted survival model). Evaluate calibration and discrimination in a held-out temporal split, with uncertainty from bootstrap or cross-fitting. The alternative is scientifically useful only if it improves interpretation of recovery timing/trajectory, not merely a small predictive gain.

## Required data and bindings to verify

MIMIC-IV patient demographics, ICU stays, creatinine laboratory measurements, urine output/flow-sheet records, fluid balance inputs/outputs, RRT/renal replacement therapy encounters, and mortality/discharge timing. The exact source paths, table names, item IDs, units, join keys, and time fields must be inspected before the experiment is frozen. No rows or notes are being joined across datasets.

## Interpretation and limitations

Supportive results would show that discordance carries incremental prognostic information after adjustment, but would not prove that urine-output recovery is causal or that fluid balance mediates it. Adverse results would weaken the proposed incremental value; an imprecise null would be inconclusive. Clinical adjudication of true RRT initiation, fluid balance accuracy, and reason for deterioration would be needed for stronger clinical claims. Missing evidence includes exact item-level availability, measurement completeness, and external validation.
