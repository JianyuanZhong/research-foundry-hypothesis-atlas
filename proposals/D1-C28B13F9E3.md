TITLE
Can an ICU clinician safely stop systemic vancomycin when a contemporaneous MRSA nares screen is reported negative in a patient being treated for suspected pneumonia? A target-trial emulation at the result-time decision point.

CLINICAL QUESTION AND ADVANCE
Empiric vancomycin is often started for severe pneumonia because missed MRSA pneumonia can be fatal, but unnecessary continuation adds nephrotoxicity, monitoring, and antimicrobial pressure. Current evidence supports a high negative predictive value for MRSA nasal testing and reduced anti-MRSA exposure when testing is paired with stewardship. A 2026 systematic review of 15 studies (2,872 patients) reported shorter therapy with pharmacist-driven PCR protocols, but its Bayesian analysis assigned <=36% probability of exceeding prespecified clinically important differences for AKI, mortality, and readmission; this leaves the patient-level safety/benefit of acting on a negative result unresolved (Timbrook et al., DOI 10.1093/ofid/ofag178; frozen full text source [source checksum]). MIMIC is especially informative because it timestamps the specimen, reported result, actual medication administrations, physiology, organ support, and death. It cannot by itself establish a universal policy or assay performance outside one center.

STRONGEST CURRENTLY SUPPORTED CLAIM VS UNTESTED CLAIM
Supported before this experiment: negative nares screens commonly occur in vancomycin-treated ICU patients and often prompt de-escalation; screening-plus-stewardship reduces anti-MRSA exposure. In the inspected MIMIC snapshot, 72,281 screen rows were text-classified negative; 1,507 adult ICU stays had a negative result reported while still in ICU and an actual systemic vancomycin administration during the preceding 24 hours. Among these, 868 had no systemic vancomycin administration in the next 24 hours and 639 had at least one. A strict feasibility phenotype requiring a peri-admission respiratory culture and contemporaneous chest report yielded 514 stays (261 no administration and 253 any administration in the next 24 hours); 418 also had a conservative pneumonia/airspace-opacity keyword in the report (218/200). These are feasibility counts, not outcome results.
Untested claim: among adults in ICU receiving empiric systemic vancomycin for clinically suspected pneumonia, whose MRSA nares screen is newly reported negative and who have no independently compelling anti-MRSA indication, a strategy of stopping vancomycin promptly is noninferior for 7-day mortality and reduces 7-day AKI compared with continuing it.

HYPOTHESIS
At the exact time a negative MRSA screen becomes available, prompt discontinuation of systemic vancomycin (no administration in the following 12 hours) is noninferior to continued administration (at least one administration in the following 12 hours) for all-cause in-hospital death within 7 days, using an absolute risk-difference noninferiority margin of 3 percentage points, and lowers KDIGO creatinine-defined AKI within 7 days. The mortality claim is primary; AKI is a superiority key secondary outcome and cannot rescue failure of mortality noninferiority.

TARGET TRIAL
Eligibility/time zero:
1. First eligible ICU stay per hospitalization in adults age >=18.
2. A MRSA screen specimen collected from 24 hours before through 24 hours after ICU `intime`, identified in `microbiologyevents` by `test_name` in {MRSA SCREEN, Staph aureus Screen, Staph aureus Preop PCR} and a negative result explicitly encoded by `comments`/`org_name` (for example, "No MRSA isolated" or "MRSA negative").
3. Time zero is `microbiologyevents.storetime`, used as the proxy for result availability; it must be 0-72 hours after ICU `intime`, precede ICU `outtime` and death, and have nonmissing `hadm_id`.
4. At least one actual systemic vancomycin administration in EMAR during [-24 h, 0] relative to time zero. Exclude oral, enema, ophthalmic, catheter-lock and other clearly non-systemic products by medication string and, where available, route/detail.
5. Suspected pneumonia must be present before time zero: at least one lower-respiratory microbiology specimen (`spec_type_desc` SPUTUM, BRONCHOALVEOLAR LAVAGE, BRONCHIAL WASHINGS, Mini-BAL, BRONCHIAL BRUSH, or TRACHEAL ASPIRATE) from ICU -24 to +48 h AND a chest radiology report from ICU -24 to time zero adjudicated as showing a new or persistent infiltrate/consolidation/airspace process compatible with pneumonia. Reports solely showing edema, atelectasis, postoperative change, or line placement without an infectious airspace process do not qualify.
6. Exclude an anti-MRSA indication evident by time zero other than pneumonia: any positive sterile-site or blood culture with MRSA, a positive MRSA respiratory culture already reported, endocarditis, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, CNS infection, deep surgical infection, or a discharge/clinical narrative clearly documenting another intended vancomycin indication. Also exclude comfort-measures-only at time zero, prior renal replacement therapy during the admission, and missing creatinine both before and after time zero (AKI analysis only).

Strategies and grace period:
A. Prompt stop: no actual systemic vancomycin administration in (0,12 h] after result availability.
B. Continue: at least one actual systemic vancomycin administration in (0,12 h].
Emulate assignment at time zero using cloning, censoring, and inverse-probability-of-censoring weights across the 12-hour grace period: clone every eligible stay to both strategies; censor the stop clone at a post-result administration, and censor the continue clone at 12 hours if no administration occurs. Start outcome follow-up at time zero in the weighted clone analysis; also report a landmark-at-12-hour analysis among those alive and uncensored as a sensitivity analysis. This avoids simply labeling patients using future treatment and then attributing immortal grace time to a strategy.

Outcomes:
Primary: all-cause in-hospital death from time zero through min(7 days, hospital discharge), from `admissions.deathtime`/`hospital_expire_flag`; discharge alive before day 7 is counted alive through day 7 only for the in-hospital estimand and the limitation is explicit.
Key secondary: new KDIGO creatinine AKI through 7 days, using serum creatinine itemids 50912 and 52546. Baseline is the lowest value in [-48 h,0]; AKI is >=0.3 mg/dL increase within 48 h or >=1.5 times baseline within 7 days. Report stage 2/3 separately. Urine-output AKI is sensitivity-only because catheterization/completeness varies.
Other secondary: systemic vancomycin administrations and vancomycin levels (itemid 51009) through 7 days; ICU-free days alive through day 7; new renal-replacement therapy; new MRSA-positive lower-respiratory culture reported within 7 days; and vancomycin restart after a >=24-hour gap. The latter two are safety signals, not validated recurrence endpoints.

CONFUNDING CONTROL AND ANALYSIS
Estimate intention-to-strategy per-protocol analogues with pooled logistic models for censoring weights during the grace period and a weighted outcome model, clustered by patient and with stabilized weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles. Prespecify overlap diagnostics, standardized mean differences, effective sample size, and weight distribution. If positivity fails (strategy propensity <0.05 or >0.95 in >10% of eligible stays, effective sample size <50% of nominal, or either strategy has <100 adjudicated stays), do not make a causal or noninferiority claim.
Baseline covariates must precede time zero: age (`patients.anchor_age`, `anchor_year` combined with admission year), sex, race, admission type/location, ICU care unit, calendar year, prior admissions; Charlson components from prior/current ICD diagnoses; time from ICU entry and specimen to result; screen label (culture-like vs explicitly PCR-labelled); prior MRSA history in microbiology; respiratory specimen type and preliminary respiratory results; minimum/maximum creatinine, lactate (50813/52442/53154), WBC (51301/51755/51756), platelets (51265/53189), temperature, heart rate, MAP, oxygen/ventilation variables, GCS; invasive ventilation; vasopressor infusions; fluid balance; baseline AKI; number/timing of vancomycin administrations and level; concomitant antipseudomonal and nephrotoxic drugs; code-status/comfort-language adjudication; and radiology phenotype/severity. Use flexible splines and missingness indicators only where clinically defensible; multiple imputation is the main handling for partially missing structured covariates. Report unadjusted, overlap-weighted, and doubly robust estimates with bootstrap 95% CIs clustered by `subject_id`.
Primary contrast is 7-day mortality risk difference (stop minus continue). Noninferiority requires the upper bound of a two-sided 95% CI to be <+3.0 percentage points and all falsification gates below to pass. Test AKI superiority only after this condition, with 95% CI entirely below 0 for risk difference. Also provide E-values/bias functions for an unmeasured binary severity factor and a quantitative tipping-point analysis.

FALSIFICATION, NEGATIVE CONTROLS, AND SENSITIVITY ANALYSES
1. Temporal negative controls: rerun the weighted contrast on outcomes fixed before result availability (pre-index creatinine change, lactate, vasopressor use, and ventilation in [-12 h,0]). Any clinically material post-weight association (absolute standardized difference >0.10, or an apparent >2-point risk difference for pre-index organ support) falsifies exchangeability and blocks a causal safety claim.
2. Future-exposure placebo: shift the decision time 24 hours before the actual result while retaining future stop/continue classification. A material mortality/AKI association at this placebo time suggests treatment-selection bias and blocks noninferiority interpretation.
3. Result-timing check: repeat using `storetime` only (primary), excluding results stored >48 hours after specimen; stratify explicitly PCR-labelled versus culture-labelled tests. Do not generalize culture findings as rapid-PCR effects.
4. Pneumonia adjudication sensitivity: strict dual-reviewed phenotype; automated high-specificity radiology phenotype; respiratory-culture-positive subset; ventilated and nonventilated strata; CAP-like (screen near admission) versus hospital-onset strata.
5. Exclude anyone with any MRSA-positive culture within 48 hours before time zero, any vancomycin indication uncertainty, severe neutropenia/transplant language, or death/discharge within 12 hours. Repeat with 6-hour and 24-hour strategy windows.
6. Active-comparator treatment-pattern sensitivity: compare prompt vancomycin stopping while non-MRSA pneumonia antibiotics continue versus vancomycin continuation, excluding cessation of all antibiotics, to reduce confounding by global treatment limitation or diagnostic reversal.
7. Negative-control outcome: packed-red-cell transfusion during days 2-7, which should not plausibly be prevented by stopping vancomycin over 12 hours; a substantial association is evidence of residual illness-severity confounding, not a benefit.
8. Clustered patient bootstrap, first hospitalization only, complete-case analysis, alternate weight truncation (2.5/97.5), propensity-score matching, and a conventional 12-hour landmark analysis.

EXACT FALSIFICATION / DECISION CRITERIA
Supportive: adjudication reliability kappa >=0.80 on the required duplicate sample; positivity gates pass; temporal/placebo controls do not trigger; upper 95% CI for mortality risk difference is <+3.0%; no excess new MRSA respiratory isolation/restart safety signal whose CI excludes no difference; and AKI risk difference is <0 with its 95% CI excluding 0. This supports that prompt discontinuation is a reasonable stewardship strategy in this selected MIMIC population, not that every negative screen mandates stopping.
Adverse: lower 95% CI for mortality risk difference is >0 or upper CI is >=+3% with a point estimate suggesting harm; or a credible increase in stage 2/3 AKI-independent clinical deterioration/MRSA rescue signal. This argues against using the negative screen alone to stop therapy in comparable patients and motivates subgroup/assay review rather than post hoc threshold changes.
Inconclusive: mortality CI crosses the +3% margin; fewer than 100 patients per strategy after adjudication; low effective sample size/positivity failure; falsification controls trigger; assay type cannot be separated; or adjudication kappa <0.80. Report effect estimates but make no safety claim. A lower AKI rate with inconclusive mortality remains inconclusive for the clinical decision.

TEXT ADJUDICATION BURDEN
The structured screen and EMAR screen produced 1,507 candidate stays; all require review of chest radiology reports available before time zero and relevant discharge-note sections to confirm pneumonia, competing indications, immune status, code status, and reason for vancomycin. A strict structured-plus-report prescreen leaves 514 stays, and a conservative keyword filter leaves 418, but manual inspection of 20 radiology reports sampled with seed 20260908 showed genuine ambiguity among pneumonia, edema, atelectasis, ARDS, hemorrhage, and postoperative change, so keywords cannot be the final gold standard. Two blinded clinical reviewers should independently adjudicate a random 100-report calibration set; disagreements go to a third reviewer. If kappa >=0.80, single review with 10% duplicate audit may process the remainder; otherwise dual-review all candidates. Expected burden is approximately 1,028 primary report/stay reviews plus 100 duplicate reviews under the strict 514-stay route, or up to 3,014 independent reviews if the broader screen requires dual review. Automated extraction can triage but cannot establish eligibility without validated adjudication.

ACTUAL INSPECTION / SAMPLING RECORD
The archive headers/catalog schemas and actual rows were inspected read-only. Full scans counted all microbiology rows and screened the relevant EMAR rows. Eligibility used one earliest eligible negative result per ICU stay. The observed 1,507 count additionally required result reported while alive/in ICU and systemic vancomycin administered in the prior 24 hours; the 514 strict count required a respiratory culture and chest report. Twenty reports were randomly sampled from accumulated candidate chest reports using Python `random.Random(20260908)`; examples demonstrated pneumonia-positive, pneumonia-negative, and ambiguous edema/atelectasis/ARDS/postoperative findings. No clinical outcomes by strategy were computed.

EVIDENCE LIMITS / REQUIRED ADDITIONAL DATA
`storetime` is only a proxy for clinician-visible result time, assay platform is incompletely identified, indication and goals-of-care require retrospective text adjudication, outpatient death/readmission and antibiotic resistance consequences are incomplete, and one-center treatment choice is strongly confounded by evolving severity, respiratory cultures, clinician judgment, and stewardship practice. An automatic verifier can check cohort construction, timestamps, weights, effect estimates, CIs, balance, falsification tests, and whether conclusions obey the decision rules. It cannot verify clinical correctness of pneumonia/indication adjudication, prove exchangeability, establish that a screen was seen by the team, or authorize a policy. A prospective multicenter randomized noninferiority trial, assay metadata, clinician-visible result audit logs, and expert infectious-disease review are required for a universal recommendation.
