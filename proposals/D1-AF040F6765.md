# Persistence benchmark for the frozen repeat-TACE assay forecast

## Status and scientific question

This is a targeted child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the frozen HCC snapshot, source bindings, population, repeat-event definition, complete-triplet denominators, assay-specific B/P/Y clocks, inherited deterministic outer folds, executed M1 model, prognostic estimand, and strict noncausal boundary. It adds the clinically substantive benchmark that the parent did not test.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> In selected repeat-TACE episodes with a complete assay triplet, the inherited learned M1 forecast provides reproducible out-of-fold predictive improvement for post-event Y not only over the remote-baseline model, but over the transparent decision-time forecast `Yhat=P`; that improvement should also persist against a leakage-safe affine persistence forecast calibrated using only each outer training fold.

Raw persistence and calibrated persistence are distinct benchmarks:

- **Raw persistence:** `Yhat=P`, with no fitting and no outcome training.
- **Training-fold calibrated persistence:** within each inherited outer split, estimate `Y=a+bP` by ordinary least squares using only the other four folds, then predict the held-out fold. This uses the same P and Y definitions and changes no population, event, clock, or fold membership. It is a defensible transparent benchmark because post-event held-out Y never enters its calibration.
- **M1:** the inherited ridge forecast `Y ~ B + P + systemic_arm + age + sex + days + P_lead_hours`, with age imputation, missingness/sex indicators, zero-variance filtering, standardization, and ridge selection over `{0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100}` learned only within each training fold.

The strongest claim already supported by the parent is that correctly linked P adds reproducible short-horizon prognostic information beyond B and the compact core in selected complete-triplet episodes. That result does not establish that M1 improves on an obvious P-based forecast. The present executed benchmark tests that stronger claim.

## Interpretation, falsification, and branches

The prespecified substantive claim requires M1 to improve reproducibly on both raw persistence and leakage-safe calibrated persistence for both assays. The execution provides strong evidence against uncalibrated `Yhat=P`, but it does not support improvement over affine calibrated persistence. For albumin the two trained forecasts are empirically indistinguishable at this sample size; for total bilirubin point estimates and all 20 partition comparisons favor calibrated persistence, although bootstrap intervals still include zero. Thus the composite stronger hypothesis is operationally adverse, not confirmed.

The strongest evidence-supported conclusion is:

> In these selected complete-triplet repeat-TACE episodes, the inherited M1 forecast predicts Y better than raw `Yhat=P`, but its advantage is not reproduced against a transparent affine persistence forecast calibrated only on outer-training-fold data. Much or all of M1's apparent advantage over raw persistence can therefore be explained by correcting assay-specific level/slope miscalibration rather than demonstrated added value from B and the compact covariates.

This does not prove that B/core variables have no predictive information, that calibrated persistence is equivalent or universally superior, or that either forecast is clinically useful. No equivalence margin was specified, and sample sizes are small. The benchmark is prognostic and observational.

Falsification branches are explicit:

- **Supportive for added M1 value:** positive M1-minus-calibrated-persistence standardized RMSE gain for both assays, uncertainty intervals away from zero, stable positive sign across outcome-blind partitions, and acceptable comparative calibration. This pattern was not observed.
- **Adverse:** nonpositive gains, intervals centered at or below zero, or recurrent partition results favoring calibrated persistence. Albumin is near-null/inconclusive and bilirubin is directionally adverse; together they reject the strong claim that M1 reproducibly adds value beyond calibrated persistence for both assays.
- **Inconclusive:** denominator/clock drift, failed execution, nonestimable resampling, intervals spanning both clinically meaningful benefit and harm, or inability to define an assay-unit clinical margin. No reconstruction failure occurred, but the absence of units and a prespecified clinical/equivalence margin makes clinical magnitude and formal equivalence unresolved.

## Claim limits and required additional evidence

The dataset lacks laboratory units and validated reference ranges, adjudicated hepatic injury or illness, albumin administration, transfusion/fluid records, imaging and tumor burden, procedural details, verified medication administration, treatment rationale, reliable mortality, and outside care. It also lacks an externally validated action threshold or loss function. Therefore neither improved statistical error nor calibration establishes bedside usefulness, safety, treatment response, toxicity, benefit, mechanism, or a treatment effect.

A claim of clinical utility would require assay units and harmonization, expert adjudication of the forecast target and actionable timing, prespecified clinically meaningful error/equivalence margins, decision-curve or cost-sensitive evaluation tied to real decisions, and external or temporal validation. A causal claim would require a separate causal design and additional confounding/adjudication evidence. Automatic verification can check source hashes, denominator and temporal gates, fold leakage, formulas, paired metrics, intervals, partition signs, and whether prose follows the computed outputs; it cannot adjudicate clinical meaning, missing units, exchangeability, or utility.
