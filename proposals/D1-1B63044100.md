# Candidate: Stop systemic anti-MRSA therapy after a finalized negative MRSA surveillance screen in ICU pneumonia?

## Decision, unresolved question, and falsifiable hypothesis

**Decision at issue.** An adult in the ICU is receiving empiric intravenous anti-MRSA therapy for suspected pneumonia. A nasal/surveillance MRSA screen has now finalized as negative, but the patient remains ill. Should anti-MRSA therapy be stopped promptly or continued?

**Strongest claim already supported.** MRSA nasal screening has high negative predictive value in many pneumonia settings, and screening coupled to active stewardship reduces anti-MRSA treatment duration. A 2025 randomized study of result notification reported shorter antibiotic duration and fewer renal injuries without a detected mortality increase, while a 2026 meta-analysis of 15 studies (2,872 patients) found the clearest evidence for reduced treatment duration and trough monitoring; its Bayesian probability of exceeding a clinically important difference was <=36% for harder outcomes. The STOP-Vanc protocol explicitly states that safe reduction in ICU vancomycin use had not previously been tested in a randomized trial. These sources support de-escalation as plausible and exposure-reducing; they do **not** establish that actual discontinuation is causally safe for a heterogeneous US ICU population, that a slow culture-based screen is interchangeable with PCR, or that kidney and mortality benefits are real.

**Unresolved claim to test.** Among adult ICU patients still receiving systemic anti-MRSA therapy for a high-specificity, pre-index phenotype of suspected pneumonia when a negative MRSA surveillance result becomes available, a strategy of stopping anti-MRSA treatment within 24 hours (with rescue for newly available MRSA-positive cultures) reduces 7-day death-or-severe-AKI risk by at least 3 absolute percentage points while increasing 14-day all-cause mortality by less than 3 percentage points, compared with continuing treatment through 48 hours.

This joint hypothesis is falsifiable. It separates the expected renal benefit from the safety claim and uses absolute effects. The clinical advance would be evidence about the treatment decision **after a negative result is available**, especially for culture-era ICU screening and high-acuity patients, rather than another demonstration that ordering a rapid test shortens therapy.

## Target trial protocol

### Population and time zero

Use the first eligible hospitalization per `subject_id`.

Include patients who meet all criteria:

1. Age >=18 at `admittime`, calculated as `anchor_age + year(admittime) - anchor_year`.
2. An ICU stay (`icu/icustays`) is active at time zero.
3. Time zero, `t0`, is the earliest `hosp/microbiologyevents.storetime` during that ICU stay at which a surveillance record (`spec_type_desc` or `test_name` equal to normalized "MRSA SCREEN" or "Staph aureus Screen"/"Staph aureus swab") has an explicit negative result phrase ("No MRSA isolated" or "MRSA negative") in `comments`/`org_name`. Use `storetime`, not specimen `charttime`, because it approximates result availability.
4. At least one systemic anti-MRSA administration in the 24 hours before `t0`: eMAR `medication` normalized to IV vancomycin, linezolid, or ceftaroline; `event_txt` indicates Administered/Started; route is systemic IV in `emar_detail.route`. Vancomycin is primary; include linezolid/ceftaroline only in the class-strategy analysis and report vancomycin-only sensitivity.
5. Suspected pneumonia is present *before t0*: (a) a chest radiology report with `charttime <= t0` and >= `t0-48h`, whose Impression/Findings contains an affirmed pneumonia-compatible term (pneumonia, new/focal consolidation, airspace opacity/disease, or infiltrate) after prespecified sentence-level negation/uncertainty rules; and (b) at least one non-MRSA systemic pneumonia antibiotic administration in `t0-24h` (prelisted ceftriaxone, cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem/imipenem, ampicillin-sulbactam, aztreonam, levofloxacin/moxifloxacin, or azithromycin). Freeze the vocabulary before outcome analysis.
6. At least one serum creatinine measurement in `t0-24h`.
7. Alive, not discharged, and not receiving renal replacement therapy at `t0`.

Exclude a positive MRSA screen or a positive MRSA blood/respiratory/sterile-site result whose `storetime <= t0`; known chronic dialysis; kidney transplant admission; anti-MRSA therapy clearly administered by intrathecal, inhaled, oral, ophthalmic, lock, or enema route; and a radiology report containing only explicitly negated/uncertain compatible terms. Do not use post-t0 information for eligibility. Current-admission pneumonia ICD codes are a sensitivity phenotype only because they are discharge-level and untimed.

### Strategies, assignment, follow-up, estimands

Clone each eligible person at `t0` into both strategies:

- **Prompt stop:** no systemic anti-MRSA administration after `t0+24h` through `t0+48h`. Administrations during the 24-hour grace period are allowed. If a new MRSA-positive respiratory, blood, or sterile-site result becomes available after t0, rescue therapy is permitted from its `storetime`.
- **Continue:** at least one systemic anti-MRSA administration in each interval `(t0,t0+24h]` and `(t0+24h,t0+48h]`. Therapy after 48h is usual care.

Artificially censor each clone when observed care becomes incompatible; estimate stabilized inverse-probability-of-censoring weights in 6-hour intervals. Follow from t0 to 7 days for the primary outcome and 14 days for mortality, censoring only at administrative data end, not at ICU or hospital discharge. Discharge alive before a horizon counts as alive and free of subsequent in-hospital severe AKI, while death after discharge is obtained from `patients.dod`; explicitly report the fraction reliant on this assumption.

Primary estimand: per-protocol 7-day risk difference and risk ratio for death or new KDIGO stage 2/3 AKI under prompt stop versus continue. Key safety estimand: 14-day all-cause mortality risk difference, with +3 percentage points as the noninferiority margin. Secondary estimands: component severe AKI; new RRT; days alive and anti-MRSA-free through day 7; ICU-free and hospital-free days through day 14; subsequent MRSA-positive blood/respiratory culture; and anti-MRSA rescue.

Define baseline creatinine as the latest valid serum creatinine in `t0-24h`; require a sensitivity analysis using the minimum from admission to t0. New stage 2 is >=2.0 times baseline; stage 3 is >=3.0 times baseline, creatinine >=4.0 mg/dL, or new RRT. Exclude those already meeting stage 2/3 relative to admission nadir before t0. New RRT is a post-t0 `procedureevents` start for item IDs 225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809, or 225955.

## Confounding control and analysis

Baseline (`t0-24h`, or all prior history where stated) includes age, gender, race, admission type/location, ICU unit, anchor-year group, time from screen collection to result, anti-MRSA doses/hours before t0, vancomycin level, baseline creatinine and trajectory, urine output, lactate, WBC, platelets, bilirubin, MAP, HR, RR, FiO2, mechanical ventilation, vasopressors, prior MRSA, prior admissions, prior comorbidity, concurrent antibiotics, piperacillin-tazobactam and other nephrotoxins, and missingness indicators. Time-varying censoring models update hemodynamics, vasopressors, ventilation, creatinine, urine output, cultures, and other antibiotics every 6 hours. Continuous values use restricted cubic splines. Multiple imputation is fit without future outcomes; structural absence and measured missingness remain distinct.

Estimate standardized risks with weighted pooled logistic models using robust subject-level variance; truncate weights at 1st/99th percentiles and also report untruncated results. Report flow counts, missingness, adherence, effective sample size, weight distribution, positivity by decile, and standardized differences (<0.10 target). Prespecified heterogeneity is limited to baseline eGFR <45 versus >=45 and concomitant piperacillin-tazobactam, with interaction estimates and multiplicity-aware intervals; subgroup findings are exploratory unless the overall joint criterion is met.

Baselines: raw unadjusted risks; a conventional t0+24h landmark propensity-overlap analysis among survivors; and the clone-censor-weight estimate. Sensitivities: vancomycin-only; eMAR versus prescriptions/inputevents exposure; strict radiology phenotype plus respiratory culture collected pre-t0; pneumonia discharge ICD phenotype; exclude any post-t0 MRSA recovery; 12h and 36h stopping grace; baseline creatinine definitions; complete case versus imputation; and quantitative bias analysis for an unmeasured binary severity factor.

## Feasibility already established (not an outcome analysis)

A full streaming scan counted 3,988,224 microbiology rows, including 77,647 `MRSA SCREEN` specimen rows, and 20,292,611 prescription rows, with systemic vancomycin orders spanning 85,787 admissions. A joined proxy screen found 18,610 adult admissions with a negative MRSA result finalized during an ICU stay; 9,287 had systemic vancomycin prescribed around result availability; 5,324 also had a pneumonia-family discharge code. Prescription-stop proxies split 5,422 versus 3,865 at 24 hours. These are feasibility counts only: final inclusion requires pre-t0 radiology phenotype, actual eMAR administrations, creatinine, and exclusions. The analysis must report every attrition step and may still be underpowered after exact phenotyping.

## Falsification and interpretation

Before effect interpretation, require: at least 100 patients and 25 primary events per strategy after weighting; effective sample size >=50 per strategy; all key baseline SMDs <0.10; no gross positivity violation; and a process contrast of >=24 adjusted vancomycin-free hours alive through day 7. Failure is **inconclusive**, not evidence for either strategy. As temporal negative controls, post-t0 strategy assignment must not materially predict the pre-t0 24-hour creatinine slope or pre-t0 lactate change after weighting (absolute standardized effect <0.10); failure blocks causal language.

- **Supportive:** prompt stop lowers the 7-day death-or-severe-AKI risk by >=3 points, its two-sided 95% CI excludes 0, and the upper 95% bound for the 14-day mortality risk difference is <+3 points, with diagnostics and major sensitivities concordant.
- **Adverse:** the lower 95% bound for mortality difference is >=+3 points, or prompt stop significantly increases culture-proven MRSA progression/rescue with no severe-AKI benefit. A renal benefit with failed mortality noninferiority is adverse for the decision.
- **Evidence against the hypothesized benefit:** a precise interval excludes a 3-point primary benefit while mortality is noninferior; this supports little clinically important renal benefit in this phenotype, not universal equivalence.
- **Inconclusive:** all other patterns, including wide intervals crossing benefit and harm, failed diagnostics, weak exposure separation, phenotype failure, or conflicting eMAR/prescription results.

Harbor can verify cohort logic, timestamps, joins, weights, diagnostics, event counts, estimates, intervals, and whether the submitted conclusion matches this matrix. It cannot verify that the lexical chest-report phenotype is clinically pneumonia, that a screen was PCR versus culture (the dataset does not state assay platform), that clinicians had actually read a result at `storetime`, absence of undocumented outside outcomes, exchangeability, or a practice recommendation. Before clinical inference, infectious-disease/critical-care review and blinded manual adjudication of at least 100 phenotype-positive and 100 phenotype-negative radiology episodes are essential. A multicenter external emulation and preferably a randomized discontinuation trial are required for a clinical mandate.
