# Grip-triggered cystatin C allocation: fair-capacity and frailty-control replication in UK Biobank

## Clinical decision and unresolved claim

A service that can order cystatin C for only 20% of adults with race-free creatinine eGFR 60-89 mL/min/1.73 m2 needs an allocation rule that identifies otherwise undocumented low-filtration reclassification, not merely a model with a better C-statistic. The selected parent made that policy operational: it ranks the pre-assay population, assays exactly the same fraction under each policy, preserves denominators when retrospective cystatin labels are absent, and evaluates both laboratory and subsequent inpatient kidney-event yield in two untouched partitions.

Its strongest remaining threat is attribution. Its grip model has extra terms beyond the baseline model, so a gain could reflect added capacity. Grip is also a broad marker of health and frailty. A higher later hospitalization yield could therefore be generic vulnerability rather than information related to muscle-dependent creatinine. This matters clinically: if walking pace or self-rated health achieves the same assay yield at the same complexity, there is no decision-relevant reason to privilege grip; if grip only selects frailer people, kidney-event enrichment does not validate a muscle mechanism.

The strongest evidence currently supports only this premise: creatinine and cystatin C have different non-GFR determinants, muscle mass can contribute to their discordance, and routinely available characteristics can internally predict discordance in UK Biobank. Yim et al. (PMCID PMC9747759; full text inspected) studied bioimpedance muscle mass and circumferences in 138 Korean participants; it did not measure handgrip and did not use measured GFR. Chen et al. (PMCID PMC10986041; full text inspected) internally predicted creatinine-cystatin eGFR differences in UKB but did not test equal-budget allocation, external validity, measured GFR, or clinical benefit. Thus neither study establishes grip-triggered testing.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> In the inherited pre-assay UKB population and locked partitions, a 20% policy adding one standardized baseline maximum-grip value to age, sex, BMI and eGFRcr will identify at least 2 more combined-equation low-filtration reclassifications per 1,000 assays than the clinical baseline and the best same-one-coefficient generic-function policy selected in development. The gain will replicate, exceed frailty-stratified permutation policies that hold the fitted grip model and assay budget fixed, and enrich a prespecified creatinine-cystatin discordance label. Separately, if event counts permit inference, the grip policy will add at least 1 reclassified person with a subsequent 10-year inpatient N17/N18 event per 1,000 assays versus baseline without being clinically inferior to the generic-function policy.

This child tests whether grip contains participant-specific allocation information beyond model dimension and two available frailty proxies. It does not test whether grip is muscle mass, whether cystatin eGFR is true GFR, or whether testing prevents an event.

## Estimands and analysis

For policy S in a partition:

Y_H(S)=1000*sum(S*H)/n, Y_D(S)=1000*sum(S*D)/n, and Y_HK(S)=1000*sum(S*H*K)/n.

The inherited clinical allocation estimand remains Delta_H(G:B)=Y_H(G)-Y_H(B) with ratio Y_H(G)/Y_H(B). Grip must produce at least 2 additional H per 1,000 assays, ratio at least 1.25, and simultaneous two-sided 95% paired intervals excluding 0 and 1 respectively in each locked partition.

The fair-comparator estimand is Delta_H(G:F). Require at least 2 additional H per 1,000 and a simultaneous 95% paired interval above 0 in each locked partition. Decompose both contrasts exactly into equal-size G-only gains minus comparator-only losses; report overlap and Jaccard index. A positive aggregate yield with nonpositive G-only-versus-F-only yield is adverse.

Attribution has two gates. First, in each partition actual Delta_H(G:B) must exceed the 97.5th percentile of the 500 frailty-conditioned placebo differences; report the empirical p value (1+#placebo>=actual)/501. Second, Delta_D(G:F) must be positive in both locked partitions and its prespecified pooled, partition-stratified paired 95% interval must exceed 0. These results would show added participant-level biochemical-discordance information beyond equal dimension and the selected available generic-function proxy. They would not establish muscle mass as the mechanism.

The key decision-relevance estimand remains Delta_HK(G:B). If event sufficiency passes, require at least 1 additional H*K case per 1,000 assays and a pooled paired 95% interval above 0. Against F, require noninferiority: the one-sided 95% lower bound for Delta_HK(G:F) must exceed -1 per 1,000. Superiority to F is reported only if the two-sided interval exceeds 0. Kidney-event yield is policy-specific enrichment, not benefit from testing and not a mechanistic test.

Use 2,000 paired participant bootstrap resamples within partition; pooled intervals resample within locked partition and preserve partition strata. Models are not refit. For the two primary H contrasts and their ratios use a max-|t| simultaneous bootstrap interval, with percentile fallback if studentization fails. Also report H and D prevalence, sensitivity, PPV, selected-count calibration, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score and decision curves expressed per 1,000 assays. A G calibration slope outside 0.8-1.2 in either partition blocks a supportive classification.

Report baseline pace, health, age, sex, BMI, eGFRcr and standardized grip in G-only, B-only and F-only sets. As a generic-vulnerability diagnostic, report 10-year death without prior K among H-positive selections and its G:B and G:F contrasts. This diagnostic has no automatic mechanistic threshold; excess non-kidney death warns that generic frailty remains plausible.

For K, estimate 10-year Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence with death competing in each selected, overlap and unique set. Cause-specific Cox and Fine-Gray models are explanatory only. They cannot replace assay-yield estimands or imply an intervention effect.

## Event sufficiency, falsification and result meanings

Before interpreting H*K, require at least 100 H*K events in G union B union F across both locked partitions, at least 20 in each of G-only versus B-only, B-only versus G-only, G-only versus F-only and F-only versus G-only pooled swap sets, and at least 30 in a partition for any positive partition-specific statement. If a condition fails, report point estimates and bounds but classify clinical relevance inconclusive; do not alter endpoints or pool development outcomes.

Mandatory falsification checks are exact 20% counts; no participant overlap; one-to-one joins; exact code/date pairing; no locked-label access before freeze; formula fixtures; no outcome predictor; and deterministic tie/permutation reproduction. Relative grip is permuted only within prespecified strata and all 500 policies retain n. Systematic actual performance inside the placebo distribution is adverse to participant-specific grip value.

Repeat G with maximum, mean and minimum hand definitions; the parent's richer raw-grip model; complete-valid pace/health; absolute discordance below -15; one- and two-year outcome lags; and 10%/30% budgets. A sign reversal across hand definitions, benefit confined to poor-health/slow-walking strata, a G-only set with no higher H or D than F-only, or a result driven by one locked partition is adverse or unstable. Sensitivities cannot rescue a failed primary 20% result.

Result classifications are prespecified:

- Fully supportive: all G:B and G:F H thresholds pass in both partitions under adverse missing-label bounds and simultaneous intervals; G exceeds the frailty-conditioned placebo distribution in both; pooled D attribution passes with concordant partition signs; calibration and falsification checks pass; and, after event sufficiency, H*K beats B and is noninferior to F. This supports prospective comparison of three selective-testing policies, not routine adoption.
- Biochemical-only supportive: every H and attribution gate passes, but H*K is sparse or its interval is inconclusive. Grip then improves biochemical allocation beyond tested complexity/frailty controls, while clinical consequence remains unresolved.
- Adverse: G has Delta_H<=0, ratio<=1, misses either inherited materiality threshold, fails to beat F or placebo, has nonpositive pooled D attribution, produces inferior H*K versus B when sufficiently powered, fails swap decomposition, or shows reproducible leakage/calibration failure. A strong W/Q result is informative evidence for a cheaper generic-function policy, not a failed experiment.
- Inconclusive: intervals cross decision boundaries; partitions disagree; more than 1% of O lacks cystatin; adverse sharp bounds fail; event rules fail; any denominator is zero; code/date integrity is unresolved; F selection is not frozen; or hand-definition/frailty-stratum dependence prevents a stable interpretation.

Aggregate feasibility, overall association, AUC improvement, or a pooled positive point estimate alone can never trigger support.
