## Decision, unresolved question, and claim boundary

Prioritize the oxygen design. The actionable question is not whether “hyperoxia is harmful” in general, nor whether a low oxygen target reduces mortality. It is: **among adults currently receiving invasive mechanical ventilation who have stable SpO2 at or above 97% on FiO2 0.40–0.60, does a protocol-compatible FiO2 decrease of at least 5 percentage points within 60 minutes reduce the next-six-hour burden of clearly unnecessary oxygen, without increasing sustained hypoxemia or rescue escalation, compared with not promptly decreasing FiO2?**

The formal hypothesis is joint:

1. prompt de-escalation reduces time with both SpO2 >=97% and FiO2 >=0.40 by at least 30 minutes during minutes 60–360 after eligibility; and
2. prompt de-escalation is non-inferior for six-hour sustained hypoxemia/rescue, with a risk-difference margin of +2.0 percentage points.

The strongest existing evidence supports only a narrower claim: oxygen-target interventions can materially change oxygen exposure, while large randomized trials have not established a general ventilator-free-day or mortality benefit from conservative targets. In the ICU-ROX abstract (PMID 31613432; DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1903297), conservative therapy reduced time above SpO2 96% but did not improve 28-day ventilator-free days. The HOT-ICU abstract (PMID 33471452; DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2032510) reported similar 90-day mortality with PaO2 targets of 60 and 90 mm Hg. The prematurely stopped LOCO2 abstract (PMID 32160661; DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1916431) supplies a safety warning against treating lower oxygen as automatically better. These were inspected as focused PubMed abstract extractions, not full articles. Frozen web provenance IDs are `[source checksum]`, `[source checksum]`, and `[source checksum]`.

The unresolved claim is the joint hypothesis above for a *specific bedside response to a stable hyperoxemic state*. This is a substantive advance over associating “ever hyperoxemic” with mortality: it aligns eligibility, action, and follow-up at an actual decision point, estimates a near-term safety/effectiveness tradeoff, and asks whether effects transport across hospitals with different titration practices. EICU can support that observational estimate; it cannot establish a mortality mandate, biological oxygen toxicity, the clinician’s reason for changing FiO2, or equivalence to random assignment.

## Bias audit and mandatory repairs

| Threat | How the naive design fails | Required repair |
|---|---|---|
| Immortal time | Labeling patients “titrated” if FiO2 ever falls requires survival and continued observation until the fall. | Time zero is the first eligible decision timestamp. Clone each eligible patient into both strategies at time zero; apply a 60-minute grace period, artificial censoring at strategy deviation, and inverse-probability-of-artificial-censoring weights. Follow-up starts at time zero for safety; effectiveness is summarized from minute 60. No “ever treated” groups. |
| Reverse causation | Improving gas exchange causes both FiO2 reduction and better outcomes; deterioration prevents reduction. | Require a stable safety-eligible state; adjust only for pre-time-zero levels and slopes of oxygenation, ventilator settings, hemodynamics, vasopressors, and blood gases. Do not use post-time-zero severity. Run a pre-exposure negative-control outcome. |
| Measurement-density bias | Frequently monitored patients have more chances to document both titration and desaturation. Missing FiO2 is not “no titration,” and missing SpO2 is not “normal.” | Model treatment-documentation and outcome-bin observation; censor unknown treatment status and weight for observation. Include pre-index charting density. Report arm-specific observed-bin fractions. Do not impute normal oxygenation. |
| Ventilation misclassification | FiO2/PEEP also occur with NIV; `ventstartoffset`/`ventendoffset` contain many zeros and cannot alone define an invasive interval. | Use a high-specificity, time-local composite of explicit invasive airway plus invasive settings. Repeat with strict and broad classifiers; manually adjudicate a blinded sample. |
| Outcome leakage | APACHE “actual” outcomes, untimed APACHE worst values, later diagnoses, revised later labs, or future extubation can enter baseline models. | Freeze all covariates at time zero by event offset. Exclude all `actual*`, `predicted*`, and untimed APACHE physiology from treatment/censoring/outcome models. Never exclude eligibility based on what happens after time zero. |
| Hospital practice confounding | Hospitals differ in oxygen protocols, documentation, case mix, staffing, and outcomes. | Estimate a within-hospital effect using hospital fixed effects/strata, hospital-specific positivity gates, and hospital-clustered bootstrap uncertainty. Report leave-one-hospital-out and hospital-specific effects, not merely a pooled patient-level model. |

## Target trial

### Population and time zero

Use one decision trial per `patientunitstayid`: the first qualifying timestamp from 360 through 4,320 minutes after ICU admission. Include:

- adult age >=18 (map the EICU string `> 89` to 90 only for adjustment);
- `unitvisitnumber=1`;
- high-specificity invasive ventilation at that timestamp;
- current normalized FiO2 0.40–0.60 from an exact `respchartvaluelabel == "FiO2"` row;
- at least two `vitalPeriodic.sao2` readings >=97%, 5–15 minutes apart, in the preceding 15 minutes, and no reading <94% in the preceding 30 minutes;
- latest PEEP <=10 cm H2O, with no FiO2 increase >=5 percentage points or PEEP increase >=2 cm H2O in the preceding 30 minutes;
- latest MAP >=65 mm Hg in the preceding 15 minutes and no MAP <60 in the preceding 30 minutes, using invasive `systemicmean` preferentially and noninvasive `noninvasivemean` otherwise;
- no end-of-life limitation documented by time zero: exclude when `cpleoldiscussionoffset > 0` and `cpleoldiscussionoffset <= time zero`, or when `cpleolsaveoffset > 0` and `cpleolsaveoffset <= time zero`; treat offset 0 as missing/unknown rather than proof of a time-zero discussion.

The stability restrictions deliberately target the population in whom a reduction is genuinely discretionary. They trade generalizability for less reverse causation and greater safety. Do not require a future FiO2 or SpO2 measurement for eligibility, do not exclude later extubation/death, and do not use a PaO2 drawn after time zero.

Normalize FiO2 values in 0.21–1.00 by multiplying by 100; retain values 21–100 and express them as fractions thereafter. Resolve duplicate rows at an event offset by the largest chart ID, then the latest entry offset no later than the analytic data cutoff; record all duplicate-resolution counts.

**High-specificity invasive-ventilation classifier:** latest `respiratoryCare.airwaytype` at or before time zero and no more than 1,440 minutes old is one of `Oral ETT`, `Nasal ETT`, `Tracheostomy`, `Double-Lumen Tube`, or `Cricothyrotomy`; and both PEEP plus either `Tidal Volume (set)` or `Vent Rate` are charted within +/-15 minutes. A later airway record must not already say `No Artificial Airway`. Do not treat `ventendoffset=0` as a valid extubation time. Strict sensitivity requires Oral/Nasal ETT or tracheostomy and all three settings within +/-10 minutes. Broad sensitivity accepts explicit invasive airway plus PEEP within +/-30 minutes. NIV-only labels, including `Non-invasive Ventilation Mode`, are excluded.

### Strategies and assignment emulation

At time zero, clone each patient to:

- **Prompt decrease:** decrease FiO2 by >=5 percentage points from the time-zero value by minute 60, without decreasing below 0.30. A transient rescue-trigger state (any SpO2 <92 or MAP <60 after time zero) permits delay; the clone remains adherent if reduction occurs within 30 minutes after recovery and by minute 90.
- **No prompt decrease:** do not decrease FiO2 by >=5 points during the first 60 minutes. Clinically required increases are allowed.

Use the first valid FiO2 setting 5–60 minutes after time zero to detect adherence; a prompt clone is censored at minute 60 if no qualifying decrease, and a control clone is censored at the first qualifying decrease. If no FiO2 is charted, treatment is unknown—not control—and both relevant clones are censored at minute 60 with observation weights. After minute 60 (or 90 for protocol-permitted rescue delay), care is unrestricted.

This is an observational per-protocol estimand: the average effect among the safety-eligible EICU population under measured exchangeability, consistency, positivity, correct classifier, and missing-at-random assumptions. A secondary landmark analysis among those alive and still ventilated at minute 60 is descriptive only because it conditions on post-baseline survival/ventilation.

### Outcomes and temporal boundaries

Co-primary outcomes:

1. **Unnecessary-oxygen burden:** minutes during time-zero +60 through +360 in which FiO2 >=0.40 and SpO2 >=97%. Construct a 5-minute grid. Use the median SpO2 in a bin; interpolate only between readings no more than 10 minutes apart. Carry FiO2 forward for at most 120 minutes. Bins outside those rules are missing, not normal. After a positively adjudicated extubation, invasive-oxygen burden is zero but follow-up for safety continues.
2. **Six-hour safety composite:** first occurrence from time zero through +360 of (a) sustained hypoxemia—two SpO2 readings <90% separated by 5–15 minutes—or (b) PaO2 <60 mm Hg, or (c) rescue escalation—FiO2 >=10 percentage points above baseline or PEEP >=3 cm H2O above baseline. Report all components separately; management escalation is not itself proof of physiologic injury.

Secondary outcomes are SpO2 area below 92%, time to first rescue, six-hour extubation, ICU mortality and hospital mortality. Longer outcomes are explicitly exploratory because a single short titration decision is weakly identified for mortality and because EICU lacks post-discharge 28/90-day vital status. `unitdischargeoffset`, `unitdischargestatus`, and `hospitaldischargestatus` may define those exploratory outcomes but never baseline covariates. Do not use `apachePatientResult.actualventdays` or other `actual*` fields in the primary analysis.

### Baseline variables and confounding control

Use only values at or before time zero:

- demographics and context: age, gender, ethnicity, admission weight, ICU type/source/stay type, admission diagnosis, hours since ICU admission;
- respiratory state over the prior two hours: FiO2, SpO2, PEEP, set/observed tidal volume, vent rate, mode when available, number of observations, current value, minimum/maximum, and robust slope;
- hemodynamics over the prior two hours: MAP, heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature and their slopes/measurement counts;
- most recent pre-index PaO2, PaCO2, pH, lactate, creatinine and hemoglobin within 24 hours, plus age of measurement;
- vasopressor exposure and dose trajectory during the prior two hours, separately for norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, and dopamine;
- pre-index diagnoses and treatment markers only when their recorded offset <= time zero;
- hospital ID and ICU unit type.

Do not use `apacheApsVar` physiology in the primary model because it has no event time and can summarize measurements after time zero. `apachePatientResult` is outcome-derived and barred from all adjustment.

Fit pooled logistic models for adherence/artificial censoring and observation, stratified by hospital with flexible splines and prespecified interactions for current FiO2, PEEP, SpO2 slope, PaO2/FiO2 when available, vasopressor dose, and time since admission. Use stabilized weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentile; report untruncated results. Estimate weighted risk differences for safety and weighted mean differences for oxygen burden with a doubly robust outcome regression. Obtain 95% intervals from 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples. If fewer than 10 eligible hospitals remain, use a patient bootstrap clustered by hospital and label transport/hospital uncertainty inconclusive.

Prespecified effect modifiers are ARDS-compatible diagnosis before time zero, COPD history, sepsis diagnosis before time zero, baseline FiO2 0.40–0.49 versus 0.50–0.60, and neurologic versus non-neurologic admission. Interaction estimates are exploratory with false-discovery-rate control; they cannot establish a personalized protocol.

## Concrete falsification and validity gates

The analysis is not interpretable as a target-trial estimate unless all gates pass:

1. **Feasibility/positivity:** >=1,000 eligible stays, >=200 observed prompt decreases, >=10 hospitals with both strategies, and weighted effective sample size >=500 per strategy. Within each included hospital require >=10 patients per observed strategy; otherwise pool only for description.
2. **Balance:** after weighting, every prespecified covariate has absolute standardized mean difference <0.10 and the five core variables (FiO2, SpO2 slope, PEEP, MAP, vasopressor dose) each <0.05. Failure after model revision falsifies measured exchangeability.
3. **Weights:** <1% of observations have an unstabilized weight >20 and neither strategy loses >50% effective sample size. Otherwise the causal contrast is non-identifiable in these data.
4. **Measurement:** >=80% of 5-minute SpO2 bins and >=70% of FiO2 bins are observed under the stated rules in each strategy; adjusted between-strategy differences in coverage must be <5 percentage points. Missing action status by minute 60 must be <30%. Failure makes the physiologic endpoint inconclusive.
5. **Ventilation adjudication:** blinded clinician review of 200 randomly sampled eligibility episodes (100 classifier-positive, 100 near-miss/alternative classifier), using only notes/charting up to time zero, must yield positive predictive value >=90% and kappa >=0.80 for invasive ventilation. Below either threshold falsifies the primary classifier; this cannot be automated honestly.
6. **Temporal negative control:** replace each patient’s six-hour outcome by the homologous burden/safety summary in the two hours *before* time zero. After adjustment, the prompt-strategy label must have an absolute standardized mean difference <0.10 for burden and a safety risk ratio whose 95% interval includes 1.0 and lies within 0.80–1.25. A residual temporal association falsifies the causal interpretation, though not the descriptive association.
7. **Hospital robustness:** leave-one-hospital-out estimates must retain the same sign for oxygen burden in >=90% of runs; no single hospital may contribute >25% of total weight. For safety, substantial directional heterogeneity (I-squared >75% with hospitals on both sides of the +2% margin) makes the pooled safety claim inconclusive.
8. **Classifier/specification robustness:** strict and broad ventilation classifiers, FiO2 carry-forward limits of 60 and 120 minutes, and SpO2 thresholds of 96 and 97 must agree in direction. A sign reversal of oxygen burden or >2-point shift in safety risk difference is a failed robustness check.
9. **Null/sham action check:** a 1–4 percentage-point FiO2 change, below normal charting increments and the treatment definition, should not reduce the oxygen-burden endpoint by >=30 minutes after adjustment. If it does, documentation cadence rather than treatment likely drives the result.

**Supportive result:** oxygen-burden mean difference <= -30 minutes with its 95% upper bound <0, *and* the upper 95% bound for safety risk difference is <+2.0 percentage points, with all validity gates passed.

**Adverse result:** the lower 95% bound for safety risk difference is >+2.0 points, or sustained hypoxemia itself has a lower 95% bound >0, regardless of oxygen-burden reduction. This argues against routine prompt reduction in this target population, not against all conservative oxygen practice.

**Falsification of benefit:** the oxygen-burden point estimate is >-30 minutes or its interval includes 0 while safety is not clearly better. This rejects the prespecified clinically meaningful joint hypothesis; it does not prove equivalence.

**Inconclusive:** either co-primary interval spans both clinically favorable and adverse regions, any validity gate fails, or direction depends materially on hospital/classifier. Report this as inability of EICU to resolve the question, not a null effect.

Only the cohort construction, exposure classification, covariate timing, weights, outcomes, contrasts, uncertainty, and thresholds are computationally checkable. Whether a desaturation was clinically acceptable, why FiO2 changed, whether airway classification is correct, and whether a +2% margin is acceptable require blinded clinical adjudication/expert review. Mortality benefit, long-term neurocognitive safety, and protocol adoption require a prospective randomized study with 28/90-day follow-up.

## Competing actionable design and prioritization

**Competitor: prompt norepinephrine up-titration for sustained hypotension.** In adults already receiving norepinephrine, define time zero as the first two MAP values <65 mm Hg 5–15 minutes apart while dose has been stable for 30 minutes; compare a >=20% dose increase within 15 minutes with holding the dose for 15 minutes, using the same clone–censor–weight framework. The primary response is MAP >=65 for at least 30 minutes during minutes 15–60; safety is heart rate >130, new tachyarrhythmia markers, or dose escalation of a second vasopressor. Bind `infusionDrug.infusionoffset, drugname, drugrate, infusionrate, patientweight` to `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset, systemicmean, heartrate` and `vitalAperiodic.observationoffset, noninvasivemean`, with `lab.labresultoffset, labname, labresult` for pre-index lactate/pH and `patient.hospitalid` for hospital strata. A direct scan verified 580,856 norepinephrine/Levophed rows in 19,313 stays, all with nonempty `drugrate`.

This competitor is clinically consequential but lower priority. Drug names encode incompatible units (for example mcg/min versus mcg/kg/min), co-titration and fluid boluses are incompletely identified, MAP indication changes faster than the 15-minute exposure window, and arrhythmia adjudication is weak. Before candidacy it needs unit-specific normalization, a matched MAP feasibility pass, and falsification gates analogous to those above. The oxygen branch has a cleaner action, dense physiologic endpoint, and more defensible near-term safety estimand; neither branch should claim mortality causality.

The source files remain read-only. The schema guide, relevant table JSONs, raw CSV headers, representative rows, respiratory label frequencies, airway values, norepinephrine frequency, the existing branch script `oxygen_feasibility.py`, and its output `oxygen_feasibility.json` were inspected locally. That existing permissive screen found 75,234 stays with exact-label FiO2, 73,190 with both valid FiO2 and SpO2, and 31,034 stays with at least three preceding SpO2 values having median >=96 at a FiO2 0.50–1.00 moment in the first 48 hours. It did not apply this proposal’s age, first-stay, high-specificity invasive-airway, hemodynamic-stability, FiO2 0.40–0.60, or first-episode rules, so these are feasibility counts—not the final cohort.
