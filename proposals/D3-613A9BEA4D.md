# Within-macrophage inflammatory state, not composition, drives the macrophage-associated signal

## Question and clinical importance

In rheumatoid arthritis, macrophage-associated inflammatory signals are often interpreted as evidence of macrophage recruitment or abundance. However, the same signal could instead reflect a shift in within-macrophage inflammatory state. Resolving this distinction is clinically consequential: if the signal is driven by composition, interventions should target macrophage recruitment or tissue infiltration; if it is driven by within-cell state, interventions should target intracellular signaling or macrophage reprogramming.

## Hypothesis

The macrophage-associated inflammatory signal in E-MTAB-8322/8873 is better explained by within-macrophage inflammatory state than by macrophage abundance/composition. A donor-aware model trained on macrophage-only pseudobulk expression (E-MTAB-8322) will predict the inflammatory signal in the targeted macrophage–FLS coculture data (E-MTAB-8873) better than a composition-only baseline.

## Falsification criteria

- **Supportive:** The within-state model explains a significantly larger fraction of donor-level variance than the composition-only baseline, with non-overlapping bootstrap confidence intervals.
- **Adverse:** The composition-only baseline explains a significantly larger fraction of donor-level variance than the within-state model.
- **Inconclusive:** The two models explain similar fractions of donor-level variance with overlapping confidence intervals, or the donor-level split yields too few independent donors for reliable comparison.

## Unresolved objections and limitations

- The `cells` field is a cell count per sample, not a whole-tissue composition measurement; the composition-only baseline is a proxy.
- The 445-feature panel in E-MTAB-8873 is not a whole-transcriptome measurement; cross-dataset validation is conditional on sufficient feature overlap.
- Multi-donor samples in E-MTAB-8873 require a donor-aware model; ordinary donor-level inference is disabled.
- SA149/SA149P relatedness in E-MTAB-8322 is unresolved; these samples must be excluded from simple independent-donor screens.
- Cross-sectional disease/treatment groups do not establish longitudinal transitions.
