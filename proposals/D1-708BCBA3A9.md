# Hypothesis: Hypotension Duration Predicts Acute Kidney Injury in ICU Patients

## Scientific Question

**Primary Question:** Does the **duration of hypotension** (defined as systolic blood pressure < 70 mmHg) below a critical threshold predict subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI) in mechanically ventilated patients, independent of minimum blood pressure, vasopressor use, and severity of illness?

This question addresses a critical clinical uncertainty: while minimum blood pressure has been used as a proxy for hemodynamic stability, it fails to capture the temporal burden of hypotension. The duration of hypotension may be a more stable and clinically actionable risk measure than minimum blood pressure alone.

## Clinical Importance

Acute kidney injury (AKI) occurs in 20-40% of ICU patients and is associated with:
- Increased mortality (2-3x higher)
- Longer hospital stays and ICU length of stay
- Higher healthcare costs
- Long-term complications including chronic kidney disease

Understanding risk factors for AKI is essential for:
- Early identification of high-risk patients
- Targeted interventions to prevent AKI
- Optimization of hemodynamic management strategies

## Hypothesis Statement

**Primary hypothesis:** Among mechanically ventilated patients, longer duration of hypotension (SBP < 70 mmHg) below a critical threshold predicts subsequent AKI, independent of minimum blood pressure, vasopressor use, and APACHE severity score.

**Expected effect:** Each additional hour of hypotension below SBP < 70 mmHg increases AKI risk by approximately 5-10%, after adjustment for confounders.

## Key References (Three Inspected Works)

### K1: Lehman et al. (2010)
- **Claim:** Each additional hour of hypotension (MAP < 70, 60, or 50 mmHg) increases AKI risk by 2%, 5%, and 22% respectively
- **Contribution:** Establishes that hypotension duration (not just severity) is an independent predictor of AKI
- **Limitation:** Used MIMIC II; focused on MAP rather than SBP; limited adjustment for vasopressors
- **Source:** [Computing in Cardiology, 2010](doi:10.1016/j.compbiomed.2010.01.004)

### K2: Izawa et al. (2016)
- **Claim:** Early-phase cumulative hypotension duration is associated with progression to severe-stage AKI
- **Contribution:** Investigates cumulative hypotension duration specifically in the early phase of AKI
- **Limitation:** Single-center Japan study; focused on oliguric AKI only
- **Source:** [Critical Care, 2016](doi:10.1186/s13054-016-1564-2)

### K3: Bagshaw et al. (2009)
- **Claim:** Duration of hypotension prior to antimicrobial therapy impacts clinical outcomes in septic shock
- **Contribution:** Examines hypotension duration in septic shock context, providing mechanistic insight
- **Limitation:** Focused on septic shock subset; limited generalizability
- **Source:** [Intensive Care Medicine, 2009](doi:10.1007/s00134-008-1367-2)

## Study Population

- **Population:** Adult patients in eICU-CRD with:
  - Mechanical ventilation (respiratoryCare table)
  - Continuous vital sign monitoring (vitalPeriodic table)
  - Lab measurements including creatinine (lab table)
  - Vasopressor administration (infusionDrug table)

- **Inclusion criteria:**
  - Age ≥ 18 years
  - ICU stay ≥ 48 hours
  - SBP recorded ≥ 3 times

- **Exclusion criteria:**
  - Pre-existing CKD (pastHistory table)
  - Prior AKI before ICU admission
  - Missing > 20% of vital sign data

## Main Data and Variables

### Primary Exposure: Hypotension Duration
- **Definition:** Time spent with SBP < 70 mmHg
- **Measurement:** Sum of consecutive intervals in vitalPeriodic
- **Time unit:** Hours
- **Source:** `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz` (table-a22c6d6981a32279.json)
  - Columns: patientunitstayid, timestep, systolic, diastolic, mean, heartrate, spo2, temperature, device, patient

### Primary Outcome: Acute Kidney Injury
- **Definition:** KDIGO AKI stage ≥ 1 within 48 hours of ICU admission
- **Measurement:** Creatinine increase ≥ 0.3 mg/dL or ≥ 50% from baseline
- **Source:** `lab.csv.gz` (table-79bdb33275339b1a.json)
  - Columns: patientunitstayid, timestamp, measure, measurename, system, value, unit

### Covariates
1. **Minimum Blood Pressure:** Minimum SBP during ICU stay
2. **Vasopressor Use:** Nephrotoxic vasopressors (norepinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine)
3. **APACHE II Score:** From apachePredVar.csv.gz
4. **Hospital:** From hospital.csv.gz
5. **Admission Diagnosis:** From admissionDx.csv.gz

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **falsified** if:
1. No association between hypotension duration and AKI after adjustment (p > 0.05)
2. Hypotension duration shows weaker association than minimum SBP (β_duration < β_minSBP)
3. Association entirely explained by vasopressor use
4. Effect is heterogeneous across hospitals (significant interaction)

## Supportive Results

Supportive evidence would include:
1. Independent association between hypotension duration and AKI (p < 0.001)
2. Hypotension duration shows stronger association than minimum SBP
3. Association persists after adjusting for vasopressor use and severity
4. Consistent effect across hospitals

## Scientific Advance Over Existing Knowledge

This study advances beyond existing research by:
1. **Temporal dimension:** Moving from minimum/maximum BP to duration as a risk measure
2. **Multivariate adjustment:** Controlling for vasopressor nephrotoxicity
3. **External validity:** Testing consistency across multiple hospitals
4. **Clinical actionability:** Duration is potentially more modifiable than minimum BP
