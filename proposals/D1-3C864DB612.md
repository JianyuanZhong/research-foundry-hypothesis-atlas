# Interhospital Transfer and ICU Mortality: Does the Association Persist After Matching on Organ Injury at Arrival?

## A Falsifiable, Data-Bound Hypothesis for eICU-CRD (evolved from expert seed eicu-07)

---

### 1. Hypothesis

**Leading claim (to be tested):** The crude association between interhospital transfer and increased ICU mortality in the eICU-CRD is largely explained by greater organ injury at arrival. After propensity score matching on a composite of organ injury at arrival (APACHE organ score + key laboratory markers), the mortality difference between transferred and directly-admitted patients becomes statistically non-significant or is attenuated by ≥50% relative to the crude estimate.

**Strongest rival:** The transfer-mortality association is not merely a severity artifact. Transferred patients have a genuine excess mortality risk attributable to the transfer process itself (transport-related physiological stress, delayed definitive care, loss of continuity) or to unmeasured confounders (pre-arrival disease trajectory, source hospital quality, transport duration) that are not captured by arrival-time organ injury scores. Under this rival, matching on arrival severity will leave a persistent, statistically significant excess mortality in transferred patients (adjusted OR > 1.20 with 95% CI excluding 1.0).

**What the two claims predict differently:**
- If the leading claim is correct: crude OR > 1.5, but post-matching OR ≈ 1.0 (CI includes 1.0), and the APACHE-adjusted model shows a large attenuation (>50% reduction in log-OR).
- If the rival is correct: crude OR > 1.5, post-matching OR remains > 1.20 (CI excludes 1.0), and the attenuation is modest (<30% reduction in log-OR).
- Inconclusive: post-matching OR between 1.0 and 1.20 with CI spanning 1.0, or matching fails (insufficient overlap, >30% of transferred patients unmatched).

### 2. Scientific Opening

**Established:**
- Interhospital transfer of critically ill patients is common: in eICU-CRD, 8,587 of 200,859 ICU admissions (4.3%) were from "Other Hospital" or "Other ICU" [K2].
- The only systematic review of interhospital ICU-to-ICU transfer outcomes (Brock 2026) is restricted to COVID-19 patients: 9 studies, ~6,100 transferred vs 29,200 non-transferred, pooled adjusted OR 1.29 (95% CI 0.84–1.98) — no significant mortality disadvantage [K1]. The authors explicitly note that "pre-pandemic data from general ICU populations suggested that inter-hospital transfer may be associated with longer ICU stays and higher mortality, although these associations were generally attenuated or absent after adjustment for illness severity" [K1].
- A small prospective cohort (Yüce Aktepe 2026, n=331, single center, Turkey) found external ICU transfer independently associated with mortality (OR 3.26, 95% CI 1.70–6.26) among internal medicine patients, but this was a single-center study with a specific patient population (deteriorating internal medicine patients) and did not use propensity matching or organ-injury composite matching [K3].

**Unresolved:**
- No large, multi-center, non-pandemic study has tested whether the transfer-mortality association in a general ICU population persists after rigorous matching on organ injury at arrival. The Brock 2026 review [K1] explicitly identifies this as a gap: the included studies were all COVID-19 specific, and the pre-pandemic literature is heterogeneous and underpowered.
- The eICU-CRD [K2] provides 8,587 transferred patients across 208 hospitals (2014–2015, pre-pandemic), with APACHE scores, laboratory data, and vital signs — sufficient to construct an organ-injury composite and perform propensity score matching at a scale not previously attempted for this question.
- The Yüce Aktepe finding [K3] (OR 3.26) raises the possibility that the association is real and not merely a severity artifact, but the small single-center design and lack of rigorous matching prevent generalization.

**Why this matters:** If the association is entirely a severity artifact, it supports the safety of interhospital transfer as a capacity-management strategy (consistent with the COVID-19 evidence [K1]) and suggests that transfer decisions can be made based on clinical need without mortality penalty. If a genuine excess persists, it identifies a potentially modifiable risk (transport-related harm) that could inform transfer protocols, transport team staffing, and patient selection for transfer.

### 3. Study Population

**Source:** eICU-CRD v2.0, 200,859 ICU admissions, 208 US hospitals, 2014–2015 (pre-pandemic) [K2].

**Inclusion criteria:**
- All ICU admissions (any unit type: ICU, SDU, NICU excluded if present)
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Available APACHE score (apachePatientResult table)
- Available admission source (unitadmitsource)

**Exclusion criteria:**
- ICU length of stay < 24 hours (too short for meaningful outcome assessment; estimated ~5% of cohort)
- Missing unitdischargestatus (outcome)
- Missing APACHE total score (required for severity matching)

**Transfer definition:**
- **Transferred group:** unitadmitsource ∈ {"Other Hospital", "Other ICU"} (n ≈ 8,587)
- **Non-transferred group (comparator):** unitadmitsource ∈ {"Emergency Department", "Floor", "Direct", "Operating Room", "Recovery Room", "Acute Care", "SDU", "ICU", "PACU", "Other"} — all other admission sources (n ≈ 192,272)

**Rationale for comparator choice:** Using all non-transfer sources maximizes sample size and captures the full range of non-transferred admissions. Sensitivity analysis will restrict the comparator to ED + Floor + Direct (the most clinically comparable non-transfer pathways, excluding post-surgical and intra-hospital transfers).

**Outcome:** ICU mortality, defined as unitdischargestatus = "Expired" (n ≈ 10,907 overall, 5.4%).

**Time window:** Full ICU stay (admission to discharge/death). The 24-hour minimum stay exclusion removes patients who died or were discharged before a meaningful post-admission window.

### 5. Analysis Plan

#### 5a. Descriptive Analysis
- Crude ICU mortality by transfer status (transferred vs non-transferred)
- Baseline characteristics table: age, gender, APACHE total/organ/age scores, key labs at arrival, ICU LOS, by transfer status
- Mortality by transfer status within strata of APACHE total score (0–10, 11–20, 21–30, >30) to visualize the severity gradient
- Hospital-level transfer rates and mortality (distribution across 208 hospitals)

#### 5b. Propensity Score Matching (Primary Analysis)
- **Propensity score model:** Logistic regression predicting transfer status from:
  - APACHE total score (continuous)
  - APACHE organ score (continuous)
  - Age (continuous)
  - Gender (binary)
  - Key arrival labs (creatinine, platelet, INR, bilirubin, lactate, glucose — each continuous, with missingness indicator)
  - MAP at arrival (first 6h mean, continuous)
  - Heart rate at arrival (first 6h mean, continuous)
  - ICD-9 major diagnosis category (top 10 most common, as categorical)
- **Matching:** 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching with caliper = 0.2 × SD of logit propensity score. No replacement.
- **Balance check:** Standardized mean difference < 0.1 for all covariates post-matching.
- **Primary estimate:** Risk difference and odds ratio for ICU mortality in the matched cohort, with 95% CI.
- **Expected matched sample:** ~6,000–7,000 transferred patients matched (assuming 70–80% overlap).

#### 5c. Multilevel Logistic Regression (Secondary Analysis)
- **Model:** ICU mortality ~ transfer status + APACHE total score + age + gender + key labs + hospital (random intercept)
- **Random intercept:** hospitalid (208 levels) to account for clustering of patients within hospitals and unmeasured hospital-level factors (transfer practices, staffing, case mix)
- **Estimate:** Adjusted odds ratio for transfer status with 95% CI, accounting for hospital-level heterogeneity
- **Variance decomposition:** Proportion of variance attributable to hospital level vs patient level

#### 5d. Model Comparison
| Model | Description | Purpose |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| M0 | Crude (unadjusted) | Baseline association |
| M1 | APACHE total score adjustment | Severity adjustment (single score) |
| M2 | Propensity score matched | Organ injury composite matching |
| M3 | Multilevel logistic (hospital RE) | Hospital-level adjustment |
| M4 | Propensity score + hospital RE | Combined matching + multilevel |

- Compare attenuation: (log-OR_M0 − log-OR_Mi) / log-OR_M0 for each model
- Primary comparison: M0 vs M2 (crude vs matched)

#### 5e. Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Comparator restriction:** Restrict non-transferred comparator to ED + Floor + Direct only (exclude post-surgical, intra-hospital)
2. **APACHE-only matching:** Propensity score using only APACHE total score (no labs) — tests whether the composite adds information beyond the single score
3. **Different caliper widths:** 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 × SD
4. **Inverse probability weighting (IPW):** Instead of matching, use stabilized IPTW on the full cohort
5. **Exclusion of very short stays:** LOS < 48h instead of < 24h
6. **Subgroup by APACHE stratum:** Matched analysis within APACHE 0–10, 11–20, 21+
7. **Transfer source:** "Other Hospital" vs "Other ICU" separately
8. **Hospital-level transfer rate interaction:** Does the transfer-mortality association vary by hospital transfer volume?

#### 5f. Method Comparison (Baseline vs Alternative)

**Baseline (M1):** Crude comparison + APACHE total score adjustment
- Simple, interpretable, uses a single validated severity score
- Limitation: APACHE total score is a single number that combines age and organ injury; it does not capture the specific organ systems injured, non-linear severity effects, or hospital-specific transfer practices

**Alternative (M2 + M3):** Propensity score matching on organ injury composite + hospital random intercept
- What it reveals that the baseline loses:
  1. **Organ-specific injury patterns:** Two patients with the same APACHE total score may have different organ systems injured (e.g., renal vs hepatic vs respiratory). The composite captures this heterogeneity.
  2. **Non-linear severity effects:** Propensity score matching handles non-linear relationships between severity and transfer probability without assuming a specific functional form.
  3. **Hospital-specific transfer practices:** The random intercept captures unmeasured hospital-level factors (e.g., a hospital that transfers more patients may have different staffing, transport protocols, or case mix). The baseline model ignores this clustering.
  4. **Overlap assessment:** Matching explicitly identifies the region of covariate space where transferred and non-transferred patients are comparable, making the causal interpretation more transparent.

**Resource/time budget:** All analyses are CPU-only. The propensity score model and matching on ~200K patients with ~15 covariates will run in < 5 minutes on a single CPU core. The multilevel logistic regression with 208 random intercepts on ~200K observations will run in < 10 minutes. Total compute: < 1 hour on 4 CPUs, well within the solver budget (16 CPUs, 8h).

### 6. Falsification Criteria

**Supportive of leading claim (severity artifact):**
- Crude OR > 1.5 (transferred patients have higher mortality)
- Post-matching OR < 1.20 with 95% CI including 1.0
- Attenuation > 50% (log-OR reduction from M0 to M2)
- Balance achieved (SMD < 0.1 for all covariates)
- Consistent across sensitivity analyses (comparator restriction, IPW, APACHE-only matching)

**Supportive of rival (genuine excess risk):**
- Crude OR > 1.5
- Post-matching OR > 1.20 with 95% CI excluding 1.0
- Attenuation < 30%
- Hospital random intercept variance > 0 (significant hospital-level heterogeneity)
- Association persists in APACHE stratum-specific analyses

**Inconclusive:**
- Post-matching OR between 1.0 and 1.20 with CI spanning 1.0
- Matching fails: > 30% of transferred patients unmatched (insufficient overlap)
- Balance not achieved despite caliper adjustment
- Crude OR < 1.5 (no meaningful association to explain)

**What each outcome would and would not establish:**
- Supportive: Suggests the transfer-mortality association is a severity artifact in this population. Does NOT establish that transfer is universally safe (single time period, US-only, no transport data).
- Adverse: Suggests a genuine excess mortality risk from transfer. Does NOT establish causation (unmeasured confounding: pre-arrival trajectory, transport duration, source hospital quality). Would require transport-specific data and potentially a prospective study.
- Inconclusive: The question cannot be resolved with this data. Would suggest that the association is small and imprecisely estimated, or that the matching approach is inadequate.

### 7. Limitations

1. **Pre-arrival course incomplete:** eICU-CRD contains no data from the source hospital or during transport. The organ injury composite reflects arrival status, not the trajectory of deterioration. A patient who was stable 2 hours before transfer but deteriorated en route would be captured as "sick at arrival" without distinguishing the cause.
2. **Observational design:** Propensity score matching controls for measured confounders only. Unmeasured confounders (transport duration, source hospital quality, pre-arrival interventions, patient/family preferences for transfer) cannot be addressed.
3. **Single time period:** eICU-CRD covers 2014–2015 only. Transfer practices, transport technology, and ICU staffing may have changed since.
4. **US-only:** Generalizability to other healthcare systems (with different transfer protocols, transport infrastructure, and insurance structures) is uncertain.
5. **ICU mortality, not hospital mortality:** unitdischargestatus captures ICU death only. Patients who survived the ICU but died later in the hospital are counted as survivors.
6. **APACHE score timing:** APACHE scores are calculated from the first 24 hours. For transferred patients, this window includes the post-arrival period, not the pre-arrival period. The organ injury composite uses the first 6 hours, which is a reasonable approximation of "arrival status" but is not a true pre-arrival measurement.
7. **No transport data:** Duration of transport, transport team composition, and transport-related adverse events are not recorded.
8. **Selection bias in transfer decision:** The decision to transfer is made by clinicians based on clinical judgment, which may incorporate information not captured in the database (e.g., expected trajectory, family preferences, bed availability). This is a form of confounding by indication that propensity matching can only partially address.

### 9. What This Proposal Adds Beyond the Closest Work

1. **Scale:** 8,587 transferred patients across 208 hospitals vs 331 in a single center [K3] or ~6,100 in a meta-analysis of small COVID-19 studies [K1].
2. **Non-pandemic, general ICU population:** Unlike [K1], this study includes all causes of critical illness, not just COVID-19.
3. **Rigorous organ-injury matching:** Unlike [K3] (multivariable logistic regression) or [K1] (heterogeneous adjustment approaches), this study uses propensity score matching on a composite of organ injury at arrival, explicitly testing whether the association is a severity artifact.
4. **Hospital-level adjustment:** The multilevel model with hospital random intercept accounts for clustering and unmeasured hospital-level factors, which neither [K1] nor [K3] addresses.
5. **Pre-pandemic data:** The 2014–2015 time window provides a "natural experiment" of routine transfer practices, not pandemic-driven surge transfers.

### 10. Claims That Are Computationally Checkable vs Require Clinical Adjudication

**Computationally checkable:**
- Crude mortality by transfer status
- Propensity score estimation and matching
- Balance diagnostics (SMD)
- Post-matching mortality difference and OR
- Multilevel logistic regression estimates
- Attenuation calculations
- All sensitivity analyses

**Require clinical adjudication / additional data:**
- Whether the residual association (if any) is due to transport-related harm vs unmeasured confounding
- Whether the findings generalize to other populations, time periods, or healthcare systems
- Whether the transfer-mortality association (if confirmed) is modifiable by changes in transfer protocols
- Causal interpretation of the association (requires transport-specific data, prospective design, or instrumental variable analysis)
