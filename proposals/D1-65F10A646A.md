# Episode 7 candidate: can a prior pH observation make hidden hyperoxemia actionable?

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The inherited, concurrent EICU hypothesis asks whether, when monitor SpO2 is 96–97%, an acidemic same-panel pH is associated with a clinically large increase in observed PaO2 >120 mm Hg. That is an important transportability test, but same-panel pH is only available after the blood gas is drawn. It cannot by itself validate a bedside rule that decides whether to obtain a future gas.

The actionable unresolved question is narrower and different: among adults with a qualifying invasive-airway episode and SpO2 96–97%, does a **strictly pre-draw** pH measurement from the preceding 5–60 minutes identify a subsequent blood-gas observation with a clinically important excess probability of PaO2 >120 mm Hg? If so, the result would justify a prospective study of selective synchronized blood-gas reassessment or oxygen-titration decision support. If not, a pH-conditioned trigger would add complexity without reliable advance warning.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis:** in the pre-specified lagged cohort below, prior acidemia (most recent valid pH <7.35 measured 5–60 minutes before the index PaO2 draw), compared with prior pH 7.35–7.45, is associated with an observation-standardized risk difference of at least +10 percentage points for index PaO2 >120 mm Hg, after standardization to the index SpO2, strictly nonfuture FiO2, PaCO2/missingness, six-hour time block, and hospital-free case mix. The exposure must be recorded before the index draw; current same-panel pH is not used in the primary exposure or model.

This is an observational forecasting/measurement hypothesis. It is not an intervention on pH, proof that acidemia causes hyperoxemia, proof of tissue hyperoxia or oxygen toxicity, or evidence that changing FiO2 improves outcomes.

## What is already supported and what this tests

A current public search result for Delgado et al., *Antioxidants* 2026, DOI 10.3390/antiox15020235, reports a single-center retrospective study of 21,406 patients and 717,064 paired measurements: SpO2 96–97% generally corresponded to physiologic PaO2, while acidotic pH and higher FiO2 increased hyperoxemia risk. The retrieved result is model-mediated public evidence, not a complete local copy of the article. It supports the narrower existence of a pH/FiO2-modified concurrent association in one center.

The strongest evidence available here therefore supports only a concurrent, single-center measurement association. It does not establish that a **prior** pH observation forecasts a later hidden-hyperoxemia event, that the signal transports across EICU hospitals, that selective ABG measurement improves care, or that oxygen treatment should be changed. This candidate tests the first two unresolved links while preserving the parent’s concurrent analysis as a mechanistic/measurement sensitivity, not as a substitute for the lagged estimand.

## Outcomes, estimand, baselines, and analysis

The primary outcome is index observed PaO2 >120 mm Hg. Secondary outcomes are PaO2 >150, continuous PaO2, and PaO2 <60; the last is a gas-state safety description, not tissue hypoxia.

The primary estimand is the observation-weighted marginal standardized risk difference for index PaO2 >120 comparing prior acidemia with prior normal pH. Standardize over each observed row’s index SpO2, strict FiO2, PaCO2 and missingness, age, sex, unit type, admission source, six-hour index block, and hospital-free covariates. Set only the prior-pH exposure to each group; do not standardize current pH or any post-index variable. This is descriptive forecasting standardization, not a causal intervention.

Use fixed models:

- B0: intercept + median SpO2 centered at 96.5.
- B1: B0 + FiO2 per 10 percentage points + PaCO2 per 10 mm Hg with missing indicator + six-hour block.
- B2: B1 + prior acidemia + prior acidemia × FiO2.
- B3, sensitivity only: B2 plus age, sex, unit type, admission source, and hospital descriptors; B3 cannot replace the parsimonious primary model.

Categorical levels with fewer than 10 primary rows are collapsed to Other based on predictor counts before inspecting PaO2. FiO2 bands 21–39, 40–59, and 60–100 are a fixed functional-form sensitivity. Fit population-average logistic GEE with exchangeable correlation clustered by `uniquepid`. Estimate standardized risks and RD with a 1,000-replicate hospital-cluster bootstrap; copied hospital draws receive new hospital and person-cluster IDs. Also report person-clustered sandwich intervals, convergence, and failed fits.

Prespecified sensitivities are: earliest qualifying observation per stay; equal-stay weighting; earliest qualifying draw per stay-specific six-hour block; 24-hour restriction; last instead of median SpO2; 10-minute SpO2 window; 5- and 60-minute FiO2 lookbacks; chart-offset-only FiO2; fixed FiO2 bands; complete PaCO2; prior-pH windows 10–30, 5–120, and 5–180 minutes; latest prior pH versus any prior pH; exclusion of current panels with current pH in the opposite group; and one-at-a-time deletion of each exposure/documentation-adequate hospital. A missing-linkage sensitivity may use stabilized weights based only on pre-draw patient, hospital, block, and respiratory-chart availability, with 1st/99th percentile truncation; it cannot repair informative blood-gas ordering.

The concurrent parent estimand is a prespecified secondary bridge: among the same index panels, compare current acidemic versus current normal pH using the parent’s exact latest-revision, SpO2, FiO2, and model rules. A large concurrent effect with a null lagged effect means the phenomenon may be real but not forecastable from a prior pH; it must not be reported as support for an actionable alert.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support for the **actionable lagged hypothesis** requires all of:

- B2 standardized lagged RD >= +10 points;
- two-sided hospital-bootstrap 95% interval excludes 0 and its lower bound is >= +10 points for the strong threshold;
- positive within-hospital RD in at least 70% of the five or more pre-outcome exposure/documentation-adequate hospitals;
- no deletion of one adequate hospital reverses direction;
- the random-effects prediction interval does not include an important opposite effect below -5 points;
- no primary timing, lag-window, SpO2, or ventilation sensitivity changes the RD by >5 points or reverses its sign; and
- the concurrent bridge is labeled separately and does not substitute for the lagged criterion.

Because the lower-bound criterion is deliberately stringent, a point estimate above +10 with a lower bound below +10 is partial evidence, not full support.

Adverse results include an upper 95% bound below +10, which falsifies the clinically important magnitude even if a smaller positive association remains; an upper bound below 0; or nonpositive RDs in at least half of adequate hospitals. A precise +6-point lagged RD is adverse to this +10-point actionable hypothesis, not evidence of no relationship. If the concurrent bridge is positive but the lagged analysis is adverse, conclude that same-panel acidemia is a measurement correlate but not a validated pre-draw trigger.

Inconclusive results include failure of the fixed feasibility gates, an interval spanning 0 and +10, >10% failed bootstrap fits, severe exposure nonoverlap, a site prediction interval spanning both <=-5 and >=+10, a >5-point timing/lag sensitivity shift, failure to reproduce the frozen cohort, or inability to estimate all site contrasts with bias-reduced methods. A positive pooled estimate with failed transportability is “pooled lagged association supported; multi-hospital actionability inconclusive.”

## Evidence limits and clinical adjudication

The EICU files cannot establish that exact-offset analytes came from one arterial specimen, that the airway persisted, that zero-ended respiratory records denote continuous ventilation, that charted FiO2 was delivered, that monitor SpO2 was artifact-free or comparably calibrated, or that blood-gas/monitor observation was missing at random. Public narrative note sections are removed; structured note fields do not supply reliable clinical adjudication. The prior pH may also be a clinician-selected test reflecting unmeasured severity.

The computable claim is limited to an association between a recorded pre-draw pH and a later recorded PaO2 threshold under the stated EICU linkage rules. Clinical informatics and device review are needed for specimen identity, airway truth, delivered FiO2, waveform quality, and whether the prior pH would actually be available at a decision time. A prospective synchronized study must confirm that prior pH is available before the decision, measure ABG ordering and oxygen changes, and include hypoxemia, organ injury, and patient-centered outcomes. Only a randomized or carefully designed prospective intervention study can test whether selective reassessment or oxygen titration improves outcomes.
