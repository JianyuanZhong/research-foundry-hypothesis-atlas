# Provisional UKB repair: discordant HbA1c and weight trajectories

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with two valid assessment-center visits, no diabetes at the second visit, and a comparable rise in HbA1c, weight loss is associated with greater subsequent incident-diabetes risk than weight gain or stability. The clinically relevant question is whether the same glycemic deterioration has different prognostic meaning when accompanied by weight loss, rather than treating HbA1c change as a single risk state.

This is a prognostic association, not a causal or mechanistic claim. It does not claim that weight loss causes diabetes or identifies occult cancer.

## Falsifiable test

After catalog verification, define a second-visit index among participants with paired HbA1c, glucose, weight, and BMI; exclude prevalent diabetes and cancer at or before index; follow from index through a prespecified 2-year primary window and 5-year secondary window. Compare weight-loss versus weight-gain/stability groups at comparable HbA1c rise, adjusting for interval, age, sex, baseline BMI, smoking, and available medication indicators. A supportive result requires a positive weight-loss contrast with uncertainty excluding a clinically negligible effect and consistency in the trajectory analysis; an adverse result is no contrast or disappearance after early-event/treatment/interval handling; inconclusive results include sparse overlap, inadequate events, or unstable uncertainty. Clinical adjudication is still required for diabetes onset, treatment intent, unintentional weight loss, and preclinical disease.
