These changes do not alter population, clocks, estimand, or evidence claims.

## Executable partial-identification layer

Run for every assay, origin, and future block over **all** BP-eligible rows, including missing Y. Fit M0/M1 only on pre-origin observed complete triplets; freeze finite predictions `p0_i,p1_i` for every BP row. If any required prediction is nonfinite, stop that block's bound and label it computationally invalid rather than dropping the row. Let `N` be all BP-eligible rows and `n_mis` the missing count.

Set `L_train=min(Y_train)` and `U_train=max(Y_train)` over the exact finite training parser. Construct this range before inspecting future Y. If the training range is empty or degenerate, or any observed future Y is outside [L_train,U_train], label the temporal bound `unsupported` and do not widen it. A full-source range may be emitted only as a separately labeled descriptive check. If no future Y is observed, use the training observed-Y mean only as a training-derived low-information reference for the tipping coordinate; this does not create a future outcome.

For an observed row, define `e0=(Y-p0)^2`, `e1=(Y-p1)^2`, `d=e0-e1`. For a missing row and j in {0,1},

```
e_j,min = (clip(p_j,L_train,U_train)-p_j)^2
e_j,max = max((L_train-p_j)^2,(U_train-p_j)^2)
```

For the contrast use the direct affine identity

```
d(y) = (y-p0)^2-(y-p1)^2
     = 2*y*(p1-p0) + p0^2-p1^2.
```

Thus, writing `a_i=2*(p1_i-p0_i)` and `b_i=p0_i^2-p1_i^2`, missing-row contrast extrema are exactly `min(d(L),d(U))` and `max(d(L),d(U))`. Add observed d terms and divide by fixed N to produce sharp conditional `Delta_lower`/`Delta_upper` MSE bounds for M0-M1, overall and by arm. The arm bounds use each arm's own BP denominator. A positive Delta means lower M1 squared error. Report missing counts/fractions and all three endpoint scenarios: all missing at L, all at U, and each missing row at its least-favorable endpoint (L when a_i >= 0, U when a_i < 0 for the lower M0-M1 contrast).

The intervals above are deterministic mathematical identification regions conditional on the frozen predictions and observed rows, not 95% confidence intervals. Do not attach a sampling-confidence interpretation. If uncertainty for the bounded quantities is requested, the only permitted optional analysis is a separately labeled patient-cluster bootstrap that recomputes the entire fixed-prediction endpoint aggregate within each resample; it must not be merged with the deterministic region or used to make a clinical claim. It is not required for the primary output.

For each model, divide its SSE bounds by N to obtain MSE bounds. A conservative, non-primary RMSE-difference enclosure is
`[sqrt(MSE0_lower)-sqrt(MSE1_upper), sqrt(MSE0_upper)-sqrt(MSE1_lower)]`; never call it sharp and never standardize it by an unidentified test SD. Primary standardized RMSE gain remains observed-complete-triplet only. If the observed test Y SD is nonfinite or zero, G and its CI are non-estimable, while unstandardized bound arithmetic may still be reported if valid.

### Fixed-prediction primary uncertainty

For every estimable primary future result, use a paired, arm-stratified patient-frequency bootstrap with a declared seed and B=2000 replicates (or a recorded smaller number only if resource failure is reported). Resample patients with replacement within each arm and include all their selected events, preserving within-patient pairing. Keep the pre-origin fits and every held-out prediction fixed; do not refit and do not impute missing Y. In each replicate calculate the numerator RMSE difference on observed complete triplets and divide by the **original point-estimate** `SD_test(Y)` for that assay/block, so the CI targets the same conditional standardized estimand. Use the percentile 95% interval and report replicate count, failures, and effective observed patients. A zero/undefined denominator, no observed triplets, or fewer than two observed patient clusters makes the primary metric non-estimable, not zero. This CI is not evidence against missing-outcome selection and does not alter Delta bounds.

### Deterministic common-displacement tipping point

Let `m_ref` be the observed future-Y mean if at least one future Y exists; otherwise use the training observed-Y mean and mark it training-derived. For all missing rows simultaneously set `y_i(delta)=clip(m_ref+delta,L_train,U_train)`. Define

`Delta(delta) = [sum_observed d_i + sum_missing d_i(y_i(delta))] / N`.

Because each `d_i(y)` is affine and clipping creates only slope changes, Delta(delta) is continuous **piecewise linear**, with breakpoints at `L_train-m_ref` and `U_train-m_ref` (shared clipping) and no quadratic segment. Evaluate the fixed observed component once; on each segment use slope `sum_{missing,unclipped} 2(p1_i-p0_i)/N` and solve the linear equation analytically, retaining roots only within that segment and verifying by deterministic endpoint evaluation. Report every root in [L_train-m_ref,U_train-m_ref], then choose smallest absolute |delta|, ties by smallest delta. If Delta(0)=0 report zero; if no root exists report sign-robust for this common-displacement scenario. Also report whether the endpoint interval crosses zero. This is a sensitivity coordinate, not an imputation, estimate, or clinical plausibility statement.

## Interpretation, falsification, and verification

Supportive evidence requires successful reconstruction/clock assertions, both outcome-blind and complete-triplet arm gates, an estimable prespecified primary result in an adequately supported block, no material ascertainment warning, and Delta bounded strictly positive throughout the declared training-range scenario. This supports only a selected short-horizon prognostic association in that calendar mixture. A positive deterministic bound is not a confidence statement and does not establish transport.

Adverse evidence is a nonpositive primary contrast in an adequately supported block, repeated M1 degradation, or a bounded Delta sign reversal. Selection-sensitive evidence is favorable observed-case performance with Delta crossing zero, a near-boundary tipping point, a strong observation gradient, or discrepant ascertainment patterns. Inconclusive evidence includes failed gates, unsupported/degenerate range, nonestimable SD or bootstrap, low ESS, influential clusters, non-estimable small cells, nonfinite predictions, or dependence on the full-source range. Existing parent audit findings may leave 2022 locally supportive and 2023/2024 transport-inconclusive; no new bound finding is claimed here.

The compiler/verifier must test: (1) a favorable observed RMSE with Delta crossing zero is selection-sensitive; (2) failed arm gates keep a favorable point estimate descriptive; (3) future Y outside [L_train,U_train] makes the temporal bound unsupported; (4) the affine contrast gives piecewise-linear, not quadratic, tipping arithmetic; (5) untimed documents are never placed in the Y window; (6) examination/encounter presence is never called completed follow-up; (7) endpoint bounds do not alter the primary fit or its fixed-denominator bootstrap; (8) deterministic bounds are not labeled confidence intervals; (9) model-specific MSE bounds are not mislabeled as sharp RMSE-difference bounds; and (10) bounded robust signs retain adverse, unsupported, and inconclusive branches and are never upgraded to causality, treatment effect, utility, safety, transport, or benefit.

Computationally checkable outputs include hashes, headers/schema IDs, duplicate-safe joins, denominator/event reconstruction, clocks, outcome-blind funnels, diagnostics, finite-range checks, endpoint arithmetic, piecewise-linear roots, frozen predictions, and bootstrap bookkeeping. Units/reference ranges, clinical severity, imaging response, administration or intent, external-care capture, mortality, hepatic failure, completed follow-up, causal effects, utility, actionability, and patient benefit require adjudication and an independent temporal/external/prospective cohort.
