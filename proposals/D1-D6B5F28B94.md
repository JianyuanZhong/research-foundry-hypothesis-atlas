This is a substantive successor. It retains the parent’s ICU-transition question and exact trajectory contrast, but makes the decision-relevant estimand explicit: worsening is evaluated at a comparable final recorded state, not allowed to stand in for final burden. It also prespecifies a 48-hour immediate-return sensitivity because a signal close to transfer is more actionable than a generic seven-day association. The 7-day outcome remains primary.

## Unresolved question, claim boundary and clinical importance

ICU transfer changes the available monitoring and rescue environment. Two patients can have the same final recorded heart rate, pressure or oxygenation but differ in whether observed abnormality is settling, stable, or worsening over the preceding day. The unresolved question is whether a worsening trajectory immediately before ICU outtime identifies a clinically distinct risk state beyond:

- the final-day abnormality burden;
- the final recorded physiologic values;
- how often the patient was observed in both windows; and
- ordinary admission and ICU-utilization context.

This matters because a reproducible, calibrated signal could identify patients for targeted ward surveillance or prospective discharge-readiness evaluation. A null result, or a result that disappears after measurement-density adjustment, would be equally useful: it would argue against treating a trajectory derived from selectively charted data as a discharge marker.

The strongest claim supported before the experiment is a data/provenance claim only: the frozen snapshot contains linked admissions, patients, ICU stays, transfers, ICU chart events and item dictionaries with timestamps and the specified fields. The parent and seed do not establish that observed worsening predicts a competing-risk outcome, that it is independent of charting intensity, or that a discharge intervention would improve outcomes.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among adults whose first eligible ICU stay ends while the same hospital admission continues, a higher change in observed abnormal vital burden from the preceding 24 hours to the final 24 hours (final burden minus preceding burden) is associated with higher seven-day cumulative incidence of first later same-admission ICU return or death before ICU return, conditional on final burden, final last values, and measurement density in both windows; adding this change improves locked-test calibration and/or competing-risk Brier score over the current-state model.

This is a prognostic and incremental-prediction hypothesis. It is not a claim that discharge caused the event, that delaying transfer is beneficial, or that charted oxygen equals delivered oxygen.

A prespecified clinical-proximity sensitivity repeats the locked analysis with a 48-hour horizon, censored at discharge, while keeping the same index, features, partitions and model recipe. It cannot replace the 7-day primary result.

## Population and temporal order

All parsing is timezone-naive. MIMIC timestamps are deidentified; only within-subject intervals are interpreted. Cohort selection precedes chart-event feature construction and future-outcome inspection.

1. Inner/left join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, then to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id). Record unmatched keys and impossible chronology.

2. A candidate index stay is eligible if the patient is an adult (`anchor_age >= 18)), `intime`, `outtime) and hospital `dischtime) are nonmissing, `outtime < dischtime), ICU `los >= 1/24) day, and `deathtime) is missing or strictly later than `outtime). This is an ICU stay ending alive before hospital discharge.

3. Within each `(subject_id, hadm_id)), sort eligible candidates by `intime), then numeric `stay_id), and select exactly the earliest as the primary index. Do not use chart events, transfers after index, or outcomes in this choice. Retain an audit row for every candidate. A secondary all-eligible-stay analysis repeats the same rules without collapsing to the first stay, but is not substituted for the primary estimand.

4. Set `t0 = outtime). Define contiguous half-open windows split into six four-hour bins:
   - preceding A: `[t0 - 48 hours, t0 - 24 hours));
   - final B: `[t0 - 24 hours, t0)).
   A row exactly at `t0-24h` belongs to B; a row exactly at `t0) is excluded. Retain only the selected `stay_id` a matching admission with another stay is excluded from feature construction. `storetime` never changes this ordering.

5. The paired trajectory estimand requires at least four of six bins with a valid vital in A and at least four of six in B. This is a prespecified measurement-eligibility rule, not an event-driven filter. Report the all-index manifest and compare paired-gate failures by outcome, care unit, anchor-year group, and density. The all-eligible sensitivity uses development-only imputation and missing indicators; it is a distinct prediction estimand.

## Feature and estimand construction

Use `valuenum) for numeric features. Apply validity before summaries and count invalid rows; never silently repair them:

- HR 20–250;
- arterial and non-invasive MAP 20–200;
- RR 4–80;
- SpO2 50–100;
- temperature 30–43 °C after Fahrenheit conversion `(F-32)/1.8`;
- oxygen flows nonnegative;
- FiO2 accepts 0.21–1.00 as a fraction or (1,100] as a percentage divided by 100, then requires 0.21–1.00.

A vital bin is covered if it has at least one valid HR, either MAP, RR, SpO2 or temperature row. An abnormal valid observation is HR <50 or >100, RR <8 or >24, SpO2 <92%, MAP <65 mmHg, temperature <36 °C or >=38 °C. Keep arterial and non-invasive MAP separate; for the burden feature, combined MAP is the minimum valid MAP within a bin.

For window w in {A,B}:

`covered_w = number of covered bins`  
`abnormal_w = number of covered bins containing at least one abnormal vital`  
`burden_w = abnormal_w / covered_w`.

Let `P = burden_A`, `F = burden_B`, and `Delta = F-P`. Positive Delta means greater observed abnormality close to ICU outtime. The primary nested comparison includes F and Delta, so the Delta coefficient is the change contrast at fixed final burden. Do not also add P to that same primary model: F and Delta already determine the planned decomposition. Report P descriptively and in the all-eligible sensitivity.

For B, derive last-value features as the median of valid observations at the latest `charttime) per domain; ties at that time use the median. Include missing/observed indicators. Keep final arterial MAP, non-invasive MAP and combined MAP separate. For oxygen, derive maximum valid flow across 223834/227287/227582 and maximum normalized FiO2, plus item-specific observed indicators. Device and code-status text are observed/not-observed indicators and aggregate frequency audits only; they are not translated into support mode or treatment-limitation labels.

Persist aggregate data only: IDs needed for internal joins, six-bin coverage/abnormality vectors, burden/change, row counts, invalid counts, domain last values, oxygen aggregates, missingness and gate flags. Do not write raw chart rows, notes or text values to derived outputs.

## Outcomes and estimands

For each index define `tau_7 = min(t0 + 7 days, dischtime)`; events at `tau_7) are included. A candidate return is a later ICU stay with the same `(subject_id, hadm_id)), different `stay_id), and `intime >= t0). A later stay with `intime < t0) is an overlap/chronology anomaly, not a return. Select the earliest return by `intime), then numeric `stay_id).

- Cause 1: first qualifying ICU return by `tau_7), strictly earlier than qualifying death.
- Cause 2: `deathtime > t0) and `deathtime <= tau_7), strictly earlier than the first qualifying ICU return.
- Equal-time return/death is tie/indeterminate; exclude from the primary competing-risk fit and report sensitivities assigning all ties to return and all to death.
- No event by `tau_7) is a no-cause record. Death after a return is not cause 2.
- Primary outcome: composite of causes 1 and 2, with cause-specific descriptions secondary.
- Sensitivities: 48-hour and 12-hour horizons, 48-hour immediate-return analysis, 12-hour-gap exclusion for returns immediately after t0, and follow-up through hospital `dischtime).

For the 48-hour sensitivity use `tau_48 = min(t0+48h, dischtime)` and identical event precedence. It is a horizon sensitivity, not a new index or a post hoc change in the primary claim. Patients discharged alive before the horizon are administratively censored; outpatient events are not observable.

## Baselines, analysis and uncertainty

Use the identical index manifest, outcome labels, preprocessing, subject partition and locked-test subjects for every model:

- B0: gender, anchor_age, admission_type, admission_location, insurance, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU LOS, hours from `admittime) to t0, and number of earlier ICU stays ending by t0, with missing indicators.
- B_last: B0 plus final last valid HR, separate/combined MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, oxygen flow and FiO2 with missing indicators.
- B_density2: B_last plus final and preceding six-bin coverage vectors, total valid-vital rows, per-domain row counts, oxygen observation indicators and `log1p) counts in both windows.
- B_final: B_density2 plus F and fixed final per-domain abnormal indicators.
- B_traj: B_final plus Delta. This is the primary incremental contrast.
- B_oxygen: B_traj plus final/preceding maximum flow and FiO2, interpreted only as extra charted-oxygen information.

Fit ridge-penalized cause-specific Cox models for first ICU return and death before return. Choose penalties by inner development cross-validation only. Derive composite and cause-specific cumulative-incidence predictions from fitted cause-specific hazards; report nonparametric Aalen–Johansen estimates. Do not treat the competing cause as ordinary noninformative censoring in cumulative-incidence reporting.

Partition by subject-level SHA-256 into 60% development, 20% validation and 20% locked test; all admissions/stays for a subject remain together. Fit imputation, scaling, encoding, penalties and any calibration map using development/validation rules only. Keep the locked test untouched until final evaluation. Prohibit post-t0 charting, discharge destination, hospital-expire flag, future transfers, or outcome-derived cut points from predictors. If anchor_year_group is used, include it in every model as a categorical covariate; it is not a chronological split.

Primary locked-test estimand: paired difference in seven-day composite competing-risk Brier score for B_traj minus B_final, with calibration intercept/slope and absolute predicted-risk difference across prespecified observed Delta percentiles. Report cause-specific coefficients per development-cohort SD of Delta and F with subject-clustered uncertainty. Report B_final minus B_density2 and B_oxygen minus B_traj secondarily.

Use 1,000 fixed-seed subject-bootstrap resamples of locked-test subjects for paired 95% intervals for Brier, calibration and risk contrasts; use a subject-clustered bootstrap or robust estimator for coefficients. Before fitting, require:

- total cohort: >=1,000 paired indexes, >=100 composite events, and >=60% of all eligible indexes meeting the four-bin gate in both windows;
- locked test: >=20 composite events and >=10 of each cause when cause-specific inference is reported.

A failed gate is sparse/infeasible: do not lower thresholds, change windows or substitute an easier model. If cause-specific gates fail, give descriptive Aalen–Johansen estimates and uncertainty only for that cause. If paired eligibility fails but all-eligible prediction is feasible, label the results separately and never call imputed all-eligible results the paired trajectory association.

Reference implementation budget: CPU-only, up to 4 CPUs and 16 GiB, 7,200 seconds, within `inputs.json` science_seconds=7200. Read small cohort/outcome members first, then make one sequential projection pass through the chart-events member for fixed item IDs/windows. Do not materialize all `chartevents), make one pass per item, or write raw rows.

## Falsification, integrity and interpretation

Fail closed on duplicate primary index, selected chart row at or after t0/outside A/B, `storetime`-based eligibility, mismatched stay ID, impossible admission/ICU/death chronology, inconsistent tie handling, subject leakage, preprocessing fit on locked data, prohibited post-index predictors, or a conclusion not linked to a computed output.

Required checks:

1. Earlier-window placebo: construct A0=`[t0-72h,t0-48h)` and B0=`[t0-48h,t0-24h)` with identical rules, outcome labels, partitions and model recipe. A similar or stronger change signal weakens transfer-proximity interpretation.
2. Density stress test: compare B_last, B_density2 and B_final. If final burden or Delta is attenuated by density, label ascertainment dependence. Delta cannot be called physiologic if it adds no value over B_final.
3. Current-burden stratification: use final-burden quartiles learned in development only and show Delta-risk gradients within strata. A marginal signal caused only by different F distributions is not evidence for worsening at comparable current state.
4. Outcome permutation: permute labels within locked subject partitions while preserving exposure coverage and cause proportions; locked performance should return to chance within bootstrap uncertainty. Failure invalidates the computation, not the clinical hypothesis.
5. Definition robustness: repeat arterial versus non-invasive MAP, abnormal-row fraction versus abnormal-bin fraction, the 12-hour return-gap rule, 12/48-hour horizons, equal-time tie assignments and all-eligible imputation. Large divergence is definition/selection dependence.
6. Documentation audit: compare density, invalid rates and paired-gate failures by outcome, care unit, anchor-year group and partition. Audit device/code-status frequencies without clinical classification.
7. Chronology audit: verify the earlier placebo reads no final-window rows and reuses the exact same index/outcome manifest; verify no chart row is selected using `storetime`.

Supportive results require: a prespecified positive/monotonic Delta association and directionally compatible cause-specific results in locked test; B_traj improves calibration and/or Brier over B_final with a paired interval excluding no improvement and a non-negligible absolute-risk separation; persistence after both-window density; no equally strong earlier placebo; current-burden-stratified gradient; robustness across MAP source, return-gap, tie and horizon rules; and chance-level label permutation. This supports only that the recorded trajectory carries reproducible prognostic information beyond the specified recorded state and ascertainment proxies.

Adverse results include null/reversed Delta, no incremental locked-test value, stronger placebo, attenuation after density, disappearance within burden strata, or major definition instability. These argue that the trajectory adds no reliable information beyond current recorded burden/charting or is mainly an ascertainment proxy. They do not establish ICU-transfer safety.

Inconclusive results include failed cohort/coverage/event gates, unstable bootstrap intervals, inability to separate burden from density, contradictory competing-risk/placebo results, or failed permutation/integrity checks. Failed computation is not evidence against the clinical hypothesis.

## Evidence limits and required adjudication

Computationally checkable claims are source/member/schema availability, fixed item labels, cohort/index ordering, window eligibility, valid/invalid counts, aggregate burden/change/density, event labels/ties, Aalen–Johansen estimates, model metrics, bootstrap intervals, sensitivity outputs and conclusion-ledger linkage.

MIMIC cannot adjudicate true bedside instability, work of breathing, delivered FiO2, actual oxygen device, functional/cognitive readiness, staffing, ward capacity, clinician intent, treatment limitation, or why an ICU return occurred. Code-status/device observations are not validated substitutes. The endpoint is same-admission ICU return or death through a truncated horizon; outpatient events and return reasons are unavailable. Timestamps cannot support cross-subject calendar trends. Observational associations cannot establish causality, a safe discharge threshold, transportability, equity, prospective utility or benefit from delaying transfer. Those stronger claims require expert adjudication, richer prospective physiology/oxygen-delivery and goals-of-care data, external validation, and a prospective surveillance or target-trial/interventional study.
