# HCC documentary-M2: an auditable packet-offer validity layer for fixed-capacity review

Parent anchors: `[prior hypothesis]` (leading visibility-separated design) and `[prior hypothesis]` (monotone-onset and identity-robust design).

## 1. One substantive repair

The mature design answers a defensible retrospective question: whether a fixed-capacity queue retains patients and source content under coupled source, clock, reader and pathology uncertainty. Its remaining clinically consequential weakness is transport from a ranked retrospective queue to an auditable review action. A queue position is not itself an offered packet. It does not say which immutable report version was attached, whether all required content was rendered, whether the packet was offered by the decision cutoff, whether it was reviewable in the intended interface, or whether a reviewer actually received and acted on it.

This successor adds exactly one layer: an explicit, packet-level **offer-validity** estimand and ledger. It preserves the existing content, identity, onset, capacity, comparator, deletion and non-causal rules. It does not claim that a patient was clinically reviewed or that a clinician acted. It asks whether the proposed policy can be connected to a packet that is both the selected patient’s complete eligible source content and demonstrably offered by (t_dec). On the present HCC snapshot, the packet lifecycle is not observed; the design therefore reports computable content bounds and a separately identified offer-validity interval, rather than treating stored CSV content as an action.

The clinical advance is decision validity: a positive ranking result can no longer be mistaken for evidence that a review team could have received the intended information at the preoperative decision point. If the added bridge cannot be established, the result is an explicit action-validity failure/inconclusive state, not a silently strengthened retrospective claim.

## 2. Question, hypothesis and evidence boundary

The clinically relevant unresolved question is:

> At fixed review capacity, does a locked report-semantic queue retain documentary-M2 patients and, for each retained patient, produce a complete, identifiable packet that was eligible to be offered by the 24-hour preoperative cutoff?

Primary hypothesis (H_A) is a finite-corpus, non-causal hypothesis:

> A threshold (q^*<1), selected only in 2020, passes the inherited patient-identity/content and fixed-capacity gates in 2020 and the untouched 2021 evaluation, and the same coupled worlds also pass the pre-specified packet-offer validity contract at (
ho=.10,d=24).

The hypothesis does not assert report release, clinician viewing, treatment selection, benefit, safety, causality, transportability or biological microvascular invasion. “Documentary-M2” means a reader-book label based on the available report surface and same-encounter pathology form; it is not specimen-linked biological MVI.

Strongest claim supported before execution: the frozen HCC snapshot contains linked encounter, procedure, examination, pathology, medication and order records sufficient to replay a source-bound documentary experiment, with exact source limitations recorded below. Existing parental designs support a computable fixed-capacity/identity/content test in principle. They do not support an action claim.

The experiment will test only whether the locked policy preserves patients/content and satisfies an explicit packet-offer certificate under a defined evidence world. A supportive finite-corpus result remains historical documentary evidence. A stronger claim that a clinician could actually review the packet requires an external archive/workflow audit with immutable version and interface telemetry.

### 7.1 Packet definition

For a selected patient (i), year (y), cutoff (d=24), state (w), threshold (q), and original queue position (k), define a packet candidate (P_{i}(w,q)) containing:

### 7.2 Offer-validity evidence states

For each selected slot, define a lifecycle state (lambda_i) for the packet as a whole, not independently per phrase or component:

- (L0): no packet instance/version can be identified;
- (L1): packet assembled/stored but not finalized by (t_dec);
- (L2): finalized but not released/offered to the intended review channel by (t_dec);
- (L3): released/offered but not demonstrably retrievable/renderable by (t_dec);
- (L4): retrievable and renderable with all packet components available by (t_dec), but receipt/review is not documented;
- (L5): receipt by the intended review channel is documented by (t_dec);
- (LX): contradictory, missing, or unresolved lifecycle evidence.

These are evidence states, not latent clinical facts. “Offered” requires an immutable packet/version identifier, event timestamp and timezone provenance, destination/channel, and a replayable payload hash. “Reviewable” additionally requires a rendering/availability check for all required components. A queue position without these fields is not (L4) or (L5).

Define:
[
OFFER_i(lambda)=1{lambda_iin{L4,L5}},qquad
RECEIVED_i(lambda)=1{lambda_i=L5}.
]
The primary action-validity endpoint is OFFER, not RECEIVED or clinician review. It is deliberately the weakest auditable bridge to a review offer. RECEIVED is reported only when explicitly evidenced.

## 10. Interpretation contract

Use one primary status with this precedence:

1. **computationally_inconclusive** — incomplete source, state, score, packet, lifecycle, threshold, witness or replay proof.
2. **feasibility_inconclusive** — computation complete but (N,K), event or nonvacuity gate fails.
3. **maximum_content_signal_adverse** — all-clean-content (G) fails a primary comparator/random/deletion gate.
4. **identity_or_content_adverse** — maximum content passes but protected patients or complete packets are not retained.
5. **action_validity_inconclusive** — documentary gates pass but lifecycle/packet-offer bounds are not identified or external bridge is absent.
6. **action_validity_adverse** — a valid external workflow audit identifies the offered packet states and every compatible state fails the offer contract.
7. **finite_corpus_documentary_supportive_action_bridge_pending** — documentary gates pass, but action validity remains unresolved; this supports only historical concentration of a documentary endpoint.
8. **externally_bridged_packet_offer_supportive** — all documentary and externally identified offer gates pass. This still does not establish review, clinical action or benefit.
9. **preference_adverse** or **preference_heterogeneous** — only if a stakeholder interval ([p_L,p_U]) is supplied and compared with the attained coupled (p^*(A)=min H_G/K); equality is not support.

Supportive means only the locked policy concentrates the historical same-encounter documentary endpoint at fixed capacity, with the stated packet/availability qualification. Adverse means the corresponding falsifying gate was observed. Inconclusive means the evidence needed to distinguish worlds is absent. None supports biological MVI, treatment selection, recurrence, survival, safety, causal effect, fairness, transportability, workflow feasibility or patient benefit.
