# Research Proposal: Rate of Hemodynamic Recovery Modulates Hypotension-Related Kidney Injury Risk

## Unresolved Question

**Current Knowledge Gap**: Clinical sepsis management emphasizes maintaining blood pressure (BP) above threshold targets. However, a critical unresolved question remains:

> **Does the rate of recovery from hypotension (not just total duration or minimum BP) predict acute kidney injury (AKI), and is this relationship modified by hemodynamic reserve and vasopressor burden?**

**The Gap**: Existing research focuses on:
- Minimum BP as a static threshold metric [K2]
- Total duration of hypotension below threshold [K1]
- Cumulative time below threshold [K3]

These approaches miss a critical temporal dimension: **how rapidly BP recovers after hypotension episodes**. Two patients with identical hypotension duration may have vastly different outcomes if one recovers rapidly while the other remains hypotensive despite vasopressor support.

## Clinical Importance

### 1. Risk Stratification Beyond Static Metrics
A "recovery rate" metric could:
- Identify patients at high risk despite meeting BP targets
- Predict AKI in patients with adequate minimum BP but poor hemodynamic recovery
- Guide de-escalation of vasopressor support in patients with rapid recovery

### 2. Treatment Optimization
Understanding the rate of hemodynamic recovery could:
- Inform more aggressive BP targets for patients with poor recovery
- Help identify patients who need additional organ support (e.g., CRRT)
- Guide timing of vasopressor weaning

### 3. Mechanistic Insight
The hypothesis tests whether the **rate of recovery** (slope of BP trajectory) is more informative than:
- Cumulative exposure (duration × severity)
- Minimum threshold (sparse summary statistic)

This addresses a fundamental question about **time-varying treatment effects** in critical care.

## Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis**: In patients with septic shock, the rate of hemodynamic recovery (slope of SBP trajectory after hypotension episode) independently predicts AKI, after adjusting for:
- Total duration of hypotension
- Minimum SBP during hypotension episode
- Vasopressor burden (cumulative vasopressor exposure)
- Hemodynamic reserve (baseline SBP, comorbidities)

**Moderating Effect**: The relationship between recovery rate and AKI is stronger in patients with:
- Low hemodynamic reserve (baseline SBP < 100 mmHg)
- High vasopressor burden (cumulative vasopressor dose > threshold)

## Study Population

**Population**: Adult patients (≥18 years) with septic shock in the eICU-CRD database who:
- Have continuous SBP measurements (≥15 min of data)
- Experience at least one hypotension episode (SBP < 100 mmHg)
- Have complete infusionDrug records for vasopressors
- Have lab measurements for AKI diagnosis (creatinine)

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Patients with pre-existing CKD (baseline creatinine > 1.5 mg/dL)
- Patients with vasopressor use before admission
- Patients with SBP measurements < 10 min (insufficient temporal resolution)

**Temporal Boundaries**:
- Index date: Admission to ICU
- Exposure window: Days 0-7 post-admission
- Outcome window: Days 0-14 post-admission

## Key Variables

### Exposure: Rate of Hemodynamic Recovery
- **Definition**: Slope of SBP trajectory (mmHg/min) during recovery phase
- **Recovery Phase**: Time from SBP < 100 mmHg to SBP ≥ 100 mmHg
- **Calculation**: Linear regression of SBP over time during recovery
- **Alternative**: Time to recovery (hours from hypotension to SBP ≥ 100 mmHg)

### Covariates:
- **Hemodynamic Reserve**: Baseline SBP (first 24 hours), CHOP score
- **Vasopressor Burden**: Cumulative vasopressor dose (mcg/min × hours), number of vasopressor types
- **Hypotension Severity**: Minimum SBP during episode, duration below threshold
- **Organ Support**: Intubation, vasopressors, dialysis, vent

### Outcome: AKI
- **Definition**: KDIGO criteria for AKI
- **Measurement**: Change in creatinine ≥0.3 mg/dL or ≥1.5× baseline within 48 hours
- **Alternative**: Cystatin C-based AKI if available

## Baseline Models

### Model 1: Cumulative Exposure Model (Baseline)
- Exposure: Total duration of hypotension (hours)
- Outcome: AKI within 14 days
- Adjustments: Minimum SBP, vasopressor burden, hospital, comorbidities

### Model 2: Recovery Rate Model (Primary)
- Exposure: Rate of SBP recovery (mmHg/min)
- Outcome: AKI within 14 days
- Adjustments: Same as Model 1

### Model 3: Interaction Model
- Exposure: Recovery rate × Vasopressor burden
- Outcome: AKI within 14 days
- Adjustments: Same as Model 1

## Alternative Models

### Model A: Latent Trajectory Model
- Use hierarchical modeling to estimate individual SBP trajectories

- Compare with observed slope

### Model B: Machine Learning Baseline
- Random Forest or XGBoost with recovery rate as feature

### Model C: Time-Varying Cox Model
- Model AKI as time-to-event with time-varying recovery rate

## Falsification Criteria

### Hypothesis Would Be Falsified If:
1. **No independent effect**: Recovery rate shows no association with AKI after adjusting for duration and severity
2. **Direction reversal**: Higher recovery rate is associated with LOWER AKI risk (suggesting rapid recovery is beneficial)
3. **No moderation**: Vasopressor burden does not modify the recovery rate-AKI relationship
4. **Confounding**: All associations disappear after adjusting for hospital, comorbidities, and treatment intensity

### Supportive Evidence Would Include:
1. **Independent association**: Recovery rate predicts AKI after adjusting for duration and severity
2. **Dose-response**: Faster recovery (steeper slope) associated with higher AKI risk
3. **Moderation**: Effect stronger in high vasopressor burden patients
4. **Robustness**: Associations persist across different AKI definitions and time windows

## Methodology

### Data Processing
1. **Identify hypotension episodes**: SBP < 100 mmHg for ≥5 min
2. **Identify recovery phases**: SBP ≥ 100 mmHg following hypotension
3. **Calculate recovery rate**: Linear regression slope during recovery
4. **Calculate vasopressor burden**: Sum of (rate × duration) for all vasopressors
5. **Identify AKI**: KDIGO criteria within 14 days

### Statistical Analysis
1. **Descriptive statistics**: Distribution of recovery rates, covariates, outcomes
2. **Unadjusted associations**: Logistic regression of AKI on recovery rate
3. **Multivariable models**: Adjust for covariates
4. **Interaction tests**: Recovery rate × vasopressor burden
5. **Sensitivity analyses**:
   - Different AKI definitions
   - Different time windows
   - Different hypotension thresholds
   - Hospital-level adjustments

## Clinical Implications

### If Hypothesis Supported:
1. **Risk Stratification**: Recovery rate could identify high-risk patients despite adequate BP
2. **Treatment Guidance**: Patients with rapid recovery may need more aggressive BP targets
3. **Resource Allocation**: Earlier CRRT consideration for rapid recovery patients
4. **Protocol Development**: New BP targets based on recovery rate rather than minimum

### If Hypothesis Falsified:
1. **No new metric**: Recovery rate not useful for AKI prediction
2. **Focus shift**: Emphasis on duration/severity metrics
3. **Further study**: Need to investigate other mechanisms

## Limitations

1. **Observational**: Cannot establish causality
2. **Temporal resolution**: Vital sign monitoring frequency limits recovery rate estimation
3. **Missing data**: Some patients may have incomplete vital sign records
4. **Generalizability**: eICU population may not represent all ICU patients
5. **Confounding**: Unmeasured confounders may affect both recovery and AKI
