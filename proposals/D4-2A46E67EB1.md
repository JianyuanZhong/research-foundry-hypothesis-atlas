# Sparse CBC trajectories and colorectal cancer prognosis after a coverage-certified day-366 landmark

## Substantive evolution and clinical question

This child evolves `[prior hypothesis]` by making the unresolved cancer/death observation problem an executable, auditable decision gate. It preserves the parent’s sparse-CBC prognostic hypothesis, exact field pairing, delayed entry, low-dimensional analysis, event-count gates, and falsification limits. The new contribution is a pre-registered **coverage certificate**: no participant is treated as having an observed cancer-free or death-free interval merely because no event appears in the rectangular export, and no endpoint is inferred from the maximum observed event date.

Among UK Biobank repeat attenders aged 40–75 who have paired protocolized CBC measurements, are alive and free of malignant cancer through day 365 after the second assessment, does the prespecified joint trajectory of haemoglobin, MCV and platelets add prognostic information for colorectal cancer (CRC) during days 366–1825 beyond age, sex and the repeat-assessment CBC values, and is that increment greater than the corresponding increment for first non-CRC malignant cancer?

A positive result would support external validation in clinically timed CBC data. A null or adverse result would discourage using sparse CBC trends as CRC-prioritisation evidence. This is prognostic research only. It does not establish screening, FIT, colonoscopy, referral, stage shift, treatment, mortality benefit, individual clinical utility, benefit–harm, or cost-effectiveness.

The evidence-supported claim is limited to the feasibility of constructing this risk set and the plausibility of longitudinal routine-data cancer prognosis. The untested claim is the incremental, CRC-specific prognostic value in this exact delayed-entry population. The cancer demonstration’s main article and full STAR Methods were unavailable in this episode; only its available supplement is eligible for the limited contextual statement above.

## Coverage certificate and censoring policy

The primary analysis has two possible outcomes, selected before looking at model performance.

1. **Certified analysis.** Accept the dataset for inferential follow-up only if a frozen, authoritative release/linkage document tied to this exact rectangular snapshot (or an explicitly version-matched UKB release) identifies cancer-registry and death-linkage coverage endpoints, including nation-specific differences for England, Wales and Scotland where relevant. The certificate must specify which endpoint applies to each source, whether the endpoint is inclusive, and whether absence of an event can be interpreted as observable through that date. Set each participant’s administrative endpoint to the minimum applicable certified cancer endpoint, certified death endpoint, and `H`; use the common minimum across sources for the primary analysis if participant nation/source membership cannot be bound in these files. If the certified endpoint is before `E`, that participant cannot enter the primary risk set. If it is after `E`, follow-up runs through the earlier of `H`, death, event, loss of observation, and the certified endpoint.

2. **Uncertified audit-only analysis.** If no such certificate exists, do not estimate incidence, cumulative incidence, hazard ratios, discrimination, calibration, IPCW quantities, PPV, or capacity yields as coverage-valid. Report only the raw-export flow and event audit already specified below, labelled `nominal/coverage-independent`. A fixed calendar date mentioned in a paper, web search result, or observed maximum is not a certificate and cannot rescue inference. Any exploratory truncation at a chosen date must be labelled descriptive sensitivity only and cannot be used to claim complete non-events.

This hierarchy is the substantive repair: it makes the strongest defensible conclusion possible when documentation is absent, while preventing silent immortal-time/uncertain-observation bias. It also separates an administrative end-of-study date from empirical maxima. If later approved data include participant-level linkage coverage or nation, add it as a new documented data requirement rather than guessing it from `eid` or dates.

Required coverage evidence includes: exact release/snapshot match; cancer registry source and endpoint; death registry source and endpoint; nation-specific linkage rules; handling of delayed registration; and explicit meaning of a missing cancer/death value. Required clinical adjudication includes whether field values meet plausible units/ranges, whether same-day cancer and death can be ordered, and whether code/date records represent incident diagnoses rather than administrative corrections.

## Cohort, timing and endpoints

Let `E=L+366` and `H=L+1825`, using calendar-day arithmetic. Washout is `[L,E)`, days 0–365. Follow-up includes `E` and ends at the earliest of `H`, the applicable certified endpoint, death, CRC, non-CRC malignant cancer, or certified loss of observation. A diagnosis or death exactly on `E` is a follow-up endpoint/competing event, not washout.

The primary entry population requires age 40–75 at `L`, valid finite primary CBC values at both instances, an ordered visit interval of 365–2,922 days, no malignant diagnosis before `L`, alive at `L`, and no CRC, non-CRC malignant cancer or death in `[L,E)`. Entry also requires `E` to be inside the certified common coverage interval. The alternate sensitivity population has the same restrictions but retains non-CRC cancer during washout; it excludes washout CRC and death. It is a cancer-experienced survivor estimand and cannot be used for the primary CRC-versus-first-non-CRC specificity contrast.

Parse ICD-10 codes only after authoritative syntax confirmation. Accept three-character, undotted child-code and dotted child-code forms; malignancy is C00–C97, CRC is C18, C19 or C20 including children, and non-CRC is malignant excluding C18–C20. Collapse exact duplicate `eid`/date/code pairs. A date containing both CRC and non-CRC codes is classified CRC-first for date-level analysis, with all codes retained for audit. A cancer date after earliest death is excluded from observable endpoint analyses and reported in a data-quality audit. Same-day cancer/death is death-first in the primary endpoint because within-day ordering is unavailable; cancer-first is a prespecified sensitivity rule.

The existing full-row feasibility audit gives the following coverage-independent checks: 502,370 joined rows; 20,341 ordered repeat visits; 18,379 complete three-primary-marker pairs; 20,275 age-eligible at `L`; 18,328 base cohort; 16,542 prior-cancer-free and alive at `L`; 16,298 primary no-washout-event entry candidates; 75 nominal first CRC statuses and 118 nominal first deaths in the primary entry set through `H`; 79 CRC diagnoses before death; 3 cancer/death same-day cases; zero malignant code-dates after death; and 8 malignant code-dates on a death date. ## Variables and statistical analysis

For marker `m`, define

`slope_m = (m_1-m_0) / ((L-53-0.0)/365.2425)`.

The primary adverse-direction terms are falling haemoglobin, falling MCV and rising platelets. Keep exactly three continuous one-degree-of-freedom slopes. The comparator is age at `L`, sex and instance-1 haemoglobin, MCV and platelets; the trajectory model adds the three slopes. RDW and its slope are a separately labelled secondary extension requiring four-marker completeness.

Use a locked, low-dimensional ridge-penalized cause-specific Cox model with left truncation at `E`. Estimate cumulative incidence with Aalen–Johansen, treating death as a competing event. Fine–Gray is sensitivity only. For prediction, use identical participant-level repeated outer splits, with scaling, imputation, censoring estimation and penalty fitting within training folds. Report paired out-of-fold time-dependent AUC/Uno C-index, IPCW integrated Brier change and horizon-specific calibration only with adequate event and effective-sample support. For specificity, refit the identical pipeline for first non-CRC malignancy on identical people and splits and report `D = incremental CRC AUC - incremental non-CRC AUC` with participant-bootstrap intervals.

At fixed 1%, 2% and 5% review capacities, rank by strictly out-of-fold five-year CRC cumulative incidence. Report flagged N, CRC events, competing deaths, administrative censoring, effective sample size, sensitivity, PPV, events per 1,000 flagged and fraction captured, with IPCW/Aalen–Johansen estimation and training-fold weight truncation. These operating characteristics remain descriptive and cannot be interpreted as clinical utility.

Event gates are unchanged: at least 60 certified CRC events permits the locked analyses; 30–59 permits parsimonious association and descriptive censoring-aware analyses but no flexible model or strong calibration claim; fewer than 30 permits flow, event audits, cumulative incidence and at most the simplest prespecified penalized association, with incremental prediction inconclusive. Failure of the coverage certificate or field semantics is a hard stop.

## Falsification and interpretation

Permute endpoint processes within sex and five-year age strata and rerun all fold-local steps; any systematic retained increment indicates leakage or implementation error. Attempt to predict pre-`L` CRC using the post-`L` pipeline as an impossible-temporal-outcome check; apparent performance triggers date and cohort audits. Unit tests must cover suffix-preserving pairing, undotted/dotted ICD parsing, exact inclusion of day 366, same-day precedence, post-death exclusion, left truncation, fold isolation, and IPCW training-fold estimation.

Supportive evidence requires an adverse-direction joint slope association with uncertainty excluding the null, a positive paired out-of-fold increment without Brier or calibration deterioration, and clean falsification checks. CRC specificity additionally requires paired `D>0` with an interval excluding zero and compatible Brier findings. Adverse evidence is a precise null/opposite-direction association with no positive prediction increment, or precise `D<=0`; this does not refute clinically timed CBC models. Broad intervals, unstable weights, discordant metrics, underpowered non-CRC analyses, or an absent certificate are inconclusive.

Only computation can establish whether the specified cohort construction, parsing, timing, model fitting, resampling and reported uncertainty were executed correctly. Clinical meaning of units, registry ascertainment, same-day ordering, transportability, and any action threshold require expert review or another dataset. External clinically timed CBC validation is required before transportability claims, and a prospective impact study is required before claims about screening, referral, stage, mortality or benefit.
