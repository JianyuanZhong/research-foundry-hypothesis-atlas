# Proposal draft — Hospital-level heterogeneity in speed of liberation from
# mechanical ventilation in eICU-CRD, and whether observable practice differences
# (not case mix) explain it.

Seed: [starting question] (parent [prior hypothesis]).
Dataset: eICU-CRD 2.0. Status: data-bound DRAFT for Lead promotion.

## 1. Scientific opening
Mechanical ventilation is required by 50–70% of ICU patients, and weaning/liberation
is among the most consequential and most variable decisions in critical care
[K1]. Two prior facts, both now verified against the configured eICU-CRD snapshot,
open this question:

1. **Weaning outcomes differ across institutions.** Sheikhalishahi et al. [K1] found that
   weaning-prediction performance varied by database/institution, i.e. the
   weaning-outcome distribution is institution-specific (abstract-level evidence).
2. **Ventilator practice is measurable and variable in eICU-CRD.** Samanta et al.
   [K2] measured low-tidal-volume-ventilation adherence from EHR-recorded ventilator
   settings (including eICU-CRD) and showed implementation is poor and variable
   (abstract-level evidence).

Yet **no inspected study decomposes hospital-level heterogeneity in the *speed* of
liberation and tests whether that heterogeneity is explained by *observable practice
differences* rather than case mix.** That is the unresolved question here.

## 2. Hypothesis (leading claim + strongest rival)
- **Leading claim (H1):** After adjusting for illness severity (case mix), hospitals
  differ in the speed of liberation from MV, and a meaningful fraction of that
  residual between-hospital variance is associated with observable practice
  differences (ventilator settings and sedative practice) recorded in the EHR.
- **Strongest rival (H0 / case-mix-only):** All between-hospital variation in
  liberation speed is explained by differences in case mix (severity, age, unit
  type, admission source); once severity is adjusted, the hospital random effect
  shrinks to near zero and practice variables add no explanatory power.
- **Secondary rival (H2 / documentation artifact):** Apparent hospital differences
  in "time to liberation" reflect differences in *recording density* (when/whether
  MV treatment rows and ventilator settings are charted), not true clinical
  practice. This is a severity-adjacent, measurement artifact and is the main
  threat to interpretation; the design below includes explicit checks for it.

The claim is **descriptive/associative, not causal**: an association between
practice and liberation speed, surviving severity adjustment, does not identify a
mechanism or a quality difference. Per the seed and [K3], residual hospital
differences **must not** be used for simplistic hospital rankings.

## 3. Estimand and outcome
- **Population:** adult ICU stays in eICU-CRD 2.0 with ≥1 mechanical-ventilation
  treatment row (treatment.csv.gz, `treatmentstring` contains "mechanical
  ventilation"). Verified: **42,111 MV stays across 190 hospitals; 146 hospitals
  with ≥20 MV stays.**
- **Time origin:** `treatmentoffset` of the first MV row (hours from unit admit).
- **Event (liberation):** `treatmentoffset` of the last MV treatment row in the
  stay. **Time-to-liberation = last_MV_offset − first_MV_offset.**
- **Censoring:** if no MV row ends before discharge, censored at
  `unitdischargeoffset` (patient.csv.gz). ICU death
  (`unitdischargestatus` = "Expired") censors the event (competing event).
- **Sensitivity outcome:** `actualventdays` (apachePatientResult.csv.gz) as an
  independent, pre-computed vent-day measure; and a settings-based liberation
  (last ventilator-setting row offset) to probe the documentation artifact (H2).
- **Exclusions (pre-specified):** MV duration < 12 h (likely brief/procedural
  ventilation, not a weaning process); stays with missing `hospitalid` (none
  verified); stays with no APACHE score (severity unmeasurable) — reported, not
  silently dropped.

**Practice-variable construction (per stay, plausibility-filtered):**
- Ventilator settings: for each label (Tidal Volume (set), PEEP, FiO2, Vent Rate,
  Plateau Pressure, Peak Insp. Pressure), filter to physiologic ranges
  (e.g. FiO2 20–100, PEEP 0–24, TV 200–1500 mL, plateau 10–40) to remove the
  negative-minima data-entry noise found in the probe; summarize as median and
  early-stay (first 24 h) value. Tidal volume normalized to predicted body weight
  using `admissionheight` + `gender` (PBW per [K2]).
- Sedation: per-stay presence and duration (drugstartoffset→drugstopoffset) for
  propofol, midazolam, dexmedetomidine (verified available: 29,973 / 27,890 /
  6,882 rows among MV stays).
- **Hospital-level practice summary:** the within-hospital median of each practice
  variable (a "practice profile"), used as the cluster-level moderator.

## 5. Analysis plan
**Primary model (hierarchical, time-to-liberation):**
- Frequentist random-intercept model for time-to-liberation. Baseline:
  - **M0 (severity-only / case-mix):** log(time-to-liberation) ~ APACHE II + age +
    sex + unit type + hospital random intercept (u_h ~ N(0, σ_h²)).
  - **M1 (severity + practice):** M0 + hospital-level practice profile (ventilator
    settings + sedation) as fixed cluster-level moderators, and/or stay-level
    practice terms.
- **Key estimands:**
  1. **σ_h² (between-hospital variance)** and the **intraclass correlation (ICC)**
     from M0 — quantifies residual hospital heterogeneity after case-mix.
  2. **Change in σ_h² / ICC from M0 → M1** — the central test: does adding
     observable practice variables reduce the unexplained between-hospital
     variance? A meaningful reduction supports H1; no reduction supports H0.
  3. **Standardized hospital effects** (hospital-specific liberation-speed
     residuals / observed-expected ratio, per [K3]) — reported descriptively,
     **not** as a ranking.
- **Model form:** primary is a linear mixed model on log(time) (robust to the
  right-skewed duration); a Cox proportional-hazards model with hospital random
  effect (frailty) is a sensitivity analysis for the time-to-event structure.
  Bayesian hierarchical version (per [K3]) as a further sensitivity analysis.

**Baseline vs learned alternative (method comparison, same question):**
- **Simple baseline:** the hierarchical model above (interpretable, directly
  yields σ_h², ICC, and the practice-moderation test).
- **Learned alternative:** XGBoost (per [K1]) predicting time-to-liberation (or
  prolonged-ventilation binary, e.g. vent days > 7) with **whole-hospital
  holdout** (train on ~120 hospitals, test on ~26 held-out hospitals).
  - What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses: (i) whether
    hospital-level heterogeneity is *predictable* from observable features — if
    XGBoost on held-out hospitals generalizes, the between-hospital signal is
    structured and learnable, not noise; (ii) feature-importance/SHAP shows which
    practice variables (vs severity) drive cross-hospital differences; (iii) it
    captures nonlinear practice–severity interactions the linear model cannot.
  - What it does **not** replace: the random-intercept model is the instrument for
    the *variance decomposition* (σ_h², ICC, practice-moderation), which a
    black-box model does not provide. The two are complementary; the linear model
    is primary for the hypothesis, XGBoost is secondary for generalizability and
    feature attribution.
- **Split:** whole-hospital (cluster) holdout to avoid leakage; no patient from a
  held-out hospital appears in training.

**Documentation-artifact check (H2):** correlate each hospital's recording density
(rows per stay in treatment.csv.gz and respiratoryCharting.csv.gz) with its
residual liberation-speed effect. If the residual effect tracks recording density
rather than practice, H2 is implicated and the claim is downgraded to
"inconclusive for true practice differences."

## 6. Falsification criteria
- **Supportive (H1):** σ_h²/ICC in M0 is non-negligible (e.g. ICC > 0.02) AND
  adding practice variables in M1 reduces σ_h² by a pre-specified margin (e.g.
  ≥ 20% relative reduction, with a confidence/credible interval excluding 0),
  AND the residual effect is not explained by recording density (H2 check).
- **Adverse (H0):** σ_h²/ICC in M0 is near zero (case mix explains hospital
  variation) OR practice variables do not reduce σ_h² (no practice association).
  Either result is a valid, informative negative finding.
- **Inconclusive:** residual effect is driven by recording density (H2), or
  liberation definition is unstable across the treatment-based vs settings-based
  vs `actualventdays` definitions, or APACHE coverage is too low to adjust
  severity reliably. Then the claim is narrowed or the study deferred.
- **Not established by this study (require more data / adjudication):** any causal
  or quality-of-care interpretation, any hospital ranking, and any generalization
  beyond eICU-CRD (single-source, US, tele-ICU network).

## 7. Limitations
1. **Observational, non-causal.** Practice variables are confounded by severity
   and indication; adjustment is not proof of a mechanism.
2. **No ranking claims.** Residual hospital effects are descriptive; per the seed
   and [K3] they cannot be read as quality-of-care differences.
3. **Recording density / documentation artifact (H2).** "Last MV row" is a proxy
   for true liberation; variable charting behavior across hospitals can mimic
   practice differences. Mitigated by the recording-density check and the
   `actualventdays` sensitivity outcome, but not fully eliminable.
4. **Liberation definition.** Treatment-based liberation may miss weaning to
   non-invasive support; the settings-based and `actualventdays` definitions are
   sensitivity analyses, not a gold standard.
5. **APACHE row duplication.** apachePatientResult has multiple rows per stay in
   some cases (71,462 rows for 42,111 stays); dedup rule (most complete / earliest)
   must be applied and is a potential source of mild misclassification.
6. **Single dataset.** eICU-CRD only; no external validation.
7. **Reference inspection limits.** K1-K3 are cited at abstract level by the Lead
   (full-text XML frozen and identity-verified; specific numeric details in the
   full text were not independently re-verified by the Lead).

## 8. What would be newly fitted / estimated (deliverable)
- A per-stay dataset (MV stays) with time-to-liberation, censoring, APACHE
  severity, and stay- + hospital-level practice variables.
- **Fitted:** M0 and M1 hierarchical models → σ_h², ICC, practice-moderation

- **Completion outputs:** variance-decomposition table (M0 vs M1), ICC with CI,

  results to supportive/adverse/inconclusive.
