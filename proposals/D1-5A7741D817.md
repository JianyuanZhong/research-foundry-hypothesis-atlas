# How much of the eICU within-hospital "less lab drawing → deterioration" signal can one documented comfort-care transition explain? A pre-registered EOL-anticipation bound

Lead working proposal — eICU 2.0 island, **episode 26**. Operation: **evolve** of true parent
`[prior hypothesis]` (valid, delivery-complete, mu 33.00, generation 11; episode-25
evolve of `[prior hypothesis]`; seed origin `[starting question]`, "reduced testing
and subsequent deterioration"). Population, exposure, outcome, landmark, severity adjustment, the
within-vs-between decomposition, the E-value decision rule and the LOHO transport test are **frozen
from the parent**. This is a bounded evolution, not a rewrite. Status: **design owned by the Lead for
compilation.** No solver/reference pipeline was executed at discovery — this is design + grounding +
one bounded, executed data probe. It is not a solved result.

## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

Clinicians decide how often to draw ICU labs. [K1] Agniel 2018 showed, across 272 test types and
669,452 patients, that the *presence and timing* of a test order independently predicts survival
(order presence significant in 233/272 tests; timing beat the value in 118/174) — draw intensity
encodes a **care process**, not just physiology. The decision at stake is "**is less ICU lab testing
harmful here, or is it low-value testing we can safely reduce?**" — the exact question now under
randomized test by [K2] WICH-ICU (stepped-wedge Choosing Wisely de-implementation; protocol/SAP
inspected abstract-only; its numeric target difference is NOT asserted here).

The parent decomposed the association into within-hospital (β_W) and between-hospital (β_B) components
and bounded the residual threat with an E-value ([K3] VanderWeele & Ding 2017). Its *remaining open*
rival R5 is **within-hospital prognosis/anticipation**: clinicians stop drawing labs *because they
already judge the patient will deteriorate or die* (e.g., a decision to transition to comfort care),
so β_W reflects residual confounding by an anticipated transition rather than a monitoring failure.
R5 was previously only a verbal caveat. This child makes **one concrete anticipation channel —
documented end-of-life (EOL) care-plan transition — empirically measurable and falsifiable**.

## 2. What is already supported vs the unresolved claim

- Already supported (not re-claimed): (a) test-order presence/timing carries survival signal [K1];
  (b) sicker patients receive more monitoring; (c) a severity-adjusted within-patient panel draw-rate
  change is associated with later escalation (parent branch).
- **Unresolved claim this experiment tests:** the within-**hospital** β_W is a patient-level
  unmet-need signal that is *not* explained by a documented comfort-care transition (EOL care plan) or
  by unmeasured confounding of plausible strength — i.e. H1 survives the EOL-anticipation channel AND
  the E-value ≥ 2.0. The strongest rival (R5) denies the first part.

## 3. Leading claim H1 vs rivals, and the observation that separates them

- **H1 (leading, falsifiable):** β_W < 0 (draw-intensity drop → later escalation), high within-hospital
  variance share S_W, E-value ≥ 2.0 for the point estimate, stable LOHO transport, **and β_W retained
  after censoring/excluding documented EOL transitions.**
- **R2 (between-hospital practice fingerprint):** β_W ≈ 0, β_B ≠ 0, low S_W, poor LOHO transport.
- **R4 (reverse ordering):** addressed by strict temporal ordering + washout + hour-12 placebo landmark.
- **R5 (the target rival, NEWLY QUANTIFIED here):** the effect is anticipation — clinicians taper
  monitoring when a comfort-care transition is being planned/implemented. **Separating observation:**
  the maximum bias R5 can induce is bounded by the prevalence and timing of *documented* EOL care plans;
  a pre-specified EOL-censoring sensitivity analysis must leave β_W materially unchanged.

## 4. Executed data probe (this episode; real, read-only source)

`carePlanEOL.csv.gz` joined to `patient.csv.gz` on `patientunitstayid` (percentages of 200,859 stays,
208 hospitals; source snapshot `896e1fb7…` per `datasets/eicu/README.md`):

- mortality (`unitdischargestatus=='Expired'`): **5.43%**
- any documented EOL care plan (`cpleolid` non-null): **1,230 stays = 0.71%** overall
- EOL prevalence among decedents **4.11%** vs survivors **0.52%**
- EOL documented in only **96 of 208** hospitals; `cpleoldiscussionoffset` present for 0.71%
- among expired+EOL stays (n=451): median EOL-save → unit-discharge = **775 min**; **94.9%** saved
  before discharge (consistent with an anticipated, pre-terminal transition)

**Interpretation (bounded):** the *documented* EOL-transition channel has very low exposure prevalence
(0.71%) and covers only ~4% of deaths, concentrated in <half of hospitals. Therefore a documented
comfort-care transition can explain at most a small fraction of any within-hospital draw-rate→
deterioration association; this channel alone is unlikely to be a sufficient R5 mechanism. It does not
test *undocumented* prognostication, which the E-value bound addresses.

## 6. Baseline vs substantive alternative (same question)

- **Baseline:** single uncentered logistic regression of escalation on panel draw-rate change
  (no decomposition, no sensitivity). Loses the ability to attribute a hospital-mean draw practice
  (R2) separately from a patient-level signal (H1).
- **Substantive alternative (proposed):** multilevel model with random hospital intercept **and**
  random draw-rate slope, explicit within/between centering (β_W, β_B, S_W, ICC), **plus** the
  pre-registered EOL-censoring sensitivity analysis and the E-value/negative-control outputs. The
  alternative reveals whether the harm-direction signal is a patient-level process or a hospital
  fingerprint, and whether a documented anticipation channel can absorb it — information the baseline
  cannot produce. CPU-only, minutes-scale; inside `solver_planning_limits`.

## 7. Pre-specified outcomes (supportive / adverse / inconclusive)

- **Supportive (H1 over R5-EOL):** β_W<0 with 95% CI excluding 0; unchanged direction/significance
  after EOL censoring/exclusion; E-value(point) ≥ 2.0; negative-control (chronic organ-insufficiency
  diagnosis) association null.
- **Adverse:** β_W attenuates to include 0 after EOL censoring, or E-value < 1.5 → a plausible
  anticipation/transition channel (R5) or weak effect cannot be excluded; H1 not distinguishable.
- **Inconclusive:** wide CI / sparse EOL events across hospitals / unstable LOHO → no decision-grade
  statement; report as such.

## 8. Falsification and honest limits

Falsification is pre-specified (Section 7) before computation. Honest limits retained: readiness only
— the multilevel, E-value and LOHO pipeline is specified but **NOT executed** at discovery; the executed
probe covers only the EOL channel prevalence/timing. EOL is *under-coded* (listwise/care-plan capture),
so a null EOL finding bounds documented transitions only, not undocumented prognostication. The E-value
assumes one unmeasured confounder and RR-scale approximation from OR. LOHO is internal transport, not
external validation. Causal/"unmet need" and policy-grade claims require stewardship/quality-registry
data ([K2] randomized result) or expert clinical adjudication.
