Let `N_s` be all BP-eligible rows in stratum s and `n_mis,s` its missing count. For observed rows, `d_i=(Y_i-p0_i)^2-(Y_i-p1_i)^2`. For missing rows, evaluate the affine contrast

```
d_i(y) = 2*y*(p1_i-p0_i) + p0_i^2-p1_i^2
```

at L and U. The fixed-prediction stratum bounds are

```
Delta_s,lo = (sum_observed d_i + sum_missing min(d_i(L),d_i(U))) / N_s
Delta_s,hi = (sum_observed d_i + sum_missing max(d_i(L),d_i(U))) / N_s.
```

These are deterministic identification regions, not confidence intervals. Also report all-missing-at-L, all-missing-at-U, and least-favorable assignments. Report model-specific SSE/MSE endpoint components and the conservative (not sharp) RMSE-difference enclosure
`[sqrt(MSE0_lo)-sqrt(MSE1_hi), sqrt(MSE0_hi)-sqrt(MSE1_lo)]`, without standardizing it by an unidentified SD.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support for robust incremental prognostic information requires successful source/hash/reconstruction/clock assertions, both support gates, an estimable observed-case result, no material differential-observation warning, and a global Delta interval strictly above zero under the declared training-range scenario. Even then, the claim is limited to selected short-horizon prognostic association in the tested calendar mixture; category-specific positivity is not sufficient for robust support.

Adverse evidence is a nonpositive primary result in an adequately supported block, repeated M1 degradation, or a global bound wholly nonpositive. This challenges incremental prediction but does not prove B sufficiency or mechanism.

Selection-sensitive evidence is favorable complete-case performance with a global bound crossing zero, a nearest common-displacement tipping root near zero, materially different albumin versus bilirubin observation fractions, category intervals with opposite signs, or large observed/missing covariate gradients. This is the principal expected outcome under severe selective observation and must not be reported as support.

Unsupported/inconclusive evidence includes failed support gates, small strata, empty/degenerate training range, future out-of-range Y, nonfinite predictions, nonestimable SD/bootstrap, low transport or observation ESS, influential patient clusters, or dependence on full-source ranges. A 2022 local result cannot be generalized to 2023/2024 absent adequate support and observation robustness.

The verifier must test both positive and negative synthetic branches: a favorable observed-case gain with zero-crossing all-BP bounds must be labeled selection-sensitive; a nonpositive observed gain must remain adverse even if an ascertainment stratum is favorable; missing Y must never be recoded; albumin and bilirubin must never be pooled; category presence must never be treated as completed assay follow-up; documents must never supply time; all-BP denominators must be preserved; deterministic bounds must never be labeled CIs; and out-of-range future Y must invalidate, not widen, the temporal bound.

Computational checks can establish hashes, headers, duplicate-safe joins, clocks, funnels, differential observation tables, prediction finiteness, endpoint arithmetic, roots, and bootstrap bookkeeping. They cannot establish assay units, clinical severity, tumor burden, imaging response, TACE intent/administration, completed care, external-care capture, mortality, hepatic failure, causality, utility, actionability, or benefit. Those require clinical adjudication and independent temporal, external, or prospective evidence.

## Pre-specified negative controls and falsification

Negative controls are run separately for albumin and bilirubin, use the same outcome-blind folds and pre-origin preprocessing where applicable, and cannot alter the primary estimand or repair a failed support gate.

1. **Ascertainment-process negative control.** Among every BP-eligible row, predict the binary outcome `A=1` when the post-event window has a valid examination or additional timed encounter (the union of `exam+followup`, `exam_only`, and `followup_only`) using the same M0 covariates and M1 plus P. The document-only and none categories remain defined but are not silently recoded into `A=1`. This process outcome is observed for all BP rows by construction and must be analyzed before reading Y. A sizable P increment, or materially different process performance by assay, is a prespecified observation-selection warning; it is not evidence of clinical harm or proof of non-MAR.

2. **Delayed-window negative control.** If an assay has at least 20 BP-eligible rows overall and at least 10 per arm with a finite assay value in `(event+72h,event+144h]`, define a separate delayed outcome using the nearest value to `event+96h` under the inherited deterministic tie rule. Fit/report it as a distinct endpoint, never substitute it for Y, and apply the same training-range and support checks. Incremental P information that is no weaker for the delayed endpoint than for the prespecified Y is a timing-specificity warning; a sparse or unavailable delayed endpoint is `not_estimable`, not a negative result.

3. **P-linkage placebo.** Within each frozen test row set, randomly reassign only the numeric P values using a declared seed, while preserving B, the pre-event core, Y, row membership, patient-cluster folds, arm, and broad calendar block. Repeat the complete fixed-design comparison for 1,000 permutations (or record resource failure). This is a restricted linkage placebo, not a conditional-randomization test: it should generally attenuate an association dependent on patient-specific P linkage, but a null or positive placebo does not prove mechanism. It is a falsification warning if the placebo is comparable to or better than the observed incremental result.

The negative-control outputs must include counts, estimability status, point estimates, and uncertainty/identification status separately from the primary result. The precedence order is: invalid source/clock or missing required columns -> `unsupported`; failed BP support -> `inconclusive_support`; nonpositive primary observed gain -> `adverse`; primary global envelope crossing zero -> `selection_sensitive`; only then can a finite, positive primary result receive `supportive_surrogate_translation`, and it remains surrogate-level. Negative-control warnings override that label to `selection_sensitive` for robustness interpretation but never convert an observed laboratory association into a patient-outcome claim.
