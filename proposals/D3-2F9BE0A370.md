# MerTK-conditioned macrophage–fibroblast communication in rheumatoid arthritis

Episode 1; evolved as a targeted repair of expert seed D3-M2. This is a planned Harbor experiment, not an executed biological result.

## Scientific question and hypothesis

Rheumatoid-arthritis (RA) synovium contains macrophage and fibroblast states associated with either destructive inflammation or remission. The strongest inspected evidence supports three separate observations: HBEGF+ macrophages can induce an EGFR-linked inflammatory/invasive fibroblast response in human RA tissue [K1]; MerTK-positive synovial macrophage subsets are associated with remission and can induce a fibroblast repair response [K2]; and MERTK+ macrophage and fibroblast states are co-enriched in clinically distinct RA synovial tissue contexts [K3]. None of those observations establishes that a MerTK-conditioned macrophage state is the directional driver of a fibroblast transcriptional response, and co-enrichment can reflect shared tissue context or composition.

Hypothesis: among human RA FLS samples cocultured with MerTK/CD206-positive macrophages, macrophage pre-treatment with the recorded MerTK inhibitor will reduce a prespecified coherent inflammatory-FLS program relative to the matched untreated coculture. The reduction should be larger and more coherent than the available MerTK/CD206-negative control, should not be reproduced simply by LPS pre-treatment, and should remain after modeling the contributing FLS/macrophage donors. This is a directional perturbation hypothesis about an in-vitro molecular state, not a claim that MerTK blockade will improve RA clinically.

The key unresolved comparison is:

- MerTK-conditioned communication: Mpos macrophage pre-treatment changes the FLS program in a reproducible direction, with coherent reversal of an inflammatory score.
- Strong rival: generic macrophage activation, donor mixture, FLS composition, batch/cell-number effects, or inhibitor off-target pharmacology explains the same pattern. Those rivals predict no Mpos-specific, donor-consistent reversal or a comparable effect in Mneg/LPS controls.

What would change practice or research is not a small predictive gain. A positive result would justify target-engagement and functional coculture studies aimed at preserving or restoring a pro-resolving macrophage–stromal circuit in difficult-to-treat or relapsing RA. An adverse result would prevent MerTK state association from being promoted to a MerTK-directed fibroblast mechanism.

## Closest inspected work and substantive advance

[K1] established a human RA macrophage–fibroblast communication axis and an EGFR-dependent fibroblast consequence, but it did not test the configured MerTK-pretreated macrophage mixtures. The present experiment adds a matched perturbation boundary and explicitly acknowledges that HBEGF and EGFR are absent from the targeted E-MTAB-8873 panel.

[K2] associated MerTK-positive macrophage subsets with remission and fibroblast repair. The present experiment asks whether a recorded perturbation of the macrophage input changes a prespecified inflammatory FLS output, rather than treating a remission association as a directional mechanism.

[K3] defined clinically meaningful co-enriched MERTK+ macrophage and fibroblast states and candidate communication structure. The present experiment is a small, donor-aware perturbational test designed to distinguish communication from shared tissue context, selection and composition. It does not reproduce K3’s clinical cohort or spatial evidence.

The advance is therefore boundary testing: state association and inferred communication become a falsifiable, bounded perturbation hypothesis, with explicit failure criteria and a direct next experiment.

## Sampling, preprocessing, analysis, and uncertainty

The solver must first emit a binding audit: each matrix header must join one-to-one to a design sample_id; each primary pair must have exactly one untreated and one pre-inhibitor Mpos sample; all excluded/conflicting and mixed-ratio records must be listed. Source files are read-only and derived matrices/results go to the workspace.

For E-MTAB-8873, apply the documented targeted-matrix convention, preserve the full transcript identifiers, remove no features merely because they are unmeasured, and filter only samples failing the explicit header/design or numeric-value audit. Normalize within matrix using a predeclared library-size/size-factor method, log-transform for score modeling, and retain raw counts for a negative-binomial sensitivity analysis. Do not merge duplicate identifiers. Report per-sample cell count, detected-feature count, library size, and all filtering decisions.

Primary outcome is the sample-level inflammatory FLS score: the standardized mean of the eight prespecified row values after normalization, with each gene’s direction fixed before fitting. The primary pairwise effect is delta_k = score(Mpos + MerTK-inhibitor)_k - score(Mpos untreated)_k for the three usable matched mixtures. A supportive direction is negative attenuation in all or nearly all pairs, with a coherent panel-level estimate and uncertainty. Secondary contrasts compare untreated Mpos with untreated Mneg where the FLS donor contribution is recorded; the one available R1 Mneg inhibitor/no-inhibitor pair is a negative-control check, not a stable estimate. LPS-treated Mpos/Mneg records are a generic activation control and are never relabeled as MerTK perturbations.

Fit the simple baseline as a prespecified per-feature donor-aware model (negative-binomial count model or limma-voom equivalent, with size factor, experiment/run and FLS/macrophage contributing-donor terms when identifiable) plus the eight-gene score model. Do not use an ordinary donor t-test on the 33 E-MTAB-8873 columns. With only three usable matched mixtures, report paired estimates, donor/mixture bootstrap or Bayesian posterior intervals, and effect directions; do not manufacture asymptotic p-values or call the result independently replicated.

Fit the substantive alternative on the same inputs: a small hierarchical multivariate latent-factor model in which the eight-gene inflammatory score is a prespecified loading block and the remaining measured 445-feature matrix can contribute only through regularized factors, with random effects for FLS donor, macrophage donor/contributing mixture, and run. The target is coherent FLS-state reversal under inhibitor, not prediction of a clinical label. Fit the alternative only on the training donor clusters and report a leave-one-cluster-out stress test across R1/R2/R5; with three clusters this is a fragility diagnostic, not external validation. Compare the baseline and alternative on signed paired effect, uncertainty coverage by donor/mixture resampling, coherence of gene-level reversal, and sensitivity to the conflicting donor record. The latent model is scientifically useful because the baseline can hide a distributed, partially correlated state behind noisy per-gene tests; it can also reveal that apparent reversal is driven by one technical module or donor. It is deferred if the sample-level design cannot support partial pooling without unstable factors.

E-MTAB-8316 is analyzed separately as a donor-paired compound-response context: average repeated other_donor_id contexts within each of the four verified donor_id clusters before any exploratory comparison, as required by the dataset guide. Use it to ask whether the compound produces a coherent monocyte transcriptional shift consistent with the proposed macrophage-state perturbation, not to confirm FLS direction or target specificity. E-MTAB-8322 and GSE95588 may be used only for source-specific descriptive context after their own feature/lineage limitations are honored.

No GPU is needed for the planned discovery or solver: the matrices are small enough for CPU linear/multivariate models, and a 16-CPU, 32–64 GB RAM allocation should be sufficient; this estimate is reasoned from the 33×445 targeted matrix and is not a measured fit benchmark. The configured planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 8 GPUs, 262144 MiB and 28800 seconds; it is a future solver envelope, not an instruction to use GPUs. A bounded CPU fit should be under minutes, but convergence and model-library availability remain unverified. No full scientific screen was executed in this episode; only schema/header/design and source-literature inspection were performed.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive result: the three usable Mpos matched pairs show a negative inhibitor effect on the inflammatory score, the direction is coherent across the prespecified genes, the effect is not as large in the available Mneg control, donor-aware sensitivity does not erase it, and the multivariate alternative agrees without being driven by an unmeasured feature. This supports a MerTK-conditioned FLS communication hypothesis and justifies independent factorial coculture with target engagement and functional outcomes. It does not prove direct ligand–receptor binding, MerTK target specificity, clinical efficacy, remission causality, or a mechanism involving HBEGF/EGFR.

Adverse result: the score is unchanged or increases under Mpos inhibitor, the sign is inconsistent across matched mixtures, a comparable effect occurs in Mneg or LPS controls, the effect vanishes after donor/run adjustment, or the latent alternative localizes it to a technical/composition factor. This falsifies the proposed MerTK-specific directional interpretation for this assay and redirects work toward generic activation, donor-specific biology, or assay validity. It does not prove that MerTK is irrelevant in RA.

Inconclusive result: the three matched mixtures cannot be reconciled, the donor contribution is not identifiable, counts are too sparse, the panel is not measured reliably, or UNC106238/“MerTK inhibitor” identity and exposure cannot be verified. The correct action is to obtain protocol/target-engagement information and run a new balanced factorial coculture, not to interpret a null as biological refutation.

Computationally checkable claims are the header/design joins, measured-feature availability, normalization/model execution, paired estimates, uncertainty, sensitivity results, and whether the conclusion matches the prespecified outcome. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for direct target engagement, secreted cytokines, fibroblast invasion/matrix damage, tissue localization, treatment response, flare risk, and patient benefit. The verifier must reject both incorrect computation and unsupported claims that a QA result proves mechanism or clinical efficacy.

## Method alternatives and selection record

The simple donor-aware score/feature model was selected as the primary baseline because it is transparent, aligned to the limited matched design, and gives an interpretable falsification test. The hierarchical multivariate factor alternative is retained because it can reveal distributed state coherence and donor heterogeneity that separate gene tests lose; it is not selected as the sole method because three usable Mpos pairs and the targeted panel do not support an unconstrained latent model. A mechanistic ligand–receptor mediation model is deferred: the primary targeted matrix is FLS-focused and lacks HBEGF/EGFR, direct ligand abundance, protein secretion, target engagement and a balanced factorial design. Evidence that would justify revisiting it is a verified ligand-containing assay, independent FLS/macrophage donors, vehicle/washout details, direct MerTK engagement, and a functional FLS endpoint.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted, donor-aware estimates of the Mpos inhibitor effect on the prespecified FLS score and feature panel, plus a fitted/assessed multivariate alternative and the binding/QC audit. Completion requires those outputs, uncertainty and the supportive/adverse/inconclusive classification; a readiness package alone is not a biological result.

## Demonstration and seed dispositions

The three clinical demonstrations were treated as ambition references, not topic requirements. Delphi’s learned dated-disease-history method is not pursued because these are molecular endpoint assays without longitudinal EHR histories; its lesson is to compare a substantive learned alternative against a simple baseline, which is applied here with a multivariate FLS-state model. Oncoformer is not pursued because the configured domain has no routine EHR or chest-X-ray modality; its lab-only adaptation would answer a different predictive question, not this mechanism question. ALADYNOULLI is not reproduced because genetic inputs and longitudinal patient trajectories are unavailable; a latent-factor adaptation is retained only as a compact descriptive alternative for the current perturbation question.

D3-M1 is deferred: E-MTAB-8322 is macrophage-only and E-MTAB-8873 is targeted FLS/coculture, so whole-tissue composition versus within-cell regulation cannot be identified without tissue composition or matched whole-tissue data. D3-M3 is deferred as a longitudinal persistence claim: E-MTAB-8322 has cross-sectional treatment-naive, resistant and remission groups and no transition times; it can later support a bounded state comparison, not persistence/remission transition inference. D3-M2 is developed because its MerTK-labeled pre-treatment contrasts provide the clearest bounded perturbation test, while the unresolved target-specific and functional evidence is explicitly retained.
