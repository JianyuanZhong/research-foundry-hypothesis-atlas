# Rising HbA1c with concurrent weight loss as a prodromal pancreatic cancer signal: a power-aware paired-trajectory analysis in UK Biobank

Parent: expert seed UKB-40 ([prior hypothesis]), evolved in episode 1.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

Pancreatic cancer is usually diagnosed at advanced stage, with 5-year survival under 13%. Any window in which a routine, measurable metabolic signature identifies occult disease earlier would matter for enrichment of early-detection studies. The closest precedent [K1] showed in pooled US cohorts that recent-onset diabetes combined with weight loss is associated with elevated pancreatic cancer risk in the following few years. What that work could not do, and what remains unresolved, is separate two mechanistic explanations using *measured within-person trajectories* at similar glycemic deterioration:

1. **Occult-tumor explanation**: an undiagnosed pancreatic tumor drives both catabolic weight loss and glycemic worsening; predicts a large, short-latency, cancer-site-specific association concentrated in the first years after the trajectory is observed.
2. **Obesity/metabolic explanation**: rising HbA1c is the usual progression of obesity-related metabolic disease [K3]; predicts graded risk tied to baseline adiposity, no weight-loss-specific excess beyond general deterioration, and no pancreatic-cancer specificity.

An umbrella review of EHR-codable risk factors [K2] confirms that dynamic trajectories (glycemic change with concurrent weight change) remain outside established risk models, which rely on static factors; insulin use and diabetes are among established factors but trajectory-based enrichment is unmapped.

**Hypothesis (testable, falsifiable)**: among adults whose HbA1c rises between two assessments, those with concurrent >=3% weight loss have higher short-latency pancreatic cancer incidence than those with stable/gained weight, with the excess concentrated in 0-3 years after the second assessment and specific to pancreatic cancer (not seen for other cancers).

## Design (Harbor experiment, CPU-only)

- **Population**: adults with HbA1c and weight at both instance 0 (2006-2010) and instance 1 (2012-2013); exclude pancreatic cancer diagnosed before index (visit-1 date); exclude self-reported/medication-evidenced insulin or sulfonylurea use at either instance (treatment-driven weight change), with sensitivity analysis retaining metformin-only users.
- **Index time**: visit-1 date. **Follow-up**: to first pancreatic cancer (40005/40006, C25*), death (40000), or administrative end.
- **Exposure**: DHbA1c (mmol/mol) and Dweight (%) between instances, modeled (a) categorically at pre-specified thresholds (>=+5 mmol/mol rise; >=3% loss) and (b) continuously with restricted cubic splines (3-5 knots) and their interaction.
- **Primary outcome**: incident pancreatic cancer (C25). **Pre-specified time windows**: 0-3 y (recent/prodromal) vs >3 y (distant), reported separately (landmark splitting).
- **Primary analysis**: Firth-penalized Cox regression (35 events demand penalization) adjusted for age at index, sex, baseline BMI, smoking status, baseline HbA1c, and interval length. Report HR per contrast with 95% CI.
- **Baseline comparison (simple)**: exact Poisson/binomial incidence rates and rate ratios per exposure cell with exact CIs - transparent, assumption-light, but discards dose-response and covariate structure.
- **Substantive alternative (chosen)**: penalized Cox with spline trajectories. What it adds: distinguishes the two mechanisms by shape and timing - the occult-tumor explanation predicts a sharp non-linear excess concentrated at extreme joint trajectory values and the 0-3 y window; the obesity explanation predicts a graded risk that tracks baseline BMI and survives without a weight-loss-specific excess. The baseline cannot make this separation.
- **Specificity control (pre-specified negative-control outcome, not endpoint padding)**: repeat the primary contrast for non-pancreatic cancers in the same cohort; a prodromal signal should be pancreatic-specific or far stronger for pancreatic cancer.
- **Method selection rationale**: CPU seconds-to-minutes for either; no GPU benefit at n=12.8k. Neural models are not scientifically useful here (35 events cannot support them); documented rather than banned.

## Falsification criteria (power-aware, from probe)

With 35 post-index events the study detects only large effects; a null with wide CI is inconclusive, not refuting.

- **Supportive**: HR >= 2 for weight loss among rising-HbA1c participants in the 0-3 y window with lower 95% CI > 1, plus specificity (non-pancreatic cancer HR materially lower).
- **Adverse**: point HR <= 1 with upper 95% CI < 1.5 (excludes clinically meaningful prodromal signal), stable across windows.
- **Inconclusive**: wide CI spanning 1 without excluding HR ~2-3 (the honest expectation given 35 events); the correct conclusion is bounded precision, not absence of association.
- What stronger conclusions would need: external replication in a larger repeat-assessment cohort, clinical adjudication of cancer registry completeness, and treatment linkage (full primary-care prescribing is not in this snapshot).

## Competing explanations and their handling

Intentional weight loss (adjust baseline BMI, smoking; sensitivity excluding weight-loss-reporting subgroups), glucose-lowering treatment (20003/20004 exclusion/sensitivity), other cachexia (negative-control cancer outcome; exclude pre-index cancer), healthy-volunteer repeat attendance (generalizability limit, stated), registry completeness (limitation, stated).

## Key references

- [K1] Yuan C, Babic A, Khalaf N, et al. "Diabetes, Weight Change, and Pancreatic Cancer Risk." JAMA Oncology 2020 (PMID 32789511, PMC7426876). Closest precedent: recent-onset diabetes + weight loss associated with elevated pancreatic cancer risk. Limits: US cohorts, self-reported diabetes/weight, no measured paired glycemic-trajectory contrast.
- [K2] Moore SF, Price S, Konya J, et al. "Risk factors for pancreatic cancer in electronic health records: an umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses." eClinicalMedicine 2025 (PMC12206054). Bounds current EHR risk-factor knowledge (80 factors; 38 risk-increasing); dynamic trajectories unmapped.
- [K3] Amadou A, Freisling H, Mercoeur B, et al. "Anthropometric traits, metabolic biomarkers, and pancreatic cancer risk: a causal mediation analysis in UK Biobank." Br J Cancer 2026 (PMC13578719). Same dataset, obesity-metabolic pathway (BMI/WHR HR ~1.20-1.24, metabolic-biomarker mediation) - the consequential rival explanation measured at baseline; does not address within-person trajectories.

## Honest limitations

35 events => precision-bounded; single trajectory pair (no instance 2/3 HbA1c); medication codes are treatment-episode records, not complete prescribing; healthy-volunteer repeat subset; findings inform enrichment strategies, not screening policy.
