## Decision question, evidence, and substantive advance

The expert UKB-03 seed asks whether creatinine can miss renal risk that cystatin C and strength reveal. The mature parent correctly converts unsupported “severe AKI” into a recorded inpatient N17 endpoint and asks whether cystatin C adds value beyond creatinine, age, sex, repeat spacing and grip in a fixed review list, with an early-fit/no-refit-2013 test.

This successor addresses the remaining clinically consequential uncertainty: the parent conditions on having valid cystatin C and therefore estimates assay value only in a measurement-selected subset. A cystatin-C signal can look useful because people with particular renal risk, health-care contact, or assay availability are overrepresented. The data can test observed measurement-selection robustness, but cannot recover an unmeasured clinical ordering indication.

The primary unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among repeat-assessment participants with a valid creatinine-based baseline and a first recorded renal endpoint after T1, adding contemporaneous cystatin-C eGFR to the parent baseline improves held-out fixed-capacity N17 review capture in the assay-complete cohort, and the increment remains directionally present after fold-local inverse-probability weighting to the broader creatinine-and-grip-eligible target population; the same assay increment is not materially larger for N18 than N17 under the prespecified endpoint-specific analysis and early-fit/no-refit-2013 transport test.

The N17 component retains the parent's decision question. N18 is a prespecified specificity check: a result restricted to recorded CKD would not establish that cystatin C identifies acute kidney-failure risk, while discordant N17/N18 results are clinically informative about endpoint dependence. The fixed list is an analogue of limited review capacity, not a treatment policy.

The strongest available evidence supports only that the frozen UKB snapshot contains the required structured fields, repeated assessment dates, exact creatinine/cystatin-C/grip columns, one-row-per-eid tables, 259 paired diagnosis/date positions, and death-date fields. The local kidney-function paper supports physiological plausibility in 38 mechanically ventilated ICU patients using cystatin C, creatinine, iohexol and ultrasound; it does not support this UKB prognosis. No fitted result, effect, mechanism, disease onset, assay benefit, or clinical recommendation is claimed.

## Selection/indication robustness estimand

Define S=1 before outcomes as valid T1 cystatin C plus valid T1 M0 inputs, among T. Fit a fold-local regularized logistic selection model for S using only variables available before the cystatin-C result: age, sex, T0 calendar year, T1 calendar year, L, T0 creatinine, T1 creatinine/eGFRcr, T0 and T1 grip when valid, and explicit missingness indicators. No outcome, N17/N18 date, death, cystatin-C value or post-T1 field is allowed. Fit selection models in early folds only and apply unchanged to 2013; report predicted-propensity overlap, stabilized weights p(S=1)/p(S=1|X), truncation thresholds (early 1st/99th percentiles), standardized mean differences and effective sample size. This is a measured-selection sensitivity under a missing-at-random assumption, not a causal correction for unrecorded indication.

The assay increment is estimated in two linked ways: (i) the parent's complete-assay fixed-K capture contrast in S, and (ii) inverse-probability-weighted held-out N17 Brier/log-loss and calibration contrasts in S, interpreted as transport to T only when overlap and ESS pass. Do not claim a fixed-K policy for unassayed participants; cystatin C is not observed there. A sensitivity uses complete-case versus fold-local imputation, with the primary complete-case definition frozen before outcomes.

## Falsification and interpretation

Falsification controls include outcome permutation within era/fold; cystatin-C permutation within sex, M0-risk, spacing and selection strata; independent cystatin permutation preserving M0 risk and missingness; random-K lists; early/late label permutation; complete-case versus fold-local missingness; a pre-T1 negative-control endpoint; and deliberately shifted 41270/41280 pairing, which must fail integrity. If the M1 gain persists after breaking cystatin-C linkage while preserving M0 risk, S, weights, spacing and workload, cystatin attribution is falsified. A selection model with no common support, extreme weights or ESS below the gate makes transport inconclusive rather than “robust.”

Supportive N17 evidence requires all semantic/unit/timing/code-date/horizon/integrity gates, >=100 eligible N17 events overall and >=40 in each era where transport is reported, K>=40, adequate overlap and weighted ESS (at least 200 and at least half unweighted in every reported band), late weighted N17 capture increment and its 95% interval wholly above the parent's 0.05 research margin, no early/late sign reversal, and no M1 calibration degradation beyond 0.05 intercept or 0.10 slope relative to M0. The complete-assay fixed-K result and weighted score result must agree in direction. These are prespecified research criteria, not clinical-benefit thresholds.

Endpoint-specific support requires the N18 and joint endpoint gates to pass before describing specificity. A larger N18 increment supports endpoint dependence only if the N17 contrast is also reported and the interaction interval excludes zero; it does not establish CKD biology. Adverse evidence includes no gain, reversal after weighting, gain explained by spacing/current risk/selection, calibration harm, failed cystatin permutation, or a positive result confined to one unsupported endpoint. Inconclusive evidence includes failed field semantics or specimen timing, incomplete follow-up, malformed pairs, sparse endpoint cells, poor positivity/ESS, wide intervals, disagreement between weighted and unweighted estimates, or contradiction between recorded and certified prospective modes.

A supportive result establishes conditional incremental predictive information for recorded inpatient renal codes in the defined UKB repeat-assessment population and, at most, measured-covariate transport from assay-complete people toward that target. It does not establish clinical ordering indications, diagnosis, disease onset, AKI severity/KDIGO stage, outpatient disease, measured GFR, muscle mechanism, causal effect of testing, treatment benefit, external validity or a threshold for action. Those require linkage/ascertainment documentation, assay and specimen review, clinical adjudication, treatment/indication data, external validation and a prospective policy/impact study.
