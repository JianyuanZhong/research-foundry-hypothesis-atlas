## Unresolved Question

At the same minimum blood pressure, do patients with longer-lasting hypotension in the first 6 hours of ICU care experience more subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI)? Specifically, does time-weighted hypotension burden (time below SBP 90 mmHg in the first 6h) add independent predictive value beyond the minimum blood pressure alone, after adjusting for illness severity, and is this association consistent across hospitals?

## Clinical Importance

- Minimum blood pressure is the standard bedside trigger for vasopressor escalation, but it discards all duration information. Two patients with the same nadir SBP of 80 mmHg may have very different renal perfusion if one was hypotensive for 10 minutes versus 4 hours.
- Time-weighted hypotension burden captures both severity and duration.
- If duration at the same nadir independently predicts AKI, it supports shifting from threshold-based to burden-based monitoring in the ICU, with direct implications for vasopressor titration and nursing assessment frequency.
- eICU-CRD contains 208 hospitals (verified), enabling a test of cross-site consistency.

## Evidence for the Opening

Three inspected works establish the foundation (same as parent, already hash-receipted):

**[K1]** Liu Z et al. (2026, Front Med) [10.3389/fmed.2026.1916589] demonstrated in 7,405 perioperative GI cancer surgery patients that cumulative duration of MAP <65 mmHg was associated with postoperative ICU admission (OR 1.78 for ≥30 min vs none), and that time-weighted area below MAP 65 mmHg was independently associated (OR 1.07 per 50 mmHg·min). **Limitation:** perioperative setting, ICU admission (not AKI) as outcome, single database.

**[K2]** Ao Q et al. (2026, Clin Interv Aging) [10.2147/CIA.S615407] showed in 1,248 hospitalized older adults with AKI that a three-dimensional dynamic MAP framework (onset value, change rate, recovery time) improved prediction of major adverse kidney events at 90 days (AUC 0.71→0.76). **Limitation:** single center, older adults only, exploratory, retrospective.

**[K3]** Flick M et al. (2026, Br J Anaesth) [10.1016/j.bja.2026.04.042] prospectively quantified postoperative hypotension in 1,002 noncardiac surgery patients: 37.7% had MAP <65 mmHg, with median cumulative duration 180 min. Participants with hypotension had higher rates of AKI. **Limitation:** postoperative ward (not ICU), single center, AKI was secondary/exploratory.

**Gap:** No study has tested whether time-weighted hypotension burden in the *first hours of ICU care* independently predicts AKI beyond minimum BP, in a multi-hospital cohort with hierarchical adjustment.

## Hypothesis

In ICU patients with ≥20 blood pressure measurements in the first 6 hours, time below SBP 90 mmHg (minutes) is independently associated with KDIGO-defined AKI within 72h, beyond the minimum SBP, after adjusting for APACHE IV score, age, sex, and vasopressor use, with a consistent direction of effect across hospitals.

## Study Design

### Estimand
The target estimand is the **conditional average treatment effect** of an additional 30 minutes of hypotension (SBP <90) in the first 6h on the probability of KDIGO AKI within 72h, conditional on:
- Survival to the end of the first 6h (exclusion of early deaths)
- Availability of ≥20 SBP readings in the first 6h (measurement availability)
- Availability of baseline creatinine (first 6h) and follow-up creatinine (6-72h)

This is a **conditional estimand** — it applies to patients who survive the first 6h and have adequate measurement. It does not estimate the effect in the full ICU population.

### Population
- ICU stays from eICU-CRD (2.0) with:
  - ≥20 systolic BP readings in the first 6 hours (offset 0–360 min) of ICU stay
  - ≥1 creatinine measurement in the first 6 hours (baseline)
  - ≥1 creatinine measurement between 6h and 72h (for AKI assessment)
  - APACHE IV score available
  - Hospital ID available
  - Hospital discharge status available (for secondary outcome)

### Exposure (first 6h, offset 0–360 min)
- **Primary:** Time below SBP 90 mmHg (minutes) — computed from vitalPeriodic readings
- **Secondary:** Time-weighted area below SBP 90 mmHg (mmHg·min) — Σ(max(0, 90 − SBP_i) × Δt_i)
- **Baseline comparator:** Minimum SBP in the same window

### Outcome
- **Primary:** KDIGO AKI within 72h of ICU admission:
  - Creatinine rise ≥0.3 mg/dL from baseline, OR
  - Creatinine ≥1.5× baseline
  - Baseline = first creatinine in first 6h
  - AKI assessed using lab values with offset between 360 and 4320 min
- **Secondary:** Hospital mortality (hospitaldischargestatus = "Expired")

### Covariates
- APACHE IV score (apachePatientResult.apachescore)
- Age, sex (patient.age, patient.gender)
- Vasopressor use in first 6h (infusionDrug: norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine, dobutamine)
- Hospital (random intercept)

### Analysis Pipeline (Exact Computation Steps)

1. **Data extraction:**
   - From `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`: extract patientunitstayid, observationoffset, systemicsystolic where 0 ≤ observationoffset ≤ 360
   - From `lab.csv.gz`: extract patientunitstayid, labresultoffset, labresult where labname = "creatinine"
   - From `apachePatientResult.csv.gz`: extract patientunitstayid, apachescore
   - From `patient.csv.gz`: extract patientunitstayid, hospitalid, age, gender, hospitaldischargestatus
   - From `infusionDrug.csv.gz`: extract patientunitstayid, drugname, drugstartoffset, drugstopoffset where drugname matches vasoactive agents

2. **Feature computation (per stay):**
   - `n_sbp_6h`: count of SBP readings in 0-360 min
   - `min_sbp_6h`: minimum SBP in 0-360 min
   - `time_below_90`: total minutes where SBP < 90 in 0-360 min (trapezoidal rule on reading intervals)
   - `area_below_90`: Σ(max(0, 90 − SBP_i) × Δt_i) in mmHg·min
   - `baseline_creat`: first creatinine in 0-360 min
   - `peak_creat_72h`: maximum creatinine in 360-4320 min
   - `aki`: (peak_creat_72h − baseline_creat ≥ 0.3) OR (peak_creat_72h ≥ 1.5 × baseline_creat)
   - `vasopressor_6h`: 1 if any vasoactive infusion started in 0-360 min
   - `mortality`: hospitaldischargestatus == "Expired"

3. **Primary model:** Multilevel logistic regression:
   - `aki ~ min_sbp_6h + time_below_90 + apachescore + age + gender + vasopressor_6h + (1 | hospitalid)`
   - Test: is `time_below_90` significantly associated with AKI after adjusting for `min_sbp_6h`?
   - Report: OR per 30 min of time_below_90, 95% CI, p-value

4. **Secondary model:** Replace `time_below_90` with `area_below_90`

5. **Hospital consistency:** Direction of effect in ≥80% of hospitals with ≥20 patients

6. **Sensitivity analyses:**
   - a. MAP <65 mmHg threshold (use systemicmean)
   - b. Exclude patients with <30 SBP readings (recording density sensitivity)
   - c. Exclude patients with vasopressor use in first 6h (treatment indication sensitivity)
   - d. MAP <65 threshold with area metric

7. **Learned alternative (exploratory):** Gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM) with the same features, evaluated by AUC and calibration on a held-out hospital split (20% of hospitals held out). This tests whether non-linear interactions between hypotension duration and severity exist, and whether the model learns hospital-specific patterns vs. patient-level physiology.

### Falsification Criteria
- **Adverse (hypothesis not supported):** time_below_90 is not significantly associated with AKI (p > 0.05) after adjusting for min_SBP and covariates, in the primary model.
- **Inconclusive:** Significant association in the full cohort but inconsistent direction across hospitals (<50% concordant), or significant only in one threshold definition.
- **Supportive:** time_below_90 is significantly associated with AKI (p < 0.05) after adjusting for min_SBP, with consistent direction in ≥80% of hospitals, and the association persists with MAP <65 threshold.

### What Each Outcome Would and Would Not Establish
- **Supportive:** duration adds information beyond nadir BP for AKI risk in the early ICU period. Would NOT establish causality (confounding by severity, vasopressor timing) or identify an optimal intervention threshold.
- **Adverse:** nadir BP captures the relevant perfusion information; duration may not matter once depth is fixed. Would NOT rule out duration effects in specific subpopulations (e.g., septic shock).
- **Inconclusive:** suggests hospital-level heterogeneity or measurement limitations; would warrant investigation of recording density and artifact handling.

## Method Comparison: Baseline vs. Learned Alternative

**Baseline (primary):** Multilevel logistic regression with fixed effects for min_SBP, time_below_90, APACHE, age, sex, vasopressor; random intercept for hospital. This directly tests the hypothesis with interpretable coefficients and hospital-level consistency.

**Learned alternative (secondary):** Gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM) with the same features, evaluated by AUC and calibration on a held-out hospital split. This could reveal:
- Non-linear interactions between hypotension duration and severity
- Whether the model learns hospital-specific patterns vs. patient-level physiology
- Feature importance ranking to confirm whether duration or nadir dominates

**Resource estimate:** Baseline model: <10 min CPU. Learned model: <30 min CPU for ~2,000 patients. Both well within budget.

## Limitations
1. eICU vitalPeriodic recording density varies by hospital; time-weighted burden is sensitive to measurement interval. **Mitigated by sensitivity analysis (b) above.**
2. Vasopressor timing may be confounded with hypotension severity (treatment indication bias). **Mitigated by sensitivity analysis (c).**
3. AKI definition relies on creatinine, which has delayed kinetics; early AKI may be missed.
4. No data on renal perfusion pressure directly; SBP is a surrogate.
5. Retrospective design; cannot establish causality.
6. Conditional estimand: results apply only to patients surviving 6h with adequate measurement.

## Required Additional Evidence
- Clinical adjudication of AKI cases (creatinine-based definition is standard but not perfect)
- Prospective validation in a separate cohort
- Data on renal perfusion pressure or urine output for a more complete AKI assessment
