# Is the laboratory-observation signal invariant to a hospital's surveillance regime?

Status: Episode-29 substantive successor to [prior hypothesis]. This proposal preserves the assigned parent's adult first-stay cohort, ICU-relative six-hour landmark, revision-safe laboratory rows, recorded ICU-death competing-risk outcome, whole-hospital transport, and equal-review-burden discipline. It adds one unresolved, decision-relevant question: whether a laboratory-process score transports across hospitals that routinely observe laboratories at different baseline intensities. No model has been fitted and no clinical benefit is claimed.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The parent establishes a disciplined way to ask whether laboratory observation history adds information beyond recorded vital state and general vital-observation intensity. It does not settle whether that increment is invariant to the hospital's surveillance regime. A high-volume tertiary ICU and a lower-intensity ICU can assign very different meanings to the same count, gap, or missingness pattern. A raw process score may therefore appear useful in pooled data while using local testing practice as a hidden site marker.

The clinically consequential question is:

> Among adults at ICU-relative minute 360, does the laboratory-observation increment for later recorded ICU death remain favorable and approximately stable when evaluated in hospitals with low versus high outcome-blind baseline laboratory-surveillance intensity, or does its value depend on the local testing regime?

This affects whether a hospital-blind review-prioritization score could be transported to a new ICU. A regime-dependent score could over-review patients in low-testing hospitals, under-review patients in high-testing hospitals, or encode access/interface practice rather than a stable patient-linked signal. A within-hospital relative-process representation is a bounded alternative: it tests whether a patient's laboratory activity relative to the local regime transports better than absolute counts.

The strongest available evidence is an outcome-blind feasibility audit inherited from the parent: 135,680 eligible adult first ICU stays, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows known by minute 360. These are feasibility facts, not fitted performance, evidence of clinician intent, or physiologic validation. The local schema and source headers confirm hospital identifiers, laboratory result/revision offsets, and the required vital fields. The expert seed [starting question] raises the related testing-practice concern, but remains an untested source idea, not evidence.

The unresolved claim is not that testing causes death or that more testing is beneficial. It is that a retrospective process increment is transportable across observed surveillance regimes and can support equal-burden retrospective review ranking. The substantive advance is an explicit regime-heterogeneity estimand and a same-input, outcome-free local-normalization contrast. It distinguishes a stable patient-relative process signal from a signal that relies on absolute site-specific surveillance.

## Falsifiable hypothesis

Primary hypothesis H1 (regime-invariant process increment):

1. In each supported regime, adding laboratory process to the recorded-state baseline produces a favorable held-out-hospital incremental integrated competing-risk Brier score.
2. The macro-hospital increment is not materially different between low- and high-surveillance regimes: the absolute regime contrast is less than 0.01 integrated Brier units over the prespecified follow-up window.
3. At the same per-hospital review burden, the process model's recorded-death capture/PPV advantage over the state baseline does not reverse between regimes and does not require more reviews.

For hospital h, let
D_h = IBS_h(Bp_raw - IBS_h(Bv),
where lower Brier is favorable and IBS_h integrates the 24 discrete post-landmark bins. Define the primary transport estimand

Gamma = mean_h(D_h | R_h = high) - mean_h(D_h | R_h = low),

where each mean gives equal weight to supported held-out hospitals, not episodes. H1 requires both regime-specific D_h means to be favorable and a 95% hospital-clustered interval for Gamma contained in [-0.01, 0.01]. A sign reversal, a regime contrast whose interval is clearly outside that margin, or a favorable increment confined to one regime falsifies the invariant interpretation. The 0.01 margin is a prespecified practical transport margin for this study; it is not a claim of clinical equivalence.

Secondary mechanistic hypothesis H2 is that replacing raw process magnitudes with within-hospital empirical percentiles, without outcome or hospital ID in the patient model, reduces |Gamma| while preserving the within-hospital process increment. H2 is supportive only if the rank alternative is fit on the same labels, folds, and patient-level raw inputs and its improvement is reproducible across hospitals. H2 is not allowed to rescue a failed H1 by replacing the primary estimand.

## Population, time, outcome, and estimands

Use eICU snapshot [source checksum].

Construct one row per person's first valid ICU stay:

- retain numeric patient.age >= 18, recoding ages greater than 89 to 90;
- within patient.uniquepid, select the smallest valid numeric patient.unitvisitnumber, breaking ties by numeric patientunitstayid;
- require patient.unitdischargeoffset > 360 and a valid numeric patientunitstayid;
- retain only stays reaching the fixed ICU-relative minute-360 landmark.

The predictor window is ICU-relative minutes [0,360], inclusive at 0 and 360. No post-landmark predictor may enter any model.

A laboratory row is retained for process/state construction only when lab.labresult is numeric, lab.labresultoffset in [0,360], and lab.labresultrevisedoffset <= 360 or null. A result whose revision was not known by minute 360 is not treated as available at the landmark. The analyte codebook, valid ranges, duplicate rule, and compatible system/interface encodings must be frozen from development hospitals without outcomes. The local lab schema has no separate unit column; unresolved unit or interface meaning is an exclusion or separately supported stratum, never an invented unit assumption.

A vital row is retained when observationoffset in [0,360]. Vital state and observation-intensity summaries use the same fixed window and the parent’s support-gated fields.

The outcome is the first discharge event represented by the selected patient row after the landmark and no later than minute 1,800:

- event of interest: normalized unitdischargestatus == Expired and unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800];
- competing event: normalized unitdischargestatus == Alive and unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800];
- administrative censoring: neither event occurs through minute 1,800.

Unexpected statuses, duplicates, invalid/negative offsets, and impossible status/offset combinations are audited and reported before fitting; they are not silently assigned. Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, latent death, and post-ICU outcomes are outside the estimand.

Fit cause-specific discrete-time hazards for 24 one-hour bins covering (360,1800], then derive cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death with alive ICU discharge competing. The primary metric is macro-hospital integrated competing-risk Brier score over these bins. Secondary outcomes are time-specific Brier/calibration, cumulative incidence at minute 1,800, and the exact equal-burden review metrics below.

## Baseline, alternative, and fitting protocol

Use five outer folds grouped by hospitalid), holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once. Keep all rows with the same uniquepid together. Stratify hospital folds by eligible episode and event counts when feasible. All codebook, valid-range, scaling, imputation, regularization, threshold, and calibration choices are development-only.

### Transparent baseline and raw process model

- Bv): pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic hazards using admission context plus supported laboratory state/availability, recorded vital state/availability, and no laboratory-process features.
- Bp_raw: exactly Bv) plus the prespecified laboratory-process features: total valid rows, distinct analytes, distinct result times, first/last result time, recency, span, inter-result-gap summaries, analyte-specific counts/recency, and revision-known status. No hospitalid) enters.
- Bp_raw-Bv is the patient-process increment. The same selected episodes, labels, folds, and scoring horizons are used for both.

### Mechanistic within-hospital alternative

- Bp_rank: exactly the Bp_raw feature set and the same elastic-net competing-risk likelihood, but transform each laboratory-process magnitude by an empirical within-hospital percentile map. The map is fit without outcomes from 50 deterministic, hash-selected eligible calibration episodes per held-out hospital; those calibration episodes are excluded from evaluation. The map is applied to the remaining episodes, and a development-hospital pooled model is fit using development-only rank maps. If a hospital has fewer than 50 calibration episodes and 20 separate evaluation episodes, Bp_rank is unsupported there rather than using a fallback.
- The calibration sample is selected by a fixed hash of numeric patientunitstayid, with seed 20260914, before any outcome is read. Its only fields are the same raw [0,360] patient-level process features and their missingness indicators. No status, discharge offset after 360, hospitalid), hospital descriptor, or post-landmark field is used.
- The mechanism being tested is that absolute laboratory activity contains a site-regime component, while within-site rank captures a patient’s relative surveillance pattern. Bp_rank can reveal regime-stable relative deviations that Bp_raw loses; it loses absolute testing intensity, which may itself carry signal, and needs a small unlabeled local calibration sample. A gain after normalization is evidence for a measurement-regime explanation, not evidence that the normalized score is causal or clinically actionable.
- To compare on identical inputs, the primary Bp_raw and Bp_rank comparison uses the common non-calibration evaluation episodes in hospitals passing the rank support gate. Report the raw model on all eligible held-out episodes separately, but do not use that non-common analysis to claim superiority.

The selected alternative is deliberately mechanistic rather than a larger prediction contest. The parent’s compact six-bin GRU remains a deferred secondary option: it tests temporal ordering/persistence, an already-separated uncertainty that does not identify cross-hospital surveillance invariance. It may be run only as an exploratory sensitivity on the same channels and folds, with order-shuffle and parameter-matched non-temporal controls, and cannot replace Bp_raw, Bp_rank, or Gamma.

## Transport evaluation and equal-burden policy

For each held-out hospital, report D_h, regime label, number of calibration and evaluation episodes, recorded deaths, lab-row regime statistics, and whether the rank support gate passed. The primary Gamma is the macro-hospital difference between low- and high-regime D_h values on the prespecified common supported set.

In each held-out hospital h and for q in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, define the Bv) reference policy as episodes with held-out Bv) cumulative-incidence score at least q. Let k(h,q) be its alert count. Rank the same episodes by Bp_raw and Bp_rank, selecting exactly k(h,q) with deterministic tie-breaking. For Bp_rank, use only its common supported evaluation episodes; the Bv) reference is recomputed on that same episode set. Report review fraction, recorded-death capture, recorded-death PPV, calibration among reviewed/non-reviewed, and incremental membership. Summarize separately by regime and as paired macro-hospital differences. Extra alerts cannot create a policy gain.

The policy result is secondary and retrospective. A reported q must have at least 20 supported evaluation hospitals across the analysis, at least 10 alerts per supported hospital on average, at least 100 alerted episodes, and at least 20 recorded deaths in supported cells. Unsupported q values are exploratory only.

## Uncertainty, support gates, and readiness

Use hospital-clustered uncertainty for all primary contrasts: resample hospitals within regime with replacement, preserving each hospital’s held-out predictions and its calibration/evaluation designation, for at least 1,000 resamples if feasible. Report 95% intervals for each regime’s D_h mean, Gamma, the rank-vs-raw change in |Gamma|, calibration drift, and policy differences. Report all hospital-level values and fold directions; pooled episode metrics are descriptive companions. Repeat the rank analysis with calibration sizes 25 and 100 only as a declared sensitivity, preserving the 50-episode primary analysis.

Before fitting, write the cohort flow, outcome-blind codebook, fold manifest, regime/calibration manifest, feature-support manifest, and leakage/timestamp audit. Primary H1 interpretation requires:

- at least 1,000 recorded deaths and 50 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes overall;
- at least 20 supported held-out hospitals in each surveillance regime, with at least 20 evaluation episodes per hospital; if the common rank-supported set is smaller, H1 may still be estimated for Bp_raw but Bp_rank is descriptive/inconclusive;
- at least 50 calibration and 20 separate evaluation episodes for every hospital included in Bp_rank;
- at least 200 recorded deaths pooled in each regime for the primary Gamma interval, unless the data audit shows a prespecified lower bound is unavoidable, in which case label the regime contrast inconclusive rather than relaxing it after seeing results;
- overlapping Bv)-risk and vital-coverage strata for process permutation in each regime;
- every primary model converges in all five hospital folds and produces finite held-out predictions for the evaluated episodes;
- no patient, uniquepid), hospital, outcome, post-landmark field, calibration outcome, or revision-unknown result leaks into a predictor.

Run readiness checks against the exact local paths, gzip ordinary-file headers, schema IDs/hashes, joins, offset bounds, numeric parsing, duplicate handling, first-stay selection, fold isolation, and finite fixture predictions. Readiness is packaging/probe validation, not evidence for H1.

## Locked falsifications and interpretation

Lock these before reading held-out outcomes:

1. Compare D_h by regime and compute Gamma. A clear adverse regime contrast, sign reversal, or regime-specific support failure falsifies transport invariance.
2. Fit Bp_raw on development hospitals in one regime and evaluate it in the opposite regime, where both training/evaluation cells pass support. Strong cross-regime degradation with acceptable within-regime results identifies transport failure rather than generic model failure.
3. Apply Bp_rank on the identical common evaluation episodes. If rank normalization reduces |Gamma| and preserves the process increment, it supports the local-relative measurement mechanism; if it does not, do not claim that simple normalization solves transport.
4. Permute laboratory-process channels within held-out hospital, stratified by Bv) predicted-risk quintile, vital-coverage stratum, and surveillance regime while holding state fixed. The process increment should attenuate. Persistence indicates residual site/interface or leakage explanation.
5. Repeat with the common analyte panel and revision-known-only rows. Loss under defensible codebook/revision rules is adverse to a robust patient-linked interpretation.
6. Fit vital-observation-only and hospital-descriptor-only diagnostics using hospital.numbedscategory, teachingstatus), and region) only as site-level diagnostics. If these reproduce the contrast or it is concentrated in one site/interface/analyte family, the general patient-linked interpretation is falsified.
7. Repeat regime labeling with distinct result times, report site-label disagreement, and perform leave-one-hospital-out influence summaries. A result driven by a small number of hospitals is not a transport claim.
8. Recompute q policies with exactly the same per-hospital k(h,q). A gain caused by increased review burden is invalid. If the deferred GRU is run, require attenuation under bin-order permutation and a parameter-matched non-temporal control.

Supportive results require passed gates, favorable D_h in both regimes, Gamma within the declared margin with uncertainty, no unacceptable calibration drift, equal-burden policy results that do not reverse by regime, attenuation under process permutation, and no descriptor/site/codebook/revision artifact. They establish a transportable retrospective recorded-death proxy under the observed eICU regimes—not a benefit from ordering tests, better patient outcomes, or clinician actionability.

Adverse results include a null/reversed increment in one regime, a clearly material Gamma, cross-regime degradation, persistence under process permutation, descriptor/site reproduction, calibration harm, or loss under codebook/revision controls. They support explanations based on local workflow, selective testing, interface/revision artifacts, or omitted physiology and argue against a single hospital-independent process score.

Inconclusive results include failed regime/event/overlap/calibration gates, too few supported hospitals or deaths, unstable fold directions, failed convergence, leakage, wide intervals spanning the practical margin, substantial regime-label disagreement, or unsupported rank normalization. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.

## Unavailable evidence and required next studies

The eICU snapshot has no validated laboratory order time, specimen-collection time, clinician reason for ordering, clinician review/acknowledgement, treatment response, alert exposure, patient-centered benefit/harm endpoint, or independent physiologic adjudication. labresultoffset and labresultrevisedoffset cannot be called specimen time; the lab table lacks a separate unit column. Public narrative note sections are removed, structured fields do not establish intent, and vitalPeriodic is not raw waveform. Hospital descriptors are coarse and cannot fully measure staffing, interface, access, or shift regime. Recorded ICU discharge status is not post-ICU survival.

Therefore computation can check cohort construction, regime definition, model fit, transport contrasts, calibration, uncertainty, and whether conclusions follow from those outputs. It cannot establish that a test should be ordered, that a clinician would review an alert, that laboratory surveillance changes treatment, that the signal reflects true physiology, or that patient outcomes improve. Those claims require expert analyte/unit and physiology adjudication, external validation in separately governed ICUs, and a prospective silent-alert study followed by an impact or stepped-wedge evaluation measuring review burden, responses, harms, and patient-centered outcomes.
