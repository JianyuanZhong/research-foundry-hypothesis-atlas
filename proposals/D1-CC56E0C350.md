# Provisional HCC hypothesis: post-hepatectomy laboratory trajectory and 30-day deterioration

Status: provisional repair for episode 9. This is a proposed relationship and experiment, not a completed analysis or clinical conclusion.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with a recorded liver resection in the HCC snapshot, the early post-operative trajectory of liver-related laboratory abnormalities will be associated with a clinically important deterioration signal during the subsequent 30 days, beyond baseline disease severity and operative context. The unresolved claim is whether a reproducible trajectory, rather than a single post-operative value, identifies a distinct recovery/deterioration pattern that could inform surveillance or escalation research.

This is not established. The available evidence establishes that the HCC snapshot contains longitudinal procedure, laboratory, encounter, diagnosis, medication, examination, pathology, and clinical-document data. It does not establish the proposed association, a causal mechanism, or clinical utility.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

A positive result would distinguish an early warning pattern that merits prospective validation from the simpler explanation that late values merely reflect a single postoperative value or baseline severity. It could inform whether trajectory-aware monitoring is a worthwhile next study. A null or adverse result would be useful: it would argue against investing in a trajectory-based escalation rule for this endpoint and would redirect attention to endpoint validity, measurement timing, or competing explanations.

The substantive advance is a discriminating comparison of a low-dimensional postoperative trajectory against a single-value baseline, with explicit temporal ordering and falsification. The HCC data can test descriptive predictive information and trajectory shape, but cannot by itself establish that a trajectory causes recovery, that a clinical intervention would improve outcomes, or that a laboratory pattern is a biological mechanism.

## Proposed population and time window

Index cases are adults with a non-missing procedure `开始时间` whose `手术` label is an explicit liver-resection label (for example, liver partial/lobe/segment resection or half-liver resection). The primary cohort excludes rows with missing index time, duplicate index events without a deterministic tie-break, and encounters without a valid encounter join. A sensitivity cohort includes all broad resection labels and reports the missing-time fraction rather than silently imputing time.

The exposure window is postoperative day 0 through day 7, using `检验时间` relative to the procedure start. The primary outcome window is postoperative day 8 through day 30. The primary outcome is a pre-specified, computable deterioration proxy: a new encounter or documented postoperative clinical event in the HCC snapshot, with the exact event rule frozen before fitting. Because the available tables do not provide a validated postoperative complication endpoint, the primary analysis will report this as an operational proxy and will not call it liver failure, readmission, or mortality. A secondary sensitivity outcome will use a conservative laboratory-only deterioration definition (for example, a prespecified worsening of the same assay within its own assay-specific scale), explicitly labeled exploratory because units and clinical thresholds are unavailable.

The primary estimand is the incremental predictive association of a low-dimensional trajectory summary with the 30-day proxy outcome among index cases, conditional on baseline covariates and operative context. This is not a causal treatment effect.

## Variables and leakage control

Baseline covariates are restricted to information available before or at the index procedure: age, sex, encounter department, and preoperative laboratory values when timestamps permit. Operative context includes the exact procedure label, laparoscopic/open label when present in the label, and procedure start/end fields where available. No post-index laboratory, examination, diagnosis, medication, or document field may enter the baseline model.

The primary laboratory trajectory uses only assays with a stable exact `检验` label and a parseable numeric `定量结果`; qualitative results are not silently converted to numbers. Assays are handled separately, without pooling across assays or assuming common units. The primary trajectory features are a small, prespecified set of per-assay summaries: first postoperative value, last value in days 0–7, slope/linear trend, and time-to-first measurable value, with missingness indicators. The exact assay set and any assay-specific scaling will be frozen from the training period only. If a unit dictionary cannot be verified, the analysis will use within-assay standardized values for prediction and will not interpret absolute clinical thresholds.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative

Baseline: a regularized logistic regression (or penalized logistic regression with prespecified missingness handling) using preoperative covariates, operative context, and the first postoperative value of each eligible assay. This tests whether a single early value is sufficient.

Substantive alternative: a low-dimensional trajectory model fitted to the same eligible assays and the same temporal split, using the prespecified first/last/slope/availability features and a regularized logistic outcome model. The alternative is scientifically substantive because it can reveal whether direction, persistence, and timing of recovery contain information lost by a single value. It is not a high-capacity black-box model and does not require GPU. A flexible nonlinear trajectory model or neural sequence model is deferred: the current data have missing units, irregular sampling, and an unvalidated endpoint, so added flexibility would not resolve the central scientific uncertainty and would complicate falsification.

Both models use the same temporal split: training and tuning on earlier index dates, with a held-out later-date cohort for final evaluation. The split is by index procedure date, not random row order. The solver must report calibration, discrimination, missingness, and uncertainty (bootstrap or prespecified confidence intervals), plus a direct comparison of the trajectory model against the single-value baseline. The scientific deliverable is the fitted comparison and held-out estimates, not a claimed clinical recommendation.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive result: the trajectory model improves held-out discrimination/calibration over the single-value baseline by a prespecified margin, with uncertainty excluding a clinically negligible improvement, and the direction is stable across assay subsets and the broad versus strict resection-label sensitivity analyses. This would support the claim that postoperative dynamics carry incremental descriptive information, not a mechanism or treatment effect.

Adverse result: the trajectory model performs no better than the baseline, or its apparent gain disappears under temporal holdout, assay-specific sensitivity, missingness checks, or the conservative laboratory-only outcome. This would falsify the incremental trajectory claim for this endpoint and favor baseline severity, measurement timing, or endpoint artifacts as the explanation.

Inconclusive result: wide uncertainty, insufficient valid index cases, sparse assay coverage, or inability to define a reproducible outcome proxy. This would not refute the hypothesis; it would identify the missing evidence needed before another study.

A positive association would not establish that changing the trajectory improves outcomes. Mechanistic or causal claims require prospective data, validated complications/mortality endpoints, assay units and clinical adjudication, treatment timing, and ideally external validation. The current HCC snapshot lacks validated diagnosis extraction, complete front-page/transfer content, images, waveforms, and a separate laboratory unit column.
