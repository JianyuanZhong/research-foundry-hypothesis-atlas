# Does one leakage-safe intermediate albumin or bilirubin value improve the near-term laboratory forecast beyond current state, a remote anchor, and measurement intensity before repeat TACE?

## Clinical question, existing evidence, and untested claim

For an adult already selected for a recorded first repeat TACE, the clinically relevant information question is not whether an old laboratory value predicts a later one in isolation. It is whether reviewing one additional, leakage-safe same-assay value from the interval since TACE2 improves the forecast of the next observed albumin or total-bilirubin value after the clinician already has the current pre-repeat value `P`, the remote pre-TACE2 anchor `B`, and knowledge of whether, how often, and how recently that assay was measured.

This distinction matters because repeated liver-function assessment is clinically salient in retreatment decisions, but a repeated value may add no information after the current value is known. A 2025 retrospective study of 300 HCC patients reported that persistent ALBI-grade deterioration after initial TACE, considered with baseline liver condition and tumour burden, identified subgroups without a significant survival benefit from repeat TACE (Chen et al., *Hong Kong Medical Journal*, DOI `10.12809/hkmj2311208`; inspected frozen full HTML source `[source checksum]`). That literature supports the clinical importance of longitudinal liver-function state; it does not establish that one intermediate assay result has incremental value beyond a current result, nor does it validate the present short-horizon observed-laboratory endpoint.

The strongest claim supported by the available HCC evidence is feasibility only. The assessed-valid parent’s complete-source, outcome-direction-blind audit scanned all five required sources and reconstructed 319 systemic-record and 1,491 comparator pathway patients, 159 and 526 selected repeat events, 40/118 albumin `B/P/Y` triplets, and 41/119 total-bilirubin triplets. Under the locked leakage-safe clock, an intermediate value exists in 37/40 systemic and 108/118 comparator albumin triplets and 38/41 systemic and 108/119 comparator bilirubin triplets. Median `N_H` was one in both arms and assays; only 18 systemic and 42 comparator patients per assay had at least two intermediate timestamps. The audit did not inspect `Y` direction, predictor–outcome associations, or model performance.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> For albumin or total bilirubin separately, the latest eligible intermediate numeric value `L` improves out-of-sample prediction of the next observed same-assay value `Y` by at least 0.05 outcome SD in RMSE beyond `P`, `B`, a compact pre-event core, and the assay’s pre-event observation pattern.

This is a prognostic information experiment. Support would justify retaining and externally validating the extra historical numeric value in a future repeat-TACE decision-support study. A precise null would argue that, under this clock and model, `P`, `B`, and measurement-pattern information already capture any design-sized short-horizon signal. Neither result determines whether an assay should be ordered, whether repeat TACE should proceed, or whether monitoring improves outcomes.

## Locked B/P/Y and leakage-safe intermediate history

For each selected repeat event and assay define:

- `B`: latest valid uncensored result on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1; day 0 is excluded.
- `P`: latest valid uncensored result in `event_encounter` during `[event_time−72 hours,event_time)`; exact `event_time` is excluded.
- `Y`: valid uncensored result in the same `event_encounter` during `(event_time,event_time+72 hours]` nearest nominal `event_time+24 hours`; choose the earlier timestamp on an exact-distance tie.

The assay-specific evaluation cohort requires `B`, `P`, and `Y`. Expected counts are albumin 40 systemic/118 comparator and total bilirubin 41/119. Albumin and bilirubin remain separate native-value outcomes and patient sets; no cross-assay pooling, ALBI construction, rank score, change outcome, threshold search, or joint primary score is allowed.

Define all candidate intermediate rows `H_rows` by the exact same assay label and all conditions:

1. `检验时间 >= tace2_time`;
2. `检验时间 < event_time−72 hours`;
3. `检验时间 < P_time`;
4. normalized `就诊号 != event_encounter`;
5. valid uncensored parser and locked duplicate handling.

Preserve every qualifying timestamp for counting. `N_H` is the number of distinct qualifying timestamps; `H_any=1[N_H>0]`; `logN=log(1+N_H)`. The only numeric-history feature is `L`, the latest qualifying value, resolving an exact-time tie by final source order. `R_hours=event_time−L_time` when observed. Do not derive a slope, spline, range, variability, threshold, sequence embedding, or cluster: the audited median `N_H=1` and only 18/42 patients with at least two intermediate timestamps do not support those features.

Assert that no `H_row` is in `event_encounter`, at or after `P`, in the 72-hour current-state buffer, or post-event. Any violation stops inference.

## One parsimonious nested experiment per assay

Use every assay-specific `B/P/Y` triplet patient, including those without `L`. The two models differ only by the numeric intermediate value:

`M_obs: Y ~ P + B + arm + age + sex + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_lead_hours + H_any + logN + R_hours`

`M_value: Y ~ P + B + L + arm + age + sex + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_lead_hours + H_any + logN + R_hours`

where `P_lead_hours=event_time−P_time >0`. Thus `M_obs` knows whether, how often, and how recently the assay was observed, but not the intermediate numeric result. The paired difference `M_obs` versus `M_value`, not a history-containing model versus `P` alone, isolates the incremental numeric value from measurement intensity and timing.

Use deterministic leave-one-patient-out cross-fitting (LOOCV), eliminating arbitrary outer-fold and penalty choices. In each held-out iteration:

- fit ordinary least squares with an unpenalized intercept on all other assay-cohort patients;
- training-median-impute age; include age-missing only if it varies in training;
- encode literal male as reference with fixed female and unknown/other/missing indicators, dropping a zero-variance indicator identically from shared model terms;
- training-median-impute `R_hours` and `L` when `H_any=0`, while retaining `H_any` in both models;
- center and scale continuous predictors using training data only;
- require each post-drop training matrix to have full column rank and finite nonzero variance for each included continuous predictor.

No outcome-driven feature selection, ridge tuning, replacement algorithm, interaction, arm-specific effect, or nonlinear model is permitted. The parent’s outcome-blind audit found the corresponding five-fold matrices full rank after deterministic removal of age-missing and sex-unknown; LOOCV rank remains an execution gate rather than an assumed fact.

For assay `a`, the sole primary estimand is

`Delta_value,a = [RMSE_a(M_obs) − RMSE_a(M_value)] / SD_a(Y)`.

Compute RMSEs from paired pooled LOOCV predictions and compute the sample `SD(Y)` once over the full assay cohort. Higher values favor the intermediate numeric value. Also report native RMSE and MAE for both models, `Delta_MAE=[MAE(M_obs)−MAE(M_value)]/SD(Y)`, pooled cross-fitted R-squared, prediction variance, calibration intercept/slope, arm-stratified descriptive errors, and each patient’s paired squared-error contribution. Coefficients are descriptive and cannot replace out-of-sample performance.

## Uncertainty and compact falsification checks

1. **Paired uncertainty and multiplicity.** Use 2,000 fixed-seed (`20260910`) patient bootstraps of the frozen paired LOOCV tuples `(arm,Y,pred_obs,pred_value)`, sampling within arm and recomputing both RMSEs, `SD(Y)`, and `Delta_value`. Require at least 95% estimable replicates. Report the percentile 95% interval and one-sided tail probability `(1+#Delta<=0)/(1+#estimable)`. Apply Holm correction across albumin and bilirubin only for assay-family claims. This interval is conditional on the fitted LOOCV pipeline; state that limitation rather than adding a costly, weakly identified model-reselection bootstrap.
2. **Patient-linkage destruction.** Among `H_any=1` patients, permute `L` values within arm 500 times while preserving `H_any`, `N_H`, `R_hours`, `P`, `B`, `Y`, covariates, and the exact LOOCV procedure. Singleton arms/strata are unchanged and reported. Support requires observed `Delta_value` above the 95th permutation percentile. This directly asks whether the numeric value is patient-specific after preserving the measurement process.
3. **Missing-history sensitivity.** Repeat the identical LOOCV comparison among `H_any=1` patients only. This is a sensitivity, not a replacement cohort. A sign reversal versus the all-triplet primary makes a favorable result missing-history-sensitive/inconclusive.
4. **Triplet-observation selection.** Among all 159/526 selected repeat-event patients, model assay-specific triplet observation with five-fold cross-fitted logistic regression using only arm, age, sex, TACE2-to-repeat days, and event year. Among observed triplets, recompute paired LOOCV metrics using stabilized inverse-observation weights truncated at the prespecified 1st/99th percentiles. Report probability range, weight concentration, and arm-specific effective sample size. A favorable result is selection-sensitive/inconclusive if systemic ESS is below 30, comparator ESS below 80, one patient carries over 10% of total weight, weighted `Delta_value` reverses sign, or `|weighted−unweighted Delta_value|>0.05`. This MAR-style diagnostic cannot recover missing outcomes or remove collider bias.
5. **Two timing sensitivities only.** Without selecting among results, repeat the experiment with (a) `P` restricted to the latest eligible value in the 48 hours before `event_time`, and (b) `Y` set to the first eligible post-event value in `(event_time,event_time+72 hours]`. Recompute triplets and `H` exactly; identify identical selections. A sign reversal in an empirically distinct variant retaining at least 75% of the primary cohort makes favorable evidence timing-sensitive/inconclusive.
6. **Influence.** Report each patient’s contribution to the paired reduction in squared error and leave-one-patient-out deletion of the aggregate `Delta_value` using the frozen LOOCV predictions. If deleting any patient reverses the sign or changes `Delta_value` by more than 0.05, a favorable result is influence-sensitive/inconclusive.
7. **Negative feasibility.** Preserve the parent audit’s pseudo-current finding: only 0 systemic/1 comparator patient per assay had `B`, an event-days −14 to −8 intermediate candidate, and primary `P`. The pseudo-current experiment is non-estimable and must not be widened after outcomes are inspected. Its absence blocks event-specific or mechanistic interpretation, not the generic prediction estimand.
8. **Repeat-event selection.** Report the 319/1,491 to 159/526 cascade and event timing by arm. Do not weight to patients without a repeat event because their repeat-event-centred `P/Y` endpoint is undefined.

## Deterministic interpretation branches

Apply branches separately by assay. Selection/timing/missing-history/influence checks can downgrade only a favorable monitoring claim; they do not convert a null or adverse estimate into support.

1. **Negative-feasibility/reconstruction-inconclusive:** source/snapshot mismatch; unexplained failure to reproduce pathway, repeat-event, triplet, parser/duplicate, history-support, or source-binding facts; join or clock violation. Do not widen windows or replace `L`.
2. **Computationally inconclusive:** a required LOOCV matrix is rank deficient, an included feature has zero/nonfinite training variance, pooled `SD(Y)=0`, OLS fails, or fewer than 95% of paired bootstraps are estimable.
3. **Linkage-, missing-history-, selection-, timing-, or influence-sensitive/inconclusive:** a favorable point estimate fails the corresponding locked check. Report the specific mechanism and retain the conditional estimate.
4. **Adverse degradation:** prior computational gates pass and the 95% interval lies wholly below zero. Conclude only that adding `L` worsened generalization under the locked model.
5. **Supportive numeric-history evidence:** prior gates pass; `Delta_value>=0.05`; its unadjusted 95% interval excludes zero; Holm-adjusted one-sided tail probability is below 0.05; `Delta_MAE>=0`; the observed gain exceeds the 95th linkage-permutation percentile; and no missing-history, selection, timing, or influence check reverses or materially destabilizes it. Conclude only that one prespecified leakage-safe intermediate same-assay value adds reproducible near-term prediction beyond current `P`, remote `B`, compact covariates, and observation intensity/timing in selected observed repeat-TACE episodes.
6. **Precise null for the design-sized increment:** computational gates pass and the interval upper bound is below `+0.05`. Conclude that this design rules out a gain of 0.05 standardized RMSE under the locked model and conditional uncertainty convention. This supports parsimony for this forecasting task, not equivalence, absence of hepatic biology, or a recommendation to omit testing.
7. **No demonstrated numeric-history increment:** point estimate below 0.05 or linkage permutation is comparable, without a wholly adverse or precise-null interval. State that current `P`, remote `B`, and/or observation pattern may exhaust the available signal.
8. **Inconclusive:** the interval spans both meaningful degradation and `+0.05`, or any remaining prespecified ambiguity exists.

A two-assay panel statement is permitted only if both assays satisfy branch 5 after Holm correction. Discordant assays are reported separately. The `0.05` margin is a design convention on standardized forecast error, not a validated clinically important difference.

## Clinical and causal limits; verifier boundary

HCC lacks laboratory units/reference ranges and analyzer harmonization, so a validated ALBI score or clinical threshold cannot be constructed. It also lacks adjudicated hepatic decompensation/complications, Child–Pugh/MELD context, tumour burden or radiographic response, technical TACE success/intent, transfusion/albumin infusion/fluid balance, infection/bleeding, verified medication administration/adherence/dose, outside care, and reliable mortality. Diagnoses are lexical encounter proxies without native time. Repeat-event and triplet conditioning can induce selection/collider bias; `Y` is the next observed laboratory value, not hepatic failure, toxicity, benefit, or utility.

A verifier can check hashes, full scans, paths/columns/keys, cohort/event reconstruction, parsing and duplicate handling, exact `B/H/P/Y` clocks, LOOCV training-only preprocessing, matrix rank, paired predictions and metrics, bootstrap/Holm arithmetic, linkage permutation, timing and selection sensitivities, influence arithmetic, and branch precedence. It cannot establish clinical importance, treatment effect, toxicity, hepatic failure, mechanism, administration, decision utility, or policy. Stronger conclusions require unit-validated assays, tumour and liver-function context, adjudicated actionable outcomes, verified treatment/procedure details, complete follow-up, external/prospective validation, and expert decision-curve or trial evaluation.
