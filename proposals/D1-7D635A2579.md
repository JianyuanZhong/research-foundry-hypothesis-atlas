# Working child: urine-output trajectory versus treatment response and endpoint artifacts

## Scientific opening

The parent asks whether a falling urine-output trajectory predicts near-term ICU deterioration beyond current state. Its strongest remaining uncertainty is whether any apparent trajectory signal is actually a marker of treatment response or a documentation/endpoint artifact. Diuretics can directly increase urine output, fluids and vasopressors can alter renal perfusion and output, and clinicians may chart urine more often or initiate RRT/ventilation after deterioration is already evident. Thus a trajectory association need not identify renal dysfunction or a treatment target.

The proposed substantive change is to make the estimand a treatment- and documentation-adjusted incremental predictive contrast, and to test whether the trajectory remains informative when the same exposure and measurement processes are represented explicitly. This is still a predictive-association question, not a causal claim about diuretics, fluids, vasopressors, or treatment benefit.

## Hypothesis and rival explanations

Among adult MIMIC-IV ICU stays at a prespecified landmark, a prespecified urine-output trajectory over the preceding 12 hours will add held-out information for a first mutually exclusive deterioration event in the next 12 hours beyond current organ state, treatment exposure, and measurement intensity. The leading rival is that the apparent signal is generated by treatment response or documentation intensity; a second rival is that the endpoint is an artifact of treatment initiation or competing risk rather than deterioration itself.

The experiment can distinguish these explanations only in a bounded observational sense: if the trajectory increment disappears after explicit treatment/measurement adjustment, is confined to low-coverage strata, or is reproduced by treatment/documentation negative controls, the trajectory interpretation is weakened. If it persists with stable calibration and is not explained by the controls, that supports incremental predictive information, not a mechanism or causal treatment effect.

## Population, landmark, and estimand

Use adult ICU stays from `icu/icustays`, joined to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`. Restrict to `outtime - intime >= 24 hours`, a valid prespecified urine-output dictionary, and a landmark at ICU hour 12. Eligibility must use only information available at or before the landmark.

The primary estimand is the 12-hour risk of the first mutually exclusive event after the landmark: new renal replacement therapy, new invasive mechanical ventilation, or death. Events already present before the landmark are excluded. Censor at ICU `outtime` when earlier. Report component risks separately; do not treat repeated events or overlapping windows as independent observations. Non-invasive ventilation is not counted as invasive ventilation. If the procedure dictionary cannot be frozen reproducibly, the result is inconclusive rather than redefined.

A secondary competing-risk analysis estimates cause-specific cumulative incidence for RRT, invasive ventilation, and death, with the other events as competing events, and reports absolute risk differences rather than only hazard ratios. This addresses the concern that a binary composite can conceal clinically different endpoints and treatment-selection timing.

## Treatment and documentation construction

Freeze the item dictionary before fitting. From `inputevents`, construct pre-landmark 6- and 12-hour summaries for: diuretics (furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide and any prespecified equivalent), crystalloid/colloid/fluid inputs, and vasopressors (norepinephrine, epinephrine, phenylephrine, vasopressin, dopamine and any prespecified equivalent). Use `starttime`/`endtime` for exposure intervals, `storetime` for documentation lag, `amount`/`rate` for dose when units are valid, and `statusdescription`/`continueinnextdept` to exclude cancelled or non-delivered records when a reproducible rule exists. Do not infer treatment response from a urine value alone.

Construct measurement intensity from `outputevents` and `chartevents`: number of urine records, coverage fraction, time since last urine record, chart-to-store lag, and number of distinct charting events in the preceding window. These are adjustment/negative-control features, not evidence of renal recovery. Keep the primary trajectory model and the measurement-adjusted baseline on the same landmark, split, and endpoint.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

B0 is a regularized logistic model for the first-event endpoint using current/last urine output, current vital signs, demographics, admission type, care unit, and preceding treatment summaries. B1 adds urine observation count, coverage, time since last urine record, and chart/store-time gaps. B2 adds prespecified fluid, diuretic, and vasopressor exposure summaries and timing. The primary scientific contrast is T1 versus B2.

T1 is a prespecified trajectory/persistence model over hourly urine-output bins, with slope, persistence, recovery, and explicit missingness. A small GRU/Transformer or gradient-boosted sequence encoder is a deferred alternative only if the bounded trajectory model is feasible and the artifact-adjusted contrast remains unresolved. The alternative can reveal ordered decline and recovery information discarded by the latest value, but model capacity is not the scientific claim.

## Analysis and falsification

Use subject-level train/validation/test splits, no patient leakage, and bootstrap or repeated-split uncertainty. Compare discrimination (AUROC/AUPRC), calibration intercept/slope, and decision-curve/net benefit at prespecified alert thresholds. Report subgroup analyses by care unit, admission type, and measurement density. Ablate urine-output trajectory, treatment features, and measurement-intensity features.

Supportive: T1 improves held-out discrimination and calibration beyond B2, with stable direction and clinically meaningful net benefit; the gain persists in high-coverage strata and is not reproduced by a shuffled trajectory or pre-landmark charting-gap negative control.
Adverse: no incremental held-out gain, poor calibration, gain confined to low-coverage strata, or disappearance after treatment/measurement adjustment; this weakens the claim that trajectory adds information.
Inconclusive: insufficient eligible landmarks, sparse urine output, unstable item mapping, or wide uncertainty; do not interpret as no signal.

## Evidence and limitations

[K1] Haines et al. (2023) inspected the full-text excerpt for a prospective study of 38 mechanically ventilated critically ill patients. It found that creatinine-based eGFR systematically overestimated kidney function after prolonged critical illness, with cystatin C closer to measured GFR; this supports the proposal's caution against treating creatinine or a single bedside measure as renal recovery. The study was small, single-center, and did not test urine-output trajectories or near-term deterioration.

[K2] Tang et al. (2026) inspected the full-text excerpt for a multicenter MIMIC-IV/eICU-CRD landmark study predicting observed CRRT initiation. Urine output was among retained predictors, but external calibration was poor and incremental value was limited; the authors explicitly did not interpret prediction as CRRT indication or treatment benefit. This bounds the present proposal's claim and motivates calibration, incremental-value, and artifact analyses rather than a treatment recommendation.

[K3] Jakobsen et al. (2026) inspected the full-text excerpt for a systematic review of experimental porcine sepsis studies. It found that AKI classification depends on measurement strategy: urine-output criteria require sustained oliguria, creatinine may be blunted, and GFR reporting was associated with higher criterion fulfilment. Although the population is experimental and heterogeneous, this supports treating urine output as an observable measurement with context-dependent meaning, not as measured GFR.

No clinical adjudication of RRT/ventilation indications, fluid balance validity, or treatment causality is available automatically; those stronger conclusions require expert review or another study.
