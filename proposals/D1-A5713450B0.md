# Hospital heterogeneity in clinically consequential renal escalation after a recorded creatinine rise

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly construct the parent’s auditable eICU six-hour landmark cohort and event ledger, then estimate one clinically more consequential quantity:

> the supported-hospital 90th-minus-10th percentile spread in the standardized 48-hour cumulative incidence of a renal-escalation composite after a first qualifying recorded creatinine rise.

Renal escalation is prespecified as the first of:

1. a distinct later qualifying creatinine confirmation, with the parent’s six-hour minimum gap; or
2. a later, newly documented renal-support record: a time-stamped renal-dialysis modality in `treatment`, or a high-specificity dialysis/CRRT/hemofiltration record in `intakeOutput`.

The solver must report the composite and its two components separately, including the hospital spread for each component, repeat-test opportunity, confirmation conditional on repeat testing, renal-support documentation conditional on the first rise, and unit death/alive exit/unknown-status censoring. If the renal-support component is too sparse for the prespecified support rule, the composite is not promoted to a clinically consequential conclusion and the parent’s recorded-confirmation spread remains the primary computable result.

Completion therefore requires: a frozen cohort and 30-minute ledger; component-specific event counts and support; standardized hospital CIFs; the composite H90-10 and hospital-cluster interval; the component decomposition; the parent confirmation-spread comparison; held-out-hospital transport; and all endpoint, timing, unit-boundary, lab-clock, and renal-record sensitivities. No result has been fitted in discovery.

The substantive advance over the parent is an explicit test of whether hospital variation in a documented creatinine signal reaches a downstream state that is closer to a consequential renal-care escalation. A persistent recorded rise that varies by hospital but has no corresponding variation in renal-support documentation is primarily a surveillance/measurement-process result. A spread that persists for both confirmation and renal-support documentation, after case-mix and observation processes, would justify concern that the portability of the recorded signal affects clinically relevant endpoint ascertainment. This remains an observational documentation study, not a biological-AKI, quality-ranking, treatment-effect, or dialysis-initiation study.

## Evidence-supported and unresolved claims

The inspected evidence supports only local data availability and observation semantics. The frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative MAP observations, ICU-keyed laboratory and treatment rows, labelled intake/output rows, disposition, hospital identifiers, severity descriptors and documented infusion rows. Direct schemas and bounded source scans verified:

- `intakeOutput` has `intakeoutputid`, `patientunitstayid`, `intakeoutputoffset`, `intaketotal`, `outputtotal`, `dialysistotal`, `nettotal`, `intakeoutputentryoffset`, `cellpath`, `celllabel`, `cellvaluenumeric`, and `cellvaluetext`;
- `treatment` has `treatmentid`, `patientunitstayid`, `treatmentoffset`, `treatmentstring`, and `activeupondischarge`;
- a full read-only scan found 40,060 renal/dialysis treatment rows across 8,284 stays, and 58,473 dialysis/CRRT/hemofiltration intake-output rows across 6,159 stays;
- urine-labelled intake-output rows are common, but include urine, catheter, Foley, voided amount and occurrence labels with no uniformly documented collection duration or denominator.

These counts are feasibility audits, not cohort prevalences, event rates, or fitted results. They also do not establish that a treatment row means administration, that a flowsheet row means initiation, or that any creatinine rise is biological AKI.

The unresolved claim is:

> After standardizing patient severity, early MAP history, first-rise characteristics, prior testing opportunity and hospital context, hospitals differ materially in the 48-hour probability of a downstream renal-escalation record after a first recorded creatinine rise, and the residual spread is not entirely explained by repeat-creatinine opportunity or ICU exit.

Primary hypothesis: among supported development hospitals, the standardized renal-escalation H90-10 is at least 5 percentage points, its two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval excludes zero, and at least half of the transparent unadjusted spread remains after explicit repeat-testing and competing-exit processes are represented. This threshold is a prespecified scale for endpoint portability, not a claim about a treatment threshold or patient-level benefit.

The strongest available evidence does not distinguish true renal injury, chronic dialysis, a new dialysis episode, fluid-removal-only therapy, a lab interface artifact, or local documentation practice. These counts are feasibility audits, not cohort prevalences, event rates, or fitted results. The primary pre-rise exclusion is a safeguard against mislabelling ongoing dialysis as a new escalation, not evidence that unseen pre-ICU dialysis history is absent. The experiment tests whether the *recorded composite process* is hospital-dependent. It does not test whether hypotension causes AKI or dialysis.

## Exact population and ICU observation boundary

Use eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`. The unit is `patientunitstayid`; repeated unit stays remain separate. `uniquepid` is used only for clustering and connected-component split checks.

Retain the parent cohort exactly:

- adults with `patient.age >= 18`, parsing age `> 89` as 90 and retaining an age-censored flag;
- at least two valid raw `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` observations in minutes 0–360, restricted to 20–200 mmHg;
- a valid baseline creatinine at or before the first raw low MAP;
- first raw periodic `systemicmean < 65` in [0, 360];
- no qualifying baseline-plus-0.3 mg/dL creatinine rise through minute 360; and
- `patient.unitdischargeoffset > 360`.

Let U = `unitdischargeoffset`, E = min(2880, U). A source observation is eligible only while its ICU-relative time t < U; if U > 2880, an event at exactly 2880 is eligible, while if U <= 2880 discharge takes precedence over an event at that minute. The index stay is not concatenated with later ICU stays. Transfer to another unit, floor, hospital or location ends this unit’s observability.

At U, `unitdischargestatus=Expired` is recorded unit death, `Alive` is an alive unit exit, and NULL is unknown-status administrative censoring. NULL is never recoded as alive. `unitdischargelocation` is context only, with a sensitivity using `Death` when status is missing; disagreements remain visible. `hospitaldischargeoffset` is never substituted for the ICU boundary.

## Index rise, MAP, and recorded confirmation

Use raw `vitalPeriodic` observations for the primary MAP ledger, deduplicating exact `vitalperiodicid`; unresolved same-stay/time valid duplicates use their median and are counted. The primary early burden is the trapezoidal integral of max(65-MAP,0) over 0–360 minutes, bridging only gaps <=30 minutes. Report raw and bridged minutes, coverage, minimum MAP, low-MAP runs and recovery. `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` is a separate sensitivity.

For creatinine, use case-folded `lab.labname=creatinine`, numeric `labresult` in [0,60], and explicit mg/dL verification from both nonblank `labmeasurenamesystem` and `labmeasurenameinterface`. Conflicting or non-mg/dL rows are excluded; accepting one explicit mg/dL field when the other is blank is a sensitivity. Do not convert unverified mmol/L. Deduplicate exact `labid`; at a selected offset use the median of remaining valid rows and report the count. The baseline is the earliest qualifying result in [0,60], not the minimum. The first qualifying rise is the first post-landmark result at least 0.3 mg/dL above baseline. The early-injury exclusion uses all qualifying results through 360 and never uses post-landmark data for baseline.

A confirmed recorded rise requires a distinct `labid` from the first-rise row, a result at least 360 minutes after the first-rise offset, value at least baseline+0.3 mg/dL, and result time <=2880 and <U as applicable. Same-minute rows, exact duplicates and revised versions of one `labid` cannot confirm. The primary clock is `labresultoffset`; `labresultrevisedoffset` is a prespecified sensitivity.

## Renal-support component: operational definition and limits

The renal-support component is **newly documented after the first rise among stays without a prior strict support record**, not proven initiation or treatment receipt. For the primary renal-escalation analysis, exclude a first-rise stay if any strict support record occurs from ICU time 0 through the first-rise time (inclusive); report that prevalent-support group separately. Among remaining stays, count the first eligible post-rise time r_s when either source records a high-specificity renal-support modality:

- `treatment.treatmentstring` contains a case-insensitive renal dialysis modality such as `renal|dialysis|hemodialysis`, `renal|dialysis|C V V H D`, `C V V H`, `C A V H D`, `peritoneal dialysis`, or `SLED`; or
- `intakeOutput.cellpath`/ `celllabel` identifies `Dialysis (ml)|In`, `Dialysis (ml)|Out`, `CRRT Actual Pt Fluid Removed`, `CRRT Out`, `CRRT - UF removed`, `CRRT- UF removed`, or `Hemofiltration`, with a valid numeric `cellvaluenumeric` or a nonzero `dialysistotal`.

Exclude catheter insertion, dialysis-access surgery, line flushes and generic terms without a modality. Exclude the treatment-string category `ultrafiltration (fluid removal only)` from the strict primary component because it is not necessarily renal replacement; include it only in a broad fluid-removal sensitivity. Use the earliest source offset after r and before E. Exact duplicate `treatmentid` or `intakeoutputid` rows are deduplicated.

A source-strict sensitivity uses only modality-specific `treatment.treatmentstring` rows. A flowsheet-strict sensitivity uses only the named `intakeOutput` labels. A broad sensitivity includes modality-specific treatment plus dialysis/CRRT/hemofiltration flowsheet evidence. A secondary post-rise-documentation analysis retains prevalent-support stays but labels them as ongoing-or-new documentation, not escalation. The primary estimate is described as “newly documented after the rise among stays with no prior strict support record,” not “new renal failure.”

The composite event time is min(first confirmed-rise time, first strict renal-support time). If both occur at the same minute, retain both component indicators and count one composite event. Its transition ledger must retain no qualifying test, first rise, repeat test, confirmed rise, renal-support record, unit death, alive exit and unknown-status censoring. The solver must not use treatment or support rows after unit discharge.

### Why urine output is not a primary AKI component

The full `intakeOutput` file contains 3,300,489 rows labelled `Urine`, 406,364 `URINE CATHETER` rows and many catheter, Foley, voided-amount and occurrence variants. However, the available schema supplies offsets and values, not a validated collection interval for every labelled row; labels can represent different collection systems; and a reliable time-varying weight denominator is not available. Therefore this proposal does not call low urine output oliguria, does not apply a KDIGO urine-output criterion, and does not combine heterogeneous urine labels into a biological AKI endpoint.

Urine is retained as an observation-process audit: report the presence, count, offset density, label family and coverage of urine-labelled records around the rise, and repeat the confirmation/composite models with prespecified urine-observability covariates or strata where support permits. Any low-output threshold analysis requires an explicit additional validation of interval semantics and is a deferred branch, not an unreported analytic option.

## Primary estimand and retained parent estimand

For each supported development hospital h, let R_h^RE be stays with a first qualifying rise before E and no strict renal-support record from ICU time 0 through that rise. Estimate q_h^RE, the 48-hour CIF of the renal-escalation composite after the first rise, with recorded unit death and alive unit exit as competing events and unknown-status discharge as censoring. Standardize every hospital to the development renal-escalation risk-set distribution of:

- age, sex, ethnicity, unit/admission source and unit type;
- baseline creatinine, first-rise value and delta;
- first-low MAP, burden, coverage, runs and recovery;
- time since previous qualifying creatinine and pre-rise test count;
- documented pressor timing;
- admission acutephysiologyscore, apachescore and predictedicumortality; and
- hospital bed category, teaching status and region as context, without hospital identity in standardization.

For hospitals with at least 50 first-rise stays and at least 10 composite events, define H90-10^RE = Q90(q_h^RE)-Q10(q_h^RE). Also report the same spread for confirmed rise alone, strict renal-support documentation alone, repeat-test opportunity, confirmation after repeat testing and exit processes. If fewer than 10 hospitals meet support, the new composite spread is inconclusive. Do not substitute an unregularized hospital proportion.

The parent estimand remains a required secondary in its original first-rise risk set: standardized 48-hour confirmation CIF H90-10, the any-rise/confirmed-rise contrast by documented pressor timing, and raw-versus-bridged MAP burden sensitivities. The child’s question is whether the parent signal has downstream documented renal-care relevance; it does not erase or silently replace the parent endpoint.

## Matched analysis: transparent baseline

Fit a regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic model on identical 30-minute intervals, cohort, split, landmark covariates and uncertainty procedure for the composite and each component. Include separate transitions for repeat qualifying test, confirmed rise, renal-support documentation, unit death, alive exit and unknown-status censoring. Use prespecified linear/spline elapsed-time terms, common covariate slopes, transition-specific hospital random intercepts and a hospital-by-transition time slope only when supported.

Nested fits are:

- B0: case-mix and common elapsed-time effects, no hospital heterogeneity;
- B1: B0 plus transition-specific hospital random intercepts;
- B2: B1 plus hospital-context fixed effects;
- B3: B1 plus repeat-testing opportunity and urine-observability terms;
- B4: B1 without hospital-by-time slopes, a stability/null-heterogeneity reference.

Derive standardized hospital CIFs, H90-10^RE, component spreads, observed-versus-predicted curves and parent secondary estimands from the same fitted model. Post-rise lab values are outcomes, never predictors.

## Substantive mechanistic alternative

Fit an ordered piecewise-exponential multi-state model on the same risk sets, covariates, hospital split, standardization distribution and bootstrap procedure. States are first rise, repeat-test opportunity, confirmed rise, renal-support documentation, composite renal escalation, unit death, alive exit and unknown-status censoring. Keep first-rise-to-repeat, repeat-to-confirmation and renal-support transitions distinct; enforce the six-hour confirmation delay structurally. Use transition-specific hospital random effects with shrinkage and hospital context only where estimable.

This model reveals whether hospital heterogeneity is carried by surveillance opportunity, confirmation after testing, independent renal-support documentation, the sequence from confirmation to support, or competing exit. The additive baseline loses this ordered clinical-process information by representing causes in parallel. Agreement on H90-10^RE supports a robust documented-process finding. A spread restricted to repeat testing supports surveillance variation; a spread in renal-support documentation after support-aware adjustment is more clinically consequential but still not proof of treatment or biological injury. A large confirmation spread with null support spread argues against treating the recorded confirmation as a downstream clinical escalation marker.

A learned sequence model is deferred, not prohibited. Revisit it only if both prespecified models show reproducible held-out, nonlinear miscalibration in the same estimand and a learned model can preserve the component decomposition and hospital-held-out evaluation. A causal pressor model is deferred because `infusionDrug` records do not validate administration, start, dose, indication, intent or complete exposure. A biological-AKI model is deferred because validated urine-output intervals, renal reserve, adjudication and complete treatment context are unavailable.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence for the new hypothesis requires: supported H90-10^RE >=5 percentage points; a two-sided hospital-cluster interval excluding zero; the same qualitative ordering in the additive and multi-state models; renal-support component support; and a nontrivial remaining spread after repeat-test and exit processes. The result is still evidence about documented renal escalation, not AKI or treatment.

Adverse evidence is a null/reversed composite spread, interval compatibility with zero, disappearance after repeat-test or urine-observability adjustment, support only in an unvalidated broad flowsheet definition, or instability between treatment-only and flowsheet-only definitions. A large confirmation spread with no renal-support spread means the parent endpoint is locally non-comparable but has not been shown to alter downstream renal-care ascertainment. A large composite spread caused only by renal-support documentation with no confirmation support is a renal-support recording heterogeneity finding and does not validate the creatinine signal.

A process falsification requires the model to report the hospital spread in a short-gap repeat measurement (repeat within 0–6 hours after the first rise) as an observation-process control. It is expected to show testing-practice variation; failure to reproduce it can indicate inadequate process measurement or model instability. It cannot be used as a clinical endpoint. Endpoint-clock, strict-vs-broad renal-support, unit-boundary, unknown-status, MAP-channel, confirmation-gap and hospital-split sensitivities must be concordant before a supportive interpretation.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 10 supported hospitals, fewer than 10 supported renal-support events per hospital, sparse composite events, poor timing overlap, extreme weights, bootstrap instability, failed convergence, strong dependence on NULL-status handling, treatment/flowsheet disagreement, or held-out calibration failure. Inconclusive is not evidence for or against a renal intervention.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes and headers, table joins, offsets, deduplication, unit verification, strict ICU boundary, component state transitions, deterministic split, model fits, standardization, support counts, intervals, calibration and sensitivity reproducibility. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a recorded creatinine rise is biological AKI; whether a treatment/flowsheet row means actual renal replacement initiation; whether chronic dialysis preceded the stay; whether urine output is oliguria; and whether the process reflects treatment response, organ failure, or local charting. Expert review is required before changing repeat-testing, dialysis referral or treatment policy. No result establishes a causal pressor effect, MAP target, hospital quality ranking, renal benefit/harm, or biological renal null.
