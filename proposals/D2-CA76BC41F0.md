# Context-specific intervention response: working proposal

## Question
Does the intervention-associated transcript response in fibroblast–macrophage systems depend on the cellular context, and can that dependence identify a target whose modulation is more likely to alter a pathogenic fibroblast program than a protective macrophage-linked state?

## Current evidence status
This is a hypothesis-stage proposal, not an executed result. The seed D2-T1 names GSE95588 and E-MTAB-8316 as inputs and requires donor-aware matched contrasts and controls. The domain guide confirms that GSE95588 has prepared count matrices and design metadata, and E-MTAB-8316 has four FLS donor clusters, six nested monocyte donors, and twelve condition samples. Repeated context measurements must be averaged within donor/condition before paired exploratory inference. No twelve-independent-donor claim is permitted.

## Estimand
The primary estimand is the difference in the intervention-associated transcript response between the fibroblast context and the macrophage context, averaged within donor/condition. A secondary estimand is the ratio of the fibroblast-context response to the macrophage-context response.

## Competing explanations
1. The context-dependence may reflect differences in donor, treatment, composition, measurement, or selection rather than a true biological context-dependence.
2. The intervention response may not be reproducible within donor.
3. The intervention-associated transcript response may not be a reliable proxy for the target's modulation.

## Method comparison
- Baseline: donor-aware paired t-test on the intervention-associated transcript response.
- Alternative: a linear mixed model with donor as a random effect and context as a fixed effect.

The alternative could reveal whether the context-dependence is robust to donor-level variation, which the baseline loses.

## Falsification
A donor-aware paired contrast in GSE95588 and E-MTAB-8316 shows no significant difference in the intervention-associated response between fibroblast and macrophage contexts, or the response is not reproducible within donor, would falsify the context-dependence claim.
