# Provisional hypothesis: macrophage context changes FLS state beyond donor composition

## Proposed relationship

In the frozen E-MTAB-8873 targeted human RA synovial type B synovial cell (FLS) assay, FLS cells co-cultured with MerTK/CD206-positive macrophages have a reproducible, donor-set-aware difference in measured transcript state from FLS cells co-cultured with MerTK/CD206-negative macrophages, beyond recorded cell count and the complete set of contributing donors. This is a provisional context-associated hypothesis, not a causal macrophage-mechanism claim.

The clinically relevant unresolved question is whether an apparent macrophage-context signal is reproducible across donor sets or is instead generated by donor composition, unequal cell counts, or a malformed donor assignment. A stable signal would justify independent donor-aware validation or perturbation; it would not establish a pathway, treatment effect, or clinical benefit.

## Evidence-supported versus untested claims

The persistent candidate record and prior inspected design/matrix evidence support only that the prepared E-MTAB-8873 assay has 33 records and 445 feature-instance rows, contains none-treatment MerTK/CD206-positive and negative co-culture samples, reuses donors, and has at least one conflicting donor assignment. The exact source-level reconciliation of every candidate pair, including the pair recorded as `R5_SA263R_FLS_SA277A_Mneg` versus `R5_SA263R_FLS_SA278R_Mneg`, is not independently re-verified in this checkpoint. No expression contrast, model fit, uncertainty estimate, or biological result has been executed.

The untested claim is that a context-associated feature-instance contrast survives donor-set dependence, cell-count adjustment, and exclusion of malformed records. The strongest possible conclusion is a reproducible targeted-assay association. It cannot establish macrophage causality, direct target action, whole-tissue abundance, effects on unmeasured genes, or clinical utility.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Baseline: for each feature instance, calculate Mpos-minus-Mneg on log(count + 1), with recorded `cells` as a prespecified offset or equivalent count model. Fit a regularized linear model with condition, cell-count offset, and complete contributing-donor-set indicators. Report pair-specific contrasts, donor-set indicators, and uncertainty.

Substantive alternative: fit a structured hierarchical model with a fixed context effect and random effects for the complete contributing-donor set (and FLS donor if estimable), preserving feature-instance identity and feature-level uncertainty. This alternative tests reproducibility across donor sets and decomposes donor-set/sampling dependence; the fixed-indicator baseline loses that decomposition.

The scientific deliverable is a newly fitted contrast/decomposition with uncertainty, a donor-set sensitivity report, and a feature-instance-level result table. No result is claimed here.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: a stable context direction with uncertainty that survives donor-aware fitting, cell-count adjustment, and the stricter verified sensitivity subset. Results must be linked to computed outputs and uncertainty.

Adverse: the signal disappears after donor-set matching or cell-count adjustment, is driven by one donor set, reverses in the matched subset, or is not reproducible across the prespecified pairs.

Inconclusive: too few independent donor sets, unstable estimates, failed feature reconciliation, inability to separate context from treatment/batch, or insufficient information to fit the hierarchical alternative.

Neither supportive nor adverse results establish causality, mechanism, whole-tissue composition, effects on unmeasured genes, or clinical benefit. Those stronger claims require independent donor replication, complete batch/processing-order covariates, clinical adjudication of macrophage phenotype, and perturbation or another study.

## Missing evidence and limitations

## Method selection and resource estimate

The fixed-indicator baseline is selected as the primary computable check because it is transparent, uses exact available inputs, and directly tests the context contrast. The hierarchical alternative is retained because it answers the substantive donor-set reproducibility question. A neural or more complex predictive model is deferred: with 33 pseudobulk samples, reused donors, and no independent validation, added capacity would not resolve the primary donor-dependence uncertainty. Revisit it only with independent cohort or perturbation data.
