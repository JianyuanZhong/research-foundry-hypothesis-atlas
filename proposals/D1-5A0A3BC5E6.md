# Faithful propagation of interval-specific incidental pulmonary-nodule CT recommendations into the final discharge artifact

## Clinical question, supported evidence, and falsifiable claims

The consequential MIMIC-observable decision is whether an actionable radiology recommendation survives the inpatient-to-outpatient documentation handoff. MIMIC cannot establish whether follow-up occurred outside its system, whether an order or appointment was placed, whether the plan was discussed with the patient, or whether the recommendation was clinically appropriate. It can directly test whether a recommendation that was available to the inpatient team is faithfully represented in the final discharge artifact and can secondarily ascertain CT reports that later appear in the same MIMIC system.

**Strongest claim already supported.** Published observational work supports a communication problem but not a causal effect of discharge wording. Kwan et al. reported that 35/65 patients with explicit pulmonary-nodule follow-up suggestions lacked imaging in the recommended interval and that discharge-summary mention was associated with timely imaging (J Hosp Med 2019; PMID 30794133; PMCID PMC6625441; bibliographic record and abstract inspected in this invocation). The FIND tracking-program report observed follow-up imaging in 34/67 post-implementation versus 16/52 pre-implementation patients (J Digit Imaging 2023; PMCID PMC10287591; full public XML inspected), showing feasibility of active tracking rather than proof that discharge text alone causes completion. A systematic review of 31 intervention studies found tracking systems promising but substantial heterogeneity and serious or critical risk of bias in most nonrandomized evidence (Chest 2025; PMID 40081655; PMCID PMC12264345; full public XML inspected). These studies support rigorous measurement and prospective testing; they do not establish guideline appropriateness, care-loop closure, cancer benefit, or causality.

**Unresolved primary process hypothesis.** Among adults with an adjudicated, unconditional recommendation for chest/thoracic CT at exactly 3, 6, or 12 calendar months for an incidental pulmonary nodule, where the recommendation-bearing report state was stored before discharge and before the discharge artifact, fewer than 80% have faithful propagation into the final discharge artifact stored no later than 24 hours after discharge. The 80% threshold is a prespecified operational quality target, not an evidence-derived clinical standard. The primary estimand is a process proportion and full multinomial disposition, not an intervention effect.

**Unresolved secondary surveillance hypothesis.** In the same eligible population, faithful propagation is associated with a higher all-eligible risk of a recommendation-timed chest CT documented in the MIMIC system than omission after measured baseline standardization. The null standardized risk difference is zero. This is explicitly an association with **MIMIC-observed recommendation-timed CT**, not true all-system completion, adherence, care-loop closure, or benefit.

The completed lexical audit is evidence of computational feasibility only. It does not establish either hypothesis and its proxy labels will not enter the final analysis as clinical truth.

## Report/addendum graph and availability state

The no-row-sampling chain audit found 25,735 unique directed parent-addendum edges represented reciprocally in both detail fields. Of these, 25,600 linked a present `RR` parent to a present `AR` addendum; every resolved pair had the same subject and admission, and every addendum `storetime` was at or after its parent. The remaining 135 edges had missing radiology nodes (129 both absent, three missing parent, three missing addendum). These aggregate facts validate reciprocal graph reconstruction but require an explicit unresolved-chain state.

For each candidate base `RR`, construct its connected report chain from reciprocal detail edges. At the primary knowledge cutoff `dischtime`, create the **available report state** from the base report and linked `AR` nodes with nonmissing `storetime <= dischtime`. The recommendation must also be available before the discharge artifact: the note version that first contains the qualifying recommendation must have `storetime <= discharge.storetime`. A recommendation introduced only in an addendum stored after discharge is not an eligible pre-discharge recommendation. Future addendum content is not used to redefine eligibility or exposure; the frequency and direction of later addendum changes are a separately adjudicated sensitivity. If any referenced node needed to interpret the pre-discharge chain is absent, has a missing availability time, crosses subject/admission, or has contradictory links, classify chain availability as unresolved, tabulate it in the unconditional cascade, and exclude it from confirmatory propagation inference rather than guessing.

Radiology adjudicators review all text available at the cutoff and determine whether a pre-discharge addendum negates, supersedes, changes, or newly introduces the recommendation. The index due date is the base examination `charttime` plus the adjudicated 3, 6, or 12 calendar months, using calendar arithmetic (for example, `dateutil.relativedelta`), not 30-day approximations.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The unit is the first eligible recommendation admission per subject, selected chronologically by base index `charttime`, then `storetime`, then `note_id`, without future outcome information.

Include:

- age at admission >=18;
- a CT examination with `(subject_id, hadm_id)` matching the admission and `admittime - 12 hours <= base charttime <= dischtime`;
- chest CT, CTPA/CTA, or abdominal CT that includes lung bases, classified by all `radiology_detail` exam fields with blinded text adjudication for ambiguous coverage;
- an incidental pulmonary/lung nodule or indeterminate focal nodular pulmonary opacity; and
- an affirmed, patient-specific, unconditional recommendation for chest or thoracic CT at exactly 3, 6, or 12 months.

Exclude:

- scheduled lung-cancer screening or an index examination explicitly performed for nodule surveillance;
- lung-cancer staging/oncologic surveillance, a known primary pulmonary malignancy being managed, or recommendations directed to a nonpulmonary nodule;
- conditional or optional recommendations dependent on unavailable risk information, or explicit no-follow-up recommendations;
- hospice/comfort-only transition, in-hospital death, or recorded death before discharge-artifact ascertainment;
- a pre-artifact CT that explicitly supersedes or satisfies the recommendation; and
- unresolved pre-discharge report chains.

Do not require that the nodule is “new,” Fleischner-eligible, or guideline-concordant because morphology, longitudinal stability, smoking pack-years, and clinical context are incomplete. Abstract new/changed/stable/unknown, size, type, multiplicity, and prior same-system imaging when stated. Repeat eligibility with a zero-hour admission boundary and the available `edregtime`/`edouttime` window. Repeat key analyses among subjects with at least two years of prior same-system radiology history.

The unconditional cohort cascade reports, per 100 adjudicated eligible recommendation opportunities: (1) resolvable chain; (2) recommendation-bearing state stored by discharge; (3) canonical discharge artifact present with known `storetime`; (4) artifact stored by discharge, by 24 hours, and by 7 days; and (5) each propagation disposition. The confirmatory primary denominator requires the recommendation state stored by discharge and before the canonical artifact, and the artifact stored no later than `dischtime + 24 hours`. The by-discharge subset is a strict sensitivity; the 7-day subset diagnoses delayed artifacts but is not substituted into the primary denominator.

## Blinded adjudication and propagation states

Every rule-screened candidate undergoes adjudication; lexical proxies are only a high-sensitivity screen.

1. Two radiology-trained reviewers, blinded to discharge text and future outcomes, independently decide eligibility and abstract index exam coverage, incidental status, recommendation action, exact interval, certainty/conditionality, nodule site, size/type/multiplicity/newness when available, and effect of each pre-discharge addendum.
2. Two transition reviewers, blinded to the index report and all future outcomes, independently review the canonical discharge text and abstract patient-specific nodule content, CT action, interval or due date, responsible clinician/service if stated, and an explicit alternate resolution.
3. Outcome reviewers, blinded to propagation state, classify later CT coverage and whether the CT is nodule-focused versus acute/symptom-driven.
4. Consensus or a third reviewer resolves every disagreement before analysis. The independently abstracted radiology and discharge fields are matched algorithmically only after both abstractions are locked.

The mutually exclusive discharge dispositions are:

- **Faithful propagation:** an affirmed patient-specific pulmonary nodule, chest/thoracic CT action, and the same 3/6/12-month interval or an unambiguously equivalent calendar due date.
- **Incomplete/discordant propagation:** nodule without action, CT action without usable timing, or a different action/interval without an explicit alternate clinical resolution.
- **Omission:** no patient-specific actionable nodule follow-up content.
- **Explicit alternate resolution:** documented patient refusal/preference, goals-of-care choice, specialist takeover with a concrete alternative plan, already scheduled outside-system follow-up, or a documented clinical decision that supersedes CT. This captures documentation only; reviewers do not determine medical correctness, and this category is neither silently successful nor failed propagation.

Negated, historical, or family-history mentions, generic “follow up with PCP,” copied guideline boilerplate, and copied radiology prose without an active patient-specific plan are not faithful. A missing or >24-hour discharge artifact is an artifact-availability state, not omission.

### Annotation and agreement gates

Report raw agreement, Gwet's AC1 with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, and kappa for eligibility, exact interval, and the four-state disposition. Confirmatory process inference requires: complete consensus resolution; raw independent agreement >=0.80 and AC1 >=0.70 for each of eligibility, interval, and exact four-state disposition. If a gate fails, revise the codebook, independently re-adjudicate all affected candidates, and reassess once; persistent failure limits results to descriptive reviewer-specific bounds.

Against the consensus reference, the automated candidate screen must have sensitivity >=0.95 with lower 95% Wilson bound >=0.90. Automated faithful and omitted labels, if used for scaling or quality checks, must each have PPV >=0.90 with lower 95% Wilson bound >=0.80. Otherwise automation cannot supply analysis labels. Primary process inference additionally requires at least 200 eligible, artifact-timed recommendations; otherwise report exact descriptive proportions only.

## Outcomes and observation-aware secondary estimand

Surveillance time zero is `max(dischtime, discharge.storetime)`. No event at or before time zero is an outcome. The due date is `index charttime + adjudicated interval` calendar months. The primary secondary event is the first MIMIC chest CT/CTA with `charttime` in `[due date - 42 days, due date + 42 days]` and after time zero. Report +/-30-day and +/-90-day sensitivities. Classify nodule-focused surveillance from indication/comparison/impression language under blinded review. Also report any post-time-zero chest CT through `due+42`, and separate CTs occurring only during a later acute admission; neither is automatically called surveillance.

Assign each eligible person one joint state using this precedence:

1. **MIMIC-observed recommendation-timed CT:** qualifying CT before recorded death.
2. **Recorded death before window end:** no earlier timed CT and `deathtime` or `dod <= due+42`; mortality is known to be incomplete.
3. **No timed CT with continued same-system trace:** no timed CT or recorded death, but a later MIMIC radiology event or admission occurs after `due+42`.
4. **No subsequent trace / unknown capture:** none of the above; outside imaging and loss of system capture cannot be distinguished.

The all-eligible risk of state 1 is the primary secondary estimand. State distributions are reported by propagation arm. Restriction to state-3-observable patients is descriptive only because observability is post-exposure and may be a collider. Model continued-trace status as an outcome. Inverse-probability-of-observation analyses are sensitivity analyses under an explicitly unverifiable missing-at-random assumption, never replacements for the all-eligible estimand. Absence of a MIMIC CT is not labeled nonadherence, failed follow-up, or absence of outside care.

## Statistical analysis and stopping gates

### Primary process

Estimate

`P(faithful | adjudicated eligible recommendation, resolvable pre-discharge report state, recommendation available before discharge and artifact, canonical artifact stored by dischtime+24h)`

with an exact binomial 95% confidence interval. Report simultaneous multinomial confidence intervals for faithful, incomplete/discordant, omitted, and alternate resolution, plus the unconditional availability cascade. The operational threshold hypothesis is supported only if the upper 95% confidence bound for faithful propagation is below 0.80. Do not convert this benchmark into a clinical guideline.

### Secondary association

The prespecified contrast is faithful versus omitted; incomplete/discordant and alternate resolution remain separately descriptive and are not data-dependently reassigned. Report crude risks, risk difference, risk ratio, interval-specific exact estimates, and then standardized risk difference and ratio using overlap weighting and regression standardization if gates pass.

Freeze the parsimonious baseline adjustment set before viewing adjudicated outcomes: recommendation interval; index coverage (chest/CTA versus lung bases); abstracted nodule size/type/newness; prior-year same-system chest CT count and admission count; age category; sex; cancer-history category excluding active staging; COPD/emphysema; ICU exposure; medical versus surgical final service; length of stay; discharge destination; language; and insurance. Repeat without language/insurance. No future note, later addendum content, outcome, or post-exposure observation marker enters exposure models.

Adjusted inference requires all of:

- >=50 faithful and >=50 omitted subjects;
- >=15 timed CT events in each arm;
- at least 90% of each arm within the prespecified propensity range 0.05-0.95 and within the empirical common-support intersection;
- overlap-weighted effective sample size >=40 in each arm;
- no zero-exposure arm in any interval-by-index-coverage stratum containing >=10 subjects;
- weighted absolute standardized mean differences <=0.10 for every prespecified key covariate; and
- no model separation or influential-weight pathology.

If any gate fails, stop at crude and exact/parsimoniously stratified estimates and call the adjusted association inconclusive. Limit outcome-model degrees of freedom to available events and use bootstrap confidence intervals. Neither weighting nor standardization is described as causal because clinician diligence, patient preference, outpatient relationships, orders/referrals, direct notification, and outside-system care remain unmeasured.

## Negative controls, sensitivities, and falsification

1. **Temporal placebo:** chest CT in an 84-day window centered on `index charttime - recommendation interval`, necessarily before discharge propagation. A same-direction standardized association as large as the surveillance association indicates baseline utilization/team selection, extraction error, or residual confounding.
2. **Generic utilization:** recommendation-window non-chest radiology. A comparable association indicates general same-system engagement rather than nodule-specific action.
3. **Differential observation:** continued same-system trace after `due+42` is itself an outcome. A large propagation association warns that capture differs by arm.
4. **Mechanism specificity:** nodule-focused timed CT must be at least as directionally supportive as any chest CT. A result driven by acute/non-nodule CT does not support a recommendation handoff mechanism.
5. Repeat using zero-hour and ED timing, artifact stored by discharge, +/-30 and +/-90 days, CTPA-only, chest CT versus lung-base abdominal CT, exclusion of cancer codes, exclusion of CTs occurring only in later acute admissions, and >=2 years of prior local radiology history.

A secondary result is not supportive if the temporal placebo or generic-utilization standardized absolute risk difference is at least as large and in the same direction, if acute CT drives the estimate while nodule-focused CT does not, if positivity/annotation gates fail, or if differential observability plausibly accounts for the estimate.

## Preserved complete-source audit findings

The completed no-row-sampling lexical audit scanned all 2,321,355 radiology rows and the relevant complete note/core sources. It found 1,291 broad lexical candidate reports, 1,241 with CT metadata, and 1,194 first candidate subjects. Of these, 1,022 had a discharge artifact by seven days, 770 by 24 hours, and 285 by discharge. Its crude gates retained 643 proxy cases: 403 faithful, 188 omitted, and 52 incomplete/discordant. These are feasibility proxies, not adjudicated counts.

Differential capture is material: the audit's continued-system trace occurred in 253/403 faithful (62.8%) and 99/188 omitted (52.7%) proxy cases. Among those with continued trace, recommendation-timed CT was nearly null across the two main arms: 44/253 (17.4%) versus 16/99 (16.2%); any chest CT by the window end was 77/253 (30.4%) versus 30/99 (30.3%). In all proxy-gated subjects, timed CT was 49/403 (12.2%) versus 17/188 (9.0%). Prior-year chest CT was 11.7% versus 15.4%, and continued-trace non-chest timely imaging was 36.4% versus 41.4%. These findings argue against treating an observed-only subset as complete follow-up and provide no compelling crude evidence that discharge propagation improves surveillance.

The dedicated-heading branch is retired. Only eight of 1,022 summary-linked proxy candidates and six of 643 proxy-gated cases had a dedicated `RECOMMENDATION(S)` heading; only two of those six were proxy-faithful. `radiology_detail` has no section labels, and `discharge_detail` has no usable section structure. Report location may be abstracted descriptively, but no heading comparison is permitted unless adjudication unexpectedly yields >=50 per arm and all ordinary overlap/event gates; it is not a planned mechanism claim.

The complete chain/version audit further found 25,735 reciprocal chain edges, 25,600 fully resolved chronological same-admission `RR->AR` pairs, 135 edges with absent radiology nodes, one `DS` row per admission, and 17 discharge rows with missing `storetime`. This supports graph-based availability reconstruction and a separately reported unresolved-chain category while showing that draft-versus-final discharge selection is not an empirical issue in this snapshot.

## Interpretation matrix

**Supportive primary result:** upper 95% confidence bound for faithful propagation is <0.80, a substantial fraction is omitted or incomplete/discordant rather than valid alternate resolution, and all adjudication/availability gates pass. This establishes a measurable report-to-discharge documentation gap and justifies prospective testing of an interval-specific discharge task. It does not establish that the original recommendation was appropriate, delivered to the patient, or beneficial.

**Adverse to the discharge-target hypothesis:** faithful propagation is >=0.90 with lower confidence bound >=0.80, or apparent failures are predominantly documented alternate resolutions. The discharge artifact is unlikely to be the principal bottleneck; active tracking, scheduling, patient communication, or cross-system exchange may be more consequential targets.

**Inconclusive primary result:** insufficient eligible cases, unresolved availability chains, poor agreement, or an interval spanning the operational threshold. Only the cascade and descriptive dispositions are supportable.

**Supportive secondary result:** standardized faithful-minus-omitted risk difference for MIMIC-observed, preferably nodule-focused, timed CT is >0 with a confidence interval excluding zero; all event, overlap, balance, and annotation gates pass; observation-state imbalance does not explain the estimate; and negative controls are smaller and nonsupportive. This establishes a robust same-system association and motivates a randomized or stepped-wedge prospective workflow study, not a causal claim from MIMIC.

**Adverse secondary result:** an adequately precise risk difference is <=0, or faithful documentation is associated only with early/acute non-nodule CT. Documentation alone may be ineffective or may accompany low-value cascades; prioritize active tracking and prospective clinical adjudication.

**Inconclusive secondary result:** confidence interval spans -0.10 to +0.10, arm/event/positivity/accuracy gates fail, observability differs materially, or negative controls are positive. MIMIC cannot resolve the downstream association. The upstream process distribution remains independently reportable if its gates pass. The existing near-null lexical audit is a preserved negative feasibility finding, not a reason to manufacture a positive conclusion.

## Verification boundary and additional evidence required

An automatic verifier can check source hashes, archive members, joins, reciprocal chain construction, timestamp cutoffs, one-artifact-per-admission logic, candidate flow, adjudication-table completeness, calendar-month arithmetic, outcome windows, state precedence, confidence intervals, agreement/PPV/event/overlap gates, control calculations, model outputs, and whether reported conclusions follow from those outputs. It cannot establish true nodule pathology, Fleischner eligibility, smoking exposure, recommendation appropriateness, whether clinicians or patients received the plan, existence or absence of outside imaging, order/referral completion, undocumented preference/refusal, cancer diagnosis or stage, radiation harm, survival, or patient benefit. Those stronger claims require radiology and clinical expert review, linked outpatient/outside-system data, and a prospective intervention study.
