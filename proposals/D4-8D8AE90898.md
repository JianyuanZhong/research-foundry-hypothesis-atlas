# Metabolic discordance and incident type 2 diabetes: a two-pathway hypothesis

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank participants with elevated body mass index (BMI), does the presence of elevated fasting glucose identify a subgroup with higher incident type 2 diabetes (T2D) risk beyond what BMI alone predicts, and is this excess risk partly explained by insulin resistance markers?

The clinically important unresolved question is whether metabolic discordance in the context of elevated BMI represents a single latent process (where hyperglycaemia is merely a marker of overall metabolic ageing) or two separable pathways: (1) adiposity-driven insulin resistance driving T2D independent of glucose, and (2) hyperglycaemia marking a distinct pre-diabetic trajectory that accelerates T2D onset. This distinction has direct implications for screening and prevention: if the two pathways are separable, then dual lifestyle intervention (diet + exercise) targeting both adiposity and glucose would be more effective than adiposity-targeted interventions alone for the discordant subgroup.

The leading explanation (single latent process) is that elevated BMI and elevated fasting glucose are both downstream of a shared metabolic ageing trajectory, so their co-occurrence adds no independent T2D risk beyond what either measure alone predicts. The strongest rival is a two-pathway model where adiposity drives insulin resistance (pathway 1) and hyperglycaemia marks a distinct pre-diabetic trajectory (pathway 2), so their co-occurrence adds independent T2D risk.

## Population and temporal boundaries

- **Population:** UK Biobank participants aged 40–75 at baseline assessment, with valid BMI, fasting glucose, and no prior T2D diagnosis (ICD10 E11) at baseline.
- **Temporal boundaries:** Baseline assessment (field 53, Date of attending assessment centre) defines time zero. Follow-up extends to the earliest of: (a) first in-patient T2D diagnosis (field 41280, Date of first in-patient diagnosis - ICD10), (b) death, or (c) end of follow-up (2023-12-31).
- **Exclusions:** Participants with T2D diagnosis before baseline, missing BMI or fasting glucose, or BMI < 18.5 or > 45.

## Competing explanations

**Leading claim (single latent process):** Elevated BMI and elevated fasting glucose are both downstream of a shared metabolic ageing trajectory. Their co-occurrence adds no independent T2D risk beyond what either measure alone predicts. This is supported by the known correlation between BMI and glucose in the general population.

**Strongest rival (two-pathway model):** Adiposity drives insulin resistance (pathway 1) and hyperglycaemia marks a distinct pre-diabetic trajectory (pathway 2). Their co-occurrence adds independent T2D risk because the two pathways operate through different biological mechanisms. This is supported by the known independent associations of BMI and fasting glucose with T2D risk.

**Key distinction:** The single latent process model predicts that the interaction between BMI and fasting glucose on T2D risk is null after adjustment for shared metabolic factors. The two-pathway model predicts a significant interaction or additive effect.

## Analysis plan

### Baseline model (Model 0)
Cox regression with BMI and fasting glucose as separate predictors, adjusted for age and sex.

### Adjusted model (Model 1)
Cox regression with BMI and fasting glucose as separate predictors, adjusted for age, sex, waist circumference, blood pressure, and HbA1c.

### Interaction model (Model 2)
Cox regression with BMI × fasting glucose interaction term, adjusted for age, sex, waist circumference, blood pressure, and HbA1c.

### Mediation analysis
Cox regression with BMI and fasting glucose as predictors, with HbA1c as a mediator. Test whether the excess risk of the discordant subgroup is partly explained by HbA1c.

### Learned alternative (Model 3)
XGBoost survival model with BMI, fasting glucose, HbA1c, age, sex, waist circumference, and blood pressure as predictors. Evaluate held-out C-index and compare to Cox regression.

## Falsification criteria

- **Supportive:** Model 2 shows a significant BMI × fasting glucose interaction (p < 0.05) and the discordant subgroup (high BMI + high glucose) has higher T2D risk than the concordant subgroup (high BMI + normal glucose) after adjustment.
- **Adverse:** Model 2 shows a null interaction and the discordant subgroup has no higher T2D risk than the concordant subgroup after adjustment.
- **Inconclusive:** Model 2 shows a weak interaction (p < 0.10) or the discordant subgroup has modestly higher T2D risk (HR 1.1–1.3) after adjustment.

## What supportive, adverse, and inconclusive results would mean

- **Supportive:** The two-pathway model is supported. Dual lifestyle intervention targeting both adiposity and glucose would be more effective than adiposity-targeted interventions alone for the discordant subgroup.
- **Adverse:** The single latent process model is supported. Adiposity-targeted interventions alone would be sufficient for the discordant subgroup.
- **Inconclusive:** The distinction between the two models is not clear. Further study with larger samples or different metabolic markers would be needed.

## Method comparison

**Simple baseline (Cox regression):** Models the hazard of T2D as a function of BMI, fasting glucose, and covariates. Assumes proportional hazards and linear effects.

**Substantive learned alternative (XGBoost survival):** Models the hazard of T2D using gradient-boosted trees. Can capture non-linear effects and interactions without explicit specification.

**What the alternative could reveal that the baseline loses:** XGBoost can detect non-linear BMI-glucose interactions and threshold effects that Cox regression would miss. If XGBoost shows a significant BMI-glucose interaction that Cox regression does not, this supports the two-pathway model and suggests that the interaction is non-linear.
