# UKB 39: Does low blood lipids with low albumin indicate occult liver disease

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

Those with low ApoB accompanied by decreased albumin and abnormal liver enzymes have a higher near-term risk of liver cancer than those with low ApoB alone, and the long-term association is weaker.

## Study population and key data

Individuals without liver cancer at baseline; biochemistry, repeat-measurement subset, medication use, and liver cancer registry.

## Basic Approach

Compare different biochemical backgrounds of low ApoB, separate near- and long-term liver cancer, and exclude known cirrhosis; liver fat on MRI as a supplementary measure.

## Significance of the Research

Distinguish a metabolically protective phenotype from disease-related hypolipidemia.

## Main Challenges

Complete hepatitis and treatment information is lacking; hepatic synthetic function cannot be inferred from a single albumin measurement.

## References

## Life Sciences

Tumor development, host reserve, and hematopoiesis

## Competing Explanations

Lipid-lowering medication, nutritional status, or inflammation simultaneously causes low lipid levels and low albumin.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

First check medication use and known liver disease; if the signal appears only recently, explicitly characterize it as a prodromal clue rather than low blood lipids causing cancer.
