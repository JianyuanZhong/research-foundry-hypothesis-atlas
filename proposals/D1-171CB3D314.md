# Episode 64: does late lactate add review value after a time-safe laboratory and bedside-physiology state?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and advance

At a fixed 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine entry, clinicians may decide whether a patient warrants escalation, closer review, or another diagnostic assessment. The unresolved claim is whether the released late lactate contributes material *incremental prognostic decision value* after the information that is plausibly available in the preceding six hours is represented by a broad, time-indexed laboratory and cardiorespiratory state.

This is a deliberately narrower and more consequential question than whether lactate is associated with death. The primary falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In the frozen EICU 24-hour risk set, adding the released late lactate (the selected valid lactate collection from t0+720 through t0+1440, with result revision available by L24) to a prespecified model containing early lactate, non-lactate laboratory history, and six-hour heart-rate, respiratory-rate, and SpO2 summaries increases threshold-averaged hospital-mortality review net benefit by at least 1 net true-positive-equivalent per 100 people across thresholds 0.30 through 0.50.

The advance is a clinically closer comparator than an untimestamped APACHE score or a labs-only comparator: it tests whether late lactate adds information after measured organ-state and monitoring context, while preserving an explicitly noncausal interpretation. It could justify a prospective study of a lactate-assisted review workflow, or provide useful negative evidence against treating a repeat lactate as independently decision-changing.

Neither model estimates the effect of ordering lactate, resuscitation, vasopressor treatment, oxygen treatment, or acting on a score. The cohort is conditional on a recorded early pressor entry, survival and hospitalization to L24, baseline lactate >=2, and a repeat lactate, so it is not all shock patients and cannot estimate the value of obtaining a repeat test.

## What is known and what remains untested

The inspected full-text XML of Baysan et al. (Crit Care Explor 2022; DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407; frozen source ID [source checksum]; [source checksum]) reports that, in a different sepsis population, adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV improved the C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64 and that admission plus 24-hour lactate had favorable model fit. That supports modest serial prognostic information, not material decision value beyond a time-indexed laboratory-plus-physiology state, transportability across held-out EICU hospitals, or patient benefit.

The inspected full-text XML of Ye et al. (2026; DOI 10.1186/s40842-026-00323-2; PMCID PMC13435416; frozen source ID [source checksum]; [source checksum]) develops an eICU model in a selected sepsis-plus-heart-failure population in which lactate, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation were among selected predictors. That supports relevance of these variables in another selected model; it does not test this landmark, comparator, hospital-held-out design, or +1-per-100 decision margin.

The research-ambition README was read. The demonstrations are not evidence for this hypothesis. The cancer demonstration's main article and STAR Methods remain unavailable; no claim here relies on having inspected them. The late-lactate parent lineage provides feasibility and prior modeled results, not proof of the present conditional hypothesis: in the APACHE-IVa subset, late lactate had a positive point ΔINB but its clustered interval included +1; in the 33-hospital trajectory/physiology lineage, prior increments were small or uncertain. Those results motivate the stricter test and must not be substituted for it.

## Frozen predictors

R0 is the primary comparator and contains:

- age and age squared;
- Female and Male indicators, omitting Other/Unknown;
- t0/1440 and its square;
- baseline collection lag (t0-B_offset)/360, ln(B), and ln(B)^2;
- follow-up measurement age (L24-F_offset)/720, without the F value;
- for each of 23 fixed non-lactate markers, six features: missing indicator, log1p(number of valid collections), last value, recency (L24-last collection)/1440, minimum, and maximum;

## Analysis and estimand

Use unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10000, tol=1e-8, no class weighting, and seed 20260961. Use hospital-level leave-one-hospital-out prediction. Within each training fold only, winsorize observed continuous predictors at the 1st/99th percentiles, standardize by training mean and population SD, and mean-impute missing continuous values after scaling to zero; retain missing indicators. Apply training constants to the held-out hospital. Do not tune features, thresholds, preprocessing, calibration, or model choice using held-out outcomes. Require exactly one finite held-out prediction from each model per person.

At p in {0.30,0.31,...,0.50}, alert when held-out risk >=p. Compute NB=TP/N - FP/N*p/(1-p), with treat-none=0 and treat-all using pooled prevalence. The sole primary contrast is physiology-augmented R1 minus physiology-augmented R0. The primary estimand is:

ΔINB = 100/(0.50-0.30) × integral_[0.30,0.50] [NB_R1(p)-NB_R0(p)] dp,

computed by the trapezoid rule over all 21 thresholds. The +1 per 100 margin is a prespecified review-value scenario, not a validated bedside utility or standard-of-care threshold.

Bootstrap uncertainty samples the 33 hospitals with replacement 2,000 times, seed 20260961. Copies of one original hospital remain one held-out group and are excluded from that fold's training data. Apply multiplicity weights as row copies for fitting and evaluation. Refit preprocessing and both models for each replicate; use percentile 95% intervals from finite converged replicates. Report the point estimate and interval for ΔINB, all 21 ΔNB values, ΔAUROC, Brier and log-loss changes, calibration slope and calibration-in-the-large for R1, mean absolute probability change, and all 33 fixed-prediction site deletions. Diagnostics do not rescue the primary label.

Computability requires: matching source hashes and headers; the exact audited 1,229/507/722/33 cohort and outcome-blind flow; >=1,000 people, >=30 hospitals, >=200 deaths and survivors; both outcomes at every hospital; maximum hospital share <=15%; >=80% post-QC coverage for each physiology stream; >=20/23 marker richness; finite positive training SD for every continuous feature in every fold; one finite prediction per person/model; all thresholds; >=95% finite converged refits; and >=80% finite site deletions. A failed gate yields Not computable and no scientific label.

## Falsification and interpretation

Apply these ordered rules only after computability:

- Supportive: the 95% lower ΔINB bound is >=+1; R1 calibration slope is 0.80-1.20; absolute calibration-in-the-large <=0.05; R1 beats the better of treat-all and treat-none at all 21 thresholds; no primary ΔNB is below -0.5 per 100; at least 80% of fixed-prediction hospital deletions are positive; and both Brier and log-loss point changes improve.
- Mixed: the lower ΔINB bound is >=+1 but any guardrail fails. Default strategies are guardrails, not the primary comparator; the labs-only parent curve cannot rescue or determine this label.
- Opposite: the 95% upper ΔINB bound is <0, regardless of defaults or secondary curves.
- Adverse to materiality: the upper bound is <+1 but >=0, with no earlier branch.
- Inconclusive: the interval spans +1 or otherwise none of the preceding branches applies.
- Not computable: any required source, flow, marker, coverage, fit, prediction, threshold, bootstrap, or deletion gate fails.

Support means only a material modeled increment for mortality-review decisions among this selected EICU population under the assumed threshold grid. It does not establish that lactate ordering improves outcomes, that acting on R1 improves survival, that a lactate measurement is safe or necessary, or that the model is transportable. An adverse label rejects the fixed +1 materiality claim in this setting, not every smaller increment. Opposite means worse modeled decisions, not harm from lactate. Mixed and inconclusive findings require independent cohorts and clinician-elicited utilities rather than threshold narrowing, outcome-derived subgrouping, or model expansion.

Clinical adjudication remains essential for what constitutes an appropriate review action, whether late lactate was actually available to clinicians (labresultrevisedoffset is only a database proxy), the validity of death status and cause, and whether model outputs would change management. Prospective synchronized collection, richer bedside covariates, external validation, and an impact trial are required before a clinical recommendation.
