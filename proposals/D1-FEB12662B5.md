# Provisional episode-2 hypothesis: does discordant AKI recovery add information beyond current severity?

Status: provisional research task; no effect or cohort count is claimed.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with an ICU stay and a prespecified creatinine-defined AKI episode who survive to a fixed landmark, a pattern of creatinine improvement without concurrent urine-output improvement will be associated with higher subsequent renal deterioration or in-hospital death than concordant recovery, after adjustment for the same landmark severity, treatment state, and observation intensity. The claim being tested is incremental prognostic information, not a causal mechanism or treatment effect.

## Opening and evidence boundary

The expert seed observes that creatinine and urine output can recover discordantly and that creatinine-only recovery may miss persistent renal vulnerability. The episode-1 candidate already bound this idea to MIMIC-IV and inspected evidence that creatinine-based kidney-function estimates can be misleading during critical illness, while also documenting the standard creatinine/urine-output AKI framework. Those sources support the measurement concern and the operational definition; they do not establish that discordance is prevalent, independent of severity, or prognostic. The unresolved claim is whether the pattern remains informative after current state and measurement/treatment context are accounted for.

## Falsifiable test and design

Use only the configured MIMIC-IV source archive and its documented table/item bindings. Define an adult ICU cohort with a creatinine-defined AKI episode, select a fixed landmark after the episode (for example 48 hours), and require survival and adequate measurements at that landmark. Compare creatinine-improving/urine-output-nonimproving versus concordant recovery, with outcomes restricted to events after the landmark (subsequent renal deterioration and in-hospital death). Predefine missingness and measurement-density rules, exclude post-landmark information from predictors, and use a patient-level held-out split or prespecified temporal validation.

The simple baseline is a landmark logistic/Cox model using current creatinine, urine output, AKI stage, severity and treatment variables. The substantive alternative is a trajectory model using the pre-landmark creatinine and urine-output series (for example a prespecified slope/area or a regularized recurrent model), with the same outcomes and split. The alternative can reveal whether the discordant pattern reflects a persistent trajectory rather than a single noisy landmark value; it does not by itself identify fluid status or renal physiology. Compare calibration, discrimination, and decision-curve or threshold performance only as secondary checks; the primary scientific comparison is whether discordance adds reproducible information beyond current state.

Supportive results require a prespecified direction and uncertainty interval for incremental information, stability across the held-out split and sensitivity to measurement density, and no material reversal under a negative control or severity-only baseline. Adverse results are a null or reversed association after adjustment, instability across splits, or disappearance under measurement-density controls; these would weaken the claim that discordance is clinically informative. Inconclusive results include too few eligible patients, sparse urine measurements, wide intervals, or inability to separate observation intensity from physiology. None establishes a mechanism.
