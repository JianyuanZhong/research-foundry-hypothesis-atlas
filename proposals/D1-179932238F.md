# Outcome-blind support and observation-process stress test for frozen P-beyond-B repeat-TACE HCC validation

## Unresolved question and substantive advance

In the frozen adult HCC repeat-TACE population, does the contemporaneous pre-repeat laboratory value `P` improve short-horizon prediction of the post-repeat value `Y` beyond remote baseline `B` and the locked decision-time covariates when evaluated strictly forward in calendar time? The claim is prognostic and noncausal. The unresolved threat is transportability: complete-triplet results may look favorable because later calendar blocks have sparse systemic/comparator support and because `Y` is observed selectively after the repeat event.

This proposal preserves the frozen estimand and adds a computable audit that separates three processes that must not be conflated: (1) selection of the repeat event, (2) pre-outcome eligibility for `B` and `P`, and (3) post-event observation of `Y`. It creates the outcome-blind risk-set funnel before inspecting post-event assay availability, reports calendar support using only pre-`Y` variables, and reports outcome-observation selection separately. These diagnostics contextualize the primary result; they do not redefine the complete-triplet estimand, reweight its RMSE, impute `Y`, or turn the systemic-record label into a treatment variable.

The strongest available predecessor claim is limited to incremental prediction in the adequately supported 2022 forward block. The experiment tests whether such gains remain interpretable in later calendar mixtures with adequate pre-`Y` support and outcome observation. A favorable result in a block with failed support or observation collapse is not transport evidence.

## Frozen population, clocks, and estimand

For each selected repeat event at `event_time`:

- `B` is the latest valid uncensored assay on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1.
- `P` is the latest valid assay in the selected repeat encounter during `[event_time−72 hours,event_time)`.
- `Y` is a valid assay in that same repeat encounter during `(event_time,event_time+72 hours]`, selected nearest +24 hours with deterministic tie handling.
- Require `B_time < P_time < event_time < Y_time`, positive P lead, one patient-assay row, and no assay values in event selection.

The primary estimand is unchanged, separately for albumin and bilirubin:

```text
M0: Y ~ B + systemic-record indicator + age + sex indicators
       + TACE2-to-repeat days + P-lead hours
M1: M0 + P
standardized gain = [RMSE(M0) - RMSE(M1)] / SD_test(Y)
```

Use origins `2021-01-01`, `2022-01-01`, `2023-01-01`, and `2024-01-01`. For each origin, fit only on events with `event_date < origin` and test on `[origin, origin+365 days)`. Blocks are non-overlapping. Learn age median imputation, age-missingness, sex coding, variance filtering, centering/scaling, and ridge alpha by training data only; choose alpha by inner leave-one-patient-out validation over `[0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100]`. Use paired arm-stratified patient-frequency bootstrap intervals conditional on each fixed pre-origin fit. MAE, bias, arm-specific errors, temporal drift, and influence are secondary.

## Outcome-blind risk-set funnel

Construct the event-level table from the full inherited event population and freeze it before reading or selecting post-event `Y` values. For each assay report counts overall, by systemic-record arm, and by each training/future calendar block:

1. selected repeat event;
2. `has_B`, under the frozen B clock;
3. `has_P`, under the frozen P clock;
4. `BP_eligible`, requiring both assays, strict ordering, positive P lead, and no post-event information;
5. `Y_observed`, assessed only after the BP table is frozen;
6. `complete = BP_eligible AND Y_observed`, exactly the primary complete-triplet analysis population.

Use the prespecified descriptive support gate of at least 20 BP-eligible episodes overall and at least 10 per arm in both training and future blocks. Report the complete-triplet gate separately with the same thresholds. A failed gate makes the block descriptive regardless of its point estimate or interval; it does not delete rows or change the primary estimand.

## Calendar transport diagnostics

Among BP-eligible records, fit a standardized logistic calendar-membership model using only pre-`Y` variables: `B`, `P`, age, sex, P-lead hours, TACE2-to-repeat days, and the prespecified arm label. For each future block report the future membership propensity range relative to training, fraction outside the training range, normalized training transport effective sample size, maximum normalized weight, and arm-specific transport ESS. Do not use these weights in the primary RMSE.

In the standardized pre-`Y` feature space, calculate same-arm nearest-neighbor distances from each future record to training records. Define the outcome-blind extrapolation warning as a future distance above the 95th percentile of leave-one-training-neighbor distances. Report the fraction warned overall and by arm.

Flag calendar support descriptively if any future propensity is outside the training range, any arm-specific transport ESS is below 10, or more than 5% of same-arm future records exceed the nearest-neighbor threshold. These flags are warnings, not post-hoc exclusion rules.

## Outcome-observation selection diagnostics

Among BP-eligible records, define `Y_observed` without using the numeric assay value. Report its fraction and Wilson 95% interval overall and by arm. Fit a training-only pre-origin logistic model for `Y_observed` using the same pre-`Y` variables, apply it to future BP-eligible records, and report predicted-probability range, observed fraction, Brier score, and inverse-observation-probability ESS among observed cases. These are selection diagnostics only: do not claim missing at random, reweight complete-triplet RMSE, or impute `Y`.

Report standardized mean differences between observed and unobserved future records for `B`, `P`, P-lead, TACE2-to-repeat days, and age when estimable. Flag observation selection if future observation is below 80%, its Wilson interval is clearly below the pre-origin rate, selection ESS is below 10, or any absolute pre-`Y` observed-versus-missing SMD is at least 0.5.

## Falsification and interpretation branches

Supportive evidence requires successful source/hash and denominator checks, both outcome-blind and complete-triplet support gates, a positive locked RMSE gain with uncertainty excluding a practically negligible or nonpositive gain under the prespecified criterion, and no material support or observation warning. It supports only incremental short-horizon prediction in that selected calendar mixture. It does not establish treatment response, toxicity, safety, clinical benefit, recovery, survival, utility, or actionability.

Adverse evidence includes a nonpositive or repeated degraded M1 in adequately supported blocks, or a reproducible material support failure under adequate event counts. This challenges incremental prediction in the tested selected population; it does not prove that `B` is sufficient or identify a mechanism. A favorable RMSE paired with severe observation collapse, low selection ESS, or large observed-versus-missing pre-`Y` imbalance is adverse to transport interpretation, not evidence of clinical benefit.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed arm support, sparse complete triplets, calendar extrapolation, outcome-observation collapse, influential episodes, denominator/clock drift, or unresolved laboratory units/reference ranges. The already executed broad audit motivates this distinction: later years contain substantial post-event observation loss, but arm-specific conclusions await the required ontology-preserving reconstruction. No result may be upgraded from descriptive to transport evidence merely because its point estimate is favorable.

## What computation cannot establish

The data do not provide laboratory units or reference ranges, verified medication administration, reliable external-care capture, adjudicated repeat-TACE rationale, tumor burden, imaging response, transfusion/fluid context, mortality, or a clinically validated decision threshold and loss function. Computation can verify source hashes, full scans, joins, literal filters, duplicate handling, frozen clocks, denominators, support funnels, model fitting, predictions, uncertainty, and diagnostics. Expert adjudication and independent temporal, external, or prospective data are required for clinical-outcome, causal, utility, or actionability claims.
