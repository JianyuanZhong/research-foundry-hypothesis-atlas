# Hospital heterogeneity in vasopressor initiation and escalation after a confirmed hypotension landmark

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly produce an auditable, uncertainty-quantified description of how hospitals differ in the *observed timing and agent path* of vasopressor support after the same measured early hypotension pattern, and whether those observed strategy differences coexist with different 24-hour hemodynamic/disposition states.

Completion requires:

1. a frozen source/header/schema audit, prespecified vasopressor-name map, MAP-source map, offset-validity audit, and cohort flow;
2. one analysis row per eligible ICU stay with the hypotension landmark, pre-landmark physiology, vasopressor strategy path, observation boundary, competing states and hospital-held-out split;
3. a transparent hierarchical initiation/outcome baseline and a matched mechanistic multi-state alternative fitted on the same rows and inputs;
4. standardized hospital-level strategy probabilities, outcome-state probabilities, between-hospital heterogeneity, held-out-hospital calibration/transport and uncertainty;
5. all prespecified measurement, offset, pretreatment, case-mix and hospital-permutation falsifications; and
6. a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive, with every scientific claim linked to a computed output and with observational database evidence separated from causal or clinical-adjudication claims.

Discovery inspected schemas, source headers and representative rows but did not fit either model and claims no hospital difference, treatment benefit, or direction of association.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The expert seed asks whether hospital differences in vasopressor strategy are related to timing and titration practices. The strongest claim currently supported is narrower: the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-stay-linked infusion events with drug names and offsets, ICU-relative vital observations, patient and hospital identifiers, admission/discharge fields, and severity descriptors that can be joined on documented keys. The direct audit also found several vasopressor names in multiple dose conventions and malformed/placeholder values. These facts establish feasibility of a bounded observational study, not a strategy effect.

The unresolved clinical interpretation is:

> Among adults with a newly confirmed, observed low-MAP episode and no mapped vasopressor record before the episode, do hospitals differ in the probability of early versus delayed/no vasopressor initiation and in the subsequent single-agent versus multi-agent path after standardizing the measured hypotension trajectory and admission context; and are these observed strategy differences accompanied by differences in 24-hour hemodynamic recovery versus death or alive ICU exit?

Primary hypothesis: the standardized hospital interquartile range (IQR) for early initiation (within 60 minutes of the confirmed low-MAP landmark) is at least 10 percentage points, and the hospital-specific strategy distribution is associated with a non-trivial difference in the 24-hour competing-state distribution after measured-case-mix adjustment. The target is hospital heterogeneity in an observed strategy and its associated outcomes, not the causal effect of starting earlier, a quality ranking, or a recommended MAP threshold.

This is clinically important because the decision to start or add a vasopressor is time-sensitive and may alter exposure to catecholamines, monitoring intensity and downstream organ support. A reproducible difference in timing/pathway conditional on the same recorded instability would identify a transportability and protocol-design problem worth prospective adjudication. A null result would be equally useful: it would weaken the claim that a coarse hospital-strategy signal is detectable in this database after measurement and severity controls.

The substantive advance over the current hypotension/creatinine and monitoring-process designs is that the estimand is treatment-strategy heterogeneity itself. It separates (i) a hospital's observed threshold for recording/initiation after a common low-MAP landmark from (ii) the later physiology/disposition trajectory, while refusing to call either component causal in data that lack treatment intent, reliable dose semantics and a counterfactual policy.

## Population, time zero and temporal boundaries

Use the first eligible ICU stay per `uniquepid` in the primary analysis; retain all eligible stays clustered by `uniquepid` as a sensitivity analysis. Include adults with age parsed as numeric (the eICU `> 89` value is mapped to 90 before any split), nonmissing `hospitalid`, a valid `patientunitstayid`, determinable `unitdischargeoffset` and `unitdischargestatus`, and at least two valid MAP observations in the first 6 ICU hours.

Define a valid MAP observation without interpolation:

- primary source is `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` at `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset`;
- if no systemic mean exists at that event time, use `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` at `vitalAperiodic.observationoffset`;
- retain a source indicator and never average the two sources at the same time;
- `vitalPeriodic.pamean` is not substituted for arterial MAP in the primary analysis because it is pulmonary-artery pressure; it is a prespecified exploratory sensitivity only.

A qualifying hypotension landmark is the first valid MAP below 65 mmHg in ICU minutes [0, 360] followed by a second valid MAP below 65 within 120 minutes, with at least 30 minutes between the two observations when the data permit. The index is the timestamp of the first qualifying low MAP. The confirmation rule, 65-mmHg threshold and 120-minute gap are frozen before fitting; sensitivity analyses use a 60-minute confirmation window, a 70-mmHg threshold, and a no-confirmation single-observation definition. Exclude a stay if a mapped vasopressor event has a valid onset at or before the index in the primary cohort; include such stays in a left-truncated/pretreated sensitivity with a separate baseline state.

At time zero, construct only history available at or before the index:

- MAP level, slope, minimum, number of low observations, minutes from first observation to index, number of MAP observations, and source/coverage indicators in [0, index];
- whether a valid MAP was available in the preceding 120 minutes;
- age, sex, ethnicity, admission source, unit type, unit admission source, unit visit number and unit stay type;
- hospital context;
- no laboratory, infusion or disposition value after the index.

Follow from the index to the earliest of 24 hours, `unitdischargeoffset`, death, or alive ICU exit. All offsets are ICU-relative minutes. A post-index infusion event is eligible only when its `infusionoffset` is strictly greater than the index and no future row is used to define an earlier feature.

## Exposure: observed strategy path, not dose treatment

Freeze an auditable name map before splitting:

- norepinephrine: case-insensitive names beginning with/containing `norepinephrine` or the prespecified equivalent spelling;
- epinephrine: names containing `epinephrine` or the prespecified adrenaline synonym;
- vasopressin: names containing `vasopressin`;
- phenylephrine: names containing `phenylephrine` or `neosynephrine`;
- dopamine: names containing `dopamine`;
- angiotensin: names containing `angiotensin`.

The raw `drugname` is retained beside the mapped class. Unmatched names, empty names and non-pressor agents are not silently classified as pressors. The solver must emit the full name map, unmatched-name counts, and examples by hospital before fitting. The map is a prespecified data-cleaning rule, not a label learned from outcomes.

From `infusionDrug`, use `infusionoffset` as an event time and deduplicate repeated rows for the same stay and mapped drug within a prespecified 30-minute update window, retaining the earliest valid event and an update-count field. Treat blank, nonnumeric or negative offsets as invalid; report zero-offset rows separately and use them only in a zero-inclusive sensitivity. Do not infer an infusion duration from this table.

The primary four-level early strategy exposure is:

1. no mapped pressor event through 360 minutes after index;
2. first mapped pressor event within 0–60 minutes, one agent class through 360 minutes;
3. first mapped pressor event after 60 but by 360 minutes, one agent class;
4. any mapped second agent class by 360 minutes, with onset timing (early/late) retained.

For interpretation, also report the finer path: early norepinephrine, early non-norepinephrine, late first agent, early second agent, late second agent, and no recorded initiation. A second agent means a distinct mapped class, not a rate update. Dose fields `drugrate`, `infusionrate`, `drugamount`, `volumeoffluid` and `patientweight` are audited and descriptive only; they are not converted to dose equivalents, because the direct audit showed ml/hr, mcg/min, mcg/kg/min, units/min, blank units, zeros and nonnumeric values. The study therefore cannot answer whether one dose or titration intensity is superior.

## Outcomes and competing states

The primary outcome is a 24-hour categorical competing-state endpoint, observed only while the patient remains in the ICU unit:

1. death: `unitdischargestatus = Expired` and `unitdischargelocation = Death`, with `unitdischargeoffset` before any other terminal state;
2. alive unit exit: `unitdischargestatus = Alive` before 24 hours, including floor, home, another ICU/hospital or transfer;
3. sustained hemodynamic recovery: before the first terminal exit, two valid MAP observations at least 30 minutes apart with MAP >=65 and no subsequent valid MAP <65 before 24 hours or exit;
4. observed persistent/recurrent instability: alive and still in the unit at 24 hours without qualifying sustained recovery.

If recovery and discharge share a minute, apply death first, alive exit second, recovery third, and repeat with tied records excluded. Recovery is a recorded MAP state, not shock resolution, organ recovery or a treatment-success label. If an alive unit exit occurs before a later observation, it is observation loss, not recovery. Hospital death fields are not used in the primary endpoint because the unit boundary is the last guaranteed ICU observation; hospital-discharge fields are a sensitivity only.

The solver also reports 6-, 12- and 24-hour cause-specific transition probabilities for first MAP recovery, death and alive exit, but the prespecified clinical-scale contrast is the 24-hour standardized state distribution.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

Both models use the identical eligible stays, index definition, 30-minute follow-up grid, exposure map, outcomes, source universe, hospital-held-out split, and uncertainty procedure. This is a scientific comparison of interpretation, not a contest for a small predictive gain.

### Transparent baseline: two linked hierarchical competing-risk components

Fit a regularized, additive discrete-time model with a 30-minute grid from index through 24 hours. Component A is a multinomial model for the observed strategy path (no start, early single-agent, late single-agent, multi-agent), using only pre-index MAP trajectory, demographics/admission context and hospital context, with hospital random intercepts. Component B is a cause-specific complementary-log-log model for first recovery, death and alive exit, using the same pre-index variables plus the prespecified observed strategy categories and interval time. Include hospital random intercepts and hospital-by-strategy slopes only if the frozen support audit identifies enough information; otherwise report them as non-identifiable.

Estimate, under a common measured case mix, each hospital's standardized strategy probabilities and 24-hour competing-state probabilities. The baseline is auditable and gives an interpretable hospital threshold/path signal, but it treats the strategy as a categorical event and loses the distinction between an unstable patient who recovers before escalation and one who transitions through sustained pressor support.

### Mechanistic alternative: joint observed-state transition model

Fit a piecewise-exponential, 30-minute multi-state model on exactly the same index rows and source-derived inputs. States are:

- confirmed low-MAP, no mapped pressor yet;
- pressor-supported unstable;
- recovering with MAP >=65 but not yet sustained;
- sustained recorded MAP recovery;
- death;
- alive unit exit/observation loss.

Transitions include no-pressor-to-first-pressor, first-pressor-to-second-agent, unstable-to-recovery, recovery-to-recurrent-low-MAP, and death/alive exit. The policy transitions use the same hospital and pre-index variables as the baseline. Physiologic transitions use only time-aligned MAP history, source/coverage, prior strategy state and the same measured admission context. Hospital effects are allowed separately on policy transitions and physiology/exit transitions, with shrinkage and an explicit no-effect fallback if unsupported.

This alternative reveals information the baseline loses: whether hospital heterogeneity is concentrated in the transition to initiation, in adding a second agent, or in recovery/exit after the same observed policy state. It also reveals whether a hospital's apparent outcome difference is mainly a different observed patient-state trajectory or a different policy transition. It does not identify what would have happened under a counterfactual vasopressor policy, because treatment is time-varying and confounded by evolving illness.

Fit both models in development hospitals only after a hospital-level outer split; all stays sharing `uniquepid` remain in one partition. Preprocessing, name auditing, plausibility filters, regularization, scaling and any latent-state initialization are learned/frozen in development data. For an unseen hospital, use the training-population intercept and report the held-out calibration shift; do not estimate a test-hospital fixed effect from test outcomes.

## Estimands, uncertainty and transport

The primary descriptive estimand is the hospital IQR and between-hospital variance of standardized probability of early initiation, late initiation, multi-agent escalation and no recorded initiation under a common distribution of measured pre-index covariates. Report shrinkage estimates with 95% hospital-cluster bootstrap intervals and the raw unadjusted distributions beside them.

The primary outcome estimand is the standardized 24-hour difference in the competing-state probability vector associated with observed hospital strategy composition, not a treatment contrast. Report component-specific contrasts for sustained recovery, death and alive exit, with uncertainty and the proportion of outcome variation attributable to the policy-transition versus physiology/exit components in the multi-state model. Label all such estimates as observational associations.

Transport requires five-fold repeated hospital-held-out evaluation, with no hospital represented in both train and test. Report per-hospital and pooled calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, state-specific log loss, observed-versus-predicted strategy probabilities, and the coverage of uncertainty intervals. A strategy/path claim is transport-supported only if the sign and magnitude of the early-initiation heterogeneity remain compatible with the development estimate in at least 70% of held-out hospitals with >=100 eligible index stays and the pooled held-out calibration slope lies in [0.8, 1.2]. Hospitals below the support threshold are shown, not silently excluded. These are transport criteria, not a quality ranking.

Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates for primary intervals and a `uniquepid`-clustered bootstrap sensitivity. For the multi-state model report transition counts, occupancy, convergence from at least three initializations, parameter uncertainty and posterior/predictive checks. AUROC/AUPRC are secondary and not a success criterion.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Supportive evidence requires all of the following:

- the adjusted hospital IQR for early initiation is at least 10 percentage points with uncertainty that makes a zero or clinically trivial (<5-point) IQR implausible;
- the same qualitative hospital ordering/heterogeneity appears in the linked policy-transition alternative, not only in the categorical baseline;
- the policy-transition and physiology/exit components are separately identifiable and the result is not explained by one hospital, one region or one MAP source;
- the strategy/outcome associations have compatible direction in development and held-out hospitals, with held-out calibration meeting the prespecified rule;
- the finding persists under no interpolation, noninvasive-MAP-only, systemic-mean-only, 60-minute confirmation, 70-mmHg threshold, zero-offset exclusion, zero-offset inclusion, and pretreated-stay sensitivity.

Adverse findings include an adjusted IQR below 5 percentage points with useful precision, reversal across MAP-source or offset rules, disappearance after the uncertain APACHE sensitivity, failure of held-out calibration, or a hospital effect that is entirely driven by a single institution. These findings reject the claim of a robust hospital-strategy signal; they do not prove hospitals use the same policy or that a treatment is safe.

Inconclusive findings include fewer than 30 hospitals with at least 100 eligible index stays, near-universal/no initiation, too few second-agent events or recovery/death states, non-identifiable policy versus physiology transitions, unstable name/unit mapping, nonconvergence, extreme measurement-density dependence, or intervals spanning the 5- and 10-point decision margins. Inconclusive is not supportive evidence.

A computational falsification will permute hospital labels within prespecified bins of baseline MAP, age, unit type and index-time observation density, refit the heterogeneity summary in a bounded subset, and require the observed between-hospital statistic to exceed the permutation distribution. The placebo audit will test whether apparent strategy differences are explained by pre-index MAP observation density and source composition; if so, the interpretation is documentation/measurement heterogeneity, not a treatment-practice difference.

## Evidence limits and unavailable clinical adjudication

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes and headers, table/column use, joins, offset ordering, name mapping, deduplication, cohort flow, split integrity, exposure/state construction, standardized probabilities, transition parameters, uncertainty, calibration, transport and reproducibility of sensitivities.

The eICU snapshot does not provide a universal vasopressor dose unit or reliable start/stop exposure interval; order time, indication, clinician intent, arterial-line/central-line status, fluid bolus and fluid responsiveness, bedside shock phenotype, lactate context in a synchronized treatment ledger, treatment limitations, and a counterfactual treatment policy are not available for this question. Narrative and care-plan fields cannot silently recover them. The infusion records also cannot distinguish an order/update/interface artifact from actual bedside administration in every row.

Clinical review is required to determine whether a mapped infusion event was administered, why it was initiated or escalated, whether MAP recovery represents shock resolution, whether an alive unit exit was safe liberation versus transfer with ongoing support, and whether any strategy is beneficial or harmful. A prospective or richer retrospective study with adjudicated indication, dose/time, fluid context, line status, serial lactate/organ outcomes and external hospitals is required before a clinician should change vasopressor policy. Hospital effects must not be called quality differences or used for ranking.

## Alternatives retained and selection record

The transparent hierarchical initiation/outcome model is the matched simple baseline because its coefficients and standardized probabilities make the hospital-strategy estimand auditable. The joint multi-state model is selected as the substantive alternative because it can distinguish a hospital-specific initiation/escalation transition from a hospital-specific recovery/exit transition using the same inputs and outcomes.

A recurrent neural sequence model over 30-minute MAP and infusion channels is deferred. It could model nonlinear trajectory shape, but current source evidence does not establish that a black-box prediction gain would resolve the policy-versus-physiology interpretation. Revisit only if both prespecified models converge, held-out residuals show reproducible sequence structure in multiple hospitals, and a bounded CPU/GPU feasibility probe supports calibration under the same strategy/state estimands. The evidence to revisit is transition-level residual structure, not a small AUC improvement.

Dose-response and causal policy learning are deferred because mixed dose units, missing start/stop semantics, indication confounding and no valid IV/intent variable make them scientifically underidentified. Revisit requires expert-verified dose normalization, treatment indication/timing, synchronized fluid and lactate context, and a design with a defensible treatment-policy estimand. Hospital preference is not an instrumental variable.

The actual scientific completion is a new fit of the two linked baseline components and the joint transition model, followed by standardized hospital heterogeneity, uncertainty, held-out transport and falsification outputs. No fitted result is claimed here.
