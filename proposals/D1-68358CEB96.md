# Bridge-specific early failure after first ICU discharge: a selection-robust lactate/support study

The primary output is a transition-specific recorded-data estimand, not a bedside rule or a treatment effect.

## Evidence-supported claim and unresolved hypothesis

The relevant expert seed (`[starting question]`) proposes that persistent high lactate can have different meanings when perfusion is improving versus worsening, while explicitly warning that repeated lactates are selectively obtained and cannot diagnose microcirculatory dysfunction. The available evidence establishes only that this snapshot has dated lactate, vital-sign, vasoactive-input, ICU-stay, transfer, death, and discharge records. The locally read MIMIC-IV documentation card does not establish the hypothesis or provide a clinical adjudication of perfusion.

The parent’s recorded-data design supports testing whether persistent measured lactate and recorded support state are associated with early failure under explicit selection and topology assumptions. It does not establish that “recorded-off” means a deliberate successful withdrawal, that a later ICU stay was unplanned, or that a return was preventable. The unresolved, narrower claim is:

> Among all adults alive at their first eligible ICU outtime, does the persistent-high/recorded-off phenotype have a higher probability of a documented-bridge ICU re-entry within 24 hours, especially in the 6–24-hour interval, than the low/recorded-off phenotype after accounting for selective serial-lactate observation; or is the apparent difference explained by observation intensity, death before return, or unresolvable transfer topology?

The prespecified primary contrast is the standardized absolute CIF difference

`Delta_B,24 = CIF(documented-bridge rapid return by 24 h | persistent-high/recorded-off) - CIF(... | low/recorded-off)`.

Secondary contrasts are `Delta_B,0-6`, `Delta_B,6-24`, `Delta_B,24-48`, the analogous any-return `Delta_U,h`, U-only return, death, and alive-discharge CIF contrasts, plus the return-versus-death composition. The 0–6 versus 6–24 decomposition is clinically consequential: a signal confined to the first six hours suggests immediate transition instability or documentation timing, whereas a reproducible 6–24-hour signal is more compatible with a post-transfer surveillance window. Neither pattern proves a biological mechanism.

Persistent-high/recorded-off versus low/recorded-off is the primary comparison. Low/active-support and persistent-high/active-support are severity/context strata, not causal controls. Resolving-high, indeterminate, R=0, ambiguous support, and contradictory topology remain reported strata.

The strongest supportable conclusion, if obtained, would be that a specified recorded lactate/support pattern is associated with a specified topology-defined early event under a stated observation model and within stated identification intervals. It would not support tissue hypoperfusion, microcirculatory dysfunction, successful support withdrawal, unplanned readmission, preventability, clinical utility, or benefit/harm from changing surveillance or treatment.

## Bridge-specific competing outcomes and partial identification

For each index discharge, find every later same-admission `icustays` row with later `intime`. Define U as any later ICU start in the relevant horizon. Define the primary documented-bridge return B as a U candidate whose return time is preceded by at least one same-admission `transfers` row with parseable `intime/outtime`, `intime >= index outtime`, `outtime <= later ICU intime`, and a non-null recorded interval. Primary boundary tolerance is zero; 15/30/60-minute tolerances are sensitivity analyses. The row need not be clinically interpreted as a ward transfer: B means only that a transfer interval documents a bridge in the record. A transfer interval that overlaps the index ICU stay, overlaps the later ICU stay without a preceding interval, has contradictory bounds, or cannot be joined is not B and is retained as unknown topology. Do not infer non-ICU status from an unvalidated careunit string.

The first-event categories are mutually exclusive, with death-first tie handling primary:

- B return: first eligible ICU return is B, labelled by return time in 0–6, 6–24, or 24–48 hours.
- U-only/unknown-topology return: U occurs but B is not verifiable; it contributes to U and not B.
- death: first recorded in-hospital death before return, using `admissions.deathtime` when present. If `hospital_expire_flag=1` but `deathtime` is absent, retain death with unknown exact time, use `dischtime` for the primary administrative timing, and interval-censor it between the last known alive time and `dischtime` in sensitivity analysis.
- alive hospital discharge: `admissions.dischtime` before return/death, treated as a competing event.
- administrative censoring: no event by horizon or no valid admission boundary.

The primary estimand is the standardized B CIF contrast at 24 hours and its interval-specific decomposition. U, U-only, death, and alive discharge are competing outcome descriptions, not interchangeable “readmission risk.” A larger B or U CIF with a larger death CIF is not summarized as a single benefit or harm.

Report three layers: (i) observed-pair contrasts among R=1; (ii) full eligible-cohort contrasts transported under a conditional observation assumption; and (iii) assumption-light intervals. Estimate R from pre-landmark values/masks, static fields, support/physiology, and prior measurement intensity only. Fit observation models on training subjects, use stabilized inverse-probability weights capped at 10 primarily and 5/20 in sensitivity, report uncapped weights, positivity and ESS, and vary a locked selection-odds multiplier Gamma in {0.5,1,2}. This is not causal.

For bounds, keep recorded event time and first-event category fixed and allow R=0, indeterminate lactate, ambiguous support, and unknown topology to take every phenotype/topology assignment compatible with deterministic constraints. Solve the finite assignment minimum and maximum for each B/U/death CIF contrast and composition at each horizon; report assignment counts. These identification intervals remain separate from bootstrap confidence intervals around a fixed weighted estimand or fixed endpoint.

### Learned alternative: observation-aware bridge semi-Markov model

Fit a compact two-domain hidden semi-Markov model on exactly the same twelve-row ledger and splits. The metabolic domain has low, persistent-high, resolving, and unknown states; the circulatory domain has active, recorded-off, and unknown states. Add an explicit topology-observation process whose recorded states are no-verifiable-bridge, bridge-observed, and ICU-return-observed; it models transfer observation rather than asserting a clinical ward state. State durations span the twelve rows. An event head predicts the same one-hour B, U-only, death, and discharge hazards; an observation head predicts lactate/support/transfer-record availability from latent states, prior ledger history, and observed intensity.

The alternative may reveal whether within-window persistence and ordering, separated from the process of recording lactate or transfer, produce a stable transition decomposition that endpoint categories lose. ## Falsification, interpretation, and evidence gates

Prespecified falsification checks are:

- permute lactate values and their temporal order within support-intensity strata while preserving assay count, masks, timestamps, and R;
- permute recorded support timing within support-intensity strata while preserving medication counts and interval lengths;
- compare observation-only, support/physiology-only, lactate-only, and full-ledger models;
- substitute `storetime` for lactate/chart and support interval times as timestamp negative controls;
- repeat at pseudo-landmarks 6 and 12 hours before `outtime`, shifting predictor and outcome origins together;
- vary assay, lactate threshold, pair/window rule, support boundary tolerance, transfer bridge tolerance, death-time interval, and return/death tie order;
- stratify by first/last careunit, admission type, and anchor-year group without treating shifted cross-subject dates as calendar time;
- report R prevalence, cell counts, unknown/contradictory topology, observation-model calibration, weight positivity/ESS, latent entropy, and B-versus-U interval width.

Supportive evidence requires adequate primary-cell counts and positivity; a positive full-cohort `Delta_B,24` with uncertainty excluding zero under the declared Gamma sensitivity; an assumption-light interval that is non-vacuous or explicitly reported as vacuous; concordant baseline and learned timing/decomposition; acceptable held-out calibration; attenuation after lactate permutation; and persistence across assay/window/threshold/bridge sensitivities. A 6–24-hour signal is specifically required for the claim of a post-transition surveillance window; a 0–6-only signal supports only an immediate recorded-event association.

Adverse evidence is a null/reversal after selection adjustment, an observation-only explanation, persistence after lactate permutation, dependence on one assay or topology rule, death dominating the alleged return pathway, or a U-only signal with a null/wide B interval. This falsifies the proposed bridge-specific interpretation but can still describe competing-risk patterns.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse cells, no positivity, high R=0/unknown prevalence, unstable weights, wide phenotype/topology intervals, unresolved death timing, split instability, high latent entropy, method disagreement, or insufficient event support. It must not be relabelled as a null.

The computation can establish feature provenance, recorded B/U topology labels, observed-pair and conditional selection-standardized estimands, competing-risk CIFs, partial-identification bounds, prediction/calibration, and sensitivity consistency. It cannot establish clinical deterioration on the ward, intentional or unplanned transfer, preventability, discharge readiness, perfusion biology, treatment response, causal benefit/harm, clinical utility, or outcomes after the admission. Those require expert adjudication of charts and intent/treatment limitation, complete ward-monitoring and intervention data, external validation, and likely a prospective or causal study.
