# Serial SOFA Trajectories Predict ICU Mortality Beyond Baseline Severity

## Scientific Question

Does the temporal pattern of Serial SOFA score trajectories (not just baseline SOFA) provide prognostic information about ICU mortality that exceeds what baseline SOFA alone can provide?

## Hypothesis

Patients with specific serial SOFA trajectory patterns—particularly **rapid early deterioration** (consecutive increases over 2-3 days) or **delayed improvement** (failure to improve after initial recovery)—have higher ICU mortality risk than patients with stable or improving trajectories, even after adjusting for baseline SOFA score and covariates.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Early Warning**: Current practice relies heavily on single-timepoint SOFA assessments. Serial trajectory analysis could identify high-risk patients earlier, before deterioration becomes apparent in static measurements.

2. **Treatment Guidance**: Different trajectory patterns may suggest different underlying pathophysiology (e.g., progressive organ failure vs. delayed recovery), potentially informing treatment intensification or de-escalation decisions.

3. **Risk Stratification**: Could refine risk scores beyond baseline severity, capturing dynamic aspects of organ dysfunction that single measurements miss.

## Study Population

- **Source**: MIMIC-IV ICU stays (icu/icustays, icu/chartevents, icu/inputevents, icu/outputevents, hosp/patients)
- **Inclusion**: ICU stays with ≥3 serial SOFA score measurements (≥48 hours apart)
- **Exclusion**: Patients with pre-existing organ failure (baseline SOFA ≥3 before ICU admission), non-ICU transfers
- **Outcome**: ICU mortality (death before discharge)

## SOFA Score Extraction

SOFA scores are available in icu/chartevents with itemid=53384. Extract values with timestamps to construct temporal trajectories.

## Baseline Comparison

**Baseline approach**: Compare ICU mortality by baseline SOFA score (0-4, 5-6, 7-9, 10-12, ≥13) at ICU admission (first SOFA measurement).

This captures only the initial severity but misses:
- Subsequent deterioration
- Failure to improve
- Trajectory shape (rate of change)

## Mechanistic Alternative: Trajectory-Based Modeling

**Trajectory approach**: Model SOFA scores as a function of time using:
1. **Linear trajectory**: SOFA(t) = β₀ + β₁×t + ε
2. **Exponential trajectory**: SOFA(t) = β₀ + β₁×(1 - e^(-β₂×t)) + ε
3. **Piecewise trajectory**: Different slopes before/after day 3

**Key features to extract**:
- Initial slope (rate of change in first 48 hours)
- Time to improvement (days to SOFA decrease)
- Final trajectory (slope on last 3 days)
- Trajectory curvature (acceleration/deceleration)

**Hypothesis**: Specific trajectory features (e.g., rapid initial increase, delayed improvement) predict mortality independent of baseline SOFA.

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. No association between trajectory features and mortality after adjusting for baseline SOFA and covariates (OR < 1.1, 95% CI includes 1, p > 0.05)
2. Baseline SOFA alone explains all prognostic information (adding trajectory features doesn't improve C-statistic)
3. All trajectory patterns show similar mortality risk (no distinct high-risk patterns)

## Supportive Results

Supportive evidence would show:
1. Specific trajectory patterns (e.g., rapid early deterioration, delayed improvement) associated with higher mortality
2. Trajectory features add prognostic value beyond baseline SOFA (ΔC-statistic > 0.03)
3. Risk stratification by trajectory outperforms baseline-only stratification

## Inconclusive Results

Inconclusive if:
1. Weak associations (OR 1.1-1.3) that may be clinically meaningful but not statistically significant
2. Confounding by treatment (need propensity adjustment or instrumental variable analysis)
3. Heterogeneous trajectory definitions requiring sensitivity analysis
