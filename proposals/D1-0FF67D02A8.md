# POD2 coupled recovery and invasive fluid rescue after first HCC resection

Status: reference-complete, feasibility-audited proposal. No outcome model has been fitted and no hypothesis result is claimed.

## Clinical opening and falsifiable hypothesis

At 48 hours after a first recorded liver resection for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), clinicians must decide who needs intensified respiratory/fluid surveillance and early imaging rather than routine ward recovery. Existing work shows that post-hepatectomy complications are clinically consequential and that intervention-requiring pleural or abdominal fluid collections are recognized adverse outcomes [K1–K3]. It does not establish whether the *ordered, coupled early recovery pattern* adds information beyond the patient's latest POD2 state.

**Strongest claim already supported.** Intervention requirement is a clinically meaningful way to grade surgical complications [K3]. In hepatectomy cohorts, thoracentesis/thoracic drainage has been used to define clinically relevant postoperative pleural effusion [K2], and a recent primary-liver-cancer model included thoracentesis and abdominal drainage in an adjudicated 30-day complication outcome [K1]. In this HCC extract, a bounded source audit found enough timestamped procedures and 0–48-hour laboratory/medication data to compute the proposed experiment.

**Unresolved claim to test.** Among adults still hospitalized and free of recorded pleural/abdominal fluid rescue at 48 hours after their first recorded HCC liver resection, a coupled 0–48-hour state characterized by failure of oncotic recovery, inflammatory activation, and impaired renal/fluid handling will improve 48-hour prediction of first invasive pleural or abdominal fluid rescue through postoperative hour 336 beyond a transparent model of latest POD2 state, operation burden, treatment indicators, and observation intensity. The claim is about prognostic temporal information, not a causal effect of albumin, diuretics, inflammation, or fluid management.

**Why resolution matters.** A supported result would justify prospective adjudication of a POD2 monitoring phenotype for targeted respiratory assessment, fluid review, or imaging. An adverse result would prevent a complex trajectory model from displacing a simpler POD2 snapshot. An inconclusive result would identify coding/adjudication, rather than model capacity, as the next bottleneck.

## Closest inspected work and advance

Wang et al. used 397 patients, clinician-adjudicated 30-day Clavien–Dindo grade II or higher complications, preoperative/intraoperative predictors, and a random 7:3 split [K1]. Sakamoto et al. defined clinically relevant pleural effusion by thoracentesis or drain placement in 382 hepatectomy patients and discussed competing fluid-overload, hypoalbuminemic, and inflammatory explanations [K2]. Dindo et al. established a treatment-based complication hierarchy in which invasive intervention is grade III and length of stay is not a severity criterion [K3].

The substantive advance is not another static nomogram or length-of-stay model. It is a temporally locked test of whether early multiorgan *ordering and coupling* predicts a timestamp-native invasive rescue after the prediction landmark, with explicit challenges from current severity, treatment indication, measurement intensity, coding source, and calendar drift. This is distinct from pathology-grade prediction and from the parked delayed-discharge trajectory question.

## Rival explanations and differing predictions

1. **Leading temporal-state explanation.** A latent early state linking low/falling albumin, inflammatory activation, and renal/fluid-handling stress precedes later drainable pleural/abdominal accumulation. Prediction: an ordered multichannel state-space model improves proper scores beyond latest values; destroying early order while preserving latest state and observation schedule removes much of the gain; learned factor loadings and transitions align across pleural and abdominal components.

2. **Current-severity/treatment-response rival.** Latest POD2 values and the fact that clinicians have already prescribed albumin, loop diuretics, antibiotics, vasopressors, or hemostatics contain all usable information. Medication timing reflects indication and clinician concern, not treatment effects. Prediction: the transparent latest-state baseline performs as well as the temporal model, or any apparent trajectory gain persists after order destruction.

3. **Measurement/coding-era rival.** More recent eras record procedures and tests more completely, and intervention thresholds may change. Prediction: a timing-only model performs materially well; gains collapse under same-year/source controls; effects reverse between `Anesthesia System` and `Medical Record Surgery`; or 2024–2025 calibration fails despite 2023 recalibration.

These observations cannot distinguish vascular leak from inflammation mechanistically without fluid balance, imaging, pleural/ascitic chemistry, and indication review. The fitted latent factors are phenotypes, not biological mechanisms.

## Frozen population, landmark, and outcome

1. Adults age ≥18.
2. An encounter has both an explicit HCC diagnosis and a procedure name containing `liver` and `resection`, excluding names containing `transplant|donor|donor liver|living-donor`.
3. For each patient, choose the chronologically first qualifying resection encounter across all available years. Define T0 as the earliest matching `anesthesia system` `start time`; exclude the encounter if no anesthesia-system anchor exists. Require T0 within one day of the recorded admission/discharge interval.
4. Require recorded discharge after T0+48 hours, so the patient is observable and at risk at the landmark.
5. Exclude any primary fluid-rescue procedure in [T0,T0+48h].
6. Primary modeling era is 2018–2025 because anesthesia-source outcome support is zero before 2018 and all-source event rates are near zero before 2015. Earlier years remain a documented feasibility/drift audit, not training data.

**Primary outcome:** first procedure with `start time` in (T0+48h, T0+336h], no later than recorded discharge plus two hours, matching either:

- pleural intervention: procedure name contains `chest cavity` and one of `puncture|fluid aspiration|catheter placement|closed`, excluding `biopsy`;
- abdominal/operative-bed fluid intervention: `abdominal cavity.*(puncture|catheter placement).*drainage|abdominal puncture and drainage|abdominal catheter placement and drainage|operative-site effusion.*drainage|subphrenic effusion.*drainage|percutaneous.*liver.*drainage|liver abscess.*drainage`.

Both procedure sources count, with patient-level deduplication at the first timestamp. Freeze the resulting exact-name lexicon before fitting. This is “recorded invasive fluid rescue,” not adjudicated pleural effusion, ascites, abscess, bile leak, Clavien grade, or unplanned procedure.

**Audited support:** 24,907 adults had an anesthesia anchor, remained at risk, and had no primary event by 48 hours; 336 (1.35%) had a primary event by 336 hours, including 265 pleural and 100 abdominal events with overlap. In 2018–2025, N=17,733/events=326. Development 2018–2021: 6,991/85; tuning 2022: 1,891/36; calibration 2023: 2,968/46; locked test 2024–2025: 5,883/159. Counts are a feasibility observation, not a fitted result.

Secondary outcomes, reported separately without redefining the primary composite, are pleural-only, abdominal-only, and an expanded severe-rescue set (reoperation/hemostasis, biliary drainage, airway support, renal replacement). The expanded components are sparse and cannot rescue a failed primary test.

## Predictors and leakage controls

All predictors must be recorded at or before T0+48h. Outcome procedures, discharge duration, pathology, examinations after the landmark, later medications/labs, and all clinical documents are forbidden predictors.

Common static inputs: age, sex, operation year, duration from the earliest index-procedure start/end where valid, missing-duration indicator, laparoscopic lexical flag, and coarse major-resection flag (hemihepatectomy, extended/major, or ≥3 named segments). Freeze lexical rules using development data only and report frequencies.

Common dynamic inputs use exact labels listed above. For each exact lab channel, retain numeric values, timestamps from T0, specimen type, count, last-observation time, and missingness. Transform skewed values with train-only signed log where appropriate, then robust-standardize within exact assay label; never infer units. Medication inputs are group/route/order start-stop timestamps and counts, not doses or treatment effects.

No forward filling across T0 or past 48 hours. Duplicate same-assay/same-timestamp values are median-collapsed after reporting duplicates. Measurements after an outcome timestamp are impossible in the primary risk set because outcomes begin after 48h. Patient IDs are removed before modeling. Hyperparameters, lexicons, thresholds, and transforms are frozen before opening 2024–2025 outcomes.

## Methods developed before selection

### B0: transparent POD2 landmark baseline

Fit elastic-net logistic regression with restricted cubic splines for age, operation duration, and each latest lab value in [24,48h]. Add missingness, time since latest value, 0–48h measurement count, static operation flags, and whether/when each medication group was first ordered. Include a small prespecified interaction set: albumin×diuretic, albumin×creatinine, INR×bilirubin, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio×procalcitonin. Use class-weighting only during optimization; restore observed prevalence before calibration. Select penalty on 2022 Brier score, refit on 2018–2022, and fit intercept/slope recalibration only on 2023.

This baseline answers whether current POD2 severity, treatment response, and observation process suffice. It deliberately loses within-patient ordering and cross-channel transition information.

### T1: coupled irregular-time latent-state alternative

Fit a task-specific, masked dynamic factor/state-space model to eight 6-hour bins over [0,48h]. Per bin, use the last exact-assay value, missingness, time-since-observation, and medication-order starts. Prespecify three domains—oncotic/synthetic (albumin, INR, bilirubin, fibrinogen), inflammatory/injury (neutrophils, lymphocytes, procalcitonin, separate CRP labels, ALT/AST), and renal/fluid handling (creatinine, urea, sodium, albumin/diuretic timing)—but allow sparse cross-loadings. Fit two to five latent factors by masked likelihood on 2018–2021 without outcome labels; select factor count/regularization using 2022 masked likelihood plus downstream Brier score. Then fit a penalized logistic outcome head on latent state at 48h, state changes, innovation magnitude, and static/common predictors; refit the chosen representation on 2018–2022 and recalibrate in 2023.

T1 is a learned phenotyping model, not proof of physiology. It can reveal whether a coupled transition (for example, worsening inflammatory state with stalled oncotic/renal recovery) carries information that B0's latest values discard. Report loadings, transition coefficients, posterior-state summaries, and stability across five seeds; do not assign biological labels unless signs and measures are stable.

**Why select T1 rather than a deep sequence network:** the training set has only 121 primary events through 2022. A transformer/GRU-D with supervised representation learning is deferred because its parameter count and seed instability would obscure the scientific comparison. Revisit only if the latent model shows stable order-dependent signal, an external cohort becomes available, or substantially more adjudicated events are obtained. A static gradient-boosted tree remains a nonprimary nonlinear control; it cannot answer whether temporal ordering matters.

## Falsification and controls

- **Order ablation:** within patient, preserve all values, latest 24–48h state, medication presence, and measurement timestamps, but permute the 0–24h value-to-time assignment within assay/domain. Repeat 100 times. A trajectory claim requires loss of at least half of T1's Brier gain.
- **Timing-only control:** counts, missingness, and timestamps without values. If it matches T1, the result supports observation practice rather than physiology.
- **Late-state nonlinear control:** gradient boosting on B0 features. T1 must beat this to show ordering rather than generic nonlinearity.
- **Source controls:** recompute outcomes separately from `Numbness and Tingling System` and `Medical Record Surgery`; report overlap within 24h and model contrasts for both. Primary direction must agree.
- **Era control:** report annual risk-set size, event rate, source coverage, assay coverage, and medication coverage. Refit a 2020–2023 development/calibration sensitivity and test 2024–2025. If source/coverage changes explain the gain or calibration cannot be restored, classify as inconclusive.
- **Component control:** test pleural-only and abdominal-only separately; neither is used to tune the primary model. Directional agreement is informative, not required when intervals are wide.
- **Negative controls:** permute labels within year×procedure source; shift outcome timestamps by +28 days within encounter where possible; assert chance performance and no pre-landmark feature leakage.
- **Treatment-indication control:** compare T1 with and without medication channels. A medication-associated gain cannot be called treatment benefit and may reflect clinician concern.
- **Missingness control:** complete-value subset and values-only model; compare with missingness/timing model. Do not claim biological trajectory if gain exists only through measurement intensity.

## Evaluation and decision rules

Primary estimand is the paired patient-level difference in locked-test Brier score, T1−B0. Also report log loss, AUROC, AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope, calibration plot, and decision curves at 1%, 2%, 3%, and 5% risk thresholds. Use 2,000 paired test-patient bootstrap replicates for frozen-model uncertainty and at least 200 end-to-end bootstrap refits as a model-fitting sensitivity. Report exact event counts and effective sample sizes. No significance-only interpretation.

**Supportive** requires all of:

1. T1−B0 Brier ≤−0.0010 with paired 95% CI below zero;
2. absolute AUPRC gain ≥0.02 with CI above zero;
3. T1 calibration slope 0.8–1.2 and intercept −0.20 to 0.20 after 2023-only recalibration;
4. order ablation removes ≥50% of the Brier gain and timing-only/late-state nonlinear controls do not explain it;
5. Brier direction agrees for anesthesia-only and case-record-only outcomes, with no catastrophic annual/source instability.

This would support prognostic value of an early coupled temporal phenotype for recorded invasive fluid rescue. It would not prove mechanism, clinical utility, or treatment benefit.

**Adverse** means event support and source stability are adequate, but T1 misses the effect-size/proper-score gates, the interval excludes the prespecified meaningful gain, or order ablation preserves the gain. Prefer B0 and abandon the temporal hypothesis for this endpoint.

**Inconclusive** means intervals span clinically important benefit and harm; calibration fails; event/source/assay drift dominates; latent factors are seed-unstable; source-specific directions reverse; or phenotype adjudication shows poor positive predictive value. Revise measurement or obtain adjudication rather than declaring support or absence.

Smallest next-decision check: before full fitting, have two hepatobiliary clinicians independently review a stratified sample of 100 deidentified event encounters (50 pleural, 30 abdominal, 20 source-discordant) and 50 non-events using procedure context and notes. Continue if positive predictive value for unplanned complication treatment is ≥0.80 and Cohen's κ≥0.70; revise the lexicon if 0.60–0.79; defer/abandon this endpoint if <0.60. This adjudication is essential and is not currently available.

## Evidence limits and checkability

Automatically checkable: source hashes/headers, joins, cohort/time boundaries, exact regex/lexicon, counts, temporal split, no post-landmark features, model inputs, predictions, metrics, intervals, ablations, source sensitivities, and whether reported statistical conclusions satisfy frozen rules.

Requires clinical adjudication or another study: whether each intervention was unplanned and complication-driven; whether pleural/abdominal collections share a mechanism; clinician intent; fluid balance and urine output; oxygen/respiratory status; imaging and drain output/chemistry; laboratory units/reference ranges and assay equivalence; outside-hospital rescue/death; causal effects of albumin/diuretics; prospective decision utility; and external transportability. Transfers and vitals are unavailable payloads. An automatic verifier must not certify these clinical claims.
