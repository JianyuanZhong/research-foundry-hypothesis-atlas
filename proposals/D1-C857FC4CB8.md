# Target-trial emulation: which vasopressor should be discontinued first after dual-agent shock begins to resolve?

## One clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

Among adults in MIMIC-IV 3.1 who have received concurrent norepinephrine and vasopressin for at least 6 hours and reach a clearly defined, hemodynamically stable weaning decision point, does a strategy of discontinuing vasopressin first, versus discontinuing norepinephrine first, change 48-hour hemodynamic relapse?

**Hypothesis to test:** vasopressin-first discontinuation increases the 48-hour risk of hemodynamic relapse (restart of the discontinued drug, escalation/addition of vasopressors, or sustained MAP below 65 mmHg) compared with norepinephrine-first discontinuation. This is a pragmatic care-process question: the two agents are commonly co-administered, but clinicians must choose an order when shock resolves, and relapse can prolong organ support and expose patients to recurrent hypoperfusion.

The primary estimand is the per-protocol 48-hour risk difference for the dynamic treatment strategy actually initiated at time zero, standardized to the eligible population. An intention-to-treat analogue based on first observed discontinuation will be reported as a secondary estimand.

## Evidence already supported versus unresolved

Supported evidence is deliberately modest. Current published observational literature indicates that weaning order is unsettled; a recent 100-patient single-center retrospective cohort reported associations favoring vasopressin-first for mortality/mechanical-ventilation duration, while summarizing earlier evidence as suggesting less clinically significant hypotension when norepinephrine is stopped first and no clear patient-outcome difference (Alshreef et al., 2026, PMID 41347381, PMCID PMC12825015, DOI 10.5414/CP204891). Those nonrandomized findings do not establish either strategy as causal.

The unresolved claim is whether, at a comparable point of improving shock, the order itself affects short-term relapse. MIMIC can test that association under an explicit target-trial design with dense treatment and MAP timing; it cannot by itself establish clinician intent, tissue perfusion, or transportability beyond one center.

For rigor rather than topic selection, I inspected the locally available full article extraction for *Learning the natural history of human disease with generative transformers*, especially its explicit temporal holdout/simulation-to-observed evaluation and baseline comparison (local `references/research-ambition/natural-history/article.readable.txt`, lines 742-759). This proposal similarly fixes time zero before outcome ascertainment and requires explicit baselines, but it is causal inference rather than prediction.

## Target trial protocol

### Eligibility and time zero

Construct drug-exposure episodes from ICU `inputevents` itemid 221906 (norepinephrine) and 222315 (vasopressin). Merge adjacent records for the same drug when the gap is at most 15 minutes, retaining valid positive-duration administrations and harmonizing plausible units/rates. Candidate decision times occur at the end of a continuous overlap of both drugs lasting at least 6 hours.

At each candidate time, require:

1. age at admission at least 18 years (computed from `patients.anchor_age`, `anchor_year` and admission year); ICU stay ongoing;
2. both drugs actively running during the preceding 2 hours, with no epinephrine, dopamine, phenylephrine, dobutamine or milrinone active at time zero;
3. improving/stable physiology: at least two MAP measurements in the prior 2 hours, latest MAP at least 65 mmHg, no MAP below 60 in the prior hour, and norepinephrine dose nonincreasing over the prior hour; require no increase in total norepinephrine-equivalent dose greater than 10% during that hour;
4. an observed separation: one study drug ceases and remains absent for at least 2 hours while the other continues. Time zero is the first drug's stop time. Stops within 2 hours are excluded from the primary trial as near-simultaneous, not forced into an arm;
5. time zero at least 48 hours before `icustays.outtime` unless death occurs first, to avoid outcome loss after ICU discharge; first eligible episode per hospital admission only.

Primary population is all qualifying shock episodes, because vasopressors plus stability define the decision. A prespecified specificity analysis additionally requires ICD-9 78552 or ICD-10 R65.21/T81.12* at discharge. This diagnosis is post-baseline and therefore must never be used in the primary treatment/confounder model; it is only a phenotype sensitivity analysis. Exclude comfort-measures-only/withdrawal decisions identified by prespecified discharge-note phrases in the 24 hours around time zero, but report results both with and without this NLP exclusion because discharge notes are retrospective summaries and phrase timing may be unreliable. Manual clinician review is required to validate the phrase list on a blinded sample.

### Strategies

* **Vasopressin-first:** cease vasopressin at time zero, continue norepinephrine for at least 2 hours, and do not initiate another vasopressor during that grace period.
* **Norepinephrine-first:** cease norepinephrine at time zero, continue vasopressin for at least 2 hours, and do not initiate another vasopressor during that grace period.

A drug is considered restarted after a gap greater than 15 minutes in the main definition. Sensitivity analyses use 5-, 30-, and 60-minute episode gaps and separation grace periods of 1 and 4 hours. Pauses, rewritten orders, implausible rates and overlapping duplicate bags require explicit cleaning; status descriptions are supportive but interval timing defines administration.

### Outcomes

**Primary: 48-hour hemodynamic relapse composite**, first occurrence after the 2-hour treatment grace period of:

* restart of the drug stopped first for at least 30 minutes;
* start of epinephrine (221289 or 229617), dopamine (221662), phenylephrine (221749/229630/229631/229632), or another vasopressor after baseline, or increase of the retained norepinephrine-equivalent dose by at least 0.1 micrograms/kg/min above its baseline value;
* sustained hypotension: MAP below 65 mmHg in at least two readings 15-120 minutes apart within any rolling 2-hour interval, or any MAP below 55 mmHg.

Death before relapse is a competing event and, in a safety composite, is counted as adverse. Report (i) relapse-or-death risk as primary, (ii) relapse with death as competing risk, and (iii) death separately. This prevents a strategy from appearing successful merely because a patient dies before restart.

Secondary outcomes: vasopressor-free alive hours through 48 hours; cumulative norepinephrine-equivalent dose through 48 hours; lactate change and clearance at 6/12/24 hours where measured; new CRRT initiation through 7 days; invasive-ventilation-free alive days through day 7; ICU and hospital mortality. Lab outcomes are secondary because measurement is clinically driven. A negative-control outcome is new positive microbiology result within 48 hours based on specimen collection time; a strong immediate association would suggest residual illness-severity/diagnostic-intensity confounding rather than a plausible effect of weaning order.

### Confounding control and analysis

The treatment decision is strongly confounded by severity, clinician preference and drug dose. Build a two-hour baseline history in 15-minute bins and a 24-hour summary using only measurements recorded before time zero: current and trajectories of MAP (arterial preferred; noninvasive fallback), heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO2, temperature, urine output, fluid input, lactate, creatinine, bilirubin, platelets, WBC, pH, PaO2/FiO2 where available; current/running doses and slopes of both study drugs and all other vasoactives; duration of shock and dual therapy; invasive ventilation, CRRT, sedatives, corticosteroids, fluids and antibiotics; ICU day/time, care unit/service, admission type, age, sex, race, comorbidity ICD history from prior admissions only, and calendar period. Include missingness indicators and measurement frequency because clinician monitoring intensity is informative. Do not use post-time-zero variables to predict treatment.

Estimate treatment propensity with cross-fitted gradient boosting and a prespecified logistic-regression baseline. Trim propensity outside 0.05-0.95; report overlap, effective sample size and standardized mean differences before/after weighting. Primary estimator is cross-fitted augmented inverse-probability weighting/TMLE for 48-hour risk difference and ratio, with patient-level bootstrap confidence intervals and clustering by `subject_id`. A weighted pooled-logistic discrete-time model estimates survival/competing-risk curves. If strategy deviations after the 2-hour grace period are analyzed per protocol, use inverse-probability-of-censoring weights updated every 2 hours from time-varying physiology; also report the simpler first-strategy ITT analogue. Compare against unadjusted, multivariable outcome-regression, overlap-weighted and propensity-matched baselines.

Prespecified sensitivity analyses: exact stable-MAP thresholds; no note-based exclusions; coded septic-shock subset; mechanically ventilated subset; no other vasopressor for 6 hours; dose strata at time zero; first ICU stay only; complete MAP ascertainment; E-values/tipping-point analysis for unmeasured confounding; falsification on pre-time-zero pseudo-outcomes (the assigned future arm must not predict hypotension during the prior 6 hours after conditioning). Assess positivity separately by ICU type and calendar period. No causal estimate is reported for strata with effective arm size below 100 or maximum weight above 20; those results are inconclusive.

### Falsification and interpretation

* **Supportive:** the adjusted vasopressin-first minus norepinephrine-first risk difference for relapse-or-death is above +5 percentage points, its 95% CI excludes 0, balance/positivity diagnostics pass, component outcomes point coherently toward more hypotension/restart, and results persist across gap/stability definitions without a negative-control signal. This supports a harmful short-term hemodynamic effect of stopping vasopressin first, not a mortality benefit from the opposite sequence.
* **Adverse/refuting:** the adjusted risk difference is below -5 points with CI excluding 0 and diagnostics/sensitivities pass. This refutes the directional hypothesis and supports lower short-term relapse with vasopressin-first. A coherent mortality or organ-support signal would remain exploratory.
* **Evidence for practical equivalence:** the entire 95% CI lies within -5 to +5 percentage points with adequate effective sample sizes and stable estimates.
* **Inconclusive:** CI crosses both -5 and +5; substantial residual imbalance, positivity failure, endpoint sensitivity, discordant relapse components, strong pseudo-outcome or negative-control associations, or excessive missing MAP. Inconclusive is not evidence of no effect.

A clinically meaningful result would motivate a protocolized randomized weaning trial. It would not establish long-term survival benefit, because MIMIC lacks random assignment, reliable treatment intent, clinician rationale, standardized lactate/MAP measurement, post-discharge functional outcomes and broad multicenter transportability.

## Verified feasibility

Full streaming of actual source rows found 459,800 norepinephrine rows and 37,163 vasopressin rows. After the prespecified simple interval construction, 5,032 ICU stays had any overlap and 3,934 had overlap for at least 6 hours. Requiring a 2-hour separation gave 1,363 norepinephrine-first and 1,117 vasopressin-first stays; 1,454 were near-simultaneous. Among 2,308 episodes also carrying a septic-shock discharge code, preliminary arms were 761 and 678 (plus 869 near-simultaneous). These are upper-bound feasibility counts before age, stability, dose/unit, competing-drug, follow-up and goals-of-care exclusions, and therefore are not claimed as final sample size. Exact sampling/filtering code and counts are in the support files.

## Claims requiring adjudication or another study

An automatic computation can verify interval construction, eligibility, arm assignment, measured covariate balance, outcome definitions, estimates and uncertainty. It cannot verify that cessation represented an intentional weaning decision rather than line failure, drug shortage, bedside artifact or transition to comfort care; blinded clinician review of sampled timelines/notes is essential. It also cannot prove no unmeasured confounding or make treatment recommendations. A multicenter pragmatic randomized trial is required before changing practice, and external data are needed for transportability.
