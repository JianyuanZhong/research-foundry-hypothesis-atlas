# Duration of hypotension and renal deterioration: an eICU transportability successor

## Decision target and hypothesis

The primary question is whether, among adults with the same observed minimum MAP and comparable pre-window clinical state, *duration* of MAP below 65 mmHg in the preceding six hours identifies more subsequent renal deterioration than minimum MAP alone, and whether that duration association is reproducible across hospitals.

The falsifiable hypothesis is: in first ICU stays with adequate MAP observation and a creatinine result available before and after a landmark, longer time-weighted exposure to MAP <65 mmHg is associated with higher 24-hour risk of a creatinine-defined renal deterioration event after adjustment for the observed minimum MAP, baseline physiology, pressor/support status and hospital-transported covariates. A secondary hypothesis is that adding duration improves calibration and standardized risk differences in held-out hospitals more consistently than adding the laboratory-testing process variables in the parent proposal.

This is an observational risk-marker hypothesis. It does not estimate the causal effect of allowing hypotension, of giving a pressor, or of targeting MAP 65; treatment is confounded by illness severity and clinician intent. The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that the expert seed is clinically plausible and that the configured eICU schema contains offset-based blood-pressure, creatinine, pressor and ICU-discharge fields. No eICU effect, AKI rate, or cross-hospital result has yet been established. The experiment tests the unresolved claim that a density-aware duration summary contains reproducible information beyond a minimum value.

This is a stronger consequential target than the parent’s principal “new documented pressor/ventilation record” composite because renal deterioration is closer to the organ injury clinicians seek to prevent and does not define deterioration by a documentation event. It remains weaker than adjudicated AKI: the available release lacks a guaranteed urine-output interpretation, baseline outpatient creatinine and clinical adjudication.

## Population and landmarks

Retain the first ICU stay per person: adult (\`age >=18\`; map \`> 89\` to 90 and report all other nonnumeric ages), \`unitvisitnumber=1\`, nonmissing hospital and numeric `unitdischargeoffset` (ICU-relative admission is offset 0), with at least 36 hours between unit admission and discharge. If multiple rows have \`unitvisitnumber=1\` for one \`uniquepid\`, keep the earliest valid admission offset and report the conflict rather than counting the person twice.

Candidate landmarks are every 6 hours at t = 12, 18, ..., 72 hours after ICU admission, retaining only t >= 6 hours and unit discharge at least 24 hours after t. Require at least 6 hours before t and at least 24 hours after t. Exclude a landmark with an EOL proxy offset <=t. The primary analysis uses all eligible landmarks with patient-clustered inference; one randomly selected eligible landmark per stay (fixed seed recorded in the manifest) is a sensitivity analysis. No predictor may use a row whose clinical/result timestamp is >t.

## Exposure construction

Primary MAP is valid numeric \`vitalPeriodic.systemicmean\` in [-50, 300] mmHg; retain the exact plausible-value rule and count exclusions. Sort observations within stay and use each valid observation to represent the following \`min(5, next_observation_offset - observation_offset)\` minutes, with no carry-forward over gaps and no interval beyond t. Thus the observation window is [t-360,t), covered minutes and low-MAP minutes are deterministic from the observed data. Require >=80% coverage (report 70% and 90% sensitivity thresholds). Define:

- \`low_map_minutes\`: covered minutes with systemicmean <65;
- \`low_map_fraction\`: low_map_minutes / covered minutes;
- \`minimum_map\`: minimum valid systemicmean in [t-360,t);
- \`low_map_episodes\`: contiguous below-65 runs separated by <=10 minutes of valid observations, reported descriptively; the primary estimand uses minutes, not episode count.

The primary contrast is standardized 24-hour risk for low-MAP burden >60 versus 0-60 minutes within minimum-MAP support strata (minimum MAP 55-64, 45-54, and <45 when each has both burden levels), with minimum MAP also modeled continuously using a prespecified restricted cubic spline. Report a continuous 60-minute burden contrast and prespecified categories 0, >0-60, >60-120 and >120 minutes as secondary analyses. This makes “same minimum BP” explicit rather than claiming that regression adjustment has eliminated confounding.

Repeat exposure construction using only \`vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean\`; a combined source rule (periodic first, aperiodic only when periodic is absent) is a sensitivity analysis. Repeat thresholds at MAP <60 and <70. Never infer true continuous hypotension from an unobserved gap.

## Outcome and estimand

The primary outcome is a **creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal** in (t,t+24h]: among landmarks with a valid pre-landmark creatinine baseline and at least one valid future creatinine, the first future value is >= baseline + 0.3 on the retained source scale or >=1.5 times baseline. Baseline is the last numeric creatinine with \`labresultoffset <=t\` in [t-48h,t] and \`labresultrevisedoffset <=t\]. If baseline is missing, outcome is missing—not negative. Require baseline >0 and apply a prespecified plausible range after examining the exact label/system/interface. If the source does not establish comparable units, report this as a feasibility failure/inconclusive rather than relabeling the endpoint as KDIGO AKI. A secondary outcome uses the last pre-t value rather than the first future value; do not count measurements after the first event as additional events.

This endpoint is intentionally not called adjudicated AKI: the release does not guarantee urine output semantics, pre-ICU baseline creatinine, or clinical diagnosis. Secondary outcomes are ICU death in (t,t+24h], discharge/transfer before 24h as a competing event, and documented new pressor record/ventilation start only as parent-comparator process outcomes. Do not use future creatinine as a predictor.

The primary estimand is the standardized 24-hour risk difference for >60 versus 0-60 low-MAP minutes, separately within minimum-MAP strata and pooled with a burden-by-minimum-MAP interaction, under the observed covariate distribution of eligible landmarks. Use patient-clustered, hospital-stratified bootstrap 95% intervals (resample patients within hospital) and report missing-outcome support. The transport estimand is the difference in calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, standardized risk difference and interaction reproducibility between random patient-level validation and each held-out hospital with at least 200 eligible landmarks and both exposure/outcome levels.

## Adjustment, baseline and learned alternative

The simple interpretable baseline is a regularized pooled logistic model for the 24-hour outcome with restricted cubic spline minimum MAP, low-MAP burden indicator/category, pre-window MAP mean/SD/coverage, last MAP, heart rate/respiration/SpO2 summaries and slopes, last available creatinine and its pre-window change, age, sex, ethnicity, time since admission, admission-only Apache fields/scores, current documented ventilation and pressor-record indicators, EOL exclusion, and missingness indicators. Hospital is a stratification variable, not a feature. A duration-free model (minimum MAP but no low-MAP minutes) is the baseline comparator; the duration-added model is the prespecified test of incremental scientific information. Standardize predictions to the common eligible landmark distribution.

The substantive alternative fits a small causal temporal-convolution or GRU over six preceding one-hour bins, using the same admission covariates and time-safe hourly summaries/masks of MAP, heart rate, respiration, SpO2, creatinine, pressor records and ventilation records. Fit duration-free \`T_min\` and full trajectory \`T_shape\` variants to the same outcome; \`T_shape\` can reveal brief-versus-sustained patterns, recovery slope and irregular gaps that a minimum-plus-total-minutes summary loses. Train only on patients from training hospitals; reserve a patient-level validation split within those hospitals for early stopping and hyperparameter choice; evaluate untouched hospitals. No hospital ID predictor is allowed. Primary uncertainty is hospital-stratified patient bootstrap; repeat three seeds for learned-model stability. Compare standardized risk differences, calibration, Brier score and decision curves at thresholds frozen before fitting; AUROC is secondary and never determines clinical value.

Compute budget under \`inputs.json\` (\`science_seconds=7200\`): two CPU cores, up to 8 GiB, approximately 20-40 minutes for streaming extraction/landmarks, <15 minutes per regularized model, and 45-90 minutes for both small temporal variants with early stopping. GPU is deferred because it does not answer a different scientific question and is not needed for this bounded sequence model. If support is too sparse for the endpoint or site holdout, stop at the feasibility gate; do not replace renal deterioration with an easier prediction target.

## Falsification and robustness

1. Require both exposure levels, outcome support and creatinine label/unit consistency before interpreting any estimate. Report eligible landmarks, MAP coverage, low-MAP categories, baseline/future creatinine support, event timing and all counts by hospital.
2. Repeat MAP thresholds (60, 65, 70), coverage (70/80/90%), burden cut points and source priority. A result present only under one arbitrary rule is weak.
3. Permute the order of pre-landmark MAP observations within stay/window while preserving MAP values, observation count and coverage. The duration-specific association should attenuate while minimum-MAP and coverage summaries remain comparable.
4. Use a pre-landmark placebo outcome: apply the same creatinine-rise rule in [t-24h,t) using only values whose timestamps precede t. A strong “future” duration association with an equally strong placebo association indicates reverse-time severity/documentation bias.
5. Exclude EOL-proxy landmarks, landmarks within 12 hours of discharge/transfer, and landmarks with abrupt loss of all source coverage; compare estimates.
6. Repeat with one landmark per stay, with patient-level rather than landmark-level splits, and with hospitals held out one at a time. Do not rank hospitals.
7. Report whether adjustment for documented pressor/ventilation status changes the estimate. Because infusionDrug lacks end times and medication/nutrition records are imperfect, this is sensitivity analysis, not causal adjustment for complete therapy.
8. Test the parent monitoring-gap exposure in the same eligible cohort as a prespecified comparator: non-overlapping [t-18h,t-6h) and [t-6h,t) core-lab counts, with the parent’s exact source-audited labels. Do not declare duration superior from AUROC alone; require better calibrated, site-reproducible standardized risk differences for the renal endpoint.

Supportive results are a positive burden contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero, persistence across source/threshold/coverage/time-placebo checks, and trajectory-model calibration or shape evidence that reproduces in held-out hospitals. This supports a transportable observational renal-risk marker and a prospective monitoring/organ-protection study; it does not support a MAP or pressor treatment target.

Adverse results are a near-zero/negative burden contrast with adequate precision, disappearance after time permutation or exclusion checks, a strong placebo association, or failure of the duration signal in held-out hospitals. This would argue that minimum MAP or documentation severity—not duration—carries the reproducible signal.

Inconclusive results include missing comparable creatinine units, too few paired baseline/future results, inadequate MAP coverage, no exposure contrast within minimum-MAP strata, label instability, unresolved open support intervals, or wide intervals that cannot distinguish the above. No clinical recommendation follows.

## Unavailable evidence and interpretation boundaries

Computationally checkable: source hashes/snapshot, exact row filters and joins, timestamp safety, MAP coverage and duration arithmetic, label normalization, paired-creatinine construction, cohort/event support, fitted parameters, standardized risks, uncertainty, calibration/Brier/decision metrics, split integrity, site holdouts and falsification outputs.

Not computationally established from this release: whether a low-MAP record reflects true perfusion, whether creatinine rises represent adjudicated KDIGO AKI, urine output and pre-ICU baseline creatinine, clinician intent, fluid/pressor dose and stop times, treatment limitation beyond the EOL proxy, lab-interface outages versus deliberate monitoring, staffing/bed capacity, and whether acting on a warning improves outcomes. These require clinical adjudication, richer treatment/measurement metadata or a prospective study.

## Scientific deliverable and alternatives not chosen

Completion requires a machine-readable manifest plus newly fitted duration-free and duration-added regularized models and \`T_min/T_shape\` models; exact source/label/unit and site-support audits; primary and secondary standardized risks with patient/hospital uncertainty; calibration/Brier/site-held-out outputs; comparison with the parent monitoring-gap feature set; and every falsification result. The declared deliverable is newly estimated duration-versus-minimum information, not a reproduction of any demonstration paper and not an input audit.

The parent monitoring-gap proposal remains a valid comparator because its testing-process estimand is clinically important and more mature, but its principal composite relies on documentation proxies. Duration of hypotension is selected as the primary successor because its exposure is directly computable from the dense MAP stream and its organ-injury endpoint is closer to a consequential decision. Revisit the parent as primary if paired creatinine support is inadequate or units cannot be harmonized.

Hyperoxia (expert seed eICU-04; candidate \`[prior hypothesis]\`) is deferred: eICU has admission \`apacheApsVar.fio2/pao2\`, \`respiratoryCare.setapneafio2\`, and flexible \`respiratoryCharting\` fields, but the source does not establish a complete time-varying FiO2/PaO2 pair with reliable units and pulmonary-status adjudication. It would require a bounded label audit and should not use SpO2 as a PaO2 substitute.

Hypoglycemia after insulin (expert seed eICU-09; candidate \`[prior hypothesis]\`) is deferred: \`medication\` supplies ordered/start/stop timestamps and \`infusionDrug\` supplies infusion records, while \`lab\` supplies glucose, but neither guarantees administration, dose continuity or nutrition interruption. \`intakeOutput\` labels cannot be interpreted as absence of nutrition. This is potentially valuable and should be revisited only with a verified administration/nutrition operational definition.

Other unused seeds (vasopressor strategy, ventilatory burden, nighttime discharge, transfer risk and recovery speed) remain hypotheses, not negative findings. They are deferred because their key causal/practice variables or adjudicated outcomes are less directly represented than MAP duration plus paired creatinine. Evidence that would justify revisiting them is a completed support audit showing adequate time-linked fields, event support and a non-documentation estimand.
