# Admission-anchored and transportability-tested HCC care-transition pathways

## Scientific deliverable and hypothesis

The new deliverable is a locked, admission-anchored competing-risk study that estimates a six-day recorded-care transition profile from the first eligible HCC-coded inpatient admission, while testing whether the early signal transports across calendar eras and admitting services. Completion requires:

1. a frozen first-observed adult HCC-coded encounter cohort, exact diagnosis and procedure dictionaries, and complete row/join/time audits;
2. a primary temporal design with laboratory predictors observed only in [0,24) hours after admission and liver-directed procedure events only in [72,144) hours, preserving the parent's 48-hour buffer;
3. an admission-anchored outcome that retains patients discharged before 72 hours as a competing event instead of conditioning them out;
4. separate embolization/interventional (E), ablation (A), resection (S), mixed/ambiguous (X), and early/late discharge outcomes when exact-string support permits;
5. a transparent pooled-logistic baseline and a substantive irregular latent trajectory model with partial pooling across prespecified service/era domains, fit on identical inputs and patient splits;
6. locked held-out predictions, absolute cumulative-incidence profiles, calibration, domain transportability, paired patient-bootstrap uncertainty, and all falsification outputs;
7. a claim-to-output table distinguishing computational checks from clinical adjudication and future-study requirements.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among adults with a first observed HCC-coded inpatient admission and no valid-start liver-directed therapeutic procedure in the first 72 hours, exact assay trajectories recorded in the first 24 hours are associated with the subsequently recorded six-day care transition profile—embolization/interventional, ablation, resection, or discharge—and the pathway-differential signal remains directionally coherent and calibrated across prespecified calendar-era and admitting-department strata.

The scientific contrast is not "which treatment should be chosen." It is the standardized difference in admission-anchored cumulative incidence at 144 hours between a prespecified worsening-versus-reference early reserve/stress profile for each recorded pathway, especially E versus S and E versus A, with D0 (discharge before 72 hours) and D1 (discharge in [72,144)) retained as competing events. The direction, size, and transportability are unknown and must be estimated. If exact A/S event support is inadequate after the dictionary is frozen, the predeclared primary coarsening is A+S=R, with A and S reported descriptively; this decision must be made from fit/selection data before locked-test evaluation.

This is more consequential than the parent's conditional E-versus-(resection/ablation) question because it estimates the full observed transition profile from admission, including early discharge, and asks whether any signal is portable across care settings and eras. It still cannot identify indication, resectability, treatment intent, treatment choice, benefit, or an appropriate policy.

The strongest available evidence supports only data feasibility and a temporal-validity repair. The source contains exact-pair-linked encounters, diagnoses, dated laboratory rows, and dated procedure rows. A bounded source audit found 105,044 encounter rows, 43,815 participant identifiers, no duplicate encounter keys, 3,604 encounter rows with invalid or unparsable admission/discharge timing, 338,040 procedure rows, 15,874 missing procedure starts, and 183,354 procedure starts at minute 00:00. The laboratory source was header-inspected but not fully row-counted in discovery because it is approximately 2.2 GB. These are source audits, not locked cohort or hypothesis results. The broad provisional diagnosis scan used for the audit is not the final HCC definition.

## Population, temporal boundaries, and outcome

### Index cohort

Use the exact patient-level partition policy in the catalog. For model development, use participant hash buckets 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for dictionary/feature/model selection, and 70–79 as a locked internal test. Buckets 80–99 are reserved and inaccessible; no external-validation claim is permitted.

1. Read `encounters` and `diagnoses`, joining only on the exact pair (`Patient Master Index`, `Encounter Number`). Normalize whitespace/encoding only. Require age `Age >= 18`, parseable native `Admission Time` and `Discharge Time`, `Discharge Time >= Admission Time`, admission in [2011-01-01, 2026-01-01), and a frozen lexical HCC diagnosis rule based on exact audited strings in `Diagnosis Name`. `Diagnosis Type` is retained descriptively, not treated as a gold-standard diagnosis.

2. Select the earliest eligible encounter per `patient master index`, ordered by native `admission time` then `encounter number`. Report invalid earliest encounters rather than silently replacing them with a later encounter. Report duplicate keys, diagnosis multiplicity, unmatched keys, earlier HCC-coded encounters, overlapping stays, and the distribution of `diagnosis type`.

3. Exclude an index encounter only when a procedure with a valid native `Start Time` is classified E/A/S/X and falls in [0,72) hours from native `Admission Time`. Missing or invalid procedure starts are not evidence of no procedure; report them and include a predeclared unknown-time sensitivity. This exclusion protects the late-event temporal estimand but is a selection limitation.

4. Do not require `discharge time >= admission time + 72h`. A valid discharge before 72 hours is retained as D0, preventing the parent’s still-inpatient-at-72 rule from conditioning the primary analysis on a later observed care decision. The index encounter remains the unit of analysis; later encounters are not silently joined as readmissions.

5. Primary predictors are valid numeric values in `labs` during [0,24) hours from `Admission time`. The exact candidate assay channels are `albumin`, `total bilirubin`, `creatinine`, `platelets`, and `prothrombin time`, after an exact-name audit. Use `quantitative result` for numeric values, retain `qualitative result` and `specimen type` for missingness and audit, and use native `test time`. There is no laboratory unit or reference-range column; do not pool channels as if they share units or call a derived state a validated organ-failure diagnosis. Values exactly at 24 hours are excluded. Report invalid dates/results, duplicate rows, exact-boundary rows, assay-name coverage, and measurement density.

6. Pre-admission covariates use the same exact assay channels in [admission−90 days, admission), with nearest value, recency, count, missingness, and within-channel slope only when supported. No post-admission order, medication, examination, document, pathology, discharge, length-of-stay, or procedure information is a predictor.

### First-event categories

For each retained index encounter, classify the first outcome in the following mutually exclusive sequence:

- E: exact frozen embolization/interventional therapeutic terms, including explicit hepatic-artery embolization, TACE/chemoembolization, and therapeutic hepatic-artery catheter intervention.
- A: exact liver-lesion ablation terms, including explicit radiofrequency, microwave, cryoablation, or percutaneous ethanol ablation.
- S: exact hepatic resection terms, including hepatic lobe/segment/partial resection.
- X: mixed or ambiguous liver-directed therapeutic strings that cannot be assigned defensibly. Same-time evidence from more than one family is X unless a deterministic composite rule is frozen before modelling.
- D0: native `discharge time` in [0,72) with no qualifying E/A/S/X procedure before discharge.
- D1: native `Discharge Time` in [72,144) before E/A/S/X.
- C: no E/A/S/X/D0/D1 by 144 hours, or administrative censoring at the valid encounter end when the source cannot establish continued observation.

The primary late procedure window is [72,144) from admission. A procedure is eligible only if native `Start Time <= Discharge Time`; `End Time` is descriptive/audit information and never defines onset. Procedures after discharge are ineligible. A missing discharge or procedure time is handled as unknown, not as a negative event.

The primary estimand is admission-anchored `CIF_k(144h)` for k in E, A, S, X, D0, and D1, with six-hour cause-specific pooled-logistic hazards and standardization over the competing process. D0 is the only event before 72 hours in the primary late-pathway design; E/A/S/X/D1 can occur in [72,144). The principal contrast is the vector of standardized absolute-risk differences between a prespecified early worsening-reserve profile and a reference profile, with E-versus-A, E-versus-S, and procedure-versus-discharge contrasts reported. The profile must be defined from fit/selection data, using within-assay quantiles or a clinically cautious standardized direction, and must not use locked-test outcomes. Report absolute risks and two-sided 95% intervals; no threshold is interpreted as a treatment decision rule.

A secondary landmark estimand reproduces the parent’s risk-set interpretation: among subjects without an early procedure who remain inpatient at 72h, estimate [72,144) CIFs. It is secondary because it is conditional on observed continuation. The parent’s 48-hour predictor/event design is a labelled bridge sensitivity only.

## Domain transportability design

The primary locked test is patient-level buckets 70–79. Before opening those outcomes, freeze four calendar eras (2011–2016, 2017–2020, 2021–2023, 2024–2025) and a service grouping based only on exact `Department of Visit`: liver tumor medicine, hepatobiliary/transplant surgery, interventional procedure room, digestive medicine, and an "other/unknown" group. Exact mapping and small-cell rules are frozen from fit/selection data. Do not call these independent hospitals or external cohorts.

The model is fit in buckets 0–59 and selected in 60–69. First report overall locked performance without recalibration. Then report, within each sufficiently supported era/service stratum, absolute CIF calibration, calibration intercept/slope, competing-risk Brier score, E/A/S (or R) discrimination, and the pathway contrast. The prespecified transportability estimand is the worst-stratum minus overall absolute calibration error and the range of standardized pathway contrasts across strata. No test-stratum refitting is allowed for the primary report.

A secondary domain-holdout analysis uses only buckets 0–59/60–69: leave one prespecified era or service domain out of fitting, tune only within the remaining development data, and evaluate in the held-out domain. This measures domain shift inside the available snapshot, not external validity. If a domain has inadequate events, report calibration/coverage and mark discrimination inconclusive rather than pooling it opportunistically.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

B0 is a transparent six-hour cause-specific pooled-logistic model for the primary event process, using age, sex, admitting department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, diagnosis-type descriptors, prior encounter/inpatient counts, prior local liver-directed procedure counts, and exact-assay pre-admission nearest value, recency, count, missingness, and supported slope. It includes no post-admission information.

B1 adds early [0,24) first value, last value, within-assay change, elapsed time, count, distinct timestamp count, missingness/availability, and supported slope for each exact assay. It uses predeclared service/era interactions only if selected in buckets 60–69. B1 is the transparent primary comparator and uses the same rows, target, split, competing outcomes, and uncertainty as the alternative.

The substantive alternative is a low-dimensional irregular hierarchical state-space competing-risk model:

- Inputs are the identical B0/B1 covariates plus every valid early numeric assay token, assay identity, qualitative-result/missingness indicators, specimen type, relative `test time - admission time`, and measurement density. No value from a later time window or any order/medication/document/procedure row is an input.
- A patient-level irregular latent state has separate assay-specific latent hepatic-reserve and renal-stress coordinates with assay-specific offsets/noise and patient shrinkage. It may use a monotone smooth trajectory or a continuous-time Gaussian-process/ODE approximation chosen in buckets 60–69, without treating numeric channels as commensurate. The state output includes level, slope, coupled movement, and posterior uncertainty.
- Cause-specific six-hour heads estimate E/A/S/X/D0/D1 hazards; hierarchical domain effects provide partial pooling of pathway intercepts/slopes by the frozen era/service groups. The hierarchical heads are trained only in buckets 0–59 and do not use locked-stratum outcomes.
- The fitted target is the observed competing-risk event process, not treatment assignment or a causal potential outcome. Evaluation is on held-out predictions, standardized CIFs, calibration, domain error, and uncertainty, not latent-state plausibility alone.

The alternative could reveal nonlinear ordering, irregular timing, coupled movement, and whether the physiology-to-recorded-pathway relationship varies by service/era—information B1 compresses into a few endpoint summaries and a pooled model. B1 remains scientifically necessary because a latent model that does not beat or clarify it has not demonstrated a substantive gain.

Use patient-level identical splits, identical endpoint definitions, and paired patient bootstrap (at least 500 resamples) for B1−B0 and alternative−B1 differences, CIF contrasts, and transportability errors. For each model report cause-specific hazard discrimination, competing-risk Brier/integrated Brier, 144-hour CIF calibration, calibration intercept/slope, and absolute pathway risks. Secondary AUROC/PR-AUC is descriptive only. Use shrinkage/regularization selected in 60–69; do not inspect locked-test outcomes for feature, dictionary, state dimension, or threshold choices.

## Method choice, compute, and deferred branches

The method choice is driven by the scientific question. The transparent baseline measures whether early assays add robust, auditable information. The irregular hierarchical state model is retained because the question explicitly concerns timing and transportability; it can expose observation-timing and domain heterogeneity that endpoint summaries lose. It is not selected because it is complex or GPU-based.

CPU-first is the measured/defensible starting plan for CSV aggregation, sparse pooled-logistic fits, CIF calculation, bootstrap, and a low-dimensional state model. The future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 256 GiB RAM, up to 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds; the discovery compute limit is separate. Full laboratory feature construction, state fitting, 500-bootstrap inference, and all domain strata were not benchmarked. If a GPU materially reduces repeated state fitting, request one allocated A100 and use `cuda:0`; GPU use is an implementation option, not evidence. Any neural GRU/transformer sequence model is deferred because the current hypothesis is about transparent timing and transportability and the latent model directly represents those quantities with more auditable outputs. Reconsidering it requires evidence that the latent model fails to represent ordering despite adequate convergence, and a new scientific child if the estimand changes.

Text/image multimodality is deferred: HCC images are not present, narrative examinations/documents lack validated temporal labels, pathology has no native time, and the full Oncoformer article/STAR Methods were unavailable in the local bundle. Orders and medications are deferred as predictors because their intent and indication are not validated; they may appear only in labelled leakage/concordance audits. A causal treatment-effect branch is deferred because no treatment assignment intent, eligibility, stage, resectability, mortality, outside-hospital capture, or adjudicated outcomes are available.

## Analysis, falsification, and interpretation

Required flow outputs are: all encounter rows; exact diagnosis match; adult/time filters; earliest-patient selection; early-procedure exclusion; D0 retention; lab parse/assay/boundary availability; exact E/A/S/X dictionary counts; first-event precedence; missing-time and midnight audits; split integrity; and domain-stratum support. Preserve row-level audits separately from modelling aggregates.

Predeclare:

1. Refit after adding/removing lab count, missingness, gaps, and measurement-density terms. A large attenuation indicates observation-process selection, not a physiologic pathway signal.
2. Permute lab timestamps within patient and assay while preserving values, assay identity, and missingness. A timing/trajectory claim should attenuate; persistence indicates value/density or a recording artifact.
3. Repeat with exact same-assay/same-timestamp aggregation and after duplicate-row exclusion.
4. Remove procedure starts at 00:00 and repeat the full late-window analysis. The primary should remain directionally similar; disappearance implicates recorded-time convention.
5. Leave one exact E/A/S/X procedure string out at a time and compare explicit composite-string rules. If conclusions depend on one string or forcing X into a family, the pathway claim fails.
6. Retain X as competing event; report X-excluded results only as sensitivity. Do not relabel mixed events to rescue support.
7. Compare admission-anchored D0-retaining results with the parent’s still-inpatient-at-72 secondary estimand. A large discrepancy is evidence of conditioning on observed continuation and must be reported, not averaged away.
8. Use central-line/venipuncture-only workflow terms, if a sufficient frozen negative-control endpoint exists, as a care-intensity negative control. Similar performance undermines a pathway-specific interpretation.
9. Use pre-admission laboratory timing as a negative timing control and verify that no primary token is outside [0,24).
10. Report era/service calibration and contrast heterogeneity; if the model's apparent gain is confined to one domain, label transportability failure.
11. Use early orders only in a labelled concordance/leakage audit. They may be correlated with recorded pathway, but cannot be called intent or entered in the primary model.
12. Compare the competing-risk process with a naive model that ignores D0/D1 and X as negative analysis; do not use it for the primary claim.

Supportive computational evidence requires a frozen coherent dictionary; adequate support for each claimed primary family; a nonzero pathway contrast with an interval that is not compatible with clinically meaningful null/opposite values as defined before the locked test; calibrated overall predictions; and no material collapse after measurement-process, timestamp, midnight, X, string, or negative-control checks. Transportability support additionally requires prespecified era/service strata with acceptable calibration and no severe contrast reversal. These results support prospective adjudication or a planning aid, not a recommendation to select E, A, or S.

Adverse evidence includes null or opposite contrasts, poor CIF calibration, no gain over B0/B1, disappearance after observation adjustment or midnight removal, persistence after timestamp permutation, negative-control equivalence, one-string dependence, D0-sensitive reversal, or severe era/service calibration drift. A negative result is scientifically valuable.

Inconclusive evidence includes inadequate event support, unresolved procedure semantics, high missing/unknown-time burden, large X fraction, insufficient early assay support, nonconvergence, unstable standardization, small domain cells, or intervals too wide to distinguish meaningful differences. Inconclusive is not support.

Computationally checkable claims include source/schema hashes, exact files and ordinary-file archive status, row counts, joins, filters, dictionaries, timestamps, split integrity, model convergence, predictions, CIFs, calibration, bootstrap intervals, and falsification outputs. The HCC snapshot cannot establish validated diagnosis, pathology confirmation, stage, tumor burden, vascular invasion, resectability, treatment intent, scheduling, multidisciplinary recommendation, procedure necessity, treatment benefit, toxicity, mortality, recurrence, progression, organ failure, AKI, transplant eligibility, outside-hospital events, or complete capture. These require clinical dictionary adjudication, acquisition-timestamped imaging/pathology, laboratory units/reference ranges, indication/scheduling data, mortality/transplant linkage, external validation, and prospective clinical or causal/implementation studies.
