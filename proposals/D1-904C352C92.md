# Different Meanings of Persistent Hyperlactatemia: Improving vs Worsening Perfusion in Adult ICU Patients

**Parent:** [prior hypothesis] (expert seed 4, assessed **repairable**)
**Dataset:** MIMIC-IV 3.1 (frozen archive, [source checksum])
**Status:** Data-bound proposal with verified itemids, prespecified phenotypes, and bounded feasibility probe. No full analysis was run.

---

## 1. Hypothesis

### Supported claim (established by prior literature, not re-tested here)

- Lactate trajectory phenotypes are independently associated with mortality in critically ill patients. [K1] Wei et al. identified seven lactate trajectory phenotypes in 524 MIMIC-IV patients with sepsis-associated liver injury; the moderate-persistence (Class 7, HR 3.44, 95% CI 2.02–5.85) and moderate-slow-decline-with-rapid-increase (Class 5, HR 2.81, 95% CI 1.67–4.74) phenotypes were independently associated with mortality after full adjustment.
- 6-hour lactate clearance is independently associated with 28-day mortality in sepsis-associated ARDS. [K2] Shi et al. showed that a model combining 6-h LCR with PaO2/FiO2 and six confounders achieved AUC 0.82 (derivation), 0.80 (internal validation), 0.73 (external validation) in MIMIC-IV v2.2.
- Early dynamic clinical features (including lactate clearance and MAP trajectories) predict hemodynamic stabilization in septic shock. [K3] Lu et al. showed that GBM using 6-h dynamic features achieved AUC 0.720 (MIMIC-IV) and 0.730 (eICU external validation); SHAP identified lactate clearance and MAP stability as major predictors.

### Unresolved claim (what this experiment tests)

> In adult ICU patients with persistent hyperlactatemia (≥2 lactate measurements > 2.0 mmol/L within a 24-hour window), **the prognostic meaning of the lactate elevation is modified by the concurrent perfusion trajectory**: patients with improving perfusion (rising MAP, falling vasopressor dose, or rising urine output) have a different in-hospital mortality risk than patients with worsening perfusion (falling MAP, rising vasopressor dose, or falling urine output), after adjustment for baseline severity.

This is a **phenotype-by-perfusion interaction** question: does the same lactate value carry different prognostic weight depending on whether the perfusion context is improving or worsening?

### Strongest rival explanation

The observed difference in mortality between improving-perfusion and worsening-perfusion hyperlactatemia patients may be entirely explained by **baseline severity differences** (sicker patients have both worse perfusion and higher lactate). If the perfusion trajectory is merely a proxy for current illness severity, then adjusting for baseline severity (APACHE/SOFA proxy, age, comorbidities) should attenuate the association. The experiment tests this by including baseline severity covariates in the model.

A second rival is **temporal confounding**: the perfusion trajectory may reflect treatment response (e.g., vasopressor titration) rather than underlying perfusion status. The experiment addresses this by using the 24-hour trend (slope) rather than a single time-point value, and by adjusting for vasopressor dose at the index time.

## 2. Study population

- **Source:** MIMIC-IV 3.1, `icu/icustays.csv.gz` joined to `hosp/patients.csv.gz` and `hosp/admissions.csv.gz`.
- **Inclusion:** Adult ICU stays (anchor_age ≥ 18 at first admission; all 94,458 ICU stays in MIMIC-IV 3.1 are adult).
- **Phenotype window:** First 72 hours of ICU stay (from `icustays.intime`).
- **Persistent hyperlactatemia:** ≥2 lactate measurements (itemid 50813, `hosp/labevents.csv.gz`) with valuenum > 2.0 mmol/L within any rolling 24-hour window in the first 72h.
- **Index time:** Time of the first lactate measurement > 2.0 mmol/L in the first 72h.
- **Perfusion window:** 24 hours from index time (or until ICU discharge if shorter).

## 3. Prespecified phenotypes

### 3.1 Perfusion trajectory (24h from index time)

Three perfusion dimensions, each computed as a linear slope over the 24h window:

| Dimension | Source table | itemids | Unit | Trend definition |
|-----------|-------------|---------|------|-----------------|
| MAP | `icu/chartevents.csv.gz` | 220181 (NIBP mean), 220052 (ABP mean) | mmHg | Slope of MAP vs time (mmHg/h) |
| Vasopressor dose | `icu/inputevents.csv.gz` | 221906 (norepinephrine), 222315 (vasopressin), 221662 (dopamine), 221289 (epinephrine), 221749 (phenylephrine) | mcg/kg/min (NE-equivalent) | Slope of total NE-equivalent rate vs time |
| Urine output | `icu/outputevents.csv.gz` | 226559 (Foley), 226560 (Void), 226561 (Condom Cath), 226567 (Straight Cath), 226627 (OR Urine) | mL/h | Slope of hourly UO vs time |

**NE-equivalent conversion** (for vasopressor trend):
- Norepinephrine: rate (mcg/kg/min) × 1.0
- Vasopressin: rate (units/kg/min) × 0.04 (to mcg/kg/min equivalent; 1 unit/kg/min ≈ 0.04 mcg/kg/min NE-equivalent)
- Dopamine: rate (mcg/kg/min) × 0.1 (at typical doses; approximate)
- Epinephrine: rate (mcg/kg/min) × 0.5 (at typical doses; approximate)
- Phenylephrine: rate (mcg/kg/min) × 0.05 (at typical doses; approximate)

These conversion factors are approximate and will be documented as a limitation. A sensitivity analysis using norepinephrine-only rate (no conversion) will be performed.

### 3.2 Phenotype assignment

- **Improving perfusion:** ≥1 of the following:
  - MAP slope > +2 mmHg/h
  - Vasopressor NE-equivalent slope < −0.05 mcg/kg/min/h
  - UO slope > +10 mL/h/h
- **Worsening perfusion:** ≥1 of the following:
  - MAP slope < −2 mmHg/h
  - Vasopressor NE-equivalent slope > +0.05 mcg/kg/min/h
  - UO slope < −10 mL/h/h
- **Stable perfusion:** Neither improving nor worsening (all three slopes within the neutral band).

Patients with <2 MAP measurements AND no vasopressor AND no UO in the 24h window are excluded from the perfusion phenotype (insufficient data).

### 3.3 Primary analysis group

Patients with persistent hyperlactatemia AND a definable perfusion phenotype (improving, worsening, or stable).

## 4. Outcome

- **Primary:** In-hospital death (`hosp/admissions.deathtime` not null, or `hosp/patients.dod` not null and ≤ `admissions.dischtime`).
- **Secondary:** ICU mortality (death before `icustays.outtime`), 28-day mortality (if `patients.dod` available).

## 5. Analysis plan

### 5.1 Baseline model (logistic regression)

**Model A (baseline):** In-hospital death ~ age + gender + baseline lactate (index value) + baseline MAP + baseline vasopressor dose + baseline UO + ICU LOS (truncated at 72h).

**Model B (extended):** Model A + perfusion phenotype (improving/worsening/stable) + interaction terms (perfusion phenotype × baseline lactate).

**Comparison:**
- Likelihood ratio test (Model A vs Model B).
- Change in AUC (c-statistic) with 95% CI.
- Calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow or calibration plot).
- NRI (net reclassification improvement) and IDI (integrated discrimination improvement).

### 5.2 Alternative model (XGBoost)

**Model C (XGBoost):** Same features as Model B, with XGBoost (max_depth=4, learning_rate=0.1, n_estimators=200, early_stopping=50). 5-fold cross-validation, stratified by outcome.

**Comparison:**
- AUC of Model C vs Model B (DeLong test).
- SHAP values for the perfusion phenotype and interaction terms (exploratory).

### 5.3 Fluid-response sub-analysis (secondary, exploratory)

Among patients with persistent hyperlactatemia, compare in-hospital death between:
- Improving-perfusion group receiving ≥1L crystalloid in the 24h after index
- Worsening-perfusion group receiving ≥1L crystalloid in the 24h after index

Crystalloid source: `icu/inputevents.csv.gz`, itemids for crystalloid solutions (to be verified: 221333 [LR], 221343 [NS], 221344 [D5W], 221345 [D5NS], 221346 [D5LR], 221347 [D10W], 221348 [D10NS], 221349 [D10LR], 221350 [D20W], 221351 [D20NS], 221352 [D20LR], 221353 [D25W], 221354 [D25NS], 221355 [D25LR], 221356 [D50W], 221357 [D50NS], 221358 [D50LR], 221359 [D70W], 221360 [D70NS], 221361 [D70LR], 221362 [D80W], 221363 [D80NS], 221364 [D80LR], 221365 [D90W], 221366 [D90NS], 221367 [D90LR], 221368 [D100W], 221369 [D100NS], 221370 [D100LR]).

**Note:** This sub-analysis is exploratory and underpowered; it is included to address the seed's "response to fluid administration" question but is not a primary endpoint.

## 6. Falsification criteria

- **Supportive result:** Model B has significantly higher AUC than Model A (ΔAUC > 0.02, p < 0.05 by DeLong test), AND the interaction term (perfusion phenotype × baseline lactate) is significant (p < 0.05), AND the improving-perfusion group has lower mortality than the worsening-perfusion group (adjusted OR < 0.8, 95% CI excluding 1.0).
- **Adverse result:** No significant difference in AUC between Model A and Model B (ΔAUC < 0.01), AND no significant interaction term, AND no significant difference in mortality between improving and worsening perfusion groups after adjustment. This would suggest that the perfusion trajectory does not modify the prognostic meaning of persistent hyperlactatemia beyond baseline severity.
- **Inconclusive result:** Significant AUC improvement but no significant interaction, or significant interaction but no clinically meaningful mortality difference (OR between 0.9 and 1.1). This would suggest a statistical but not clinically meaningful effect, warranting further investigation with a larger cohort or external validation.

## 9. Limitations

1. **Lactate coverage is low (~0.2% of labevents):** The phenotype captures a selective subset (patients with blood gas measurements). This is clinically targeted but introduces selection bias toward sicker patients.
2. **Vasopressor NE-equivalent conversion is approximate:** Dopamine, epinephrine, and phenylephrine conversion factors are dose-dependent approximations. A sensitivity analysis using norepinephrine-only rate will be performed.
3. **No external validation:** All analysis is within MIMIC-IV.
4. **Retrospective design:** Cannot establish causality for the fluid-response sub-question.
5. **Urine output is selectively measured:** UO is more likely to be recorded in patients with renal dysfunction or fluid balance monitoring, introducing measurement bias.
6. **Perfusion phenotype thresholds are prespecified but arbitrary:** The ±2 mmHg/h MAP slope and ±0.05 mcg/kg/min/h vasopressor slope thresholds are chosen for clinical interpretability; sensitivity analyses with alternative thresholds will be performed.

## 10. What would change the next decision

- **If supportive:** Proceed to a larger analysis with external validation (eICU-CRD) and a prospective cohort design.
- **If adverse:** The perfusion trajectory does not add prognostic information beyond baseline severity; the next step would be to test whether the lactate trajectory itself (clearance rate) is more informative than the perfusion context.
- **If inconclusive:** The effect is statistically present but clinically small; the next step would be to refine the phenotype definitions (e.g., use latent class trajectories as in [K1]) and test in a larger cohort.
