# Catabolic co-trajectory (concurrent ApoB and albumin decline) as the discriminating moderator of the HbA1c-rise-with-weight-loss prodromal pancreatic-cancer signal

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (episode 2; itself child of the episode-1 leader [prior hypothesis], expert seed UKB-40). Episode 5; this design was drafted in episode 4 (recovered checkpoint balanced-draft:[internal reference]:lead:ukb40-pdac-catabolism) which failed on a runtime timeout before publication.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The parent separated an inflammation-linked from a non-inflammation-linked weight-loss trajectory with a CRP moderator, but stated its own limit: CRP "distinguishes inflammation-linked from non-inflammation-linked weight loss, not tumor from non-tumor causes." Non-tumor inflammation (chronic infection, autoimmune disease, smoking-related inflammation) elevates CRP and can cause weight loss, mimicking the prodromal signature. The consequential unresolved uncertainty is therefore **whether the glycemic-rise-with-weight-loss prodrome behaves like cachexia-like host-reserve depletion — a multi-analyte catabolic state — or like intentional/metabolic weight change**. The two rivals make divergent, observable predictions about non-glycemic analytes measured at both assessments:

1. **Occult-tumor catabolism**: concurrent decline of ApoB AND albumin alongside weight loss (negative protein/lipoprotein balance), with the cancer excess concentrated in that catabolic stratum, short latency, and digestive-site specificity.
2. **Intentional/metabolic weight loss**: stable-or-improving ApoB/albumin with weight loss; any risk tracks baseline BMI; no digestive-site gradient.

Resolution matters clinically: a multi-analyte catabolic signature would define an enrichment phenotype for early-detection trials (and distinguish it from the common intentional-weight-loss phenotype that should NOT trigger alarm), refining the prodromal marker beyond what [K1] established with recalled weight change and what [K3] quantified as the baseline obesity-metabolic pathway. [K2] shows EHR-codable static factors bound current risk-stratification knowledge; co-trajectories are unmapped.

## Hypothesis (falsifiable)

Among the paired-assessment cohort with HbA1c rise >= +5 mmol/mol and >=3% weight loss (the parent's exposure), those with a concurrent composite catabolic decline (ApoB <= -0.10 g/L AND albumin <= -2.0 g/L between instances) have a higher short-latency (0-3 y) risk of digestive-tract cancers than weight-losers without the catabolic decline; the intentional/metabolic rival predicts no such concentration (excess, if any, among non-decliners), risk tracking baseline BMI, and no site gradient.

## Design (Harbor experiment, CPU-only)

- **Population / index / follow-up / exclusions**: unchanged from parent — paired-assessment cohort (n=12,818), index = visit-2 date (`53-1.0`, the trajectory-ascertainment date, as in the parent's executed probe), follow-up to first outcome cancer of the group under analysis, death, or administrative end; exclude pre-index pancreatic cancer and insulin/sulfonylurea users (20003 codes); metformin-only sensitivity retained.
- **Exposure**: parent's dHbA1c / dWeight% grid (>= +5 mmol/mol; <= -3%) plus continuous splines.
- **New pre-specified moderator (mechanism-level)**: composite catabolic co-trajectory = ApoB decline <= -0.10 g/L AND albumin decline <= -2.0 g/L; single-analyte versions secondary; continuous-spline sensitivity. CRP-rise moderator (>= +1 mg/L) retained and jointly reported: the four-way signature (CRP rise + ApoB decline + albumin decline + weight loss) is the full cachexia-like phenotype (38 cohort-wide; 7 in the key cell).
- **Outcomes**: parent hierarchy unchanged — primary incident pancreatic cancer (C25) 0-3 y vs >3 y windows; site-resolved panel (other-digestive 182 events; lung/breast/prostate comparators; 1,595 non-pancreatic any).
- **Primary analysis**: Firth-penalized cause-specific Cox, parent covariates (age, sex, baseline BMI, smoking, baseline HbA1c, interval) + baseline ApoB, baseline albumin, statin-use flag; interaction weight-loss-contrast x catabolic-signature among HbA1c risers; Fine-Gray sensitivity for competing risks.
- **Baseline (simple)**: exact Poisson incidence rate ratios per exposure cell x catabolic stratum per site group — assumption-light, discards timing/moderation.
- **Substantive alternative (chosen)**: penalized Cox with spline trajectories + moderator interactions. What the baseline loses: latency structure, dose-response shape, and the moderator itself. Neural models not scientifically useful at 35 primary events (documented; not banned) — no uncertainty here that deep models resolve better than penalized splines.
- **Resource budget**: CPU only; probes ran in 10-11 s; full analysis well under 30 min solver compute.

## Falsification criteria (power-aware, pre-specified)

- **Supportive**: weight-loss-associated digestive-cancer excess (including pancreatic) concentrated in the composite-catabolic stratum — interaction direction consistent, digestive-site HR elevated with non-digestive weaker, 0-3 y window — with or without a precise pancreatic-specific CI (the moderator is precision-bounded: 466 composite-stratum members, 14 in the key exposure cell).
- **Adverse (metabolic/intentional rival supported)**: weight-loss excess absent at all sites, OR present but NOT concentrated in catabolic decliners (equal-or-stronger among non-decliners), with risk tracking baseline BMI — this would drop the catabolic-prodrome interpretation and support treating rising HbA1c with weight loss as ordinary metabolic progression unless it carries the catabolic signature.
- **Inconclusive**: strata CIs overlap without excluding clinically meaningful concentration; or site-specificity direction inconsistent.
- **Pre-stated interpretation limits**: an association surviving adjustment is not a mechanism; the composite signature distinguishes cachexia-like multi-analyte decline from intentional weight change, but non-tumor catabolism (malnutrition, liver disease, chronic illness) can produce the same signature — bounded by the digestive-site-specificity prediction and by covariates, not eliminated. Stronger conclusions need external repeat-assessment replication, prescribing linkage, registry adjudication.

## Competing explanations and handling

Parent's set retained (intentional weight loss, glucose-lowering treatment, other cachexia, healthy-volunteer repeat subset, registry completeness). Specific to this extension: **statin initiation between visits** produces ApoB decline without catabolism — handled by statin-code sensitivity (exclusion/adjustment; code 1140861958 with 279 atorvastatin users identified in episode-4 probe) AND by the composite requiring concurrent albumin decline, which statins do not cause; **non-tumor catabolism** (malnutrition, alcohol, liver disease, chronic infection) — bounded by site-specificity and by albumin/ApoB being hepatic-synthesis markers whose decline with weight loss specifically indicates negative protein balance rather than acute inflammation; **reverse causation of measurement** (illness prompting assessment attendance) — bounded by the site-resolved hierarchy.
