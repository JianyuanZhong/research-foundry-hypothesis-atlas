# Proposal: Smoking cessation between dated assessments and near-term cardiovascular hospitalization

## Question and clinical importance

Stopping smoking is standard preventive care, but observational cohorts can show a transiently higher event rate among recent quitters because illness prompts cessation (the “sick-quitter” problem). The unresolved, falsifiable UK Biobank question is whether participants whose validated smoking-status code changes from current smoking at baseline to former smoking at a later dated assessment have lower cardiovascular-event incidence after a reverse-causation washout than participants who remain current smokers. Resolving the timing pattern matters for counseling and for avoiding a misleading interpretation of early post-cessation events.

## Evidence and claim boundary

The inspected snapshot contains repeated smoking-status values (`20116-*`), explicit assessment dates (`53-*`), measured cardiometabolic covariates, hospital ICD-10/date arrays (`41270-*`, `41280-*`), death dates, sex, and age. A deterministic prefix audit found high baseline completeness and repeat observations, but it did not establish coding meanings or population prevalence.

Hypothesis: among baseline current smokers with a later dated assessment, a transition to former smoker is associated with lower standardized 5-year incidence of first major cardiovascular hospitalization beginning 365 days after the later assessment than continued current smoking. The early 0–364-day interval is examined separately for sick-quitter bias.

This is not a causal effect of a cessation intervention: cessation date, intensity, treatment, adherence, symptoms, and time-varying confounding are unavailable. Benefit of a specific cessation program requires another study.

- Exposure: baseline current -> former at index (“observed cessation transition”). Comparator: current -> current.
- Exclude current -> never or uninterpretable transitions; report them as data-quality findings. Do not infer when cessation occurred between assessments.

## Outcomes and estimand

Primary outcome: first post-washout major cardiovascular hospitalization, defined by a frozen ICD-10 list comprising acute myocardial infarction (I21–I22), ischemic stroke (I63), and heart failure (I50), from day 365 through day 2,190 after index. For each array position k, pair `41270-0.k` with `41280-0.k`; take the earliest qualifying date. Death is a competing event.

Primary estimand: standardized 5-year risk difference and risk ratio for observed cessation transition versus continued smoking among eligible repeat attendees, conditional on surviving event-free through day 365. This landmark estimand must not be described as the total effect from the time of quitting.

Secondary outcomes: individual cardiovascular components and all-cause death. Diagnostic early outcome: the same composite during days 0–364.

## Analysis

- Report complete cohort flow, field missingness, date-order violations, impossible transitions, unmatched code/date positions, and administrative coverage.
- Adjust for age, sex, baseline BMI, baseline mean systolic/diastolic BP (mean of validated replicate values), elapsed time, and baseline calendar year. A second model adds index BMI and BP but is labeled potentially mediator-adjusted.
- Model repeat-assessment participation in the full eligible baseline-current-smoker cohort with validated baseline covariates; use stabilized inverse-probability-of-observation weights, 1st/99th percentile truncation, positivity and effective-sample-size reports. This cannot address unmeasured selection.
- At the day-365 landmark, estimate weighted Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence with death competing and standardized pooled-logistic risks with flexible time. Use 1,000 participant bootstraps for 95% CIs. Complement with cause-specific Cox estimates; check proportional hazards.
- Sensitivities: washouts of 180, 365, and 730 days; restrict baseline-to-index interval to 2–6 years; weighted/unweighted and complete/imputed analyses; require smoking transition consistency at a third assessment where available; sex-stratified estimates; exclude events in first 730 days.

## Falsification checks

1. **Sick-quitter timing test:** compare interval-specific associations for days 0–179, 180–364, 365–730, and 731–2,190. A harmful early association that attenuates or reverses after washout is compatible with illness-prompted cessation; persistent harm after long washout contradicts the protective hypothesis.
2. **Future-transition control:** among participants with a third dated smoking measure, a cessation transition occurring only after index must not predict events before that later date. Association indicates temporal/selection bias; future status is never used in the primary model.
3. **Impossible-transition audit:** excess former/current-to-never transitions or code/date mismatches triggers inconclusive status until coding is resolved.
4. **Expected-direction secondary pattern:** lower longer-term respiratory hospitalization may be explored only with a predeclared ICD list; it is supportive pattern evidence, not validation.

## Result-dependent interpretation

Supportive: after the 365-day landmark, the standardized cardiovascular risk difference is negative with 95% CI excluding zero; direction persists after a 730-day washout and weighting; early hazards are no more favorable than late hazards; and the future-transition control is near null. Conclusion: an observed current-to-former transition marks lower subsequent cardiovascular incidence among selected repeat attendees. Do not claim an intervention effect.

Adverse: the post-washout risk difference is positive with its 95% CI excluding zero, remains so after 730 days, and is not explained by evident coding/negative-control failure. This contradicts the proposed protective association in this cohort, but does not overturn randomized evidence about cessation.

Inconclusive: the 95% CI includes both zero and a prespecified clinically interpretable -1 percentage-point 5-year difference; positivity or effective sample size is poor; metadata/pairing/coverage is unverifiable; conclusions change materially across washouts; or the future-transition control is non-null. The -1 point benchmark is not a clinical policy threshold.

## Verification and unavailable evidence

Computationally checkable: headers/hashes, `eid` joins, validated code mapping, chronological repeat selection, transition logic, exclusions, paired outcomes, washouts, cohort flow, models, uncertainty, and conclusion-rule consistency.

Requires expert review or another study: whether reported cessation is accurate and sustained, exact cessation date and intent, illness prompting cessation, event adjudication beyond administrative coding, causal effects, intervention choice, and transportability. Clinical guidance cannot be inferred from automated QA.
