# Cumulative hypotension duration beyond nadir as a predictor of incident creatinine-defined AKI in eICU

Status: proposed Harbor experiment; no full study, model fit, or result has been computed. Episode-3 substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. Adaptation of expert seed eicu-03, not reproduction of a demonstration paper.

## Scientific opening, hypothesis and advance

The expert seed asks whether, at the same minimum blood pressure, longer-lasting hypotension precedes more AKI. Inspected evidence supports narrower premises: temporal hypotension patterns based on duration and recurrence can be prognostic in a single-center perioperative cohort [K1]; a clinical AKI guideline bounds endpoint definition [K2]; and a randomized blood-pressure-target trial makes clear why observational hypotension associations must not be presented as treatment effects [K3]. None answers whether a simple duration summary is transportable after accounting for measurement process, current severity and recorded treatment.

H1: among adult first eICU ICU stays with adequate early BP and kidney ascertainment, cumulative covered minutes with systemic MAP below 65 mmHg in ICU minutes 0-360 adds reproducible prognostic information for a new creatinine-defined event in minutes 360-2880 beyond nadir MAP, pre-index severity and observation coverage, including evaluation on hospitals not used for fitting.

The leading explanation is persistent hemodynamic stress. Strong rivals are current severity, care intensity/measurement density, and treatment response: sicker patients may be measured more often, receive pressors, have lower nadirs and have different creatinine ascertainment. A residual association would be descriptive/prognostic only; it would not establish renal hypoperfusion, a physiologic threshold, causality, hospital quality, or benefit from raising MAP. The advance is a data-bound test that can separate a portable duration summary from process rivals and richer temporal information, rather than another association plot or treatment claim.

## Exposure and outcome

Construct common 5-minute bins over 0-360. Prefer valid systemicmean, otherwise noninvasivemean; do not interpolate across gaps over 10 minutes or treat absent measurements as normal. Primary exposure is covered minutes with MAP below 65. Preserve low-time fraction, nadir, area below threshold, episode count, first-low offset, recovery slope, covered count, median gap, modality mix and missingness. Sensitivities use MAP ranges 30-180, thresholds 60/70 and common-density thinning.

Primary target is a creatinine-only incident event in 360-2880. Baseline is the last valid eligible serum-creatinine result in -24-0. Audit normalized labname, labmeasurenamesystem and labmeasurenameinterface; accept only numeric labresult values in a frozen serum-creatinine vocabulary, deduplicating same clinical offsets by latest labresultrevisedoffset. First post-window result at least 0.3 mg/dL above baseline defines the event. Because lab has no units column, there is no unverified conversion; if scale/vocabulary cannot be made reproducible, stop the primary fit and report label-readiness failure. Do not parse labresulttext, substitute BUN, or silently use apacheApsVar.creatinine. A 1.5-times-baseline endpoint over 7 ICU days is a distinct sensitivity. Do not call the primary label full KDIGO because urine semantics are not validated.

A no-event label requires valid baseline, alive/in ICU at 360, and either a valid creatinine at or after 2880 or an observed competing/censoring endpoint. Early discharge/transfer without qualifying lab is right-censored, not negative. Death before 2880 without an event is a competing event. Dialysis is not a primary label; an interpretable intakeOutput dialysis row may be a separate support/competing-event sensitivity, while apacheApsVar.dialysis alone cannot establish timing. EOL fields are process covariates/sensitivity flags, not intent. Fit cause-specific/discrete-time and inverse-probability-of-observation sensitivities, reporting complete-follow-up and competing-event counts.

After freezing the primary analysis, define a pre-index negative control among stays with valid creatinine in -48--24 and a distinct result in -24-0, using the same 0.3 mg/dL rise rule. A stronger pre-index duration association is adverse evidence for baseline severity/measurement process; it is not a causal claim.

## Baseline, alternative and analysis

B0 is regularized logistic/discrete-time cause-specific regression using the same target and split: pre-index creatinine, nadir MAP, initial MAP/HR/respiration, demographics, hospital/ICU type, BP modality/missingness and pre-index lab coverage. B1 adds duration, low-time fraction, episodes and recovery with a restricted spline and fixed-nadir bands. Primary estimands are held-out incremental log loss, Brier score and calibration; AUROC/AUPRC are secondary. Use whole-hospital 60/20/20 train/validation/test, keeping each uniquepid in one split; if hospital count makes this unstable, use repeated grouped-hospital cross-validation and state the limitation. Use hospital-clustered bootstrap intervals.

M is a small GRU or temporal convolution over the same 72 bins, with MAP, HR, respiration, modality, time-since-last-observation, masks, recorded pressor indicators/rates and pre-index severity. Fit only on training hospitals, early-stop/calibrate on validation hospitals and evaluate once on held-out hospitals. M can reveal persistence, oscillation, recovery timing and BP-treatment coupling that a scalar duration/nadir loses; B1 provides the transparent clinical summary. Compare treatment-masked, coverage-only, treatment-inclusive and common-density variants. Treatment-inclusive results remain observational and cannot identify a pressor effect.

Tabular fits use CPU. GPU is optional only if a bounded parsing/sequence-fit measurement justifies it; inside an allocated job use cuda:0 and do not infer GPU absence from ordinary shell. Future planning envelope is 16 CPU, up to 8 GPU, 262144 MiB and 28800 seconds; these are solver limits, not measured discovery results. The deliverable is newly fitted B0/B1/M plus cohort flow, label audit, held-out predictions, calibration/uncertainty, fixed-nadir curves, hospital heterogeneity, ablations, negative-control output and resource logs.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support requires label readiness, held-out B1 improvement over B0 with uncertainty excluding no improvement, stable calibration, monotone duration-risk within fixed-nadir bands, persistence under process/treatment/common-density checks, no material pre-index negative-control signal, and reproducible M trajectory information across hospital holdout. This supports a transportable prognostic summary only.

Adverse results are null/non-monotone duration, low-density-only gain, a strong mask-only or pre-index-control gain, disappearance after recorded-treatment/process checks, hospital-specific behavior, or label failure. These favor severity, selection or measurement explanations and argue against a robust duration signal.

Inconclusive results are wide intervals, inadequate creatinine coverage, exposure-dependent censoring, too few hospitals, unstable holdout, or unresolved vocabulary/scale semantics. They do not refute H1; they require clinical label adjudication, external validation or a narrower BP-process study. Clinical adjudication is essential for creatinine vocabulary/units, urine/dialysis semantics, EOL interpretation and BP modality; causal and treatment conclusions require another study.

## Limits

Available data lack clinician-adjudicated AKI, reliable pre-ICU kidney function, verified lab units, complete fluid balance, calibrated raw BP waveforms, renal replacement intent, staffing/bed context and independent external validation. An automatic verifier can check source binding, leakage control, fitting, uncertainty and whether conclusions match outputs; it cannot establish mechanism, causality, clinical benefit or a treatment target.
