# Provisional eICU monitoring-gap hypothesis

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a continuous eICU stay who are physiologically unstable at a prespecified index time, a reduction in laboratory-monitoring intensity during the immediately preceding 12 hours may be associated with a higher risk of a time-local clinical deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond measured current severity and recent support.

This is a provisional association hypothesis, not a causal claim. The key rival is that reduced testing reflects end-of-life/de-escalation, discharge/transfer, or sparse documentation rather than a monitoring-process signal. The design must distinguish these explanations where the available data permit it.

## What is supported and what remains untested

The eICU catalog identifies longitudinal timestamped tables and the within-dataset join key `patientunitstayid`, with `hospitalid` available for hospital-level stratification. The catalog also identifies candidate laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, care-plan, discharge, and mortality fields. These metadata facts support feasibility of a bounded study; they do not establish that testing reduction predicts deterioration, that deterioration is clinically adjudicated, or that a causal effect exists.

No cohort analysis has been run in this checkpoint. The exact source headers and representative rows still require inspection before execution. The parent proposal is the substantive prior design, but its source-row audit and three inspected key-reference receipts are incomplete.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use adult `patient` stays with a valid `unitdischargeoffset` greater than 18 hours and a valid unit interval. Restrict the primary cohort to continuous ICU stays with no transfer or early discharge before the outcome window. Index at hourly times from 6 through `unitdischargeoffset - 12` hours. Require at least one valid vital observation and one laboratory observation in the preceding 24 hours, while retaining an explicit indicator for missing documentation rather than treating missingness as zero testing.

The primary exposure is a testing reduction in (t-12,t] versus (t-24,t-12], using `lab` result times. Count distinct laboratory episodes/analytes, with revisions not counted as new tests unless their result time is distinct. Primary reduction is zero current distinct-analyte count after a positive prior count, or at least a 50% reduction when the prior interval has at least two episodes. Continuous change and episode-count analyses are secondary.

The primary outcome is the first qualifying deterioration in (t,t+12]: new or escalated respiratory support from `respiratoryCare` or new/escalated infusion support from `infusionDrug`. Death, discharge, and transfer before the event are competing events, not ordinary non-events. `apachePatientResult` mortality fields are secondary outcomes only.

## Rival explanations and competing transitions

The strongest rival is end-of-life/de-escalation: reduced testing may follow a documented or undocumented decision to limit care. Use `carePlanEOL` timestamps and `activeupondischarge` to define prespecified EOL-context strata and a sensitivity analysis excluding documented EOL plans before t. Because this table is sparse and documentation-dependent, missing EOL documentation is not equivalent to absence of EOL intent.

Also model discharge/transfer context and recent support de-escalation. A monitoring-process interpretation requires that the association persist after prespecified handling of these transitions and in held-out hospitals, while negative controls behave as expected.

## Baseline versus longitudinal alternative

The simple baseline is an adjusted discrete-time logistic model at the same hourly index times, with competing-event censoring, hospital fixed or clustered effects, and clustered uncertainty. It uses only information available at or before t: recent vital measurements, respiratory state, recent infusion/support, age, sex, unit type, and hospital.

The substantive alternative is a longitudinal recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard model with time-varying testing intensity and physiologic trajectories. It can reveal whether any association is transient, persistent, or preceded by a trajectory that the single-index baseline loses. It is not justified by predictive gain alone; it is selected because the scientific uncertainty concerns the timing and persistence of the monitoring signal versus de-escalation.

Use hospital-held-out validation, calibration, discrimination, absolute risk difference, and uncertainty intervals. Negative controls include within-hospital permutation of testing intervals preserving stay length/outcome, a post-index testing exposure that should not predict prior deterioration, and a non-laboratory documentation-timestamp control where available.

Supportive evidence would be a reproducible held-out-hospital association after state and EOL/discharge handling with null negative controls. Adverse evidence would be attenuation, instability, or disappearance after explicit rival adjustment. Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals, sparse events, or inability to distinguish interface interruption from clinical intent. None of these outcomes establishes causality or a recommendation to increase testing.

## Method selection and resource plan

The baseline is a CPU-discrete-time fit with hospital-clustered uncertainty; the longitudinal alternative is a CPU recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard fit on the same cohort and outcome. Both use the same split by hospital, with one held-out hospital group for validation. Approximate future solver resources are within the configured CPU envelope (up to 16 CPUs and 262,144 MiB memory); GPU is not required. These are planning estimates, not executed results.

The actual scientific deliverable is a fitted baseline and longitudinal alternative, with held-out-hospital calibration/discrimination, uncertainty, competing-event accounting, negative-control results, and an interpretation limited to monitoring-process association. No result is claimed here.
