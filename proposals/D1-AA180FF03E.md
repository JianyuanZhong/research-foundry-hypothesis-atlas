# Working proposal: transient versus sustained liver-reserve change after TACE

## Question and hypothesis

In adults with a recorded hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) diagnosis and a first observed transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does an early within-person shift in liver-related laboratory results identify a sustained change in the same analytes 30–90 days later, beyond the pre-TACE level and trajectory? The clinically important uncertainty is whether early postprocedure laboratory abnormalities mostly resolve as transient physiological/measurement changes or signal a persisting reduction in hepatic reserve that merits closer prospective monitoring before another locoregional treatment.

**Falsifiable hypothesis:** Among patients with valid, same-assay measurements, larger early increases in bilirubin and/or decreases in albumin or worsening of a clinically validated coagulation assay, relative to the preprocedure value and trend, are associated with a more persistent adverse within-person change at days 30–90. Adding the time-ordered early trajectory to a pre-TACE reserve/trend model will improve held-out estimation of late assay values and show a post-TACE discontinuity not seen in the pre-index trend. This is a prognostic/descriptive hypothesis among selected treated patients; it does not claim TACE caused the change, predict adjudicated decompensation, or establish that repeat TACE should be withheld.

This distinction matters. Literature supports the clinical consequence of lost hepatic reserve after locoregional HCC therapy, but the local data do not provide reliable dated, adjudicated decompensation or mortality. The closest study [K1] used four-center TACE/TARE data and defined hepatic decompensation (HD) using ascites, variceal bleeding, or portosystemic encephalopathy at baseline or within six months after therapy; it reported ALBI as a predictor and lower survival among patients with HD. It supports the value of the clinical problem, not the validity of this HCC source's diagnosis proxy. A 162-patient retrospective study [K2] compared TACE alone with TACE plus targeted and immune treatment using time to baseline ALBI-grade progression; it shows that hepatic reserve trajectory is consequential but differs in regimen and population. Delphi [K3] demonstrates modeling time-ordered disease histories and their dependencies, while its UK Biobank/Danish data and disease-code objectives do not establish post-TACE liver physiology. This proposal adds an explicit within-patient before/early/late contrast, a pretrend and observation-intensity rival, and temporally held-out testing in the local TACE population rather than reproducing any of those studies.

**Leading explanation versus strongest rival:** A post-index time-localized shift followed by a sustained adverse same-assay change is consistent with clinically relevant loss of reserve. The strongest rival is that low baseline reserve, progressive tumor/cirrhosis, other illness or procedures, selection into TACE, or more intensive inpatient monitoring caused both more extreme early measurements and more later measurements. Irregular testing, assay-name/unit drift, regression to the mean, or a pre-existing slope can mimic a treatment-time discontinuity. Adjustment cannot establish a mechanism or eliminate unmeasured confounding.

The source is HCC snapshot `[source checksum]`. All sources remain read-only; the analysis uses filtered derived tables only. Exact source files, paths, hashes and complete schemas are recorded in `datasets/hcc/README.md`, `datasets/hcc/metadata.json`, and the linked table schemas. The needed direct sources are:

- `HCC/data_基本信息_2500296761891079109.csv` ([source checksum]), `encounters`: `患者主索引`, `就诊号`, `年龄`, `性别`, `就诊时间`, `入院时间`, `出院时间`, `就诊科室`. Use only these non-direct-identifier fields. Link procedures, labs and diagnoses on the pair (`患者主索引`, `就诊号`); check duplicate keys, procedure/encounter chronology and local date coverage before deriving intervals.
- `HCC/data_检验_609065997844652188.csv` ([source checksum]), `labs`: `患者主索引`, `就诊号`, `检验`, `定性结果`, `定量结果`, `标本类型`, `检验时间`. Timestamp by `检验时间`; retain assay name, numeric parse, and specimen type for mapping/quality checks. There is no separate unit or reference-range column, so compute only same-name assay-specific changes after assay/pathology review. Do not calculate ALBI or combine assay variants without verified units and method equivalence.
- `HCC/data_诊断_7504718184492840569.csv` ([source checksum]), `diagnoses`: `患者主索引`, `就诊号`, `诊断名称`, `诊断类型`. Use with encounters for cohort HCC diagnosis and a secondary, clinician-reviewed later-encounter outcome only. Diagnoses have no independent event timestamp.

Data audit observed 11,612 procedure rows with TACE-like labels, 4,471 patients with at least one such record, and 10,256 TACE-like rows with a start time. In the corresponding patient-indexed laboratory scan, bilirubin-like names appeared for 3,743 patients during days −30 to 0 and 4,061 during days 0–14; albumin-like names appeared for 3,185 and 3,945, respectively. These are availability counts, not final paired-cohort counts: assay synonyms, exact first-index timing, units, specimen equivalence, chronology and eligibility remain to be audited. The data dictionary reports no lab unit field. A preliminary encounter-linked diagnosis term scan, excluding patients with any prior matching diagnosis, found only 3 patients with a later ascites diagnosis and 6 with later upper-GI-bleed terms within 180 days after first TACE, with no corresponding encephalopathy/liver-failure terms in that narrow scan. Lexical labels are not adjudicated clinical events; this confirms that a decompensation endpoint is too sparse/unvalidated for the primary experiment.

Other available HCC files are not necessary for the primary assay-trajectory test: medication, non-drug orders, examinations, pathology, clinical documents, vital-sign and transfer tables, and nominal front-page data. Clinical documents have no row-level time and duplicate diagnosis headers; lexical text extraction is not validated for clinical diagnoses. Transfers and vitals are identifiers only; images, waveforms, mortality and external-care data are unavailable. No other dataset's people or identifiers will be joined.

## Defined experiment

**Population/unit:** adults (age ≥18 years at index) with HCC diagnosis on the index encounter or a prior local encounter, and a first observed, timestamped TACE procedure linked to an encounter. One index per `患者主索引`. Require at least one valid same-name baseline measurement in days −30 to −1 and later measurements for trajectory analysis. Report cohort flow and measurement availability by analyte. A first *observed* TACE is not necessarily a true first lifetime treatment because prior history may be truncated. If the HCC diagnosis/TACE dictionary or clinical review cannot establish the cohort, describe it as adults with recorded TACE and documented HCC diagnosis, not a complete HCC registry.

**Index/boundaries:** procedure `开始时间` is time zero. Baseline window −30 to −1 days; pretrend uses all eligible measurements from −90 to −1 days; early postprocedure window +1 to +14 days; late persistence window +30 to +90 days. Preserve same-day ambiguity and exclude records not reliably ordered. The primary late outcome is assay-specific within-person change from the pre-index mean/nearest valid result to the late-window mean (with a robust median sensitivity), after exact-name and unit consistency checks. Early exposure is the change from that same pre-index reference to the early-window mean and maximum adverse value; report changes separately by analyte, never pool raw values across analytes. The scientific target is the association and held-out value of early changes for late changes, conditional on baseline value and pretrend. This is not a causal effect of intervention.

**Primary baseline and substantive alternative:**

1. **Simple baseline:** assay-specific patient-level linear model for late value/change using pre-TACE level and slope, age, sex, prior local encounter/procedure intensity, and calendar time. A second prespecified nested fit adds the early change. Compare the paired-patient estimates and holdout performance for identical analytes, windows and cohort; report effect/trajectory estimates with patient-level bootstrap confidence intervals, late-value RMSE/MAE, interval coverage and calibration of modelled values. Avoid classification thresholds absent clinical assay/reference review.
2. **Substantive learned/trajectory alternative:** a segmented mixed-effects longitudinal model, fit separately for each assay to all numeric measurements from days −90 to +90 with patient random intercept/slope, time spline and a prespecified knot at TACE; include assay identity only after mapping has verified equivalence, specimen type, observation count/rate, index-admission status and measured encounter intensity. Its target is the time-varying assay value and within-person post-index level/slope departure, with uncertainty. Unlike a baseline summary, it can distinguish an abrupt but resolving shift from persistent drift and a pre-existing worsening slope, while explicitly displaying observation density; it cannot correct informative measurement or prove injury. Compare its predicted late values and held-out uncertainty against the simple model on the same patients and analytes, not on a different population.

**Split and evaluation:** freeze term mapping, eligible assays, windows and exclusions before the held-out analysis. Split by index time at the 70th percentile into earlier development and later temporal confirmation; keep all rows for each patient in one split. Report counts/paired availability by split and patient-level bootstrap uncertainty. Evaluate late-window prediction error and interval coverage, plus the primary substantive contrast: early-to-late change association and post-index level/slope difference versus pre-index trend. Repeat a negative-control timing analysis with a pseudo-knot during the pre-TACE period and a sensitivity restricted to patients with comparable pre/post measurement density. If the later split or measurement support is too small for informative intervals, report inconclusive; do not move the split repeatedly to seek a favorable result.

**Falsification and interpretation:** support requires a consistent early-to-late association in held-out patients, post-index departure beyond the antecedent trend, clinically coherent directions for same-assay bilirubin/albumin/coagulation measures, and robustness to measured testing intensity and pre-index pseudo-knot checks. This would motivate a prospective monitoring study, not a clinical rule. Adverse evidence is no post-index departure, early changes returning toward baseline by days 30–90, or the signal disappearing with pretrend/testing-intensity controls; that would favor transient physiology, underlying disease or observation artifacts. Inconclusive evidence includes unstable assay units, low paired coverage, sparse later follow-up, wide intervals, or differential measurement missingness. A descriptive null with low precision does not refute biological injury.

## Clinical limits, next decision, and methods deferred

The strongest claim current data support is that dated TACE procedures and serial lab results are present and can be analyzed for same-assay within-person trajectories. The unresolved claim is whether a time-localized early change predicts a sustained later biochemical shift beyond baseline trajectory/measurement intensity. The primary outcome is biochemical trajectory, not adjudicated hepatic decompensation, mortality, tumor response, or treatment benefit. Essential adjudication includes TACE terminology, HCC diagnoses, assay equivalence/units/reference ranges, specimen timing, and interpretation of changes by hepatology/pathology experts. Clinical encounter-level decompensation requires a separate adjudicated study; the sparse initial scan is not adequate evidence for a confirmatory prognostic model. A stronger retreatment recommendation would require radiology/tumor burden and response, validated liver stage/units, clinically adjudicated decompensation, complete death and out-of-network follow-up, and ultimately a prospective treatment-strategy study.

Delphi's transformer is deferred: date-ordered sequence modeling is not dismissed, but a full disease-token generator is poorly matched to this narrowly stated assay-change estimand and absent external cohort; it could be revisited if simple/segmented models leave a supported question about nonlinear multianalyte sequences. Oncoformer is not adapted as multimodal because no local imaging payload exists; a lab-only adaptation would need a distinct question and would not reproduce it. ALADYNOULLI's genetic analysis cannot be reproduced without genotypes; an EHR-only latent disease-signature model is deferred because this study tests a small number of clinically mapped assays and has no validated broad disease outcome. None of these is rejected because it is neural or GPU-based. The segmented longitudinal model was selected because it can resolve transient-versus-persistent changes while controlling for pretrend, directly addressing the leading mechanistic uncertainty. The transparent paired/pretrend model remains its baseline.

**Workload:** bounded diagnostics already read selected HCC columns and counted TACE/lab availability (the 2.2-GB lab CSV scan took about 46 seconds locally; this is measured). Future solver plan: CPU, 8 cores and 16–32 GiB RAM; chunk/filter source tables, then assay-specific mixed models and patient bootstrap, estimated 2–6 CPU-hours after availability checks. Library/model runtime is unmeasured; no GPU is required for these tabular fits. This is within the 16-CPU/256-GiB/8-hour solver planning envelope. Discovery computation is separate; no model was trained and no result of the proposed trajectory comparison is claimed.
