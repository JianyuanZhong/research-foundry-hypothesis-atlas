## Clinical question, existing evidence, and advance

At postoperative hour 48 after a first recorded HCC liver resection, does signed early change in INR, fibrinogen, ALT, and procalcitonin improve prediction of a first context-supported recorded invasive pleural/abdominal fluid intervention in (48,336] hours beyond current POD2 severity, observation/treatment context, and an unordered change-magnitude rival?

Intervention-requiring collections are clinically consequential. Wang et al. included thoracentesis and abdominal drainage in a clinician-adjudicated 30-day complication composite [K1]. Sakamoto et al. used thoracentesis or thoracic drainage to define clinically relevant post-hepatectomy pleural effusion and discussed fluid-retention and inflammatory explanations [K2]. Clavien–Dindo assigns invasive intervention to grade III [K3]. These sources do not validate this extract's intervention phenotype, establish unplanned intent, or show that assay direction adds prognostic information.

The strongest claim supported before model fitting was that the repaired extraction identifies 315 context-supported recorded interventions among 17,733 eligible encounters. The unresolved claim tested here was incremental prognostic information in signed assay history. The substantive advance is a locked temporal test of that claim against both a same-information unordered rival and a nonlinear latest-state rival, together with endpoint, observation, treatment, era, and leakage controls.

## Rival explanations and controls

Event-linked observability met the adverse-support floor: A≥1 events were 37/81 development, 16/35 tuning, 15/45 calibration, and 44/154 test; development+tuning had 2,478 A≥1 patients and 53 events.

Current severity/nonlinearity: G0 had much stronger rank discrimination than T1 (AUROC 0.675, AUPRC 0.066), while remaining catastrophically miscalibrated. This indicates nonlinear current-state/observation information is more promising than signed slopes for discrimination, but does not establish deployable risk prediction.

Observation practice: timing-only AUROC was 0.533 and Brier 0.025615, essentially matching B0/T1; values-only collapsed to AUROC 0.5 after recalibration. The available linear signal is therefore compatible with observation process rather than physiologic direction.

Treatment context: removing medication features left T1−B0 negligible and calibration poor.

Endpoint rivals: T1−B0 Brier differences remained approximately zero for all-candidate S1, repaired-plus-unresolved S2, pleural, abdominal, examination-free, order-free, and cross-source outcomes. No endpoint sensitivity supported the signed-history claim.

Era/calibration: 2024 T1 AUROC was 0.487, versus 0.581 in 2025; year-specific calibration differed sharply. A 2020–2022 fit with 2023 calibration remained poorly calibrated on test (T1 intercept −1.375, slope 0.531). This makes era/calibration drift a consequential rival and independently enforces inconclusiveness.

Leakage control: within-year outcome permutation produced T1 AUROC 0.479 and AUPRC 0.0242, without above-chance signal. No leakage trigger fired.

The prespecified 200 end-to-end bootstrap refits were not completed in this bounded episode. The 2,000 intervals condition on fitted transforms, tuning, and 2023 recalibration and may understate total pipeline uncertainty. This omission cannot turn the result supportive or adverse because failed locked-test calibration independently fixes the frozen overall label as inconclusive.

## What follows clinically

Supported: signed four-assay direction supplied no material incremental Brier or AUPRC improvement in this frozen execution, and the nonlinear latest-state rival ranked better. This is a descriptive/prognostic result for a recorded intervention phenotype.

Not supported: assay direction as mechanism; planned versus unplanned intent; complication cause or grade; treatment effects; decision utility; or transportability. Clinician adjudication remains necessary for stronger endpoint claims. External or later-era recalibration data are required before assessing G0 as a clinical model. A fully resampled 200-refit analysis would strengthen numerical uncertainty but cannot repair the observed calibration failure.

Decision: do not advance signed direction for this endpoint. Treat the branch as inconclusive rather than a clean adverse closure because calibration and era instability invalidate the frozen adverse interpretation. Revisit only with an independently calibrated evaluation cohort, denser repeated assays, or clinically adjudicated outcomes—not by changing thresholds or endpoints after seeing these results.
