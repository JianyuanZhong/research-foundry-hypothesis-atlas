# Earlier natural menopause and cardiac structural remodelling beyond blood-pressure load

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank women with self-reported natural menopause, does earlier age at menopause remain associated with left-ventricular myocardial mass and left-atrial structure/function after adjustment for age, body composition, and measured blood-pressure load?

The clinically important unresolved question is whether menopause timing marks a subgroup with earlier subclinical cardiac structural change, or whether the apparent association is explained by residual haemodynamic load and shared metabolic ageing. This matters because menopause timing itself is not modifiable, but if it identifies a subgroup with earlier cardiac remodelling, it could motivate earlier cardiovascular surveillance and risk-factor control.

The leading explanation is that earlier natural menopause is associated with higher cumulative blood pressure and/or adverse body composition, which in turn drives left-ventricular hypertrophy and left-atrial remodelling. The strongest rival is a sex-hormone-related biological transition: loss of oestrogen may independently alter cardiac structure or function beyond what measured haemodynamic and metabolic covariates capture.

## What answering would change

If the association persists after adjustment for measured blood pressure, body composition, and metabolic covariates, the result would support the hypothesis that menopause timing is an independent marker of earlier cardiac structural change. This would not establish a hormonal mechanism, but it would justify prospective validation and consideration of earlier cardiovascular surveillance in women with early natural menopause.

If the association disappears after adjustment, the result would support the competing explanation that the apparent signal is explained by residual haemodynamic and metabolic load. This would argue against using menopause timing as an independent risk marker and would redirect attention to the measured risk factors themselves.

If the association is present only in a narrow age range or only among women without HRT use, the result would be inconclusive for a general independent effect and would require a larger or differently selected population.

## Method comparison

**Simple baseline:** Adjusted linear regression of LV mass and LA structure/function on age at natural menopause, with prespecified adjustment for age, BMI, visceral fat, systolic/diastolic blood pressure, HbA1c, and HRT use. This is a standard epidemiological analysis that can be completed on CPU in under an hour.

**Substantive alternative:** A mixed-effects model with participant-level random intercepts to account for repeated cardiac MRI measurements (if available) and a prespecified interaction between menopause timing and HRT use. This would reveal whether the association is modified by HRT exposure, which the simple baseline cannot answer. The mixed-effects model requires a GPU for repeated fits but is feasible within the configured budget.

**Selection reason:** The simple baseline is sufficient to test the primary hypothesis. The mixed-effects model is a valuable sensitivity analysis but is not required for the primary test. If the simple baseline shows a persistent association, the mixed-effects model can be added as a follow-up.

## Falsification criteria

- A null or reversed association after adjustment for age, BMI, visceral fat, systolic/diastolic blood pressure, HbA1c, and HRT use would falsify the proposed independent association.
- Loss of significance in sensitivity analyses excluding women with HRT use or restricting to a narrow age range would weaken the claim.
- If the association is present only in a subgroup defined by a post-hoc variable, the result would be exploratory and would require independent confirmation.

## What would require additional data

- Hormone measurements over time to test the sex-hormone-related mechanism directly.
- Repeated cardiac MRI measurements to test for temporal changes in cardiac structure.
- External validation in a different population to confirm the generalizability of the association.

## Honest limitations

- Menopause timing is self-reported and may be subject to recall bias.
- HRT use is measured at a single time point and may not capture the full duration of exposure.
- The cardiac MRI subset is a selected population and may not be representative of all women with natural menopause.
- The analysis is observational and cannot establish causation.
