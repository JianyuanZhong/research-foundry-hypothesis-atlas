# Record-compatible respiratory transition and topology-bounded early failure after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly fit and audit one leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 prognostic experiment. Its primary scientific deliverable is a topology-bounded, observation-aware estimate of whether mutually exclusive record-compatible respiratory evidence tiers have different 48-hour competing-event patterns after the first ICU discharge landmark.

Completion requires these workspace outputs:

## Strongest supported claim and unresolved hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the assessed parent and current evidence is limited: heterogeneous, recorded respiratory and vasoactive evidence near ICU outtime may be associated with different early competing outcomes, but an oxygen/FiO2 record, a PEEP record and a procedure marker are not interchangeable clinical measurements. A procedure row is not proof of continuous ventilation, and missing support documentation is not proof that support was absent. The parent’s topology audit was feasibility evidence only and did not estimate the subtype associations proposed here.

The unresolved question is whether the apparent “respiratory support withdrawal” signal is specific to a reproducible, temporally compatible evidence tier after accounting for physiology and the observation/documentation process, or whether it is mainly a documentation and discharge-topology artifact.

The prespecified hypothesis is:

- the record-compatible extubation/ventilatory-transition tier has a different 48-hour compatible ICU-escalation contrast from the record-compatible oxygen-only tier;
- recorded vasoactive cessation has a distinct death-versus-return composition;
- the respiratory-tier-by-recorded-vasoactive-cessation interaction changes the escalation/death composition, even if the total return contrast does not.

These are retrospective association hypotheses about observable records and partially identified ICU-escalation pathways. They are not hypotheses that extubation caused an outcome, that ICU discharge was appropriate, or that an intervention would improve safety.

## Exact exposure construction

Let O be icustays.outtime. Define the pre-index window W=[O-12h,O), preceding half P=[O-12h,O-6h), and final half F=[O-6h,O). All clinical measurements are indexed by charttime, starttime or endtime; storetime is never a primary clinical time. Each one-hour bin is left-closed/right-open.

A valid respiratory chart observation has a non-null key and parseable charttime, finite valuenum, and one of:

- O2 flow item 223834 with valuenum > 2 L/min;
- Inspired O2 Fraction item 223835 with valuenum > 0.21;
- PEEP set item 220339 with valuenum > 0 cmH2O.

A valid MAP observation uses item 220052 (Arterial Blood Pressure mean) or 220181 (Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean), with finite valuenum and charttime. Procedure markers use procedureevents.starttime for item 224385 (Intubation), 227194 (Extubation) and 225794 (Non-invasive Ventilation). A procedure marker is valid when its key/item/time parse and endtime is null or not earlier than starttime; it is transition evidence only, never a continuous-support measure.

A valid vasoactive interval is an inputevents row with one of norepinephrine 221906, epinephrine 221289/229617, dopamine 221662, phenylephrine 221749/229630/229631/229632, or vasopressin 222315, parseable starttime and endtime, and starttime < endtime. Its overlap with P or F is recorded; statusdescription, order fields and storetime are retained as process variables and no status is silently interpreted as delivered treatment.

Primary respiratory coverage requires at least two one-hour bins with a valid respiratory observation in each of P and F, and at least two one-hour bins with a valid MAP observation in each half. An observed value below a positivity threshold is observed non-positive. A bin with no relevant row is uninformative absence. The primary population retains an explicit unknown/inadequate stratum; it is never silently called “off.”

For complete-coverage stays, assign exactly one respiratory state in the following priority order:

1. R_extubation_compatible: a valid extubation marker in W, prior PEEP-positive evidence in P or a prior intubation marker in P, no respiratory-positive chart evidence in F, and no contradictory intubation-after-extubation sequence in W. This means compatible with a ventilatory liberation transition, not proven invasive ventilation or successful extubation.
2. R_PEEP_NIV_transition: no class-1 assignment; PEEP-positive evidence or an NIV marker in P, no respiratory-positive chart evidence in F, and no intubation/extubation marker in W.
3. R_oxygen_only_transition: no class-1/2 assignment; O2-flow or FiO2 positive evidence in P, no PEEP-positive evidence, no procedure marker in W, and no respiratory-positive chart evidence in F. This is an oxygen/FiO2 evidence tier, not proof that oxygen was the only support.
4. R_recorded_off: no respiratory-positive chart evidence in either half and no respiratory procedure marker in W.
5. R_active_or_nontransition: adequate coverage with respiratory-positive evidence in F, or a valid marker pattern that does not meet classes 1–3.
6. R_unknown_discordant: inadequate coverage, malformed timing that affects classification, or contradictory markers that cannot be ordered.

The final-half “no positive evidence” condition is based on valid chart observations; it does not treat absent rows as observed non-positive. The ledger also records all raw rows and supports secondary 15-minute and 60-minute procedure-marker boundary rules, a procedure-free classification, and the parent’s broad respiratory-positive definition.

Vasoactive evidence is deliberately orthogonal, with exactly one state: H_recorded_cessation (a valid vasoactive interval overlaps P and none overlaps F); H_recorded_active (any valid interval overlaps F); H_no_preceding_record (no valid interval overlaps P); or H_invalid_unknown. H_no_preceding_record is not “hemodynamically off.” The prespecified “joint withdrawal” contrast is the interaction (R in {R_extubation_compatible,R_PEEP_NIV_transition,R_oxygen_only_transition}) × H_recorded_cessation, not a separate category that can become unreachable by definition.

For each of six q scenarios q in {0,.10,.25,.50,.75,1}, only uninformative no-row support bins receive a fixed-seed Bernoulli(q) imputation; 20 replicate seeds are locked and averaged. Observed non-positive values are never recoded. The ledger, models and estimates retain replicate identity and report the full q frontier. This is a sensitivity analysis for unobserved support, not an identified missingness model.

## Population, index, follow-up and topology

Use one earliest valid ICU stay per subject across all admissions, sorting eligible icustays.intime and then smallest stay_id. Require the subject_id,hadm_id admission/patient joins, anchor_age >= 18 (retain top-coded age 91), intime < outtime < dischtime, non-null dischtime, no deathtime <= O, and at least 12 hours between intime and O. The index admission is the hadm_id attached to the selected stay. The primary analysis is the coverage-eligible population; unknown/discordant exposure rows remain visible and are included as explicit categories or excluded only in a declared contrast with standardized target and overlap reporting.

Follow-up is [O,min(O+48h,dischtime)]. A later ICU return is an icustays.intime > O in the same subject_id,hadm_id within follow-up. For each first later ICU return, construct the admission-wide ICU-unit set from all icustays.first_careunit and last_careunit values for that subject/admission. A primary documented bridge is a hosp/transfers row with eventtype='transfer', non-null/non-UNKNOWN careunit not in that ICU-unit set, valid intime < outtime, and clipped overlap from max(O-5min,transfer.intime) to min(later_icu.intime+5min,transfer.outtime) of at least 30 minutes. Every candidate and rejection reason is retained. Sensitivities include 0/5-minute endpoint tolerance, 0/15/30/60-minute duration, inclusion of all transfer event types, careunit-only logic, and ICU-unit matching.

The first post-index event is mutually exclusive:

- L: first later ICU return with at least one qualifying documented non-ICU bridge;
- U-L: first later ICU return with no qualifying bridge under the primary rule;
- death: admissions.deathtime non-null, no later return first, and deathtime <= dischtime;
- alive discharge: dischtime before the other events, with no in-hospital death at discharge.

U=L+(U-L) is any later ICU return. The latent true ward-to-ICU escalation CIF T is not observed and is bounded by L <= T <= U. For an exposure contrast A-B, the compatible interval is [L_A-U_B,U_A-L_B], with no midpoint interpretation. Apply a one-minute tie tolerance with priority documented return, unbridged return, death, discharge; repeat at 0 and 5 minutes. The primary estimands are adjusted absolute 48-hour compatible contrasts, with 6- and 24-hour CIFs as prespecified temporal checks, for T, death and alive discharge.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods use the same cohort, twelve hourly ledger, q replicates, R/H states, process covariates, topology labels, target standardization and subject-level splits.

The transparent baseline is a pooled one-hour discrete-time cause-specific hazard model for L, U-L, death and alive discharge, plus an aggregate-return model for U. Use ridge-penalized logistic hazards with penalty selected on validation data only. Predictors are R/H indicators and their prespecified interaction, pre-index MAP and respiratory summaries/slopes, support duration/intensity, observed-positive and uninformative masks, hourly row counts, chart/procedure/input channel indicators, pre-index transfer counts, ICU LOS, admission context, age, gender and anchor-year group. Fit nested physiology/support-only, observation/process-only and full models. Do not include any field first known after O, including discharge location, hospital-expire flag, future notes or post-index transfers. Standardize predictions descriptively to the common coverage-eligible target; do not call this a causal correction.

The scientifically substantive alternative is an observation-aware factorized hidden semi-Markov model using exactly the same hourly ledger and covariates. Its respiratory states are extubation-compatible, PEEP/NIV-compatible, oxygen/FiO2-only, recorded-off, active/nontransition and unknown; hemodynamic states are recorded-cessation, recorded-active, no-preceding-record and unknown. It learns duration-dependent state persistence/transitions and emissions for physiology, support values, procedure markers, source masks and row counts, with a missingness/observation submodel. A competing-hazard decoder predicts the same L,U-L, death and discharge targets. Constraints prevent impossible duration/order transitions, but latent states remain record states rather than clinical truth. Fit state, duration, emission, observation and decoder parameters on training data with validation-selected regularization; lock test outputs before any q/topology sensitivity.

The baseline answers the observable association with maximal auditability. The alternative can reveal within-window ordering, persistence, respiratory/hemodynamic discordance and an observation-dependent recovery buffer that fixed half-window summaries lose. Its added value is scientific only if latent-state patterns are stable, improve calibrated held-out observable outcome characterization beyond the baseline, and remain within [L,U]; a small predictive gain alone is not a reason to prefer it.

## Splits, uncertainty, checks and interpretation gates

Use subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test partitions with locked seeds 17, 29 and 43. No row-level split, cross-subject calendar feature or post-index measurement is allowed. The primary report uses q=.25 and the primary topology/tie rules; q, topology, tie, coverage and procedure-free analyses are robustness frontiers. Use subject bootstrap (200 replicates for primary baseline estimates; 100 complete refits for the alternative and major sensitivity frontiers), 95% percentile intervals, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier scores, event counts, overlap and effective sample size. Report split stability and HSMM state entropy/occupancy and reject any latent T estimate outside its observed [L,U] bounds.

Prespecified falsifications are: storetime-only pre-index process replacement restricted to storetime<O; within-window order shuffling; support-mask-only, physiology-only, process-only and combined ablations; removal of procedures; each respiratory channel alone; broad-parent recoding; q/window/coverage frontiers; transfer-careunit permutation; a +6-hour transfer-time shift; a pre-landmark pseudo-return control; discharge-as-censoring; and first-careunit, admission-type, anchor-year and transfer-density strata. A genuine support-tier interpretation should weaken under transfer permutation and should not be recreated by storetime-only or order-shuffled features.

Supportive evidence for a positive escalation contrast requires both endpoints of the compatible interval for the prespecified comparison to be above zero at the stated horizon (or both below zero for a negative claim), adequate event support and overlap, stability across q/topology/tie rules and split seeds, concordant observable predictions from baseline and alternative, and no dependence on a single channel or procedure marker. A result present only in U but absent in L supports at most any-return/documentation association, not a transition interpretation. A stable death contrast is reported separately from the escalation interval.

Adverse evidence is a direction reversal, collapse after observation adjustment, dependence on a single source/procedure rule, persistence after transfer permutation, or a latent estimate outside the bounds. Inconclusive evidence is a compatible interval crossing zero, sparse subtype events, high unknown/discordant prevalence, non-positivity, wide bound width, unstable q/topology frontier, high HSMM entropy or poor transport overlap. If subtypes are sparse, do not merge them merely to obtain significance; report the broad recorded-instability result, if computed, as a different and weaker estimand.

Every conclusion must identify whether it concerns L, U-L, U, T's compatible interval, death, discharge or predictive performance. Automatic verification can check source hashes, schema/column use, time leakage, joins, exposure transformations, model fits, CIF algebra, uncertainty, calibration, falsifications and prose-to-output linkage. It cannot determine whether a transfer was clinically non-ICU, whether a return was planned, preventable or appropriate, whether support was actually delivered when unrecorded, or whether acting on risk would improve outcomes.
