# Provisional eICU hypothesis: laboratory-monitoring reduction during persistent instability

Status: provisional and untested. This is a design checkpoint, not a cohort result, causal estimate, or clinical recommendation.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a continuous eICU stay who remain physiologically unstable at a prespecified hourly landmark, a reduction in laboratory-monitoring intensity during the preceding 12 hours is associated with a higher risk of a time-local clinical deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond contemporaneously measured severity and recent support. The association is expected to attenuate when end-of-life documentation, discharge/transfer context, and support de-escalation are handled explicitly. A residual association in held-out hospitals would support a monitoring-process association, not proof that changing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

The configured eICU snapshot and catalog support feasibility of this design: it contains longitudinal timestamped laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, patient, hospital, care-plan, discharge, and mortality fields. The within-dataset join key is `patientunitstayid`; `hospitalid` supports hospital-level stratification and held-out validation. The expert seed `[starting question]` supplies the opening that laboratory-testing frequency/missingness may encode hospital practice and may transport poorly. This is an opening, not evidence that the association exists.

The unresolved claim is whether a monitoring reduction is associated with a subsequent deterioration after accounting for current severity and care-context transitions. No cohort counts, endpoint validation, model fit, or clinical conclusion has been executed.

## Population and temporal design

Use adults with one continuous ICU stay, excluding stays without a valid 18-hour interval (preceding 12 hours plus following 12 hours) and excluding landmark times too close to ICU discharge/transfer. Define an hourly landmark. At each landmark, require a prespecified instability rule based on available contemporaneous physiology and support, such as abnormal mean blood pressure, respiratory rate, heart rate, oxygen saturation, or active respiratory/vasopressor support; the exact thresholds must be fixed before outcome analysis and reported.

The exposure is a prespecified reduction in laboratory-monitoring intensity from the preceding 12-hour interval relative to the patient's immediately prior 12-hour interval, using `lab.csv.gz` fields `patientunitstayid`, `labresultoffset`, `labtypeid`, `labname`, `labresult`, and `labresultrevisedoffset`. Count distinct laboratory measurements or distinct laboratory types only after a fixed deduplication rule; do not use post-landmark records.

The primary outcome is a time-local deterioration in the next 12 hours, defined prospectively as a new or worsening respiratory-support event or vasopressor/infusion escalation using `respiratoryCare.csv.gz` (`patientunitstayid`, `respcarestatusoffset`, `airwaytype`, `ventstartoffset`, `ventendoffset`) and `infusionDrug.csv.gz` (`patientunitstayid`, `infusionoffset`, `drugname`, `drugrate`, `infusionrate`). The final operationalization must be frozen before outcome extraction. Death, discharge/transfer, and documented end-of-life/de-escalation are competing transitions and must be reported, not silently treated as non-events.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is an adjusted discrete-time logistic or pooled-logistic model for the next-12-hour deterioration, with hospital fixed effects or hospital-held-out validation, landmark severity, recent support, and prespecified care-context strata. It estimates an interpretable association and can be audited for whether the apparent signal is concentrated in discharge/EOL contexts.

The substantive alternative is a longitudinal recurrent-event model or state-transition analysis over consecutive landmarks, allowing the prior monitoring trajectory, current severity, and support state to evolve and explicitly modeling death/discharge/EOL transitions. It can reveal whether the association is a persistent trajectory pattern or a one-off landmark artifact, information the baseline loses. A neural sequence model is not required for this question; if used later, it must be compared on the same outcome and hospital-held-out split, with calibration and uncertainty, not judged by a small predictive gain.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence would be a prespecified, uncertainty-quantified association that remains after context adjustment and is directionally consistent in held-out hospitals, with a nontrivial negative-control result absent for a temporally unrelated monitoring measure. Adverse evidence would be attenuation to null after explicit EOL/discharge/de-escalation handling, failure to replicate across hospitals, or a comparable association for a negative-control exposure; this would favor documentation/care-transition explanations. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse laboratory coverage, unstable endpoint semantics, wide intervals, or insufficient competing-event information; it would motivate endpoint adjudication or a narrower study rather than a null conclusion.

A positive association cannot establish that reducing laboratory orders causes deterioration, identify a biological mechanism, or justify changing monitoring practice. Clinical adjudication of deterioration, intent to treat, and reasons for testing are not fully available. The public eICU narrative note sections are removed; structured note fields are retained, and raw waveforms are unavailable. Any causal or treatment recommendation requires additional expert review and another study.
