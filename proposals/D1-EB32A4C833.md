# Branch C — residual instability before ICU discharge

Status: candidate-ready proposed Harbor experiment; no cohort scan, model fit, or clinical result was run in discovery.

Parent seed: [prior hypothesis]. This branch repairs the seed by making the ICU-discharge landmark, 48-hour outcomes, exact MIMIC bindings, competing events, observation process, treatment limitation, discharge destination, and method comparison executable. It does not reproduce any cited paper.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

[K1] establishes that deterioration after ICU dismissal is clinically important and that discharge-time respiratory/neurologic impairment and ICU context can predict readmission, while explicitly leaving open whether changing discharge decisions improves outcomes. [K2] shows that learned discharge-risk models can outperform a traditional score internally but can lose performance on external data. [K3] documents that MIMIC measurements are modular, deidentified, and “sporadic and noisy,” reflecting care processes as well as physiology.

The unresolved claim is narrower than “patients were discharged too early” and is not causal:

> Among adults discharged alive from an ICU to a non-ICU destination after at least 24 hours, a prespecified residual-instability phenotype in the final 24 clinical hours—persistent oxygen support or repeated abnormal vital-sign bins—has higher risk of a first 48-hour post-ICU adverse event (ICU readmission or hospital death) than an observed-stable phenotype, after accounting for measured pre-discharge severity and testing discharge destination, observed treatment limitation, and recording intensity as rival explanations.

The strongest supported claim is only that discharge-time instability and ICU context have prognostic relevance in prior cohorts, and that MIMIC contains linked ICU/hospital measurements. The experiment will test whether this specific MIMIC-IV phenotype is reproducible over a short, clinically actionable horizon and whether its signal is temporal/physiologic or instead explained by who was discharged where, who had treatment limitations, or who was observed most intensively. An association will not establish a mechanism, true oxygen need, clinician error, or benefit from delaying discharge.

## Actual scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly produce:

1. A cohort-flow file from eligible ICU stays to 48-hour follow-up, with exclusions and counts for age, invalid times, ICU stay <24 hours, ICU-to-ICU transfer, unclear destination, death at/before ICU outtime, missing discharge-window data, and each exposure state.
2. An auditable item-label and unit-conversion table, four six-hour-bin feature table, and observation/missingness table.
3. A prespecified 48-hour cause-specific competing-risk analysis estimating standardized absolute risks and risk differences for high residual instability versus observed stable status, with separate readmission and death cumulative-incidence outputs.
4. A transparent fixed-summary baseline and a learned temporal alternative fitted on the identical patient-level split, with held-out discrimination, calibration, Brier score, uncertainty, and risk summaries by exposure state.
5. Destination- and treatment-limitation-stratified sensitivity estimates, an observation-density falsification check, and a table of supportive, adverse, and inconclusive interpretations linked to computed outputs.

Completion requires saved computed outputs and uncertainty intervals linked to the cohort, feature windows, event times, model fits, and sensitivity analyses. A readiness probe, coefficient, AUROC, or favorable direction is not completion.

## Population and leakage-safe temporal design

Use the first valid ICU stay per hospital admission, selected by earliest `icu/icustays.intime`, with `intime < outtime`, ICU length of stay at least 24 hours, and an adult patient (`hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`). Join the stay to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`. To avoid correlated repeat stays in fitting, keep all admissions from one `subject_id` in one split; the primary cohort still has at most one index stay per `hadm_id`.

The index landmark is `t_L = icustays.outtime`. Retain only a discharge from the index ICU to a non-ICU destination. Determine the first `hosp/transfers` row with `eventtype='transfer'` and `intime >= t_L`; classify a destination as ICU if normalized `careunit` contains ICU, CCU, CSRU, SICU, or TSICU. If that row is absent, use `admissions.discharge_location` only as a labeled fallback. An unclassifiable destination is excluded from the primary analysis and reported. An ICU-to-ICU transfer is not an ICU discharge for this study. Destination is not a primary exposure; it is a measured selection/clinical-context variable.

Require the patient to be alive at `t_L`: `admissions.deathtime` is null or strictly after `t_L`. If `hospital_expire_flag=1` but a death time is unavailable or not strictly after `t_L`, classify the timing as unresolved and exclude from the primary risk set rather than inventing a discharge-survivor time. Patient-relative intervals are used; cross-patient calendar ordering is invalid under the configured MIMIC deidentification.

All primary features use only the final 24 hours `[t_L-24h, t_L)`. For `icu/chartevents`, require both `charttime < t_L` and `storetime <= t_L` as a conservative availability screen; no row documented after the landmark is eligible. The same rule is applied to `icu/outputevents`. No discharge destination, `dischtime`, `deathtime`, later ICU stay, or any post-landmark observation is an input. Rows from an input/procedure interval that crosses `t_L` are excluded from primary summaries rather than partially crediting future volume or support.

## Residual-instability exposure

Partition `[t_L-24h,t_L)` into four fixed six-hour bins. The primary physiologic input set is audited in `icu/d_items` and extracted from `icu/chartevents`:

- item 220045, “Heart Rate” (bpm);
- item 220210, “Respiratory Rate” (insp/min);
- items 220052, “Arterial Blood Pressure mean” and 220181, “Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean” (mmHg);
- item 220277, “O2 saturation pulseoxymetry” (%);
- item 223834, “O2 Flow” and 227287, “O2 Flow (additional cannula)” (L/min);
- item 223835, “Inspired O2 Fraction” (convert values in `[0,1]` as fractions and values in `(1,100]` to fractions by division by 100; values outside the resulting physiologic range are invalid). The conversion rule is fixed before outcome inspection.

In each bin, calculate the median and count of each available physiologic stream, an abnormality indicator, and a missingness mask. The fixed clinical boundaries are HR <50 or >110 bpm, RR <8 or >25/min, MAP <65 mmHg, SpO2 <92%, oxygen flow >=4 L/min, or FiO2 >=0.40. A bin is “abnormal” if any valid stream meets its boundary; a respiratory-support bin is positive if oxygen flow or FiO2 meets its boundary. These thresholds are prespecified clinical classification rules, not claims of a validated discharge score.

Primary exposure states:

- observed stable: at least 3 of 4 bins have all required vital/oxygen streams observed, no abnormal bin, and no respiratory-support bin;
- persistent instability: at least 2 of 4 bins are abnormal or at least 2 of 4 bins have respiratory support;
- intermittent instability: exactly 1 abnormal/support bin;
- insufficient observation: fewer than 3 qualifying bins or no valid value for a required stream in all bins.

The primary contrast is persistent instability versus observed stable. Intermittent and insufficient states are retained, not folded into stable or imputed as normal. A secondary continuous burden is the number of abnormal/support bins divided by the number of qualifying bins, with component-specific burdens reported. Values are summarized by clinical `charttime`; `storetime` is used only for the conservative availability filter and a prespecified documentation-timing sensitivity.

A sensitivity adds respiratory support signals from item 226732, “O2 Delivery Device(s),” and excludes alarm items and APACHE-derived items. Alarm and APACHE items are not primary physiology. The study cannot recover actual device interface, delivered FiO2, work of breathing, or clinician assessment from these fields.

## Outcomes and competing events

Follow from `t_L` to `t_L+48h`, bounded by the same admission’s `admissions.dischtime` and observable MIMIC follow-up. Define the first event:

- ICU readmission: a subsequent `icu/icustays` row with the same `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, a different `stay_id`, and `intime >= t_L` and `intime < t_L+48h`;
- hospital death: `admissions.deathtime` in `[t_L,t_L+48h)` and before or at hospital follow-up end;
- alive hospital discharge before 48 hours: `admissions.dischtime < t_L+48h` with no earlier readmission or death;
- event-free at 48 hours: no event and still under hospital follow-up at the window end.

If death and readmission have an identical recorded time, death is first by a fixed tie rule and the tie is reported. A death time missing despite an expiration flag is not silently reconstructed in the primary outcome.

Primary estimand: the 48-hour cumulative incidence of the composite first ICU readmission or hospital death, with alive discharge a competing event, and the standardized risk difference persistent minus stable over the eligible population. Secondary estimands are cause-specific cumulative-incidence contrasts for readmission with death and alive discharge competing, and death with readmission and alive discharge competing. These are prognostic estimands among ICU survivors discharged to non-ICU care, not an onset-cohort effect and not the causal effect of discharge timing.

## Baseline analysis and estimand safeguards

The transparent baseline uses only the four-bin fixed summaries above plus prespecified context: age, sex, admission type, index ICU length of stay, first/last careunit, hours from admission to ICU entry, number of qualifying bins, and the pre-discharge values/burdens of each physiologic component. It does not adjust away the primary exposure with post-landmark data.

Fit separate cause-specific risk models for first readmission, first death, and alive discharge (or an equivalent multinomial first-event model), then obtain 48-hour cumulative incidence and standardized risks by setting the exposure state to persistent versus stable while averaging over the observed eligible population. Report unadjusted state-specific risks first, then a context-adjusted model. Use patient-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals with a fixed seed and at least 1,000 resamples if the solver envelope permits; otherwise report the prespecified lower count and justify it before fitting. Calibration, Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC for the composite, and event-specific calibration are evaluated on held-out patients, but no discrimination metric replaces the risk estimand.

Three nested artifact checks are mandatory:

1. Add the first non-ICU destination category from `transfers.careunit` or the labeled `admissions.discharge_location` fallback. A residual contrast that disappears only after this addition is destination-dependent association, not evidence of intrinsic physiology.
2. Extract `icu/chartevents` item 223758, “Code Status,” and 228687, “Code Status.” Use the last eligible value before `t_L` to form observed limitation categories only when the value explicitly indicates DNR, DNI, comfort measures, hospice, or no escalation; unknown values remain unknown. This is an observed documentation proxy, not a complete goals-of-care adjudication. Report strata and a sensitivity adding the proxy; do not call it a treatment effect.
3. Compute pre-landmark observation density from counts of eligible vital/oxygen rows and distinct six-hour bins. Compare outcome risk across density strata and run a process-only diagnostic using non-oxygen/non-vital `chartevents` count in the same window. If the process-only gradient is as strong as the physiologic contrast and the contrast attenuates with density, the physiologic interpretation is weakened. Missingness is a state and a result, never a zero.

## Learned temporal alternative

The alternative is a small masked temporal GRU-D-style model on the same four six-hour bins, with the same raw physiologic streams, the same context variables, explicit value masks, time-since-last-observation, and the same three/four first-event targets. It is fit only on the training partition, early-stopped on validation, and evaluated once on a locked patient-level test partition. No destination, code-status value, outcome proxy, or post-landmark data enters the primary temporal model; an artifact-aware extension adding destination and observed code-status proxy is a prespecified secondary diagnostic.

This model is scientifically substantive because it can retain ordering, persistence, within-window co-movement, and informative observation gaps that mean/last/min/max summaries lose. The question is whether those temporal patterns change the interpretation of “persistent” versus “intermittent” instability, not whether a neural network wins a small AUROC margin. Compare the temporal model with the baseline on held-out calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, event-specific AUROC/AUPRC, calibration by exposure state and destination, and the observed-risk versus predicted-risk contrast for persistent, intermittent, stable, and insufficient states. Use five fixed random seeds or a patient bootstrap for model uncertainty, report all seeds, and do not select a favorable seed.

Selection rationale: retain the baseline as the primary inferential analysis because its fixed summaries, competing-event estimand, and standardized risk difference are auditable. Retain the GRU-D alternative as a secondary test of whether temporal ordering and missingness carry clinically relevant information beyond the baseline. A mechanistic state-space model was deferred: the configured data lack adjudicated respiratory diagnoses, delivered-device verification, work-of-breathing assessments, and reliable treatment-intent labels, so a latent “physiologic mechanism” would be weakly identified and would add assumptions rather than resolve the question.

Approximate future solver budget (planning estimate, not measured here): baseline extraction and CPU fitting 1–3 hours with 8 CPUs and 32 GB RAM; patient bootstrap and artifact checks 2–4 additional CPU hours; the temporal model 2–6 hours on one allocated A100 with 8 CPUs and 32 GB RAM, depending on cohort size and epochs. The future solver envelope permits 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds. No GPU or scientific fit was run in this discovery branch; ordinary shell CUDA visibility is not evidence about allocated-job feasibility. If the temporal alternative cannot meet the declared envelope after a bounded readiness check, defer it with the baseline still primary rather than changing the estimand or shrinking the cohort.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence would be a clinically nontrivial, directionally consistent persistent-versus-stable 48-hour risk difference with uncertainty excluding a negligible range, persistence across destination and observed-code-status strata, no comparable process-only gradient, and adequate calibration. If the temporal model improves held-out calibration/Brier and separates persistent from intermittent trajectories beyond the fixed baseline, it supports added temporal information; it still does not prove mechanism or causality.

Adverse evidence would be no reproducible contrast, reversal, high risk concentrated in insufficiently observed patients, attenuation after observation-density adjustment, or disappearance within destination/code-status strata. That would favor residual severity, discharge selection, treatment limitation, or surveillance artifacts over a portable physiologic discharge signal. A model improvement that vanishes on held-out patients or occurs only in one destination/observation stratum is adverse to a general physiologic interpretation.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse event cells, unstable bootstrap intervals, high missingness, unresolved discharge timing, poor calibration despite discrimination, or inadequate support for destination/code-status strata. It should trigger replication or measurement work, not a favorable conclusion.

Additional expert review or another study is required to determine whether a signal represents true oxygen requirement, respiratory physiology, clinician intent, appropriate discharge timing, or a beneficial intervention. Prospective delayed-discharge or monitoring trials and an external hospital are required for causal or transportability claims.
