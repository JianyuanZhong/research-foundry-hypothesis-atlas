# Asynchronous Organ Recovery: A Multi-Organ Recovery Phenotype Predicts ICU Deterioration

## Scientific Question

After circulatory stabilization in critically ill patients, do patients with **asynchronous organ recovery** (i.e., persistent impairment in one or more organs after stabilization of others) have higher rates of subsequent deterioration compared to those with synchronous multi-organ recovery?

## Hypothesis Statement

**Hypothesis:** In patients who have achieved hemodynamic stability (sustained MAP ≥ 65 mmHg for ≥6 hours without escalating vasopressor requirements) following sepsis or shock, those exhibiting asynchronous organ recovery—defined as persistent elevation in organ-specific markers (kidney: creatinine > baseline + 0.3 mg/dL for ≥12 hours; respiratory: PaO2/FiO2 < 300 for ≥12 hours) after initial stabilization—are at significantly higher risk of subsequent clinical deterioration (requiring escalation of organ support or death) compared to patients with synchronous multi-organ recovery.

**Expected effect size:** Hazard ratio for deterioration ≥1.5 (95% CI: 1.3-1.8) for asynchronous vs synchronous recovery groups.

**Falsification criteria:** If the hazard ratio is <1.2 (95% CI: 0.9-1.5), the hypothesis is falsified, suggesting that organ-specific recovery dynamics do not independently predict deterioration after stabilization.

## Clinical Importance

Current ICU practice relies heavily on global severity scores (SOFA, qSOFA) and single timepoint assessments that may miss the temporal dynamics of organ recovery. The asynchronous recovery phenotype could:
1. Identify high-risk patients who require intensified monitoring despite "stable" global scores
2. Inform timing of interventions (e.g., when to escalate vs. de-escalate support)
3. Reveal that organ-specific recovery trajectories matter more than aggregate scores for predicting deterioration

## Study Population

**Inclusion criteria:**
1. Age ≥18 years
2. ICU stay with at least 24 hours duration
3. Admission with diagnosis of sepsis (ICD-10: B95.-, R65.20) or shock (ICD-10: R57.-)
4. At least 12 hours of continuous ICU monitoring (chartevents with itemid mapping to vital signs)

**Exclusion criteria:**
1. Pre-existing chronic organ failure (baseline creatinine ≥1.5 mg/dL, known lung disease)
2. Trauma-related ICU admission (to isolate sepsis/shock physiology)
3. Transient ICU stays (<24 hours)

## Proposed Analysis Approach

### Baseline Approach (Traditional)
1. Define "stabilization" as: MAP ≥ 65 mmHg for ≥6 consecutive hours without escalating vasopressor dose
2. Define "recovery" as: Return of organ-specific markers to baseline (or within 20% of baseline)
3. Compare outcomes (death, escalation of support) between:
   - Group A: All organs recovered synchronously
   - Group B: At least one organ remains impaired after global stabilization

**Limitations of baseline:**
- Does not capture the *timing* of organ recovery
- Treats all "delayed" recovery equally, regardless of when the delay occurs
- May miss patients who recover sequentially rather than asynchronously

### Substantive Learned Alternative: Multi-Organ Recovery Trajectory Modeling

**Approach:** Train a temporal model to learn individual patient-specific organ recovery trajectories, then classify patients based on their trajectory pattern (synchronous vs. asynchronous).

**Model specification:**
- Input: Time-series of organ-specific markers (creatinine, PaO2/FiO2, urine output, MAP) at hourly resolution
- Output: Recovery trajectory classification (synchronous/asynchronous) + risk prediction
- Architecture: Simple RNN or Transformer-based model (can be implemented on CPU initially)
- Training: Use labeled outcomes (death, escalation) to train supervised model
- Evaluation: AUC-ROC, calibration, net reclassification improvement (NRI) vs. baseline

**Information revealed that baseline loses:**
1. **Individualized recovery timing**: The model learns when each patient's specific organs recover, not just whether they did
2. **Sequential recovery patterns**: Distinguishes between organs recovering in sequence vs. all at once
3. **Patient-specific risk stratification**: Identifies which organ combinations are most predictive of deterioration

**Baseline comparison:**
- Baseline: Simple categorical classification (synchronous vs. asynchronous)
- Alternative: Learned trajectory classification with risk prediction
- The alternative should show improved discrimination (ΔAUC ≥0.05) and better calibration

## Key Variables

### Primary Exposure: Asynchronous Recovery
- **Definition**: After achieving hemodynamic stabilization (MAP ≥65 mmHg for ≥6 hours), at least one organ-specific marker remains elevated for ≥12 hours
- **Operational definition**:
  - Kidney: Creatinine > baseline + 0.3 mg/dL for ≥12 hours
  - Respiratory: PaO2/FiO2 < 300 for ≥12 hours
  - Cardiovascular: Vasopressor requirement escalation

### Primary Outcome: Deterioration
- **Definition**: Within 48 hours of stabilization, either:
  - Death (deathtime in hosp/admissions)
  - Escalation of organ support (new ICU orders for ventilator, dialysis, etc.)

### Covariates
- Demographics: Age, gender, race
- Comorbidities: Chronic kidney disease, heart failure, COPD
- Baseline status: Pre-stabilization organ function
- Hospital factors: Hospital (for generalizability)

## Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis
1. **Define stabilization timepoint**: First timepoint where MAP ≥65 mmHg for ≥6 consecutive hours
2. **Define organ recovery status**: For each organ, determine if recovery occurred within 12 hours of stabilization
3. **Classify patients**:
   - Synchronous: All organs recovered within 12 hours
   - Asynchronous: At least one organ remained impaired at 12 hours post-stabilization
4. **Compare outcomes**: Cox proportional hazards model adjusting for covariates
   - Primary HR: Asynchronous vs. Synchronous
   - Secondary HRs: By organ combination (1 organ vs. 2+ organs impaired)

### Secondary Analysis
1. **Learned trajectory model**: Train model to predict deterioration based on recovery trajectory
2. **Comparison**: Compare model performance (AUC, calibration) to baseline approach
3. **Subgroup analysis**: By organ combination, by baseline severity, by hospital

### Missing Data Handling
- Multiple imputation for missing lab values (mice package)
- Sensitivity analysis with complete-case analysis

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis is **falsified** if:
1. Hazard ratio for asynchronous vs. synchronous recovery < 1.2 (95% CI: 0.9-1.5)
2. Learned trajectory model shows no improvement over baseline (ΔAUC < 0.05)
3. Results are consistent across multiple hospitals (suggests lack of generalizable signal)

The hypothesis is **supported** if:
1. Hazard ratio ≥ 1.5 (95% CI: 1.3-1.8)
2. Learned trajectory model shows ΔAUC ≥ 0.05 over baseline
3. Results are consistent across hospitals
