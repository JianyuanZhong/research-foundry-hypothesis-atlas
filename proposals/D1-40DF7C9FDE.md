# Provisional hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery and subsequent vulnerability

Status: provisional and evidence-bounded; no cohort counts, effect estimates, or clinical conclusions are claimed.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with an ICU stay and a prespecified creatinine-defined AKI episode who survive and remain observed at a fixed 48-hour landmark, persistent creatinine improvement without concurrent urine-output improvement may identify higher risk of subsequent renal deterioration or in-hospital death than concordant recovery, after accounting for the same landmark severity, treatment state, and observation intensity.

This is an incremental prognostic hypothesis, not a causal treatment effect, mechanism, or proof of true renal recovery. The strongest rival is that discordance is generated by current illness severity, selective measurement, diuretic/RRT exposure, fluid balance, or clinician-driven urine documentation rather than persistent renal vulnerability.

## Evidence boundary and opening

The available MIMIC-IV catalog supports the required measurement structure: ICU stays, laboratory events, output events, treatment/procedure events, and hospital outcomes can be inspected in the frozen source. The exact creatinine and urine-output item IDs, AKI stage rules, landmark feasibility, cohort size, and measurement density have not yet been verified in this episode. No result is claimed. The unresolved claim is whether the discordant trajectory adds information beyond a strong current-state baseline, rather than merely reflecting severity or monitoring practice.

## Falsifiable test

Define a leakage-safe 48-hour landmark after an explicitly prespecified creatinine-defined AKI episode. Compare a creatinine-only/current-state baseline with a trajectory model that adds the creatinine/urine discordance pattern, while matching or adjusting for landmark severity, treatment state, and observation intensity. The primary outcome is subsequent renal deterioration or in-hospital death, with a prespecified time window and censoring rules. Evaluate held-out discrimination/calibration and uncertainty, plus sensitivity analyses for measurement density, treatment exposure, and alternate AKI definitions.

Supportive evidence would be a reproducible incremental association with calibrated uncertainty in held-out data, not merely a larger model. Adverse evidence would be no incremental information or attenuation after severity/observation stress tests. Inconclusive evidence would be sparse discordant episodes, unstable estimates, or inability to verify the required measurements. None of these outcomes establishes mechanism, fluid status, measured GFR/cystatin C, or treatment benefit.

## Method comparison

The simple baseline is a prespecified current-state model using landmark creatinine, urine output, vital/laboratory severity, treatment indicators, and observation counts. The substantive alternative is a trajectory model or mechanistic state comparison that retains the pre-landmark creatinine and urine-output path and explicitly tests whether discordance is informative beyond current state. The alternative can reveal whether apparent recovery is a persistent trajectory signal or a transient/severity artifact that the baseline loses. A neural or GPU model is not required; a CPU tabular fit is adequate for the proposed baseline and a bounded trajectory model unless feasibility inspection shows otherwise.
