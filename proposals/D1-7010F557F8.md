# Recovery order after circulatory stabilization and 72-hour organ-support re-escalation

Status: complete experiment proposal; no outcome analysis has been run. Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`.

## Clinical opening and exact claim

The strongest existing evidence supports a bounded statement: longitudinal organ-dysfunction patterns stratify sepsis prognosis beyond an admission snapshot [K1]; renal improvement is prognostically relevant but does not erase risk [K2]; and residual circulatory support can alter the risk associated with ventilator liberation [K3]. These observations do **not** establish that kidney-versus-respiratory recovery order after circulation has stabilized is independently informative.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis.** Among adult first ICU stays with a clinically meaningful episode of concurrent vasopressor-treated circulatory failure, invasive ventilation, and acute kidney dysfunction, followed by 12 hours without vasopressor infusion, the ordering of kidney and respiratory recovery observed during the next 24 hours predicts new respiratory, circulatory, or renal support in the subsequent 72 hours beyond a complete same-landmark severity snapshot and observation intensity. Prespecified contrast: kidney-first versus respiratory-first among stays in which both recover by the landmark. The direction is intentionally two-sided; the experiment estimates which ordering, if either, is higher risk.

This is a descriptive/prognostic, not causal or mechanistic, hypothesis. “Kidney-first” may mark persistent pulmonary injury; “respiratory-first” may mark residual renal/fluid vulnerability. The strongest rivals are (i) current severity/treatment burden and (ii) clinician observation/treatment intensity, which can create apparent recovery times.

**Clinical importance.** If order adds calibrated short-horizon information when both organs look recovered at the landmark, it can justify different post-stabilization monitoring or step-down caution. If it does not, clinicians and risk systems should prefer current state and charting context rather than infer “recovery debt” from sequence.

## Population, time zero, and no-leakage windows

Unit is the first ICU stay per `subject_id` in MIMIC-IV v3.1. Include age >=18 at ICU admission. Follow-up need not be complete: eligibility requires survival and continued ICU presence only through the landmark, after which ICU discharge is a competing event. Require:

1. **Qualifying joint-support episode:** within ICU hours 0–72, overlap of (a) any running vasopressor infusion, (b) invasive ventilation procedure interval, and (c) acute kidney dysfunction: KDIGO stage >=1 by creatinine or urine output, or RRT. Baseline creatinine is the minimum serum creatinine from hospital admission through 6 h after ICU admission; if unavailable, use the first ICU-window value and flag this weaker baseline. Kidney staging follows KDIGO thresholds; urine output requires available weight and >=18 observed hours in a 24-h window. This cohort enriches for genuine multiorgan recovery; it is not claimed to be all sepsis.
2. **Circulatory stabilization time `s`:** first time after the last overlap at which all qualifying vasopressor infusion intervals have ended and no vasopressor is recorded for the next 12 h. `s` is known only after that 12-h confirmation.
3. **Recovery-observation window:** `[s, s+24h)`. Define fixed landmark `t0=s+24h`. Exclude support re-escalation during this window from the primary analysis; it means stabilization failed before phenotype ascertainment and is reported separately.
4. At `t0`, patient is alive, still in the same ICU stay, without vasopressor or RRT, not invasively ventilated, and both organs meet recovery definitions. This restriction makes the primary order comparison hold the same binary organ-support state at the landmark.
5. **Follow-up:** `[t0,t0+72h]`, censored at ICU discharge. In-ICU death without prior re-escalation is a competing event, not counted as successful non-event. Discharge alive is also competing for cause-specific sensitivity and is the non-event state in the primary 24-h person-period model.

Respiratory recovery time is the end of the last invasive-ventilation interval in `[s,t0)`, with no new invasive ventilation through `t0`; extubation itself is not directly recorded, so this is a procedure-interval proxy. Kidney recovery time is the first 6-h boundary after which creatinine is <1.5x baseline, rolling urine output is >=0.5 mL/kg/h where observed, no RRT is active, and these conditions persist to `t0`. If creatinine or adequate urine/weight observation cannot establish recovery, kidney recovery is unknown—not failure. Primary order is kidney-first if kidney recovery precedes respiratory recovery by >=6 h, respiratory-first for the reverse, and synchronous if absolute difference <6 h. Primary model uses kidney-first versus respiratory-first; synchronous is retained descriptively and in secondary models.

Secondary full-cohort state groups at `t0`: both recovered, kidney-only recovered, respiratory-only recovered, neither recovered, and indeterminate coverage. They must not be interpreted as the primary order estimand.

## Outcomes and falsification

Primary outcome is first **support re-escalation** within 72 h:

- new invasive ventilation interval (item 225792) after >=6 h free;
- new vasopressor infusion (prespecified inputevents items below) after the 24-h recovery-observation period;
- new RRT procedure interval (225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809, 225955) after no RRT at `t0`.

Report components separately. Death without prior support is a competing event. Sensitivities: 48-h and 96-h horizons; exclude comfort-measures/limitation cases only if a prespecified diagnosis/note adjudication algorithm can be validated—otherwise report this as missing clinical adjudication, not an automated exclusion.

**Supportive:** the adjusted kidney-first versus respiratory-first risk difference has a 95% CI excluding 0 and absolute magnitude >=5 percentage points, is directionally stable across component outcomes and sensitivities, improves held-out integrated Brier score by >=0.005 or calibration-slope-corrected log loss over the complete current-state baseline, and survives observation-intensity adjustment/stratification.

**Adverse/falsifying:** CI excludes effects larger than ±5 points, or held-out performance improvement is <0.005 with calibrated current-state baseline, or the association reverses/vanishes after observation-intensity restriction while a charting-intensity negative control carries the signal. This refutes clinically useful independent ordering information, not all value of trajectories.

**Inconclusive:** CI spans both ±5 points, either order group has <100 outcome events, recovery times are unknown for >30% of otherwise eligible stays, calibration is unstable, or sensitivity estimates disagree materially. Revise measurement or seek another dataset; do not call a null.

## Variables, rivals, and analysis

At `t0`, baseline model covariates are age, sex, admission type/care unit, ICU time, Charlson comorbidity indicators, baseline creatinine-quality flag, current creatinine and ratio to baseline, 6/12/24-h urine output and coverage, current respiratory support status, last 6-h worst SpO2, FiO2 and respiratory rate, last PEEP/ventilator mode, current/last-6-h MAP, heart rate, temperature, GCS components, lactate, bilirubin, platelets, sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, BUN, hemoglobin, WBC, cumulative fluid input/output, weight, diuretic dose, prior RRT, invasive-ventilation duration, vasopressor duration and peak/count, and each organ's worst KDIGO/respiratory state in the recovery window. Include missingness indicators; median imputation is learned in training only. This deliberately “overmatches” current severity before adding order.

Observation-intensity rival variables are per-window counts and time since last measurement for creatinine, urine output, SpO2, FiO2, respiratory rate, MAP, blood gas, and lactate; distinct chart hours; and fraction of expected urine/chart bins observed. Primary comparisons are sequential:

- B0: prespecified order groups plus age/sex/time/care unit in a discrete-time complementary-log-log model.
- B1: B0 + all current severity/treatment covariates.
- B2 (primary): B1 + observation-intensity variables.
- Negative-control artifact check: charting/store lag (`storetime-charttime`) and counts predict apparent recovery order; rerun in dense-observation stratum (creatinine at least twice, urine coverage >=18 h, respiratory charting in >=12 distinct hours). A dominant intensity signal or order attenuation supports the measurement rival.
- Cause-specific Cox models for re-escalation, death, and discharge are sensitivity analyses. Use cluster-robust SE by subject (one stay makes this conservative), restricted cubic splines for continuous covariates, and variance-inflation/positivity checks. Report standardized 72-h risks and kidney-first minus respiratory-first risk difference with 1,000 subject bootstrap CIs.

No p-value-only claim. Complete-case analysis is sensitivity. Missing current values use training-fold imputation; missing recovery evidence creates “indeterminate,” never “not recovered.”

## Substantive longitudinal alternative

Fit a masked time-aware GRU (alternative acceptable: temporal convolutional network) to 6-h bins from the qualifying overlap through `t0`, using the same raw organ, treatment, and observation-intensity inputs plus masks and time-since-last-measurement. Target is discrete 24-h hazards for first re-escalation/death/discharge. It can reveal nonlinear duration, relapse, and cross-organ lag patterns that the single order label and landmark snapshot discard. Freeze architecture/hyperparameters before test evaluation; use training only for early stopping. Compare B2 versus B2+order and GRU on identical splits using 72-h AUPRC/AUROC, integrated Brier score, log loss, calibration intercept/slope, and component risks; bootstrap paired differences. The learned model is scientifically selected only if calibration is adequate and improvement is clinically material (Brier >=0.005) with stable ablation evidence that temporal order—not missingness masks alone—accounts for gain. Otherwise defer it and retain the transparent baseline.

## Split and multiplicity

Patient-level temporal split by deidentified anchor year: earliest 70% train, next 15% validation, latest 15% test; if anchor-year ties prevent target proportions, allocate whole years nearest those proportions. Fit preprocessing only on train. Test set is opened once after design freeze. Primary contrast/outcome/horizon is single. Component outcomes, state groups, thresholds, learned model, and 48/96-h horizons are secondary; use Benjamini–Hochberg within the secondary family and report unadjusted CIs as exploratory.

Clinical adjudication remains essential for goals-of-care limitations, extubation intent/failure, whether support was prophylactic/procedural, and whether urine records contain irrigation. MIMIC alone cannot establish mechanism, treatment benefit, transportability, or a monitoring threshold. Those require chart review, external validation (e.g. eICU with harmonized definitions), and ultimately a prospective study.

## Interpretation contract

Supportive results establish incremental prognostic information in this selected MIMIC cohort, not a biological recovery mechanism or benefit from intervention. Adverse results favor the same-time severity/observation explanation and argue against using order for monitoring. Inconclusive results motivate measurement validation or more data, not confirmation or refutation.
