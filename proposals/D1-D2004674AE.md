## Unresolved question, importance, and falsifiable hypothesis

After vasopressor-treated shock has resolved, should a markedly fluid-positive adult who remains invasively ventilated at ICU hour 48, with adequate recorded MAP, urine output and potassium, start or restart an ICU-input-system recorded furosemide or bumetanide delivery during the next 12 hours while the linked recorded ventilation interval remains open, or avoid that delivery until the interval closes or 12 hours elapse?

The decision is consequential because active fluid removal may assist airway liberation but may also worsen kidney injury, dialysis risk, death or unfavorable disposition. Relevant passages were inspected in frozen full XML for Kuriyama et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13476641; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100120; source `[source checksum]`), Bircher et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13154636; DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100075; source `[source checksum]`), and Hansford et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC12927355; DOI 10.1136/bmjmed-2024-001280; source `[source checksum]`). They support that fluid burden matters, optimal diuretic timing remains uncertain, and eligibility/assignment/follow-up must align; they do not establish benefit from this policy or remove observational confounding. The research-ambition papers are demonstrations only. No unavailable cancer main text or STAR Methods is claimed as read.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is that this is an important unresolved decision and that MIMIC contains computable but imperfect recorded delivery, ventilation, physiology and same-admission outcomes. The experiment tests:

> In the prespecified measured-overlap discovery population, assignment-compatible follow-up under A12 increases the lower bound of five-day same-admission recorded 48-hour airway-event-free extubation by more than 0.05 versus B12, while one-sided upper limits for record-defined 48-hour creatinine rise, observed RRT7 and terminal/hospice/acute-transfer harm remain below +0.03, +0.05 and +0.03. A12 can be satisfied only by qualifying loop onset strictly before the linked recorded airway boundary C.

This is a falsifiable dynamic recorded-policy association, not a pharmacologic receipt effect, proof of durable extubation or renal safety, or a recommendation.

## Falsification, interpretation and verification

Output separate execution, validity, directional-result and safety-alert axes.

- Infeasible: a required computation fails; no directional claim.
- Validity-inconclusive: computation succeeds but any frozen positivity, balance, calibration, negative-control, ambiguity, contamination, timing, observation, proxy, process-control or material-reversal gate fails.
- Adverse-FWER: validity passes and at least one positive adverse-oriented family is Holm-rejected. This is an observational concern, not proof of toxicity.
- Supportive-recorded: validity passes, no adjusted adverse family, airway lower limit >+0.05, chart-augmented and continuous-ICU lower limits >0, renal/RRT/terminal upper limits remain below margins, disposition is not adverse and every proxy diagnostic passes.
- Margin-falsified: no adjusted adverse family and airway upper limit ≤+0.05; this rejects the prespecified recorded-proxy improvement, not every physiologic benefit.
- Statistically inconclusive: every other feasible, validity-passing result.

Always report safety alerts for positive adverse estimates, raw p<0.05 and Holm rejection; exploratory alerts cannot be promoted to formal adverse evidence. Correct computation paired with causal, durable-extubation, renal-safety, equivalence or recommendation language must fail verification.
