## 1. Unresolved question (unchanged)

Patients discharged alive from the ICU are frequently readmitted or die in hospital, yet ICU
outcome scores use the *mean level* of vitals. It is unresolved whether pre-discharge
physiologic **instability** — variability and trend of continuously-charted vitals and rising
oxygen requirement in a fixed window before ICU discharge — carries prognostic information
**beyond the mean level of the same signals** and beyond **observation intensity**.

- **H-instability (leading claim):** genuine residual dysregulation → variability/trend
  features add discrimination beyond level on a held-out, temporally locked split.
- **R-level/measurement (strongest rival):** apparent "instability" is explained by (a) the
  mean severity level and (b) observation/review intensity — sicker patients are charted more
  often, and charting frequency/missingness is itself outcome-correlated and value-free [K3].
- **R-death (rival added this episode):** the composite outcome conflates two competing events;
  readmission and death may have different pre-discharge signatures [see §5.1].

## 2. Hypothesis vs already-supported conclusions

Already supported (NOT re-tested): ICU readmission models are confounded and only moderately
discriminative [K1]; dynamic variability carries prognostic information in critical illness
[K2]; value-free measurement patterns alone predict ICU outcome [K3]. The unresolved,
falsifiable claim tested here is the **separation** of pre-discharge variability/trend from
mean level *and* observation intensity, at the **discharge-decision** boundary — none of
[K1]-[K3] isolates that.

## 4. Estimand, baselines, learned alternative (families unchanged; traps added)

Locked temporal split over `patients.anchor_year_group` for all models. Primary estimand =
**incremental discrimination ΔAUROC (M1 − M0)** with prespecified **equivalence margin 0.01**;
plus calibration (slope/intercept), AUPRC at the COMPUTED 9.72% prevalence, and decision-curve
net benefit at that same rate. Cluster bootstrap by `subject_id`.

- **B0 (interpretable snapshot baseline):** penalized logistic regression on **level only** —
  last pre-discharge MAP/HR/RR/SpO2/FiO2/temperature, vasopressor-active, ventilation-active,
  age/sex, ICU LOS.
- **B1 (interpretable instability model):** B0 **+** within-window variability/trend per signal
  (SD, IQR, range, CV, Theil-Sen slope, fraction outside a predeclared unstable band) and
  O2-requirement trend.
- **A1 (substantive learned alternative):** a small sequence model over the irregular 12-h
  multivariate series (GRU or 1-D temporal conv) with **time-since-last-observation channels**,
  trained on the same TRAIN split, predicting the same composite. **What A1 can reveal that
  B1 loses:** *order and coupling* of fluctuations — e.g. a monotone deterioration trajectory,
  late-window acceleration, or coupled HR–RR–SpO2 excursions — which scalar variance/trend
  summaries compress away. Target = composite outcome; outputs = calibrated probabilities;
  uncertainty = cluster-bootstrap AUROC CIs + reliability curve + ECE/Brier vs B1; baseline
  comparison = ΔAUROC(A1−B1) and ΔAUROC(A1−B0) on the locked TEST set. **Resources:** B0/B1 CPU
  (minutes); A1 ~ tens of thousands of short sequences, one A100 80 GB via `ehr_job_submit`
  (gpus=1) with early stopping — estimated <1 h per seed, 3 seeds; falls back to CPU if the
  bounded probe shows no convergence benefit. A1 is *not* required to state the negative.

**Negative controls (value-free):** `M-obs` = {chartevents rows/hour in window, time since last
measurement, fraction of window charts in last hour} — NO physiologic values [K3].
`M-obs-only` must not match B1, and adding M-obs to B1 must not collapse B1's ΔAUROC.
**Permutation control:** shuffled labels → AUROC ≈ 0.5.

## 5. Hardening against the strongest rivals (new this episode)

### 5.1 Competing risk of death vs readmission
The composite hides two competing events. Primary analysis keeps the composite (both are
adverse discharge outcomes), but we add a **cause-specific / Fine–Gray sensitivity**: landmark
at `outtime`, event 1 = ICU readmission (censoring at death in hospital w/o readmission), event
2 = in-hospital death w/o readmission; report subdistribution HR/AUROC for each cause
separately. Interpretation rule: if variability predicts readmission but NOT death (or vice
versa), the composite is not a single mechanism — state that and report per-cause estimates
rather than a single number. COMPUTED support: 4,901 readmission vs 1,521 death-in-7d events
distinct; 2,646 deaths at any time after `outtime` — the death component is non-trivial.

### 5.2 Discharge-destination / treatment-limitation confounding
`hosp/admissions.discharge_location` (home vs facility/hospice) and `icu/icustays.last_careunit`
are **directly observed**; patients discharged to hospice/comfort care or with treatment
limitations (DNR/DNI proxy) may be "readmitted less but die more" or vice versa. Hardening:
(1) **stratify/restrict** a sensitivity analysis to `discharge_location` home/home-health vs
facility; (2) include `discharge_location`, `last_careunit`, and a decedent flag
(`patients.dod`/`admissions.deathtime`) as covariates in B1' to test whether ΔAUROC survives;
(3) treat any large shift as evidence the instability signal is partly a discharge-disposition
artifact. `admissions.discharge_location` value vocabulary must be inspected in the frozen data
before use (NOT-COMPUTED this episode).

### 5.3 Observation intensity (value-free)
