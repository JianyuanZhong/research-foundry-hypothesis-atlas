# Working proposal: discordant visceral fat and thigh muscle fat infiltration

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

UK Biobank body MRI provides simultaneous visceral fat volume and posterior thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI), while repeated assessments provide handgrip strength, falls, HbA1c, and incident diagnoses. The unresolved question is whether these two fat-distribution measures identify distinct longitudinal risk pathways: visceral fat for metabolic disease and MFI for subsequent functional decline or falls.

The testable hypothesis is: among adults with baseline MRI and no prevalent metabolic disease or functional limitation, the standardized five-year risk difference for incident type 2 diabetes associated with one standard deviation higher visceral fat is larger than that associated with one standard deviation higher MFI; and the standardized five-year risk difference for a subsequent fall associated with one standard deviation higher MFI is larger than that associated with one standard deviation higher visceral fat. The comparison is made within the common baseline population and after conditioning on total body fat percentage, age, sex, smoking, physical activity proxy, and baseline disease. This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim.

The available evidence supports that visceral fat and muscle infiltration are measurable and associated with health outcomes, but does not establish distinct longitudinal risk profiles after conditioning on total fat. The experiment will test whether the differential pattern exists and is robust to adjustment and temporal ordering.

## Why it matters

A person with normal BMI but high visceral fat may need metabolic surveillance, while a person with high muscle infiltration may be at higher risk of disability and falls. Separating these patterns could inform which clinical pathway to prioritize. However, the available data are observational and cannot establish causality.

## Strongest evidence and rival explanations

The strongest rival explanation is reverse causation and low activity: existing disease or low physical activity may cause fat redistribution and later outcomes, rather than fat distribution causing outcomes. Other rivals include measurement error, selection into MRI participants, and self-reported falls subject to recall and ascertainment differences.

The key comparison is within a common baseline population with similar total fat, comparing visceral fat and MFI across metabolic versus functional outcomes. A substantive result would require a differential association pattern that persists after adjustment for total fat, age, sex, smoking, activity proxy and baseline disease. If the pattern is absent or disappears after adjustment, the hypothesis is not supported.

Use repeat assessments for handgrip strength, falls, and HbA1c if available, but do not use concurrent measurements as outcomes.

## Exact estimand

The primary estimand is a contrast in standardized five-year risk differences:
- For metabolic outcomes: the difference in standardized five-year risk of incident type 2 diabetes between one standard deviation higher visceral fat and one standard deviation higher MFI, within the common baseline population.
- For functional outcomes: the difference in standardized five-year risk of a subsequent fall between one standard deviation higher MFI and one standard deviation higher visceral fat, within the common baseline population.

The estimand is a standardized risk difference, not a hazard, because the question is about the magnitude of risk at a clinically relevant time horizon (five years) and the comparison is between two exposure measures. Standardization to the common baseline population removes confounding by age, sex, smoking, activity proxy, and baseline disease.

## Strongest rival explanation and comparison

The strongest rival is reverse causation and low activity. To distinguish it, the analysis will:
1. Exclude prevalent metabolic disease and functional limitation at baseline.
2. Adjust for baseline handgrip strength and falls in the last year.
3. Use a temporal index at baseline MRI and follow forward.
4. Perform a sensitivity analysis excluding outcomes occurring in the first year after baseline.
5. Compare the pattern in a subset with repeat assessments to check for consistency.

If the differential pattern is absent or disappears after adjustment, the hypothesis is not supported.

## Falsification and interpretation

- Support: visceral fat has stronger association with metabolic outcomes and MFI has stronger association with functional outcomes, with a prespecified interaction or differential standardized effect, after adjustment.
- Adverse: no differential pattern, or MFI is not associated with functional outcomes after adjustment.
- Inconclusive: insufficient overlap in total fat, too few events, or associations driven by early outcomes/missingness.

A positive result would support distinct risk pathways but not causality. It would justify prospective validation and potentially targeted screening. It would not establish that changing fat distribution prevents disease.

## Method comparison

Baseline: adjusted Cox regression and standardized risk differences for each outcome, comparing visceral fat versus MFI within total-fat strata. This is interpretable and feasible on CPU.

Learned alternative: a flexible gradient-boosted or neural model predicting outcomes from fat distribution, total fat and covariates, with permutation importance and calibrated uncertainty. This could reveal nonlinear interactions that linear models miss, but adds complexity and interpretability cost. For this question, the baseline is sufficient to test the primary hypothesis; the learned alternative is deferred unless the baseline shows a strong but nonlinear pattern.
