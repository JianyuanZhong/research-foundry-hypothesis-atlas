# Ventilatory Burden and Liberation Failure in eICU: Complete Proposal

## Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

**Unresolved question:** Among mechanically ventilated ICU patients, does a composite measure of ventilatory burden that accounts for body size and respiratory mechanics (peak pressure, compliance) provide more information about subsequent liberation failure than tidal volume alone?

**Clinical importance:** Tidal volume (Vt) is the most widely monitored ventilation parameter, but the same Vt represents different mechanical stress for patients of different body sizes and with different respiratory mechanics. If a size- and mechanics-adjusted burden measure adds prognostic information, this could inform individualized ventilation strategies and liberation decisions. Liberation failure (failure to be liberated from mechanical ventilation within 48 hours of a weaning attempt) is associated with prolonged ICU stays, increased ventilator-associated complications, and higher mortality.

**Strongest claim already supported by available evidence:** eICU contains timestamped ventilation parameters including tidal volume, peak pressure, plateau pressure, respiratory rate, compliance, and FiO2 in the respiratoryCharting table, and patient demographics including height and weight in the patient table. The respiratoryCare table contains ventstartoffset and ventendoffset for defining ventilation episodes.

**Untested claim:** A composite ventilatory-burden measure (incorporating Vt, peak pressure, compliance, and body size) independently predicts liberation failure beyond tidal volume alone.

## Scientific Opening

The scientific opening is that tidal volume alone may not capture the full mechanical stress imposed by ventilation. The same Vt represents a different burden for patients of different body sizes and with different respiratory mechanics. A patient with low compliance (stiff lungs) receiving 6 mL/kg IBW has a higher peak pressure and greater mechanical stress than a patient with normal compliance at the same Vt.

The closest relevant prior work inspected includes:
1. Mao & An (2026) [K1] — Review of mechanical ventilation strategies, VILI mechanisms, and lung-protective ventilation
2. Burns et al. (2026) [K2] — Review of liberation from mechanical ventilation in ARDS/AHRF
3. Sato et al. (2026) [K3] — Meta-analysis of mechanical power and mortality in mechanically ventilated patients

The remaining question is whether a size- and mechanics-adjusted burden measure adds information beyond tidal volume in a multicenter eICU cohort.

## Hypothesis

**Primary hypothesis:** Among mechanically ventilated ICU patients, a composite ventilatory-burden measure (incorporating tidal volume, peak pressure, compliance, and body size) independently predicts liberation failure (failure to be liberated from mechanical ventilation within 48 hours) beyond tidal volume alone.

**Specific formulation:** At the same tidal volume, patients with higher ventilatory burden (higher peak pressure, lower compliance, higher Vt/kg) have a higher risk of subsequent liberation failure.

## Experiment Design

### Population
- **Inclusion criteria:**
  - ICU patients in eICU with at least one mechanical ventilation episode (ventstartoffset present in respiratoryCare)
  - Age ≥ 18 years
  - At least one recorded tidal volume in respiratoryCharting
  - Height and weight available in patient table
  - Ventilation duration ≥ 24 hours (to ensure meaningful ventilation exposure)
- **Exclusion criteria:**
  - Patients with < 24 hours of ICU stay
  - Patients with missing height or weight
  - Patients with no respiratoryCharting data during ventilation

### Temporal Boundaries
- **Index time:** Time of first mechanical ventilation episode (ventstartoffset from respiratoryCare)
- **Exposure window:** First 24 hours of mechanical ventilation (from ventstartoffset to ventstartoffset + 24 hours)
- **Outcome window:** 48 hours from index time (liberation by ventstartoffset + 48 hours)
- **Follow-up:** Until liberation or 48 hours, whichever comes first

### Variables

#### Exposure: Ventilatory Burden Measures
1. **Tidal volume (Vt):** Mean Vt during exposure window (from `Tidal Volume Observed (VT)`)
2. **Vt/kg IBW:** Tidal volume per kg ideal body weight (calculated from Vt and admissionheight using Devine formula)
3. **Peak pressure:** Mean peak pressure during exposure window
4. **Plateau pressure:** Mean plateau pressure during exposure window
5. **Compliance:** Mean compliance during exposure window
6. **Composite ventilatory burden score (VBS):**
   - VBS = (Vt/kg IBW) × (Peak Pressure / 30) × (1 / (Compliance / 50))
   - This captures the interaction between volume, pressure, and mechanics
   - Higher VBS = greater ventilatory burden

#### Covariates
1. **Demographics:** Age, gender, race, height, weight, BMI
2. **Severity:** APACHE II score (from apachePatientResult), GCS (from apacheApsVar)
3. **Comorbidities:** From diagnosis table (sepsis, ARDS, pneumonia, heart failure, COPD)
4. **Ventilation settings:** FiO2, PEEP, ventilation mode, inspiratory flow
5. **Respiratory status:** RSBI, spontaneous respiratory rate, sedation depth
6. **Other ICU factors:** Vasopressor use (from infusionDrug), fluid balance (from intakeOutput)

#### Outcome
- **Primary outcome:** Liberation failure — failure to be liberated from mechanical ventilation within 48 hours of index time
  - Defined as: ventendoffset > ventstartoffset + 48 hours, OR no ventendoffset recorded within 48 hours
  - Liberation = ventendoffset present and ≤ ventstartoffset + 48 hours
- **Secondary outcomes:**
  - Time to liberation (hours)
  - ICU mortality (unitdischargestatus)
  - Hospital mortality
  - Ventilator-free days at 48 hours

### Baselines
1. **Null model:** Prevalence of liberation failure in the cohort
2. **Vt-only model:** Logistic regression with Vt (and Vt/kg IBW) as the sole predictor
3. **Clinical model:** Logistic regression with standard clinical variables (age, APACHE II, FiO2, PEEP) but no ventilation-specific burden measures

### Analysis Plan

#### Primary Analysis
1. **Descriptive analysis:**
   - Cohort characteristics by liberation status (liberated vs. failed)
   - Distribution of Vt, peak pressure, compliance, VBS by liberation status
   - Missing data patterns

2. **Univariate analysis:**
   - Association of each ventilatory parameter with liberation failure
   - AUROC, sensitivity, specificity for each parameter

3. **Multivariate analysis:**
   - **Model 1 (Vt-only):** Logistic regression with Vt and Vt/kg IBW
   - **Model 2 (VBS model):** Logistic regression with VBS (composite)
   - **Model 3 (Full model):** Logistic regression with Vt, peak pressure, compliance, VBS, and clinical covariates
   - Compare models using:
     - AUROC (with 95% CI)
     - Calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow test, calibration plot)
     - Likelihood ratio test
     - AIC/BIC
     - Net reclassification improvement (NRI)

4. **Subgroup analysis:**
   - By severity (APACHE II < 15 vs. ≥ 15)
   - By ARDS status
   - By ventilation mode (volume-controlled vs. pressure-controlled)
   - By hospital (multicenter check)

#### Sensitivity Analysis
1. **Alternative VBS definitions:**
   - VBS without compliance (Vt/kg × Peak Pressure)
   - VBS with plateau pressure instead of peak pressure
   - VBS with different normalization constants
2. **Alternative exposure windows:**
   - First 12 hours instead of 24 hours
   - First 48 hours instead of 24 hours
3. **Alternative outcome definitions:**
   - Liberation within 24 hours
   - Liberation within 72 hours
4. **Missing data handling:**
   - Complete case analysis
   - Multiple imputation (if > 5% missing)

### Falsification Criteria
The hypothesis is **not supported** if:
1. **No incremental predictive value:** VBS does not significantly improve AUROC beyond the Vt-only model (ΔAUROC < 0.01, p > 0.05)
2. **Confounding by severity:** The association disappears after adjustment for APACHE II and other severity markers
3. **Inconsistency across hospitals:** The association is not directionally consistent across ≥ 80% of hospitals
4. **No clinical significance:** Even if statistically significant, the absolute risk difference for VBS quartiles is < 5%
5. **Calibration failure:** The model is poorly calibrated (Hosmer-Lemeshow p < 0.05) without improvement from VBS

### Interpretation of Results
- **Supportive:** VBS significantly improves AUROC and calibration beyond Vt-only, with consistent direction across hospitals and subgroups
- **Adverse:** VBS does not improve prediction beyond Vt, suggesting that tidal volume alone captures the relevant information
- **Inconclusive:** Small cohort, high missing data rates, or wide confidence intervals

## Method Comparison

- **Simple baseline:** Logistic regression with tidal volume and body-size-adjusted tidal volume (mL/kg) as predictors of liberation failure.
- **Learned alternative:** Gradient boosting with time-series features including tidal volume trajectory, respiratory rate, plateau pressure, and vasopressor exposure.

The learned alternative could reveal nonlinear relationships and interactions between ventilation parameters that the linear baseline would miss. If the learned model does not substantially outperform the baseline, this would suggest that the relationship between ventilatory burden and liberation failure is adequately captured by linear measures.
