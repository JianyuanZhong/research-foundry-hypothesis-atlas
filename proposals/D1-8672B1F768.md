# Testing-process dependence and abrupt reduced testing before observable ICU escalation

## Question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

The eICU-02 seed asks whether models using testing frequency and missingness transport worse across hospitals; eICU-08 asks whether abrupt reduced testing during instability precedes deterioration. The strongest pre-analysis evidence is only that the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot has ICU-relative lab result times/values, vital observations, infusion and respiratory-care records, patient disposition, hospital identifiers, and admission-severity fields alignable by ICU stay. The lab table records result events, not orders, clinical intent, reasons for missingness, interface completeness, or adjudicated physiology.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among adult stays alive and observable at a 12-hour landmark, a process-augmented early-warning model has a larger held-out-hospital transport penalty in calibration and/or Brier score than a matched clinical-state model that uses the same rows, value channels, outcome and split but excludes explicit lab counts, test-free gaps, analyte-panel composition and result recency. It may improve mixed-hospital validation yet transport worse, indicating hospital-specific observation practice.

Secondary hypothesis: among intervals with measured instability in hours 6–12 and a qualifying lab result in both hours 0–6 and 6–12, an abrupt observed testing reduction in hours 6–12 is associated with the subsequent observable escalation/death composite in hours 12–36 after adjustment for measured state, baseline risk, hospital and coverage. Null/reversal, disappearance after coverage/interface/disposition sensitivity, or failure in held-out hospitals falsifies it. Neither hypothesis is causal: a result gap is not a monitoring intervention and the composite is not adjudicated deterioration.

Clinical importance is deployment safety: if testing-process features help locally but damage cross-hospital calibration, deployment should exclude or transport-calibrate them rather than mistake observability for physiology. A stable reduced-testing signal would motivate prospective monitoring review. This is a substantive alternative to the parent hypotension-duration/observed-creatinine question because it tests the transportability of measurement-dependent prediction, not pressure exposure; it does not claim greater causal importance.

## Falsification and evidence limits

Supportive transport evidence requires a reproducible process-model held-out penalty, especially calibration or Brier, with uncertainty excluding zero or a clinically material threshold, possible mixed-hospital benefit, and persistence across lab normalization, revisions, coverage, disposition and complete-case sensitivity without one-hospital dominance.

Supportive reduced-testing evidence requires a positive adjusted association among measured-unstable intervals, stable for the binary exposure, component outcomes, coverage-adjusted models and held-out hospitals. Adverse results are no penalty, equal/better held-out calibration, null/reversed reduced-testing association, or disappearance after controls; these do not prove monitoring irrelevant. Inconclusive results include sparse hospitals/intervals/events, unstable normalization, missing disposition timing, inability to distinguish interface interruption, nonconvergence, one-site dominance, or intervals spanning clinically important benefit and harm.

Computationally checkable outputs are hashes/headers, cohort flow, normalization/deduplication, test windows, instability flags, splits, onset rules, fits, held-out predictions, calibration/Brier/AUPRC/AUROC, decision curves, convergence, uncertainty and sensitivities. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for true deterioration, test appropriateness/intent, end-of-life limitation, interface completeness, whether extra testing improves outcomes, and deployment decisions. Missing evidence includes test orders, rationale, complete goals-of-care, pre-ICU physiology, external validation hospitals and adjudicated deterioration labels. No verifier may call this causal.
