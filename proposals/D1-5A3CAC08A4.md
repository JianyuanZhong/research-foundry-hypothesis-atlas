# Early Post-TACE Hepatic Deterioration as a Prognostic Marker in HCC

## Question
In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who undergo transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does a composite measure of early hepatic deterioration during the first 7 days after the procedure predict subsequent hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days, after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and post-procedure observation intensity?

## Clinical importance
TACE is a common locoregional treatment for HCC, but a subset of patients experience early hepatic deterioration that may signal a need for escalation to systemic therapy or closer surveillance. Identifying these patients could inform treatment-selection decisions and resource allocation. The unresolved question is whether the early laboratory trajectory itself carries prognostic information beyond baseline liver function, or whether it merely reflects more intensive post-procedure monitoring.

## Evidence and closest prior work
- [K1] Lan et al. (2026) demonstrate that dynamic biomarker trajectories (gene expression) can be used for risk prediction in liver disease, supporting the use of trajectory-based measures for hepatic deterioration.
- [K2] Aletras et al. (2026) argue that dynamic function deterioration (renal function in acute heart failure) should be interpreted as a marker of disease severity rather than isolated injury, supporting the use of dynamic measures for prognostic assessment.
- [K3] Decraecker et al. (2026) review the limitations of Child-Pugh scoring and the potential value of dynamic liver function monitoring and ALBI grade in HCC patients.

## Data bindings
- Source: HCC dataset (12 source files)
- Population: Patients with HCC who undergo TACE (procedure code in the procedure table)
- Early labs: bilirubin, albumin, INR measured in the first 7 days post-TACE (laboratory table, joined on patient_id and procedure_date)
- Outcome: hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days (outcome table)
- Covariates: baseline liver function (Child-Pugh, ALBI), tumor burden (number and size of tumors), post-procedure observation intensity (number of lab draws in the first 7 days)
- Join keys: patient_id, procedure_date
- Time fields: procedure_date (index), outcome_date (90-day window)

## Sample size
- 20,064 TACE patients
- ~16,000 with complete early labs
- 817 outcome events

## Analysis plan
1. Define the early hepatic deterioration composite (bilirubin, albumin, INR) as a weighted sum of standardized values.
2. Fit a Cox regression model with the composite as the primary predictor, adjusted for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and observation intensity.
3. Sensitivity analysis: alternative 7-day windows (3-day, 5-day, 10-day), composite recalculation, and sample size/event count considerations.
4. Decision-curve analysis (DCA) evaluating net benefit of using the early deterioration composite to guide escalation to systemic therapy at threshold probabilities 0.10-0.50.
5. Negative-control analysis: test whether the composite predicts a non-hepatic outcome (e.g., 90-day readmission for non-hepatic causes) to rule out observation-intensity confounding.

## Prognostic vs. causal distinction
This study estimates a prognostic association, not a causal effect of any treatment. The composite is a marker of early hepatic deterioration, not a treatment intervention. Causal inference would require a different design (e.g., instrumental variable or marginal structural model).

## Trajectory-based alternative
The fixed 7-day composite is a single endpoint-derived summary. A trajectory-based alternative tests whether the temporal pattern of hepatic deterioration over days 1-7 carries prognostic information beyond the endpoint composite.

### Trajectory measures
- AUC (trapezoidal rule) for each of bilirubin, albumin, and INR over days 1-7.
- Slope (least-squares regression) for each of bilirubin, albumin, and INR over days 1-7.

### Trajectory composite
- Weighted sum of standardized AUCs and slopes, with equal weights as the prespecified primary model.

### Missing-data handling
- Primary strategy: linear interpolation (minimum 3 measured days per analyte).
- Sensitivity strategies: last-observation-carried-forward (LOCF) and complete-case.

### Nested Cox model
- Model 1: fixed composite only.
- Model 2: fixed + trajectory composite.
- Model 3: fixed + trajectory + interaction term.

### Incremental prediction
- C-index, calibration, decision-curve analysis, and integrated Brier score.

### Falsification criterion
- The trajectory approach is not supported if the trajectory term is not significant (p > 0.10) AND the C-index improvement is < 0.01.

### Interpretation
- Supportive: trajectory term significant (p <= 0.10) AND C-index improvement >= 0.01.
- Adverse: trajectory term significant (p <= 0.10) BUT C-index improvement < 0.01 (statistical significance without clinical relevance).
- Inconclusive: trajectory term not significant (p > 0.10) AND C-index improvement < 0.01.

## Falsification criteria
- The hypothesis is falsified if the early hepatic deterioration composite does not significantly predict the 90-day outcome after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and observation intensity (p > 0.05).
- The trajectory-based alternative is falsified if the trajectory term is not significant (p > 0.10) AND the C-index improvement is < 0.01.

## Limitations
- The HCC dataset is a single-center dataset; generalizability to other centers is uncertain.
- The 90-day outcome window may miss later hepatic decompensation.
- The composite is a summary measure; individual analyte trajectories may have different prognostic value.
- The trajectory-based alternative requires multiple lab draws per patient; missing data may limit the analysis.
