# Grip-triggered cystatin C testing to uncover clinically consequential creatinine–cystatin discordance

## Decision problem and hypothesis

Creatinine can make kidney function look reassuring in people with low muscle strength, yet routine cystatin C testing is uncommon. The decision is **which adults with creatinine eGFR (eGFRcr) at least 60 mL/min/1.73 m² should receive a confirmatory cystatin C assay** when only 20% can be tested.

**Primary hypothesis:** among UK Biobank adults with baseline eGFRcr 60–89 and no prior inpatient acute kidney injury (AKI), adding measured hand-grip strength to an otherwise available selective-testing rule (age, sex, BMI, eGFRcr) will (i) increase by at least 25% the number of people detected per assay whose combined creatinine–cystatin estimate is below 60, in a held-out sample, and (ii) those reclassified people will have at least 50% higher adjusted 10-year risk of first AKI hospitalization than unreclassified people. This is a joint hypothesis: both claims must hold.

This does **not** hypothesize that cystatin C is generally prognostic; that is already supported. It tests whether an inexpensive strength measurement adds enough *incremental targeting utility* to change who should receive a confirmatory assay, and whether the reclassification found by that rule marks subsequent kidney vulnerability.

## What is known, what remains unresolved, and why it matters

The strongest existing evidence supports that cystatin-C reclassification stratifies risk, not that either marker is a gold-standard measurement of GFR. A full-text meta-analysis of 90,750 general-population participants reported that cystatin-based downward reclassification was associated with higher death, cardiovascular-death, and end-stage kidney disease risk, with net reclassification improvement 0.23 for death and 0.10 for end-stage kidney disease (Shlipak et al., NEJM 2013, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1214234; inspected full PMC text, frozen source c4353d6f...). A full-text Cardiovascular Health Study analysis linked a lower eGFRcys−eGFRcr difference to frailty and mortality, while explicitly noting creatinine's sensitivity to muscle mass (Potok et al., AJKD 2020, DOI 10.1053/j.ajkd.2020.05.018; inspected full PMC text, frozen source 02931ae9...). A 138-person Korean inpatient study proposed muscle-mass cutoffs for cystatin testing but lacked measured GFR, clinical-outcome validation, a large diverse population, and external validation (Kim et al., Front Med 2022, PMCID PMC9747759; inspected full text, frozen source f13f1132...). A July 2026 dual-cohort study abstract reports higher mortality with eGFRcys/eGFRcr ≤0.70 despite eGFRcr ≥60, but only modest incremental prediction and non-robust nonfatal cardiovascular associations; only its abstract was available and inspected (PMID 42410266).

Thus the unresolved claim is not “low eGFRcys is bad.” It is whether grip strength contributes actionable information beyond age, sex, BMI, and eGFRcr for **selective testing**, and whether the specific threshold-crossing it reveals precedes AKI. This matters because missed kidney impairment can affect monitoring and dosing of renally cleared drugs, while universal cystatin testing has cost and workflow implications. UKB can resolve comparative targeting and prognosis, but cannot show that ordering the assay or changing a dose improves outcomes.

## Exact Harbor experiment

### Population and temporal boundary

Use only the configured UKB discovery partition (participant SHA-256 buckets 0–79); do not inspect or tune on reserved buckets 80–99. Within discovery, deterministically assign 70% development and 30% confirmatory test by a second namespace-separated SHA-256 hash of `eid`. All modalities remain participant-grouped.

Index is baseline assessment `53-0.0` (normally 2006–2010). Include participants who:

1. are age 40–69 at recruitment;
2. have sex, baseline date, creatinine, cystatin C, BMI, and at least one hand-grip measurement;
3. have 2021 race-free eGFRcr 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m²; and
4. have no N17* inpatient diagnosis paired to a date on or before index.

Use a fixed 10-year window: `(index, index + 10 years]`. This avoids relying on an undocumented dataset-wide censoring date. For AKI time-to-event analysis, death before AKI is a competing event. A hospital code without a valid paired date is not an incident event and is counted in a data-quality report. Exclude impossible or nonpositive analyte values; report every filter count and missingness, with no global reinterpretation of UKB negative codes.

### Variables and equations

- Creatinine: µmol/L converted to mg/dL by division by 88.4.
- eGFRcr: 2021 CKD-EPI race-free creatinine equation.
- eGFRcys: 2012 CKD-EPI cystatin-C equation.
- Combined eGFRcr-cys: 2021 CKD-EPI race-free equation:
  `135 × min(Scr/k,1)^alpha × max(Scr/k,1)^−0.544 × min(Scys/0.8,1)^−0.323 × max(Scys/0.8,1)^−0.778 × 0.9961^age × 0.963(if female)`, where `k=0.7, alpha=−0.219` for females and `k=0.9, alpha=−0.144` for males.
- Grip: maximum valid baseline left/right grip in kg. If one hand is missing use the other; exclude if both are missing. Also report minimum-hand and mean-hand sensitivity analyses.
- **Target condition:** eGFRcr-cys <60 among people whose eGFRcr is 60–89.
- Secondary discordance: eGFRcys/eGFRcr ≤0.70.
- Primary clinical outcome: first inpatient N17* code after index and within 10 years.
- Secondary outcomes: first I50* heart-failure hospitalization and all-cause death within 10 years. They cannot rescue a failed primary result.

### Testing rules and estimands

Fit two prespecified logistic models in the 70% development set, with restricted cubic splines whose knots are fixed at development-set 5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th percentiles:

- **Clinical baseline rule:** age, sex, BMI, and eGFRcr.
- **Grip rule:** the same variables plus maximum grip and sex×grip interaction.

Use ridge penalization selected by five-fold participant-level cross-validation inside development only. Freeze coefficients, preprocessing, knots, and missing-data rules before opening the 30% test set. At an equal assay budget, each rule selects its highest predicted-risk 20% of the held-out eligible cohort. Additional comparators are random 20% selection (expected cohort prevalence), oldest-first 20%, and lowest-eGFRcr-first 20%. Universal testing is the upper-cost reference, not a competing equal-budget rule.

The first primary estimand is the held-out **detection-yield ratio**:
`[target cases detected / assays under grip rule] / [target cases detected / assays under clinical baseline rule]`.
Report its participant-bootstrap 95% CI (2,000 resamples), absolute extra cases per 1,000 assays, sensitivity, PPV, and overlap of selected participants. Also report whether grip improves calibration; a higher AUC alone is insufficient.

The second primary estimand is the cause-specific adjusted hazard ratio for first 10-year AKI comparing target-positive versus target-negative participants, restricted to the union selected by either model and adjusted for the baseline-rule covariates. The full eligible cohort analysis is secondary. Report cumulative incidence with death competing, proportional-hazards diagnostics, absolute 10-year risk difference standardized to the held-out covariate distribution, and 95% CIs. As sensitivity analyses, add systolic BP, HbA1c, CRP, and urinary albumin:creatinine ratio; use Fine–Gray regression; exclude events in the first 1 and 2 years; require two-sided diagnosis-code/date alignment; and repeat with eGFRcys/eGFRcr ≤0.70.

Clinical utility is secondary: compare 10-year AKI Brier score, calibration slope/intercept, and decision-curve net benefit at 2%, 5%, and 10% intervention thresholds for outcome models with versus without cystatin-derived reclassification. No claim rests on C-index alone.

### Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive:** in the untouched test set, the grip-rule yield ratio point estimate is ≥1.25 with 95% CI entirely >1.00; target-positive participants have adjusted AKI HR ≥1.50 with 95% CI entirely >1.00; the absolute risk direction agrees; and calibration is not materially degraded (slope 0.8–1.2). This supports prospective evaluation of grip-triggered cystatin testing.
- **Adverse:** grip adds no yield or loses yield (ratio ≤1), reclassified participants have equal/lower AKI incidence, or the association disappears after excluding the first two years. This refutes the proposed targeting rule and/or suggests reverse causation, ascertainment, or non-GFR cystatin determinants.
- **Inconclusive:** point estimates are in the hypothesized direction but CIs cross 1, the yield gain is <25%, event counts are inadequate, outcome models are badly calibrated, or results depend on one hand definition or early events. Report this as inconclusive, not supportive.
- Multiplicity: the two primary conditions form a joint gate; secondary endpoints and sensitivity analyses are descriptive with 95% CIs and false-discovery-rate labels where many subgroups are explored. No subgroup may replace the primary claim.

## Claims the verifier may and may not check

Computationally checkable: source hashes and column presence; `eid` joins; participant partitioning; positional ICD/date matching; equations and units; cohort chronology; filter counts; model isolation; equal 20% assay budgets; bootstrap estimates; event models; uncertainty; and whether the reported conclusion follows the supportive/adverse/inconclusive rules. Verification must include fixtures where calculations are correct but the narrative overclaims, and fixtures for all three result directions.

Not computationally established: true measured GFR, sarcopenia diagnosis, whether an N17 code is clinically adjudicated AKI, continuity of hospital capture, assay cost-effectiveness, medication-dose appropriateness, treatment benefit, transportability beyond UKB, or that testing causes fewer events. Those require chart/clinical adjudication, exogenous measured GFR, prescription and dose data, formal economic inputs, expert review, and ultimately a prospective implementation trial. Negative findings remain clinically useful: they would argue against using grip to ration cystatin C testing.
