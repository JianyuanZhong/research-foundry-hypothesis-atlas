# Provisional eICU hypothesis: laboratory-monitoring reduction during persistent instability

Status: provisional and untested. This is a design checkpoint, not an analysis result or causal claim.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a continuous eICU stay who remain physiologically unstable at a prespecified index time, a reduction in laboratory-monitoring intensity during the preceding 12 hours is associated with higher risk of a time-local clinical deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond measured current severity and recent support.

The strongest rival is that reduced testing reflects end-of-life/de-escalation, discharge/transfer, or sparse documentation rather than a monitoring-process signal. The experiment must distinguish these explanations where the available data permit it. A residual association in held-out hospitals would support a monitoring-process association, not prove that changing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

The configured eICU catalog and parent design identify longitudinal timestamped laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, patient, hospital, care-plan, discharge, and mortality fields. Within-dataset joins use `patientunitstayid`; hospital stratification uses `hospitalid`. These metadata facts support a bounded feasibility study. They do not establish that testing reduction predicts deterioration, that deterioration is clinically adjudicated, or that the relationship is causal.

No cohort analysis has been run in this checkpoint. Exact source headers and representative rows still require inspection before execution. The parent expert seed supplies the opening that testing frequency/missingness may encode hospital practice and may transport poorly; it is not evidence that the association exists.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use adult `patient` stays with a valid `unitdischargeoffset` greater than 18 hours and a valid unit interval. Restrict the primary cohort to continuous ICU stays with no transfer or early discharge before the outcome window. Index at hourly times from 6 through `unitdischargeoffset - 12` hours. Require at least one valid vital observation and one laboratory observation in the preceding 24 hours, while retaining an explicit indicator for missing documentation rather than treating missingness as zero testing.

The primary exposure is a testing reduction in `(t-12,t]` versus `(t-24,t-12]`, using `lab` result times. Count distinct laboratory episodes/analytes, with revisions not counted as new tests unless their result time is distinct. Primary reduction is zero current distinct-analyte count after a positive prior count, or at least a 50% reduction when the prior interval has at least two episodes. Continuous change and episode-count analyses are secondary.

The primary outcome is the first qualifying deterioration in `(t,t+12]`: new or escalated respiratory support from `respiratoryCare` or new/escalated infusion support from `infusionDrug`. Death, discharge, and transfer before the event are competing events, not ordinary non-events. `apachePatientResult` mortality fields are secondary outcomes only.

## Rival explanations and competing transitions

The strongest rival is end-of-life/de-escalation. Use `carePlanEOL` timestamps and `activeupondischarge` to define prespecified EOL-context strata and a sensitivity analysis excluding documented EOL plans before `t`. Sparse EOL documentation is not equivalent to absence of EOL intent.

Also model discharge/transfer context and recent support de-escalation. A monitoring-process interpretation requires persistence after prespecified handling of these transitions and in held-out hospitals, with negative controls behaving as expected.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative

The simple baseline is an adjusted discrete-time logistic model at the same hourly index times, with competing-event censoring, hospital fixed or clustered effects, and clustered uncertainty. It uses only information available at or before `t`: recent vital measurements, respiratory state, recent infusion/support, age, sex, unit type, and hospital.

The substantive alternative is a longitudinal recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard model with time-varying testing intensity and physiologic trajectories. It can reveal whether any association is transient, persistent, or preceded by a trajectory that the single-index baseline loses. It is selected because the scientific uncertainty concerns timing and persistence of the monitoring signal versus de-escalation, not because of predictive gain alone.

Use hospital-held-out validation, calibration, discrimination, absolute risk difference, and uncertainty intervals. Negative controls include within-hospital permutation of testing intervals preserving stay length/outcome, a post-index testing exposure that should not predict prior deterioration, and a non-laboratory documentation-timestamp control where available.

Supportive evidence would be a reproducible held-out-hospital association after state and EOL/discharge handling with null negative controls. Adverse evidence would be attenuation, instability, or disappearance after explicit rival adjustment. Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals, sparse events, or inability to distinguish interface interruption from clinical intent. None establishes causality or a recommendation to increase testing.

## Method selection and resource plan

The baseline is a CPU discrete-time fit with hospital-clustered uncertainty; the longitudinal alternative is a CPU recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard fit on the same cohort and outcome. Both use the same split by hospital, with one held-out hospital group for validation. Approximate future solver resources are within the configured CPU envelope (up to 16 CPUs and 262,144 MiB memory); GPU is not required. These are planning estimates, not executed results.

The actual scientific deliverable is a fitted baseline and longitudinal alternative, with held-out-hospital calibration/discrimination, uncertainty, competing-event accounting, negative-control results, and an interpretation limited to monitoring-process association. No result is claimed here.
