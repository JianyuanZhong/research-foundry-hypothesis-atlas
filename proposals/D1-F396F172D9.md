# Episode 82 child: rank transport separated from calibration transport for the numeric repeat-lactate queue

## Audit finding and substantive advance

The selected Episode-82 design asks whether a model trained in one outcome-blind repeat-lactate measurement-practice stratum transports to the other. Its fixed-capacity rank estimand is clinically meaningful. Its consequential weakness is that an absolute target calibration-in-the-large threshold is a hard prerequisite for interpreting that rank estimand. A practice-stratum shift in baseline risk can fail that threshold while preserving the ordering that determines a review queue. Conversely, passing calibration-in-the-large does not show that the availability stratum is a real workflow construct, that a repeat was ordered or visible, or that review changes care.

This child preserves the exact audited population, clocks, source bindings, revision/conflict rules, feature models, capacities, uncertainty and noncausal limits. It repairs the interpretation by prespecifying two separable transport claims:

* **H-rank (primary):** does the numeric repeat-lactate model produce a material, worst-capacity mortality-review queue increment when trained in the opposite practice stratum?
* **H-cal (secondary transport diagnostic):** are the resulting source-trained probabilities also compatible with target absolute risk, without target recalibration?

A positive H-rank with failed H-cal is a useful and explicitly limited result: rank portability is supported, but probability-scale deployment is not. H-cal cannot veto or rescue H-rank.

The clinically important advance is a deterministic distinction between a portable prognostic ordering and a portable calibrated risk model. That distinction identifies whether future work needs only an independently validated ranking implementation or must first collect target labels for recalibration. It remains an observational EICU result, not evidence that testing or review should occur.

## Evidence-supported and untested claims

The parent’s inspected full-text evidence from Baysan et al. (2022; DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407) supports incremental association of 24-hour lactate in a selected measured sepsis cohort, not observation-process adjustment or cross-practice transport. The inspected Sisk et al. review (2020; DOI 10.1093/jamia/ocaa242; PMCID PMC7810439) motivates treating observation patterns as informative, but does not establish that this EICU proxy measures clinician attention, staffing or capacity. The research-ambition README was also inspected: the cancer demonstration’s main article is unavailable here, so no claim uses its unavailable main text or STAR Methods.

The complete-source EICU audit supports feasibility only: 2,302 people, 30 hospitals, 1,163 eligible repeat lactates, 607 D7 events and 779 eventual deaths; outcome-blind median practice strata are low 15 hospitals/1,011 people/229 D7 events/782 non-events and high 15 hospitals/1,291 people/378 D7 events/913 non-events. These counts do not test transport.

The strongest claim this experiment could support is only that a source-stratum-fitted score changes the observed composition of equal-size, target-hospital queues for the source-defined outcome. It cannot support ordering, specimen validity, release/viewing, clinician review, actual workload or capacity, preventability, patient benefit, causal treatment effect, fairness, safety, or external transportability.

## Strata, directed transport and capacity

Before any outcome fitting, calculate each hospital’s complete eligible-population proportion with F_available. Split hospitals at the outcome-blind hospital median: low is below the median and high is at or above it, with ties retained in high. Do not use outcomes for strata, preprocessing, capacity or fold assignment.

For each direction low->high and high->low, fit separately on all source-stratum hospitals and score only target-stratum hospitals. Use source hospitals only for 1st/99th-percentile winsorization, mean/SD standardization, imputation and fitting. Fit unweighted logistic regression with intercept, L2 C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10,000, tol=1e-8, seed 20260973, no class weights. The raw linear predictor is the queue score; rank it within every target hospital, ties by ascending patientunitstayid. Target outcomes never affect fitting, preprocessing, strata, capacities or scores.

For each target hospital h and c in C={0.05,0.075,0.10,0.125,0.15,0.175,0.20}, set k_h(c)=ceil(c*n_h), select the top k by each score, and let K_b(c)=sum_h k_h(c) over target-stratum hospitals. Define:

Delta7(a->b,c) = [sum_h {D7 in Q_full,h(c) - D7 in Q_process,h(c)}] / K_b(c)

and tau7(a->b)=min_c Delta7(a->b,c). The primary estimand is the pair of directed minima, with pooled worst case tau7=min{tau7(low->high),tau7(high->low)}. Report per-hospital values, macro-hospital mean, event capture, false positives, unflagged events, queue overlap, additions/removals, ties and denominators. Repeat the same queues with Dinf to obtain tauInf; it is a horizon-tradeoff guard, not the primary outcome. For zero substitutions, fixed-slot Delta is zero and substitution yield is undefined.
