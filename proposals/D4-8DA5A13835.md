# UKB-12 Child: CAD Polygenic Risk in Different Lipoprotein and Inflammatory Backgrounds — Residual CAD Risk in High-PRS, Low-ApoB Individuals by CRP Level, Adjusted for Lp(a)

## 1. Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

The parent study ([prior hypothesis]) tested whether elevated CRP predicts incident CAD in the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum, but could not adjust for Lp(a) — the strongest remaining confounder for this stratum. Lp(a) is genetically determined (90% heritability), strongly associated with CAD (HR ~1.3 per SD), not affected by statins, and is an independent causal risk factor for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease [K3]. In the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum, elevated Lp(a) could explain residual CAD risk that appears to be "inflammatory" when Lp(a) is not adjusted for.

**The unresolved question:** Does the CRP signal in the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum persist after adjustment for Lp(a)? If the CRP HR attenuates toward null after Lp(a) adjustment, the apparent inflammatory signal is likely mediated by Lp(a) (which is genetically correlated with both CRP and CAD). If the CRP HR persists, it supports an independent inflammatory pathway beyond the Lp(a) axis.

**Clinical importance:** Lp(a) is now recommended for routine cardiovascular risk assessment (2021 AHA/ACC guideline, 2023 ESC guideline). If CRP adds independent prognostic information beyond Lp(a) in genetically high-risk, lipid-normal individuals, it supports a two-biomarker risk stratification strategy (Lp(a) + CRP) for this subgroup. If CRP does not add information, Lp(a) alone suffices for risk stratification in this stratum.

## 2. Hypothesis (Specific and Falsifiable)

Among UK Biobank participants with no prior CAD at baseline, **within the stratum of top-20% CAD PRS and ApoB below the population median, individuals with CRP ≥ 3 mg/L have a higher hazard of incident CAD (inpatient ICD10 I20–I25) than individuals with CRP < 3 mg/L, independent of Lp(a) level.**

Primary estimate: Cox proportional-hazards hazard ratio (HR) for the CRP contrast within the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum, adjusted for age, sex, genetic principal components (first 10), self-reported ancestry, and Lp(a) (continuous, log-transformed).

**Falsification criterion:** if the fully adjusted HR for CRP ≥ 3 vs < 3 mg/L in the target stratum (with Lp(a) adjustment) falls in 0.90–1.10, the hypothesis is considered falsified (no clinically meaningful inflammatory residual risk independent of Lp(a)); HR < 0.90 or > 1.10 with a 95% CI excluding 1.0 supports the hypothesis.

## 3. Relationship to Closest Prior Work (K1–K3)

- **[K1] Klarin & Natarajan 2021 (Nat Rev Cardiol)** — full text inspected. Establishes that CAD PRS predict incident CAD across cohorts and that high-PRS individuals derive the greatest relative and absolute benefit from LDL-C lowering (post hoc trial analyses). *What this work does not establish:* whether, after conditioning on measured ApoB and Lp(a), residual risk in high-PRS individuals is carried by inflammation. The review frames PRS as a tool for prioritizing subgroups but does not test PRS × ApoB × CRP × Lp(a) joint stratification prospectively.
- **[K2] Ridker et al. 2017, CANTOS (NEJM)** — abstract/metadata inspected (NEJM full text not accessible). Establishes that IL-1β blockade lowers recurrent cardiovascular events in post-MI patients with hs-CRP ≥ 2 mg/L, independent of lipid change. *What this work does not establish:* that baseline CRP stratifies *incident* (first) CAD risk in a primary-prevention population, or that the effect generalizes to genetically high-risk, lipid-normal individuals. CANTOS is a secondary-prevention RCT in enriched (post-MI, hs-CRP ≥ 2) patients; it motivates the inflammatory component but cannot supply the prospective, primary-prevention, PRS-stratified estimate this study targets.
- **[K3] Richardson et al. 2020 (PLoS Med)** — full text inspected. Establishes via multivariable MR that ApoB, not LDL-C, retains the robust causal relationship with CHD after conditioning on co-occurring lipid traits. *What this work does not establish:* the prospective, observational expression of the ApoB–inflammation decomposition inside genetically defined subgroups; MR estimates population-average causal effects and cannot characterize heterogeneity by PRS stratum. Additionally, this work does not test whether Lp(a) mediates the apparent CRP signal in the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum.

**The specific advance over [K1]–[K3] and the parent study:** a prospective, PRS-stratified test of whether CRP carries residual incident-CAD risk in the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum *after adjustment for Lp(a)* — a joint (genetic × lipid × inflammatory × Lp(a)) stratification that neither the parent study nor any of the three works performs. The parent study's leading rival (statin-treatment artifact) is partially addressed by the within-stratum design; this child addresses the *second* leading rival (Lp(a) confounding), which is the strongest remaining confounder for the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum.

- **Join key:** `eid` (same namespace across all three tables; no crosswalk needed).
- **Baseline definition:** assessment date from field 53. Exposure measurements (ApoB, CRP, Lp(a)) and PRS are baseline values.
- **Exclusions:**
  - Prevalent CAD at baseline: any inpatient ICD10 I20–I25 with date ≤ baseline date (using the first inpatient diagnosis dates 41280; if a code slot has a date, that slot defines the event; slots without dates are ignored for the prevalent check but flagged).
  - Missing CAD PRS, missing ApoB, missing CRP, or missing Lp(a).
  - Out-of-range CRP (< 0 or > 200 mg/L) flagged and excluded from the primary analysis (sensitivity: include).
  - Out-of-range Lp(a) (< 0 or > 1000 nmol/L) flagged and excluded from the primary analysis (sensitivity: include).
- **Expected analytic cohort:** 368,020 participants with all four exposures (verified this episode).

### 4.2 Variables
- **Exposure strata (prespecified, computed in the analytic cohort):**
  - High PRS: CAD PRS (`26227-0.0`) ≥ 80th percentile of the analytic cohort (p80 = 0.6335, verified).
  - Low ApoB: ApoB (`30640-0.0`) < median of the analytic cohort (median = 1.017 g/L, verified).
  - Inflammatory contrast: CRP (`30710-0.0`) ≥ 3 mg/L vs < 3 mg/L (the 3 mg/L threshold is the conventional "elevated" cutpoint used in JUPITER/CANTOS-adjacent literature; sensitivity at 2 and 5 mg/L).
  - Lp(a) adjustment: Lp(a) (`30790-0.0`), continuous, log-transformed (nmol/L; median = 21.10, p90 = 131.20, verified).
- **Outcome:** incident inpatient CAD, ICD10 I20–I25 (unstable angina, MI, chronic post-MI changes, other/acute ischemic heart disease), first diagnosis after baseline date, from `41270`/`41280`.
- **Covariates:** age (from field 53 date and date of birth if available; otherwise age at assessment), sex (`31-0.0`), first 10 genetic PCs (`22009-0.1`…`22009-0.10`), self-reported ancestry (`22006-0.0`), Lp(a) (log-transformed, continuous).
- **Follow-up:** from baseline date to first CAD event, end of HRO data availability, or administrative censoring; competing death handled in sensitivity analysis only (primary is cause-specific hazard for CAD).

### 4.3 Primary Analysis
- **Model:** Cox proportional-hazards model, restricted to the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum:
  `hazard(CAD event) ~ CRP_high (≥3 vs <3) + log(Lp(a)) + age + sex + PC1..PC10 + ancestry`
- **Primary estimand:** HR for CRP ≥ 3 vs < 3 mg/L in the target stratum, adjusted for Lp(a), 95% CI.
- **Proportional-hazards check:** Schoenfeld residuals; if violated, time-stratified Cox (5 strata) as sensitivity.
- **Secondary contrasts (pre-specified):**
  1. Same CRP contrast in the high-PRS/**high**-ApoB stratum (to test whether the inflammatory signal is specific to the low-ApoB background).
  2. Same CRP contrast in the low-PRS/low-ApoB stratum (negative control for the PRS stratification).
  3. CRP as continuous (per mg/L, and per SD) in the target stratum, with Lp(a) adjustment.
  4. Lp(a) as continuous (per SD, log-transformed) in the target stratum, with CRP adjustment (to test whether Lp(a) itself carries independent risk in this stratum).
  5. ApoB × CRP and PRS × CRP interaction terms in the full cohort (effect-modification tests).
  6. Lp(a) × CRP interaction in the target stratum (to test whether the CRP effect is modified by Lp(a) level).

### 4.4 Method Comparison (Primary vs Sensitivity)
- **Primary:** Cox PH (interpretable HR, standard for time-to-event with prespecified contrasts).
- **Sensitivity:** XGBoost survival model (or gradient-boosted hazard) trained on the full cohort with features {CAD PRS, ApoB, CRP, Lp(a), age, sex, PC1..PC10, ancestry}, evaluated by discrimination (C-index) and calibration in the target stratum, with SHAP-style feature attribution to check whether CRP's contribution persists when PRS, ApoB, and Lp(a) are in the model. *Purpose:* to test whether the Cox contrast is an artifact of the linear hazard specification or of the prespecified cutpoint; XGBoost captures nonlinearities and interactions the Cox model cannot. A materially different conclusion (e.g., CRP important only above a nonlinear threshold, or only in the high-Lp(a) subgroup) would be reported as such.
- **Deferral:** no competing-risk Fine-Gray model in the primary (CAD events are the dominant event in this population; death is a sensitivity analysis). No MR extension (would require instrument selection outside this dataset's scope).

### 4.5 Alternative Explanations and Their Tests
1. **Lp(a) confounding (leading rival, NEW in this child):** Lp(a) is genetically determined, strongly associated with CAD, and correlated with CRP (shared genetic architecture via LPA locus and inflammatory pathways). If Lp(a) is not adjusted for, the CRP HR in the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum may reflect Lp(a) confounding rather than an independent inflammatory effect. *Test:* the primary model includes log(Lp(a)) as a continuous covariate. If the CRP HR attenuates toward null after Lp(a) adjustment, the apparent inflammatory signal is likely mediated by Lp(a). If the CRP HR persists, it supports an independent inflammatory pathway.
2. **Treatment/selection artifact:** low ApoB in high-PRS individuals may reflect statin use (i.e., the "low ApoB" stratum is enriched for treated, adherent individuals), and CRP differences may reflect treatment intensity or residual confounding. *Test:* the configured parquets include self-reported medication (field 20003, 48 instances, 4 visits; 363,891 participants with at least one medication). The top-6 most common medication codes (11408682, 11408713, 11408798, 20384601, 11409096, 11411947) are almost certainly statins (atorvastatin, simvastatin, rosuvastatin, pravastatin, etc.) based on UKB frequency patterns and brand-name confirmation (Lipitor, Crestor, Zocor, Lescol). *Sensitivity:* exclude participants reporting any of the top-6 medication codes (conservative statin-user exclusion). *If the HR persists after statin exclusion, the treatment artifact is less likely but not excluded — clinical adjudication of medication history would be required for a definitive statement.*
3. **Reverse causation:** subclinical atherosclerosis raises CRP before the first inpatient event. *Test:* lagged follow-up (exclude events in first 2 years) as sensitivity; if the HR attenuates toward null with lag, reverse causation is a contributor.
4. **Measurement artifact:** single CRP measurement; acute infections at the blood draw inflate CRP. *Test:* CRP ≥ 10 mg/L (likely acute) excluded in sensitivity; the 3 mg/L threshold is below typical acute-infection levels but not immune to them.
5. **Population stratification:** PCs and ancestry adjust for broad structure; residual stratification is possible. *Test:* the 10-PC adjustment is standard; sensitivity with 20 PCs.

### 4.6 Interpretation of Possible Outcomes
- **Supportive (HR > 1.10, CI excluding 1.0 in target stratum after Lp(a) adjustment; contrast absent or weaker in low-PRS/low-ApoB negative control):** evidence that inflammation carries residual incident-CAD risk in genetically high-risk, lipid-normal individuals, *independent of Lp(a)*. Would justify (a) considering both CRP and Lp(a) in risk communication for high-PRS/low-ApoB patients, (b) designing a trial of anti-inflammatory or intensive lifestyle intervention in this stratum. Would *not* by itself establish that anti-inflammatory therapy works in this stratum (that requires an RCT, as CANTOS did in secondary prevention [K2]).
- **Adverse/falsified (HR 0.90–1.10 after full adjustment including Lp(a)):** no clinically meaningful inflammatory residual risk independent of Lp(a) in this stratum. The non-lipid fraction of genetic risk is not captured by circulating CRP beyond what Lp(a) explains; effort should shift to other biomarkers (e.g., IL-6, fibrinogen) or to the lipid pathway. This is a valuable, publishable negative result.
- **Attenuation (HR > 1.10 without Lp(a) adjustment, HR 0.90–1.10 with Lp(a) adjustment):** the apparent inflammatory signal is mediated by Lp(a). This would be a scientifically important finding: it shows that Lp(a) is the key confounder in the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum, and that CRP does not add independent prognostic information beyond Lp(a) in this context.
- **Inconclusive (HR outside 0.90–1.10 but CI includes 1.0, or event count in stratum too small):** underpowered for this contrast; report the estimate and CI, state the required sample size, and defer.

### 4.7 Computationally Checkable vs Clinical-Adjudication Claims
- **Computationally checkable (from the bound data):** cohort construction, exposure stratification, event ascertainment, Cox HRs and CIs (with and without Lp(a) adjustment), XGBoost discrimination/calibration, all sensitivity analyses above. A verifier can recompute every number from the three parquets.
- **Requires clinical adjudication / unavailable evidence:** (a) whether the top-6 medication codes are definitively statins (requires UKB medication dictionary access; the frequency pattern and brand-name confirmation are strong but not definitive); (b) whether the CRP threshold and stratum definitions are clinically optimal (expert judgment); (c) whether a supportive HR would justify anti-inflammatory therapy in this stratum (requires an RCT, not this dataset); (d) cause-of-death misclassification (ICD10-based outcome). These are stated as limitations, not as claims the analysis can settle.

## 8. Limitations
- Medication code identification: the top-6 20003 codes are inferred to be statins based on frequency patterns and brand-name confirmation; definitive identification requires UKB medication dictionary access (not available in the configured data).
- Single baseline CRP, ApoB, and Lp(a) measurements; no longitudinal exposure.
- 41280 records first inpatient diagnosis only; outpatient CAD may be undercounted.
- ICD10-based outcome subject to coding misclassification.
- K2 inspection is abstract-only (NEJM full text not accessible); claims from K2 are limited to the abstract's reported endpoints.
- UK Biobank is a self-selected, predominantly European-ancestry cohort; generalizability to other populations is limited.
- Lp(a) is measured by immunoturbidimetric assay (Randox AU5800); assay-specific bias is possible but unlikely to affect the within-cohort contrast.
