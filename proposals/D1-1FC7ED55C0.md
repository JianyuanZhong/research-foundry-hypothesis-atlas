# Scientific Proposal: Discordant Medication and Procedure Patterns Predict HCC Progression Better Than Static Disease Stage

## Unresolved Question

### Clinical Problem
In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), current risk stratification and treatment planning rely heavily on static disease staging systems (BCLC, TNM) and single-time-point laboratory values. However, patients with identical baseline staging exhibit vastly different treatment responses and disease progression patterns. The treatment landscape has expanded dramatically with immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and interventional procedures, creating complex treatment trajectories that are not captured by static assessments.

### Key Uncertainty
Does the **temporal pattern of treatment initiation and medication adherence** over the first 90 days post-diagnosis predict which patients will experience treatment response, disease progression, or transplant eligibility better than static disease stage at diagnosis? Existing models treat treatment as a binary decision (treated vs. untreated) or use single-time-point assessments, rather than capturing the dynamic interplay between treatment intensity, timing, and patient response.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Treatment Optimization**: More accurate prediction of treatment response could enable personalized treatment selection, reducing exposure to ineffective therapies and their associated toxicities.

2. **Transplant Allocation**: Better prediction of disease progression could optimize liver transplant allocation, reducing waitlist mortality while ensuring appropriate urgency.

3. **Clinical Trial Enrichment**: Identifying patients with favorable treatment patterns could enrich clinical trials for new therapies.

4. **Healthcare Resource Utilization**: Early identification of patients at risk of progression could enable proactive intervention strategies.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

### Distinct from Prior Work
Unlike previous proposals focusing on discordant **laboratory trajectories** (INR, albumin, bilirubin), this proposal examines discordant **treatment and medication patterns**. This is a fundamentally different dimension of patient heterogeneity:

- **Prior work**: Focuses on organ function trajectories (synthetic, coagulation, metabolic parameters)
- **This proposal**: Focuses on treatment behavior trajectories (medication initiation timing, adherence patterns, procedure timing)

### Novel Hypothesis
Patients who exhibit **discordant treatment patterns** (e.g., initiating multiple therapies at different times, varying medication adherence, delayed procedure initiation) may have different prognoses than those with consistent treatment patterns, independent of their baseline disease stage.

## Analysis Plan

### Statistical Approach

1. **Baseline Model**: Cox proportional hazards model with BCLC stage as predictor
2. **Treatment Pattern Features**:
   - Time to first treatment initiation
   - Number of treatment types initiated
   - Treatment initiation clustering (multiple treatments within 30 days vs. staggered)
   - Medication adherence score (proportion of prescribed days covered)
   - Procedure timing relative to treatment initiation
3. **Interaction Terms**: Treatment patterns × baseline characteristics
4. **Model Comparison**:
   - Static stage model vs. Treatment pattern model
   - Static + Treatment pattern model
   - Dynamic trajectory model (using time-varying covariates)

### Evaluation Metrics
- **Primary**: C-index (concordance index) for time-to-event outcomes
- **Secondary**:
  - Calibration plots
  - Net reclassification improvement (NRI)
  - Decision curve analysis
  - Time-dependent AUC

### Falsification Criteria
- **Supportive**: Treatment pattern model achieves ΔC-index ≥ 0.05 over static stage model, p < 0.05
- **Adverse**: Treatment pattern model performs no better than random (ΔC-index < 0.01), p > 0.05
- **Inconclusive**: Intermediate performance (0.01 ≤ ΔC-index < 0.05)

## Method Comparison

### Baseline: Static Stage Model
- **Input**: BCLC stage at diagnosis
- **Assumption**: Disease progression depends primarily on baseline disease severity
- **Limitation**: Ignores treatment dynamics and patient-specific treatment patterns

### Substantive Alternative: Dynamic Treatment Pattern Model
- **Input**: Temporal patterns of medication and procedure initiation
- **Assumption**: Treatment dynamics capture prognostic information beyond baseline stage
- **Advantage**: Can identify patients who respond well to aggressive treatment vs. those who need de-escalation

### Information Revealed by Alternative
The dynamic model could reveal:
- Patients who benefit from early, aggressive treatment
- Patients whose progression is driven by treatment resistance rather than baseline disease
- Temporal patterns that predict response to specific treatment modalities
- Optimal treatment initiation timing
