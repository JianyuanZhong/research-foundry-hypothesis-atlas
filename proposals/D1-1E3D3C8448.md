# Non-overlapping forward validation of the frozen P-beyond-B repeat-TACE estimand

## Unresolved question and advance

In the frozen adult HCC repeat-TACE complete-triplet population, does the contemporaneous pre-repeat assay value `P` improve prediction of the subsequent same-encounter assay value `Y` beyond remote baseline `B` and the identical compact decision-time core when evaluated strictly forward in calendar time? The parents establish the reconstruction, exact clocks, nested prognostic estimand, and outcome-blind rolling validation, but their 365-day test windows overlap and later eras are sparse and compositionally unstable. This successor makes the temporal validation blocks disjoint and adds two outcome-blind checks for covariate drift and single-episode influence.

The tested claim is prognostic transport of incremental predictive information, not a treatment effect. The systemic-record/comparator variable is a prespecified covariate and a composition diagnostic only. No causal, treatment-response, toxicity, safety, benefit, clinical-utility, or actionability claim is made.

## Computable experiment

Use fixed calendar origins 2021-01-01, 2022-01-01, 2023-01-01, and 2024-01-01. For each origin, training contains all selected complete-triplet events with `event_date < origin`; testing is `[origin, origin+365 days)`. These four future blocks are non-overlapping, and all test outcomes occur after all training outcomes for that origin.

For each assay and origin compare the locked nested ridge models with alpha grid `[0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100]`:

```
M0: Y ~ B + systemic-record indicator + age + sex indicators
         + TACE2-to-repeat days + P-lead hours
M1: M0 + P
```

Training-only age median imputation, age-missingness indicator, sex coding, variance filtering, centering/scaling, alpha selection by inner leave-one-patient-out error, and fitting are performed separately within each pre-origin training set. No test `Y` is used in preprocessing, tuning, feature selection, or fitting. The estimand is assay-specific standardized held-out RMSE gain, `RMSE(M0) − RMSE(M1)`, divided by test-set `SD(Y)`; MAE, bias, arm composition, and arm-specific errors are secondary diagnostics. Assays are not numerically pooled.

Uncertainty is a paired arm-stratified patient-frequency bootstrap conditional on the fixed pre-origin predictions. A support gate requires at least 20 total and at least 10 in each descriptive arm in both training and test. Ungated estimates remain descriptive even when their conditional interval excludes zero. Outcome-blind covariate drift is the standardized mean difference between pre-origin training and future test values of `B`, `P`, `P_lead_hours`, and `days`. Fixed-prediction leave-one-test-episode influence recomputes the metric after omitting each test episode without refitting; it diagnoses concentration, not independent replication.

Only the 2022-01-01 block passed the locked support gate for both assays (training n=78, 22 systemic/56 comparator; test n=28, 10/18). In that block, albumin standardized RMSE gain was 0.1781 with conditional 95% bootstrap interval [0.0838, 0.3367]; bilirubin gain was 0.3342 with interval [0.1005, 0.5550]. Both descriptive arms improved for both assays.

The 2021 block had positive pooled gains (albumin 0.1335; bilirubin 0.2500) but failed the training systemic-count gate (8). The 2023 block was comparator-dominated (5 systemic/24 comparator), failed the gate, and had positive but imprecise gains (albumin 0.0986, interval [−0.0228, 0.2874]; bilirubin 0.0575, interval [−0.0698, 0.3334]). The 2024 block was extremely sparse (albumin 1/13; bilirubin 2/14); favorable pooled point estimates (0.2170 and 0.4736) are not transport evidence, and its conditional intervals included zero for bilirubin. The 2023 albumin systemic diagnostic was adverse in RMSE (gain −0.3315, n=5), and later arm-specific results are explicitly descriptive rather than arm effects.

Outcome-blind drift and influence diagnostics do not repair sparse support. Examples include albumin 2022 drift SMDs of 0.191 for B, −0.189 for P, 0.248 for P lead, and −0.200 for days; its leave-one-episode standardized gain ranged 0.1503–0.2147 around the full 0.1781. Bilirubin 2024 had P drift SMD 0.431 and a full gain 0.4736 with leave-one-episode range 0.1576–0.4895, demonstrating concentration despite a favorable pooled point estimate.

## Interpretation and falsification criteria

The result is supportive only for the adequately supported 2022 cutoff: both assays improve with conditional intervals above zero, while the frozen reconstruction and temporal separation pass. It is not evidence of reproducible multi-era transport. The overall conclusion remains inconclusive because only one origin passed the prespecified support gate and all later systemic arms were sparse. A future adverse result would be a gated interval at or below zero or repeated gated degradation of M1; that would challenge stable incremental prediction in the tested population, not prove that B alone is sufficient. An inconclusive result includes sparse arms, wide intervals, influential episodes, denominator/clock drift, or unavailable assay units.

The computation can verify source hashes/paths, row scans, filters, joins, frozen counts, clocks, non-overlapping origins, train/test separation, nesting, preprocessing scope, predictions, metrics, bootstrap, drift, and influence. It cannot determine clinical importance, validate units or reference ranges, adjudicate disease severity or complications, verify medication administration, establish causality or treatment benefit, or overcome selection/collider bias from conditioning on repeat TACE and complete observed triplets. Those claims require clinical adjudication and independent temporal, external, or prospective data with imaging, tumor burden, validated lab metadata, reliable outcomes, and verified treatment exposure.
