# Robustness of time-weighted hypotension burden for early AKI: summary choice, measurement-density and baseline-severity controls

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP), after controlling for measurement density, baseline illness severity, and after showing that the signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined)?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density. The strongest rival is that the apparent burden signal is an artifact of recording density, measurement timing, or baseline illness severity, or that it is specific to one arbitrary choice of blood-pressure summary. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

Minimum or mean arterial pressure is a common bedside summary, but it can understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

## Prior evidence

- [K1] Sun et al. (2024) demonstrate that time-aware blood-pressure representations can improve prediction of acute kidney injury in a large ICU cohort, supporting the plausibility that temporal structure matters. Their work does not establish that a simple time-weighted burden measure adds information beyond MAP after controlling for measurement density and baseline severity.
- [K2] Henry et al. (2022) show that improving the representation of blood pressure can improve prediction of AKI, but their analysis does not isolate the contribution of duration and time-weighting beyond a simple MAP baseline.
- [K3] Nasr et al. (2009) provide a clinical framework for AKI risk stratification and emphasize that early identification is important, but do not test whether time-weighted hypotension burden adds prognostic information beyond conventional summaries.

## Data and bindings

Use the eICU 2.0 dataset. The primary tables are:

- `eicu_cdm.patients` (patient demographics, ICU admission time, hospital identifier)
- `eicu_cdm.vital_signs` (systolic and diastolic blood pressure, measurement time)
- `eicu_cdm.lab_results` (creatinine, baseline kidney function)
- `eicu_cdm.medication` (vasopressor exposure)
- `eicu_cdm.disposition` (ICU discharge, death)

Join on `patient_id` and `encounter_id` where applicable. Use `icu_admission_time` as the temporal anchor. Restrict to adult patients (age >= 18) with at least 10 systolic and 10 diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours.

## Exposure and outcome

Exposure: time-weighted hypotension burden in the first 24 hours, defined as the integral of the indicator that MAP < 65 mmHg over time, normalized by the observation window. Also compute the 24-hour mean MAP, minimum systolic, minimum diastolic, and a combined summary.

Outcome: AKI within 48 hours of ICU admission, defined by KDIGO criteria (creatinine increase >= 0.3 mg/dL from baseline within 48 hours, or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for >= 6 hours).

## Analysis

1. Fit a logistic regression with AKI as the outcome, including the time-weighted burden, 24-hour MAP, baseline severity (APACHE IV with SOFA fallback), vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density.
2. Repeat the analysis using systolic, diastolic, and combined blood-pressure summaries to test robustness to the choice of summary.
3. Perform severity stratum analysis (low, medium, high APACHE IV) and missing-severity sensitivity analysis.
4. Report confidence intervals, calibration metrics, and hospital-level analysis.
5. Falsification: the burden coefficient is not positive with a 95% CI excluding zero after adjustment, or the burden signal disappears in severity strata, under missing-severity sensitivity analysis, or under the alternative blood-pressure summaries.

## Solver deliverable

The solver must fit the logistic regression models, report the burden coefficient with 95% CI, calibration metrics, and hospital-level analysis. The solver must also report the results of the robustness analysis (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined) and the severity stratum analysis.
