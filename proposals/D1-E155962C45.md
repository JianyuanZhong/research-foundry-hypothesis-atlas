# Hepatic-injury context as a test of the lactate-by-perfusion monitoring signal

## Episode and scientific deliverable

This proposal is a substantive successor of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent’s first eligible adult live ICU-exit population, one-hour availability buffer, 12-hour dual clinical/store-time feature window, lactate-by-measured-perfusion phenotype, 48-hour competing R/D/S/N/U endpoint, L+B development to contemporary C transport, fixed 10% queue and 50% baseline reserve, uncertainty, process controls and noncausal boundaries.

The new deliverable is a prespecified clinical effect-modification test:

> Does the observed near-term risk and fixed-capacity monitoring value of persistent hyperlactatemia with improving versus worsening measured perfusion differ according to concurrent record-defined hepatic injury burden?

The clinical uncertainty is whether a lactate signal should be interpreted similarly in patients with evidence of hepatic injury, where impaired lactate clearance may make persistent lactate less specific for ongoing circulatory danger. MIMIC cannot establish clearance physiology or guide treatment, but it can test whether the observed prognostic meaning and queue capture are heterogeneous in a way that would justify liver-context stratification in a future silent-mode alert evaluation.

The strongest available evidence is design/availability evidence only. The seed and parent motivate the distinction; neither establishes an association, effect modification, or clinical benefit. The local snapshot exposes bilirubin, ALT and AST with clinical and store times, but does not provide measured lactate clearance, microcirculation, clinician intent, fluid responsiveness, a complete liver-failure adjudication, or external validation.

Completion requires a pre-fit gate report and a frozen analysis package containing: exact cohort and label flow; row-level source and time provenance; liver-state support and overlap; fitted B1/Bdisc/Bmask and masked chronological M2 predictions; primary interaction and standardized risk contrasts; fixed-capacity safety-net results stratified by liver state; subject-bootstrap intervals; all falsifications; and a conclusion ledger tied to computed outputs. A gate failure is a reportable not-estimable result, not permission to alter cutoffs or outcomes.

Use the parent’s deterministic cohort without replacement:

- Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id,hadm_id)`, and `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`.
- Retain adults (`anchor_age >=18`, including MIMIC’s `anchor_age=91` representation), one first valid ICU stay per admission sorted by `(outtime,intime,stay_id)), with `intime < outtime` and at least 13 hours of ICU history.
- Require a known live ICU exit at `t0=outtime`: nonmissing `deathtime > t0`, or null `deathtime` with `hospital_expire_flag=0`. Set `tL=t0-1 hour`; features may not use clinical or store time after `tL).
- Use the 12-hour window `[t0-13h,tL]`, split into early `[tL-12h,tL-6h)` and late `[tL-6h,tL]`. Retain only the parent’s strict coverage-complete subjects for the primary estimands.
- Ascertain the first transition in `(t0,t0+48h]): later ICU stay in the same admission (R), timed in-hospital death (D), or alive hospital discharge with `hospital_expire_flag=0` (S). N is known event-free follow-up; U is unresolved ascertainment and is never relabeled N. Preserve the parent’s deterministic tie order and all-eligible/coverage/label audits.

The primary target eras remain historical L (2008–2013 labels) and contemporary C (2017–2022 labels), with B (2014–2016) used descriptively. Fit on L+B and freeze before scoring C outcomes. Subject-level 70/15/15 splits are stratified only by original era group, primary phenotype cell and endpoint label; no patient appears in multiple partitions.

## Exposure/context construction

Retain the parent’s primary phenotype definitions and all indeterminate cells:

- Lactate P: both six-hour half medians of item 50813 are defined and at least 2.0 mmol/L; NL: both defined and at least one below 2.0.
- Perfusion I: late minus early MAP >=5 mmHg and late unioned target-pressor minutes no greater than early minutes.
- Perfusion W: MAP change <=-5 mmHg and late pressor minutes no less than early minutes.
- Other combinations remain indeterminate and are descriptive only.
- Each half requires at least one valid lactate, at least two MAP observations at distinct clinical times, and one positive-duration pressor interval. No pressor row is interpreted as zero exposure.

Define the new context before outcomes are read. Hepatic-injury burden H is a three-level record phenotype computed from the late half only, using values available by `tL`:

- H0 (low recorded injury): at least one valid bilirubin, ALT or AST and none meets its training-derived upper-tertile threshold;
- H1 (intermediate): at least one marker is at or above its training-derived upper-tertile threshold;
- H2 (high/multi-marker): at least two of bilirubin, ALT and AST are at or above their corresponding training-derived upper-tertile thresholds.

Thresholds are calculated using the training partition only, separately for each lab and its finite values in the eligible training population; they are frozen before validation, L-test or C outcomes. If any marker has inadequate training support, its threshold and H2 contribution are unavailable. The primary analysis uses H0/H1-H2 (any elevated marker versus none); H2 is a prespecified secondary multi-marker sensitivity. “Hepatic injury” here means a record-defined biochemical pattern, not impaired clearance, liver failure, or adjudicated etiology. Availability/missingness is retained and reported.

## Estimands and falsification

For each target era and hepatic stratum, estimate the 48-hour cumulative incidence of R/D with S competing, standardized to a common covariate distribution. The primary scientific estimand is the interaction contrast:

`Theta_H = [CIF_RD(P,I,H1+H2)-CIF_RD(NL,I,H1+H2)] - [CIF_RD(P,I,H0)-CIF_RD(NL,I,H0)]`.

The main clinical interpretation is whether the P-versus-NL contrast under improving measured perfusion changes with H, not whether H causes outcome. Report the analogous P-versus-NL contrast under W, the R-specific and D-specific CIF interactions, and simultaneous 95% subject-bootstrap intervals. Prespecify a meaningful heterogeneity region of +/-0.05 absolute 48-hour CIF; intervals entirely outside that region support heterogeneity, intervals wholly inside it support no clinically meaningful heterogeneity, and overlapping/failed support gates are inconclusive.

For the operational estimand, calculate the parent’s 10% queue and 50%-reserved safety-net policy separately within H0 and H1+H2, using predictions frozen before target outcomes. Report B1, Bdisc, safety-net Bdisc, random benchmark, queue overlap, R/D capture, PPV, rescue yield, and paired bootstrap intervals. A prespecified clinically meaningful policy interaction is a difference of at least 0.03 in safety-net gain between H strata in both L and C, with intervals excluding zero and the direction consistent with the primary interaction. This is a ranking/allocation contrast, not patient benefit.

Support gates are fixed before fitting: at least 40 subjects and 10 known R/D events per primary phenotype-by-H stratum in each evaluated target where an interaction is claimed; at least 90 known-label target subjects, 10 known R/D events, at least 10 subjects in each queue component, at least 5 known R/D events outside the reserved B1 set, and U <=10% for each safety-net target stratum. Otherwise report descriptive or not-estimable results. Report effective sample size, selection overlap, event counts and bootstrap failures.

Falsification and process controls:

1. Refit/evaluate Bmask, which retains counts, masks, test presence, observation intensity and context but removes physiologic values. Similar H-specific interactions or safety-net gains in Bmask weaken a physiology interpretation.
2. Permute outcomes within fixed era and partition; interactions and rescue gains should collapse toward the permutation/random benchmark.
3. Shuffle early/late ordering while preserving values and missingness. Persistence after this test weakens the trajectory interpretation.
4. Use liver-marker availability and number-of-tests as process-only controls; if they reproduce the result, selective testing is a more plausible explanation.
5. Repeat with bilirubin-only, transaminase-only, continuous training-frozen H burden, and 4.0 mmol/L lactate threshold as sensitivities; none replaces the primary.
6. Audit clinical/store timestamps, duplicate collapse, lab units and `ref_range` use, same-subject split exclusion, ties, endpoint ascertainment, and no target outcome use in thresholding, strata, queue reserve or fill.
7. Report whether H strata differ in ICU type, pressor coverage, Foley charting, lactate/MAP availability, store lag and discharge process. These are diagnostics, not causal adjustment.

Supportive results require both target eras to pass support gates, the primary interaction to exclude the +/-0.05 null region, directionally concordant R/D decomposition, and no comparable Bmask/process-only interaction or falsification signal. Adverse results are a reproducible null, reversal, process-control replication, calibration degradation, or safety-net result inconsistent with the hypothesized clinical interpretation. Inconclusive results include sparse strata, high U, inadequate overlap, unstable bootstrap, missing liver markers, or intervals too wide to resolve the prespecified region.

A supportive result establishes only heterogeneous observed risk/ranking under MIMIC’s documentation process and motivates adjudication plus prospective silent-mode evaluation. It does not establish lactate clearance, microcirculatory dysfunction, treatment effect, the advisability of fluids/pressors, preventability, net benefit, or a discharge/monitoring policy.

## Matched methods and compute

B1 is the simple order-blind aggregate logistic/competing-risk baseline with the parent’s 12 bins, context, masks and organ/process summaries but no new H interactions. Bdisc is the transparent learned-free model adding frozen lactate/perfusion and H interactions; all models use the same subject split, target, source whitelist and queue evaluation. The substantive learned alternative M2 is a small masked chronological GRU over the identical 12-bin tensor, masks, process indicators, context, liver-marker values and frozen phenotype flags, predicting discrete-time competing R/D/S hazards. M2 can reveal nonlinear and order-dependent coupling or whether the apparent H interaction is already encoded by trajectory shape; disagreement is representation evidence, not biological proof. A two-component trajectory mixture is deferred unless M2 stability fails; a full transformer is deferred because it adds capacity without a distinct clinical uncertainty. A treatment-effect model is deferred because clinician intent, fluid responsiveness and adequate time-varying confounding control are unavailable.

Task-specific fitting is expected to be CPU-feasible for B1/Bdisc and may use one allocated A100 (`cuda:0`) for M2; GPU necessity is unverified. The measured discovery audit is the parent’s approximately seven-second archive/header/dictionary/eligibility probe; full hepatic feature coverage, stratum/event support, model convergence, bootstrap runtime and GPU need are unmeasured. The future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds; discovery is limited to 7,200 science seconds with concurrency 2. No proposer/solver weights are trained in discovery.

## Alternative disposition and claim boundary

The selected scientific alternative is Bdisc because it makes the liver-context interaction auditable and directly maps to the clinical uncertainty. M2 is retained as a substantive sensitivity because fixed summaries can lose temporal coupling and nonlinear recovery patterns. The liver interaction cannot be interpreted causally without adjudicated liver disease/clearance, treatment indication and counterfactual outcomes. Clinical experts must review marker units, etiologic meaning, competing discharge/readmission definitions and whether a 10% review queue represents a feasible intervention. External validation and prospective silent-mode evaluation are required before any practice recommendation.
