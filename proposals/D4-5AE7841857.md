# UKB-03 repaired direction: MRI muscle fat infiltration and same-visit functional discordance

Status: delivery-ready design, not fitted. This is an independent evolution of expert seed UKB-03, with the prior outcome-ascertainment branch retained as context only.

## Recommendation

Make the cross-sectional imaging-visit question primary. Use the first repeat-imaging change question only as a prespecified secondary, visit-ordered sensitivity. The reason is scientific and data-bound: instance-2 complete overlap is large (37,778 records with all MRI, bilateral grip, walking pace and falls fields), whereas only 765 participants have all of those fields at both instances 2 and 3. Instances 2 and 3 are explicitly the imaging visit and first repeat imaging visit in the UKB field definitions, but this derivative has no nonmissing 53-2.0 or 53-3.0; therefore repeat changes cannot be converted into a rate or exact elapsed-time effect.

## Opening, supported claim and unresolved hypothesis

The strongest inspected evidence supports a bounded association: MRI-derived adverse muscle composition, usually combining low muscle quantity with high fat infiltration, is associated with poorer self-reported function in a CKD-focused UKB imaging analysis [K1]. UKB MR/observational evidence also links anterior and posterior thigh MFI with grip strength [K2]. A consensus statement says imaging is complementary to grip and gait/function, not a replacement for those assessments [K3]. None of these establishes that MFI supplies clinically meaningful information beyond thigh muscle quantity and grip for a broader imaging population, and none turns walking pace or falls at an imaging visit into future outcomes.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among UKB participants at the instance-2 imaging visit with usable bilateral thigh MRI composition, bilateral grip, usual walking pace and falls responses, higher posterior-thigh MFI is associated with worse same-visit functional status after adjustment for thigh muscle quantity and grip, age, sex, BMI and smoking; specifically, adding MFI to the quantity-plus-grip model produces a prespecified clinically meaningful increase in the standardized probability of slow usual walking pace.

Primary outcome: the UKB ordinal usual-walking-pace response 924-2.0, retaining its documented categories and excluding only special/missing codes. The primary clinical contrast is the standardized probability of slow pace for MFI at the training-set 90th versus 10th percentile, with a 5-percentage-point absolute difference prespecified as clinically meaningful. Secondary outcomes are the full 2296-2.0 falls-response distribution and a binary any-fall contrast only after the UKB coding table is bound and its positive category is verified. These are responses about the visit, with falls referring to the last year; they are not prospective falls outcomes.

This is a descriptive/prognostic cross-sectional association, not a causal effect, diagnostic claim, validated sarcopenia phenotype or mechanism. A positive result would justify external validation against objective gait and adjudicated falls and could inform whether MRI composition deserves consideration when function and grip appear discordant. It would not justify screening, treatment, or inferring that fat infiltration causes weakness.

## Why this is an advance

The closest UKB study [K1] asks about a combined adverse-composition phenotype in a CKD-enriched comparison. This experiment asks whether MFI itself adds functional information once quantity and grip are already known, in the available general imaging cohort, and defines a clinically interpretable discordance contrast. [K2] supports MFI–grip relevance but does not test walking pace/falls conditional on grip and quantity. [K3] identifies the unresolved measurement boundary; the proposed nested models test it directly. Thus the advance is not another association or a small leaderboard gain: it tests whether composition measures a distinct functional dimension that would remain visible when conventional quantity and strength measures are already available.

## Models and estimand

Freeze a participant-level hash split (70% train, 15% validation, 15% locked test; seed 36), stratified after assignment by walking-pace category, falls category and sex. No participant may contribute to more than one split.

B0 is an interpretable ordinal logistic model for walking pace with age spline, sex, BMI, smoking, and Q_thigh plus grip. B1 adds standardized MFI_post. Standardization, imputation and the MFI 10th/90th percentiles are fit on training data only. Estimate the standardized 90th-versus-10th MFI slow-pace probability difference over the locked test covariate distribution, with participant bootstrap 95% intervals. Report odds ratios only as secondary summaries; the primary scale is absolute standardized probability. Repeat B0/B1 for the falls response as ordinal/multinomial outcome; do not collapse codes without verified code meanings.

B2 is a small CPU gradient-boosted multiclass/ordinal-compatible model using exactly the B1 inputs and split, with fixed shallow-tree settings selected on validation ordinal log loss. Its substantive purpose is to reveal stable nonlinear MFI thresholds or MFI-by-quantity/grip regions that the linear MFI term erases, especially high-MFI/normal-quantity or high-MFI/preserved-grip discordance. Report held-out ordinal log loss, Brier score, calibration, category-specific discrimination, permutation importance and held-out partial-dependence/effect tables. These curves are descriptive, not mechanisms. A B2 predictive gain without a stable B1 standardized contrast is not evidence that MFI adds clinically meaningful information.

Supportive primary evidence requires: (a) B1’s locked-test 90th-versus-10th MFI slow-pace contrast is at least 5 percentage points with a 95% interval excluding zero; (b) the direction is stable under bilateral/volume-indexing and disease-history sensitivities; (c) the effect is not explained by a matching grip/quantity permutation or by a large complete-case weighting correction; and (d) B2, if used, shows reproducible nonlinear/interaction structure rather than only an unstable test-score gain. Adverse evidence is a reversed/near-zero contrast, a contrast confined to one side or missingness pattern, disappearance after age/sex/disease adjustment, or a matching signal after MFI permutation. Inconclusive evidence is a wide interval, sparse valid outcome categories, unstable code interpretation, inadequate overlap for observation weights, or inability to bind disease timing.

Computationally checkable outputs are source hashes and columns, participant flow, raw-code distributions, derived variables, nested model fits, locked-test predictions, standardized contrasts, calibration and uncertainty. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for objective gait, validated functional testing, fall adjudication, pain/neurologic disease severity, treatment status, causality, mechanism, external validity and a clinical decision threshold.

## Rival explanations and falsification

- Selection: MRI attendance and complete MFI/function fields select healthier volunteers. Compare included participants with all instance-2 MRI participants on age, sex, BMI and smoking; model observation probability and apply stabilized inverse-probability weights as one sensitivity. Report extreme weights and overlap; this cannot remove UKB volunteer bias.
- Age and sex: MFI, quantity and function are age/sex patterned. Use flexible age, sex adjustment and prespecified sex-stratified estimates. Do not interpret residual association as biology.
- Medical disease: CKD, diabetes, obesity, pain and neurologic/musculoskeletal disease could cause both MFI and poor function. Use the paired pre-2014 ICD-10/date sensitivity above, plus BMI and smoking, and report how much the MFI contrast attenuates. No claim of complete disease control is allowed.
- Measurement: outcomes are self-report; falls is a last-year recall field and walking pace is a questionnaire category. Report code frequencies, special codes, left-right MFI agreement, implausible/extreme values and grip side discordance. Repeat imaging is not a replacement for objective performance.
- Timing: instances 2 and 3 are imaging visits by UKB field definitions, but no participant-level 53-2.0/53-3.0 values exist in this Parquet. Do not infer dates from file metadata, instance labels or current date.
- Leakage: permute MFI within sex once, permute outcome once, and rerun the locked pipeline. Any retained signal invalidates the corresponding computation.
- Disease-history negative control: compare the MFI contrast before and after pre-2014 disease adjustment; a large attenuation is evidence that disease composition, not an independent MFI dimension, may explain the association.

## Alternatives and resource choice

The simple baseline is retained because it directly tests the conditional estimand on an absolute probability scale and makes age, sex, quantity, grip and disease attenuation auditable. The learned alternative is retained because it can reveal threshold/interaction structure—such as high MFI despite preserved volume or grip—that a single linear MFI coefficient loses. Both use the identical participant split, fields, outcome construction and uncertainty evaluation. A transformer or mechanistic muscle model is deferred: no image payload, histology, objective gait, inflammatory mechanism, exact imaging dates or intervention labels are available. Revisit it only after objective functional validation or richer longitudinal dates are admitted.
