# Episode 14: does a lower SpO2 band relocate acidemic PaO2 into 60–89 without increasing <60?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and advance

For invasively ventilated adults with concurrent acidemia, does a released monitor SpO2 of 94–95%, rather than 96–97%, map to at least 10 percentage points more observed PaO2 values in 60–89 mm Hg while limiting any increase in PaO2 <60 mm Hg to less than five points?

This is a three-category relocation question: <60, 60–89, and >=90 mm Hg. It is clinically more informative than counting only PaO2 >=90 because a reduction above 90 is not beneficial if observations move below 60. It also avoids calling PaO2 90–120 “hyperoxemia” or assigning utility weights to the categories. Both components are required; neither can rescue the other.

The inspected full-text OXY-BREATHES review (Nguyen et al., Critical Care Medicine 2026; DOI 10.1097/CCM.0000000000007031; PMCID PMC13134654; frozen XML source [source checksum], [source checksum]) included nine randomized trials and 20,447 ventilated adults. Conservative definitions included SpO2 88–94% or PaO2 <80 and liberal definitions included SpO2 >=94% or PaO2 >=90; 90-day mortality was similar overall (RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.94–1.09), with condition-specific uncertainty. This motivates a consequential category question but does not identify a safe acidemia-specific target or validate the 10- and 5-point margins.

The inspected full-text Delgado study (Antioxidants 2026; DOI 10.3390/antiox15020235; PMCID PMC12938712; frozen XML source [source checksum], [source checksum]) reports a single-center retrospective cohort of 21,406 adults and 717,064 paired measurements, with higher PaO2 in acidotic states at comparable SpO2. It supports physiologic plausibility, not this EICU contrast, causality, safety, or treatment benefit.

The strongest EICU evidence currently supports feasibility and an imprecise mapping pattern. The parents reconstructed 556 acidemic assigned-band observations and an outcome-blind measured-overlap cohort. The episode-13 tradeoff parent reported favorable point estimates but its support code bootstrapped PaO2 <70 while labeling that interval PaO2 <60. Therefore that interval is not evidence for its stated safety endpoint. The valid sibling correctly bootstrapped PaO2 <60 in a 199-row clinical-pH-stratum overlap population. Its point estimates were +12.878 points for 60–89 and +4.283 points for <60, but 95% intervals were −7.973 to +28.521 and −10.724 to +12.525. The strongest supported claim is thus computational feasibility plus an observed but highly uncertain relocation pattern. Material middle-category gain and bounded severe-hypoxemia cost remain unresolved.

The substantive advance is one coherent compositional hypothesis whose benefit component uses the information-rich middle category (139 raw observations) while a non-rescuing severe-tail component prevents favorable relabeling of downward overshoot. It also repairs the endpoint/interval provenance defect and explicitly notes that PaO2 >120 barely changed in the parent analysis, so “less hyperoxemia” is not supported.

**Co-primary unresolved hypothesis**, in the measured-overlap population below:

- W = P(PaO2 60–89 | SpO2 94–95) − P(PaO2 60–89 | SpO2 96–97) is at least +10 percentage points; and
- H = P(PaO2 <60 | SpO2 94–95) − P(PaO2 <60 | SpO2 96–97) is less than +5 percentage points.

These margins are prespecified decision anchors, not validated MCIDs. This is a concurrent recorded-measurement estimand, not the effect of assigning an oxygen target.

Baselines are unweighted category risks in all 556 eligible observations and symmetric overlap omitting pH strata. Neither can replace the primary. Secondary decomposition splits >=90 into 90–120 and >120 and reports PaO2 <=70, <=80, E[min(PaO2,120)], weighted quantiles, and complete range. These diagnose destination and threshold sensitivity; they cannot rescue a co-primary failure.

## Full-source reference result and falsification

The correct 5,000-replicate clinical-pH-stratum analysis has weighted risks:

- lower band: 8.409% <60, 77.077% 60–89, 14.513% >=90;
- higher band: 4.126% <60, 64.199% 60–89, 31.675% >=90.

Thus W=+12.878 points (95% interval −7.973 to +28.521) and H=+4.283 points (−10.724 to +12.525), with 5,000/5,000 defined replicates. This is **inconclusive for both material relocation and bounded severe-tail cost**. It does not support “safe,” “beneficial,” “less hyperoxemia,” “no difference,” or assignment of either target.

Support for the joint hypothesis requires W lower bound >=+10, H upper bound <+5, and all diagnostics. The strongest allowed claim is limited to the retrospective recorded-measurement composition and justifies prospective synchronized study, not care change.

Adverse to the relocation margin means W upper bound <+10; if also <0, the middle-category mapping is opposite. Adverse to the severe-tail margin means H lower bound >=+5. Either falsifies the joint hypothesis. Crossing either margin, diagnostic failure, or sensitivity conflict is inconclusive. Support for one component with the other inconclusive leaves the joint claim inconclusive.
