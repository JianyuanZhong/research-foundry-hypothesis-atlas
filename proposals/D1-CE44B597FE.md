# Assay comparability and clinical-meaning audit for frozen HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B validation

## Narrow competing repair

This is a targeted successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent's frozen adult HCC repeat-TACE cohort, systemic-record/comparator definition, selected repeat-event rule, B/P/event/Y clocks, annual forward validation origins, nested ridge estimand, all-BP missing-Y identification, bootstrap, source-specific observability horizons, and noncausal interpretation. It does not add a covariate to M0/M1, alter an inclusion window, impute Y, or claim a new HCC result.

The remaining validity threat is narrower than follow-up availability: a finite number in `quantitative result` is not thereby comparable, valid, or clinically interpretable. The HCC lab table has no unit, reference-range, analyzer, method, result-status, collection-time, or specimen-quality field. `specimen type` is present but its coding and adequacy are not validated. Thus a numeric albumin or bilirubin value may be useful as a within-dataset recorded value, but cannot be assumed to be in a common clinical scale across encounters, departments, calendar periods, or assay implementations.

The repair asks:

> Does the frozen P-beyond-B prognostic comparison remain directionally and operationally stable when finite assay results are audited by exact assay identity, specimen/result validity, calendar/department/encounter heterogeneity, and assay-specific transformations that use no invented units or reference ranges?

This is falsifiable. The primary raw-scale observed-case estimand remains unchanged; the audit can instead force `inconclusive`, `selection_sensitive`, or `unsupported` when its assumptions are not met.

## What is supported, what is untested, and clinical importance

The available data support only that the linked `labs` rows contain recorded assay labels, optional qualitative text, optional specimen labels, and numeric strings at recorded times. The parent design supports a carefully specified test of incremental prediction of a subsequently recorded same-assay value, subject to its observation and horizon gates. Neither supports that values are standardized across analyzers, that a specimen was adequate, that a result was final, or that a recorded value represents hepatic function, tumor response, treatment tolerance, or a patient-important endpoint.

The untested claim is robustness of the P increment to assay-semantic and encounter heterogeneity. This matters because an apparent P gain can be a calendar/department recording signature, a specimen-validity artifact, or a scale shift rather than reproducible information about the patient. A conservative audit can distinguish a reproducible association on an explicitly defined recorded-value scale from a clinically meaningful biomarker claim. It cannot establish the latter without laboratory metadata and clinical adjudication.

## Pre-specified result/specimen validity ledger

For every raw lab row with an exact target assay, retain a row-level ledger rather than collapsing it prematurely. Report counts by assay, calendar year/block, `encounter department`, and encounter identity, including duplicate key counts.

1. **Lexical numeric validity.** Parse only a finite numeric scalar from `quantitative result`. Reject null, empty, nonnumeric, NaN/Inf, interval/censored forms (for example `<x`, `>x`, `≤x`, `≥x`), mixed numeric text, and values whose parser consumes only a partial token. Do not reinterpret `qualitative result` as a number.
2. **Qualitative-only separation.** A nonempty `qualitative result` without a valid quantitative scalar is a qualitative record, not a numeric result and never B, P, or Y. A qualitative flag accompanying a numeric result is retained as metadata; it does not prove validity or invalidate the scalar by itself.
3. **Specimen-known flag.** Nonempty `specimen type` is `specimen_label_present`; it is not specimen adequacy, collection provenance, or serum/plasma equivalence. Because the coding dictionary and quality status are unavailable, primary clocks preserve the parent's finite-value rule but report a required sensitivity restricted to nonempty specimen labels. If the executing implementation treats unknown specimen labels as a known specimen class, fail closed.
4. **Temporal validity.** Parse `test time` under one declared parser. Reject malformed times, impossible chronology relative to the selected clock, and duplicate ties unless the parent's deterministic tie rule resolves them. Result time is not collection time and does not establish preanalytic timing.
5. **Assay identity.** Exact `test` equality is mandatory. No unit conversion, reference-range normalization, analyzer harmonization, or albumin/bilirubin pooling is permitted. Rows outside the two exact labels are audit-only and cannot become a target through text matching.
6. **Extreme-value handling.** Do not winsorize, clip, physiologically reject, or replace values based on external clinical thresholds. Such rules would import units/clinical meaning not present in the source. Report finite extreme values as recorded and assess influence by a predeclared leave-one-row-out or robust audit, not by silently deleting them.

## Assay-specific transformations without invented units

The primary model remains on its inherited assay-specific recorded numeric scale. For a sensitivity audit only, construct a transformation separately within each assay using **training data available before each origin** and no external clinical constants:

* Let `F_a,t` be the empirical CDF of valid training values for assay `a` in that training fold, with deterministic midranks for ties and a fixed finite tail convention.
* Map each value to a dimensionless `z_a = Φ^{-1}(clip(F_a,t(value), 0.5/n, 1-0.5/n))`, where n is the number of valid training values and Φ is the standard normal CDF. If n is too small, all values are retained on the raw recorded scale and the transformed audit is `inconclusive`, never imputed.
* As a second, less distribution-sensitive audit, use the training median and IQR only within the same exact assay, with `asinh((value-median)/IQR)`; if IQR is zero/undefined, mark that transformed fit unavailable. These are dimensionless ordering/dispersion transforms, not albumin or bilirubin units, reference-range scores, or clinical severity indices.
* Fit preprocessing and transformations on training rows only; apply frozen maps to held-out rows. Never estimate a map jointly across albumin and bilirubin, across calendar blocks using future data, or using Y-missing rows as pseudo-outcomes.

Compare the sign and uncertainty of the locked P increment under raw, rank-normal, and robust within-assay audit scales. This is a robustness diagnostic, not a license to select the most favorable scale. A transformed result cannot be described as clinically normal/abnormal or as a change in hepatic function. If the raw and every valid assay-specific audit scale disagree materially in sign, label `selection_sensitive` or `inconclusive` rather than average them.

Do not estimate unstable subgroup gains in cells below the parent's minimum; display counts and mark sparse cells. A calendar or department gradient in observation, specimen labels, or value distributions is an audit warning, not evidence of an assay shift, because analyzer and method are unavailable.

Run two negative controls as pipeline checks, preserving the clinical estimand separately.

* **Permutation negative control for P.** Within each assay, arm, origin training split, and calendar/department stratum when feasible, permute P values across BP-eligible patients while preserving the number of P rows and missingness; do not permute B or Y. Refit the complete locked pipeline over a prespecified seed set and report the null gain distribution. Failure of the distribution to center near zero, or any gain arising in a synthetic dataset where P is generated independent of Y conditional on M0, indicates leakage, duplicate joining, or preprocessing error and is `unsupported`; it is not a clinical null test.
* **Non-target/qualitative negative control.** Re-run the row classifier with qualitative-only and exact-non-target lab rows presented to the target selector. They must produce no B/P/Y and no change in any denominator or model matrix. If any such row becomes a numeric target through coercion, fuzzy matching, or fallback to `Qualitative Result`, fail closed. This control tests assay identity and parser specificity, not biological absence of association.

A third synthetic directionality control must include a post-event value that is accidentally made available to P construction; the expected result is a hard failure, not a reported gain. No empirical negative control may be interpreted as proof of no biology.

## Fail-closed decision tree

Apply precedence after computational verification:

1. `unsupported` for source/schema/hash/header mismatch; duplicate-causing joins; fuzzy assay matching; units or reference ranges invented; target/clock rows leaking into a transformation or horizon; invalid parser; non-target or qualitative coercion; negative-control failure; or any claim that a department is a site/analyzer or a finite recorded value is clinically valid.
2. `inconclusive` for failed inherited cohort/arm/BP gates; nonestimable transformed map; sparse or degenerate strata; nonfinite predictions; unavailable SD/bootstrap; insufficient assay-specific training support; or missing specimen/result metadata when the requested claim requires it.
3. `adverse_primary` only under the parent's frozen primary rule (nonpositive adequately supported observed gain or wholly nonpositive global all-BP envelope). Assay-audit instability cannot be called adverse clinical evidence.
4. `selection_sensitive` when raw-scale support is favorable but transformed scales disagree, specimen-label/qualitative/department/calendar gradients are material, assay observation differs materially, or permutation/stratified diagnostics reveal dependence that the frozen estimand cannot resolve. A complete-case gain with these warnings is not supportive.
5. `supported_recorded-scale_association` only when the inherited primary gates pass, each assay remains separate, raw and every estimable prespecified within-assay audit scale have concordant direction with declared uncertainty, no material validity/heterogeneity warning is present, and the parent's global all-BP bound is strictly positive throughout the declared training-range scenario. This label supports only a selected short-horizon association on a recorded-value scale, not a biomarker interpretation, clinical decision, treatment effect, benefit, or utility.

If site/analyzer/specimen adequacy or final-result status is required for a stronger claim, the answer is `unavailable`, not inferred from `Encounter Department`, `Specimen Type`, `Qualitative Result`, encounter closure, orders, medications, or procedures.

## Substantive advance and remaining uncertainty

Relative to the parent, this proposal adds a falsifiable assay-semantic firewall rather than another availability proxy: a row-level validity ledger, exact assay separation, explicit qualitative/specimen limitations, calendar/department/encounter heterogeneity diagnostics, negative controls for parser/leakage failure, and train-only dimensionless assay-specific sensitivity transformations. It preserves the primary estimand and all inherited safeguards. It does not manufacture units, reference ranges, site labels, clinical outcomes, or empirical HCC findings. The main unresolved uncertainty remains whether the recorded values are analytically comparable and clinically meaningful; this proposal can expose instability and prevent overinterpretation, but cannot solve that missing-data problem.
