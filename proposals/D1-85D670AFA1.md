# Provisional repaired hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery in MIMIC-IV

Parent: [prior hypothesis]
Status: provisional repair; exact schema and cohort checks are in progress. This draft does not claim computed results.

## Proposed relationship

Among adult ICU patients with an acute kidney injury (AKI) episode who have a creatinine measurement at or near the 48-hour landmark, patients whose creatinine has improved but whose urine output remains low or whose cumulative fluid balance remains positive will have a higher risk of subsequent in-hospital death or persistent/worsening kidney dysfunction than patients with concordant creatinine and urine-output recovery, after accounting for pre-landmark illness severity and treatment.

The clinically useful claim is narrower than “creatinine is wrong”: discordance may represent delayed clearance, ongoing tubular injury, fluid overload, or measurement/monitoring differences. The experiment tests whether the discordant phenotype adds prognostic information beyond a creatinine-only recovery phenotype.

## Population and temporal design (to be verified against source headers)

Use MIMIC-IV `hosp/admissions`, `icu/icustays`, `hosp/labevents`, `icu/outputevents`, and `icu/inputevents`, with dictionary tables `hosp/d_labitems` and `icu/d_items` for item identification and units. Join within the same `subject_id` and `hadm_id`; do not join across datasets. The proposed landmark is the first ICU stay after admission at 48 hours after ICU admission, restricted to patients alive and still in the ICU at that time. Creatinine and urine-output summaries use only measurements recorded before or at the landmark. Fluid balance uses input/output records through the landmark. Outcomes are measured after the landmark: in-hospital death from `hosp/admissions.deathtime`/`hospital_expire_flag`, and a prespecified kidney endpoint based on post-landmark creatinine/urine-output records. No post-landmark value is used to define exposure.

## Falsifiable test and alternatives

Primary comparison: risk difference or adjusted hazard for post-landmark death/renal deterioration between discordant and concordant recovery groups, with a creatinine-only baseline model and a model adding urine-output/fluid-balance discordance. Use prespecified uncertainty intervals and calibration/discrimination as secondary descriptive measures, not proof of mechanism.

Supportive result: the discordant group has a materially higher adjusted risk and the incremental model improves held-out calibration or discrimination with stable uncertainty. Adverse result: no clinically meaningful association, or the association disappears under a prespecified sensitivity analysis for measurement density/treatment, would weaken the proposed prognostic claim. Inconclusive result: sparse item coverage, wide intervals, or unstable landmark eligibility would justify revising the phenotype or acquiring richer data rather than declaring no effect.

The simple baseline is a prespecified creatinine trajectory (baseline-to-landmark change or recovery indicator) with adjustment for age, admission type, ICU timing, and pre-landmark severity proxies. The substantive alternative is a time-aware model using the sequence of creatinine, urine-output, and fluid-balance observations through the landmark, with a held-out temporal split and uncertainty estimates. The alternative can reveal whether the pattern and timing of discordance—not merely the final creatinine value—carry information; it must be compared on the same population and outcomes. A mechanistic claim about tubular injury or delayed clearance requires additional clinical adjudication or measured kidney function and is not supported by this dataset alone.
