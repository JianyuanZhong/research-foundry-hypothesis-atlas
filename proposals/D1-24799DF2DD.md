# Are between-hospital differences in liberation from invasive ventilation patient recovery or ascertainment?

## Status, opening, and deliverable

This is a substantive repair of the untested eICU recovery-speed seed (`[prior hypothesis]`), not an executed study. The seed's broad “organ support recovery” claim is narrowed to invasive mechanical ventilation (IMV), the support with the strongest longitudinal evidence in this eICU snapshot.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is that IMV discontinuation practices vary across ICUs and that death before an attempt is common [K1]; in a large prospective cohort, death while ventilated and transfer without successful separation were frequent, and successful liberation required surviving without reintubation [K2]. Longitudinal EHR models can estimate respiratory transitions, but their labels and transport depend on local recording systems [K3]. None of these works establishes that eICU hospitals have case-mix-adjusted differences in patient recovery, that such differences survive competing-event and documentation audits, or that residual variation is caused by care quality.

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** Among adults still invasively ventilated 24 hours after a documented first IMV start, the target-standardized 14-day probability of documented sustained liberation (alive and without reconstructed reinstitution of IMV for 48 hours) differs materially between eligible hospitals after baseline severity adjustment, and this heterogeneity persists after a longitudinal model accounts for the preceding respiratory-recovery trajectory and observation process. The primary supportive pattern is a cross-fitted 90th–10th percentile hospital contrast of at least 10 percentage points with a cluster-bootstrap 95% lower bound above 5 points in both models, directionally stable under source-balanced and timing sensitivities. This is an associational hospital-context claim, not a hospital ranking or causal practice effect.

The leading explanation is a persistent hospital-context component compatible with differences in weaning, sedation, mobilization, staffing, or other unmeasured practices. The strongest rivals are: (1) different patient severity and nonlinear recovery trajectories; (2) informative death; (3) ICU/hospital discharge or interhospital transfer truncating observation; (4) reintubation hidden by calling the first “off” event success; and (5) hospital-specific respiratory documentation. The experiment is designed to distinguish persistent heterogeneity from these rivals, but cannot distinguish a beneficial practice from unmeasured referral, goals-of-care, clinician judgment, or data-system effects.

The future solver must newly construct and clinically validate the episodes, freeze the cohort and splits, fit both specified multistate models, estimate standardized hospital contrasts and uncertainty, and produce the tables/figures listed below. A planned study is complete only when those computed outputs exist and every reported conclusion is linked to them.

## Population, clocks, and episode semantics

1. Build one hospital timeline per `patienthealthsystemstayid` by joining all unit rows in `patient`. For any unit-relative event offset (u), define hospital-relative time as (u-	exttt{hospitaladmitoffset}). Verify that `hospitalid` is constant within the hospitalization; exclude discordant chains and report them. Keep all linked ICU units so an “Other ICU” transfer is not mistaken for discharge.
2. Define candidate IMV intervals from `respiratoryCare.priorventstartoffset/priorventendoffset` and nonzero `ventstartoffset`, corroborated by invasive airway values (`Oral ETT`, `Nasal ETT`, `Double-Lumen Tube`, or `Cricothyrotomy`), `respiratoryCharting` invasive ventilator/device rows, `treatment` mechanical-ventilation rows, and APACHE `intubated=1` and `vent=1`. Noninvasive CPAP/BiPAP and tracheostomy are not extubation equivalents.
3. Index time (t0) is the earliest high-confidence IMV start in a hospitalization. A high-confidence start requires a valid respiratoryCare interval plus either APACHE intubated/vent or temporally concordant respiratoryCharting/treatment evidence. Primary patients must be continuously in the invasive state through (L=t0+24) hours. Exclude age <18 or unparseable, tracheostomy at/before (L), missing hospital, hospital exit before (L), and no valid start. Include at most one analytic episode per hospital stay; retain repeated people but assign every `uniquepid` to one split and bootstrap cluster.
4. Include hospitals only after phenotype validation if they retain >=50 index episodes and >=20 candidate sustained-liberation endpoints and satisfy the source-balance gate below. Freeze hospital inclusion before fitting outcomes.
5. Follow from (L) through (L+14) days, hospital death, or observation-ending discharge/transfer, whichever occurs first. Do not use post-(L) physiology as a predictor; it defines outcomes only. Inputs for both models end strictly before (L).

The observed multistate process is:

- **V:** invasively ventilated at the landmark.
- **P:** provisional liberation at the first high-confidence end of an invasive interval.
- **S:** sustained documented liberation after 48 hours alive and observed without a new invasive start (absorbing primary success).
- **R:** reconstructed reinstitution of invasive ventilation within 48 hours after P; R may return to P.
- **D:** death while in V, P, or R, using unit/hospital discharge status and timing (absorbing competing event).
- **X:** observation-ending alive hospital discharge, external transfer, or an ICU exit after which the linked hospitalization cannot establish respiratory state through the needed 48 hours (absorbing competing event, reported by subtype).
- **C:** administratively still V/P/R at day 14.

Transfers between linked ICU units in the same `patienthealthsystemstayid` are stitched, not X. A candidate P cannot be generated by chart silence. It requires a valid historical end interval or explicit `RT Vent On/Off=Off/off/Suspended` plus a subsequent noninvasive/room-air device state, with source-specific rules frozen before outcome analysis. `treatment` “ventilator weaning” is supportive context, never proof of extubation. A later high-confidence invasive start defines R; `treatment` reintubation is corroboration. The primary 48-hour sustain window matches [K3]'s reintubation window; a seven-day sustain sensitivity follows [K2]. Tracheostomy after (L) is reported as a separate competing transition, not liberation.

Primary estimand: for each eligible hospital, the standardized 14-day cumulative incidence of S if that hospital's fitted transition effects applied to the pooled eligible-cohort baseline covariate distribution, while preserving death and X as competing events. Primary heterogeneity is the P90–P10 absolute standardized risk difference. Secondary estimands are hospital random-effect SD/MOR for V→P, and standardized 14-day risks of D, X, R after P, and remaining ventilated. No simple ratio of liberated survivors is allowed.

## Phenotype validation and smallest decision-changing check

The bounded audit in `data-audit.md` found 865,381 respiratoryCare rows across 44,772 stays, but `ventendoffset` is zero in 865,224 rows; historical interval fields, not current end fields, carry most candidate ends. Explicit RT Vent On/Off is present in only 2,741 stays and strict O2-device evidence is concentrated in six hospitals. A conservative provisional reconstruction produced 5,201 adult 24-hour landmark stays in 37 hospitals, 4,648 candidate interval ends by day 14, and 381 later invasive starts within 48 hours. These are feasibility counts, not hypothesis results.

Before model fitting, two blinded ICU clinicians independently adjudicate 300 timeline packets sampled across hospital, apparent outcome (S/R/D/X), and source combination, with identifiers masked. Packets include only relevant structured respiratory rows, airway state, treatment events and exits. Continue only if both invasive-support and sustained-liberation PPV are >=0.85 with lower Wilson 95% bound >=0.80, Cohen kappa >=0.70, >=30 hospitals retain >=50 index cases and >=20 endpoints, and no one event source produces >90% of endpoints in >25% of hospitals. Revise rules once using disagreements, validate on a fresh 150-event sample, and then freeze. If the gate still fails, abandon the between-hospital recovery claim and report a measurement-validation result; do not substitute `actualventdays`.

## Common inputs, split, and preprocessing

Both models receive exactly the same pre-(L) information: fixed demographics/admission/APACHE/EOL variables plus six-hour bins from (t0) to (L) containing respiratory settings, oxygenation/ventilation, vital signs, selected labs, sedative/opioid/pressor exposure, measurement counts, missingness masks, time since last observation, and chart-entry delay. The transparent model reduces each stream to prespecified first/last/min/max, linear slope, area outside clinical ranges, and observation-count summaries. The alternative receives the same four-bin tensor and masks. Neither gets hospital-volume, outcome-period data, post-landmark physiology, `actualventdays`, discharge destination, nor an outcome-derived feature.

Create five patient-grouped folds by deterministic hash of `uniquepid`, stratified so every included hospital contributes to each fold. All stays for a person remain together. Within each training fold, fit units, winsorization limits, imputation distributions, feature selection and hyperparameters; never fit preprocessing on held-out rows. Use identical folds for both models. A whole-hospital holdout sensitivity trains the shared patient component on four hospital groups, then estimates no target-specific patient representation; it evaluates whether patient-state encoding transports, not target-hospital ranking.

## Model B: transparent competing-risk multistate baseline

Fit a hierarchical semi-Markov piecewise-exponential model for allowed transitions V→P, P→S, P→R, R→P, and V/P/R→D or X (plus tracheostomy), with 6-hour intervals for days 1–3 and daily intervals thereafter. Use prespecified restricted cubic splines for continuous baseline summaries, diagnostic group and unit type, and partially pooled hospital random intercepts for each transition. Generate standardized 14-day state-occupation probabilities by g-computation over the pooled baseline distribution. Report transition-specific coefficients, hospital shrinkage, P90–P10 contrasts and bootstrap confidence intervals. This baseline preserves mutually exclusive clinical paths and interpretability but loses within-day order, nonlinear covariation, informative missingness structure beyond summary counts, and patient-specific latent transition dynamics.

## Model L: mechanistically structured learned longitudinal alternative

Fit a shared latent-state semi-Markov model with a small GRU encoder (two layers, 64 hidden units, dropout 0.2) over the four six-hour bins. The encoder outputs two explicitly separated states:

1. a **physiologic recovery state**, constrained by supervised auxiliary reconstruction of next-bin respiratory burden (FiO2, PEEP, pressure/volume, SpO2/PaO2, vasopressor and neurologic summaries); and
2. an **observation state**, trained to reconstruct next-bin label availability, counts and entry delays.

Both states and fixed covariates feed the same allowed transition hazards as Model B, with partially pooled hospital intercepts. A gradient-reversal penalty prevents hospital prediction from the physiologic state conditional on case mix, while the observation state may encode site workflow. Hospital effects are estimated only in transition heads, not embedded into patient states. Tune the penalty and hidden dimension in training folds against a lexicographic objective: valid multistate likelihood/calibration first, then hospital leakage, never the desired direction of heterogeneity.

Model L is scientifically useful because it can test whether nonlinear recovery ordering and observation dynamics explain variation that Model B's summaries attribute to hospitals. It is not interpreted as discovering a biological mechanism. Required ablations remove the observation branch, recovery branch, hospital adversary, and missingness/count inputs. A learned model that improves likelihood but hides hospital identity in its recovery state or worsens state calibration is rejected.

## Evaluation, falsification, and interpretation

On held-out patients, compare:

- integrated Brier score and state-specific calibration at days 3, 7 and 14;
- transition log likelihood;
- calibration and AUROC/AUPRC for 48-hour R after P (descriptive, not the primary objective);
- standardized hospital P90–P10 S-risk contrast, random-effect SD, Spearman stability across folds, and bootstrap uncertainty;
- D, X, R, tracheostomy and still-ventilated risks separately;
- performance and contrasts by age, sex, broad race/ethnicity, surgical/medical status, and baseline severity;
- source-agreement strata, chart-entry delay, respiratory-row density, hospital data-system signature, 7-day success, 72-hour sustain, exclusion of EOL records, and stricter dual-source episodes.

Use 1,000 cluster bootstraps sampling hospitals and then people within hospital. Prespecify the following interpretations:

- **Supportive:** both admissible models show P90–P10 >=10 percentage points with lower 95% bound >5; hospital ordering is stable (fold and primary-sensitivity Spearman >=0.70); no D/X/R tradeoff explains an apparent advantage; strict dual-source and source-balanced results retain direction; and Model L does not fail calibration/leakage gates. This supports persistent adjusted hospital-context heterogeneity in documented sustained liberation. If Model L attenuates Model B's hospital-effect variance by >=25% while retaining residual support, nonlinear recovery trajectories explain part of the descriptive variation.
- **Adverse/falsifying:** the contrast is <5 points or its interval includes zero in either admissible model; rankings are unstable; the signal disappears/reverses with source balancing, timing perturbation or dual-source episodes; “better” liberation is offset by higher death, X or R; or hospital can be recovered from the purported physiologic state above the prespecified leakage ceiling (macro-AUROC >0.65). Conclude that eICU does not support a stable patient-recovery difference; documentation, competing events or case mix is the stronger account.
- **Inconclusive:** event adjudication fails, <30 hospitals survive, confidence intervals span both <5 and >=10 points, models are materially miscalibrated, Model B and L disagree without a resolved diagnostic, or source-specific missingness prevents comparable episodes. Report the measurement limitation and defer hospital comparison.
- **Measurement-only result:** if the phenotype gate fails, the strongest valid conclusion concerns nonportability of eICU ventilation episode fields. It cannot answer the hospital-recovery hypothesis.

No association, attenuation, latent state, or hospital random effect identifies a treatment mechanism or quality. A stronger claim that sedation/weaning practice causes faster safe liberation requires reliable time-varying practice measurements, clinician readiness/SBT information, positivity, causal assumptions and preferably a prospective or quasi-experimental study. Hospital feedback or public ranking requires ICU-clinician review, site metadata verification, fairness review and independent contemporary validation.

## Outputs and verifier boundaries

Required machine-readable outputs: `cohort_flow.json`, `phenotype_validation.json`, `source_discrepancy_by_hospital.csv`, frozen `split_assignments.csv`, `state_transitions.parquet`, model manifests/checkpoints, held-out `state_metrics.json`, `hospital_standardized_risks.csv`, `hospital_contrasts.json`, `sensitivity_results.csv`, `ablation_results.csv`, bootstrap replicates, and a report mapping each claim to output cells.

The automatic verifier can check paths/hashes, joins and clocks, leakage, state exclusivity, split grouping, transition logic, model inputs, competing-risk arithmetic, uncertainty calculations, thresholds, and whether conclusions match supportive/adverse/inconclusive outputs. It cannot adjudicate clinical truth, validate chart-review expertise, infer a causal practice effect, certify a hospital as high quality, or establish generalizability.

## Resources and alternatives

Measured discovery work: the full respiratoryCare/respiratoryCharting/treatment audit took about 8.5 CPU-minutes with 4 CPUs and 16 GiB; focused scans took seconds to tens of seconds. Proposed full solver envelope: phenotype construction and bootstrap baseline on 16 CPUs/64 GiB for 2–4 hours; Model L on one allocated A100-80GB, 8 CPUs and 64 GiB for approximately 1–3 hours per fold, with checkpoints, then bootstrap/refits on CPU/GPU within the configured 8-hour, 16-CPU, 8-GPU planning ceiling. GPU runtime is an unverified estimate; no GPU pilot was needed to establish scientific feasibility. The baseline alone remains valid if Model L fails technically, but cannot satisfy the trajectory-explanation claim.

Alternatives not selected: a cause-specific Cox model was rejected because it collapses P→R and competing exits; `actualventdays` regression was rejected because it has no transition times; a pure extubation-failure classifier was deferred because it conditions on clinician selection for extubation; an end-to-end transformer was deferred because four six-hour bins offer little extra temporal resolution over the structured GRU. Revisit higher-resolution sequence models only if the frozen cohort retains substantially denser cross-hospital data and the GRU shows scientifically relevant residual temporal error.
