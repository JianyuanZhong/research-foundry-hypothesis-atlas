# Repair child: observed discordant renal recovery at the ICU-discharge landmark

Status: planned design only. No MIMIC rows were read into this branch, no model was fitted, and no clinical conclusion is claimed. Parent: [prior hypothesis]. This is a substantive child focused on ascertainment and interpretation, not a topic or estimand change.

## Scientific question and advance

The parent asks whether a fall in serum creatinine without concurrent urine-output recovery in the last 24 hours before ICU discharge identifies short-horizon risk beyond measured severity. The strongest available evidence supports two narrower premises: creatinine and urine output are complementary clinical observations ([K1]), and creatinine can misrepresent filtration when muscle production and ICU physiology change ([K2]). Dynamic renal assessment can be prognostic while extra biomarkers add inconsistent incremental value ([K3]). None of those observations establishes that an EHR-defined discordance at ICU discharge is a reproducible risk marker, nor that a repeated longitudinal pattern is different from a brief or poorly documented episode.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> In adults surviving a first ICU stay of at least 48 hours, among patients with adequate and sufficiently dense urine-output observation, creatinine improvement without urine-output recovery during [t0-24 h,t0) is associated with higher 48-hour post-ICU-discharge risk of readmission or in-hospital death after adjustment for pre-landmark severity and discharge-selection proxies; the association is stronger and more reproducible when the same discordance recurs across earlier 6-hour bins than when it appears only in the final bin.

This is a non-causal prognostic claim. It does not say that delaying discharge, changing diuretics, or restoring urine output would improve outcomes. The substantive advance is a boundary test: it separates a clinically interpretable observed phenotype from three alternatives that the parent did not yet operationalize tightly enough—missing urine measurement, residual illness/discharge selection, and transient threshold/charting noise.

The result that would change the next decision is the contrast between (a) a dense-observation, severity-adjusted landmark association and (b) a repeated-versus-transient contrast in a held-out sample. If the association disappears with observation coverage and discharge proxies, the useful conclusion is that the phenotype is largely ascertainment/selection, not that renal recovery is biologically irrelevant.

## Ascertainment repair: urine observation is a measured variable

The parent’s requirement of “at least one urine record” is insufficient: one record can be a missed chart, a zero, a partial interval, or a late retrospective total. The experiment must preserve coverage information rather than treating all unobserved volume as zero.

Required urine records are icu/outputevents rows restricted to d_items item IDs 226559 (Foley), 226560 (Void), and 226561 (Condom Cath), joined within the selected stay. Use charttime, numeric nonnegative value, and valueuom; reject incompatible units rather than silently converting. The readiness manifest must prove these item labels are unique and present in icu/d_items.

For each 6-hour bin and the final 24-hour window, construct:

1. urine_volume_observed: sum of valid nonnegative volumes, with no imputation of unobserved volume.
2. urine_n_records: number of valid output rows.
3. urine_active_hours: hours between the earliest and latest valid urine chart time, clipped to the bin/window; this is a documentation-span measure, not a claim of continuous collection.
4. urine_bin_coverage: urine_active_hours / bin_hours, capped at 1.
5. urine_any: an indicator that at least one valid row exists.
6. urine_zero_fraction: fraction of valid rows whose volume is zero.
7. item-source indicators for Foley, Void, and Condom Cath, plus the count of distinct source types.

Coverage is a documentation/measurement proxy, not a validated completeness measure. A record timestamp does not prove that all urine was collected or charted. Define the primary ascertainable cohort as final-window urine_bin_coverage >= 0.75, at least two valid records in each of the four 6-hour bins, and at least one source-type indicator. Preserve the full eligible cohort as a pre-specified observation-sensitivity cohort with coverage features and missingness indicators; do not silently replace it with the dense subset. Also report a complete-case ladder: any record, >=2 records, >=0.75 coverage, and the strict four-bin rule, with cohort flow and outcome counts.

The low-output rule is retained as an operational threshold: total valid volume <400 mL in 24 hours. It is intentionally unweighted because MIMIC binding does not guarantee a valid weight field in this proposal. A weight-indexed analysis is deferred unless a verified weight field and compatible timing are added in a new approved design. Sensitivity checks use continuous volume and the zero/low/high categories, never negative values or unit-mixed sums. Urine data from other item IDs are not used as if they were measured equivalents.

Creatinine remains item 50912 in hosp/labevents, linked to hosp/d_labitems. Require at least two valid creatinine observations at least six hours apart, with the final observation in [t0-24h,t0). Define C_peak as the maximum valid value in that window, C_last as the final value, and improvement as C_peak-C_last >=0.3 mg/dL. Record creatinine count, span, and time since the peak. This makes a two-point improvement distinguishable from a long, repeatedly observed trajectory.

## Baseline versus the longitudinal latent alternative

The method comparison is scientific, not a contest for a small AUROC gain.

B0 is a penalized logistic regression for the primary 48-hour outcome. Exact inputs are age, sex, admission type, ICU LOS, admission-to-t0 time, C_peak, C_last, creatinine improvement, time since creatinine peak, final 24-hour urine volume, low-output indicator, urine coverage, urine record count/source indicators, last and preceding MAP, available lactate and platelets, vasopressor/ventilation/RRT indicators, and all corresponding measurement/missingness indicators. B1 adds the four-level final-window group: improved/high, improved/low (discordant), not-improved/high, and not-improved/low. Continuous creatinine and urine quantities are retained alongside the group so B1 does not confuse a threshold with information already represented by its components. Use standardization fixed from training data and a prespecified penalty selected on the tuning split.

The bounded alternative is a three-state multivariate HMM/state-space model, fit only if the readiness gate passes, on 6-hour bins from ICU admission to t0. Its emissions use the same measured channels: creatinine level and change, urine volume, urine coverage/record count/source indicators, MAP, available lactate/platelet channels, support indicators, and missingness indicators. Do not interpolate unobserved urine as zero. Use an explicit observation mask and coverage channel; bins with no valid output are missing emissions, not low output. Fit the model on training subjects only; select the number/regularization of states and emission family on the 15% tuning set, with fixed subject-level 70/15/15 train/tune/test membership made before fitting. The test set is untouched until final held-out evaluation. Use 200 patient bootstrap resamples for intervals, with a reproducible seed and patient-level resampling.

The latent model’s scientific target is not “better prediction.” It estimates:

- posterior probability of a renal-observation state at each bin;
- occupancy and number of transitions;
- time since the last transition;
- posterior probability that the final discordance is persistent across at least two prior bins versus isolated to the last bin;
- held-out outcome risk conditional on those summaries.

The specific discriminating comparison is repeated discordance versus transient discordance at matched final-window observed discordance and matched B0 risk. The rival charting-noise explanation predicts that the pattern will be concentrated in low-coverage bins, have unstable state assignments, show little persistence, and lose its association after coverage/support adjustment. A repeated-physiology explanation predicts higher posterior persistence, consistent transitions across bins, and a held-out risk contrast that survives coverage and support-restricted analyses. Neither pattern establishes renal mechanism or causality.

A simpler mechanistic alternative was considered: a deterministic two-compartment filtration/urine-balance model using creatinine change, urine volume, and support/coverage. It is deferred because MIMIC lacks measured GFR, cystatin C in the bound design, fluid composition, reliable body weight, complete diuretic exposure, and a trustworthy creatinine-generation parameter. Fitting such a model would create untestable assumptions rather than reveal filtration. A transformer/LSTM is also deferred because it would add opaque sequence capacity without a separable scientific observable. The HMM is retained only because persistence, transition timing, and observation masking are direct outputs that answer the repeated-versus-transient uncertainty.

Selection rule: if the test set has fewer than 200 subjects or 30 primary events, or if the observation-mask/coverage channels cannot be represented without imputation, do not fit or interpret the HMM; report B0/B1 feasibility and defer the latent question. If state separation is poor (pre-specified emission overlap and transition instability across bootstrap fits), treat the HMM as inconclusive, not as evidence for noise or physiology.

## Analysis, uncertainty, and falsification

Primary analysis:

1. Freeze the item manifest, unit checks, cohort flow, coverage thresholds, outcome construction, and split before fitting.
2. Fit B0 and B1 on training data, tune penalty on tuning data, and report held-out standardized group risks, risk differences/ratios, Brier score, log loss, AUROC/AUPRC as descriptive discrimination, calibration slope/intercept, and calibration plots.
3. Estimate uncertainty with 200 patient-level bootstrap resamples of the held-out test predictions for performance and standardized contrasts; for the HMM, repeat posterior summaries within each bootstrap using fixed fitted parameters unless a convergence/fit instability rule requires refitting, which must be stated.
4. Report missingness, coverage, support, and exclusions for each cohort and outcome. Never turn a confidence interval crossing zero into proof of no association.

Prespecified falsification and robustness checks are: permutation of discordance within baseline-risk/ICU-LOS/admission strata; 12-hour versus 48-hour windows; continuous creatinine slope; final-window coverage strata; complete-case ladder; measurement-density and ICU-care-unit strata; no-support restriction; RRT exclusion; creatinine-only, urine-only, and interaction models; and a pseudo-landmark 24 hours before t0. The pseudo-landmark cannot share future data with the primary feature window. A stable result should have directionally similar estimates in the dense and full cohorts, while a documentation artifact should attenuate as coverage and measurement proxies are controlled. These are diagnostic patterns, not causal proof.

Supportive result: in the dense cohort, B1’s discordant contrast is directionally stable with calibrated held-out risk above concordance after measured severity/support adjustment, and the HMM shows reproducible persistence with a held-out risk contrast for repeated versus transient discordance. This supports an observable prognostic phenotype and justifies external validation or adjudicated urine completeness; it does not support delaying discharge or a renal mechanism.

Adverse result: the contrast is absent after coverage adjustment, reverses in the support-restricted cohort, tracks low measurement density, or is explained by B0 severity/support variables; the HMM finds isolated low-coverage transitions. This supports ascertainment, residual severity, or discharge-selection explanations and should end the direction unless a better measurement source is obtained.

Inconclusive result: sparse dense-cohort membership, fewer than 30 events, wide intervals, missing/invalid units, unstable item labels, poor state separation, calibration failure, or a readiness failure. Then report feasibility and the exact missing evidence; do not select a favorable subgroup or claim a null.
