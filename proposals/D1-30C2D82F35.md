# Safer cross-hospital early warning for recorded renal deterioration: physiology versus laboratory-process dependence

## Proposal status and clinical decision

This is a substantive eICU child of `[prior hypothesis]`, with supporting design foundations from `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It also audits the unused expert seed `[starting question]` (imported candidate `[prior hypothesis]`), which is assessed here as repairable for eICU but not executable as written.

The consequential decision is whether a six-hour ICU surveillance rule intended for use at a new hospital should rely on early physiology alone or also on laboratory values and recorded testing practice. A model that appears strong in a random split can be unsafe after transport if local laboratory ordering, interfaces, or follow-up ascertainment differ. The primary hypothesis is therefore a safety/transportability hypothesis:

> Among adult ICU stays reaching minute 360, an early physiology-only model will have more stable held-out calibration and high-sensitivity risk performance for a first later recorded creatinine rise across hospitals than models augmented with laboratory values and laboratory-testing-process features; any apparent augmentation benefit will be attenuated or reverse in hospitals with different recorded testing intensity.

The null is that laboratory augmentation has no reproducible cross-hospital disadvantage or that it transports at least as well as physiology alone. This is an observational prediction experiment for a recorded laboratory phenotype. It does not estimate the effect of testing, treatment, renal perfusion, insulin, nutrition, or a clinical alert.

## Existing evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported before computation is structural: the frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-stay and hospital identifiers, ICU-relative timestamps, structured vital-sign observations, timestamped laboratory rows with label/unit-interface fields, and sparse end-of-life documentation. The local source index, relevant schema JSONs, raw headers and representative rows were inspected. They establish availability and data semantics, not an association, transportability, complete AKI label, or clinical utility.

The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable: whether an early physiology representation is safer to transport between eICU hospitals than representations that encode laboratory values and the local testing process, for the explicitly defined observed-creatinine-rise endpoint. “Safer” means lower held-out probability error and calibration failure, plus no worse performance at a prespecified high-sensitivity operating point; it does not mean safer patient care until clinicians review thresholds and prospective harms.

The substantive advance over the prior renal line is to make cross-hospital deployment safety the primary estimand, not merely incremental prediction in the pooled cohort. The substantive repair of seed 08 is to represent only auditable recorded testing gaps and ascertainment states. “Reduced testing,” clinical instability, interface outage, treatment limitation, clinician intent, true deterioration, and biological AKI remain unobserved or incompletely observed; none is treated as a causal exposure or conclusion.

## Population, landmark and temporal boundary

The dataset is eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`, with catalog `[internal dataset path]` (catalog [source checksum]). All listed eICU source files are ordinary read-only gzip CSV files; no nested archive member is assumed. There is no cross-dataset join.

The primary index is one `patientunitstayid`; repeated stays are retained, and `uniquepid` is used only in a person-clustered sensitivity. Include:

- adult stays with numeric `patient.age >= 18`; report the eICU `> 89` age convention and missing-age flow;
- `patient.unitdischargeoffset >= 360`, so the six-hour history can be complete;
- at least three valid primary MAP observations in minutes 0–360 and at least 60 covered minutes under the fixed 30-minute carry-forward rule;
- at least one valid baseline creatinine in minutes 0–360 for the primary ascertainable-outcome cohort.

The full physiology-eligible flow must retain stays lacking baseline creatinine as baseline-unknown, with hospital distribution and observation features reported. They are not coded as normal and are not silently removed from cohort accounting. The minimum event, hospital, overlap and ascertainment gates below determine whether the primary comparison is interpretable.

The landmark is ICU minute 360. Predictors use only source observations with timestamp in the closed interval [0, 360]. Follow-up starts strictly after minute 360 and ends at the earliest of ICU minute 2,880, `patient.unitdischargeoffset`, ICU death/discharge, or the last usable creatinine observation before censoring. No `labresultrevisedoffset`, post-landmark field, first-24-hour Apache aggregate, or future discharge information may enter a predictor.

## Recorded endpoint and ascertainment

Use exact normalized `lab.labname` = `creatinine` for the primary endpoint. Accept numeric `labresult` in a prespecified plausibility range of 0.1–30 mg/dL only after an audit of `labmeasurenamesystem` and `labmeasurenameinterface`. The audit must enumerate every encountered label and system/interface combination. No post hoc synonym or unit conversion is allowed. Use `labresultoffset` as availability time; `labresultrevisedoffset` is not a clinical availability timestamp.

Baseline creatinine is the earliest valid qualifying creatinine in [0, 360], tie-broken by `labid`. The primary event is the first valid post-landmark creatinine satisfying:

`360 < labresultoffset <= 2880` and `labresult >= baseline_creatinine + 0.3 mg/dL`.

Report first-event time, maximum observed rise, number and timing of follow-up tests, and a sensitivity using a relative 50% rise only as a descriptive robustness check. The primary label is explicitly a “first later observed creatinine rise,” not KDIGO AKI.

Every primary-eligible stay receives one mutually exclusive ascertainment state:

1. observed creatinine rise;
2. post-landmark creatinine observed without the threshold rise;
3. ICU death/discharge before a qualifying follow-up test;
4. no valid post-landmark creatinine before the horizon/censoring, hence unknown ascertainment.

Death and discharge are not normoglycemia-like non-events and are reported separately by hospital, timing and discharge status. A no-test stay is not a negative event. If there is a valid below-threshold value, it is only a known observation without the event at that time; it does not guarantee later absence of a rise.

The main evaluation has two views: (a) a state-aware view retaining unknown as a separate state, and (b) an ascertainable-event view using inverse-probability-of-observation/censoring weights estimated only from pre-landmark information. The latter estimates performance for the observed event under the recorded observation process, not unobserved biological AKI.

### Fixed predictor blocks

Use the same transparent regularized discrete-time cause-specific hazard model family, feature budget, hyperparameter search and preprocessing for all nested blocks. Fit label maps, unit decisions, imputation, scaling and tuning within training hospitals only.

- `M0`: context: age, gender, ethnicity, `hospitaladmitsource`, `unittype`, and `unitstaytype`. It excludes hospital ID, laboratory values and post-landmark fields.
- `Mphys`: primary portable candidate: M0 plus fixed pre-landmark summaries of `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean`, `vitalPeriodic.heartrate`, `temperature`, `respiration`, `sao2`, `systemicmean`, and `pamean`:  median, minimum, maximum, last value, interquartile range where defined, and linear slope. Keep systemic and pulmonary-artery means separate. Accept only finite values within prespecified physiologic ranges; invalid values become missing. Primary imputation is training-hospital median without missingness indicators.
- `Mlab`: Mphys plus exact normalized analytes `creatinine`, `bun`, `sodium`, `potassium`, `chloride`, `bicarbonate`, `glucose`, and `lactate`,  using first, last, minimum, maximum and slope in [0, 360], plus analyte-available indicators. The preparation audit must enumerate exact labels and units before model fitting.
- `Maug`: Mlab plus recorded testing-process features from `lab`: total valid rows, distinct `labresultoffset` timestamps, distinct analytes in the fixed inventory, per-analyte counts/availability, time to first lab result, time from last lab result to minute 360, median inter-test interval, maximum inter-test gap, and the fraction of the six-hour interval covered by at least one valid lab result. Deduplicate only by `labid`; never multiply rows through joins. These are descriptors of recorded testing, not clinician intent.

The primary contrast is deployment safety of Mphys versus Maug, with Mlab decomposition. M0 is a clinical-context baseline. For a clinically interpretable safety check, select a high-sensitivity threshold (target 0.90 sensitivity) using training-hospital predictions only. Evaluate held-out sensitivity, false-negative rate, alert rate and calibration within each hospital; if the target cannot be reached without extrapolation or sparse events, report the gate failure rather than changing the threshold.

## Seed 08 process audit

Seed 08 is not promoted to a causal exposure. Its auditable component is the pre-landmark recorded lab-gap phenotype in Maug. The prespecified descriptive audit will stratify stays by early physiology and the maximum/last lab gap, and report subsequent ascertainment states. “Unstable” is operationalized only for this audit as at least one accepted pre-landmark noninvasive MAP <65 mmHg or an accepted heart rate >120, while retaining the continuous physiology summaries in the primary model. This is a recorded-state definition, not expert adjudication. “Abrupt reduction” is the transition from a patient-specific pre-landmark median inter-test interval to a gap at least twice that median, requiring at least two prior test intervals and a minimum 60-minute gap; if the patient-specific median is undefined, classify the phenotype unknown. The audit asks whether this recorded gap predicts the recorded endpoint conditional on Mphys, not whether reduced testing causes deterioration.

No gap is interpreted when it is plausibly explained by discharge before the gap, absent ICU time, documented EOL record by minute 360, or a gap extending beyond the stay; these are reported as competing/unknown process states, not silently excluded. Interface interruption cannot be identified from these files. Therefore the seed analysis is a secondary process/measurement audit and a falsification/heterogeneity check for Maug, not a stand-alone causal study.

## Analysis, uncertainty and transportability

Use five grouped hospital folds assigned by a fixed hash of `hospitalid`; all stays from one hospital stay in one fold. A leave-one-hospital-out sensitivity is required for hospitals with enough events. No random patient split is primary. Keep all preprocessing, label/unit decisions, feature tuning, imputation, calibration and observation-weight fitting inside the training hospitals.

Fit cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards in bins 6–12, 12–24, 24–36 and 36–48 ICU hours. The observed creatinine-rise event is one absorbing cause; death/discharge before qualifying follow-up are separate competing causes; unknown laboratory follow-up is not a negative event. Report paired held-out predictions for M0, Mphys, Mlab and Maug for every fold and hospital.

Report, by fold and hospital: event, no-rise-observed, death/discharge-competing and unknown counts; Brier score; calibration intercept and slope; AUROC and precision-recall area; high-sensitivity threshold sensitivity, false-negative rate and alert rate; and paired Maug−Mphys differences. Use hospital-cluster bootstrap intervals (resample hospitals, not rows), fold-level ranges, and a `uniquepid`-clustered sensitivity.

The primary transport estimand is the hospital-weighted mean difference in held-out Brier score and calibration error between Maug and Mphys, with the distribution of hospital-specific differences as a required result. A supportive transport result requires a directionally consistent Mphys advantage across multiple hospitals, an interval excluding no difference, no single-hospital dominance, and no loss of high-sensitivity performance. If Maug improves AUROC but worsens calibration or false-negative rate in some hospitals, report a safety trade-off rather than calling it a clinical improvement.

Observation weights estimate the probability of a qualifying post-landmark creatinine observation using only pre-landmark covariates: M0, early physiology and density, early lab counts/availability, hospital, unit type, admission source, baseline creatinine availability/value. Report positivity, effective sample size and truncation. An excessive or unstable unknown fraction, nonpositive weights or large sensitivity to truncation is inconclusive.

## Computational falsification and interpretation

The following patterns falsify or materially weaken the primary claim:

- Mphys is not at least as well calibrated or accurate as Maug in held-out hospitals, or its advantage changes sign across hospitals;
- Mphys’ advantage disappears after adding common observation-density/gap features, hospital-held-out calibration, observation weighting, or the leave-one-hospital-out sensitivity;
- Maug’s apparent benefit exists only under random patient splits, complete follow-up, revised timestamps, Apache aggregates, or a single hospital;
- Maug has higher held-out false-negative rate or alert burden at the common training-selected high-sensitivity threshold in low-testing hospitals;
- a pre-landmark creatinine-change negative control, with matched observation structure, shows the same transport effect;
- permutation of lab-process features within hospital and broad physiology/coverage strata leaves the claimed process advantage unchanged, or permutation of physiology summaries leaves the claimed physiology advantage unchanged;
- process-gap association persists when the gap is shifted to an impossible pre-landmark pseudo-outcome or when event times are permuted within stay;
- unit/interface strata, analyte ambiguity or one laboratory field account for the entire result;
- a clinically large apparent difference is present without a corresponding change in computed Brier/calibration, or confidence intervals do not cover the reported point estimate.

Supportive results would show that Mphys maintains lower held-out Brier/calibration error and no worse high-sensitivity false-negative performance across multiple hospitals, while Maug’s local gain attenuates in external hospitals and the gap audit is associated with ascertainment/observed event only as a measurement-process phenomenon. This would support choosing a physiology-first transport design for further review. It would not show that laboratory testing is unnecessary, that reduced testing causes deterioration, or that an alert improves care.

Adverse results would show that Maug transports as well or better, or that Mphys is unstable after density and weighting controls. This rejects the proposed physiology-first transport claim for this recorded endpoint, not the value of laboratory tests or physiology.

Inconclusive results would arise from insufficient events/hospitals, excessive unknown follow-up, nonpositive weights, poor overlap, unresolved unit semantics, or inability to separate discharge/death and interface-related missingness. The next study would need validated laboratory order/collection and administration semantics, raw device-quality/waveform data, complete pre-ICU renal history, urine-output and dialysis adjudication, richer treatment/goals-of-care context, and prospective multi-hospital validation. A clinical policy claim additionally requires clinician adjudication, decision thresholds, harms/costs and prospective or quasi-experimental impact evaluation.

Computationally checkable claims are source integrity, exact joins, cohort flow, label/unit counts, timestamp compliance, feature formulas, ascertainment states, model matching, held-out predictions, calibration, uncertainty, threshold metrics and permutations. Clinical adjudication is required for true AKI, biological deterioration, treatment intent, interface outage, clinician intent, completeness of testing, whether a gap reflects appropriate care, and whether a surveillance policy benefits patients.
