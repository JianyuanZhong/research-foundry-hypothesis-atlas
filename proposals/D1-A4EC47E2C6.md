# Persistent hyperlactatemia conditional on improving versus worsening measured perfusion at live ICU exit

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly produce a leakage-controlled, competing-risk study of whether the prognostic meaning of persistent hyperlactatemia depends on the direction of measured systemic perfusion immediately before an observed live ICU exit. Completion requires:

1. a reproducible all-eligible flow and a separately reported phenotype-complete cohort;
2. an index defined at the first eligible live ICU exit per hospital admission, with a one-hour pre-exit landmark and a primary feature matrix whose rows were both clinically charted and stored by that landmark;
3. a prespecified persistent-lactate × perfusion-trajectory interaction, with 48-hour risk contrasts and uncertainty, rather than an unqualified subgroup claim;
4. matched held-out predictions from a transparent latest-state baseline (B0), an order-blind generic-history comparator (B1), a process-only comparator (Bmask), and an auditable phenotype model (Bdisc), plus a training-only learned trajectory sensitivity (M2);
5. exact source/member/item/unit/timing provenance, coverage and ascertainment flow, subject-level splitting, and sensitivity analyses for charting process, perfusion components, endpoint ascertainment, and exit selection;
6. falsification outputs (outcome permutation, early/late permutation, process-only comparison, and near-exit negative control);
7. a conclusion ledger tying every claim to computed outputs and explicitly separating prognostic association from microcirculatory diagnosis, fluid response, discharge appropriateness, causal treatment, utility, and transportability.

No clinical result is claimed by this proposal. Readiness or a future hypothesis-confirming result is not completion.

## Unresolved question, evidence boundary and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence supports only that the configured MIMIC-IV 3.1 snapshot contains repeated lactate, MAP, vasoactive-interval, urine-output and organ-function records that can define candidate trajectories. The expert seed is explicitly untested. The MIMIC documentation establishes the database provenance and its internal within-person timing convention; it does not establish that these proxies measure microcirculatory perfusion or that any phenotype is prevalent enough for inference. The mature parent establishes a useful design context—observed live ICU-exit prognosis with process and selection audits—but does not resolve lactate/perfusion heterogeneity.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among first eligible live ICU exits with repeated lactate and paired hemodynamic measurements, is the excess 48-hour risk associated with persistent hyperlactatemia smaller when measured perfusion is improving than when it is worsening?

The primary estimand is the interaction at 48 hours:
`[CIF(R/D | persistent lactate, improving) - CIF(R/D | nonpersistent, improving)] - [CIF(R/D | persistent lactate, worsening) - CIF(R/D | nonpersistent, worsening)]`.
The directional hypothesis is negative: persistent lactate should carry less excess risk in the improving-perfusion stratum. The prespecified primary clinically meaningful null region is an interaction absolute risk difference between -2 and +2 percentage points; the exact-zero estimate and interval are also reported. A directionally negative estimate that remains inside this region is not primary support.

This matters because the same elevated lactate may reflect unresolved systemic illness, impaired clearance, adrenergic metabolism, or measurement/selection processes. If the interaction is robust, clinicians and future prospective studies would have evidence that a single lactate threshold is an inadequate prognostic description. If it is absent, the result would argue against adding this physiologic distinction to retrospective risk stratification. Neither result determines what treatment should be given.

## Population, landmark, temporal boundaries and outcomes

Use one index per `hadm_id`: the first `icu/icustays` row with valid `intime < outtime`, a live exit at `t0 = outtime`, and enough time for the fixed landmark window. Define the primary landmark `tL = t0 - 1 hour`; require `intime <= tL - 12 hours` so every primary 12-hour window is fully in the ICU. Exclude reversed/missing ICU intervals and index exits with `deathtime <= t0`. A row with `hospital_expire_flag=1` and missing `deathtime` is retained in the flow as an unresolved-live/death-ascertainment category and excluded from the timed primary estimand unless an adverse-bound analysis is explicitly reported. Admissions with no matching admission row are counted as linkage failures, not silently dropped.

The primary feature window is `[tL-12h, tL]`, divided into early `[tL-12h,tL-6h]` and late `(tL-6h,tL]` halves. For the primary available-by-landmark matrix, a charted lab/vital/output row requires both its clinical time (`charttime`) and recording time (`storetime`) at or before `tL`. An input interval requires `starttime <= tL`, `storetime <= tL`, and a valid interval; its contribution is clipped at `tL`, never using an `endtime` beyond the landmark. No feature uses `outtime`, `los`, `last_careunit`, a future transfer, an endpoint field, a note, or any row after `tL`. A chart-time-only sensitivity admits rows on clinical time alone and reports which rows and patients change.

The phenotype-complete primary cohort additionally requires:

- at least one valid lactate in each half, with at least two valid lactate results overall;
- valid paired MAP summaries in each half; and
- valid paired vasoactive-support summaries in each half for the primary concordant trajectory rule.

All eligible live exits and every successive coverage restriction are reported. Missingness is not normal physiology and is not imputed to zero. A phenotype cell with fewer than 30 subjects or fewer than 10 R/D events is reported but cannot support the primary interaction; a 48-hour interaction interval wider than 6 percentage points is inconclusive.

Define persistent hyperlactatemia as both early and late lactate medians at least 2.0 mmol/L after verifying the recorded unit. A 4.0 mmol/L threshold is a fixed sensitivity. Values in incompatible units, nonnumeric values, or unsupported conversions are rejected and their counts reported; no held-out outcome may determine a cutoff.

Define primary perfusion trajectory using only directionally interpretable, predeclared domains:

- MAP is the median of valid `icu/chartevents` values for items 220052, 220181 and 225312 in each half. The primary change is late minus early MAP.
- Vasoactive support is the number of minutes with valid overlap of prespecified `icu/inputevents` intervals for items 221906, 222315, 221289, 229617, 221749, 229630, 229631, 229632, 221662, 221653 and 221986 in each half. It is a support-presence/duration measure, not a dose or fluid-response measure; rate units are not pooled in the primary definition.
- Improving perfusion requires MAP increase or nondecrease of at least 5 mmHg and late vasoactive minutes no greater than early minutes.
- Worsening perfusion requires MAP decrease of at least 5 mmHg and late vasoactive minutes no less than early minutes.
- All other cases are indeterminate and are retained in a three-level descriptive analysis, not forced into either primary category.

A secondary perfusion analysis adds normalized valid Foley output from `icu/outputevents` item 226559 as a third corroborative domain, with output presence/absence and log-volume sensitivities. It is not allowed to rescue a missing primary MAP/pressor classification. Additional prespecified sensitivities are MAP-only, arterial-MAP-only (225312), pressor-only, continuous standardized changes, and leave-one-domain-out analyses. Liver markers (ALT 50861, AST 50878, bilirubin 50885), creatinine 50912, bicarbonate 50882, hemoglobin 51222, platelets 51265 and WBC 51300 are covariates/sensitivity descriptors, with value and testing indicators separated.

Define mutually exclusive first transitions in `(t0,t0+48 hours]):

- R: a different `stay_id` in the same `hadm_id` with `intime > t0` and `intime <= t0+48h`;
- D: `admissions.deathtime` in the interval;
- S: an alive `admissions.dischtime` in the interval before a later R or D, an observation-boundary transition rather than a safe outcome;
- N: no R, D or S while the admission remains ascertainable through 48 hours;
- U: unresolved timed-death ascertainment, including `hospital_expire_flag=1` with missing `deathtime`, never silently relabeled N.

At equal timestamps, D precedes R; S is assigned only when alive and before the other event. Report R, D, S, R/D and N with cause-specific hazards and 48-hour cumulative incidence. A 7-day and complete-ascertainment sensitivity, plus adverse/non-adverse U bounds, are required. The primary interaction is estimated for R/D with S competing; component R and D interactions are secondary.

## Models, estimand comparison and uncertainty

Freeze a deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split before outcome-based thresholding, stratified only by endpoint and anchor-year group. No subject crosses partitions. For the primary phenotype comparison, fit and evaluate every model on the same phenotype-complete population and same split; an all-live-exit coverage extension is secondary. Fit clipping, unit rules, scaling, imputation, calibration, q, thresholds and learned assignments using training/validation only.

B0 is the transparent baseline: penalized discrete-time cause-specific complementary-log-log hazards for R, D and S using admission/demographic/current-ICU context, terminal-bin physiology, terminal missingness and observation-process variables. It represents the latest-state information a simple model can use.

B1 is the fair generic-history comparator: B0 plus order-blind summaries in twelve one-hour bins across the same physiology, laboratory, output, pressor and observation-process fields. It tests whether a phenotype increment is more than generic late instability and measurement intensity.

Bmask contains the same context, masks, counts, test indicators, Foley-charting and pressor-presence history with physiologic values removed. If Bdisc does not exceed Bmask, physiology-specific interpretation fails.

Bdisc is the selected primary substantive alternative. It adds persistent-lactate status, the predeclared improving/indeterminate/worsening perfusion category, early and late lactate values, MAP/pressor changes, their interaction, organ-marker values and separate availability indicators to B1. The primary scientific output is the interaction and its standardized CIF contrast, not a black-box score. Bdisc can expose a clinically interpretable cross-domain interaction that B0's terminal state and B1's order-blind summaries can lose.

M2 is a learned sensitivity on the same phenotype-complete training cases. Fit a two-component diagonal-covariance Gaussian mixture to training-only standardized early-to-late change vectors (lactate, MAP, pressor minutes and, secondarily, output/organ markers). Use posterior component probability in the same cause-specific model. Give a component an improving/worsening label only if its training means satisfy the fixed direction rules; otherwise retain it unlabeled and do not use it to confirm the clinical hypothesis. M2 can reveal nonlinear coupled trajectories and heterogeneity lost by the hand rule, but it is deferred as primary because the clinical question requires an auditable phenotype and sparse/selective measurement may make mixture labels unstable. A chronological GRU is not needed for this branch: the parent already tests chronology against order-blind late history, while this study's new uncertainty is cross-domain lactate/perfusion effect modification. A causal fluid-response model is deferred because `inputevents` lacks fluid responsiveness, indication/intent, counterfactual dose response, treatment limitations and adequate time-varying confounding control.

Report held-out 48-hour CIF and transition-aware Brier scores for R, D, S, R/D and N; calibration intercept/slope; AUROC/AUPRC for component outcomes; paired Bdisc-B1 and B1-B0 differences; interaction estimates; and subject-bootstrap 95% intervals. Use fixed 5/10/20% test-set review workloads only as descriptive risk-capture tables. These are operating characteristics, not clinical utility, staffing recommendations or evidence that review changes care.

## Selection, falsification and interpretation rules

Repeat the mature live-exit selection audit as a sensitivity: fit an outcome-free pooled ICU-stay-hour one-hour live-exit score q using only information available by each hour, report overlap/positivity/ESS and q-stratum metrics, and never describe q or any odds tilting as recovery of a discharge-policy counterfactual. Non-exit hours do not have observed post-exit outcomes. Low overlap/ESS or calibration collapse makes the selection sensitivity inconclusive, not a reason to change the primary estimand.

Required falsifications and audits are:

1. Verify item labels, `linksto`, units, numeric ranges, clinical/recording timestamps, valid interval clipping, joins, duplicate handling, first-exit selection, and prohibited-field exclusion with row-level provenance.
2. Compare Bdisc with Bmask and a phenotype-only process model. A similar gain without physiologic values invalidates a physiology-specific interpretation.
3. Permute early versus late assignment within subject while preserving counts and availability. The interaction and learned interpretation should attenuate; persistence indicates construction or leakage failure.
4. Permute outcomes within partitions and refit. Persistent held-out improvement indicates leakage or evaluation failure.
5. Compare available-by-landmark with chart-time-only matrices. A gain that appears only after admitting late-stored rows is adverse to real-time availability.
6. Repeat with lactate 4.0, arterial MAP only, MAP-only, pressor-only, output corroboration, continuous changes, 4/6/8 valid-bin thresholds, 7-day endpoints and U bounds. Do not choose among these after seeing held-out outcomes.
7. Report R, D and S separately. A gain confined to S or administrative continuation does not support adverse perfusion biology.
8. Fit the parent’s outcome-free six-hour live-exit negative-control model. A much stronger phenotype signal for exit-process prediction than R/D supports selection/process explanation.
9. Report q strata, care-unit strata derived only from transfer intervals covering `tL`, anchor-year-group strata, phenotype prevalence, indeterminate fraction, measurement intensity, and held-out calibration.

Supportive evidence requires adequate coverage and events; a negative interaction below the -2-point null region with a 95% interval entirely below that region; a corresponding lower persistent-lactate R/D CIF in improving than worsening perfusion; Bdisc's calibrated held-out R/D improvement over B1; Bdisc exceeding Bmask; and stability across timing, unit, coverage, q-overlap and endpoint sensitivities. M2 may corroborate but cannot validate a proxy phenotype alone.

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversed interaction, Bdisc matching B1 or Bmask, process-only or chart-only explanation, instability under component/coverage/selection sensitivities, gains confined to S/N, or outcome-permutation resistance. The primary result is inconclusive when phenotype cells/events are sparse, indeterminate/U fractions are high, units are incompatible, overlap/ESS is poor, calibration is unstable, or the interval remains wide.

Computationally checkable claims are provenance, cohort flow, timing, units, item support, joins, phenotype prevalence/labels, split integrity, model fitting, predictions, CIF/Brier/calibration, interaction contrasts, uncertainty, selection audit and falsifications. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for true microcirculatory dysfunction, lactate etiology, clinician intent, fluid responsiveness, appropriateness or preventability of ICU discharge, causal fluid-treatment or discharge-policy effects, monitoring benefit, net utility, ward surveillance, external transportability and bedside recommendations. This study cannot recommend a fluid strategy or discharge policy.
