# Scientific Proposal: eGFR Discordance Predicts Acute Kidney Injury

## Hypothesis

Among UK Biobank participants with normal creatinine-based eGFR at baseline, greater discordance between creatinine-based and cystatin-C-based eGFR estimates, combined with lower grip strength (proxy for muscle mass), predicts increased incidence of severe acute kidney injury (AKI) during follow-up.

**Clinical Question**: Does routine creatinine-based eGFR testing miss high-risk individuals who would be identified by cystatin-C-based eGFR and muscle mass assessment?

## Scientific Significance

Routine creatinine-based eGFR is the standard for kidney function assessment in clinical practice. However, creatinine production depends on muscle mass, meaning individuals with low muscle mass may have normal creatinine-based eGFR despite reduced kidney function. Cystatin-C, a protein produced at a constant rate independent of muscle mass, may better detect early kidney dysfunction in these individuals.

This hypothesis addresses a critical gap: identifying high-risk individuals who are at elevated risk of AKI but may be missed by routine creatinine-based testing alone. Early identification could enable preventive interventions and closer monitoring.

## Study Population

- **Dataset**: UK Biobank (ukb672073 snapshot)
- **Inclusion criteria**:
  - Age ≥40 years at baseline (population table)
  - Complete baseline measurements for:
    - Creatinine-based eGFR (main table)
    - Cystatin-C based eGFR (biological_samples table)
    - Grip strength (additional_exposures table)
  - No prior diagnosis of CKD or AKI at baseline (population table)
- **Exclusion criteria**:
  - Missing any required baseline measurements
  - Baseline creatinine eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m² (severe CKD at baseline)

## Methods

### Baseline Method: Cox Proportional Hazards
- Model: `hazard(t) = h0(t) * exp(β1*discordance + β2*grip_strength + β3*age + β4*sex + ...)`
- Output: Hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals
- Assumption: Proportional hazards (check with Schoenfeld residuals)

### Alternative Method: Gradient Boosting (XGBoost/LightGBM)
- Model: Tree-based ensemble for survival analysis (e.g., XGBoost survival learner)
- Outputs:
  - Prediction probabilities
  - Feature importance (gain, cover)
  - Partial dependence plots
  - SHAP values for interpretability
- Advantages over Cox:
  - No proportional hazards assumption
  - Captures nonlinear interactions (e.g., discordance × grip strength)
  - Handles missing data
  - Provides feature importance rankings

### What the Alternative Reveals
The gradient boosting approach can reveal:
1. **Nonlinear effects**: Whether the risk increases exponentially with discordance rather than linearly
2. **Interaction effects**: Whether grip strength modifies the effect of eGFR discordance
3. **Feature importance**: Which predictor contributes most to risk prediction
4. **Calibration**: How well the model predicts observed risks

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **rejected** if:
1. No significant association between eGFR discordance and AKI (HR ≈ 1, p > 0.05)
2. The gradient boosting model performs no better than Cox (C-index difference < 0.02)
3. Grip strength shows no independent predictive value after adjusting for eGFR discordance

The hypothesis would be **supported** if:
1. Significant positive association: higher discordance → higher AKI risk
2. Gradient boosting outperforms Cox on discrimination metrics
3. Grip strength adds independent predictive value

## Clinical Importance

**If supported**:
- Routine testing should include cystatin-C and grip strength for high-risk populations
- Easier to identify individuals at risk of AKI who are missed by creatinine alone
- Could enable targeted preventive interventions (hydration, medication review, monitoring)
- May identify populations for clinical trials of AKI prevention

**If not supported**:
- Current creatinine-based testing may be sufficient
- Resources should not be diverted to additional testing without proven benefit

## Limitations

1. **Field code uncertainty**: Specific field codes for cystatin-C, grip strength, and AKI need verification through data inspection
2. **Reverse causation**: Baseline grip strength may be affected by undiagnosed illness
3. **Measurement error**: Single baseline measurement may not represent long-term muscle mass
4. **Selection bias**: UK Biobank participants may not be representative of general population
5. **Causality**: Association does not prove mechanism (inflammation, malnutrition, etc.)
