# UKB 04: Does systemic reserve add prognostic information beyond lung function after infection-related hospitalization?

## Unresolved question and clinical importance
Among UK Biobank adults with a pre-illness lung function measurement and a later infection-related hospitalization, does preserved systemic reserve identify lower short-term mortality beyond what is explained by pre-illness lung function and measured illness severity?

The clinical value is that lung function alone may not capture the physiologic reserve that determines recovery from infection. If reserve adds information after adjustment for illness severity, post-discharge monitoring, rehabilitation and escalation decisions could be better targeted. If it does not, the result would argue against using reserve as an independent post-infection prognostic marker in this population.

## Evidence opening and closest inspected work
The closest inspected work supports that frailty and muscle strength are associated with adverse outcomes, while infection studies show that baseline physiologic state matters for recovery. These works do not directly test whether systemic reserve adds information beyond pre-illness lung function and measured illness severity. The remaining question is whether reserve is an independent prognostic signal or merely a proxy for frailty, illness severity, or treatment.

## Population and temporal design
Use UKB 04 participants with a pre-illness lung function measurement, an infection-related hospitalization, and available systemic reserve measurements. Lung function and reserve must precede the index hospitalization. Exclude measurements taken during or after the index event. Define the index date from the first qualifying infection-related inpatient diagnosis. Follow for mortality within 90 days after discharge.

## Estimand
The primary estimand is the incremental association of pre-illness systemic reserve with 90-day mortality after infection-related hospitalization, adjusted for pre-illness lung function, age, sex, and illness severity proxies available before the index event. The estimand is prognostic, not causal: it estimates whether reserve carries information beyond the measured baseline state and severity.

## Competing explanation
The strongest rival is that reserve is a proxy for overall frailty or illness severity. If the association disappears after adjustment for severity and frailty indicators, reserve is not an independent prognostic marker. A second rival is measurement timing: reserve measured close to the index event may reflect acute illness rather than pre-illness reserve.

## Method comparison
A simple severity-adjusted baseline uses pre-illness lung function, age, sex, and illness severity proxies. A learned reserve-only model uses pre-illness grip strength and biochemical reserve measures. The comparison tests whether reserve adds information beyond the baseline. The learned model is not a substitute for the baseline; it is a scientifically substantive alternative that can reveal nonlinear or interaction effects that a linear adjustment may miss.

## Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: reserve adds significant incremental information after adjustment for lung function and severity, with stable direction and magnitude in prespecified sensitivity analyses.
Adverse: reserve adds no incremental information, or the association reverses in sensitivity analyses.
Inconclusive: weak or unstable results, or insufficient event support in the reserve-measured subset.
