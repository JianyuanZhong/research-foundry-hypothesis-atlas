## Clinical question, supported evidence, and unresolved hypothesis

### Clinical question and importance

Among adults with recorded hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who reach the same strictly defined second qualifying TACE (TACE2), is a systemic HCC-medication record during TACE2 calendar days 1–14 associated with a different subsequent albumin and total-bilirubin trajectory than an ambiguity-excluded TACE2-only record, and can any difference be interpreted consistently once the clinical context in which follow-up assays were obtained and intervening recorded care are made explicit?

This matters because albumin decline or bilirubin rise may influence whether clinicians repeat locoregional therapy, add or continue systemic treatment, or reconsider hepatic reserve. A measurement obtained during a later admission or around repeat TACE may reflect a procedure episode, acute illness, surveillance intensity, or management response rather than a stable outpatient state. Likewise, a days 1–14 pathway label does not remain an isolated exposure when patients subsequently cross over, continue systemic therapy, or undergo another TACE. Without this repair, even a statistically favorable contrast could be mislabeled as preservation attributable to the initial pathway.

### Strongest claim already supported by complete-source evidence

Complete-source scans support feasibility and establish severe outcome-context anchoring; they do not establish benefit, harm, causation, treatment delivery, or intent.

The frozen reconstruction exactly reproduces 2,992 patients before medication classification, 319 systemic-record patients, 1,491 TACE2-only patients, and joint exact-assay pairs for 99/319 and 387/1,491. In the full frozen arms, recorded events on days 15–90 were common and differential:

- systemic-record arm: later nonplacebo non-bevacizumab systemic record 147/319 (46.08%), later strict TACE 159/319 (49.84%), either event 190/319 (59.56%);
- TACE2-only arm: later systemic record 105/1,491 (7.04%), later strict TACE 526/1,491 (35.28%), either event 552/1,491 (37.02%).

Among the frozen joint-pair patients, albumin and bilirubin follow-up selections came from the same encounter for 99/99 systemic and 387/387 comparator patients and from the same timestamp for 99/99 and 386/387. Every selected joint-pair follow-up timestamp fell within its linked encounter’s recorded admission-to-discharge interval under the prespecified one-day discharge-granularity margin. A strict TACE episode was within plus or minus 7 calendar days of at least one selected follow-up assay for 77/99 (77.78%) systemic and 262/387 (67.70%) comparator patients. A later systemic record or strict TACE had occurred by the selected follow-up day for 68/99 (68.69%) and 167/387 (43.15%), respectively.

Assay-specific results reinforce this finding: each exact assay had a deterministic follow-up for 169 systemic and 683 comparator patients; all were encounter-matched and admission-linked. A strict TACE was within plus or minus 7 days for 133/169 (78.70%) systemic and 468/683 (68.52%) comparator follow-ups, and an intervening systemic record or TACE had occurred by selection for 115/169 (68.05%) and 336/683 (49.19%). Median selected day was 59 in both arms, so target-day similarity does not remove the care-context problem.

These facts support only the claim that the recorded outcome is predominantly admission- and procedure-contextual and often postdates further care. `within_admission` is a timestamp proxy, not adjudicated acute-care status; proximity does not establish that TACE caused the laboratory value; medication records do not prove administration.

### Unresolved, falsifiable hypothesis

Conditional on the day-14 institutional-observation population, measured pre-TACE2 factors, adequate overlap, and observed exact assays, the systemic-record pathway will be associated with less albumin decline or greater increase from baseline to target-nearest day 60, without a larger total-bilirubin increase, than the TACE2-only recorded pathway. A clinically interpretable supportive result additionally requires compatible direction in prespecified selected-pathway context sensitivities and no evidence that the contrast is generated by differential assay context, observation, pre-exposure trajectory, or calendar nonpositivity.

The primary hypothesis concerns a fixed-baseline recorded-pathway association through subsequent observed care. It is not a hypothesis of medication administration, adherence, isolated early-treatment effect, sustained-strategy effect, toxicity causation, imaging response, progression, survival, comparative effectiveness, or clinical benefit.

## Precision, context, and interpretation gates

1. **Reconstruction gate:** reproduce source-row flow, 319/1,491 arms, 99/387 joint pairs, the 13 frozen classification changes, and the day-0 boundary localization. Any unresolved mismatch invalidates execution.
2. **Frozen-boundary gate:** never change TACE2, era, day-14 landmark, days 1–14 classification, assay labels, or baseline/follow-up windows to improve counts or results. The primary is precision-limited because only 99 systemic joint pairs exist.
3. **Overlap/balance gate:** both arms must retain propensity support; no year/arm may dominate weight; all core post-weighting absolute SMDs must be at most 0.10. Otherwise report descriptive/inconclusive findings.
4. **Precision gate:** report robust intervals and arm ESS. Systemic ESS below 80, total ESS below 300, or an interval unable to exclude clinically plausible benefit and harm on the observed scale makes the assay inconclusive. Clinical adequacy cannot be automated because units and validated margins are absent.
5. **Observation gate:** extreme observation probabilities, poor calibration, major combined-weight ESS collapse, or material directional conflict between complete-case and observation-weighted estimates makes the biochemical result inconclusive.
6. **Outcome-context anchoring gate:** always label the primary result admission/procedure-contextual because the audit found 100% admission linkage. If more than 50% of either arm’s paired outcomes lie within plus or minus 7 days of strict TACE, or the arm difference in pre-assay later-event prevalence exceeds 10 percentage points, no result may be described as stable hepatic-reserve preservation. Both conditions are already met by the value-blinded feasibility audit; execution can only quantify their consequence, not remove the label.
7. **Selected-pathway support gate:** a context restriction with fewer than 30 systemic patients, systemic ESS below 20, or fewer than 20 patients in either arm is descriptive only. Directional conflict between the primary and adequately supported no-event or procedure-distant sensitivities makes the overall clinical interpretation inconclusive, not evidence for either isolated strategy.
8. **Negative-control gate:** a material adjusted pre-exposure contrast capable of explaining the post-index result falsifies newly emergent-pathway interpretation. Null does not establish causality.
9. **Timing/ontology gate:** direction reversal across locked selectors, dependence on the excluded day-0 boundary, or unexplained reversal under strict/broad TACE or bevacizumab-partner rules establishes instability.
10. **Outcome-coherence gate:** favorable albumin with clearly worse bilirubin, or favorable bilirubin with clearly worse albumin, is mixed/adverse, not supportive.

## Falsifiable result branches

- **Supportive, but context-bounded:** reconstruction, overlap, balance, observation, precision, negative-control, timing, and ontology gates pass; the primary shows less albumin decline or greater increase without larger bilirubin increase; and adequately supported no-event and procedure-distant sensitivities are directionally compatible. This supports only a favorable association between the initial recorded pathway and biochemical state observed through the evolving institutional care pathway. Because the anchoring gate is already active, it cannot support isolated liver preservation, treatment benefit, or causal superiority.
- **Adverse:** the gates otherwise pass, but the systemic-record arm has greater albumin decline and/or bilirubin increase with compatible context sensitivities. This argues against assuming a favorable biochemical association; it does not establish medication-induced hepatotoxicity.
- **Precise null:** clinically reviewed intervals are narrow around no detectable contrast, both assays are coherent, and all computational gates pass. Report no detectable recorded-pathway difference, not equivalence, equal safety, or absence of individual harm.
- **Context-falsified:** an apparent primary contrast disappears or reverses in adequately supported no-event/procedure-distant subsets, is concentrated around later TACE, or tracks differential post-baseline pathway evolution strongly enough that a stable hepatic-state interpretation fails. Report context dependence. This does not identify which post-baseline event caused the difference because conditioning is noncausal.
- **Methodologically falsified:** the contrast is reproduced before exposure, depends on the day-0 boundary, reverses under locked ontologies/timing rules, lacks overlap, or relies on unresolved reconstruction. State the failed premise rather than selecting a favorable alternative.
- **Inconclusive:** low ESS, wide intervals, unstable observation weights, calendar nonpositivity, discordant assays, universal admission anchoring, sparse procedure-distant/no-event support, or unavailable clinical adjudication prevents stable interpretation. A favorable primary estimate remains inconclusive when context evidence cannot distinguish stable physiology from procedure-associated observation.

## Evidence limits and stronger-data requirements

Medication rows are records or orders, not verified administrations; no adherence, delivered dose, combination delivery, indication, line of therapy, duration, or intent can be inferred. Later systemic records may be continuation or crossover, and later TACE may reflect planned care, response, progression, toxicity, or clinician preference. Encounter admission intervals do not validate acute illness. Procedure proximity does not establish causation. Diagnosis times are encounter proxies. The source lacks laboratory units, validated biochemical clinical margins, complete outside-care capture, reliable performance status, complete Child-Pugh components, adjudicated TACE refractoriness, treatment rationale, imaging response/progression, toxicity attribution, and linked vital status. Institutional observation is not survival.

Therefore do not calculate ALBI, use external cut points, pool differently named assays, infer stable outpatient hepatic reserve, attribute toxicity, claim medication effect, or infer comparative effectiveness, progression, survival, or clinical benefit. A causal sustained-strategy comparison would require longitudinal treatment administration, time-varying severity and treatment-decision covariates, explicit dynamic strategies, informative-censoring assumptions and methods, and preferably external/prospective validation. Imaging response requires temporally adjudicated imaging reports or images. Survival requires complete vital-status linkage.

An automatic verifier can check source counts, joins, timestamps, fixed calendar boundaries, ontology matches, arm labels, laboratory selection, pair counts, context flags, weights, balance, ESS, intervals, sensitivity attrition, and whether the reported branch follows computed outputs. It cannot establish why a test or later procedure occurred, whether medication was delivered, whether an admission represented acute deterioration, clinical importance on unknown assay scales, causal mediation, imaging response, progression, survival, or treatment superiority. Those require clinical adjudication or additional data.
