# Documented treatment limitation and utility of post-ICU surveillance alerts

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly fit and evaluate a leakage-safe, first-ICU-discharge study that preserves the parent's observation-process and transfer-topology repairs while addressing one remaining substantive weakness: the parent estimates prognostic contrasts but does not test whether those contrasts support a clinically interpretable allocation decision or whether treatment limitation explains apparent “failure after discharge.”

Completion requires these outputs, all generated rather than asserted:

1. `cohort_audit.parquet`: one row per eligible adult first ICU stay, with inclusion/exclusion flags, first-stay rule, complete 12-hour window, support-observation coverage, code-status/palliative availability and recency, temporal-integrity checks, and primary/broad populations.
2. `hourly_features.parquet`: 12 one-hour rows in `[outtime-12h,outtime)`, with chart-time physiology, positive support evidence, observed non-positive values, no-row hours, contradictions, source masks, gaps, transfer/process summaries and no post-`outtime` predictors.
3. `support_observation_audit.parquet`: the parent's respiratory/vasoactive domain-by-half ledger and every locked missing-support `q` scenario.
4. `limitation_observation_audit.parquet`: raw pre-landmark code-status/palliative rows and timestamps, the locked mapping to documented full-code, documented limitation, palliative-plan, unknown/stale/discordant states, and the exact abstention flags. Do not infer intent from discharge notes or disposition.
5. `transfer_outcome_audit.parquet`: the parent's complete earliest-later-ICU audit, bridge rows, ambiguity flags and topology-rule sensitivities.
6. Patient-level held-out probabilities and recommendations from both the transparent cause-specific baseline and the same-input observation-aware two-domain HSMM.
7. `policy_utility.parquet`: 6-, 24- and 48-hour risk, alert decisions at fixed thresholds (0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20) and fixed alert budgets (5%, 10%, 20%), captured events, false-alert counts and decision-curve net benefit.
8. Bootstrap uncertainty, calibration, IPCW Brier, AUPRC, overlap/effective-sample-size diagnostics, subgroup and missing-support sensitivity outputs, and `interpretation_map.parquet` linking every conclusion to an output row, estimate, interval, event count and evidence limitation.

The actual scientific deliverable is not a leaderboard. It is a fitted, calibrated risk-to-alert policy comparison plus a falsifiable assessment of whether documented treatment limitation and observation quality change its interpretation. No clinical association or utility result is claimed before execution.

## Unresolved question and evidence boundary

The parent supports a narrower methodological claim: recorded respiratory/vasoactive withdrawal, observation process and transfer topology can be represented with an auditable competing-event design using MIMIC-IV 3.1. The parent does not establish that such a signal is actionable, nor that ICU discharge or post-ICU surveillance changes outcomes. “Later ICU stay” still includes patients for whom escalation was limited, status was unknown, or the transition was only partially documented.

The unresolved question is:

> At the first ICU outtime, among adults alive and leaving the ICU, does a calibrated model of the preceding 12-hour recovery/observation trajectory identify a subgroup with sufficiently high risk of a documented non-ICU-to-ICU return or in-hospital death within 48 hours to justify an auditable recommendation for enhanced ward surveillance at a fixed alert budget, and is that signal stable after separating documented treatment limitation, unknown limitation status and inadequate observation?

Primary hypothesis (directional and falsifiable): in the documented-full-code stratum with adequate support observation, the full trajectory model will yield better-calibrated 48-hour risk and greater net benefit than “alert nobody” and a prevalence-calibrated baseline at one or more prespecified alert budgets, while the same apparent benefit will be attenuated, reversed or rendered uninterpretable in documented-limitation and unknown-status strata. A secondary hypothesis is that recent withdrawal and discordant domain recovery carry incremental signal over durable recorded-off status, but only if the signal survives observation-process, transfer-topology and timing falsifications.

This is a retrospective prediction/decision-analytic estimand. It does not test whether withholding or continuing support causes ICU return, whether delaying discharge is beneficial, whether surveillance prevents death, or whether a patient is “ready” for discharge. MIMIC has no randomized enhanced-surveillance intervention, no reliable counterfactual ICU-retention record and no validated goals-of-care adjudication.

## Population, time origin and exact estimand

Use the MIMIC-IV 3.1 snapshot and the same first-ICU-stay construction as the parent:

- adults with `patients.anchor_age >= 18` (preserve the source's age representation);
- one earliest ICU stay per `subject_id,hadm_id`, ordered by `icustays.intime`, with a deterministic `stay_id` tie break;
- index time `L = icustays.outtime`; exclude missing/invalid `intime/outtime`, ICU death, and stays without a complete `[L-12h,L)` window;
- retain broad and primary populations in counts. Primary support eligibility requires at least two respiratory observation bins per half and at least two MAP bins per half, exactly as in the parent; no-row hours are uninformative, not negative evidence;
- follow-up is `[L, min(L+48h, admissions.dischtime))`. A first event is transition-confirmed ICU return, immediate/unconfirmed ICU return, in-hospital death before return, or alive hospital discharge. Preserve the parent's one-minute tie precedence and repeat at 0- and 5-minute tolerances.

The primary endpoint is the parent's transfer-confirmed return: the earliest later ICU stay in the same `subject_id,hadm_id` with `later.intime > L) and before the 48-hour/hospital-discharge boundary, with a valid documented non-ICU bridge. The frozen bridge rule is unchanged: inspect valid `hosp/transfers` rows with non-null `careunit`, `intime < outtime), interval start no earlier than `L-5 minutes` and end no later than `later.intime+5 minutes`; require at least 30 minutes of clipped interval and careunit different from the later ICU's `first_careunit` and `last_careunit`; choose greatest clipped duration, then earliest transfer `outtime), then smallest `transfer_id`. This is a record-topology label, not proof of unplanned intent or clinical deterioration. Immediate/unconfirmed return remains explicit rather than being treated as benign. Death uses `admissions.deathtime`; alive discharge uses `admissions.dischtime`.

For patient i and policy g, report:
- event-specific 6-, 24- and 48-hour cumulative incidence functions (CIFs) under the four competing events;
- calibration and discrimination of each event probability;
- at threshold t, `NB(t) = TP(t)/N - FP(t)/N * t/(1-t)`, with `t` treated as a value-of-information/risk-threshold sensitivity, not a clinical fact;
- at each alert budget b, the top-b policy's event capture per 100 alerts, false-alert rate, alerts per confirmed return/death, and abstention rate;
- incremental net benefit versus alert-none, alert-all, the prevalence-only intercept model and the transparent baseline.

All policy quantities are standardized to the common eligible test population and then repeated by documented full-code, documented limitation/palliative, unknown/stale, coverage-adequate and coverage-inadequate strata. Patient bootstrap, not row bootstrap, supplies 95% intervals. If uncertainty intervals span zero or the denominator/event count is too small for stable policy estimates, label the comparison inconclusive.

## Treatment-limitation ascertainment and confounding repair

Use only pre-landmark ICU chart rows. `icu/d_items` verifies `itemid=223758` “Code Status,” `228687` “Code Status.” and `229784` “Code Status (Intubation),” plus palliative-care plan items `229150, 229152, 229154, 229155`. Extract matching `icu/chartevents` rows with `charttime < L), retaining `value`, `valuenum`, `valueuom`, `warning`, `charttime` and `storetime`. Create a raw-value audit before any mapping.

The solver must not invent semantic mappings from text values. In validation, predeclare a value dictionary for explicit full-code, DNR/DNI/limited-code and palliative/comfort terms; preserve unmapped values as unknown and report their counts. A patient is “documented limitation” if a mapped limitation or palliative-plan row occurs in `[L-48h,L)); “documented full-code” if a mapped full-code row occurs in that interval and no later contradictory limitation is present; “unknown/stale” if no such current row exists; “discordant” if current mapped rows conflict. A row is usable for prediction only if `charttime < L`; `storetime` is metadata and never substitutes for clinical time. Palliative-plan presence is a documentation marker, not a validated goals-of-care label.

The primary policy abstains when status is unknown/stale/discordant, support coverage is inadequate, or model posterior entropy is above a threshold fixed on validation. Also report the no-abstention and “unknown as separate eligible” analyses. Never call unknown full code, never use `admissions.discharge_location` as a pre-landmark predictor, and never use discharge-note text to recover intent. A documented-status subgroup result is conditional association; it does not identify the latent escalation-eligible population. If code-status coverage is sparse, report a quantitative tipping-point table over unknown-status fractions and do not collapse the unknown stratum.

## Matched methods, falsification and uncertainty

Both methods receive identical patient-level splits, feature ledger, code-status raw audit, q scenarios and outcome labels. Thresholds, imputation, mappings, q handling and abstention entropy are chosen on training/validation only.

Transparent baseline: pooled one-hour cause-specific hazards for the four events, plus the parent aggregate-return/death/discharge model. Include nested recorded-physiology/support, masks/process, limitation-status-only, context-only and full models. The baseline's interpretable coefficients, calibration and policy table show what incremental information is attributable to recovery versus documentation and status.

Substantive alternative: the parent's factorized two-domain observation-aware HSMM, with duration-constrained respiratory and hemodynamic chains, explicit observation model and four-event head, plus code-status/palliative state as a landmark event-head covariate. It can expose ordered duration and discordant recovery information lost by fixed summaries. Report it only if states are reproducible, split-stable, calibrated and not high-entropy charting artifacts. A GRU/TCN is deferred because it adds capacity without a new clinical uncertainty; a causal discharge/treatment model is deferred because counterfactual ICU retention, surveillance exposure and adjudicated goals of care are absent. These branches should be revisited only with those dependencies or an external cohort.

Predeclare:

- permutation of hourly times and a `storetime`-for-`charttime` negative timing control;
- masks-only, support-only, physiology-only, status-only, context-only and full ablations;
- removal of procedure corroboration, separate arterial/NIBP and FiO2/O2-flow/PEEP channels, and last-value versus interval coding;
- parent support/window/coverage/tie/bridge-duration/careunit/eventtype and q-grid sensitivities;
- transfer careunit permutation within admission preserving times/durations and a +6-hour transfer-interval shift;
- topology-refined versus aggregate/unconfirmed outcomes and discharge-as-censoring;
- complete-case, coverage-restricted, unknown-status-restricted and coverage-transport-standardized analyses;
- subgroup estimates by first careunit, admission type, anchor-year group, transfer density and code-status state;
- temporal audit proving every predictor has `charttime < L) and no post-outtime disposition/note text enters fitting;
- policy stress tests across the locked threshold and alert-budget grid, with abstention versus no-abstention.

Supportive evidence requires: calibrated held-out probabilities, positive and precise net benefit over alert-none and the intercept comparator at a prespecified budget; no net-benefit collapse in the documented-full-code, adequate-coverage stratum; stability across q, endpoint and status mappings; and agreement or a scientifically interpretable disagreement between baseline and HSMM. It supports only a retrospective surveillance-triage hypothesis.

Adverse evidence includes: utility reversal after status/coverage adjustment; signal confined to documented limitation or unknown strata; reliance on storetime, one channel, one bridge rule or aggregate unconfirmed returns; careunit-permutation persistence; poor calibration despite apparent net benefit; non-positivity; or HSMM states with no stable meaning. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse mapped status events, high unknown/stale fraction, few confirmed returns/deaths, wide net-benefit intervals, unstable splits, high state entropy, or a threshold/budget frontier inside the uncertainty interval. A negative result is not evidence that surveillance cannot help.

## What requires additional evidence

Computationally checkable: cohort construction, source-column availability, timing integrity, status-row coverage, transfer-topology classification, held-out risk calibration, CIFs, bootstrap intervals, event capture, alert counts, net-benefit arithmetic, ablations, permutation falsifications and agreement between methods.

Not established by this experiment: clinical intent, treatment limitation as a complete latent construct, planned versus unplanned return, discharge appropriateness, preventability, whether enhanced surveillance changes outcomes, the utility threshold clinicians should use, causal effects of diuretic/support timing, ward monitoring quality, equity in deployment, or transportability beyond this MIMIC snapshot. These require expert chart adjudication, validated goals-of-care and ward-monitoring data, an external cohort, and ultimately prospective or causal evaluation.
