# Persistent versus resolving instability at nighttime ICU discharge: an eICU transportability test

## Episode-9 successor and clinical decision

This is a substantive child of assessed valid candidate `[prior hypothesis]`. It retains expert seed `[starting question]` and the parent’s clinical decision: whether a nighttime ICU discharge signal should prompt closer prospective review primarily when the patient remains physiologically unstable. It makes a substantive change by separating a proximal *persistent* abnormal state from a transient or resolving abnormal state in the final six ICU hours, rather than treating all six-hour instability burden as one exposure. It also makes follow-up ascertainment and competing discharge/death states explicit.

The consequential decision is whether discharge-readiness review should distinguish patients whose recorded physiology is still abnormal late in the discharge window from those whose abnormalities are resolving. This could identify a subgroup for prospective safety review and post-ICU monitoring. The eICU data cannot decide whether delaying discharge, adding staffing, or changing treatment improves outcomes.

## Evidence-supported claim versus unresolved hypothesis

The strongest claim supported before computation is structural: the frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-stay and hospital identifiers, ICU-relative discharge offsets and clock strings, structured timestamped vital observations, hospital disposition fields, and a small EOL-care-plan table. The local audit found 200,859 patient rows, 208 hospital rows, 189,918 ICU-alive discharges, and 174,971 ICU-alive stays with unitdischargeoffset >360 and a valid discharge clock. It found 52,712 nighttime and 122,259 daytime stays in that flow. These facts establish computability, not a nighttime effect, accurate physiologic adjudication, or causal safety.

The unresolved primary hypothesis is:

> Among adults discharged alive from an ICU after at least six hours, the association between nighttime ICU discharge (23:00:00–06:59:59) and recorded hospital death within the following 48 hours is larger for stays with persistent late-window recorded physiologic instability than for stays with low or resolving instability, after adjustment for admission context and hospital and when evaluated with hospital-held-out predictions.

The null is no interaction, an interaction that disappears after accounting for observation density/coverage, or an interaction that does not reproduce across held-out hospitals. This is a conditional recorded association, not an effect of the clock time.

The substantive advance is clinically sharper than the parent’s binary six-hour burden: a patient with frequent abnormalities early but improving before discharge is separated from one whose late state remains abnormal. The experiment can therefore falsify the claimed state-specific association against a density-only explanation and against a non-proximal state-window explanation.

## Population, time zero, exposure and outcome

Use eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`, catalog `[internal dataset path]` (catalog [source checksum]). Sources are read-only ordinary gzip CSV files; the archive member is the ordinary file.

The analytic unit is `patientunitstayid`; retain repeated ICU stays and use `uniquepid` only for a person-clustered sensitivity. Include numeric adults `age >= 18` (report missing age and the eICU `> 89` convention), `unitdischargestatus = Alive`, `unitdischargeoffset > 360`, and a parseable `unitdischargetime24`. The ICU discharge offset is time zero. Exclude ICU deaths from the decision population but report their flow. Do not require or infer a calendar date or timezone.

Nighttime is `23:00:00` through `06:59:59`; daytime is `07:00:00` through `22:59:59`. Malformed or missing clocks are unknown and excluded from the primary clock comparison but retained in the flow. A fixed sensitivity uses 22:00–06:59 versus 07:00–21:59.

The primary state window is [`unitdischargeoffset - 360`, `unitdischargeoffset`) in ICU-relative minutes. Split it into an early half [d-360,d-180) and late half [d-180,d). No measurement at or after discharge may enter features. Divide each half into 30-minute bins. In each stream, carry the latest finite, physiologically plausible value forward no more than 30 minutes; do not carry across longer gaps and never impute missing as normal.

Use the following stream-specific plausibility bounds: mean pressure 20–200 mmHg, heart rate 20–250/min, respiration 3–80/min, and SpO2 50–100%. A bin is flagged if any available stream is abnormal: mean pressure <65, heart rate >120, respiration >30 or <8, or SpO2 <90. These are reproducible recorded-state thresholds, not treatment targets.

Require, for each half used in a state classification, at least 30 covered minutes and at least one contributing stream; require at least 60 covered minutes and at least two contributing streams over the complete six-hour window, at least three raw observations per contributing stream, and at least two flagged bins for any high-instability classification. Define early and late flagged minutes from the covered carried-forward bins:

- Persistent late instability: late flagged minutes >=60, late flagged bins >=2, and late coverage meets the requirements; late flagged minutes are no more than 30 minutes lower than early flagged minutes. This encodes abnormality that remains present rather than a purely resolving state.
- Resolving instability: early flagged minutes >=60 with early flagged bins >=2, but late flagged minutes <60 or at least 30 minutes lower than early flagged minutes, with both halves adequately covered.
- Low recorded instability: neither half meets the high-instability rule, with both halves adequately covered.
- Unknown state: either half fails its coverage requirement, the six-hour stream requirement fails, or values are invalid. Unknown is never classified as low.

The primary interaction uses persistent versus the combined resolving/low state to preserve power; the three-level state analysis is prespecified secondary. Sensitivities use 15 and 120 flagged-minute thresholds, a two-distinct-stream abnormality rule, and a continuous late-minus-early flagged-minute change. Report covered minutes, raw counts, number of streams, maximum gap, last-observation recency and state counts by hospital.

The primary outcome is `hospitaldischargestatus = Expired` with
`0 < hospitaldischargeoffset - unitdischargeoffset <= 2880`. Assign every clock-valid eligible stay to one of these documented states: death within 48 hours; documented alive hospital discharge by 48 hours; documented hospital disposition after 48 hours (known 48-hour non-event); or unknown/inconsistent follow-up because status/offset is missing, nonpositive, or otherwise cannot establish the window. An expired disposition after 48 hours is a known non-event for the 48-hour endpoint. Do not use Apache predicted mortality or any post-discharge feature. Report unknown states rather than treating them as survival.

## Analysis, baselines and uncertainty

The primary estimand is the difference between the hospital-standardized 48-hour recorded hospital-death risk difference for nighttime versus daytime discharge in persistent states and the corresponding difference in nonpersistent (resolving/low) states. Fit a prespecified logistic model with nighttime, state, and their interaction, adjusting for age, gender, ethnicity, hospital-admission source, unit type, unit-stay type, and pre-discharge coverage. Hospital fixed effects are used for the full-cohort association estimate; ICU length at discharge is not in the primary model because discharge length is a selection variable, and adding it is a labeled sensitivity. Do not adjust for discharge destination, which is downstream of the decision.

Use these nested transparent baselines:

- M0: admission context plus nighttime;
- Mstate: M0 plus early/late state summaries, coverage and missingness;
- Minteraction: Mstate plus the prespecified nighttime × persistent-state interaction;
- Mdensity: observation density, recency, gap and discharge timing without physiology;
- Mlearn: bounded ridge logistic model on the same allowed pre-discharge variables; a shallow fixed-depth tree is secondary only if event and overlap gates pass.

Use five grouped hospital folds from a fixed hash of `hospitalid`; all stays from one hospital stay in one fold. Learn preprocessing and tuning in training hospitals only. Report hospital-held-out Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC, precision-recall area, alert rate, and sensitivity/false-negative rate at a sensitivity target selected within training hospitals. No hospital ID is a prediction feature. The transparent interaction estimand remains primary even if Mlearn has better discrimination.

Report standardized risk differences, interaction contrast, cell counts and event rates by hospital and state, hospital-held-out predictions, and 95% uncertainty intervals from a hospital-cluster bootstrap. Add leave-one-hospital-out estimates where cells support them and a `uniquepid`-clustered sensitivity. A complete-case analysis is labeled an ascertainable-record analysis. Unknown outcome and unknown state counts are reported by hospital, clock category and state; optional inverse-ascertainment weighting is sensitivity only and cannot silently turn unknown into negative outcome.

## Falsification and interpretation

Computational falsification occurs if the interaction changes sign across hospitals, is driven by one hospital, vanishes in leave-one-hospital-out or hospital-held-out analyses, disappears under the fixed threshold/stream/gap sensitivities, or is reproduced by the density-only model while the physiology model adds no stable information. The hypothesis is also weakened if a deliberately earlier 12-hour state window is as strong as or stronger than the proximal six-hour state, if nighttime labels or state labels permuted within hospital and coverage strata retain the association, if a pre-discharge pseudo-outcome shows the same interaction, or if leakage/post-discharge fields enter the feature table. Implausibly narrow intervals, calibration failure, or an infeasible high-sensitivity threshold falsify the claimed transportable safety signal.

Supportive results require adequate overlap, several hospitals contributing each key cell, a consistent persistent-state interaction direction, hospital-clustered uncertainty excluding no interaction (or a margin declared before fitting), persistence in hospital-held-out and density/threshold sensitivities, and a conclusion linked to the computed risk-difference table and interval. This supports a reproducible recorded association that could justify prospective discharge-readiness review; it does not justify delaying discharge or changing staffing.

Adverse results include no interaction, a reversed interaction, a stronger association in resolving/low states, or a signal that is present only in random splits or density features. These are evidence against the stated subgroup hypothesis and may indicate selection or measurement processes. A better Mlearn AUROC with worse calibration or held-out false-negative performance is not a clinical improvement.

Results are inconclusive when state or outcome ascertainment is too sparse, unknown follow-up dominates, hospital overlap is inadequate, or the state is not reproducible across operational definitions. Inconclusive does not mean the hypothesis is false.

## Claims requiring clinical evidence beyond this computation

Computationally checkable claims are cohort flow, recorded clock classification, state algorithm, documented 48-hour disposition, hospital-standardized interaction, held-out calibration/discrimination, heterogeneity, and whether conclusions match computed outputs and uncertainty.

This dataset cannot establish expert-adjudicated physiologic instability, delivered treatment, discharge readiness, staffing, bed pressure, handoff quality, goals-of-care intent, post-ICU monitoring intensity, cause of death, or whether changing discharge timing would improve survival. It lacks raw waveforms, reliable calendar date/timezone linkage, complete pre-ICU course, clinical adjudication, and operational data. Expert review is required to validate the state screen and interpret deaths; a prospective multi-hospital or target-trial study with discharge-readiness, staffing, bed-capacity, treatment-intent and post-discharge monitoring data is required for a causal policy claim. Hospital estimates must never be presented as quality rankings.
