# Provisional hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery in MIMIC-IV

Status: provisional checkpoint draft; no cohort counts, model fits, or clinical conclusions are claimed.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in their first MIMIC-IV ICU stay who have a documented AKI episode and remain observable through a prespecified 48-hour landmark, a creatinine-improved but urine-output-impaired phenotype will have worse subsequent in-hospital death and/or persistent or worsening kidney dysfunction than a concordant creatinine-and-urine-output recovery phenotype. This is a prognostic association hypothesis, not a causal claim.

The strongest rival explanation is a monitoring/measurement artifact: creatinine is sampled at clinician-selected times, urine output is recorded only when collection is present, and fluid balance is a treatment/monitoring summary rather than a direct organ state. Residual illness severity and treatment selection are additional rivals.

## Falsifiable test

Use a first qualifying adult ICU stay per hospitalization with an AKI episode and a creatinine measurement near the 48-hour landmark. Derive exposure, covariates, and all predictors only from information available before the landmark; outcomes begin after it.

Primary comparison: creatinine-improved/urine-output-impaired versus concordant recovery. Outcomes: subsequent in-hospital death and persistent or worsening kidney dysfunction, with the latter defined prospectively from creatinine and urine-output measurements. A prespecified secondary analysis will test whether fluid balance adds information beyond the creatinine/urine phenotype.

The simple baseline is a creatinine-only recovery phenotype with prespecified landmark covariates. The substantive alternative is a time-aware model using the pre-landmark creatinine trajectory, urine-output trajectory, fluid balance, and monitoring intensity. The alternative can reveal whether apparent discordance is a reproducible temporal pattern rather than a single noisy measurement, but it cannot identify a mechanism or causal effect. Compare held-out discrimination/calibration and uncertainty intervals, not only a small performance gain.

Supportive results would show a reproducible excess risk for discordance beyond the creatinine baseline, with uncertainty excluding the prespecified clinically meaningful range and consistency across sensitivity analyses. Adverse results would show no incremental association or a reversed association, weakening the proposed prognostic distinction. Inconclusive results would be imprecise estimates, inadequate landmark coverage, or instability across measurement definitions; they would motivate a measurement audit or larger/external cohort, not a clinical conclusion.

## Evidence status and missing evidence

This draft is grounded in the inherited board question and the MIMIC-IV data domain, but it is not yet a validated experiment. Exact MIMIC-IV table identifiers, join keys, item IDs and units, bounded cohort coverage, and the three required inspected key-reference receipts remain unresolved. No literature claim is being made here. The source catalog and schema audit, plus evidence acquisition, must be completed before selection.

## Concrete deliverable

The future solver must construct the landmark cohort, estimate the phenotype-specific outcome risks and uncertainty, fit the creatinine-only baseline and time-aware alternative on the same split, and report whether the discordant phenotype adds prognostic information. Completion requires cohort counts, missingness/coverage, effect estimates with uncertainty, held-out evaluation, sensitivity analyses, and conclusions linked to computed outputs. Clinical adjudication or external validation would be required for mechanistic or treatment claims.
