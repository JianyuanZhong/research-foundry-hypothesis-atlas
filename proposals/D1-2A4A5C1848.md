# Episode 87 successor: does status-specific target recalibration remain valid for both repeat-lactate states without changing a fixed-capacity queue?

## Unresolved clinical question and substantive advance

A transported early-lactate score can be used in two different ways: to rank a fixed number of patients for review, or to display an absolute probability and apply a probability threshold. The parents establish the importance of separating those decisions, and they show that pooled calibration can conceal cancellation between patients with and without a recorded repeat lactate. The remaining clinically consequential weakness is the proposed remedy itself. A single target-hospital intercept can repair an average target risk while leaving systematic error in the (F\_available=0) and (F\_available=1) groups. Conversely, a status-specific intercept may repair those displays but could be too unstable to validate across hospitals, and it must not be allowed to silently alter the fixed-capacity ranking claim.

This child tests one falsifiable repair: after a source-only R_full fit, estimate one target-label intercept separately for each repeat-lactate availability state, with the target hospital held out from calibration. The primary question is whether the resulting probability display satisfies one simultaneous, uncertainty-aware worst-cell error criterion across both directed transports, both repeat-availability states, and both sides of the prespecified 0.10 threshold. The remedy is evaluated on held-out target hospitals and is compared descriptively with the parent's pooled-intercept remedy. Its effect on fixed-capacity queues is tested as an exact rank-invariance property and is never treated as evidence of clinical benefit.

The strongest available evidence is narrower than this claim. The inherited evidence record reports modest incremental discrimination from a 24-hour lactate in a measured sepsis cohort and highlights that observation/missingness processes are often incompletely represented; the completed EICU audit shows nonempty F_available strata in both target practices (low-target: 631 without and 380 with F; high-target: 508 without and 783 with F). Those facts support studying measurement-status heterogeneity. They do not support the untested claim that a status-specific intercept transports, that it is adequate for individual calibration or clinical thresholding, or that any queue changes patient outcomes. I have not treated the unavailable cancer main article or its full STAR Methods as inspected; the research-ambition README makes only its supplement and metadata available.

## Primary hypothesis and estimand

Let (d\in\{L\to H,H\to L\}) denote the directed source-to-target practice transfer. Let (s=F\_available\in\{0,1\}). For each target hospital (h), let (\eta_i) be the target raw R_full linear predictor from the source-only fit, and estimate a status-specific intercept (\delta_{h,s}) using D7 labels from target hospitals other than (h), restricted to status (s). Define the held-out probability

[
p^{SS}_i=\operatorname{expit}(\eta_i+\delta_{h(i),s(i)}).
]

Define the deployment-side indicator (z_i=1[p^{SS}_i\ge 0.10]), including exact ties. For each (d,s,z), compute the held-out target mean-risk error

[
e^{SS}(d,s,z)=
\operatorname{mean}_{i\in T_d:F_i=s,z_i=z}(D7_i-p^{SS}_i).
]

The single primary estimand is the worst status- and threshold-side error across both directed transports,

[
Q_{SS}=\max_{d,s,z}|e^{SS}(d,s,z)|.
]

This is a mean-risk calibration-in-the-large screen within prespecified deployment cells, not individual calibration, a calibration slope, or proof that a 0.10 threshold is clinically appropriate.

Primary hypothesis: status-specific recalibration is transport-valid for this defined probability-display task if (Q_{SS}\le 0.05). The primary supportive rule is a two-sided copied-hospital-refit percentile interval with upper 95% bound (\le0.05), with all eight original cells having at least 30 people and at least 1,900 of 2,000 finite joint replicates. The primary adverse rule is a valid computation with lower 95% bound (>0.05). All other computable patterns are inconclusive. A point estimate below 0.05, a pooled target error, or a favorable calibration regression cannot support the hypothesis.

The primary estimand is deliberately one quantity. The following are secondary diagnostics, not alternate primary gates:

- (Q_{pool}): the same maximum for the parent's one-intercept, held-out target-hospital remedy.
- (Q_{source}): the source-trained unmodified R_full probabilities.
- A target-stratum constant-risk display using source-training target-stratum prevalence, to establish whether any benefit is more than a prevalence-only display.
- Cellwise errors, flag fractions, observed and predicted means, Brier score and AUROC.
- A common-cell diagnostic that evaluates source and pooled predictions on the status-specific deployment cells defined by (p^{SS}). This prevents an apparent improvement from being attributed merely to changing the partition. It does not replace the primary deployed-remedy estimand.

A status-specific intercept is monotone within a hospital and status, but it can move patients across the 0.10 probability threshold. Therefore threshold flag counts and agreement with source and pooled flags must be reported. It cannot change raw-score ranks or any top-k queue.

## Status-specific recalibration and leakage control

For each directed target and each target hospital (h), use the source-fitted (eta_i) as an offset. For each status (s\in\{0,1\}), solve by deterministic bisection on [-20,20], tolerance 1e-12, the same uniquely penalized equation used by the parent:

[
\sum_{i\in T_d,\,hospital(i)\ne h,\,F_i=s}
[D7_i-\operatorname{expit}(\eta_i+\delta_{h,s})]-\delta_{h,s}=0.
]

The penalty coefficient is exactly 1. No D7 label from hospital (h) may enter either (delta_{h,0}) or (delta_{h,1}). If a status-specific calibration set is empty, the root cannot be bracketed or the root/predictions are nonfinite, that hospital/status prediction is noncomputable; do not silently pool statuses or revert to the unstratified intercept. This is a historical-label validation procedure, not a claim that a new hospital can deploy it without outcome data.

For the parent comparator, estimate one (delta_h) from all statuses in target hospitals other than h by the same penalized equation and use (p^{pool}=expit(eta+delta_h)). The unmodified source score is (p^{source}=expit(eta)). All three probability sets are evaluated out-of-fold at the person level. A target calibration regression, if emitted, is optional descriptive output and never feeds scores, flags, queues or validity gates.

## Ordered result states and falsification

First apply these primary calibration states:

1. **Not computable**: any source/hash/header/schema/key mismatch; cohort/outcome audit mismatch; changed clocks, feature definitions, revision/conflict/tie rules, strata, folds, fitting constants or threshold; target-label leakage; nonfinite original fit, root or score; an empty primary cell or undefined required prediction; or missing audit/output artifacts.
2. **Calibration adverse**: computation is valid, all primary cells and at least 1,900 joint replicates are finite, and the lower 95% bound for Q_SS is >0.05.
3. **Calibration inconclusive**: computation is valid but the interval for Q_SS crosses 0.05, a primary cell has fewer than 30 people, the deletion stability gate fails, a required deletion representation is insufficient, or fewer than 1,900 joint replicates are finite. A nonestimable optional regression alone does not cause this state.
4. **Calibration supportive**: all primary gates pass, the upper 95% bound for Q_SS is <=0.05, all original cells have n>=30, at least 1,900 joint replicates are finite, and deletion stability passes.
Then report the secondary queue state independently: supportive, adverse, or inconclusive under its inherited (	au_7,	au_{inf},Delta_7), capacity and deletion rules. The joint deployment interpretation is:

- supportive calibration + supportive queue: the specified probability-display and fixed-slot transport criteria both passed in these 30 EICU hospitals;
- supportive calibration + adverse/inconclusive queue: an absolute-risk display passed the screen but the repeat-lactate ranking increment is not supported;
- adverse/inconclusive calibration + supportive queue: fixed-slot ranking evidence may remain useful, but no displayed probability or 0.10 threshold claim is supported;
- any not-computable state: no scientific result.

An adverse calibration state does not show that lactate has no prognostic value, and an adverse queue state does not show that recalibration is useless elsewhere. Inconclusive is not equivalence. Status-specific calibration support does not establish calibration at every risk value, subgroup fairness, transport beyond these hospitals, or benefit from review.
