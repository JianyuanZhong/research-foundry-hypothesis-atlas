# Laboratory observation after the documented bedside: a cross-modality eICU transport experiment

Status: Episode-21 substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. This proposal preserves the parent's adult first-stay population, ICU-relative landmark, competing recorded-outcome estimand, whole-hospital transport, and transparent-versus-temporal comparison. It adds a prespecified aggregate nursing/respiratory documentation control to test the parent's remaining workflow-confounding limitation. It makes no fitted-result, causal, physiologic-truth, clinician-intent, or clinical-benefit claim.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

At ICU-relative minute 360, does the patient-linked history of laboratory observations identify later recorded ICU death beyond (i) recorded physiologic state, (ii) general vital-observation intensity, and (iii) the intensity and timing of other bedside documentation? This matters because a monitoring-support system could be interpreted very differently depending on the answer. A laboratory signal that survives these controls is a more credible candidate for cross-hospital retrospective risk stratification at a fixed review burden. A signal that disappears after aggregate nurse/respiratory documentation adjustment is evidence that the apparent testing signal is primarily an ascertainment or workflow marker; implementing it as a patient surveillance rule would then be poorly justified.

The strongest available evidence is outcome-blind feasibility and schema evidence, not a result: the parent's audit reports 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows known by minute 360. These facts support a bounded whole-hospital experiment; they do not establish physiology, intent, causal effect, or clinical benefit.

The unresolved claim is specifically that a laboratory-observation increment is patient-linked and transportable rather than a proxy for unmeasured instability, general bedside documentation, local interface, revision practice, or hospital workflow. The substantive advance over the parent is a nested cross-modality test of that claim. It also separates a retrospective recorded-death proxy from any claim that ordering, reviewing, or reducing tests changes outcomes.

## Falsifiable hypothesis

Primary hypothesis:

> Among adults reaching ICU-relative minute 360, adding patient-level laboratory-observation process features to a transparent competing-risk model containing recorded state, vital-observation intensity, and aggregate nursing/respiratory documentation intensity improves the held-out-hospital macro-hospital integrated competing-risk Brier score for recorded ICU death by minute 1,800. At the same prespecified per-hospital review burden, the laboratory-process model captures more recorded ICU deaths or has higher recorded-death PPV than the documentation-adjusted baseline. A compact temporal model using the same six-hour channels will show a further substantive gain only if the gain depends on bin order/persistence rather than parameter count.

The patient-linked interpretation is falsified or materially weakened if the lab increment disappears or reverses after documentation adjustment, is not transported across hospitals, fails the exact equal-burden comparison, persists after process permutation, is reproduced by site/interface/documentation diagnostics, or vanishes under defensible analyte and revision rules. It is also falsified as a temporal claim if a time-shuffled or parameter-matched non-temporal control performs similarly. None of these tests can establish that a real monitoring intervention benefits patients.

## Population, time, outcome, and estimand

Use eICU snapshot [source checksum].

Construct exactly one row per person's first valid ICU stay from patient:

- age at least 18; recode numeric ages greater than 89 to 90;
- within uniquepid, select the smallest valid numeric unitvisitnumber, breaking ties by patientunitstayid;
- require numeric patientunitstayid and patient.unitdischargeoffset greater than 360;
- retain only stays reaching the landmark at ICU-relative minute 360.

The predictor window is [0,360] minutes, inclusive at both ends. A laboratory row is eligible only when numeric labresult, labresultoffset in [0,360], and labresultrevisedoffset less than or equal to 360 or null. A vital or charting row is eligible only when its event offset is in [0,360]. Result/event time and revision/entry time remain separate: information not known by minute 360 cannot be used as known at the landmark. All negative, invalid, duplicate-stay, impossible timestamp, and unexpected status cases are audited before fitting and never silently remapped.

The primary outcome is the first discharge event represented by the selected patient row after the landmark and no later than minute 1,800:

- event of interest: normalized unitdischargestatus = Expired and unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800];
- competing event: normalized unitdischargestatus = Alive and unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800];
- administrative censoring: neither event through minute 1,800.

Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, latent death, and post-ICU outcomes are outside the estimand. Estimate cause-specific discrete-time hazards in 24 one-hour bins covering (360,1800], then derive the cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death with alive ICU discharge competing. Predictors are landmark-window features only.

## Outcome-blind feature definitions

Freeze the analyte/name/system/interface codebook using only development hospitals before any outcome evaluation. Candidate lab fields are potassium, sodium, hemoglobin, hematocrit, glucose, creatinine, BUN, calcium, WBC, platelets, bicarbonate, FiO2, PaO2, PaCO2, pH, anion gap, HCO3, oxygen saturation, magnesium and base excess, subject to observed encoding support. lab has no separate unit column. A variable name does not verify its unit: unresolved unit, interface, or system compatibility requires exclusion or separately supported strata. Keep only numeric values under a predeclared valid-range, duplicate and revision-known rule. labresulttext is not used to infer a unit or clinical state. Preserve name/system/interface indicators only as process diagnostics when their use does not encode site identity.

Laboratory state Ls uses predeclared first, last, median, range and slope or a robust equivalent for supported analytes, with explicit availability masks. Laboratory process Lp uses total valid rows, distinct analytes, distinct result times, first/last result time, recency, span, inter-result-gap summaries, analyte-specific counts/recency, and revision-known status. Process features must not include any post-landmark row.

Vital state Vs uses the same predeclared summaries and explicit masks for supported vital fields from vitalPeriodic and vitalAperiodic. Vital intensity Vd uses periodic and aperiodic row counts, distinct observation times, last time, span and coverage in six one-hour bins. It is an ascertainment control, not a measure of all monitoring or patient physiology.

The new aggregate documentation control D uses nurseCharting and respiratoryCharting rows with event offsets in [0,360], regardless of whether a value is numeric. For each table separately and combined, use row count, distinct event time count, first/last event time, span, six-bin coverage, and entry-lag summaries computed as entry offset minus event offset after auditing invalid lags. Do not use nursingchartvalue, respiratorychartvalue, label text, or category/type names in the primary D features: the intended construct is documentation intensity/timing, not physiology, treatment, clinician review, or intent. A sensitivity analysis may use predeclared category/type groups only if their mapping and per-hospital support are frozen in development; it is a workflow diagnostic, not a patient model.

## Transparent and learned models

Admission context A is gender, ethnicity, admissionweight, hospitaladmitoffset, hospitaladmitsource and unitstaytype. No hospital ID, ward ID, hospital descriptor, discharge field, outcome, post-landmark row, or future revision enters a patient predictor.

Fit the following pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazard models with identical episodes, 24 labels, preprocessing rules, grouped folds and hyperparameter-selection procedure:

- Bv = A + Ls + Vs;
- BvV = Bv + Vd;
- Bd = BvV + D;
- BpV = BvV + Lp;
- BpD = Bd + Lp.

The primary scientific contrast is BpD minus Bd: laboratory observation process after recorded laboratory/vital state, vital intensity and broad aggregate charting intensity. BpV minus BvV is retained as the parent/intermediate contrast, permitting direct attribution of what changes after the new control. Coefficient summaries are descriptive; transport conclusions use held-out predictions and hospital-clustered uncertainty.

### Temporal alternative

If support gates pass, fit two identical compact one-layer GRUs, T0 and T1, each with hidden size 32, dropout 0.1 and development-only early stopping. Use six fixed bins [0,60), [60,120), [120,180), [180,240), [240,300), [300,360); observations at exactly 360 are included in landmark summaries but not assigned to a half-open sequence bin unless the bin convention is explicitly changed before fitting. Each bin contains supported laboratory state/masks, vital state/masks, vital-intensity channels, and aggregate D count/coverage/entry-lag channels. T0 receives the channels corresponding to Bd. T1 receives exactly T0 plus the Lp channels. Admission context is concatenated to the final representation. Both have competing death and alive-discharge heads for the same 24 post-landmark bins and no hospital ID.

T1 versus T0 is the learned analogue of BpD versus Bd. The summaries in Bd deliberately compress the six-hour record; T0/T1 preserve bin order and joint timing. A temporal result is substantive only if it has stable held-out-hospital calibration or equal-burden policy improvement, attenuates under bin-order permutation, and exceeds a parameter-count-matched non-temporal control. A small AUROC change alone is not a temporal or clinical result.

## Hospital transport, uncertainty and exact equal burden

Use five grouped outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once. Keep all stays with the same uniquepid together and stratify by eligible episode/event counts when feasible. No hospital is split across train and evaluation. Freeze codebooks, support rules, transformations, imputation, scaling, regularization, temporal hyperparameters and policy definitions in development hospitals only.

Primary performance estimand is the macro-hospital paired difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score over (360,1800] for BpD versus Bd, with lower Brier favorable. Secondary summaries are CIF at minute 1,800, time-specific Brier, calibration, BpV versus BvV, and T1 versus T0. Report each hospital and fold, pooled results only alongside macro-hospital results, and 95% hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals with at least 1,000 resamples if feasible. Prespecify multiplicity reporting across contrasts and metrics; do not select a conclusion post hoc from the metric table.

For each held-out hospital h and q in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, define Bd's landmark cumulative-incidence policy as episodes with Bd risk at least q. Let k(h,q) be its alert count. Rank the same held-out episodes by BpD, BpV, T1, and any control and select exactly the top k(h,q), with deterministic ties. No held-out outcome is used. If k(h,q)=0, that cell has no PPV or capture assignment. Report review fraction, recorded-death capture, recorded-death PPV, calibration among reviewed and non-reviewed episodes, and incremental membership relative to Bd. Macro-hospital paired differences are primary; pooled values are secondary. This is a label-relative retrospective comparison, not net clinical benefit.

An implementation-oriented interpretation requires at least 20 evaluation hospitals, at least 10 alerts per evaluation hospital on average, at least 100 alerted episodes, and at least 20 recorded deaths pooled across supported held-out cells for the reported q. Unsupported q values are exploratory.

## Readiness and support gates

Before fitting, emit a frozen data dictionary, outcome-blind codebook, cohort flow, invalid-row audit, fold manifest, feature-support manifest, and time/leakage audit. Allow the primary interpretation only if:

- there are at least 1,000 recorded deaths and 50 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes;

- every retained lab/vital field meets a development-frozen overall and per-hospital support rule (minimum 30% overall and 10% in at least 80% of evaluation hospitals, unless the field is excluded);
- D features have nontrivial overlap with the prespecified Bd-risk and vital-coverage strata used for process permutation;
- BpD, Bd, BpV and BvV converge in all folds and produce finite predictions for every eligible evaluation episode;
- the strict BpD versus Bd comparison has at least 10 episodes in every populated evaluation hospital/stratum used for permutation;
- reported q cells pass the equal-burden support gate;
- all timestamp, first-stay, patient-grouping, source-join and hospital-leakage audits pass.

A failed gate is retained as a support result and labeled descriptive or inconclusive. Readiness validates packaging and bounded probes; it does not resolve the hypothesis.

## Locked falsifications and robustness

1. Permute Lp within each held-out hospital, stratified by held-out Bd-risk quintile, vital-coverage stratum and D-coverage stratum, while holding Ls, Vs, Vd and D fixed. The BpD increment should attenuate if it is patient-linked rather than an incidental process assignment.
2. Compare BpV minus BvV with BpD minus Bd. Disappearance only after D adjustment supports a broader documentation-workflow explanation; it does not prove absence of physiology.
3. Repeat with a strict common analyte panel and revision-known-only rows. Loss under defensible unit/interface/revision rules prevents a robust analyte interpretation.
4. Fit documentation-only, vital-intensity-only and hospital-descriptor-only diagnostics. Descriptor/site/interface reproduction or concentration in one hospital or analyte family falsifies a general patient-linked interpretation.
5. Repeat with outcome-free within-hospital percentile normalization of Lp and D, requiring at least 50 calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes per hospital. This tests whether local workflow scaling, rather than absolute counts, drives transport.
6. For T0/T1, permute six-bin order, and compare each with a parameter-count-matched non-temporal model using the same channels and labels. A temporal conclusion requires attenuation under order permutation and a consistent advantage over the matched control.
7. Recompute all policy metrics with the identical per-hospital k(h,q). More alerts cannot create a gain.
8. Report leave-one-hospital-out influence, fold direction, and per-hospital calibration. A pooled improvement driven by one hospital is not transport evidence.
9. As a negative-control timing diagnostic, shift or permute chart entry-lag features within held-out documentation strata while retaining event counts. If the lab result changes only with arbitrary entry metadata, its interpretation is workflow-sensitive.

## Interpretation rules

Supportive evidence requires the support gates, a favorable held-out macro-hospital BpD minus Bd Brier contrast with uncertainty excluding no improvement, consistent direction in a prespecified majority of folds, and an equal-burden capture or PPV improvement without unacceptable calibration loss. The increment should attenuate under Lp permutation and should not be reproduced by site/interface/documentation-only diagnostics. T1 supports a temporal extension only after order permutation and matched-control criteria pass. Even then, the conclusion is limited to a retrospective patient-linked recorded-ICU-death review proxy.

Adverse evidence includes a null or reversed BpD minus Bd contrast, no equal-burden improvement, calibration harm, persistence under process permutation, reproduction by D or hospital descriptors, concentration in a site/interface/analyte family, or loss under defensible codebook/revision controls. These results support workflow, missing-physiology, interface, or revision explanations and argue against treating laboratory history as a transportable patient-linked signal.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed support/overlap gates, sparse policy cells, unstable fold directions, nonconvergence, finite-prediction or leakage failures, wide intervals spanning clinically important favorable and adverse values, or an unsupported temporal fit. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.
