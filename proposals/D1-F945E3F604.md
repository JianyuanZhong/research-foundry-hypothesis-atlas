## Question, evidence and hypothesis

The expert seed and parent raise a clinically important measurement question: can apparent creatinine recovery conceal persistent low recorded urine output? The strongest local evidence is narrower. Haines et al. (CJASN 2023, DOI 10.2215/CJN.0000000000000203; the downloaded readable text was inspected) found in a selected critical-illness cohort that creatinine-based kidney-function estimates can overestimate measured function after muscle loss. That does not establish a Foley-output phenotype, its prevalence, its mortality association, or a causal mechanism in MIMIC-IV.

The original hypothesis remains:

> Among adults with an observed early creatinine-AKI-like rise who are alive at 72 hours in their first ICU stay and have adequate paired measurements, creatinine recovery without late Foley-recorded output recovery is associated with higher subsequent in-hospital mortality than recovery of both observed measures.

This is a noncausal, measurement-defined prognostic claim. It does not identify KDIGO AKI, true GFR, Foley patency, volume status, muscle loss, renal indication, or a treatment effect. A reproducible association could justify confirmatory kidney-function assessment and monitoring; it cannot justify starting, stopping, delaying or withholding dialysis, fluids or diuresis.

Because the paired support is currently unavailable, the distinct fallback hypothesis is:

> Among the same adults with an observed early creatinine-AKI-like rise who survive to 72 hours, observed creatinine recovery versus persistent non-recovery is associated with subsequent in-hospital mortality.

The fallback answers whether the creatinine trajectory itself carries prognostic information, without attributing incremental meaning to missing or selectively charted urine output. It is scientifically weaker on mechanism and novelty but stronger on estimability and transport within this database. It must not be described as a test of discordant recovery.

## Baseline versus substantive temporal alternative

The baseline is newly fitted ridge logistic regression for the relevant primary outcome. For the paired branch it excludes Foley, weight-standardized output and fluid channels; for the fallback it is creatinine-centered and uses admission context, pre-t0 creatinine summaries, vitals/lactate, support indicators, CKD proxy and missingness. The baseline tests whether the renal phenotype adds information beyond transparent severity and creatinine history.

The alternative is newly fitted CPU HistGradientBoostingClassifier (or a fixed equivalent) on the identical subjects, outcome, t0 and 80/20 subject-held-out split. Build six-hour bins over [0,72h), with last/mean/min/max/slope/variability/count/missingness for creatinine, Foley output and weight-standardized output when paired support passes, plus MAP, HR, respiratory rate, SpO2, lactate, vasopressor, ventilation and RRT trajectories. For the fallback, the urine/output channels are absent by definition; the substantive information is nonlinear timing and interaction among creatinine and physiologic-support trajectories. Fit and tune only within training subjects with grouped inner validation and training-fold calibration. Evaluate once on held-out subjects using AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope and curves, with subject-cluster bootstrap intervals. The paired branch additionally fits a urine/fluid-channel ablation.

The alternative could reveal whether a transient fall, timing of the peak, or interaction with perfusion/support carries information that a summary/additive baseline loses. It is selected for scientific trajectory information, not a small predictive gain. Budget: 2–4 CPU cores, at most 8 GB RAM, and a few hours for streaming extraction/fitting under the configured 7,200-second science limit. A neural sequence model and a latent mechanistic GFR model are deferred: cystatin C, measured GFR, muscle mass, fluid-status adjudication and treatment indication are absent, and a more complex model cannot repair that missing evidence.

## Falsification and decision rules

Prespecify absolute-only versus relative-only recovery thresholds; 12 versus 18 observed Foley hours; ICU-only, hospital-only and combined creatinine; weighted versus unweighted output; source-specific creatinine; pre-t0 RRT exclusion/stratification; coverage adjustment; and a within-stay permutation that shuffles Foley hourly records across the 72 hours while preserving total volume and observed-hour count. If a purported timing-specific signal persists after this permutation, the discordance interpretation is falsified or is measurement-density/total-volume confounding.

Supportive paired evidence requires the support gate, a positive adjusted RD with a 95% interval excluding zero, direction retained in measurement-dense and at least two source/threshold sensitivities, no materially stronger time-permuted association, and reproducible improvement in held-out Brier/calibration or clinically prespecified risk stratification over both baseline and output ablation. Supportive fallback evidence requires the creatinine-only support gate, a prespecified recovery/non-recovery contrast with stable direction across source/threshold checks, and temporal-model calibration or proper-score improvement over the baseline. Prediction improvement alone cannot establish the renal mechanism.

Adverse means the prespecified contrast is near/below zero, reverses across coverage/source/threshold analyses, or is preserved by timing permutation. Inconclusive/no-go means missing source members, absent dictionary confirmation, sparse cells/events, differential coverage that cannot be modeled, unstable estimates, failed calibration, or no observable follow-up. The verifier can check joins, filters, leakage, predictions, estimates and uncertainty; it cannot establish KDIGO AKI, true GFR, Foley patency, volume status, mechanism, treatment utility, clinical adjudication or external validity. Those claims require cystatin C/measured GFR, expert chart review and prospective/external validation.

## Alternatives not chosen and revisit evidence

The paired Foley branch is retained because it tests a clinically substantive discordance question, but it is not allowed to survive unsupported measurement. The creatinine-only fallback is selected for episode 5 execution if paired support is unavailable. A discharge-instability question using terminal vital/oxygen states was not chosen: it is a different clinical decision and does not resolve the renal measurement uncertainty. Diuretic timing and RRT-treatment effects remain deferred because indication and time-varying confounding are not adequately observed. A neural sequence model would be revisited only if paired measurements are prevalent, the temporal interaction is stable on held-out data, and the added information survives measurement-process controls; a mechanistic kidney model would require cystatin C, measured GFR, muscle mass or adjudicated volume status.

The actual scientific completion is a fitted, uncertainty-quantified paired contrast if the support gate passes, otherwise a fitted, uncertainty-quantified creatinine-only contrast and temporal-vs-baseline evaluation. Neither branch may report a stronger clinical conclusion than its observed data support.
