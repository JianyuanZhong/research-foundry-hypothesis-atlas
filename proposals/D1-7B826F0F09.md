## Scientific opening and claim boundary

The strongest supported claim remains only record-level and predictive: within-person change in recorded assays through day 42 may add held-out information for a later operational repeat-TACE record beyond a matched observation clock and stable pre-index assay profile. The HCC files do not adjudicate TACE intent, technical completion, indication, response, progression, survival, outside care, or utility.

The unresolved selection claim is:

> Conditional on the eligible index population and a pre-outcome day-28 information set, does the Delta42 increment remain when day-42 availability is transported from the day-28-at-risk population using a prespecified competing observation-process model, and is it also present in a day-28 landmark analysis that never uses records after day 28?

The leading explanation is a within-person assay-content signal that remains informative after stable profile, measured clock, and the day-42 observation process are separated. The strongest rivals are:

1. Continued-care selection: patients with dense non-TACE care, scheduling, or impending retreatment are more likely to generate both late assays and a later TACE record.
2. Observation loss: patients whose records stop before day 42 are systematically different; conditioning on R42 creates a selected population.
3. Stable phenotype or recording intensity: the apparent change is explained by persistent severity, assay availability, or documentation pattern.
4. Boundary artifact: the increment is driven by days 29–42 and disappears when the check is fixed at day 28.

The competing process outcome makes these rivals observable as operational contrasts, not as claims about a latent clinical state.

[K1] supports strict temporal masking in longitudinal record prediction and warns that recorded trajectories are not automatically biological trajectories. [K2] supports separating stable structure from temporal deviations and motivates selection adjustment under explicit assumptions, while not making weighting causal here. [K3] establishes the clinical importance of post-TACE laboratory trajectories in a separately adjudicated cohort; it does not validate the present operational endpoints. The attached excerpts and hash-linked receipts are in this branch.

## The new pre-outcome competing observation process

Define R28 and R42 using the inherited all-source observation-end rule, but retain a separate non-procedure process audit so future TACE evidence cannot define its own selection correction.

Among eligible index patients with R28=1, define the first valid non-procedure activity in (t0+28,t0+42] from encounters, examinations, orders, medications, or labs. Exclude all later TACE rows and all O0–O4 evidence from Q construction. Q is assigned mutually exclusively:

- Q=R42: inherited all-source observation reaches at least t0+42.
- Q=care-truncated: R42=0, but at least one valid timed non-procedure care activity occurs in the interval. Record source family, first time, days from t0, encounter/order status completeness, and last observed time. This means continued recorded care without a day-42 landmark, not a clinical competing event.
- Q=record-loss: R42=0 and no valid timed non-procedure activity occurs in the interval.
- Q=unknown: required timestamps are malformed or the source audit cannot determine the interval status; do not force it into loss.

The Q audit must include source-specific fields: encounters 就诊时间, 入院时间, 出院时间; examinations 开始时间; orders 开立时间, 开始时间, 结束时间, 医嘱状态; medications 开始时间, 结束时间; labs 检验时间. Validity, cancellation handling, and duplicate handling are frozen before fitting. The procedure table is used for t0 and later endpoint provenance, not for Q.

Fit a multinomial observation model for Q using only information available by day 28: M0, B0, pre-index P_obs/P_care, C28 non-result opportunity features (counts, valid-time indicators, encounter-day coverage, source-family counts, timestamp/status completeness), and R28. Result values, Delta28, Delta42, future procedure fields, Q itself, and O0–O4 are prohibited. The primary day-42 weight is a development-fold-frozen stabilized transport weight for Q=R42 versus all observable Q alternatives, with numerator containing only M0/B0 and denominator containing the permitted day-28 process features. Report full multinomial probabilities, support, tail truncation rule, effective sample size, and Q frequencies. A descriptive sensitivity adds day-28 result availability indicators without result values; it does not enter the primary weight.

If positivity is inadequate for R42 in any prespecified baseline/process stratum, do not truncate toward a favorable answer: mark the weighted estimand inconclusive and report unsupported strata. Weights are not inverse-probability treatment weights and do not identify a causal effect.

## Comparable methods and alternatives

Both methods use identical patients, endpoint rules, Q labels, risk grid, right-censoring masks, ordinary/weighted arms, R28/R42 boundaries, feature masks, split, and uncertainty family. Q and all post-tp capture fields are never predictors.

Transparent baseline: pooled-logistic discrete-time hazard with intercept and risk-bin indicators, elastic-net alpha=0.5. Fit A_clock42, A_clock42+P_stable, A_clock42+P_stable+Delta42, and A_full42; repeat with H28 features in the R28 pre-outcome arm. Fit the multinomial Q observation model separately. Parsers, qualitative token dictionaries, robust scaling, imputation, reference construction, lambda, observation-model choices, and weight truncation are learned within five grouped development folds only. The baseline is scientifically adequate because the nested blocks, Q process variables, missingness, and support can be audited directly.

Matched learned alternative: two-stream GRU with clock/process stream and stable/within-person stream, the inherited compact specification: one GRU layer, hidden size 32, dropout 0.10, 32/16 head, Adam 1e-3, weight decay 1e-4, maximum 50 epochs, eight-epoch early stopping, seeds 17/29/41. Fit clock-only, clock+stable, clock+stable+within, and full-content variants on identical folds and weights; fit Q as a separate multinomial process model. The GRU may reveal irregular timing and nonlinear assay combinations hidden by the block-additive baseline. It is judged by the same G_within estimand and uncertainty, not by complexity or a small predictive gain.

A transformer, narrative NLP, imaging model, mechanistic response simulation, or causal treatment model is deferred because the required validated units, images, intent/response labels, reliable narrative temporality, technical TACE data, and patient-important outcomes are absent. Frozen falsifiers:

1. Remove all result content while retaining identical clocks and process features.
2. Permute Delta42 within patient among same assay label/sample type, preserving event time, missingness, and availability.
3. Fit timing/source-only models with no assay values; if they match full content, the assay-content interpretation fails.
4. Remove assay rows in (t0+35,t0+42] while retaining earlier content and the same endpoint.
5. Compare R28 pre-outcome G_within,O4^28 with R42 ordinary and weighted G_within,O4.
6. Stratify day-42 contrasts by Q=R42, care-truncated, and record-loss; Q is diagnostic only.
7. Compare O4 with O0/O1/O2/O3 and O_planproxy_cap; do not create a new clinical endpoint.
8. Report G_within,O4 in capture-latency bands [0,24) and [24,72]; a band without events or paired predictions is unresolved.
9. Permute Q-model covariates within development-only baseline strata as a process-model diagnostic, not an exchangeability claim.

Supportive: both methods show a positive simultaneous lower bound for weighted and ordinary G_within,O4; the direction is present in the R28 pre-outcome arm; weighted and ordinary estimates are not separated by a selection-sensitive reversal; Q support/positivity and ESS pass; timing-only, Delta permutation, late-window, and process-control results do not reproduce the increment; and the result is not confined to one latency band. This supports only a stable-profile-adjusted, observation-robust record-level assay-content increment for O4.

Adverse: weighting removes the increment; R28 is null while only R42 is positive; Q is dominated by care-truncated or record-loss strata with poor support; the Q process or timing-only model matches full content; Delta permutation survives; O_planproxy_cap is positive; the effect is closure/latency-specific; or only one method/seed supports it. These results favor selection, workflow, stable severity, recording phenotype, or representation sensitivity, without identifying which unmeasured cause is responsible.

Inconclusive: Q unknowns, closure unknowns, or weight tails dominate; positivity/ESS fail; baseline-supported Delta or O4 support is inadequate; intervals are wide; R28 and R42 disagree without precision; or methods disagree. Do not rescue by changing windows, endpoint rules, strata, seeds, truncation, or gates. Continue only with better observation capture, assay calibration, or clinical adjudication.
