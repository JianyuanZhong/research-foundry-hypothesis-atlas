# Susceptibility-informed antimicrobial de-escalation after ICU culture results

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]` (`[starting question]`)
Dataset: MIMIC-IV 3.1, snapshot `[source checksum]`
Author role: Lead evolution. This proposal designs an executable study; it does not report study results.

## Scientific question and advance

After a pathogen and susceptibility results become available, clinicians often either narrow broad empiric coverage or continue it. Existing MIMIC evidence at a fixed day-4 landmark found antipseudomonal de-escalation not harmful and associated with lower mortality [K1], but that strategy was centered on elapsed time, not the actual moment susceptibility information became available. Reviews emphasize that de-escalation definitions, eligibility, timing, and safety evidence remain fragmented [K2]. Randomized biomarker-guided stopping can shorten antibiotic duration without a detectable mortality difference, but is not powered for mortality and does not model microbiological susceptibility [K3].

The unresolved question is therefore not whether broad-spectrum antibiotics should generally be stopped. It is whether de-escalation initiated within 24 hours of the first **available susceptibility result**, among patients already receiving at least one in-vitro active narrower agent, changes short-term clinical risk after conditioning on pre-result severity and organism/treatment context. The proposed adaptation advances [K1] by making information availability—not elapsed ICU day—the index, requiring an in-vitro susceptible target agent, and testing trajectory/state heterogeneity rather than reporting only an average effect.

The untested hypothesis is: **among eligible ICU patients, administration-confirmed narrowing of broad empiric coverage within 24 hours after first susceptibility availability is associated with lower 30-day all-cause mortality than continued broad coverage, with the association concentrated in patients with improving or stable organ-support trajectories.** This is an associational target-trial estimand, not proof of a causal mechanism.

## Target-trial emulation

- **Eligibility:** Adults aged at least 18 years on their first qualifying ICU stay; alive and hospitalized; suspected/confirmed bacterial infection based on a qualifying culture and broad empiric therapy; first organism plus antimicrobial susceptibility available during the stay. Use one first qualifying stay per `subject_id`.
- **Qualifying culture:** A non-null `hosp/microbiologyevents.org_name`, `ab_name`, and categorical `interpretation` from blood, respiratory, urine, or normally sterile fluid; culture collection (`charttime`) from ICU admission minus 24 hours through ICU day 7. Restrict to a single named organism within a `micro_specimen_id` to avoid ambiguous polymicrobial narrowing; record the excluded polymicrobial count.
- **Broad baseline therapy:** An anti-MRSA agent (e.g., vancomycin, linezolid, daptomycin) and/or antipseudomonal/resistant Gram-negative agent (e.g., piperacillin-tazobactam, cefepime, ceftazidime, aztreonam, meropenem, imipenem-cilastatin, aminoglycoside, ciprofloxacin/levofloxacin) ordered before susceptibility availability and active at that time. Oral non-absorbed vancomycin is not systemic anti-MRSA therapy.
- **Eligible narrow target:** At least one in-vitro susceptible, narrower systemic agent is identified among tested antibiotics. The mapping from `ab_name` to spectrum and to `prescriptions.drug`/`emar.medication` must be emitted as a versioned codebook; ambiguous synonyms are excluded and counted.
- **Time zero (`T0`):** First qualifying susceptibility `storetime`; if absent, `chartdate` at date resolution is a documented fallback. `storetime` is the operational availability time, not proof of clinician awareness.
- **Assignment window / exposure:** `T0` to `T0+24h`. De-escalation requires stopping or replacing at least one broad class with a susceptible narrower agent, without losing all active systemic coverage against the cultured organism. Continuation retains the broad class through the end of the window. Patients with death, ICU/hospital discharge, comfort-only documentation, or unclassifiable therapy before `T0+24h` are excluded before assignment. This is a landmark assignment strategy that avoids immortal-time bias.
- **Follow-up:** Starts at `T0+24h` and extends 30 days for the primary outcome. Secondary hospital outcomes extend to 7, 14, and 28 days. Alive discharge is censoring for time-to-event hospital outcomes; death is the event. The all-cause mortality outcome uses `hosp/patients.dod`, with date-boundary ties assigned conservatively to death.
- **Primary outcome:** All-cause death within 30 days after `T0+24h`, expressed as an overlap-weighted absolute risk difference and relative risk.
- **Secondary outcomes:** (1) KDIGO stage 2–3 creatinine-based AKI within 7 days among patients without chronic dialysis; (2) ICU-free and hospital-free days through day 14/28; (3) death-adjusted antibiotic-free days through day 14/28; (4) recurrent or persistent positive culture with a resistant organism within 14 days, interpreted cautiously because follow-up cultures are ordered selectively.
- **Exclusions:** Death or discharge before assignment; no eligible narrow agent; polymicrobial qualifying specimen; chronic dialysis; severe immunosuppression or long-course/specialized infections identified from `hosp/diagnoses_icd` and `hosp/procedures_icd` (endocarditis, osteomyelitis, CNS infection, necrotizing/prosthetic/burn infections, persistent bacteremia); comfort-measures-only before `T0`. Exclusion counts are reported in a flow diagram.
- **Estimand:** The average assignment-strategy effect in the overlap population of eligible patients, comparing a 24-hour de-escalation strategy with continuation, under a randomized trial with the same eligibility and no loss to clinical outcome follow-up.

## Baseline method

1. Construct one row per patient/qualifying stay and assignment-strategy group.
2. Estimate a propensity score for de-escalation with a prespecified logistic model using only pre-`T0` variables: age, sex, admission type, ICU care unit, culture site and organism, susceptibility profile, broad-therapy class/duration, Charlson comorbidity markers, infection exclusion flags, first ICU day, baseline SOFA-proxy components, vasopressor/ventilation/renal-replacement support, latest creatinine/bilirubin/platelets/lactate/white-cell count, and pre-T0 slopes over the prior 24–48 hours.
3. Apply overlap weights (`w=1-e` for treated, `w=e` for controls), trim only extreme weights prespecified as probability outside [0.01, 0.99], and report standardized mean differences before and after weighting.
4. Estimate 30-day absolute risk difference and relative risk with weighted logistic regression, with the exposure indicator and no post-baseline covariates. Use patient-level nonparametric bootstrap (2,000 replicates) for 95% confidence intervals; report cluster-robust standard errors as a sensitivity.
5. Pre-specify no claim if overlap is poor, weighted absolute standardized differences exceed 0.10 for critical clinical variables, or the confidence interval includes clinically important harm (risk difference > +1 percentage point).

This baseline directly addresses the average strategy comparison while keeping adjustment transparent and computable on CPU.

## Substantive alternative: cross-fitted dynamic-state model

The alternative tests whether the average comparison obscures clinically meaningful heterogeneity across evolving patient states.

- **Inputs:** Only observations from ICU admission through `T0`: SOFA-proxy components and time-delta summaries (`labevents` creatinine, bilirubin, platelets, lactate, white-cell count; `chartevents` MAP, urine output, vasopressor dose, ventilation/renal replacement/GCS flags), antimicrobial classes, organism/susceptibility tokens, and baseline demographics/support.
- **Fitting target:** A discrete-time hazard model for the 30-day outcome and an auxiliary calibration model for residual 7-day organ worsening. Use gradient-boosted trees or a small regularized neural sequence model as a super-learner candidate; the selected architecture must be fixed before outcome analysis. CPU is expected to suffice. If a future solver uses a neural model, request one A100 only if measured pilot CPU time exceeds the planning envelope.
- **Split:** Patient-level 70/15/15 train/calibration/test, stratified by exposure and 30-day death. No post-`T0` outcome data enters feature fitting. Refit nuisance models on training folds during cross-fitted effect estimation.
- **Evaluation:** Report test AUROC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, and overlap by decile. Then estimate the average effect and exposure-by-predicted-risk or exposure-by-organ-state interaction using cross-fitted doubly robust/AIPW estimates on the held-out and full analysis sets. Report bootstrap confidence intervals.
- **Scientific addition:** The baseline can estimate an average contrast but may average together safe de-escalators and high-risk patients. The dynamic-state alternative asks whether observed information about trajectory changes the effect estimate and uncertainty. It can reveal whether apparent benefit is concentrated in improving/stable patients or persists after nonlinear severity adjustment.
- **Limitation retained:** Even a well-calibrated learned model cannot adjust for unrecorded source control, clinician concern, or actual awareness of results. Improved prediction is not evidence of causation.

Alternative not selected for the primary method: a full marginal structural model with time-varying treatment after `T0`. It could handle later switching but requires stronger modeling of subsequent treatment and censoring and changes the estimand from the initial 24-hour strategy. It should be revisited if >10% of patients switch strategies after assignment.

## Confounding, selection, and falsification diagnostics

- **Confounding by indication:** compare baseline severity, organ trajectory, organism, specimen, and susceptibility; present weighted balance and covariate-stratified estimates. If de-escalators are much less sick and residual imbalance remains, treat any protective association as hypothesis-generating.
- **Information/timing artifact:** primary `T0=storetime`; sensitivity analyses use `charttime` and a 48-hour assignment window. Quantify results with unavailable `storetime` and document the fallback.
- **Administration/order mismatch:** primary exposure uses `emar` records indicating administration; sensitivity uses `prescriptions` orders only. Report discordance and stop/restart rates.
- **Selection:** flow counts for death/discharge before assignment; compare included/excluded baseline variables. For hospital time-to-event outcomes, use inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting after alive discharge.
- **Unmeasured source control/clinician concern:** use proxies (drainage/procedure codes, ICU day, severity trajectories), compute E-values, and perform a sensitivity grid for an unmeasured binary confounder. These are diagnostics, not proof.
- **Negative-control checks:** (1) a pre-specified pre-`T0` outcome should not be affected by post-`T0` exposure; (2) a clinically implausible exposure variant (narrowing unrelated to the cultured organism) should be null if classification is not capturing prognosis; (3) an omitted-sensitivity analysis excluding patients with a source-control procedure should test whether the association is driven by source control.
- **Multiplicity:** primary outcome is the sole confirmatory estimate. Secondary outcomes and interaction tests are labeled exploratory and controlled for false discovery rate if reported.

## Interpretation

- **Supportive:** If de-escalation shows lower 30-day mortality with a confidence interval excluding zero and all overlap/balance criteria pass, the strongest supported claim is an association in this overlap cohort under the operational definition. It can inform selection of patients for prospective stewardship evaluation, not a recommendation to de-escalate.
- **Adverse:** If the point estimate favors continuation, especially with a lower confidence bound showing harm, the study would challenge automatic susceptibility-triggered narrowing and identify a high-risk state for safer decision support. It would not prove that continuing broad therapy is beneficial.
- **Inconclusive:** If confidence intervals include clinically important benefit and harm, overlap is poor, or exposure classification discordance is high, the study would not resolve the question. A precise null would only support the bounded operational claim that average 30-day mortality is not materially different; it would not establish safety for subgroups or long-term resistance outcomes.
- **Claims requiring clinical adjudication or other data:** actual clinician awareness of the report; appropriateness of an in-vitro susceptible narrow agent given source, penetration, allergies, renal dosing, and toxicity; source-control adequacy; goals of care; and organism-level clinical significance. External multicenter validation and a prospective stewardship trial are required for a practice-changing causal or safety claim.
- **Computationally checkable claims:** cohort counts, exposure windows, outcome timing, mortality, adjusted estimates, confidence intervals, balance, and classification discordance. Claims about clinical appropriateness, causality, and real-time decision safety are not automatically checkable.
