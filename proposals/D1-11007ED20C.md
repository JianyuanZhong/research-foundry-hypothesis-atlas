# Informative post-classification observation can be audited, but does not rescue the adverse multicenter fitness result

## Substantive repair and clinical question

This proposal is a substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. The parents established that (i) exhaustive outcome-blind expansion of the exported Ventilation vocabulary adds only a handful of single-hospital rows and worsens bounded broad-deployment prediction, and (ii) zeros reflect a mixed exported interface footprint rather than a single absent-opportunity mechanism. Both analyses nevertheless made their main prediction comparison after restricting to rows with a complete later respiratory-chart outcome window. The unresolved selection question is whether early ICU exit, ICU death, or respiratory documentation loss is informative with respect to pre-landmark state, generic care-plan process, hospital, and receipt, such that complete-case restriction could reverse the fitness-for-use conclusion.

The falsifiable hypothesis is: among all classification-complete eligible rows, post-freeze outcome completeness is sufficiently associated with the pre-landmark baseline, generic care-plan process, hospital, and outcome-blind receipt that unweighted complete-case semantic incremental prediction is materially distorted; after stabilized/truncated inverse-probability-of-completeness weighting and bounded pattern-mixture/composite-state analyses, the receipt comparison changes enough to cross the inherited adverse gate. A competing hypothesis is that observation is nearly ignorable for the available covariates and the adverse result persists. A null result is clinically useful: it supports treating the earlier complete-case finding as robust to measured observation selection, while still not proving missingness at random or validating the labels.

The clinical importance is measurement safety. If missing later chart windows disproportionately occur after ICU death, discharge, or sparse documentation, an apparently weak care-plan predictor could be an artifact of ascertainment. Conversely, if weighting cannot rescue performance, this prevents unobserved-outcome correction from becoming a post hoc route to clinical or quality claims. The strongest evidence already supported is only about exported structured-documentation coverage and prediction of a later respiratory-chart proxy. This experiment tests missing-outcome selection and semantic incremental prediction; it does not test actual ventilation, extubation, SBT, readiness, care quality, benefit, harm, or causality.

## Exact estimand and analysis

The primary population is every classification-complete `full_window` row before `complete_outcome_window` restriction, analyzed separately by airway definition and pooled only with definition indicators when explicitly labeled. The primary estimand is the hospital-held-out difference in log loss and Brier score between a pre-landmark baseline model and the same model plus outcome-blind care-plan receipt. The baseline contains landmark and only pre-landmark respiratory-chart summaries: `pre_chart_n`, `pre_distinct_labels`, `pre_robust_n`, latest valid `pre_fio2`, `pre_peep`, `pre_pressure_support`, and `pre_vent_rate`. Generic care-plan process is represented by classification-window counts/presence from `carePlanGeneral` (`any carePlanGeneral`, count, distinct groups, any Ventilation group, Ventilation count, distinct Ventilation values); it is process documentation, not treatment. Receipt is added as a separate semantic feature, never used for eligibility.

First compare complete-case unweighted fits with stabilized/truncated IPW fits. Completeness `C` is the inherited `complete_outcome_window` indicator, with `Y=robust_reduction` required for observed outcome scoring. The selection model estimates `P(C=1 | baseline, generic process, hospital)` without receipt and without any post-outcome variable; hospital-held-out predictions are used where both completeness classes occur. Weights are stabilized by the marginal completeness probability and truncated at the prespecified 99th percentile, with minimum predicted probability 0.02. Report hospital-specific positivity, minimum/maximum predicted probabilities, effective sample size, weight quantiles, and calibration plots/tables by hospital. A model with receipt in the selection equation is secondary and diagnostic only: if its inclusion materially changes weights, this is evidence of informative ascertainment rather than permission to claim MAR.

For each weighting scheme, fit baseline and baseline-plus-receipt logistic models within every leave-one-hospital-out fold and score only observed outcomes in the held-out hospital. Report micro row-weighted and macro equal-hospital log loss/Brier, AUROC only when both classes exist, calibration slope/intercept or observed-versus-predicted deciles, receipt-stratified results, and the paired incremental delta. Models are refit within every fold, with median imputation, missing indicators, standardization, and no hospital feature. Repeated landmarks remain clustered by `sid`; uncertainty resamples complete stays (all landmarks for a sampled `sid` move together) within each held-out hospital. If a hospital has no positivity for an outcome or no variation in completeness, it is retained descriptively but excluded from that fold's estimand with an explicit reason.

Because MAR is not established, do not report IPW as a corrected truth. Use two sensitivity families. First, bounded pattern-mixture analysis assigns each missing `Y` either the observed minimum or observed maximum within the corresponding airway definition and receipt stratum, and reports the full interval for receipt-specific prevalence, risk difference, log-loss-relevant prevalence, and any gate quantity. Second, use a multinomial composite-state outcome with mutually exclusive `chart_observed`, `icu_discharge_exit`, `icu_death_exit`, and `documentation_loss`; the composite is descriptive and does not rank discharge above death or documentation loss. A competing-state model may predict state from baseline plus generic process, but it must not convert discharge into favorable outcome or infer bedside status. If component-level chart outcome is partially observed, report componentwise unknown bounds rather than coding missing components as no reduction.

## Falsification gates

Supportive evidence for informative selection capable of changing the prior conclusion requires all of: (1) hospital-specific completeness or positivity/calibration failures that are not resolved by generic process controls; (2) a prespecified clinically/materially sized change, at least 5 percentage points in an outcome probability or at least 0.01 absolute log-loss/Brier change, between complete-case and IPW receipt contrasts; and (3) the receipt-versus-baseline direction crosses the inherited adverse/supportive gate in unbounded and at least two bounded-airway definitions under stay-clustered hospital-held-out uncertainty. Even then, the result supports only an ascertainment-sensitive documentation association and requires direct clinical adjudication before any bedside use.

Adverse evidence is a stable near-uniform completeness model, adequate hospital positivity, calibrated selection predictions, ESS near the observed count, complete-case/IPW deltas below the materiality threshold, and pattern-mixture bounds that remain adverse to receipt. Inconclusive evidence is sparse hospital cells, extreme weights, failed overlap, wide pattern-mixture intervals, or disagreement among definitions. A null/adverse finding is valuable and does not imply that missingness is formally MAR.

## Executed scans and current results

The executable `[internal dataset path]` scanned all 3,115,018 `carePlanGeneral` rows, 20,168,176 `respiratoryCharting` rows, and the full `patient` table. It retained 5,507 care-plan rows in inherited classification windows and 11,404 care-plan rows in later windows, and 119,934 respiratory-chart rows in classification windows and 220,982 later rows for the frozen stay-landmark keys. Across the 22,421 inherited rows, the mutually exclusive ascertainment audit found 20,129 rows with later respiratory-chart support, 1,420 documentation-loss rows, 525 ICU-discharge-exit rows, and 347 ICU-death-exit rows. These counts are observation states, not favorable or unfavorable clinical outcomes.

Among the 22,121 `full_window` rows, 21,549 had a complete inherited observation window and 572 did not. `robust_reduction` was nonmissing in 20,009 rows overall; the executed complete-case prediction score used 19,606 rows after requiring both the inherited completeness flag and nonmissing outcome. A baseline-only completeness model using only `landmark`, `pre_chart_n`, `pre_distinct_labels`, `pre_robust_n`, `pre_fio2`, `pre_peep`, `pre_pressure_support`, and `pre_vent_rate` had marginal completeness 0.9741, hospital-held-out predicted probabilities from 0.9272 to 0.9800, stabilized/truncated observed-row weight median 0.9984, and 99th percentile 1.0308. Effective sample size was 19,629.6 in the separate audit, indicating no practical weight explosion under measured baseline variables; this does not validate MAR.

The preliminary hospital-held-out semantic prediction comparison used all 77 observed hospitals with at least five held-out rows and adequate training variation. Complete-case baseline log loss/Brier were 0.54959/0.18258; adding `target_receipt` yielded 0.54974/0.18263. Stabilized/truncated IPW baseline log loss/Brier were 0.54874/0.18221; adding receipt yielded 0.54889/0.18226. Thus receipt worsened log loss by 0.00015 and Brier by 0.00005 under both complete-case and IPW scoring. The observed change is far below the materiality gate and does not reverse the inherited adverse fitness interpretation. It is not evidence of harm and does not prove that receipt has no clinical meaning.

## Interpretation and remaining uncertainty

The current result is adverse to the claim that measured informative outcome observation can rescue receipt as a transportable structured predictor: selection probabilities are not extreme, IPW barely changes the estimate, and receipt remains directionally worse under both weighting schemes. The result is not a proof of missingness at random because unmeasured workflow, severity, and undocumented care can affect observation. The pattern-mixture and composite-state bounds remain essential when MAR is unsupported; their worst/best assignments are sensitivity bounds, not estimates.

The computation can verify source hashes, all-row scans, frozen joins/windows, pre-freeze selection features, receipt-blind eligibility, state exclusivity, weight diagnostics, LOHO separation, and stay-clustered resampling. It cannot establish that a chart row means invasive ventilation, that discharge is success, that a care-plan value denotes an evaluation/SBT/extubation decision, or that any association is causal. Stronger conclusions require direct device and airway event data, local interface and workflow metadata, blinded clinical adjudication, and prospective external validation. Hospital-held-out uncertainty covers the fixed observed hospitals; it does not establish transport to unseen institutions.
