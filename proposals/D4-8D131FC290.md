## Scientific opening, evidence, and unresolved claim

The strongest inspected evidence supports three limited claims. Large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, this difference was associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes, so a combined equation can lose prognostic information [K2]. Administrative AKI coding can be insensitive and subgroup-dependent relative to serum-creatinine-defined AKI [K3]. These findings do not establish that equal-combined-eGFR negative discordance predicts biological AKI, that UKB first-appearance dates measure contacts, or that N17 and broad code thresholds are biologically exchangeable.

The unresolved hypothesis is: among supported P− and P0 profiles at the same combined eGFR, P− has a larger 10-year contrast for first recorded N17 than for first recorded N18 and than for three profile-blind, incidence-commensurate nonrenal first-appearance summaries—total accumulation B30, discovery-date frequency F15, and high-density bundles H10—after measured pre-index first-appearance propensity is balanced.

The leading interpretation is recorded acute-renal selectivity. Its strongest rival has two observably different parts:

1. Stable severity/contact/recording propensity should persist from pre-index history, increase distinct discovery dates, and produce positive P−/P0 contrasts for F15 and B30 as well as renal outcomes.
2. Conditional high-density coding should increase H10 and concentrate any N17 contrast on N17 dates with at least 10 newly appearing nonrenal groups, potentially without increasing F15.
3. Healthcare contacts, admission severity, testing, disease onset, coding intent, and export completeness are absent. The available data cannot distinguish those latent causes from morbidity or coding.

The advance is a falsifiable discrimination among measured first-appearance patterns. Even fully supportive results mean only “recorded N17 selectivity beyond measured pre-index discovery frequency and conditional bundle density”; encounter- and laboratory-adjudicated validation remains necessary.

Let V be valid non-N17/N18 three-character groups from paired all-position fields. A post-S discovery is a V group whose first date is after S; groups sharing a date form a bundle.

Outcomes are:

- `F15`: first passage to 15 distinct post-S discovery dates.

## Simpler substantive alternative: correlated date/mark process

The baseline endpoints reduce trajectories to threshold crossings. Fit a two-part joint model that directly targets the rival while retaining interpretability:

1. A yearly piecewise-exponential recurrent discovery-date process with participant random effect `u_i`.
2. Conditional on a discovery date, a zero-truncated negative-binomial bundle-size mark model with participant random effect `v_i`.
3. Estimate `corr(u_i,v_i)`; model death as absorbing.
4. Fit N17 and N18 competing first-transition hazards with freely estimated loadings on pre-index-inferred `u_i/v_i`, baseline covariates, and the G/delta profile surface.

Infer persistence using only pre-index history: fit population parameters in training, use [index−10,index−5) to predict [index−5,index), then infer participant effects at S from all [index−10,index). Never update effects with post-S dates, bundles, renal outcomes, or death for estimand calculation.

Compare four prespecified variants: fixed covariates only, intensity-only, mark-only, and correlated two-factor. Train on 70%, calibrate/select on 15%, and evaluate once on untouched 15%. Report yearly discovery-date counts and zero counts, bundle mean/variance/quantiles/tail, B30/F15/H10 CIF calibration and Brier scores, N17/N18/death calibration, joint deviance, factor correlation, early-to-late transport, and bootstrap stability.

Retain a learned result only if every sufficiently supported binary endpoint has |calibration intercept|<=0.10 and slope 0.8–1.2; observed/expected yearly counts are 0.8–1.25 in at least 8/10 years; 90th bundle-quantile error is <=1 group or <=20%; and no Brier score is worse than the transparent model by >0.01. Otherwise it is diagnostic failure.

Code-composition NMF is deferred because it is not needed to separate frequency from density and may absorb severity/code salience in an uninterpretable way. Revisit only if the frozen two-factor model fails a held-out process target and training/validation NMF improves at least one failed target without worsening another by >0.01 Brier or >5% deviance. A transformer remains deferred because only first appearances are available. A latent true-GFR model remains deferred without measured GFR or repeated-marker error data.

The two-factor model can show whether stable measured date intensity or conditional density explains multiple endpoints. It cannot label `u_i` as contact, `v_i` as admission intensity, or a residual N17 contrast as biological AKI. Conditioning on pre-index effects changes the contrast and may block morbidity pathways; call it a conditional diagnostic, not a causal direct effect.

## Falsification and interpretation

All interpretation requires verified C/field semantics, clinical code review, support/matching, per-profile event gates, convergence/calibration, >=90% bootstrap success, joint 95% intervals, and no material principal/tie or cause-specific/CIF reversal.

Supportive for bounded recorded-N17 selectivity requires:

- lower bounds of theta17 and D >0;
- matched/principal direction agreement;
- lower bounds of QK, QF, and QH >0;
- lower bound of `theta17-low−thetaH` >0 and upper bound of Cbundle < log(1.25);
- a calibrated two-factor test result retaining a positive conditional N17 contrast without an equally large N18 or broad-process contrast.

This supports external adjudication only and does not exclude unmeasured contact or renal-code salience.

Adverse/stable-propensity evidence is thetaF and thetaK positive with upper bounds of QF and QK <=0; or adequate pre-index transport plus intensity/two-factor attenuation of the N17 advantage to a precise clinically small interval (upper bound <=log 1.25) across renal and broad outcomes. This is compatible with stable morbidity, contact, or capture.

Adverse/high-density evidence is thetaH positive with QH upper bound <=0 and Cbundle lower bound >log(1.25), especially when thetaF is near null with an interval excluding RR 1.25. This is compatible with admission/coding intensity or clustered disease presentation, not proof of either.

D upper bound <=0 is adverse to acute-versus-chronic renal selectivity. Both renal theta upper bounds <=0 are adverse to both profile claims. Death-driven reversal is inconclusive.

Inconclusive includes unverified C; support/matching failure; any B30/F15/H10 profile-cell failure; sparse low/high N17 context; intervals spanning material alternatives; failed pre-index transport; process miscalibration/instability; <90% bootstrap success; method disagreement without a calibrated pathway; or principal/tie reversal. An imprecise null does not refute a rival.
