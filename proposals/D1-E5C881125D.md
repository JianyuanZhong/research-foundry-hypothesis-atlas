# Day-42 landmark HCC child: assay payload versus care process, with an irregular-event audit

Status: delivery-ready proposed child of [prior hypothesis]. No fitted model, held-out metric, or clinical conclusion is claimed. Counts described as source audits are inherited feasibility observations and must be re-audited by the solver before fitting.

## 1. Scientific opening, claim boundary, and clinical importance

The unresolved question is whether a recorded post-TACE assay signal contains information beyond the process that produced the record. A patient can have a laboratory value because of current liver/tumour state, because clinicians scheduled a review or planned retreatment, because the patient is more severely ill, or because this institution observed them more intensively. A repeat TACE code is clinically consequential, but in this snapshot it is an operational care event: it does not identify treatment intent, radiologic viable tumour, progression, treatment failure, or benefit.

The closest inspected clinical evidence supports a narrow opening, not the target claim. Liu et al. [K1] report retrospective prognostic associations for pre-TACE F-NLR, AFP and prealbumin, but do not separate assay state from measurement opportunity or test a later exact-code repeat-TACE endpoint. Tsurusaki et al. [K2] establish why HCC treatment effect requires imaging concepts such as necrosis/viable enhancement and why mRECIST/RECICL have practical measurement issues; the HCC files have no adjudicated imaging response labels. Gu et al. [K3] describe heterogeneous, lagging and assay-dependent biomarker surveillance and argue for adjunctive rather than standalone risk signals. These works support plausibility and limitations, not this proposal's result.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among patients reaching a day-42 landmark after a first dated locally recorded exact-code TACE without an early exact-code repeat, the recorded assay payload (numeric value, qualitative result and inequality/censoring flag) adds reproducible held-out information about the hazard of a later exact-code TACE during days 43–180 beyond baseline history and the observation/scheduling process. A compact irregular-event model may expose transient ordering/interactions that fixed window summaries lose, but only if the increment survives process/timestamp/value falsification controls and is not a small uncalibrated performance gain.

The primary estimand is descriptive/predictive, not causal:

- Population: eligible patients at day 42, not all TACE patients and not patients selected by future 180-day completeness.
- Event: first later unique patient/start-time exact-code procedure with normalized 手术 == "tace" and 43 <= (procedure start - index start) in elapsed days < 181.
- Risk: discrete-time cause-free hazard for that operational event through day 180, with right censoring at the last recorded observation.
- Primary contrasts: assay-payload increment over process plus baseline; process increment over baseline; and whether the learned time-aware model reveals an ordering/interactions increment beyond the transparent summary.
- Clinical decision changed by resolution: whether to invest in a linked imaging/intent/measurement-validation study of a day-42 risk-stratification workflow, or abandon a state-triggered direction and study care-process mechanisms instead.

A supportive result establishes only incremental information in recorded assay payload for an operational repeat-TACE record. It does not establish a biological liver state, tumour response, progression, repeat-treatment need, treatment effect, or mechanism. An adverse result can be clinically useful if it shows that the apparent signal is explained by observation/scheduling process. An inconclusive result is not a biological refutation.

## 2. Leading explanation and substantive rival

Leading state explanation: after accounting for pre-index history and how intensely the patient is observed, assay-specific values/result flags available by day 42 carry information about later repeat-TACE hazard. Its prediction is a payload increment in both the transparent and learned models, stable across temporal holdout and assay/censoring sensitivities, while process/timestamp permutations do not reproduce it.

Strongest rival, care-process/severity explanation: baseline severity, planned retreatment, access, scheduling, or measurement intensity generates both the assay record and the repeat procedure. Its prediction is that the apparent increment is explained by process features, disappears when assay payload is shuffled while timing/counts are preserved, or is reproduced when timestamps/process marks are permuted. A third possibility is mixed: state payload and process timing are entangled in routine care, so the study can support incremental recorded-signal utility but cannot identify a biological mechanism.

The design deliberately does not treat adjustment as proof of mechanism. In particular, the presence and timestamp of a lab are themselves process variables. The strict state claim is therefore the value/result-payload increment at matched process information; a time-aware state-plus-process gain is labelled a combined recorded-signal/process representation unless the controls separate it.

## 3. Exact landmark population and endpoint

Use the HCC snapshot [source checksum] only.

1. Read procedures and trim whitespace then Unicode case-fold only the 手术 field. The primary procedure code is exactly "tace"; Chinese/combined procedure strings and broad substring matches are not silently merged.
2. Require nonmissing 患者主索引, 就诊号, and procedure 开始时间. Select the earliest dated strict-code start per patient in the available snapshot, collapse exact same patient/start duplicates for event counting, and retain a duplicate audit. This is first dated exact-code TACE in this snapshot, not first-ever TACE.
3. Inner-join the index to encounters on (患者主索引, 就诊号) and report unmatched rows. A strict TACE row with missing start is not an event and is never evidence of a negative.
4. Flag any patient with an undated strict TACE anywhere in the available procedure history. Primary analysis may retain that patient as a first-dated index with an unresolved-order flag; the pre-specified history sensitivity excludes any patient with an undated strict TACE. No claim about prior treatment order is made.
5. Exclude a patient from the day-42 risk set if any later unique-start strict TACE occurs in 0 < delta_days <= 42. Report early repeats separately; they are not negatives for the later-repeat estimand. The index row is an anchor, not a predictor.
6. Require all-source obs_end >= index_start + 42 days for landmark eligibility. Patients without recorded observation through day 42 are not biological negatives and are outside this decision landmark.
7. Follow eligible patients from day 42 to the earlier of a later exact-code TACE or obs_end, with the event interval [43,181) and exact elapsed timestamps, not date rounding. Use 20 fixed seven-day bins [43,50), [50,57), ..., [176,181); the final bin is truncated at day 181. No repeat event and obs_end < index+181 is censored, not a negative. A no-event record with obs_end >= index+181 is event-free through day 180.

The primary analysis is a pooled-logistic discrete-time hazard with IPCW for observation censoring, fit inside development folds. A complete-180-day known-status analysis is a sensitivity only, never the primary population.

## 4. Three disjoint information channels

All feature materialization is frozen at the day-42 landmark. The solver must emit a row-level leakage report proving that no feature timestamp is after index_start + 42 days and no outcome flag, obs_end, future visit count, future label, file order, or post-landmark field entered predictors.

### H: baseline history

H contains only information available before the index:

- encounters in [-365,0) and index-linked age 年龄, sex 性别, department 就诊科室, encounter count, admission/discharge durations, and gaps from 就诊时间, 入院时间, 出院时间;
- pre-index procedure counts by exact normalized label from 开始时间 (and a separate strict-TACE history audit);
- pre-index diagnosis dictionary counts joined to encounter keys, with no diagnosis time invented;
- pre-index exact-label assay summaries and directions, using only lab rows in [-365,0).

No names, identity numbers, phone numbers, insurance numbers or hospital numbers are predictors. H is the baseline/history comparison for both model families.

### S: recorded assay payload

S contains response-window assay payload from 0 < delta_days <= 42 for a frozen exact-label codebook. The minimum audited labels are 甲胎蛋白, 白蛋白, 前白蛋白, 总胆红素, 直接胆红素, 丙氨酸氨基转移酶, 血小板, and 血小板计数. Add another exact raw label only if its development-only codebook audit records frequency, patient coverage, time coverage, numeric/qualitative/censored fractions, and calendar stability before test evaluation.

For each exact label retain:

- ordinary numeric 定量结果 only when parseable;
- 定性结果 as a separate recorded-result category;
- inequality/censoring markers and blank/invalid result flags as separate payload fields;
- assay label and elapsed time from index for the time-aware representation;
- assay-specific changes only when both a valid pre-index value in [-365,0) and a response value in (0,42] exist.

There is no lab units or reference-range column. Do not convert units, pool labels, apply clinical thresholds, call these values liver function or tumour burden, or turn >60500 into an exact number. Keep albumin/prealbumin, total/direct bilirubin, and platelet/platelet count separate. Fold-fitted imputation and standardization are permitted only inside development folds.

For the strict channel comparison, the S mask supplies payload fields but not counts of encounters/orders, missingness rates, or request/opportunity indicators. This does not magically remove selection: a recorded payload requires a measurement. That remaining ambiguity is tested by process-preserving value shuffles and timestamp destruction, and is stated in the conclusion.

### P: observation and scheduling process

P contains only response-window opportunity/process information from (0,42]:

- encounter counts, observed-day counts and inter-event gaps from encounters 就诊时间, 入院时间, 出院时间;
- examination counts and gaps, exact 检查 categories and examination timing;
- lab request/availability counts by exact 检验 label and gaps, but no 定量结果 or 定性结果;
- non-drug order counts, exact order categories/status/frequency and timing from 开立时间, 开始时间, 结束时间;
- medication counts/classes and timing from 开始时间, 结束时间.

No post-index procedure token is a predictor, including early TACE or any other procedure. The endpoint procedure table is read after feature materialization. P is not a measure of treatment intent; it is a recorded opportunity/scheduling proxy. A sensitivity permits the process stream to include non-TACE procedure categories only if they are materialized before the endpoint and the leakage report demonstrates that no exact repeat-TACE row entered predictors; this sensitivity cannot replace the strict primary exclusion.

The baseline comparisons are H, H+S, H+P, and H+S+P. The primary state increment is H+S+P versus H+P, with H+S versus H as a complementary payload comparison. This makes the state, process and baseline roles explicit rather than allowing missingness to silently stand in for physiology.

## 5. Transparent baseline and learned alternative

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted, patient-held-out estimates of the four channel contrasts, censoring-aware risk predictions through day 180, calibration/uncertainty, and falsification outputs. No result is pre-supplied.

### Auditable hazard baseline

Fit a fold-preprocessed elastic-net pooled-logistic discrete-time hazard model on the 20 landmark intervals. Use the same H/S/P masks and same split for every contrast. Numeric scaling, imputation, categorical encoding, codebook additions and IPCW models are fit within development folds only. Use fixed regularization choices decided by grouped cross-validation inside development; record them before test evaluation.

The baseline's value is auditability: each increment maps to a known summary, its censoring weight and its held-out interval prediction. Its limitation is that a count, last value, slope, minimum/maximum and coarse gap summary may lose a short-lived reversal, cross-assay ordering, or interaction between a value and a nearby observation/scheduling event.

### Compact irregular-event learned alternative

Fit a small gated sequence model (GRU or equivalent) with the same patient split, folds, target bins, masks, censoring target and IPCW evaluation. The sequence window is [-365,+42] days. Each token contains only fields allowed by its mask:

- event category and exact assay label/category;
- numeric value after exact-label audit, qualitative-result and censor/inequality flags;
- elapsed days from index and time since prior token, with a missing-time token excluded rather than imputed;
- channel tag H, S or P;
- for H and P, only the corresponding event payload; for S, no encounter/order/availability counts.

Cap each patient's tokens prospectively (for example 512, retaining the most recent tokens plus all S tokens, with the rule frozen before test evaluation), report truncation, use at most two gated layers, hidden width 64–128, dropout and three fixed seeds. The output is the same 20-bin hazard. Train/fitting targets and all preprocessing remain the same as the transparent baseline.

The learned alternative could reveal a transient assay ordering or interaction—such as a short-lived payload change near a dense observation burst—that a fixed summary loses. It may instead learn process timing or severity. Therefore:

- A learned gain over the transparent model alone is not clinical value.
- The learned state claim requires H+P+S to beat H+P on weighted loss/Brier with calibration, and the gain to attenuate under within-patient timestamp permutation or same-assay value shuffling in the specified direction.
- If the learned model adds no stable information beyond the transparent model, defer it and retain the transparent model as the scientifically adequate deliverable.
- If it improves only when process/timestamp information is available, label the finding process/timing utility, not state biology.

### Split and censoring

Freeze one temporal patient-level split by index date: earliest 80% of eligible index dates for development and latest 20% for untouched test. No patient appears in both. Use five grouped development folds for all codebook, preprocessing, hyperparameter, imputation and censoring choices. A stratified random patient split is a secondary transport check only.

For each interval, censor at the first time the patient has no further recorded observation by obs_end, or at the endpoint event. Fit a pooled-logistic censoring model inside each training fold using H plus process history available up to that interval; a sensitivity adds the landmark assay payload. Truncate extreme weights only at a pre-registered rule such as the 1st/99th development-fold percentiles, report the rule, positivity, weight distribution and effective sample size. Do not use future records to make a patient event-free.

Primary metrics on the untouched temporal test are IPCW interval log loss summed over the risk set and IPCW Brier risk by day 180; calibration slope/intercept and observed-versus-predicted risk are co-primary for decision usefulness. AUROC/AUPRC are secondary and cannot establish value. Use paired patient bootstrap intervals for increments and calibration, preserving the fixed test predictions. For the sequence, report the three fixed-seed range rather than selecting the best seed.

## 6. Falsification and negative controls

Run the same folds, split, masks, censoring weights and held-out metrics for:

1. Within-patient permutation of response-window timestamps, preserving assay/result multiset and counts. A state increment that remains unchanged is compatible with a window payload or process-count explanation, not transient timing.
2. Within-development-fold shuffling of P process marks across patients matched on index-calendar block and process-count strata, without moving assay payload. If this reproduces the S increment, the process rival remains credible.
3. Same-assay S-value shuffling across patients within development folds, preserving timing and process counts. This tests whether patient-specific payload carries information beyond collection structure.
4. A capacity-matched P-only model for both model families. A larger model must not win by parameter count.
5. Development outcome-label permutation with true-label untouched test evaluation as a leakage/sanity control.
6. Exact-code sensitivity using a frozen high-specificity codebook and undated-history exclusion; observation-end sensitivity using encounters-only and all-source definitions, reported as design sensitivities rather than relabelled biology.
7. A peri-index timing sensitivity that adds same-timestamp rows (delta_days == 0) to S/P. The primary excludes them because within-day order is unavailable; a materially different result is an unresolved timing artifact.

A result is adverse to the state interpretation if the state increment disappears after P, is reproduced by the P/timestamp controls, is driven by censored/qualitative handling, or is unstable under exact-code/undated/observation sensitivities. A result is adverse to the learned alternative if it has no calibrated held-out improvement, only a seed-specific gain, or no reproducible ordering/interactions beyond transparent summaries. A label-permutation gain is a leakage failure and invalidates the package.

## 7. Supportive, adverse and inconclusive outcomes

Supportive evidence requires all of the following, decided before test inspection:

- at least 50 observed later repeat events in the landmark analysis as a precision screen;
- completed procedure, observation, assay-label/calendar and leakage audits;
- state payload improves over H+P in held-out IPCW log loss and Brier with a paired 95% interval excluding no improvement and acceptable calibration;
- direction is stable across the temporal test, random-split transport check, three sequence seeds, exact-code sensitivity, undated-history sensitivity and observation definition;
- no comparable gain from process/timestamp/value permutations;
- if the learned model is claimed to add information lost by summaries, the learned H+S+P gain over H+P must be reproducible and its order-sensitive increment must exceed the corresponding timestamp-destroyed control under the pre-specified uncertainty rule.

This supports a next-step linked imaging/intent/assay-validation study. It does not justify a treatment rule.

Adverse evidence, with adequate event and censoring precision, is a null state increment after P, a process-only or permutation gain matching it, poor calibration, or strong sensitivity instability. This supports abandoning a state-triggered validation direction and prioritizing measurement/scheduling process characterization.

Inconclusive evidence is fewer than 50 events, too few day-42 landmarks, unstable exact-label/calendar scales, unsupported censoring weights or effective sample size, wide intervals, severe truncation, or incomplete learned fitting. An imprecise null is not biological refutation. If the learned fit exceeds the approved envelope, complete H and S/P transparent hazard models and controls, label the learned comparison incomplete, and do not change the estimand.

## 9. Evidence limits and what would be needed next

Unavailable or unvalidated evidence includes treatment intent (planned versus rescue), outside-hospital procedures, death/transplant ascertainment, radiologic viable enhancement/necrosis, mRECIST/RECICL/LI-RADS adjudication, tumour burden/stage, validated laboratory units/reference ranges, TACE technical details, adherence and clinician scheduling rationale. The local diagnoses lack time; clinical documents/pathology lack usable event dates; vitals and transfers carry no measurements. Examination text is not a validated response label.

A positive computation requires expert imaging and treatment-intent review, unit/reference-range validation, and independent temporal or external validation before any clinical workflow claim. A process-dominant or inconclusive computation still informs whether that validation is worth doing. No automatic verifier can adjudicate tumour response, mechanism, causality, clinical benefit, or outside-care completeness.

## 10. Compute budget and alternatives not chosen

Discovery budgets and future solver planning are separate. The configured discovery limit is 9,000 science seconds with at most two concurrent GPU slots. The future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 8 GPUs, 262,144 MiB memory and 28,800 seconds; this is a planning envelope, not a promise of allocation. The current compute limits must not be used to shrink the scientific question.

Measured versus unverified estimates:

- Exact procedure/encounter audits are inherited bounded feasibility observations; the full 2.2-GB labs scan, full feature materialization, model fitting and bootstrap remain uncomputed in this proposal.
- Use chunked CSV reads/checkpoints with 8–16 CPUs and 64–128 GiB for labs/examinations/orders; write only derived files to the workspace.
- Elastic-net hazards and patient bootstrap are CPU-suitable.
- The compact GRU may use one allocated A100, explicitly on cuda:0 inside the allocation, for three seeds; it must not assume CUDA visibility in ordinary shell. It is also permitted to run on CPU if measured feasible. GPU use is not evidence of scientific value.
- Expected end-to-end materialization/fit time of roughly 2–6 hours is unverified and must be measured by the solver readiness/execution job. Preserve checkpoints and report actual resource use.

Alternatives considered at matching specificity:

1. Transparent elastic-net hazard (selected as the primary baseline): inputs H, S summaries and P summaries; fit target is the 20-bin IPCW hazard; temporal 80/20 holdout; metrics are weighted log loss, Brier, calibration and bootstrap; CPU, auditable and sufficient if no order-sensitive information is found.
2. Compact time-aware GRU (retained as a secondary scientific alternative): inputs H/S/P event tokens, exact assay payload, result flags, elapsed time/gaps and channel masks; same target/split/metrics, three fixed seeds; one A100 optional or CPU. It can reveal transient order/interactions, but is not preferred for interpretability and is retained only to test that substantive uncertainty.
3. Mechanistic liver/tumour state-space model (deferred): it would target a latent physiologic/tumour state and simulate trajectories, but units, reference ranges, imaging, treatment intent and adjudicated tumour burden are absent. Adding complexity would invent identifiability rather than resolve the question.
4. Larger transformer/multimodal model (deferred): the HCC source has no images and no temporally valid clinical-document/pathology stream; event support is limited and a larger model would not answer a new clinical uncertainty beyond the compact order test.

Revisit the learned alternative only if the audit finds enough landmark/event/censoring support, stable exact-label scales, and the transparent model leaves a plausible order-sensitive residual. Abandon or defer it if it adds only a small unstable predictive gain, fails permutation controls, or exceeds the envelope. This preserves the scientific deliverable while keeping complexity subordinate to the state-versus-process question.
