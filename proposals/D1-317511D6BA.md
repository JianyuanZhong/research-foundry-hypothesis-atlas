## Clinical opening and hypothesis

The parent’s outcome-blind hospital-reference (`H)) versus within-stay dynamic residual (`D)) decomposition narrows the observation-process explanation, but it does not yet answer the clinically important source question: does a dynamic observation opportunity predict a later *recorded respiratoryCare transition* specifically, or does it predict whatever documentation occurs when nursing and vital observation intensify?

The strongest claim supported by the inspected evidence is only that eICU links ICU-relative, multi-center source tables and hospital identifiers, while bedside monitoring is only partly archived for clinical documentation [K1]. Temporal EHR representations and structured temporal preprocessing are feasible precedents [K2, K3]. Neither establishes that a respiratoryCare row is treatment delivery or physiologic onset, or that a residual association is respiratory-specific.

The unresolved claim is:

> In adult first-ICU stays reaching minute 360 and beginning in the parent’s respiratoryCare-negative strata, does pre-360 dynamic respiratory observation opportunity add a reproducible association with a strict recorded respiratoryCare interval-start after generic nurse-charting, vital-observation, care-plan process, hospital-regime, physiology/state, selection, and timing controls—and does the same respiratory block fail to predict a strict non-respiratory nursing-documentation endpoint?

The falsifiable alternative is generic documentation/monitoring: the apparent respiratory association should be similar for a non-respiratory nurse-chart endpoint, be explained by nurse/vital/care-plan opportunity, or survive schedule-preserving source swaps that remove source identity. A positive result would support respiratory-specific *record provenance* only. It would not establish clinical deterioration, a delivered intervention, indication, mechanism, causality, or utility.

The substantive advance over the parent is a matched endpoint/source-swap experiment. The parent’s H/D decomposition asks where observation intensity comes from; this child asks whether the residual source identity carries information specific to the recorded respiratory transition after generic process controls. This can change the next study: stable respiratory specificity supports source/device adjudication, while generic replication redirects effort toward workflow and documentation calibration.

## Falsification, uncertainty and interpretation

Prespecify hospital-held-out folds and report each held-out hospital, starting stratum, EOL/process stratum and observation-density stratum. Use hospital-clustered uncertainty or a hospital bootstrap/influence interval, with at least five viable held-out hospitals required for a supportive transport claim. A pooled estimate from one hospital or one stratum cannot support specificity.

Falsifiers:

1. **Endpoint source swap.** Repeat the exact M0–M3 analysis with `N_start_doc`. Respiratory specificity requires a stable nonzero (Delta_R) for `R_start_doc` while (Delta_N) is materially smaller, uncertain or null after the same generic controls; similar (Delta_R) and (Delta_N) supports generic documentation.
2. **Generic-process adjustment.** Compare raw parent observation blocks, G-only, H-only and H+D. Strong attenuation of (Delta_R) after nurse/vital/care-plan controls supports a generic monitoring explanation, not a respiratory mechanism.
3. **Source-label permutation with schedule preserved.** Within hospital×starting-stratum×total-density deciles, permute respiratory source labels/payloads across stays while preserving each index stay’s 12-bin total observation schedule, counts and endpoint risk set. Refit using training-fold permutations. Persistence indicates density/schedule leakage; disappearance is required for a source-specific interpretation.
4. **Dynamic-order falsifier.** Circularly shift the 12-bin respiratory R sequence within stay by a prespecified nonzero 30-minute block shift, retaining counts, masks and H/D marginals. A similar result after shifts indicates timing-insensitive density or documentation artifact.
5. **Timing/backfill.** Report treatment pre-landmark, mid-window and after-start partitions for 10/30/60 minutes; respiratoryCharting clinical versus entry offsets, exact ties, duplicate/backfill flags and lags. Concentration after 360, at exact ties, after the endpoint or in entry-lag rows is adverse to a pre-landmark interpretation. Treatment has no entry timestamp, an essential unavailable dependency.
6. **Selection and boundary.** Compare unweighted, W6^G and W6^{G+R}; report early exits, ICU status, EOL activity, density strata, [0,330] predictors plus gap, and state-matched analyses. Failed positivity/ESS, strong weight attenuation or one EOL/density stratum makes the corresponding standardized result inconclusive.
7. **Negative respiratory vocabulary audit.** Repeat `N_start_doc` with the frozen exclusion dictionary and a stricter label-only subset; do not choose the better result after seeing outcomes. Divergence indicates endpoint measurement sensitivity.

Supportive results would establish a reproducible pre-landmark, held-out, respiratory-specific association in recorded source data, conditional on measured generic process and selection controls, with no matching association for the non-respiratory endpoint and falsifier attenuation. They would not establish treatment delivery, ventilator use, physiologic onset, deterioration, indication, mechanism, causal effect or a clinical decision rule.

Adverse results include similar respiratory and generic endpoint increments; disappearance after generic controls; persistence under schedule/source permutations; post-landmark/backfill concentration; H-only dominance; W6^{G+R} attenuation; low ESS/positivity; fewer than five viable hospitals; or endpoint instability under vocabulary audits. These redirect interpretation toward generic workflow, stable site regime, selection, timing or measurement. They do not prove absence of a true clinical signal.

Inconclusive results include inadequate strict non-respiratory endpoint support, unresolved interval semantics, sparse H reference cells, unstable weights, broad hospital-cluster uncertainty, or inability to separate nursing labels from respiratory content. An imprecise null is not refutation. Continue to expert source adjudication only if the pre-landmark comparison is stable enough to warrant it; otherwise revise or abandon the stronger provenance claim.
