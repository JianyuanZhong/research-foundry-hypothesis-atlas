# Multi-Organ Recovery Trajectory Hypothesis

## Scientific Question

After initial stabilization in critical illness, patients whose recovery trajectories across multiple organ systems show **asynchronous patterns** have worse outcomes than those with **synchronous recovery**.

This hypothesis extends the fundamental insight from [K1] that WBC-platelet co-regulation defines universal inflammatory recovery trajectories, to a multi-organ framework. While Foy et al. demonstrated that exponential WBC decay followed by delayed linear PLT growth characterizes favorable inflammatory recovery, this proposal tests whether this temporal coordination principle generalizes across multiple organ systems, and whether deviations from synchronous recovery (asynchronous patterns) predict adverse outcomes.

## Clinical Importance

The resolution of this hypothesis has direct clinical implications:

1. **Early Risk Stratification**: Identifying patients with asynchronous organ recovery trajectories could enable earlier intervention before deterioration becomes irreversible.

2. **Precision Risk Models**: Moving beyond aggregate organ scores (SOFA, APACHE) to trajectory-based risk assessment that captures the temporal dynamics of recovery.

3. **Clinical Trial Design**: Enriching trials for patients with synchronous recovery patterns who are most likely to benefit from interventions.

4. **Understanding Recovery Mechanisms**: Determining whether asynchronous recovery reflects true biological heterogeneity or is an artifact of measurement timing and severity.

## Hypothesis and Falsification Criteria

**Primary Hypothesis**: Patients exhibiting asynchronous multi-organ recovery trajectories (where different organ systems recover at significantly different rates relative to a reference trajectory) have higher 28-day mortality compared to patients with synchronous recovery.

**Falsification Criteria**:
- The hypothesis is falsified if: (a) synchronous vs. asynchronous recovery shows no significant association with 28-day mortality after adjustment for baseline severity, or (b) the association is reversed (asynchronous recovery has lower mortality).

**Supportive Results**: Patients with synchronous recovery have significantly lower 28-day mortality than those with asynchronous recovery, even after adjusting for baseline SOFA score, comorbidities, and treatment.

**Adverse Results**: No association between recovery trajectory synchrony and mortality, or a protective effect of asynchronous recovery.

## Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Outcome
- **28-day mortality**: Death within 28 days of ICU discharge, ascertained from `hosp/patients` table with `dischtime` and follow-up information.

### Exposure: Recovery Trajectory Synchrony

**RTI (Recovery Trajectory Index)**: A composite metric quantifying the degree of synchrony in multi-organ recovery:

1. **Organ Selection**: Focus on 4-5 key organ systems with frequent measurements:
   - Respiratory (PaO2/FiO2 ratio, ABG events)
   - Renal (Creatinine, BUN, urine output)
   - Cardiovascular (MAP, lactate, vasopressor scores)
   - Hematologic (WBC, PLT)
   - Hepatic (bilirubin, AST, ALT)

2. **Trajectory Estimation**: For each organ, estimate the recovery trajectory using:
   - Exponential decay model for acute phase markers
   - Linear recovery model for chronic phase markers
   - Fit parameters: decay rate (k) and recovery time (τ)

3. **Synchrony Metric (RTI)**:
   - Calculate inter-organ correlation of recovery rates
   - RTI = correlation coefficient between organ-specific recovery trajectories
   - RTI ranges from -1 (perfectly asynchronous) to +1 (perfectly synchronous)
   - RTI = 0 indicates no coordinated recovery pattern

### Cox Regression Model

```
hazard(t) = h0(t) × exp(β × RTI + γ × baseline_severity + δ × covariates)
```

**Covariates**:
- Baseline SOFA score at ICU admission
- APACHE II score
- Age
- Sex
- Race/ethnicity
- Comorbidity index (MDCalc)
- Admission type (elective vs. emergency)
- ICU charge (proxy for severity)

**Falsification Criteria**:
- β < 0: Asynchronous recovery (lower RTI) associated with LOWER mortality → hypothesis falsified
- β = 0: No association → hypothesis falsified
- β > 0: Asynchronous recovery (lower RTI) associated with HIGHER mortality → hypothesis supported

### Baseline Comparison
- **Baseline Model**: Cox regression using only baseline SOFA score as predictor
- **Advanced Model**: Cox regression using RTI (recovery trajectory index) as additional predictor
- **Comparison**: Does RTI add predictive value beyond baseline severity?

### Model Selection Rationale
- **Baseline**: Tests if organ dysfunction severity predicts mortality (established knowledge)
- **RTI Model**: Tests if the TEMPORAL PATTERN of recovery (not just severity) predicts mortality (new knowledge)
- **Information Gained**: RTI captures dynamics that static severity scores miss, potentially identifying high-risk patients with similar baseline scores but different recovery patterns

## Key References

### [K1]: Foy et al. 2022 (FULL TEXT INSPECTED)
**Title**: Human acute inflammatory recovery is defined by co-regulatory dynamics of white blood cell and platelet populations
**Journal**: Nature Communications
**PMID**: 35995789
**DOI**: 10.1038/s41467-022-32222-2
**Claim**: Universal inflammatory recovery is characterized by exponential WBC decay followed by delayed linear PLT growth; deviation from this trajectory predicts 9-32x increased mortality risk across diverse inflammatory conditions including cardiac surgery, COVID-19, sepsis, and myocardial infarction
**Contribution**: Establishes the fundamental principle that co-regulated recovery trajectories are universal biomarkers of favorable outcomes, providing a generic approach for identifying high-risk patients
**Limitation**: Focuses primarily on two blood cell lines (WBC and PLT); generalization to multi-organ systems across different organ systems remains untested
**Locator**: Abstract and Results sections (pages 1-7): "We identified a universal recovery trajectory defined by exponential WBC decay and delayed linear growth of platelet count (PLT). Co-regulation of WBC-PLT dynamics is a fundamental mechanism of acute inflammatory recovery and provides a generic approach for identifying high-risk patients: 32x relative risk (RR) of adverse outcomes for cardiac surgery, 9x RR of death from COVID-19, 9x RR of death from sepsis, and 5x RR of death from myocardial infarction."

**Title**: The Third International Consensus Definitions for Sepsis and Septic Shock (Sepsis-3)
**Journal**: JAMA
**PMID**: 26903338
**DOI**: 10.1001/jama.2016.0287
**Claim**: Organ dysfunction in sepsis should be measured by SOFA score increase of ≥2 points, which is associated with >10% in-hospital mortality; septic shock is defined by vasopressor requirement to maintain MAP ≥65 mm Hg plus serum lactate >2 mmol/L, associated with hospital mortality >40%
**Contribution**: Provides standardized framework for measuring multi-organ dysfunction and defining sepsis/septic shock, replacing previous SIRS-based definitions
**Limitation**: Static snapshot measure at admission; does not capture temporal dynamics of organ dysfunction resolution or recovery trajectories
**Locator**: Abstract: "Sepsis should be defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection.
