# Hypotension duration and subsequent acute kidney injury in eICU

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

At the same minimum blood pressure, a longer duration of hypotension may carry more risk of subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI) than a short episode. This is an important unresolved question because a minimum-pressure threshold can conceal the clinical information in the time that pressure remains below that threshold.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is not the hypothesis itself. The available eICU snapshot supports a descriptive, temporally limited association: among patients with recorded blood pressure and laboratory measurements, it is computationally possible to construct episode-level hypotension duration and subsequent laboratory-based AKI risk. The public eICU documentation describes the data as 5-minute summary observations, not raw waveforms, and the configured source has no narrative clinical adjudication. A short episode and a long episode are therefore not clinically equivalent, and sparse or artifacted pressure readings cannot be assumed to represent continuous physiology.

The experiment will test the falsifiable claim:

> Among adults with a complete prespecified blood-pressure recording window and no documented end-of-life treatment limitation, the adjusted risk of a subsequent laboratory-based AKI episode is higher for patients with a longer episode of hypotension than for patients with a shorter episode of the same minimum recorded pressure.

The estimand is the adjusted difference in 14-day AKI risk, with a prespecified 95% confidence interval, between patients in the longest and shortest eligible hypotension-duration strata, conditional on the prespecified covariates. This is a descriptive, prognostic association, not a treatment effect and not evidence of hypotension as a mechanism.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

The advance is to test whether duration adds clinically meaningful information beyond the commonly used minimum blood pressure, while explicitly testing whether the apparent association is driven by measurement density, artifact, or competing care decisions. If duration adds information after accounting for severity and recording intensity, the result would justify treating hypotension duration as an additional prognostic signal in research and in future prospective validation. If the association disappears or reverses when density and artifacts are modeled, the result would support the alternative explanation that sparse or unreliable monitoring drives the apparent risk.

The strongest plausible rival is that apparent duration is a proxy for measurement intensity or for a change in care practice. A patient with frequent readings may have more documentation, and a patient whose blood pressure was only recorded for a short time may have had a different disposition, treatment, or end-of-life decision. The experiment separates these explanations by making recording density an explicit covariate and by testing an independent artifact signal.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The primary population is adult ICU patients with at least one qualifying blood-pressure observation and at least one qualifying creatinine measurement in the 14 days after the last qualifying observation. The primary index is the last qualifying observation in each eligible patient's ICU stay.

A qualifying observation is a nonmissing, finite value in vitalPeriodic.systemicsystolic or vitalPeriodic.systemicdiastolic, or a nonmissing, finite value in vitalAperiodic.noninvasivesystolic or vitalAperiodic.noninvasivediastolic. The primary composite is the lower of the two recorded pressures, with the corresponding timestamp. A pressure is retained only after the prespecified artifact rule below; otherwise it is treated as missing.

The primary window is from the qualifying index offset through the next qualifying observation, up to 14 days after the index. The primary duration is the number of minutes from the index to the next qualifying observation. Patients with no next qualifying observation by 14 days are censored at 14 days, not assigned a duration.

The primary exposure strata are:

- short: duration 0–4 hours
- long: duration >4 hours

The minimum pressure is reported and used as a secondary sensitivity analysis. A 90th-percentile duration threshold is reported only as a prespecified sensitivity analysis, not as a replacement for the primary estimand.

The population excludes:

- patients with a qualifying observation before the index that would violate the 14-day window;
- patients with an explicit end-of-life treatment limitation in intakeOutput.celllabel equal to Treatments|Patient Preference|Do Not Resuscitate or Treatments|Patient Preference|Do Not Attempt Resuscitation;
- patients with a qualifying observation after the index that is less than 14 days from the index;
- patients with more than one qualifying observation within 14 days, unless a prespecified secondary sensitivity analysis is run on the first observation only.

The primary analysis is restricted to patients with at least one creatinine measurement after the index. A sensitivity analysis uses a creatinine measurement in the first 24 hours after the index and another measurement by day 14. These are laboratory definitions, not adjudicated AKI.

## Outcomes and uncertainty

The primary outcome is laboratory-based AKI, defined as at least one qualifying creatinine measurement with labname equal to Creatinine and a nonmissing labresult in the index-to-day-14 window. The creatinine units are not standardized in the public eICU release, so the primary analysis uses a prespecified creatinine-change definition: a 24-hour increase in log creatinine of at least 0.30, with a second qualifying creatinine measurement by day 14. A sensitivity analysis uses a 0.50 increase.

The primary outcome is binary: AKI by day 14 versus no AKI by day 14. The primary uncertainty is a 95% confidence interval for the adjusted risk difference between the long and short duration strata. Secondary outcomes are AKI incidence rate, 28-day AKI, and AKI within 6 hours of the index. The primary analysis reports absolute risks and risk differences, not only an effect estimate.

For the alternative explanation, the solver must report the proportion of patients in each duration stratum with a qualifying observation in the index window, the number of observations per 14 days, the median time since the last observation, and the proportion with a missing pressure reading. It must also report the proportion with a documented DNR/DNI preference and the distribution of vasopressor exposure. These are descriptive diagnostics, not substitutes for adjudication.

## Simple baseline

The baseline is a standard logistic regression with a binary duration indicator (long versus short), minimum recorded pressure, age, sex, ICU day, and a prespecified interaction between minimum pressure and duration. It includes:

- vitalPeriodic observation density in the index window, measured as the number of qualifying observations per 14 days;
- vitalAperiodic observation density, measured as the number of qualifying observations per 14 days;
- infusionDrug vasopressor exposure, represented by the number of rows with a nonmissing drugname and a finite infusionoffset in the index window;
- lab creatinine measurement density, measured as the number of qualifying creatinine rows in the index window;
- intakeOutput DNR/DNI preference, represented by the number of rows with the two exact cell-label values above;
- hospitalid as a hospital-level random intercept or fixed effect, with one fixed effect per hospital only if the number of hospitals is small enough to be reported without creating a sparse model.

The baseline uses a 5-minute median rule only for the prespecified vitalPeriodic.systemicmean, vitalPeriodic.pamean, and vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean sensitivity analyses. It does not treat these medians as continuous physiology.

The baseline is fit separately in the primary 14-day window and in the 24-hour-to-day-14 sensitivity window. It must report the estimated risk difference, 95% confidence interval, and adjusted odds ratio. It is the primary comparison because it is transparent and tests whether duration remains associated after accounting for density and competing care.

## Substantive learned and mechanistic alternative

The alternative is a prespecified multivariate survival model with a duration and minimum-pressure interaction, using the same primary 14-day window. It is not a flexible neural model for the sake of complexity. The target is the same binary 14-day AKI outcome and the same risk difference. The model is fit with penalized logistic regression, with a prespecified L2 penalty and a maximum of 10% of the number of eligible observations used for regularization. It includes the same covariates as the baseline, plus:

- the duration of the hypotension episode;
- the minimum recorded pressure;
- the number of qualifying observations per 14 days;
- the time since the last qualifying observation;
- the number of qualifying creatinine measurements;
- the number of qualifying vasopressor rows;
- the number of DNR/DNI rows;
- hospitalid as a fixed effect or a hospital-level random intercept.

The alternative can reveal whether duration has a nonlinear or threshold-like association with AKI that the simple binary baseline loses, and whether the association is concentrated in patients with sparse or artifact-prone recording. It can also quantify the interaction between minimum pressure and duration. This is a substantive alternative because it tests a different scientific question—whether the duration effect is concentrated or nonlinear—rather than merely adding a predictor.

A mechanistic alternative is deferred because the available data do not identify a causal mechanism. The data can show an association and a relationship with vasopressor exposure, but not the effect of treatment or the pathophysiology of AKI. A causal interpretation would require a treatment assignment, an intervention or randomized trial, or a well-specified instrumental-variable analysis with additional data that the configured snapshot does not provide.

The alternative must be evaluated with the same prespecified primary and sensitivity windows, the same outcome definitions, and the same hospital-level validation. It must report the same absolute risks and risk differences. A small difference in AUROC or a small predictive gain is not a scientific conclusion.

## Baseline comparison and selection

The simple baseline is selected as the primary analysis because it is transparent, interpretable, and directly tests the duration-versus-minimum-pressure question. The multivariate survival model is selected as the substantive alternative because it can reveal nonlinear or threshold-like duration effects and whether they are concentrated in patients with sparse or artifact-prone recording. The learned alternative is not selected for predictive performance alone; the primary comparison is the adjusted risk difference.

The solver must report the baseline and alternative results side by side. If the alternative changes the direction or the confidence interval of the primary risk difference, that is a substantive result that must be reported and explained. If the alternative is too sparse or unstable, it is reported as inconclusive and the baseline remains the primary analysis.

## Falsification and sensitivity analyses

The experiment must run the following falsification and sensitivity analyses:

1. Observation-density falsification: compare the primary result with the duration effect after conditioning on the number of qualifying observations per 14 days and the time since the last observation. A null or reversed association after this adjustment supports the alternative explanation.
2. Artifact sensitivity: repeat the analysis with each pressure field separately, excluding readings outside the prespecified valid range, and excluding patients with more than one observation in 14 days. The primary range is 0–200 mmHg for systolic and 0–140 mmHg for diastolic, with values outside the range flagged as artifacts. A result that disappears when artifact-prone readings are excluded supports the artifact explanation.
3. Minimum-pressure sensitivity: repeat the primary analysis with minimum pressure as a continuous variable and with a prespecified 90th-percentile duration threshold.
4. Outcome sensitivity: repeat with a 24-hour-to-day-14 creatinine window and with the 0.50 creatinine-change threshold.
5. Hospital validation: split the analysis by hospital, using a fixed hospital effect or a hospital-level random intercept. A result that is not stable across hospitals supports the hospital-practice explanation.
6. Positive-control falsification: test the association between a prespecified laboratory-density variable and subsequent AKI. This is a positive control, not a clinical claim. If the laboratory-density variable has no association, it supports the general validity of the density analysis.
7. Artifact-positive-control falsification: test whether the number of nonmissing pressure readings predicts a subsequent missingness flag. This is a diagnostic, not a clinical claim.

The primary result is supportive only if the adjusted long-versus-short risk difference is positive and its 95% confidence interval excludes zero, and the result is directionally consistent after the observation-density, artifact, and hospital validation analyses. It is adverse if the confidence interval excludes zero and the direction is opposite to the hypothesis, or if the result is reversed after the prespecified adjustment. It is inconclusive if the confidence interval includes zero, if the model is unstable, or if the density/artifact analyses are too sparse to report.

## Interpretations

A supportive result would establish a reproducible, adjusted prognostic association between longer hypotension duration and subsequent laboratory-based AKI in this eICU cohort. It would not establish that hypotension causes AKI, that a duration threshold is a treatment target, or that the finding generalizes to other hospitals or populations.

An adverse result would show that the apparent duration effect is not present or is reversed after accounting for the prespecified variables. It would support the alternative explanation that minimum pressure, duration, or measurement density is not a useful prognostic signal in this cohort.

An inconclusive result would mean that the available data cannot resolve the question. It would justify collecting more frequent or more reliable blood-pressure measurements, adjudicating AKI clinically, or studying a different population. It would not justify changing the clinical threshold.

## Clinical adjudication and unavailable evidence

The experiment cannot establish adjudicated AKI because the public eICU release does not include narrative clinical adjudication or a validated clinical AKI label. The laboratory definition is a computationally checkable outcome, but it is not a substitute for clinical adjudication. The solver must explicitly state that the primary conclusion is about a laboratory-based prognostic association.

The experiment cannot establish causality because there is no treatment assignment, intervention, or reliable instrumental variable. It cannot establish that a duration threshold should be used in clinical practice. It cannot establish the mechanism by which hypotension affects the kidney. Those claims require clinical adjudication, treatment data, a prospective study, or another study with more reliable physiology.

The public eICU release also has no raw waveforms and no narrative note sections. The solver must not infer continuous blood pressure from 5-minute summaries or from sparse readings. The solver must not treat a missing pressure reading as a normal pressure or as evidence of no hypotension.

The future solver must newly estimate the adjusted 14-day AKI risk difference between the long and short duration strata, with a confidence interval and the prespecified secondary outcomes. It must report the baseline and alternative model results, the density and artifact diagnostics, the hospital validation, and the falsification analyses. It must not report a fitted model as a scientific result unless the primary estimand and uncertainty are reported.
