# Prospective risk-set quarantine and label-shuffle falsification for frozen HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B validation

## Targeted successor and unresolved claim

This is a single-parent successor of `[prior hypothesis]`. It does not change the scientific schema. The frozen question remains:

> In the frozen adult HCC repeat-TACE population, among episodes with an outcome-blind eligible pre-event history and an observed assay outcome, does contemporaneous pre-repeat assay value `P` improve strictly forward prediction of the subsequent same-encounter assay value `Y` beyond remote baseline `B` and the locked decision-time core?

The strongest supported claim remains limited to the predecessor's adequately supported 2022 block: a complete-triplet, selected-episode estimate showed incremental short-horizon prognostic prediction for that calendar mixture. The unresolved claim is reproducible prospective performance when the eligible risk set and the observed-outcome subset are kept separate. In particular, the 2023 and 2024 blocks cannot be treated as transport evidence while systemic-arm support and `Y` observation are sparse or compositionally selected.

The substantive repair is an explicit *risk-set quarantine* and an executable *held-out label-shuffle falsification*. Every selected event receives an outcome-blind eligibility status before `Y` is read. The observation process is described, but no future outcome, future calibration, future scaling, or future support count is allowed to affect a pre-origin fit. A fixed-prediction permutation distribution, stratified within assay, future block, and recorded arm, tests whether a reported positive RMSE increment is distinguishable from a no-association outcome assignment on exactly the same held-out rows. This is a computational falsification of the incremental-prediction claim, not a causal randomization test and not a remedy for missing outcomes.

## Frozen clocks, population, and estimand

Keep the exact predecessor clocks and strict ordering. `B` is the latest valid uncensored assay on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1. `P` is the latest valid assay in the selected repeat encounter in `[event_time−72 hours,event_time)`. `Y` is the selected valid assay in that encounter in `(event_time,event_time+72 hours]`, nearest +24 hours, with deterministic tie handling. Require `B_time < P_time < event_time < Y_time`, positive P lead, one patient-assay row, and no laboratory value in event selection.

Use fixed annual origins 2021-01-01, 2022-01-01, 2023-01-01, and 2024-01-01. For each origin, training events satisfy `event_date < origin`; the future test block is `[origin, origin+365 days)`. Blocks are disjoint. The one-event-per-patient reconstruction and all inherited pathway labels remain fixed.

For each assay, compare exactly:

```
M0: Y ~ B + systemic-record indicator + age + sex indicators
       + TACE2-to-repeat days + P-lead hours
M1: M0 + P
```

Use only training rows for age median, age-missingness handling, sex coding, variance filtering, centering/scaling, inner leave-one-patient-out alpha selection over `[0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100]`, and ridge fitting. The primary noncausal estimand is assay-specific standardized held-out RMSE increment `[RMSE(M0)−RMSE(M1)]/SD_test(Y)`, with paired arm-stratified patient-frequency bootstrap intervals conditional on each fixed pre-origin fit. MAE, bias, arm-specific error, drift, and influence remain secondary. No diagnostic supplies weights, imputes `Y`, changes row membership, or changes the estimand.

## Explicit outcome-blind eligibility boundary

Construct one canonical event-level table before any model fit and before selecting or reading `Y`. It must contain a status for every reconstructed selected repeat event and assay, including events that never enter a complete-triplet model:

1. `event_selected`: the procedure/encounter/diagnosis/medication reconstruction succeeded; no lab rows are consulted.
2. `has_B`: a valid `B` exists under the frozen B clock.
3. `has_P`: a valid `P` exists under the frozen P clock.
4. `BP_eligible`: `has_B` and `has_P`, strict `B_time < P_time < event_time`, positive P lead, and no post-event information used.
5. `Y_observed`: only after the first four statuses are frozen, a valid `Y` exists under the frozen Y window.
6. `complete`: `BP_eligible and Y_observed`; this is the unchanged complete-triplet analysis population, not a redefined target population.

Persist counts, denominators, and arm-specific counts for this funnel overall and within every training and test block. The compiler must assert that the event-selection and BP-eligibility code path does not call the Y selector or inspect post-event rows. The output may contain aggregate counts and anonymized row keys needed for joins, but no names, identifiers, clinical notes, or raw records.

The outcome-blind support gate is prespecified: at least 20 BP-eligible episodes overall and at least 10 in each descriptive arm in both pre-origin training and future test. The complete-triplet gate is reported separately with the same thresholds. Failure of either gate makes that block descriptive, regardless of a favorable point estimate or interval. These gates are interpretation gates only; they never delete a row from the frozen population after seeing its outcome.

## Executable falsification and negative-feasibility diagnostic

After each origin's M0/M1 fits are frozen and predictions on complete future test rows are generated, run a fixed-prediction held-out label-shuffle control separately for each assay and annual test block:

- retain the exact test rows, pre-origin predictions, arm labels, event dates, and missingness pattern;
- permute observed `Y` values only within the same assay × annual test block × recorded-arm stratum, without replacement; never permute `B`, `P`, core covariates, predictions, eligibility, or `Y_observed`;
- use 2,000 deterministic-seed permutations (record seed and algorithm), recompute the same standardized RMSE gain for every permutation, and report its median, 2.5th/97.5th percentiles, fraction of null gains at least as large as the observed gain, and the observed-minus-null median;
- retain the actual paired gain and its bootstrap interval from the frozen pipeline; the permutation is a negative-control/falsification distribution, not a replacement uncertainty interval.

A supported incremental-prediction claim for a block requires the existing reconstruction, BP-support, complete-triplet, and observation-selection gates, an observed gain above zero with its prespecified paired interval, and observed gain above the 97.5th percentile of the stratified label-shuffle distribution. If the observed gain fails the shuffle criterion, label the block falsified for incremental predictive association or, when the block is too small to resolve the null, inconclusive; do not call it supportive merely because its unpermuted point estimate is positive. A null or adverse shuffle result does not prove that `B` is sufficient and does not imply a treatment or clinical-utility conclusion.

As an independent negative-feasibility output, report the number of complete test rows available to the shuffle in every arm and block. If any stratum has fewer than two complete rows, no within-stratum permutation inference is claimed; the block is automatically inconclusive for that assay. This makes the 2023/2024 sparse-observation limitation executable rather than rhetorical. The permutation cannot repair informative missingness: a low `Y` observation fraction, low observation ESS, or large observed-versus-missing pre-Y SMD still blocks transport interpretation even if a label-shuffle result is nominally positive.

## Prespecified interpretation and evidence limits

Supportive evidence is restricted to an annual block that passes reconstruction and clock assertions, outcome-blind BP and complete-triplet support, the observation-selection warnings, adequate permutation strata, positive paired RMSE evidence, and the held-out label-shuffle criterion. Under the predecessor's executed counts, 2022 may remain supportive only for its selected, observed calendar mixture; the 2023 block has only 9 systemic BP-eligible episodes and 29/43 observed outcomes, and 2024 has 9 systemic episodes and 14/70 observed outcomes, so those blocks remain descriptive/inconclusive irrespective of a favorable point estimate. The new control is expected to sharpen, not broaden, that conclusion.

An adverse result is a nonpositive gated gain, repeated degradation, or a held-out gain no larger than its label-shuffle null. A selection adverse result is low observation, low selection ESS, or a large measured pre-Y observed-versus-missing imbalance. An inconclusive result includes sparse strata, failed support, calendar extrapolation, influential episodes, denominator/clock drift, or absent assay units. None establishes mechanism, treatment effect, recovery, toxicity, safety, patient benefit, utility, or actionability.
