# Provisional hypothesis: discordant HbA1c rise and weight trajectory predict incident diabetes

## Status

This is a provisional, evidence-limited hypothesis for episode 3. It is not a validated finding. The prior episode identified this as a repairable restart after a pancreatic-cancer endpoint failed a zero-event feasibility gate, but exact source/table bindings, incident-diabetes ascertainment, and public-literature support still need verification. No cohort result is claimed here.

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants without diabetes or cancer at a later assessment-center visit, a similar rise in HbA1c from one visit to the next may be associated with higher subsequent incident diabetes risk when accompanied by weight loss than when accompanied by weight gain. The clinically relevant question is whether the same biochemical worsening should be interpreted differently when it co-occurs with weight loss, which could signal a distinct metabolic or illness-related process.

This is a predictive/explanatory association, not a causal claim. Weight loss may be a consequence of occult disease, medication, or other processes; HbA1c and weight may share measurement and selection artifacts. The strongest currently supported claim is only that this question is worth testing because the prior branch found the pancreatic endpoint infeasible and this metabolic endpoint is plausibly more common. The unresolved claim is whether the interaction between HbA1c change and weight trajectory is reproducible and clinically informative after excluding prevalent diabetes and defining a valid incident outcome.

## Proposed falsifiable test

Define a second-visit baseline after verifying the available assessment date and repeat-measurement fields. Restrict to participants without prevalent diabetes, cancer, or other prespecified exclusionary conditions at baseline. Construct HbA1c change and weight change between the two visits, and define incident diabetes using only post-baseline diagnosis records with a date field. Compare a simple adjusted model for HbA1c change, weight change, and their interaction against a baseline model without the interaction. Use prespecified supportive, adverse, and inconclusive criteria:

- Supportive: the interaction is directionally consistent, statistically precise, and robust to excluding early events, prevalent disease, and plausible measurement/selection strata; the alternative model improves held-out calibration or discrimination by a clinically meaningful margin.
- Adverse: no interaction, reversal, or instability across repeat-measurement and timing analyses, or the association disappears after excluding prevalent disease/early events.
- Inconclusive: too few incident events, too few repeat measurements, wide uncertainty, or inability to verify the diagnosis date/field lineage.

A simple adjusted Cox or discrete-time model is the baseline. A substantive alternative is a multistate or trajectory model that jointly represents HbA1c and weight states and distinguishes delayed versus immediate transitions; this could reveal whether the apparent interaction reflects a persistent discordant trajectory rather than a single cross-visit contrast. It is deferred unless the exact repeat fields and event support are verified. Neither model establishes mechanism.

## Exact data requirements and current uncertainty

The design must be bound to the actual UK Biobank source groups and tables in the catalog, with participant identifiers, assessment-center dates, HbA1c, body-weight, diagnosis codes, diagnosis dates, cancer/diabetes exclusions, and follow-up end date. The exact table names, column names, archive members, and join keys are not yet verified in this provisional draft. In particular, diagnosis ascertainment and date ordering must be checked before any event count or model is interpreted. Source-specific identifiers must not be joined across incompatible namespaces.

## Missing evidence and next checks

1. Verify the catalog and source paths for the repeat HbA1c, weight, assessment-date, diagnosis-code, diagnosis-date, cancer, and follow-up fields.
2. Audit overlap, missingness, date ordering, and incident-event counts under a prespecified prevalent-disease exclusion.
3. Inspect three relevant public works and attach exact receipts/excerpts before final selection.
4. Compare the simple interaction model with the trajectory/multistate alternative only if the data support it.
5. Record whether results are supportive, adverse, or inconclusive without upgrading an association to mechanism or clinical truth.

## Demonstration and seed disposition

The prior pancreatic-cancer direction is parked because its feasibility gate failed. The three research demonstrations and expert seeds remain context, not evidence for this hypothesis. The current branch is a bounded restart toward a common metabolic transition; it must not be selected until the data and reference requirements above are satisfied.
