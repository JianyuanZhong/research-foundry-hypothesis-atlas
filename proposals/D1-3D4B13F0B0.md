# Six-hour hypotension burden as a transportable renal-surveillance signal

## Scientific deliverable and unresolved claim

The future solver must newly construct an auditable eICU six-hour landmark cohort and interval ledger, fit two prespecified models on identical inputs and hospital splits, and emit uncertainty-quantified estimates of one primary quantity:

Among adult ICU stays with a valid creatinine before the first observed MAP <65 episode, no observed qualifying creatinine rise through minute 360, and ICU observation extending beyond minute 360, estimate the standardized 48-hour cumulative-incidence risk-difference interaction for a P25-to-P75 increase in 0–6-hour area-under-65 MAP burden, comparing documented prompt-after-low versus delayed/no documented target-pressor timing.

Let G_c be the standardized 48-hour CIF contrast for burden P75 versus P25 in timing category c. The primary estimand is D = G_prompt - G_delayed/no-record, with delayed and no-record also reported separately and pooled only when overlap/support are adequate. Standardization fixes minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, MAP coverage/trajectory, measured admission risk, hospital distribution and timing-category support.

This is a noncausal recorded-process estimand: the endpoint is an observed creatinine-rise signal, not KDIGO AKI, and the infusion category is a documentation timestamp, not treatment exposure. The prespecified clinical-interpretation margin is |D| >= 0.02 absolute risk; this is not a treatment-effect margin.

Completion requires source/schema/value-map audits and cohort flow; stay-level and 30-minute ledgers; fitted transparent competing-state and ordered observation/transition models; development and whole-hospital held-out D/D_h, calibration, uncertainty and process decomposition; sensitivity outputs; and conclusions linked to computed artifacts. No scientific model or clinical estimate has been fitted in discovery.

The strongest supported pre-fit claim is only data availability: the frozen snapshot contains ICU-relative MAP summaries, ICU-keyed labs with result/revision offsets, named infusion rows, ICU disposition, admission descriptors and hospital identifiers that can be joined by the catalog keys. Parent audit material reports 200,859 patient rows and 208 hospital rows and sampled creatinine/pressor names; these checks do not establish administration, causation, clinical AKI or transport.

The unresolved hypothesis is that, conditional on the same minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, recovery/coverage, measured risk and hospital, a P25-to-P75 burden increase has a larger observed-rise CIF in prompt-after-low than in delayed/no-record timing, and that this interaction transports after explicitly representing testing and ICU exit. A null, reversal, within-hospital instability, held-out failure or disappearance after process decomposition falsifies the corresponding claim. None would prove pressor equivalence, harm, safety or a biological renal null.

Clinical importance is decision-bound: a stable signal could inform intensified renal surveillance after the six-hour landmark and stratification of future studies; an unstable or ascertainment-dependent signal would warn against treating time-below-threshold as a portable renal trigger. No result justifies changing a MAP target or vasopressor.

## Population and time windows

Use eICU snapshot \`[source checksum]\`. Unit: ICU stay keyed by \`patientunitstayid\`; repeated stays retained. Keep all stays sharing \`uniquepid\` in one split and run a uniquepid-clustered sensitivity. Time zero is ICU admission; offsets are integer minutes.

Primary cohort:

- age >=18 from \`patient.age\`; parse \`> 89\` as 90 and retain an age-censored flag;
- valid nonnegative \`patient.unitdischargeoffset > 360\`; an exit at/before 360 is a pre-landmark exclusion, never post-landmark no event;
- at least two numeric \`vitalPeriodic.systemicmean\` values in [0,360], restricted to 20–200 mmHg;
- first raw low-MAP time is the earliest valid periodic \`systemicmean <65\` in [0,360];
- a numeric creatinine in [0,60] with case-folded \`labname=creatinine\` and both unit fields explicitly verified as mg/dL; unknown/incompatible units are excluded, not converted;
- baseline is the first qualifying result at the smallest \`labresultoffset` in [0,60], not the minimum. Exact duplicate \`labid\` rows are removed; multiple valid rows at that offset use prespecified median and are counted;
- baseline occurs before or at first low MAP; no valid result >= baseline +0.3 mg/dL occurs after baseline through minute 360.

The no-pre-low-baseline cohort, post-exposure baseline cohort and no-low-MAP companion cohort are labelled sensitivities/descriptive results, not replacements for the primary estimand.

Exposure history is closed at minute 360. Collapse same-stay/time MAP rows by median. Primary burden is trapezoidal area-under-65 in mmHg-min, bridging only gaps <=30 minutes; retain observed/bridged minutes, unbridged gaps and coverage. Secondary features are observed minutes below 65, longest low run, episode count, coverage fraction, minimum observed MAP, recovery slope and last valid MAP. First-low time and timing category always use raw observations.

Define first documented target-pressor as the smallest \`infusionDrug.infusionoffset\` in [0,360] whose case-insensitive \`drugname\` matches a frozen map for norepinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine or epinephrine. Categories: pre-low/concurrent (record <= first low), prompt-after-low (> first low and <=30 minutes later), delayed-after-low (>30 minutes and <=360), and no documented target-pressor by 360. Same-minute is pre-low/concurrent. This is not verified administration, start, order, dose, indication or intent. Do not infer dose from \`drugrate\`, \`infusionrate\`, \`drugamount\`, \`volumeoffluid\` or \`patientweight\).

Follow-up starts strictly after 360 and ends at \`min(2880, unitdischargeoffset)\). First qualifying post-landmark creatinine is at \`labresultoffset >360\), before unit exit, and >= baseline +0.3. Unit death before a rise is \`unitdischargestatus=Expired\` or \`unitdischargelocation=Death\`; non-death unit discharge/transfer is an alive-exit competing observation boundary. A test without a rise is not terminal. Result exactly at 360 belongs to baseline. In 30-minute intervals, only lagged information may predict the interval. The primary process is decomposed into test opportunity, rise conditional on test, death before rise/test and alive exit before rise/test. No component is called mediation.

## Transport and matched analysis

Hash-split hospitals 80/20 before any fitting, using a documented seed and no outcome stratification. If a \`uniquepid\` spans hospitals, assign connected hospital components together. Held-out hospitals cannot choose maps, thresholds, interpolation, percentiles, regularization or features.

Transport is evaluable only with >=10 held-out hospitals and >=5 supported hospitals, each with >=50 landmark stays, both timing groups, event support and burden overlap. Report patient-standardized held-out D, equal-hospital mean D_h, hospital-cluster bootstrap 95% intervals, between-hospital variance/prediction interval, sign consistency (>=70% supported hospitals matching development sign in >=80% bootstrap replicates), calibration intercept/slope and Brier score. Support requires same sign in development/held-out patient-standardized D, |D|>=0.02 with interval excluding zero, equal-hospital agreement with interval excluding zero, sign consistency and adequate calibration. Pooled/equal-hospital disagreement, insufficient support or interval including zero is inconclusive, not repaired by pooling.

Both methods use the identical cohort, 30-minute ledger, features, split, standardization, event ordering and uncertainty.

Transparent baseline: regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic models for observed rise, death and alive exit, plus a first-test model. B0 contains minimum MAP, non-burden trajectory/recovery, MAP coverage, baseline creatinine, demographics, admission/unit source/type, unittype, hospital context, admission-risk descriptors, time and fixed timing category. B1 adds burden; B2 adds burden-by-timing interaction (primary); B3 adds lagged test-opportunity/history; B4 is B3 without the interaction; B5 adds development-hospital random slopes/intercepts and reports D_h. Use grouped development folds for regularization; report condition numbers, convergence, calibration, CIFs and secondary AUROC/AUPRC. Small discrimination gains are not the scientific criterion.

Mechanistic alternative: on the same ledger, fit a piecewise-exponential multi-state model with separate test-opportunity, rise-given-test, death and alive-exit transitions. Pre-landmark first low, pressor category, recovery, coverage and burden are frozen history; post-landmark terms are lagged. Development-only hospital effects are used; held-out evaluation uses frozen parameters. The alternative reveals whether D is carried by testing intensity, recorded rise conditional on a test, recovery/order, death or alive exit—information a marginal baseline loses. It is selected for this substantive uncertainty, not complexity. A generic neural sequence model is deferred unless both models leave reproducible shape-specific held-out residual miscalibration after process accounting. A causal pressor model is deferred because administration, dose, intent and confounding are not identified.

Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstraps plus uniquepid sensitivity. Report inverse-opportunity weighted versus joint-process estimates, development 1st-percentile weight truncation and truncation fraction; this is not a missing-not-at-random solution. Required sensitivities include no interpolation, non-invasive MAP, revised lab time, confirmed-rise, alternate unit/48-hour boundary, 30/60-minute prompt cut, positive-rate record, pressor map, connected components, Apache severity, and no process adjustment.

## Falsification and evidence limits

Supportive means model-direction agreement; development and held-out D meet the 2-point/interval rule; equal-hospital transport agrees; within-hospital signs are stable; and the interaction persists after test/exit decomposition and core sensitivities. This supports a transportable association among recorded histories and an observed lab signal only.

Adverse means null/reversed interaction, burden explained by minimum MAP/trajectory, loss of transport, poor calibration, or attenuation attributable to test opportunity/exit. Inconclusive means invalid maps, sparse events/timing/overlap, dominant early exit, too few supported hospitals, nonconvergence, collinearity, extreme weights, boundary variance or pooled/equal-hospital disagreement.

Computationally checkable claims are hashes/headers, schemas, joins, filters, deduplication, offsets, maps, split integrity, ledger states, fits, standardization, intervals, calibration, transport and sensitivity reproduction. Clinical adjudication/another study is required for actual administration/start, dose units, indication/order/intent, completeness of infusion capture, intended MAP target, time-varying confounding, pre-ICU renal reserve, complete urine-output/KDIGO evidence, dialysis context, causes of missing tests/exits and whether a creatinine rise is true injury. eICU lacks images, raw waveforms and complete narrative context. The design cannot establish that hypotension causes AKI or that bedside treatment should change.

## Demonstrations, compute and retained branches

The three demonstrations were read as methodological context. Delphi’s accessible paper/methods show structured dated disease-history transformer learning; we retain temporal-order motivation but do not reproduce its UKB/Danish cohorts and defer a generic transformer. ALADYNOULLI’s accessible paper shows longitudinal latent signatures with genetic integration; eICU lacks those genetic inputs/cohorts, so no reproduction or genetic claim is made; the selected multi-state model addresses a narrower observable process question. For Oncoformer, only supplement/metadata are available; the main article and complete STAR Methods remain unavailable and eICU has no images. We make no claim to have inspected unavailable text or to reproduce its multimodal method; its same-input ablation principle is retained.
