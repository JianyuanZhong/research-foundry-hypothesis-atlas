# Care-plan ventilator strategy and near-term ventilator-management trajectory

## Change from the parent and scientific question

This child preserves the parent candidate `[prior hypothesis]` and its completed negative endpoint audit. The parent showed that the available eICU fields cannot ascertain true durable ventilator liberation: independent liberation markers were uncommon (13/309 daily-evaluation rows and 4/231 no-trial rows), strict 48-hour observation after a union signal was incomplete (49.3% and 52.6%), and care-plan, respiratory-therapy, ETT, and discharge-bridge proxies cannot establish true extubation or sustained liberation. This proposal does not retry that endpoint and does not reinterpret those findings.

The new, falsifiable question is narrower and clinically consequential:

> Among mechanically ventilated eICU landmark decisions for which the first exact ventilation care-plan label is either `Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation` or `Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial`, is the documented care-plan choice associated with a different near-term ventilator-support trajectory or independently charted respiratory mechanics/weaning-assessment process?

The unit of inference is a documented management trajectory, not a treatment effect. The study will not claim that an SBT occurred, that extubation was attempted, that extubation succeeded, or that either care-plan choice causes liberation.

The strongest available evidence before this study supports only that the two exact labels define observable, temporally ordered care-plan states and that ventilator settings and selected respiratory process fields are recorded in `respiratoryCharting`. The unresolved claim is whether the label selected at time zero is associated with subsequent charted support reduction or process documentation after accounting for hospital and pre-decision history. That association is testable without pretending that the chart contains a valid SBT or extubation endpoint.

## Population, exposure, and time zero

Use the frozen parent strict cohort without adding or dropping rows based on post-decision data: 540 person-landmark rows, 309 daily-evaluation rows and 231 no-trial rows, representing 443 ICU stays and 443 unique patients. The parent landmark times are 1,440, 2,880, 4,320, 5,760, and 7,200 minutes after ICU admission. For each landmark `L`, exposure was selected as the first exact qualifying `carePlanGeneral.cplitemvalue` in `[L,L+360]`; time zero is its exact `carePlanGeneral.cplitemoffset`, denoted `D`. Join all longitudinal tables by `patientunitstayid`; use `uniquepid` only for patient-level clustering and repeated-landmark identification.

Exposure is binary and exact:

* evaluation: `Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation`
* no-trial: `Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial`

The parent cohort's `strategy`, `decision_offset`, `landmark`, `hospitalid`, and clinical summary variables are retained as derived inputs. No care-plan row after `D` may define a baseline covariate or alter exposure assignment.

Primary comparative analyses should be restricted to hospitals with both exposure labels and a prespecified minimum of 10 rows in each group, initially hospitals 248, 252, and 420 (130 evaluation and 173 no-trial rows in the parent cohort). The full 540-row cohort is a secondary descriptive analysis. This restriction is not a claim that those hospitals are representative; it prevents a pooled contrast from being driven by hospitals that never use one label or use incompatible respiratory-chart vocabularies. Report the full hospital-by-strategy table and do not silently discard single-strategy hospitals.

## Outcomes and ascertainment gates

### Primary outcome: charted ventilator-support reduction

For each decision, identify the last valid respiratory setting in `[D-360,D)` and all valid settings in `(D,D+720]` using `respiratoryCharting.respchartoffset`, not entry time. Harmonize only the prespecified labels below:

* FiO2: `FiO2`, `FIO2 (%)`, and `Set Fraction of Inspired Oxygen (FIO2)`; normalize fractions to 0–1 and percentages to fractions.
* PEEP: `PEEP` and `PEEP/CPAP`; retain values in 0–30 cm H2O.

A decision is support-trajectory-ascertainable if at least one of FiO2 or PEEP has both a valid pre-D value in `[D-360,D)` and a valid post-D value in `(D,D+720]`. The primary support-reduction indicator is present if the first or any post-D valid value is at least 0.05 lower than the last pre-D FiO2, or at least 1 cm H2O lower than the last pre-D PEEP. These thresholds are fixed before comparative modeling. If both parameters are available, preserve parameter-specific indicators and the composite; do not call the composite an extubation or weaning success measure.

The ascertainment gate is part of the result, not a post hoc exclusion: report the number and proportion gated by strategy, hospital, and landmark. The primary estimand is the descriptive risk difference in support reduction among gated decisions, evaluation minus no-trial. A companion all-row estimand codes ungated decisions as no documented reduction and is explicitly interpreted as a documentation-plus-action estimand; it must not be described as a physiologic treatment effect. A sensitivity analysis uses only the FiO2-gated and PEEP-gated subsets separately.

Secondary support outcomes are (i) support escalation using increases of at least 0.05 FiO2 or 1 cm H2O PEEP, (ii) first-versus-last post-D absolute changes, and (iii) the same outcomes in `(D,D+360]` and `(D,D+1440]`. Escalation is an adverse-direction or competing trajectory, not evidence against the care plan in a causal sense.

### Secondary outcome: independently charted respiratory process

In `(D,D+720]`, identify exact `respchartvaluelabel` values `Total RSBI`, `4. RSBI (RR/Vt)`, and `SBT Method`. The primary process indicator is any of those exact fields, with field-specific counts reported. The labels must be called “documented respiratory mechanics/weaning-assessment process.” They must not be called an SBT, readiness assessment, or extubation attempt. `RSBI`, `CCU Ventilator Weaning Protocol Started`, `Sedation Wean`, and `ETT Sedation Vacation` are retained as prespecified exploratory exact fields, including zero counts; they are not merged into the primary endpoint merely to increase events.

A 6-hour and 24-hour process window are sensitivity analyses. For multiple rows at the same offset, an indicator is counted once and the earliest qualifying `respchartoffset` determines time-to-first-process. `respchartentryoffset` is used only in a delayed-entry sensitivity; it must not replace the clinical chart offset in the primary analysis.

Baseline variables are measured at or before `D` only. The parent frozen cohort supplies landmark, decision delay, hospital, and its admission/landmark clinical summaries, including admission-level oxygenation and physiology fields. Where re-derived, use the following exact sources and fields:

* `infusionDrug.csv.gz`: pre-D point-charted sedative/analgesic records for a sensitivity analysis only, using `patientunitstayid`, `infusionoffset`, `drugname`, `drugrate`, `infusionrate`, `drugamount`, `volumeoffluid`, and `patientweight`; these rows do not establish continuous active infusion intervals.
* `carePlanGeneral.csv.gz`: the exposure source, using `patientunitstayid`, `cplitemoffset`, `cplgroup`, `cplitemvalue`, and `activeupondischarge`.
* `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`: only pre-D summaries if needed, using `patientunitstayid`, `observationoffset`, `sao2`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, `systemicmean`, and `etco2`.
* `vitalAperiodic.csv.gz`: pre-D blood-pressure summaries using `patientunitstayid`, `observationoffset`, and `noninvasivemean`.
* `lab.csv.gz`: pre-D laboratory summaries using `patientunitstayid`, `labresultoffset`, `labname`, `labresult`, and `labresulttext`.

Potential baseline adjustment variables are hospital, landmark, decision delay, age category, unit type, admission source, the parent's pre-D physiology summaries, pre-D respiratory setting presence/value, respiratory-chart row density in `[D-1440,D)`, and whether any prespecified process field was charted in `[D-1440,D)`. Include pre-D process history as a documentation-history covariate and temporal negative-control comparison. Do not adjust for any respiratory value, care-plan label, sedation value, process field, or discharge variable occurring after `D`.

The primary analysis is a hospital-adjusted descriptive association. Fit a binomial model for the gated primary outcome with exposure, hospital fixed effects, landmark indicators, and prespecified pre-D covariates; use robust standard errors clustered by `uniquepid` so repeated landmarks from the same patient do not act as independent patients. If sparse cells or separation prevent a stable model, report hospital-stratified risks and a Mantel-Haenszel or standardized risk difference rather than substituting a flexible predictive model. The estimand remains an association, not a causal effect. Report both crude and adjusted risk differences with confidence intervals and the number of unique patients.

For the support-trajectory algorithm, the output shows the following descriptive feasibility counts (not adjusted and not inferential):

* In `(D,D+720]`, the FiO2/PEEP baseline-plus-follow-up gate was met for 277/309 evaluation rows and 226/231 no-trial rows; support reduction occurred in 51/277 and 31/226, respectively.
* In the three-hospital comparative subset, the same 12-hour gate was met for 126/130 evaluation and 169/173 no-trial rows; support reduction occurred in 17/126 and 23/169.
* The corresponding crude gated risk differences were approximately +0.047 in the full cohort and -0.001 in the three-hospital subset, with wide unadjusted intervals crossing zero. These numbers are feasibility evidence, not a conclusion about the care-plan choice.
* In the same 12-hour window, escalation occurred in 47/277 versus 37/226 in the full cohort and 19/126 versus 25/169 in the restricted subset. This competing-direction result must be reported even if reduction favors evaluation.

The full respiratory-process feasibility scan found any prespecified process field by 12 hours in 37/309 evaluation and 37/231 no-trial rows; by 24 hours, 56/309 and 58/231. The dominant exact field was `Total RSBI`, and documentation varied sharply by hospital. In the restricted subset, 12-hour process counts were 35/130 versus 32/173 and 24-hour counts were 48/130 versus 50/173. These data support a measurable secondary process outcome but do not validate its clinical meaning without expert review.

## Falsification, robustness, and decision rules

1. **Pre-decision temporal negative control.** Repeat the process and support-field ascertainment in `[D-1440,D)`. A large exposure contrast already present before D, especially after hospital adjustment, would indicate documentation selection or baseline imbalance rather than a post-decision process association.
2. **Documentation-gate balance.** Compare FiO2 and PEEP availability, chart-row density, and entry-delay distributions by strategy and hospital. A strategy difference that disappears after explicit gate reporting is a documentation finding, not a trajectory finding.
3. **Hospital heterogeneity.** Report every hospital with both labels, hospital-specific risks, interaction or heterogeneity intervals, and leave-one-hospital-out results. Hospital 252 contributes much of the process signal; if its removal eliminates the process association or makes the endpoint too sparse, the multicenter claim is inconclusive.
4. **Landmark heterogeneity.** Repeat by landmark and include landmark indicators. A result present only at one landmark is not a general near-term association.
5. **Window and field robustness.** Require the direction to be qualitatively stable across 6-, 12-, and 24-hour windows, and report FiO2, PEEP, `Total RSBI`, `4. RSBI (RR/Vt)`, and `SBT Method` separately. Do not rescue an adverse composite by selecting a favorable field or horizon.
6. **Delayed-entry sensitivity.** Repeat using `respchartentryoffset` only as a sensitivity to evaluate retrospective chart-entry timing. Discordance limits temporal interpretation.
7. **Negative control for nonventilator charting.** If feasible, compare a prespecified unrelated chart-density measure before and after D. A broad increase in all charting after one exposure label would suggest workflow/documentation intensity rather than respiratory management.
8. **Repeated-landmark sensitivity.** Repeat with one randomly or prospectively specified landmark per ICU stay and compare with the clustered repeated-landmark analysis.

Supportive results would be a reproducible, hospital-adjusted association between the exact care-plan label and a post-D support trajectory, with little or no pre-D contrast, adequate and similar ascertainment gates, consistent direction across reasonable horizons, and no single-hospital dependence. Even then, the conclusion is only that the care-plan label is associated with a subsequent documented management trajectory or process.

Adverse results include a null or reversed association, more support escalation than reduction, strong pre-D imbalance, materially different ascertainment gates, or loss of direction when hospital 252 is omitted. These would falsify the proposed general association or show that the apparent contrast is documentation/site selection; they would not prove that the care-plan choices are clinically equivalent.

Results are inconclusive if process fields remain too sparse, the support trajectory is observed only in one site, model estimates are unstable, missingness differs materially by strategy, or leave-one-hospital-out estimates change sign. Inconclusive results should preserve the parent negative liberation result rather than be converted into a claim about extubation.

## Evidence limits and required follow-up

Computationally checkable claims are the exact exposure counts, offset windows, label harmonization, setting changes, process-field indicators, ascertainment gates, hospital/landmark heterogeneity, and statistical association estimates. The database cannot by itself establish that a `Total RSBI` row was collected during an SBT, that any respiratory setting change was intentional weaning, that a care-plan decision was followed, or that a patient was extubated and remained liberated. Those claims require expert chart adjudication using richer clinical context and, for durable liberation, reliable extubation and reintubation timestamps through at least 48 hours. A causal treatment-effect claim would require a design addressing clinician selection and time-varying confounding, ideally a prospective or quasi-experimental study. The absent or incomplete evidence identified by the parent remains binding.
