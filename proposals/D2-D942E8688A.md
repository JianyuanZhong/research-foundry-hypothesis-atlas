# Provisional hypothesis: macrophage–fibroblast context modifies the intervention-associated fibroblast transcript response

Seed: D2-T1
Status: provisional, evidence-bound draft; no inferential result has been computed yet.

## Proposed relationship

Among rheumatoid-arthrititis fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) exposed to the interventions represented in the available E-MTAB-8316 assay, the direction and magnitude of the intervention-associated transcript response will depend on the macrophage context (monocyte/macrophage phenotype or coculture condition), rather than being a context-independent FLS response. Operationally, a context-dependent effect is a reproducible interaction between intervention and macrophage context after accounting for donor pairing and repeated context measurements.

This is a hypothesis about an intervention-associated expression pattern, not a claim that any drug acts directly on a specific target or that the expression change is causal.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

Target prioritization is consequential when an intervention appears effective in one inflammatory microenvironment but ineffective or potentially harmful in another. A context-dependent response would identify candidates whose therapeutic interpretation must be stratified by the local macrophage/fibroblast state and would motivate perturbation or patient-level validation. A null interaction would be equally useful: it would weaken the rationale for context-specific prioritization and redirect attention to intervention-specific or donor-specific effects.

The available evidence supports only that these datasets contain condition-specific FLS and macrophage-linked measurements and that the seed question is biologically plausible. It does not establish an interaction, a target mechanism, selectivity, or clinical benefit. The proposed advance is a donor-aware test of whether intervention response is context-dependent, with explicit controls for repeated donors, condition composition, and measurement differences.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Baseline: a donor-aware additive model, with separate intervention and macrophage-context main effects and no interaction. It answers whether either factor alone explains the response.

Substantive alternative: the same donor-aware model plus an intervention-by-context interaction, evaluated with a prespecified interaction statistic and uncertainty. This is scientifically informative because it tests the proposed heterogeneity directly; it is not merely a more complex predictor. A sensitivity analysis can use a hierarchical/shrinkage interaction across transcripts, but the primary decision must remain interpretable at the donor/condition level.

The alternative reveals whether the intervention response changes sign or magnitude across macrophage contexts, information the additive baseline loses. It does not reveal direct drug-target binding, off-target effects, or causal mechanism.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive result: a reproducible, donor-aware interaction with a prespecified direction or magnitude, stable across the available condition controls and not driven by one donor, assay batch, or a small number of transcripts. This would support context-dependent prioritization and justify a targeted perturbation or independent validation study.

Adverse result: no interaction, a context-independent intervention effect, or an interaction that reverses under donor-aware correction/condition controls. This would argue against context-specific prioritization from these data and favor an intervention-specific or donor-specific explanation.

Inconclusive result: too few verified conditions/donors, missing intervention/control labels, unstable feature mapping, or wide uncertainty. This would not refute the hypothesis; it would identify the missing assay or metadata needed before a stronger claim.
