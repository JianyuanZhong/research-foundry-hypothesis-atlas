# UKB 04: Post-infection outcomes when lung function and systemic reserve are discordant

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

Among those with poor lung function but preserved muscle strength and metabolic status, short-term mortality risk after hospitalization for infection is lower than among those with similar lung function but poor overall reserve.

## Study population and key data

Individuals with lung function, grip strength, and biochemical measurements before disease onset, and subsequent infection-related hospitalization.

## Basic Approach

Model both the occurrence of infection-related hospitalization and death within 30 days after admission, compare the interaction between lung function and systemic reserve, and use non-infection-related hospitalizations as controls.

## Significance of the Research

Separate respiratory susceptibility from systemic reserve for coping with acute illness.

## Main Challenges

Lung function measurements may have been taken many years after infection; case-only analyses are subject to selection bias.

## Competing Explanations

Reserve indicators may merely be proxies for prior disease or healthcare-seeking behavior.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

Examine the distribution of time from pre-illness measurement to admission; if the association is present for all hospitalizations, limit the claim to general reserve rather than infection specificity.
