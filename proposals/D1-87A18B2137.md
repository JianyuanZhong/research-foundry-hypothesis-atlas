# Post-TACE AFP Kinetics as a Dynamic Predictor of HCC Recurrence at 12 Months

## 1. Scientific Question and Hypothesis

**Question:** Does the dynamic change in alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) from pre-TACE to 4–8 weeks post-TACE predict HCC recurrence within 12 months, independent of baseline AFP level, liver function, and tumor burden?

**Hypothesis:** In patients with HCC treated with TACE, a greater proportional decline in AFP (≥50% reduction from pre-TACE baseline) within 4–8 weeks post-treatment is associated with significantly lower 12-month recurrence risk (HR < 0.70) after adjustment for baseline AFP, liver function (Child-Pugh proxy: albumin, total bilirubin), and tumor burden (number of TACE sessions as proxy for tumor extent). This dynamic AFP response captures information about treatment efficacy and residual tumor biology that is not available from the baseline AFP measurement alone.

**Falsification:** If the adjusted HR for AFP decline ≥50% vs. <50% is ≥0.85 (i.e., no meaningful reduction in recurrence risk) and the incremental C-index improvement over a baseline-only model is <0.02, the hypothesis is not supported.

## 2. Clinical Importance

- HCC recurrence after TACE occurs in 60–80% of patients within 2 years, driving mortality [K1].
- Current prognostic models for post-TACE recurrence use **baseline** (pre-treatment) AFP as a static predictor [K1, K3]. The dynamic response of AFP to treatment is a biologically distinct signal: it reflects both tumor necrosis (successful treatment) and residual/progressive disease (treatment failure).
- The Palihati 2026 review explicitly identifies "dynamic serum biomarkers such as AFP kinetics" as an emerging but under-studied dimension for treatment response prediction [K2].
- Neves & Soares 2026 systematic review of 36 AI-driven recurrence models confirms that no model has been generalized in clinical practice, and external validation is rare (6/36) [K1-ref]. This underscores the need for well-characterized, data-driven biomarker studies in large single-institution cohorts.
- Clinically, identifying patients with poor AFP response at 4–8 weeks post-TACE could trigger earlier intensification of surveillance (e.g., 6-week vs. 12-week imaging) or earlier consideration of alternative therapies (ablation, systemic therapy), potentially improving outcomes.

## 4. Population Definition

### Inclusion Criteria
1. Adult patient (age ≥ 18) with HCC diagnosis (诊断名称 contains `肝细胞癌` or `肝癌` or `HCC`)
2. Received TACE (procedure name contains `栓塞` or `TACE` in procedures table)
3. Has pre-TACE AFP measurement (甲胎蛋白 in labs, 定量结果 parseable, within 14 days before TACE `开始时间`)
4. Has post-TACE AFP measurement (甲胎蛋白 in labs, 定量结果 parseable, between 28 and 56 days after TACE `开始时间`)
5. Has ≥12 months of follow-up (next encounter or recurrence diagnosis ≥365 days after TACE, or last known follow-up ≥365 days)

### Exclusion Criteria
1. Prior HCC treatment (surgery, ablation, or prior TACE) before the index TACE — identified by prior encounters with HCC diagnosis and/or prior TACE procedures
2. Concurrent or prior systemic therapy (medications table: contains `索拉非尼`, `仑伐替尼`, `帕博利珠`, `阿替利珠`, `信迪利` etc.)
3. Hepatic resection or liver transplantation before or within 30 days of index TACE
4. AFP value > 1000 ng/mL at baseline (potential assay saturation, different biological behavior)
5. Missing baseline liver function (albumin AND total bilirubin both unavailable within 7 days of TACE)

## 5. Outcome Definition

**Primary outcome: HCC recurrence within 12 months of index TACE**

**Definition (composite, due to free-text diagnosis limitation):**
A patient is classified as "recurrence" if ANY of the following is met:
1. **Explicit recurrence diagnosis:** Any encounter within 365 days post-TACE has a diagnosis matching recurrence patterns: `术后复发`, `综合治疗后复发`, `复发术后`, `复发` (in context of liver/HCC diagnosis)
2. **New HCC diagnosis:** Any encounter >30 days post-TACE has a diagnosis matching `肝细胞癌` or `肝癌` (new HCC, not the original diagnosis)
3. **Repeat TACE for new lesions:** Any TACE procedure >60 days post-index-TACE (suggesting new/progressive disease requiring re-treatment)

**Censoring:**
- Last known follow-up (last encounter date) if < 365 days post-TACE
- Death (if identifiable from encounters or diagnoses: `死亡诊断`)
- Loss to follow-up

**Time scale:** Days from index TACE `开始时间` to recurrence or censoring.

**Critical limitation:** The diagnoses table has no timestamp. Recurrence timing is imputed from the encounter's `就诊时间`. This introduces potential misclassification if a recurrence diagnosis is recorded at a follow-up visit months after actual recurrence. The 12-month window is a pragmatic compromise.

## 6. Exposure Definition (AFP Kinetics)

**Index TACE date:** `开始时间` of the first qualifying TACE procedure.

**Pre-TACE AFP (AFP₀):**
- Most recent `甲胎蛋白` measurement within 14 days before index TACE `开始时间`
- Must be numeric-parseable from `定量结果`

**Post-TACE AFP (AFP₁):**
- Most recent `甲胎蛋白` measurement between 28 and 56 days (4–8 weeks) after index TACE `开始时间`
- Must be numeric-parseable from `定量结果`
- If multiple measurements in window: use the LAST one (closest to 8 weeks)

**Derived variables:**
1. **AFP decline ratio:** `AFP₁ / AFP₀` (lower = greater decline)
2. **AFP decline percentage:** `(AFP₀ - AFP₁) / AFP₀ × 100`
3. **AFP response category (primary):**
   - "Complete response": AFP₁ < 20 (near-normalization)
   - "Partial response": AFP₁ ≥ 20 AND decline ≥ 50%
   - "No response": decline < 50%
   - "Progression": AFP₁ > AFP₀ (increase)
4. **Log-transformed AFP₁:** `log(AFP₁ + 1)` (for continuous analysis)

**Sensitivity analyses:**
- Alternative windows: 21–42 days, 42–84 days
- Alternative categorization: median split of decline ratio
- Exclude patients with AFP₀ < 20 (already near-normal; limited dynamic range)

## 8. Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis
**Model 1 (Baseline-only Cox):**
```
Cox PH: recurrence ~ AFP₀ + albumin + total_bilirubin + age + sex + tumor_burden
```

**Model 2 (Dynamic AFP Cox):**
```
Cox PH: recurrence ~ AFP₀ + AFP_decline_ratio + albumin + total_bilirubin + age + sex + tumor_burden
```

**Model 3 (Categorical response Cox):**
```
Cox PH: recurrence ~ AFP_response_category + AFP₀ + albumin + total_bilirubin + age + sex + tumor_burden
```

### Discrimination
- C-index (Harrell's) for each model, with 95% CI (200 bootstrap resamples)
- **Key comparison:** ΔC-index = C-index(Model 2) − C-index(Model 1)
- NRI (Net Reclassification Improvement) and IDI (Integrated Discrimination Improvement) for Model 2 vs. Model 1

### Calibration
- Calibration plots (predicted vs. observed) at 12 months for Model 1 and Model 2

### Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Alternative recurrence definition:** Use only explicit recurrence diagnoses (exclude repeat TACE as recurrence proxy)
2. **Alternative AFP window:** 21–42 days and 42–84 days
3. **Exclude low-baseline AFP:** AFP₀ < 20
4. **Time-varying AFP:** If ≥3 AFP measurements available, fit a simple linear slope (AFP change per week) in the first 8 weeks
5. **Competing risk:** If death data available, use Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model

### Statistical Testing
- Primary: Adjusted HR for AFP decline ≥50% vs. <50% (Model 3)
- Secondary: Continuous AFP decline ratio (per 0.1 decrease in ratio)
- Interaction: AFP decline × baseline AFP (does the effect of decline depend on starting level?)
- Two-sided α = 0.05, no multiplicity adjustment (exploratory)

### Software
- Python 3.11: pandas, scipy, lifelines (Cox PH), scikit-learn (C-index, NRI)
- CPU only; expected runtime < 30 minutes for cohort construction + model fitting

## 9. Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis is **NOT SUPPORTED** if:
1. Adjusted HR for AFP decline ≥50% vs. <50% is ≥ 0.85 (95% CI includes 1.0)
2. AND ΔC-index (Model 2 vs. Model 1) < 0.02
3. AND the categorical response model shows no significant trend (p > 0.10)

The hypothesis is **SUPPORTED** if:
1. Adjusted HR for AFP decline ≥50% vs. <50% < 0.70 (95% CI excludes 1.0)
2. AND ΔC-index ≥ 0.03
3. AND the categorical response shows a significant dose-response trend (p < 0.05)

**Inconclusive:** HR between 0.70–0.85, or ΔC-index 0.02–0.03, or significant trend but HR not < 0.70.

## 10. Limitations

1. **Free-text diagnosis:** Recurrence identification relies on pattern matching in Chinese free-text. Sensitivity is limited; some recurrences may be missed (under-ascertainment). The composite definition (explicit recurrence + new HCC diagnosis + repeat TACE) mitigates but does not eliminate this.
2. **No unit column for labs:** Cross-assay pooling is unsupported. We assume a single AFP assay platform. If multiple platforms were used over 15+ years, AFP values may not be directly comparable. This is a significant limitation.
3. **No diagnosis timestamp:** Recurrence timing is imputed from encounter date, not the actual date of recurrence detection. This introduces right-censoring bias.
4. **Single institution:** Results may not generalize. The Neves & Soares review [K1-ref] highlights that 75% of HCC recurrence prediction models are single-center.
5. **Tumor burden proxy:** We use number of TACE sessions as a proxy for tumor burden, not actual tumor size/number (which would require imaging data not available in this dataset).
6. **No imaging data:** Recurrence is defined by diagnosis/procedure, not by imaging. This may miss radiographic-only recurrences.
7. **Selection bias:** Patients who receive follow-up AFP at 4–8 weeks may differ from those who do not (healthier, more compliant). This is a form of informative censoring.
8. **No HBV status:** HBV-related HCC may have different AFP kinetics. We cannot stratify by etiology.
9. **15-year span (2010–2026):** Treatment practices, assay platforms, and diagnostic criteria may have changed over time.

## 11. Method Alternatives

### Alternative A: Machine Learning (XGBoost)
- **Approach:** XGBoost with SHAP for feature importance, as in Zhu X et al. [K3]
- **Inputs:** Same covariates + AFP kinetics features (AFP₀, AFP₁, decline ratio, decline %, log AFP₁)
- **Target:** Binary recurrence at 12 months
- **Split:** 60/20/20 (train/validation/test) by time (not random) to avoid leakage
- **Evaluation:** AUC, calibration, SHAP for interpretability
- **Advantage:** Captures non-linear interactions (e.g., AFP decline × baseline AFP)
- **Disadvantage:** Less interpretable for clinical decision-making; requires more data for stable estimates; harder to establish causal direction
- **When to prefer:** If the Cox model shows non-proportional hazards or if the relationship is clearly non-linear

### Alternative B: Landmark Analysis with Time-Varying Cox
- **Approach:** Treat post-TACE AFP as a time-varying covariate in a Cox model
- **Advantage:** Uses all available AFP measurements, not just the 4–8 week window
- **Disadvantage:** More complex; requires careful handling of informative censoring; may be overpowered by measurement frequency rather than biological signal
- **When to prefer:** If the 4–8 week window shows weak signal but a longer-term trend is informative

### Preferred Method: Cox Regression (Model 1 vs. Model 2)
**Rationale:**
1. The primary scientific question is whether AFP kinetics **adds predictive value beyond baseline AFP** — this is a direct, interpretable comparison that Cox regression handles naturally.
2. The expected cohort size (2,000–5,000) is adequate for Cox with ~10 covariates but may be insufficient for stable XGBoost with 20+ features.
3. The HR for AFP decline is directly clinically interpretable: "patients with ≥50% AFP decline have X% lower recurrence risk."
4. The ΔC-index directly quantifies the incremental predictive value, which is the key scientific question.
5. CPU-only, fast, transparent.

**Decision rule:** If the Cox model shows significant non-proportional hazards (Schoenfeld residuals p < 0.05) or if the AFP decline effect is strongly non-linear, proceed to XGBoost as a secondary analysis. Otherwise, Cox is sufficient.

## 13. What Would Change Understanding

**If supported:** Establishes that post-TACE AFP kinetics is an independent, clinically actionable predictor of 12-month recurrence beyond baseline AFP and liver function. This would:
- Provide a simple, inexpensive (routine blood test) biomarker for risk stratification
- Inform the timing of follow-up imaging (earlier for poor responders)
- Guide decisions about treatment intensification (add ablation, systemic therapy)
- Add a dynamic dimension to current static prognostic models

**If not supported:** Suggests that the AFP response to TACE is largely determined by baseline tumor biology and liver function, and that the dynamic change does not add independent predictive value. This would:
- Redirect attention to other dynamic biomarkers (e.g., AFP-L3 ratio, PIVKA-II, inflammatory markers)
- Support the use of baseline AFP and liver function as sufficient for risk stratification
- Reduce unnecessary follow-up AFP testing in patients with good baseline profiles

**If inconclusive:** May indicate that the 4–8 week window is suboptimal, or that the effect is modified by baseline AFP level (interaction), or that the cohort is too small for adequate power. Would motivate a larger multi-institutional study or a different time window.
