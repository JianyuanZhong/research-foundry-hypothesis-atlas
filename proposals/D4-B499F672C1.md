# UKB-09: White Blood Cell Composition and Post-Infection Mortality — A UK Biobank Cohort Study

## Unresolved Question

Total white blood cell (WBC) count and C-reactive protein (CRP) are routinely measured inflammatory markers used to assess infection severity. However, it remains unclear whether the *composition* of WBCs — specifically the lymphocyte-to-neutrophil ratio (LNR) — provides independent prognostic information for post-infection mortality beyond what is captured by total WBC and CRP. This distinction is clinically important: if LNR adds predictive value, it could inform risk stratification and monitoring in infected patients; if it does not, the added complexity of reporting WBC differentials for prognostic purposes is not justified.

## Hypothesis

In UK Biobank participants with baseline blood counts, those with lower LNR (lymphopenia relative to neutrophilia) have significantly higher 30-day mortality after first infection hospitalization, independent of total WBC, CRP, age, sex, and BMI.

## Clinical Importance

Infection is a leading cause of hospitalization and death, particularly in older adults. Current clinical practice relies on total WBC and CRP for infection severity assessment, but these markers do not capture the balance between innate (neutrophil) and adaptive (lymphocyte) immunity. Lymphopenia has been associated with worse infection outcomes in specific populations (viral pneumonia, sepsis), but whether this association is independent of total inflammatory burden in a general population cohort is unknown. Resolving this question could:
1. Justify or refute the routine reporting of WBC differentials for prognostic purposes
2. Identify a subpopulation (low LNR) that may benefit from closer monitoring or immunomodulatory interventions
3. Clarify whether the prognostic signal is driven by lymphopenia (immune deficiency) or neutrophilia (excessive innate response)

## Evidence Base

**Supporting evidence:**
- [K1] Deng et al. (2026) showed NLR (inverse of LNR) had superior AUC (0.847) for predicting severe acute cholangitis compared to WBC (0.657) and CRP (0.711), and high NLR was associated with lower survival during hospitalization.
- [K2] Wu et al. (2026) demonstrated that lymphopenia was independently associated with in-hospital mortality in viral pneumonia (2363 patients), with the highest mortality in immunocompromised patients with lymphopenia (27.4%) vs. immunocompetent without lymphopenia (7.3%).

**Challenging/bounding evidence:**
- [K3] Zhao et al. (2026) found that in a stroke cohort (2,937 patients), NLR showed NO independent association with 12-month mortality after adjustment for CRP (OR 1.06, 95% CI 1.00-1.13, P=0.064), while CRP was independently associated (OR 1.01, 95% CI 1.01-1.02, P<0.001). This suggests that in some contexts, WBC composition adds nothing beyond CRP.

The gap: No large prospective cohort study has tested whether LNR independently predicts post-infection mortality in a general population, adjusting for both total WBC and CRP simultaneously.

## Study Design

### Population
- UK Biobank participants with baseline (visit 0) WBC, lymphocyte, and neutrophil counts
- Exclusion: hematologic malignancy (ICD10 C81-C96) or solid cancer (C00-C97) diagnosed before the index infection
- Exclusion: missing WBC, lymphocyte, or neutrophil at v0
- Exclusion: neutrophil count < 0.5 × 10⁹/L (to avoid division-by-zero and extreme LNR values)

### Exposure
- LNR = lymphocyte count / neutrophil count at v0
- Analyzed as: (a) continuous (per SD increase), (b) quartiles (Q1 = lowest LNR = highest risk)
- Sensitivity: NLR (inverse) as alternative

### Outcome
- Primary: Death within 30 days of first infection hospitalization
- Infection defined by ICD10 codes J00-J99 (respiratory) or A00-B99 (infectious) in 41270, with corresponding date in 41280
- Secondary: Death within 90 days; all-cause mortality (any time)

### Temporal Boundaries
- Index date: first infection hospitalization date (41280)
- Baseline: v0 blood draw (53-0.0)
- Requirement: v0 blood draw must precede infection date
- Sensitivity: restrict to infection >2 years after v0 (to reduce reverse causation)

### Covariates
- Age (53-0.0 minus 34-0.0), sex (31-0.0), BMI (21001-0.0)
- Total WBC (30000-0.0), CRP (30710-0.0)
- Albumin (30600-0.0) as nutritional/inflammatory marker

### Analysis
1. **Primary model**: Cox PH with LNR (continuous), adjusted for age, sex, BMI, total WBC, CRP
2. **Quartile model**: Cox PH with LNR quartiles, Q1 as reference
3. **Sensitivity analyses**:
   - Restrict to infection >2 years after v0
   - Exclude deaths <7 days after infection (to reduce peri-procedural deaths)
   - NLR (inverse) instead of LNR
   - Compositional analysis: log(lymphocyte/neutrophil) with total WBC as constraint
4. **Interaction**: LNR × age, LNR × sex

### Falsification Criteria
- If the fully adjusted HR for LNR (per SD) is between 0.9 and 1.1 (95% CI), the hypothesis is falsified: WBC composition adds no independent prognostic information beyond total WBC and CRP.
- If the HR is significant but driven entirely by the <2-year subgroup, the hypothesis is supported only for recent infections (reverse causation concern).
- If the HR is significant only in the unadjusted model and disappears after CRP adjustment, the hypothesis is falsified (CRP captures the signal).

### Expected Sample Size and Power
- Baseline WBC available: ~478,000
- Infection ICD10 codes: ~305,000 in first 30 instances (likely ~500,000+ total across 259 instances)
- Deaths: 44,499 total; post-infection 30-day deaths expected: ~5,000-10,000
- With 5,000+ events, the study is well-powered to detect HR differences of 1.1-1.2 per SD of LNR

## Competing Explanations

1. **Underlying disease**: Lymphopenia may be a marker of occult cancer, HIV, or malnutrition rather than an independent risk factor. Mitigation: exclude prior cancer; adjust for albumin (nutritional marker).
2. **Reverse causation**: Severe infection may cause lymphopenia (stress response), making LNR a consequence rather than a cause. Mitigation: require v0 blood >2 years before infection; exclude deaths <7 days.
3. **Age and comorbidities**: Older age drives both lower LNR and higher mortality. Mitigation: adjust for age; test LNR × age interaction.
4. **CRP captures the signal**: If CRP fully explains the LNR association, WBC composition adds no information. This is the primary falsification test.

## Method Comparison

- **Baseline (primary)**: Cox PH with LNR as continuous variable, adjusted for total WBC and CRP
- **Alternative (sensitivity)**: Compositional analysis using Aitchison geometry, treating lymphocyte and neutrophil as parts of the WBC whole. This could reveal whether the signal is driven by lymphopenia or neutrophilia specifically.
- **Why baseline is sufficient for primary analysis**: The Cox PH with LNR + total WBC + CRP adjustment directly tests the hypothesis. Compositional analysis is a refinement, not a prerequisite. If the primary model shows no signal, compositional analysis is unlikely to reveal one.

## Limitations

1. 41280 is first recorded inpatient diagnosis, not complete admission history — may miss some infections
2. Single baseline measurement may not represent stable WBC composition
3. Infection ICD10 codes from 41270 may include outpatient diagnoses (not all are hospitalizations)
4. No adjustment for immunosuppressive medications (20003 in AC parquet, separate join)
5. UKB population is relatively healthy (self-selected); results may not generalize to frail or immunocompromised populations
6. 30-day mortality window may be too short for some infections; 90-day sensitivity analysis addresses this
