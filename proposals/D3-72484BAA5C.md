# Donor-blocked, perturbation-anchored macrophage phenotype effects on rheumatoid arthritis FLS

## Scientific question

Does co-culture with **MerTK/CD206-negative** rather than **MerTK/CD206-positive** synovial tissue macrophages shift FLS toward a coherent inflammatory rather than repair program, and is that phenotype-specific axis sensitive to macrophage-directed MerTK inhibition?

The question is deliberately narrower than “macrophage–FLS crosstalk exists.” The tested unresolved claim is whether the measured macrophage phenotype predicts a reproducible FLS program after donor blocking, and whether a macrophage-directed perturbation moves FLS along the same axis. The experiment does **not** claim that co-expression identifies the ligand, receptor, or causal direction of any individual signaling pair.

## Clinical and mechanistic importance

RA synovium contains fibroblast and macrophage states that can sustain inflammation or support repair. If a MerTK/CD206 phenotype contrast changes a coherent FLS inflammatory/repair program, and macrophage-directed MerTK inhibition shifts that program in the predicted direction, the result would support a testable resolution-restoring target rather than a nonspecific co-culture response. Conversely, a null or donor-specific result would caution against treating an aggregate macrophage label as a mechanistic switch.

The strongest evidence already established by prior work is reciprocal stromal–myeloid signaling and macrophage heterogeneity in RA [K1, K2, K3]. None of the inspected works establishes that this particular MerTK/CD206 contrast produces a donor-blocked, perturbation-sensitive FLS program in the available ex vivo assay.

- Phenotype markers `MERTK` and `MRC1` are measured and retained for QC; they are sorting markers, not the FLS outcome program.
- Covariates/QC: `run`, FLS donor, macrophage donor, `cells`, and total normalized expression.
- Temporal boundary: each sample is a 48-hour post-intervention snapshot. No longitudinal transition, disease-progression claim, or pre/post within-cell state is inferred.

### Normalization

1. Parse the gene symbol before the first `|` in `feature`; verify that the 20 prespecified genes occur exactly once each.
2. Construct a sample-by-gene count matrix by exact `sample_id` join.
3. Compute sample size factors with median-ratio normalization (or edgeR TMM) across all 445 features; include `log(cells)` as a QC diagnostic, not as a substitute for biological blocking.
4. Transform to `log2(size-factor-normalized count + 1)` for the score analysis.
5. Standardize each gene using the mean and SD across all 33 samples before forming program scores; report sensitivity to using only the 10 primary phenotype samples for standardization.
6. Exclude a block only for a predeclared QC failure: missing sample join, zero total count, or a genotype/feature lookup failure. Do not exclude a block for an unfavorable direction.

## Primary estimand and baseline analysis

For each FLS-donor block \(b\), define:

\[
D_b = \left[\text{mean}_g z(\text{inflammatory genes})\right]_{Mneg}
-
\left[\text{mean}_g z(\text{inflammatory genes})\right]_{Mpos}
-
\left[\text{mean}_g z(\text{repair genes})\right]_{Mneg}
+
\left[\text{mean}_g z(\text{repair genes})\right]_{Mpos}.
\]

A positive \(D_b\) means the Mneg co-culture is more inflammatory and/or less repair-oriented than the paired Mpos co-culture. Report the inflammatory and repair components separately as secondary outcomes so a composite cannot mask an effect driven by only one component.

### Donor-blocked exact test

- Null hypothesis: within each FLS-donor block, phenotype labels are exchangeable and the median block-level composite difference is zero.
- Observed statistic: median of \(D_1,\ldots,D_5\).
- Randomization distribution: all \(2^5=32\) within-block phenotype sign flips, preserving the FLS donor block.
- Two-sided p-value: proportion of sign-flipped statistics with absolute value at least as large as the observed statistic.
- Uncertainty: exact sign-flip interval obtained by inverting the randomization test; report block-level \(D_b\), the median, and the interval in standardized units.
- Sensitivity: repeat with only the four exactly macrophage-donor-matched blocks; separately report inflammatory-only and repair-only scores.

This design prevents the invalid practice of treating ten samples as independent or applying an ordinary donor t-test. With five blocks, the smallest attainable two-sided randomization p-value is 0.0625. Therefore, the primary claim must not be framed as conventional \(p<0.05\); effect size, directional consistency, interval width, and perturbation anchoring are part of the interpretation.

## Substantive multivariate alternative

The baseline composite tests a fixed direction but cannot show whether many genes move coherently or whether MerTK inhibition lies on the same transcriptional axis. The substantive alternative is a donor-blocked latent-axis analysis.

### Fitting target

1. For every gene \(g\), compute the five block-level untreated phenotype differences \(D_{bg}=z_g(Mneg)-z_g(Mpos)\).
2. Fit the first principal component on the 20 prespecified gene-level \(D_{bg}\) vectors. This is a fitting target for a coordinated phenotype-difference axis, not a held-out-donor phenotype classifier.
3. Orient the component so positive loadings align with the prespecified inflammatory-over-repair direction.
4. Project the four MerTK-inhibitor shifts \(Q_{bg}=z_g(\text{inhibited Mpos})-z_g(\text{untreated Mpos})\) onto the same fixed axis.
5. Estimate the feature-level concordance between the phenotype difference vector and inhibitor shift vector, and the block-level concordance of \(D_b\) and projected \(Q_b\).

The scientific quantity of interest is whether MerTK inhibition moves the Mpos FLS state toward the Mneg/inflammatory side of the same axis. A positive projected shift is the prespecified mechanistic direction. An opposite shift is adverse to the MerTK-mediated interpretation, even if it is biologically interpretable through compensatory pathways.

### Uncertainty

- Use donor-block bootstrap over the five phenotype blocks, resampling FLS donor blocks with replacement. For each resample, refit the latent axis only when at least two distinct positive and negative directions remain; otherwise mark the resample unstable.
- For inhibitor anchoring, bootstrap the four available Mpos blocks with replacement and report the proportion of resamples with a positive projected shift and a positive feature-level correlation.
- Report bootstrap intervals as descriptive uncertainty, not as validation of an independent held-out population.
- If loadings change sign across bootstrap resamples or the projected inhibitor shift has an interval crossing zero, the multivariate axis is unstable and cannot support a directional mechanism.

## Perturbation interpretation

The MerTK-inhibitor anchor is corroborative, not a second independent proof of phenotype specificity. It has only four matched contrasts and no external validation. The FLS-only inhibitor pair is one block and can only check for an obviously large direct FLS response.

A directional claim that a particular macrophage ligand engages a particular FLS receptor requires orthogonal evidence: ligand and receptor measurement in the relevant cells, perturbation of the candidate pair, or protein-level/functional follow-up. Co-expression in this targeted panel is at most hypothesis generation.

## Falsification criteria

### Supportive result

The strongest available-data result would show:

- Median \(D_b\) is positive and at least four of five blocks are positive.
- The exact sign-flip interval excludes zero and the effect exceeds a prespecified minimal standardized median of 0.25.
- The result remains directionally positive in the four macrophage-donor-matched blocks.
- At least three of four MerTK-inhibitor contrasts project positively on the phenotype axis, with a positive bootstrap feature-level concordance interval.

This would support a phenotype-specific, donor-blocked FLS program and a MerTK-sensitive axis in this ex vivo assay. It would not establish clinical efficacy, in vivo causality, or a specific ligand–receptor mechanism.

### Adverse result

- Median \(D_b\) is near zero or negative, with inconsistent block directions.
- The interval includes zero, or the effect is driven entirely by the unmatched block 3.
- MerTK-inhibitor shifts are null or opposite the phenotype axis.

This would weaken the claim that the measured MerTK/CD206 phenotype drives a coherent FLS program in this assay. It would not disprove macrophage–FLS crosstalk generally.

### Inconclusive result

- Directions are consistent but intervals are wide because only five blocks are available.
- The primary composite is positive but inflammatory and repair components disagree.
- The phenotype effect is stable but MerTK-inhibitor anchoring is unstable or absent.

An inconclusive result should be reported as underpowered or mechanistically unresolved, not as evidence of no crosstalk.

## Compute and method-selection record

- CPU only; no GPU allocation is needed. Expected runtime is seconds to a few minutes for normalization, exact 32-sign-flip enumeration, PCA, and 2,000 block bootstrap replicates.
- The fixed composite baseline is retained because it directly tests the scientific direction with transparent donor-level uncertainty.
- The multivariate latent-axis alternative is retained because it can reveal coordinated gene movement and perturbation alignment that the composite average loses.
- A leave-one-FLS-donor-out predictive classifier is **not** the primary analysis: five blocks and phenotype-donor imbalance do not support a generalization claim. Leave-one-block-out stability may be reported only as exploratory sensitivity, never as the primary evidence.
- Neural-network or GPU models are deferred because they add no scientific information for five paired blocks and would obscure uncertainty.
