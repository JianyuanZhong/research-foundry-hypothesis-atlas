# Ventilatory Burden and Liberation from Mechanical Ventilation in ICU Patients

## A Falsifiable, Data-Bound Hypothesis for eICU-CRD (evolved from expert seed eicu-05)

---

### 1. Hypothesis

**Leading claim (to be tested):** In mechanically ventilated ICU patients, a composite ventilatory burden index (mechanical power, computed from tidal volume, respiratory rate, plateau pressure, and PEEP) provides more information about liberation failure (death before successful extubation, or re-intubation within 72 hours) than tidal volume alone, after adjusting for illness severity, body size, and hospital.

**Strongest rival:** Tidal volume is the dominant determinant of ventilatory burden, and the additional information from mechanical power (which incorporates respiratory rate, plateau pressure, and PEEP) is negligible or entirely explained by illness severity. Under this rival, the mechanical power index does not improve discrimination beyond tidal volume + APACHE score.

**What the leading claim would establish:** That a composite, body-size-adjusted ventilatory burden metric captures clinically meaningful heterogeneity in liberation risk that is lost when clinicians focus on a single parameter (tidal volume). This would support individualized ventilation targeting and inform the design of liberation-readiness algorithms.

**What it would not establish:** Causal effect of reducing mechanical power on liberation outcomes (requires RCT, e.g., PERMISS trial [K3]). Association with hospital-level practice differences. Mechanism by which ventilatory burden affects liberation (VILI vs. diaphragm dysfunction vs. both).

---

### 2. Scientific Opening

**Established:**
- Lung-protective ventilation with low tidal volume (6 mL/kg IBW) reduces mortality in ARDS (ARMA trial, 2000).
- Mechanical power (MP = 0.098 × Vt² × f + 4 × Vt × (Pplat − PEEP)) integrates tidal volume, respiratory rate, and pressure variables into a single metric of ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI) risk. [K3]
- Mechanical power normalized by dynamic compliance (MPCdyn) predicts liberation success in tracheostomized patients (cutoff ≤454.72 J/min). [K1]
- Driving pressure (ΔP = Vt/Cdyn = Pplat − PEEP) is independently associated with mortality in mechanically ventilated patients.
- Physiology-guided ventilation monitoring (including mechanical power, P0.1, diaphragm activity) is an emerging framework for individualized care. [K2]

**Unresolved:**
- Whether mechanical power (or a similar composite burden index) adds discriminative information for LIBERATION FAILURE specifically, beyond tidal volume alone, in a large, multi-hospital, non-ARDS-specific ICU population.
- The existing evidence for mechanical power and liberation is limited to a single-center, single-country study of tracheostomized patients (n=160). [K1]
- No large-scale study has tested whether the composite index outperforms the single parameter (tidal volume) that is already routinely targeted in clinical practice.
- Whether the relationship holds across diverse ICU populations (not just ARDS) and across hospitals with different ventilation practices.

**Why this matters clinically:**
- Prolonged mechanical ventilation (>7 days) is a major ICU complication associated with ventilator-associated events, diaphragm weakness, and increased mortality and cost.
- Current liberation decisions rely heavily on clinical judgment, RSBI, and simple bedside parameters. A validated composite burden index could support more objective liberation-readiness assessment.
- The PERMISS trial [K3] is actively testing whether reducing respiratory rate (and thus mechanical power) is feasible and safe, but results are not yet available. Our observational analysis can provide a large-scale prior for the expected effect size and identify subgroups most likely to benefit.

**Analytic cohort definition:**
- Patients with ≥1 measurement of ALL four parameters (plateau pressure, tidal volume set, PEEP, vent rate) during the first 48 hours of ICU stay.
- Expected N ≈ 20,379 (to be confirmed after temporal filtering and height requirement).
- Additional requirement: `admissionheight` present in patient table (for IBW calculation).
- Additional requirement: `apacheapdischarge` present (for severity adjustment).

**Liberation failure definition (primary outcome):**
- **Death before successful extubation** (unitdischargestatus = "Dead in Hospital" or "Dead in Unit") OR
- **Re-intubation within 72 hours of first extubation** (identified by `RT Vent On/Off` transitions in respiratoryCharting: off → on within 72h).
- Patients who are never extubated during the ICU stay and are discharged alive are classified as "incomplete" (censored) and handled in sensitivity analysis.

**Exposure definition:**
- **Mechanical power (MP):** Computed as MP = 0.098 × (Vt/1000)² × f + 4 × (Vt/1000) × (Pplat − PEEP), where Vt is in mL, f is respiratory rate (breaths/min), Pplat and PEEP are in cmH₂O. Units: J/min.
- **Body-size adjustment:** MP normalized by ideal body weight (IBW in kg). IBW calculated from admissionheight (cm) and gender: IBW_male = 50 + 0.91 × (height_cm − 152.4); IBW_female = 45.5 + 0.91 × (height_cm − 152.4).
- **Time window:** Mean of all MP values in the first 48 hours of ICU stay (or the last available value if <3 measurements).
- **Tidal volume (baseline):** Mean of `Tidal Volume (set)` in the first 48 hours, in mL/kg IBW.

**Covariates:**
- APACHE II score (`apacheapdischarge`)
- Age, gender (patient table)
- FiO2 (mean of `FiO2` in first 48h, if available)
- Duration of ventilation before first extubation (hours)
- Hospital (`hospitalid`, random intercept)

### 4. Analysis Plan

**Primary analysis:**
1. **Baseline comparison:** Compare MP (and MP/IBW) between patients who succeed vs. fail liberation. Report median (IQR), p-value.
2. **Model 1 (baseline):** Multilevel logistic regression with hospital random intercept. Outcome: liberation failure (binary). Predictors: tidal volume (mL/kg IBW), APACHE II, age, gender, duration of ventilation.
3. **Model 2 (alternative):** Same as Model 1 but replacing tidal volume with mechanical power (J/min/IBW).
4. **Model 3 (combined):** Include both tidal volume AND mechanical power to test whether MP adds information beyond Vt.
5. **Model comparison:** Compare Model 1 vs. Model 2 using:
   - Change in AUC (area under ROC curve)
   - Change in C-statistic
   - Likelihood ratio test (nested models)
   - Calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow or calibration plot)
6. **Sensitivity analyses:**
   - Restrict to ARDS patients (diagnosis table: ICD-9 518.81 or ICD-10 J80/J81)
   - Restrict to first 24h exposure window
   - Use median instead of mean for MP
   - Exclude patients with <3 MP measurements
   - Use driving pressure (Pplat − PEEP) as alternative to MP
   - Whole-hospital holdout validation (train on 80% of hospitals, validate on 20%)

**Alternative method (XGBoost):**
- Fit XGBoost classifier with the same features as Model 3.
- Compare AUC and calibration against the logistic regression.
- Report feature importance to assess whether MP or its components dominate.
- Whole-hospital holdout for transportability assessment.

**Falsification criteria:**
- **Supportive:** Model 2 (MP) has AUC > Model 1 (Vt) by ≥0.02, AND the MP coefficient in Model 3 is significant (p<0.05) after adjusting for Vt. The whole-hospital holdout AUC drop is <0.05.
- **Adverse:** Model 2 AUC ≤ Model 1 AUC (no improvement), OR the MP coefficient in Model 3 is non-significant (p>0.10), indicating MP adds no information beyond Vt.
- **Inconclusive:** AUC difference 0.005–0.02, OR significant MP coefficient in Model 3 but whole-hospital holdout AUC drop >0.05 (poor transportability).

### 5. Method Comparison: Baseline vs. Learned Alternative

**Baseline (Model 1):** Tidal volume + APACHE + demographics in a multilevel logistic regression. This is the "current clinical practice" model — clinicians target Vt ≤ 6 mL/kg IBW and use APACHE for severity.

**Learned alternative (XGBoost):** Non-parametric, captures interactions and non-linearities that logistic regression misses. Specifically:
- Can detect whether the MP–liberation relationship differs by APACHE stratum (effect modification).
- Can capture the interaction between Vt and respiratory rate (which is implicit in MP but not in Vt alone).
- Feature importance reveals which component of MP (Vt²×f vs. Vt×ΔP) drives the signal.

**What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses:**
- Non-linear threshold effects (e.g., MP may matter only above a certain APACHE level).
- Interaction between ventilatory burden and illness severity.
- Whether the composite index's advantage is driven by a single component (e.g., driving pressure) or the full composite.

**Why both are needed:** The logistic regression provides interpretable coefficients and is the standard for clinical prediction. XGBoost provides a performance ceiling and reveals non-linear structure. If both show the same conclusion (MP > Vt), the finding is robust. If they diverge, the discrepancy is itself informative (suggesting the relationship is non-linear and the linear model is misspecified).

**Resource estimate:**
- Logistic regression: <1 min on CPU.
- XGBoost: <5 min on CPU for 20K patients, 10 features.
- Whole-hospital holdout: 5-fold by hospital, <30 min total.
- Total compute: <1 hour CPU. No GPU needed.

### 6. Limitations

1. **Observational design:** Association, not causation. MP is a marker of ventilation strategy, not a randomized intervention. Confounding by indication (sicker patients may receive higher MP) is addressed by APACHE adjustment but not fully eliminated.
2. **Recording density:** MP is computed from discrete measurements, not continuous monitoring. The first-48h mean may not capture the full exposure. Patients with sparse data (<3 measurements) are excluded in sensitivity analysis.
3. **Plateau pressure availability:** Only 23,390 patients have plateau pressure. This is a subset of the 37,448 with tidal volume, introducing potential selection bias (plateau pressure is measured more often in ARDS or when clinicians are concerned about lung mechanics).
4. **Liberation failure definition:** Re-intubation within 72h is a proxy. Some re-intubations may be for non-ventilatory reasons (e.g., airway protection). Death before extubation includes patients who were never candidates for liberation (e.g., DNR).
5. **Single time period:** eICU-CRD covers 2014–2015. Ventilation practices may have evolved since then.
6. **US-only:** Generalizability to other healthcare systems is uncertain.
7. **No sedation data:** Sedation level affects liberation readiness but is not captured in a structured field in eICU (nurse notes may contain it but are not parsed).
8. **IBW calculation:** Uses admission height, which may be inaccurate (bedside measurement). No weight-based alternative is available.

### 8. What This Proposal Adds Beyond Inspected Work

1. **Scale and population:** First large-scale (N≈20K), multi-hospital (208 hospitals), non-ARDS-specific test of whether mechanical power outperforms tidal volume for liberation prediction. [K1] is n=160, single-center, tracheostomized only.
2. **Direct comparison:** Explicitly tests MP vs. Vt in the same model (Model 3), rather than comparing MPCdyn to other variables in a univariate screen.
3. **Transportability:** Whole-hospital holdout validation addresses whether the relationship generalizes across hospitals with different ventilation practices.
4. **Non-linear analysis:** XGBoost alternative reveals whether the MP–liberation relationship is non-linear or modified by severity, information that a linear model cannot provide.
5. **Actionable output:** If supportive, the result provides a validated composite index for liberation-readiness assessment that can be implemented at the bedside (all four parameters are routinely available on ventilator displays).
