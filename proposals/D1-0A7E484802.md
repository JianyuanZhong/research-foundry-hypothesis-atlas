# Is monitoring de-escalation a patient-level deterioration signal or a hospital practice fingerprint?

Lead working proposal - eICU 2.0 island, episode 4. Operation: evolve from expert seed
`[starting question]` (true parent `[prior hypothesis]`, "Reduced
testing and subsequent deterioration"). Transition context (not ancestry):
`[prior hypothesis]`, the episode-3 selection that tested static
observation-process features under hospital transport. Status: design frozen by Lead for
compilation; probe executed (numbers below); no reference solution executed at discovery.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

Intensive-care patients are monitored by repeated laboratory draws, and clinicians decide
how often to draw them. [K1] showed the *presence* and *timing* of a test order, not just
its value, carries survival signal (233/272 tests), so ordering behaviour encodes the care
process. The clinically consequential, unresolved question is dynamic and decision-facing:
when a patient is still physiologically unstable but the laboratory draw rate suddenly
falls, does that signal the patient is deteriorating and being under-monitored, or does it
simply mean the treating team judged them to be improving (and, at some hospitals, that
"less testing" is simply the local convention)?

These two readings are not rhetorical: they imply opposite actions (alert/escalate vs. no
alert). [K2] shows a validated deterioration model can keep discrimination (AUC 0.80)
while its risk estimates vary across hospitals, so the failure mode to exclude is not only
lost discrimination but non-transportable calibration and between-hospital effect
heterogeneity. Resolving this changes a monitoring-safety decision: whether a "testing
drop while unstable" flag is a usable patient-level trigger, a hospital fingerprint that
must be recalibrated per site, or a confounded artefact of improving severity.

Leading claim (H1): a sustained fall in the lab-ordering rate during hour 12-24 while an
instability score stays elevated predicts subsequent ICU deterioration **beyond** current
severity, with a roughly homogeneous patient-level effect across hospitals.
Strongest rivals: R1 severity/improvement confounding (the drop is a *consequence* of the
clinician's correct judgement that the patient improved; the effect should vanish after
conditioning on the concurrent instability/severity state); R2 practice artefact (the
effect exists on average but varies so much between hospitals that it is a site
fingerprint, not a portable patient signal).

## Design

Population: adult ICU stays, `age`>=16, `unitdischargeoffset`>=1440 min (>=24 h at risk),
decisive unit outcome (`unitdischargestatus` in {Alive, Expired}), hospital with >=200
eligible stays (so leave-one-hospital-out is estimable). Unit of analysis = stay.

Time zero (index) = 24 h after unit admission. Windows: exposure [0,12) vs [12,24) h;
outcome (24,48] h (unit death, and escalation: new vasopressor start from
`infusionDrug`/`treatment` in (24,48] h as a secondary composite).

Exposure (`D`, monitoring de-escalation): let `n_a` = draws in [0,12) h, `n_b` = draws in
[12,24) h; `Δ = log((n_b+1)/(n_a+1))`; `D = 1` if `Δ <= -log 2` (>=50% rate fall) **and**
the hour-24 instability score is in the upper tertile of the cohort. Rivals use `Δ`
continuously.

Instability: MEWS-like score (heart rate, respiration, systolic BP, temperature, SpO2)
computed from `vitalPeriodic` values nearest hour 24. Severity: `apachescore` and
`predictedicumortality`.

Estimands and baseline vs learned alternative:
1. Baseline (simple, interpretable): logistic regression of outcome on instability +
   APACHE + age, with vs without `D`; report adjusted OR for `D` and ΔAUROC.
2. Learned alternative (substantive): gradient-boosted trees (fallback: regularised
   logistic with splines) on the full 24 h lab+vital time-series summary (counts, rates,
   slopes, per-analyte values/missingness) with vs without monitoring-intensity/`D`
   features. A recurrent/sequence model is a further option if the tabular alternative
   shows the intensity channel matters; it is not required to answer the question.
   What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses: temporal *ordering and rate
   dynamics* (change in observation process) rather than a snapshot of current values.
   Approx budget: CPU, 132,900 stays, ~10^2 features, GBT fits < ~20 min per configuration
   on 16 CPUs; LOHO over ~30-60 qualifying hospitals. Measured vs unverified: row/hospital
   counts are measured; fit times are unverified estimates.
3. Decomposition: mixed-effects logistic model with random hospital intercept and random
   `D` slope; report between-hospital SD of the `D` slope vs the pooled slope (R2 test).

Split: leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO) transport; pooled out-of-fold predictions for AUROC,
Brier, calibration slope, O:E, net benefit. Report per-hospital calibration, not only pooled.

## Falsification criteria (pre-specified)

- Supportive (H1): adjusted OR for `D` > 1 with 95% CI excluding 1; ΔAUROC (with vs
  without monitoring-intensity features) >= 0.01 with 95% CI excluding 0; LOHO calibration
  slope in [0.8, 1.2]; between-hospital SD of the `D` slope < 50% of the pooled slope.
- Refuted: adjusted OR 95% CI includes 1 or ΔAUROC < 0.005 (R1), OR between-hospital SD of
  the `D` slope >= 100% of the pooled slope with pooled AUROC retained (R2: the signal is a
  hospital fingerprint, not a portable patient trigger).
- Inconclusive: wide CI / few qualifying hospitals / unstable sign across LOHO folds.

What each outcome means: supportive -> a candidate monitoring-safety flag for prospective
evaluation; refuted via R1 -> testing drop mainly tracks judged improvement, so it should
not be used as an alert; refuted via R2 -> any flag must be site-recalibrated and pooled
estimates are misleading; inconclusive -> more hospitals or chart/order-set data needed.
Scope: association/transport, not mechanism. A causal "under-monitoring causes harm"
claim would need order-set, staffing and bed-capacity data absent from eICU, plus clinical
adjudication of deterioration timing, i.e. another study.

## Unresolved / limitations

Exact `labname` strings bound at run time; the >=50% threshold and upper-tertile cut are
pre-specified but their sensitivity is untested. The drop may coincide with unit discharge
planning; stays discharged within the window are excluded by construction. No causal
mechanism; no chart/order-set/staffing data in eICU.
