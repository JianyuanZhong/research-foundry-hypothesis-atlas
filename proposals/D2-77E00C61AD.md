# Context-dependent fibroblast response to macrophage conditioning

## Seed and question
Develops expert seed D2-T1 (`[prior hypothesis]`). The unresolved question is whether a fibroblast transcript response to a defined intervention is context-dependent on the macrophage phenotype present in the coculture, rather than being a uniform fibroblast response.

## Clinical importance
Fibroblasts are a major target of fibrotic disease, but their pathogenic program may be induced, amplified or restrained by macrophage signals. A target that is responsive only in a particular macrophage context could be a more selective intervention point than a target whose expression merely differs between disease and control. Resolving this question can prioritize candidates for perturbation and reduce false prioritization from a global expression contrast.

## Evidence and closest work
The available data include E-MTAB-8316 (four FLS donor clusters, six nested monocyte donors, twelve condition samples) and GSE95588 (coculture HT-Seq counts, including r1/r2 derived from earlier GSE57723 and therefore not independent validation). E-MTAB-8316 has an explicit FLS/monocyte context design; GSE95588 provides a coculture validation set with the caveat that r1/r2 are not independent.

Closest inspected works:
- [K1] A study establishing the macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk framework in fibrotic disease, supporting the biological plausibility of context-dependent fibroblast responses.
- [K2] A study on donor-specific variability in fibroblast responses, motivating the donor-aware baseline and the need to separate donor effects from context effects.
- [K3] A study on transcriptomic profiling of macrophage-fibroblast cocultures, providing the methodological precedent for the mixed-model analysis used here.

## Hypothesis
In E-MTAB-8316, the fibroblast transcript response to macrophage conditioning is context-dependent: the direction or magnitude of the fibroblast response differs across macrophage phenotypes beyond what a uniform fibroblast response plus donor baseline can explain.

## Unresolved claim
Whether the observed fibroblast response pattern is genuinely context-dependent on macrophage phenotype, or whether it is explained by donor-specific baseline differences, batch effects, or the non-independent structure of GSE95588 r1/r2 samples.

## Analysis design
1. **Donor-aware baseline**: Fit a model with a uniform fibroblast response plus donor fixed effects. This establishes the null hypothesis that the fibroblast response is uniform across macrophage phenotypes once donor differences are accounted for.
2. **Mixed-model alternative**: Fit a model with an interaction term between macrophage phenotype and fibroblast response, plus donor random effects. This tests whether the fibroblast response is context-dependent on macrophage phenotype.
3. **Comparison**: Compare the two models using likelihood ratio test or AIC. The hypothesis is supported if the interaction term is significant after donor-aware adjustment and the pattern is directionally consistent across donors.

## Falsification criteria
- The interaction term is non-significant after donor-aware adjustment.
- The pattern is explained by donor or batch terms.
- The pattern is not directionally consistent across donors.

## Outcomes
- **Supportive**: The interaction term is significant, the pattern is directionally consistent across donors, and the result is robust to donor-aware adjustment.
- **Adverse**: The interaction term is non-significant, or the pattern is explained by donor/batch terms.
- **Inconclusive**: The interaction term is marginally significant, or the pattern is inconsistent across donors, or the result is sensitive to the choice of donor-aware adjustment.

## Limitations
- The data are transcriptomic and do not establish direct target action; perturbation validation is required for causal claims.
- GSE95588 r1/r2 are derived from GSE57723 and are not independent validation.
- The available metadata may not capture all relevant covariates (e.g., age, sex, batch).
