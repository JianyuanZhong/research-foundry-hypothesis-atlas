# Residual physiologic instability immediately before alive ICU discharge: a competing-risk MIMIC-IV test

Status: complete design proposal; no cohort scan, model fit, Harbor execution, or clinical result was run in this branch.

Parent/seed: expert-20260913-mimic-10, imported as [prior hypothesis]. This is an independent discharge-readiness direction, not an extension of the selected AKI branch.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The seed's clinical opening is concrete: an ICU discharge is an irreversible change in monitoring intensity, yet a patient can leave the ICU alive while still requiring oxygen or showing unstable vital signs. The unresolved question is whether a prespecified, persistent pattern in the final day identifies two different near-term failures—return to the ICU versus hospital death—or merely reflects where the patient was sent, how densely they were observed, or unrecorded treatment limitations.

[K1] supports the opening: in a small single-center surgical cohort, ward vital/laboratory flags after ICU transfer were associated with ICU readmission or death within 48 hours. That is evidence for a short-horizon association, not for a MIMIC-IV pre-discharge phenotype or a discharge policy. [K3] supports separating support, dependency, and destination: a small very-elderly cohort associated post-discharge care and respiratory-support indicators with one-year mortality. Its endpoint and population do not answer the 48-hour question. [K2] is a consequential limitation for the method choice: the ICU-readmission deep-learning literature has variable outcome windows, high heterogeneity and risk of bias, rare external validation, and limited interpretability. These works were inspected as abstract/provider-response excerpts only; no unavailable full text or supplementary method is claimed.

Strongest supported claim: residual abnormal observations and discharge-phase respiratory/dependency variables can be associated with adverse outcomes after ICU transfer or discharge in selected cohorts. Unresolved claim:

> Among adults discharged alive from an index ICU stay, using only information charted before ICU outtime, does a persistent multi-domain instability phenotype identify a higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of first same-hospital ICU readmission or hospital death, with the association remaining within immediate-destination and observation-opportunity strata?

Primary hypothesis: relative to adequately observed patients with no persistent abnormal domain, patients with at least two persistent domains in the final 24 hours—where oxygen support is reported separately from hypoxemia—have a higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU readmission or hospital death. Readmission and death are distinct causes; alive hospital discharge before 48 hours is a competing event. This is an observed prognostic association, not a causal effect of delaying discharge, increasing monitoring, changing oxygen, or altering destination.

The clinically consequential advance is not a new score. It is a time-ordered boundary test: it asks whether a simple, auditable discharge-phase phenotype has a component-specific signal after explicitly exposing the strongest nonphysiologic rivals. A positive result would justify external validation and clinician adjudication of discharge readiness; it would not by itself justify keeping patients in the ICU.

## Explanations and competing predictions

The leading explanation is residual physiologic instability. It predicts a graded contrast from stable to one to at least two persistent domains, component-specific readmission and death risks, and directionally similar effects among patients with adequate observation and across destination strata.

The strongest rivals are:

1. Destination/selection: immediate ward, step-down, hospice/palliative, or other destination changes both monitoring and the opportunity to be readmitted. A destination-stratified or standardized contrast that attenuates substantially supports selection/transport rather than a destination-independent physiology claim. Destination occurs at the index ICU exit and is not a pre-discharge confounder; the primary analysis therefore reports, rather than hides, this descriptive selection boundary.

2. Treatment limitation: hospital death may be concentrated among patients whose care was limited. MIMIC-IV fields available here do not provide a verified, structured goals-of-care or code-status variable. Hospice/palliative destination, hospital_expire_flag, discharge location, or death cannot be used as substitutes for treatment limitation. They are observable disposition/outcome markers, not adjudication. A death-only association cannot be interpreted as residual instability without this missing evidence.

3. Observation process: densely monitored patients have more chances to generate an abnormal value. If non-instability documentation density predicts outcomes similarly, or the instability contrast disappears in density strata, the physiologic interpretation is weakened. A missing measurement is unknown, never normal.

4. Residual severity: the final-day contrast may be a proxy for pre-window illness severity. Pre-exposure severity variables are used for standardization and strata; post-outtime data are prohibited. Disappearance within severity strata weakens the residual-instability claim but does not prove no physiology.

5. Treatment/support meaning: O2 flow or FiO2 can reflect chronic support or recording practice rather than hypoxemia. Hypoxemia, oxygen-support presence, flow, and FiO2 are reported separately. The data do not establish home oxygen, work of breathing, device setting, clinician intent, or respiratory failure.

6. Timing and competing events: death precludes later ICU readmission, and early hospital discharge precludes further in-hospital readmission observation. First-event clocks and Aalen–Johansen estimands make this explicit.

## Population and temporal boundary

Dataset is MIMIC-IV 3.1. The unit is one adult hospital admission and the first valid ICU stay for that admission.

1. Join `hosp/patients` to `hosp/admissions` on `subject_id`, then `icu/icustays` to admissions on `subject_id, hadm_id`.
2. Require `patients.anchor_age >= 18`.
3. Keep ICU rows with `intime < outtime`; select the earliest `intime` per `subject_id, hadm_id`. Do not select the earliest stay per subject across separate admissions.
4. Let the index ICU discharge landmark be `t_d = icustays.outtime`. Retain only patients alive at `t_d`: `admissions.deathtime` is null or strictly after `t_d`. Do not require survival to hospital discharge.
5. The primary predictor window is the half-open interval `[t_d - 24 hours, t_d), split into four consecutive 6-hour bins. `charttime` is the clinical measurement clock. `storetime` is not used to move measurements into the window; a storetime-lag sensitivity is reported only if prespecified before outcome inspection.
6. No predictor, dictionary-derived feature, note text, destination, or value at or after `t_d` is allowed. The immediate destination and outcome clocks begin at `t_d`.
7. The primary all-eligible flow retains exposure-unknown patients. The classifiable primary contrast requires adequate core observation in at least 3 of 4 bins for each required domain; a missing bin is not normal. Report unknown/insufficient as its own state and never silently exclude it from the flow.

Pre-specified boundary sensitivities are: at least one valid SpO2 or oxygen-support record in 3 bins, all 4 bins, and a final 6-hour-only window. These are sensitivity analyses, not opportunities to choose a favorable window.

## Exposure construction

The exposure is built only from `icu/chartevents` joined to `icu/d_items` by `itemid`, filtered by `stay_id` and the index window. Required chartevents fields are `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, charttime, storetime, itemid, value, valuenum, valueuom, warning`. Required d_items fields are `itemid, label, abbreviation, linksto, category, unitname, param_type, lownormalvalue, highnormalvalue`.

The exact item set is:

- 220045 Heart Rate;
- 220052 Arterial Blood Pressure mean;
- 220210 Respiratory Rate;
- 220277 O2 saturation pulseoxymetry;
- 223834 O2 Flow;
- 223835 Inspired O2 Fraction;
- 223761 Temperature Fahrenheit and 223762 Temperature Celsius, with Fahrenheit converted to Celsius;
- 226732 O2 Delivery Device(s), descriptive only and not converted into a numeric instability value.

The prior frozen MIMIC dictionary audit recorded these label and linksto mappings. The Harbor readiness gate must re-read the d_items rows from the configured archive and fail closed if any item label, linksto, unitname, or param_type is inconsistent; it must not substitute an item by approximate label. Numeric values must be finite and unit-compatible. Text-only values and values marked invalid by the source quality fields are excluded from that bin. For each bin use the median for vital signs and the maximum for oxygen flow/FiO2; do not impute a missing bin.

Domain flags are fixed before outcome inspection:

- hypoxemia: any SpO2 < 92%;
- oxygen support: any O2 Flow > 0 L/min or FiO2 > 0.21, reported separately from hypoxemia;
- hemodynamic: any MAP < 65 mmHg or heart rate > 100 or < 50 beats/min;
- ventilatory pattern: any respiratory rate > 24 or < 8 breaths/min;
- temperature: any temperature < 36.0 or > 38.3 Celsius.

A domain is persistent if flagged in at least 2 of 4 bins. The four primary exposure states are:

- stable/no persistent physiologic domain;
- one persistent domain;
- at least two persistent domains;
- unknown/insufficient opportunity.

A patient is classifiable only when the required core measurements are available in at least 3 bins; if a domain lacks opportunity, assign unknown rather than normal. The primary contrast is at least two persistent domains versus stable. Report one-domain contrasts, each domain, hypoxemia versus support, adjacent-bin changes, and within-window range/slope as secondary descriptors. These cutoffs are not to be tuned after observing outcomes.

## Outcomes and competing-risk estimands

Outcome risk begins at `t_d`.  Use the first event in the half-open interval `[t_d, t_d + 48 hours)`:

- ICU readmission: a subsequent `icu/icustays.intime` for the same `subject_id, hadm_id`, with `intime >= t_d` and `intime < t_d + 48h`; never count the index stay.
- Hospital death: `hosp/admissions.deathtime` in the interval.
- Alive hospital discharge: `hosp/admissions.dischtime` in the interval, provided no earlier death or ICU readmission.
- Event-free at 48 hours: alive, no readmission and not discharged before the endpoint.

If death and readmission timestamps tie, assign the earliest clinical time using a frozen precedence rule and emit a data-integrity flag; do not duplicate the person in both causes. A readmission followed by death is counted as a first-event readmission in the primary competing-risk estimand; report death after readmission as a secondary descriptive outcome, not as a replacement label.

Primary estimands are the 48-hour cumulative incidence function (CIF) for first ICU readmission, first hospital death, and their composite, each with alive hospital discharge as a competing event, and the absolute risk difference between at-least-two-persistent-domains and stable. Use Aalen–Johansen estimates with bootstrap confidence intervals. The adjusted analysis fits cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards in six-hour intervals for readmission, death, and alive discharge, then standardizes the 48-hour CIFs over the observed covariate distribution. A multinomial first-event model is a sensitivity analysis only. Report cause-specific hazard ratios as secondary associations, never as the primary clinical risk measure.

The same-hospital restriction means the study cannot see post-discharge readmissions after alive hospital discharge. It is a limitation of the estimand, not a missing-data fix.

## Rivals and audit variables

Immediate destination is derived from the first `hosp/transfers` row after `t_d`, joined on `subject_id, hadm_id`, ordered by `intime`. Use `transfer_id, eventtype, careunit, intime, outtime`; if no row is available, destination is unknown. Keep all destinations, including ward, step-down/intermediate if represented, hospice/palliative, and unknown. Ultimate disposition is separately `hosp/admissions.discharge_location` and is never substituted for the immediate destination.

Pre-discharge residual severity uses only information strictly before `t_d`: age, gender and admission type from patients/admissions; ICU timing and length from icustays; creatinine (primary labevents item 50912), lactate, hemoglobin, platelet, WBC and glucose from `hosp/labevents` plus `hosp/d_labitems`; and pre-window support/procedure indicators from `icu/procedureevents`. Required labevents fields are `labevent_id, subject_id, hadm_id, itemid, charttime, storetime, value, valuenum, valueuom, ref_range_lower, ref_range_upper, flag`; d_labitems fields are `itemid, label, fluid, category`. Exact labels/fluids/units must be dictionary-verified with fail-closed collision handling. No post-`t_d` data, outcome, destination, or learned prediction is an adjustment variable.

Observation opportunity is measured per 6-hour bin as counts of valid core chartevents and all non-core chartevents, labevents, inputevents and outputevents. Use quartiles of non-instability documentation density defined without the outcome. The observation audit is a process diagnostic, not a negative-control outcome. A similar density gradient for a process-only feature or strong attenuation after density stratification weakens the physiologic interpretation.

Treatment/support evidence is descriptive only. Bind `icu/procedureevents` by `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id` and use `starttime, endtime, storetime, itemid, value, valueuom, ordercategoryname, ordercategorydescription, statusdescription`; bind `icu/inputevents` with its start/end and amount/rate/totalamount fields for observation and administered-support summaries; bind `icu/outputevents` by `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id` and `charttime, itemid, value, valueuom`. These records show documentation/administered support, not indication, response, goals, or treatment effect.

Discharge-note text is excluded from primary predictors and adjustment because it can be charted after `t_d` and contain future information. The configured note tables expose `note/discharge.note_id, subject_id, hadm_id, note_type, note_seq, charttime, storetime, text` and `note/discharge_detail.note_id, subject_id, field_name, field_value, field_ordinal`. `hosp/omr` is not a verified goals-of-care field. No reliable structured treatment-limitation variable is available in the verified catalog. A blinded clinical adjudication of code status, comfort-focused care, discharge intent, and chronic home oxygen is essential for a stronger interpretation.

## Matched methods: baseline and substantive temporal alternative

The method comparison is about a scientific uncertainty—whether persistence, volatility and missingness carry signal beyond fixed summaries—not about chasing a small AUROC increase.

### Auditable baseline (primary)

Inputs are identical to the temporal model: four six-hour bins in [t_d-24h,t_d), the eight physiologic channels/item bindings, domain flags, missingness/opportunity indicators, pre-t_d severity, age/gender/admission type, ICU timing and observation counts. The baseline represents each channel with bin medians/maxima, last value, min/max, adjacent-bin change/slope, persistent-domain indicators, support/hypoxemia indicators, missingness and observation density. It fits cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards for readmission, death and alive discharge, with training-frozen standardization and prespecified regularization. It estimates standardized 48-hour CIFs and bootstrap intervals. Destination-stratified and unadjusted contrasts are reported; an analysis including immediate destination is explicitly post-landmark descriptive sensitivity, not causal adjustment.

### Learned temporal alternative (secondary)

Use the same patients, predictors, outcome precedence, 48-hour interval and deterministic subject-level split. Represent the final day as 12 two-hour bins with the eight physiologic channels, missingness masks, elapsed time, observation counts, domain indicators, and the same pre-t_d static severity. A GRU with hidden size 32, dropout 0, cause-specific hazard heads for readmission/death/alive discharge, Adam optimizer, fixed seed, at most 50 epochs and early stopping on validation cause-specific Brier score is fitted. Split by SHA-256(subject_id) into 70% train, 15% validation and 15% test; no subject or admission crosses splits. Normalization, exposure-state thresholds, destination categories and sparse-cell rules are fit/frozen on training data only.

Primary learned evaluation is held-out 48-hour cause-specific Brier score and calibration slope/intercept for each cause and the composite, with bootstrap confidence intervals over subjects. AUROC/AUPRC are secondary. Compare models on the identical held-out subjects and report whether temporal inputs improve calibrated competing-risk prediction and whether the prespecified persistent phenotype remains directionally interpretable. Saliency or attention-like summaries are not mechanisms.

The GRU may reveal whether order, persistence, volatility and missingness contain information that medians/last values lose. The baseline is selected as primary for auditability and direct clinical interpretation. The temporal alternative is retained because it tests a substantive temporal uncertainty and is feasible as a small tabular-sequence model. Transformers, causal-fused models and GPU-dependent large models are deferred: missing treatment limitation, clinician intent, discharge rationale and intervention data would remain unresolved regardless of capacity. Revisit them only after adjudicated treatment-limitation and external validation data.

## Falsification and decision rules

Supportive result: the at-least-two-persistent-domain group has a higher standardized 48-hour composite CIF than stable, with uncertainty excluding a clinically negligible prespecified difference; the direction is not confined to one immediate destination or the highest observation-density stratum; readmission and/or death components are reported coherently; and ordered temporal features improve held-out calibration while the phenotype contrast remains. This supports a reproducible prognostic association compatible with residual instability. It does not establish a causal discharge rule, mechanism, or benefit of extra monitoring.

Adverse result: the primary contrast is absent with adequate precision; the association is only oxygen support and not hypoxemia/vital domains; it disappears after severity or destination stratification; a process-only documentation-density gradient is similar; or it depends on post-discharge observation. This favors selection, severity, treatment/support meaning or observation artifacts and argues against claiming an intrinsic residual-instability phenotype. Predictive improvement without a stable phenotype contrast is predictive utility only.

Inconclusive result: sparse events, high unknown fraction, unit/dictionary mismatch, excessive tie/clock anomalies, or intervals that remain compatible with both clinically meaningful benefit and null. Do not move thresholds, merge states, or change windows after seeing outcomes. Continue only with a pre-specified replication, broader cohort or adjudication plan. A clinical conclusion about treatment limitation, discharge intent, chronic home oxygen, work of breathing, or whether changing monitoring improves outcomes requires clinician review, direct evidence or a prospective intervention study.

Prespecified process falsifications include: (a) a future/leakage audit that injects post-t_d measurements must show a large artificial performance increase and is a packaging check, not a scientific result; (b) randomly permuted exposure within subject must remove the ordered contrast; (c) a process-only documentation-density feature should not reproduce the full physiologic signal; (d) shifting the window earlier by 24 hours should attenuate a truly immediate discharge signal, while a stable earlier signal would favor baseline severity; and (e) split integrity and duplicate stay/admission checks must be zero-tolerance. These tests are diagnostic and are not evidence for physiology by themselves.

## Missing evidence and scope limits

This MIMIC experiment cannot adjudicate code status, comfort-focused goals, clinician discharge intent, chronic home oxygen, work of breathing, bedside examination, nursing concern, or actual post-ICU monitoring intensity. It cannot observe readmission after alive hospital discharge, external-hospital events, or total-body oxygen/volume physiology. Discharge notes are available but excluded from the primary model because of future-information leakage and lack of validated structured extraction. It cannot prove that a residual instability association is causal or mechanistic. Stronger conclusions require blinded clinical adjudication, external validation in another hospital, richer respiratory/device data, and, for an intervention claim, prospective monitoring or discharge-policy evaluation.
