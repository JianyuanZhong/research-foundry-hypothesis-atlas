# Incremental decision information from creatinine–cystatin C discordance in UKB

## Child decision and unresolved clinical claim

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`. This child preserves the parent’s exact UKB snapshot, four-table binding, recorded-code evidence boundary, population concept, five-year competing-risk follow-up and discordance-by-grip question. It makes one substantive repair: the primary estimand now asks whether adding cystatin C changes out-of-sample renal-risk information beyond a creatinine-based assessment, while the discordance-by-grip interaction becomes a prespecified secondary heterogeneity estimand.

The strongest available evidence is narrower than the proposed claim. The locally read Haines et al. critical-illness study supports concern that creatinine-based filtration estimates can be misleading during muscle loss, but it does not establish a community UKB association, a grip interaction, the meaning of a UKB N17 code as adjudicated AKI, or benefit from cystatin-C testing.

The primary falsifiable claim is:

> In UKB participants aged 40–69 with valid baseline creatinine, cystatin C, bilateral grip and index date, after excluding previously recorded paired N17/N18 codes, adding cystatin-C information (represented by D=eGFRcys−eGFRcr) to a creatinine-based risk model with age, sex, BMI and grip improves held-out five-year prediction and changes a prespecified hypothetical risk-triggered review decision for a subsequently recorded paired N17 code.

This is an incremental prognostic-information claim. It is not a claim that cystatin C improves measured GFR, identifies true muscle mass, causes events, or has proven clinical utility. A positive result would motivate external validation and a prospective decision-impact study of selective cystatin-C measurement; it would not by itself justify changing care.

## Exposure construction

After units are verified, convert creatinine from micromol/L to mg/dL and retain cystatin C in mg/L. Freeze the race-free CKD-EPI 2021 equations before fitting:

`eGFRcr = 142 × min(Scr/k,1)^alpha × max(Scr/k,1)^−1.200 × 0.9938^Age × 1.012^Female`, with female `k=.7, alpha=−.241` and male `k=.9, alpha=−.302`;

`eGFRcys = 133 × min(Scys/.8,1)^−.499 × max(Scys/.8,1)^−1.328 × .996^Age × .932^Female`.

Define `D=eGFRcys−eGFRcr`. Standardize eGFRcr, D and grip using training-split parameters only. Lower D is the prespecified direction, but is not called filtration error or low muscle mass.

## Primary estimand: incremental information beyond creatinine

Freeze an eid-level 60/20/20 train/validation/test split before fitting. All imputation, transformations, selection weights, tuning and censoring weights are learned in training only. Fit transparent cause-specific N17 and death models with competing-risk CIF integration and these nested predictors:

- `M_CR`: eGFRcr, age, sex, BMI;
- `M_CRG`: `M_CR` plus grip;
- `M_CYS`: `M_CRG` plus D;
- `M_INT`: `M_CYS` plus D×grip.

The primary incremental comparison is `M_CYS` versus `M_CRG`, because it asks whether cystatin information adds to creatinine-based renal assessment after function and standard covariates are available. `M_CRG` versus `M_CR` separates the contribution of grip. Report `M_CYS` versus `M_CR` as the broader creatinine-only comparison.

On the untouched test set, estimate:

1. the five-year N17 CIF for each model with death competing;
2. calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope and risk-stratified observed-versus-predicted CIF;
3. time-dependent discrimination;
4. IPCW Brier score and integrated Brier score through five years, with `DeltaIBS = IBS(M_CRG)−IBS(M_CYS)`; positive values favor cystatin augmentation;
5. reclassification counts and observed CIFs in prespecified illustrative risk bands `<1%`, `1–<5%`, `5–<10%`, and `>=10%`.

Use participant bootstrap intervals, preserving eid-level dependence and repeating the complete prediction/evaluation pipeline within each resample where feasible. The primary computational support criterion is a positive, stable held-out `DeltaIBS` with calibration not materially degraded; a small AUC change alone is not evidence of clinical value.

To make decision relevance explicit without pretending that UKB supplies a clinical action threshold, calculate decision-curve net benefit for a hypothetical action “flag for intensified kidney review or confirmatory testing” at risk thresholds 1%, 5% and 10%, and compare `M_CYS` with `M_CRG`, treat-all and treat-none. These are scenario analyses, not validated thresholds or treatment recommendations. Report flagged counts, observed events and bootstrap intervals; do not interpret a threshold if either action cell has fewer than 20 observed N17 events or fewer than 20 non-events. Clinical utility remains unestablished because the dataset has no treatment, referral, testing-harm, resource or patient-preference outcomes.

## Support gates, falsification and interpretation

Required gates are authoritative code/date semantics, units, biomarker/index compatibility, death meaning, administrative cutoff, at least 100 verified post-index N17 events and at least 20 participants in each four-sign D/grip quadrant for the transparent interaction. The learner and decision curves additionally require at least 200 N17 events, 100 competing deaths, adequate support at every reported grid/action cell and the decision-cell minimums above. If provenance or the transparent gate fails, report only the audit and cohort flow as inconclusive. If only the learner gate fails, fit and report transparent models and defer the learner.

Prespecified checks are:

- strict equal-suffix code/date pairing and same-day death ordering;
- N17-only versus N17/N18 baseline exclusion;
- two-year survivor/landmark analysis, labelled as a changed population;
- raw-biomarker sensitivity and repeat-biomarker sensitivity only if timing is verified;
- training-seeded permutation of D within sex-by-age strata, with any persistent incremental signal treated as leakage or implementation failure;
- K35 negative-control comparison only after exact semantics/support are verified;
- complete-case versus selection-weighted A-target comparison, with weight diagnostics;
- calibration and support plots before any decision-curve interpretation.

Strong supportive evidence requires all provenance/support gates, positive held-out `DeltaIBS` with justified uncertainty, no material calibration harm, reproducible positive decision-curve increment at a supported prespecified threshold, and a qualitatively stronger N17 than verified K35 signal. A positive `I5` alone is association/effect-heterogeneity evidence, not support for decision information.

Adverse evidence is a null or negative held-out increment, calibration degradation, unstable reclassification, loss under strict timing or selection sensitivity, a comparable supported K35 signal, or a learned surface that is poorly calibrated/unsupported. Inconclusive evidence includes missing semantics, units, timing or cutoff; inadequate events/cells; failed positivity; unpaired dates; or uncertainty too wide to distinguish useful from negligible information.

Computationally checkable claims include joins, missingness, code/date pairing, fitted equations, split integrity, CIFs, calibration, Brier scores, discrimination, reclassification, decision curves, interaction contrasts, support and permutation behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to establish whether N17 is AKI, whether events are clinically meaningful or complete, whether discordance reflects filtration or muscle physiology, whether a patient-management action improves outcomes, and whether the result transports externally. Prospective decision-impact testing and independent validation are required before clinical recommendations.
