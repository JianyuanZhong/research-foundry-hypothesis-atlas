## Interpretation and falsification

Supportive evidence requires positive adequately precise full-pipeline gain, stable sign across at least 18/20 locked partitions and reasonable timing selectors, no single-patient dominance, adequate overlap/ESS, linkage-placebo extremeness, and no comparable estimable pre-event placebo gain. It supports only reproducible short-horizon predictive information in selected observed repeat-TACE episodes.

Adverse evidence includes zero/worse M1 performance, intervals excluding the prespecified minimum useful gain in the adverse direction, partition/timing instability, a comparable pre-event gain, failure against the correct P-linkage placebo, or dominant influence. These favor serial persistence, measurement workflow, regression to the mean, or selection explanations and prohibit event-specific language. A precise null may rule out only a prespecified small standardized gain in this selected observed cohort, not biological equivalence. Sparse falsification panels, failed denominators, poor positivity, broad intervals, or unresolved unit semantics are inconclusive.
