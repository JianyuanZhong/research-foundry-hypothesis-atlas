# Leakage-safe repeated assay history beyond current state and a remote anchor after repeat TACE

## Unresolved question and substantive advance

Among adults already selected for a recorded first repeat TACE and with an observed complete same-assay `B/P/Y` triplet, does the latest leakage-safe *intermediate* albumin or total-bilirubin value add reproducible prediction of the next observed same-assay value `Y` beyond (i) the contemporaneous pre-repeat value `P`, (ii) the remote pre-TACE2 anchor `B`, (iii) a compact decision-time core, and (iv) how often and how recently the assay was observed between TACE2 and repeat TACE?

This is a prognostic monitoring experiment, not a treatment-effect experiment. The principal parent established that an assay-specific `B`-beyond-`P` design is computable. This child asks the more clinically informative longitudinal question: after both current state and a single remote anchor are known, is an additional pre-repeat measurement informative, or is the apparent value of “history” exhausted by those two levels and by the observation process?

The strongest evidence-supported claim is now feasibility only. A complete-source, outcome-direction-blind audit scanned every row in the five required HCC sources. It reproduced 319 systemic-record and 1,491 comparator patients, 159 and 526 selected repeat events, 40/118 albumin triplets, and 41/119 total-bilirubin triplets. Under the leakage-safe history clock, an intermediate value exists for 37/40 systemic and 108/118 comparator albumin triplets and 38/41 systemic and 108/119 comparator bilirubin triplets. The audit did not summarize `Y`, its direction, predictor–outcome associations, or any model result.

The unresolved falsifiable claim is that the numeric intermediate value adds out-of-fold prediction after the current value, remote anchor, observation-pattern controls, and compact core. Support would establish only incremental serial predictive information for the next *observed* assay value in this selected and observed cohort. It would not establish toxicity, hepatic failure, treatment benefit, clinical utility, causation, or a reason to alter TACE or systemic therapy.

## Locked B/P/Y and leakage-safe repeated-history clocks

For each selected repeat event and assay define:

- `B`: latest valid uncensored result on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1; day 0 is excluded.
- `P`: latest valid uncensored result in `event_encounter` during `[event_time−72 hours,event_time)`; exact `event_time` is excluded.
- `Y`: valid uncensored result in the same `event_encounter` during `(event_time,event_time+72 hours]` nearest nominal `event_time+24 hours`; choose the earlier timestamp on an exact-distance tie.

The assay-specific evaluation cohort requires `B`, `P`, and `Y` under those selectors. Expected counts are albumin 40 systemic/118 comparator and bilirubin 41/119. Albumin and bilirubin remain separate native-value outcomes and patient sets; no cross-assay pooling, rank score, change outcome, or joint primary score is allowed.

Define candidate intermediate-history rows `H_rows` using the exact same assay label and all of these conditions:

1. `检验时间 >= tace2_time`;
2. `检验时间 < event_time−72 hours`;
3. `检验时间 < P_time`;
4. `就诊号 != event_encounter` after normalized string comparison;
5. valid uncensored parser and locked duplicate rule.

Thus no history row can be `P`, share the selected P/Y encounter, occur in the 72-hour current-state buffer, or occur post-event. Preserve every qualifying timestamp for counting, but lock the numeric repeated-history feature `L` to the latest qualifying row, resolving an exact-time tie by final source order. Define pre-outcome observation-process variables: `H_any=1[N_H>0]`, `N_H` as the number of distinct qualifying timestamps, `logN=log(1+N_H)`, and `R_hours=event_time−L_time` when observed.

The outcome-blind audit supports this parsimonious representation: `H_any` is observed for 37/40 and 108/118 albumin patients and 38/41 and 108/119 bilirubin patients. Median `N_H` is one in both arms and assays; only 18 systemic and 42 comparator patients per assay have at least two *intermediate* timestamps. Therefore do not fit an intermediate-history OLS slope, spline, minimum/maximum, variability, threshold, sequence model, or trajectory clustering. A slope from `B` plus one intermediate value is merely a two-point secant and adds avoidable time/value interaction at this sample size. The one latest intermediate numeric value is the locked substantive history feature; `H_any`, `logN`, and recency are controls for observation intensity/timing, not biological trajectory claims.

When `H_any=0`, training-fold median-impute `R_hours` and `L`; include `H_any` in both nested models so imputation does not equate “unobserved” with a median measured patient. Center/scale continuous variables using outer-training data only.

## Three locked nested models and primary estimand

For each assay fit the same complete-triplet patients and same folds:

`M_P: Y ~ P + arm + age + sex + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_lead_hours`

`M_B: Y ~ P + B + arm + age + sex + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_lead_hours + H_any + logN + R_hours`

`M_L: Y ~ P + B + L + arm + age + sex + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_lead_hours + H_any + logN + R_hours`

`P_lead_hours=event_time−P_time` and is positive. `M_B` deliberately controls whether, how often, and how recently intermediate testing occurred while withholding its numeric value. This separates incremental numeric history from a clinician/observation-process signal. `M_P` is the contemporaneous benchmark; its comparison with `M_L` is a required decomposition, not permission to call observation intensity a laboratory-history effect.

The sole primary assay-specific estimand is

`Delta_repeat,a = [RMSE_a(M_B) − RMSE_a(M_L)] / SD_a(Y)`.

Both RMSEs use paired pooled out-of-fold predictions; `SD(Y)` is the sample standard deviation over the full assay-specific evaluation cohort, computed once. Higher values favor the latest intermediate numeric value. Also report the contextual total-history increment `Delta_total,a=[RMSE(M_P)−RMSE(M_L)]/SD(Y)`, native RMSE and MAE for all three models, `Delta_repeat,MAE`, pooled cross-fitted R-squared, prediction variance, calibration intercept/slope, fold errors, and arm-stratified descriptive errors. `Delta_total` cannot support a value-history claim if `Delta_repeat` does not, because it combines remote `B` with observation variables.

Use ridge linear regression with unpenalized intercept and penalties `{0.01,0.1,1,10,100}`. Select penalty separately for each model and outer training fold by inner leave-one-patient-out MSE; exact ties choose the smaller penalty. Age is numeric and training-median imputed; include an age-missing indicator only when it has training variance. Encode literal male as reference with fixed female and unknown/other/missing indicators; drop any variance-zero indicator from all compared models within that fold under the same deterministic rule. All imputation, variance filtering, centering/scaling, tuning, and fitting occur inside outer training data. No replacement algorithm may be chosen after outcomes are inspected.

## Uncertainty, falsification, and observation/selection mechanisms

1. **Primary uncertainty.** Use 2,000 fixed-seed (`20260910`) stratified patient bootstrap replicates of frozen r00 paired OOF tuples `(arm,Y,pred_MB,pred_ML,pred_MP)`, sampling patients within arm. Recompute RMSE, `SD(Y)`, and both deltas. Require at least 95% estimable replicates. Report two-sided percentile intervals and one-sided tail probability `(1+#Delta_repeat<=0)/(1+#estimable)`. Apply Holm correction across albumin and bilirubin only when making assay-family claims. A 200-replicate stratified full-pipeline bootstrap reruns fold construction, preprocessing, tuning, and fitting; fewer than 90% estimable or sign reversal versus r00 is instability-inconclusive.
2. **Temporal leakage assertions.** Assert every `B`, `H`, `P`, and `Y` clock exactly; no `H` row is in `event_encounter`, at/after `P`, in the 72-hour buffer, or post-event; and no held-out values enter preprocessing. Any violation stops inference.
3. **Patient linkage destruction.** Within arm and outer fold, permute paired `(L,L_time)` only among `H_any=1` patients while preserving `H_any`, `logN`, `R_hours`, `P`, `B`, `Y`, covariates, and folds. Refit `M_L` for 500 fixed permutations. Support requires observed `Delta_repeat` above the 95th permutation percentile; otherwise there is no demonstrated patient-specific numeric-history increment.
4. **Observation mechanism for intermediate history.** Report `H_any`, `N_H`, and recency distributions by arm and fold. Fit a descriptive five-fold cross-fitted logistic model for `H_any` within triplets using only arm, age, sex, TACE2-to-repeat days, event year, and `P_lead_hours`; report AUC, calibration, probability range, and arm-specific prevalence. Do not weight on `H_any`: all triplet patients remain in the primary analysis, and missing numeric history is explicitly represented. A strong prediction of `H_any` is evidence of structured measurement, not a reason to claim biology.
5. **Triplet-observation selection.** Among all 159/526 selected repeat-event patients, define assay-specific triplet observation under the locked parser/selectors. Fit a five-fold cross-fitted logistic model using arm, age, sex, TACE2-to-repeat days, and event calendar year only. Among observed triplets, use stabilized inverse-observation weights truncated at the prespecified 1st/99th percentiles to recompute paired OOF metrics. Report probability range, balance, maximum/concentration, and arm-specific ESS. If any probability is `<0.05` or `>0.95`, systemic ESS `<30`, comparator ESS `<80`, one patient carries `>10%` total weight, weighted `Delta_repeat` reverses sign, or `|weighted−unweighted Delta_repeat|>0.05`, a favorable result is selection-sensitive/inconclusive. Weighting is an MAR-style sensitivity and cannot recover unobserved outcomes or remove collider bias.
6. **Repeat-event selection.** Report the cascade from 319/1,491 frozen pathway patients to 159/526 selected events and event timing by arm. Do not weight the primary outcome back to patients without a repeat event because their repeat-event-centered `P/Y` outcome is undefined. This explicitly bounds the estimand to selected repeat events.
7. **Pseudo-current negative feasibility.** The complete-source audit found only 0 systemic/1 comparator patient per assay with the required `B`, event-days −14 to −8 intermediate candidate, and primary `P`. Therefore the inherited pseudo-current experiment is locked as non-estimable and must not be widened after outcome inspection. This absence blocks an event-specific or mechanistic interpretation but does not invalidate the narrow generic prediction estimand.
8. **Timing sensitivity.** Without choosing among results, repeat latest pre 24/48/168 hours, first eligible pre within 72 hours, post nearest +48 hours, first eligible post, calendar days +1–3, concordant duplicates only, and common-stage timestamps. Recompute `H` against each changed `P` clock while preserving the exact history boundary and encounter exclusion. Identify identical selections. A sign reversal in any empirically distinct variant retaining at least 75% of primary patients, or fewer than four distinct estimable variants, makes support timing-sensitive/inconclusive.
9. **Influence and calibration.** Report OLS transparency diagnostics using the locked columns, leverage, Cook's distance, DFBETA for `L`, and leave-one-patient-out changes in `Delta_repeat`. Flag Cook's distance `>4/n` or `|DFBETA|>2/sqrt(n)`. If deleting all flagged patients reverses sign, any single deletion changes delta by `>0.05`, or `M_L` materially worsens calibration slope/intercept relative to `M_B`, classify a favorable point estimate as influence/calibration-sensitive.

No arm interaction, pathway-specific effect, assay threshold search, binary toxicity classifier, non-target assay control, joint rank score, or clinical utility analysis is permitted.

## Deterministic interpretation branches

Apply precedence separately by assay, then restrict any two-assay statement by Holm correction and concordance:

1. **Negative-feasibility/reconstruction-inconclusive:** snapshot/source mismatch; failure to reproduce 319/1,491, 159/526, triplet counts, parser/duplicate facts, or the audited history/fold/rank support; clock/join violation. Do not widen the history clock or replace `L`.
2. **Computationally inconclusive:** post-drop rank/variance gate fails, pooled `SD(Y)=0`, locked ridge is not executable, fewer than 95% primary or 90% full-pipeline bootstraps are estimable.
3. **Selection-, timing-, partition-, influence-, calibration-, or linkage-sensitive/inconclusive:** any corresponding gate fails. These labels take precedence over favorable point estimates.
4. **Adverse degradation:** prior gates pass and the r00 95% interval for `Delta_repeat` lies wholly below zero. Conclude only that adding the latest intermediate numeric value worsened prediction under the locked model.
5. **Supportive repeated-history evidence:** prior gates pass; `Delta_repeat>=0.05`; its unadjusted 95% interval excludes zero; Holm-adjusted one-sided tail probability `<0.05`; `Delta_repeat,MAE>=0`; `Delta_total>=0`; linkage destruction does not reproduce the gain; at least 16/20 partitions are positive with median `>=0.05`; and timing, selection, influence, calibration, and full-pipeline checks are nonadverse. Conclude only that one prespecified leakage-safe intermediate same-assay value adds reproducible prediction beyond `P`, `B`, compact covariates, and observation timing/intensity in selected observed repeat-TACE episodes.
6. **Precise null for the prespecified increment:** prior gates pass and the interval upper bound is `<0.05`. This rules out a gain of 0.05 standardized RMSE under this design/model and uncertainty convention, not all history effects, biology, equivalence, or clinical value.
7. **No demonstrated repeated-history increment:** point estimate `<0.05`, linkage permutation is comparable, or `Delta_total` is favorable but `Delta_repeat` is not, without an adverse or precise-null interval. State that contemporaneous `P`, remote `B`, and/or observation pattern may exhaust the available signal.
8. **Inconclusive:** interval spans meaningful degradation and `+0.05`, or any remaining prespecified ambiguity exists.

A panel-level supportive statement requires both assays to meet branch 5. Discordant assays are reported separately. The `0.05` margin is a design convention on standardized prediction error, not a validated clinically important difference.

## Evidence limits and verifier boundary

HCC lacks laboratory units/reference ranges, analyzer harmonization, adjudicated hepatic decompensation/complications, Child–Pugh/MELD context, tumor burden or radiographic response, technical TACE success/intent, transfusion/albumin infusion/fluid balance, infection/bleeding, verified medication administration/adherence/dose, outside care, and reliable mortality. Diagnoses are lexical encounter proxies without native time. Repeat-event and triplet conditioning can induce selection/collider bias; `Y` is an observation-process endpoint.

A verifier can check hashes, full scans, paths/columns/keys, cohort/event reconstruction, parsing, duplicate handling, `B/H/P/Y` clocks, fold hashes and support, rank/variance removal, training-only preprocessing, nested ridge predictions, paired OOF metrics, bootstrap/Holm arithmetic, permutation, timing, influence, selection diagnostics, and branch precedence. It cannot establish clinical importance, toxicity, hepatic failure, causation, treatment benefit, mechanism, administration, decision utility, or policy. Stronger conclusions require unit-validated assays, adjudicated actionable outcomes, verified exposures and procedure details, complete follow-up, external/prospective validation, and expert decision-utility review.
