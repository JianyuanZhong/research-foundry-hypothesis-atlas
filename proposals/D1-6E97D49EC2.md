# Observation-aware domain recovery after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe adult first-ICU-discharge cohort, derive separate respiratory and vasoactive recovery states plus an auditable observation-intensity/process ledger in the final 12 hours, fit a prespecified competing-risk baseline and an observation-aware two-domain hidden semi-Markov alternative, and return locked patient-level test estimates. Completion requires:

1. cohort, temporal-integrity, join, exposure-coverage and event-count audits;
2. one-hour pre-landmark physiology, support intervals, observation masks and process covariates;
3. adjusted 6-, 24- and 48-hour cumulative-incidence contrasts for aggregate ICU return, in-hospital death and alive hospital discharge, with topology-specific return as a secondary outcome;
4. baseline-versus-alternative predictions, calibration, IPCW Brier scores, state/observation diagnostics and patient-bootstrap uncertainty;
5. order, mask, source-channel, window, process and pseudo-landmark falsifications; and
6. an interpretation file mapping each claim to computed output rows and labelling it supportive, adverse or inconclusive.

No result is asserted here. The actual scientific deliverable is the newly fitted exposure/process-adjusted prognostic estimand and the model comparison outputs, not a readiness check or a more complex score.

## Unresolved question and strongest supported claim

The expert seed `[starting question]` asks whether persistent oxygen requirements or fluctuating vital signs before ICU discharge predict ICU return or in-hospital death. Existing board work makes a narrower version computable: among adults at first ICU outtime, separate respiratory-support withdrawal from vasoactive cessation, use the last 12 hours without post-landmark leakage, treat alive hospital discharge as a competing event, and distinguish returns with an observed non-ICU interval from topology-uncertain returns. The catalog and a bounded archive probe support availability of the dated tables and fields needed for that construction. They do not support an association, causality, clinical readiness, planned/unplanned return label, or treatment-limitation label.

The remaining unresolved claim is whether a domain-specific recent-withdrawal signal reflects a clinically meaningful recovery trajectory rather than selective measurement and disposition documentation. In MIMIC, recorded absence of support is not continuous absence of support; a patient with few observations can appear to have a long recorded-support-free buffer. ICU type, length of stay, admission type and transfer patterns can also influence both discharge documentation and outcomes. These are observable process proxies, not adjudicated treatment limitations.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among adults at the first ICU outtime with low final recorded respiratory and MAP burden and adequate source-concordant coverage, recent respiratory-support withdrawal and recent vasoactive cessation have distinct adjusted 48-hour cumulative incidences of first ICU return or death. The domain contrast and any dual-withdrawal interaction will remain after adjustment for pre-landmark observation intensity and observable discharge-process proxies; if it attenuates to the coverage/process-only estimate, the apparent recovery signal is better explained by documentation or selection than by a distinct recovery state.

The estimand is prognostic, not causal: standardized absolute CIFs and contrasts at 6, 24 and 48 hours for mutually exclusive first aggregate ICU return, death and alive hospital discharge, with topology-confirmed/non-confirmed return split as secondary. The reference exposure is durable recorded-off in both domains. Primary strata are durable/durable, recent respiratory only, recent vasoactive only and recent in both; active, residual, discordant, unobserved and inadequate-coverage strata are retained as audit/secondary strata rather than forced into the contrast.

Supportive evidence is a reproducible domain or interaction contrast after process adjustment, adequate positivity and calibration, and stability across observation and coding falsifications. Adverse evidence is a null/reversed contrast, attenuation to the observation/process-only model, or dependence on masks, storetime, procedure corroboration, transfer topology or discharge censoring. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse cells/events, non-positivity, unstable time intervals, large uncertainty, poor state identifiability or inadequate transfer observability. Even supportive evidence would establish only retrospective prognostic association; clinical monitoring or discharge policy would require clinician adjudication, external validation and prospective/causal study.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

ICU discharge is a high-consequence transition under constrained beds and uneven ward surveillance. If recent respiratory withdrawal remains informative after accounting for how densely the patient was observed, it could motivate targeted respiratory reassessment; if vasoactive cessation dominates, circulatory-transition review may be more relevant. If the signal disappears after observation/process adjustment, that negative result is valuable because it warns against interpreting sparse EHR records as physiologic recovery. The advance over the parent is an explicit estimand for the distinction between recovery information and record-generation information, not another generic prediction model.

The study cannot establish bedside readiness, intent, preventability, treatment limitation, or planned/unplanned return. `hosp/admissions.discharge_location` is post-landmark and is a descriptive outcome-process audit only. Discharge notes are not a validated ICU-readiness or goals-of-care label and are excluded from primary predictors. No clinical adjudication is available.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative

Use patient-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits by `subject_id`, fixed seeds 17, 29 and 43. All item mapping, imputation, threshold selection and fitting are training/validation-only.

The simple baseline is a cause-specific Cox/pooled discrete-time hazard model for aggregate return, death and alive discharge with exposure pattern, final/preceding physiology, observation-intensity fields and pre-landmark process covariates. Report nested models: physiology/support only; observation-only; process-only; and full adjustment. Standardize held-out CIFs and report Aalen-Johansen, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier score, absolute CIF contrasts, interaction contrasts and subject-bootstrap 95% intervals. The nested decomposition directly tests whether adjustment changes the scientific contrast.

The substantive alternative is an observation-aware factorized hidden semi-Markov model. Separate respiratory and hemodynamic latent states (durable-off/low burden, recent withdrawal, active/residual, unobserved/discordant) with sparse transitions and dwell distributions over eight six-hour intervals. Model the observation mask/intensity as a separate visit/documentation process conditional on available pre-landmark covariates, rather than treating missingness as physiologic support absence. Fit emissions, state transitions, observation process and event-specific hazards only on training data; select restrictions on validation; lock posterior states and competing CIFs on test data. Its scientific value is that it can reveal whether ordered duration and missingness patterns distinguish latent recovery from record-generation behavior that a last-value or pooled-support baseline loses. Retain it only if states are identifiable, diagnostics reproduce across split seeds, and it adds stable calibrated or domain-specific information under a predeclared margin; otherwise the transparent baseline is the scientific result.

## Evidence boundaries

MIMIC lacks raw waveforms, imaging, continuous support status between observations, clinician intent, goals of care, treatment limitations, ward monitoring, discharge rationale and adjudicated post-ICU deterioration. The local metadata explicitly records raw waveforms and MIMIC-CXR images as unavailable. Computational results can establish cohort construction, event ordering, recorded-support associations, calibration, uncertainty and sensitivity consistency. They cannot establish causality, bedside readiness, preventability, planned/unplanned status, clinical utility or a discharge policy without clinical review and external/prospective evidence.
