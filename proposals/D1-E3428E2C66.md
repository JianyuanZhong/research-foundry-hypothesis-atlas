# Episode 73 successor: does optional repeat lactate beat an attention-matched review queue?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and substantive advance

At a fixed 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed entry, does an optionally available repeat lactate improve a fixed-capacity hospital-mortality review queue beyond information already carried by other laboratory surveillance, or does it mostly identify patients whom clinicians are measuring more intensively?

This distinction is consequential. A lactate-specific queue may appear useful because repeat testing is informative: sicker patients, or patients causing greater clinical concern, tend to generate more observations. If a small set of time-bounded non-lactate measurement-process summaries captures the same re-ranking, a lactate-dependent workflow is less specific, less transportable, and cannot be used to justify repeat testing. If repeat-lactate process and value still add a material, stable increment after that comparator, an independent prospective validation of an optional-result review queue becomes worthwhile.

The Episode 72 parent already improves substantially on complete-case analyses: it retains people without repeat lactate, separates process from numeric value, uses hospital-held-out prediction, and tests robustness across hospitals with different repeat-lactate availability. Its unresolved weakness is the comparator. Although its state model includes missing indicators, measurement ages, and six-hour vital counts, it does not explicitly summarize repeated non-lactate laboratory surveillance in the same 12-to-24-hour interval as the repeat lactate. Consequently, “repeat-lactate value” may still be generic informative observation.

Baysan et al. (2022; DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407) were inspected in full-text XML from frozen source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]. They reported, in 4,440 MIMIC patients with sepsis, a recalibrated C-statistic of 0.62 for APACHE IV and 0.64 after adding 24-hour lactate. This supports a modest prognostic increment in a measured cohort, not optional-result queue value, lactate-specificity relative to surveillance intensity, multicenter robustness, ordering benefit, or patient benefit.

Sisk et al. (2021; DOI 10.1093/jamia/ocaa242; PMCID PMC7810439) were inspected in full-text XML from frozen source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]. Their systematic review defines informative presence/observation as the presence, absence, or timing of routinely collected data carrying health information because collection is driven by patients and clinicians; 24 of 36 included articles used missing indicators or summary measures as predictors. This supports explicitly representing the measurement process. It does not show that the proposed summaries remove confounding or transport across hospitals.

The strongest claim currently supported is therefore narrow: late lactate may add modest prognostic information, and routine observation patterns themselves may be predictive. The unresolved claim tested here is:

> Among adults who reach a fixed 24-hour landmark after an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed entry and have a valid baseline lactate at least 2 mmol/L, adding the observed optional repeat-lactate process and value to a non-lactate-surveillance-matched state model captures at least 3 additional percentage points of eventual hospital deaths in a within-hospital top-10% review queue, with nonnegative increments in both low- and high-repeat-availability hospital strata.

The three-point margin is inherited and prespecified as a decision-relevance anchor for this modeled fixed workload. It is not a stakeholder-validated utility threshold.

## Four locked models

All models are unweighted L2 logistic regressions predicting D. The point is a controlled comparator, not model complexity.

### R_state: inherited clinical-state baseline

R_state contains:

- age, age squared, Female and Male indicators with Other/Unknown as reference;
- t0/1440 and its square;
- baseline collection lag (t0-B_offset)/360, ln(B), and ln(B)^2;
- the latest valid value in [t0,L24], its age (L24-offset)/1440, and a missing indicator for each fixed non-lactate laboratory stream below;
- for heart rate, respiration, and SpO2, the last, median, minimum, maximum, resolved-timestamp count, and no-valid-observation indicator in [L24-360,L24].

The 16 laboratory streams and hard ranges are:

- potassium | mmol/L | [1,10]
- sodium | mmol/L | [100,180]
- chloride | mmol/L | [50,150]
- BUN | mg/dL | [1,300]
- glucose | mg/dL | [20,1000]
- creatinine | mg/dL | [0.1,20]
- bicarbonate | mmol/L | [2,60]
- calcium | mg/dL | [2,20]
- Hgb | g/dL | [2,25]
- platelets x 1000 | K/mcL | [1,2000]
- WBC x 1000 | K/mcL | [0.1,500]
- albumin | g/dL | [0.5,8]
- total bilirubin | mg/dL | [0.1,50]
- paO2 | mm Hg | [20,700]
- paCO2 | mm Hg | [10,200]
- pH | blank/missing unit | [6.5,8.0].

Names and units are trimmed and case-folded for matching. For every stream and collection offset, require finite values, `labresultrevisedoffset <= L24`, keep the greatest eligible revision, invalidate differing final-revision ties, and collapse identical ties to smallest `labid`. Choose the latest resolved timestamp for the state value.

For `vitalPeriodic`, use `observationoffset` and accept heart rate 20-250/min, respiration 2-80/min, and `sao2` 40-100%. Resolve each stream independently at each person/timestamp: differing valid values invalidate that stream/timestamp, identical ties collapse to smallest `vitalperiodicid`. Counts are resolved timestamps, not export rows. These five-minute summaries have no entry, validation, or display timestamp.

### R_attention: non-lactate-surveillance-matched baseline

R_attention contains all R_state terms plus six frozen summaries computed only from valid resolved non-lactate rows among the 16 streams during [t0+720,L24], with revision deadline L24:

- ln(1 + number of resolved routine marker-time pairs), where routine means the first 13 streams through total bilirubin;
- ln(1 + number of resolved blood-gas marker-time pairs), where blood gas means paO2, paCO2, and pH;
- ln(1 + number of distinct `labresultoffset` values across all 16 streams);
- number of distinct stream keys observed, divided by 16;
- recency (L24 minus the latest qualifying non-lactate `labresultoffset`)/720 when any exists;
- no-qualifying-non-lactate-row indicator; recency is missing and training-imputed when this indicator is 1.

A marker-time pair is counted after the same range, unit, revision, conflict, and duplicate rules used for R_state. Lactate is always excluded. These summaries are proxies for usable recorded surveillance, not verified orders, clinician effort, concern, workload, or bedside viewing.

The audit found late non-lactate marker-time counts from 0 to 120 (median 12, IQR 3-19), 19.2% with none, and Spearman correlation 0.412 with F availability. Those numbers establish variation and motivate the comparator; they do not establish confounding control or tune any feature.

### R_process and R_full

R_process adds to R_attention:

- `F_available`;
- F measurement age (L24-F_offset)/720 when available;
- missing-age indicator.

R_full adds ln(F) and ln(F)^2. When F is absent, those continuous terms are missing and are imputed using training-fold preprocessing; `F_available` remains explicit.

No model may contain hospital ID, diagnosis, treatment, clearance, F/B, post-L24 data, outcome-derived information, post hoc interactions, or selected features.

## Estimands and baselines

For each retained hospital h with n_h people, set k_h=ceil(0.10 n_h). Rank outer-hospital-held-out raw linear predictors within h; the queue Q_m,h for model m contains the k_h highest scores, with ascending `patientunitstayid` breaking exact ties. A monotone recalibration cannot improve this within-hospital ranking, so calibration is deliberately not a primary gate.

For any hospital set S, define death capture:

`C_m(S) = sum_{h in S} sum_{i in Q_m,h} D_i / sum_{h in S} sum_i D_i`.

The primary estimand is:

`Delta10_attention = C_Rfull(all hospitals) - C_Rattention(all hospitals)`.

The low- and high-availability estimands use the same formula with denominators restricted to deaths in their respective frozen hospital strata. A 20% queue using ceil(0.20 n_h) is secondary and cannot rescue the primary.

Required decompositions are:

- `Delta10_naive = R_full - R_state`: how favorable the result would appear without explicit late non-lactate surveillance matching;
- `Delta10_process = R_process - R_attention`: lactate availability/age information beyond non-lactate surveillance;
- `Delta10_numeric_measured`: R_full versus R_process after refitting both models in the inherited F-measured population, using within-hospital 10% quotas there. This conditional decomposition cannot replace the all-population primary;
- attenuation = `Delta10_naive - Delta10_attention`, reported descriptively without a causal label;
- paired queue overlap, additions, removals, true-positive changes, precision, sensitivity, false positives and unflagged deaths by model and hospital.

The primary comparison answers whether the observed optional lactate workflow adds modeled ranking information beyond a generic recorded-surveillance proxy. It does not estimate the effect of ordering, withholding, displaying, or acting on lactate.

## Hospital-held-out estimation

Use leave-one-hospital-out prediction on the fixed original hospital set. For each outer hospital and each model:

1. Exclude the outer hospital completely.
2. On all remaining hospitals, winsorize continuous predictors at training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize with training means and population standard deviations, and mean-impute missing continuous values to zero after scaling. Counts enter after their stated transformations. Do not scale binary indicators.
3. Fit unweighted logistic regression with intercept, L2 penalty, C=1, `lbfgs`, `max_iter=10000`, `tol=1e-8`, no class weighting, and seed 20260973.
4. Score the outer hospital using only its training-fold constants. No feature can be dropped. A feature with no finite training values, zero post-winsorization variance, nonconvergence, or nonfinite prediction makes the required model not computable.
5. Use the raw linear predictor for within-hospital ranking. Save the corresponding raw logistic probability only for descriptive calibration plots, AUROC, Brier score, and log loss. Do not label those probabilities clinically calibrated or use threshold decision curves.

This demotion of nested Platt recalibration is intentional. The decision is a fixed within-hospital rank quota, so a positive-slope monotone transform cannot alter queue membership. A negative fitted slope would reverse rankings rather than validate them. If a future study displays probabilities or uses risk thresholds, honest external calibration becomes essential and must be tested there.

## Ordered falsification and interpretation

Apply in this order.

1. **Not computable:** source/header/hash mismatch; failure to reproduce the frozen flow without a documented mechanical reason; missing key/clock; incorrect one-person, revision, conflict, unit, range, or window logic; fewer than 30 hospitals or any other population gate failure; outer-hospital leakage; altered feature list; incomplete predictions or quotas; nonfinite/nonconverged required fits; or fewer than 1,900 valid bootstrap replicates. Do not substitute random person splits, complete-case primary analysis, calibration-based ranks, changed windows, or post hoc features.
2. **Supportive, surveillance-matched, and availability-robust:** all gates pass; the pooled `Delta10_attention` 95% lower bound is >=+3 percentage points; both low- and high-availability stratum lower bounds are >=0; >=80% of pooled and >=70% of each stratum’s finite deletion estimates are positive; and the 20% pooled contrast is not opposite. This supports only material modeled re-ranking beyond the specified recorded-surveillance comparator in this EICU snapshot.
3. **Pooled but site-process-dependent:** the pooled lower bound is >=+3, but either availability stratum has an upper bound <0 or its deletion criterion fails. This opposes the robustness part of the hypothesis and bars a transportability interpretation.
4. **Surveillance-explained adverse result:** `Delta10_naive` meets the +3-point criterion but `Delta10_attention` has an upper bound below +3. This shows that the locked lactate-specific materiality claim does not survive the stronger comparator. It does not prove that clinician attention causes the naive signal.
5. **Opposite:** the pooled primary upper bound is <0, or both availability-stratum upper bounds are <0. This falsifies benefit in the proposed direction for this comparator, population and quota.
6. **Adverse to materiality:** the pooled interval is wholly nonnegative but its upper bound is <+3, with no opposite stratum. This rejects the locked material increment while allowing a smaller nonnegative association.
7. **Inconclusive:** every other computable outcome, including intervals spanning zero or +3, discordant strata not meeting the site-dependent rule, unstable deletions, or a favorable point estimate without the required bounds.

Attach a cross-cutting mechanism label without changing the primary state:

- **numeric component supported** only if the measured-population `Delta10_numeric_measured` lower bound is >0;
- **process-dependent** if primary support occurs but the numeric component upper bound <=0 or the point estimate of the process increment is at least as large as the point estimate of the full primary increment;
- **numeric component unresolved** otherwise.

Supportive results justify independent prospective validation of a fixed-capacity optional-result queue, not deployment. A surveillance-explained, adverse, or opposite result would argue against treating the retrospective gain as lactate-specific. An inconclusive result means the available multicenter snapshot cannot resolve the locked margin; favorable point estimates cannot rescue it.
