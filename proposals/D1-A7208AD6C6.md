# eICU laboratory-process prediction and hospital transport

## Question, evidence and importance

Among adults alive and still in their first ICU stay at hour 24, does adding first-day laboratory testing volume/repetition/timing to measured vital-sign physiology improve prediction of subsequent in-hospital death, and is that predictive increment attenuated at hospitals withheld from fitting?

The inspected eICU database description establishes the multicenter critical-care resource [K1]. Recent ICU external-validation work reports cross-database performance degradation and site-specific shortcuts, motivating local transport tests [K2]. A study of missing-data strategies reports that ICU test missingness can be informative of severity and alter model evaluation, making severity/selection a substantive rival [K3]. These findings are motivation, not proof of the hypothesis. The unresolved clinical-methodologic issue is whether an explicit testing-process block carries incremental risk information beyond measured vitals and whether that information survives hospital shift. If fragile, a model using lab process alone may miscalibrate when moved to another hospital; if stable, it may be a useful predictive proxy in this task. Neither result licenses reducing testing or establishes a causal mechanism.

**Leading account:** the process block partly captures site/workflow/data-capture conventions, so its incremental predictive gain will diminish in held-out hospitals. **Strongest rival:** testing intensity reflects genuine, incompletely measured severity or clinician concern and retains predictive value beyond available physiology at unseen hospitals. Case mix, care pathways, site lab mapping, coding, and 24-hour survivor/continued-stay selection are also plausible explanations. The design tests predictive utility/transport, not whether hospitals cause mortality or whether testing causes harm.

## Population, time, outcome and estimand

Population: one first ICU stay per person; adult age ascertainable under a predeclared rule for EICU’s obfuscated age values; alive and still in ICU at offset 1,440 minutes; and later hospital disposition ascertainable. Report cohort flow and every exclusion. This landmark conditions on survival and remaining in ICU for 24 h and does not represent all ICU admissions.

Feature interval: ICU offsets 0–1,440 minutes inclusive only when an event was available by the 24-hour prediction instant. Outcome: subsequent in-hospital death, coded from `hospitaldischargestatus` and `hospitaldischargeoffset` after a clinical review of status semantics; ambiguous or unavailable outcome labels are excluded from the primary cohort and reported. The endpoint is not fixed-horizon mortality after discharge.

Primary estimand: within identical evaluation patients, the paired Brier-score increment for physiology-plus-process versus physiology alone (`Brier(P+L) − Brier(P)`), compared between patient-grouped internal validation and held-out-hospital validation (`delta_heldout − delta_internal`). More-positive attenuation means the addition is less helpful out of hospital. Calibration intercept/slope are co-primary diagnostic summaries; AUROC/AUPRC are secondary, never a substitute for the prespecified proper score. Report paired differences and uncertainty, not only p-values.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative

**Primary simple model:** L2-regularized logistic regression for the binary endpoint, with two matched arms: P uses only prespecified first-day vital summaries and missingness indicators; P+L adds only the lab-process aggregates above. Select penalty and preprocessing within training groups only. This directly estimates the intended feature-block increment and is inspectable.

**Learned alternative:** a compact GRU binary classifier using hourly bins 0–23 from the same vital channels and lab process channels, with explicit missingness masks and elapsed-time/event indicators; train P and P+L arms on the same population and binary target. Use fixed hospital assignments, patient identity guards, training-only scaling/imputation, a fixed small configuration and three prespecified random seeds; early stopping/tuning occur only inside training hospitals. The GRU may preserve order, clustered test bursts and dynamic vital/test co-occurrence that the logistic summaries lose. It may also learn institution-specific cadence. Compare paired Brier increments, hospital attenuation and calibration with seed and hospital uncertainty; do not declare a winner based on a small AUROC gain. Keep logistic as primary; retain GRU as a predeclared secondary method comparison because the sequence information could reveal whether the same process signal behaves differently when order is preserved. Defer the GRU only if its input tensor cannot be built from the exact bound fields without altering the feature contract, and record the dependency rather than claim a result.

## Split, analysis and uncertainty

Run patient-grouped internal validation and leave-hospital-out evaluation. In the latter, hold out whole hospitals; if a `uniquepid` appears in any held-out hospital, remove that person’s other stays from training for that fold. Apply the same person-safe test assignments to all feature arms and model families. Freeze the splits before fitting; no held-out tuning. Check event/site counts, feature cutoff/leakage, unique patient overlap and key uniqueness before training. Report pooled predictions and per-hospital metrics; estimate uncertainty by resampling hospitals for transport and unique patients for internal evaluation, with GRU seed variability reported separately. If hospital/event support is sparse or a metric not estimable, do not discard such folds silently; report inconclusive support.

**Falsification:** supportive if P+L improves primary Brier internally and the paired gain is less favorable in hospital holdout with uncertainty sufficiently precise to distinguish attenuation. This supports a task-specific site-fragile predictive increment only. Adverse to attenuation if held-out benefit is similar or better with adequate site/event support and precise intervals. Adverse to any incremental value if P+L is null/worse internally with precision. Wide intervals, sparse positive-event sites, ambiguous labels or unstable mapping are inconclusive, not confirmation or refutation. If GRU and logistic disagree, state the model-dependent pattern and do not use the learned model to rescue the primary conclusion.
