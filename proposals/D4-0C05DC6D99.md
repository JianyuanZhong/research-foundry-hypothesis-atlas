# Working proposal: discordant visceral fat and thigh muscle fat infiltration

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

UK Biobank body MRI provides simultaneous visceral fat volume and posterior thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI), while repeated assessments provide handgrip strength, falls, HbA1c, and incident diagnoses. The unresolved question is whether these two fat-distribution measures identify distinct longitudinal risk pathways: visceral fat for metabolic disease and MFI for subsequent functional decline or falls.

The testable hypothesis is: among adults with baseline MRI and no prevalent metabolic disease or functional limitation, the standardized five-year risk difference for incident type 2 diabetes associated with one standard deviation higher visceral fat is larger than that associated with one standard deviation higher MFI; and the standardized five-year risk difference for a subsequent fall associated with one standard deviation higher MFI is larger than that associated with one standard deviation higher visceral fat. The comparison is made within the common baseline population and after conditioning on total body fat percentage, age, sex, smoking, physical activity and baseline HbA1c for diabetes, while conditioning on age, sex, smoking, physical activity and baseline handgrip strength for falls.

## Why it matters

A person with normal BMI but high visceral fat may need metabolic surveillance, while a person with high muscle infiltration may be at higher risk of disability and falls. Separating these patterns could inform which clinical pathway to prioritize. However, the available data are observational and cannot establish causality.

## Strongest evidence and rival explanation

The available evidence supports the descriptive observation that visceral fat and MFI are measured simultaneously and are associated with different clinical phenotypes, but it does not establish that the two measures predict distinct five-year outcomes after accounting for total body fat. The strongest rival explanation is that low physical activity, pre-existing subclinical disease or reverse causation simultaneously produces higher MFI, poorer function and subsequent falls, while visceral fat remains a marker of metabolic risk. A second rival is that the apparent discordance is an artifact of conditioning on total body fat, which can induce collider bias if total fat is influenced by both exposures and outcomes.

## Design and estimand

The primary estimand is a standardized five-year risk difference. For each outcome, the risk is estimated in the common baseline population after standardization to the observed distribution of age, sex, smoking, physical activity and the outcome-specific baseline covariates. The contrast is the difference in standardized risk between one standard deviation higher visceral fat and one standard deviation higher MFI, holding total body fat percentage at its observed distribution. This is an association estimand, not a causal effect.

To address the collider-bias rival, the analysis will report the primary contrast both with and without adjustment for total body fat percentage. If the discordance disappears or reverses after adjustment, the result is interpreted as evidence that total-body-fat composition, rather than a distinct pathway, explains the association. A sensitivity analysis will use a propensity-score overlap-weighted estimate restricted to participants with positive overlap in the joint distribution of visceral fat, MFI and total body fat.

The reverse-causation/low-activity rival is tested by restricting to participants without baseline functional limitation or prior falls, and by adjusting for baseline handgrip strength and physical activity. A secondary analysis will exclude participants with a baseline diagnosis of diabetes, cardiovascular disease or cancer. If the MFI-fall association is explained by baseline handgrip strength or prior falls, the hypothesis is not supported.

## Method comparison

The baseline method is a standardized risk difference estimated with a logistic regression model that includes the two fat measures and the specified covariates. The alternative is a propensity-score overlap-weighted estimate that weights each participant by the inverse of the propensity to have the observed joint fat pattern, restricted to the overlap region. The alternative can reveal whether the association is driven by a small subgroup with extreme fat composition and poor overlap, which the baseline logistic model would average over. The primary result is the standardized risk difference; the overlap-weighted estimate is a sensitivity analysis.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if, after adjustment for total body fat percentage and the specified covariates, the standardized five-year risk difference for incident type 2 diabetes associated with one standard deviation higher visceral fat is not larger than that associated with one standard deviation higher MFI, or if the standardized five-year risk difference for a subsequent fall associated with one standard deviation higher MFI is not larger than that associated with one standard deviation higher visceral fat. A null result with wide confidence intervals is inconclusive, not evidence against the hypothesis.

## Future-solver deliverable

The future solver must fit the logistic regression and overlap-weighted models on the UKB subset, estimate the standardized five-year risk differences for incident type 2 diabetes and subsequent falls, and report the primary contrast with 95% confidence intervals. The solver must also report the sensitivity analyses without total-body-fat adjustment and with the overlap restriction. The deliverable is a set of tables and a short interpretation that distinguishes supportive, adverse and inconclusive results. The solver must not claim causality or clinical utility from this observational analysis.

## Limitations

The analysis is observational and cannot establish causality. The follow-up window is limited to five years. The fall outcome is based on self-report and hospital diagnosis, which may miss non-hospitalized falls. The analysis cannot account for unmeasured confounding such as diet, sleep or detailed medication use. The results are limited to the UK Biobank population and may not generalize to other populations.
