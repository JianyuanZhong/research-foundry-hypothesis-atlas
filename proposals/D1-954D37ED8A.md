# Provisional checkpoint repair

This draft preserves the inherited hypothesis and design from [prior hypothesis]: among adults in eICU with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during the first 24 hours of a qualifying ICU stay, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of new or worsening acute kidney injury during the next 48 hours, after accounting for baseline severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone. This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

The unresolved question is whether duration and trajectory add clinically meaningful information beyond the nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, measurement frequency, or treatment response. A single nadir may be transient or artifact-prone; sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. Resolving this could inform whether early AKI risk assessment should use time-integrated blood pressure and whether prospective monitoring or resuscitation studies should target duration rather than a single minimum.

The inherited design specifies adults (age >=18 at admission), a single qualifying ICU admission with valid ICU start time and at least one valid systolic BP <65 mmHg in the first 24 hours; exposure is cumulative minutes below 65 mmHg in ICU time 0–24 hours using prespecified piecewise-linear interpolation without extrapolation; the primary outcome is new or worsening KDIGO-compatible creatinine AKI in the subsequent 24–72 hours. Baseline is the last valid creatinine before or at ICU time 0 when available, otherwise the first creatinine in a prespecified pre-exposure window, with missingness reported rather than imputed as normal. The primary baseline model is a hospital-stratified logistic model using nadir, age, sex, baseline creatinine, and available baseline severity/vasopressor context. The time-aware alternative adds cumulative hypotension minutes, interval-specific time below threshold, last BP before window end, and a simple recovery/trajectory feature while retaining nadir. Both use the same hospital-held-out split, outcome definition, uncertainty procedure, calibration, and discrimination evaluation. The duration hypothesis is supported only if duration adds reproducible information beyond nadir with prespecified direction and uncertainty and is not explained solely by observation density or one hospital. A null, reversal, artifact confinement, sparse AKI ascertainment, or unstable timestamp support is adverse or inconclusive as specified. Causal, treatment-threshold, and mechanistic claims require additional clinical adjudication, richer treatment/etiology data, and prospective or quasi-experimental evidence.
