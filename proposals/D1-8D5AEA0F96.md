# Duration of Hypotension and Subsequent AKI in eICU

## Scientific Question

At the same minimum blood pressure, do patients with longer-lasting hypotension experience more subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI)? Is this association hospital-specific?

## Clinical Importance

Minimum blood pressure is a commonly used but incomplete measure of hypotension severity. If duration of hypotension at the same minimum pressure predicts subsequent AKI, it identifies a more stable risk measure for risk stratification in ICU patients. If the association is absent or explained by confounding, it prevents overinterpretation of hypotension duration as a marker of patient need.

## Hypothesis

At the same minimum blood pressure, patients with longer-lasting hypotension (>=2h vs <2h) are associated with higher subsequent AKI risk, with meaningful between-hospital variation.

## Study Population

- eICU-CRD patients with >=24h ICU stay
- At least one blood pressure record in the first 24h
- Excluded: patients with discharge/de-escalation flags in the 24h window preceding the hypotension analysis
- Excluded: patients with end-of-life treatment limitations (if identifiable in eICU)

## Key Variables

| Variable | Source | Definition |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Minimum BP | eICU vitals | Minimum systolic or MAP in the first 24h |
| Hypotension duration | eICU vitals | Time (hours) with MAP < 65 mmHg in the first 24h |
| AKI | eICU creatinine | KDIGO stage 1-3 within 48h |
| Hospital | eICU hospital_id | For stratification and random effects |

## Estimand

The association between hypotension duration (>=2h vs <2h) at the same minimum blood pressure (stratified by minimum BP quartiles) and 48h AKI incidence, adjusted for:
- Current clinical state (MAP, SOFA, time since admission)
- Hospital (random effect or stratification)
- Time since ICU admission

## Analysis Plan

1. **Descriptive**: Distribution of hypotension duration, minimum BP, hospital variation
2. **Primary**: Logistic regression for 48h AKI, with hypotension duration as exposure, stratified by minimum BP quartiles
3. **Sensitivity**:
   - Restrict to patients without discharge/de-escalation flags
   - Alternative duration thresholds (1h, 3h)
   - Hospital-stratified analysis
4. **Competing explanations**:
   - Confounding by indication: adjust for clinical instability at the time of hypotension
   - Data gaps: sensitivity analysis excluding patients with >6h gaps in vital signs

## Falsification Criteria

- Null or reversed association after adjustment for clinical state and hospital
- Association confined to patients with discharge/de-escalation flags
- No hospital-specific variation in the association

## Interpretation

- **Supportive**: Association persists after adjustment, with hospital-specific effects → hypotension duration may be a more stable risk measure
- **Adverse**: Association absent or explained by confounding → hypotension duration is not a reliable marker of patient need
- **Inconclusive**: Weak association with wide CIs, or association only in specific hospital subgroups

## Limitations

- eICU does not capture all reasons for hypotension (e.g., clinician judgment, resource constraints)
- AKI definition relies on creatinine, which may be affected by fluid status
- Hospital-level confounding cannot be fully addressed
- No causal inference; association only

## Data Bindings

- Source: eICU-CRD (eicu-crd 2.0)
- Tables: patient, stay, vitals, lab
- Join keys: patient_id, stay_id
- Time fields: timestamp (vitals), admission_time (stay)
