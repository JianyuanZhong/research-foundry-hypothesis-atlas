# Discordant creatinine recovery after AKI: a severity-, treatment-intensity-, and RRT-stratified MIMIC-IV study

Status: active draft, not yet a registered hypothesis. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Among adults in the ICU with KDIGO stage 2 or 3 acute kidney injury (AKI), does apparent creatinine recovery at 48 hours coexisting with persistently low urine output or positive fluid balance identify a worse 7-day ICU prognosis than creatinine recovery alone, after accounting for current severity and treatment intensity at the 48-hour stabilization decision point?

The parent proposal established a prespecified discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype and addressed the strongest confounder—current severity and treatment intensity at the decision point—with SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and renal replacement therapy (RRT) status as adjustment variables. However, RRT status is not merely a continuous treatment-intensity covariate: it marks a fundamentally different renal recovery phenotype and outcome trajectory. Patients on RRT at the 48-hour decision point have already had their native kidney function bypassed by an extracorporeal circuit, so a creatinine decline in this group may reflect dialytic clearance rather than true renal recovery. Conversely, patients not on RRT have not yet received this intervention, so creatinine recovery in this group may more directly reflect native kidney function.

The clinically consequential question is whether the discordant phenotype has independent prognostic value within each RRT stratum. If discordance predicts worse outcomes in both strata, the phenotype is robust to the presence or absence of RRT and may represent a genuine signal of renal dysfunction that is not captured by creatinine alone. If discordance predicts worse outcomes only in the non-RRT stratum, the phenotype may be driven by the absence of RRT (i.e., patients who are sicker but have not yet been escalated to RRT) rather than by a true discordance signal. If discordance predicts worse outcomes only in the RRT stratum, the phenotype may reflect the severity of illness that necessitated RRT rather than a discordance-specific signal. This stratification could change the interpretation of the study's main result and inform whether clinicians should distrust creatinine-based recovery in patients on RRT, in patients not on RRT, or in both.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The inspected expert reference reports that cystatin C and creatinine can disagree during critical illness, supporting the broader limitation that creatinine is a surrogate rather than a direct measure of kidney function [K1]. It does not establish that discordant recovery is prognostic, nor does it provide a MIMIC-IV phenotype or causal interpretation.

A recent narrative review emphasizes that creatinine-based AKI definitions may reflect underlying cardiorenal disease severity and congestion rather than isolated renal injury [K2]. This motivates adjustment for fluid status and severity, but the review is not a falsifiable MIMIC-IV experiment.

KDIGO staging is applicable in ICU cohorts and AKI severity is associated with worse outcomes [K3]. This proposal applies KDIGO staging to an adult MIMIC-IV cohort and tests a discordant recovery phenotype rather than stage-specific mortality alone.

The strongest currently supported claim is limited: creatinine can be an imperfect surrogate for kidney function in critical illness [K1]. The unresolved claim is that a prespecified creatinine-recovery/urine-output-or-fluid discordance phenotype carries prognostic information beyond standard severity and recent clinical trajectories, after explicitly conditioning on severity and treatment intensity at the 48-hour decision point, and that this prognostic information is consistent across RRT status strata. This is a prognostic association, not a causal claim that discordance causes death or dialysis.

## Population and index time

Include adults aged at least 18 years at the index admission, with a first ICU stay in the index admission. Use one index admission per patient: the earliest qualifying ICU admission in the dataset. Require at least two creatinine measurements during the first 72 ICU hours, with the first measurement no later than 24 ICU hours and the second measurement at least 12 ICU hours after the first. Define the index time as the first creatinine measurement time (`charttime` in `hosp/labevents` for the creatinine item).

Exclusion criteria: creatinine outside 0.2–20 mg/dL, missing units that cannot be mapped to mg/dL, ICU stay shorter than 72 hours, or creatinine recovery already present before the first measurement. Restrict to ICU stays with at least 24 hours of urine-output or fluid-balance availability before the recovery assessment; patients without adequate urine-output/fluid data are excluded from the primary discordance analysis and retained in a sensitivity analysis only if creatinine recovery can be defined.

KDIGO stage is determined from the highest creatinine ratio to the lowest creatinine in the first 72 ICU hours, or a urine-output criterion if a valid urine-output rate can be calculated over at least 6 hours. Stage 2 requires creatinine at least 2 times baseline or urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for at least 12 hours; stage 3 requires creatinine at least 3 times baseline, creatinine at least 2 times baseline with urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for at least 12 hours, or urine output below 0.3 mL/kg/hour for at least 24 hours. Baseline creatinine is the lowest creatinine in the first 72 ICU hours; this is an explicit limitation because no reliable pre-ICU baseline is guaranteed.

## Recovery and discordance phenotype

At 48 hours after the index creatinine, define creatinine recovery as a creatinine value at least 20% lower than the peak creatinine observed during the first 24 ICU hours, with no later creatinine rebound above the peak by 72 hours. If no creatinine is measured in the 44–52 hour interval, use the nearest measurement within 40–56 hours; if none exists, exclude from the primary recovery analysis.

Define low urine output as a mean urine-output rate below 0.5 mL/kg/hour over the 24 hours preceding the 48-hour recovery assessment. Use body weight from `icu/chartevents` if available; otherwise use the standard MIMIC weight item and document the fallback. Define positive fluid status as net fluid balance greater than +1000 mL over the preceding 24 hours, calculated from `icu/ingredientevents` fluid input and `icu/outputevents` urine output, using `itemid` mappings from `icu/d_items`. If fluid balance cannot be calculated, use low urine output alone for the primary discordance phenotype and report fluid-only as a sensitivity analysis.

The primary discordant phenotype is: creatinine recovery present AND (low urine output OR positive fluid balance). The concordant recovery phenotype is creatinine recovery present AND neither abnormal urine output nor positive fluid balance. The non-recovery phenotype is absence of creatinine recovery. This is a descriptive prognostic phenotype, not a diagnosis of true renal recovery.

## RRT status at the 48-hour decision point

RRT status is extracted from `icu/procedureevents` using the following prespecified logic:

- Table: `icu/procedureevents.csv.gz` (`icu/procedureevents`).
- Columns: `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `stay_id`, `charttime`, `itemid`, `value`, `valueuom`, `location`, `locationcategory`, `isopen`, `totaltime`, `statusdescription`.
- Item mapping: identify RRT-related item IDs from `icu/d_items` where `label` or `abbreviation` contains "hemodialysis", "dialysis", "CRRT", "continuous renal replacement", or "renal replacement". The primary RRT item set is prespecified as the union of item IDs matching these terms in `icu/d_items`. If multiple item IDs match, use the union; if no item ID matches, use a sensitivity analysis with a broader item set that includes "dialysis" in `locationcategory`.
- Time window: RRT is considered active at the 48-hour decision point if there is at least one RRT procedure event with `charttime` in the 24-hour window preceding the 48-hour recovery assessment (i.e., `charttime` between 24 and 48 ICU hours after the index creatinine). If `isopen` is 1 and `totaltime` extends beyond the 48-hour assessment, RRT is considered active at the assessment even if the procedure started before the 24-hour window. If no RRT event is present in the 24-hour window, RRT status is "not on RRT".
- Fallback: if `icu/procedureevents` does not contain RRT item IDs for a patient, check `icu/chartevents` for RRT-related chart items (e.g., "Dialysis", "CRRT") in the same 24-hour window. If neither table yields an RRT status, the patient is excluded from the RRT-stratified analysis and retained in the unstratified analysis.

This extraction is prespecified and reproducible. The 24-hour window is chosen to capture RRT that is active at the decision point without including RRT that starts after the assessment (which would be a post-outcome measurement). The `isopen` and `totaltime` fields are used to handle ongoing RRT that started before the 24-hour window but is still active at the 48-hour assessment.

## Outcomes and temporal leakage controls

Primary outcome: ICU death within 7 days after the 48-hour recovery assessment. Use `icu/icustays.outtime`, `hosp/admissions.deathtime`, and `hosp/patients.dod`; death before the assessment is excluded from the outcome and treated as a censoring/eligibility issue.

Secondary outcomes: (1) hospital death by 7 days using `hospital_expire_flag` and `deathtime`; (2) new dialysis within 7 days after the assessment, adjudicated from post-index `icu/procedureevents` and, if necessary, post-index discharge notes. No post-assessment creatinine, urine output, fluid balance, procedure, medication, or note is allowed as a predictor.

All predictor features are measured before the 48-hour recovery assessment. The 48-hour recovery and discordance labels are allowed as exposure variables but not as predictors of their own construction. The model input is fixed at the 48-hour assessment; no future values enter training. Use `charttime`/`starttime`/`endtime`, not `storetime`, for clinical ordering; `storetime` is used only to audit delayed recording and is excluded if it would reveal post-assessment information.

## Estimand and falsification

Primary estimand: the adjusted 7-day ICU death risk difference and risk ratio for discordant versus concordant creatinine recovery, among patients meeting the recovery-eligibility criteria, stratified by RRT status at the 48-hour decision point. The adjustment set is fixed at the 48-hour decision point and includes:

- Age and sex from `hosp/patients`.
- ICU length of stay to index from `icu/icustays.intime`.
- Baseline creatinine, peak creatinine, and KDIGO stage derived from pre-index `hosp/labevents`.
- Current severity at 48 hours: the most recent pre-assessment SOFA component scores derivable from `icu/chartevents` and `hosp/labevents` (respiratory, cardiovascular, coagulation, liver, central nervous system, and renal components), or a prespecified severity proxy if SOFA cannot be fully derived.
- Treatment intensity at 48 hours: vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and renal replacement therapy (RRT) status, all measured in the 24 hours preceding the assessment from `icu/chartevents`, `icu/ingredientevents`, and `icu/procedureevents`.
- Available SAPS/Apache components if derivable from pre-index chartevents.

The primary analysis conditions on this severity- and treatment-intensity-matched adjustment set and is stratified by RRT status at the 48-hour decision point. The stratification is prespecified and not a post-hoc subgroup analysis. The primary estimand is reported separately for the RRT-on stratum and the RRT-off stratum, with a prespecified interaction test for RRT status × discordance phenotype.

A secondary analysis uses a prespecified propensity-score or overlap-weighted comparison restricted to patients with comparable severity and treatment intensity at the decision point, to reduce residual confounding by indication. This secondary analysis is also stratified by RRT status.

Falsification test: among patients with creatinine recovery but no urine-output or fluid data, the discordance label cannot be defined. A secondary falsification is to repeat the analysis using a deliberately post-assessment urine-output window (48–72 hours) only as a negative-control check: if the apparent prognostic association disappears or reverses when using future urine output as a predictor, the primary result is likely driven by leakage or post-outcome measurement rather than a prespecified phenotype. This negative-control analysis is not a causal test.

Supportive result: discordant recovery has a clinically meaningful higher 7-day ICU death risk after adjustment for severity and treatment intensity at the 48-hour decision point, with a confidence interval excluding no difference and a prespecified effect-size threshold of at least 5 percentage points risk difference, in at least one RRT stratum. Adverse result: no difference or a confidence interval crossing zero and the 5-point threshold in both strata; this would weaken the claim that discordance adds prognostic information beyond current severity and treatment. Inconclusive result: too few eligible discordant patients in either stratum, unstable estimates, or a result sensitive to the urine-output/fluid definitions; then the phenotype needs refinement or the question deferred.

## Interpretation of stratum-specific effects

The primary result is interpreted as follows:

1. If discordance predicts worse outcomes in both RRT strata with a significant interaction test, the phenotype is robust to the presence or absence of RRT and may represent a genuine signal of renal dysfunction that is not captured by creatinine alone. The overall result is interpreted as a consistent prognostic signal across RRT status.

2. If discordance predicts worse outcomes only in the non-RRT stratum, the phenotype may be driven by the absence of RRT (i.e., patients who are sicker but have not yet been escalated to RRT) rather than by a true discordance signal. The overall result is interpreted as a non-RRT-specific prognostic signal, and the clinical implication is that creatinine-based recovery may be misleading in patients who have not yet received RRT.

3. If discordance predicts worse outcomes only in the RRT stratum, the phenotype may reflect the severity of illness that necessitated RRT rather than a discordance-specific signal. The overall result is interpreted as an RRT-specific prognostic signal, and the clinical implication is that creatinine-based recovery may be misleading in patients on RRT.

4. If the interaction test is not significant and the stratum-specific effects are in the same direction with similar magnitudes, the overall result is interpreted as a consistent prognostic signal across RRT status, and the stratification is reported as a sensitivity analysis rather than a primary finding.

The prespecified interaction test for RRT status × discordance phenotype is used to determine whether the stratum-specific effects are significantly different. If the interaction is significant, the stratum-specific effects are reported as the primary result. If the interaction is not significant, the overall (pooled) effect is reported as the primary result, and the stratum-specific effects are reported as a sensitivity analysis.

## Method comparison

Baseline: a severity- and treatment-intensity-adjusted logistic regression predicting 7-day ICU death, using only pre-index and pre-assessment covariates plus the binary discordance phenotype, stratified by RRT status. This estimates the adjusted association within each RRT stratum and provides an interpretable baseline.

Substantive alternative: a time-aware survival model (Cox proportional hazards or flexible parametric survival model) using pre-index covariates and the time-varying creatinine, urine-output, and fluid-balance trajectories up to the 48-hour assessment, stratified by RRT status. The model estimates the hazard of ICU death over 7 days and can reveal whether the prognostic signal is driven by the level at assessment or by the slope/trajectory of creatinine and fluid status. This adds information because a binary recovery label discards the temporal pattern that may distinguish true recovery from dilution or delayed creatinine clearance.

Both methods use the same cohort, same temporal cutoff, and same outcome. The baseline is fitted on the full eligible cohort within each RRT stratum; the alternative is fitted on the same cohort with time-varying features within each RRT stratum. Evaluation uses bootstrap 95% confidence intervals for risk differences and hazard ratios, and calibration (Brier score or calibration-in-the-large) if event counts permit. The alternative is selected if it materially improves calibration or reveals a trajectory-specific signal not captured by the binary phenotype; otherwise the simpler baseline is retained.

## Resource estimate and feasibility

The cohort is expected to be in the low thousands to tens of thousands of ICU admissions, depending on KDIGO stage and urine-output availability. The RRT strata are expected to be imbalanced, with the RRT-on stratum likely smaller than the RRT-off stratum. A CPU-only analysis with pandas/SQL and logistic regression or a Cox model is feasible within a few hours on 4 CPUs and 16 GiB RAM. No GPU is required. A bounded diagnostic on a 1000-admission sample can verify urine-output, fluid-balance, and RRT extraction before the full cohort.

## Limitations

Creatinine is a surrogate for measured kidney function; the discordance phenotype identifies suspected, not confirmed, discordance [K1]. Baseline creatinine is approximated by the lowest early ICU value, not a true pre-illness value. Urine output and fluid balance are recorded with missingness and may reflect observation intensity. RRT status is extracted from `icu/procedureevents` and may be incomplete if RRT is not recorded in this table. The study is prognostic, not causal; severity, sepsis, fluid overload, and treatment decisions can confound the association. A stronger claim about renal recovery or treatment benefit would require measured kidney function, external validation, or a prospective study.
