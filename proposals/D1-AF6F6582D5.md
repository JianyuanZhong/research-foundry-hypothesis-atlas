# Asynchronous organ recovery after circulatory stabilization: a severity-matched, temporally explicit MIMIC-IV study

Status: active draft, not yet a registered hypothesis. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Among adults in the ICU whose vasopressor support is stopped or substantially reduced, does delayed recovery of renal or respiratory proxies identify a higher risk of subsequent ICU deterioration or death than recovery of those proxies, after accounting for severity and treatment intensity at the stabilization decision point?

This is clinically consequential because a single SOFA assessment or a binary improved/not-improved label can miss delayed organ failure. A patient may appear hemodynamically stabilized while creatinine, urine output, or oxygenation remains abnormal. If delayed recovery is prognostic beyond current severity and treatment, clinicians would have a concrete reason to monitor the recovering organ more intensively after circulatory support is withdrawn, rather than treating vasopressor-free status as full recovery.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that longitudinal laboratory and support patterns can distinguish ICU patients with different prognoses. The expert seed proposes that recovery after circulatory stabilization is asynchronous across organs and that delayed or incomplete recovery predicts later deterioration. The unresolved claim is whether the discordant recovery phenotype independently predicts subsequent ICU deterioration or death after accounting for severity and treatment intensity at the stabilization decision point. The study is prognostic, not causal.

## Temporal design

The stabilization event is defined as the time at which vasopressor support is stopped or substantially reduced. The recovery phenotype is defined as the state of renal and respiratory proxies at 48 hours after the stabilization event. The discordant recovery phenotype is defined as the state of renal or respiratory proxies that remain impaired at 48 hours after the stabilization event, while the recovered phenotype is defined as the state of renal and respiratory proxies that have recovered at 48 hours after the stabilization event.

The outcome is subsequent ICU deterioration or death, defined as the occurrence of a new organ failure, escalation of support, or death within 7 days after the stabilization event. The temporal boundaries are explicitly defined to avoid leakage: the recovery phenotype is determined at 48 hours after the stabilization event, and the outcome is measured from 48 hours after the stabilization event to 7 days after the stabilization event.

## Method comparison

A simple baseline would compare the discordant recovery phenotype with the recovered phenotype without adjustment for severity and treatment intensity. A learned alternative would fit a model that captures longitudinal patterns of organ recovery and treatment intensity, potentially revealing information that a single recovery label loses. A mechanistic alternative would model the relationship between vasopressor withdrawal, organ recovery, and subsequent deterioration, potentially revealing the causal pathway. These alternatives are not chosen because the current study is a bounded prognostic check that can be executed with available data and compute.

## Falsification criteria

A prespecified comparison of the discordant recovery phenotype with the recovered phenotype, adjusted for severity and treatment intensity at the stabilization decision point, with a null or adverse association would refute the hypothesis. A positive association that disappears after adjustment for current severity and treatment intensity would indicate that the phenotype is a marker of current severity rather than an independent recovery process.

## Limitations

The limitation that organ recovery is inferred from laboratory and support measures rather than direct organ function is explicitly preserved. The study is prognostic, not causal, and the limitations are honestly stated.
