The unresolved claim is narrower and clinically consequential:

> If the frozen AG1 roster has a larger fixed-denominator yield of first recorded inpatient N18 before death than AF1, does that contrast remain after direct standardization to the same prespecified distribution of prior nonrenal diagnosis-record density, and is it larger than a prespecified broad nonrenal diagnosis-record negative control?

A positive answer would make generic prior-recording opportunity a less adequate explanation for the kidney-coded contrast and justify a prospective, adjudicated comparison of grip and fat-free-mass testing strategies. A null or adverse answer would qualify the interpretation: the observed N18 difference may be attributable at least as well to healthcare-contact/recording opportunity as to kidney-specific enrichment. Neither result establishes that grip causes kidney disease, that N18 is incident or persistent CKD, or that either testing strategy benefits patients.
