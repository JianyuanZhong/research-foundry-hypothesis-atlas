# Outcome-missingness tipping-point transport analysis for the frozen HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B design

## Targeted successor and unresolved clinical question

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent's exact frozen repeat-TACE reconstruction, complete-triplet estimand, outcome-blind support funnel, non-overlapping calendar blocks, and executed findings. It does not pool eras, change the model, redefine the endpoint, or convert the systemic-record label into a treatment comparison.

The remaining decision-relevant limitation is sharper than generic calendar drift. The only future block that passes both outcome-blind support and complete-triplet arm gates is 2022; 2023 has 9 systemic BP-eligible episodes and 29/43 outcomes observed, while 2024 has 9 systemic BP-eligible episodes and only 14/70 outcomes observed (1 systemic complete outcome). Thus a favorable complete-triplet RMSE in a later block could reflect the subset for which a post-event assay was recorded. Conversely, simply discarding those blocks leaves unresolved whether the observed incremental signal would survive plausible outcome-selection patterns.

The unresolved, falsifiable question is:

> In each fixed future calendar block, how much would the unobserved post-repeat assay values among outcome-blind B/P-eligible episodes have to differ from the pre-origin observed outcome range to erase the locked M1-versus-M0 predictive contrast, and does the conclusion remain unchanged under transparent, assay-specific worst-case and tipping-point completions?

This is a partial-identification/sensitivity question about the *same prognostic prediction contrast*, not a claim that missing outcomes are missing at random and not a recovery of unobserved outcomes. It provides a scientifically useful boundary beyond the single supported cutoff: later blocks can be classified as robustly adverse/supportive under bounded completions, or honestly remain unidentified when the required outcome departure is outside the available support.

The strongest evidence already supported by the parent is limited to the selected observed complete-triplet population: the 2022 forward block passed both support gates and had high Y observation (28/29 for each assay), whereas 2023 and 2024 failed support and had marked outcome-observation loss (29/43 and 14/70 overall). The new experiment tests robustness of the *interpretation* of those later blocks; it must not be described as evidence of transport if the bounds include both signs.

### New outcome-missingness stress test

For every assay and origin, fit the locked M0/M1 models on the same pre-origin observed complete triplets as the parent and calculate predictions for every future complete triplet. For each future BP-eligible episode, retain a row-level status (`Y_observed` or missing), arm, calendar block, pre-Y features, and—only when observed—the actual `Y`. No future outcome is used in eligibility, calendar-support, or observation modeling.

For each origin, define assay-specific outcome bounds *using training observed Y values only*: `L_train = min(Y_train)` and `U_train = max(Y_train)`, after the exact assay-specific numeric parser and before any test outcome is read. If a training range is degenerate, or if a later observed Y falls outside `[L_train,U_train]`, report the bounded stress test as unsupported rather than silently widening the range. This is a transparent support scenario, not an assumption that the missing outcomes lie in range. As a secondary descriptive check, repeat with the full-source assay range without using it for fitting; label this as non-temporal and do not use it for transport evidence.

For each future BP-eligible episode, let `p0_i` and `p1_i` be fixed predictions from M0 and M1. For observed Y, calculate `e0_i=(Y_i-p0_i)^2`, `e1_i=(Y_i-p1_i)^2`, and `d_i=e0_i-e1_i`. For missing Y, do not impute a single value. Compute the sharp interval for each model's missing-case squared-error sum over `Y in [L_train,U_train]`; because squared error is convex, its minimum is at `clip(pj_i,L_train,U_train)` and its maximum is at one of the two endpoints. Sum these with observed-case errors to obtain lower and upper MSE for M0 and M1 over *all BP-eligible future episodes*. Also calculate the sharp interval for the M1-minus-M0 error contrast directly; its per-row difference is affine in unknown Y, so its extrema occur at `L_train` and `U_train`.

Report, separately overall and by systemic/comparator label:

1. observed-complete-triplet RMSE gain (the unchanged primary quantity) and its paired bootstrap interval;
2. the bounded all-BP-eligible MSE contrast and RMSE-difference interval under `[L_train,U_train]`, with the number and fraction of missing Y episodes;
3. a tipping-point value: the smallest common displacement pattern of missing Y values from the observed-case empirical mean, constrained to `[L_train,U_train]`, that makes the all-BP-eligible MSE contrast equal zero. If no such displacement exists in the interval, state that the sign is robust under the bounded scenario; if the interval contains zero, state that the sign is unidentified under that scenario;

## Falsification and interpretation branches

- **Supportive for bounded robustness:** the inherited reconstruction and clocks pass; a block passes both outcome-blind and complete-triplet support gates; the observed complete-triplet primary contrast meets the parent's prespecified RMSE/uncertainty criterion; and the all-BP-eligible bounded contrast remains favorable throughout `[L_train,U_train]` with no material arm-specific reversal. This supports robustness of a short-horizon prognostic association under the explicitly bounded outcome-support scenario in that selected calendar mixture only.
- **Adverse:** the locked observed-case contrast is at or below zero in an adequately supported block; M1 degrades repeatedly; or a bounded completion reverses the sign in an otherwise supported block. This challenges incremental prediction in the selected episodes or shows that its interpretation is selection-sensitive; it does not prove B suffices, establish a mechanism, or imply treatment harm.
- **Selection-sensitive:** observed complete-triplet gain is favorable but the bounded interval includes zero, or the tipping point is close to the observed-case range. The result is not transport evidence; report that outcome observation can explain the apparent contrast under the stated bounds.
- **Inconclusive/unbounded:** any support gate fails; arm counts are below threshold; observed Y lies outside the pre-origin range; training Y range is degenerate; test SD is unidentified; the interval is wide or computationally unstable; or the result depends on the non-temporal full-source range. This is the expected branch for much of 2023–2024 given the parent's 29/43 and 14/70 observation rates, and especially for the 2024 systemic arm with one observed complete outcome.

A favorable bounded result does not establish multi-era transport, laboratory clinical validity, recovery, toxicity, imaging response, survival, mortality, safety, treatment effect, utility, actionability, or patient benefit. It remains conditional on recorded repeat-TACE selection, observed B/P eligibility, the complete-source assay support scenario, and institutional observation. A negative result does not establish that P has no prognostic information; it can reflect sparse support, model misspecification, or selection. A null result is not evidence of equivalence.
