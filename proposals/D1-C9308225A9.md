# Stable-baseline hypotension duration and observed renal deterioration: eICU transport experiment

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

Among adults in a first ICU stay with a reliable pre-landmark creatinine trajectory, does greater time-weighted MAP below 65 mmHg during the preceding six hours identify a higher 24-hour cumulative incidence of a subsequent creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal beyond minimum MAP and pre-window clinical state, and does the association reproduce in hospitals not used for fitting?

The primary hypothesis is that, within prespecified minimum-MAP strata, greater low-MAP minutes has a positive, non-zero standardized risk difference for the first observed renal deterioration signal after adjusting for baseline renal level and pre-landmark trend, physiology, support and the observation process. The secondary hypothesis is that an ordered temporal model will identify recovery-versus-persistence patterns beyond minimum and total duration, but only a prespecified pattern reproduced in held-out hospitals counts as evidence.

This is an observational risk-marker experiment. It does not estimate the causal effect of permitting hypotension, giving a pressor, or targeting MAP 65. Existing evidence and the parent design support only that eICU contains ICU-relative MAP, creatinine-labelled lab rows, competing discharge/death fields and hospital identifiers. The unresolved claim is whether duration remains associated with a future observed renal deterioration signal when a noisy single pre-landmark creatinine is replaced by a stable, comparable two-result baseline and trend. The clinical advance is a more credible kidney-signal phenotype and an explicit test of whether duration adds information beyond minimum MAP, rather than a treatment recommendation.

## Cohort, stable baseline and time boundaries

Retain the first ICU stay per `uniquepid`: age >=18 (map age >89 to 90), `unitvisitnumber=1`, nonmissing hospital and numeric `unitdischargeoffset >=2160`. Resolve a conflicting first-visit row by earliest valid admission and report the conflict.

Use candidate landmarks t = 12, 18, ..., 72 hours. Retain only t with >=6 hours of prior observation, >=24 hours before recorded ICU discharge, no EOL offset at or before t, and the stable-baseline criteria below. Predictors have timestamps <=t; for labs `labresultrevisedoffset <=t` is also required.

The primary stable creatinine baseline requires at least two numeric creatinine observations in [t-48h,t], each with result and revised offsets <=t, with the retained label/system/interface mapping passing the audit and a comparable source scale. Use the median of the two most recent comparable results as baseline; record their offsets and slope (difference divided by elapsed hours). Do not use an Apache creatinine or any future result to define this baseline. A sensitivity cohort permits one pre-t result and uses the parent’s last-value rule, but it cannot replace the stable-baseline primary analysis.

For valid numeric `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean`, sort by `observationoffset`. Each value represents the following min(5, next offset-current offset) minutes, with no carry-forward over gaps and no interval past t. In [t-360,t), require >=80% observed coverage and calculate covered minutes, low-MAP minutes for MAP <65, low-MAP fraction, minimum, mean, SD, last value and coverage. Repeat 70% and 90% coverage, MAP thresholds 60 and 70, and the separate aperiodic source analysis.

## Outcome, competing events and estimand

The primary future signal is the first retained comparable numeric creatinine in (t,t+24h] that is >= baseline+0.3 or >=1.5*baseline. A later result does not create another event. The ordered mutually exclusive processes are: (1) renal deterioration signal before death/discharge and within 24h; (2) ICU death before a renal signal; (3) alive ICU discharge/transfer before a renal signal; (4) no signal by 24h while in ICU with a future comparable creatinine observation; and (5) no future observation, including observation loss or discharge/death without an observable future creatinine, treated as observation censoring rather than a negative.

The primary estimand is the standardized 24-hour cumulative-incidence risk difference comparing >60 versus 0-60 low-MAP minutes within minimum-MAP strata, under the eligible-landmark distribution, with death and alive discharge competing events and observation censoring handled by an observation model using pre-t covariates and early-window observation history. Truncate inverse-probability-of-observation weights at the 1st/99th percentiles. Report cause-specific and subdistribution summaries secondarily. Report the stable-baseline inclusion rate and compare it with the one-result sensitivity; a large selection difference is itself a limitation.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Fit a regularized multinomial competing-risk pooled-logistic baseline M_min with minimum-MAP spline but no low-MAP duration, pre-window MAP/heart-rate/respiration/SpO2 summaries and slopes, coverage, stable creatinine median, pre-landmark creatinine slope and interval, age/sex/ethnicity, time since admission, admission Apache variables/scores, current documented ventilation and pressor-record indicators, and missingness indicators. Fit M_duration by adding low-MAP minutes/fraction and its prespecified minimum-MAP interaction. Both output CIFs, standardized risks, competing-event probabilities, calibration, Brier score and frozen-threshold decision curves.

Fit a substantive six-bin temporal convolution or small GRU T_shape on the same cohort and target, using hourly MAP summaries/masks, heart rate, respiration, SpO2, creatinine value/observation history and slope, pressor/ventilation record history, admission covariates and explicit masks. Fit duration-free T_min and full trajectory T_shape. T_shape can reveal sustained versus transient hypotension, recovery slope, ordering of MAP and renal observations and irregular observation gaps that M_min/M_duration discard. It is useful only if it improves calibration or reproduces a prespecified shape pattern in held-out hospitals, not from AUROC alone.

Split by patients within training hospitals for fitting/early stopping and evaluate untouched hospitals with >=200 eligible landmarks and both exposure/event levels. Use three learned seeds and patient-within-hospital bootstrap intervals; do not use hospital ID as a predictor. Under inputs.json’s 2-CPU, <=8-GiB budget, expect 20-40 minutes extraction, <15 minutes per regularized model and 45-90 minutes for both temporal variants; GPU is deferred because it does not answer a different scientific uncertainty.

## Falsification and interpretation

Gate interpretation on the stable-baseline label/interface/scale audit, plausible values, sufficient paired pre/future support, MAP coverage, exposure contrast within minimum strata, stable observation weights and supported held-out hospitals. Compare primary stable-baseline and one-result sensitivity, periodic/aperiodic MAP, 60/65/70 thresholds and 70/80/90% coverage. Permute the order of pre-t MAP observations while preserving values/counts/coverage; a shape-specific association should attenuate. Use the pre-landmark placebo creatinine-rise rule in [t-24h,t), and compare future-lab observation as a process outcome. Exclude EOL proxies, near-discharge landmarks, abrupt MAP coverage loss and implausible jumps. Compare observation-weighted, complete-case and unweighted estimates.

Supportive results require a positive duration contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero, persistence across primary sensitivity checks, weak placebo association, non-dominance by death/discharge, and either a reproducible duration effect or prespecified recovery-shape pattern in held-out hospitals. This supports only an observational marker for an observed creatinine deterioration signal and motivates prospective monitoring research.

Adverse results include a precise null/negative contrast, disappearance after observation adjustment or order permutation, a strong placebo, domination by competing discharge/death differences, or held-out failure; these support minimum MAP, severity or documentation selection as the reproducible signal. Inconclusive results include non-comparable creatinine scales, too few stable paired baselines/future observations, absent exposure contrast, unstable weights, insufficient sites, or wide intervals. No treatment recommendation follows.

Computationally checkable are source hashes, schemas, filters/joins, stable-baseline construction, MAP integration, label audit, timestamp safety, event classification, fitted models, CIFs, uncertainty, calibration/Brier/decision metrics, split integrity, transport and falsifications. Not established are adjudicated KDIGO AKI, urine output, true perfusion, outpatient baseline, true pressor dose/stop time, fluid/nutrition status, clinician intent, reasons for missing tests or benefit from acting on a warning; these require expert adjudication, richer data or a prospective study.

## Alternatives and provenance

This child is a substantive repair of [prior hypothesis]: it changes the renal phenotype from a potentially unstable single last pre-t value to a comparable two-result baseline plus pre-t slope and makes stable-baseline selection support an explicit output. It preserves the parent’s competing events, observation censoring, corrected ICU-relative time, minimum-versus-duration baseline, temporal alternative and hospital transport.

The unused eICU hyperoxia seed remains deferred because longitudinal PaO2/FiO2 semantics and pulmonary-status adjudication are not verified; SpO2 is not PaO2. Ventilatory burden remains deferred because the inspected schema has no plateau pressure or delivered tidal volume. Nighttime discharge, transfer risk and hospital recovery remain observationally plausible but need stronger post-ICU outcomes or organizational evidence. Insulin-hypoglycemia remains deferred because eICU medication/infusion records do not establish administration continuity or nutrition interruption.

The three demonstrations are contextual rather than reproductions. Delphi’s UKB sequence-learning evidence supports considering a bounded temporal alternative, but its original cohort is not eICU.
