# Is a plasma-protein–disease association a disease-specific signal (A) or a shared systemic axis (B)? Separating a distributed systemic axis from an acute-phase-cluster artifact

## Unresolved question and clinical importance
Plasma-proteomic screens repeatedly nominate a single circulating protein (GDF15, FABP1, CHI3L1,
IL-6/CRP family) as a disease-specific preclinical biomarker. It is unresolved whether a
protein–diagnosis association marks a disease-specific process (**claim A**) or mainly a *shared
systemic* morbidity/inflammation/aging axis elevated across many diagnoses and tracking cumulative
disease burden (**claim B**) [K2]. Prediction is not specificity: a signature predictive of one
disease can be more parsimoniously explained by a shared exposure and extend across unrelated
conditions [K1]. This decides whether a circulating protein is a credible disease-specific
early-detection target or only a generic risk/severity correlate — decisive for any biomarker or
prevention programme.

## The genuinely new rival added this episode (why it changes interpretation)
The island's prior episodes established that a dominant shared proteomic component exists (ep34
coherence diagnostic: f1 = 0.173 on a 300-protein subsample). But a dominant shared component can
arise from at least two materially different structures whose observable predictions differ:

- **Rival R (new): an acute-phase / inflammation protein cluster artifact.** The "shared axis" is
  largely a small canonical acute-phase block (CRP, IL6, SAA, HGF, SERPINA3, ORM, LBP, HP,
  fibrinogen, APCS, IL1B, IL18, TNF, CXCL8, CCL2/3/4, CHI3L1) whose members are tightly
  co-regulated by a common acute inflammatory driver. R predicts that PC1 *loadings* concentrate on
  those proteins and that removing the block **collapses f1 toward the permutation band**.
- **Distributed systemic axis D (the parent's working reading).** The shared component is a diffuse
  average over many unrelated proteins (a morbidity/aging/technical structure). D predicts that
  PC1 loadings are spread across many proteins outside any inflammation block and that removing the
  acute-phase block leaves f1 **essentially unchanged**.

These two predict different block-removal behavior, so the rival is separable *descriptively*, and —
decisively — it changes how a positive Δ_D (claim A) or a near-zero Δ_D (claim B) should be read:
if the shared axis is an acute-phase cluster, a negative Δ_D is trivially explained by a handful of
reactants rather than by "systemic morbidity". [K1] bounds claim A (prediction ≠ specificity);
[K3] bounds the exposure (measured protein levels are imperfect proxies with substantial genetic/
technical architecture, so a shared assay/batch component can inflate PC1); [K2] supplies the
concrete learned shared-axis alternative (ProAL50).

## Pre-specified solver test (estimand + the new block-removal coherence criterion)
1. **Participant-level split** (inherited): fit all signatures/axes on a discovery half of `n_eid`;
   evaluate only on the held-out half; report the split manifest and seed.
2. **Primary estimand — held-out cross-disease transfer gap.** For a target disease D (≥200 cases),
   train an L2-penalized logistic protein signature for D on the discovery half. On the held-out
   half report AUC(D) and the mean AUC over a pre-specified set of *unrelated* diseases D′ (different
   ICD-10 chapters, each ≥200 cases). Define **Δ_D = AUC(D) − mean_AUC(D′)**.
   - Claim A predicts Δ_D > 0 beyond the permutation band; claim B predicts Δ_D ≈ 0 within the band.
3. **New pre-specified coherence criterion (this episode's delta).** Recompute f1 on the same frozen
   full 2,923-protein panel **and** on the block-removed panel, with the same estimator and the same
   permutation-band construction, and report `Δf1 = f1_full − f1_blockremoved` with its band.
   - **Rival R (acute-phase-cluster artifact) requires Δf1 ≥ ~+0.5·f1_full**, i.e. f1 collapsing a
     large fraction of the way toward the band when the block is removed, **and** acute-phase
     proteins among the top PC1 loadings / a concentrated loading.
   - **Distributed axis D requires Δf1 ≈ 0** (block removal leaves f1 essentially unchanged) with
     loadings spread outside the block.
   - A **positive Δ_D that survives block removal and axis orthogonalization** supports claim A;
     a **near-zero Δ_D within the band** supports claim B — but only if Δf1 ≈ 0, otherwise a
     near-zero Δ_D is confounded by the acute-phase cluster and is *not* evidence for a distributed
     systemic axis.
4. **Secondary estimands retained from the parent** so the two discriminants can be compared: the
   per-(protein, disease) attenuation fraction AF = 1 − |β_adj|/|β_unadj| after adding the shared-axis
   term; and the over-adjustment two-axis sensitivity (axis with disease status excluded vs axis
   orthogonalized to the target disease indicator).

## Baselines and the learned alternative (what the alternative reveals)
- **Baseline:** age + sex logistic model (no proteins) — the information the axis must beat.
- **Simple protein baseline:** a single-protein logistic signature on the standardized value, plus
  the naive full-panel L2 logistic.
- **Learned alternative:** elastic-net / latent-axis model (penalized multi-protein score, in the
  spirit of ProAL50 [K2]) fit on the discovery half. The alternative can reveal a *multi-protein
  shared axis* that the single-protein baseline cannot, and — via Δ_D and Δf1 — whether that axis is
  distributed (D) or an acute-phase cluster (R). The comparison tests a substantive scientific
  uncertainty (is the shared structure distributed or a small reactant cluster?), not a small
  predictive gain.

## Supportive / adverse / inconclusive
- **Supportive of claim A:** Δ_D > 0 beyond the permutation band for ≥1 high-burden disease, stable
  across two split seeds, **and** Δf1 ≈ 0 (the axis is not an acute-phase cluster), **and** it
  survives block removal and axis orthogonalization.
- **Supportive of claim B:** Δ_D ≈ 0 for all diseases (within band) while AUC(D) still exceeds the
  age+sex baseline — *provided* Δf1 ≈ 0; a near-zero Δ_D with a collapsing Δf1 is confounded by the
  acute-phase cluster and is not evidence for a distributed axis.
- **Adverse / design caution:** large in-sample-vs-held-out attenuation drop (axis overfit); large
  sensitivity of AF to the disease-orthogonalized axis (over-adjustment); Δ_D driven entirely by the
  acute-phase block.
- **Inconclusive:** <200 cases for tested blocks; non-identifiable axis (degenerate dominant PC);
  permutation band overlapping all Δ_D; or block-removal test inconclusive because several canonical
  reactants (CRP, SAA1/2, ORM2, HP, FGB, FGG) are absent from the panel.
