# Persistent hyperlactatemia with improving perfusion: a prespecified MIMIC-IV study

Status: active draft, not yet a registered hypothesis. Parent seed: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Among adults in the ICU with repeatedly elevated lactate, does persistent lactate elevation at 24 hours coexisting with improving perfusion identify a worse 7-day ICU prognosis than comparable lactate trajectories with worsening perfusion?

The seed's bedside ambiguity is that a persistently elevated lactate concentration can reflect residual tissue hypoperfusion, impaired hepatic clearance, reduced production, or observation intensity, rather than a single pathophysiological state. If perfusion appears to improve while lactate remains elevated, clinicians may underestimate ongoing risk. Conversely, the phenotype could be a marker of current severity, treatment selection, or selective measurement. The study is clinically consequential only if the phenotype predicts subsequent outcomes beyond standard severity and time-varying perfusion measurements.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The inspected literature reports that lactate clearance is associated with ICU outcomes, but repeated measurements are selectively obtained and lactate is a surrogate for tissue oxygen delivery and utilization. The unresolved claim is whether persistent hyperlactatemia with improving perfusion is an independent prognostic phenotype or a marker of severity, observation intensity, or treatment selection.

[K1] Lactate is a context-dependent risk marker and metabolic signal rather than a kidney-specific biomarker, supporting the need to interpret lactate trajectories in the context of perfusion and organ function. [K1]

[K2] Accurate quantification of microvascular assessment is challenging due to fragmented methodologies, supporting the need for convergent, translatable inference rather than reliance on single-metric claims. [K2]

[K3] MIMIC-IV is a public dataset with exact data bindings and temporal structure, supporting the feasibility of a prespecified MIMIC-IV study of persistent hyperlactatemia with improving perfusion. [K3]

## Study population and data bindings

Source: MIMIC-IV v3.1, `datasets/mimic/` (see `datasets/README.md` for exact paths and schema).

- `patsim` / `patients`: patient ID, age, sex, admission time, ICU stay.
- `labevents`: lactate (`itemid` for lactate), creatinine, bilirubin, platelets, WBC.
- `chartevents`: MAP, heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature, urine output, fluid balance.
- `inputevents`: vasopressor agents (norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine).
- `outputevents`: urine output.
- `prescriptions` / `drug_events`: fluid administration (crystalloid, colloid).
- `d_icu_stays`: ICU admission/discharge times.
- `d_lab_items` / `d_items`: item ID crosswalk.

Join keys: `subject_id` for patient-level joins; `stay_id` for ICU-stay-level joins; `charttime`/`storetime` for temporal alignment.

## Prespecified phenotype

1. **Cohort**: Adults (age >= 18) with >= 2 lactate measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU stay, with at least one lactate >= 4 mmol/L.
2. **Perfusion status**: Defined by MAP >= 65 mmHg and vasopressor dose (norepinephrine equivalent) at the time of the second lactate measurement.
3. **Phenotype groups**:
   - **Persistent hyperlactatemia with improving perfusion**: Lactate at 24h >= 4 mmol/L, MAP >= 65 mmHg, and vasopressor dose decreased by >= 50% from baseline.
   - **Persistent hyperlactatemia with worsening perfusion**: Lactate at 24h >= 4 mmol/L, MAP < 65 mmHg, or vasopressor dose increased by >= 50% from baseline.
   - **Lactate clearance**: Lactate at 24h < 4 mmol/L (reference group).
4. **Follow-up**: 7 days from the 24-hour phenotype assessment time.

## Outcomes

- Primary: ICU death within 7 days.
- Secondary: 28-day mortality, ICU length of stay, need for mechanical ventilation at 7 days.

## Baseline and alternative analysis

- **Baseline**: Cox proportional hazards model with baseline covariates (age, sex, SOFA score, MAP, vasopressor dose, urine output, fluid balance) and phenotype group as a categorical variable.
- **Alternative**: A trajectory-based model that incorporates the slope of lactate and perfusion measures over the first 24 hours, testing whether the trajectory adds prognostic information beyond the endpoint phenotype.
- **Comparison**: The alternative model should reveal whether the endpoint phenotype captures the relevant information or whether the trajectory is necessary.

## Falsification criteria

- If the phenotype group does not predict 7-day ICU death after adjustment for baseline and time-varying severity/perfusion measures, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the association is explained by observation intensity (number of lactate measurements) or prior treatment (vasopressor dose at baseline), the phenotype is not an independent prognostic marker.

## Limitations

- Lactate is a surrogate for tissue oxygen delivery and utilization, not a direct measure of microcirculatory dysfunction.
- Repeated measurements are selectively obtained; observation intensity may confound the phenotype.
- The study is prognostic, not causal; association does not establish that fluid administration causes the observed outcomes.
