# Rolling-origin cutoff and recent-window calibration of repeat-TACE laboratory persistence

## Parent and unresolved question

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent showed, at one outcome-blind 2023-01-01 split, that an earlier-period affine persistence forecast (`Yhat = a + bP`) beat raw persistence and inherited M1 in later complete-triplet episodes. That result supports only a prognostic transport signal in the selected HCC repeat-TACE population. It does not establish that the cutoff is representative, that the affine parameters are stable, or that a recent calibration window is clinically preferable.

The falsifiable extension is: across multiple prespecified outcome-blind annual rolling origins, does a trailing two-calendar-year affine calibration outperform an expanding-history affine calibration, while affine calibration continues to beat raw persistence and inherited M1? The competing explanations are clinically distinguishable: stable transport (expanding and recent are similar, both beat raw), temporal drift favoring recent calibration (recent wins consistently with improved calibration), stale-history/adverse transport (expanding loses raw or recent), or low-support inconclusiveness (wide uncertainty, especially in systemic arm).

## Rolling-origin experiment

Origins were fixed before reading outcome metrics: 2022-01-01, 2023-01-01, and 2024-01-01. Each test window is the following calendar year, [origin, origin + 1 year); 2025 was not used because the snapshot is incomplete for a full calendar year. For every assay and origin, training uses only episodes with `event_date < origin`; the recent model uses only `origin - 2 calendar years <= event_date < origin`; the disjoint test block uses `origin <= event_date < origin + 1 calendar year`.

The four locked forecasts are: (1) raw persistence `P`; (2) expanding-history affine `a+bP`; (3) trailing-two-year affine `a+bP`; and (4) expanding-history inherited M1 ridge model using B, P, age, sex, systemic-arm indicator, event timing (`days`), and P lead hours. Affine coefficients, M1 preprocessing, and alpha selection use training outcomes only. No test Y is used for window choice or tuning. Primary outcomes are RMSE, MAE, mean prediction bias, calibration intercept/slope, and paired RMSE differences. Uncertainty is a 5,000-replicate patient-frequency bootstrap stratified by arm and disjoint test-year block, conditional on the fixed rolling predictions.

Albumin had 28, 29, and 14 test episodes at the three origins (71 pooled). Pooled RMSE was raw 3.836, expanding affine 3.314, recent affine 3.335, and M1 3.488. Thus expanding affine gained 0.523 RMSE versus raw and 0.174 versus M1; recent lost 0.021 RMSE versus expanding. Recent beat expanding at only 1/3 origins. Bootstrap for recent-minus-expanding gain was -0.0219 (95% interval -0.0662 to 0.0203), while expanding-minus-raw was 0.5345 (0.1515 to 0.9668). The affine advantage over raw is therefore reproducible in this bounded sample, but there is no evidence that a recent window improves albumin calibration.

Total bilirubin had 28, 29, and 16 test episodes (73 pooled). Pooled RMSE was raw 11.135, expanding affine 8.671, recent affine 8.580, and M1 9.840. Expanding affine gained 2.464 RMSE versus raw and recent gained an additional 0.092 versus expanding; recent beat expanding at all 3 origins. The recent-minus-expanding bootstrap median was 0.084 (95% interval -0.050 to 0.274), so the direction favors recent calibration but uncertainty includes no gain. Expanding-minus-raw was 2.488 (1.556 to 3.268), supporting robust improvement over raw persistence. M1 did not improve over expanding affine in pooled bilirubin (expanding minus M1 = -1.169 RMSE), although one 2023 origin favored M1; this is not a claim of model equivalence.

## Interpretation and falsification

The strongest supported conclusion is assay-specific. Affine persistence consistently beat raw persistence at all three origins for both assays, with large pooled bootstrap gains, but recent-window superiority is not established: it is absent for albumin and directionally favorable but imprecise for bilirubin. The data are compatible with stable expanding-history transport and with modest bilirubin temporal drift; they reject neither because the recent-window confidence intervals are broad. The apparent 2023 advantage from the parent is not sufficient evidence for a universal recent recalibration policy.

A falsification of the stable expanding-history claim would be a prespecified future origin where expanding affine loses raw persistence or recent affine wins by a clinically meaningful, uncertainty-supported margin. A falsification of recent-window superiority is the observed albumin pattern and the bilirubin interval crossing zero. A clinically adverse result would mean temporal nontransportability or changing measurement/practice processes, not treatment harm. Low systemic-arm support is a major uncertainty: annual systemic counts were as low as one albumin episode in 2024 and two bilirubin episodes, so arm-specific clinical conclusions are not estimable here.

The experiment is prognostic only. It cannot adjudicate hepatic decompensation, tumor response, treatment benefit/toxicity, or causal effects. The source lacks validated assay units/reference ranges, adjudicated outcomes, imaging/tumor burden, reliable outside-care capture, and a clinical action threshold/loss function. Stronger conclusions require assay-unit harmonization, clinician adjudication, a larger multi-institution temporal cohort, and prospective evaluation. The bootstrap intervals are conditional on the selected complete-triplet cohort and fitted forecasts and do not quantify universal transport uncertainty.
