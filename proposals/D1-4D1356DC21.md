# Perfusion–lactate discordance and the next crystalloid decision

Status: substantive repair of expert seed `[prior hypothesis]`. No outcome contrast, treatment effect, phenotype interaction, or learned-state result has been computed.

## Clinical opening, evidence, and hypothesis

Persistent lactate is clinically consequential but is not a unique marker of fluid-responsive hypoperfusion. Lactate can remain high because of impaired clearance (especially hepatic dysfunction), adrenergic/metabolic production, or other causes [K2]. In early septic shock, targeting peripheral perfusion rather than lactate did not significantly reduce 28-day mortality, although the peripheral-perfusion arm had less 72-hour organ dysfunction [K1]. After at least 1 L of resuscitation, CLASSIC found that a restrictive strategy reduced fluid volume but did not improve 90-day mortality [K3]. These works support heterogeneity and uncertainty; none establishes the proposed phenotype or a benefit from another bolus.

The unresolved, falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among adults with repeated hyperlactatemia, the 24-hour risk difference for an additional isotonic-crystalloid bolus versus no bolus is more favorable when strictly pre-decision MAP, vasopressor dose, and urine-output trajectories are worsening than when they are improving.

“Favorable” means lower risk of death or persistent vasopressor dependence, not measured cardiac-output responsiveness. The primary interaction is (Delta=RD_{worsening}-RD_{improving}); negative values favor relatively greater benefit or less harm in the worsening phenotype. A material interaction is 5 absolute risk points, frozen before outcomes.

The advance over the inspected work is a result-availability landmark, an explicit additional-bolus target trial, a pre-treatment multi-domain trajectory, liver-clearance and observation-process controls, and a scientifically matched longitudinal alternative. The question could inform whether persistent lactate should trigger uniform additional fluid or a prospective phenotype-stratified trial. This study alone cannot justify bedside treatment.

Use the first qualifying ICU stay per `subject_id` and the first qualifying pair in that stay.

3. The second lactate must be within ICU hours 6–24. Time zero (t0) is its `storetime`, the first computable result-availability proxy. Require non-null `storetime`, `charttime <= storetime <= charttime+2h`, and the prior lactate’s `storetime < t0`.
4. Require (t0+2h <= icustays.outtime) or death during the grace period. Do not exclude early death after (t0).
5. For the transparent phenotype, require weight 30–250 kg, >=2 valid MAPs in each pre-(t0) 3-hour half, and >=1 urine event in each half. This is an observability restriction, not evidence of adequate output.
6. Exclude an eligible NS/LR bolus already active across (t0). Ongoing maintenance/carrier fluid is measured as baseline treatment burden but is not itself the strategy.

No discharge diagnosis or post-(t0) note is a baseline predictor. Report every exclusion, missingness pattern, qualifying-lactate interval, and measurement/store lag.

### Outcome and estimand

Primary outcome (Y24): death in ((t0,t0+24h]) or any eligible vasopressor infusion overlapping ([t0+23h,t0+25h]). Patients alive and discharged from ICU before hour 23 without a later ICU pressor record are negative. Report the two components separately.

Primary estimands are standardized 24-hour risks and risk differences, bolus minus no bolus, in improving and worsening phenotypes and their interaction (Delta). Estimate both:

- a target-trial contrast under consistency, positivity, no interference, correct timing/models, and conditional exchangeability; and
- the explicitly safer description “adjusted historical outcome contrast.”

Primary conclusions use the latter wording unless the indication audit and all confounding gates pass. Even then, no individual causal response is claimed.

Secondary outcomes: hospital/28-day death; new invasive ventilation or RRT by 48 hours among those unsupported at (t0); total fluid and pressor-free hours by 24 hours. Six-hour lactate change is exploratory only among remeasured patients, with inverse probability of remeasurement; it is not “clearance” and is not the primary response outcome.

## Pre-decision phenotype and rivals

Split ([t0-6h,t0)) into early and late 3-hour halves. All event and store times, where present, must be <(t0).

- MAP improves if late minus early median >=5 mmHg; worsens if <=−5.
- Norepinephrine-equivalent dose improves if it falls >=0.05 mcg/kg/min or all pressors stop; worsens if it rises >=0.05 or a pressor starts. Zero in both halves is stable.
- Urine improves if late rate is >=0.5 mL/kg/h and rises >=0.2 mL/kg/h; worsens if late rate is <0.5 and falls >=0.2.

Improving requires >=2 improving domains and no worsening domain. Worsening requires >=2 worsening and no improving domain. Other patterns are “indeterminate” and reported but excluded from the confirmatory interaction. Threshold sensitivities (MAP 3/7, pressor 0.03/0.07, urine change 0.1/0.3) cannot replace the frozen primary.

Leading explanation: persistent lactate with worsening recorded perfusion may retain a different benefit–harm profile for additional fluid, whereas improving perfusion may indicate delayed lactate normalization or non-flow causes.

Competing explanations make different predictions:

1. **Severity/indication:** worse patients receive fluid and fare worse. If weighting fails balance, a future-treatment negative control is strong, or the interaction attenuates after pre-initiation updates, classify treatment-process-consistent.
2. **Observation process:** repeat tests and dense charting encode concern. If masks/counts/store lag reproduce the interaction or the result disappears in a fixed-observation subset, classify observation-process-consistent.
3. **Pressor-created MAP improvement:** if “improvement” occurs only with rising pressor dose and falling urine, the scalar phenotype should be unstable while the longitudinal model identifies discordance.
4. **Impaired clearance:** bilirubin/INR/AST/ALT and liver-dysfunction strata should alter lactate persistence but need not alter the perfusion–treatment interaction. Strong attenuation after liver adjustment bounds the perfusion interpretation.
5. **Unavailable intent/physiology:** bleeding, congestion, echo/passive-leg-raise response, capillary refill, source control, cardiac function, and goals of care can make the observational explanations indistinguishable. A blinded clinical indication audit is essential for stronger causal language.

## Method A: transparent target-trial baseline

Fit 30-minute pooled-logistic strategy-initiation/artificial-censoring models using restricted cubic splines and the common pre-bin history. Estimate a baseline strategy propensity for diagnostics. Truncate stabilized weights at development 1st/99th percentiles; prespecify untruncated and 2.5/97.5 sensitivities.

Fit a weighted doubly robust logistic outcome model with strategy, continuous domain changes, frozen phenotype, strategy×phenotype, liver dysfunction, and baseline covariates. Standardize risks in each test phenotype. Use 2,000 subject bootstraps that repeat nuisance fitting and truncation within development/validation roles; report percentile and influence-function intervals.

Before outcomes, require within each phenotype and development propensity decile >=20 observed initiators and non-initiators and estimated strategy probability 0.05–0.95. Validation and test each require >=200 per phenotype-strategy cell. Require post-weight absolute SMD <0.10 for all prespecified covariates (<0.05 for baseline lactate, MAP, pressor dose, urine, prior fluid, liver measures, and observation counts), effective sample size >=100/cell, and no truncated weight >30. Failure is `inconclusive_positivity_or_balance`, not evidence for either treatment.

## Method B: outcome-blind longitudinal alternative

Use the identical population, (t0), strategies, outcome, estimand, split, and weights. Build twelve 30-minute bins over ([t0-6h,t0)) containing MAP median/minimum, NE-equivalent mean/maximum, urine mL/kg/h, HR, prior crystalloid, lactate, pH/base excess, creatinine, bilirubin/AST/ALT, ventilation/RRT, plus mask/count/time-since/store-lag channels.

Fit a masked switching state-space model on development only. Its training target is observed-data likelihood and next-bin MAP/pressor/urine prediction; it never sees outcomes, post-(t0) treatment, or test data. Compare K=2/3/4 states and latent dimensions 4/8; select by validation next-bin NLL, requiring each state in >=10% of validation landmarks and non-degenerate posterior entropy. Freeze the model, infer test state posteriors/transitions, and interact posterior trajectory with strategy in the same weighted estimator.

Run ten random initializations; align states by Hungarian matching and require >=8/10 with adjusted Rand index >=0.70. Do not give states physiological names without clinician review. Evaluate whether the state-based outcome model improves validation/test weighted log loss >=0.01 over Method A and whether its strategy interaction is stable across initializations and exceeds 95/100 outcome-blind within-patient time-permutation null pipelines.

This alternative preserves asynchrony, transient reversals, MAP–pressor–urine discordance, uncertainty, and observation timing that the three-threshold baseline discards. It tests a substantive uncertainty—not a generic predictive gain. It cannot rescue failed overlap, balance, timing, or negative controls.

Negative controls:

1. Masks/count/store-lag-only state model. Comparable interaction => `observation_process_consistent`.
2. Within-patient pre-(t0) value-time permutation preserving masks/counts/last value. Observed state gain and interaction must exceed 95/100 nulls.
3. Predict eligible crystalloid initiation at (t0+24)–26 h among survivors. Comparable “effect modification” => treatment-preference/severity concern.
4. Repeat the exposure interaction with D5W itemid 220949. Similar magnitude weakens isotonic-crystalloid specificity.

A supportive Method A result with null Method B retains the transparent finding. A stable Method B result with null Method A supports learned EHR-state heterogeneity only; it does not prove physiology. Precise nulls from both abandon this direction. Imprecise disagreement is inconclusive.

## Result states and falsification

- **Supportive:** all support/balance/timing gates pass; upper 95% CI of (Delta<-0.05); upper 95% CI of (RD_{worsening}<0); lower 95% CI of (RD_{improving}>-0.02); weight/threshold sensitivities agree; observation/treatment negative controls do not reproduce the result.
- **Adverse:** gates pass and lower 95% CI of (Delta>0), or (Delta)’s entire CI lies within [−0.05,+0.05] while both phenotype CIs exclude a 5-point benefit. This falsifies the prespecified clinically material direction; it does not prove equivalence beyond those bounds.
- **Inconclusive:** failed positivity/balance/timing, cell/ESS thresholds, unstable weights, material missingness, interaction CI spanning material benefit and no difference, conflicting sensitivities/models, or a positive process control. An imprecise null is not adverse evidence.

Supportive results justify prospective external validation and an indication-adjudicated study, not a fluid recommendation. Adverse results argue against this phenotype/threshold as a trial stratifier. Observation-process results redirect work toward measurement validation. Positivity failure means MIMIC cannot answer this contrast as designed.
