# D3-M1 repair: Composition versus within-cell regulation in the active-RA vs remission synovial macrophage contrast

Seed: D3-M1 (literature-informed; parent [prior hypothesis])
Domain: Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses
Status: hypothesis-stage proposal with executed bounded probe ([research job])

## 1. Scientific opening

Whole-tissue and macrophage-enriched bulk transcriptomic studies of RA flares and remission
interpret differential-expression signatures as per-cell transcriptional programs. But a bulk
pseudobulk mean is a mixture: observed per-gene change = (change in per-cell expression) +
(change in subpopulation proportions). Single-cell atlases of RA synovium show that synovial
tissue macrophages (STM) are not one population: Alivernini et al. [K1] resolved 9 phenotypic
clusters in 4 STM subpopulations from 32,000 STMs, with remission characterized by distinct
MerTKposTREM2high and MerTKposLYVE1pos clusters enriched in negative regulators of
inflammation, and a low MerTKpos proportion in remission associated with flare risk after
treatment cessation. Kuo et al. [K2] independently defined an HBEGF+ inflammatory macrophage
cluster enriched in RA and a MERTK+ cluster shaped by the microenvironment. Both atlases
therefore predict that the active-vs-remission macrophage contrast could arise substantially
from shifting proportions of a small number of subpopulations rather than (or in addition to)
a coherent within-cell state program.

The unresolved question: in the E-MTAB-8322 macrophage pseudobulk (27 donors, 33,694
features), does the active-RA vs remission contrast look like a small coherent within-cell
state program (H-state: few genes, large per-cell effect, directionally consistent across
donors) or like subpopulation composition shifts (H-comp: broad shallow, donor-heterogeneous
changes)? This matters clinically because it changes how we read bulk RA signatures: if
composition dominates, "upregulated in active disease" genes may mark an expanding
subpopulation (a population-level target) rather than a cell-intrinsic transcriptional
response (a cell-intrinsic target), and signatures will not transfer across cohorts with
different baseline subpopulation proportions.

Closest inspected work: [K1] (composition framework + remission signatures, but measured in
its own scRNA-seq cohort, not in E-MTAB-8322 pseudobulk), [K2] (HBEGF+ state defined in 10
RA donors; does not test composition vs state in a remission contrast), [K3] (demonstrates in
a different tissue that bulk DE can be dominated by composition confound: adjusting for
cell-type composition collapsed genome-wide-significant genes from 1,501 to 4; not RA, not
macrophage subpopulations). No inspected work has quantified, in this dataset, whether the
active-vs-remission macrophage contrast is state-like or composition-like.

## 2. Established evidence vs the untested claim

Established (from inspected sources + verified local data):
- E-MTAB-8322 contains 27 macrophage pseudobulk samples, 33,694 ENSEMBL features;
  conditions: Healthy control n=4, Naive RA n=6, Remission RA n=7, Resistant RA n=6,
  Undifferentiated arthritis n=4; SA149/SA149P flagged possible_related_or_repeated_donor
  and excluded from inference (verified in design.json, sha256 4ef38a70...).
- All 10 candidate subpopulation markers (MERTK, MRC1, TREM2, LYVE1, CSF1R, CD163, CD68,
  HBEGF, C1QA, ITGAM) are present in the feature set and expressed in 100% of donors
  (probe, executed).
- [K1] MerTKpos STM proportion is a flare-risk-associated variable in remission.

Untested claim (this hypothesis): the active-RA vs remission contrast in this dataset is
dominated by one of two generative mechanisms — a coherent within-cell state program
(H-state) or subpopulation composition shifts (H-comp) — and the dominance can be bounded
with donor-level effect-size spectrum, donor coherence, and marker-based composition
estimates.

## 3. Hypotheses and falsification criteria

- H-state: the contrast is carried by a small set of genes with large, directionally
  consistent per-donor effects (a within-cell state program, e.g. an HBEGF+/inflammatory
  state [K2] or a remission-resolution state [K1]).
- H-comp: the contrast is carried by broad, shallow, donor-heterogeneous changes consistent
  with changing proportions of MerTKpos vs MerTKneg (or TREM2high vs LYVE1pos) macrophage
  subpopulations [K1][K2].
- H-mixed (explicit alternative): neither mechanism alone; a moderate state component plus
  a composition component.

Pre-registered decision bands (computed on the active(n=12) vs remission(n=7) contrast,
donor as unit; probe already executed):
- H-state supported if: top-50 |log2FC| genes carry >=15% of total |log2FC| mass AND mean
  donor-direction coherence at K=50 >= 80% (>=80% of donors match the consensus sign on
  >=80% of the top-50 genes).
- H-comp supported if: coherence at K=50 < 50% AND coherence at K=1000 >= 80% (coherence
  emerges only at large K, i.e. the contrast is broad and shallow) AND a 2-component
  marker-based composition score (MERTK vs LYVE1/MRC1) differs significantly between
  active and remission (donor-level t-test, p<0.05) and explains >=30% of the variance of
  the genome-wide contrast (R^2 of per-gene log2FC regressed on the composition score).
- Inconclusive (H-mixed or indeterminate): anything between the bands.

## 4. Executed bounded probe ([research job], CPU, succeeded)

Normalization: log2(1+CPM), CPM scaled to median library size per donor; contrast =
group-mean difference, active (Naive+Resistant, n=12) minus remission (n=7); Welch t-test
per gene with donor as unit; SA149/SA149P excluded.

Headline results (full numbers in result.json, sha256 f80556f6...):
- Effect-size spectrum: |log2FC|>1: 859 genes (2.5%); >0.5: 4,418 (13.1%); >0.25: 10,856
  (32.2%); median |log2FC| = 0.128; mean = 0.223.
- Concentration: top-1% of genes carry 7.0% of total |log2FC| mass; top-50 carry 1.45%;
  top-1000 carry 16.5%; Gini = 0.608; top-50 mean |log2FC| = 2.18 vs genome median 0.128.
- Donor coherence (fraction of top-K genes whose per-donor direction matches consensus):
  K=50: 56.6% overall (active donors 60.7%, remission donors 48.0%; min 30%);
  K=200: 39.9%; K=1000: 32.1%. Coherence DECREASES with K — the opposite of the
  composition prediction (coherence emerging only at large K).
- Subpopulation markers: all 10 present, expressed in 100% of donors. Active-vs-remission
  log2FC: LYVE1 -1.089 (down in active), CD163 +0.636, CD11b +0.431, MERTK -0.266, MRC1
  +0.196, TREM2 -0.067, CSF1R +0.119, CD68 +0.285, HBEGF -0.042, C1QA -0.060. Donor CVs
  0.08-0.24 (per-donor score variance adequate for a 2-component score). Marker
  orthogonality (donor-level correlations): MERTK-MRC1 r=0.553, MERTK-LYVE1 r=0.586,
  MRC1-LYVE1 r=0.743 — moderately correlated, so a 2-component deconvolution is feasible
  but not cleanly orthogonal; LYVE1 is the most contrastive marker.
- Confound check: active donors all female, ages 53-77 (mean 61.4); remission all female,
  ages 42-72 (mean 56.4) — age ranges overlap (no sex confound within the contrast; mild
  age difference, mean 5.0 years, to be regressed out in the full analysis). HC donors are
  3M/1F, so HC comparisons are sex-confounded and are not part of this contrast.

Probe interpretation (honest, pre-decision-band): the result is NOT cleanly H-state
(coherence at K=50 is 56.6%, far below the 80% bar; top-50 mass 1.45% << 15%) and NOT
cleanly H-comp by the coherence criterion (coherence does not rise to 80% at K=1000; it
falls). The spectrum is intermediate: a real heavy tail (859 genes >1 log2FC, Gini 0.608)
on top of a broad shallow base (32% of genes >0.25). This is the pre-registered
inconclusive/mixed band. The marker data are consistent with a composition component
(LYVE1 down ~1 log2 in active; CD163/CD11b up), but the composition score must be built
and tested (Step 3 below) before H-comp can be supported or rejected.

## 5. Analysis plan (baseline vs alternative)

Step 1 (baseline, effect-spectrum + coherence, EXECUTED as probe): per-donor log2CPM
pseudobulk, active-vs-remission per-gene log2FC + Welch t (donor as unit), effect-size
spectrum, concentration (top-1%/50/200/1000 mass fractions, Gini), donor-direction
coherence at K=50/200/1000. This is the transparent, non-parametric baseline; it bounds
state-likeness without any model of subpopulations.

Step 2 (alternative A, per-gene AUC + composition score): build a 2-component
composition score per donor from the marker panel (e.g. score = mean log2 expression of
{LYVE1, MRC1, TREM2, MERTK} minus mean of {CD163, CD11b, CSF1R}, or a non-negative least
squares 2-component deconvolution of the full matrix against the two marker-defined
profiles). Then (a) test the score difference active vs remission (Welch t, donor as unit,
age as covariate); (b) regress per-gene log2FC on the score and report R^2 — the fraction
of the genome-wide contrast explained by composition; (c) per-gene AUC of the score for
classifying active vs remission, compared against the top-50-gene mean as a state-based
classifier. Information gained over the baseline: the baseline says the contrast is
neither pure state nor pure composition; Step 2 quantifies HOW MUCH is composition
(R^2) and whether a small marker-derived score separates donors better than the top-50
state genes (AUC comparison). If R^2 is small and AUC(score) <= AUC(top50), the
composition explanation is bounded out and the contrast is a mixed/broad state program;
if R^2 >= 0.3 and AUC(score) > AUC(top50), H-comp gains support.

Step 3 (alternative B, subclustering feasibility, deferred): the domain guide notes
original single-cell cells remain available for justified subclustering. A full
subclustering-based deconvolution (fitting Alivernini [K1] cluster proportions per donor)
would be the strongest composition test but requires the raw per-cell matrices and a
subclustering budget; it is deferred to a future solver episode and recorded here as the
next test if Step 2 is inconclusive.

Step 4 (validation context, secondary): E-MTAB-8873 (445-gene targeted panel, FLS/coculture)
can be checked for presence of the top-50 state genes and the 10 markers as a secondary
consistency check; it cannot resolve composition (targeted panel, no macrophage
subpopulation resolution) and is not part of the primary decision.

Evaluation and uncertainty: all tests use donor as the unit (n=12 vs 7); report effect
sizes with 95% CIs (bootstrap over donors, 1000 resamples) rather than relying on p-values
alone; multiple-testing context: 33,694 features, but the decision bands use aggregate
statistics (mass fractions, coherence, R^2), not per-gene FDR, so the primary claims are
robust to multiple testing by construction; per-gene claims (top-50 list) are exploratory.

## 7. What each outcome would and would not establish

- Supportive of H-state (top-50 mass >=15% AND K=50 coherence >=80%): the contrast is
  dominated by a small coherent program; would justify cell-intrinsic target prioritization
  from the top-50 list. Would NOT establish mechanism (which pathway drives the state) or
  causality (cross-sectional).
- Supportive of H-comp (K=50 coherence <50%, K=1000 >=80%, composition score p<0.05 and
  R^2>=0.3): the contrast is dominated by subpopulation proportion shifts; would justify
  population-level (subpopulation-targeting) interpretation of bulk RA signatures. Would
  NOT identify which subpopulations without subclustering (Step 3) or flow validation.
- Inconclusive/mixed (observed probe band): the contrast is a mixture; would argue for
  reporting both a state component and a composition component in bulk RA signature
  interpretation, and for subclustering (Step 3) as the decisive next test.
- Adverse to the whole framing (e.g. composition score fails AND coherence flat across K
  AND no heavy tail): the active-vs-remission contrast in this dataset is a diffuse,
  donor-heterogeneous shift without a parsimonious generative account; would argue the
  dataset is underpowered for mechanism-level claims and the question should move to
  larger scRNA-seq cohorts.

## 8. Limitations

- Cross-sectional disease/treatment groups; no paired flare-remission transitions; groups
  do not establish transitions (domain-guide limit).
- Macrophage-only pseudobulk; not whole tissue; subpopulation proportions are not
  directly measured — marker scores are proxies, not deconvolution ground truth.
- Small n (12 vs 7 donors); donor-level inference has limited power; bootstrap CIs are
  wide.
- Age differs mildly between groups (mean 61.4 vs 56.4); sex is balanced within the
  contrast (all female) but not vs HC; treatment history differs by design (naive vs
  resistant vs remission) and cannot be separated from disease state.
- SA149/SA149P excluded (possible related/repeated donor) — reduces UA group to 2 usable
  donors; UA not used in the primary contrast.
- No wet-lab validation; no independent cohort validation of the composition score.
- Pseudobulk normalization (median-library CPM) assumes comparable capture; per-donor
  cell counts vary (726-10,775 cells), which itself may carry composition information.
