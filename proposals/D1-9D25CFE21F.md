# Does hospital variation in a recorded creatinine rise extend to newly documented renal-support escalation?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly construct an auditable eICU six-hour landmark cohort and 30-minute event ledger, then estimate whether hospitals differ in a *newly documented renal-support pathway* after a first qualifying recorded creatinine rise. The primary deliverable is the standardized development-hospital 90th-minus-10th percentile spread in the 48-hour cumulative incidence of a renal-escalation composite:

1. a distinct later qualifying creatinine confirmation; or
2. a first eligible, high-specificity renal-support record after the rise.

The primary risk set excludes any stay with a strict renal-support record at or before the first rise. This exclusion is the substantive repair over the parent: a continuation of dialysis or another pre-existing support episode cannot be labelled a new post-rise escalation. All-stay and prior-support-stratified analyses remain mandatory secondary outputs so the cost and consequences of that exclusion are visible.

The solver must report the composite, confirmation-only, renal-support-only, repeat-test opportunity, confirmation conditional on testing, support after confirmation, unit death, alive unit exit and unknown-status censoring as separate quantities. Completion requires the frozen cohort, event ledger, support provenance flags, supported-hospital estimates, hospital-cluster uncertainty, decomposition by monitoring/support/exit pathway, held-out-hospital evaluation, and prespecified sensitivity results. No fitted result is claimed here.

The advance over the parent is not another confirmation-spread estimate. It asks whether a hospital-dependent recorded creatinine signal reaches a downstream renal-care documentation state after removing detectable pre-existing support. If only repeat testing or confirmation varies, the signal is primarily surveillance-dependent. If a support-only spread remains after support-aware case-mix and monitoring adjustment, the finding is more consequential for renal-care ascertainment, although it is still not proof of dialysis initiation, biological AKI, treatment response, or hospital quality.

## Evidence-supported claim and unresolved hypothesis

The inspected local evidence establishes data availability and field semantics, not the hypothesis. The eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative laboratory and vital-sign records, unit disposition, hospital identifiers, treatment rows, and labelled intake/output rows. The exact schemas expose creatinine values and measurement-system/interface fields, ICU-relative offsets, support-related treatment strings, support-related intake/output labels, and unit discharge status. The parent’s bounded read-only scans found 40,060 renal/dialysis-related treatment rows across 8,284 stays and 58,473 dialysis/CRRT/hemofiltration-labelled intake/output rows across 6,159 stays. These are feasibility counts, not index-cohort event rates or associations.

The strongest evidence available does not establish that:

- a treatment or flowsheet row means treatment was initiated or received;
- a support row represents renal replacement rather than continuation, fluid removal, or a documentation artifact;
- a recorded creatinine rise is biological AKI;
- a patient was not on chronic dialysis before this ICU stay;
- urine labels have a validated collection interval or denominator; or
- hospital differences represent quality or causal treatment effects.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among ICU stays with a first recorded creatinine rise and no strict renal-support record at or before that rise, hospitals differ materially in the 48-hour probability of a downstream renal-escalation record; after accounting for repeat-testing opportunity and competing ICU exit, part of that difference remains specifically in newly documented renal-support records.

Primary hypothesis: among supported development hospitals, the standardized 48-hour renal-escalation spread H90-10^RE is at least 5 percentage points, its two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval excludes zero, and the support-only component has a nonzero residual hospital spread after the monitoring pathway and competing exits are represented. The 5-point threshold is a prespecified scale for concern about endpoint portability and renal-care ascertainment, not a treatment threshold.

Supportive evidence requires both a composite signal and evidence that the renal-support component is not entirely explained by repeat testing, confirmation, or exit. A large confirmation spread with no support spread is adverse to the clinically consequential extension, though it remains evidence of documentation heterogeneity. A large support spread without confirmation support is interpreted as renal-support recording variation and cannot validate the creatinine signal.

## Population and ICU observation boundary

Use snapshot `[source checksum]`. The observational unit is one `patientunitstayid`; repeated unit stays remain separate. `uniquepid` is used only for clustering and connected-component split checks, never to concatenate ICU stays.

Include stays satisfying all of the following:

- `age >= 18`, parsing the eICU value `> 89` as 90 and retaining an age-censored flag;
- at least two valid raw `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` observations in minutes 0–360, restricted to 20–200 mmHg;
- a valid, unit-verified baseline creatinine at or before the first raw low-MAP observation;
- a first raw periodic `systemicmean < 65` in [0, 360];
- no qualifying baseline-plus-0.3 mg/dL creatinine rise through minute 360;
- `unitdischargeoffset > 360`; and
- no strict renal-support record at an eligible offset ≤ the first rise, for the primary cohort.

Let U = `unitdischargeoffset` and E = min(2880, U). A source observation at ICU-relative time t is eligible only when t < U. If U > 2880, a time-2880 event is eligible; if U ≤ 2880, discharge takes precedence over an event at the same minute. At U, `unitdischargestatus=Expired` is recorded unit death, `Alive` is an alive unit exit, and NULL is unknown-status administrative censoring. NULL is never recoded as alive. `unitdischargelocation` is destination context, with a sensitivity using location Death when status is missing. `hospitaldischargeoffset` is never substituted for the ICU boundary. Transfer out of the index unit ends observation even if a later unit stay exists.

The solver must publish a flow table: all patient rows, adult rows, MAP-supported rows, baseline-creatinine-supported rows, landmark rows, no-early-rise rows, rise rows, prior-support exclusions, final primary risk set, and hospital/split/support exclusions.

## Index signal and covariates

### MAP landmark and observation process

Use raw `vitalPeriodic` observations for the primary MAP ledger, deduplicating exact `vitalperiodicid`. If multiple valid rows remain at the same stay/time, use their median and report the count. Restrict early MAP to 0–360 minutes and 20–200 mmHg. Record the first raw low-MAP offset, minimum, low-MAP run count and duration, recovery offset and slope, valid-observation count, coverage, raw minutes below 65, and a trapezoidal burden of max(65-MAP,0) with only gaps ≤30 minutes bridged. Raw no-interpolation burden and minutes are required sensitivities. Use `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` as a separate channel sensitivity, never pooled with periodic MAP.

For documented pressor timing, use `infusionDrug.infusionoffset` and case-folded `drugname` matching norepinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine or epinephrine. Categorize the first record as pre-low/concurrent (offset ≤ first low), prompt-after-low (> first low and ≤30 minutes), delayed-after-low (>30 through 360), or no target-pressor record. `drugrate`, `infusionrate`, `drugamount`, `volumeoffluid` and `patientweight` are audit fields only. These are documented records, not validated administration, dose, indication, intent, or complete exposure; pressor timing is a covariate/sensitivity, never a causal treatment variable.

### Creatinine

Use case-folded `lab.labname=creatinine`, numeric `labresult` in [0,60], and explicit primary-unit verification requiring both nonblank `labmeasurenamesystem` and `labmeasurenameinterface` to normalize to mg/dL. Exclude conflicting, non-mg/dL, urinary-creatinine, nonnumeric, and out-of-range rows. A sensitivity accepts one explicit mg/dL field when the other is blank; do not convert unverified mmol/L. Deduplicate exact `labid`; if multiple valid rows remain at one offset, use their median and retain a count.

The baseline is the earliest qualifying result in [0,60] by `labresultoffset`, not the minimum. The first qualifying rise is the first post-landmark result at least 0.3 mg/dL above baseline. The early-injury exclusion uses all valid qualifying results through minute 360, including the baseline, and never uses post-landmark observations to construct baseline or eligibility. A distinct confirmation requires a different `labid`, at least 360 minutes after the first-rise result, value at least baseline+0.3 mg/dL, time ≤2880 and t<U. Same-minute rows, exact duplicates, and revised versions of the same `labid` cannot confirm. The primary clock is `labresultoffset`; rerun with `labresultrevisedoffset` where present.

## Newly documented renal-support endpoint

Define a strict support record as the earliest eligible post-rise row from either of these sources:

- `treatment.treatmentstring` matches a case-insensitive, modality-specific dialysis term such as renal dialysis, hemodialysis, CVVHD, CVVH, CAVHD, peritoneal dialysis, or SLED; or
- `intakeOutput.cellpath` or `celllabel` matches an explicit high-specificity label such as Dialysis (ml)|In, Dialysis (ml)|Out, CRRT Actual Pt Fluid Removed, CRRT Out, CRRT - UF removed, CRRT- UF removed, or Hemofiltration, with valid `cellvaluenumeric` or nonzero `dialysistotal`.

Exclude catheter insertion, access surgery, line flushes, generic renal terms, and `ultrafiltration (fluid removal only)` from strict support. Include the latter only in a broad sensitivity. Deduplicate exact `treatmentid` and `intakeoutputid`; retain source, raw label/string, offset, numeric/text value, and active-upon-discharge flag as provenance.

The primary cohort excludes stays with any strict support record from ICU time 0 through the first-rise offset, including an exact-rise record. The all-stay secondary retains them and reports a prior-support indicator, time from first support to rise, and whether the first post-rise row is plausibly continuation by its source and timing. A second secondary stratifies by no prior support versus prior support without asserting chronic dialysis status. Because absence of a record does not prove absence of support, the primary estimand is “newly documented after an observed support-free interval,” not “incident renal replacement.”

Use four prespecified source definitions:

1. treatment-strict only;
2. intake/output-strict only;
3. union-strict (primary);
4. union-broad including fluid-removal-only terms.

The composite time is the earlier of distinct confirmation and strict support after the first rise. If both occur at the same minute, retain both component indicators and count one composite event. Support occurring before the first rise is never a post-rise event in the primary cohort. The solver must not use treatment or intake/output rows after unit discharge.

Do not use urine output as AKI or oliguria. Report urine-labelled observation presence, count, label family and offset coverage around the rise as an observation-process audit only. The schema has offsets and values but no uniformly valid collection interval or reliable time-varying weight denominator.

## Estimands and decomposition

For supported development hospital h, let R_h be primary-cohort stays with a first qualifying rise before E. Estimate q_h^RE, the 48-hour CIF of the renal-escalation composite, with recorded unit death and alive exit as competing events and unknown-status discharge as censoring. Standardize every q_h to the same development risk-set distribution of age, sex, ethnicity, hospital/unit/admission source and unit type, baseline creatinine, first-rise value and delta, time since prior creatinine and pre-rise test count, first-low MAP and burden/coverage/runs/recovery, pressor timing, admission `acutephysiologyscore`, `apachescore`, `predictedicumortality`, and hospital bed category/teaching status/region. Hospital identity is not a standardization covariate.

Report H90-10^RE across development hospitals with at least 50 primary risk-set stays and at least 10 composite events. If fewer than 10 hospitals meet support, the new hospital-spread conclusion is inconclusive. Do not replace this with an unregularized hospital proportion.

The decomposition reports:

- repeat-creatinine opportunity CIF after the rise;
- confirmation CIF and confirmation conditional on a repeat-test transition;
- strict support-only CIF after the rise;
- support after confirmation, with a separately defined support-before-confirmation path;
- composite CIF;
- recorded unit death, alive exit, and unknown-status censoring; and
- the same quantities under all-stay and prior-support-stratified secondary cohorts.

A support-only H90-10 is required when at least 10 hospitals have at least 10 support events; otherwise it is reported as sparse and the composite cannot be described as support-validated. Avoid unstable ratios: report transition probabilities and CIFs directly.

The parent estimand is retained as a secondary, not silently replaced: standardized 48-hour confirmation CIF spread, confirmation versus any-rise contrast by documented pressor timing, and raw-versus-bridged MAP sensitivities.

## Matched methods

### Transparent baseline

Fit a regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic model on identical 30-minute intervals, cohort, split, landmark covariates, event clocks and support definitions. Use transition-specific hospital random intercepts, prespecified linear/spline elapsed-time terms, common covariate slopes, and a hospital-by-transition time slope only where support permits. Model separate hazards for repeat test, confirmation, strict support, unit death, alive exit and unknown-status censoring.

Nested fits are:

- B0: case-mix and elapsed-time terms, no hospital heterogeneity;
- B1: B0 plus transition-specific hospital random intercepts (transparent primary);
- B2: B1 plus hospital context;
- B3: B1 plus repeat-testing and urine-observability terms;
- B4: B1 with no hospital-by-transition time slope.

Derive standardized hospital CIFs, the composite and support-only spreads, component curves, observed-versus-predicted curves, and retained parent estimands. Post-rise lab values and support rows are outcomes/process history, not baseline predictors.

### Substantive support-gated alternative

Fit an ordered piecewise-exponential multi-state model on exactly the same primary risk set, covariates, split, standardization distribution, endpoint clocks, and bootstrap procedure. States/transitions are:

first rise -> repeat-test opportunity -> confirmation;
first rise -> support before confirmation;
confirmation -> newly documented support;
and from each at-risk state -> unit death, alive unit exit, or unknown-status censoring.

The six-hour confirmation delay is structural. Prior-support exclusion is structural in the primary cohort, while the all-stay sensitivity includes a prior-support state/indicator. Transition-specific hospital random effects use shrinkage; fixed hospital context is included only where estimable.

This alternative answers information the additive baseline loses: whether hospital heterogeneity is in the chance of being retested, the value of the repeat result, support documentation independent of confirmation, support after confirmation, or competing exit. It also tests whether the composite is an artefact of combining a surveillance endpoint with a support endpoint. Agreement in the composite and support-only spreads across the two models is stronger evidence for a robust documented-process difference. A spread confined to repeat testing is surveillance variation; a spread that survives the support gate and appears in support after confirmation is more consequential but still documentation-bounded.

A learned sequence model is explicitly deferred, not prohibited. Revisit it only if both prespecified models show reproducible held-out, shape-specific miscalibration in the same component estimands and a learned model can preserve the support gate, competing exits, hospital-held-out evaluation, and uncertainty ledger. A causal pressor model is deferred because infusion rows lack validated administration, dose, indication, intent, and complete exposure. A biological-AKI/KDIGO model is deferred because validated urine intervals, renal reserve, adjudication, and complete treatment context are unavailable.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires:

- H90-10^RE >= 5 percentage points;
- a two-sided hospital-cluster interval excluding zero;
- the same qualitative hospital ordering in the additive and support-gated multi-state fits;
- a support-only spread meeting its support rule and remaining after monitoring/exit decomposition; and
- concordance across treatment-strict, intake/output-strict, union-strict, lab-clock, confirmation-gap, unit-boundary, MAP-channel, status, prior-support, and split sensitivities.

Adverse evidence includes a null or reversed composite, an interval compatible with no material spread, disappearance after repeat-test or exit accounting, support-only sparsity, disagreement between treatment and flowsheet sources, disappearance when prior-support stays are excluded, or a spread confined to broad fluid-removal labels. A large confirmation spread with null support spread is adverse to the downstream clinical extension. A large support spread with null confirmation spread is a recording-heterogeneity finding, not validation of the creatinine signal.

The short-gap repeat test within 0–360 minutes after the first rise is an observation-process falsification/control: it should expose testing-practice variation, but it is not a clinical endpoint. Failure to reproduce that expected process contrast indicates inadequate process measurement or instability. Negative findings are valuable and must not be converted into evidence of no biological AKI or no benefit from renal support.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 10 supported development hospitals, fewer than 10 support events per supported hospital, sparse primary risk sets, extreme weights, poor overlap, bootstrap instability, failed convergence, dependence on NULL-status handling, treatment/flowsheet disagreement, support-gate sensitivity, or failed held-out calibration/transport. Inconclusive means the proposed hospital-process claim was not resolved.

Computationally checkable claims include source and schema hashes, read-only headers, joins, deduplication, unit verification, offsets, strict ICU boundaries, prior-support exclusion, state transitions, deterministic split, model fits, standardization, event/support counts, intervals, calibration, and reproducibility. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a rise is biological AKI, whether a treatment/flowsheet record means actual renal replacement initiation, whether support was chronic or ongoing before the stay, whether urine output is oliguria, and whether a hospital process caused patient benefit or harm. Expert review would be required before changing repeat-testing, nephrology referral, dialysis policy, or hospital evaluation. No result establishes a causal pressor effect, MAP target, quality ranking, treatment effect, or biological renal null.
