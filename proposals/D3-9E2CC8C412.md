# Working proposal: MerTK-dependent macrophage -> FLS resolution signal vs composition effect

Domain: disease_mechanisms (Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses)
Seeds engaged: D3-M2 (primary; directional macrophage-fibroblast mechanism, perturbation),
D3-M1 (composition vs within-cell axis), D3-M3 (persistence vs remission clinical anchor).
Status: hypothesis-stage, data-bound, not yet executed. No result is claimed as observed.

## 1. Unresolved question

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), remission is associated with MerTK+CD206+ (mannose-receptor+)
synovial macrophages, while treatment-resistant disease is associated with inflammatory
macrophage states [K1]. This is a cross-sectional association. What remains unresolved is
whether the resolution-associated signal is an *active, MerTK-dependent macrophage effector
program acting on synovial fibroblasts (FLS)*, or merely a *composition/abundance* effect
(i.e. more resolution-state cells present) with no required signaling step. These explanations
make different predictions under an experimental perturbation, so they are separable.

## 2. Clinical importance

If resolution requires active MerTK signaling in macrophages, then the pathway is a
mechanistically testable pharmacologic handle for "difficult-to-treat" RA; if the effect is
purely compositional, MerTK-directed therapy is mis-specified. This changes what a next
experiment should target.

## 3. Strongest claim the current evidence supports vs the claim to test

- Supported (evidence): distinct synovial macrophage subsets, including MerTK/CD206/mannose-receptor
  family states, are associated with inflammation and remission in RA [K1]; RA synovium shows
  expansion of specific inflammatory cell states (e.g. IL1B+ monocytes, THY1+HLA-DRAhi fibroblasts)
  measured cross-sectionally [K3]; NOTCH3 signaling is a demonstrated endothelium->fibroblast
  stromal crosstalk axis that can be blocked [K2].
- Unresolved claim this experiment tests: the FLS response to MerTK+CD206+ macrophages is
  (a) specific to that macrophage state and (b) dependent on active MerTK signaling in the
  macrophage, rather than explained by macrophage presence/abundance or by unrelated stimulation.

## 4. Falsifiable hypothesis (H1)

In donor-matched RA FLS, co-culture with MerTK+CD206+ macrophages induces a resolution-like
FLS profile (lower inflammatory-panel signal, higher resolution-panel signal) that is
(i) differentiated from the MerTK-CD206- macrophage co-culture and (ii) attenuated when
macrophages are pre-treated with a MerTK inhibitor.

Predictions of H1:
- P1 (state identity): FLS(Mpos) != FLS(Mneg) on the pre-specified panels.
- P2 (perturbation): FLS(Mpos + MerTK inhibitor) shifts back toward FLS(Mneg)/uncoupled.
- P3 (specificity): an LPS pre-treatment control does not reproduce the Mpos resolution profile.
- Orthogonal (E-MTAB-8316): direct MerTK-inhibitor treatment of RA monocytes changes the same
  MerTK/CD206 resolution-program genes, consistent with an on-state effect.

### Competing explanations (rivals)
- R1 Composition/abundance: FLS response tracks the number/state mixture of macrophages present,
  not active MerTK signaling in the co-cultured macrophages.
- R2 Stimulation/stress artifact: any co-culture or LPS produces the observed FLS change.
- R3 Donor/batch/round structure: differences reflect donor or experimental round (R1/R2/R5),
  not the macrophage state.
- R4 Measurement scope: the 445-feature targeted panel does not contain the genes needed to
  resolve the claim (unmeasured genes are unavailable).

## 5. Population, variables, outcomes, baselines

Population: RA synovial FLS ("type B synovial cell"), E-MTAB-8873, 33 pseudobulk samples.
Primary contrast: condition {co-cultured with MerTK+CD206+ macrophages} vs {co-cultured with
MerTK-CD206- macrophages} and vs {not co-cultured}; within-donor/replicate where possible.
Perturbation variable: treatment {none, "macrophages pre-treated with MerTK inhibitor",
"macrophages pre-treated with lipopolysaccharide"}.
Outcome: per-sample targeted-panel scores (pre-specified inflammatory panel: IL1B, IL6, TNF, ...
and resolution/remodelling panel: markers on the measured feature list, e.g. MRC1, THY1, ...
to be fixed from the 445 measured features before testing) and feature-level differential signal.
Baseline: donor-blocked paired feature-level test.
Alternative (mechanistic/learned): a low-rank factor model (NMF/PLS) of the FLS response that
defines latent response programs and tests whether the Mpos effect is a coordinated resolution
program vs a diffuse shift, using the same split. Rationale: the baseline cannot distinguish a
single coordinated effector program from many small independent changes; the factor model can.

## 6. Analysis and falsification

- Donor-aware: average/explicitly block repeated contexts per donor; treat composite donor_id
  strings ("SA258R, SA267R, SA266A") as dependent; no 12-independent-donor claim; no ordinary
  donor t-test. Use permutation/sign-flip within donor and round.
- Pre-specify panels and thresholds before testing; report effect sizes with uncertainty.
- Falsification of H1: if FLS(Mpos) is not distinguishable from FLS(Mneg) on the panels under
  donor-blocked analysis (P1 fails), OR MerTK-inhibitor pre-treatment does not attenuate the Mpos
  effect (P2 fails) in a way inconsistent with R1/R2/R3.
- Supportive: P1+P2 hold and survive the factor-model alternative and the E-MTAB-8316 orthogonal check.
- Adverse: P1 holds but P2 fails (macrophage state matters but not via MerTK) -> reframes the
  handle from MerTK signaling to state composition.
- Inconclusive: panels underpowered, R3/R4 unresolved, or effects within donor/round noise.
