## Falsification, interpretation, and strongest supported claim

**Supportive result:** not observed. Support required calibrated global rejection, coherent adverse orientation, benchmark and sensitivity concordance, and no audit failure. The primary `p=0.79846`; the benchmark and primary haemoglobin signs disagree.

**Adverse result:** not established under the parent's definition. Although all three global procedures are non-rejecting, no clinically meaningful residual-moment equivalence margin was prespecified, all marginal intervals cross zero and remain broad relative to their estimates, and one sensitivity nuisance is severely miscalibrated. The data therefore cannot support a precise-near-zero claim or proof of absence.

**Defensible result:** `INCONCLUSIVE_WITH_CONCORDANT_NONREJECTION; SUPPORTIVE_HYPOTHESIS_NOT_SUPPORTED`. The strongest computational claim is that, for this frozen 75/923 selected recorded-malignancy contrast and these declared models, the three methods found no statistically detectable adjusted linear residual-moment signal, while the primary score was numerically stable and multiplier calibration behaved plausibly. This weakens, but does not falsify, the proposed reproducible adjusted signal. It does not establish conditional independence, a null biological effect, or absence of a signal under other valid nuisance functions.

The unresolved scientific claim remains whether adverse-oriented CBC trajectories distinguish CRC from other malignancies after comparator adjustment in an independently adjudicated population. Resolving it requires a new cohort with protocolized repeat CBC, pathology- or registry-adjudicated CRC and non-CRC outcomes, release-matched linkage completeness, prespecified meaningful effect/equivalence margins, and sufficient CRC events for calibrated nuisance estimation. Clinical review is required for diagnosis correctness and biological meaning.
