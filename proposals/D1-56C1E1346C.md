# Provisional hypothesis: reduced laboratory testing during persistent instability

## Status

Provisional, data-grounded adaptation of expert seed `[starting question]` (parent candidate `[prior hypothesis]`). This is a hypothesis and experiment design, not a completed analysis. No effect, event rate, or clinical conclusion has been estimated.

## Proposed relationship

Among adult eICU stays that remain physiologically unstable, an abrupt reduction in laboratory testing during a prespecified 6-hour window is associated with a higher probability of a prespecified deterioration event in the following 6 hours, compared with continued testing at a similar rate, after adjustment for immediately preceding physiology, laboratory state, time since admission, hospital, and available treatment-limitation indicators.

The estimand is an adjusted observational association, not a causal effect of withholding tests. The unresolved claim is whether reduced testing is a signal of information loss during an unstable period, rather than merely a marker of current severity, discharge/transfer, end-of-life care, or observation practice.

## Population and temporal design

Construct one index window per eligible stay, with a prespecified 6-hour exposure window and a subsequent 6-hour outcome window. Restrict to adult stays (`age` not coded as pediatric), valid `unitdischargeoffset`, at least one prior 6-hour laboratory window, and at least one subsequent 6-hour observation before discharge/transfer. Exclude windows after an EOL plan is recorded and perform a prespecified sensitivity analysis excluding windows immediately before discharge/transfer.

For each index window, calculate distinct laboratory tests and distinct `labname` values in the prior 6 hours and the change from the preceding 6 hours. Define an abrupt reduction as a decrease of at least 50% or at least two tests; the exact rule must be frozen after one descriptive audit and before outcome modeling. A zero test count is not treated as proof that testing stopped; it is labeled missing observation.

## Prespecified outcome

The primary outcome is a binary deterioration event in the next 6 hours, defined from available physiologic and laboratory measurements. Use a transparent composite of new or worsening hypotension, tachycardia, hypoxemia, or a prespecified abnormal laboratory trajectory. Thresholds must be fixed before outcome modeling and reported; no threshold is asserted here. Secondary outcomes are ICU/hospital mortality from `apachePatientResult.csv.gz` and discharge status from `patient.csv.gz`.

## Rival explanations and safeguards

The leading rival is severity: sicker patients may be tested less often and deteriorate more often. Other rivals are end-of-life treatment limitation, discharge/transfer, interface or recording interruption, and hospital-specific observation practice. Adjust for prior physiology and laboratory state, include hospital fixed effects or hospital-held-out validation, and repeat the analysis after excluding EOL/discharge windows. These safeguards do not identify causality or rule out unrecorded downtime or intentional monitoring decisions.

## Baseline versus time-aware alternative

The simple baseline is a prespecified adjusted logistic model using testing counts, prior physiology, laboratory values, time since admission, hospital, and available severity measures. A substantive alternative is a time-aware sequence model using ordered laboratory and vital observations, retaining test identity, timing, and evolving state that the count baseline loses. The alternative is scientifically useful only if it improves discrimination/calibration for the same deterioration estimand and reveals whether the signal is concentrated in particular trajectories; a small predictive gain alone is not a scientific advance. CPU is appropriate for the baseline and likely sufficient for a bounded sequence model; GPU use is not required at this stage.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: the adjusted association persists across hospitals and after excluding EOL/discharge windows, with uncertainty excluding a clinically negligible association. This supports a monitoring-alignment signal worth prospective validation, not causality.

Adverse: the association disappears after adjustment for current state, is confined to end-of-life/discharge windows, or reverses across hospitals. This weakens the proposed interpretation and favors severity or observation-practice explanations.

Inconclusive: sparse testing, wide intervals, unstable outcome definitions, or inadequate event support make the result non-informative; this justifies a measurement audit or another study rather than a clinical claim.

## Missing evidence and limitations

The available data do not establish whether a laboratory was unavailable, whether a test was ordered but not recorded, whether interface downtime occurred, or whether a clinician intentionally reduced monitoring. There is no direct bedside decision, staffing, bed-capacity, or treatment-limitation adjudication. Mortality and discharge fields are administrative/derived outcomes and do not validate deterioration. Exact row counts, missingness, offset ranges, event rates, and threshold support remain to be audited.
