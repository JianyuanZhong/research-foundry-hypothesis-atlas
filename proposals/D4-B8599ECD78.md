# UKB two-panel metabolic change and route-specific transition to a second cardiometabolic domain

## Child repair and scientific deliverable

This episode-10 child preserves the leading UK Biobank question and repairs a substantive model-comparison flaw. The parents compare a pooled “any second domain” Cox model with a route-specific multistate model. That comparison can attribute an apparent gain to the richer transition structure rather than to biomarker change. The future solver must therefore fit the same route-specific state space twice:

- M0: transition-specific cause-specific hazards using current biomarker levels and prespecified covariates only.
- M1: the identical transition-specific hazards, population, split, censoring and evaluation, adding the three dated biomarker changes and their prespecified interactions with the existing domain.

The scientific deliverable is newly constructed data and newly fitted coefficients, route-specific cumulative-incidence and five-year risk predictions, uncertainty, calibration/Brier/discrimination/RMST/decision-curve contrasts, and falsification outputs. Completion is the complete computed comparison and a conclusion classified as supportive, adverse or inconclusive under the rules below. Readiness, a successful fit, a positive coefficient, or a better-fitting model alone is not completion.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is narrower: baseline metabolic measurements are prognostically associated with later coded diabetes, cardiovascular disease or chronic kidney disease, and a subset of participants has two dated biomarker panels. The inspected UKB snapshot and available literature do not establish that observed within-person change adds information after the latest level, nor that it predicts the route from one coded domain to a second.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 with exactly one of diabetes, cardiovascular disease or chronic kidney disease at a dated repeat-panel landmark, annualized worsening in HbA1c, triglycerides and/or HDL provides incremental prognostic information for the first transition to a second domain beyond the latest levels, existing-domain identity and prespecified covariates. The added information will be estimable within the same route-specific transition model and may differ by existing and newly occurring domain; it should not be reproduced for acute appendicitis.

This is a prognostic association and prediction hypothesis, not a causal effect of changing a biomarker, evidence that monitoring improves outcomes, or a chart-adjudicated disease onset claim. The one-domain restriction is the primary estimand. A separate zero-domain-to-one-domain analysis may be reported descriptively, but it is not pooled into the primary model comparison.

## Outcome phenotype and event construction

Before locked outcomes are examined, freeze a codebook that normalizes case and whitespace, preserves decimal detail for audit, and uses explicit prefixes:

- diabetes: ICD-10 E10, E11, E12, E13, E14; ICD-9 250;
- cardiovascular disease: ICD-10 I20–I25, I50, I60–I69; ICD-9 410–414, 428, 430–438;
- chronic kidney disease: ICD-10 N18 and N19; ICD-9 585;
- negative control: acute appendicitis, ICD-10 K35; ICD-9 540.

Iterate every suffix in both complete diagnosis/date arrays. Pair code and date only at the identical suffix; require a valid parsed date; retain code-only/date-only mismatches in the audit and allow neither to create an event. A domain's first event is the minimum valid paired date among all assigned codes, not the first array position. This is a first coded occurrence, not confirmed onset. Do not substitute narrative extraction or an unverified phenotype algorithm.

At any time t, domain history contains events dated on or before t. If two domains first occur on the same date, both are recorded. For the primary one-domain risk set, that participant is excluded if the simultaneous event produces two domains at the risk origin. If an event and death share a follow-up date, death is absorbing in the primary competing-risk convention; reverse tie handling is a sensitivity.

## Population, landmark, and censoring

Eligibility is age 40–69 inclusive at recruitment, valid sex, finite field-valid HbA1c/TG/HDL at main instances 0 and 1, valid dates d0 and d1 with d1>d0 and a gap of 365–3,650 days, and no contradiction among nonmissing assessment dates 53-0.0 through 53-3.0. Main and assessment 53-0/1 dates are an integrity cross-check; disagreement after date normalization is a recorded data-quality failure, not a silent choice.

For marker j compute the only observed change:
`g_j=(x_{j,1}-x_{j,0})/((d1-d0)/365.25)`; reverse HDL's sign so worsening is positive. Standardize using training-partition means and SDs. Instance labels are not treated as elapsed time and unavailable instances 2/3 are not imputed or synthesized.

The index is d1. Construct history through d1, then exclude participants with two or more domains on or before d1, death on or before d1, or a second-domain event/death in (d1, d1+365 days]. Follow-up begins strictly after d1+365 days and ends at the first second-domain event, death, administrative end, or five years after risk origin. Report the maximum trusted outcome date and the number with a full five-year opportunity. Sensitivities use 0, 2 and 5 years of washout and panel gaps 182–5,475 days.

The primary state is one of three existing-domain identities (diabetes-first, cardiovascular-first, CKD-first). The event state records which new domain occurs; death is absorbing. This makes the estimand a competing-risk transition from exactly one domain to a second, with route-specific hazards estimated for each existing/new-domain pair. No person can contribute multiple first-transition events to the primary analysis.

Use deterministic participant hash buckets 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for model/threshold decisions, 70–79 for one locked test evaluation, and 80–99 reserved and never read for fitting or threshold selection. Split by `eid`, never by rows, dates or events; record the namespace and hash construction without publishing eids.

## Missingness and repeat-panel selection

The primary estimand is conditional on complete two-panel core-marker availability and the dated landmark: association and five-year performance among participants who actually have two usable panels. It is not generalized silently to all UKB participants.

Among those with a valid panel-0 core panel, define S=1 only when panel-1 HbA1c, TG, HDL and d1 pass the same rules. Fit an availability model using panel-0 values, age, sex, BMI, smoking and BP only. Report S by age, sex and panel-0 risk strata, missingness patterns, positivity and stabilized-weight diagnostics. If positivity is adequate, refit with stabilized inverse-probability-of-S weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles, labeling this as a weighted/transported association sensitivity rather than a causal correction. It cannot recover unmeasured repeat-testing indication, treatment, adherence, or socioeconomic selection. Do not impute core markers or outcome dates. Covariate missingness uses field-specific validity rules and training-only indicators if prespecified; negative numeric values are not globally recoded as missing.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

Both models use exactly the primary population, the same route-specific state space, preprocessing, participant split, competing-death convention and evaluation.

M0 is the simple interpretable baseline: transition-specific cause-specific Cox hazards for each existing-domain/new-domain route and death, with latest HbA1c `30750-1.0`, triglycerides `30870-1.0`, HDL `30760-1.0`, existing-domain identity, age, sex, latest assessment BMI, BP, smoking, center and panel gap. Use training-frozen splines, robust participant-level uncertainty, and no outcome-driven selection. Secondary creatinine `30700-1.0` and cystatin-C `30720-1.0` are a predeclared extension, not primary inputs.

M1 is the substantive temporal/mechanistic alternative with the identical route hazards and all M0 inputs plus the three dated changes. Changes may interact with existing-domain identity only under a prespecified training rule. The primary contrast is M1 minus M0 in route-specific log hazards and locked five-year risk; because the state space is identical, the contrast tests whether change adds information rather than whether multistate structure is preferable. Aggregate route predictions to risk of any second domain only after both models are fit. M1 can reveal whether worsening glycaemia, TG or HDL is linked preferentially to diabetes, cardiovascular, or kidney deepening, which the pooled current-level baseline loses. It does not establish a biological mechanism or treatment effect.

Evaluate the joint change null, route-specific estimates, cumulative incidence, five-year Brier score, calibration intercept/slope and integrated calibration error, discrimination, RMST and decision-curve net benefit. Use paired participant bootstrap intervals on the locked test set and preserve seeds and resampling indices. Report route-specific event support before interpreting interactions.

## Thresholds and interpretation

The 5%, 10% and 20% predicted five-year risks are fixed operating-point sensitivities, not validated clinical cutoffs. At each point report alerts, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, number needed to review, calibration and decision-curve net benefit using the corresponding harm/benefit odds. No threshold is selected from the test set. Net benefit is computationally checkable but does not establish clinical utility without a specified review action, acceptable alert burden and expert-approved harm/benefit tradeoff.

Supportive evidence requires a prespecified worsening-direction joint association after latest levels and gap; route-specific estimates compatible with the proposed transition heterogeneity; locked-test improvement in calibration/Brier or net benefit without material subgroup calibration harm; persistence across washout/gap/selection-weighting/competing-death sensitivities; a null or materially weaker appendicitis signal; and no comparable within-state, sex, or gap-bin permutation signal.

Adverse evidence is a null or reverse-direction joint change association, loss after current levels and gap, a comparable appendicitis association, a permutation signal, or locked performance/calibration deterioration. This falsifies the incremental prognostic claim in this selected population; it does not prove biomarkers are harmless or rule out a causal mechanism.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few complete panels or one-to-two events, code/date mismatch, inadequate selection-model positivity, intervals spanning clinically relevant benefit and no benefit, or severe subgroup calibration failure. A threshold result without a clinically specified action is inconclusive for policy value even if computed correctly.

Computationally checkable claims include source paths/hashes, columns, joins, field rules, code/date pairing, first-event dates, cohort counts, timing exclusions, split integrity, matched model fits, uncertainty, predictions, calibration, Brier, decision curves, permutations and sensitivity analyses. Clinical adjudication, exact assay dictionaries, treatment changes, reason for repeat assessment, outside-care capture, external validation, threshold transport and intervention benefit are unavailable and require expert review or another study.

## Alternatives, resources, and retained dispositions

A compact recurrent or temporal-attention model with the same inputs, target, split and metrics remains deferred: two observations provide little sequence structure, and extra capacity cannot resolve selection or coded-phenotype uncertainty. The unsupported four-instance slope/mixed-effects design remains rejected because main has no assay instances 2/3.
