# Suspected discordant renal recovery after early ICU AKI

## Candidate and scientific deliverable

Parent selected: `[prior hypothesis]` (discordant recovery after acute kidney injury). The other assessed branches were retained as alternatives, not conclusions: residual instability before ICU discharge, diuretic timing, and persistent deep sedation.

The deliverable is a newly derived, landmarked MIMIC-IV cohort and a newly estimated prognostic analysis. Completion requires:

1. a frozen cohort/feature table with one row per eligible first ICU stay, phenotype counts, missingness/measurement-density counts, and a provenance manifest;
2. prespecified adjusted associations between renal phenotype and post-landmark outcomes with 95% confidence intervals;
3. a fitted creatinine-only baseline and a fitted nonlinear longitudinal model using the same held-out subject split;
4. held-out predictions, AUROC/AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, and a nested comparison/ablation showing whether urine-output/fluid trajectories add information beyond creatinine;
5. sensitivity analyses over phenotype thresholds, measurement coverage, and exclusion of early renal replacement therapy.

No fitted result is asserted in this proposal.

## Unresolved clinical question

During early ICU acute kidney injury, does an apparent fall in serum creatinine without adequate concurrent urine output identify a group with higher subsequent risk than patients whose creatinine and urine output recover together?

The practical issue is that clinicians often read a falling creatinine as recovery. Critical illness changes creatinine generation and distribution, and urine output is a different, time-sensitive signal. A reproducible discordance phenotype could justify closer renal monitoring and a prospective study of how recovery is assessed. It would not by itself justify a treatment change.

The strongest directly inspected external evidence is Haines et al. (CJASN 2023; DOI 10.2215/CJN.0000000000000203; local source `references/expert-seeds/papers/kidney-function/article.readable.txt`; acquired source ID `[source checksum]`). In a small, selected cohort of 38 mechanically ventilated patients with paired cystatin C, creatinine, and iohexol measurements, creatinine-based kidney function increasingly overestimated measured function during prolonged critical illness and was related to muscle loss. That supports concern that creatinine can misrepresent recovery. It does not establish the proposed urine-output discordance phenotype, its frequency or prognostic value in MIMIC-IV, or a causal benefit from changing management.

The unresolved claim tested here is narrower and falsifiable: among patients meeting the observed early-AKI and measurement criteria, the creatinine-recovered/urine-output-not-recovered phenotype has a higher adjusted risk of subsequent in-hospital death than concordant recovery. This is an observational prognostic claim, not a causal claim about creatinine, diuresis, or an intervention.

## Population and temporal design

Use the MIMIC-IV v3.1 snapshot, adults (patients.anchor_age >= 18) with a first ICU stay per hospitalization, linked to the corresponding admission. Exclude neonatal data, duplicate or invalid intervals, and stays lacking a valid ICU `intime` or `outtime`.

The landmark is:

- `t0 = icustays.intime + 72 hours`.
- Require the patient to be alive and still in the index ICU stay at `t0` (`t0 < icustays.outtime`, and no `admissions.deathtime <= t0`).
- The observation window is `[intime, t0)), and the recovery window is `[intime+48h, intime+72h)).
- Follow-up starts at `t0`; no post-`t0` value may enter a predictor.

Use the first ICU stay within each `hadm_id` as the primary unit so that an ICU return is a meaningful later event. A sensitivity analysis may use one row per first ICU stay across a subject, but all train/test splitting remains by `subject_id`.

### Observed early-AKI eligibility

Construct serum-creatinine observations from:

- ICU `chartevents` itemid 220615 (“Creatinine (serum)”) using `valuenum`, `valueuom`, `charttime`, `stay_id`, `hadm_id`;
- hospital `labevents` itemid 50912 (“Creatinine”, blood chemistry) as a backfill when it falls within the admission/index-ICU interval, using `valuenum`, `valueuom`, `charttime`, `hadm_id`; join to the index stay by `hadm_id` and timestamp containment in `icustays.intime/outtime`.

The analysis will use mg/dL values, retain only physiologically plausible positive numeric results (flag all excluded values), collapse duplicate same-time results deterministically, and preserve a source indicator. The 72-hour feature table must contain at least one valid creatinine in `[0,24h)` and one in `[48,72h)`.

Define an **observed early creatinine-AKI** cohort, explicitly not a complete KDIGO cohort: let (C_{early}) be the minimum creatinine in `[0,24h)), and require a creatinine in `[24,48h)) or a maximum in `[0,48h)) that is at least (C_{early}+0.3) mg/dL or at least (1.5 C_{early}). Record both components and a continuous peak change. This criterion is intentionally bound to observed ICU data; lack of prehospital baseline prevents claiming that it captures all AKI or correct KDIGO staging.

### Exposure/phenotype

Let (C_{peak}) be the maximum valid creatinine in `[0,48h)), and (C_{late}) the minimum valid creatinine in `[48,72h)). Define creatinine recovery as (C_{late} le C_{peak}-0.3) mg/dL or (C_{late}/C_{peak} le 0.75). Keep the continuous absolute and relative changes for sensitivity analysis.

Use ICU `outputevents` itemid 226559 (“Foley”) and `value`, `valueuom`, `charttime`, `stay_id` to calculate hourly urine volume. Require `valueuom = mL`, nonnegative values, and at least 12 distinct observed hourly timestamps in each 24-hour urine window. Use the latest available ICU weight before the window from `chartevents` itemid 226512 (“Admission Weight (Kg)”) or 224639 (“Daily Weight”), using `valuenum`, `valueuom`, `charttime`, and `stay_id`; require a plausible positive kg weight. If weight is absent, do not assign the primary standardized phenotype; report an unweighted sensitivity using mL/h.

Define late urine-output recovery as late observed urine volume divided by observed hours and latest weight being at least 0.5 mL/kg/h. The primary discordant phenotype is **creatinine recovered + late urine-output not recovered**. The reference phenotype is **creatinine recovered + late urine-output recovered**. The other two combinations (neither recovered, and urine recovered without creatinine recovery) are retained as secondary categories. The primary contrast and direction are fixed before fitting. A secondary continuous analysis uses change in urine output from `[0,24h)` to `[48,72h)`; it does not replace the binary primary phenotype.

Because Foley output is selectively documented, the analysis will report coverage and include an explicit “measurement-dense” restriction (>=18 distinct hourly timestamps per window) as a sensitivity analysis. Missing output is not silently treated as zero.

## Outcomes

Primary outcome: in-hospital death after `t0`, using `hosp/admissions.hospital_expire_flag` and `deathtime`, with the event time required to be after `t0`. The primary association is a risk ratio/risk difference from a prespecified adjusted model; a cause-specific time-to-event analysis uses `deathtime` and censors at `dischtime`. Report the number and timing of deaths, not only a p-value.

Secondary outcomes:

- ICU readmission after the index ICU `outtime` and before the same admission `dischtime`: a later row in `icu/icustays` with the same `hadm_id` and later `intime`; report this only among patients discharged alive from the index ICU and treat death before readmission as a competing event.
- New renal-replacement support after `t0`, using `icu/procedureevents.starttime/endtime/itemid` for itemids 225441 (Hemodialysis), 225802 (CRRT), 225803 (CVVHD), 225805 (Peritoneal Dialysis), 225809 (CVVHDF), and 225955 (SCUF). This is a charted procedure outcome, not proof of a specific renal indication.
- A descriptive composite of death or new renal-replacement support after `t0`; do not use it as a substitute for the primary outcome.
- ICU and hospital length of stay, treated as descriptive/resource outcomes because discharge practices and death make them vulnerable to competing-risk and selection effects.

## Confounders and prespecified covariates

All covariates must be measured at or before `t0`:

- `patients.subject_id, anchor_age, gender`;
- `admissions.subject_id, hadm_id, admission_type, admission_location, insurance, hospital_expire_flag, admittime, dischtime, deathtime` (outcome fields are not predictors);
- `icustays.first_careunit, intime`, and pre-landmark ICU elapsed time;
- pre-landmark creatinine level, minimum, maximum, slope, AKI change and measurement density;
- pre-landmark MAP from `chartevents` itemid 220181 (“Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean”), HR 220045, respiratory rate 220210, pulse-oximetry 220277, with `charttime`, `valuenum`, `valueuom`;
- blood lactate from `labevents` itemids 50813, 52442, or 53154 where `d_labitems.label` is Lactate, using `charttime`, `valuenum`, `valueuom`, and `hadm_id`;
- exposure to vasoactive support before `t0` from `icu/inputevents`: norepinephrine 221906, dopamine 221662, phenylephrine 221749/229630/229631/229632, epinephrine 221289/229617, using `starttime`, `endtime`, `amount`, `rate`, and `statusdescription`;
- invasive ventilation before `t0` from `icu/procedureevents` itemid 225792, and non-invasive ventilation itemid 225794 as a separate support indicator;
- pre-landmark RRT exposure from the RRT itemids above;
- a measured-fluid-volume proxy: sum valid mL `icu/inputevents.amount` rows linked through `icu/d_items.itemid` with `linksto=inputevents` and `unitname=mL`, minus mL `icu/outputevents.value` rows with `valueuom=mL`, reported as a sensitivity covariate rather than a diagnosis of congestion;
- admission comorbidity proxy from `hosp/diagnoses_icd(subject_id,hadm_id,icd_code,icd_version)`, joined to `hosp/d_icd_diagnoses(icd_code,icd_version,long_title)`; CKD/ESRD history is identified from dictionary text and reported as an imperfect admission-coded proxy.

Do not adjust for post-`t0` treatments or outcomes. Do not use `hospital_expire_flag`, `deathtime`, later ICU stays, or post-landmark procedures as features.

## Analysis and falsification

First report phenotype prevalence, urine/creatinine coverage, outcome incidence, and a missingness table by phenotype. Estimate the primary contrast with a robust logistic/binomial risk model adjusted for the covariates above; use clustered bootstrap confidence intervals by `subject_id` or a suitable sandwich estimator. Fit a cause-specific Cox model as a secondary time-to-event check, with discharge handled as censoring and death as the event. For ICU readmission, use a competing-risk description rather than interpreting a discharge-conditioned estimate as population mortality.

The simple baseline is a prespecified creatinine-only prognostic model: penalized logistic regression for post-`t0` hospital death using age, sex, ICU unit, early-AKI/creatinine trajectory features, pre-landmark vital/lactate/vasopressor/ventilation/RRT covariates, and missingness indicators, but no urine-output or fluid-volume features. The phenotype association model remains the primary scientific analysis.

The substantive learned alternative is a nonlinear temporal model using the same pre-`t0` case-mix variables plus 6-hour bins from 0–72h for creatinine, Foley urine output, weight-standardized urine output, measured fluid volume, MAP, HR, respiratory rate, SpO2, lactate, vasoactive exposure, ventilation, and RRT. For each bin, retain last value, mean, minimum, maximum, slope, variability, and measurement count/missingness. Fit scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingClassifier (or an equivalent CPU tree booster available in the Harbor environment) with nested subject-grouped cross-validation and a fixed 20% subject-level held-out test set. Its urine/fluid ablation must be fitted on the same split. The scientific comparison is whether nonlinear timing/interactions and the independent urine-output signal improve calibrated risk and reveal risk heterogeneity missed by a creatinine-only model, not whether a small AUROC change wins a leaderboard.

Report AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, calibration plots, and bootstrap intervals on the held-out set. Compare nested likelihood/Brier performance and decision curves only as exploratory clinical-utility summaries. Inspect learned feature effects only with a prespecified grouped permutation procedure; do not convert feature importance into causation.

Falsification criteria are fixed:

- **Supportive:** the primary adjusted discordant-versus-concordant estimate is above the null with uncertainty that excludes the null, the direction persists in the measurement-dense and threshold sensitivity analyses, and the learned model's urine/fluid ablation shows reproducible calibration or risk-stratification information beyond the creatinine-only baseline.
- **Adverse to the hypothesis:** the estimate is precisely near or below the null, or the direction reverses under the measurement-dense analysis; this would falsify the proposed prognostic contrast in this snapshot. A learned-model improvement alone cannot rescue a failed prespecified phenotype contrast.
- **Inconclusive:** wide intervals from sparse events, major phenotype-dependent missingness, failed calibration, or unstable estimates across plausible coverage thresholds. An AUROC difference without uncertainty or without an ablation is inconclusive.

A phenotype that only predicts later creatinine values is not sufficient; the primary claim requires an outcome association. Conversely, a positive association cannot show that creatinine recovery is harmful, that oliguria is the mechanism, or that withholding/starting a treatment changes outcome.

## Required interpretation boundary

If positive, the result supports a reproducible observed prognostic signal and motivates validation with cystatin C/measured GFR, better weight and volume-status ascertainment, and clinician adjudication. It does not establish persistent renal dysfunction, muscle-loss mediation, or benefit/harm from any clinical action. A causal treatment conclusion requires a separate target-trial design with complete treatment indications and, preferably, prospective/randomized evidence. External validation in another ICU population is required before using the phenotype as a bedside decision rule.
