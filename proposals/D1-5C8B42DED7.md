# Discordant renal recovery in MIMIC-IV: a falsifiable, temporally separated prognostic study

## Question and clinical importance

Among adult ICU patients with KDIGO acute kidney injury (AKI), does a discordance between creatinine-based and urine-output-based recovery at a fixed 72-hour post-onset landmark predict worse 28-day in-hospital mortality beyond what is explained by current illness severity?

The clinical decision is consequential: clinicians must decide whether to escalate monitoring, involve nephrology, initiate renal replacement therapy (RRT), or modify fluid management when creatinine and urine output tell different stories about renal recovery. A model that identifies patients whose discordance reflects true residual kidney injury rather than measurement or severity artifacts could support earlier nephrology review and more informed RRT decisions. This study does not claim that discordance itself causes worse outcomes.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

**Already supported by evidence:** AKI is common in ICU patients and associated with increased mortality; creatinine and urine output can disagree in AKI staging and recovery; AKI recovery is an important prognostic signal [K1]. Creatinine is an imperfect surrogate for measured kidney function during critical illness, particularly in AKI, because muscle mass, fluid shifts, and changing clearance alter its interpretation [K2]. The KDIGO creatinine and urine-output criteria define complementary but non-equivalent pathways for staging AKI [K3].

**Unresolved claim:** Among patients meeting KDIGO AKI criteria, a 72-hour discordance in which creatinine has returned toward baseline while urine output remains below the KDIGO recovery threshold is associated with higher 28-day in-hospital mortality than concordant recovery, after adjustment for illness severity and measurement opportunity. This association is not established by the prior literature and is the specific hypothesis tested here.

**Strongest rival explanation:** Discordant recovery may reflect differences in illness severity, fluid balance, diuretic or RRT exposure, or selective measurement rather than true residual kidney injury. The design below addresses this with a landmark analysis, measurement-opportunity adjustment, and negative-control checks.

## Scientific advance over existing knowledge

Prior work has compared creatinine and urine output for AKI staging and has shown that creatinine can over- or under-estimate measured GFR during critical illness [K2]. The KDIGO criteria acknowledge that either criterion can define AKI, but do not test whether a specific discordant recovery pattern carries incremental prognostic information [K3]. This study adds a temporally separated, landmark-based test of whether discordance at 72 hours predicts mortality beyond current severity, using a simple baseline and a learned alternative on the same question. It does not reproduce a prior cohort study and does not claim causal effects.

## Population and temporal design

**Source dataset:** MIMIC-IV v3.1, available at `datasets/mimic/` with source files listed in `datasets/mimic/README.md`. The exact source files are:
- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.hosp.patients.csv.gz` (patient identifiers, discharge time, death indicator)
- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.hosp.admissions.csv.gz` (admission/discharge times, ICU admission flags)
- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.icu.icustays.csv.gz` (ICU stay identifiers, admission/discharge times)

- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.labevents.csv.gz` (laboratory measurements including creatinine)
- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.chartevents.csv.gz` (vital signs, urine output, fluid balance)
- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.prescriptions.csv.gz` (medication orders including diuretics and RRT-related agents)
- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.inputevents.csv.gz` (fluid and medication administration)
- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.outputevents.csv.gz` (urine output and other output records)
- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.services.csv.gz` (service assignments)
- `datasets/mimic/mimiciv.diagnoses_icd.csv.gz` (diagnoses for AKI and comorbidity context)

**Population:** Adult patients (age >= 18 at ICU admission) with a first ICU stay in MIMIC-IV who meet KDIGO AKI Stage 1-3 criteria within the first 72 hours of ICU admission. KDIGO AKI is defined as any of: (a) serum creatinine >= 3x baseline within 48 hours; (b) serum creatinine >= 2x baseline and an increase within 48 hours; (c) urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for >= 12 hours or < 0.3 mL/kg/h for >= 24 hours. Baseline creatinine is the minimum creatinine in the 90 days prior to ICU admission, or the first creatinine in the admission if no prior value exists.

**Landmark:** 72 hours after AKI onset (the time of the first creatinine meeting KDIGO criteria or the first urine-output criterion, whichever is earlier). Patients must have at least one creatinine measurement and at least 12 hours of urine-output data in the 72-hour window to be eligible for discordance classification.

**Discordance definition:** At the 72-hour landmark, creatinine-based recovery is defined as creatinine returning to within 1.5x baseline. Urine-output-based recovery is defined as urine output >= 0.5 mL/kg/h for the preceding 12 hours. Discordance is creatinine-based recovery without urine-output-based recovery. Concordant recovery is both criteria met. Concordant non-recovery is neither criterion met. The primary discordance group is creatinine-recovered / urine-output-not-recovered.

**Outcome:** 28-day in-hospital mortality, defined as death before hospital discharge or by 28 days after ICU admission, whichever comes first. The death indicator is from `mimiciv.hosp.patients.csv.gz` (discharge_to_location or `hosp.death` field as available in the source).

**Covariates (baseline and landmark):** Age, sex, admission type, ICU service, KDIGO AKI stage at onset, baseline creatinine, landmark creatinine, landmark urine output (mL/kg/h), fluid balance in the 72-hour window, vasopressor use (norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin), diuretic use, RRT initiation before landmark, SOFA score components available at landmark (platelets, bilirubin, MAP, GCS if available), and measurement opportunity (number of creatinine and urine-output records in the 72-hour window).

## Baseline and learned alternative

**Simple baseline:** A Cox proportional hazards model (or logistic regression for 28-day mortality) with the following covariates: age, sex, KDIGO AKI stage, landmark creatinine, landmark urine output, fluid balance, vasopressor use, diuretic use, RRT use, and measurement opportunity. This baseline tests whether discordance adds information beyond a parsimonious set of clinical variables.

**Learned alternative:** A gradient-boosted tree model (e.g., XGBoost or LightGBM) trained on the same covariates plus time-varying features from the 72-hour window (hourly urine output trajectory, creatinine trajectory, fluid balance trajectory). The learned model can capture nonlinear interactions and trajectory shapes that the linear baseline loses. The comparison is on the same question: does discordance predict mortality beyond current severity? The learned model is not a substitute for the baseline; it tests whether the discordance signal is robust to a more flexible functional form.

**Training/fitting target:** Both models are fit on the full eligible cohort with 5-fold cross-validation. The primary analysis is the baseline model; the learned model is a sensitivity analysis. No GPU is required; CPU is sufficient for the expected cohort size (estimated 500-2000 eligible patients based on KDIGO AKI prevalence in MIMIC-IV).

**Split:** Time-based split is not appropriate for a landmark analysis with a fixed 72-hour window. Instead, 5-fold cross-validation with stratification by outcome is used. The primary estimand is the hazard ratio (or odds ratio) for discordance vs. concordant recovery, adjusted for covariates.

**Outcome/uncertainty evaluation:** The primary outcome is 28-day in-hospital mortality. The primary estimand is the adjusted hazard ratio (or odds ratio) for discordance vs. concordant recovery. 95% confidence intervals are reported. The learned model is evaluated by AUC and calibration (Brier score) on held-out folds. The comparison between baseline and learned model is a sensitivity analysis, not a primary endpoint.

**Resource/time budget:** CPU-only. Estimated runtime: 30-60 minutes for data extraction and cohort definition, 10-20 minutes for baseline model fitting, 20-40 minutes for learned model fitting. Total: under 2 hours on 4 CPUs. No GPU required.

**Selection/deferral reason:** The baseline is selected as the primary analysis because it is transparent, interpretable, and directly tests the discordance hypothesis. The learned alternative is retained as a sensitivity analysis to test robustness to nonlinearities. If the baseline shows no significant discordance effect but the learned model does, this would suggest the signal is driven by nonlinear interactions and would warrant further investigation. If both show no effect, the discordance hypothesis is not supported. If the baseline shows a significant effect but the learned model does not, this would suggest the effect is driven by a small number of patients or is sensitive to model specification.

## Analysis and falsification

**Primary analysis:** Adjusted Cox proportional hazards model (or logistic regression) with discordance as the primary exposure and 28-day in-hospital mortality as the outcome. Covariates are as listed above. The primary estimand is the adjusted hazard ratio (or odds ratio) for discordance vs. concordant recovery.

**Sensitivity analyses:**
1. Learned model (gradient-boosted trees) on the same covariates plus trajectory features.
2. Restriction to patients with complete measurement opportunity (>= 6 creatinine and >= 24 hours of urine-output data in the 72-hour window).
3. Restriction to patients without RRT initiation before the landmark.
4. Negative-control analysis: test whether discordance predicts a non-renal outcome (e.g., 28-day in-hospital length of stay) that should not be directly affected by renal recovery. A positive association with a non-renal outcome would suggest confounding by severity or measurement.

**Falsification criteria:** The discordance hypothesis is falsified if:
1. The adjusted hazard ratio for discordance vs. concordant recovery is not significantly different from 1.0 (95% CI includes 1.0) in the primary analysis.
2. The discordance effect is driven entirely by a small number of patients (e.g., the effect disappears when the top 10% of patients by covariate extremity are excluded).
3. The negative-control analysis shows a stronger association with a non-renal outcome than with the renal outcome, suggesting confounding.

**Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive results:**
- **Supportive:** The adjusted hazard ratio for discordance vs. concordant recovery is significantly greater than 1.0 (95% CI excludes 1.0) in the primary analysis, and the effect is robust to sensitivity analyses. This would support the hypothesis that discordance is associated with worse mortality beyond current severity.
- **Adverse:** The adjusted hazard ratio is not significantly different from 1.0, or the effect is driven by confounding (negative-control analysis). This would argue against the hypothesis.
- **Inconclusive:** The adjusted hazard ratio is directionally consistent but the 95% CI includes 1.0, or the effect is present in the baseline but not the learned model (or vice versa). This would warrant further investigation with a larger cohort or different covariate set.

## Limitations and missing evidence

1. **No measured GFR:** MIMIC-IV does not contain measured GFR. Creatinine and urine output are surrogates for kidney function. The discordance signal may reflect differences in measurement rather than true kidney injury. This is a fundamental limitation that cannot be addressed with the available data.
2. **Selective measurement:** Urine output and creatinine are measured at different frequencies depending on clinical context. Patients with more severe illness may have more frequent measurements, leading to differential measurement opportunity. The design addresses this with measurement-opportunity adjustment, but residual confounding is possible.
3. **Observational design:** This is an observational study. The discordance signal may be confounded by unmeasured severity, fluid balance, or treatment decisions. The design cannot establish causal effects.
4. **Single-center data:** MIMIC-IV is a single-center dataset. Generalizability to other ICUs is uncertain.
5. **No external validation:** The study is not externally validated. The results may not generalize to other populations or time periods.

## What would require additional data or another study

- **Measured GFR:** To confirm that discordance reflects true kidney injury rather than measurement artifacts, measured GFR would be needed. This is not available in MIMIC-IV.
- **External validation:** To establish generalizability, the study would need to be replicated in an external cohort with similar data availability.
- **Causal analysis:** To establish causal effects, a randomized trial or a causal inference design (e.g., instrumental variables, marginal structural models) would be needed. The observational design cannot establish causality.
- **Clinical adjudication:** To confirm that discordance reflects true kidney injury rather than measurement artifacts, clinical adjudication by a nephrologist would be needed. This is not feasible at scale in MIMIC-IV.
