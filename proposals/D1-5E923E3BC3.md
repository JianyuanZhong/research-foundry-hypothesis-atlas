# Interpretable recovery phenotypes after ICU discharge

Parent: [prior hypothesis]
Dataset: MIMIC-IV v3.1 snapshot [source checksum]

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

When an adult leaves the ICU, does a clinically interpretable pattern of recovery, persistence, oscillation and support de-escalation during the preceding 24 hours add prognostic information beyond the most recent physiologic state and the charting process? The decision relevance is immediate: ward surveillance and ICU-bed review need to distinguish durable stability from a patient who reaches a superficially acceptable last value while remaining unstable or dependent on support.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that residual oxygen/hemodynamic abnormality and repeated structured observations are clinically plausible warning signals and are recorded in this MIMIC snapshot. Existing evidence available to this proposal does not establish an effect size, the causal value of a discharge decision, whether a later ICU admission was planned, or that any summary changes outcomes. The hypothesis below is therefore prognostic and observed-process based.

Among adults discharged alive from an index ICU stay, a prespecified recovery summary of the final 24-hour structured physiology and respiratory-support trajectory will improve locked patient-held-out 48-hour cause-specific risk estimation for first subsequent ICU admission, without material degradation of death calibration, compared with a last-state-plus-charting-process baseline. A secondary representation test asks whether a small ordered GRU-D-style encoder adds residual information beyond that interpretable summary.

A supportive B1-versus-B0 result would identify reproducible, reviewable persistence/recovery information in the recorded trajectory. A B2-versus-B1 result would show that ordered nonlinear structure remains after those clinical summaries, not merely that a larger model predicts better. Neither result establishes a causal recovery mechanism, unplanned readmission, or benefit from delaying discharge or adding monitoring.

## Predictors and recovery construct

Use `valuenum` when present, convert Fahrenheit to Celsius, normalize FiO2 explicitly, prefer arterial over non-invasive MAP within a bin while retaining source, reject fixed invalid ranges (HR 20--250, RR 3--80, SpO2 50--100, MAP 20--200, temperature 25--45 C, oxygen flow 0--80 L/min, FiO2 0.21--1.0), and report rejected-value counts. Alarm items are excluded.

Read `mimic-iv-3.1/icu/procedureevents.csv.gz` (table `icu/procedureevents`: `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, caregiver_id, starttime, endtime, storetime, itemid, value, valueuom, location, locationcategory, orderid, linkorderid, ordercategoryname, ordercategorydescription, patientweight, isopenbag, continueinnextdept, statusdescription, originalamount, originalrate`). The checked dictionary maps item `225792` (Invasive Ventilation) and `225794` (Non-invasive Ventilation) to `procedureevents`. For each six-hour bin, record whether a valid clipped interval intersects it and use the maximum support class (invasive > non-invasive); also retain support class at the landmark. Oxygen flow/FiO2 are continuous support proxies. This is not a complete device or waveform measure. Device-text mapping for `226732` is not allowed into the primary recovery score unless a fixed mapping is documented before fitting; it is an audit-only field otherwise.

Aggregate each variable into four consecutive six-hour bins with median, last value, count, mask, and time from bin end to last observation; compute minimum, maximum and standard deviation only when at least two observations exist. The baseline receives only final valid state, recency, support, counts, masks and gaps. All preprocessing is fit on training subjects only.

The recovery-summary model adds the following fixed, data-independent features to that baseline:

- For HR, RR, MAP, SpO2 and temperature: first-to-last change; robust slope across the four bin medians; persistence ratio (signed net change divided by total absolute change, set to zero when there is no movement); number of sign changes in successive bin differences; and fraction of observed bin medians outside MAP <65, HR >100, RR >24, SpO2 <92, and temperature <36 or >38 C. Every summary has a domain-availability indicator and is missing when fewer than two valid bins are available.

- For oxygenation/support: first-to-last FiO2 change, FiO2 slope, first-to-last oxygen-flow change, number of support escalations, number of support de-escalations, maximum support class, and last-bin support class. Escalation/de-escalation is defined by the ordered class invasive > non-invasive > oxygen proxy > none, with ties unchanged. Continuous oxygen measures retain their own missingness indicators.

These are mechanism-informed clinical summaries, not validated physiologic recovery scores. Treatment, clinician measurement and support changes are observational; the analysis cannot claim that the summaries mediate or cause ICU return. The fixed screening thresholds are used to test transportable interpretability, not to define clinical readiness.

Sparse labs are not required for the primary deliverable. If used as a secondary sensitivity, read `mimic-iv-3.1/hosp/labevents.csv.gz` (table columns `labevent_id, subject_id, hadm_id, specimen_id, itemid, order_provider_id, charttime, storetime, value, valuenum, valueuom, ref_range_lower, ref_range_upper, flag, priority, comments`) joined to `mimic-iv-3.1/hosp/d_labitems.csv.gz` (columns `itemid, label, fluid, category`), with the parent’s listed lactate/creatinine/sodium/hemoglobin/WBC IDs and unit/fluid validation. Labs cannot rescue a sparse primary sequence or change the estimand.

## Outcomes and exact estimand

Start follow-up exactly at ICU `outtime`. The primary horizon is 48 hours; the secondary horizon is seven days. Use eight six-hour cause-specific hazard bins for 48 hours and 28 six-hour bins for seven days. For every index transition, define the at-risk interval as ending at the earliest of horizon, first event, hospital `dischtime`, or unresolved event timing.

Cause 1 is the first later `icu/icustays` row for the same (`subject_id, hadm_id`) with `intime > index outtime` and `intime <= outtime+horizon); event time is that ICU `intime`. It is any subsequent ICU admission, not a reliably adjudicated unplanned return.

Cause 2 is death before readmission: `hosp/admissions.deathtime > outtime`, within the horizon, and earlier than the first later ICU `intime`. Death after readmission is described separately. If no event occurs, censor at alive hospital `dischtime` if it is after `outtime` and before the horizon. If `hospital_expire_flag=1` while `deathtime` is missing, mark event timing unresolved and exclude that index from the primary timed analysis; report its count and a sensitivity assigning death at `dischtime` only when no later ICU admission is recorded. Contradictory or non-monotone timing fields are audited and treated as unresolved, not silently repaired.

The estimand is the observed-process cause-specific cumulative incidence of first subsequent ICU admission and first death by 48 hours and seven days in this defined index population, with death a competing event for ICU admission. It is not a treatment effect, discharge-policy effect, monitoring-policy effect, or claim about unplanned readmission.

## Models, comparison and analysis

Fit three prespecified models on the identical cohort and splits:

B0, the interpretable last-state-plus-charting-process baseline: L2-regularized pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific hazards with interval indicators, age, gender, admission type/location, first/last ICU careunit, ICU duration, final valid physiologic values, support, recency, counts, masks and gaps, and only prespecified SpO2-by-FiO2 and support-by-device interactions.

B1, the primary recovery-summary model: B0 plus the fixed recovery/persistence/oscillation/abnormal-burden/support-transition features above, with group labels that permit coefficient and feature-family reporting. The primary incremental estimand is the paired difference in held-out 48-hour ICU-admission Brier score and CIF calibration between B1 and B0.

B2, an independently fitted one-layer 32-unit GRU-D-style encoder over the four ordered bins, using the same physiology, support, masks, counts and delta-time channels plus baseline covariates, dropout 0.1, and two cause-specific hazard heads trained with competing-risk discrete-time negative log likelihood. Use a fixed maximum of 40 epochs and validation-loss early stopping with fixed patience. B2’s incremental comparison is B2 versus B1, not merely B2 versus a weak model. A last-bin-only version and a process-only null are required controls.

Use deterministic patient-level 70/15/15 subject splits with five fixed hash seeds; no `subject_id` or `hadm_id` may cross splits. All mappings, imputation/decay, standardization and feature availability decisions are training-only. Preserve split manifests by internal IDs. Report paired 48-hour ICU-admission and death Brier scores, cause-specific CIF calibration intercept/slope, integrated Brier, AUROC/AUPRC and decision-curve net benefit at thresholds frozen before test evaluation. Use 2,000 subject-level paired bootstrap replicates, all seed results, event counts and uncertainty intervals. Treat 0.005 absolute ICU-admission Brier reduction as an interpretation benchmark, not a universal clinical-utility threshold or proof of benefit.

## Falsification, interpretation and limits

Required checks and ablations are:

1. A zero-violation pre-outtime timestamp audit, one-row-per-index-hospitalization audit, first-event ordering audit and explicit exclusion ledger.
2. Last-bin-only, value permutation with masks/counts preserved, order permutation with values/masks preserved, removal of mask/delta channels, and process-only null.
3. Within-outcome-rate-stratum label permutation, which must remove discrimination and calibration advantage.
4. Primary observable versus unrestricted-cohort sensitivity, and a transfer-destination audit using `mimic-iv-3.1/hosp/transfers.csv.gz` (table `hosp/transfers`: `subject_id, hadm_id, transfer_id, eventtype, careunit, intime, outtime`; join on `subject_id, hadm_id`; first `intime >= outtime` within six hours). Destination is never an at-outtime predictor.

Support for the primary hypothesis requires B1 to improve locked 48-hour ICU-admission prediction over B0 with uncertainty compatible with a nonzero improvement, retain acceptable death calibration, show interpretable feature-family stability across subject seeds, and lose its gain under value/order permutation. Stronger support for a learned residual representation additionally requires B2 to improve over B1 and lose information when order/value channels are destroyed. Such results support prognostic information in recorded recovery patterns, not a causal mechanism or clinical policy.

Adverse results include no incremental B1 improvement, death-calibration harm, unstable or counterintuitive feature groups, no loss under value/order perturbation, label-permutation discrimination, or B2 improvement that disappears against B1. These refute the specified incremental representation claim in this snapshot, not the existence of residual instability.

Inconclusive results include too few ICU-admission/death events, broad intervals spanning no effect, high domain missingness, unresolved death timing, poor linkage, split instability, or convergence failure within the declared budget. A failure of the observability sensitivity to transport is a selection limitation, not evidence that recovery has no prognostic value.

Computationally checkable claims include cohort flow, joins, timestamp exclusion, feature construction, model fitting, locked predictions, CIFs, calibration, bootstrap intervals, ablations and convergence. Clinical adjudication remains necessary for planned versus unplanned ICU return, treatment limitation and clinician intent, the fidelity of procedure intervals to delivered support, and whether a feature is actionable. Functional readiness, bedside assessment, goals of care, raw waveforms and MIMIC-CXR images are unavailable here. Clinical review, external/prospective validation and a decision-impact study are required before deployment or policy claims.

## Demonstrations and alternative disposition

The natural-history demonstration was used only to motivate a bounded ordered structured-EHR adaptation; its UK Biobank/Danish cohorts and lifetime follow-up are absent, so this is not a reproduction or external-validation claim. The Bayesian demonstration’s genetic inputs are unavailable and are not required for this ICU question. The cancer demonstration’s main article and complete STAR Methods remain unavailable; only its accessible supplement supports the general idea that modality ablations matter. No unavailable paper text is used as evidence.

The simple baseline, recovery-summary model and GRU-D model were all specified before selection in `episode5_method_and_catalog_note.md`. B1 is selected as the primary new deliverable because it tests a clinically reviewable claim with the same data and estimand; B2 is retained to test whether a richer ordered representation contains residual information. Revisit B2 or a richer mechanistic model only if B1 is positive but opaque/too lossy, with expert validation of support mappings and thresholds. If both alternatives fail with adequate events and valid falsification, preserve that negative result rather than adding complexity.
