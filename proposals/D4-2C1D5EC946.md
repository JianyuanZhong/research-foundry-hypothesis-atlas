# Proteomic Discordance and Multimorbidity Progression Patterns

## Hypothesis to Test

**Primary Hypothesis**: Among individuals with multiple proteomic measurements, discordance between proteome states across different time points (proteomic instability) predicts faster progression to multimorbidity (cardiometabolic syndrome, cardiorenal syndrome, or other major disease clusters) beyond what static baseline proteomic profiles predict.

**Specific Predictions**:
1. Individuals exhibiting proteomic discordance (large inter-measurement variability in proteome composition) will have higher rates of multimorbidity progression compared to those with stable proteome states, even after adjusting for baseline proteomic profiles
2. The magnitude of proteomic discordance will be associated with the rate of disease progression, with greater discordance predicting faster progression
3. Proteomic discordance will provide independent predictive value beyond traditional risk factors (age, sex, BMI, smoking, alcohol, genetic ancestry)

## Scientific Rationale

### Unresolved Question
Current biomarker research predominantly focuses on static baseline measurements to predict disease risk. However, biological systems are inherently dynamic, and proteomic changes over time may capture unique biological processes (e.g., inflammatory flares, metabolic adaptation, organ stress responses) that static snapshots miss. The key unresolved question is: **Do temporal proteomic dynamics (discordance/instability) provide unique predictive information for disease progression that static baseline profiles cannot capture?**

### Clinical Importance
- **Early Risk Identification**: Proteomic discordance could identify individuals at high risk for multimorbidity progression before traditional risk factors manifest, enabling earlier intervention
- **Precision Stratification**: Discordance patterns may stratify patients into distinct risk trajectories, informing personalized monitoring and intervention strategies
- **Biological Insight**: Discordance may reflect underlying biological instability (e.g., compensatory mechanisms, emerging inflammation, metabolic adaptation) that precedes clinical disease manifestation

### Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

**Complement to Static Baseline Studies**: While proteomic signatures at a single time point have been extensively studied for disease prediction, no study has systematically examined whether temporal proteomic dynamics (discordance) provide *additional* predictive power beyond static profiles.

**Complement to Trajectory Studies**: The existing candidate work (ca56240d62a2f81cb410a9c5, f5b5d3917855b9d35019a115) focuses on identifying discrete trajectory patterns (e.g., rapid change vs. stable). This proposal takes a complementary approach by focusing on the *magnitude of discordance* (inter-measurement variability) as a distinct phenotype, which may capture different biological processes than trajectory classification.

**Complement to Organ-Age Clocks**: The organ-age clock work (K1: Wang et al., Nature Aging 2025) focuses on predicting disease onset using proteomic aging patterns. This proposal extends this by examining whether proteomic *instability* (discordance) predicts *progression rates* rather than just onset, addressing a different clinical question.

## Study Population and Main Data

**Population**: UK Biobank participants with:
- ≥2 proteomic measurements (biological_samples table)
- At least one incident multimorbidity diagnosis during follow-up (health_outcomes table)
- Complete covariate data (age, sex, BMI, smoking, alcohol, genetic ancestry from population table)

**Exclusions**:
- Participants with <2 proteomic time points
- Participants without any incident multimorbidity diagnosis during follow-up
- Participants with missing key covariates

**Primary Outcome**: Time to first incident multimorbidity (cardiometabolic syndrome, cardiorenal syndrome, or other major disease clusters)

**Exposure**: Proteomic discordance metric (inter-measurement variability in proteome composition)

## Scientific Approach

### Baseline Approach (Null Model)
- Cox proportional hazards regression with static baseline proteomic profile as predictor
- Adjust for age, sex, BMI, smoking, alcohol, genetic ancestry
- **What this tests**: Does static baseline proteomics predict multimorbidity progression?

### Alternative Approach (Discordance Model)
- Cox proportional hazards regression with proteomic discordance (inter-measurement variability) as predictor
- Adjust for same covariates as baseline
- **What this tests**: Does proteomic instability (discordance) predict progression beyond static profiles?

### Key Comparison
- Compare hazard ratios for proteomic discordance vs. baseline proteomic profile
- Test whether discordance provides *additional* predictive value beyond baseline (e.g., via nested model comparison)
- Evaluate concordance between discordance-based predictions and clinical outcomes

### Statistical Methods
1. **Proteomic Discordance Metric**: Calculate inter-measurement variability (e.g., coefficient of variation, distance from mean) across proteome states
2. **Cox Regression**: Estimate hazard ratios for discordance vs. baseline proteomics
3. **Model Comparison**: Nested model comparison (baseline vs. discordance) using likelihood ratio tests
4. **Validation**: Internal validation (bootstrapping) and external validation (if applicable)

## Evaluation Criteria

### Primary Endpoint
- Hazard ratio for proteomic discordance predicting multimorbidity progression
- Statistical significance (p-value < 0.05)

### Secondary Endpoints
- Discrimination (C-index) for discordance-based vs. baseline-based predictions
- Calibration of predictions
- Net reclassification improvement (NRI) when adding discordance to baseline model

### Falsification Criteria
- **Adverse Result**: No association between proteomic discordance and multimorbidity progression after adjusting for baseline proteomics and covariates
- **Supportive Result**: Proteomic discordance predicts progression independently of baseline proteomics
- **Inconclusive Result**: Weak/ambiguous association; may require larger sample size or different metrics

## Clinical Significance

### If Supported
- Proteomic discordance could serve as an early warning signal for multimorbidity progression
- May enable earlier intervention strategies targeting biological instability
- Could refine risk stratification beyond traditional factors

### If Not Supported
- Suggests that proteomic instability is not a distinct risk phenotype
- May indicate that static baseline proteomics captures sufficient predictive information
- Could redirect research toward other temporal biomarkers (e.g., metabolomics, imaging)

## Main Limitations

1. **Measurement Frequency**: UK Biobank proteomic measurements may be sparse, limiting ability to capture true biological dynamics
2. **Reverse Causation**: Disease processes may influence proteomic measurements (e.g., inflammation from early disease affecting proteome)
3. **Confounding**: Unmeasured confounders (lifestyle, environmental factors) may influence both proteomic discordance and disease progression
4. **External Validity**: Findings limited to UK Biobank population; external validation needed
5. **Clinical Actionability**: Discordance patterns may not translate to actionable interventions without further research
