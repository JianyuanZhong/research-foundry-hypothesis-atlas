# Fixed-window label-to-action audit: the 12-hour label signal is not independently supported

## Status, parents, and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the exact parent cohort and exposure and does not retry the falsified durable-liberation endpoint. The first parent established that durable liberation cannot be validated in this eICU snapshot; the second established that short-horizon charted setting and infusion-rate outcomes are computable. This child completes the prespecified fixed-window association audit and tightens the estimand around an independently charted action.

The unresolved question is: among the same 540 ventilated person-landmarks, does `Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation`, compared with `Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial`, have independently measurable 12-hour operational meaning as a subsequent charted ventilator-setting or sedation-infusion-rate de-escalation? The question concerns documented management trajectories only. It is not a test of an SBT, extubation, readiness, bedside delivery, clinical appropriateness, or causal effect.

The strongest claim supported by the completed computation is adverse/inconclusive rather than supportive: the broad composite reduction is not greater after the evaluation label, its apparent event rate is dominated by `Peak Insp. Pressure`, and the clinically more interpretable controlled-setting composite is sparse and unstable under hospital restriction and landmarks. Sedation-rate reductions are similarly compatible with no difference and vary by hospital. Therefore this snapshot does not establish an independently generalizable 12-hour implementation meaning for either label. It does not show that no bedside action occurred; unrecorded action and local documentation remain possible.

## Locked estimands and ascertainment

The primary estimand is the difference in probability of a **documented** qualifying reduction during `(D,D+720]`, using available-case denominators requiring at least one paired numeric component. Missing post-D values are unavailable action ascertainment, never a no-action value.

The primary controlled-setting composite is any decrease of at least 5 percentage points in FiO2, 1 cmH2O in PEEP, 1 unit in pressure support, or 1 unit in set ventilator rate. `Peak Insp. Pressure` is retained as a secondary field because it may be a measured pressure rather than a commanded setting. The broad parent composite including peak pressure is reported only to expose component dominance. A second sensitivity requires support-core fields without peak pressure.

The sedation outcome requires the same drug to have a numeric rate strictly before D within `(D-1440,D)` and a numeric rate in `(D,D+720]` decreasing by at least 10% of baseline or at least 0.01 rate units. This is a rate-record proxy, not a sedation interruption or bedside action.

The analogous pre-label trajectory uses the fixed prior window `(D-720,D]` with baseline in `(D-1440,D-720]`. It is a temporal diagnostic, not a no-treatment control. Secondary process fields (`Mechanical Ventilator Mode`, `SBT Method`, `RT Vent On/Off`) remain feasibility outcomes only; exact source inventory yielded zero spontaneous-mode and SBT-method positives under the strict algorithm and 2 versus 1 RT-Off records. None is called SBT completion or extubation.

Uncertainty is reported with available-case Wald intervals for descriptive risk differences, Fisher exact tests as a small-sample descriptive check, and a pre-D-only linear probability model restricted to overlap hospitals with HC covariance clustered by `uniquepid`, including hospital, landmark, and the prespecified pre-D physiology/delay covariates. The model is associative, not causal. Repeated landmarks are explicitly retained: 76 stays contribute more than one landmark, 173 person-landmark rows are from repeated stays, and up to 5 landmarks occur in one stay. The overlap analysis is limited to hospitals 248, 252, and 420, the only hospitals with at least 10 rows of each strategy; no hospital-level generalization beyond this overlap set is claimed.

The primary 12-hour broad setting composite (including peak pressure) was 91/282 (32.3%) for evaluation versus 85/227 (37.4%) for no-trial: risk difference `-5.18` percentage points, 95% Wald interval `-13.51 to +3.16`. The available-case ascertainment itself was 282/309 (91.3%) versus 227/231 (98.3%), a `-7.01` percentage-point difference, so an all-row documented-event sensitivity was also required and gave `-7.35` points (91/309 versus 85/231; interval `-15.38 to +0.68`). These results do not support an evaluation-label excess.

The broad composite is not a reliable operational action endpoint because it is driven by peak pressure: peak reductions were 71/197 (36.0%) versus 73/199 (36.7%), risk difference `-0.64` points (95% interval `-10.12 to +8.83`). Removing peak pressure leaves only controlled-setting reductions in 28/281 (10.0%) versus 16/227 (7.0%), risk difference `+2.92` points (95% interval `-1.92 to +7.75`) across all rows. In the three-hospital overlap, however, the corresponding available-case rates were 39/127 (30.7%) versus 72/170 (42.4%), standardized risk difference `-10.70` points; the pre-D-only adjusted estimate was `-10.88` points (cluster-robust 95% interval `-23.00 to +1.23`, p=`0.078`). The no-peak overlap estimate was `-2.92` points standardized (5/126 versus 12/170), with adjusted estimate `-3.23` points (95% `-8.60 to +2.15`). Thus the small all-row positive signal is not stable under hospital overlap and cannot be treated as support.

Sedation-rate reductions were 30/151 (19.9%) versus 25/140 (17.9%), risk difference `+2.01` points (95% `-6.98 to +11.00`) all rows. In overlap hospitals they were 10/70 (14.3%) versus 15/110 (13.6%), standardized difference `+5.14` points; the pre-D-only adjusted estimate was `+8.07` points (95% `-3.58 to +19.72`, p=`0.175`). Hospital-specific rates ranged from evaluation 0/28 versus no-trial 1/16 in hospital 420 to 9/27 versus 9/60 in hospital 252; this is too unstable for a generalizable label meaning. Only 4 evaluation and 4 no-trial rows had both controlled-setting and sedation-rate reductions.

The temporal diagnostic is adverse to a simple post-label interpretation. Among rows with both windows, post-label broad setting reduction was 91/281 (32.4%) versus 84/226 (37.2%), RD `-4.78` points, while the pre-label trajectory was 113/281 (40.2%) versus 87/226 (38.5%), RD `+1.72` points. The difference-in-risk-differences was `-6.50` points; the overlap pre-D-adjusted score was `-14.06` points (95% `-31.95 to +3.83`, p=`0.123`). This is not evidence of causality or a valid counterfactual, but it does not show a post-label excess beyond pre-existing trajectory.

Landmark estimates are directionally inconsistent in the controlled primary composite: overlap RDs were approximately `-22.5` points at 1440, `-11.8` at 2880, `-7.2` at 4320, `-27.8` at 5760, and `+12.4` at 7200, all with wide uncertainty. This repeated-landmark pattern is incompatible with claiming a stable 12-hour operational effect. Zero spontaneous-mode/SBT-method positives and zero direct nurse-charted interruption outcomes remain adverse to those stronger action definitions, but are also potentially field-sparsity limitations.

## Prespecified interpretation thresholds

A result would support the narrow label-to-documented-action claim only if the controlled-setting and sedation estimates each had a directionally positive point estimate, the overlap-hospital standardized estimate and every landmark estimate did not reverse, the lower uncertainty bound exceeded 5 percentage points for at least the primary controlled-setting outcome, ascertainment was at least 80% and differed by no more than 10 points, and the post-label contrast exceeded the pre-label trajectory in the hypothesized direction. This threshold is intentionally substantive rather than a p-value rule.

Adverse evidence is a point estimate at or below zero for the broad or controlled composite with a clinically important adverse lower direction, a positive broad result explained by peak-pressure dominance, reversal across overlap hospitals or landmarks, or a post-label contrast no larger than the pre-label trajectory. The completed data meet several adverse criteria: broad RD `-5.18` points; overlap broad standardized RD `-10.70`; peak pressure accounts for 71/91 evaluation and 73/85 no-trial broad events; and landmark directions reverse.

Inconclusive evidence is an interval spanning both a clinically meaningful positive and negative effect, sparse paired infusion data, differential ascertainment, or a temporal diagnostic unable to distinguish routine chart timing from label-linked action. Most component estimates and sedation results are inconclusive rather than proof of no action. The appropriate overall conclusion is that the label's independently charted 12-hour operational meaning is not demonstrated in this snapshot, not that the label is clinically useless or that no action occurred.

## Falsification, limitations, and prohibited conclusions

The computation can check exact label/cohort reconstruction, fixed D and windows, source-row availability, numeric paired deltas, missingness, component dominance, hospital/landmark direction, repeated-landmark structure, and pre/post trajectory diagnostics. It cannot establish actual bedside delivery, clinical intent, a sedation interruption, an SBT, extubation, readiness, durable liberation, post-ICU reintubation, patient benefit, or causality. Those require expert adjudication, richer ventilator/device records, and/or external prospective validation.

No durable liberation endpoint is re-estimated. No causal treatment-effect, SBT, extubation, readiness, bedside-action, or clinical-benefit conclusion is authorized. In particular, `Peak Insp. Pressure` reductions must not be called ventilator weaning, and infusion-rate reductions must not be called sedation vacations. Missing post-D observations remain unknown. The parent’s durable-liberation failure is retained as a hard boundary rather than repaired by this process analysis.
