# Is late laboratory availability patient-linked after nursing and respiratory workflow adjustment?

Status: Episode-43 targeted successor to [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test-ordering study, hospital-quality ranking, treatment recommendation, or evidence that changing surveillance improves survival. No model has been fitted and no clinical result is claimed.

## Unresolved question and advance

The parent establishes a revision-safe comparison of lab result-time and conservative information-availability timing on identical rows. The remaining ambiguity is clinically important: late lab availability may track a patient's evolving condition, or may be only a fingerprint of generally busy nursing/respiratory documentation. A second ambiguity is transport: identical lab counts or late bins can mean different things in hospitals with different surveillance regimes.

Strongest supported evidence is source/schema availability and an outcome-blind parent feasibility audit. The frozen eICU snapshot contains lab result/revision offsets, nursing and respiratory event/entry offsets, vital observations, sparse structured end-of-life documentation and recorded ICU disposition. The parent audit supports 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals and 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes. These are feasibility counts, not fitted results. The data do not establish specimen collection/order/initial-posting time, clinician intent, true physiology, complete goals-of-care documentation or unrecorded mortality.

Primary falsifiable claim: among adults alive and still in ICU at minute 360, conservative revision-safe late laboratory information availability adds patient-linked information for subsequent recorded ICU death after adjustment for general nursing/respiratory documentation workflow, and the increment is stable across supported, outcome-free hospital surveillance regimes.

This advances the parent through workflow-adjusted, endpoint-specific transport testing. Positive evidence supports a retrospective silent-mode information-timing review only; it does not support ordering more tests.

## Hypotheses and estimands

H1: adding six-bin revision-safe laboratory availability features to a workflow-adjusted baseline improves held-out-hospital prediction of recorded ICU death in (360,1800] without material death-calibration harm. The corresponding alive-discharge increment is smaller or non-positive.

H1-clock: on the same retained rows, availability-time and result-time increments are comparable as recorded timing associations, and both attenuate after within-stay temporal-order or patient-link destruction.

H1-transport: the lab increment is directionally consistent across low, middle and high outcome-free hospital surveillance regimes, with regime heterogeneity and uncertainty reported.

Nulls/falsifiers: lab increment disappears after workflow adjustment; it is as large for discharge as death; matched workflow timing performs as well or better; it survives patient-link/order permutation; exists only with post-360 revisions; is confined to one interface, hospital or regime; or has unstable calibration. A high-capacity model winning alone is not supportive.

## Matched models and transport

B0W is pooled elastic-net cause-specific logistic hazards using admission context, landmark lab state, aggregate revision-safe lab process, vital state/process, carePlanEOL channels and aggregate nursing/respiratory workflow. B_availW adds six-bin lab availability timing. B_resultW uses the identical retained rows and bins result time. W_time adds six-bin workflow availability timing without lab timing. The primary lab increment is B_availW minus B0W; matched workflow increment is W_time minus B0W.

T_W is a small one-layer GRU over six chronological bins containing exactly B_availW input blocks, with separate death/discharge heads and no hospital ID. Compare parameter-matched non-temporal MLP and within-stay order shuffle; T_result is paired on same rows/dimensions. Fit only after transparent models/specification are frozen, with development-hospital early stopping/tuning. This alternative can expose temporal persistence, bursts and lab/workflow co-movement lost by additive B0W. A GRU does not establish biology or causality.

Five outer folds hold out approximately 20% of hospitals; all uniquepid stays stay together. Development uses 75/25 hospital fit/validation. Codebooks, category lists, scaling, imputation, feature selection, calibration and thresholds are learned only in fit hospitals.

Construct surveillance regime from development-hospital medians of per-eligible-stay counts of distinct conservative-availability times in lab, vitalPeriodic, nurseCharting and respiratoryCharting during [0,360]. Set low/middle/high tertiles using development hospitals only; assign held-out hospitals without outcomes or test-patient features. Regime labels are not patient predictors. Report macro-hospital lab increment, calibration/Brier heterogeneity and regime-by-model interaction; regime claim is inconclusive if support/distance extrapolation is large. Repeat regime construction without lab process as circularity sensitivity.

## Outcomes, uncertainty and support gates

Primary metrics: macro-hospital mean held-out death-specific integrated competing-risk Brier difference and calibration at 600, 1200 and 1800 minutes. Report alive-discharge CIF/calibration, patient-weighted summaries and fixed-burden review diagnostics at thresholds 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10 (reviews/100, death capture, PPV, false-review burden). Threshold cell needs >=200 episodes, >=20 deaths, >=20 alive discharges and >=20 hospitals; otherwise unsupported and not a clinical utility claim.

Carry parent gates: >=100 eligible hospitals, >=50 event-bearing, >=1,000 deaths and discharges, about 20 hospitals/fold where feasible, >=30% coverage each retained vital field or >=20% with >=2 observations, overlapping lab state/process in >=80% held-out hospitals. Add >=80% eligible stays with at least one nurse/respiratory event, >=40% source-specific coverage, dual-source lab/workflow support in >=80% held-out hospitals, non-sparse early/late bins, and >=20 hospitals plus >=1,000 episodes per regime. Failure makes only that claim inconclusive.

Use >=1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples for 95% intervals when stable, otherwise a prespecified hierarchical fold/hospital model with reason. Report macro-hospital primary uncertainty, patient-weighted secondary, fold/regime support and heterogeneity.

## Falsification and interpretation

Save before interpretation:
1. Within-stay lab-bin permutation preserving counts, analyte state, revisions and missingness.
2. Within-hospital/baseline-risk lab-history reassignment breaking patient linkage.
3. Equivalent nursing/respiratory permutations and matched W_time comparison.
4. Death-CIF versus discharge-CIF increment.
5. Strict revision-safe, common-analyte, revision-known-only and interface-stratified analyses.
6. Mask late (300,360] and separately early bins.
7. B_availW versus B_resultW on identical rows; post-360-revision results are leakage-only.
8. T order shuffle and parameter-matched MLP.
9. Separate nurseAssessment/nurseCare sensitivity, absent documentation unknown.
10. Check no post-360 workflow entry, narrative payload, APACHE mortality/APS, hospital ID, target-fold preprocessing or outcome-dependent codebook.
11. Rebuild surveillance regimes without lab process.

Supportive: all applicable gates; positive adjusted lab increment with uncertainty compatible with meaningful improvement and no material death-calibration harm; smaller/non-positive discharge increment; attenuation after temporal-order/patient-link permutation; no comparable workflow increment; stability across revision/interface/common-analyte restrictions and more than one supported regime.

Adverse: null/adverse adjusted lab increment, discharge increment as large/larger, workflow increment as large/larger, persistence under permutations, post-360-revision dependence, one interface/hospital/regime concentration, or reversal under conservative revision handling. These reject patient-linked timing interpretation despite high discrimination.

Inconclusive: failed event/overlap/workflow/regime gates, interval spanning zero and meaningful effect, unstable folds, excessive invalid/missing entry times, absent dual-source support, non-reproducible codebooks, or timestamp ambiguity that cannot separate posting from clinical action.

## Scientific deliverable, compute and limitations

The deliverable is newly fitted: cohort/source/schema audit; outcome-blind revision and workflow lag audits; fold-specific codebooks and regime manifest; reproducible feature partitions; B0W/B_availW/B_resultW/W_time/T_W/control fits; held-out competing-risk predictions; calibration/Brier/review-burden and hospital-cluster uncertainty; all clock/regime/permutation/leakage outputs; and an interpretation trace linking every conclusion to output files, folds, metrics and intervals.

The future solver envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, 8 GPUs and 28800 seconds. Streaming extraction, elastic-net and evaluation use CPU. One allocated A100 with explicit cuda:0 is optional for GRU/TCN; end-to-end convergence/runtime are unverified. Discovery used a bounded 4-CPU/16-GiB streaming workflow audit and did not fit a model; its final status/output must be recorded before compilation.

The data provide recorded charting/results/offsets, not verified orders, specimens, initial posting, true physiology, intent, complete EOL or complete mortality. Clinical adjudication/source audit with order/collection/result timestamps and prospective silent-mode or impact study is required for biology, action or benefit. External health-system data are required for transport beyond this eICU snapshot. No hospital ranking is allowed.
