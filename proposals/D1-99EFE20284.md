# Hypothesis: Discordant Treatment Response Predicts ICU Outcomes Beyond Aggregate Severity

## Scientific Question

After initiating critical care interventions, patients whose organ systems respond discordantly to treatment escalation are at higher risk of adverse outcomes, independent of aggregate severity scores.

This hypothesis refines and extends the existing "discordant organ recovery" concept ([prior hypothesis]) by shifting focus from *natural recovery trajectories* to *treatment-induced discordant responses*. The key distinction:
- **Organ Recovery Discordance**: Natural physiological recovery trajectories diverging >1.5 SD between systems
- **Treatment Response Discordance**: Discordant organ-level responses to specific medical interventions

## Rationale and Unresolved Question

Existing research on discordant organ recovery (MIMIC-IV based) has established that trajectory divergence predicts outcomes. However, three important questions remain unresolved:

1. **Does treatment-induced discordance predict outcomes better than baseline discordance?**
   - Organ recovery trajectories are endogenous and may reflect underlying disease biology
   - Treatment responses are exogenous and may capture therapeutic efficacy or resistance
   - Discordant treatment responses may be more actionable for clinical decision-making

2. **Which organ pairs show the most clinically relevant discordance?**
   - Respiratory-Kidney has been studied extensively
   - Cardiovascular-Neurological, Gastrointestinal-Respiratory, and other pairs remain understudied
   - Discordance may be context-dependent (sepsis vs. non-sepsis, mechanical ventilation vs. non-ventilated)

3. **Can treatment response discordance guide de-escalation decisions?**
   - If multiple organ systems respond discordantly to treatment, does this signal suboptimal therapy?
   - Can discordant response patterns predict which patients will fail to improve despite aggressive therapy?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Actionable for treatment optimization**: Unlike passive recovery trajectories, treatment responses can inform whether to escalate, maintain, or de-escalate therapy
2. **Early warning system**: Discordant treatment responses may emerge before aggregate severity scores worsen
3. **Resource allocation**: Patients with discordant treatment responses may benefit from intensified monitoring or alternative therapies
4. **Understanding treatment resistance**: Discordance may reflect heterogeneous treatment effects within patient populations

## Operational Definition

### Discordant Treatment Response (DTR)

A patient exhibits discordant treatment response when:

1. **Stable baseline**: Organ function scores (e.g., SOFA components, APACHE II organ scores) are within 1 SD of each other for ≥24 hours before treatment escalation

2. **Treatment initiation**: At least one intervention is initiated (vasopressors, vasoprotective drugs, antibiotics, sedation, etc.)

3. **Discordant response**: Within 48 hours of treatment:
   - At least 2 organ systems show divergent responses
   - Divergence measured as absolute difference in standardized response >1.5 SD
   - Response = post-treatment score - pre-treatment score (standardized to baseline SD)

4. **Organ pairs to consider**:
   - Respiratory (PaO2/FiO2 or SOFA respiratory) vs. Kidney (Creatinine or SOFA renal)
   - Cardiovascular (MAP or SOFA cardiovascular) vs. Neurological (GCS or sedation depth)
   - Gastrointestinal (BISAP GI or stool output) vs. Respiratory

### Primary Outcome

**90-day mortality** (adjusted OR >1.2, 95% CI excludes 1.0)

### Secondary Outcomes

- ICU length of stay (mean difference >2 days, p<0.05)
- Need for mechanical ventilation
- Dose of vasopressors required
- ICU readmission within 30 days

### Covariates to Adjust For

- Baseline severity (SOFA, APACHE II, qSOFA at admission)
- Age, sex, comorbidities
- Admission diagnosis (sepsis vs. non-sepsis)
- Prior ICU stays
- Socioeconomic factors (if available)

### Temporal Analysis Strategy

1. **Baseline period**: 24-48 hours before treatment escalation
   - Calculate organ function scores (SOFA components)
   - Ensure stability (within 1 SD of each other)

2. **Intervention window**: Time of treatment initiation
   - Identify specific interventions (vasopressors, vasoprotective drugs, etc.)

3. **Response window**: 0-48 hours post-intervention
   - Calculate post-intervention organ function scores
   - Compute discordance metric

## Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis

**Logistic regression** with:
- Outcome: 90-day mortality (yes/no)
- Exposure: DTR (yes/no)
- Covariates: Age, sex, comorbidities, baseline severity, diagnosis type

**Effect estimate**: Adjusted OR for DTR on mortality

### Secondary Analyses

1. **Different organ pairs**: Compare DTR effect by organ system pair
2. **Temporal trends**: Does DTR predict outcomes better than baseline recovery discordance?
3. **Subgroup analyses**: By sepsis status, admission diagnosis, treatment type
4. **Dose-response**: Is higher discordance associated with worse outcomes?

### Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
- Adjusted OR for DTR ≤ 1.0 (no association or protective effect)
- 95% CI includes 1.0
- Discordance metric shows no gradient association with outcomes
- Results consistent across different organ pairs

## Baseline and Alternative Models

### Baseline Model: Aggregate Severity

Traditional models use aggregate severity scores (SOFA, APACHE II) as primary predictors.

**Information lost by baseline**:
- Cannot distinguish whether poor outcomes are due to severe baseline disease vs. discordant treatment response
- Cannot identify which organ systems are driving outcomes
- Cannot inform treatment optimization

### Alternative Model 1: Machine Learning (XGBoost)

- **Goal**: Compare supervised learning with aggregate vs. discordance features
- **Features**: SOFA scores, individual organ scores, DTR metric, treatment timing
- **Evaluation**: AUC-ROC, calibration, decision curve analysis
- **Scientific question**: Does incorporating discordance improve predictive performance beyond aggregate severity?

### Alternative Model 2: Latent Trajectory Model

- **Goal**: Learn individual patient organ trajectory patterns
- **Method**: Gaussian process or hierarchical model with organ-specific random effects
- **Evaluation**: Model fit (R²), prediction accuracy, interpretability of latent factors
- **Scientific question**: Can latent trajectory patterns capture more than simple discordance metrics?

### Selection Rationale

1. **Baseline (aggregate severity)**: Necessary to establish that DTR adds value
2. **ML model**: Tests whether DTR is a useful feature in modern predictive frameworks
3. **Latent trajectory**: Tests whether DTR captures a fundamental biological process

All models will be evaluated on the same training/validation split to ensure fair comparison.
