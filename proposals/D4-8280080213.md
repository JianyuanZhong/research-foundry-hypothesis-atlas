MATCHED CONTROL EXPERIMENT AND RESULTS
For each control, the matched sample requires finite positive baseline and repeat control values in addition to frozen renal eligibility. The renal P1/P2 models are rerun on that identical matched sample. Each renal residual exposure is then entered into a control follow-up model with the same final-model structure: natural-log control outcome, complete matched-cohort outcome sample-SD scaling, RCS baseline control and elapsed time, age, sex, age-by-sex, and fold indicators. Nuisance fits remain renal and direction-specific. Each matched sample uses 1,000 paired participant-bootstrap replicates, integer multinomial multiplicities, fixed original folds/final knots, and full nuisance and outcome-scale recomputation. Both controls achieved 1,000 successes in 1,000 attempts with no failures.

Direct LDL matched n=16,467. P1 matched renal beta was 0.086862, LDL beta -0.044132, and renal-minus-LDL 0.130994 (bootstrap SD 0.008260; raw centered two-sided p=0.000999). P2 matched renal beta was 0.037416, LDL beta 0.022744, and contrast 0.014671 (SD 0.008014; raw p=0.058941).

Vitamin D matched n=15,357. P1 matched renal beta was 0.086573, vitamin-D beta -0.046703, and renal-minus-vitamin-D 0.133275 (SD 0.008768; raw p=0.000999). P2 matched renal beta was 0.038541, vitamin-D beta 0.030709, and contrast 0.007833 (SD 0.009282; raw p=0.404595).

Four-test Holm-adjusted p-values are P1-LDL 0.003996, P1-vitamin-D 0.003996, P2-LDL 0.117882, and P2-vitamin-D 0.404595. A conservative simultaneous 95% interval family across all four contrasts uses each control's centered studentized bootstrap distribution at the marginal 98.75th percentile (Bonferroni alpha 0.05/4), avoiding any invented dependence coupling between different matched samples. Intervals are: P1-LDL [0.109874, 0.152113]; P2-LDL [-0.005448, 0.034791]; P1-vitamin-D [0.111174, 0.155377]; P2-vitamin-D [-0.014942, 0.030607]. Thus P1 discriminates from both controls, whereas both P2 intervals include zero. Within-control max-statistic intervals are also reported but are not mislabeled as four-family simultaneous intervals.

This asymmetry is adverse to bidirectional renal specificity. Similar P2 control associations weaken a renal-specific interpretation, but do not prove laboratory artifact: LDL and vitamin D may share selection, treatment, seasonal, health, and assay pathways. Control-marker suitability itself requires clinical/laboratory expert review.

TEMPORAL AND PERMUTATION DIAGNOSTICS
Regressing standardized elapsed years on each cross-fitted residual exposure with baseline-date RCS, age, sex, and age-by-sex adjustment gives P1 beta 0.007398 elapsed-time SD per residual SD (HC3 SE 0.002166; 95% CI [0.003153, 0.011642]) and P2 -0.000497 (SE 0.002183; CI [-0.004776, 0.003781]). P1 therefore retains small detectable temporal structure and this caveat cannot be discarded; P2 does not.

In 1,000 within-stratum residual-exposure permutations, stratified by frozen fold, sex, recruitment-age decile, and baseline outcome-marker decile, observed P1 0.087062 lay beyond a null distribution with mean 0.012509, SD 0.004472, and 2.5–97.5% interval [0.003368, 0.020951]; observed P2 0.037159 lay beyond mean 0.006251, SD 0.004252, interval [-0.002037, 0.014914]. Both empirical p-values were 0.000999. There were 1,858/1,977 strata and 191/58 singleton strata for P1/P2. The nonzero null means, particularly P1, show this is not an exact zero-centered randomization test: conditioning is coarse and singleton/small strata preserve residual structure. It supports incompatibility with this specific stratified-shuffle null, not a causal or universal-null claim.

REPEAT-ATTENDANCE SENSITIVITY
The baseline pool consists of 468,887 participants with parseable baseline candidate date, valid baseline renal markers, sex, and age. The event is membership in the frozen 16,546-person repeat cohort, prevalence 0.035288. Ten-fold cross-fitted logistic models use baseline-only RCS log creatinine, RCS log cystatin-C, their centered product, age, sex, age-by-sex, RCS baseline calendar day, and cyclic day-of-year sine/cosine. Field 54 is omitted because its semantics are uncertified and it has no usable same-center sensitivity in this cohort. All ten 19-column fits were full rank and converged in six iterations.

Observed attendance by P1 discordance decile declined from 4.24% to 2.26%. Individual predicted probabilities ranged 0.000302–0.180954 in the baseline pool and 0.004823–0.162623 among attenders. Therefore an individual-level minimum-attender threshold of 0.005 would fail narrowly; no decile summary is substituted for that threshold. Stabilized inverse-attendance weights among attenders ranged 0.217–7.317 before truncation and were truncated at linear empirical 1st/99th percentiles 0.2983/2.3681 (166 at each tail). ESS was 13,662.5, 82.6% of the repeat cohort.

Descriptive standardized mean differences versus the baseline pool improved after weighting for log creatinine 0.0319 to 0.0044, log cystatin-C -0.1043 to -0.0029, centered product -0.0369 to -0.0044, age 0.0883 to 0.0218, sex 0.0896 to 0.0105, and baseline calendar day -0.3732 to -0.0006. Cyclic sine improved -0.0499 to 0.0072; cosine changed -0.0137 to 0.0587, still modest. Cross-fitted calibration deciles are reported in aggregate.

With truncated weights, fully rerunning weighted renal nuisance residualization and outcome scaling gives point estimates P1 0.079200 and P2 0.038219 versus unweighted 0.087062 and 0.037159. Neither reverses sign. This supports point-level robustness only under measured-baseline conditional exchangeability and the fitted model. A valid full uncertainty analysis must resample the baseline pool and rerun all ten attendance models, truncation, renal nuisance fits, and outcome standardization. That computationally intensive bootstrap was not run; treating weights as fixed would understate uncertainty. Unmeasured or MNAR repeat outcomes remain unidentified.
