# Cross-fitted adjusted incremental inference for repeat-attender CBC trajectories

## Parent, change, and clinical question

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent's frozen 998-person selected-case population, source snapshot, dates, CBC fields, cancer pairing, diagnosis-lag definition, and recorded-case outcome. It changes the inferential procedure because the parent's lag-by-sex CRC-label permutation is exchangeable only under a stronger random-label null and does not isolate the incremental contribution of slopes conditional on all comparator variables.

The falsifiable question is: among UK Biobank repeat attenders aged 40–75 whose first qualifying recorded malignant diagnosis occurs during the prespecified follow-up interval, is there a nonzero adjusted linear score association between the three adverse-oriented CBC trajectories and recorded CRC versus recorded non-CRC malignancy after conditioning on age, sex, repeat-visit CBC levels, and diagnosis-lag stratum?

The primary target is deliberately narrower than the parent's informal “adds information” language. Let `Y=1` for CRC and `Y=0` for another qualifying malignancy, `C` be the comparator vector, and `S` be the three adverse-oriented annual CBC slopes. The primary null is the joint residual-moment null

`E[(Y - m(C))(S - g(C))] = 0`,

where `m(C)=E[Y|C]` and `g(C)=E[S|C]` under the prespecified nuisance models. This is a conditional score/association target, not a claim of conditional independence for every nonlinear function of `S`, a causal effect, prospective cancer risk, or clinical utility. A positive result would warrant a separately designed validation study, not deployment.

## Prespecified variables and adjusted score

`C` contains exactly: age at `L`, sex, instance-1 haemoglobin, instance-1 MCV, instance-1 platelet count, and the late-diagnosis indicator. `S` contains exactly the three annualized slopes above. Do not add symptoms, medications, diagnoses, stage, treatment, other CBC fields, or any post-landmark variable.

Use repeated five-fold cross-fitting with folds jointly stratified by `Y` and lag category and held fixed across all score calculations. To keep the finite-sample procedure auditable, use 20 prespecified fold repetitions generated from a documented seed list; no fold is allowed to contain zero CRC cases or zero non-CRC cases. Within each training portion, standardize continuous `C` and each component of `S` using training means and scales only; leave binary sex and the late indicator on their defined scales.

For each held-out fold, estimate:

1. `m(C)`: logistic ridge regression of `Y` on the comparator vector, with an intercept and the late indicator unpenalized, and penalty selected only inside the training portion by five-fold training loss from the parent's grid `10^-4,...,10^4`.
2. `g_j(C)`, `j=1,2,3`: separate ridge linear regressions of each standardized slope on the comparator vector, with penalty selected only inside the training portion by five-fold squared error over the same grid. If a training fit is nonfinite or a selected penalty is undefined, that repetition fails rather than being repaired by looking at held-out data.

For held-out person `i` in fold `k`, calculate `u_i=Y_i-m_hat^{-k}(C_i)` and `v_i=S_i-g_hat^{-k}(C_i)`, and define the three-dimensional score contribution `psi_i=u_i v_i`. Do not permute residuals: cross-fitting removes training/held-out reuse but does not make residuals exchangeable. The primary statistic is the global quadratic Wald statistic

`T = n * mean(psi)' * V_hat^{-1} * mean(psi)`,

where `V_hat` is the heteroskedasticity-robust covariance of `psi_i` with a small ridge only if needed for numerical inversion, declared before looking at results. Report the three component estimates, a global 95% confidence region, and the global test before any component-level result. Component p-values, if reported, use a prespecified multiplicity adjustment and are secondary.

Because this is a 75-event selected-case contrast, use a fold-respecting multiplier bootstrap for the global null calibration: independently draw mean-zero unit-variance multipliers at the participant level, recompute the centered score statistic from the fixed cross-fitted `psi_i`, and use a plus-one tail proportion over at least 2,000 replicates. This is a calibration of the cross-fitted score, not an exact randomization test. In parallel, fit the ordinary low-dimensional logistic model with `C` and `S` and report its slope-block score or likelihood-ratio benchmark with a prespecified parametric/bootstrap calibration. Agreement in sign and global conclusion across the orthogonal score and benchmark is required for a robust result; disagreement is inconclusive, not a reason to select the favorable method.

## Audits and falsification gates

The analysis is invalid if any of these occur: a source hash changes; a participant is duplicated across joins or folds; a cancer date is paired across suffixes; day `E` is assigned to washout; a post-death cancer qualifies; noncases enter the selected-case analysis; a CBC value, lag, scaler, fold, penalty, or outcome is learned using held-out rows; fold stratification lacks both outcome classes; nuisance predictions are nonfinite or outside `[0,1]`; or the observed and bootstrap statistics use different score definitions.

Before interpreting the score, report for every repetition: convergence, selected penalties, fold CRC counts, comparator cross-fitted log loss and calibration, slope-nuisance prediction error, residual covariance conditioning, and the proportion of finite score contributions. Require the primary nuisance models to beat their intercept-only training baselines only descriptively; failure to predict a nuisance well does not become evidence for the scientific null, but severe instability or lack of overlap triggers an inconclusive result. Require sensitivity to the low-dimensional benchmark and to a prespecified alternative nuisance specification (unpenalized logistic/linear models with the same variables, where numerically possible). A result is not supportive if it appears only under one unstable nuisance choice.

The primary falsification is a negative-control score using a pre-specified permutation of the three slope columns among participants within the same lag-by-sex strata while leaving `Y` and `C` fixed. This tests implementation/calibration behavior only and is not a scientific null for the real slopes. Its global statistic should follow the declared bootstrap calibration and should not repeatedly produce extreme evidence. A second falsification is replacing `S` by the RDW trajectory from `30070-0.0/1.0`, which must be labeled exploratory because RDW was not part of the frozen hypothesis; it cannot be used to rescue a failed primary analysis.

The method is considered computationally checkable only if all gates pass, the multiplier calibration is finite, the parametric benchmark runs, and the conclusion is stable across the declared repetitions and nuisance sensitivity. No automatic check establishes the exchangeability of the selected cases, correctness of cancer diagnosis, registry completeness, biological mechanism, transportability, or clinical benefit.

## Interpretation and decision rule

Supportive evidence requires a two-sided global result that rejects the joint null after the declared calibration, a coherent direction for the prespecified adverse trajectory block, concordance with the parametric benchmark and nuisance sensitivity, no audit failure, and an effect estimate sufficiently precise to distinguish a clinically meaningful selected-case association from tiny noise. Even then, it supports only an adjusted association within the 75/923 recorded-case contrast and motivates external validation; it does not establish prospective CRC risk or utility.

Adverse evidence is a precise estimate near zero or in a direction inconsistent with the prespecified block, accompanied by stable calibration and concordant null behavior in the benchmark. It would weaken the claim that these CBC slopes carry a reproducible adjusted signal in this selected-case setting, but would not prove absence in an unselected population.

Inconclusive evidence includes broad confidence regions, fewer than the required fold-event support, nuisance or covariance instability, calibration failure, disagreement between score and benchmark, failed null calibration, or a result that depends on an alternative specification. Given only 75 CRC cases, collider selection on future recorded malignancy, post-landmark lag adjustment, and the parent's poor absolute recorded-case calibration, inconclusive is a likely and scientifically acceptable outcome.

The parent’s completed random-label analysis remains a separate stronger-null falsification guard (`p=0.211788`) and is not silently reinterpreted as this adjusted test. This child addresses the exchangeability flaw by conditioning the score on the full comparator through cross-fitted nuisance functions; it does not claim an exact conditional-randomization p-value.

## Evidence limits and substantive advance

The analysis remains conditional on a future recorded malignancy and is therefore vulnerable to collider selection. Diagnosis lag is a post-landmark nuisance adjustment and does not make the design deployable. The data do not provide certified release-matched registry completeness, pathology adjudication, stage, symptoms, FIT, endoscopy, treatment, routine-care CBC timing, harms, costs, or outcomes in participants without recorded malignancy. Accordingly, the experiment cannot estimate incidence, screening performance, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, lead time, referral value, causal effects, or patient benefit. A population-level or endpoint-ascertainment-safe conclusion requires linkage documentation and an independent cohort with protocolized repeat CBC and adjudicated outcomes.

The substantive advance is a clearly labeled, orthogonal adjusted score target that avoids the parent’s invalid shortcut of permuting CRC labels while destroying age/current-CBC associations. It supplies a reproducible nuisance strategy, participant-level cross-fitting, robust uncertainty, a finite-sample calibration attempt, an ordinary model benchmark, and explicit stop rules. What remains uncertain is whether the residual-moment target is informative enough in this sparse selected population and whether its finite-sample calibration is reliable; that uncertainty is resolved only by the declared audits and, for clinical meaning, by a new externally adjudicated study.
