# Plasma Lp(a) as a modifier of aspirin's benefit-harm balance in primary prevention

## Clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

**Unresolved claim to test.** Among UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 years who are free of clinically recorded atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) and major bleeding before their baseline assessment, does regular aspirin use at baseline have a more favorable 10-year vascular-benefit/major-bleeding profile when plasma lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] is elevated (>=125 nmol/L) than when it is not?

The primary hypothesis is an effect-modification hypothesis on the absolute-risk scale: after prespecified adjustment and overlap restriction, the 10-year aspirin-versus-no-aspirin risk difference (RD) for major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) will be at least 1.0 percentage point more favorable in participants with Lp(a) >=125 nmol/L than in those with Lp(a) <125 nmol/L, while the upper 95% confidence limit for aspirin's 10-year major-bleeding RD in the elevated-Lp(a) stratum is below +0.5 percentage points. This is falsifiable and separates benefit from harm rather than hiding an unvalidated clinical utility weight in a composite.

**Strongest claim already supported.** Prior evidence supports that elevated Lp(a) marks higher atherothrombotic risk and that aspirin may be associated with lower coronary risk in this subgroup. In MESA, an observational propensity-matched analysis reported aspirin-associated lower CHD risk among participants with Lp(a) >50 mg/dL (HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.32–0.94), but explicitly required randomized confirmation (Bhatia et al., JAHA 2024, DOI 10.1161/JAHA.123.033562; frozen full-text source `[source checksum]`, acquired 2026-09-08). A secondary randomized ASPREE analysis found suggestive aspirin benefit in older people selected by LPA genotype, but plasma Lp(a) was not the selection marker (Lacaze et al., JACC 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2022.07.027; bibliographic/abstract evidence frozen as Europe PMC source `[source checksum]`). These results do not establish that a plasma-Lp(a)-guided aspirin strategy has net clinical benefit.

**Substantive advance.** This experiment tests external transport to a large middle-aged cohort, estimates absolute rather than only relative effects, and jointly evaluates vascular benefit and serious bleeding using a measured plasma marker and a prespecified clinically interpretable interaction margin. It could prioritize—or deprioritize—a definitive biomarker-stratified aspirin trial. It cannot itself recommend aspirin.

## Analysis

1. Apply the catalog's participant hash policy. The reserved buckets 80–99 are inaccessible and must not be claimed as external validation. Use discovery buckets 0–59 for nuisance/model specification and buckets 60–79 as a locked internal evaluation set; hash `sha256(namespace + NUL + "ukb" + NUL + eid)` modulo 100. Pool estimates only after all choices are frozen and show partition interaction.
2. Emit auditable long-form paired event tables and reject duplicate `eid`, unpaired array slots, impossible dates, post-cutoff dates, and overlapping copies that disagree. Report every exclusion, aspirin-code prevalence, Lp(a) distribution, outcome counts, follow-up, overlap, and missingness by exposure and Lp(a) stratum.
3. Prespecify confounders measured at baseline: age, sex, ethnicity, smoking, averaged SBP/DBP, BMI, diabetes, total/HDL/LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, kidney function, CRP, antihypertensive and lipid-lowering medication indicators, family history if coding is validated, and assessment year/month. Do not adjust for post-baseline measures.
4. Estimate propensity for aspirin with gradient-boosted trees and outcome regressions with flexible survival learners in discovery data; truncate stabilized weights at the 1st/99th percentiles. Primary estimator is cross-fitted augmented inverse-probability weighted pseudo-observation regression for 10-year cumulative-incidence RDs with death as a competing event. Include aspirin, continuous Lp(a) spline, and their interaction. Cluster bootstrap 1,000 times by `eid` for 95% CIs. A conventional weighted cause-specific Cox interaction is secondary.
5. Require positivity: standardized differences <0.10 after weighting within each Lp(a) stratum; effective sample size >=50% of unweighted aspirin users and nonusers; no propensity outside common support [0.05,0.95] in >10% of either group. If failed, restrict the target population to empirical overlap and label the changed estimand; if still failed, primary result is inconclusive.
6. Robustness: high-dimensional propensity score from pre-baseline ICD-10 code groups; quantitative E-value/bias analysis; alternative Lp(a) thresholds 75 and 150 nmol/L; complete-case versus multiple imputation (20 chained imputations, exposure/outcomes never imputed); exclude events in first 1 and 2 years; negative-control outcome of traumatic fractures/accidents from a clinician-locked ICD-10 set; and repeat the primary analysis among participants with no baseline antiplatelet/anticoagulant codes. A detected protective association with the negative control of similar magnitude is evidence against causal interpretation.
7. Multiplicity: the absolute-scale interaction for 10-year MACE is the single primary efficacy contrast; major bleeding is a required safety gate. Other analyses are descriptive with false-discovery-rate control within outcome families.

## Decision and falsification rules

**Supportive:** point estimate for the high-minus-low Lp(a) aspirin MACE RD interaction <=−1.0 percentage point, its 95% upper limit <0, and the elevated-Lp(a) bleeding RD upper limit <+0.5 percentage points; overlap/balance pass; direction persists in the locked evaluation subset and landmark sensitivities; no comparable negative-control effect. This supports a replicated, clinically meaningful association and the rationale for a randomized plasma-Lp(a)-stratified trial—not prescribing aspirin.

**Adverse / hypothesis falsified:** the interaction point estimate is >−1.0 percentage point with a 95% lower limit >−1.0, aspirin's elevated-Lp(a) MACE RD is non-beneficial, or bleeding RD is >=+0.5 percentage points with lower 95% limit >=0; robust evidence of harm also falsifies a favorable benefit-harm profile. This would argue against prioritizing Lp(a)-guided aspirin absent contrary randomized data.

**Inconclusive:** confidence intervals cross either clinical margin, event counts are inadequate, overlap/balance fails, censoring differs materially, negative controls indicate residual confounding, medication coding is incomplete, or results reverse under prespecified lag/cutoff analyses. “Not significant” alone is not evidence of no effect.

## What computation can and cannot establish

The verifier can recompute joins, pair code/date arrays, validate temporal exclusions, cohort flow, exposure/modifier definitions, balance/positivity, RDs/CIs, sensitivity results, and ensure that any reported interpretation follows the rules above. It must test synthetic/supportive, adverse, and inconclusive outputs, including a deliberately correct computation paired with an unsupported causal or prescribing conclusion and reject that conclusion.

Automated QA cannot establish medication indication, dose/adherence, complete capture, clinical severity of bleeding, phenotype validity, exchangeability, transportability, or net patient preference. Clinician review must lock the ASCVD and major-bleeding code lists and assess overlap with antithrombotic indications before unblinding. A claim that Lp(a)-guided aspirin improves outcomes requires a randomized trial with adjudicated ischemic and bleeding outcomes; implementation requires external calibration, contemporary medication ascertainment, and patient-preference/cost evidence.
