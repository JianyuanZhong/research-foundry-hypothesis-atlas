## 1. Unresolved question and why it matters clinically

**Question.** Among ICU patients who survive to liberation from invasive mechanical ventilation, is hospital-level variation in the speed of liberation (time from first documented weaning attempt to last ventilator charting, and ventilator-free days to ICU discharge) independent of case mix (age, sex, APACHE score, admission diagnosis), and is the residual between-hospital variation associated with documented weaning-process variables (weaning strategy label, ventilatory-burden measures) rather than explained by severity alone?

**Why it matters.** Duration of mechanical ventilation is a standard ICU quality benchmark, but the drivers of between-hospital variation are incompletely understood. K2 (Msemakweli et al. 2026, eICU-CRD, 43,880 patients, 114 hospitals) showed that case mix explains only 3.7% of between-hospital variance in 7-day liberation, while hospital structure and measurable processes explain a further 31.6% — but K2 does not test whether specific weaning-process variables (strategy labels, ventilatory burden) explain residual variance. K3 (Burns et al. 2024, JAMA, 797 patients, 23 ICUs) showed that weaning-process details (screening frequency, SBT technique) can affect liberation timing in ways that depend on the specific process combination, but in a controlled RCT with a narrow variable set. If documented weaning-process variables explain residual hospital-level variation, this would identify modifiable process targets for quality improvement; if they do not, the residual variation is more likely due to unmeasured factors (staffing, bed management, organizational practice) and should not be attributed to weaning strategy alone.

**Rival explanations, with different observable predictions:**
- **R1 (severity/case mix).** Hospitals differ because they sicker; within-hospital adjustment removes the association. Prediction: hospital random-intercept variance is negligible after adjusting for age, sex, APACHE, admission dx; leave-one-hospital-out coefficients are unstable.
- **R2 (practice/process).** Residual hospital effects remain after adjustment; correlated with weaning strategy labels and ventilatory burden. Prediction: hospital random-intercept variance remains significant; weaning-process variables add significant explanatory power; leave-one-hospital-out coefficients are stable.
- **R3 (documentation artifact).** Weaning rows / respiratoryCharting completeness varies by hospital; the association is an artifact of differential documentation. Prediction: weaning-process variables are strongly correlated with documentation completeness (e.g., number of respiratoryCharting rows per stay); the association disappears when conditioning on documentation completeness.

## 3. Study design

**Population.** ICU stays in eICU-CRD 2.0 with:
1. ≥1 treatment row with `treatmentstring` containing "ventilator weaning" and valid `treatmentoffset`;
2. `hospitaldischargestatus` = "Alive";
3. ≥1 respiratoryCharting row with `respchartoffset` ≥ first weaning offset (ventilator-end proxy available);
4. APACHE row available (apachescore non-missing).

Expected n ≈ 4,200–5,000 stays across ~128 hospitals (measured: 4,225 with end proxy; 5,019 with APACHE; intersection to be computed by solver).

**Time origin.** ICU admission (offset 0). All offsets in minutes.

**Exposures (weaning-process variables):**
- `wean_strategy`: last weaning strategy label (active / slow / rapid / unlabeled), from `treatmentstring` suffix.
- `wean_count`: number of weaning rows per stay.
- `vent_burden_24h`: mean PEEP, max Plateau Pressure, mean FiO2 in the 24 h before first weaning (from respiratoryCharting rows with `respchartvaluelabel` in {PEEP, Plateau Pressure, FiO2} and `respchartoffset` in [first_wean − 1440, first_wean]).
- `documentation_completeness`: total respiratoryCharting rows per stay (R3 check).

**Covariates (case mix):**
- `age`, `gender` (patient)
- `apachescore` (apachePatientResult)
- `apacheadmissiondx` (patient, categorical)
- `hospitalid` (random intercept)

**Outcomes:**
- Primary: `wean_to_end_h` = (last respiratoryCharting offset − first weaning offset) / 60, in hours.
- Secondary: `vent_free_days` = (unitdischargeoffset − last respiratoryCharting offset) / 1440, in days.

**Analysis plan:**
- **Model M1 (baseline):** OLS (or Gamma GLM for positive skewed outcomes) with covariates only (age, gender, APACHE, admission dx). No hospital effect.
- **Model M2 (augmented):** M1 + hospital random intercept (GLMM, lme4 or statsmodels MixedLM).
- **Model M3 (process-augmented):** M2 + weaning-process variables (wean_strategy, wean_count, vent_burden_24h).
- **R1 vs R2 test:** Compare M1 vs M2: is hospital random-intercept variance significant (LRT or ICC)? If M2 ICC ≈ 0, R1 is supported. If M2 ICC > 0 and M3 reduces ICC, R2 is supported.
- **R3 test:** Add documentation_completeness to M3; if wean-process coefficients become non-significant after conditioning on documentation_completeness, R3 is supported.
- **Leave-one-hospital-out stability:** For each of the 128 hospitals, refit M3 excluding that hospital; report the distribution of wean_strategy and vent_burden coefficients. Stability (narrow coefficient distribution, consistent direction) supports R2; instability supports R1/R3.
- **GBM sensitivity:** Fit a gradient-boosted-tree model (sklearn or LightGBM, CPU) with the same features; compare feature importance and out-of-sample R² to the GLMM. GBM can capture nonlinearity and interactions that the GLMM cannot; if GBM R² is substantially higher and wean-process features are top-ranked, this supports R2 with a more flexible functional form.

**Falsification criteria (pre-specified):**
- **Supportive of R2 (practice/process):** M2 ICC > 0.05 (significant hospital variance); M3 wean_strategy or vent_burden coefficients significant (p < 0.05); leave-one-hospital-out coefficient distribution for at least one wean-process variable has >80% in the same direction; GBM feature importance ranks at least one wean-process variable in the top 5.
- **Supportive of R1 (severity):** M2 ICC < 0.01 or non-significant (LRT p > 0.10); M3 wean-process coefficients non-significant (p > 0.10); leave-one-hospital-out coefficients unstable (>40% flip direction).
- **Supportive of R3 (documentation artifact):** wean-process coefficients significant in M3 but non-significant after adding documentation_completeness (p > 0.10); documentation_completeness is the top-ranked GBM feature.
- **Inconclusive:** M2 ICC between 0.01 and 0.05; wean-process coefficients borderline (0.05 < p < 0.10); leave-one-hospital-out stability between 60–80%.

## 4. Method comparison and deferred alternatives

**Baseline (M1–M3 GLMM):** Hospital random-intercept GLMM is the appropriate primary method for hierarchical data with a continuous outcome; it directly tests R1 vs R2 via ICC and provides interpretable coefficients for wean-process variables.

**Learned alternative (GBM sensitivity):** A gradient-boosted-tree model on the same features can capture nonlinearity and higher-order interactions (e.g., wean_strategy × APACHE, vent_burden × documentation_completeness) that the GLMM cannot. The GBM is a sensitivity analysis, not a primary model: it tests whether the R2 signal is robust to a more flexible functional form. If GBM R² is substantially higher than GLMM R² and wean-process features are top-ranked, this strengthens the R2 case. If GBM R² is similar and wean-process features are not top-ranked, this weakens the R2 case.

**Deferred alternatives (with revisit criteria):**
- **Survival analysis (Cox / competing-risk):** The primary outcome is a continuous duration (wean-to-end hours), not a time-to-event with censoring. A survival analysis would be appropriate if the outcome were time-to-extubation with death as a competing event; K2 already addresses this at the 7-day horizon. Revisit if the solver finds that the continuous-duration framing is inappropriate (e.g., >30% of stays have missing end proxy).
- **Multilevel model with hospital-level covariates:** Could add hospital structure variables (bed count, teaching status) if available in eICU-CRD; not available in the current snapshot. Revisit if hospital-level covariates become available.
- **GPU-accelerated fitting:** Not needed; the dataset is ~5,000 rows × ~15 features, well within CPU capacity. The solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs / 256 GB RAM / 8 h; a GLMM + GBM + 128-fold leave-one-hospital-out fits comfortably within this on CPU.

## 6. What each possible result would and would not establish

- **Supportive of R2 (practice/process):** Residual hospital-level variation in liberation speed is associated with documented weaning-process variables. This would identify modifiable process targets for quality improvement. It would not establish causality (observational data; residual confounding by unmeasured hospital factors). It would not establish that changing weaning strategy improves outcomes (would require an RCT).
- **Supportive of R1 (severity):** Hospital-level variation in liberation speed is explained by case mix. This would argue against using liberation speed as a hospital quality benchmark without risk adjustment. It would not establish that all hospitals provide equivalent care (unmeasured factors may still vary).
- **Supportive of R3 (documentation artifact):** The apparent association between weaning-process variables and liberation speed is an artifact of differential documentation. This would argue for caution in using eICU-CRD treatment/respiratoryCharting fields as process measures. It would not establish that weaning-process variables are unimportant (they may be important but poorly documented in eICU-CRD).
- **Inconclusive:** The data cannot distinguish R1, R2, and R3. This would argue for a targeted study with more granular weaning-process data (e.g., SBT timing, extubation criteria) or a natural experiment.
