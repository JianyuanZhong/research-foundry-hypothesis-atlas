# Provisional timing-anchored eICU hypothesis

Among adults in an eICU ICU admission with at least one valid systolic BP <65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. Cumulative duration is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

The inherited evidence package [K1–K3] motivates the question and its limitations: time-weighted hypotension may predict AKI, AKI is heterogeneous and dynamic, and missingness can alter EHR prediction. It does not establish this eICU association or validate the local fields. The unresolved claim is whether duration adds reproducible information beyond nadir under explicit pre-exposure baseline and post-exposure outcome timing.

Exact eICU bindings are read-only and joined only by documented identifiers: `patient.csv.gz` (`patientunitstayid`, `patienthealthsystemstayid`, `uniquepid`, `hospitalid`, `age`, `gender`, `unitadmittime24`, `unitdischargeoffset`, `unitdischargestatus`); `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz` (`patientunitstayid`, `observationoffset`, `systemicsystolic`); `vitalAperiodic.csv.gz` (`patientunitstayid`, `observationoffset`, `noninvasivesystolic`); `lab.csv.gz` (`patientunitstayid`, `labresultoffset`, `labresultrevisedoffset`, `labname`, `labresult`, `labmeasurenamesystem`); `infusionDrug.csv.gz` (`patientunitstayid`, `infusionoffset`, `drugname`, `drugrate`, `infusionrate`, `drugamount`); `intakeOutput.csv.gz` (`patientunitstayid`, `intakeoutputoffset`, `intakeoutputentryoffset`, `outputtotal`, `celllabel`, `cellvaluenumeric`, `cellvaluetext`); and `hospital.csv.gz` (`hospitalid`, `numbedscategory`, `teachingstatus`, `region`). Longitudinal joins use `patientunitstayid`; hospital context uses `hospitalid`. No cross-dataset identity matching is permitted.

The primary endpoint is creatinine-defined: use the last valid creatinine at or before ICU time 0 when available; otherwise use a prespecified pre-exposure creatinine and report missingness rather than impute normal. The event is the earliest creatinine increase >=0.3 mg/dL from that baseline within 48 hours, or >=1.5 times baseline if documented within the outcome window, retaining time to event. A post-exposure creatinine must never be baseline. Missing baseline or outcome-window measurements are indeterminate, not negative. Urine-output AKI is secondary and only retained after semantic and timing validation of `intakeOutput` labels, numeric/text values, event versus entry offset, duplicates/cumulative behavior, and availability; otherwise it is unavailable.

The simple baseline is a prespecified hospital-stratified logistic model using nadir systolic BP in 0–24 hours, age, sex, baseline creatinine, and available baseline severity/vasopressor context. The substantive time-aware alternative adds cumulative hypotension minutes, time below threshold in prespecified intervals, last BP before window end, and a simple recovery/trajectory feature while retaining nadir. Both use the same rows, hospital-held-out split, outcome, and uncertainty procedure. The alternative can reveal whether duration and recovery timing carry information lost by nadir alone; a complex sequence model is deferred unless a bounded diagnostic shows the simpler representation is materially inadequate.

Supportive results require reproducible duration information beyond nadir with prespecified direction and uncertainty, not confined to one hospital or observation density. Adverse results include no incremental information, reversal after prespecified adjustment, or artifact-prone measurements. Inconclusive results include sparse/invalid timestamps or creatinine ascertainment; an imprecise null is not decisive refutation. Causal, treatment-threshold, or mechanistic claims require clinical adjudication, richer treatment/etiology data, and prospective or quasi-experimental evidence.

No eICU effect estimate has been computed. The strongest evidence-supported claim is only that prior work motivates testing time-weighted hypotension as an AKI predictor and that the proposed endpoint/observation process is uncertain; the unresolved claim is the incremental, timing-anchored eICU association. The actual deliverable is a newly fitted baseline and time-aware comparison with frozen cohort, endpoint semantics, uncertainty, hospital-held-out evaluation, and conclusions linked to computed outputs.
