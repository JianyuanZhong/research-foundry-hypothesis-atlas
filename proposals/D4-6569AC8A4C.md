# Same-current HbA1c history versus fixed-baseline deterioration: a UK Biobank landmark experiment

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`  
Seed: `[starting question]`, “Metabolic deterioration and multimorbidity”  
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`  
Status: prospective observational hypothesis; this proposal contains no fitted result.

## Clinical question and substantive advance

The clinically consequential unresolved question is whether two people who have the same current HbA1c should receive different prognostic interpretation because one arrived there from a lower prior HbA1c. If history adds material information, a future risk tool or guideline might justify recording the trajectory when interpreting a repeat HbA1c. If it does not, the result would support the simpler use of the current value for this narrow prognostic purpose. Neither result would establish that repeat testing or acting on a trajectory improves health.

The expert seed asks whether deterioration in metabolic measures adds information beyond current levels. This successor narrows it to a computable two-measure HbA1c question and a clinically consequential transition: the first occurrence of a second distinct inpatient-coded disease family among diabetes-coded disease, chronic kidney disease and heart failure. It makes two scientifically different contrasts explicit:

1. history at a fixed current HbA1c: a recent rise versus a stable prior history while holding the current value fixed; and
2. deterioration at a fixed baseline HbA1c: a higher current value versus a stable current value while holding the prior value fixed.

These are not interchangeable. With current (C=B+Delta), conditioning on (C) means that a larger (Delta) necessarily means a lower (B). A coefficient described merely as “change” can therefore be misread as the effect of deterioration. The primary estimand here is the first contrast; the second is a prespecified secondary estimand. Current HbA1c, baseline HbA1c and change will not be entered as three unrestricted covariates in one model.

The strongest evidence currently supporting the premise is narrower than the hypothesis. The inherited frozen Europe PMC search record for Xue et al. (DOI `10.1186/s12933-026-03255-4`, source identifier `[source checksum]`) reports a large prospective UK Biobank analysis in which static metabolic/atherogenic indices were associated with cardiometabolic multimorbidity. I have not treated that abstract/search record as evidence about repeated HbA1c history, and I have not claimed to inspect unavailable full-text methods or supplements. A current Europe PMC search also retrieves a 2026 UK Biobank multi-state study of static serum uric acid and transitions to cardiometabolic multimorbidity (frozen search source `[source checksum]`); this supports the relevance of disease-transition analyses, not the HbA1c-history hypothesis.

The untested claim is therefore: among people who survive and remain free of the selected disease families until a valid repeat measurement, a lower prior HbA1c at the same current HbA1c is associated with a clinically material difference in subsequent risk of a second distinct family. This study tests a noncausal prognostic association in a selected landmark population. It does not test a treatment effect, the value of ordering a repeat test, or an adjudicated disease transition.

## Hypotheses and estimands

Let (B_i) be baseline HbA1c, (C_i) the strictly later current/repeat HbA1c, and (Delta_i=C_i-B_i), all converted to NGSP percentage points only after unit verification. Let (T_{2i}) be the time from the repeat landmark to the second distinct qualifying disease family, with death a competing event.

Primary directional hypothesis:

> At the same current HbA1c, a trajectory with (B=C-0.5) percentage points (a 0.5-point rise) has a higher 5-year cumulative incidence of the second distinct disease family than a trajectory with (B=C) (stable history), after standardization to the eligible landmark cohort.

The primary estimand is the standardized, model-based, noncausal 5-year risk difference:

[
RD_mathrm{history}
= E_X[
 P(T_2le5mid C_i, B_i=C_i-0.5, X_i)
 -P(T_2le5mid C_i, B_i=C_i, X_i)
].
]

Here (X_i) consists of prespecified baseline covariates. The contrast is evaluated only where both ((B,C)=(C_i-0.5,C_i)) and ((C_i,C_i)) are supported by the observed joint exposure distribution. It is not a hypothetical HbA1c intervention.

Secondary fixed-baseline deterioration estimand:

[
RD_mathrm{deterioration}
= E_X[
 P(T_2le5mid B_i, C_i=B_i+0.5, X_i)
 -P(T_2le5mid B_i, C_i=B_i, X_i)
].
]

This asks whether increasing the current value by 0.5 points is associated with risk when the prior value is held fixed. It must be reported separately and never relabeled as the primary history result. An interquartile-range contrast is a descriptive sensitivity display, not a replacement for the prespecified 0.5-point contrasts.

If the assay unit cannot be verified or converted to NGSP percentage points, the computation may report standardized within-dataset HbA1c contrasts, but the clinical 0.5-point hypotheses are not considered tested.

The primary outcome is the date of the second distinct family among:

- diabetes-coded inpatient ICD-10 prefixes E11–E14;
- chronic kidney disease, N18.x; and
- heart failure, I50.x.

“Diabetes-coded” is deliberate: these codes are not an adjudicated type-2 diagnosis, and the local data do not establish outpatient disease, phenotype, treatment, or type. A same-date pair of two distinct families counts as a second-family event. Repeated codes within one family do not create another disease. The endpoint is not “any diagnosis.”

### Exposure and landmark construction

The primary population is the repeat-observed landmark population, not all UK Biobank participants:

1. Baseline assessment date (A0_i) is the parsed value of `assessment.53-0.0`; repeat assessment date (A1_i) is `assessment.53-1.0`. Both must be valid ISO dates and (A1_i>A0_i).
2. Baseline HbA1c is `biological_samples.30750-0.0`; repeat HbA1c is `biological_samples.30750-1.0`. Both must be numeric, within prespecified physiologic/data-quality bounds, and not a retained UKB negative/missing code.
3. The local source has assessment dates but no sample-collection date columns. Field-instance indices are not elapsed time, and the catalog explicitly says exact dates remain server-side. Therefore a hard sample-temporality gate must be passed before the clinical analysis: UKB metadata must verify that `30750-0.0` was collected at (A0_i) and `30750-1.0` at (A1_i), or supply actual sample dates that can replace (A0_i,A1_i). Instance numbers alone are not evidence of temporal linkage. If that verification is unavailable or fails, stop at a feasibility audit and classify the scientific result as inconclusive; do not infer a trajectory.
4. The primary repeat interval is (365le A1_i-A0_ile2190) days (1 to 6 years), prespecified to make “trajectory” temporally interpretable. Report the full strictly-later interval distribution. A strictly-later repeat outside this window is excluded from the primary analysis and included in a sensitivity analysis only if its sample dates are verified.
5. Baseline eligibility requires valid `population.21022-0.0` age and `population.31-0.0` sex, valid baseline HbA1c, and complete or prespecified-imputed baseline covariates. Baseline age is in years; sex is retained using the verified coding rather than guessed numeric meanings.
6. The landmark is (L_i=A1_i), the date at which the repeat result is observed. No outcome person-time before (L_i) enters the primary risk model. Eligibility is assessed using all qualifying diagnosis/death information available on or before (L_i).
7. Exclude anyone with a qualifying family code/date on or before (L_i), anyone who died on or before (L_i), and anyone with an unresolved qualifying code for which a valid date cannot be established. Do not treat an undated qualifying code as evidence of disease-free status. Report separately those excluded for diagnosis, death, missing date, or missing repeat.
8. No baseline participant without a valid repeat is a control in the primary model. Compare baseline-eligible people with and without a valid repeat descriptively to quantify selection. The repeat-observed landmark is a principal/selected stratum: conditioning on survival, remaining disease-free, returning for assessment and having a valid repeat can induce selection and collider bias. It cannot be repaired by ordinary adjustment.

The target population is thus explicitly “people with verified baseline and repeat HbA1c temporally linked to assessments, alive and free of the three code-defined families at the repeat landmark, with the required baseline information.” It is not a causal population who would have been offered repeat testing.

### Outcome construction and death

Use only aligned diagnosis/date cells from `health_outcomes`:

- diagnosis code columns are `41270-0.0` through `41270-0.258`;
- corresponding dates are `41280-0.0` through `41280-0.258`.

For each array index (j=0,ldots,258), parse the pair ((41270-j.0,41280-j.0)) together. Never sort all codes and all dates independently, and never pair a code with a date from another (j). A qualifying code with no valid paired date is an ascertainment failure, not a negative outcome. Validate dates as ISO dates and retain only code-date pairs whose date is on or before the administrative endpoint.

For each family (k), let (D_{ik}) be the earliest valid aligned date strictly after (L_i). If any qualifying code is dated on or before (L_i), exclude the participant from the primary disease-free cohort. For post-landmark follow-up, (T_{2i}) is the later of the earliest dates for the first two distinct families; report family identities, same-day ties, and participants with only zero or one family.

Death is taken from `health_outcomes.40000-0.0` and `40000-1.0`, after verified interpretation of these fields as death dates. If both are valid and disagree, retain the disagreement audit and fail the death-date gate unless the data dictionary provides a prespecified reconciliation; do not silently choose one. The analysis death date is the verified earliest valid date when the dictionary confirms that the two fields are alternative death-date sources, otherwise the reconciled date specified by that dictionary. Death on or before (L_i) excludes.

Follow from (L_i) through the earliest of (T_{2i}), death, or 2023-03-31. Death before the second-family event is a competing event, not ordinary censoring. Because dates have day rather than time resolution, the primary tie rule is death-first: if death and a qualifying second-family date are the same calendar day, assign the competing death event and do not claim that the disease preceded death. Repeat the analysis with disease-first as a tie sensitivity and report the number of ties. If death and diagnosis-date semantics cannot be verified, the primary result is inconclusive.

For the 5-year estimand, use the Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence for the second-family event with death competing. Administrative censoring at 2023-03-31 is treated as censoring under the stated independent-censoring assumption. Report the proportion with less than 5 years of possible follow-up and do not imply complete 5-year ascertainment when it is unavailable.

A one-year post-landmark washout, in which events on ((L_i,L_i+365]) are not counted and follow-up begins after that interval, is a reverse-causation sensitivity. It does not change the primary landmark estimand. A sensitivity restriction to a 1–6-year baseline-to-repeat interval is already primary; a full strictly-later interval analysis is secondary.

## Baseline covariates and analysis

The baseline covariate set is age (`21022-0.0`), restricted cubic spline), sex (`31-0.0`), BMI (`21001-0.0`), systolic BP (`4080-0.0`), and diastolic BP (`4079-0.0`). Baseline HbA1c is an exposure-history component, not a post-landmark covariate. Creatinine (`30700-0.0`) and cystatin C (`30720-0.0`) may be included only if their units and a prespecified eGFR equation are verified; otherwise they are omitted and their absence reported. Do not use repeat covariates or post-(L_i) measurements. Medications, outpatient laboratory results, validated disease registries, treatment indication, disease severity/stage, and adjudicated outcomes are unavailable for this proposal and must not be substituted by unvalidated proxies.

Fit models in the identical primary landmark cohort:

- (M0): current HbA1c (C) plus baseline covariates.
- (M1): current HbA1c (C), baseline HbA1c (B), and a prespecified restricted-spline joint ((B,C)) surface plus baseline covariates. This is the primary history model.
- (M2): baseline HbA1c (B), change (Delta=C-B), and the same prespecified spline complexity plus baseline covariates. This is the fixed-baseline deterioration parameterization.

Do not fit current, baseline and change as separate unrestricted terms. Select spline knots and maximum interaction complexity from the exposure distribution before viewing outcome associations; if the event count cannot support the planned surface, reduce complexity by a rule written before outcome fitting or classify the requested contrast as infeasible. The model’s surface is used to standardize each participant to the two history settings, with no extrapolation beyond the support gate. Report cause-specific hazard ratios only as model parameters; report the primary quantities as standardized 5-year cumulative-incidence risk, risk difference and risk ratio, with participant-level bootstrap 95% intervals. Use Aalen–Johansen for competing death; Fine–Gray is a sensitivity model, not a replacement for the primary estimand. Report cumulative-incidence curves and absolute event counts.

Use participant-level resampling for uncertainty and optimism correction. Any imputation and any repeat-selection weighting must occur within each resampling partition. Calibration, Brier score and optimism-corrected incremental prediction from (M1) over (M0) are secondary descriptive checks. A small AUC or machine-learning improvement is not evidence of clinical utility. The analysis is not a causal inference.

### Selection sensitivity

The primary analysis reports, in stages, baseline-eligible participants, those alive and disease-free at the candidate repeat date, those with temporally verified paired samples, and those meeting the 1–6-year interval. It also compares baseline covariates and follow-up availability between baseline-eligible participants with and without a valid repeat.

A stabilized inverse-probability-of-valid-repeat weight based on baseline variables may be computed as a sensitivity analysis, with truncation fixed before outcome fitting and positivity, effective sample size and weight distribution reported. It is not used to redefine the primary population, and it cannot recover people who died, developed a component disease, were not re-contacted, or had an unverified sample date. If weights are unstable or positivity is inadequate, that sensitivity is inconclusive; the unweighted primary estimate remains a selected-landmark association, not a population effect.

## Falsification, feasibility, materiality and interpretation gates

Set the working clinical-materiality threshold before outcome fitting at an absolute 5-year cumulative-incidence difference of 2 percentage points. This is a decision-relevance threshold for interpreting the result, not a validated treatment threshold and not a recommendation.

Hard feasibility gates must all pass before an inferential result is reported:

- exact source/schema/header and `eid` join checks;
- HbA1c unit and negative/missing-value verification;
- sample-instance-to-assessment-date verification;
- valid (A1>A0) ordering and the prespecified 1–6-year interval;
- complete aligned code/date interpretation, code-list version and date parsing;
- disease-free/death-free landmark ascertainment;
- verified death-date semantics and the prespecified death-first tie rule;
- adequate follow-up to support the stated horizon;
- at least 100 second-family events overall, at least 10 events in each displayed trajectory/contrast stratum, and empirical support for both standardized histories;
- a nonsparse, non-extrapolative joint exposure surface; and
- stable uncertainty computation and, if used, stable repeat weights.

The primary history contrast is supportive of the directional hypothesis when all gates pass, the point estimate is at least +2 percentage points, its 95% interval excludes 0, and the current-plus-history model is acceptably calibrated. A lower confidence bound above +2 points is the stronger “materially supported” category. Consistency in the one-year washout, full-interval sensitivity, death-tie sensitivity and repeat-weighted sensitivity strengthens—but is not required for—the primary descriptive finding.

Results adverse to the expected direction are a clinically material negative history contrast (point estimate at most -2 points with the 95% interval excluding 0), or a robust positive result that disappears after the washout or verified selection sensitivity. These would challenge the expected trajectory interpretation and may indicate reverse causation, health-care selection, or a different biology; they do not establish a protective causal effect.

Evidence against a clinically material history increment, while not proving exact equivalence, is obtained when all gates pass and the 95% interval lies wholly below +2 points and the model is calibrated. If the interval is also wholly within -2 to +2 points, the result supports the narrower statement that no clinically material history increment was detected in this selected landmark population, not that current HbA1c is universally sufficient. A strong current-HbA1c association in (M0) with no increment in (M1) supports current-level prognostic stratification but not the dynamic claim.

A result is inconclusive if any hard gate fails; if fewer than 100 events or fewer than 10 events in a required displayed stratum occur; if the interval includes both a clinically material increase and a trivial/no increase; if support is extrapolative; if code/date or death ordering is unresolved; or if the repeat-weight sensitivity is unusably unstable. A sparse appendicitis negative control cannot be substituted for the primary endpoint. If sufficiently observed, construct a prespecified negative-control outcome from aligned K35.x appendicitis codes, which should not show an association of comparable magnitude; a large negative-control association is a warning about utilization/ascertainment confounding, not proof that the primary result is false. Report its event count and do not condition the primary conclusion on a sparse negative control.

All reported conclusions must link to computed audit/model outputs: flow counts, event/tie tables, overlap diagnostics, exposure-surface support, standardized risks and differences, bootstrap intervals, competing-risk estimates, calibration and sensitivity results. Computation can establish availability, selection, date ordering, inpatient-code-defined disease order, death competing-risk estimates, associations, uncertainty and whether these gates were met. It cannot establish outpatient disease, adjudicated diabetes/CKD/heart failure, disease stage or severity, treatment effects, causal utility of repeat testing, representativeness, or that clinicians should change management. Those claims require validated dictionaries and dates, outpatient laboratory/medication data, clinical adjudication, external validation and a prospective testing-and-management study.
