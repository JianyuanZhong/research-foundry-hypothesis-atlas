# Source-identity and actionability bridge for the frozen pulmonary handoff protocol

## Parent and targeted repair

This is a targeted child of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It keeps the highest-ranked assessed-valid parents' scientific schema unchanged: the first-eligible adult, one-admission-per-subject MIMIC pulmonary-opportunity frame; the reciprocal pre-discharge radiology-report/addendum chronology with `D = dischtime`; exactly one canonical discharge summary; the exact target atom `g=(E, terminal curr_service, W)` and its route; sealed route-concealed C/S panels; inherited H1/H2/H3 and holistic controls; route-independent unit enumeration and all-unit adverse precedence; pre-label target selection and target-freeze state machine; positive-probability two-phase whole-admission sampling with no unit sampling; the additive distinct-admission workload ledger with fixed controls and one charged reserve; finite-population Horvitz–Thompson/Hájek inference with nested design uncertainty; and the silent-validation-only, noncausal conclusion ceiling.

The single repaired weakness is the bridge from a reproducible stored-text placement to a defensible prospective silent handoff candidate. A route can be reproducible while relying on an unidentified note, a copied-forward passage, or a sentence that is clinically non-actionable. The parent panels and holistic challenge protect against correlated adjudication error, but they do not require every positive/negative decision to carry an auditable source identity and a pre-specified actionability status, nor do they calibrate abstention before a simulated live replay. This child adds a source/actionability ledger and chronological bridge calibration without changing who is eligible, what the target is, when labels are allowed, or what can be concluded.

## Evidence-supported versus untested claims

The available evidence supports construction of an admission-linked, chronologically bounded corpus joining radiology text, discharge text, hospital discharge time, and service/transfer history. It can support a reproducibility estimate for the inherited target-linked documentation/routing constructs under sealed adjudication. It does not establish that a note is the source actually available to a receiving clinician, that an extracted sentence gives an actionable next step, that a route is appropriate or safe, or that silent replay performance transports to a live EHR.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is narrower: **conditional on the frozen parent frame and labels, can a route decision be reproduced in chronological replay while every non-deferred decision has an identifiable, pre-discharge source span and a separately coded actionability status, with calibration of the route's abstention/decision probabilities preserved on a later unseen time block?** The experiment tests this documentary/operational bridge only. It does not test delivery, receipt, correctness of care, appropriateness, follow-up, safety, benefit, or causality.

## New pre-label source/actionability ledger

For every inherited sampled unit and every route output, create a deterministic `source_actionability` record before opening any parent target label, C/S adjudication, or holistic challenge label. The record is an audit feature, not a new clinical outcome.

1. **Source identity.** Store `source_note_id`, `source_table` (`note/radiology` or `note/discharge`), `source_note_type`, `source_note_seq`, `source_charttime`, `source_storetime`, and `source_role` (`radiology_report`, `radiology_addendum`, `canonical_discharge_summary`, or `none`). The selected note must be identified by its actual `note_id`; ties are not silently resolved. If the inherited rule yields no unique note, set source identity to `UNRESOLVED` and force `DEFER` for the bridge endpoint.
2. **Text-span provenance.** Store zero-based `span_start` and `span_end` in the exact unmodified `text` field, plus a SHA-256 hash of the exact text and a normalized whitespace-independent hash. A span is valid only if `0 <= start < end <= len(text)` and the substring is byte-for-byte reproducible from the frozen source row. If a decision uses more than one sentence, store one ordered span per source note; do not concatenate notes into a synthetic source. Copied-forward or duplicated wording is not declared copied-forward by inference: mark `copy_status=unknown` unless an explicit parent rule identifies it.
3. **Temporal/source availability flags.** Code `available_at_D = (source_storetime <= D)` and `report_before_D = (source_charttime <= D)` as separate fields; retain missing timestamps as `UNKNOWN`, never as false. A source used in a decision must have `available_at_D=TRUE`; otherwise its bridge status is `UNRESOLVED` and the route is `DEFER`. This is a storage-availability audit, not proof that a clinician read or received it.
4. **Actionability code.** Independently of target truth, assign one of `ACTIONABLE`, `INFORMATIONAL_ONLY`, `CONTRADICTORY`, `MISSING_ACTION`, or `UNRESOLVED` using a sealed five-state codebook frozen before labels. `ACTIONABLE` requires an explicit pulmonary finding/status plus an explicit next-step, monitoring instruction, or responsibility/time anchor in the same source span; `INFORMATIONAL_ONLY` has a finding/status but no next step; `CONTRADICTORY` contains incompatible current-status/next-step statements within the cited span; `MISSING_ACTION` is a required field absent under the parent atom's fixed output; and `UNRESOLVED` covers illegible, missing, or codebook-ambiguous text. Do not use medication administration, outcomes, or post-discharge data to assign this code. This code is a construct audit, not an adjudication of whether the action is clinically correct.
5. **Identity/actionability gate.** A non-`DEFER` route may be bridge-eligible only when all cited source notes have valid identity, valid span hashes, `available_at_D=TRUE`, and actionability `ACTIONABLE`. Any `UNRESOLVED`, `INFORMATIONAL_ONLY`, `CONTRADICTORY`, `MISSING_ACTION`, timestamp ambiguity, or source conflict is retained as an adverse bridge outcome and is not silently recoded as a negative target. The inherited route label and target label remain separate columns.

The codebook, parser version, hash algorithm, source-row key, and ledger schema are sealed before any label-dependent work. Reviewers see only the cited span(s) and the fixed codebook, not the target label, route name, parent model score, or downstream outcome. A second independent reviewer challenges a random positive and negative subset and all `CONTRADICTORY`/`UNRESOLVED` records; disagreement is `UNRESOLVED`, not majority-washed. Report raw agreement, disagreement, missingness, and category-specific intervals.

## Prospective-style bridge calibration without schema drift

Use a deterministic chronological replay over the already frozen parent sample. The replay order is ascending `D`, then `(subject_id, hadm_id)` as the tie-break. Partition only after parent eligibility and sampling are frozen: the earliest 60% of distinct eligible admissions by `D` is development, the next 20% is calibration, and the latest 20% is silent bridge evaluation. If the parent has a different pre-registered temporal partition, inherit that partition exactly; the 60/20/20 split is a fallback implementation detail and must not override a frozen parent boundary. No text from a later partition, including codebook revisions, thresholds, or source/actionability adjudications, may inform an earlier partition.

The development portion can fit the already inherited route score/decision rule. On calibration admissions only, estimate reliability of the parent route's pre-specified decision probability separately by inherited route and by the four source/actionability statuses (`ACTIONABLE` versus non-actionable; non-actionable statuses remain pooled only for precision, with the unpooled table reported). Use isotonic regression only if the parent already specifies a probability output; otherwise report calibration-free counts and do not invent probabilities. Freeze calibration maps and the abstention threshold before opening bridge-evaluation labels. The bridge evaluation is a one-pass silent replay: emit `AUTO`, `RECON`, `TIMING`, or `DEFER` and the source/actionability record, but do not alter the parent target label or seek feedback during replay.

Primary bridge endpoint: among bridge-evaluable non-`DEFER` outputs, the weighted proportion satisfying the identity/actionability gate, with a 95% finite-population interval that includes sampling and adjudication uncertainty. Secondary computable endpoints are (a) route-stratified calibration error/Brier score only where a parent probability and frozen target label exist, (b) `DEFER` rate, (c) exact-source identity failure rate, (d) actionability-category distribution, (e) chronological degradation from calibration to bridge evaluation, and (f) proportion of admissions with any adverse bridge record under all-unit adverse precedence. Report every endpoint overall and by inherited route and target/control status without conditioning away failures.

Retain the parents' Horvitz–Thompson/Hájek weights and nested variance calculation. Source/actionability review is an outcome-assessment subsample and its review inclusion probabilities must be recorded; never treat reviewed records as a simple random sample if they were enriched for disagreements. The workload ledger charges one fixed unit per admission plus the parent-defined fixed controls and one reserve; source/actionability challenge review is charged within that same admission ledger and cannot create additional admissions.

## Baselines, falsification, and decision rules

Compare the bridge endpoint with the unchanged parent route-only reproducibility endpoint, and with a deliberately source-blind extraction that has the same text, chronology, model, sample, and labels but omits source identity/actionability metadata. The source-blind arm is a measurement baseline, not a competing clinical strategy. Also report a negative-control ledger made from the inherited non-target/control universe; it must not acquire a positive target-linked source merely because similar pulmonary words occur.

Falsification criteria are pre-specified. The bridge claim is falsified if any of the following holds in the silent bridge evaluation, using the parent-specified tolerance and interval convention: (i) the lower confidence bound for the identity/actionability-gated proportion is below the parent bridge-readiness threshold; (ii) source-blind and source-aware decisions have materially different route distributions or error patterns, demonstrating that apparent route reproducibility depended on untracked provenance; (iii) `UNRESOLVED`/contradictory source statuses exceed the pre-specified missingness ceiling; (iv) calibration error worsens beyond the pre-specified margin from calibration to bridge evaluation; or (v) any post-`D` text, target label, or outcome is detected in the replay dependency manifest. If the gate is met but the source-aware result is not distinguishable from the baseline, the result is supportive only of a well-audited stored-text bridge, not clinical actionability.

Supportive results mean that the frozen route can be replayed with high provenance completeness, low unresolvedness, and stable pre-calibrated probabilities on the unseen historical block. Adverse results mean the protocol should remain `DEFER`/not nominated for a live pilot, with the failing source classes and route strata reported. Inconclusive results include wide intervals, insufficient unique source spans, sparse route strata, or failed reviewer reliability; they do not count as support. No threshold may be chosen after seeing the bridge block.

## Strict interpretation ceiling

Even a supportive result establishes only a reproducible, source-auditable, chronologically replayable documentation construct in this MIMIC snapshot and may nominate a separately governed silent validation. It does not establish that information was delivered, read, understood, appropriate, complete, safe, followed, or beneficial; it does not estimate a treatment effect, causal effect, transportability, or prospective clinical performance. Clinical actionability and contradiction adjudication require expert review; actual delivery/receipt requires EHR audit logs or prospective observation; patient benefit and safety require a governed prospective study with outcomes. Any stronger claim is explicitly out of scope.

## What changed and what remains uncertain

The substantive change is a pre-label, source-row-keyed provenance/actionability ledger plus a chronological calibration-and-later-block silent replay. It makes the bridge endpoint computable, forces ambiguous or non-actionable text to `DEFER`, protects against post-`D` leakage, and measures degradation rather than assuming historical reproducibility is prospective readiness. The frozen population, chronology, target atom/route, controls, sampling/ledger, estimand, and conclusion ceiling are unchanged. Remaining uncertainties are whether MIMIC storage time approximates real availability, whether a cited span reflects what a recipient saw, whether the actionability codebook captures clinical meaning, and whether a silent bridge can transport; these require source-system audit, expert adjudication, and prospective study rather than this experiment.
