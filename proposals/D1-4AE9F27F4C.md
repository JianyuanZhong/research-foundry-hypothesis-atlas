# Provisional eICU hypothesis: early hypotension duration versus nadir and subsequent AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU admission with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, observation intensity, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Clinical importance and unresolved question
A transient nadir may reflect measurement noise or a brief event, whereas sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved claim is whether duration/trajectory adds information beyond nadir after timing and ascertainment are controlled. Resolving it could determine whether early AKI risk assessment should use a time-weighted exposure rather than only the lowest observed pressure.

## Falsifiable test
Construct a cohort from eICU patient/admission data with a pre-exposure creatinine baseline and a post-exposure 24–72-hour creatinine event window. Compare a baseline model using nadir and prespecified covariates with a model adding cumulative time below 65 mmHg, using hospital-held-out evaluation and uncertainty intervals. A supportive result requires reproducible incremental information for duration beyond nadir in the held-out hospitals; an adverse result is no reproducible increment or a directionally opposite association; an inconclusive result is wide uncertainty or inadequate creatinine/urine ascertainment. Falsification checks include a pre-exposure outcome window, negative-control timing, and sensitivity to measurement density and artifact rules.

## Method comparison
Baseline: a prespecified severity-adjusted model using nadir, baseline covariates, prior vasopressor exposure, observation intensity, and hospital. Alternative: a time-aware model using cumulative duration and trajectory features from the same blood-pressure observations. The alternative can reveal whether sustained exposure and timing matter beyond the single minimum, while the baseline tests whether a simple nadir summary is sufficient. Choose the alternative only if it addresses the scientific uncertainty rather than merely improving prediction; defer if local BP/creatinine semantics cannot support it.
