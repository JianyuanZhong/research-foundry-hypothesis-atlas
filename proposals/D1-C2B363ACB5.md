# Proposal: a leakage-safe day-45 AFP landmark for liver-specific recorded escalation

## Episode and substantive repair

This Episode-33 child evolves assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. It retains the parent's clinically important attribution question—whether AFP contributes information beyond albumin/total-bilirubin trajectory, prior recorded liver-directed history, and local observation opportunity—but repairs a temporal ambiguity that would otherwise make the estimand internally inconsistent.

The parent correctly retains all index patients and early competing transitions, while defining a profile whose post-index component is first observed on days 7–14 or 7–45. A post-index profile cannot predict a C2/C1/C0 event that happened before that profile was observed. The study therefore has two explicitly separate deliverables:

1. An all-index, pre-index-only descriptive competing-transition analysis from t0 through day 45, which estimates recorded early pathways without using post-index information.
2. The primary prospective L=45 landmark analysis. At tL=t0+45 days, retain patients with no observed primary-ontology liver-directed therapeutic transition, a valid L=45 AFP/albumin/total-bilirubin profile, and local observation at tL. Predict first subsequent recorded outcomes through t0+365 using only information available by tL.

The central unresolved question is not whether AFP is biologically a response marker—this snapshot cannot establish that. It is whether a day-45 AFP change provides incremental, liver-specific information for deciding which HCC patients warrant an adjudicated reassessment workflow, after the hepatic-reserve trajectory and observation opportunity have been accounted for. The repair makes that claim temporally falsifiable and prevents a favorable result from being generated by leakage or conditioning on future events.

## Strongest supported claim, unresolved claim, and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence supports only that the HCC snapshot contains dated local encounters, procedures, non-drug orders, examinations, medications and labs linked by patient/encounter identifiers. Existing audits found substantial procedure, examination and laboratory volume, and the source catalog verifies exact schemas and a two-key relationship. They do not support an AFP association, a treatment effect, radiologic response, recurrence, survival, or clinical utility. No fitted result is claimed here.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adult patients with a recorded HCC diagnosis and a first local TACE-like recorded episode, who remain eligible and observable at day 45 with a valid paired laboratory profile, AFP change adds a practically meaningful and stable liver-specific contrast for subsequent concordant liver-directed recorded care beyond albumin/total-bilirubin change, prior observable liver-directed history and local observation opportunity; the corresponding AFP contrast is smaller for generic non-liver procedures.

The primary estimand is the standardized 320-day post-landmark cumulative-incidence contrast:
`S_A^45(320)=CIF(C2 | favorable AFP, hepatic profile, H, O) - CIF(C2 | discordant/unfavorable AFP, same H and O)`, with the prespecified specificity contrast
`S_A^45(320)-S_N^45(320)`, where N is the analogous non-liver recorded-procedure CIF contrast. The direction is a hypothesis, not an expected result: favorable AFP change is hypothesized to have lower subsequent C2 CIF, and the C2 contrast is hypothesized to be more pronounced than the N contrast. The primary practical margin is an absolute 3 percentage-point difference in the C2-minus-N specificity contrast, with a patient-clustered 95% interval excluding zero. The margin is a decision rule, not an observed or guaranteed effect.

Clinically, support would justify only a prospective study in which AFP-triggered surveillance is compared against usual care with expert-adjudicated imaging and intent. An adverse result would caution that AFP adds little beyond reserve or merely reflects generic contact. A result restricted to the densely observed subgroup would suggest capture dependence and would not justify broad surveillance. None of these results establishes that a procedure was intended, delivered, technically successful, beneficial, or necessary.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support requires: (i) the AFP-specific C2-minus-N contrast has the prespecified direction and absolute magnitude >=3 percentage points with a patient-clustered 95% interval excluding zero; (ii) the contrast is stable under C2 timing/status variants, L=14 sensitivity, H/O strata and observation weighting; (iii) B_A improves held-out Brier or integrated dynamic score/calibration over B_H without a clinically trivial or unstable change; and (iv) the contrast is not reproduced similarly by the pre-index placebo AFP trajectory or a non-liver/control trajectory. Support remains an association with recorded local pathways, not a treatment recommendation.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed C2-specific contrast, a similar or larger N contrast, loss under timing/status/schedule controls, no incremental held-out performance, poor overlap, or concentration solely in H/O strata. This argues against AFP-specific generalization in this recorded-care population but does not prove AFP lacks value with adjudicated imaging, different assays, or other populations.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few complete landmark profiles/events, positivity failure, wide intervals crossing the margin, missing or invalid time preventing endpoint assignment, or alternative-model nonidentifiability. It should yield no clinical selection, not a forced conclusion. A positive computational result still requires expert review of assay semantics, imaging, treatment intent, technical success, disease status, mortality and outside-care capture. Those evidence are unavailable here; the examinations CSV has reports but not image files or validated radiology adjudication, diagnoses have no native timestamp, and clinical documents/pathology lack a reliable event-time framework.
