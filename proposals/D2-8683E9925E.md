# MerTK inhibitor effect on the macrophage-driven FLS program: inconsistent and non-significant attenuation in E-MTAB-8873

## Seed
D2-T2 (Protective versus pathogenic states)

## Domain
Therapeutic Target Prioritization

## Parent
[prior hypothesis] (MerTK selectivity analysis in E-MTAB-8873)

## Question
Does MerTK inhibition (UNC1062, 250 nM) of macrophages attenuate the macrophage-driven FLS program in E-MTAB-8873, and is the effect consistent across FLS donors and macrophage types (Mpos vs Mneg)?

## Background and relationship to parent

The parent candidate (6eda0349) tested whether MerTK inhibition *selectively* abolishes the protective FLS program (Mpos context) while sparing the pathogenic FLS program (Mneg context). The parent's analysis found that the "protective program" premise was not well-supported: the canonical protective markers (COL1A1, COL1A2, TGFBI) were actually *downregulated* in Mpos vs FLS_only (log2FC = -0.40, -0.43, -0.46), not upregulated as the repository description suggested. The Mpos-driven program (genes up >0.5 log2FC) was dominated by immune activation genes (MRC1, CD86, MERTK, IL1RN), not collagen/repair genes.

This candidate takes a different analytical angle: rather than testing selectivity between two programs, it isolates the *inhibitor effect itself* — what does adding UNC1062 to macrophages do to the FLS transcriptome, compared to the matched control without inhibitor? This directly answers: "Does blocking MerTK on macrophages change what those macrophages do to FLS?"

The therapeutic implication: if MerTK inhibition has no meaningful effect on the macrophage-driven FLS program, then MerTK inhibitors would not be expected to alter the macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk that drives RA pathology or repair. This would argue against MerTK as a therapeutic target in RA synovium.

## Hypothesis
MerTK inhibition (UNC1062, 250 nM) of macrophages attenuates the macrophage-driven FLS program in E-MTAB-8873, with the effect being consistent across FLS donors and present in both Mpos and Mneg macrophage contexts.

## Rival hypotheses
1. **No-effect hypothesis**: MerTK signaling is not required for the macrophage-driven FLS program. The FLS response to macrophage co-culture is driven by other macrophage-derived factors (TGFβ, IL-10, SPMs, direct contact) that are unaffected by MerTK inhibition.
2. **Donor-specific hypothesis**: The inhibitor effect is real but donor-specific, with some FLS donors responding to MerTK blockade and others not. This would reflect FLS-specific MerTK signaling capacity or alternative pathway activation.
3. **Mneg-selective hypothesis**: MerTK inhibition preferentially affects the Mneg (pathogenic) context, consistent with MerTK being more important in the inflammatory macrophage state.
4. **Off-target hypothesis**: The observed effects are due to off-target kinase inhibition by UNC1062, not MerTK-specific blockade.

## Analysis plan (executed)

1. **Load** the 445×33 counts matrix; apply log2(count+1) transformation
2. **Define programs**: Mpos-driven program (85 genes, log2FC > 0.5 in Mpos vs FLS_only), Mneg-driven program (162 genes), combined (163 genes)
3. **Compute inhibitor effect**: For each paired contrast, compute log2FC (Inhibitor - control) per gene
4. **Test program-level effect**: Mean inhibitor effect on program genes across pairs; one-sample t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test
5. **Per-donor consistency**: Group Mpos pairs by FLS donor; check direction consistency
6. **Pathway analysis**: Pre-defined gene sets (Collagen/ECM, TGFβ, Inflammatory cytokines, MMPs, Immune activation, Proliferation, Metabolism)
7. **FLS direct control**: FLS_Inhibitor vs FLS_only to assess direct drug effect on FLS
8. **Gene-level FDR**: BH correction across 445 genes for the 4-pair Mpos contrast

## Executed results

## Falsification assessment

**Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE**

The pre-specified falsification criteria were:
- *Supportive*: inhibitor attenuates the FLS program in ≥2/3 FLS donors, consistent direction
- *Adverse*: inhibitor has no effect or opposite direction
- *Inconclusive*: <2/3 FLS donors show consistent effect, or FDR eliminates all significant genes

**Result**: The criterion for "inconclusive" is met on two counts:
1. The direction is NOT consistent across donors: SA257A shows enhancement (+0.93), not attenuation
2. FDR eliminates all significant genes (0/445 at FDR < 0.05)

While 2/3 donors technically show attenuation, the overall effect is:
- Not statistically significant (p = 0.88)
- Very small in magnitude (mean = -0.08 log2FC ≈ 5% change)
- Inconsistent in direction (one donor shows the opposite)
- Not pathway-specific (all pathways attenuate modestly)

The Mneg effect (-0.54) is larger but cannot be assessed statistically (n=1).

### What this means
- **Supportive interpretation**: 2/3 donors show attenuation, and the FLS direct control confirms the effect is macrophage-mediated. The modest effect is consistent with MerTK being one of several pathways through which macrophages influence FLS.
- **Adverse interpretation**: The effect is not statistically significant, no gene survives FDR, and one donor shows the opposite direction. MerTK inhibition may simply not affect the macrophage-driven FLS program in a meaningful way.
- **Inconclusive interpretation (adopted)**: The data are insufficient to distinguish "small real effect" from "no effect." The sample size (4 Mpos pairs, 3 FLS donors) is too small for reliable inference, and the donor inconsistency (SA257A) prevents a clear conclusion.

### What would be needed for a definitive answer
- More FLS donors with inhibitor pairs (n ≥ 6-8 for adequate power)
- More Mneg inhibitor pairs (currently n=1)
- Confirmation that UNC1062 is selective for MerTK (off-target profiling)
- Functional readout (not just transcriptional): does MerTK inhibition change FLS behavior (migration, matrix production)?

## Clinical importance
- If MerTK inhibition has no meaningful effect on the macrophage-driven FLS program, then MerTK inhibitors (UNC1062, UNC106238) would not be expected to alter the macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk in RA
- This would argue against MerTK as a therapeutic target for modulating FLS behavior in RA
- However, the inconclusive result means this question remains open: a larger study could confirm or refute the effect
- The finding that the effect is not pathway-specific (all pathways attenuate modestly) is consistent with a general reduction in macrophage signaling rather than a specific pathway disruption

## Limitations
1. **Very small sample size**: 4 Mpos pairs (3 FLS donors), 1 Mneg pair. Statistical power is severely limited.
2. **Donor inconsistency**: SA257A shows the opposite direction (enhancement), which could reflect donor-specific biology or experimental noise.
3. **445-gene targeted panel**: Limited gene space; unmeasured genes may be important.
4. **No off-target data**: UNC1062 selectivity profile not available in the dataset.
5. **Mneg n=1**: The Mneg effect cannot be assessed statistically.
6. **FLS direct control is single sample**: The FLS_Inhibitor vs FLS_only comparison is n=1.
7. **No original publication found**: The experimental design is inferred from repository metadata.

## Method alternatives considered
- **DESeq2/edgeR**: Not appropriate for 445-gene targeted panel with n=4 pairs. Paired t-test on log2FC is more robust at this sample size.
- **Mixed-effects model**: With 4 pairs and 3 donors, the random effect is not well-estimated. Paired t-test is more appropriate.
- **Network analysis**: 445 genes is too few for meaningful network analysis.
- **E-MTAB-8316 validation**: The parent candidate included this, but the current analysis focuses on isolating the inhibitor effect in E-MTAB-8873. The 8316 data uses a different system (M-CSF macrophages, LPS-stimulated, bulk RNA-seq) and would add complexity without resolving the core question.
