# Durable recovery buffer after ICU support withdrawal and early failure

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe adult first-ICU-discharge cohort; derive one-hour physiology, observation masks, support intervals, and support-transition/buffer features; fit a transparent competing-event baseline and a duration-aware latent recovery-state alternative; and produce locked held-out estimates. Completion requires an auditable cohort/exclusion and temporal-integrity table, support/coverage strata counts, held-out risks and Aalen–Johansen cumulative-incidence estimates for ICU return, in-hospital death, and alive hospital discharge at 6/24/48 hours, calibration and IPCW Brier scores, uncertainty across three patient-level split seeds, transition/state diagnostics, falsification analyses, and a conclusion-to-output map. No readiness check or discovery result is a clinical conclusion.

## Unresolved claim and hypothesis

The expert seed and prior candidates support only data feasibility: MIMIC-IV contains dated ICU outtimes, later ICU stays, hospital disposition/death, charted physiology, infusion intervals and recorded respiratory procedures. They do not establish that residual support causes deterioration, that ICU return is unplanned, or that any retrospective marker should change bedside discharge.

The unresolved question is whether apparently acceptable physiology at ICU discharge has different prognostic meaning when support is still active versus recently withdrawn. The parent candidate correctly treated alive hospital discharge as a competing event, but pooled active support with recent withdrawal and could make sparse procedure intervals look like continuous ventilation. This child asks whether a measurable recovery buffer after support cessation distinguishes durable recovery from transient normalization.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among adults discharged alive from a first ICU stay with adequate final-window physiology coverage, patients with low final MAP and respiratory burden but active support or a short support-free buffer have higher 48-hour cause-specific hazard and Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence of first ICU return or in-hospital death, and lower/delayed alive-hospital-discharge incidence, than otherwise similar patients with a longer support-free buffer. The comparison is prognostic, not a treatment effect. A secondary hypothesis is that the duration/order of support withdrawal adds information beyond final values and a binary support flag.

Supportive evidence requires adequate positivity and coverage, reproducible monotone risk separation across buffer strata after case-mix adjustment, calibrated held-out improvement over the snapshot baseline, and persistence after order, mask, channel, and discharge-process falsifications. Adverse evidence is a null/reversed contrast, instability across support definitions, dependence on censoring discharge, or disappearance after observation-mask controls. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse strata/events, non-positivity, contradictory interval endpoints, wide intervals crossing a predeclared clinically meaningful margin, or disagreement between charted and procedure evidence. None supports withholding discharge or continuing support without clinician adjudication and prospective/external validation.

## Population, time origin and outcomes

Use MIMIC-IV 3.1 adults with `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18` (retain the MIMIC >89 representation), the earliest `icu/icustays.intime` within each `subject_id,hadm_id`, and alive ICU discharge with `intime < outtime < admissions.dischtime` and no `admissions.deathtime <= outtime`. The landmark is `icustays.outtime`; no source timestamp after it may enter features.

Use the window [outtime-12h,outtime), split into preceding [outtime-12h,outtime-6h) and final [outtime-6h,outtime) six-hour windows, with one-hour bins indexed by `chartevents.charttime`. Follow to `min(outtime+48h, admissions.dischtime)). First events are: later ICU `intime > outtime` for the same hospitalization within 48h; death with `deathtime > outtime` before ICU return; or alive hospital discharge with `dischtime > outtime` before the other events. Ties are ordered death before ICU return before discharge, using a prespecified timestamp tolerance/sensitivity. If no event occurs by 48h, censor administratively. Require non-null disposition for the competing-discharge analysis and report missing/excluded counts. Do not infer timing from `hospital_expire_flag`, call return “unplanned,” or claim post-discharge outcomes.

## Exposure/feature construction

For each one-hour bin, use medians and explicit observation masks; never impute missing as normal. Hemodynamic burden is fraction of observed MAP bins <65, retaining arterial/NIBP source. Respiratory burden is fraction with FiO2 >=0.40 or O2 flow >=4 L/min, retaining native channels; PEEP/RR/SpO2 are secondary. Require at least two observed MAP and two respiratory bins in each six-hour half-window for the primary adequately observed analysis; retain inadequate coverage as a separate mixed/unavailable stratum and report it.

Construct support evidence by interval overlap with bins. Vasoactive support requires a valid inputevents interval. Charted respiratory support uses FiO2/O2-flow/PEEP thresholds. Procedure rows provide only transition corroboration: an extubation/NIV/intubation event can start or terminate a recorded transition if ordered, nonnegative intervals permit it, but a short procedure row is not a ventilation episode. Define a support-free buffer as the elapsed time from the end of the last valid support interval (vasoactive and charted/procedure respiratory channels separately) to outtime, censored at 12 hours and reported by source. Lock before outcomes four contexts: stable off support with buffer >=6h; recent withdrawal with 0–<6h buffer; active support in the final 6h; and residual physiology/insufficient or contradictory evidence. Keep domain-specific and source-specific sensitivity strata rather than forcing incompatible channels into one label. The primary contrast is recent withdrawal versus stable off support among low-final-burden, adequately observed stays; active support is a separate descriptive/secondary contrast.

## Baseline, alternative, split and uncertainty

The simple baseline is two cause-specific Cox models for ICU return and death, with alive discharge as a competing event, using the locked context/buffer exposure plus final and change-in-burden summaries, masks, age, gender, ICU LOS, first/last careunit, admission type and a small prespecified admission covariate set. A nested snapshot baseline uses only final-window values, masks and case mix. Report cause-specific associations, Aalen–Johansen CIFs and absolute CIF differences at 6/24/48h, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier scores and subject-level bootstrap intervals.

The substantive alternative is a duration-aware four-state hourly latent recovery model fitted only in training data. Emissions are the same physiology, masks, support channels and transition times. States represent stable off support/long buffer, recent withdrawal/short buffer, active support, and residual respiratory/hemodynamic/dual burden. Use persistent sparse transitions and competing discrete hazards over eight six-hour post-index bins; propagate posterior state probabilities rather than hard labels. This may reveal persistence and order of withdrawal that fixed summaries lose, while state names remain analytic constructs. It is scientifically useful because it tests whether durability/transition order—not merely a more flexible predictor—explains the unresolved normalization contrast.

Use patient-level stratified 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits with three fixed seeds. Fit thresholds, scaling, transition/state parameters and any normalization on training only; lock test predictions. Compare both methods on identical inputs using joint competing-event calibration, integrated Brier score, CIF separation, and bootstrap uncertainty. Predeclare a clinically meaningful CIF margin; if no defensible margin is available, report estimates and intervals without binary clinical claims. Discovery planning is CPU-feasible for streaming extraction/Cox and likely 1–4h on <=16 CPUs/<64GB RAM; the latent fit is unverified but expected within that envelope after compact hourly materialization. No GPU is required; an A100 masked-sequence sensitivity is deferred unless the latent model fails or diagnostics show reproducible lost order information.

## Falsification and evidence boundaries

Before fitting, specify order permutation within stay, mask-only/mask-removed, physiology-only/support-removed, procedure-removed, storetime versus charttime, interval versus last-value support coding, arterial versus NIBP MAP, FiO2 versus O2 flow, threshold/window perturbations, careunit/admission-type strata, and negative-control pre-index horizons. Report the difference between treating alive discharge as censoring and as a competing event, never choosing post hoc. A signal that is driven by discharge timing, measurement intensity, or a single unreliable channel is not support for durable recovery.

MIMIC cannot adjudicate work of breathing, unrecorded support, clinician intent, treatment limitations/goals of care, discharge rationale, planned versus unplanned ICU return, staffing, bedside readiness, or post-discharge outcomes. Raw waveforms and imaging are unavailable. External validation (eICU/another hospital), clinician adjudication of support transitions and discharge readiness, and prospective or causal study are required before a clinical policy.
