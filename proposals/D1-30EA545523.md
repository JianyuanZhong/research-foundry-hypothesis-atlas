## 1. The unresolved claim, and the strongest claim the evidence already supports

### 1.1 What the evidence already supports

**Supported (strong).** Antimicrobial de-escalation of empirical broad-spectrum therapy in the ICU
is *not* associated with higher mortality in the comparisons that have been made, and it reduces
broad-spectrum antibiotic-days. The closest prior work to this proposal, a 2026 target-trial
emulation in **MIMIC-IV v3.1 itself** with supportive eICU-CRD analysis **[K1]**, reported day-4
antipseudomonal de-escalation associated with *lower* 30-day mortality (24.1% vs 29.0%; RD
-4.91 pp, 95% CI -7.27 to -2.23) and anti-MRSA de-escalation with no mortality signal
(RD -1.44 pp, 95% CI -3.75 to 1.11). The DIANA prospective cohort (1,495 patients, 152 ICUs)
found an IP-weighted RR for clinical cure of 1.37 (95% CI 1.14-1.64) favouring de-escalation and
concluded "residual confounding is likely". A 2026 scoping review of 51 publications concluded
de-escalation "appears safe but rests on low-certainty, heterogeneous evidence", that
spectrum-ranking systems are non-uniform, and that reassessment "typically occurred at 48-72 h".

**Supported (weaker, and not uniformly favourable).** The only randomized trial of the strategy
**[K2]** (Leone 2014, 116 patients with severe sepsis, 9 French ICUs) did *not* support
de-escalation on its primary endpoint: median ICU stay 9 d (IQR 5-22) in the de-escalation arm vs
8 d (IQR 4-15) in the continuation arm, mean difference 3.4 d (95% CI -1.7 to 8.5), i.e.
non-inferiority on the prespecified 2-day margin was **not** demonstrated; superinfection occurred
in 27% vs 11% (p = 0.03) and antibiotic-days were *higher* after de-escalation (9 vs 7.5,
p = 0.03). Mortality was similar. So the randomized evidence is small, neutral-to-unfavourable on
process outcomes, and underpowered for mortality; the favourable evidence is observational.

**Also supported (measurement).** Spectrum-based stewardship metrics are themselves unsettled.
The Antibiotic Spectrum Index and its relatives assign points by **expert consensus** and are then
applied retrospectively; **[K3]** states this explicitly in its "Evidence before this study" box and
shows that a resistance-benchmarked alternative (OSAR) "yielded different conclusions about
antibiotic utilisation" than SAAR and ASI across the same nine hospitals and six years. Any
de-escalation study therefore inherits an unvalidated exposure-measurement choice.

### 1.2 The specific opening

The entire literature anchors the exposure at a **clock time that is not the time the information
arrived**: "within the first 3 days of therapy" (DIANA), a "structured 48-72 h time-out" (scoping

MIMIC-IV 3.1 the actual interval from specimen collection (`charttime`) to the moment the
susceptibility panel was stored in the record (`storetime`) is wide:

  (`probe01.log`; re-computed independently in this branch, `verifyA_storetime.log`.)

The consequence is directly measurable, and it is large:

- **[OBSERVED]** in the eligible cohort, **48.9%** (1,852/3,785) of episodes have their
  conventional day-3 anchor (collection + 72 h) falling **before the susceptibility panel existed

- **[OBSERVED]** exposure classification under the conventional anchor and under the measured
  `storetime` anchor disagrees for **15.8%** (599/3,785) of episodes

So the conventional anchor misclassifies both *who* had susceptibility information and *when*, and
it mixes information-responsive de-escalation with empiric narrowing that occurred before any
result existed. No work we inspected anchors de-escalation at a measured, per-patient
result-availability timestamp (see §8, inspected-search limitation).

### 1.3 The unresolved claim this experiment tests

> **Claim U1 (measurement).** Anchoring de-escalation at the measured susceptibility-result
> availability time (`storetime` of the index isolate panel) rather than at a conventional
> day-3/culture-date anchor materially changes the estimated effect of de-escalation on 30-day
> mortality in the same patients.
>
> **Claim U2 (identification).** The protective de-escalation association reported by observational
> studies is, in substantial part, **healthy-responder selection** rather than a response to
> microbiological information: an identically defined "narrowing" exposure placed at a
> **pre-result pseudo-anchor**, at which no susceptibility information for the index specimen
> exists, will reproduce a similar protective association.

U2 is the parent's blocking objection converted from a caveat into a **measured quantity**.
**[K1]**, the closest prior work, names exactly this limitation in its own conclusion
("de-escalation may mark early recovery that is incompletely captured in structured data") and
does not measure it; the scoping review names "confounding by indication, survivor bias, and
treatment-selection bias" and does not measure it; DIANA uses IP weighting and still says residual
confounding is likely. The negative control is the advance.

## 2. Two deliverables, both of which survive a null causal estimate

### Deliverable (a) — anchor measurement-validation result

The *same* clone-censor-weight estimator, the *same* covariates, the *same* outcome, run at three
anchors in the same patients:

| Anchor | Definition | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `T_conv_cx` | index specimen `charttime` + 72 h (culture-date / day-3 convention) | comparator |
| `T_conv_rx` | first systemic antibacterial `starttime` + 72 h (the **[K1]** convention) | comparator |
| `T_info` | `storetime` of the first susceptibility panel on the index specimen | proposed |

Reported: (i) the exposure cross-tabulation and discordance (already **[OBSERVED]** at 15.8% for
`T_conv_cx`), (ii) the fraction of episodes whose conventional anchor precedes the existence of any
susceptibility result (**[OBSERVED]** 48.9%), (iii) `RD_conv_cx`, `RD_conv_rx`, `RD_info` with
subject-level paired bootstrap CIs for the *differences* `RD_conv − RD_info`.
**This deliverable is a number regardless of whether any RD is null**: it quantifies how much
anchor choice moves an estimate in this literature, in the dataset **[K1]** itself used.

### Deliverable (b) — causal emulation with a pre-result pseudo-anchor negative control

Run the identical estimator at:

| Anchor | Definition | Information state |
|---|---|---|
| `T_pre` | `T_info − 48 h` | no susceptibility panel for the index specimen exists in the record |
| `T_pre_strict` | 1 h before the earliest `storetime` of **any** `hosp/microbiologyevents` row (incl. Gram stain) for that `hadm_id`, if that time is still ≥ ICU `intime` and ≥ (first broad-spectrum `starttime` + 6 h) | no microbiology result of any kind exists |
| `T_info` | as above | susceptibility panel stored |

**Inferential logic.** At `T_pre` no clinician can be narrowing *in response to a susceptibility
result*, because none exists. Any association between "narrowed within the grace window after
`T_pre`" and 30-day mortality is therefore not an information-response effect; it is the
selection signal — clinicians narrow the patients who are already turning the corner — plus
whatever direct effect empiric narrowing itself has. Formally this is a **negative-control
exposure** in the Lipsitch sense: an exposure that shares the confounding structure of the
exposure of interest but cannot share its hypothesised causal pathway. A genuine
information-response effect therefore requires `RD_info` to be *more protective than* `RD_pre`.

- **[OBSERVED]** feasibility: 2,354/3,785 (62.2%) of index episodes remain eligible at
  `T_info − 48 h` (still in ICU, broad-spectrum agent active). De-escalation rate at the
  pseudo-anchor 121/2,354 = 5.1%, versus 15.8% at the real anchor among the *same* episodes — the
  rate rises when information arrives, which is a necessary (not sufficient) condition for the
  design to be informative.

**Assumption, stated as an assumption.** The contrast `RD_info − RD_pre` identifies the
information-response component only if *the propensity to narrow empirically is informative about
the propensity to narrow on a result* — i.e. the healthy-responder selection operating at `T_pre`
has the same sign and comparable magnitude as at `T_info`, conditional on the measured covariates.
**This assumption can fail**, in at least four ways we will report rather than hide:
1. **Different causal content.** Empiric narrowing before a result risks losing coverage; if that
   has a real harmful effect, `RD_pre` is biased toward harm and the contrast overstates an
   information effect.
2. **Different decision-makers.** Clinicians who narrow without a result may be systematically more
   risk-tolerant or treating milder suspicion; their patients differ in unmeasured ways.
3. **Different eligible sets.** Only 62.2% survive to `T_pre` eligibility
   (**[OBSERVED]**); the primary contrast is therefore computed on the **intersection** of the two
   eligible sets and re-weighted to the `T_info` population; the full-set contrast is secondary.
4. **Selection magnitude may scale with lead time.** Patients are on average sicker and less
   differentiated 48 h earlier, so selection could be weaker at `T_pre`, attenuating `RD_pre` and
   inflating the apparent information effect. Mitigation: report `T_pre` at −24 h, −48 h and −72 h
   and check whether `RD_pre` is monotone in lead time. If it is, the contrast is confounded by
   lead time and we will say so.

Because of (1)-(4), the negative control is a **bound and a diagnostic**, not an unbiased
decomposition. Its most defensible use is the adverse direction: if `RD_pre ≈ RD_info`, the
information-response explanation is not needed to account for the association, and the
literature's de-escalation associations are consistent with selection.

### 3.1 Explicit bound on the information-anchor claim

`storetime` is **the time the result row was stored in the record**. It is *not* proof that a
clinician read the result, and it is *not* the earliest time the information existed.

- Upward bound (too late): laboratories phone critical results. Verbal/preliminary telephone
  reporting is **not captured anywhere in MIMIC-IV 3.1**. **[OBSERVED]**: of 321,328 susceptibility
  rows with non-empty `comments`, **0** mention "PRELIM" (`probe01.log`).
- Downward bound (too early): nothing records when the result was opened or acted on. A patient
  whose result sat unread for 12 h is scored as informed at `T_info`.
- Preliminary information other than susceptibility *does* arrive earlier and is partially
  captured: **[OBSERVED]** 40.0% (51,596/129,011) of panel-carrying specimens have some earlier
  stored row (dominated by GRAM STAIN, 36,158; bottle Gram stains 22,684); 996 specimens have a
  preliminary organism-ID row (org_name, no `ab_name`) before the panel, lead time p10 0.1 h,
  median 45.4 h, p90 144.6 h (`probe03.log`). In the analytic cohort, **79.8%** (3,027/3,792) of
  index specimens have a same-specimen Gram stain stored before `T_info`, lead time p10 42.7,
  median 68.2, p90 123.6 h (`probe05.log`).

**This directly constrains deliverable (b)**: because the median Gram-stain lead time (68.2 h)
exceeds 48 h, `T_pre = T_info − 48 h` is a *pre-susceptibility* anchor, **not** a pre-information
anchor — a Gram stain is often already available. Gram stain gives organism class, not
susceptibility, and cannot support narrowing to an agent reported S; but it can support some
narrowing. That is exactly why `T_pre_strict` (before *any* stored microbiology row) is
pre-specified as the co-primary negative control, and why the two will be reported side by side.
If they disagree, the Gram-stain channel is the explanation and we will report it as such.

## 4. Population, temporal boundaries, strategies, outcomes, estimand, analysis

### 4.1 Population and index event (measured cascade)

Adults (`patients.anchor_age ≥ 18`) in an ICU stay, with a *first* qualifying bacterial isolate
panel per hospital admission. Qualifying panel = has `hadm_id`, non-screening organism, treated
specimen type (blood, respiratory incl. BAL/mini-BAL/bronchial washings/sputum, urine, tissue,
abscess, peritoneal/other sterile fluid, bile), non-null `storetime`. Patient must be in the ICU at
`T_info` with ICU `intime < T_info`, and must have a systemic broad-spectrum antibacterial order
active at `T_info`.

### 4.2 Temporal boundaries

Time origin = the anchor (`T_info`, `T_conv_cx`, `T_conv_rx`, `T_pre`, `T_pre_strict`).
Grace/decision window G = (anchor, anchor + 48 h] (sensitivity: 24 h). Follow-up for the primary
outcome = anchor to anchor + 30 days; for organ-support-free days = anchor to anchor + 28 days.
Admissions are used once (first qualifying panel); patients contributing several admissions are
retained with subject-level clustering in all bootstraps. MIMIC-IV 3.1 is a single-centre
2008-2022-era snapshot with per-patient random date shifting; only *within-patient* intervals are
used, which date shifting preserves exactly.

### 4.3 Treatment strategies

The regimen at any instant t = the set of systemic antibacterial orders with
`starttime ≤ t < stoptime` (route filtered to systemic; **[OBSERVED]** 980,632 antibiotic-token
prescription rows parsed, 46,059 dropped as topical/ophthalmic/oral-vancomycin/desensitisation/
unmapped across 89 distinct strings, 3,301 dropped as non-systemic route, **928,662 systemic rows
retained across 235,591 admissions**, `probe04.log`). Regimen spectrum = **max** over concurrently
active agents (not the sum), so adding an adjunct such as an aminoglycoside or rifampin cannot
masquerade as escalation.

- **Strategy N (narrow).** Within G, regimen max spectrum score falls strictly below its value at
  the anchor and stays at or below (anchor value − 1) for at least 24 h thereafter.
- **Strategy C (continue broad).** No such reduction during G.
- **Strategy X (stop all).** All systemic antibacterials stopped within G — **[OBSERVED]** 168/3,785
  (4.4%). This is *cessation*, not de-escalation; it is analysed as a separate third strategy and
  excluded from the primary N-vs-C contrast, because it conflates "infection ruled out" with
  "narrowing".

**[OBSERVED]** under the measured `T_info` anchor with G = 48 h: Strategy N = 667/3,785 (17.6%).

Two further exposure definitions are prespecified, each addressing a different measurement threat:
- **E2, panel-referenced (does not use the spectrum matrix at all):** the regimen after the
  decision contains an agent reported **S** for the index organism on that patient's own panel and
  no longer contains an anti-pseudomonal or anti-MRSA agent that the panel renders unnecessary.
- **E3, antibiogram-referenced (OSAR-style, per [K3]):** instead of an expert coverage matrix, score
  each agent by its **effective spectrum measured in MIMIC-IV itself** — the fraction of all
  isolates in `hosp/microbiologyevents` reported S to that agent within the preceding 365
  shifted-calendar days. With 1,410,258 susceptibility rows **[OBSERVED]** this is computable
  inside the dataset and makes "narrow" mean *locally* narrow, which is the explicit advance of
  **[K3]** over consensus indices.

### 4.4 Outcomes

1. **Primary: 30-day all-cause mortality from the anchor.** `admissions.deathtime` (datetime) for
   in-hospital death; `patients.dod` (date only, assigned 12:00 when it is the only source) for
   post-discharge death. **[OBSERVED]** 30-day death ascertained for 1,173/3,785 (31.0%) of index
   episodes; crude, confounded, descriptive only: 31.6% (984/3,118) not de-escalated vs 28.3%
   (189/667) de-escalated. **[OBSERVED]** 38,301/364,627 patients (10.5%) have a non-null `dod`.
   Because out-of-hospital death ascertainment in MIMIC-IV is incomplete, the prespecified
   sensitivity outcome is **in-hospital death by day 30**, counting discharge alive as survival —
   the conservative standard choice — and both are reported.
2. **Organ-support-free days to day 28.** Days alive and free of (i) invasive ventilation
   (procedureevents 225792/227194 and chartevents 223849), (ii) any vasopressor infusion
   (inputevents 221906/221289/221749/221662/221653/222315), (iii) CRRT (procedureevents 225802).
   Death within 28 days scores 0. Discharge alive before day 28 counts as support-free.
3. **Recurrent or resistant infection by day 28.** A *new* `micro_specimen_id` with `charttime` >
   anchor + 48 h and a positive isolate, classified as (a) **recurrence**: same organism genus as
   the index isolate; (b) **resistant breakthrough**: an isolate with `interpretation = 'R'` to at
   least one agent the patient was receiving at the time of collection; (c) **C. difficile**:
   positive C. difficile test row. Reported separately, each as a 28-day cumulative incidence with
   death as a competing risk (Aalen-Johansen).
   *Adjudication limit:* whether a new positive culture represents infection or colonisation, and
   whether a given narrowing was microbiologically appropriate, cannot be settled computationally
   (§6.3).

### 4.5 Estimand

Per-protocol 30-day risk difference, **RD = Pr[Y30 = 1 | strategy N] − Pr[Y30 = 1 | strategy C]**,
in the population eligible at the given anchor, under the strategies "narrow within 48 h of the
anchor and do not re-escalate for 24 h" vs "do not narrow within 48 h of the anchor", with
adherence to assigned strategy enforced by censoring and the resulting selection corrected by
inverse-probability-of-censoring weights. There is no intention-to-treat version: both arms are
*strategies*, not assignments, and the whole point is adherence over the grace window.

### 4.6 Analysis: clone-censor-weight sequential trial emulation

1. **Clone.** At the anchor, duplicate every eligible episode into both strategy arms. Both clones
   start follow-up at the anchor. This removes immortal-time bias by construction: no clone can be
   classified by future information, and the grace window is covered by both arms.
2. **Censor.** Censor the N-clone at the first hour it becomes impossible to comply (grace window
   expires with no qualifying reduction, or a reduction is reversed within 24 h); censor the
   C-clone at the first qualifying reduction. Also censor at ICU/hospital discharge for the
   organ-support outcome and at loss of mortality ascertainment (discharge alive with no `dod`) in
   the sensitivity outcome.
3. **Weight.** Stabilised inverse-probability-of-artificial-censoring weights from a pooled
   logistic hazard model of censoring per 6-hour block over the grace window, with time-varying
   covariates measured in the preceding 24 h: worst/last MAP, vasopressor norepinephrine-equivalent
   rate and its 24-h slope, lactate and its 24-h slope, WBC, temperature, creatinine, bilirubin,
   platelets, P/F ratio, GCS total, urine output, ventilation and CRRT status, plus baseline
   covariates (age, sex, first care unit, admission type, Elixhauser comorbidity from
   `diagnoses_icd`, surgical service from `hosp/services`, source-control procedure before the
   anchor, specimen type, organism, bacteraemia, hours of broad therapy before the anchor, number
   of active agents, baseline spectrum score, and the **measured collection-to-result lag**).
   Weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles; the untruncated and truncated results are both
   reported, with the weight distribution and a positivity diagnostic (proportion of the covariate
   space with predicted narrowing probability < 0.01 or > 0.99).
4. **Estimate.** Weighted Kaplan-Meier 30-day risk per arm and the risk difference; 500
   subject-level nonparametric bootstrap replicates for CIs (the same bootstrap draws are reused
   across anchors so that `RD_conv − RD_info` and `RD_info − RD_pre` get *paired* intervals).
5. **Informative censoring** is handled by (3); the key diagnostic is whether the weighted baseline
   covariate distributions of the two arms match at each grace-window block (standardised mean
   differences < 0.10 reported for every covariate, at every anchor).
6. **Anchors.** The entire pipeline runs unchanged at the five anchors in §2, producing
   `RD_conv_cx`, `RD_conv_rx`, `RD_info`, `RD_pre`, `RD_pre_strict`.

## 5. Method comparison on the same question

The scientific uncertainty that decides between methods is **not predictive accuracy**. It is: *how
much of the healthy-responder selection can the nuisance model absorb?* `RD_pre` is only a valid
measure of selection if the model of "who gets narrowed" is well specified. If the real propensity
depends on *trajectory* features — the 24-h slope of vasopressor dose, lactate clearance, fever
defervescence — and the baseline model only sees last values and linear terms, the baseline will
**underestimate** `RD_pre`, and the headline contrast `RD_info − RD_pre` will spuriously look like
an information effect. The two methods therefore test a substantive claim, not a leaderboard.

### Baseline (primary, prespecified)

- **Method:** pooled logistic regression for the censoring/treatment hazards and weighted
  Kaplan-Meier for the outcome, as in §4.6; restricted cubic splines (4 knots) on continuous
  covariates; no interactions beyond strategy × block.
- **Exact inputs:** the cohort table built from the bindings in §3 — one row per clone per 6-hour
  block over the 48-h grace window (≈ 3,785 × 2 × 8 ≈ 60,560 rows for the primary population),
  with ~55 covariate columns.
- **Fitting target:** per-block probability of artificial censoring (deviation from the assigned
  strategy), and per-block hazard of death.
- **Split:** none for the estimate itself (causal estimation, not prediction); 5-fold cross-fitting
  of the nuisance models to avoid over-fitting-induced weight instability; the cohort build is
  frozen before any outcome model is fit.
- **Uncertainty:** 500 subject-level bootstrap replicates; standardised mean differences; weight
  distributions; positivity diagnostics.
- **Resource/time:** CPU only. Cohort build streams `microbiologyevents`, `prescriptions`,
  `labevents`, `chartevents`, `inputevents`, `procedureevents`, `outputevents` from the zip; the
  recovered probes already streamed the first two end-to-end inside one attempt, and `chartevents`
  (the largest) is read once with an itemid filter. Estimate: ~3 h on 16 CPUs for build +
  500 bootstraps × 5 anchors. Within `solver_planning_limits` (16 CPUs, 28,800 s).
  *Measured:* the cohort build through the broad-spectrum filter is measured feasible
  (`probe04.log`). *Unverified:* the `chartevents`/`labevents` covariate extraction timing.

### Substantive alternative (co-primary sensitivity, prespecified)

- **Method:** longitudinal targeted minimum loss-based estimation (LTMLE) over the same
  clone-censor structure, with **cross-fitted gradient-boosted-tree** nuisance estimators
  (treatment/censoring and outcome regressions) in a small SuperLearner library
  {GBT, random forest, logistic with splines, intercept-only}, with the same stabilised-weight
  truncation.
- **Exact inputs:** identical clone-block table, plus explicitly engineered trajectory features the
  baseline discards: 6/12/24-h slopes and area-under-curve for norepinephrine-equivalent dose,
  lactate, temperature, WBC, P/F, creatinine, and a "consecutive hours of improvement" counter.
- **Fitting target:** the same nuisance functions; the target parameter is the same 30-day RD, so
  the two methods are directly comparable on the identical estimand.
- **Split:** 10-fold cross-fitting; the SuperLearner weights are chosen by cross-validated
  log-likelihood inside training folds only.
- **Uncertainty:** influence-curve-based standard errors *and* the same 500 subject-level bootstrap,
  reported together; disagreement between the two is itself reported.
- **What it reveals that the baseline discards:** whether the "already improving" trajectory signal
  — the exact mechanism the parent assessment named — is strong enough that a flexible model
  materially increases `|RD_pre|`. If LTMLE's `RD_pre` moves toward `RD_info` relative to the
  baseline's, then the apparent information effect in the baseline was model misspecification, and
  the adverse conclusion of §6 is strengthened. The baseline cannot detect this about itself.
- **Resource/time:** CPU; ~4 h on 16 CPUs for 5 anchors × 10 folds × bootstrap. Doubly robust
  estimation at this sample size does not need a GPU.
- **Selection:** run **both**; the baseline is the headline estimate, LTMLE is a prespecified
  co-primary for the `RD_info − RD_pre` contrast, and a material disagreement is reported as an
  unresolved model-dependence rather than resolved by choosing the preferred number.

### Alternative considered and deferred, with the condition for revisiting

- **Sequence model (GRU/temporal transformer) for the narrowing propensity on hourly physiology,
  1 A100, ~2 h.** *Deferred for this question.* With 3,785 index episodes, ~660 narrowing events and
  a 48-h decision window, a recurrent propensity model has far more parameters than events; the
  deliverable depends on a **calibrated** propensity, and a miscalibrated flexible model damages
  IPW more than an underfit one. Engineered slopes already capture the specific trajectory content
  the clinical story names. *Condition to revisit:* if the prespecified expansion to the
  21,372-admission ICU population (§4.1) is used, or if the LTMLE-vs-baseline `RD_pre` gap exceeds
  3 pp — i.e. if flexibility demonstrably matters — then a sequence propensity with explicit
  calibration assessment becomes worth its cost. This condition is recorded so the decision can be
  revisited on evidence rather than taste.
- **Instrumental variable (laboratory turnaround time as an instrument for information timing).**
  Deferred: turnaround correlates with specimen type and organism, which independently predict
  outcome, so the exclusion restriction is not credible. Recorded because it is the obvious
  alternative identification strategy and should not be silently dropped.

## 6. Falsification criteria and decision rules

Precision is bounded by the measured cohort. Crude, unweighted: 667 exposed / 3,118 unexposed with
~30% 30-day mortality gives SE(RD) ≈ 1.9 pp, i.e. a 95% CI half-width ≈ 3.8 pp; weighting and
cloning will widen this. We therefore prespecify an **equivalence margin of ±5 pp** and an
**inconclusive band** at a half-width > 6 pp, and we state in advance that this study cannot
adjudicate differences smaller than ~4 pp.

### 6.1 Deliverable (a), the anchor measurement result

- **Anchor matters (the claim U1 is supported):** `|RD_conv_cx − RD_info| ≥ 3 pp` with a paired
  bootstrap CI excluding 0, **or** exposure discordance ≥ 10% (already **[OBSERVED]** at 15.8%).
  Interpretation: the conventional anchor biases this literature by a quantified amount; day-3/
  culture-date anchoring should be reported with a lag-sensitivity analysis.
- **Anchor does not matter:** paired CI for `RD_conv − RD_info` contained in ±3 pp. Interpretation:
  reassuring for **[K1]** and the day-3 convention *in this dataset*; the misclassification is real
  but non-differential with respect to the outcome. This is a useful negative result and is
  reported as the primary finding of (a).

### 6.2 Deliverable (b), the negative control

- **Supportive of a genuine information-response effect:** `RD_info` is protective and
  `RD_info − RD_pre` is more protective than 0 with a paired bootstrap CI excluding 0, **and** the
  same ordering holds for `T_pre_strict` and is **not** monotone in pseudo-anchor lead time
  (−24/−48/−72 h). Interpretation: part of the de-escalation association is attributable to acting
  on susceptibility information, not only to selecting improving patients. This would still not
  establish that de-escalation *causes* benefit; it would establish that the association is not
  entirely selection.
- **Adverse (and fully interpretable):** `RD_pre ≈ RD_info`, i.e. the paired CI for
  `RD_info − RD_pre` is contained within ±5 pp. Interpretation: an identically defined narrowing
  exposure placed where **no susceptibility information existed** reproduces the same association.
  The de-escalation associations in the observational literature are then consistent with
  healthy-responder selection, and the clinical reading is that observational de-escalation
  "benefit" should not be used to justify the practice — which is coherent with the randomized
  **[K2]** result and with the scoping review's "does not establish equivalence, non-inferiority, or
  a survival benefit". **This is the result the proposal is designed to be able to deliver**, and
  it is the most likely one.
- **Adverse in the other direction:** `RD_pre` more protective than `RD_info` beyond 5 pp.
  Interpretation: the pseudo-anchor is capturing *more* selection than the real anchor (plausible
  if empiric narrowing is reserved for the clearly-improving), which invalidates the negative
  control as a clean lower bound and must be reported as such, not reinterpreted as harm from
  information-responsive de-escalation.
- **Inconclusive:** half-width > 6 pp on the key contrast. Then the prespecified expansion (§4.1)
  is run once; if still > 6 pp, the conclusion is that MIMIC-IV 3.1 alone cannot resolve U2 and a
  multi-centre dataset or a randomized design is required. An imprecise null is reported as
  imprecise, not as refutation.
- **Design-validity gate (must pass before any of the above is interpreted):** the de-escalation
  rate must be materially higher at `T_info` than at `T_pre` (**[OBSERVED]** 15.8% vs 5.1% on the
  same episodes). If a re-run does not reproduce that gap, the anchors are not distinguishing
  information states and the whole contrast is void.

### 6.3 What is computationally checkable, and what is not

**Computationally checkable in MIMIC-IV 3.1:** every count in §1-§4; the lag distribution; the
exposure cross-tabulation; all five RDs and their paired contrasts; weight and positivity
diagnostics; organ-support-free days; new-positive-culture incidence; the baseline-vs-LTMLE
comparison; the sensitivity to the three exposure definitions (spectrum matrix, own-panel S,
MIMIC antibiogram).

**Requires clinical adjudication (not resolvable by this study):**
(i) whether a given narrowing was *microbiologically appropriate* for the isolate and site — the
own-panel definition E2 approximates it but cannot judge source, dosing or penetration;
(ii) whether a positive urine or respiratory culture represented infection or colonisation;
(iii) whether a new positive culture was a true recurrence, a new infection, or contamination;
(iv) whether source control was adequate (ICD procedure codes are date-only and incomplete).

**Requires other data or another study:** whether and when the result was actually read or
telephoned (no field exists); whether de-escalation *causes* benefit (requires randomization — the
only existing trial **[K2]** is 116 patients and did not show non-inferiority on its primary
endpoint); external validity beyond one US academic centre; antimicrobial-resistance ecology
effects, which need population-level follow-up MIMIC-IV cannot provide.

## 7. Alternative explanations and threats

1. **Confounding by indication / healthy-responder selection.** The parent's blocking objection.
   Addressed by time-varying IPW *and*, decisively, by making it the measured quantity via the
   pre-result pseudo-anchor. Residual: unmeasured bedside judgement (gestalt, family discussions,
   goals-of-care changes) that precedes both narrowing and death. A goals-of-care/comfort-measures
   transition is partially visible via `discharge_location` and vasopressor withdrawal but is not
   reliably timestamped; we will report the proportion of deaths preceded by simultaneous
   withdrawal of all organ support as a descriptive flag.
2. **Source control and site effects.** Drainage or line removal both improves prognosis and
   licenses narrowing. Proxies: `hosp/procedures_icd` (date-only), `hosp/services.curr_service`.
   Incompletely observed — this is the seed's own stated limitation and it survives. Mitigation:
   stratified estimates by specimen type and by surgical vs medical service; a prespecified
   analysis restricted to bloodstream (347 episodes **[OBSERVED]**, underpowered alone) and to
   respiratory (2,283 episodes) sites.
3. **Polymicrobial and culture-negative sepsis.** The design conditions on a positive panel, so it
   excludes culture-negative sepsis entirely — this is a scope restriction, stated, not a bias.
   Polymicrobial specimens (multiple `isolate_num` on one `micro_specimen_id`) are handled by
   defining the index panel as the *earliest-stored* panel and E2 as "S for **all** isolates on that
   specimen"; a sensitivity excludes polymicrobial specimens.
4. **Spectrum-ranking measurement choice.** The primary score is an explicit additive 13-organism-
   group coverage matrix (`spectrum_ranks.tsv`, `spectrum_score.py`, attached), built so each point
   is traceable to a stated microbiology claim rather than copied from a table we could not inspect.
   **[K3]** documents that prior spectrum scores (including the ASI, whose abstract we inspected)
   were assigned by expert consensus and applied retrospectively, and that a resistance-benchmarked
   alternative changes conclusions; the scoping review reports that spectrum-ranking systems across
   the de-escalation literature "were non-uniform". We therefore (i) use regimen **max**, not sum,
   so adjuncts cannot look like escalation; (ii) prespecify E2, which uses the patient's own panel
   and does not depend on the matrix at all; (iii) prespecify E3, a MIMIC-IV-internal
   antibiogram-weighted effective spectrum in the spirit of **[K3]**. Agreement across the three is
   itself a reported result. Residual: the matrix treats all organism groups as equally important.
5. **Contaminated or colonising cultures.** URINE is 759/3,785 index episodes **[OBSERVED]** and
   urinary colonisation in catheterised ICU patients is common; coagulase-negative staphylococci in
   a single blood specimen are usually contaminants. Prespecified sensitivity: exclude urine-only
   episodes and exclude likely-contaminant organisms; report whether the key contrast changes sign.
   True adjudication needs chart review (§6.3).
6. **Unobserved verbal/preliminary reporting.** Quantified and bounded in §3.1: 0/321,328
   susceptibility `comments` mention PRELIM; 79.8% of index specimens have an earlier same-specimen
   Gram stain (median 68.2 h before `T_info`). Telephoned results would make `T_info` *late*, which
   would attenuate the measured contrast toward the null — i.e. it biases against finding an
   information effect, and therefore cannot manufacture a supportive result, only an adverse one.
   That asymmetry is stated as part of the interpretation.
7. **MIMIC-IV date shifting.** Each patient's dates are shifted by a random patient-specific offset
   into 2100-2200; *within-patient* intervals are preserved exactly, so every lag, anchor and
   window in this design is valid. What is lost: calendar-time adjustment, seasonality, secular
   stewardship trends, and any linkage to external resistance surveillance. The E3 antibiogram is
   therefore computed on shifted calendar time within patient-years, which mixes eras; this is a
   stated limitation of E3 and a reason E3 is secondary.
8. **Single-centre, single-era.** All estimates are internal to one US academic centre.
   Generalisation to settings with different turnaround times — the whole point of the anchor — is
   exactly what is **not** established; the SPEED study (abstract inspected) shows rapid phenotypic
   AST can cut time-to-de-escalation from 31 h to 16 h, so the lag distribution here is a property
   of this laboratory, not of medicine.
9. **Competing risks and discharge.** Discharge alive before day 30 with no `dod` is informative
   censoring for post-discharge death; handled by the two-outcome strategy in §4.4 and by
   Aalen-Johansen for the infection outcomes.

## 9. What changed relative to the parent, and what remains uncertain

**Changed.** The parent seed asked whether post-de-escalation risk is lower in patients with clear
infection and improving organ function — a question whose own phrasing *is* the confounder. This
child (i) replaces the implicit culture-date/day-3 exposure anchor with a **measured**
information-availability anchor and quantifies the resulting misclassification (**[OBSERVED]**
15.8% discordance; 48.9% of conventional anchors precede the result's existence); (ii) converts the
blocking objection into a **measured negative-control estimate** at a pre-result pseudo-anchor
rather than another round of covariate adjustment; (iii) binds every variable to verified MIMIC-IV
3.1 columns and itemids with a measured cohort cascade (3,785 episodes, 3,553 subjects); (iv) adds
a baseline-vs-LTMLE comparison that tests the specific uncertainty (can a flexible nuisance model
absorb the trajectory-driven selection?) rather than chasing accuracy; (v) prespecifies decision
rules, including an explicitly interpretable adverse outcome.

**Remains uncertain.** (1) `storetime` bounds but does not establish clinician awareness, and
verbal reporting is unobservable. (2) The negative control's key assumption — that empiric-narrowing
propensity is informative about result-narrowing propensity — can fail in the four ways listed in
§2, and the Gram-stain lead time (median 68.2 h) means `T_pre` at −48 h is pre-susceptibility, not
pre-information; `T_pre_strict` exists for that reason but on a smaller set. (3) Source control is
incompletely observed and this does not go away. (4) Precision: the study can adjudicate ~4-5 pp
differences, not smaller. (5) Whether de-escalation *causes* benefit cannot be settled here at all;
the deliverables are a measurement-bias quantity and a selection quantity, both of which are
informative whether or not the causal estimate is null.
