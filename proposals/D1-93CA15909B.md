# Proposal: a correctly identified, capture-inclusive reassessment boundary after TACE

## Successor objective and substantive repair

This is a substantive child of assessed candidate `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent’s validated HCC estimand: one first TACE-like index per patient, a day-45 landmark, a 320-day horizon (days 46–365), first recorded liver-directed action as the primary prognostic outcome, competing recorded actions, capture-adjusted transparent baseline B0, trajectory model B2, irregular-time alternative M3, tuning-only operating thresholds, and a locked temporal test.

The strongest remaining threat is not model capacity. It is informative disappearance from the local record. The parent uses inverse-probability-of-censoring weights and day-45 capture features, but no available field establishes death, outside care, treatment intent, eligibility, or the reason a patient is no longer observed. A threshold can therefore appear to reduce alerts because it separates patients likely to remain observable and actionable from patients who disappear, rather than because the AFP/hepatic trajectory identifies a more specific recorded reassessment pathway.

The repair is a capture-inclusive stress test, not a change to the primary recorded-care estimand. In addition to the parent’s IPCW analysis, the solver must explicitly model/report an administrative end-of-local-observation state and decompose the locked-test boundary by that state. The trajectory boundary is promoted only when its alert-burden reduction persists among patients with continued local observation and is not concentrated among patients who disappear from the local record. This does not turn observation into a clinical outcome.

## Evidence-supported claim, unresolved claim, and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by inspected local evidence is operational: HCC tables contain linkable encounter, procedure, diagnosis and dated laboratory records. The actual source audit found 105,044 encounter rows and 338,040 procedure rows. The encounter table has dated `Encounter time` for all audited rows; `Admission time` is missing in 3,587 rows and `Discharge time` in 252 rows. Procedure rows repeat encounter keys and must be aggregated before joins. These facts establish a computable local recorded-care study, not tumor response, recurrence, treatment failure, survival, or useful clinical action.

The parent’s unresolved claim is that adding an early AFP/hepatic trajectory to a capture-adjusted model can produce a more specific prospective reassessment boundary for a later recorded liver-directed action than capture-adjusted history alone.

The repaired primary hypothesis is:

> In adults with a structured HCC diagnosis and a first TACE-like recorded procedure who reach the day-45 landmark without an early liver-directed procedure, B2’s prespecified trajectory boundary will reduce the alert fraction versus the capture-adjusted B0 by at least 10 absolute percentage points at 90% sensitivity for first recorded liver-directed action during days 46–365 on the locked temporal test, with a 95% interval excluding no reduction; the reduction will persist without sign reversal in a continued-local-observation stratum and will not be reproduced for the non-liver capture control or pre-index placebo trajectory.

B0 already contains day-45 capture features, so this comparison isolates the trajectory addition; it does not conflate a trajectory gain with adding observation variables.

This is a prognostic hypothesis about recorded local care. A recorded action is not treated as recurrence, progression, treatment failure, or benefit. “Continued local observation” is an EHR ascertainment state, not a claim that the patient was clinically well or that outside care was absent.

A secondary model-information hypothesis remains the parent’s: M3 is substantively useful only if its asynchronous latent trajectory and informative-observation representation yields a prespecified additional boundary improvement over B2 at the same target, with calibration and the same capture-inclusive checks. A generic small score improvement is not sufficient.

## Population, index, landmark, and temporal boundaries

Use the same frozen parent population and vocabulary.

- One index per patient: the earliest qualifying procedure start time after collapsing qualifying rows within 24 hours.
- TACE-like procedure: case-insensitive `TACE`, a procedure name containing `transarterial chemoembolization`, or containing both `hepatic artery` and `embolization`.
- Require age >=18 and nonmissing `sex` in the joined encounter.
- Require a diagnosis name containing literal `hepatocellular carcinoma` in a joined encounter from 180 days before through 7 days after index.
- Include index dates through 2025-01-01 so a full 365-day horizon is available.
- Exclude from the day-45 risk set a procedure after index +24 hours through day 45 if it is repeat-TACE-like or alternate liver-directed. Report those early actions separately.
- Alternate liver-directed names contain `resection`, `ablation`, `radiofrequency`, or `microwave`, excluding TACE-like rows.
- Primary follow-up is index day 46 through day 365. The primary competing recorded outcomes are first repeat-TACE-like action, first alternate liver-directed action, and combined liver-directed action. First non-liver procedure remains a capture-control outcome, not a biological competing outcome.
- Exact timestamp ties between liver-directed and non-liver procedures are a tie category; exclude them from the primary ordered contrast and assign both possible ways in sensitivity analyses.

The primary estimand remains the parent’s paired-trajectory boundary among complete paired-trajectory, day-45-eligible patients. The full-index analysis still reports trajectory availability, missingness, and observation opportunity for every eligible index reaching day 45. No unobserved biological response is imputed.

## Exact capture-inclusive state

Construct a dated local-contact process from the joined `encounters` rows. For each encounter, parse any nonmissing `encounter time`, `admission time`, and `discharge time`; a contact timestamp is the maximum valid timestamp in that encounter. Do not use name, phone, ID-card, insurance-card or other identifying fields as predictors.

After removing patients with a primary outcome before the relevant time:

1. A first liver-directed action is the first qualifying procedure at days 46–365.
2. A first non-liver procedure is the first procedure not in the liver-directed vocabulary, ordered against the liver-directed outcomes as in the parent.
3. For patients with neither action by day 365, define an administrative `end_of_local_observation` time as one day after the last valid dated encounter contact after day 45, if that time is on or before day 365. If there is no dated encounter after day 45, set it to day 46. If the last valid contact is day 365, there is no end-of-observation event within the horizon.
4. Patients with neither action nor end-of-local-observation are `observed_no_action` through day 365.

This state is deliberately administrative and retrospective: it means only that the supplied HCC tables contain no later dated encounter after the final observed contact. It does not assert death, treatment discontinuation, outside care, or lack of clinical need. Report a sensitivity using a last-contact definition based on `visit time` alone, because `admission time`/`discharge time` are incompletely recorded. Also report the parent’s IPCW analysis unchanged; the capture-inclusive state is a stress test and does not replace it.

Define the stability stratum before looking at locked outcomes as patients with at least one dated encounter contact in the final 90 days of follow-up (days 276–365) and no administrative end-of-local-observation event before day 365. This is an ascertainment stratum only. If the stratum has too few liver-directed events for an interval, report the boundary stability result as inconclusive rather than relaxing the rule.

## Frozen trajectory and predictors

Use the parent’s exact trajectory:

- AFP: last parseable numeric value in days -30 through -1 and first in days 7–45; use (z=log1p(AFP)), improvement if post (z le 0.5) pre (z), and retain continuous change, assay/qualitative flags and missingness.
- Albumin, total bilirubin, `prothrombin time ratio`, and platelets: last pre-index and first early-post value; non-deterioration means post >= pre for albumin/platelets and post <= pre for bilirubin/coagulation ratio.
- Primary trajectory category: AFP improvement plus non-deterioration in at least two hepatic proxies.
- Do not call `Prothrombin Time Ratio` INR, pool assays, calculate ALBI/MELD, or pool cross-assay units; the catalog confirms no separate laboratory unit column.
- Capture features available by day 45: dated encounter counts/timing, laboratory row/day counts, assay availability, dated examination row counts/timing, non-liver procedure counts, and only declared dated order features if used. An order is not completion. Examination narrative text is excluded from primary models.

All feature construction, vocabulary, imputation, censoring models and thresholds are frozen before locked-test evaluation.

## Matched baseline and substantive irregular-time alternative

All models use the identical population, outcomes, capture-state definition, feature freeze, censoring convention, split and locked-test boundary procedure.

B0 is a regularized discrete-time cause-specific hazard model with age, sex, encounter department, pre-index diagnosis/procedure-family counts, pre-index assay summaries and missingness, day-45 capture features, and explicit hazards for liver-directed action, non-liver capture-control, and administrative end-of-local-observation. Its primary output is the 320-day liver-directed cumulative incidence; secondary outputs are non-liver and end-of-observation CIFs and calibration. B0 is the transparent capture-adjusted reference.

B2 adds only the frozen AFP/hepatic trajectory category, continuous component changes, component missingness and paired-trajectory availability. It is the primary scientific comparison against B0.

M3 is a low-rank irregular-time state-space model with AFP-informed latent activity and hepatic-function states, patient random intercepts/slopes, assay-specific informative-observation submodels, observation intensity and the same three cause-specific hazards. It estimates the day-45 state from measurements through day 45 and outputs posterior state/slope uncertainty. It may reveal asynchronous shape, discordance and measurement intensity lost by B2’s thresholded summary. It cannot recover unmeasured tumor burden, treatment intent, access, outside care or a biological response label.

The comparison asks whether trajectory shape and an explicit observation process carry stable information about the later recorded action beyond transparent capture adjustment, and whether that information survives a capture-inclusive falsification. It is not a contest for a small generic performance gain.

A transformer is deferred: current uncertainty is endpoint/capture identification and raw procedure vocabulary, not sequence capacity. Revisit it only if a future dataset adds validated longitudinal code semantics, a second institution, or enough independent data to test representation transport. A causal treatment-effect model is not selected because treatment intent, eligibility, imaging response, death and outside care are unavailable. A radiology-text model is not selected because narrative report interpretation is unvalidated and no image files or adjudicated response labels are present.

## Splits, boundary, capture stress test, uncertainty and falsification

Use patient-level temporal splits by index date: 2010–2022 fit, 2023 tuning, and 2024–2025-01-01 locked test. If the date split cannot be implemented, use a deterministic participant-hash fallback and label it internal, not external validation. For each target sensitivity in {80%, 90%, 95%}, select the largest predicted-risk threshold on the 2023 tuning set for which training-only-IPCW sensitivity is at least the target. The largest threshold minimizes the flagged fraction subject to that sensitivity; if no threshold reaches the target, record boundary failure rather than silently lowering the target. Use one threshold for tied predictions, and never retune on locked data. Bootstrap threshold uncertainty re-selects the threshold inside each tuning resample, with a fixed-threshold sensitivity analysis.

At each threshold report cause-specific and competing-risk-aware sensitivity, alert fraction, PPV, false-alert fraction, alerts per liver-directed action, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, Brier/log score, and patient-clustered bootstrap intervals. Bootstrap must repeat threshold selection inside each resample or provide a fixed-threshold sensitivity analysis. Report IPCW overlap, truncation and calibration diagnostics.

The new capture-inclusive outputs are:

- a patient-level `observation_state.csv` with first liver action, first non-liver action, administrative end-of-local-observation time, observed-no-action state, and the exact contact timestamp used;
- a joint three-state cause-specific/CIF table for liver action, non-liver action and end-of-observation;
- boundary burden and sensitivity stratified by continued-local-observation versus administrative end-of-observation;
- last-contact sensitivity using `encounter time` alone and the parent’s full contact-time definition;
- calendar-era/quarter counts and boundary calibration summaries, reported descriptively and never used to retune the locked threshold.

Support for the primary hypothesis requires the corrected largest-threshold rule and all parent criteria plus: the B2-versus-B0 alert-burden reduction remains directionally favorable in the predeclared continued-observation stratum with an interval that does not show reversal; the gain is not explained by materially higher end-of-observation risk among B2/B0 discordant patients; the last-contact sensitivity does not reverse the interpretation; and non-liver and pre-index placebo controls remain negative. M3 additionally requires stable convergence, calibrated predictions, and a >=5 percentage-point lower alert fraction than B2 at 90% sensitivity with an interval excluding no reduction, under the same capture checks.

Adverse evidence is a null/reversed overall boundary gain, gain confined to end-of-observation patients, a continued-observation reversal, a comparable non-liver or placebo gain, strong threshold drift/calibration failure across 2024 test strata, failed positivity/weight overlap, or M3 nonconvergence. If trajectory availability predicts end-of-observation more strongly than liver action, interpret that as capture/selection evidence, not biological specificity.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few paired patients or liver actions in the stability stratum, sparse end-of-observation states, high loss to local follow-up, unresolved procedure-name ambiguity, same-time ties, missing timestamp fields that change the state materially, unstable bootstrap intervals, or inadequate event support in calendar strata. Do not lower margins or alter windows after seeing locked outcomes.

## Clinical interpretation boundary

A supportive result would establish that an HCC EHR-derived rule can stratify later probability of a recorded local liver-directed action with quantified alert burden and that the result is not obviously explained by disappearance from this local record. It would justify a prospective silent reassessment and expert-adjudication study.

It would not establish recurrence, viable tumor, radiologic response, treatment failure, treatment benefit, survival, cost-effectiveness, a recommendation to repeat TACE, or a clinical decision threshold. The 90% boundary is a research operating point, not a validated cutoff.

Unavailable evidence is explicit: no image files, validated radiology response labels, reliable tumor number/size/burden, treatment intent, eligibility/access reasons, death linkage for this analysis, outside-care capture, external institution, separate laboratory-unit column, or prospective impact evaluation. Stronger conclusions require expert radiology/clinical adjudication, intent and eligibility review, validated units, mortality and outside-care linkage, external validation, and a prospective impact study.
