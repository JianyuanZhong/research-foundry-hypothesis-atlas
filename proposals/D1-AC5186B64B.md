# Positivity-resolved deferral of marker-history–specific postoperative HCC surveillance inference

Status: substantive feasibility child of [prior hypothesis]. The fixed N=15,575 day-60 cohort, full 44-week day-61:365 grid, source bindings, and prohibition on biological recurrence claims are unchanged. Two aggregate audits and one deliberately minimal fit were executed. They establish that a transparent continuous-H model is numerically fit-able, but the parent's prespecified high/persistent-versus-low/clearing estimand is not identified with usable split/era positivity. No target-specific surveillance conclusion is supported.

## Scientific opening, supported claim, and unresolved claim

The strongest clinical claim supported by inspected evidence remains that incomplete AFP response measured at a 4–6-week postoperative visit was associated with worse recurrence-free and overall survival among 549 selected resected HCC patients whose preoperative AFP exceeded 400 ng/mL [K1]. It does not establish that persistent AFP is residual tumor, that follow-up measurement availability is ignorable, or that measuring more often improves outcomes.

Routine EHR observation is generated by care: order presence and timing can carry strong prognostic information independent of values [K2], and informative presence/observation methods distinguish care contact from the process producing repeated measurements while warning against mechanistic interpretation of latent recording states [K3]. These findings support explicit modeling of the recording process; they do not show why a particular postoperative HCC patient was measured.

The parent's unresolved claim was that pre-landmark marker history H predicts subsequent HCC-targeted laboratory measurement more strongly than continued local contact A, generic laboratory testing N-L, liver-injury monitoring L-I, or oncology-panel measurement O-TM. The strongest rival is a recording process in which prior measurement propensity, liver severity, service/era workflow, and same-encounter panel bundling jointly produce both H availability and later L-M, with apparent “persistence” amplified by regression to the mean and mathematical coupling of the preoperative value to the post/pre ratio.

The present advance is negative but consequential: it resolves whether the parent’s H contrast can support that discrimination. It cannot. The binary contrast has five high/persistent patients in the entire cohort, none in validation or locked test. A continuous bivariate H fit is computationally possible, but only 328 patients have a valid same-label numeric pre/post pair and only 75 later receive L-M. Thus this snapshot can describe the measurement-selection opening and audit models, but cannot provide a confirmatory target-specific conclusion. Running the larger latent model would add precision-looking structure without resolving positivity.

## Exact H construction and executed support audit

The parent left several operational choices implicit. This child freezes them for audit:

- A preoperative result is the latest plain numeric result for an exact marker label from 90 days before surgery to strictly before the surgery timestamp. A sensitivity that ends at day −1 excludes same-day preoperative measurements.
- The postoperative result is the latest plain numeric same-label result from day 15 through day 60.
- Exact labels are AFP (`alpha-fetoprotein`), PIVKA-II (`abnormal prothrombin`), and AFP-L3 (`AFP heterogeneity`). When several labels qualify, the deterministic anchor is AFP, then PIVKA-II, then AFP-L3. Label choice may not depend on outcomes.
- Inequality-qualified, missing, multi-number, and nonnumeric results are availability evidence but are not converted to numeric limits. A pair requires preoperative value >0 and postoperative value ≥0.
- The two primary continuous axes are training-frozen preoperative empirical rank within exact label and index era, and the log(post/pre) ratio. Raw values are never pooled across labels or eras because units, reference ranges, and platforms are absent.
- For the audited dichotomy only, “high/persistent” means preoperative rank ≥0.75 and post/pre ≥0.5; “low/clearing” means rank <0.75 and post/pre <0.5. Other pairs are discordant. These thresholds are operational recording-history states, not recurrence definitions.

On the frozen snapshot, the stricter day −90:−1 construction yielded 298 valid pairs; allowing a same-day result only when it precedes the surgery timestamp yielded 328/15,575 (2.1%). The timestamp construction is preferred because it preserves temporal order. Among 328: 201 have one eligible label, 87 have two, and 40 have three; the priority rule selected 292 AFP, 35 PIVKA-II, and one AFP-L3 pair. The selected H states were five high/persistent, 165 low/clearing, 157 discordant, and one unranked. High/persistent support was four train, zero validation, zero test, one reserve.

The continuous observed-pair sample was 199 train, 31 validation, 37 locked test, and 61 reserve. Any day-61:365 mark among those patients was sparse: A 86, L-M 75, N-L 79, L-I 79, and exact CEA/CA19-9 O-TM 74. Locked test contained only 9 L-M, 10 N-L, 10 L-I, and 9 O-TM patients. Training exact-label/era ECDF groups were adequate only for AFP (68, 55, 37, and 20 across four eras); PIVKA-II groups were 1, 10, 4, and 4, and AFP-L3 had no training member. Therefore pooled “within-label/era” scaling is not valid for all labels, and PIVKA-II/AFP-L3 can be descriptive only.

Panel overlap is even more consequential within H-observed patients. In train plus validation, there were 110 L-M, 128 N-L, 128 L-I, and 96 O-TM marked weeks across 230 patients. L-M-versus-O-TM discordance was only 12 L-M-only versus one O-TM-only week; validation contributed three versus zero. This leaves little information for distinguishing target selection from oncology-panel bundling.

These are aggregate feasibility results, not an association estimate or evidence against clinical surveillance.

## Fixed population, temporal boundaries, and variables

Population and exclusions remain exactly those of the parent: adults; earliest dated qualifying liver resection in 2012–2023 within 30 days of the linked encounter and in the same encounter as non-uncertain HCC; exclusion of transplant/donor/biopsy/cyst/abscess/repeat/recurrent wording and duplicate index-encounter procedure rows; exclusion of recorded directed HCC treatment during index+1 through day 60 under the strict dictionary, with broader cytotoxic sensitivity. Prior recorded treatment in days −180:−1 is a covariate. No later mark affects eligibility.

Every patient remains on the outcome-independent 44-week grid from day 61 through 365. A comes only from encounter interval overlap; A0 uses encounter start. “No mark” means no mark in these institutional files, not alive, retained, disease-free, or untreated outside the system. Numeric H is defined only in the 328 observed-pair patients; marker absent, pre-only, post-only, invalid-numeric, and unmatched-label states remain separate ascertainment categories in the full cohort and receive no imputed values.

The full cohort still supports descriptive comparison of ascertainment states: the inherited audit found 312 patients with any pre- and post-landmark marker observation, versus 10,753 pre-only, 64 post-only, and 4,446 neither; any A was 26.6% in the both-observed state versus 11.5% in neither. This is compatible with informative observation but does not identify an H effect.

## Transparent full-grid baseline: what was and was not fit

The mandatory scientific baseline remains a stacked recurrent-mark model on a common patient-week grid. Its final design uses exactly the continuous H-observed subset, outcomes A/L-M/N-L/L-I/O-TM, week spline, separate pre-rank and log-ratio terms, H×mark interactions, baseline severity/pathology, service, index era, length of stay, prior treatment, pre-landmark injury/observation history, and only t− lagged A/mark history. It must report standardized 365-day component risks and LM-minus-A/N-L/L-I/O-TM specificity, patient-cluster bootstrap uncertainty, calibration by mark/era/service, and leave-one-era-out estimates. Pre-rank and log ratio must be reported separately; a single composite is prohibited because baseline value appears in the ratio denominator and can induce regression-to-the-mean structure.

A bounded diagnostic fit used the same 44-week grid but only train plus validation (230 patients, 50,600 stacked mark rows), with mark intercepts, quadratic week, era, label, and both H axes by mark. It converged in eight iterations without warnings. This answers a narrow compute question: ordinary logistic fitting is numerically possible on CPU. It is not the final baseline: it omits the full severity/service/history basis, standardization, bootstrap, multiplicity control, and locked test.

The diagnostic’s broad intervals and strongest workflow comparator are cautionary. Per 1-SD pre-rank, the LM-versus-O-TM interaction odds ratio was 1.10 (95% CI 0.97–1.24); for log ratio it was 1.08 (0.94–1.24). LM-versus-N-L/L-I pre-rank interactions were about 1.19 with lower limits barely above one, while log-ratio contrasts included one. These exploratory coefficients are neither adjusted materiality estimates nor confirmatory findings; repeated fitting or threshold revision to make them favorable is prohibited. They show why O-TM is essential and why panel bundling remains the strongest rival.

The binary high/persistent-versus-low/clearing baseline must not be fit: its positivity gate failed before model fitting. The continuous final baseline may be retained only as explicitly exploratory method development; it cannot satisfy locked-test replication or the inherited ≥100-effective-patient contrast gate.

Measured compute was about 116 seconds for the timestamp H audit and 115 seconds for audit plus minimal fit on ordinary CPU. A final exploratory baseline is estimated at 8 CPUs, ≤64 GiB, ≤2 hours. No GPU is scientifically needed.

## Matched substantive recording-process alternative

The scientifically matched alternative is retained as a future-data design, not fit on this snapshot. On the identical H-observed patients, weeks, split, features, and five marks, fit a joint semi-Markov recording-process model:

- U is local-record activity anchored positively by A and allowed to load on all marks.
- Q is liver-severity monitoring anchored by L-I and allowed to load on N-L and L-M.
- B is oncology-panel propensity anchored by O-TM and allowed to load on L-M.
- V is target enrichment allowed to load on L-M but constrained to zero on A/N-L/L-I/O-TM.
- A measurement-error component represents latent preoperative and postoperative marker state within exact label; it estimates shrinkage from repeated values where available and keeps pre-state and change distinct. It is a model of noisy recorded marker history, not tumor biology.
- Transitions use baseline and t− history only; era/service enter U/Q/B. Weekly emissions are multilabel, preserving co-bundled and discordant weeks.

Compare U+Q+B against U+Q+B+V on held-out marked-process deviance and posterior predictive LM/O-TM, LM/L-I, and LM/N-L cross-cells. The alternative could reveal persistent target-only covariance after representing return, liver monitoring, panel bundling, and measurement-error regression—information the transparent independent-logit basis loses. It still cannot identify retention, intent, recurrence, or a biological mechanism.

Match the baseline’s exact inputs, split, outcome definitions, era exclusions, and uncertainty target. Require null/shared-contact/panel-only/regression-to-mean/target-specific simulation recovery, three seeds, no state switching, 90–98% interval coverage, and posterior predictive reproduction of comparator cross-cells. Select V only if it improves validation LM deviance by ≥1% without ≥1% improvement or deterioration being misattributed to comparator marks, directionally replicates in locked test, and the baseline specificity gates also pass.

This alternative is deferred because current locked-test support is nine LM and nine O-TM patients and there are no high/persistent test patients. One A100, 16 CPUs, ≤128 GiB, and 4–6 hours is an unverified future estimate; no GPU job is warranted now. Revisit only after a new snapshot or linked cohort supplies at least 100 effective patients per H contrast, ≥20 H-exposed LM and O-TM patients in validation and test, viable label-era scaling, and sufficient discordant LM/O-TM weeks. These are scientific support gates, not hardware limits.

## Falsification and interpretation

The strongest claim supported now is computational: exact H can be constructed for only 328 patients; the nominal dichotomy violates positivity; and a reduced continuous stacked model converges. The unresolved scientific claim—whether marker history predicts target-specific surveillance beyond recording processes—was not tested adequately.

For a future adequately supported analysis:

- Supportive evidence requires LM specificity over A, N-L, L-I, and O-TM for both continuous H axes under Holm-adjusted 95% intervals, absolute standardized specificity risk difference ≥0.02, ratio-of-risk-ratios ≥1.25, locked-test directional replication, no era/service sign reversal, acceptable calibration, and a target-specific permutation result. U+Q+B+V is corroborative only after recovery and held-out gates.
- Adverse evidence is a precise result excluding material specificity, especially LM no greater than O-TM or L-I, together with U+Q+B matching U+Q+B+V within the 1% held-out threshold. This favors contact, liver monitoring, panel bundling, and regression-to-the-mean as adequate observed explanations.
- Inconclusive evidence includes the current positivity failure, broad intervals, sparse discordant LM/O-TM cells, unstable label-era ranks, calibration failure, era/service reversal, or latent-state non-recovery. An imprecise null does not refute target-specific surveillance.

Current conclusion: adverse to feasibility of the parent’s confirmatory binary experiment, inconclusive about target-specific surveillance. The branch should be deferred rather than compiled as a solved or confirmatory experiment. A new dataset/snapshot, linked claims, or prospectively scheduled follow-up is required to reopen it; changing the threshold, pooling assays, inspecting the test repeatedly, or dropping O-TM would not be a valid repair.

## Deliverable and verifier boundary

Delivered here: exact H rules; flow/support/positivity by split, era, and label; full-grid mark overlap; a bounded continuous fit diagnostic; matched baseline and recording-process alternative; resource estimates; and threshold-linked deferral.

An automatic verifier can check source hashes, cohort reproduction, timestamp order, parsing, label priority, split isolation, event counts, positivity gates, model convergence, use of common denominators, coefficient extraction, and that the conclusion is “inconclusive scientifically / infeasible confirmatorily.” It must reject a solver that labels the exploratory coefficients supportive, reports binary H effects, changes thresholds after seeing outcomes, pools sparse assays, or calls V recurrence/intent. It cannot adjudicate assay equivalence, outside care/death, clinical purpose, biological recurrence, or latent-state meaning; those require expert review or additional data.

## Alternatives and revisit triggers

- Binary H: abandoned in this snapshot because only 5/328 are high/persistent and none occur in validation/test.
- Continuous bivariate H: computationally feasible but exploratory only; retain separate pre-rank and log-ratio axes to expose regression-to-the-mean.
- Marker-availability state on all 15,575: useful descriptive ascertainment analysis, but it answers a different question and cannot substitute for marker-history specificity.
- Transparent stacked baseline: retained as the only justified current fit.
- U+Q+B+V model: scientifically substantive but deferred for support, not hardware, reasons.
- Imaging: remains secondary and cannot rescue the laboratory claim.
- Biological recurrence: remains frozen pending adjudicated/linked/prospective truth.
