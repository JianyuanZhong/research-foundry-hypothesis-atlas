# Physiologic adjustment of laboratory surveillance as a transportable ICU-risk signal

Status: Episode-18 substantive successor to [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process study. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that an alert benefits patients.

## Unresolved clinical question and hypothesis

Laboratory observation timing and intensity can improve short-horizon ICU-risk prediction, but that signal may be a surrogate for unmeasured physiologic instability: clinicians order more tests when bedside physiology is worsening. The parent separates raw process from hospital workflow, yet its primary state block is laboratory-centered. The next consequential question is whether laboratory-process information remains useful after adjustment for contemporaneously recorded vital physiology.

Hypothesis: among adults at a fixed six-hour ICU-relative landmark, patient-level laboratory observation process adds transportable information for recorded ICU death beyond demographics, laboratory state/availability, and recorded vital-sign state/availability. The incremental process signal should improve zero-shot held-out-hospital calibration and competing-risk Brier performance, attenuate under state- and hospital-stratified process permutation, and retain direction after outcome-free target-hospital percentile normalization. If it disappears after vital-state adjustment, the stronger interpretation is that the prior laboratory-process association primarily proxies unmeasured physiologic instability or workflow, not an independent patient-level surveillance signal.

The clinically important decision is whether a future monitoring-history alert merits prospective evaluation as an adjunct to physiologic risk scoring, or whether implementation effort should target better physiologic feature capture/recalibration instead. This retrospective study cannot decide that intervention question.

## What evidence supports, what remains unresolved

The strongest available evidence is an outcome-blind feasibility audit attached to the assessed parent: 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows known by the six-hour landmark. This establishes support for a hospital-transport experiment, not a predictive result, a causal effect, clinician intent, or clinical benefit. The current bounded diagnostic verifies that the two vital tables and their required fields are present; final vital coverage, common support, and event support after complete-case construction are solver outputs and must gate interpretation.

The unresolved claim is the hypothesis above. A result can establish only a recorded-eICU prognostic/measurement-process statement for this snapshot. Physiologic correctness, reason for ordering, missed events, patient benefit, and the effect of changing surveillance require adjudication, external data, and prospective evaluation.

## Feature blocks and estimands

Freeze eligibility, codebooks and support decisions before reading labels. For labs, derive first/last valid values, range/slope when at least two values exist, threshold flags, analyte availability, counts, distinct result times, first/last time, recency to minute 360, inter-result gap summaries, revision-known status, interface/system indicators, total rows, distinct analytes and total span.

For vitals, use a prespecified recorded-state block from available fields: first/last/median/range/slope where support permits, plus an explicit availability indicator for each selected physiologic variable. Use a separate vital-observation block (row count, distinct observation times, recency and duration) so measured state is not conflated with observation density. Vital fields with insufficient cross-hospital overlap are audit-only, not silently imputed. All transformations are fit in development hospitals.

B0 is the parent transparent baseline: pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic death and alive-discharge heads over hourly rows [360,1800], using demographics/admission context, lab state summaries and lab availability only.

Bv is the state-adequate baseline: exactly B0 inputs plus vital recorded-state and vital availability blocks. The predeclared primary incremental comparison is Bp minus Bv.

Bp is the matched transparent process model: exactly Bv inputs plus patient-level lab process features only. It excludes hospitalid and hospital descriptors. The primary estimand is the paired, macro-hospital held-out difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score and calibration (with cumulative-incidence risk at 1,800 as a secondary estimand). Alert burden and hypothetical net benefit at q in {0.01,0.02,0.05,0.10} are secondary decision diagnostics, not utility evidence.

Br is a secondary relative-process diagnostic: Bp's continuous lab-process features are replaced by outcome-free empirical percentiles computed from a fixed 10% calibration subset of eligible predictor-window episodes in each target hospital; no target outcomes, discharge status or post-landmark data are used. This is unsupervised target-site adaptation, not strict zero-shot transport. Require at least 50 calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes per hospital.

T is a substantive learned alternative on the same question: a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins within [0,360], with the same lab state, lab process, vital state and vital observation channels as Bp, no hospital ID, and death/alive-discharge heads. Fit only on development hospitals; early stopping and hyperparameters use development validation hospitals. A next-bin observation auxiliary task may characterize measurement dynamics but its output cannot be used at outcome evaluation. T is scientifically useful only if temporal ordering, persistence or co-movement improves calibrated held-out-hospital performance beyond Bp, not because AUROC is slightly higher. The simple Bv/Bp contrast is primary because it isolates the contribution of lab process after physiologic adjustment and is auditable; T is conditional because it can expose temporal structure summaries lose but may learn interface artifacts.

## Splits, uncertainty, and falsification

Use five grouped outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once, stratified by eligible episodes and event counts where feasible. Keep each uniquepid together. Fit all imputation, scaling, panel resolution, regularization, temporal stopping and thresholds inside development hospitals. The primary estimand is the macro-hospital paired Bp-minus-Bv result; patient-weighted results are secondary. Use hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals, at least 1,000 resamples if feasible, and simultaneous intervals for prespecified alert cells. Report per-hospital support, events and competing discharges.

Primary falsifications are: (a) permute lab-process channels within hospital and Bv-risk strata while holding state fixed; (b) repeat after conditioning on vital observation density; (c) strict common-panel and revision-known lab codebooks; (d) compare patient-level process results with hospital-descriptor-only predictions; and (e) test whether any gain is confined to one lab family, interface, or hospital. A process signal that survives permutation or is reproduced by site descriptors is evidence against a patient-level interpretation. A gain lost after vital-state adjustment supports the unmeasured-instability explanation and refutes the primary hypothesis as stated.

Supportive evidence requires adequate event/competing-event counts, common support across hospitals, no leakage, a directionally favorable Bp-minus-Bv calibration/Brier result in held-out hospitals, uncertainty excluding no improvement for the primary metric, attenuation under matched permutation, and no single-hospital domination. Adverse evidence is no transport improvement, reversal after strict revision/unit handling, deterioration in calibration, or persistence under state-matched permutation/site-descriptor controls. Inconclusive evidence includes vital coverage or overlap failure, sparse event-bearing hospitals after complete-case rules, unstable fits, fold reversals, unsupported alert cells, or target leakage. These outcomes refute or support only the recorded prognostic/process claim, never clinical benefit.

Its learned-sequence idea is adapted narrowly as optional T on exact eICU channels.
