# HCC branch A: treatment recovery versus care-process signal

Status: bounded design memo, 2026-10-02. This file contains no patient-level rows, identifiers, or clinical notes. HCC source files are read-only.

## Scientific opening and falsifiable hypothesis

The HCC snapshot contains a treatment cohort, not only diagnosis codes. A bounded local aggregate found 43,815 patients, 105,044 encounters, 1,810,646 diagnosis rows, and 338,040 procedure rows. The most frequent diagnosis label was `hepatocellular carcinoma` (336,570 rows). Procedure labels include transarterial embolization/chemoembolization (TACE), partial/segmental liver resection, microwave/radiofrequency ablation, transplantation, and systemic-treatment labels. These are observed records, not adjudicated stage, tumor response, or mechanism.

The closest inspected evidence leaves a consequential boundary unresolved. The accessible Oncoformer supplement [K1] reports liver-cancer recurrence-free-survival comparisons and explicitly evaluates utilization-only and utilization-spike sensitivity analyses, but it is not an HCC-treatment study and its main paper/STAR Methods were unavailable. Delphi-2M [K2] shows that dated health-event order can support prediction, while also showing that source availability and care-setting patterns can create apparently predictive associations and warning that temporal dependence is not causal evidence. ALADYNOULLI [K3] provides a latent time-varying disease-state strategy with calibration and selection-bias considerations, but it uses broad diagnosis histories and genetics rather than HCC treatment episodes and laboratory units.

**Hypothesis:** Among patients with a recorded HCC diagnosis who undergo a first HCC-directed procedure episode, worse assay-specific recovery of liver injury/reserve markers during days 0–7 after treatment predicts a higher risk of a new inpatient encounter during days 8–90, after adjustment for pretreatment severity and treatment category. A patient-state explanation is supported only if the state signal remains materially associated in the combined model and exceeds a process/measurement-only model. If the signal is reproduced by encounter, laboratory, examination, document, order, or medication intensity and attenuates after those controls, the stronger explanation is care-process/measurement ascertainment rather than physiology.

This is a predictive/descriptive hypothesis about recorded deterioration and return hospitalization, not a causal claim about treatment, laboratory schedules, or mechanism.

## Population, treatment episode, time, and outcomes

1. Keep patients with at least one exact `Hepatocellular carcinoma` diagnosis linked to encounters. A sensitivity cohort adds procedure-defined HCC cases but is reported separately.
2. On that cohort, lock an HCC procedure dictionary before fitting: resection examples `partial hepatectomy`, `hepatectomy`, `hepatic segmentectomy`, `partial hepatectomy`; locoregional examples `transcatheter hepatic arterial embolization`, `arterial chemoembolization`, `transarterial angiographic chemoembolization (TACE)`, `TACE`, `ultrasound-guided microwave ablation of a liver lesion`, `ultrasound-guided radiofrequency ablation of a liver lesion`, `percutaneous radiofrequency ablation of a liver tumor`; and transplantation examples `allogeneic liver transplantation`, `allogeneic orthotopic liver transplantation`. Only exact observed labels or predeclared normalized variants are admitted. The first qualifying procedure after the first HCC diagnosis is the index. Rows for one patient/encounter within 24 hours are clustered; episode `t0=min(start time)`, `t1=max(end time)`.
3. Require linked encounter, nonempty `t1`, and index end at least 90 days before the snapshot end. The primary landmark cohort requires no inpatient encounter through day 7 and a measurable follow-up opportunity, so it estimates risk conditional on reaching day 7.
4. Primary outcome: a new inpatient encounter in days 8–90 after `t1`, with nonempty `admission time` and `inpatient number` (fallback distinct `encounter number`) for the same patient, `admission time > t1+7d` and `≤t1+90d`. Exclude the index hospitalization by requiring a new hospitalization identifier when available. This is a recorded inpatient return, not adjudicated unplanned readmission.
5. Secondary descriptive outcomes: subsequent HCC-directed procedure by day 90; return whose linked diagnosis has exact HCC or a prespecified complication label; and time to return. These require clinician review before calling them recurrence, treatment failure, or treatment complication.
6. Pretreatment window is `[t0−180d,t0)`: diagnoses, prior procedures, inpatient utilization, medications, examinations, labs, and documents. State exposure is numeric, assay-specific lab data in `[t1,t1+7d]`, using only information available before a qualifying outcome. Retain last pre-treatment value, early value, change, robust within-assay standardized value, and availability. Do not use post-day-7 data.
7. Process features from the same windows: encounter/lab/exam/document/order/medication counts, distinct assays, first-measurement latency, and missingness. These explicitly test measurement opportunity and care intensity.

## Analysis, alternative methods, and falsification

The estimand is the adjusted day-8–90 return-risk contrast for higher versus lower state score, with continuous score primary and quintiles for readable contrasts. Assay thresholds and state-score transformations are fit on training data only.

**Transparent baseline:** penalized landmark logistic regression, with a cause-specific time-to-return sensitivity model. Fit state-only, process-only, and combined versions using treatment category, age, sex, pretreatment diagnosis severity, prior utilization, and assay-specific lab summaries.

**Substantive learned alternative:** a temporal event encoder over `[t0−180d,t1+7d]`, with separate state and process channels. State tokens are assay-specific numeric observations and missingness; process tokens are encounters, procedures, examinations, documents, orders, and medication events. Each event carries type/label and relative time; treatment category is included. The binary target is the same day-8–90 return at the day-7 landmark. Fit state-only, process-only, and joint models; channel ablation and permutation tests determine whether ordering/interactions add information lost by the summary baseline. This is an HCC adaptation, not reproduction of the demonstration papers.

Use patient-level temporal train/validation/test splits with no patient in more than one split; if event support forces a grouped split, report it. Report AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration slope/intercept, risk-decile calibration, and descriptive net benefit. Use patient-cluster bootstrap intervals for all contrasts and paired bootstrap comparisons for channel ablations. Prespecified sensitivity checks are treatment category, diagnosis type, complete-discharge-time restriction, extreme-utilization exclusion, and a pre-treatment placebo window.

Supportive state result: held-out state gradient persists in the combined model, process-only performance is materially smaller, calibration is acceptable, and the signal is not recreated by placebo/utilization-spike checks. This justifies prospective laboratory measurement validation, not a causal or mechanistic conclusion.

Adverse state result: state signal attenuates/reverses after process adjustment, process-only model matches it, or utilization/placebo controls reproduce it. That favors a care-process/measurement explanation and redirects work toward standardized follow-up ascertainment.

Inconclusive result: sparse repeated assays, unstable dictionary, few landmark episodes, wide intervals spanning clinically meaningful effects, or poor held-out calibration. This means the snapshot cannot distinguish explanations; it does not refute a patient-state relationship.

## Missing evidence and computational limits

Unavailable evidence includes images, validated tumor stage/size/number, vascular invasion, performance status, intent, adjudicated recurrence/progression, cause of admission, complication adjudication, mortality linkage, laboratory units, and validated clinical NLP. No automated verifier can establish those claims; expert chart review and external/prospective validation are required.

Baseline CPU fitting should be minutes to under an hour after aggregation. The event encoder is an unverified planning estimate of one allocated A100, 4–8 CPUs, 16–64 GiB RAM, and several hours including preprocessing and held-out evaluation. Solver planning allows 16 CPUs, 8 GPUs, 262,144 MiB, and 28,800 seconds; discovery compute is separate. A small allocated import/tensor probe should precede any solver run. If repeated numeric assay support is insufficient, defer the learned alternative and document a narrowed process/measurement study rather than silently changing the hypothesis.

**Actual scientific deliverable:** fit state-only, process-only, combined baseline, and channel-ablated temporal encoder on the frozen HCC treatment cohort; produce held-out state/process contrasts, uncertainty/calibration, placebo and utilization falsifications, and an evidence-bounded interpretation of whether the association is more consistent with patient state or care-process measurement. A planned check is not an observed result.
