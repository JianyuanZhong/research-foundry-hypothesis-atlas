# Provisional hypothesis: hypotension duration versus nadir for subsequent AKI in eICU

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU admission with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. Cumulative duration is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

A single nadir may be transient or artifact-prone, whereas sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved claim is whether duration and trajectory add clinically meaningful information about subsequent AKI beyond the nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, treatment response, or measurement intensity. Resolving this could inform whether early AKI risk assessment should use duration/trajectory rather than a single nadir.

The available evidence motivates but does not establish this eICU question. The closest inspected work discussed in the existing candidate reports that time-weighted MAP below 65 mmHg was useful in a perioperative cohort while waveform morphology added no prediction beyond that exposure and baseline covariates; it does not answer the ICU duration-versus-nadir question. ICU AKI is heterogeneous and dynamic, so creatinine-centered ascertainment is necessary but insufficient, and urine output is nonspecific. No computed effect, adjudicated AKI, or causal result is claimed here.

## Exact data and planned test

Use only the configured eICU source, with patient/stay identifiers and hospital identifiers preserved within eICU; do not join across HCC, MIMIC, or UKB. The planned cohort is adults in a qualifying ICU stay with a pre-exposure creatinine baseline and at least one valid systolic BP below 65 mmHg in ICU time 0–24 hours. The primary exposure is cumulative time below 65 mmHg in that window; the comparator is admission nadir. The primary outcome is a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, anchored to the pre-exposure creatinine baseline. Urine-output ascertainment is secondary and gated on semantic and timing validation.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is a pre-specified adjusted model using nadir, baseline severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The substantive alternative is a time-aware model using the BP trajectory/cumulative duration and pre-exposure creatinine trajectory. It can reveal whether information is distributed over time rather than concentrated in one nadir; the baseline loses that temporal structure. Evaluate both with patient-level or hospital-held-out splitting, uncertainty intervals, calibration, and prespecified falsification checks for observation intensity and measurement artifacts. A null or imprecise result is not proof of no relationship.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive outcomes

Supportive results would be a reproducible incremental association for duration beyond nadir with plausible uncertainty and robustness to hospital and measurement-intensity controls. Adverse results would be no incremental association, reversal, or instability after controls, weakening the duration claim. Inconclusive results would include inadequate creatinine/urine ascertainment, sparse events, or wide intervals; these require a narrower question or additional evidence, not a favorable reinterpretation.
