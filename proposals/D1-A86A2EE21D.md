# Two-phase clinically contextual reference challenge plus scaled component audit

## Decision question and targeted advance

The frozen decision is whether any support-qualified pulmonary-opportunity atom in MIMIC-IV can be nominated for a future, prospective **silent** validation of the inherited `AUTO`, `RECON`, `TIMING`, or `DEFER` routing logic. This remains a retrospective text reproducibility and contamination audit. It is not an evaluation of guideline appropriateness, clinician performance, patient safety, follow-up, or clinical benefit.

The parent correctly uses roster-disjoint readers, independently worded codebooks, and a holistic challenge, but asks all selected admissions to receive a costly layered challenge under a ceiling of at most 900 admissions. It also cannot distinguish a clinically meaningful routing disagreement from a benign wording disagreement when all readers see the same imperfect text. This child makes that tradeoff explicit: a small, fixed-probability, pre-label clinically contextual reference challenge is used to characterize the disagreement mechanism, while a larger fixed-probability component audit estimates coverage and adverse routing behavior. The reference panel is an additional measurement system, never a gold standard and never a consensus oracle. The larger audit is not allowed to borrow certainty from the smaller challenge; compatible-set bounds carry uncertainty forward.

## Supported evidence, unresolved claim, and falsifiable hypothesis

Available evidence supports construction of a first-eligible, all-opportunity admission frame from admission-linked radiology text, a reciprocal pre-departure text chain, a canonical discharge artifact, and structured service/ICU/time proxies. It does not establish that a report was visible at physical departure, that `storetime` is finalization or transmission time, that a service identifies the responsible clinician, that any retrospective label is clinically correct, or that a route improves care.

The unresolved claim is whether disagreement between the inherited component audit and an independent, clinically contextual reading is predominantly harmless representation variation, or instead reflects a clinically material failure to identify target/action/timing/responsibility or to route the worst recommendation unit. The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> For every atom proposed for `AUTO` or `RECON`, the clinically contextual reference challenge will show no adverse-compatible clinically material routing error above the inherited atom-specific margin, and the larger component audit will satisfy all inherited purity, support, workload, and invariance gates. Any atom for which the reference measurement is compatible with material route error is excluded or remains `DEFER`, even when component agreement is high. Unsupported domains remain `DEFER`.

This is a nomination hypothesis about observable text-system behavior. It is not the claim that the reference panel is right, that an extracted recommendation is guideline-concordant, or that a patient experienced benefit.

## Two-phase design and fixed probability sampling

Construct complete `N_A`, the five inherited outcome-blind sampling strata, final pre-label support atlas, exact atom `g=(possible-calendar class, terminal curr_service, workflow class)`, and residual envelope **before any label, reference review, or challenge transformation**. Freeze a protocol hash and random seed before sampling. No outcome-based enrichment, top-up, replacement, adaptive reallocation, atom merging, or route-triggered verification is permitted.

Let the total admission workload be `B=900`. If the inherited sentinel census plus required minimum sample sizes cannot fit `B`, return `NO_SELECTION` rather than silently reducing a safety-critical inherited sample. Otherwise use the following fixed allocation, approved before labels:

* **Phase R, clinically contextual reference challenge:** `n_R=150` admissions, or census the relevant stratum if its population is smaller, drawn once by stratified fixed-probability systematic sampling over the five inherited strata. Within each stratum, inclusion is proportional to the pre-label atom population with a deterministic random start; every occupied atom has positive probability. If the resulting census/minimum constraints exceed 900, fail closed. Phase-R inclusion probabilities `pi_Ri` are recorded for every admission.
* **Phase C, larger component audit:** draw `n_C=B-n_R` admissions from the same frozen frame using the inherited stratum allocation and fixed-probability sampling, independently of labels and independently of Phase R. If the parent’s sentinel census consumes part of `B`, `n_C` is the remaining budget after the fixed `n_R` allocation; if the required inherited formula cannot be met, `NO_SELECTION`. Phase-C probabilities `pi_Ci` and joint inclusion probabilities are recorded. A Phase-R admission may also be in Phase C only if the overlap was prescribed before sampling; otherwise use disjoint draws. The preferred implementation is disjoint Phase-R/Phase-C draws, with the exact disjoint design and probabilities frozen before execution.

The phase allocation is an efficiency choice, not evidence. Phase C estimates broad component-audit quantities under the inherited finite-population design. Phase R estimates only the contextual-reference disagreement and routing-error envelope in its prespecified support-qualified atoms. It does not justify extrapolating a reference result to an atom with no Phase-R support. If a claimable atom has fewer than 20 Phase-R admissions, its contextual gate is `INCONCLUSIVE/DEFER`, not a favorable complete-case result. If a rare atom cannot receive 20 without violating the fixed budget, it remains `DEFER`.

The larger Phase-C packets receive the inherited component audit: roster-disjoint component readings and seals, with all inherited atomic fields, clocks, exhaustive states, route-independent all-route audit, and worst-unit routing. To limit duplication burden while retaining an error check, prespecify a fixed 10% duplicate component subset within Phase C selected by the same pre-label hash (not by route, disagreement, or outcome), with a second roster-disjoint reader and independently frozen duplicate codebook. This duplicate is a diagnostic and enters the adverse-compatible component bounds; it cannot rescue a failing atom. The remaining Phase-C admissions have the primary component panel only. No holistic panel is run on Phase C.

## Phase-R clinically contextual reference challenge

Before any Phase-C or Phase-R component labels are created, seal a raw extract and packet manifest. A Phase-R packet contains the reciprocal pre-`D` radiology chain, all permitted note-version/addendum and missingness metadata, canonical DS and permitted DS detail, and the admission/service/ICU chronology fields used by the inherited decision. It includes no atom identity, sampling weight, parent label, route, component output, or post-`D` text. Images, actual viewing, transmission, orders, referrals, follow-up, and outside care are unavailable and must not be inferred.

Three roster-disjoint roles are used, all blinded to each other and to the inherited labels:

1. **Context readers R1 and R2** independently read the whole packet with separately written, independently ordered codebooks (`CB-R1`, `CB-R2`), no shared training cases, and no shared examples. They record the inherited atomic fields plus a prespecified *disagreement mechanism* taxonomy: (a) same clinically meaningful proposition, different wording; (b) target identity/lesion linkage; (c) action or replacement; (d) timing/opportunity; (e) recipient/responsibility; (f) polarity/negation/uncertainty; (g) version/addendum conflict; (h) discharge visibility/artifact limitation; (i) unsupported context. They must choose `supported`, `contradicted`, or `indeterminate` for each proposition and may select `unknown`; unknown is not favorable.
2. **Clinically anchored reference reader R3** is a credentialed radiology/pulmonary reviewer (or a prespecified equivalent expert panel) who receives the same available packet plus a one-page, pre-label clinical-context rubric defining the propositions and observable evidence requirements. The rubric may cite public guideline concepts only to define what is being asked; it cannot import absent images, actual workflow facts, or patient outcomes. R3 independently labels support/contradiction/indeterminate, identifies the disagreement mechanism, and records whether the packet is insufficient to adjudicate. R3 is an independent measurement, not a gold standard.
3. A separate audit reader R4 reruns a random fixed subset of Phase-R packets after a washout, using a separately worded codebook. This checks repeatability but is not a consensus panel. All outputs remain append-only; there is no adjudication by majority, deletion of minority labels, or consensus-as-truth.

The clinical-context rubric is frozen and hashed before packet creation. It explicitly distinguishes *textual support for an observable proposition* from appropriateness, actual responsibility, intended care, or outcome. In particular, no reader may label “follow-up completed,” “clinician saw the report,” “patient accepted the plan,” “malignancy,” or “harm,” because these are unavailable.

The Phase-R challenge therefore provides two useful signals without pretending to solve the gold-standard problem: (i) a structured estimate of which disagreements are clinically material under a transparent rubric, and (ii) a repeatability/independence check. It cannot identify the true prevalence of shared error. Every R1/R2/R3/R4 pattern is retained for partial identification.

## Partial identification and estimands

For each support-qualified atom and route, define the observed vector of component and reference measurements, including Phase-C primary/duplicate outputs and Phase-R `(R1,R2,R3,R4)` outputs. Define a latent state only as the set of clinically admissible states consistent with the supplied artifact and rubric. Do not assume conditional independence, reader exchangeability, consensus correctness, or that the expert is correct.

Report sharp or conservative compatible-set intervals for:

* clinically material route discordance, separately for target, action/replacement, timing, recipient/responsibility, polarity, and artifact-visibility limitation;
* the proportion of admissions for which `AUTO` is incompatible with any adverse route under at least one admissible latent state;
* parent-component versus contextual-reference disagreement, decomposed by the prespecified mechanism taxonomy;
* repeatability of each measurement system, route-invariant control flips, and decisive-control violations;
* `C_AUTO`, `D_MIX`, `leak_AUTO`, route capture, and workload, retaining the parent's definitions and atom-specific denominators;
* finite-population atom and all-frame quantities using recorded first-order and pairwise inclusion probabilities.

The lower bound assigns only jointly supported propositions to the favorable set; the upper bound assigns every unresolved, indeterminate, disagreement, missing, or control-infeasible pattern to the adverse state. Phase-R estimates are not treated as a gold-standard correction to Phase C. Phase-R/Phase-C transport across atoms is reported only where the design gives positive known probability and the prespecified compatibility assumptions hold; otherwise report `DEFER`. A latent-class model can be shown as a diagnostic sensitivity analysis only if conditional independence or an external validation constraint is documented before labels and finite-sample identifiability is demonstrated. It cannot narrow certification bounds.

Use exact finite-population inversion for binary challenge/control outcomes and 20,000 Rao-Wu replicates that resample at the admission level under the actual two-phase design, retaining all labels together. Use one max-statistic simultaneous 95% family over inherited gates, atom gates, phase-specific disagreement bounds, material-disagreement taxonomy, control invariance, and minimax endpoints. Account for Phase-R/Phase-C disjointness or prescribed overlap in the replicate design. Missing labels, nonresponse, packet corruption, impossible context, or failed controls are adverse-compatible, not complete-case deletions.

## Prespecified gates and fail-closed behavior

An atom can be nominated only if every inherited parent gate passes and all of the following pass:

* Phase C has at least 30 sampled candidate-route admissions and its fixed duplicate subset has at least 10 admissions where feasible; Phase R has at least 20 admissions for the atom and at least 10 candidate-route admissions for a contextual estimate. Failure is `INCONCLUSIVE/DEFER`, not a negative safety result.
* The simultaneous upper bound on clinically material contextual-reference route discordance is at most `0.05` for `AUTO`, `0.10` for `RECON`, `0.05` for `TIMING`, and `0.10` for `DEFER`, using the adverse-compatible latent set. Separate upper bounds for each mechanism cannot be pooled to pass.
* The simultaneous lower bound for R1/R2/R3 support agreement is at least `0.90` only as a reproducibility/contextual-consistency gate; it is not a truth claim. R3 disagreement with both context readers remains adverse-compatible.
* No supported atom has a simultaneous upper route-invariant control-flip bound above `0.02` or decisive-control violation bound above `0.05`. Controls are created before labels, and control infeasibility is adverse-compatible.
* Any adverse-compatible contextual label that makes an `AUTO` unit compatible with `RECON`, `TIMING`, or `DEFER` routes forces that unit to `RECON`/`DEFER` under inherited precedence. A single unresolved worst unit blocks `AUTO`.
* Phase-R and Phase-C nonresponse, missingness, packet failure, or unknown fraction has upper bound below `0.05`, unless the relevant atom is censused. “Indeterminate because the available record is insufficient” is not favorable.
* Phase-R physician-hours and Phase-C component hours, including the fixed duplicate subset, stay under the same governance-approved absolute workload budget and are reported separately. No unrecorded expert time is assumed away.

A failed atom cannot be rescued by pooled purity, a passing neighboring atom, macro-service grouping, early/late contrast, sentinel-only performance, consensus, a model estimate, hierarchical shrinkage, or a route-invariant control result. Novel/unsupported service, workflow, calendar, or context signatures default to `DEFER`. A global sampling, seal, or source-integrity failure yields `NO_SELECTION` or invalidates the affected run.

Verify source/schema hashes, exact archive members, counts and joins, reciprocal links, `D`, unit clocks, canonical DS, pre-label atlas, phase draws, `pi` and pairwise probabilities, packet/codebook hashes, roster disjointness, and seal order.

Mandatory computational fixtures are:

* all readers agree on a planted wrong plan: agreement may be high but adverse-compatible bounds must block `AUTO`;
* pooled-safe but atom-unsafe domain: the atom must fail and default to `DEFER`;
* route-irrelevant formatting/author-token perturbation: no route may change;
* one-field timing/replacement perturbation: only the locked route transition may occur;
* missing or high-weight Phase-R labels: bounds must widen rather than improve;
* a context-insufficient packet: it must be `indeterminate`/`DEFER`, never favorable;
* unsupported novel service/workflow: `DEFER`;
* latent-class nonidentifiability: primary bounds must be unchanged;
* duplicate unit versus duplicate restatement: unit-level and admission-level worst-unit precedence must be preserved;
* budget stress: if fixed allocations cannot fit 900, the program must return `NO_SELECTION` rather than change sampling after seeing labels.

Any post-label sampling, reference-panel overlap, panel roster overlap, codebook reuse, packet leakage, consensus deletion, external web upload of clinical text, or route construction before all relevant seals invalidates the affected run.

## Interpretation and evidence ceiling

**Supportive:** all inherited gates and new Phase-R/Phase-C gates pass for at least one fully supported atom, with simultaneous adverse-compatible bounds and controls passing. This supports only nomination of that exact observable MIMIC signature for a separately approved prospective within-site silent validation. It does not establish clinical correctness, safety, benefit, or transport.

**Adverse:** a material-disagreement bound crosses its route margin; a route-invariant or decisive control fails; an adverse-compatible contextual label makes `AUTO` unsafe; or component/contextual evidence reveals a target, timing, responsibility, or artifact-visibility failure. Exclude the affected atom despite favorable component agreement. If the larger audit is positive but Phase-R is adverse, the contextual result controls the allow-list decision.

**Inconclusive:** fewer than required Phase-R cases, sparse route events, infeasible control transformations, nonresponse, broken seals, wide compatible bounds, or insufficient context. Inconclusive is not evidence of safety and does not justify pooling atoms or choosing a favorable reader.

MIMIC cannot establish actual report viewing, finalization/signature semantics, physical departure visibility, communication, responsibility, orders/referrals, scheduling, outside plans, patient preference, malignancy, appropriateness, follow-up completion, harm, utility, causal effect, or forward-calendar transport. The contextual reference rubric and expert review cannot manufacture those facts. Before any live use, obtain separate prospective evidence with actual calendar dates and observed viewing/edit/sign/transmission, responsibility, orders/referrals, scheduling, outside-plan, and patient outcomes, plus clinical governance review. Novel workflows remain `DEFER`.
