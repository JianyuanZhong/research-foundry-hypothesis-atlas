# Asynchronous synthetic–excretory recovery after HCC hepatectomy

Status: new HCC restart with no scientific parent. The postoperative biomarker-surveillance branch is transition context only: its prespecified marker-history exposure had five patients and no validation/test support, so this proposal does not relax that estimand or claim recurrence. A bounded source audit was run; no outcome model or hypothesis test was fitted.

## Clinical opening, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

The strongest endpoint-specific claim supported by inspected evidence is that preoperative laboratory and operative factors were associated with time from surgery to discharge in a selected single-center cohort of 462 adults with HCC [K1]. Separately, POD1 bilirubin plus clinical factors predicted adjudicated grade B/C PHLF better than perioperative lactate alone after major hepatectomy [K2]. Formal PHLF is conventionally defined by concurrent increased INR and hyperbilirubinemia on or after POD5 and graded by management consequences [K3]. These are established observations, not results of this proposal.

The unresolved question is whether the path to the late POD5 state matters in routine HCC care. Synthetic recovery (INR) and excretory recovery (total bilirubin) may be asynchronous. The leading claim is that a bilirubin-lag pattern—INR falling while bilirubin does not fall from the early to late postoperative window—marks delayed recovery and adds temporally transportable information about discharge delay beyond late-window INR/bilirubin, operative burden, cirrhosis, age, sex, and era. The strongest rival is that earlier values add no information once current POD5 state and severity are known; any apparent trajectory effect is generated by operation severity, selective repeat testing, assay/era drift, or discharge practice.

The exact falsifiable hypothesis is: among adults with HCC undergoing a first qualifying liver resection who remain in the index encounter through 144 postoperative hours and have INR and total bilirubin measured in each prespecified window, a trajectory model will improve locked 2024–2025 prediction of recorded discharge after 240 hours over a POD5 snapshot model, and its adjusted bilirubin-lag contrast will be adverse relative to concordant recovery. This is a prognostic/phenotyping hypothesis, not a causal effect of laboratory recovery and not a PHLF diagnosis.

Why it matters: at 144 hours, deciding whether routine recovery is adequate, whether discharge planning should proceed, or whether targeted clinical review is warranted is consequential. If prior shape adds no stable information, clinicians and model builders should prefer the simpler current-state assessment. If it does, the result motivates prospective adjudication of discordant recovery rather than adding indiscriminate tests.

## Population, clock, exposure, and outcome

Index population:

1. HCC snapshot [source checksum], years 2013–2025.
2. Age >=18 at an encounter containing a procedure label with both liver and resection; exclude labels containing cholecystectomy|liver transplantation|donor liver|hepatic artery|pancreas|stomach|colon|kidney|lung. Require an HCC diagnosis (hepatocellular carcinoma|primary liver cancer) on the same patient/encounter key. The audit confirmed every such qualifying resection encounter had an HCC diagnosis.
3. Take the chronologically first qualifying resection per Patient Master Index; T0 is its valid Start Time, within Admission Time–Discharge Time.
4. Require recorded discharge 144 hours to 90 days after T0. The 144-hour landmark prevents early discharge from defining lab completeness.
5. Require numeric exact-label International normalized ratio and total bilirubin in each half-open interval [0,48), [48,96), and [96,144) hours after T0. Select the last result by test time within each interval. Broad data-error bounds are INR 0.5–20 and bilirubin 0.1–1000; these are not clinical normal ranges.

Primary outcome Y=1 is discharge time > T0 + 240 hours; Y=0 is recorded discharge by 240 hours. It is “delayed recorded index-encounter discharge,” not death, morbidity, or PHLF. Sensitivity outcome is discharge after 336 hours. Death disposition is unavailable, so the solver must not call either outcome survival.

Define train-standardized log values for INR and bilirubin at early/middle/late windows. Concordant recovery means both late-minus-early log changes <0. Bilirubin lag means INR change <0 and bilirubin change >=0; INR lag reverses those signs; concordant non-recovery has both >=0. The primary continuous trajectory variables are the two early-to-middle and middle-to-late changes plus their sum and the axis discordance (standardized bilirubin change minus standardized INR change). Quadrants are descriptive contrasts, not threshold-derived diagnoses.

## Observed feasibility audit

work/hcc_recovery_audit.py and work/hcc_recovery_audit.json implement a bounded, outcome-agnostic support audit using 48-hour windows. Among landmark-eligible first resections, complete INR/bilirubin trajectories numbered 6,975 in train (2013–2021), 2,562 in validation (2022–2023), and 2,700 in locked test (2024–2025). Delayed-discharge events were 1,851 (26.5%), 428 (16.7%), and 387 (14.3%), respectively; this era shift makes calibration and temporal validation essential. Test trajectory cells were: concordant recovery 1,163, bilirubin lag 354, INR lag 803, and concordant non-recovery 380. Operation duration was available for 12,209/12,237 complete trajectories. Exact-label lab coverage and quantiles are in the attached JSON.

This establishes endpoint/exposure support and extraction feasibility only. It does not show that trajectories predict the outcome, that either axis is mechanistic, or that prolonged stay is clinically caused by hepatic recovery.

## Matched models and analysis

All models use exactly the same eligible patients, split, outcome, and information available by 144 hours.

Transparent baseline B0: penalized logistic regression with prespecified terms for age (restricted cubic spline), sex, surgery duration (spline plus missing indicator), laparoscopic flag, major-resection flag, cirrhosis flag, calendar year, exact measurement offsets within the late window, and log late-window INR and bilirubin with their interaction. Continuous transformations and penalties are fitted in train only.

Learned trajectory alternative T1: histogram gradient-boosted trees, limited to depth 3, on all B0 inputs plus the six early/middle/late log values, exact measurement offsets, common-recovery and discordance coordinates, and two interval changes per axis. Tune learning rate, leaf size and iteration count only on 2022–2023 validation; freeze once, refit on train only if required by the implementation contract, and evaluate once on locked 2024–2025 test. This alternative can represent thresholded and non-additive paths, especially bilirubin-lag versus INR-lag at similar late values, which B0 discards. It is a task-specific learned alternative, not a reproduction of any key work.

Transparent trajectory check B1: elastic-net logistic regression on T1’s same engineered trajectory inputs. If B1 matches T1, nonlinear learning is unnecessary but the scientific trajectory claim may still be supported. If T1 alone wins, report dependence on nonlinear interactions and stability plots rather than treating complexity as mechanism.

Primary metric is paired test-set Brier-score difference T1−B0 with 2,000 patient bootstrap replicates. Secondary metrics: log loss, AUROC, AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope and reliability plot. Report all metrics for validation and locked test, plus stratified estimates for major/minor, cirrhosis flag, laparoscopic/open, and years 2024 versus 2025. Do not optimize cutoffs on test.

Primary trajectory contrast is standardized risk difference for bilirubin lag versus concordant recovery from T1, standardized over test covariates and constrained to common support in late INR/bilirubin (overlap weights estimated in train). Report INR-lag and concordant non-recovery symmetrically. Bootstrap the complete fitting/calibration pipeline.

Falsification and rival checks:

- Shuffle early and middle values/times within train-year × procedure-burden strata while preserving each patient’s late values. A temporal gain that survives this destruction is adverse to a path-specific interpretation.
- Compare T1 with a timing-only model and with T1 excluding measurement offsets. Gain attributable only to timing/count patterns supports a care-process/selection explanation.
- Repeat using first rather than last value per window, 24-hour windows centered at 24/72/120 hours, and the 336-hour outcome.
- Report annual measurement completeness, outcome rate, raw value distributions, and calibration. Mark severe unit/era discontinuity as inconclusive.
- Evaluate complete-case selection by modeling eligibility among all 20,323 landmark encounters from preoperative/operative variables; report inverse-probability-weighted sensitivity, but do not claim missing-at-random is proven.
- Negative-control physiology: replace INR/bilirubin history with ALT history while retaining late INR/bilirubin. A comparable gain weakens specificity to synthetic–excretory recovery.

## Decision and falsification criteria

Support the hypothesis only if, on locked test:

1. T1 improves Brier score over B0 by at least 0.005 and the paired 95% bootstrap CI lies below 0;
2. calibration slope is 0.8–1.2 with absolute intercept <=0.05 after validation-only calibration;
3. the bilirubin-lag standardized risk difference versus concordant recovery is >=3 percentage points with a 95% CI excluding 0;
4. temporal shuffling removes at least half of the observed Brier gain; and
5. direction is consistent in major/minor and cirrhosis/no-cirrhosis strata, allowing imprecision.

Adverse: test Brier improvement is <0.005 or CI includes 0; the bilirubin-lag contrast is <=0; a snapshot/transparent B1 is equivalent; temporal shuffling preserves the gain; or effects reverse materially across 2024/2025 or key strata. This would favor current state, nonspecific severity, or care-process explanations.

Inconclusive: fewer than 200 test events after exact implementation, gross bilirubin scale drift, poor overlap, calibration outside bounds, or wide intervals spanning clinically opposite effects. An imprecise null is not refutation.

A positive result supports only a transportable association between observed trajectory and delayed recorded discharge in this institution. It does not identify liver regeneration, cholestasis, PHLF, complication type, treatment benefit, readiness for discharge, mortality, or causality. Those stronger claims require a unit dictionary, operative extent/manual pathology review, medication/transfusion indication, timestamped complications, death/disposition, external validation, and prospective clinical adjudication. Notes may adjudicate a blinded sample but cannot silently redefine the primary endpoint.

## Alternatives not selected

- Static early-postoperative thresholding: feasible and literature-grounded [K2], but it cannot test whether path/discordance adds information; retained as a sensitivity baseline.
- Bayesian joint latent recovery model: scientifically attractive for shared versus axis-specific recovery, but with only three broad windows and no adjudicated PHLF/treatment process, latent-state labels would invite mechanistic overinterpretation. Revisit with denser labs and adjudicated outcomes.
- Medication/order rescue composite: timestamped but indications and dictionaries are not validated; defer pending clinician review.
- Notes/pathology complication phenotyping: text exists but lacks row-level timing and validated extraction. Use only for blinded adjudication, not as an automatic endpoint.
- Vitals/transfer instability: impossible because these tables contain identifiers only.
