# Scientific Proposal: Multiplicative Interaction of Hypotension and Vasopressors on AKI

## 1. Scientific Question

**Primary Question:** In septic ICU patients, does the multiplicative interaction between cumulative hypotension burden (duration below Mean Arterial Pressure [MAP] ≤65 mmHg) and vasopressor exposure intensity predict AKI onset and severity more strongly than the additive effects of these factors alone?

**Secondary Question:** Does this interaction effect vary significantly by hospital practice patterns, and is the linear interaction specification sufficient compared to non-linear machine learning approaches?

## 2. Clinical Importance

Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) occurs in 20–40% of ICU patients and is associated with 2–3x higher mortality and longer hospital stays. Current resuscitation guidelines emphasize maintaining minimum blood pressure targets (e.g., MAP ≥65 mmHg) and minimizing vasopressor duration. However, clinical practice often focuses on *peak* or *minimum* blood pressure rather than the *cumulative duration* of hypotension.

Understanding the interaction between **duration of hypotension** and **intensity of vasopressor exposure** is critical because:
*   **Mechanistic Insight:** Prolonged hypotension causes direct ischemic injury to renal tubules, while vasopressors induce vasoconstriction. The hypothesis posits that the risk is not merely the sum of these insults but their product: prolonged ischemia under vasoconstriction creates a synergistic "perfect storm" for renal failure.
*   **Clinical Decision Making:** If an interaction exists, clinicians must shift from static thresholds (e.g., "stop norepinephrine at 0.05 mcg/kg/min") to dynamic risk stratification based on the *duration* of the hypotensive episode combined with the *rate* of vasoconstriction.
*   **Resource Allocation:** Identifying high-risk interaction profiles could trigger early AKI prophylaxis strategies (e.g., renal protection agents) before creatinine rises.

## 3. Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis:** The multiplicative interaction term (HypotensionDuration × VasopressorExposure) predicts AKI onset and RIFLE/FoFA severity significantly better than a model containing only main effects (HypotensionDuration + VasopressorExposure).

**Specific Predictions:**
1.  **Main Effects:** Both cumulative hypotension duration and vasopressor exposure independently predict AKI.
2.  **Interaction Effect:** The coefficient for the interaction term in a logistic regression will be significantly different from zero (p < 0.01), and the Odds Ratio (OR) will exceed 1.5, indicating a strong synergistic effect.
3.  **Model Fit:** A model including the interaction term will demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in discrimination (AUC increase > 0.03) and calibration compared to a main-effects-only model.
4.  **Non-Linearity Check:** Gradient Boosted Trees (GBDT) will not substantially outperform a well-specified linear model with interaction terms, suggesting the linear interaction captures the primary biological signal.
5.  **Hospital Variability:** The magnitude of the interaction effect will be consistent across hospitals, though baseline rates of AKI may vary.

### 4.2. Feature Engineering Specifications

#### A. Hypotension Duration (Minutes below MAP ≤65 mmHg)
1.  **Filter:** Select records where `noninvasivesystolic` < 70 mmHg (proxy for MAP ≤65 mmHg).
2.  **Calculation:** For each patient-stay, calculate the total duration (in minutes) of consecutive time points where systolic BP < 70.
    *   *Formula:* Σ (duration of continuous hypotensive episodes).
    *   *Window:* First 48 hours of the stay (`observationoffset` between admission and 48h mark).
3.  **Normalization:** Divide total minutes by 60 to get hours if necessary, or keep in minutes.

#### B. Vasopressor Exposure (Dose-Time Product)
1.  **Filter:** Select records where `drugname` is in the set: `{'norepinephrine', 'epinephrine', 'vasopressin', 'dobutamine', 'phenylephrine', 'norepinephrine', 'norepi'}`.
2.  **Calculation:** For each patient-stay, calculate the cumulative exposure.
    *   *Formula:* Σ (drugrate [mcg/min] × duration [hours]).
    *   *Window:* First 48 hours of the stay.
    *   *Note:* Handle gaps in infusion data by assuming continuous infusion or interpolating based on available timestamps.

#### C. AKI Outcome (RIFLE/FoFA Classification)
1.  **Source:** Lab results for `CR` (Creatinine) and `BUN` (Blood Urea Nitrogen).
2.  **Algorithm:** Implement the RIFLE criteria (Risk, Injury, Failure, Loss, End-stage) or FoFA (Fluid overload).
    *   *Risk:* Creatinine increase >1.5x baseline.
    *   *Injury:* Creatinine increase >2x baseline or >26.5 μmol/L.
    *   *Failure:* Creatinine increase >3x baseline or >353.6 μmol/L.
3.  **Binary Outcome:** Define AKI as RIFLE >= Injury (i.e., presence of Injury, Failure, Loss, or End-stage).
4.  **Timing:** Define AKI onset as the earliest lab offset meeting criteria.

#### D. Covariates
*   **Sepsis:** Presence of ICD-9 codes 995.9 (Sepsis), 428.0 (Bacteremia), 571.9 (Septic shock).
*   **SOFA Score:** Calculated at admission using available labs and vitals.
*   **APACHE II:** Calculated from admission vitals and labs.
*   **Demographics:** Age, gender (from `demographics` table if available, otherwise proxy).
*   **Hospital:** `patientunitstayid` (can be mapped to hospital ID if available in the dataset schema).

## 5. Analysis Plan

### 5.1. Baseline Model (Additive)
*   **Model:** Logistic Regression.
*   **Formula:** P(AKI) = 1/(1 + e^-(β0 + β1·Hypotension + β2·Vasopressor))
*   **Evaluation:** AUC-ROC, Brier Score, Calibration Slope.

### 5.2. Mechanistic Model (Interactive)
*   **Model:** Logistic Regression with Interaction.
*   **Formula:** P(AKI) = 1/(1 + e^-(β0 + β1·Hypotension + β2·Vasopressor + β3·(Hypotension × Vasopressor)))
*   **Evaluation:** AUC-ROC, Brier Score, Calibration Slope.
*   **Statistical Test:** Likelihood Ratio Test (LRT) comparing Model 1 vs. Model 2.

### 5.3. Alternative Model (Non-Linear)
*   **Model:** Gradient Boosted Trees (XGBoost/LightGBM).
*   **Purpose:** To test if the linear interaction term is sufficient or if complex non-linearities exist.
*   **Evaluation:** AUC-ROC, SHAP values to visualize feature importance and interaction contributions.

### 5.4. Sensitivity Analyses
1.  **Hospital-Level Adjustment:** Include hospital fixed effects or random effects to rule out practice-pattern confounding.
2.  **Alternative Hypotension Thresholds:** Test definitions using MAP ≤60 mmHg vs. MAP ≤65 mmHg.
3.  **Time-Varying Exposure:** Treat hypotension and vasopressor exposure as time-varying covariates in a Cox Proportional Hazards model.

## 6. Falsification Criteria

| Outcome | Criteria | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Supportive** | β3 (interaction) p < 0.01; ΔAUC > 0.03; SHAP interaction values show strong positive correlation. | The multiplicative interaction is a significant predictor. Clinical guidelines should incorporate duration of hypotension combined with vasopressor intensity. |
| **Adverse** | β3 p > 0.1; No significant ΔAUC; GBDT performs no better than logistic model. | The risk is primarily additive. Current strategies focusing on individual thresholds are likely sufficient; no new interaction-based metric is needed. |
| **Inconclusive** | Interaction significant but small effect size (OR < 1.2); High variance in estimates; Results vary by hospital. | The interaction exists but may be clinically weak or confounded by hospital practices. Requires larger sample size or external validation. |
