# Bone-muscle discordance and incident fracture risk: a two-pathway design without residualization

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank participants aged ≥50 with valid DXA BMD and grip strength, does low grip strength predict incident fracture risk independently of BMD, and does this excess risk differ by baseline fall history?

The clinically consequential unresolved question is whether low BMD and low grip strength represent a single latent frailty process (where muscle weakness is merely a marker of overall biological ageing) or two separable pathways: (1) bone fragility driving fracture independent of muscle, and (2) muscle weakness driving falls that then cause fracture. This distinction has direct implications for screening and prevention: if the two pathways are separable, then dual anti-resorptive + resistance-exercise interventions would be more effective than bone-targeted therapy alone for the discordant subgroup.

The leading explanation (single latent process) is that low BMD and low grip strength are both downstream of a shared ageing/frailty trajectory, so grip strength adds no independent fracture risk beyond what BMD alone predicts after adjustment. The strongest rival is a two-pathway model in which muscle weakness contributes to fracture risk through a mechanism (falls, reduced activity, sarcopenic bone loading) that is not fully captured by BMD.

## Evidence base and closest prior work

[K1] Kanis et al. (JBMR 2016) established that BMD is the dominant predictor of fracture risk and that clinical risk factors add incremental prediction, but did not test whether muscle strength contributes independently of BMD in a large population cohort.

[K2] Mikkola et al. (JBMR 2019) demonstrated that muscle strength and BMD are correlated but not redundant, with grip strength predicting falls independently of BMD in older adults, providing the closest prior evidence for a separable muscle pathway.

[K3] Kanis et al. (JBMR 2018) described the FRAX model and its limitations, including the absence of muscle-strength inputs, motivating the present question of whether adding grip strength improves risk stratification beyond the standard BMD-based framework.

## Design

### Population and temporal boundaries
- UK Biobank participants aged ≥50 at baseline assessment (field 54).
- Valid DXA BMD (field 727, femoral neck) and grip strength (fields 46/47, mean of left/right).
- Baseline fall history (field 2296, falls in the last year).
- Incident fracture outcome: first fracture diagnosis after baseline (field 40005 date, field 40006 ICD10 fracture codes).
- Exclusion: missing BMD, missing grip strength, fracture before baseline, age <50.

### Primary estimand
Direct BMD × grip-strength interaction in a Cox proportional-hazards model for incident fracture, stratified by baseline fall history (yes/no). The interaction coefficient tests whether the fracture-risk effect of low grip strength differs across BMD strata, which is the key discriminant between the single-latent-process and two-pathway explanations.

### Adjustment hierarchy
- Model 0: unadjusted.
- Model 1: age, sex.
- Model 2: + BMI (field 21001).
- Model 3: + fall history (field 2296).
- Model 4: + physical activity (field 741), smoking (field 718), alcohol (field 791), ethnicity (field 52).

### Baseline model
Cox proportional-hazards regression with BMD (continuous, SD-standardized), grip strength (continuous, SD-standardized), and their product term. Proportional-hazards assumption checked with Schoenfeld residuals.

### Learned alternative
Gradient-boosted survival model (XGBoost with survival objective) on the same covariates, evaluated with held-out C-index and Brier score. The learned model tests whether non-linear or higher-order interactions between BMD and grip strength improve prediction beyond the Cox interaction, and whether the partial dependence on grip strength changes shape across BMD strata.

### Falsification criteria
- Adverse: non-significant or negative BMD × grip-strength interaction in Model 4, or grip-strength coefficient that disappears after adjustment for fall history.
- Inconclusive: significant interaction confined to the fall-history subgroup with no independent main effect for grip strength.
- Supportive: significant positive interaction in Model 4 with a consistent direction in the gradient-boosted partial dependence, and a held-out C-index improvement of ≥0.005 over the BMD-only model.

### Sensitivity analyses
- Restrict to participants without baseline fall history (to isolate the bone-fragility pathway).
- Restrict to participants with baseline fall history (to isolate the muscle-weakness/fall pathway).
- E-value calculation for the primary interaction to quantify unmeasured-confounding robustness.

## What the results would and would not establish

Supportive results would establish an association between BMD × grip-strength interaction and incident fracture, consistent with a two-pathway model. They would not establish causation or a specific biological mechanism (e.g., falls mediation), which would require longitudinal fall data or a mechanistic study.

Adverse results would support the single-latent-process explanation and argue against adding grip strength to BMD-based fracture risk models.

Inconclusive results (e.g., interaction only in the fall-history subgroup) would motivate a focused study on the fall pathway with better temporal resolution.
