# UKB 07: Inflammatory states with discordant CRP and GlycA

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

At similar CRP levels, persistently higher GlycA is more indicative of long-term severe infection than a single CRP elevation, rather than merely recent infection.

## Study population and key data

Individuals with CRP and NMR metabolomics; repeat-measurement subset and infection follow-up.

## Basic Approach

Compare joint CRP/GlycA patterns, use repeated measurements to validate stability, and distinguish recent from distant events.

## Significance of the Research

Distinguish transient inflammation from a more persistent host state.

## Main Challenges

GlycA is not a specific indicator of immune function; intervals between repeat samples are not uniform.

## References

Relevant background or data description, not evidence that the hypothesis is valid or novel: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-54357-0; field definitions: https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=30710; https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=23480; https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=21001

## Life Sciences

Immunity, infection, and injury tolerance

## Competing Explanations

Liver metabolism, obesity, or undiagnosed disease may alter both measures simultaneously.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

First assess whether the combined pattern is stable in repeated samples; if it is associated only with short-term events, this does not support a persistent inflammation explanation.
