# UKB fat-distribution divergence: metabolic versus functional/fall trajectories

## Status and scientific deliverable

This is a provisional, data-bound hypothesis. The deliverable is a newly fitted, held-out comparison of a simple total-adiposity baseline with a prespecified composition model testing whether visceral fat and posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) identify distinct subsequent metabolic and functional/fall trajectories. Completion requires cohort counts, temporal event tables, model estimates with uncertainty, calibration/discrimination, and a conclusion classified as supportive, adverse, or inconclusive. No association or clinical result has been computed.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

At similar total body fat, does higher visceral fat predict subsequent metabolic disease more specifically, while higher posterior-thigh MFI predicts subsequent functional decline or falls more specifically? A positive result would justify targeted body-composition assessment rather than treating adiposity as one generic risk factor; a negative result would argue against separating these phenotypes for risk stratification. The advance is a temporally ordered, same-cohort test of outcome-specific divergence, not merely another incremental T2D prediction model.

## Evidence and claim boundary

The inspected evidence supports only that myosteatosis/MFI is associated with metabolic and functional outcomes [K1], that IMAT/MFI spans metabolic, cardiovascular and oncologic settings but remains heterogeneous across imaging and disease contexts [K2], and that UKB abdominal MRI phenotypes including visceral fat improve internal prediction of incident T2D [K3]. These works do not establish the proposed divergence, causality, or clinical utility. The expert seed [prior hypothesis] supplies the data opening, not a result.

## Variables, outcomes, and analysis

Primary exposures are standardized visceral fat volume and mean left/right MFI, with body-fat percentage held fixed. Covariates are sex, age at baseline, ethnicity, baseline grip, and available medication/activity fields. Primary metabolic outcome is incident type 2 diabetes (E10/E11) after the lag; secondary metabolic outcomes are broad metabolic disease and incident diabetes subtypes. Primary functional outcome is decline in grip strength from assessment 2 to 3; secondary outcomes are new falls and incident musculoskeletal/fracture diagnoses, interpreted cautiously because falls are self-reported and fracture capture is incomplete.

The simple baseline is a penalized logistic/Cox model with age, sex, ethnicity, body-fat percentage, baseline grip, and medication/activity indicators. The substantive alternative is a composition model adding visceral fat and MFI, with a prespecified exposure-by-outcome interaction and separate outcome models; it can reveal whether fat distribution carries information lost by total adiposity and whether the two exposures have different outcome-specific contrasts. Fit within a participant-level train/test split, with all preprocessing and feature selection inside training folds. Report standardized contrasts, 95% confidence intervals, calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, C-index/AUC, and bootstrap or cluster-robust uncertainty. A learned trajectory model (for example a regularized multistate or recurrent model using repeated grip/falls) is deferred: it could capture time-varying functional trajectories that the single-change model loses, but is not selected because current event support and missingness make its added complexity scientifically uninterpretable without a prespecified validation target. Revisit it only if the composition model shows reproducible residual temporal structure.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: visceral fat has a positive metabolic contrast and MFI has a positive functional/fall contrast, both remain directionally distinct after lag, selection, and baseline-function checks, with credible intervals and acceptable held-out calibration. Adverse: contrasts are absent/reversed, or composition adds no information beyond total adiposity and demographics; this challenges the proposed divergence. Inconclusive: sparse events, unstable estimates, poor calibration, or large missingness/selection differences; this does not refute the hypothesis. A supportive association would not establish mechanism or treatment benefit. Causal claims require longitudinal biomarkers, treatment data, residual-confounding analysis, or a prospective intervention study; clinical adjudication is needed for fall/fracture validity and for interpreting medication and activity data.

## Demonstration dispositions and method record

The natural-history/Delphi demonstration is not pursued as a reproduction: its dated disease-history sequence is not needed to answer this fat-distribution question, and a sequence model would add complexity without a clear scientific contrast. The Bayesian/ALADYNOULLI demonstration is deferred: latent longitudinal structure could be useful for repeated functional trajectories, but the current design first needs the simpler composition comparison and stronger event support. The Oncoformer demonstration is not applicable because the full multimodal image experiment and main methods are unavailable; a lab-only adaptation would not answer this question. The selected CPU tabular design is feasible within the solver envelope; no GPU is required. The deferred learned alternative and its revisit criterion are recorded above.
