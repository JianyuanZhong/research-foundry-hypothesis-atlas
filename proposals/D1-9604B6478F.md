# Residual instability at live ICU discharge: a standardized risk contrast and a fair chronology test

## Scientific deliverable and material evolution

The future Harbor solver must newly produce:

1. a reproducible first-live-ICU-discharge cohort and a timed 48-hour label audit, including coverage exclusions and untimed-death unknowns;
2. a fixed numeric late-instability index (LII) from the final 12 hours before ICU outtime;
3. a covariate-standardized association estimate for the 48-hour composite, with competing-risk and ascertainability sensitivities;
4. paired held-out predictions from an order-blind aggregate logistic model and a chronological masked GRU that receive the same context vector, allowed source tables, patient split, and endpoint;
5. bootstrap uncertainty, calibration, endpoint/coverage sensitivities, and a preterminal chronology-shuffle falsification; and
6. an interpretation report that maps every conclusion to computed outputs and labels causal, preventability, adjudication, and transport claims as unresolved.

Readiness, a favorable metric, and confirmation of the directional hypothesis are not completion.

This is a meaningful child of the assigned assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. The question is retained because its time zero, outcome, and structured physiology are directly observable in MIMIC-IV. The evolution makes the association and sequence estimands non-overlapping, makes the model comparison fair by giving both models the same context vector, and treats a flagged death without a usable time as an unknown timed label rather than a negative.

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and evidence boundary

At the decision to transfer a patient out of the ICU, does residual measurable instability during the preceding 12 hours identify a clinically important, short-horizon risk group beyond the latest recorded state and admission/ICU context? If so, this could motivate a discharge-review or post-ICU-monitoring hypothesis for prospective evaluation. ICU readmission and early death consume scarce critical-care capacity and are consequential to patients, but this retrospective dataset cannot say that either event was preventable or that delaying transfer would improve it.

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is an observational association:

> Among first eligible live ICU discharges with adequate pre-discharge measurement coverage and an ascertainable timed outcome, a higher prespecified 12-hour numeric LII is associated with a higher probability of first same-hospitalization ICU readmission or timed in-hospital death within 48 hours of ICU outtime, after adjustment for prespecified admission/ICU context and observation coverage.

The distinct secondary representation hypothesis is:

> Given the same context vector, source whitelist, endpoint, and subject-level split, a chronological masked GRU over twelve hourly bins has better held-out probabilistic accuracy and calibration than an order-blind aggregate logistic model. Its advantage should attenuate when only preterminal bin order is shuffled within each test patient while terminal state and the patient's measurements are preserved.

The available evidence supports only the weaker claims that this frozen snapshot contains ICU episode boundaries, admission outcomes, and timestamped structured observations, and that the selected dictionary item IDs have the audited meanings and units. The expert seed does not supply a result or validated cutoffs. The current public literature includes MIMIC-IV ICU-readmission model comparisons (Remoundou et al., 2026, PMCID `PMC13409962`; the acquired full-text XML was read for its abstract/introduction/methods-relevant passages), but that supports the existence of related predictive work, not this general live-discharge phenotype, 48-hour estimand, fairness of a chronology comparison, or a causal/clinical intervention effect. The three research-ambition demonstrations are method/rigor examples, not evidence for this hypothesis and are not reproduced.

Thus, neither residual physiology as a causal discharge signal, preventability, nor transportability is already established here. Those are stronger claims the experiment cannot resolve.

## Population, index, and temporal boundaries

The source unit is an ICU stay linked to one hospital admission.

1. Read every `icu/icustays` row with non-null `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, outtime`. Reject `outtime <= intime`, `outtime < admittime`, and rows without a matching admission.
2. Inner join `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`. Require non-null `admittime` and `dischtime`.
3. Define an eligible live ICU discharge at `t0 = icustays.outtime) when `outtime < dischtime` and either `deathtime` is null or `deathtime > outtime). The primary index is the earliest eligible live discharge per `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, ordered by `outtime). A secondary all-eligible-discharge analysis clusters by subject and hospitalization.
4. Define the primary analytic population as eligible first discharges with at least four of twelve bins containing at least two valid core numeric channels and an ascertainable timed primary outcome. Retain an all-eligible flow table, including the number excluded for coverage and for an untimed death flag. This explicitly limits inference to a minimally measurable, label-ascertainable population rather than treating missing charting as normal.
5. Split all primary analytic observations by `subject_id); no person can occur in more than one partition. Cross-subject calendar alignment is prohibited because MIMIC timestamps are subject-specifically shifted.

The feature window is exactly `[t0 - 12 hours, t0]`, with twelve backward bins:
`[t0-12h,t0-11h), ...,[t0-1h,t0]`.
Use `charttime` for charted measurements and `starttime/endtime` for intervals. `storetime` is never used for ordering, inclusion, or missingness. A row with clinical time after `t0) is prohibited, including if it was stored earlier.

## Primary outcome and competing risks

For each index, define the first event in `(t0, min(t0+48h, dischtime)]):

- ICU readmission: a later `icu/icustays` row with the same `subject_id, hadm_id`, a different `stay_id), and `intime > t0);
- timed in-hospital death: `hosp/admissions.deathtime > t0), within 48 hours and no later than `dischtime).

The binary primary outcome is one if either event occurs. In a same-time tie, death is assigned first for the competing-cause table; the binary composite is unchanged. A later ICU stay that begins after `dischtime` is not a same-hospitalization event.

A record with `hospital_expire_flag=1) and null `deathtime) cannot establish whether death occurred inside the 48-hour window. It is an untimed-death unknown and is excluded from the timed primary analytic population without imputation; its count and covariate/coverage distribution are reported. A flag of zero with no `deathtime), or a non-null death time outside the window, is not an event for the timed endpoint. These rules avoid silently converting outcome ascertainment failure into a negative. A sensitivity analysis reports bounds under assigning every untimed flag to event and to non-event, with the latter not presented as the primary estimate.

Secondary outcomes are the analogous 7-day composite; readmission alone at 48 hours and 7 days; timed death at 48 hours and 7 days; and in-hospital death through `dischtime), where `hospital_expire_flag) may serve as a label-only indicator if `deathtime) is absent. For cause-specific 48-hour and 7-day analyses report mutually exclusive readmission-first, death-first, and no-event categories, cause-specific cumulative incidence, and cause-specific hazard ratios. Treat alive hospital discharge before the horizon as administrative censoring for the same-admission risk set; as a sensitivity, treat it as a competing terminal event or use inverse-censoring weights if positivity and model diagnostics support that analysis. No endpoint is called preventable.

## LII construction

Core numeric channels are HR, MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, FiO2, O2 flow, and PEEP. Use `valuenum) and `valueuom). Reject fixed impossible ranges: HR 20-250 bpm, MAP 20-200 mmHg, RR 2-80/min, SpO2 50-100%, temperature 30-43 C, O2 flow 0-100 L/min, and PEEP 0-40 cmH2O. For FiO2, divide numeric values above 1.5 by 100, then retain 0.21-1.00. These rules are fixed before outcome analysis.

Within each bin, deduplicate each item at a clinical timestamp by the median of valid numeric values. For MAP, select the highest-priority available source in this order: arterial `220052), non-invasive `220181), ART `225312); then use the median of that source's values. A valid bin has at least two observed core channels. Do not impute an unobserved channel as normal.

Abnormal indicators are fixed: HR <50 or >120; MAP <65; RR <8 or >24; SpO2 <92; temperature <36 or >=38.5 C; FiO2 >0.40; O2 flow >=4 L/min; PEEP >5 cmH2O. For each bin store numeric values, masks, abnormal flags, and observed-channel count. Define
`LII_i = mean_b( abnormal_core_count_b / observed_core_count_b )`
over valid bins, and require at least four valid bins. Report valid-bin count and channel-level coverage. Define the Q25 and Q75 contrast from LII values in the analytic cohort without using outcome labels; also report the continuous per-IQR association. A secondary analysis adds pressor/inotrope presence and overlap minutes, grouped by presence/duration rather than incomparable dose.

For clipped `inputevents), pressor/inotrope IDs are `221906) norepinephrine, `222315) vasopressin, `221289/229617) epinephrine, `221749/229630/229631/229632) phenylephrine, `221662) dopamine, `221653) dobutamine, and `221986) milrinone. If start or end time is missing or end precedes start, do not impute duration; count the malformed interval and use only a separate presence flag when its clinical time is usable. Support text/device items are not required for the primary LII, avoiding an ambiguous text tokenization dependency.

## Primary association estimand and analysis

Fit one prespecified logistic association model in the primary analytic population:
`logit P(Y48=1 | LII, Z) = alpha + beta*LII_IQR + gamma*Z`.

`Z) consists of anchor_age, gender, anchor_year_group, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU duration at `t0), admission_type, admission_location, valid-bin count, and core-channel observation coverage. Number of earlier ICU stays in the same hospitalization is computed from `icu/icustays` using `intime < t0) and is included in `Z). Use ridge or weakly regularized logistic fitting with penalty and all encoding/scaling choices fixed without outcome-driven feature selection. Report per-IQR odds ratio, standardized risk at LII Q25 and Q75 holding each observed `Z_i) fixed, and `RD75-25 = mean_i[p_i(Q75)-p_i(Q25)]), with 95% subject-level bootstrap intervals and cluster-robust standard errors. The spline and quintile gradient are prespecified descriptive checks for nonlinearity/monotonicity, not replacements for the primary estimand.

This is a conditional observational association, not an effect of delaying discharge, ICU transfer, monitoring, oxygen, or vasopressor treatment. Adjustment cannot remove unmeasured treatment limitation, clinician intent, bed availability, staffing, or severity not charted.

## Fair baseline and substantive learned alternative

Let `C_i) be the context vector above, and `S_i=(s_{i1},...,s_{i12})) be the twelve hourly vectors containing the eight numeric channels, masks, fixed abnormal flags, observed-channel count, and clipped pressor presence/minutes.

Both models use exactly the same analytic cohort, endpoint, subject split, context vector `C_i), and allowed source whitelist. The difference is representation:

- Aggregate baseline: penalized logistic regression on `C_i), terminal-bin numeric/mask values, and order-blind summaries of `S_i): per-channel mean/median/minimum/maximum/SD/IQR, abnormal-bin fraction, valid-bin count, and observation count/missingness; plus pressor presence and total clipped minutes. Exclude slopes, reversal counts, bin IDs, sequence positions, future fields, labels, discharge location, and note text.
- Learned alternative: a small masked GRU on chronological `S_i), with the same `C_i) concatenated to the final hidden representation before the sigmoid head. It uses no additional table or field. Numeric scaling, imputation constants, and categorical encodings are learned on training data only; hidden size is prespecified as 32 or 64, dropout selected on validation Brier score, early stopping on validation Brier score, five fixed random seeds, and one sigmoid head for Y48. The model is not a causal model.

The GRU can reveal persistence, abrupt recovery, fluctuation, and coupled channel order when two patients have similar aggregate summaries. The paired baseline reveals whether that extra temporal structure is reproducible and calibrated; a tiny ranking gain or neural-network complexity is not a clinical advance. A last-bin-only logistic model is a descriptive comparator. A mask-only GRU tests documentation-process dependence.

The secondary sequence estimand is:
`DeltaBrier = Brier_GRU - Brier_aggregate`
on the untouched test partition, with negative values favorable. Report paired DeltaBrier, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC, AUPRC, and Brier, with 95% subject-bootstrap intervals. The primary representation criterion is a lower Brier score and no important calibration degradation, not AUROC alone.

Falsification ablations:

- shuffle bins 1-11 independently within each test patient while holding terminal bin 12, all measurements/masks, context, and endpoint fixed; a genuinely order-dependent GRU gain should attenuate;
- shuffle all twelve bins as a secondary check, report the changed terminal state rather than hiding it;
- fit a mask-only GRU with the same architecture and imputation; if it matches the full GRU, documentation intensity rather than physiology is a likely signal;
- run fixed within-split outcome-label permutation as a computational null: discrimination and calibration improvement should collapse to chance;
- export an assertion that every feature row has clinical time <= t0 and that no prohibited endpoint/disposition field occurs in either matrix.

## Split, uncertainty, and decision rules

Before fitting, freeze one deterministic approximately stratified 70/15/15 train/validation/test split by subject. All preprocessing, scaling, regularization, vocabulary, hyperparameter, calibration-map, and operating-threshold choices use training/validation only. No test outcome is used to choose LII thresholds, features, or model settings; exposure quartiles are outcome-free and their calculation is logged.

For the association, report 1,000 subject-level bootstrap replicates (or, only if the declared execution cap prevents completion, report the exact completed number and label the interval as execution-limited), cluster-robust standard errors, calibration of the association model, and coverage/untimed-death sensitivity tables. For the predictive comparison, average five seed predictions per test patient and use 1,000 paired subject-bootstrap replicates for Brier, calibration, AUROC, AUPRC, and DeltaBrier. Preserve bootstrap seeds/checkpoints and report exact successful replicate counts.

The association is supportive only if the RD75-25 point estimate is at least 2 percentage points, its 95% interval excludes zero, the continuous gradient is directionally consistent, and the result is not reversed by coverage or all-eligible sensitivity. The 2-point rule is a predeclared interpretive threshold, not proof of clinical utility. The sequence hypothesis is supportive only if DeltaBrier is below zero with a 95% interval excluding zero, calibration is not clinically worsened, and the GRU advantage attenuates under the preterminal shuffle. Report all continuous estimates even when these rules fail.

Adverse association evidence is a flat/reversed LII gradient, disappearance after coverage adjustment, or confinement to a care-unit/documentation stratum. Adverse sequence evidence is no reproducible increment, a gain that survives order shuffling, a gain reproduced by the mask-only model, or worse calibration despite better ranking. These outcomes support the narrower conclusion that terminal state, charting intensity, or treatment selection—not recoverable temporal physiology—contains the signal.

Inconclusive evidence includes low event counts, inadequate coverage, high untimed-death unknowns, malformed intervals, unstable calibration, failed unit validation, or discordant endpoints/sensitivities. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.

Computationally checkable claims include exact source/member/column/item provenance, joins, inclusion counts, time filters, coverage and unknown-label accounting, absence of leakage, split integrity, model configurations, held-out predictions, metrics, bootstrap intervals, and shuffle/mask effects.

Unavailable or non-identifiable claims include whether ICU transfer was appropriate, whether readmission/death was preventable, whether delaying discharge or adding monitoring changes outcome, clinician intent, treatment limitations/goals of care, bed/staffing pressure, post-ICU ward surveillance, whether charted oxygen values equal delivered physiology, and external validity. Raw waveforms, MIMIC-CXR images, and validated clinician-adjudicated discharge-readiness labels are unavailable. These require clinician review, an external hospital cohort, and ultimately prospective or interventional evaluation.

## Alternative MIMIC questions and explicit selection/deferral

I screened all ten MIMIC expert cards rather than treating their numbering as rank. The relevant alternatives were:

- `mimic-01) asynchronous organ recovery: potentially important, but “infection or shock” lacks a single validated index and recovery-state labels are unavailable; a bounded adaptation would become another prognostic phenotype rather than a stronger causal question.
- `mimic-02) diuretic initiation timing: clinically actionable, but treatment-by-indication, time-varying confounding, fluid-status measurement, and a defensible no-treatment/deferred strategy are not identifiable from the supplied snapshot without a substantially different target-trial design.
- `mimic-03) discordant AKI recovery and `mimic-08) creatinine during diuresis: creatinine, Foley output, and fluid proxies are available, but measured kidney function, congestion, acute-heart-failure phenotype, and treatment indications are not; they remain promising secondary studies, not replacements.
- `mimic-04) persistent hyperlactatemia: lactate, perfusion proxies, and vasoactive items are available, but selective repeat testing and absence of microcirculation/liver-function adjudication can make the phenotype non-identifiable.
- `mimic-05) stress hyperglycemia: the required chronic HbA1c comparison is selectively measured and the exact glucose/insulin temporal target is not part of this discharge question.
- `mimic-06) persistent deep sedation: the seed requires validated sedation-depth and liberation-state definitions plus treatment indication control; no verified binding was found in this audit.
- `mimic-07) antimicrobial de-escalation: cultures/antibiotics and source control/timing are incomplete; a treatment effect would be strongly confounded.
- `mimic-09) transfusion-effect heterogeneity: hemoglobin is available, but ischemia, bleeding adjudication, transfusion assignment, and supply-demand imbalance are insufficient for the proposed causal effect.
- `mimic-10) residual instability: retained and developed because `outtime), same-admission subsequent ICU stay, timed `deathtime), and pre-outtime numeric observations are directly bindable.

The substantive alternative estimand considered was to replace the prognostic association with a causal effect of delaying ICU discharge or adding monitoring. It was rejected as non-identifiable here because no randomized policy, discharge intent, treatment-limitation data, ward monitoring, or preventability adjudication exists. The primary association plus a fair sequence-increment test is therefore the strongest evidence-bounded deliverable. Revisit a treatment estimand only with a prespecified target-trial protocol, measured treatment/intent timestamps, expert confounding review, and preferably an external or prospective design.
