# Renal-trajectory heterogeneity of hypotension duration: an eICU transportability successor

## Decision target and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent asks whether six-hour duration of observed MAP below 65 mmHg adds transportable renal-risk information beyond minimum MAP. This child preserves that question and makes the clinical refinement explicit:

Among adults with a stable, timestamp-safe pre-exposure creatinine trajectory, does the association between observed low-MAP duration and a subsequent creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal differ by pre-landmark renal trajectory, after accounting for minimum MAP, current renal level, observation density and measured illness severity?

The primary hypothesis is a duration-by-renal-trajectory interaction: a given additional low-MAP burden will have a larger standardized 24-hour renal-signal risk difference in patients whose pre-exposure creatinine is rising than in patients with a stable or falling pre-exposure trajectory. The competing possibility—that duration has a similar association across renal trajectories, or no reproducible association—is equally falsifiable and clinically important. This is a risk-marker hypothesis, not a treatment-effect hypothesis.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is limited to data availability. The configured eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative patient/discharge offsets, periodic and aperiodic MAP fields, timestamped laboratory results, documented infusion records and hospital identifiers. Direct source-header inspection and a bounded audit found labname=creatinine rows predominantly labeled mg/dL, including case variants, while urinary creatinine is a separate analyte that must not be pooled. The data do not establish any hypotension effect, AKI rate, renal-trajectory interaction or cross-hospital transport.

The unresolved claim is whether pre-existing renal trajectory identifies clinically meaningful heterogeneity in the duration signal, rather than the apparent duration association being a consequence of a single noisy creatinine baseline, renal measurement frequency, minimum-MAP severity or hospital-specific practice. A positive result would identify a phenotype for intensified observation and prospective organ-protection research. It would not establish that raising MAP, administering a pressor or changing monitoring prevents AKI.

This is a substantive successor because it changes the scientific deliverable from a population-average duration association to a prespecified effect-modification estimand that can distinguish two clinically different interpretations: duration as a broadly applicable renal-risk marker versus duration as a marker mainly for patients already showing renal deterioration. The parent duration-only estimand remains a required nested result.

## Population, landmark and temporal boundaries

Use the first ICU stay per person: age >=18, map eICU age >89 to 90 and report nonnumeric ages, unitvisitnumber=1, nonmissing hospitalid, numeric unitdischargeoffset, and at least 36 hours from ICU offset 0 to discharge. If multiple valid rows remain for one uniquepid, retain the earliest valid admission record and report the conflict.

Primary landmarks are t = 24, 30, 36, ..., 72 hours after ICU admission. This later start is required for a genuine pre-exposure renal trajectory. Retain only landmarks with unitdischargeoffset >= t + 1440 minutes. Exclude a landmark with either EOL offset at or before t. The primary analysis allows multiple eligible landmarks per stay and uses patient-clustered inference; a prespecified sensitivity samples one eligible landmark per stay using a recorded seed.

At every landmark, the time order is:
- renal-trajectory window: [t-2880, t-360] minutes;
- MAP exposure window: [t-360, t) minutes;
- outcome window: (t, t+1440] minutes.

All predictors must have a clinical/result timestamp no later than t, and renal-trajectory values additionally must have result and revision timestamps no later than t-360. Rows outside these windows are not backfilled.

## MAP exposure and observation-density handling

Primary MAP is valid numeric vitalPeriodic.systemicmean in a prespecified plausible range of -50 to 300 mmHg. Sort within patientunitstayid. Each valid observation represents at most the following min(5, next observation offset minus current observation offset) minutes, clipped to [t-360,t). Do not carry values across gaps or beyond t. Record covered minutes, valid observations, largest gap and the fraction of the six-hour window covered.

Require at least 80% periodic-MAP coverage for the primary estimand, with 70% and 90% thresholds as sensitivities. Define low_map_minutes as covered minutes with systemicmean <65, low_map_fraction as low_map_minutes divided by covered minutes, minimum_map as the minimum valid periodic systemicmean, and MAP mean, SD, last MAP and recovery slope. Low-MAP episodes are descriptive; episode count is not the primary exposure.

The primary contrast is low-MAP burden >60 versus 0-60 covered minutes, with minimum MAP modeled by a restricted cubic spline and interaction with renal trajectory retained. Report a continuous 60-minute contrast and prespecified 0, >0-60, >60-120 and >120 minute categories. The parent duration-only estimand is the marginal duration contrast standardized over this same eligible population.

Repeat with periodic thresholds 60 and 70 mmHg; 70% and 90% coverage; vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean alone; and a source-priority combination in which periodic observations are used first and aperiodic observations fill only otherwise uncovered intervals. A result that exists only under one source or threshold rule is weak. Never infer true continuous hypotension during an unobserved gap.

## Pre-exposure renal trajectory and outcome

### Primary renal phenotype

Within [t-2880,t-360], retain numeric lab.labresult from exact normalized labname=creatinine, with compatible source unit/system/interface after the label audit. Require two results separated by at least 360 minutes, both with labresultoffset <= t-360 and labresultrevisedoffset <= t-360. Let c0 be the earlier and c1 the later of the two most recent qualifying results. If more than two qualify, use the two most recent before the MAP window and report a sensitivity using all qualifying observations.

Define current pre-exposure renal level as c1 and renal trajectory as:
renal_slope = (c1-c0) / ((offset1-offset0)/1440), in mg/dL per 24 hours.

The primary interaction uses centered continuous slope, with an interaction between low-MAP minutes and renal_slope. Secondary display groups are falling (< -0.10 mg/dL/24h), stable (-0.10 to 0.10) and rising (>0.10); these are display strata, not clinical thresholds. Include c1, time between results, number of qualifying prior results and time since c1 so slope is not substituted for renal level or testing opportunity.

A landmark is ineligible for the primary estimand if the two-result trajectory cannot be constructed. The parent's one-result paired-creatinine cohort is retained as a sensitivity cohort, clearly labeled as less robust and not pooled with the primary interaction estimate.

### Renal outcome and competing/censoring events

The primary outcome is a creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal, not adjudicated AKI. Using c1 as pre-exposure baseline, define the first future lab creatinine in (t,t+1440] that is at least c1 + 0.3 mg/dL or at least 1.5 times c1, requiring c1 >0 and a prespecified plausible-value range established by the label audit. Do not count later values after the first signal. This is an operational EHR signal, not KDIGO AKI, because the release does not guarantee outpatient baseline creatinine, urine-output semantics or clinical adjudication.

ICU death and alive ICU discharge/transfer in (t,t+1440], identified from patient.unitdischargeoffset, unitdischargestatus and unitdischargelocation, are competing events. A death/discharge before a qualifying future creatinine is not coded as renal-negative. If a landmark remains in the ICU but no future creatinine is observed, renal outcome is observation-censored, not negative.

Estimate renal cumulative incidence using a prespecified observation model for any eligible future creatinine before 24 hours, with inverse-probability-of-observation weighting and weight diagnostics. Covariates known at t include c1, renal slope, prior creatinine count and gap, MAP coverage/count, minimum MAP, physiology/support summaries, admission covariates and time since admission. Report complete-case and unweighted analyses. Truncate extreme weights only at a rule frozen before outcome comparison and report the fraction truncated.

The primary estimand is the standardized 24-hour renal-signal risk difference for >60 versus 0-60 low-MAP minutes at the mean renal slope and at prespecified falling, stable and rising slope values, with a duration-by-slope interaction. Standardize to eligible landmarks; do not interpret the interaction causally. Secondary estimands are the parent marginal duration contrast, renal-signal cumulative incidence with death/discharge competing risks, and separate 24-hour death and discharge probabilities.

Use hospital-stratified patient bootstrap 95% intervals, resampling patients within hospital so repeated landmarks do not create false precision. Report eligible landmarks, exposure support, paired-creatinine support, future-lab support, competing events, censoring and weights by hospital before interpreting estimates.

## Baseline and substantive alternative before method selection

### Simple transparent baseline: nested regularized interaction models

Fit two models on the same population and outcome:

B0, duration-free: minimum-MAP spline, MAP coverage/count and recent MAP summaries; c1, continuous renal slope, missingness and measurement-gap variables; heart rate, respiration and SpO2 summaries/slopes; age, sex, ethnicity, time since admission; admission-only Apache variables/scores; current documented pressor and ventilation indicators; EOL exclusion and other timestamp-safe covariates, but no low-MAP duration.

B1, duration and interaction: all B0 terms plus low-MAP minutes/fraction and the prespecified low-MAP-minutes-by-renal-slope interaction. Minimum MAP remains in both models. Hospital is a stratification/split variable, not a feature.

Use restricted cubic splines for minimum MAP and renal slope with knots frozen from training hospitals, elastic-net regularization tuned on a separate hospital validation set, and cause-specific models for renal signal, death and discharge. The scientific comparison is the standardized interaction risk difference and calibration/transport of B1 versus B0, not AUROC. A meaningful result requires uncertainty-bounded differences that reproduce in untouched hospitals.

This baseline is primary because its estimand is transparent and clinically reviewable. Its limitation is that two-point slope and total low-MAP minutes can lose nonlinear recovery shape, irregular gaps and measurement error.

### Substantive learned alternative: observation-masked temporal model

Fit a bounded temporal convolution or GRU alternative on the same question and outcome. Inputs are exactly timestamp-safe fields: six one-hour pre-landmark MAP bins with values, valid-minute coverage and masks; heart rate, respiration and SpO2 bin summaries/masks; the six-hour low-MAP trajectory and minimum MAP; the qualifying pre-exposure creatinine sequence with values, time gaps and masks; c1, the two-point slope; documented pressor and ventilation records; admission-only covariates and time since admission. No hospital ID or future row is an input.

Fit T_min using minimum-MAP representation but no low-MAP trajectory beyond minimum, and T_shape using ordered six-hour MAP bins, allowing brief, sustained, recurrent and recovery-shaped patterns and nonlinear interaction with renal state. The target is the same 24-hour first-event outcome with renal signal, death/discharge competing events and observation censoring.

Train on patients from a deterministic 60% hospital set; use a disjoint 20% hospital set for early stopping and hyperparameter selection; reserve the remaining 20% untouched for transport. No patient crosses splits. Evaluate per supported held-out hospital and pooled held-out hospitals with at least 200 eligible landmarks and both exposure/outcome levels. Use three fixed training seeds, hospital-stratified patient bootstrap uncertainty, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, standardized risk differences at the same slope values and decision curves at thresholds frozen before fitting. AUROC is secondary.

The temporal alternative can reveal whether equal total low-MAP duration differs when hypotension is early versus late, interrupted versus sustained or followed by recovery, and whether the renal-slope interaction persists after modeling the full pre-window creatinine sequence and masks. It is a scientific probe of exposure shape and renal-state measurement error, not a search for a small predictive gain. B1 remains primary if T_shape improves prediction but does not reproduce the duration-by-slope pattern or cannot transport.

Budget under inputs.json is two CPU cores, up to 8 GiB and science_seconds=7200. Plan 20-40 minutes for streaming extraction/landmarks, under 15 minutes per regularized model, and 45-90 minutes for the two small temporal variants with early stopping and three seeds. GPU is deferred because this bounded sequence experiment does not require it. If support or memory fails, defer T_min/T_shape with the reason recorded; do not replace the renal-signal question with an easier target. The primary baseline and interaction estimate must still run or the result is inconclusive.

## Hospital transport and observation-process analysis

Use a deterministic hospital split from sorted hospitalid hashes with a recorded seed, reserving entire hospitals for validation and test. Do not allow landmarks from the same hospital into both fitting and transport. Report hospital descriptors from hospital (numbedscategory, teachingstatus, region) only descriptively.

For every held-out hospital, report eligible-landmark support, low-MAP exposure support within renal-slope strata, creatinine-label support, future-lab observation probability, death/discharge support, MAP coverage, calibration, Brier score and standardized duration contrasts. A held-out hospital with fewer than 200 eligible landmarks or lacking both exposure/outcome levels is a support failure and is not silently pooled into a success claim. The main transport claim requires at least three supported held-out hospitals or an explicit inconclusive outcome.

Observation density is an estimand threat. Report MAP coverage/count, pre-exposure creatinine count, time since last creatinine and future-creatinine observation by renal-slope group and hospital. Show observation-weighted, complete-case and unweighted estimates together. A large change is evidence of measurement-process dependence, not robust renal biology.

## Falsification and interpretation gates

1. Hard feasibility gate: do not interpret effects unless exact serum-creatinine labeling, comparable mg/dL units, positive plausible values, paired pre-exposure results, future-lab support, MAP coverage and within-slope exposure support are documented. If units cannot be justified, the primary result is inconclusive.

2. Timestamp placebo: apply the same creatinine-rise rule to a pre-landmark placebo window ending at t, using only timestamps before t and a baseline available before that placebo window. A similarly large duration-by-slope placebo interaction indicates reverse-time severity or documentation bias.

3. MAP-order permutation: permute the order of valid pre-landmark MAP observations within each stay/window while preserving values, count and coverage. A genuine duration/shape signal should attenuate while minimum-MAP and coverage summaries remain comparable.

4. Source and threshold sensitivity: repeat MAP thresholds 60/65/70, coverage 70/80/90%, periodic-only versus aperiodic-only/source-priority rules, and alternative burden contrasts. Repeat the outcome using the last pre-exposure qualifying baseline rather than c1, and using all pre-exposure trajectory observations. A result present only under an arbitrary rule is not supportive.

5. Renal-measurement sensitivity: compare two-result slope, the one-result parent cohort, all-observation sequence input, complete-case and observation-weighted analyses. Report whether interaction sign and uncertainty survive adjustment for creatinine count, gap and future-observation probability.

6. Competing-event/censoring sensitivity: compare observation-weighted cause-specific models, complete-case analyses and models treating death/discharge as explicit competing events. Never reclassify missing future creatinine as absence of deterioration.

7. Boundary/treatment sensitivity: exclude landmarks within 12 hours of discharge, with abrupt loss of MAP coverage or with an EOL proxy at or before t. Repeat with and without pressor/ventilation indicators. Because infusion records have no reliable end times and treatment intent is unavailable, these are not causal treatment adjustments.

8. Independence/transport: repeat with one landmark per stay, patient-level rather than landmark-level splitting, and each supported hospital held out in turn. Do not rank hospitals.

9. Parent comparator: in the same eligible cohort, fit the parent's duration-only model without the renal-trajectory interaction and compare the nested interaction model on standardized risks, calibration and held-out-hospital reproducibility. Do not declare the child superior based on a small AUROC change.

Supportive results require a prespecified duration-by-slope interaction whose uncertainty excludes the null in the primary analysis; directionally consistent contrasts in falling, stable and rising strata; no strong placebo interaction; attenuation under MAP-order permutation; robustness to source, coverage, label and observation-weighting sensitivities; and reproduction in supported held-out hospitals. This supports an observational, transportable renal-risk stratification signal and prospective adjudication work. It does not support a MAP threshold, pressor target or causal AKI-prevention claim.

Adverse results include an adequately precise null or negative interaction, disappearance after observation weighting or measurement-gap adjustment, persistence in the placebo, survival of minimum MAP but not duration after permutation, or failure in held-out hospitals. This would suggest that renal trajectory does not materially modify a robust duration signal, or that the apparent signal is severity/documentation/measurement process.

Inconclusive results include failure of creatinine label/unit comparability, too few paired trajectories or future observations, no exposure contrast within slope strata, unsupported held-out hospitals, unstable weights, wide intervals spanning meaningful positive and null interactions, or an alternative that cannot be fit within declared resources. No clinical recommendation follows.

## Unavailable evidence and claims requiring another study

Computationally checkable are source hashes and snapshot, exact filters/joins, ICU-relative timestamp ordering, MAP coverage and low-MAP arithmetic, creatinine label map and unit gate, paired-trajectory construction, event/censoring/competing-event counts, model fits, standardized risks, interaction estimates, bootstrap uncertainty, calibration/Brier metrics, split integrity, site support, weights and falsification outputs.

Not established are adjudicated KDIGO AKI, urine-output semantics, pre-ICU baseline renal function, true perfusion during MAP gaps, waveform-level MAP, clinician intent, complete fluid/pressor dose and stop times, nutrition or dialysis exposure semantics, treatment limitation beyond the EOL proxy, reasons for creatinine testing, lab-interface outages versus deliberate testing, staffing/capacity, or whether acting on a risk marker improves outcomes. These require clinical adjudication, richer treatment/measurement metadata or a prospective study. No causal treatment effect is estimable from this observational design.

## Scientific deliverable and alternatives not chosen

Completion requires newly fitted timestamp-safe B0 and B1 models plus T_min and T_shape when support and compute gates pass; a machine-readable provenance/label/unit/eligibility manifest; MAP and creatinine observation audits; primary interaction and parent duration-only standardized risks; death/discharge competing-event and observation-censoring outputs; patient/hospital bootstrap uncertainty; held-out-hospital calibration and risk contrasts; all source, threshold, coverage, measurement, placebo and permutation sensitivities; and an interpretation record linking every conclusion to computed outputs.

The scientific deliverable is a new estimate of whether renal trajectory modifies transportable information in hypotension duration, not a reproduction, source audit alone or prediction leaderboard.

The simple regularized interaction model is selected as the primary method because its estimand is transparent and reviewable. The temporal model is retained as a substantive alternative because it can test exposure shape and latent renal-history information that the baseline loses. It is deferred from primary interpretation if support, calibration or resources fail; predictive improvement alone does not supersede the baseline interaction result. A state-space latent-creatinine model is not chosen: it would be interesting for explicit measurement-error separation, but its assumptions about creatinine dynamics and observation intensity are less directly verifiable here than the prespecified sequence model. Revisit it only with stable repeated-lab support across hospitals, a measurement-error calibration set and a bounded implementation preserving the same outcome.

The parent monitoring-gap proposal remains a comparator, not a discarded hypothesis. Hyperoxia, insulin-after-medication, vasopressor-strategy and ventilatory-burden seeds remain deferred because available fields do not yet establish complete administered treatment, dose continuity, time-varying oxygenation units or adjudicated outcomes. Revisit them only after source-specific support audits demonstrate those dependencies. These are deferred directions, not negative findings.
