TITLE
Does the interval after preoperative TACE predict clinically interpretable postoperative biochemical hepatic decompensation after HCC resection? A continuous-time, case-mix-validated observational study

SUBSTANTIVE ADVANCE OVER PARENT
The parent primarily compares an arbitrary >28–60 versus >60–90-day cut using a bilirubin ratio. This child makes the clinically consequential endpoint—not the cut point—the primary target. It replaces the threshold contrast with a continuous elapsed-time dose-response analysis and defines a prespecified joint bilirubin/INR decompensation signal that is closer to the laboratory component of post-hepatectomy liver failure than an isolated bilirubin ratio. It also makes tumor and operative case-mix validation a required analysis gate, rather than an optional sensitivity. The available scan shows that a dynamic serial readiness exposure cannot be the primary estimand: only 32/847 patients in >14–180 days have the specified early-to-preoperative bilirubin/INR/albumin triad, and only 18 have the mid-to-preoperative triad. Those trajectories are therefore retained only as an explicitly exploratory descriptive/effect-modification analysis, not presented as feasible readiness evidence.

CLINICAL QUESTION AND HYPOTHESIS
Among adults with documented HCC who undergo a first recorded qualifying liver resection after a prior TACE, is a shorter elapsed interval from the last TACE to resection associated with a higher risk of postoperative biochemical hepatic decompensation? The falsifiable hypothesis is that the risk of a joint day-5-to-8 bilirubin and INR worsening signal follows a clinically meaningful, non-flat relationship with exact elapsed hours/24 from last TACE to resection, after adjustment for measured baseline liver function, tumor burden, and operative case mix.

Existing evidence supports that PHLF is consequential and that liver reserve, portal hypertension, tumor burden, and resection extent matter; it does not establish an optimal waiting interval after TACE. This study tests an association only. It cannot establish that delaying surgery prevents PHLF, because the cohort is selected on eventual recorded resection and lacks complete outside care, future-liver-remnant volume, ICG clearance, standardized Child-Pugh/ALBI, treatment intent, and management data required for clinical ISGLS grading.

POPULATION AND TEMPORAL BOUNDARIES
Unit: one patient and the patient's earliest qualifying recorded liver resection. Include age >=18 at the index encounter; a diagnosis-name match for 肝细胞癌, 原发性肝癌, 原发性肝细胞癌, or word-bounded HCC; a qualifying TACE-like procedure strictly before the operation; exact elapsed interval >14 and <=180 days for the primary continuous analysis; and at least one numeric preoperative total bilirubin and INR in [-7,0) days plus postoperative bilirubin and INR in [5,8) days relative to operation for the complete-case primary outcome. The >14-day lower bound is a feasibility/design boundary selected before outcome modeling to exclude same-course immediate procedures while retaining enough time range to estimate a curve; it is not interpreted as a biological threshold.

Use the earliest qualifying liver resection per patient after sorting valid 开始时间. Define TACE as 手术 matching TACE|化疗栓塞|动脉化疗栓塞, case-insensitive. Define resection as 手术 matching 肝.*(?:切除|切取)|肝切除 and excluding 移植|供肝|供体|活检|穿刺. Require TACE 开始时间 < resection 开始时间. Use exact elapsed hours/24, never rounded calendar days. Exclude impossible timestamps, nonpositive numeric bilirubin, and procedures that a blinded hepatobiliary reviewer identifies as emergency/non-TACE embolization or a non-resection operation. Report the same model in the parent-compatible >28–90-day subset but do not make its 60-day dichotomy primary.

FEASIBILITY ALREADY OBSERVED
The resource-managed full scan read 338,040 procedure rows, 105,044 encounter rows, 1,810,646 diagnosis rows, 28,159,928 lab rows, 419,996 examination rows, and 105,044 clinical-document rows. It found 25,719 first qualifying resections, 1,089 with prior TACE, and 847 in >14–180 days. In that cohort, 594 had paired preoperative and postoperative bilirubin/INR availability; 458 had any joint bilirubin/INR worsening; 82 met the exploratory moderate joint rule before restricting to the exact primary interval/model; and 4 met the very stringent bilirubin >50 plus INR >1.5 proxy. In the parent-compatible >28–90-day subset, n=604, paired outcome n=416, and moderate joint events n=55. Thus the moderate joint endpoint is feasible for an association analysis, while the severe threshold proxy and true grade B/C PHLF are not sufficiently frequent for a powered primary endpoint.

The same scan found preoperative imaging reports for 845/847 and CT/MR lexical reports for 831/847, with lexical flags for diameter in 732, multiplicity in 567, vascular invasion/thrombus in 55, ascites in 37, cirrhosis in 588, rupture in 12, and treatment-response language in 674. These are availability counts, not validated covariates. Procedure lexical rules classified 102 as major, 731 as minor, 88 as laparoscopic/robotic, and left 15 unclassified; these counts require reviewer validation and are not presumed correct. The preoperative bilirubin/INR/albumin triad was available in 762/847, but serial early-to-preoperative triads were only 32 and mid-to-preoperative only 18, so dynamic readiness cannot support a primary analysis.

PRIMARY OUTCOME AND SECONDARIES
Primary outcome: a binary biochemical decompensation signal defined, among patients with all four numeric values, as maximum bilirubin in [5,8) postoperative days >=1.50 times the latest bilirubin in [-7,0) days AND maximum INR in [5,8) days >= preoperative INR +0.20. The bilirubin ratio is unit-invariant within a patient. The INR increment is prespecified because INR is the exact assay available and its observed distribution is face-valid, but its clinical interpretation remains conditional on assay validation. If the laboratory director cannot verify comparable INR reporting across the calendar period, replace the primary INR component with a within-person standardized change and label the original composite unavailable; do not silently pool assays.

Key secondary outcomes: (i) continuous log postoperative/preoperative bilirubin ratio; (ii) postoperative maximum INR minus preoperative INR; (iii) a less stringent joint-worsening signal requiring both bilirubin and INR to increase; (iv) postoperative albumin change; (v) days from operation to discharge; and (vi) day 1–3 hemoglobin nadir change as a negative-control outcome. ISGLS-compatible laboratory worsening is described as a laboratory signal only, not as ISGLS PHLF or grade A/B/C.

Clinical exploratory outcomes require blinded hepatobiliary clinician adjudication from index documents and available orders/procedures/medications: true ISGLS PHLF and grade, encephalopathy, new/worsened ascites, bile leak, infection/sepsis, reoperation, ICU escalation, transfusion, and in-hospital death. The source's lack of document timestamps and incomplete management fields prevents automated adjudication.

EXPOSURE MODEL AND ANALYSIS
Fit a restricted cubic spline for exact interval_days over >14–180 days, with prespecified internal knots at 30, 60, 90, and 140 days or quantile-equivalent knots recorded before fitting. Report the overall nonlinearity test and predicted risk at 30, 60, 90, and 120 days with 95% confidence intervals. The estimand is the adjusted risk difference and risk ratio for clinically interpretable contrasts 30 versus 60 days and 60 versus 90 days among patients who underwent recorded resection and have the complete paired outcome. These are associational contrasts, not causal effects.

Adjust for variables measured before operation and not downstream of the interval: age, sex, calendar year, department, admission-to-operation time, number of prior TACE records, baseline bilirubin, INR, albumin, creatinine, platelets, ALT, hemoglobin, index HCC encounter status, viral hepatitis/diabetes/renal/cirrhosis/portal-hypertension/ascites diagnosis flags where available, validated procedure extent and approach, and validated imaging tumor features. Use overlap weighting based on a flexible generalized propensity model or, if positivity is inadequate across continuous time, use covariate-adjusted spline regression without pretending to identify a causal effect. Check standardized mean differences, overlap, effective sample size, and influential observations. Cluster robust uncertainty by calendar year or operative service only if the cluster count is adequate; otherwise use HC3/sandwich uncertainty with patient-level independence.

Use missingness indicators and complete-case primary analysis only for the paired outcome; perform inverse-probability-of-observation weighting as a sensitivity. Do not impute postoperative outcomes. Do not adjust for postoperative labs, length of stay, complications, or any variable occurring after the TACE-to-operation interval.

CASE-MIX VALIDATION GATE
Before the case-mix-adjusted result is treated as the principal result, a blinded reviewer sample of at least 200 deduplicated preoperative reports and at least 200 index procedure names must establish sensitivity and positive predictive value for major/minor extent, anatomic/partial extent, approach, tumor diameter/multiplicity, macrovascular invasion, ascites, cirrhosis, rupture, and post-TACE response. Require both sensitivity and PPV >=0.90 for an automated field to be used as definitive. If not achieved, report structured-only and validated-subset analyses separately and classify residual confounding as unresolved. The primary result must be shown both before and after validated case-mix adjustment; this gate is a condition for a clinically interpretable case-mix claim, not a reason to relabel unvalidated text as truth.

NEGATIVE CONTROLS, FALSIFICATION, AND SELECTION BIAS
1. Negative-control outcome: postoperative day-1–3 hemoglobin nadir adjusted for baseline hemoglobin and validated operative extent. A timing association with absolute standardized difference >=0.20 in the same direction, or p<0.01, signals residual operative-complexity/selection bias and downgrades any hepatic interpretation.
2. Preoperative pseudo-outcome: compare a late preoperative bilirubin/INR change to an earlier preoperative window. A strong timing association before operation with standardized difference >=0.20 indicates differential readiness trends and falsifies a simple post-TACE hepatic-recovery interpretation.
3. Timing-placebo: repeat with 55- and 65-day dichotomies only as descriptive checks. An isolated cut-point result without a coherent spline is unstable and not supportive.
4. Assay/calendar: repeat using routine assay names only and within 2018–2025; inspect assay-name, missingness, and event rates by year.
5. Selection audit: compare all >14–180-day patients with paired outcome versus those lacking it; report whether missing outcome is associated with interval, admission, or discharge. No claim about patients who progressed, died, received outside surgery, or were never resected may be made. The selected-resection denominator is explicit and immutable.

EFFECT THRESHOLDS AND INTERPRETATION
Supportive: the adjusted 30-day versus 60-day risk difference for the joint endpoint is >=5 percentage points or the risk ratio is >=1.30, with a two-sided 95% CI excluding no association; the spline shows a coherent decreasing risk with longer interval; the continuous bilirubin and INR secondaries agree; the case-mix validation gate passes; and neither negative control meets its failure threshold. This supports a clinically relevant association in selected recorded resection patients and justifies prospective readiness/timing evaluation, not a recommendation to delay.

Adverse/opposite: the 30-day versus 60-day risk difference <=-5 points or risk ratio <=0.77 with CI excluding no association, with concordant secondary outcomes. This argues against the hypothesis that shorter interval is biochemically worse in this selected population; it does not prove earlier surgery is safer, because channeling and unmeasured tumor progression remain possible.

Precise null: the 95% CI for the clinically relevant 30-versus-60 risk difference lies wholly within -5 to +5 percentage points, with adequate overlap and no negative-control failure. This supports absence of a clinically material association in the selected complete-outcome population only.

Inconclusive: CI crosses both no association and the +/-5-point threshold; effective sample size is <100 in either clinically relevant exposure region; overlap weights are dominated by <=10 observations; outcome missingness is strongly differential and inverse-observation weighting is unstable; composite components disagree; case-mix validation fails; or assay comparability cannot be established. Sparse true severe PHLF, absence of management timestamps, and unvalidated lexical flags independently preclude a claim about clinical ISGLS grade.

Falsifying: a strong preoperative pseudo-effect, a same-direction hemoglobin negative-control effect at or above threshold, a noncoherent isolated cut-point with a flat spline, or disappearance/reversal after validated tumor and procedure adjustment falsifies the proposed interpretation that elapsed post-TACE time itself reflects hepatic decompensation risk. These findings would redirect the question toward selection, operative complexity, or treatment indication.

EVIDENCE LIMITS
The HCC snapshot has no verified laboratory-unit column, raw imaging, future-liver-remnant volume, ICG clearance, standardized Child-Pugh/ALBI, performance status, anesthesia/blood-loss flowsheets, complete transfusion quantities, reliable exact complication timestamps, complete outside treatment, or guaranteed mortality capture. Clinical documents can be used for blinded encounter-level review but cannot automatically establish event timing or ISGLS grade. TACE and resection lexicons, emergency versus planned intent, and extent require clinician review. Automated Harbor verification can check source paths, joins, interval arithmetic, assay filters, deduplication, model fitting, confidence intervals, overlap, event counts, and whether conclusions match prespecified branches. It cannot establish clinical readiness, causal benefit, true PHLF grade, or a treatment recommendation.

WHAT WOULD REMAIN FOR ANOTHER STUDY
A prospective multicenter target-trial emulation or randomized timing strategy with explicit TACE intent, validated labs and units, future-liver-remnant volumetry, ICG/portal-pressure measures, standardized ALBI/Child-Pugh, operative blood loss, detailed postoperative management, outside-care capture, and blinded ISGLS adjudication would be required before recommending a waiting interval or claiming prevention of PHLF.
