# Provisional repaired hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery after ICU day 1

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with a new or worsening AKI during an ICU stay, patients whose creatinine has improved by a prespecified day-2/3 landmark but whose urine output remains low (or whose cumulative fluid balance remains positive) will have higher 7-day risk of death or persistent/late kidney support than patients with concordant creatinine and urine-output improvement, after adjustment for baseline illness severity and measured treatment/observation differences.

This is a prognostic and measurement-boundary hypothesis, not a causal claim that creatinine decline itself causes harm. The clinically important unresolved claim is whether urine output and fluid status add information about kidney recovery that a creatinine-only recovery label discards.

## Falsifiable test

At a fixed landmark after the first qualifying AKI measurement, classify creatinine recovery versus non-recovery and urine-output recovery versus non-recovery using prespecified, auditable thresholds. Compare 7-day all-cause mortality and kidney-support/persistent AKI outcomes between discordant and concordant groups. The primary analysis will use a simple adjusted baseline model; a longitudinal alternative will use the pre-landmark creatinine and urine-output trajectories to test whether the discordance signal is reproducible when measurement timing and observation intensity are represented explicitly.

Support would require a reproducible excess risk in the creatinine-improved/urine-output-poor group, with uncertainty and calibration reported, and a signal that is not explained by baseline severity or measurement availability alone. Adverse evidence would be no excess risk, a reversal, or a result confined to unmeasured treatment/observation artifacts. An inconclusive result would be an imprecise estimate, insufficient landmark cohorts, or failure of the longitudinal model to distinguish measurement intensity from true discordance.
