# Does renal-support heterogeneity survive a competing respiratory-documentation audit?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly construct the top parent’s exact eICU six-hour landmark cohort and 30-minute post-rise ledger, then estimate whether hospitals differ in a strict newly documented renal-support process after accounting for both ordinary surveillance and a clinically distinct respiratory-documentation process.

The primary deliverable is the supported-development-hospital 90th-minus-10th percentile spread in the standardized 48-hour cumulative incidence of first strict post-rise renal-support documentation, in the top parent’s risk set of stays with no strict renal-support record from ICU minute 0 through the first qualifying creatinine rise. The solver must produce:

- the frozen cohort flow and raw-to-derived ledger;
- strict treatment-only, strict intake/output-only, and strict union renal-support CIFs;
- the same three spreads before adjustment, after patient-level surveillance adjustment, and after adding the prespecified respiratory-process audit;
- a nonrenal respiratory-documentation process ledger, including whether it precedes, follows, or ties renal-support documentation;
- hospital rank correlation and source-difference uncertainty for treatment-only versus intake/output-only support;
- repeat-test opportunity, confirmation, renal-support order, corroboration, recorded unit death, alive unit exit, and unknown-status censoring;
- matched additive pooled-logistic and ordered multi-state estimates on identical inputs, split, standardization distribution and uncertainty procedure;
- held-out-hospital calibration, process transport and sensitivity results; and
- a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive, linked to saved computed outputs.

This is an observational study of hospital variation in documented renal-support and documentation processes. “Support” means a strict record in the available treatment or intake/output source. It does not mean renal-replacement initiation, treatment receipt, biological acute kidney injury, appropriate care, or a hospital-quality ranking.

The substantive advance over the top parent is specificity of ascertainment. The parent asks whether a recorded creatinine rise reaches newly documented renal support. This child asks whether that signal remains cross-source concordant after a separate respiratory-documentation audit. A spread that disappears when general respiratory documentation is represented is evidence of a broad support/documentation regime, not renal-specific heterogeneity. A spread that persists in both renal sources, with stable source ordering and held-out transport, is a stronger—though still documentation-bounded—finding. The respiratory process is not assumed to be clinically unrelated; it is a competing documentation process whose parallel behavior can expose surveillance intensity and source capture.

## Evidence-supported claim, unresolved question and clinical importance

The inspected snapshot and catalog support only the feasibility claim that eICU contains ICU-relative laboratory, vital, treatment, intake/output, respiratory-care, respiratory-charting and unit-disposition records that can be joined through the stated keys. A source scan found respiratoryCare rows in 44,772 stays and respiratoryCharting rows in 124,598 stays; these whole-source counts are not event rates in the target cohort and do not establish ventilator initiation, respiratory severity or complete documentation. The parent’s renal-source scans are also feasibility evidence, not prevalence or treatment estimates.

The strongest claim supported before fitting is:

> This frozen eICU snapshot can measure a recorded creatinine rise, later confirmation, strict renal-support records from two source families, ICU exits, and time-stamped respiratory documentation, but it cannot establish the clinical meaning or completeness of any treatment/documentation record.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adult ICU-unit stays with a first qualifying recorded creatinine rise and no earlier strict renal-support record, does a material hospital spread in newly documented strict renal support remain after standardizing measured case mix, creatinine-testing opportunity, MAP history, rise characteristics, competing exits, leakage-controlled pre-rise process intensity, and the independent respiratory-documentation process?

The primary hypothesis is frozen before target-cohort outcomes are inspected. Let H_T, H_IO and H_U denote supported-hospital 90th-minus-10th spreads for treatment-only, intake/output-only and strict-union support. Let H_U^R be the strict-union spread after the respiratory-process audit. The hypothesis requires:

1. H_U^R is at least 5 percentage points with a two-sided hospital-cluster interval excluding zero;
2. at least 10 hospitals meet the frozen support gate;
3. H_T^R and H_IO^R are both positive on the source-supported hospital intersection, their joint difference lies within [-5,+5] percentage points, and their bootstrap hospital-rank correlation is at least 0.50; and
4. the direction and qualitative pathway decomposition persist in the ordered model and held-out hospitals.

These margins are documentation-comparability criteria, not treatment thresholds or claims about clinical benefit. A renal result that is positive only in one source, only before the respiratory audit, or only in broad fluid-removal labels does not support the primary hypothesis.

This matters because a hospital-comparison or alerting system based on a recorded creatinine rise could confuse renal-care workflow with general ICU documentation density. Establishing source-concordant residual heterogeneity would motivate chart/order validation and site-specific ascertainment work. Showing that the signal tracks respiratory documentation would redirect attention toward process portability and away from renal-support interpretation. Neither outcome authorizes changing dialysis policy or treating a record as a causal intervention effect.

## Respiratory negative-control and competing-process audit

Respiratory data are not substituted for renal support. They provide an independent process audit with a separate source family and separate timestamps.

Define the leakage-controlled pre-rise respiratory documentation vector using only rows with raw offsets in [0,r] from:

- `respiratoryCare.respcarestatusoffset`, deduplicated by `respcareid`;
- `respiratoryCharting.respchartoffset`, deduplicated by `respchartid`.

For each stay retain the distinct-row counts, any-row indicators, count per observed pre-rise hour, first-row offset, last-row offset and the proportion of rows with nonblank respiratory fields. The pre-rise respiratory score is a frozen, development-only standardized block: log1p counts, any-row indicators and first/last timing, with r included to account for exposure time. Hospital source-capture summaries are leave-one-stay-out means of these quantities, computed only from development stays and only through each stay’s r. No post-rise renal, respiratory, confirmation or exit row may enter this score. If a source-specific score has insufficient support, retain its patient-level fields and report the hospital score as unavailable rather than imputing a post-rise value.

Define the post-rise respiratory documentation sentinel R as the first distinct `respcareid` or `respchartid` record with raw `respcarestatusoffset` or `respchartoffset) in (r,E), respectively. Ties across the two respiratory sources are retained. This is a documentation event, not a ventilation-start endpoint. As a prespecified specificity sensitivity, define a respiratory-care escalation marker only when a deduplicated `respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset` lies in (r,E), there is no valid `ventstartoffset` in [0,r], and `ventendoffset` is either absent or no earlier than ventstart; report this only if event support permits.

The audit has three outputs:

1. the hospital spread in R and its source-specific components;
2. renal-support CIFs and pathway states stratified by whether R occurred before renal support, after renal support, or not by E; and
3. the residual renal-support spread after including the pre-rise respiratory block and a time-updated R mark in the matched process model.

R is a negative-control/competing documentation process, not a claim of no biological relation to renal support. If renal and respiratory process spreads are both large and similarly ordered after case-mix adjustment, broad ICU support/documentation intensity is a competing explanation. If respiratory documentation is null but treatment and intake/output renal spreads agree, the renal-specific documentation interpretation is strengthened. If respiratory events are sparse, the result is inconclusive rather than a favorable null.

## Ordered multi-state process alternative

Fit a piecewise-exponential ordered multi-state model using exactly the same risk set, dictionaries, 30-minute grid, covariates, respiratory process variables, hospital split, standardization distribution and 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap procedure.

Represent:

- first rise with no repeat, renal support or respiratory sentinel;
- repeat-test opportunity without confirmation;
- confirmed rise without support;
- treatment-only support;
- intake/output-only support;
- both-source corroboration when the second renal source occurs within 60 minutes;
- a nonabsorbing respiratory-documentation mark when R first occurs;
- support before R, support after R, confirmation before support, support before confirmation and same-minute ties; and
- recorded death, alive unit exit and unknown-status censoring from every non-exit state.

The respiratory mark is nonabsorbing: later renal transitions remain possible. The model therefore identifies whether hospital variation is carried by repeat opportunity, confirmation, renal source capture, respiratory documentation opportunity, order of respiratory versus renal records, corroboration or competing exit. This is substantive process information lost by parallel additive causes. Agreement between B3 and the ordered model on H_U^R and source concordance is robustness. A spread only before respiratory documentation suggests an early renal-source pathway; a spread that tracks R and disappears after the audit supports a broad documentation explanation. Model disagreement is uncertainty, not a reason to select the favorable result.

A learned sequence model is deferred, not prohibited. It may be reconsidered only if both prespecified models show reproducible shape-specific held-out miscalibration or missing temporal structure for this same source-decomposed estimand, source events are adequate, and a learned model can preserve the strict gate, respiratory mark, source decomposition, calibration, hospital-held-out transport and clustered uncertainty. A urine/KDIGO branch is deferred because the available intake/output fields do not establish uniform collection duration or a valid time-varying denominator. A causal pressor branch is deferred because `infusionDrug` lacks validated administration, dose, indication, intent and complete exposure.

## Falsification, interpretation and evidence limits

Supportive evidence requires every primary criterion:

- H_U^R >= 5 percentage points with a two-sided hospital-cluster interval excluding zero;
- at least 10 supported hospitals and the frozen per-hospital risk/event support;
- positive, qualitatively concordant treatment-only and intake/output-only spreads on the supported intersection, source difference within [-5,+5], and rank correlation >=0.50;
- concordant adjusted union/source direction and pathway ordering in the additive and ordered models;
- a nontrivial residual spread after patient-level surveillance and respiratory-process adjustment;
- acceptable development and held-out calibration and timing overlap; and
- stability under the second split, revised-lab clock, status, MAP and strict-source sensitivities.

Adverse evidence includes a null or reversed adjusted renal spread; an interval compatible with zero; disappearance after testing, source-opportunity or respiratory adjustment; one-source dominance; source difference outside the equivalence band; low rank correlation; support restricted to broad fluid-removal labels; a respiratory sentinel spread that explains the renal spread; or failure to transport. A large confirmation spread with no renal-support spread is adverse to the claim that the creatinine signal reaches documented renal support, while supporting only a surveillance/confirmation interpretation. None of these adverse patterns proves absence of biological AKI or treatment need.

The respiratory-process audit is falsified as a specificity discriminator if its event timing is reproduced after shuffling respiratory timestamps within hospital and risk strata, or if renal-support conclusions are unchanged when respiratory rows are permuted while preserving source counts. The renal temporal process is falsified if strict renal-support timing is reproduced by shuffling renal event times within hospital and risk strata. Additional checks are:

- short-gap 0–6-hour repeat creatinine after the first rise, as a testing-practice control;
- 30/60/120-minute respiratory-to-renal corroboration windows;
- revised-lab clock and 3-hour/12-hour confirmation gaps;
- raw versus bridged and periodic versus non-invasive MAP;
- strict versus broad fluid-removal definitions;
- conservative versus permissive unit-discharge ties and NULL-status handling;
- exact-ID versus no-deduplication audits; and
- outcome/process permutation and split-integrity checks.

Inconclusive includes fewer than 10 supported hospitals, sparse respiratory or source-specific events, fewer than 10 source-supported hospitals, unstable bootstrap, extreme weights, inadequate overlap, near-collinearity, semantic dictionary instability, failed convergence, strong dependence on unknown-status handling, model-family disagreement, or held-out calibration/process failure. Inconclusive means the data do not resolve renal-specific documentation heterogeneity; it is not a positive or negative clinical finding.

Computationally checkable claims are source and schema hashes, headers, ordinary-file archive status, exact joins, unit filtering, deduplication, offsets, boundary rules, dictionaries, cohort flow, no-prior-support gate, respiratory score leakage audit, split integrity, ledger states, model fits, standardized CIFs, source concordance, uncertainty, calibration, transport and sensitivity reproducibility. Conclusions must cite the corresponding saved outputs and uncertainty.

Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether any treatment or intake/output row represents an order, administration, initiation, dose, indication, intent, chronic dialysis, fluid removal or a documentation artifact; whether the creatinine rise is biological AKI; whether respiratory documentation represents ventilation or severity; whether source absence means no care; and whether hospital differences reflect case mix, workflow, coding or appropriate treatment. The available data lack validated treatment administration, clinician intent, complete order history, reliable urine collection intervals/denominators, narrative adjudication, post-discharge outcomes and causal action utilities. Expert review, chart/order validation and an independent or prospective study are required before policy changes.

## Method choice, alternatives and compute record

The transparent additive baseline is selected because it makes the source-specific/union renal estimand, respiratory audit, competing exits, standardization, calibration and attenuation directly auditable. Its known information loss is event order, respiratory-before-renal timing and waiting-time dependence.

The ordered multi-state model is the substantive alternative because the unresolved scientific uncertainty is process ordering and source capture, not a small predictive gain. It can reveal whether a hospital effect is carried by repeat testing, confirmation, renal source, respiratory documentation, corroboration or exit—information the parallel baseline loses. Both models use identical rows, cohort, raw offsets, risk sets, split, covariates, dictionaries, standardization distribution and 500-replicate uncertainty.

A generic tree/transformer sequence model is deferred because neither event support nor held-out temporal miscalibration has been established, and a black-box model would not by itself distinguish broad surveillance from renal-specific capture. It can be revisited only under the held-out-misfit rule above. A GPU is not required for the proposed low-dimensional tabular models; no complexity bonus or GPU exclusion is used.

Measured discovery facts are schema/catalog inspection, live header inspection and whole-source respiratory scans (approximately 33 seconds for respiratoryCare and 33 seconds for respiratoryCharting in this environment), plus parent feasibility scans of treatment/intakeOutput. No target-cohort event rate, model fit, bootstrap or clinical conclusion was computed. Full ledger construction, model convergence, 500-bootstrap runtime, source intersection and transport are unmeasured planning quantities.
