## 1. Unresolved question, opening and clinical importance

Transfusion at a given hemoglobin is one of the most common ICU decisions, but the *effect
modifier* is unresolved. Randomized evidence disagrees by setting: restrictive is
equivalent/possibly better in general ICU (TRICC, [K1]) and non-inferior in septic shock
(TRISS, [K3]), while in acute myocardial infarction the MINT program found the strategy
question non-uniform and is expressly testing subgroup modification ([K2]). The
pathophysiologic rationale for a *liberal* trigger is that an ischemic myocardium has an
oxygen supply–demand imbalance and benefits from higher oxygen-carrying capacity. Whether
the effect of transfusing at a fixed hemoglobin is **modified by concurrent myocardial
supply–demand imbalance** remains unresolved, especially in observational data where
transfusion is strongly confounded by indication/severity.

Clinical importance: the transfusion trigger in ICU patients with evidence of myocardial
ischemia is a daily, guideline-relevant decision; a modifier-specific effect would inform
cardiac-protective transfusion thresholds, whereas its absence would argue against
ischemia-triggered liberal transfusion. A non-specific (adverse) result is a valuable
negative finding that disciplines over-claiming [K3].

Leading claim **H1**: at the same baseline hemoglobin, red-cell transfusion during the
early ICU course is associated with 28-day mortality *differently* in patients with vs
without a marker of myocardial supply–demand imbalance (non-null transfusion×modifier
interaction).
Strongest rival **H0**: the apparent interaction is confounding by indication/severity and
monitoring intensity — transfused and ischemic patients are both sicker and more observed;
after adjustment for severity, baseline hemoglobin, monitoring intensity and a
care-escalation block, any interaction attenuates to a non-specific value.

Discriminating comparison: the *stratum difference* Δ = τ(M=1) − τ(M=0) of the adjusted
transfusion effect on 28-day mortality, estimated on a held-out temporal split, with a
prespecified equivalence margin and negative/permuted controls (Section 5).

## 4. Method comparison (baseline vs scientifically substantive alternative)

- **B0 (interpretable baseline):** nested logistic/Cox model M0 (baseline) < M1 (+severity)
  < M2 (+monitoring + escalation E) with an explicit `transfusion × M` interaction; τ and Δ
  by contrast of predicted risks.
- **A1 (substantive alternative):** target-trial emulation with propensity-score IPTW
  (or doubly-robust AIPW) for the transfusion decision at `t*`, giving a marginal risk
  difference and a marginal modifier contrast Δ. **What A1 reveals that B0 loses:**
  transfusion changes subsequent hemoglobin/hemodynamics, so post-landmark physiology is a
  time-varying confounder that baseline-conditional B0 cannot address; IPTW targets the
  marginal (population) contrast directly and is less dependent on the outcome-model form.
- Deferred alternative A2: a small sequence model of the pre-`t*` hemoglobin trajectory +
  vitals (could reveal ordering information the scalar summaries compress); revisit only if
  Δ is inconclusive-but-nonzero. Recorded, not used.
- Resources: CPU-only tabular work is adequate (nested models + IPTW bootstrap over ~tens of
  thousands of stays); GPU is not required. Approx. budget: minutes–tens of minutes CPU per
  fit; bootstraps cluster by `subject_id`.

## 5. Estimand, falsification criteria, controls

Primary estimand Δ = τ(M=1) − τ(M=0) on the held-out split, risk-difference scale, with
bootstrap 95% CIs clustered by `subject_id`.
- **Supportive (H1)** if `|Δ| >= 0.02`, the 95% CI excludes Δ=0 in one prespecified
  direction, and the permuted-label contrast satisfies `|Δ_perm| <= 0.005`.
- **Adverse (H0 / no meaningful heterogeneity)** if the CI includes 0 or `|Δ| < 0.02`; a
  non-specific result is accepted as a valuable NEGATIVE finding.
- **Inconclusive** if the CI is too wide to separate the bands.
Negative controls: (a) permuted treatment labels; (b) a null-window/outcome control that
tests outcome specificity to the ischemia mechanism (see limits).

What each outcome would mean: supportive → modifier-specific association worth external
replication and a randomized modifier-stratified trial; adverse → the interaction is
confounded/absent, arguing against ischemia-triggered liberal thresholds; inconclusive →
underpowered, revisit with a larger/precision-improving design.
