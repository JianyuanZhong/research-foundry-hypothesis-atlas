## 1. Unresolved question and its clinical importance

The **stress hyperglycemia ratio SHR = admission glucose / (28.7 x HbA1c(%) - 46.7)**, i.e.
admission glucose divided by chronic-glycemia-implied average glucose, is widely reported to
predict ICU mortality (K1, K2, K3). The unresolved question is **not** whether the association
exists, but **what it is**: does the *relative* (acute-on-chronic) elevation carry prognostic
information **beyond absolute admission glucose and known diabetes**, or is any apparent
independent SHR effect produced by (a) **selective HbA1c measurement** — only a subset of ICU
patients get HbA1c, and that selection is plausibly driven by suspected dysglycemia/severity —
or by (b) **invalid/biased HbA1c** in acute illness (anemia, transfusion, renal failure)?

Clinical importance: SHR is being promoted as a "cost-free" risk-stratification and possible
trial-enrichment tool (K2). If the signal is separable from absolute glucose, that changes the
interpretation of "stress hyperglycemia" toward a relative/metabolic-reserve mechanism and
supports SHR-based stratification. If it is **not** separable once absolute glucose, severity
and measurement propensity are accounted for, then SHR should NOT be used to enrich trials or
triage — an actionable negative that prevents a redundant biomarker being deployed. Both
outcomes change a decision, so the question is consequential either way.

## 2. Leading claim vs strongest rivals (explanations with different predictions)

- **H-relative (leading):** at the same absolute admission glucose, higher SHR identifies higher
  risk (relative elevation is informative). Prediction: SHR coefficient > 0 with absolute
  glucose held fixed; effect persists within HbA1c-measured strata and is not reproduced by a
  negative-control outcome.
- **R-absolute (rival):** only absolute glucose/severity matter; SHR adds nothing once glucose
  enters the model. Prediction: SHR coefficient ~ 0 when absolute glucose + severity are in M0.
- **R-select (rival, informative measurement):** HbA1c is measured selectively, so the
  "HbA1c-measured" cohort is a severity/dysglycemia-selected subset; apparent SHR effects are
  cohort composition, not relative glycemia. Prediction: SHR effect attenuates markedly when
  we condition on/inverse-weight by the HbA1c-measurement propensity and restrict to
  comparable-measurement strata; a **negative-control outcome** (e.g. in-hospital non-glycemic
  deterioration not plausibly driven by glycemia) shows a parallel "SHR" signal.
- **R-validity (rival, measurement error):** HbA1c is unreliable in acute illness (anemia,
  transfusion, renal failure), so SHR is a noisy/biased transform. Prediction: effect is
  unstable across anemia/transfusion/renal-function strata and shrinks toward null under
  measurement-error correction.

These are separable: H-relative survives glucose-adjustment AND measurement-propensity
conditioning AND a null negative control; R-absolute does not survive glucose adjustment;
R-select produces a non-null negative control and strong propensity attenuation; R-validity
produces stratum instability.

## 3. Population, temporal boundaries, variables, outcomes (frozen design)

- **Population:** adults (>=18) with a first ICU stay and a measurable admission glucose AND a
  measurable HbA1c. Index time `t0` = ICU `intime`. Admission glucose = first `labevents`
  glucose (itemid `50931`, Chemistry; sensitivity `50809`/`52027` Blood Gas, `52569`) in the
  24 h window `[t0-6h, t0+24h]`. HbA1c = `labevents` itemid `50852` ("% Hemoglobin A1c",
  Chemistry; sensitivity `50854` "Absolute A1c") within `[t0-24h, t0+24h]`. SHR computed from
  admission glucose (mg/dL) and HbA1c(%); a prespecified valid-range filter applied.
- **Keys/joins (MIMIC-IV):** `subject_id` (patient), `hadm_id` (admission), `stay_id` (ICU
  stay). `labevents` joins on `subject_id`(+`hadm_id`); `icustays` links `hadm_id`<->`stay_id`
  with `intime`/`outtime`; `admissions` gives `deathtime`/`dischtime`; `patients` gives
  `anchor_age`/`gender`; `diagnoses_icd` + `d_icd_diagnoses` give diabetes/anemia/renal flags.
- **Exposure:** SHR (continuous, per-SD; plus prespecified quartiles Q1-Q4), with the
  prespecified **decomposition**: absolute glucose (per-SD) and SHR entered so the two are
  separable.
- **Outcome (primary):** 28-day all-cause mortality from `admissions.deathtime` (event) vs
  administrative censoring at `dischtime` if no `deathtime` (linkage to the national death
  index is NOT in the configured tables -> state this limitation; mortality is in-hospital /
  hospital-recorded).
- **Covariates:** age, gender, admission type, a severity score from `chartevents`/`labevents`
  (e.g. SOFA-style components), sepsis flag from `diagnoses_icd`, known-diabetes flag, baseline
  creatinine, anemia/transfusion indicators.
- **Adjustment ladder:** M0 = severity + age/sex (+ absolute glucose) ; M1 = M0 + SHR ;
  M2 = M1 + measurement-intensity / observation features ; M3 = permutation negative control.
- **Split:** locked earliest-70% / latest-30% by `icustays.intime` (per-subject date-shifting
  in MIMIC-IV makes this a leakage-safe ORDERING proxy, not a calendar-era split).
- **Analysis:** multivariable logistic/Cox for 28-day mortality; restricted cubic spline for
  shape (K3 reports non-linearity); **HbA1c-measurement-propensity** model -> IPW and
  measurement-strata restriction (R-select test); stratum stability across anemia/transfusion/
  renal strata (R-validity test); E-value bound for the M1 SHR coefficient.

## 4. Method exploration (baseline vs substantive alternative, SAME question)

- **Baseline B0 (interpretable, CPU):** penalized logistic regression on M0/M1, reporting the
  adjusted SHR (and absolute-glucose) odds ratio with CI and a spline for shape.
- **Alternative A1 (substantive, CPU/EM, bounded < 1h):** a small **measurement-model +
  outcome-model** that jointly (i) estimates the HbA1c-measurement propensity and (ii) fits the
  mortality model with an explicit **latent "chronic-glycemia/measurement-selection" factor**
  shared between the selection and outcome equations (a Heckman-style / pattern-mixture latent
  factor). A1 can reveal what B0 loses: how much of the SHR coefficient is attributable to
  selection on measurement vs a genuine relative-glycemia effect, and a bias-corrected SHR OR.
- **Selection/deferral reason:** A1 on the same question is scientifically useful because the
  R-select rival is *exactly* a latent-selection problem that B0 (which conditions on the
  measured cohort only) cannot identify; A1's extra latent factor is the minimal machinery
  that can separate the two. GPU is not required (tabular, small N); CPU is adequate.

## 5. Falsification criteria (prespecified)

- **Supportive (H-relative):** adjusted SHR OR > 1 with CI excluding 1 in M1, **AND** persists
  (attenuation < 25%) under measurement-propensity conditioning (M2/R-select test), **AND**
  negative-control outcome null, **AND** effect stable across validity strata.
- **Adverse (R-absolute / R-select / R-validity):** SHR OR ~ 1 (CI includes 1) once absolute
  glucose + severity are in M0 (R-absolute); OR attenuation > 50% and negative control
  reproduces the "SHR" signal (R-select); OR effect unstable/flips across anemia/transfusion/
  renal strata (R-validity). Any of these is an informative, publishable negative.
- **Inconclusive:** wide CIs, insufficient events (power gate), HbA1c measured in too few
  patients to support strata, or M2 features unavailable -> report as inconclusive, do not
  substitute an easier question.

## 6. Honest limits, missing evidence, clinical adjudication

- Single-centre retrospective MIMIC-IV; **association only**, no causal claim.
- **Mortality is hospital-recorded** (`admissions.deathtime`); out-of-hospital death is not
  observable in the configured tables -> 28-day mortality is partly administratively censored.
- **HbA1c measurement is selective** (the very rival R-select) -> prevalence, not analysable
  without the cohort; a compile-time feasibility/power gate is prescribed, not observed.
- HbA1c reliability in acute illness (anemia/transfusion/renal) is a **clinical/laboratory
  adjudication** issue; R-validity can only be bounded, not resolved, from these data.
- Microcirculatory vs stress mechanisms are **not diagnosable** here; those require another study.
- No biochemistry at the mechanism level; any mechanistic claim needs external data/expert review.
