# Is the persistent-instability/laboratory-drop signal death-specific and transportable?

Status: Episode 49 substantive child of assessed-valid [prior hypothesis]. This is a fixed-landmark observational experiment about recorded care and measurement processes. It is not a causal test of ordering, a physiologic-recovery study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that additional testing improves survival.

## Unresolved claim and clinical importance

The strongest supportable claim before fitting is only that the frozen eICU snapshot contains joinable multi-hospital ICU-relative laboratory result/revision, periodic-vital, support/documentation, care-plan and ICU-disposition fields. The parent gives a feasible test of whether a final-hour laboratory-surveillance drop is more concerning when recorded instability persists. No fitted association has been established.

The unresolved claim is more discriminating: among stays with prior laboratory surveillance and recorded instability, is the persistent-instability plus laboratory-drop association specific to later recorded ICU death, rather than a generic late documentation or discharge process, and does it transport across hospitals with different surveillance intensity? This matters because an alert based on a laboratory gap could prompt review, but a signal that predicts both death and alive discharge, disappears after hospital-regime adjustment, or is concentrated in one interface is not evidence of missed deterioration.

Primary hypothesis: after fixed patient-level state and observation-burden adjustment, the excess association of a revision-safe laboratory drop with recorded death is larger when instability persists than when it resolves, and this death-specific interaction remains directionally consistent across supported hospital surveillance regimes. The competing endpoint interaction (alive ICU discharge) is prespecified, not a post hoc negative control.

Support would motivate chart adjudication and prospective silent-mode evaluation. It would not establish unmeasured biochemical abnormality, test withholding, clinician intent, causality, benefit, or external validity.

## Endpoint-specific and transport estimands

The primary estimand is the standardized P-by-D interaction in cumulative incidence of recorded death at 1,440 minutes:
[CIF_death(D=1)-CIF_death(D=0) | P=1] - [CIF_death(D=1)-CIF_death(D=0) | P=0].
Report the same contrast for alive discharge. The key specificity contrast is the death interaction minus the alive-discharge interaction, with joint uncertainty. Also report each P-specific contrast.

Define outcome-free hospital surveillance regimes in development data from pre-landmark laboratory observation intensity and vital observation burden, using fit-fold hospital medians/quantiles and prespecified minimum support; do not use outcomes or hospital ID as patient predictors. Estimate regime-stratified effects and a P-by-D-by-regime interaction. Primary transport is five outer folds holding out whole hospitals, approximately 20 percent each; report macro-hospital and patient-weighted CIF/Brier/calibration and leave-one-regime-out summaries. A regime interaction or poor overlap is evidence against a single transportable interpretation, not a reason to change windows.

## Baselines and substantive alternative

All methods use identical rows, six 60-minute bins, fit-fold codebooks, admission context, vital state/missingness/observation burden, lab result/process through minute 300, P/D, support/documentation through minute 360, outcomes and hospital folds.

B_state is pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards for death and alive discharge, excluding final-hour D. B_discord adds D, lab count/gap/key/revision channels, P-by-D, prespecified P-by-continuous-count terms, endpoint interaction, and regime interaction. The transparent model is primary because its endpoint and regime contrasts are auditable.

T_discord is a one-layer six-bin GRU with separate death/discharge hazard heads and the same channels, without hospital identifier. It can represent cross-domain ordering, persistence, duration and nonlinear co-movement lost by additive summaries. Fit a parameter-matched MLP and an within-patient time-order shuffle. A learned advantage is relevant only if held-out death and discharge Brier/calibration improve over both transparent models and MLP with compatible uncertainty, and the advantage attenuates under order shuffle. Otherwise the transparent contrasts remain primary. A mechanistic observation-state model is deferred: specimen/order times, units, raw waveforms, verified delivered support/doses and adjudicated physiology are unavailable.

## Support, uncertainty and falsification

Before outcomes, report every P-by-D cell, hospital count, event count, observation density, interface/key overlap and regime overlap. Require each primary P-by-D cell >=200 episodes, >=20 hospitals and >=50 recorded deaths; risk set >=1,000 episodes and >=300 deaths; support categories >=1 percent fit prevalence and >=20 hospitals. Failed gates make that claim inconclusive.

Use five hospital outer folds and a 75/25 hospital-grouped development split. Learn codebooks, scaling, imputation, regularization, calibration and early stopping only in fit hospitals. Use >=1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions, or a predeclared hierarchical alternative if unstable, for 95 percent intervals.

Falsifications: permute D within hospital/P/prior intensity/risk strata; swap the two lab windows with support-compatible rows; permute vital-bin order; replace lab drops with matched treatment/respiratory/intake-output documentation drops; compare death with alive-discharge interaction; exclude then include carePlanEOL save/discussion channels; compare conservative availability with result-only, revision-known-only and common-key panels; leave surveillance regimes/hospitals out; repeat each vital threshold and ambiguous P group; mask final-hour channels; and audit for post-360 leakage, activeupondischarge, target-fold preprocessing and hospital-ID leakage. A generic documentation or discharge interaction as large as death, broken-linkage persistence, one-interface concentration or post-landmark dependence is adverse.

Supportive evidence requires gates, positive death interaction with interval excluding zero, smaller discharge interaction, held-out calibration/Brier improvement without discharge degradation, attenuation under patient/time/documentation falsifications, and stability across revision/interface/regime/EOL challenges. Adverse evidence rejects the proposed interpretation. Inconclusive evidence includes failed support/overlap, unstable folds, intervals spanning a clinically meaningful range, threshold/clock fragility, or learned improvement without temporal falsification. None establishes clinical truth.
