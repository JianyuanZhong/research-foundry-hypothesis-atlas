# Observation-aware monitoring signal after recorded insulin

Status: substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process study, not a causal insulin, feeding, AKI, hypoglycemia-treatment, or alert-benefit study.

## Repair and scientific deliverable

The parent is valid, but its 24-hour observation model treats any glucose recorded during the horizon as one missingness indicator. That cannot distinguish a check at hour 1 from a check at hour 23. This child therefore models glucose observation sequentially in hourly at-risk bins and reports two distinct targets:

1. the directly identifiable cumulative incidence of a first *detected* glucose <70 mg/dL under realized eICU surveillance; and
2. a secondary observation-standardized detected-low risk under a prespecified hourly observation model, only if positivity and sensitivity criteria pass.

The observation-gap process is reported separately, never as a clinical adverse event. The clinical deliverable is whether a recorded creatinine rise plus documented nutrition non-delivery identifies episodes needing prospective glucose-surveillance review after a positive recorded insulin-rate update. Completion requires a frozen cohort/codebook, temporal audit, support counts, transparent-model estimates and uncertainty when support passes, sequential observation diagnostics, held-out-hospital results, falsifications, and an interpretation file linked to outputs. No clinical result is claimed here.

## Question and evidence boundary

The strongest available evidence is data availability and a bounded outcome-blind feasibility audit from the parent: 10,423 insulin-plus-recent-glucose periods across 2,798 stays and 58 hospitals; 783 conservative periods across 465 stays and 32 hospitals after paired creatinine windows and prior qualifying nutrition support; 9 detected <70 events, with one in the renal-worsening/documented-gap cell. These are feasibility counts, not risk estimates. Full 24-hour event support and convergence remain unverified.

Hypothesis:

> In adults’ first ICU stays with a positive, finite, exact-label recorded insulin-rate update and a recent valid glucose, recorded creatinine worsening plus no qualifying nutrition delivery documented during a covered preceding interval is associated with higher 24-hour first-detected-low risk, and/or a longer subsequent glucose-observation gap, than the covered no-rise/no-gap cell, after accounting for insulin intensity, prior glucose trajectory, illness context and hourly observation history.

A detected low is an EHR ascertainment outcome, not proof of physiologic hypoglycemia. Documented non-delivery is not feed interruption, inadequate calories, or clinician intent. Recorded creatinine rise is not KDIGO AKI. Positive evidence would justify chart adjudication and prospective monitoring research, not an insulin or feeding order.

Restrict to age >=18 (encode `> 89` as 90), positive `unitdischargeoffset`, and the smallest valid numeric `unitvisitnumber` per `uniquepid`, breaking ties by `patientunitstayid`.

This is a recorded infusion update, not administration.

Follow 24 one-hour bins after t, ending at the earliest ICU discharge, expired discharge status, care-plan EOL save/discussion, or 24 hours. Predictors must be known by the bin time; future discharge/EOL status and future tests are excluded. The primary endpoint is the first valid glucose <70 mg/dL in an at-risk hour; <54 mg/dL is secondary. Valid >=70 is observed non-low; no observation is a separate state. Discharge/death/EOL are competing censoring events. The parent’s six-hour endpoint is prespecified as a support sensitivity; if the 24-hour gate fails, findings are descriptive/inconclusive rather than a reason to relax definitions.

## Frozen codebook and exposure cells

Freeze before examining post-index outcomes. Retain only audited quantitative glucose labels and supported `labmeasurenamesystem/interface` units; convert only with a fixed conversion, reject ambiguity, and deduplicate revisions by `labid` with `labresultrevisedoffset <= prediction time`. A later revision was not available at index.

For creatinine, take the latest audited result in [t-360,t] and latest in [t-1,800,t-1,080], requiring both. Primary recorded worsening is rise >=0.3 mg/dL; >=1.5-fold and continuous change are sensitivities. Never call it AKI.

For nutrition, use positive finite `cellvaluenumeric` rows and a frozen whitelist of oral/P.O., enteral/tube, and parenteral/TPN delivery labels from `cellpath/celllabel/cellvaluetext`. Exclude flushes, medication/insulin volume, dextrose-only rows and ambiguous labels. “Documented non-delivery” is allowed only where a prespecified interface-coverage rule is met and no qualifying delivery occurs in (t-720,t]. Keep no-interface, inadequate-coverage and ambiguous strata separate; do not recode them as a gap. Use `intakeoutputoffset` primarily and `intakeoutputentryoffset` as a timestamp sensitivity.

The primary contrast is a covered four-cell table: recorded rise/no rise crossed with documented non-delivery/no documented non-delivery. “Neither” means no recorded rise plus covered documented delivery history, never unknown nutrition documentation.

## Estimands and matched methods

First estimate realized-surveillance cumulative incidence of a first detected low within 24 hours, standardized over eligible episodes and competing discharge/death/EOL. This is directly checkable but observation-dependent.

Then fit a cross-fitted sequential observation model at each at-risk hour: probability of a valid glucose in that hour given history through that hour, including time since last glucose, prior levels/slopes/gaps, prior testing/interface density, recorded insulin history, pre-index renal/nutrition labels, admission context, early severity and hospital attributes `numbedscategory, teachingstatus, region`. Never use future outcome, discharge status or post-index exposure. Estimate an observation-standardized detected-low risk with stabilized sequential weights or equivalent g-computation only under the explicit missing-at-random assumption conditional on this history; report it as untestable. Report weight distributions, positivity, calibration and effective sample size, with prespecified trimming sensitivity. If hourly positivity fails, the standardized target is not identified.

B0 is a pooled discrete-time logistic or complementary-log-log competing-risk model over the same episode-hour rows, with prespecified splines for recorded insulin intensity/history, prior glucose trajectory/gaps, age, weight, admission source, early severity, prior testing density and hospital attributes; it excludes renal/nutrition indicators. B1 adds recorded creatinine change, documented non-delivery, coverage categories and their prespecified interaction. Report standardized joint-versus-neither risk differences/ratios for realized and, only if identified, observation-standardized detected-low risk, with patient-clustered bootstrap uncertainty. Report observation-gap risk separately.

The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over 24 one-hour pre-index bins, using the same variables plus explicit missingness, measurement-time, interface and documentation channels, with separate heads for detected low, glucose observation and competing discharge/EOL. It uses exactly the same episode set, horizons and hospital folds. Evaluate Brier score, calibration, held-out-hospital calibration slope/intercept, discrimination only secondarily, fixed-alert-burden curves, and physiology/history versus observation/documentation ablations. It is informative only if ordering, persistence or co-movement changes transportable monitoring information beyond B0; predictive gain alone is not a clinical advance.

Use 60/20/20 whole-hospital development/validation/transport folds, keeping `uniquepid` together. Held-out predictions cannot use hospital IDs; use only `hospital.csv.gz` attributes. If folds lack support, transport is unestimable.

## Gates and falsification

Require before inferential interaction claims: >=20 primary detected-low events overall, >=5 in each covered cell, >=10 hospitals with event and non-event support, effective weighted sample size >=100, no near-zero hourly observation probabilities after trimming, and stable direction across weight truncation and strict codebooks. These are stability rules, not clinical thresholds. On failure, report counts, coverage and model-free descriptive uncertainty; call inference inconclusive and do not fit an unstable interaction or confirmatory temporal learner.

Prespecify positive-rate index-time permutation within stay; renal/nutrition-history shuffling within hospital; pre-index placebo windows; strict versus broad glucose/nutrition codebooks; revision-time versus clinical-time definitions; laboratory versus bedside/interface glucose strata; complete-observable episodes; insulin-free analogous episodes; and newly observed non-glucose laboratory testing as a negative-control outcome.

Supportive evidence requires the gate, stable weights, uncertainty excluding zero for a prespecified >=2 percentage-point joint-versus-neither detected-risk difference, added calibrated monitoring value over B0, concordant six-hour direction, no single-hospital dominance, and failed placebo/permutation/negative-control tests. It supports only prospective chart-reviewed monitoring research. Adverse evidence is a null below target, reversal, disappearance after ascertainment correction or strict labels, poor transport calibration, interface/hospital confinement, or negative-control similarity; it refutes this marker in the snapshot, not insulin safety. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse cells, non-positivity, unstable weights, unsupported transport, competing-event miscalibration or nonconvergence.

Administration, calories, feed-stop time, symptoms, rescue treatment, adjudicated AKI, alert response and benefit require chart adjudication, expert review, external validation or prospective/emulated-intervention data. Automatic verification cannot establish those claims.

## Alternatives, compute and deferred dispositions

B0/B1 are primary because they expose an interpretable standardized contrast and uncertainty. The temporal model is retained because summaries can lose ordering and co-movement; it is not selected for neural complexity or GPU use. A mechanistic latent model is deferred because adjudicated feeding/insulin physiology and required genetics are absent.

The parent support audit used 4 CPUs, 16 GiB and 27.2 seconds; a raw-header audit used 2 CPUs, 4 GiB and 0.53 seconds. Full 24-hour support, sequential weights and temporal convergence are unverified. The future solver envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds. CPU is adequate for B0/B1 and observation modeling; if support passes, one allocated A100 using `cuda:0` may fit the compact temporal learner, with CPU fallback. No GPU is required.

The natural-history/Delphi demonstration is method inspiration only; UKB trajectories and Danish validation are unavailable. The Bayesian latent-trajectory demonstration is not reproduced because genetics and adjudicated physiology are unavailable. The cancer/Oncoformer demonstration is not reproduced: only its supplement was available, its main paper and complete STAR Methods were not inspected, and eICU has no chest-X-ray modality. The expert hypotension/AKI seed is plausible but deferred because artifact handling and adjudicated AKI are unresolved; revisit only if this gate fails and a new outcome-blind vital/lab audit shows materially stronger support. Other imported seeds remain deferred, not disproven.

Harbor can verify bindings, codebook, leakage control, cohort construction, observation modeling, support, fitting, uncertainty, calibration, transport and output-linked conclusions. It cannot establish unobserved hypoglycemia, feeding, administration, causality or benefit.
