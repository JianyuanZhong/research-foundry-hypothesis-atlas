# Provisional hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery and subsequent renal deterioration

Status: provisional and untested. This is a research task, not a completed analysis or clinical conclusion.

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with an ICU stay and a prespecified creatinine-defined AKI episode who reach a fixed 48-hour landmark, persistent creatinine improvement without concurrent urine-output improvement will identify higher subsequent risk of renal deterioration or in-hospital death than concordant recovery, after adjustment for the same landmark severity, treatment context, and observation intensity. The claim is incremental prognostic information from a persistent trajectory, not a causal mechanism, treatment effect, or proof of true renal recovery.

## Grounding and evidence boundary
The inherited expert seed observes that creatinine and urine output can recover discordantly and that creatinine-only recovery may miss persistent renal vulnerability. This observation motivates the question but does not establish prevalence, independence from illness severity, or outcome association. Creatinine is affected by body composition, age, and treatment; urine output is affected by fluid balance, diuretics, and measurement practice. Existing evidence that creatinine-based kidney-function estimates can be misleading during critical illness supports the measurement concern, while the KDIGO framework supports using both creatinine and urine output. Neither supports the proposed association.

The strongest claim currently supported is only that discordant recovery is a plausible, clinically meaningful pattern worth testing. The unresolved claim is whether it adds information beyond current severity and measurement intensity. A rival explanation is that discordance merely reflects persistent illness, treatment intensity, or more frequent measurement rather than renal vulnerability.

## Falsifiable test and method comparison
Use a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV landmark cohort: define AKI onset from creatinine and urine-output measurements, require survival and an adequate pre-landmark observation window, and freeze all predictors at 48 hours. Compare a simple current-state baseline (landmark creatinine/urine-output state, AKI stage, severity and treatment variables) with a substantive trajectory alternative that retains the pre-landmark creatinine and urine-output paths and explicitly models persistence. Evaluate held-out discrimination/calibration and uncertainty, with measurement-density and timing sensitivity analyses. The trajectory model can reveal whether temporal persistence contains information lost by the current-state summary; it cannot establish mechanism.

Supportive results would require reproducible incremental information and calibration in a held-out split, with the association persisting under observation-density adjustment. Adverse results would be no incremental signal, instability across reasonable landmark definitions, or explanation by measurement intensity. Inconclusive results would arise from insufficient cohort size, sparse urine-output observations, or wide uncertainty. None would establish causality or true renal recovery.
