# Insulin-Associated Hypoglycemia in the ICU: Do Worsening Kidney Function and Interrupted Nutrition Increase Risk at the Same Insulin Intensity?

## A Falsifiable, Data-Bound Hypothesis for eICU-CRD (evolved from expert seed eicu-09)

### 1. Hypothesis
At the same insulin exposure intensity, ICU patients whose kidney function is worsening (acute rise in creatinine) and/or whose nutrition is interrupted (a gap in documented enteral/parenteral/oral intake) are more likely to develop insulin-associated hypoglycemia (glucose < 70 mg/dL) than patients with stable kidney function and uninterrupted nutrition.

**Leading claim (to be tested):** After adjusting for insulin intensity, age, sex, illness severity, and hospital, (a) a rise in creatinine during the insulin exposure window is associated with higher odds of a subsequent hypoglycemic glucose, and (b) a documented interruption of nutrition is associated with higher odds of hypoglycemia — and these effects are additive (interaction).

### 2. Scientific opening
**Established.** ICU hypoglycemia is a frequent, insulin-related adverse event with serious consequences (neurologic injury, arrhythmia, death), and insulin therapy itself is a major driver. In a prospective ICU cohort, hypoglycemia < 70 mg/dL and intravenous insulin therapy were among the glycemic parameters associated with in-hospital mortality. [K1] The < 70 mg/dL threshold is the standard operational definition of hypoglycemia. [K1][K2]

**Mechanistic rationale.** Insulin is cleared partly by the kidney; impaired renal function reduces insulin clearance and prolongs its action, raising hypoglycemia risk. A recent review explicitly lists renal dysfunction as a domain that modulates hypoglycemia risk in insulin-treated patients. [K2] Separately, withholding nutrition while insulin is still being delivered removes the substrate that insulin drives into cells, a well-recognized precipitant of iatrogenic hypoglycemia.

**Unresolved.** Whether these two modifiable risk factors — worsening kidney function and interrupted nutrition — independently and additively raise hypoglycemia risk *at the same insulin intensity* in a large, multi-hospital ICU cohort. Prior work has described hypoglycemia incidence/outcomes [K1] and qualitatively listed renal dysfunction as a risk domain [K2], but has not quantified the kidney-function and nutrition effect modification while holding insulin exposure constant, nor separated a true pharmacokinetic/substrate mechanism from confounding by illness severity.

### 3. Why it matters (clinical consequence)
Insulin-associated hypoglycemia in the ICU is a **potentially preventable adverse event**. Identifying which patients at a given insulin intensity are at highest risk (worsening kidneys, interrupted feeding) would directly inform insulin-dosing and feeding-safety decisions (e.g., hold/reduce insulin when creatinine is rising or feeding is stopped) and could be used to stratify patients in glycemic-management trials. A negative result would bound the contribution of these two factors and redirect attention to other drivers (dosing error, severity).

### 4. Strongest rival explanations (to be distinguished)
1. **Confounding by illness severity.** Sicker patients receive more insulin, have worse kidneys, and are more often NPO. If the kidney/nutrition association is entirely driven by severity, adjusting for APACHE severity and insulin intensity should attenuate it. This is the central rival.
2. **Dosing error vs. true pharmacokinetic risk.** Hypoglycemia may reflect inappropriate insulin dosing rather than a physiological kidney/substrate effect. Insulin-intensity matching and GTC (glucose-triggered) coding partially address this but do not fully separate them.
3. **Recording-density / missingness artifact.** "Interrupted nutrition" must be defined from documented intake; missing nutrition records must **not** be treated as cessation (the seed's explicit caution). We require a documented intake *before* the gap and a documented intake *after* (or discharge) to call it an interruption.
4. **Measurement timing.** Hypoglycemia must be measured *after* the exposure window to avoid using future information.

### 6. Variable definitions (with honest audit notes)
- **Insulin exposure / intensity.** A patient is "insulin-exposed" if ≥1 insulin order in the ICU stay. Intensity is captured by (i) route class (IV vs subcutaneous, from `routeadmin`), (ii) number of insulin orders and total ordered duration (`drugstartoffset`→`drugstopoffset`), and (iii) for IV insulin, mean `drugrate`/`infusionrate` from infusionDrug. *Audit note:* `dosage` is free text (e.g., "2.4 units/hour") and parsed dose was sparse (mean 0.00 in probe), so intensity is primarily order-count/duration/route-based, not a clean total-dose. This is a limitation, not a blocker.
- **Worsening kidney function.** Creatinine trajectory from `lab` (labname='creatinine'). Primary: KDIGO-style AKI stage computed from the minimum creatinine in the 48 h before insulin exposure vs. the maximum creatinine during the exposure window (≥1.5× baseline or ≥0.3 mg/dL rise = AKI). *Audit note:* requires ≥2 creatinine measurements straddling the exposure window; patients with a single creatinine are excluded from the kidney sub-analysis.
- **Interrupted nutrition.** From `intakeOutput` nutrition entries. An "interruption" is a gap ≥ 12 h with a documented nutrition entry before and a documented entry after (or ICU discharge). *Audit note:* missing records are **not** treated as cessation (seed caution); patients with no documented nutrition at all are excluded from the nutrition sub-analysis.
- **Hypoglycemia (outcome).** Any glucose < 70 mg/dL [K1][K2] occurring ≥ 0 h after insulin exposure start and before ICU discharge (primary); sensitivity at < 60 mg/dL. Temporal boundary: outcome measured after the exposure window to avoid using future information.
- **Covariates.** age, gender (patient); APACHE II / predicted ICU mortality (apachePatientResult) as severity; hospitalid (random intercept).

### 7. Estimand and temporal boundaries
Target: the association between (worsening kidney function) and (interrupted nutrition), each at matched insulin intensity, and a subsequent hypoglycemic glucose, within the ICU stay. Exposure defined from insulin order start; outcome (hypoglycemia) measured after exposure start and before discharge. Creatinine "worsening" uses only pre- and during-exposure values. No post-discharge or post-outcome information is used.

### 8. Analysis plan
**Baseline (descriptive + crude).**
- Crude hypoglycemia rates by kidney-status (AKI vs no-AKI) and nutrition-status (interrupted vs not), and by insulin route/intensity strata.
- Hospital-stratified comparison (within-hospital standardized rates) to check whether the association is consistent across hospitals.

**Primary (learned/parametric model).**
- Multilevel logistic regression with a hospital random intercept: outcome = hypoglycemia (< 70 mg/dL); predictors = insulin intensity (route + order-duration), AKI (worsening kidney), nutrition interruption, age, gender, APACHE severity; plus the AKI × intensity and nutrition × intensity interactions. Report adjusted odds ratios with 95% CIs.
- Model comparison: baseline (insulin intensity + severity only) vs. alternative (+ kidney and nutrition terms) via likelihood-ratio test / AIC, to show whether kidney and nutrition add information beyond insulin intensity and severity.

**Alternative (nonparametric).**
- XGBoost classifier on the same features (hospital as a feature or group-fold), evaluated by AUROC and, importantly, by whether the kidney and nutrition features retain high importance and whether a simple interaction survives. This tests whether the parametric interaction is an artifact of model form.

**Sensitivity analyses.**
- Hypoglycemia threshold < 60 mg/dL; restrict to IV insulin only; exclude PRN-only insulin; exclude patients with < 2 creatinine or no documented nutrition (sub-analyses); leave-one-hospital-out for transportability.

### 9. Falsification criteria
- **Supportive:** adjusted OR for AKI and/or nutrition interruption is > 1 with a 95% CI excluding 1, the model-comparison test favors the alternative, and the XGBoost feature importance / interaction is concordant — after controlling for insulin intensity and severity.
- **Adverse:** adjusted ORs are ≤ 1 or CIs include 1 and the model-comparison does not favor the alternative — i.e., at matched insulin intensity and severity, kidney and nutrition status do not independently predict hypoglycemia. This would bound the contribution of these two factors and redirect to dosing/severity.
- **Inconclusive:** wide CIs straddling 1, low AKI/nutrition-interruption prevalence after audit exclusions, or discordance between the parametric and XGBoost models — indicating the data cannot resolve the question at this power/quality.

### 10. Limitations
- Medication rows are **orders**, not confirmed administration [K3]; insulin intensity is order-based (dose parsing was sparse).
- No continuous arterial glucose; hypoglycemia is lab/point-of-care sampled, so incidence is a lower bound and depends on sampling density.
- "Interrupted nutrition" is audit-dependent and excludes patients with undocumented intake; missingness is not treated as cessation.
- Observational design: residual confounding by severity and indication remains; the result is an association, not a causal or dosing-recommendation claim.
- Single time period (eICU-CRD v2.0); multi-hospital but not external-validated.

### 11. What would require more than this study
- Causal effect of insulin dosing changes (would need a trial or target-trial emulation with time-varying confounding).
- Actual administered insulin dose (would need infusionDrug titration + administration records).
- True feeding status (would need dietitian/feeding-tolerance notes, not just intakeOutput entries).
