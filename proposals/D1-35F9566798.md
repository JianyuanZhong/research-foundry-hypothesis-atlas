## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the inspected evidence is limited to availability: the frozen eICU v2.0 snapshot contains structured ICU-relative records for patient/stay and hospital identifiers, recorded infusion updates, laboratory values with result/interface/revision fields, intake/output labels and values, nurse-charting labels, and end-of-life offsets. The verified headers do not establish that an infusion was administered, that nutrition was delivered, or that a low glucose was symptomatic or clinically adjudicated.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among adults in their first ICU stay with a recent valid glucose and a numeric recorded Insulin (units/hr) update, a recorded creatinine rise in the preceding 24 hours combined with a gap in qualifying documented nutrition delivery in the preceding 12 hours is associated with a higher probability of a newly observed glucose <70 mg/dL in the next six hours, beyond insulin intensity, prior glucose trajectory and glucose-testing history; the joint contrast persists after modeling glucose ascertainment and in hospitals held out from fitting.

The primary estimand is an adjusted six-hour risk contrast for a detected low glucose, not incidence of unobserved hypoglycemia. The primary outcome is the first valid glucose <70 mg/dL after decision time; <54 mg/dL and recurrent lows are secondary. No glucose observed in the outcome window is missing outcome information, not a negative event. The clinical advance would be evidence for or against an observation-aware, interpretable trigger for prospective chart review or intensified glucose surveillance. It cannot support a dose change, feed restart, or monitoring interval.

Use labresultoffset as event time, not revision time; deduplicate exact/revised records and run a revision sensitivity. ## Analysis and alternatives

All periods are retained for an ascertainment analysis. Define O=1 if a valid qualifying glucose is recorded in (t,t+360] before a competing event; model O using only history through t, hospital-fold training data, testing density/gaps, interface, severity and discharge/EOL context available at t. Among O=1 periods, estimate detected-low risk unweighted and with stabilized inverse-probability-of-observation weights, reporting truncation and positivity. Do not describe this as recovering unobserved hypoglycemia.

B0 is a transparent pooled-logistic cause-specific model for detected low glucose with insulin rate/updates, glucose history and gaps, demographics, weight, admission/ICU context, early severity, vasopressor context, coverage, hospital effects and competing-event handling, but no renal/feed block. B1 adds continuous renal change, strict documented-feed-loss, separate no-interface/coverage categories and the prespecified interaction. Report standardized joint-versus-neither six-hour risk differences/ratios and interaction uncertainty, plus incremental calibration and alert burden beyond B0.

The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over twelve one-hour bins before t, using the same clinical variables plus explicit missingness/observation channels and static context, with heads for detected low glucose, glucose observation and competing discharge/EOL. Select hidden size 32 versus 64 only in development. Compare full, physiology-only and observation-only ablations. This can reveal whether the renal/feed signal is a stable ordering/trajectory pattern that summaries lose, or is primarily a testing/documentation artifact. A small discrimination gain alone is not success.

Use whole-hospital approximately 60/20/20 development/validation/transport folds, keeping all stays for a uniquepid together. Fit preprocessing, observation models, weights and alert thresholds only in development; early-stop on validation Brier score. Use patient-clustered bootstrap for development and hospital-aware bootstrap for transport. If paired support, events, hospital folds, positivity or temporal convergence fail prespecified minimums, report B0/B1 descriptively and mark the learned/weighted comparison inconclusive rather than changing the question.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required falsifications: permute insulin times within stay preserving rates; shuffle renal/feed histories within hospital; use a pre-index placebo low-glucose window excluding prior lows; repeat strict prior-delivery, complete-observable, direct-unit and no-carry-forward definitions; compare laboratory-only and bedside-only strata; analyze insulin-free analogous periods; audit all clinical versus revision timestamps; leave-one-hospital-out estimates with and without observation channels; and use newly observed non-glucose laboratory testing as a negative-control outcome.

Support requires adequate label/unit and paired-period support, stable weights, a prespecified clinically meaningful joint-versus-neither detected-risk difference (target 2 percentage points), an interaction 95% interval excluding zero, incremental calibration/alert value over B0, agreement with the temporal model, no single-hospital dominance, and failed placebo/permutation/negative-control falsifications. This supports only a transportable prognostic monitoring signal for prospective chart review.

Adverse evidence is a null below target, reversal, disappearance after ascertainment weighting or strict labels, poor held-out calibration, confinement to one hospital/interface, negative-control similarity, or reproduction in placebo/insulin-free periods. It refutes this measurable marker in the snapshot or implicates surveillance/documentation artifact; it does not prove insulin or feeding interruption harmless.

Inconclusive evidence is inadequate support/events, ambiguous labels, unstable weights, unsupported folds, competing-event miscalibration, or temporal nonconvergence. It triggers data-quality, clinical-adjudication or prospective work.

The stronger claims require medication-administration records, delivered-calorie/feed-stop records, bedside/chart adjudication of glucose symptoms and treatment, validated AKI adjudication, clinician intent, and a prospective or emulated-intervention study.
