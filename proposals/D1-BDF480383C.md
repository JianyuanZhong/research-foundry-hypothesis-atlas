# Robustness of time-weighted hypotension burden for early AKI: summary choice, measurement-density and baseline-severity controls

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP), after controlling for measurement density, baseline illness severity, and after showing that the signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined)?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density. The strongest rival is that the apparent burden signal is an artifact of recording density, measurement timing, or baseline illness severity, or that it is specific to one arbitrary choice of blood-pressure summary. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

MAP is the common bedside summary and a natural baseline for hemodynamic risk. Minimum systolic blood pressure alone can overstate a single low reading, while MAP may understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

KDIGO creatinine-based AKI definitions can reflect broader disease severity and clinical context rather than isolated renal injury [K1]. KDIGO criteria define AKI by creatinine rise or urine output thresholds and are widely used in ICU populations [K2]. Longitudinal ICU trajectories of routine biomarkers can identify prognostic subtypes beyond single-timepoint summaries [K3]. This proposal tests whether a time-weighted hypotension burden adds prognostic information beyond MAP, with explicit severity and measurement-density controls.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU 2.0 adult ICU admissions with at least one systolic and one diastolic blood-pressure measurement in the first 24 hours of ICU care. Define ICU time zero from the patient table's `icu_los`/`icu_admission` timestamp fields. Exclude patients with fewer than 10 paired BP measurements in the first 24 hours, because sparse recording makes burden estimates unreliable. Define AKI as a KDIGO Stage 1–3 creatinine rise within 48 hours of ICU admission, using the earliest creatinine measurement in the first 24 hours as baseline. Exclude patients without a baseline creatinine or with a creatinine measurement after the AKI window.

The primary exposure is a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden: the fraction of the first 24 hours during which MAP is below 65 mmHg, computed from paired BP measurements and weighted by the time interval between measurements. This is a descriptive burden measure, not a treatment target. The primary comparison is MAP-only versus MAP plus burden.

## Data bindings

Use the following eICU 2.0 tables and fields:

- `patient`: `patientid`, `icu_los`, `icu_admission`, `age`, `sex`, `race`, `ethnicity`, `insurance`, `icu_type`, `disposition`.
- `vital`: `patientid`, `charttime`, `systolic_bp`, `diastolic_bp`, `heart_rate`, `respiratory_rate`, `temperature`, `spo2`, `map`.
- `lab`: `patientid`, `charttime`, `lab_name`, `lab_value`, `lab_unit`, `lab_loinc`.
- `infusion`: `patientid`, `charttime`, `infusion_name`, `infusion_rate`, `infusion_volume`, `infusion_duration`.
- `apachescore`: `patientid`, `apache2_sv`, `apache2_sod`.

Join all tables on `patientid`. Use `charttime` for temporal ordering. Use `icu_admission` as the reference time for the first 24-hour and 48-hour windows.

## Baseline severity conditioning

Use APACHE IV score from the `apachescore` table as the primary baseline severity measure. If APACHE IV is missing, use the SOFA score as a fallback. Report the proportion of patients with missing severity scores and perform a sensitivity analysis excluding patients with missing severity. Stratify the primary analysis by severity (low, medium, high) to test whether the burden signal is robust across severity levels.

## Measurement density controls

Compute the number of paired BP measurements in the first 24 hours and the mean inter-measurement interval. Include these as covariates in the primary model. Perform a sensitivity analysis restricting to patients with at least 20 paired BP measurements. Report the distribution of measurement density by AKI status.

## Blood-pressure summary robustness

Compute four prespecified blood-pressure summaries from the first 24 hours:

1. MAP-only: the 24-hour mean arterial pressure.
2. Systolic-only: the 24-hour mean systolic blood pressure.
3. Diastolic-only: the 24-hour mean diastolic blood pressure.
4. Combined: a vector of MAP, systolic, and diastolic means.

Fit the primary model with each summary as the baseline. The burden measure is defined relative to MAP < 65 mmHg. Report whether the burden signal is robust to the choice of summary.

## Primary analysis

Fit a logistic regression model for AKI within 48 hours. The primary model includes:

- MAP-only (or the chosen summary)
- Time-weighted hypotension burden
- Baseline severity (APACHE IV or SOFA)
- Vasopressor exposure in the first 24 hours
- Baseline creatinine
- Hospital (fixed effect)
- Measurement density (number of BP measurements)
- Age, sex, race, ethnicity, insurance, ICU type

Report the coefficient, 95% confidence interval, and p-value for the burden term. Report the AUC, calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow), and Brier score for the MAP-only and MAP+burden models. Report the hospital-level analysis.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if:

1. The burden coefficient is not statistically significant (p > 0.05) after adjustment for baseline severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density.
2. The burden signal is not robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined).
3. The burden signal is not robust to measurement density (the signal disappears when restricting to patients with at least 20 paired BP measurements).
4. The burden signal is not robust to baseline severity (the signal disappears in the high-severity stratum).

## Solver deliverable

The solver must fit the primary logistic regression model and report:

- The coefficient, 95% confidence interval, and p-value for the burden term.
- The AUC, calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow), and Brier score for the MAP-only and MAP+burden models.
- The hospital-level analysis.
- The sensitivity analysis results for measurement density and baseline severity.
- The robustness analysis results for the choice of blood-pressure summary.

## Limitations

This is a retrospective observational study. The burden measure is a descriptive measure, not a treatment target. The study cannot establish causality. The study is limited to adult ICU patients with adequate BP measurements. The study cannot account for unmeasured confounding. The study cannot establish the clinical utility of the burden measure.

## Next steps

If the hypothesis is supported, the next step is to validate the burden measure in a prospective cohort. If the hypothesis is falsified, the next step is to revisit the design and consider alternative measures of hypotension burden.
