# Context-specific MerTK blockade: does it selectively release a pathogenic FLS program?

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence is that macrophage–fibroblast interactions in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) can reshape synovial fibroblasts and that different macrophage states can have opposing effects on fibroblast programs. The inspected [K1] Kuo et al. abstract reports HBEGF+ inflammatory macrophages shaped by resident fibroblasts and TNF, and an EGF-receptor-dependent fibroblast-invasiveness effect. The [K3] E-MTAB-8873 repository description reports that MerTK/CD206neg macrophages induced an inflammatory FLS state whereas MerTK/CD206pos macrophages, especially from remission, induced collagen/TGF-beta-associated repair responses. The [K2] Wei and Xu review frames difficult-to-treat RA as potentially involving a self-reinforcing stromal–myeloid “failed-resolution” circuit, while explicitly treating this as a proposed mechanistic framework.

The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable: in the same RA FLS donor, blocking MerTK on an inflammatory macrophage context will attenuate a reproducible inflammatory FLS transcriptional program without attenuating a distinct protective/repair-associated FLS program. This would support context-selective therapeutic targeting of macrophage–fibroblast crosstalk. It would not establish that UNC106238 acts directly on FLS, that MerTK is the sole mediator, or that clinical remission would follow.

## Hypothesis and clinical importance

Primary hypothesis: among the 445 measured E-MTAB-8873 features, the paired FLS response to MerTK inhibitor versus no inhibitor will show a larger reduction in an inflammatory FLS signature under MerTK/CD206neg macrophage co-culture than under MerTK/CD206pos co-culture, while a pre-specified protective/repair-associated signature will not show the same reduction.

A secondary, independent-context hypothesis is that the direction of the inhibitor-associated FLS response is concordant in E-MTAB-8316, where healthy-donor monocyte-derived macrophages were pre-treated with LPS and either no compound or UNC106238 (250 µM) before 48-hour transwell co-culture with four RA FLS donors. This is a perturbation screen, not independent clinical validation.

Resolving this matters because a drug that suppresses a pathogenic FLS program while preserving a repair-associated program would be more informative for target prioritization than a generic anti-inflammatory effect. Conversely, a broad suppression or reversal of the protective program would argue against selective MerTK targeting and identify a safety/therapeutic ceiling.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive result: the Mneg inhibitor contrast reduces the inflammatory signature with a precision-weighted effect and uncertainty excluding the prespecified null, while the Mpos contrast does not reduce the protective signature; the hierarchical model shows a coherent context-by-inhibitor interaction and the E-MTAB-8316 secondary contrast has the same direction in the majority of verified FLS donors. This supports selective context dependence, not causality or clinical efficacy.

Adverse result: inhibitor treatment suppresses both signatures, reverses the protective signature, or produces no reproducible inflammatory reduction. This would argue against selective MerTK-context targeting and would motivate testing broader off-target or dose effects.

Inconclusive result: intervals include both clinically meaningful benefit and harm, donor effects are discordant, feature mapping fails, or the targeted panel lacks enough informative features. This would justify additional technical replication or a broader assay, not a claim of no effect.

Clinical adjudication and direct mechanism require additional evidence: protein-level MerTK/CD206 phenotyping, drug exposure and specificity, FLS invasion/repair functional assays, patient-level treatment response, and ideally independent human cohorts. The available data cannot establish that a drug-associated expression response is a direct target action or that it predicts remission.
