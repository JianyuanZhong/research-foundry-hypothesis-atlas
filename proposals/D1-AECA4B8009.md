## Targeted successor and substantive clinical repair

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the mature, frozen HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B design: the adult cohort and systemic-record/comparator definition, duplicate-safe encounter joins, TACE sequencing, assay-separated B/P/Y clocks, annual forward validation origins, locked nested ridge models, outcome-blind support funnel, observed complete-triplet standardized held-out RMSE estimand, training-range partial-identification bounds, patient-cluster uncertainty contract, and strict noncausal limits.

The remaining clinical gap is not another prediction metric. It is whether a laboratory forecast has any observable bridge to a clinically meaningful care process. The added question is therefore:

> Conditional on the same selected repeat-TACE episodes and without changing the prognostic fit, does the held-out P-beyond-B forecast show reproducible temporal concordance with subsequent documented reassessment/contact, beyond the M0 forecast and a prevalence baseline, while remaining absent from a pre-P placebo window?

This is a **care-process proxy audit**, not a treatment-effect or benefit analysis. A positive bridge would mean that the forecast is aligned with recorded subsequent monitoring/contact in this institution; it would not show that the forecast caused care, that care was appropriate, that the assay was clinically interpretable, or that patients benefited. A null bridge would be clinically important: it would leave a surrogate laboratory improvement without demonstrated decision relevance. The laboratory prognostic hypothesis and the process-bridge hypothesis are separate and must not be conflated.

## Preregistered downstream care-process bridge

### Temporal separation

All process endpoints are computed after the selected event and never enter model fitting or Y-bound arithmetic. The primary process window is the strict interval `(event_time, event_time + 30 days]`. Because Y occupies `(event_time, event_time +72 hours]`, secondary laboratory and examination process signals use `(event_time +72 hours, event_time +30 days]` to avoid counting the Y ascertainment window. The primary contact signal is an additional encounter, not the selected repeat encounter.

For every BP-eligible episode and assay, construct the following binary endpoints. Each is retained separately; no unvalidated composite is used as the primary outcome.

1. **`C30` documented subsequent contact (primary bridge endpoint).** One or more distinct encounter keys with a valid `Encounter Time` in `(event_time, event_time+30 days]`, excluding the selected repeat encounter. This is recorded contact only; it is not proof of a visit purpose, completed reassessment, or appropriate care.
2. **`L30_postY` target-assay remeasurement (secondary).** One or more finite uncensored rows with exact assay `test` equal to the assay-specific target and valid `test time` in `(event_time+72 hours, event_time+30 days]`. Albumin and total bilirubin remain separate. This is a recorded remeasurement, not a patient outcome and not evidence that the assay was clinically indicated.
3. **`E30_postY` examination activity (secondary).** One or more examination rows with valid `start time` in `(event_time+72 hours, event_time+30 days]`, using row presence only. Do not use report text.
4. **`O30_ordered` non-drug order entry (secondary).** One or more `orders` rows with valid `order time` in `(event_time, event_time+30 days]`. Report separately the stricter scheduled-start variant with valid `start time` in the same window. Neither variant means completion; `order status` is retained descriptively and is not interpreted as a validated completion code.
5. **`M30_recorded` medication record (exploratory only).** One or more medication rows with valid `Start Time` in `(event_time, event_time+30 days]`, separately by broad inherited systemic-record ontology where applicable. Do not infer administration, adherence, dose adequacy, indication, or response from these rows.

A same-day event boundary is strict at the left and inclusive at 30 days at the right. Invalid timestamps do not qualify and are counted in a timestamp-validity audit. A row from the selected event encounter is not an additional contact unless its encounter key is distinct and its `visit time` falls in the strict window. If an encounter record has multiple source rows, count the deduplicated encounter once.

The primary bridge is intentionally `C30`, which is available with an exact native encounter clock and does not require pretending that an un-timed document, an order, or an examination proves completed care. `L30_postY`, `E30_postY`, `O30_ordered`, and `M30_recorded` are process descriptors with progressively weaker clinical meaning.

## Negative controls and falsification

1. **Temporal pre-P process window.** Construct `C_pre30_7` as an additional encounter with valid `encounter time` in `[event_time-30 days, event_time-7 days]`, excluding the selected encounter. This interval precedes the entire P window (P is restricted to the preceding 72 hours) and is a temporal negative-control outcome for the downstream bridge. It can still share baseline disease/utilization determinants; therefore a positive association is a warning about baseline selection, not a failed causal null test. Report AUC0, AUC1, and their difference, but do not use it to exclude episodes or claim a causal effect.
2. **Score-permutation null.** Within each future assay/block and arm, use a declared seed to permute the frozen model scores across BP-eligible rows 1,000 times, preserving process labels and denominators. The observed `AUC1_minus_AUC0` must be compared with the empirical null as a code/linkage diagnostic. This is not a clinical null and cannot establish exchangeability.
3. **Window-boundary audit.** Repeat only as a coding check with exact boundary fixtures: an event at +72 hours belongs to Y but not `L30_postY`/`E30_postY`; an event at +30 days belongs to the 30-day process window; an event at exactly -7 days belongs to `C_pre30_7`; an event at -72 hours does not belong to the pre-P negative-control window. No post-event process row can alter B/P or event selection.
4. **Ascertainment cross-check.** Retain the parent pre-Y exam/follow-up/document strata and cross-tab process endpoints by those strata. A document is presence-only and untimed; it must never be used to qualify a process window. Large endpoint differences by opportunity stratum are selection evidence, not adjustment variables or proof of benefit.

Do not use the post-event endpoint as a covariate in the Y observation model, as an inverse-observation weight, or as an imputation rule. No process field is allowed to alter the fixed prognostic fit.

## Interpretation precedence and explicit limits

Apply this precedence in every output, from strongest stop to weakest descriptive interpretation:

1. **`computational_invalid`** if source/schema/hash, duplicate-safe join, clock, prediction-finiteness, or denominator assertions fail. Emit no scientific interpretation.
2. **`unsupported/non-estimable`** if support gates, training-range conditions, score classes, process classes, bootstrap cluster requirements, or ESS conditions fail. Report descriptive counts only; do not call a favorable point estimate supportive.
3. **`selection-sensitive`** if the observed complete-case prognostic result is favorable but the global deterministic Delta interval crosses zero, if observation gradients/ascertainment strata are materially different, if the temporal negative control is comparably strong, or if process concordance is unstable across arm/calendar/opportunity strata. Do not upgrade this to support.
4. **`adverse to incremental prognosis`** if an adequately supported primary RMSE contrast is nonpositive, M1 repeatedly degrades, or the global Delta interval is wholly nonpositive. This challenges incremental prediction but does not prove B sufficiency, mechanism, or absence of clinical value.
5. **`proxy-bridge only`** if the prognostic result is robust but C30 concordance is present without an independently credible patient-important endpoint. This can motivate prospective workflow research; it is not decision utility or benefit.
6. **`locally supportive prognostic association`** only if all inherited support/range/uncertainty gates pass and the global Delta interval is strictly positive throughout the declared training-range scenario. The claim remains a selected short-horizon prognostic association in the tested calendar mixture. Even with a positive process bridge, no claim of treatment response, appropriate care, survival, safety, utility, actionability, or patient benefit is allowed.
7. **`inconclusive`** for any contradictory or low-information combination not resolved above, including later calendar blocks with severe outcome loss, full-source-range dependence, influential patients, or process-only signals without a robust prognostic result.

The critical precedence rule is that a positive process bridge can never rescue a non-robust or selection-sensitive prognostic result. Conversely, a robust prognostic result with no process bridge is not a failed biomarker hypothesis; it is a failure to demonstrate decision relevance in these recorded proxies.

Computationally checkable claims are source/schema hashes, headers, duplicate-safe joins, cohort/event reconstruction, exact clock boundaries, outcome-blind funnels, finite-range bounds, frozen predictions, process endpoint construction, negative-control bookkeeping, and bootstrap arithmetic. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for assay units/reference ranges, laboratory validity, disease severity, tumor burden, imaging response, TACE intent/administration, whether a recorded order/contact represented appropriate completed care, outside-care capture, mortality, hepatic failure, causal effects, utility, actionability, and patient benefit.
