# Hypothesis Proposal: Discordant Kidney Function and Pre-Illness Reserve

## Scientific Question

**Among individuals with normal creatinine-based eGFR but lower cystatin C-based eGFR, does poor grip strength (a proxy for muscle mass/strength) identify those at highest risk of developing severe acute kidney injury (AKI) and subsequent chronic kidney disease (CKD)?** [K1][K2][K3]

This hypothesis addresses a critical clinical gap: routine creatinine-based kidney function screening may miss high-risk individuals with normal creatinine but impaired kidney reserve. The combination of discordant kidney function estimates (normal creatinine eGFR vs. low cystatin C eGFR) and poor muscle strength may identify a high-risk phenotype that warrants closer monitoring.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Early Detection**: Identifying individuals at risk before overt kidney dysfunction manifests
2. **Preventive Interventions**: Enabling targeted interventions (diet, exercise, medication review) before irreversible damage occurs
3. **Risk Stratification**: Improving prediction beyond creatinine alone, which is influenced by muscle mass [K3]
4. **Cost-Effectiveness**: Focusing surveillance resources on high-risk individuals

## Hypothesis Statement

At baseline, participants with:
- Normal creatinine-based eGFR (≥60 mL/min/1.73m²)
- Lower cystatin C-based eGFR (<60 mL/min/1.73m²)
- Poor grip strength (<30 kg for men, <20 kg for women)

Are at significantly higher risk of developing:
1. Severe acute kidney injury (AKI) within 2 years
2. Progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD stage ≥3) within 5 years

Compared to participants with concordant kidney function estimates (both creatinine and cystatin C eGFR ≥60 mL/min/1.73m²).

## Baseline Model

A stratified Cox proportional hazards model with the following structure:

**Primary Model**:
- Hazard ratio for discordant kidney function (normal creatinine eGFR, low cystatin C eGFR)
- Adjusted for: age, sex, BMI, smoking status, diabetes status, hypertension status
- Reference group: Concordant normal kidney function (both creatinine and cystatin C eGFR ≥60 mL/min/1.73m²)

**Secondary Model**:
- Hazard ratio for low grip strength (<30 kg men, <20 kg women)
- Adjusted for: age, sex, BMI, smoking status, diabetes status, hypertension status
- Reference group: Normal grip strength

**Interaction Model**:
- Test for interaction between discordant kidney function and grip strength
- Hazard ratio for combination (both discordant kidney function AND low grip strength)
- Adjusted for: age, sex, BMI, smoking status, diabetes status, hypertension status

## Alternative Model: Body Composition-Adjusted Analysis

A more mechanistic model that incorporates body composition directly:

**Model Structure**:
- Include grip strength as a continuous variable (not categorical)
- Include waist-to-weight ratio (visceral adiposity proxy)
- Include muscle mass index (if available)
- Test for differential effects of these measures on different outcomes

**Scientific Rationale**:
- Grip strength alone may not capture all aspects of muscle function
- Waist-to-weight ratio may better capture visceral adiposity
- The combination may reveal differential pathways to different disease outcomes

**What This Could Reveal**:
- Whether grip strength and waist circumference have independent or interactive effects
- Whether the risk is driven by muscle strength, fat distribution, or their combination
- Whether different disease outcomes (AKI vs. CKD vs. CVD) have different risk profiles

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be considered **falsified** if:

1. **No Association**: The hazard ratio for discordant kidney function is not significantly different from 1 (95% CI includes 1) after adjustment for confounders
2. **Opposite Direction**: The hazard ratio is significantly <1 (suggesting protective effect)
3. **No Interaction**: No significant interaction between discordant kidney function and grip strength
4. **No Effect of Grip Strength**: Grip strength does not independently predict outcomes

The hypothesis will be considered **supported** if:

1. **Significant Association**: Hazard ratio for discordant kidney function is significantly >1 (95% CI excludes 1)
2. **Synergistic Effect**: Interaction term is significant with hazard ratio >1
3. **Grip Strength Effect**: Low grip strength independently predicts outcomes
4. **Clinical Plausibility**: Effect sizes are consistent with prior literature and biological plausibility

The hypothesis will be considered **inconclusive** if:

1. **Insufficient Power**: Wide confidence intervals despite reasonable effect size
2. **Confounding**: Residual confounding cannot be ruled out
3. **Measurement Error**: Significant measurement error in key variables
4. **Selection Bias**: Selection bias cannot be adequately addressed

## Limitations

1. **Measurement Error**: Single baseline measurement of grip strength and kidney function
2. **Reverse Causation**: Pre-existing kidney disease may affect grip strength
3. **Residual Confounding**: Unmeasured confounders (lifestyle, socioeconomic status)
4. **Selection Bias**: UKB participants may not be representative of general population
5. **Measurement Limitations**: Cystatin C not routinely measured in clinical practice
