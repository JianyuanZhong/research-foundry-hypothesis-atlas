## Clinical question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

After initial vasopressor-treated shock has resolved, should a still-invasively-ventilated adult with substantial positive fluid balance receive an ICU-recorded furosemide or bumetanide delivery during the next 24 hours? The clinically important unresolved claim is that the recorded-loop initiation strategy is associated with more genuine liberation from the index invasive airway, without a recorded terminal or renal-treatment burden.

Prior evidence supports only narrower propositions. The inspected 2024 ISCCM weaning position statement (Clerk et al., PMCID `PMC11369923`, DOI `10.5005/jp-journals-10071-24716`, frozen source [source checksum]) recognizes 48 hours without reinstituted ventilatory support after extubation as a conventional weaning-success window and discusses avoiding positive balance, while calling diuretic use to achieve negative balance controversial. The reviewed conservative-fluid literature supports lower recorded fluid balance but does not establish that isolated loop initiation causes respiratory benefit or is renally safe. The expert-demonstration cancer paper’s main text and full STAR Methods were unavailable and are not cited as read; none of the three demonstrations is evidence for this MIMIC claim.

The falsifiable hypothesis is: among the frozen eligible adults and the prespecified measured-overlap population, initiating a qualifying ICU-input-system recorded furosemide/bumetanide delivery before the 24-hour closure increases the AA-E48-D5 risk by more than 5 percentage points versus avoiding the same recorded event, while conservative upper risk-difference limits for recorded death/hospice/acute-hospital discharge and observed RRT by day 7 remain below 3 and 5 percentage points, respectively.

This does not test whether all-route loop treatment causes weaning, whether fluid overload caused respiratory failure, whether clinicians intended extubation, or whether the intervention is safe. It tests a carefully defined recorded-strategy association and exposes its missingness and endpoint-linkage limits.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive recorded evidence requires the lower one-sided 95% bootstrap limit for `DeltaL` to exceed +0.05; the conservative upper recorded death/hospice/acute-transfer RD to be below +0.03; the conservative upper observed-RRT7 RD to be below +0.05; no adverse ALDL7/destination reversal; unanchored and 72-hour directions to be compatible; airway-link reconciliation not to materially reverse the primary; all source, partition, clock, unit, state, overlap, calibration, negative-control and bound checks to pass; B eMAR contamination at most 5%; ambiguity at most 1%; no unresolved clinical-time/key/status/link contradiction; storetime completeness is reported but cannot gate the endpoint; each arm ESS at least 75; and no more than 25% of compatible decisions closed by ICU exit before G.

Adverse recorded evidence is declared if the upper one-sided 95% limit for `DeltaU` is at most 0; the lower recorded death/hospice/acute-transfer RD is at least +0.03; the lower observed-RRT7 RD is at least +0.05 under both date brackets; or ALDL7 `DeltaU` is at most -0.05. These are record-defined concerns, not proof of toxicity.

Margin-falsified means the upper one-sided 95% limit for `DeltaU` is at most +0.05. It rejects a clinically meaningful recorded AA-E48-D5 association, not every possible physiologic effect.

Inconclusive/infeasible includes every other result: eligible n<200; fewer than 100 A satisfactions or B completions/compatible closures; fewer than 100 definite AA-E48-D5 events; ESS<75; more than 10% of person-hours outside treatment probability [.01,.99]; >1% ambiguity/contradiction; >5% B eMAR contamination; >25% E-first closures; >40% primary intervals unknown in either arm; material reversal under unanchored/link/NIV/timing/status/72-hour/seven-day sensitivities; tracheostomy-link prevalence too high to interpret; failed negative controls; or unreconciled state/bound arithmetic. Do not rescue feasibility by counting unplanned extubations, accepting unlinked extubation rows, assuming ward liberation, changing the first opportunity, broadening exposure sources, or searching subgroups.

Supportive results justify only: “In the measured-overlap discovery cohort, the ICU-input-system recorded-loop strategy was associated with at least a five-point increase in recorded airway-anchored item-227194 48-hour extubation success without the prespecified recorded harm signals.” Adverse results identify a record-defined respiratory, terminal, or renal-treatment concern. Margin-falsified results argue against the prespecified meaningful increase. Inconclusive results mean that linkage, observation, overlap, event yield, or uncertainty prevented resolution; they do not establish equivalence.
