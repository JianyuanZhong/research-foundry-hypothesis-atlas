# Renal specificity of creatinine–cystatin C discordance at low grip strength

## Parent, unresolved question, and supported evidence

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`.

Creatinine-based eGFR can be spuriously high when creatinine generation is low; cystatin C is less directly dependent on muscle mass but is affected by nonrenal factors. The locally read Haines et al. (CJASN 2023) readable article studied 38 mechanically ventilated ICU patients with serial creatinine, cystatin C, measured GFR and muscle ultrasound. It supports a critical-illness physiological concern about creatinine-based overestimation during muscle loss. It does not establish a community UK Biobank association, an interaction with grip, a measured-filtration accuracy claim, or benefit from ordering cystatin C.

The unresolved clinical question is whether a lower cystatin-C-based estimate relative to a creatinine-based estimate identifies prospective renal vulnerability preferentially among people with lower grip strength, and whether that association is more renal-specific than a general tendency to acquire hospital diagnoses. This matters because a positive, specific signal could justify a prospective validation study of selective cystatin-C testing or closer kidney surveillance in patients whose creatinine-only estimate appears reassuring. It would not by itself justify a clinical intervention.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 with valid baseline creatinine, cystatin C, bilateral grip, BMI, sex, age and assessment date, and without a previously recorded hospital N17/N18 diagnosis, a lower standardized D = eGFRcys − eGFRcr is associated with a larger five-year risk of a first subsequently recorded hospital N17 diagnosis at lower baseline grip strength, after adjustment for eGFRcr, age, sex and BMI.

The strongest claim supported by available evidence is physiological plausibility in a small ICU study and the presence of structured biomarker, assessment, and dated hospital-code fields in this UKB snapshot. The experiment tests only the prospective association and its specificity in a selected biomarker subgroup; it does not test filtration accuracy or causality.

## Exposure and estimand

Convert creatinine from micromol/L to mg/dL only after units are verified. Use the race-free CKD-EPI 2021 creatinine equation and CKD-EPI 2012 standalone cystatin-C equation, frozen before outcome fitting:

- eGFRcr = 142 min(Scr/k,1)^alpha max(Scr/k,1)^−1.200 0.9938^Age 1.012^Female, with female k=.7, alpha=−.241 and male k=.9, alpha=−.302.
- eGFRcys = 133 min(Scys/.8,1)^−.499 max(Scys/.8,1)^−1.328 .996^Age .932^Female.

Define D=eGFRcys−eGFRcr. Standardize eGFRcr, D and grip within sex using training parameters. The primary parameter is the standardized D-by-grip coefficient in a cause-specific Cox model for first post-index recorded N17.

The primary clinical-scale estimand is a model-standardized five-year risk difference-in-differences:
`Psi = [R5(D=-1,G=-1)-R5(D=0,G=-1)] - [R5(D=-1,G=+1)-R5(D=0,G=+1)]`,
averaging age, sex, BMI and eGFRcr over the eligible cohort. R5 is obtained from the fitted AKI and death cause-specific hazards with competing death integrated. Report the interaction hazard ratio, the four component risks, and Psi with a participant bootstrap 95% interval. The prespecified direction is Psi>0. Do not invent an observed-result threshold; if event support permits, declare any minimally important absolute risk threshold before fitting.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The transparent baseline comparison uses nested cause-specific Cox models on the same participant-level splits:

1. eGFRcr + age + sex + BMI + grip;
2. model 1 + D;
3. model 2 + D-by-grip (primary).

A raw-biomarker sensitivity fits log creatinine and log cystatin C terms with age and sex to test equation artifacts. Restricted cubic splines for D and grip are a functional-form sensitivity, not a replacement primary analysis.

The substantive learned alternative uses exactly the same six inputs (eGFRcr_z, D_z, grip_z, age, sex, BMI), one-year intervals through five years, and separate AKI/death discrete-time hazards. It is a shallow gradient-boosted competing-risk hazard model (depth 2–3, fixed minimum leaf size and regularization; iteration selected on validation). Use a frozen 60/20/20 participant-level eid split; all transformations, imputation and tuning are learned on training only. Evaluate untouched test data with IPCW Brier score, time-dependent AUC, calibration slope/intercept, the same Psi risk surface, bootstrap intervals, and stability across resamples.

The learned model is scientifically informative only if it reveals a stable, calibrated, clinically interpretable threshold or non-additive D-by-grip region that the Cox interaction loses; a small metric gain alone is not an advance. If events, interval support, calibration, or surface stability fail, defer the learned model and retain the transparent estimate. Approximate budget: at most 4 CPU, 8 GiB RAM and 2 hours including bounded uncertainty; GPU is unnecessary.

## Falsification and decision rules

Before fitting, run and preserve the full source-row, one-to-one-join, missingness, units, value-range, code/date, event-support and administrative-boundary audit. The episode-5 bounded audit is `work/ukb_kidney_event_audit.json`; its counts must not be cited until the job succeeds and the file is inspected.

Additional falsification/sensitivity analyses:

- Two-year landmark: exclude N17/N18 through two years and restart follow-up at two years; persistence reduces concern about occult baseline disease but changes the population.
- Strict index-date and same-day-death alternatives.
- N18 secondary outcome and raw-biomarker sensitivity.
- Prespecified K35 nonrenal control if all support gates pass.
- Permute D within sex-by-age strata using a frozen seed; any surviving association signals leakage or pipeline error, not evidence.
- Report inclusion selection, missingness, overlap/duplicate eids, eGFRcr/eGFRcys support cells and influence diagnostics.

Supportive evidence requires verified prerequisites, adequate paired N17 events and risk sets, positive Psi with a 95% interval excluding zero, persistence under strict timing and the landmark, and no comparably strong K35 association. A stable learned surface may clarify mechanism-like nonlinearity but is not required. Adverse evidence is a precise null/opposite Psi, no increment after eGFRcr and grip, dependence on early or same-day records, comparable K35 signal, or an unstable/poorly calibrated learned surface. Inconclusive evidence includes unverified field units/timing, code semantics, administrative end, substantial code/date discordance, too few events, unsupported cells, or failed model assumptions/calibration.

## Interpretation and actual deliverable

Computationally checkable outputs are source-row and join audits, event-support counts, eGFR distributions, the Cox interaction and Psi with uncertainty, competing-risk and sensitivity estimates, K35 specificity contrast, and—if gated—the held-out learned-model metrics and stable risk surface. Every conclusion must link to those outputs and uncertainty.

The experiment cannot establish measured-filtration accuracy, muscle mediation, causal effects, treatment benefit, outpatient disease absence, AKI stage/KDIGO status, complete follow-up, transportability, or clinical utility. Those require adjudicated longitudinal creatinine/urine output, measured GFR, inflammation/body-composition measures, treatment and utilization context, independent cohorts, expert clinical review, and prospective decision-impact testing.

The required new scientific deliverable is a fitted transparent effect-modification estimate with competing-risk five-year Psi and renal-specificity contrast; the learned model is a prespecified secondary deliverable only when its support gates pass. A source audit alone is a prerequisite, not resolution of the hypothesis.
