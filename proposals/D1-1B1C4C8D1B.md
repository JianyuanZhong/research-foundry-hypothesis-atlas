# HCC successor: assay information and time-bounded recorded evidence availability

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

This child preserves [prior hypothesis]'s primary estimand and endpoint exactly. The primary remains the strict-prior assay-content-versus-capture-intensity contrast (V minus I) for the semantics-independent, admission-anchored next recorded transition: native procedure presence/coarse start-time bin versus early discharge, no procedure observed through 72 hours, or unresolved timing. It remains the only primary claim and retains the incumbent seven-state sensitivity.

The substantive addition asks whether the same assay-content signal is relevant to a consequential recorded-care workflow: whether interpretable examination evidence becomes available early enough during the admission to support record review or coordination. This is an evidence-availability question, not a claim that the evidence is correct, clinically actionable, or beneficial. A separate encounter-level availability-discordance descriptor uses clinical_documents and pathology only to characterize cross-source recording gaps; because those tables have no native event times, it is never presented as a timed sequence or causal discrepancy.

The future solver must newly produce:

1. The inherited frozen cohort, patient-disjoint random and calendar partitions, exact joins and all primary label/time audits.
2. The inherited A/L/Q/I/V predictor manifests, transparent multinomial baseline, marked irregular alternative S, S_I/S_Q ablations, locked primary predictions, proper scores, calibration, uncertainty and falsifications.
3. A frozen secondary examination-evidence availability label from examinations alone: first row with a valid native start time and nonblank examination findings or examination diagnosis in [t0,t0+72h), before discharge, with [0,6), [6,12), [12,24), [24,48), [48,72) bins; early discharge before evidence; no evidence observed by 72 hours; and unresolved examination timing when missing/invalid rows could alter the state. A 24-hour version is a prespecified sensitivity, not a post-hoc cutoff.
4. A descriptive cross-source availability table: examination evidence present by 72 hours versus any nonblank clinical_documents payload and any pathology payload for the same encounter, with the four source-availability cells and explicit unknown timing. This is a documentation-availability discordance measure, not disagreement of clinical findings.
5. Matched predictions for the secondary label using exactly the same A/L/Q/I/V inputs, splits, fitting-only preprocessing and model classes as the primary, with paired V-I, V-Q and I-Q log-loss/Brier contrasts, calibration and patient-clustered uncertainty.
6. A claim-to-output table that separates computable evidence availability from clinical adjudication, missing timing, and implementation/benefit claims.

Completion is the frozen secondary-label audit, fitted matched models, locked predictions, proper-score/calibration/uncertainty outputs, discordance table and falsification/interpretation report. A positive result is not required.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypotheses

Existing evidence and this audit support only that the HCC snapshot contains abundant recorded examinations and narrative payload, and that routine assays/capture patterns can be associated with later locally recorded care. The data do not establish active HCC, stage, tumor burden, liver reserve, resectability, examination correctness, clinician intent, treatment receipt/completion, response, survival, benefit, causality or utility.

The unresolved primary question remains:

> Among adults at their first locally recorded HCC-coded inpatient admission, do strictly pre-admission assay-specific results improve calibrated prediction of the next generic recorded procedure/discharge transition beyond admission context, current-admission laboratories, prior capture intensity and prior assay identity, and does that content increment persist in the later calendar era?

The primary directional hypothesis, unchanged from the parent, is that V improves both log loss and Brier score over I in the locked 2021–2025 calendar evaluation, without material calibration harm, and that the increment attenuates after within-assay result/value permutation. A random-split gain alone is not evidence of calendar transport.

The new key-secondary hypothesis is:

> In the same cohort and later calendar evaluation, strictly pre-admission assay content improves calibrated prediction of the time to first recorded examination evidence (a nonblank examination finding/diagnosis row) versus early discharge, no examination evidence observed by 72 hours, or unresolved timing beyond prior capture intensity and assay identity; the V-minus-I increment attenuates under result/value permutation.

This can inform a bounded operational question—whether an admission record is likely to acquire a reviewable examination artifact soon enough to prioritize chart review or coordination. It cannot establish that an examination was indicated, interpreted correctly, or useful to a patient. The cross-source table tests a further descriptive question: whether early examination evidence is recorded without same-encounter clinical-document/pathology payload, or vice versa. It does not test semantic disagreement because no source-level adjudication or timestamps are available.

## Secondary endpoint definition

For each eligible admission, create the mutually exclusive examination-evidence state:

- `E0_0_6`, `E0_6_12`, `E0_12_24`, `E0_24_48`, or `E0_48_72`: earliest qualifying examination evidence in that native-time bin, before discharge.
- `D_early`: no qualifying examination evidence and discharge at or before 72 hours.
- `N_exam_72`: no qualifying examination evidence by 72 hours and discharge after 72 hours.
- `U_exam_time`: missing/invalid examination times could change whether qualifying evidence occurs, its earliest order, or its bin.

Apply procedure/discharge tie and missing-time audit conventions from the primary without borrowing procedure semantics. A discharge at exactly 72 hours is `D_early` if no qualifying evidence occurs before the boundary; a qualifying examination exactly at 72 hours is outside the window. The 24-hour sensitivity collapses the first three bins, uses `D_early24`, `N_exam_24`, and `U_exam_time24), and is interpreted only if support passes.

The cross-source descriptor is computed after the timed examination state is frozen:

- `exam72`: any qualifying examination evidence before discharge in [t0,t0+72h).
- `doc_any`: any nonblank clinical_documents field for the encounter.
- `path_any`: any nonblank pathology field for the encounter.

Report counts and proportions for exam72/doc_any/path_any combinations, including exam72 without doc_any or path_any and doc/path payload without exam72. Do not infer which record was written first, and do not call these cells clinical disagreement. A blinded expert sample may assess whether a sample of examination and pathology text is clinically interpretable, but it is not required for the availability endpoint and cannot retrospectively supply absent timestamps.

The completed audit found 42,692 eligible first admissions; 39,066 had qualifying exam evidence in 72 hours, 35,136 within 24 hours, 35,411 had nonblank clinical_documents, and 25,113 had nonblank pathology payload. There were 22,240 admissions with all three availability flags, 4,366 with exam72 but neither document nor pathology payload, and 2,950 with document or pathology payload but no exam72. Among 175,003 qualifying examination rows, median start was 23.27 hours and the 90th percentile 51.49 hours after t0. These are feasibility/audit counts, not outcome conclusions. The full audit log is retained in supporting work.

## Predictors, baseline and substantive learned alternative

Use the parent's nested predictor sets without any new endpoint-derived predictor. A contains age, sex, admitting department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, missingness indicators and strict-history availability. L adds current [t0,t0+12h) assay-specific laboratory summaries. Q adds only prior capture intensity and availability/process counts, without names, assay identities, results, doses, routes, frequencies or statuses. I adds prior assay identity/occurrence and assay-specific count/recency/span/missingness, without payload. V adds assay-specific prior qualitative/quantitative results and within-assay dated changes/slopes. Outcome-window examinations, clinical-document fields, pathology fields, procedure/order names and future process rows are forbidden predictors.

Fit the transparent baseline as regularized multinomial logistic regression for both the inherited primary label and the new secondary label, separately for A/L/Q/I/V on identical patient-level random and calendar partitions. Tune imputation, penalties and calibration only on the selection partition; freeze them before locked evaluation. A discrete-time cause-specific hazard factorization is allowed only when it emits the same joint probabilities and is scored identically.

The substantive alternative is the parent's fixed low-dimensional continuous-time marked event encoder S with matched S_I and S_Q ablations. It consumes the same strict-prior event rows and current-lab rows, plus source/table type, elapsed time, assay identity only where allowed, payload mask and assay-specific payload, and has two output heads trained/evaluated against the fixed primary and secondary labels. The shared representation may expose nonlinear interactions, order-sensitive irregular histories and uncertainty in sparse/missing event sequences that first/last/count summaries lose. It is not justified by a small predictive gain: require seed stability, proper-score/calibration adequacy, the same primary V-minus-I direction, and a prespecified secondary improvement that survives result permutation and does not rely on unresolved labels.

The baseline is retained as the audit-facing reference because it makes the strict nesting, V-minus-I estimand, missingness handling and paired contrasts transparent. S is retained as a substantive alternative because the new endpoint is explicitly about time-to-evidence and irregular event ordering; if S only improves a small predictive metric while the V-minus-I scientific direction or calibration is unstable, it is not evidence for the hypothesis. The alternative is deferred if the future solver cannot fit it reproducibly or if the marked sequence adds no information beyond the transparent model.

## Evaluation, uncertainty and falsification

For the unchanged primary and the new secondary, report paired V-I, V-Q and I-Q differences in multinomial log loss and multiclass Brier score, calibration by state/time bin, and 24/48/72-hour cumulative probabilities where defined. Use patient-clustered paired bootstrap 95% intervals with at least 1,000 resamples; keep all encounters for one patient in the same partition and cluster. Report calendar-locked results first, random-split results as internal discrimination/feasibility context, and strata only after support gates. Control interpretation for the secondary as a key prespecified endpoint, not a replacement primary and not proof of clinical benefit.

Secondary-specific falsifications are:

1. Permute prior values/results within assay and fit strata while preserving assay identity/timing. A content signal should attenuate for both primary and evidence availability.
2. Compare Q/S_Q process-only predictors. If Q matches V, the apparent signal is capture intensity rather than assay content.
3. Permute history time/order while preserving payload. A time-to-evidence effect that persists unchanged is likely a generic capture artifact.
4. Use the negative-control [t0-730d,t0-30d) history window and anchor-linked pre-t0 rows as parent sensitivities.
5. Refit with 24-hour versus 72-hour evidence labels, procedure/discharge tie precedence, and explicit exclusion versus unresolved treatment of invalid examination times.
6. Repeat availability-discordance counts after restricting examination evidence to nonblank examination diagnosis alone and to nonblank examination findings alone; this tests dependence on one text field, not clinical validity.
7. Audit that no clinical_documents/pathology payload, examination row after t0, future order/procedure/medication row, discharge field or patient-overlap information enters predictors.

Supportive secondary evidence requires a later-era V-minus-I improvement in both secondary proper scores with a 95% interval excluding zero in the favorable direction, no material calibration harm, adequate state support, attenuation under result permutation, and consistency between the 24-hour sensitivity and 72-hour endpoint. Primary support must still meet the parent's unchanged gates. Adverse evidence includes no/worse V-minus-I, parity with Q, persistence after result permutation, time-order insensitivity, calibration failure, or dependence on one cutoff/text field. Inconclusive evidence includes more than 50% unresolved secondary labels, sparse bins, insufficient calendar support, wide intervals, nonconvergence, unstable timing audits, or failure to establish that the examination payload flag is reproducible.

Computation can establish source availability, endpoint construction, split safety, model predictions, scores, calibration, uncertainty, heterogeneity and falsification behavior. It cannot establish that examination findings are true, that a pathology field is confirmatory, that missing documentation represents a clinical gap, that a clinician needed the evidence, or that prioritizing review improves care. Those claims require expert adjudication with source provenance and timing, clinical outcome linkage, external validation, and prospective workflow/causal evaluation. The dataset lacks image files, pathology specimen/collection/report times, validated clinical-document timestamps, units/reference ranges for labs, stage/tumor burden, treatment intent/completion, mortality/recurrence and outside-hospital capture.

## Resource plan and retained alternatives

The future solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, up to 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds as recorded by the parent/input planning limits. The discovery audits were measured CPU jobs: scanning the six relevant HCC CSVs with 8 CPUs and 32,768 MiB took 27.24 seconds for key/time/payload audits and 23.39 seconds for cohort overlap/availability counts. These timings do not guarantee full-solver runtime. Aggregation, sparse multinomial fits, bootstrap and calibration should be CPU-first. S is a low-dimensional sequence model and may use one allocated A100 through the GPU job interface (inside the job, cuda:0 only); GPU feasibility is unverified for the full fit and is optional, not a scientific requirement. The solver must declare inputs/outputs, preserve checkpoints if used, and not treat readiness or a small probe as a solved result.

Retain the primary procedure-family layer only as an expert-adjudication-gated secondary because the procedures table has no validated ontology and coarse/missing timing. Retain the new evidence-availability endpoint because examination timing is actually present and support is substantial. Defer any timed clinical-document/pathology endpoint: their schemas explicitly report no temporal columns, so any claimed narrative order or pathology turnaround would be fabricated. Defer semantic NLP of examination/document/pathology text because the metadata records only an unvalidated bilingual lexical-context detector for cirrhosis and explicitly does not support general diagnosis extraction, coreference or comprehensive temporality. Defer treatment, response, survival and utility endpoints because the required outcomes and adjudication are unavailable. Defer a full multimodal Oncoformer reproduction because local images and the main article/STAR Methods are unavailable; a lab/EHR adaptation remains represented by V/I/Q and S/S_I/S_Q. Defer a Bayesian genetic adaptation because genetic inputs are absent. Revisit the deferred branches only with timestamped source data, expert labels, relevant clinical outcomes, external validation, or a scientifically demonstrated residual ordering signal. None of these deferrals is a blanket neural-network or GPU restriction.
