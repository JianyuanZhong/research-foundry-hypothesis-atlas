# Prediagnosis physiologic reserve and competing mortality after colorectal cancer

Status: planned Harbor experiment; no participant-level model fit or hypothesis result has been executed in this branch.

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (expert seed 20261002-ukb-36). This is a substantive evolution into an executable competing-risk estimand.

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

Low muscle strength, frailty, and sarcopenia are associated with adverse cancer outcomes, but the evidence is not yet the claim tested here. Zhao et al. found low handgrip strength and moderate frailty associated with postoperative complications and loss of independence in a small prospective CRC surgical cohort [K1]. An umbrella review found associations between sarcopenia and poorer survival across cancer-treatment cohorts, while rating most outcome certainty low or very low [K2]. A population-based CRC-survivor analysis found post-diagnosis pre-frailty/frailty associated with all-cause mortality, but its frailty measure could occur after diagnosis and it did not separate cancer from non-cancer death [K3].

The strongest supported claim is therefore: measures related to physiologic reserve are associated with adverse outcomes in selected CRC or cancer cohorts. The unresolved claim is more specific:

> Among adults with a registry-recorded incident colorectal cancer (CRC), does lower physiologic reserve measured at least two years before the first CRC diagnosis predict a larger five-year cumulative incidence of non-cancer death than cancer death after diagnosis?

This is not a test of a mechanism, treatment tolerance, or a referral threshold. The leading explanation is that low reserve marks vulnerability to competing non-cancer causes during survivorship. The strongest rival is ascertainment/reverse causation: occult cancer, diagnosis proximity, site/stage, or treatment selection may lower reserve and produce cancer death without a distinct non-cancer gradient. A secondary rival is generic age/comorbidity burden; adjustment and component-specific results can show whether the reserve signal is more than age, sex, BMI, smoking, diagnosis year, and CRC site, but cannot prove causality.

Clinical importance: if the event-specific contrast survives a long prediagnosis washout and is mainly non-cancer mortality, it would motivate a prospective survivorship study of rehabilitation, renal/cardiometabolic review, nutrition, and competing-risk care pathways. If it is limited to cancer death or to the near-diagnosis window, the appropriate conclusion is that reserve is a marker of cancer burden/selection, not evidence for a general survivorship intervention.

## Exposure, outcomes, and estimand

Construct one frozen reserve score without outcome-driven tuning:
- mean grip = mean of available 46-0.0 and 47-0.0; require both for the primary analysis, and use one-sided grip only in a sensitivity analysis;
- within sex and five-year age strata, standardize mean grip and albumin positively and creatinine and cystatin C negatively;
- reserve score = z(grip) + z(albumin) - z(creatinine) - z(cystatin C). Report all four component coefficients, not only the score. Higher score means greater measured reserve.
- Low and high profiles are the development-defined 25th and 75th percentile score, evaluated with covariates standardized to their observed primary-cohort distribution. The solver must preserve the quantile cut points from training data when evaluating temporal holdout.

Primary event contrast:
- Cancer death: first death after T1 with underlying cause C00-C97.
- Non-cancer death: first death after T1 with known underlying cause outside C00-C97.
- Censor at five years, administrative end, or unknown cause for cause-specific analyses.
- Estimate five-year cause-specific cumulative incidence functions using a cause-specific Cox model/Aalen-Johansen implementation. The primary estimand is:
  Delta = [CIF_non-cancer(low reserve) - CIF_non-cancer(high reserve)] - [CIF_cancer(low reserve) - CIF_cancer(high reserve)],
  standardized over the CRC cohort.
- Directional hypothesis: Delta > 0. The descriptive materiality boundary is 2 percentage points; report the estimate and 95% uncertainty interval rather than treating this boundary as a clinical referral threshold. Also report each component CIF and the all-cause contrast.

The estimand is associative and conditional on surviving to a registry CRC diagnosis. It does not estimate a causal effect of changing reserve, treatment tolerance, or rehabilitation.

## Analysis and method exploration

Interpretable baseline (primary scientific comparator):
- Cause-specific Cox models for cancer and non-cancer death with the reserve score and, in a prespecified expanded model, age, sex, BMI, smoking, diagnosis year, and CRC site (C18/C19/C20).
- Use restricted cubic splines for age and reserve only if locked before holdout; otherwise use linear terms and a separately labelled nonlinear sensitivity.
- Report cause-specific hazard ratios, five-year CIF profiles, absolute differences, bootstrap 95% intervals, calibration by event type, and Brier/log scores.

Substantive learned alternative on exactly the same inputs:
- CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time competing-risk models, one event-specific hazard per yearly interval 0-5 years, using raw grip, albumin, creatinine, cystatin C, age, sex, BMI, smoking, diagnosis year, and site. No extra variables or diagnosis-proximal leakage.
- Train on CRC diagnosis years 2007-2016 and lock a temporal holdout on 2017-2022 (the solver must report actual year counts and revise the split only before fitting if a cell is empty). Fit/calibrate using training data only; use isotonic or Platt calibration only within training cross-validation.
- Compare the learned model against the same-input interpretable model using event-specific five-year calibration slope/intercept, integrated Brier score, cause-specific log score, and absolute CIF error. Bootstrap the held-out predictions if event support permits.
- Scientific value of the alternative is not a small AUC gain: it can reveal thresholding and reserve-by-age/CRC-site interactions that a linear score loses, or show that the apparent event-specific contrast is adequately captured by a transparent score. A learned model that improves discrimination without calibration or without a stable Delta does not support a stronger clinical claim.
- Resource estimate: CPU tabular fitting, 16 CPUs, up to 64 GiB RAM, expected under 2 hours for discovery-sized fitting and under the 8-hour solver-planning envelope; measured feasibility is limited to source reads, not model fitting. GPUs are unnecessary for this cohort-sized tabular comparison; GPU capacity exists but was not used as a merit criterion.

Unchosen alternatives and revisit evidence:
- A longitudinal latent/transformer model was deferred: this design has one prediagnosis reserve landmark per primary participant, no validated dense prediagnosis sequence, and no need to infer latent disease trajectories. Revisit only if repeated reserve measures are verified and the scientific question changes to within-person trajectory.
- A high-dimensional neural model was deferred because any added predictive value would not resolve the primary cancer-versus-non-cancer interpretation unless it improves calibrated event-specific contrasts on the locked holdout. Revisit if the learned model reveals stable, clinically interpretable interactions requiring external validation.
- A causal treatment-effect analysis is not chosen: treatment, stage, recurrence, pathology, symptoms, indication, and treatment dates are unavailable. Revisit requires linked oncology treatment and stage data.

## Falsification, uncertainty, and interpretation

Prespecified threats:
1. Diagnosis proximity/reverse causation: repeat the estimand at 0-2, 2-5, and >5 years from T0 to T1. A signal confined to 0-2 years is adverse to a durable prediagnosis-reserve interpretation.
2. Cancer-burden/site selection: report CRC site strata, restrict to first malignant registry event, and compare cancer-death and non-cancer-death contrasts. A reserve gradient only for cancer death weakens the proposed competing-mortality claim.
3. Cause ascertainment: repeat with all-cause death and with known-cause deaths only; report missing-cause counts and cause-code distributions. A result that reverses across cause definitions is inconclusive.
4. Generic frailty/measurement: adjust age, sex, BMI, smoking, diagnosis year and site; report each reserve component. If the score signal disappears while one component drives all results, narrow the claim to that component.
5. Selection: compare complete-reserve participants with the broader CRC-eligible cohort on age, sex, diagnosis lag and site; use inverse-probability-of-completeness weights only as a sensitivity analysis with overlap diagnostics.
6. Negative controls: permute reserve scores within sex-age-diagnosis-year strata and repeat the full analysis; additionally report external-cause mortality (V01-Y89) if event support is adequate. A comparable signal after permutation is a computation/label failure.
7. Temporal holdout: the direction and calibration must be evaluated in the locked later diagnosis years. Poor holdout event support or wide intervals is inconclusive, not adverse.

Supportive result: in the ≥2-year cohort, Delta is positive with its interval excluding 0, the non-cancer CIF contrast is directionally stable across 2-5 and >5 years, component definitions and known-cause sensitivity agree, and calibration is acceptable on the temporal holdout. This supports a reproducible observational event-specific reserve association and justifies an indication-rich external survivorship study; it does not establish mechanism, causality, treatment tolerance, or clinical utility.

Adverse result: Delta is near zero or negative, the signal is cancer-death-only, disappears after the washout, reverses by CRC site, fails cause-code or permutation checks, or is not calibrated in holdout. This argues against the proposed durable non-cancer competing-risk interpretation and favors cancer proximity, ascertainment, or generic selection.

Inconclusive result: insufficient two-year-lag cases/deaths, empty temporal cells, poor positivity, missing/ambiguous causes, unstable component definitions, or intervals spanning clinically meaningful values. Do not call this a null finding.

Claims requiring additional evidence: stage, recurrence, treatment, pathology, cancer onset validity, symptoms and test indication, healthcare utilization, cause-of-death adjudication, race/ethnicity transportability, and whether a reserve intervention changes outcomes all require linkage, expert adjudication, external validation, or a prospective decision study.
