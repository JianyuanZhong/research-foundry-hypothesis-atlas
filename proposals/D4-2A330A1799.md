# Longitudinal HbA1c deterioration and incident cardiorenal-metabolic multimorbidity

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`  
Seed: `[starting question]` (“Metabolic deterioration and multimorbidity”)  
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`  
Status: prospective observational hypothesis; no fitted result is claimed.

## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest available evidence supports a narrower claim: baseline metabolic-risk indices are associated with incident and progressive cardiometabolic multimorbidity in UK Biobank. A recent public UKB study (Xue et al., 2026; frozen source `[source checksum]`, DOI `10.1186/s12933-026-03255-4`) studied static atherogenic indices in 346,868 participants and defined multimorbidity as at least two of type 2 diabetes, coronary heart disease, and stroke. It did not establish that deterioration between two measurements adds information beyond the current level, nor did it answer whether a repeat metabolic assessment identifies a clinically meaningful transition toward combined diabetes, kidney disease, and heart failure.

The unresolved clinical decision is whether a person with a currently measured HbA1c should be considered at materially different near-term risk because that value rose from a prior measurement, after accounting for the current value and ordinary clinical covariates. If true, a repeat HbA1c trajectory could help prioritize prevention and kidney/cardiovascular surveillance. This study cannot show that acting on the trajectory improves outcomes.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UK Biobank participants with a valid baseline and strictly later repeat assessment, no prior inpatient-coded type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease, or heart failure by the repeat date, and valid HbA1c measurements at both instances, a larger repeat-minus-baseline HbA1c increase is associated with a higher 5-year risk of developing at least two of those three conditions, conditional on the repeat HbA1c level and baseline clinical covariates.

The primary estimand is the standardized 5-year absolute-risk contrast comparing the 75th versus 25th percentile of HbA1c change while holding the repeat HbA1c distribution fixed and adjusting for the prespecified baseline covariates. The corresponding null is a zero conditional change coefficient/contrast. “Beyond current levels” means that the change term remains informative in a model already containing the repeat HbA1c; it is not a claim that HbA1c change causes multimorbidity.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

Combined diabetes, CKD, and heart failure create competing medication, renal-dosing, prevention, and follow-up decisions. Current-level risk stratification can miss whether a seemingly moderate value represents stability or deterioration. A valid positive result would quantify whether trajectory information identifies a group for whom confirmatory measurement, risk-factor review, or closer surveillance is reasonable.

The advance over existing evidence is a prespecified temporal comparison of baseline level, current level, and change in the same repeat-observed cohort. It tests a dynamic, clinically interpretable incremental claim rather than another cross-sectional association or an unconstrained prediction benchmark. It also treats multimorbidity as an ordered event process: a participant must have two distinct component disease families, not merely one diagnosis or a composite of unrelated codes.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use one row per participant, joined horizontally by `eid`; never append the UKB tables as additional samples.

- Baseline assessment is instance 0, with time zero `53-0.0`.
- Require nonmissing baseline age, sex, assessment date, HbA1c, repeat assessment date, repeat HbA1c, and the primary covariates.
- Require `53-1.0 > 53-0.0`. Report the interval distribution; the primary analysis uses all strictly positive intervals that pass field/date validation. A prespecified 1–6-year interval restriction is a sensitivity analysis, not a post hoc selection.
- Map HbA1c instance 0 to `53-0.0` and instance 1 to `53-1.0`. Because the local biological-samples table contains field instances but no independent specimen-date column, this instance-to-assessment-date mapping must be verified from approved UKB metadata before inference. Failure is an inconclusive audit, not permission to assume dates.
- At the repeat date, exclude anyone with an aligned inpatient code for type 2 diabetes, CKD, or heart failure on or before the repeat date, and exclude anyone who died before or on that date. The baseline-to-repeat interval is not follow-up in the primary landmark analysis; its diagnoses and deaths are reported in the selection audit.
- Follow from the repeat date until the second qualifying component disease, death, last available outcome date, or 2023-03-31, whichever comes first. The primary horizon is 5 years. A 365-day post-repeat washout is a sensitivity estimand for reverse causation.
- Participants with one component diagnosis appearing after the repeat date remain at risk for the second-component endpoint. A same-day occurrence of two distinct component families counts as an event on that date.

The target population is therefore explicitly conditional on surviving, remaining free of the component diagnoses, and returning for a valid paired repeat measurement. It is not the causal effect of offering repeat HbA1c testing to the entire UKB cohort.

## Exposure and outcomes

After field/unit verification:

- Convert HbA1c only as required by its verified unit, preserving the original values and conversion provenance.
- Primary exposure is repeat HbA1c minus baseline HbA1c, continuous per within-cohort standard deviation; report an interpretable interquartile-range contrast. For display, also use fixed quartiles of change defined once in the eligible cohort, with no outcome-informed cut point.
- The primary model includes repeat HbA1c and the change term. This is algebraically equivalent to including repeat and baseline HbA1c, but the change parameter makes the estimand explicit. Baseline HbA1c alone and repeat HbA1c alone are prespecified comparators.
- Baseline covariates are age (restricted cubic spline), sex, BMI, systolic BP, diastolic BP, and baseline eGFRcr/eGFRcys if their field units can be verified using the same approved metadata gate. Baseline grip is a secondary reserve covariate, not a muscle-mass measurement. Do not use repeat values of covariates measured after the landmark.
- Component disease families are defined from aligned inpatient ICD-10 code/date pairs: type 2 diabetes `E11–E14`, CKD `N18.x`, and heart failure `I50.x`. The primary endpoint date is the later of the earliest post-landmark dates for any two distinct families. Report the first-family and second-family identities.
- Secondary outcomes are each component disease, the first component, the expanded cardiometabolic endpoint adding coronary heart disease `I20–I25` and stroke `I60–I69`, all-cause death, and a composite of any component. These are secondary and cannot replace an infeasible primary multimorbidity analysis.
- A fixed temporal/leakage falsification uses a 365-day post-repeat washout. A fixed negative-control diagnosis is acute appendicitis (`K35.x`), chosen because no direct metabolic pathway is posited; sparse negative-control events are reported as a limitation rather than silently replaced.

## Analysis

First publish a flow and selection audit: source row counts; one-to-one `eid` checks and overlap agreement; baseline validity; repeat date ordering; paired HbA1c availability; exclusions for pre-repeat component diagnoses/death; disease-component cells; follow-up time; events and deaths. Compare eligible repeat-observed participants with eligible baseline participants lacking a valid repeat on every baseline covariate.

The primary analysis is a cause-specific Cox model for the first second-component event, with death as a competing event. The primary contrast is standardized 5-year cumulative incidence for the 75th versus 25th percentile of HbA1c change, holding repeat HbA1c and covariates as observed; report absolute risk difference, risk ratio, 95% bootstrap uncertainty intervals, cause-specific hazard ratio, and cumulative-incidence curves. Use Fine–Gray as a sensitivity analysis, not as a substitute for the primary estimand.

Fit the following nested specifications in the identical landmark cohort:

1. baseline HbA1c plus baseline covariates;
2. repeat HbA1c plus baseline covariates;
3. repeat HbA1c plus HbA1c change plus baseline covariates (primary incremental model).

Use participant-level bootstrap with a fixed seed for standardized contrasts. Repeat complete-case and multiple-imputation analyses only with imputation inside resampling/training partitions. Estimate inverse-probability-of-repeat weights from baseline variables, prespecify truncation before fitting, and report positivity/weight diagnostics; weighting does not remove unmeasured selection bias. Assess proportional-hazards assumptions and use a multistate descriptive analysis for the first-to-second component transition.

A bounded elastic-net Cox model is optional and secondary: fixed participant-hash 70/15/15 split, all preprocessing and tuning inside training folds, and identical baseline/current/change feature sets. Report calibration and Brier score; discrimination or net-benefit changes are descriptive and cannot establish the clinical value of repeat testing. No GPU is needed.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Support for the primary hypothesis requires all of the following:

- verified units, instance/date mapping, and code/date alignment;
- at least 100 primary second-component events overall and at least 10 events in each displayed change quartile;
- a positive, clinically material standardized 5-year risk difference for higher HbA1c change, with a 95% interval excluding zero and the change term improving on the repeat-only model in the prespecified direction;
- broadly consistent direction in the 365-day washout, 1–6-year interval restriction, competing-risk, and repeat-weighted analyses; and
- no equally large or larger positive association for the fixed appendicitis negative control, subject to reporting its event count.

Adverse evidence is a protective/opposite change contrast, a null contrast with adequate events and narrow uncertainty, disappearance after washout or repeat weighting, or an association as large or larger for appendicitis. A strong current-level association with no conditional change signal means current HbA1c matters but deterioration adds no demonstrated information; it does not support the dynamic claim.

Inconclusive evidence is failed unit/date/coding verification; fewer than 100 primary events or fewer than 10 events in a change quartile; unstable or nonpositive repeat weights; substantial temporal-ordering failure; a missing primary component field; or uncertainty wide enough to include both a clinically material increase and a trivial/no increase. CKD, death, individual disease, calibration, AUC, or a machine-learning improvement cannot substitute for an infeasible primary endpoint.

Computation can establish field availability, row/filter counts, date ordering, code-based inpatient component events, conditional associations, competing-risk estimates, uncertainty, and whether these gates are met. It cannot establish adjudicated diabetes/CKD/heart failure, outpatient disease, disease stage or severity, laboratory trajectories between the two measurements, causal effects, representativeness, or that clinicians should change management. Those claims require validated field dictionaries, outpatient laboratory data, adjudicated outcomes, medication/treatment data, external validation, expert review, and a prospective testing-and-management study.
