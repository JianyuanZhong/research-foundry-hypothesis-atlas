# Prompt FiO2 de-escalation after arterial hyperoxemia during invasive ventilation: a multicenter EICU landmark target-trial emulation

## One-sentence question and hypothesis

Among invasively ventilated adults with a first actionable arterial hyperoxemia event (PaO2 >=150 mm Hg while FiO2 >=0.40), does reducing FiO2 by at least 0.10 within 2 hours, compared with maintaining FiO2 within 0.05 of its index value, reduce subsequent hyperoxemic saturation burden without a clinically important increase in severe hypoxemia?

The falsifiable primary joint hypothesis is that prompt de-escalation (i) lowers the adjusted proportion of valid SpO2 observations >96% during hours 2-6 after the index PaO2 by at least 5 percentage points and (ii) is noninferior for the proportion <88%, with a prespecified upper 95% confidence limit below +1 percentage point. Both conditions must hold. This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion supported by the current EICU counts.

## Why the unresolved question matters, and the advance

Oxygen is lifesaving but is often left at unnecessarily high concentrations after oxygenation improves. Existing randomized trials primarily compare assigned oxygenation targets over a prolonged ICU course, not the concrete bedside response to a new arterial hyperoxemia result in a patient receiving a modifiable FiO2. ICU-ROX (1,000 ventilated adults) found no difference in ventilator-free days or 180-day mortality with conservative versus usual oxygen (DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1903297). HOT-ICU (2,928 adults with acute hypoxemic respiratory failure) found no 90-day mortality benefit from a PaO2 target of 60 versus 90 mm Hg (DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2032510). PILOT (2,541 ventilated adults) found no difference in ventilator-free days across lower, intermediate, and higher SpO2 targets (DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2208415). A 2022 meta-analysis of eight trials found no overall mortality difference (OR 0.95, 95% CI 0.74-1.22); its apparent benefit for normoxemia versus hyperoxemia was very-low-certainty because target definitions and methods were heterogeneous (Cumpstey et al., DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000652; acquired full-text source ID `[source checksum]`). A 2025 prospective single-center observational study found hyperoxemia in 1,669/4,631 observations but oxygen was decreased in only 398; clinicians were less likely to decrease oxygen at lower FiO2 (Chandrakar et al., DOI 10.4103/joacp.joacp_199_24; acquired full text).

The strongest supported claim is therefore that excessive oxygen exposure is common, bedside titration is inconsistent, and broad lower-versus-higher oxygen targets have not shown an overall outcome advantage. The unresolved claim is whether a prompt, event-triggered reduction after confirmed arterial hyperoxemia improves the near-term oxygenation profile safely. Resolving it could support an ABG-triggered respiratory-therapy protocol or show that a simple 0.10 reduction creates unacceptable hypoxemia. The substantive advance is to study a reproducible treatment decision at a defined trigger and quantify both benefit and immediate safety across many hospitals, rather than associating average oxygen exposure with mortality.

## Target trial-like protocol

### Population and temporal boundaries

Unit of analysis is the first eligible event in the first ICU stay (`unitvisitnumber=1`) of each `patienthealthsystemstayid`; retain only one hospitalization per `uniquepid` (earliest represented stay) to avoid correlated repeated participation.

Include:

1. age >=18 (map the EICU value `> 89` to 90 for eligibility only), known sex, ICU stay lasting at least 8 hours;
2. index candidate between ICU minute +120 and min(+2,880, `unitdischargeoffset-360`);
3. first `lab.labname='paO2'` with numeric `labresult` 150-600 mm Hg;
4. invasive ventilation at the event, requiring BOTH:
   - an explicit `respiratoryCare.airwaytype` in {Oral ETT, Nasal ETT, Tracheostomy, Cricothyrotomy, Double-Lumen Tube}, supported by a row/status or ventilation interval covering the event (treat a zero/blank end as “not documented ended,” not as proof by itself); and
   - a valid PEEP >0 or an active ventilator-mode/RT-vent-on record in `respiratoryCharting` within +/-120 minutes;
5. an index FiO2: last valid respiratory-charted FiO2 at or before the PaO2 and no more than 60 minutes earlier, between 0.40 and 1.00;
6. at least one post-index FiO2 measurement by +120 minutes.

Exclude:

- any SpO2 <88% in the 15 minutes immediately before the event (not a safe de-escalation decision);
- explicit index evidence of ECMO, hyperbaric oxygen/carbon monoxide poisoning, or a planned peri-procedural/preoxygenation episode;
- admission diagnosis indicating cardiac arrest, acute traumatic brain injury, or acute stroke, because their oxygen-response estimand is clinically distinct and residual indication is especially consequential;
- conflicting same-minute FiO2 records differing by >0.05, implausible FiO2 after normalization, PaO2 outside 20-600, or inability to establish an invasive airway.

The exclusion text rules must be frozen before outcome analysis and reported with counts and matched terms. Because these conditions may be incompletely documented, absence of a code is not clinical adjudication.

### Time zero, assignment window, and follow-up

Clinical trigger `t0` is `labresultoffset` of the first qualifying PaO2. EICU does not identify specimen collection, analyzer completion, clinician review, or result-display time; thus this is “recorded ABG time,” not proven treatment-decision time. Main exposure is classified at a fixed landmark `L=t0+120 minutes`. Patients must be alive, still in the ICU, explicitly invasively ventilated, and have a valid FiO2 at L. Primary physiologic follow-up is (L, L+240 minutes], i.e. hours 2-6 after t0. This landmark avoids immortal-time attribution but changes the target population to two-hour survivors who remain ventilated. It cannot estimate effects on events during the first two hours.

Sensitivity analyses use 60- and 180-minute landmarks; use `labresultrevisedoffset` as a conservative result-availability proxy; and use clone-censor-weighting over the 2-hour grace period for secondary hospital mortality (clone to each strategy at t0, censor for deviation, and weight artificial censoring).

### Strategies

Normalize FiO2 as follows: robust RFC-4180 CSV parsing; trim strings; numeric 0.21-1.00 is a fraction; numeric 21-100 is divided by 100; all other values are invalid. Accept labels exactly `FiO2`, `FIO2 (%)`, and `Set Fraction of Inspired Oxygen (FIO2)`. At duplicate stay-offset-label records, use the median only when range <=0.05; otherwise mark conflict.

- Prompt de-escalation: by L, a valid FiO2 is at least 0.10 below index, with no later pre-L valid setting >0.05 above index. The qualifying decrease must be confirmed by a second value 15-90 minutes later at or below index-0.08, unless the patient is extubated after the first decrease.
- Maintenance: every valid pre-L FiO2 remains within +/-0.05 of index and none is >=0.10 below index.
- Values between these regimes are nonadherent to both and excluded from the main per-protocol contrast; counts and characteristics are reported. A secondary dose-response analysis uses achieved change as a continuous exposure but is not allowed to replace the primary contrast.

A rescue FiO2 rise after documented SpO2 <88% is an outcome/consequence and is not reclassified as baseline confounding.

### Estimand

Primary estimands are hospital-standardized adjusted mean differences (de-escalation minus maintenance), among patients eligible at t0 who are observed alive and invasively ventilated at L, in:

1. proportion of unique 5-minute offsets with valid `vitalPeriodic.sao2` >96% during L to L+240 (superiority);
2. proportion with `sao2` <88% in the same window (noninferiority margin +0.01).

At duplicate offsets use the median; values outside 50-100 are invalid. Require >=12 unique observations spanning >=180 minutes; report exclusion and observation intensity by arm. This is a per-protocol, landmark, average treatment effect in the overlap population—not an intention-to-treat effect and not the effect of a general conservative oxygen policy.

Secondary outcomes are time-weighted SpO2 88-96%; any >=10 consecutive minutes <88%; minimum SpO2; FiO2 change and repeat PaO2 during 2-6 hours (treatment fidelity); extubation by 24 hours; ICU and hospital mortality; and hospital length of stay among survivors. Mortality and length of stay are exploratory because the acute exposure may not remain separated and EICU lacks post-discharge mortality.

## Baseline variables and confounding control

All baseline longitudinal values are restricted to offsets <=t0; nothing first documented after t0 enters a baseline model.

- `patient`: age, gender, ethnicity, admission weight/height, `hospitalid`, `wardid`, ICU type, hospital/unit source, stay type, minutes since unit and hospital admission, and `apacheadmissiondx`.
- `lab`: index PaO2 and most recent/trajectory over prior 6 hours for pH, paCO2, bicarbonate, lactate, hemoglobin, creatinine, bilirubin, platelets, and WBC, using exact observed `labname` values frozen in a data dictionary.
- `respiratoryCharting`: index FiO2; PEEP; respiratory rate; tidal volume; peak/plateau pressure; ventilator mode; prior 6-hour FiO2 trajectory and charting intensity.
- `vitalPeriodic`: pre-index 2-hour SpO2 distribution (including >96 and <88 burden), heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature and invasive blood pressures.
- `vitalAperiodic`: most recent noninvasive mean/systolic pressure.
- `infusionDrug`: active norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine and sedative infusions inferred only from rows at/before t0; record the frozen name map and do not infer an end time beyond the next chart without validation.
- `pastHistory`, `admissionDx`, and `diagnosis`: COPD/home oxygen, heart failure, chronic lung disease, sepsis, ARDS, surgical status, and exclusion diagnoses, using only entries with entry/diagnosis offsets <=t0. Admission diagnosis fields without an event time are baseline descriptors; potentially post-index APACHE physiology (`apacheApsVar`, `apachePatientResult`) is excluded from the main adjustment set and used only for sensitivity/description.

The causal diagram must include baseline respiratory severity -> titration and later SpO2; hemodynamic instability -> titration and outcome; clinician/hospital practice -> both; and charting intensity -> measured exposure/outcome. Do not adjust for post-t0 PEEP, vasopressor changes, or saturation because they may mediate or prompt rescue.

Hospitals with <20 eligible events or <5 patients in either strategy are excluded from the primary within-hospital estimand and described separately. Estimate propensity with prespecified restricted splines/interactions for index PaO2, FiO2, PEEP, baseline SpO2 burden, ventilation duration, shock, and time since ICU admission plus hospital fixed effects. Use overlap weights, report arm-specific distributions, standardized mean differences (target <0.10), propensity overlap, truncation, and effective sample size. Estimate weighted outcome regressions and augmented inverse-probability-weighted marginal means. Obtain 95% CIs by resampling hospitals (>=1,000 bootstrap replicates). Multiple imputation is permitted for covariates only, within bootstrap samples; exposure, outcome, airway, PaO2 and FiO2 are never imputed.

Prespecified effect modification (interaction estimates with multiplicity-aware intervals, not subgroup proof): baseline FiO2 0.40-0.59 vs >=0.60; PaO2 150-199 vs >=200; shock/no shock; COPD/no COPD. The main conclusion does not depend on subgroup significance.

## Falsification, diagnostics, and interpretation

### Required feasibility gates before effect estimation

- Publish the full attrition table, exact text mappings, invalid-value counts, same-time conflicts, hospital/arm counts and outcome completeness.
- At least 500 patients per strategy, 20 contributing hospitals, overlap-weight ESS >=50% of raw N, no key covariate SMD >0.10, and >=80% primary outcome availability in each arm. Failure makes the causal analysis infeasible/inconclusive; thresholds may not be relaxed after seeing outcomes.
- Manually adjudicate a random 100-event sample using available respiratory/nursing notes and nearby chart rows for true invasive ventilation, whether t0 plausibly preceded the FiO2 action, and special oxygen indications. Precision must be reported. This review cannot reveal actual bedside intent if it was never documented.

### Negative controls and robustness

1. Re-run the entire model using the pre-index SpO2 >96% burden from -240 to -60 minutes as a negative-control outcome. A material adjusted arm difference (absolute >5 points or P<0.05) indicates residual confounding/model failure.
2. Temperature change during hours 2-6 is a second negative-control outcome; association is a warning, not proof of invalidity.
3. Permuting strategy within hospital and index-FiO2 decile must yield nominal null behavior.
4. Results must be directionally stable under PaO2 thresholds 120 and 200; outcome upper saturation thresholds 96 and 98; requiring explicit respiratoryCare interval coverage; excluding tracheostomies; complete-case analysis; alternative FiO2 confirmation; hospital leave-one-out; and 60/180-minute landmarks.
5. Quantify informative monitoring: compare number/span of SpO2 observations and use inverse-probability-of-observation weighting as sensitivity. Best/worst bounds are required for missing outcomes.
6. A positive manipulation check must show materially lower achieved FiO2 after L. Failure means the strategy contrast was not delivered, regardless of outcome.

### Decision rules

Supportive: both primary gates pass—hyperoxemia burden difference <=-0.05 with its 95% CI excluding 0, and upper 95% CI for severe-hypoxemia difference <+0.01—with acceptable balance, negative controls, fidelity, and robust direction. The justified conclusion is only that prompt de-escalation is associated with a better immediate saturation profile without detectable excess severe hypoxemia in the specified EICU overlap population.

Adverse: the upper confidence bound is not the criterion; actual evidence of harm is a lower confidence bound >=+0.01 for severe-hypoxemia burden, a clear increase in sustained hypoxemia, or concordant acute rescue signals. Conclude that this fixed reduction rule may be unsafe for this population; do not generalize to all conservative oxygen protocols.

Inconclusive: either CI crosses its decision margin, feasibility/balance/negative-control/fidelity gates fail, missingness bounds reverse the conclusion, or hospital results are materially heterogeneous. A lower hyperoxemia burden alone does not support clinical adoption.

A null or adverse result falsifies the joint hypothesis and remains clinically valuable. No result from this analysis establishes oxygen toxicity, mortality benefit, optimal SpO2 target, or that clinicians should implement a protocol. Those stronger claims require prospective protocolized randomization, validated timestamps, adjudication of oxygen indications and ventilator status, patient-important follow-up, and critical-care expert review.

## Computationally checkable versus clinically unresolved

Automatically checkable: source hashes/headers, joins, filter counts, timestamp arithmetic, value normalization, exposure assignment, outcome construction, balance/positivity, fitted estimates/CIs, negative controls, and whether a submitted conclusion obeys the decision rules.

Requires clinician adjudication or unavailable evidence: whether the recorded PaO2 was arterial and visible when FiO2 changed; actual invasive-ventilation interval when fields conflict; reason for oxygen, planned procedures, rescue intent, waveform/artifact validity, undocumented neurologic indications, and clinical meaningfulness of margins. Causal effects, protocol safety, and practice adoption require another study, not an automatic verifier.
