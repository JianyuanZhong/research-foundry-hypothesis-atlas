# Scientific Proposal: Ventilatory Burden Predicts Liberation Failure in Mechanically Ventilated eICU Patients

## 1. Refined Hypothesis Statement

**Primary Hypothesis:** Ventilatory burden, defined as minute ventilation (VE) normalized for predicted body weight (PBW) and adjusted for respiratory mechanics (specifically, VE divided by static lung compliance or plateau pressure), provides incremental predictive value beyond standard lung-protective parameters (tidal volume [TV], plateau pressure [Pplat]) for predicting liberation from mechanical ventilation in critically ill patients.

**Formal Statement:** In mechanically ventilated critically ill patients, the ventilatory burden index (VBI = VE / PBW / compliance or VBI = VE / PBW / Pplat) is an independent predictor of liberation failure (failure to wean within 14 days or death before weaning attempt), beyond the predictive contribution of tidal volume, plateau pressure, SAPS III, age, and gender. This association persists after adjustment for confounding variables and demonstrates superior discrimination (C-index) compared to models containing only lung-protective parameters.

**Resolvable Question:** Does respiratory mechanics-adjusted ventilatory burden capture pathophysiological information about respiratory system failure that is orthogonal to lung-protective ventilator settings, thereby improving risk stratification for weaning failure?

## 2. Clinical Importance and Unresolved Question

**Clinical Importance:**
- Liberation failure from mechanical ventilation is associated with increased mortality, prolonged ICU stay, and higher healthcare costs
- Current lung-protective ventilation strategies (low tidal volume 6 mL/kg PBW, Pplat <30 cmH2O) reduce ventilator-induced lung injury but do not optimize weaning outcomes
- There is no validated bedside index that incorporates both ventilatory demand and respiratory mechanics to predict weaning failure
- Early identification of patients at risk of weaning failure could enable proactive interventions (e.g., prolonged ICU stay planning, early extubation trials, respiratory support escalation)

**Unresolved Question:**
While lung-protective ventilation parameters (TV, Pplat) are established lung-protective strategies, they do not directly quantify the patient's respiratory work of breathing or the mismatch between ventilatory demand and respiratory system mechanics. The ventilatory burden index (VBI) captures this mismatch by normalizing minute ventilation for PBW and respiratory mechanics, potentially identifying patients with high metabolic demand or poor respiratory mechanics who may fail weaning despite "appropriate" lung-protective settings.

**Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge:**
- **Beyond TV/Pplat:** Current weaning prediction models rely primarily on ventilator settings or simplified physiologic measures (SAPS III). This study tests whether an index incorporating both ventilatory demand (VE) and respiratory mechanics (compliance/Pplat) provides orthogonal information.
- **Mechanistic Plausibility:** The VBI captures the relationship between ventilatory demand and respiratory system properties, which is central to weaning physiology. High VBI may indicate patients with high metabolic demand, poor respiratory muscle strength, or stiff lungs requiring excessive work to ventilate.
- **Incremental Value:** The study specifically tests whether VBI adds predictive value beyond established predictors, addressing the "incremental utility" question that is often overlooked in prediction modeling.

### 3.3 Outcome Definition

**Liberation Success:** Patient successfully weaned from mechanical ventilation (extubated or transitioned to non-invasive ventilation)
- **Time to liberation:** Days from ventstartoffset to liberation event
- **Liberation failure:** Death before liberation attempt OR liberation attempt without success within 14 days

**Censoring:**
- Censored at hospital discharge if not liberated
- Censored at study end date if still ventilated

## 4. Baseline Method: Cox Proportional Hazards

- **Predictors:** TV (at weaning decision), Pplat (at weaning decision), SAPS III (admission), age (admission), gender (admission)

## 5. Alternative Method: Gradient Boosted Trees

### 5.1 Model Specification

**GBM Model:** Gradient boosted decision trees with:
- **Predictors:** All available covariates including VBI
  - Demographics: age, gender, height, weight
  - Admission severity: SAPS III
  - Ventilator settings: TV, Pplat, PEEP, FiO2, respiratory rate
  - Derived: VBI, compliance, PaO2/FiO2, etCO2
  - Time-varying: daily VBI, daily TV, daily Pplat
- **Outcome:** Time to liberation (survival analysis with time-to-event)
- **Loss function:** Log-loss with censoring support
- **Hyperparameters:** n_estimators=100, max_depth=5, learning_rate=0.1

### 5.2 Why GBM Over Cox?

1. **Non-linearity:** VBI may have non-linear relationship with liberation failure
2. **Interaction terms:** GBM can capture interactions between VBI and other predictors (e.g., VBI × SAPS III)
3. **Time-varying effects:** Can incorporate daily VBI trajectories
4. **Robustness:** Less sensitive to outliers in continuous variables

### 5.3 Comparison Rationale

The GBM serves as a "black box" alternative to test whether VBI provides incremental value beyond linear assumptions. If GBM with VBI outperforms GBM without VBI by ≥0.01 C-index, this supports the independent utility of VBI beyond what linear models can capture.

## 6. Falsification Criteria

### 6.1 Primary Falsification

The hypothesis is **falsified** if ALL of the following are true:
1. **No discrimination improvement:** ΔC-index < 0.01 when adding VBI to baseline model
2. **No independent association:** VBI p-value > 0.05 in multivariable Cox model (after adjusting for TV, Pplat, SAPS III, age, gender)
3. **No GBM improvement:** GBM with VBI performs no better than GBM without VBI (ΔC-index < 0.01)

### 6.2 Secondary Falsification

Hypothesis is **falsified** if:
- VBI shows negative association with liberation (higher VBI associated with faster liberation, contrary to physiological expectation)
- VBI association disappears after adjustment for all confounders (suggests spurious relationship)

### 6.3 Acceptance Criteria

Hypothesis is **supported** if:
1. ΔC-index ≥ 0.01 with VBI addition
2. VBI p-value < 0.05 in multivariable model
3. GBM with VBI outperforms GBM without VBI by ≥ 0.01 C-index
4. Association direction is physiologically plausible (higher VBI → slower liberation)

## 10. Uncertainty and Limitations

### 10.1 Computationally Checkable Claims

1. **C-index improvement:** Can be directly computed from model predictions
2. **VBI association p-value:** Can be computed from Cox model coefficients
3. **GBM performance difference:** Can be computed from cross-validated C-indexes

### 10.2 Claims Requiring Clinical Adjudication

1. **Physiological plausibility:** Whether higher VBI truly indicates weaning risk requires expert review
2. **Clinical utility:** Whether VBI would change clinical management decisions
3. **Causality:** Whether VBI is a cause of weaning failure or merely a marker

### 10.3 Missing Evidence

1. **Ground truth for VBI:** No external validation of VBI as a weaning predictor exists
2. **Standardized weaning definition:** Different institutions may define "liberation" differently
3. **Respiratory mechanics measurement:** Compliance may not be consistently measured in all patients
