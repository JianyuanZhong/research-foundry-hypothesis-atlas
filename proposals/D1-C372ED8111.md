# Is the early hypotension–creatinine signal biological, competing-risk dependent, or testing-dependent?

## Scientific deliverable and unresolved claim

The new deliverable is an uncertainty-quantified, hospital-transportable decomposition of an early ICU hypotension signal into:

1. minimum MAP and cumulative low-MAP burden;
2. a clinically interpretable trajectory (persistent/recurrent low MAP, recovery, instability, and early vasopressor context);
3. the competing processes that end ICU observation (unit death versus alive discharge/transfer); and
4. the opportunity to observe a creatinine result.

The future solver must emit a frozen cohort-flow and value/unit audit, a stay-interval analysis table, fitted nested competing-state models, an observation-process model, standardized 48-hour contrasts with uncertainty, held-out-hospital transport results, and the prespecified falsification analyses. Discovery has not fitted these models and makes no clinical claim.

The strongest evidence currently supported is only data availability: the frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative MAP, laboratory, disposition, infusion, admission-severity, patient, and hospital fields joinable on the stated keys. The parent audit does not establish an association, clinical AKI, or a treatment effect.

The unresolved claim is whether an apparent early hypotension association with a later *observed creatinine-rise signal* persists after explicit unit-death/alive-discharge competing states and correction for the fact that creatinine must be tested to be observed.

Primary hypothesis: among adult ICU stays alive and observable at a 6-hour landmark without an early observed rise, a persistent or non-recovering low-MAP trajectory has a higher 6–48-hour cumulative incidence of an observed creatinine-rise signal than a transient low-MAP-and-recovery trajectory at the same minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, MAP coverage, and admission risk. This trajectory contrast will retain at least 50% of its natural-observation-process contrast after the prespecified testing-process analysis and will remain directionally consistent in held-out hospitals.

Secondary residual-burden hypothesis: after the trajectory is included, cumulative area-under-65 contains no clinically meaningful additional information unless its standardized contrast is at least 2 percentage points and survives the observation and competing-state sensitivities. This distinguishes a duration/intensity signal from a shape-and-recovery signal; it is not a claim that changing MAP or vasopressors prevents AKI.

A finding that the observed-rise contrast attenuates by at least 50% under observation weighting, without a corresponding stable signal in competing death/discharge processes, supports ascertainment dependence rather than a biological null. Because testing responds to illness and disposition, this is an observational decomposition, not causal adjustment for latent AKI.

## Clinical importance and limits

A robust persistent/non-recovering trajectory signal could justify intensified renal surveillance and motivate a future intervention or adjudicated prospective study. A burden signal that disappears after trajectory and ascertainment accounting would discourage using scalar time-under-threshold measures as treatment targets and would identify a data-quality/monitoring problem. Explicit competing risks prevent early death or alive transfer from being mislabelled as “no kidney injury.”

The endpoint is deliberately an eICU-observable creatinine signal, not KDIGO AKI. The release lacks reliable pre-ICU renal reserve, complete urine output context, dialysis timing sufficient for adjudication, post-unit laboratory follow-up, treatment intent, and reasons for missing tests or transfer. No result can establish that hypotension caused injury, that testing caused an association, that a vasopressor dose is beneficial/harmful, or that a bedside MAP threshold should change. Clinical AKI adjudication, treatment-effect inference, and prospective utility require expert review and another study.

- observed creatinine rise: first accepted creatinine at (360, end_i] >= baseline + 0.3 mg/dL;

## Feature construction and observation mechanism

Partition [0,360] into twelve 30-minute bins. The trajectory vector must exclude cumulative area-under-65 and total minutes below 65, which are reserved for the burden estimand. It contains bin MAP median/minimum, covered minutes, observed-bin flag, longest consecutive low-MAP run, fraction of covered time below 65, low-MAP episode count separated by >=30 covered minutes above 65, slope from nadir to last valid MAP, last-bin MAP, recovery-to >=65 when the last bin is valid, and early vasopressor presence/onset. All trajectory features use only exposure-window information.

The analysis table must retain interval-level prior history after the landmark: most recent creatinine value/time, any qualifying test in the previous interval, number of qualifying tests, all-lab count, time since last qualifying creatinine, and MAP/observation coverage. A test indicator in interval t is `O_it=1` if at least one accepted creatinine result occurs in that interval. A rise is scored only from a result in the same interval and is never filled forward from a future result. For a same-interval duplicate, deduplicate by `labid` and median at the locked offset rule.

## Matched transparent baseline and mechanistic alternative

Every model uses the same cohort, states, intervalization, features available at the prediction time, split, outcome definitions, and held-out hospitals.

**Transparent nested baseline.** Fit regularized 30-minute cause-specific pooled-logistic models:

- B0: age/sex/ethnicity, admission source/type, unit type/stay type, hospital context, baseline creatinine, minimum MAP, MAP coverage, and early pressor presence;
- B1: B0 plus area-under-65 and minutes below 65;
- B2: B0 plus the trajectory vector without burden;
- B3: B2 plus area-under-65 and minutes below 65.

Use the same state-specific models for unit death, alive-unit observation loss, and observed creatinine rise. The principal nested contrasts are B2 versus B0 (trajectory), and B3 versus B2 (unique burden). Standardize to the 25th-to-75th trajectory profile or burden percentile at fixed minimum MAP and baseline risk. Report development correlation/condition number for burden versus trajectory; if unstable, label the unique-burden estimand inconclusive rather than choosing a regularization-dependent sign.

**Mechanistic observation-aware alternative.** Fit a cause-specific piecewise-exponential multi-state model on the identical 30-minute state intervals. The state process estimates hazards to rise, unit death, and alive-unit observation loss. In parallel, fit a discrete-time qualifying-creatinine observation model `Pr(O_it=1 | H_it)`, where `H_it` contains only prior interval values, early trajectory/baseline covariates, prior test counts/inter-arrival times, and current at-risk/disposition status. It must not use the result that defines the event or any future field.

Use the observation model in two prespecified ways: (a) stabilized inverse-observation-probability weighted transition estimating equations, truncating weights at the 1st/99th development percentiles and reporting the truncation rate; and (b) a joint likelihood/pseudo-likelihood sensitivity that models the observed-rise and test processes together. These estimate an observed-rise process under a stated missing-at-random-given-history assumption; they do not recover unobserved AKI. The natural CIF, weighted CIF, and joint-process CIF are all emitted. The primary robustness criterion is a <50% attenuation of the standardized trajectory contrast from natural to weighted/joint analysis, subject to uncertainty and transport criteria.

This alternative is scientifically substantive because it distinguishes a transient recoverable dip from a persistent trajectory and separately quantifies whether a patient had an opportunity for a creatinine rise to be recorded. The scalar baseline loses temporal shape and the observation mechanism. The alternative is not selected for a small score improvement.

## Splits, uncertainty, evaluation, and falsification

Before any label-map confirmation, imputation, scaling, feature selection, or model fitting, split hospitals 80:20 using a fixed documented seed, stratified by hospital size/region where possible; require at least 10 held-out hospitals and report exact hospital IDs/counts. Keep every `uniquepid` in one partition. All transformations and hospital effects are learned in development hospitals; an unseen hospital receives the development-population intercept/context rule.

Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates for standardized CIF contrasts and calibration uncertainty, with a paired bootstrap so models share resamples; report 95% percentile intervals. Also report a `uniquepid`-clustered sandwich or bootstrap sensitivity. Evaluate held-out hospitals with CIF calibration at 12/24/48 hours, calibration intercept/slope, integrated Brier score with competing states, cause-specific AUROC/AUPRC as secondary summaries, and decision-curve/net-benefit only if a clinically justified threshold is supplied. Never treat a small AUROC gain as evidence of clinical value.

Supportive evidence requires: (i) a >=2-point trajectory CIF contrast for the prespecified transient-versus-persistent profiles at fixed nadir and baseline risk; (ii) <50% attenuation after observation modeling, with adequate positivity and weight diagnostics; (iii) directionally consistent confirmed-rise sensitivity; (iv) explicit death and alive-discharge CIFs not hidden by censoring; and (v) direction/calibration preserved across held-out hospitals.

Unique burden is supported only by a >=2-point B3-versus-B2 contrast that remains directionally stable under no interpolation, tighter MAP coverage, non-invasive-MAP, revision-time, confirmed-rise, unit-boundary, and pressor-name sensitivities. A null or reversed contrast, >50% attenuation, poor held-out calibration, or instability across hospitals falsifies the corresponding robustness claim. An attenuation with poor test-model positivity is inconclusive, not evidence for ascertainment.

Inconclusive results include too few eligible stays/events/hospitals, unstable disposition or unit maps, weak testing variation, extreme weights, low overlap, nonconvergence, intervals spanning the 2-point margin, or a non-identifiable trajectory/burden contrast. Supportive, adverse, or inconclusive labels must cite emitted tables/figures and preserve uncertainty.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/schema/header matches, value/unit/disposition maps, cohort counts, deduplication, coverage, joins, split integrity, interval histories, state times, fitted parameters, CIFs, weights/positivity, calibration, transport, and sensitivity outputs. Clinical interpretation of AKI, reasons for testing/discharge, preventability, causal effects, treatment intent, and bedside actionability are not automatically checkable.

## Alternatives considered and revisit rules

The parent’s trajectory-versus-burden decomposition is retained but promoted to a two-estimand robustness test with explicit death, alive discharge, and testing processes. The expert eICU-02/08 seeds motivate this observation-process branch; they remain untested proposals, not evidence.

A matched learned GRU/temporal-convolution alternative was considered with the same twelve MAP bins, interval lab/test history, target states, hospital-held-out split, CIF/Brier/calibration evaluation, and uncertainty. It was deferred because the current scientific uncertainty is whether observation and competing-state accounting changes an interpretable signal; a learned model could encode hospital testing practice without resolving that question. Revisit only if held-out residuals show stable sequential structure after B2/M, and then preserve the same states, observation accounting, calibration and resource cap. This is a scientific deferral, not a ban on neural methods or GPU use.

Nighttime ICU discharge, interhospital transfer, hyperoxia, and ventilatory-burden directions were considered. The first two lack reliable staffing/bed-capacity or causal discharge-intent evidence; hyperoxia/ventilation require expert-verified meanings for generic `respiratoryCharting`/`respiratoryCare` fields, while `apacheApsVar.fio2`/`pao2` are untimed. They are lower-value for this episode unless label coverage and adjudication are established. A pure reduced-testing primary outcome was not selected because reduced testing is confounded by clinical improvement, deterioration, transfer, treatment limitation, and interface failure; it is incorporated as an observation mechanism, not called under-monitoring.

Revisit the learned branch if reproducible held-out residual trajectory structure remains; revisit respiratory/discharge branches only after expert label validation and a clinical endpoint; revisit causal MAP/pressor work only with adjudicated AKI, treatment intent/dose timing, and a design for time-varying confounding.
