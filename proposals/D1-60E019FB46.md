# Does hypotension duration add risk beyond the blood-pressure nadir?

## Unresolved claim and hypothesis

The expert seed asks whether, at the same minimum blood pressure, longer-lasting hypotension is associated with more subsequent kidney injury. The unresolved claim is an incremental, transportable prognostic association, not that hypotension causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

Primary hypothesis: among adult eICU ICU stays that are free of a creatinine-defined early injury signal at a 6-hour landmark, and conditional on the same observed minimum MAP, baseline creatinine, early severity and hospital, greater time-weighted MAP burden below 65 mmHg during ICU hours 0–6 is associated with higher 6–48-hour risk of a new creatinine-defined AKI signal. The claim is falsified by a null or reversed burden contrast, loss of direction/calibration in hospital-held-out data, or disappearance under measurement-density and artifact sensitivities.

Clinical importance: a nadir discards duration and recovery. A reproducible burden increment could improve early renal surveillance and define a clinically meaningful exposure for a future trial. It cannot establish that raising MAP or changing vasopressors prevents AKI.

The available evidence supports only data feasibility: the frozen eICU snapshot has ICU-relative longitudinal MAP, creatinine, disposition, severity and infusion fields. The seed and demonstrations do not establish this association in eICU.

The primary estimand is the adjusted cause-specific 6–48-hour AKI risk contrast per 60 mmHg-min increase in area-under-65, comparing patients with the same minimum observed MAP and measured baseline/severity covariates. Report absolute risks and risk ratios or odds ratios with 95% intervals. Because discharge and death can prevent creatinine observation, also report cumulative incidence with competing risks; do not code an unobserved post-discharge creatinine as AKI-free.

Time zero is ICU admission. Exposure uses only [0,360]. Outcome uses only (360,2880], strictly excluding rows at the landmark. Negative offsets are excluded. Follow-up ends at the earliest observable ICU or hospital discharge/death, or at 48 hours.

## Construction and endpoint

From numeric MAP in [0,360], remove impossible values under a prespecified physiologic range, collapse duplicate patient/time rows deterministically, and linearly interpolate only gaps <=30 minutes. Compute minutes below 65, area-under-65, minimum observed MAP, low-MAP episode count/duration and recovery slope after the last low-MAP episode. Preserve observedness and coverage. Repeat without interpolation and with vitalAperiodic noninvasivemean; do not claim continuous pressure.

Define the early reference as the first valid creatinine identified by a prespecified creatinine-name/system/interface rule in [0,360]. Exclude stays with any valid early creatinine >= reference +0.3 mg/dL. The primary outcome is the first valid post-landmark creatinine >= reference +0.3 mg/dL in (360,2880]. Sensitivities require two post-landmark measurements, use a 48-hour qualifying change when observation density supports it, and use a minimum-early-creatinine baseline. This is a creatinine research endpoint, not adjudicated KDIGO AKI: urine-output criteria, pre-ICU renal reserve and clinical adjudication are unavailable or incomplete.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires a clinically nontrivial burden contrast after minimum-MAP adjustment, calibrated uncertainty excluding trivial effects, directionally consistent hospital-held-out transport, and mechanistic evidence that duration/recovery rather than only measurement density or vasopressor recording accounts for the increment.

Adverse evidence is a null/reversed burden contrast, loss of direction or calibration in held-out hospitals, or disappearance under no-interpolation, independent-MAP or coverage sensitivities. This would favor the nadir-only model or reveal site-dependent ascertainment; it would not prove hypotension harmless.

Inconclusive evidence is inadequate eligible follow-up, sparse MAP/creatinine observation, unstable creatinine-name definitions, intervals spanning clinically meaningful effects, or inability to distinguish informative discharge/death from missing testing. The response is a narrower descriptive study or additional data, not a forced conclusion.

Computationally checkable outputs are cohort flow, exact retained lab strings, coverage/interpolation audit, leakage-free splits, model fits, contrasts, competing-risk estimates, calibration, uncertainty, transport metrics and sensitivity results. Clinical review or additional data are required for true KDIGO adjudication, pre-ICU kidney function, urine-output completeness, reasons for missing tests/discharge, treatment intent and any decision threshold. Observational data cannot establish that changing MAP or vasopressors changes AKI.
