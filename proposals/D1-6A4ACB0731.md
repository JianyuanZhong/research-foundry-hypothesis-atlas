# Domain-specific recovery and process-stable partial identification after first ICU discharge

## Direction and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive alternative to [prior hypothesis]. It preserves its nonparametric endpoint framing: a documented non-ICU bridge followed by ICU return is an observed lower endpoint (L), while any later ICU return is an upper endpoint (U) for a latent post-non-ICU-to-ICU transition. It does not turn an unconfirmed return into a biological category.

The alternative asks whether the clinically interpretable part of that interval depends on which support domain recently changed and on the observable discharge/documentation process. The scientific deliverable is not a better score. It is a held-out, process-standardized set of domain-specific endpoint intervals:

- L_g(h) and U_g(h), with competing death and alive-discharge CIFs, for durable recorded-off, recent respiratory withdrawal, recent vasoactive cessation, and recent withdrawal in both domains;
- lower, upper, and worst-case contrast intervals at 6, 24, and 48 hours, especially recent-respiratory-only versus durable-off and recent-vasoactive-only versus durable-off;
- a pre-specified standardized version targeting all otherwise eligible first ICU outtimes, using observed coverage/discharge-process weights as a descriptive selection sensitivity, never as causal correction;
- interval width and coverage/process dependence by domain;
- transparent-baseline predictions and an observation-aware two-domain recovery/location semi-Markov sensitivity model, each linked to the same endpoint outputs.

Completion requires a source manifest, one-row-per-stay cohort and selection audits, twelve-bin features, support-observation/q audit, transfer-topology audit, lower/upper bounds and contrast tables, held-out predictions, calibration and Brier scores for observed events, subject-bootstrap uncertainty, all falsification outputs, and an interpretation map linking every conclusion to an output row. No clinical result is claimed before those outputs exist.

## Unresolved question, supported evidence, and falsifiable hypothesis

The selected parent supports a measurement claim, not a clinical conclusion: MIMIC records enough first-stay, support, transfer, and hospital-time fields to audit a conservative topology lower endpoint and an aggregate-return upper endpoint. Its bounded discovery audit found 52,591 broad eligible first ICU outtimes, 2,661 later ICU returns within 48 hours, 2,060 with the frozen qualifying bridge, 601 without one, and 294 return audits with multiple bridge candidates. Those counts establish endpoint ambiguity and feasibility only. They do not estimate an exposure association and do not establish that a bridge was non-ICU care, that a return was unplanned, or that deterioration occurred.

The unresolved clinical question is:

> At the first ICU outtime, does the type of recently withdrawn recorded support identify different risks of a subsequent record-compatible ICU escalation, and does that distinction remain when the lower/upper endpoint interval is standardized for the observable process that determines support coverage and transfer documentation?

The primary hypothesis is an interval-valued, noncausal association hypothesis:

1. Recent respiratory-only withdrawal has a larger 48-hour lower-bound contrast with durable recorded-off than recent vasoactive-only cessation, Δ^L_R > Δ^L_V, and the same domain ordering is not created solely by a difference in observable coverage/documentation process.
2. If recent respiratory withdrawal is genuinely informative about transition-compatible early failure, its lower-bound excess should persist after the process-standardization sensitivity and show a directionally compatible upper-bound excess. A signal confined to U, or a large domain-specific U-L width, is evidence that the available records do not identify the stronger clinical interpretation.
3. The conclusion must be stable across the declared missing-support q-grid and bridge-rule grid. The strongest directional claim is falsified if its worst-case lower contrast is non-positive, if the respiratory-versus-vasoactive ordering reverses across plausible topology/q settings, or if it disappears in the predeclared observation/process adjustment and negative controls.

Formally, for domain group g, L_g(h) is the CIF of a documented qualifying non-ICU bridge followed by the earliest later ICU return by h, and U_g(h) is the CIF of any later ICU return by h. Death before return and alive hospital discharge are mutually exclusive competing first events. The nonparametric compatible risk-difference interval for g versus durable-off d is:

[L_g(h)-U_d(h), U_g(h)-L_d(h)].

The domain-ordering contrast is reported as the pair of intervals for respiratory-only and vasoactive-only, not as a point estimate obtained by averaging endpoints. Process-standardization changes the target population, not the definition of L or U, and is labelled a descriptive selection sensitivity.

## Population, landmark, exposure, and outcomes

Use one earliest valid ICU stay per subject. Require patients.anchor_age >= 18, valid subject_id and hadm_id, intime < outtime < admissions.dischtime, non-null dischtime, no admissions.deathtime <= outtime, and a complete pre-landmark window beginning no earlier than icustays.intime. Retain MIMIC top-coded anchor_age=91; do not use dod. Ties among a subject's eligible stays are broken by the smallest stay_id.

The landmark is icu/icustays.outtime. Predictors are restricted to [outtime-12 hours, outtime) in twelve left-closed/right-open one-hour bins. chartevents.charttime is the clinical observation time; inputevents.starttime/endtime and procedureevents.starttime/endtime define treatment/procedure intervals. storetime is retained only for a timing negative control and never substitutes for clinical time.

The primary analysis population is the adequate-coverage subset with one of the four named exposure groups and at least two respiratory observation bins and two MAP observation bins in each six-hour half. The broad eligible cohort is retained for the coverage-selection audit and standardization diagnostic. No patient is excluded from the audit merely because support is unobserved; such records are explicitly classified as inadequate, uninformative, active/residual, discordant, or otherwise out of the primary exposure contrast.

Support states are constructed separately for respiratory and vasoactive domains:

- durable recorded-off: no positive recorded support in either half;
- recent respiratory-only: respiratory-positive in the preceding half and no respiratory-positive evidence in the final half, with no recent vasoactive cessation;
- recent vasoactive-only: vasoactive-positive in the preceding half and none in the final half, with no recent respiratory cessation;
- recent both: both domains positive in the preceding half and absent in the final half.

Active/residual, discordant, no-row, contradiction, and inadequate-coverage strata remain in the audit and secondary analyses. “No positive evidence” is never treated as continuous physiologic absence. Each hour records positive evidence, observed non-positive evidence, uninformative absence, contradiction, invalid timing, row counts, source channels, observation fraction, maximum gap, and last recorded-positive time.

Follow-up is [outtime, min(outtime+48 hours, admissions.dischtime)). The first competing events are:

1. lower endpoint: the earliest later ICU stay in the same subject/admission within follow-up with a qualifying documented non-ICU bridge;
2. upper endpoint comparator: any earliest later ICU stay within follow-up, regardless of bridge documentation;
3. hospital death before return, using admissions.deathtime;
4. alive hospital discharge before return or death, using admissions.dischtime.

For the primary bridge label, inspect every valid hosp/transfers row between index outtime and later ICU intime, allow a five-minute endpoint tolerance, clip to the index-to-return interval, require at least 30 clipped minutes, and require careunit not to equal the later ICU stay's first_careunit or last_careunit. Select greatest clipped duration, then earliest transfer outtime, then smallest transfer_id, while retaining every candidate and ambiguity flag. Repeat the complete bounds under 0- and 5-minute tolerances, 0/15/30/60-minute minimum durations, careunit-only versus interval-only logic, exclusion of eventtype='discharge', and ICU-unit matching from all linked ICU intervals. The primary rule is locked before any model fitting. A qualifying row is documentation of topology only; it is not intent, deterioration, or preventability.

Apply event-time rules consistently: if death and return tie within one minute, death is first; if discharge ties another event, the non-discharge event is first. Re-run 0- and 5-minute tie tolerances as sensitivity analyses. Report L, U, U-L, all contrast intervals, and all competing-event CIFs.

## Observation/process standardization

The process sensitivity uses the broad eligible cohort as the target population and predicts adequate support coverage from landmark-only variables: age, gender, anchor-year group, admission type/location, first and last ICU careunit, ICU LOS, pre-index transfer count/last careunit, admission timing fields available before outtime, vital/signals row counts, support interval counts, maximum observation gaps, and contradictions. Do not use any post-landmark variable, bridge label, later ICU return, death, discharge, discharge location, or notes text.

Fit a logistic inclusion model on training subjects only, with a prespecified spline-free main-effects specification and a locked stabilized inverse-odds weight P(A=1)/P(A=1|X) for covered subjects. Estimate P(A=1) in training data; use validation only for a locked probability floor and weight cap, then apply without test tuning. Report overlap, weight distribution, effective sample size, standardized covariate differences, and the difference between unweighted adequate-coverage bounds and target-standardized bounds. If overlap or effective sample size fails the locked diagnostic, report the standardization as inconclusive rather than silently changing the target.

This is a selection/generalizability description, not an estimate of a treatment effect. It does not identify missing support or transfer paths. The primary unweighted partial-identification interval remains mandatory.

## Matched baseline and learned/mechanistic alternative

Both methods receive exactly the same cohort, twelve-bin physiology/support/mask/process variables, q scenarios, topology labels, event times, exposure definitions, and patient-level splits. All thresholds, imputation, q values, topology rules, weight floors/caps, and hyperparameters are locked from training/validation only.

### Transparent baseline

Fit pooled one-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards through 48 hours for observed confirmed return, observed unconfirmed return, death, and alive discharge, plus the aggregate any-return/death/discharge comparator. Include exposure indicators, fixed pre-landmark physiology summaries, support states, observation masks, and process/context covariates with only prespecified respiratory-by-vasoactive and exposure-by-process interactions. Fit nested versions using physiology/support only, masks/process only, and all variables. Standardize predictions to the adequate-coverage population and, separately, to the broad eligible target with the locked selection weights. Derive L and U by summing predicted category hazards, but also report empirical/Aalen-Johansen endpoint estimates; models cannot override the nonparametric bounds.

This baseline is the primary interpretive estimator because every endpoint contribution is visible. It loses the order, persistence, and duration of support withdrawal and cannot distinguish a prolonged unknown transfer location from an immediate ICU-to-ICU return.

### Observation-aware mechanistic alternative

Fit a constrained two-domain hidden semi-Markov model with:

- separate respiratory and hemodynamic recovery states, duration-dependent persistence and recent-withdrawal transitions in the twelve pre-landmark bins;
- an explicit observation model for chart masks, row counts, source channels, support-interval presence, and contradictions;
- post-landmark latent location states ICU, documented non-ICU, and unknown, with ICU intervals as high-specificity observations and transfer rows as imperfect topology observations;
- a competing-event observation head for confirmed return, unconfirmed return, death, and alive discharge.

Fit latent state emissions/transitions and observed event likelihood using training subjects; select state count, duration cap, regularization, and optimizer settings on validation. The model must output the same observed hazards and a model-based latent transition sensitivity over a predeclared grid of transfer-capture sensitivity and false-bridge rate. Constrain reported latent risks to be displayed against, and never used to replace, the nonparametric [L,U] bounds. A latent estimate outside the bounds is a model/data bug or model rejection.

The alternative reveals information the baseline loses: whether a recent change was a short terminal event or a sustained buffer, whether respiratory and vasoactive recovery were asynchronous, whether a gap is likely unobserved rather than stable-off, and whether return timing is compatible with persistent non-ICU residence. This is substantive endpoint interpretation and assumption stress-testing, not a predictive-performance contest. A flexible GRU/TCN is deferred because it would add capacity without an observation model or location constraints. Causal discharge-policy modeling, note-based intent classification, external validation, images, waveforms, and clinician adjudication are deferred for missing or unvalidated dependencies.

## Splitting, uncertainty, and falsification

Partition by subject, never by rows, into fixed 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits with seeds 17, 29, and 43. Report each seed and a predeclared aggregate. No q, topology, standardization, threshold, or model decision may be selected from held-out test estimates.

Primary uncertainty is a subject-level bootstrap, retaining draws, for L, U, U-L, lower/upper contrast intervals, process-standardized intervals, and competing-event CIFs. Report percentile or bootstrap-t intervals with the procedure locked before fitting. Include event counts, overlap, effective sample size, calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, and IPCW Brier scores for each observed endpoint; AUROC is secondary and cannot validate latent topology. Preserve q-specific and topology-specific draws rather than only a pooled summary.

Predeclared falsifications and negative controls are:

- timestamp/dependency audit proving no post-outtime predictor leakage;
- replace charttime with storetime and shuffle the twelve pre-landmark bins;
- masks/process-only, support-only, physiology-only, and full-model comparisons;
- remove procedure corroboration and separate arterial from NIBP MAP and O2 flow from FiO2/PEEP;
- vary q in {0, .10, .25, .50, .75, 1.00} by shared, domain/half-specific, and higher-vasoactive-q scenarios;
- vary support thresholds, six-hour windows, bridge duration/tolerance/careunit rules, and event tie tolerances;
- permute transfer careunit labels within admission while preserving ICU times and transfer durations;
- shift post-landmark transfer intervals by +6 hours;
- use an analogous pre-landmark pseudo-return window as a negative control;
- compare unweighted and standardized bounds, complete-case/coverage-restricted analyses, first-careunit, admission-type, anchor-year, and transfer-density strata;
- check latent-state entropy, state-label/order stability, calibration, and model-implied-bound containment.

Supportive evidence requires a clinically nontrivial, uncertainty-supported lower-bound respiratory-versus-vasoactive ordering that survives the locked process-standardization and q/topology grids, with adequate overlap/event support, no material storetime dependence, attenuation after transfer-careunit permutation, and mechanistic outputs contained within the bounds. It supports only a retrospective association with a record-compatible transition.

Adverse evidence is a non-positive worst-case lower contrast, reversal across plausible topology/q settings, an apparent effect confined to the upper endpoint, disappearance after process standardization, persistence after topology permutation, poor overlap, or a latent estimate outside [L,U]. Inconclusive evidence is a wide interval, sparse domain-specific events, inadequate coverage transport, unstable splits, high latent entropy, or unjustifiable transfer-capture sensitivity. An upper-bound signal with a null lower bound is not a confirmed positive result.

The parent’s 22.2-second, 4-CPU/16,384-MiB topology audit is retained as feasibility evidence only; this proposal has not fitted exposure-restricted outcomes, bounds, weights, or models.

Working estimates are 10–30 minutes for extraction, 30–60 minutes for baseline/bounds/bootstrap, and 1–4 hours for repeated semi-Markov fits and q/topology grids; these are unverified. Preserve checkpoints, wall time, package versions, resource use, and output hashes. The study must not silently narrow its population or replace bounds with a point model to fit the budget.
