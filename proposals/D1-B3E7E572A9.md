# Does duration of early hypotension add AKI risk information beyond nadir?

## Scientific opening

Clinicians often summarize an ICU hypotensive episode by its lowest blood pressure. However, two patients with the same nadir may have very different total exposure to low pressure. The unresolved question is whether the duration of that exposure independently predicts subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI), beyond the nadir and baseline kidney function.

## Hypothesis

Among eICU adults with a prespecified early hypotensive episode, a longer duration at or below 65 mmHg systolic BP (or MAP < 65 mmHg) is associated with higher risk of subsequent AKI, beyond what is explained by the episode nadir and baseline kidney function.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use the eICU CRD source files listed in `datasets/eicu/README.md`, with patient-level joins only within eICU. Define the cohort as adult ICU stays with at least one early blood-pressure record and a creatinine value available before or at the episode. Define the exposure episode as the first 24 hours after the first BP record, or a prespecified alternative 12-hour window if the 24-hour definition produces sparse outcomes.

Within that window, calculate:
- minimum systolic BP and minimum MAP;
- time with systolic BP <=65 mmHg and time with MAP <65 mmHg;
- fraction of observed time in that range, with explicit handling of irregular sampling;
- time from first qualifying BP to first vasopressor record, if available.

Exclude deaths before the outcome window and patients with missing baseline creatinine. Do not treat missing BP as normal pressure; report coverage and sensitivity analyses using only stays with adequate BP density.

## Outcome

Define subsequent AKI using KDIGO-compatible creatinine criteria from eICU creatinine records after the exposure window: creatinine increase to at least 2 times baseline, or at least 1.5 times baseline with a documented increase within the prespecified outcome period, or urine-output criteria only if eICU urine-output fields are complete and adjudicated. If urine output is unavailable or too sparse, use creatinine-only AKI and state that limitation.

## Baseline and alternative

Baseline: prespecified logistic regression for AKI with hospital fixed effects, age, sex, baseline creatinine, nadir systolic BP/MAP, vasopressor use, and relevant admission comorbidities available in eICU.

Learned alternative: regularized gradient-boosting model trained on the same tabular features, including duration and sampling-density indicators. Compare held-out hospital calibration, Brier score, and AUROC, not just a small AUC gain. The learned model can reveal nonlinear duration effects and interactions with baseline kidney function that the simple model may miss.

Mechanistic alternative: model cumulative low-perfusion exposure as a dose-response process. This is deferred unless the baseline suggests a nonlinear or threshold effect, because eICU does not provide enough renal perfusion physiology to identify a mechanistic dose-response without strong assumptions.

## Analysis and falsification

Fit models within hospitals and validate on held-out hospitals. The primary estimand is the adjusted association between duration categories and subsequent AKI, with hospital-level uncertainty. Support requires a positive association that persists after nadir adjustment and improves held-out hospital calibration or Brier score. Adverse results are a null or inverse duration association, or performance degradation when duration is added. Inconclusive results include sparse AKI, unstable calibration, or inability to distinguish duration from sampling density.

Falsification: if duration adds no held-out predictive information and its coefficient is null or adverse after nadir adjustment, the incremental-risk claim is not supported. A positive association that disappears after accounting for vasopressor use or BP sampling density would support an observation-practice explanation rather than a patient-risk explanation.

## Clinical value and limits

This could change how early hypotension is reported and whether duration should be included in ICU risk summaries. It does not establish that changing treatment based on duration improves outcomes; that would require a prospective evaluation or treatment-effect study. Hospital-level differences in recording density and vasopressor practices are major confounders.
