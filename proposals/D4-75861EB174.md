# Muscle quantity versus muscle quality for later mobility decline

Status: delivery-ready design after source audit. No hypothesis fit or outcome model has been run; numerical counts below are observed availability audits only.

## Opening, evidence, and hypothesis

The unresolved clinical question is whether MRI-visible thigh muscle quality adds prognostic information about later mobility beyond muscle quantity and contemporaneous hand-grip strength, or whether the apparent signal is mainly pain, neurologic/orthopedic disease, baseline severity, or selective return for repeat assessment.

The strongest inspected evidence is that MRI fat-free muscle volume (FFMV) and muscle-fat infiltration (MFI) are cross-sectionally associated with grip strength, usual walking pace and falls in UK Biobank [K1]. This does not establish later prediction, quantity-independent effects, or biological mechanism. A later UK Biobank CKD analysis links adverse muscle composition to poor function and comorbidity but uses self-reported functional outcomes and reports timing limitations [K2]. A current review finds associations between sarcopenia markers and mobility, but inconsistent mobility change and inability of observational data to separate sarcopenia from pain, neurologic, orthopedic, cognitive and environmental causes [K3].

Primary hypothesis: among participants with complete same-namespace MRI and grip at the instance-2 anchor, greater thigh MFI and lower regional fat-free muscle volume are associated with worse instance-3 usual walking pace and more falls after at least one year, and MFI retains an association within narrow quantity strata and after adjustment for baseline grip, BMI, age, baseline mobility where observed, and pre-anchor diagnosis burden. This is a predictive/descriptive association, not a causal claim that MFI causes falls or a claim that MRI diagnoses sarcopenia.

The key falsification is discordance: if an MFI signal disappears after volume matching, baseline function/grip adjustment, measured pain/neurologic/orthopedic diagnosis adjustment, or repeat-assessment weighting, the independent biological-quality interpretation is not supported.

## Computable analysis and alternatives

Simple primary baseline, identical inputs: nested ordinal logistic models for pace and falls with age field `21003-2.0), BMI `21001-2.0), anchor calendar year, bilateral grip mean and discordance, valid baseline outcome where present, total volume and MFI. Report nested clinical+grip, +quantity and +MFI contrasts. Use participant bootstrap uncertainty and diagnosis-adjusted and inverse-probability-of-repeat-assessment weighted sensitivities.

Substantive learned alternative, same rows, predictors and targets: CPU HistGradientBoosting ordinal/probability models using four regional volumes, two MFI fields, grip components/discordance, age/BMI/date and valid baseline outcome. Use a date-ordered split by `53-2.0): earliest 70% train, next 15% validation and latest 15% locked test, with tied dates kept together; hyperparameters are selected only on train/validation. Report locked-test ordinal log loss, calibration, falls Brier score and secondary discrimination, with participant-bootstrap uncertainty. Its scientific outputs are local effects/partial dependence for MFI at fixed quantity, regional discordance and MFI-by-grip/quantity interactions. This can reveal thresholds and regional structure lost by the additive baseline; it cannot establish mechanism. A small predictive gain alone is not success.

Repeat-assessment selection: within 38,070 anchors, model observation of instance-3 outcomes from pre-anchor variables, use stabilized/truncated inverse-probability weights under a frozen rule, and compare weighted/unweighted contrasts. Do not interpret complete-case estimates as population estimates without this check.

The simple model is primary for an interpretable scientific estimand; the learned model is retained because it tests a substantive nonlinear/regional uncertainty on exactly the same inputs. CPU is appropriate for 38,070/4,676 tabular rows. A GPU sequence model is deferred because there is one MRI anchor and one repeat assessment, not a dense sequence; a mechanistic force model is deferred because power, gait kinetics, pain examination and neuromuscular adjudication are unavailable. Cross-namespace sex/genetic/proteomic models are excluded pending a verified crosswalk. See `alternatives.md).

## Falsification and meaning of results

Supportive: MFI has a quantity-held-fixed association with both outcomes that survives grip/BMI/age, diagnosis adjustment, date ordering and repeat-assessment weighting, with a stable learned regional/threshold contrast. This supports an independent prognostic composition signal only; it does not establish causality, treatment benefit, mechanism or a sarcopenia diagnosis.

Adverse: MFI is null/reversed within volume strata; diagnosis adjustment removes it; only grip predicts; or weighting removes it. This favors functional confounding, disease severity or repeat-assessment selection over an independent biological-quality interpretation. Quantity-only stability supports a quantity-dominant boundary.

Inconclusive: uncertainty is wide, response coding or diagnosis support is weak, or split estimates disagree. Report detectable contrast and uncertainty rather than calling an imprecise null a refutation.

Required falsifications: volume deciles and left/right MFI sensitivity; no cross-namespace sex import; strict later-date rule; pre-anchor diagnosis exclusion and adjustment; weighted/unweighted comparison; valid-category-only and ordinal sensitivity. Death fields describe competing severity and cannot validate a muscle-quality mechanism.

Unavailable clinical evidence includes adjudicated pathology, direct force/power, gait-lab speed, objective fall dates/circumstances, pain severity/location, assistive-device use, physical examination, medication indication and validated neurologic examination. Self-reported pace/falls and diagnosis codes are imperfect proxies. The experiment can establish computational associations, temporal ordering, calibration and measured-rival robustness, not biology, causality or clinical deployment.

## Three inspected key works

[K1] Linge et al. report UKB MRI FFMV/MFI associations with grip, pace and falls, including MFI-falls association. This proposal adds later same-namespace outcomes, quantity-held-fixed temporal contrasts, diagnosis/selection falsifications and learned shape analysis; K1 is cross-sectional for these relationships.

[K2] Indurain et al. report UKB CKD adverse muscle composition associated with poor function and comorbidity, but self-reported outcomes and timing limitations remain. This proposal tests temporal mobility ordering in a broader MRI/grip cohort and treats comorbidity as a rival.

[K3] Otepova et al. review mobility associations but emphasize inconsistent change evidence and inability to separate muscle contribution from pain, neurologic, orthopedic, cognitive and environmental causes. This proposal operationalizes those limits as falsifications and adverse/inconclusive rules.
