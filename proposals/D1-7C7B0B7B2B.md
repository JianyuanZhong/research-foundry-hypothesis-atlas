# A time-safe 12-hour landmark for early physiology and later recorded-care escalation in HCC

## Scientific deliverable and repaired hypothesis

The deliverable is a newly fitted, locked **sequential all-admission prognostic analysis**. It must produce:

1. an auditable first-observed adult HCC-coded admission cohort and a patient-level split;
2. a row-level time audit showing that no post-transition or post-landmark laboratory row enters the learned early-physiology model;
3. an exhaustive terminal observed-care state vector retaining admissions with early discharge, early procedure, unresolved procedure timing, later procedure, later discharge and no later recorded procedure;
4. matched transparent baseline (B0), early laboratory model (B1), and a substantive irregular latent-state alternative (B2);
5. held-out probabilities, multiclass log loss/Brier score, calibration, standardized state contrasts and clustered uncertainty;
6. timestamp, forbidden-window, observation-process, terminal-label and procedure-dictionary falsification outputs; and
7. a claim-to-output table separating computable recorded-care claims from claims requiring clinical adjudication or another study.

The primary hypothesis is:

> Among all adults with a first observed HCC-coded inpatient admission who have no established discharge or timeable procedure transition before hour 12, hepatic/renal laboratory information observed strictly before hour 12 improves calibrated held-out prediction of the first subsequent recorded-care transition through hour 72—especially a procedure beginning in [24,72) versus discharge/no procedure—beyond admission and pre-admission information. The increment should remain present after explicitly accounting for early discharge/procedure and unresolved timing as competing observed-care transitions.

This is a sequential landmark estimand, not a claim that laboratory values cause a treatment decision. The prediction origin is hour 12. The primary clinically relevant post-buffer procedure window begins at hour 24, leaving 12 hours between the last permitted laboratory time and that endpoint. All eligible admissions remain in the population: pre-hour-12 transitions are not silently excluded, but are represented as competing states and modeled by the admission-only stage.

The clinical question is consequential because a reproducible signal by hour 12 could justify prospective review of which newly admitted patients merit earlier multidisciplinary assessment or monitoring for a later recorded procedural pathway. The snapshot cannot establish that such review would improve care. The advance over the parent is temporal: the laboratory increment is estimated only in a risk set demonstrably free of an earlier observed transition, while the all-admission standardization still reports what happens to the entire eligible admission population.

## What is known, unresolved and unavailable

The strongest supported claim is data availability, not clinical truth. The snapshot contains encounter admission/discharge times, encounter-linked diagnosis strings, assay names and numeric/qualitative laboratory results with native laboratory times, and procedure rows with native start/end times. Non-drug orders have opening/start/end/status/frequency fields. All substantive tables join to encounters on (patient master index, encounter number).

The bounded full-source audit in work/hcc_temporal_audit.json read the encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, procedure and order files in chunks. It found 105,044 encounter rows/keys, 43,815 unique patient identifiers, 28,159,928 laboratory rows, 338,040 procedure rows, 15,874 procedure rows with missing/invalid Start Time, 183,354 valid procedure starts at 00:00, and 109,892 joined procedure rows in [24,72) hours. It found 9,189,289 joined laboratory rows in [0,24) and 3,804,333 in [0,12). The broad audit rule Liver cancer|hepatocellular carcinoma|primary liver cancer|HCC matched all 105,044 encounter keys; this is a snapshot/coding fact, not validation of the rule or pathology-confirmed HCC.

The unresolved claim is therefore limited and falsifiable: does the pre-hour-12 numeric laboratory history add reproducible, calibrated information about subsequent recorded care after a no-transition landmark? The data do not establish HCC confirmation, tumor stage/burden, resectability, indication, intent, scheduling, procedure completion, response, toxicity, mortality, outside-hospital care, or benefit. Diagnoses have no native time. Laboratory units and reference ranges are absent. Clinical documents and pathology have no native time, so they cannot time-align confirmation or intent. Orders and procedure names are not evidence of a recommendation, receipt or appropriateness.

## Population, temporal rules and competing states

Use HCC snapshot [source checksum], with catalog [internal dataset path] (catalog SHA-256 [source checksum]).

Include age >=18, valid native admission time and discharge time, nonnegative stay, admission in [2011-01-01, 2026-01-01), discharge before 2026-01-01, and a frozen lexical HCC rule selected in fitting/selection data. Select the earliest eligible encounter per patient master index, ordered by native admission time then encounter number; do not replace an invalid earliest record with a later one. Exclude direct identifiers from features.

Let t0 be native admission time. A valid procedure is one whose native Start Time parses and is comparable with t0; it must also begin before native discharge to be an inpatient transition. Use half-open windows, with exact hour 12 assigned to the post-landmark window and exact hour 24 assigned to the post-buffer window.

### Stage 0: all-admission pre-landmark states

Every eligible admission receives one mutually exclusive Stage-0 state, in earliest-time precedence:

- P0: first valid procedure starts in [0,12) hours after t0 and before discharge.
- D0: discharge occurs in [0,12), before any P0.
- U0: no established P0/D0, but a linked procedure has missing/invalid Start Time that could alter the pre-12 ordering; use a conservative prespecified rule and report both conservative and optimistic missing-time bounds.
- R12: no P0, D0 or U0, and the patient is still inpatient at hour 12.

A valid earlier P0/D0 takes precedence over a later unresolved row; otherwise an unresolved row prevents calling the record “no early transition.” Rows beginning before admission or after discharge are retained for audit but cannot define a state.

Stage 0 is modeled for all admissions using B0 information only. This prevents the early laboratory window from being used to predict an event that has already occurred, while retaining the clinical burden of early discharge/procedure and uncertainty in the all-admission estimand.

### Stage 1: transitions after the hour-12 landmark

Only R12 admissions enter the conditional Stage-1 risk set. Their B1/B2 laboratory inputs are restricted to native test time in [t0,t0+12h); no value at or after hour 12 is eligible. The Stage-1 terminal state is the first applicable state through hour 72:

- P12_24: first valid procedure in [12,24) before discharge.
- D12_24: discharge in [12,24), before a procedure.
- U12_24: unresolved procedure timing that could alter the [12,24) ordering and no established earlier state.
- P24_72: no earlier Stage-1 state, then first valid procedure in [24,72) before discharge.
- D24_72: no earlier Stage-1 state, then discharge in [24,72) before a procedure.
- U24_72: no established valid state, but unresolved procedure timing could alter [24,72) ordering.
- N72: no prior state, discharge at/after hour 72, and no valid procedure through hour 72.

The primary post-buffer estimand is the standardized probability of P24_72, with the full Stage-0/Stage-1 state vector reported. The all-admission standardized probability of each state is obtained by combining Stage-0 probabilities with the conditional Stage-1 probabilities for R12; observed Stage-0 transitions are never discarded. This is an explicit sequential decomposition, not conditioning the entire study on 12-hour survival or a later procedure.

The primary analysis uses procedure presence and native Start Time only; it does not read Surgery text. A later semantic pathway analysis is secondary and cannot alter these states.

## Matched models and substantive alternative

Use one deterministic patient-level split, for example hash bucket 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for selecting prespecified preprocessing/regularization/state dimension, and 70–79 as locked evaluation. No test outcome, procedure text, or order text may select features or dictionaries.

B0 is a transparent two-stage competing-risk model. It uses age, sex, admitting department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, diagnosis-type/descriptive fields, prior encounter count, and pre-admission assay-specific nearest/count/recency/missingness summaries. B0 predicts Stage 0 for every admission and Stage 1 for R12.

B1 uses the identical target, split, preprocessing and model family, then adds only valid assay-specific information from labs in [0,12): first/last value, change, elapsed time, count, missingness/density and supported within-assay slope. No labs from [12,24) or later, orders, medications, procedures, discharge time, clinical documents or later encounters enter B1. Assays remain separate because units and reference ranges are absent. Stage 0 remains baseline-only; B1’s incremental estimand is the Stage-1 information gain and its all-admission standardized consequence.

B2 is a substantive irregular longitudinal alternative on the same R12 patients and exactly the same [0,12) lab rows: assay-specific latent hepatic-reserve and renal-stress coordinates with assay-specific offsets/noise, irregular observation times, shrinkage, posterior uncertainty, level/slope/coupled movement and competing-state heads. It may use a low-dimensional smooth or continuous-time state selected only in the selection split. B2 could reveal nonlinear ordering, irregular timing, coupled hepatic/renal movement and patient heterogeneity that B1’s first/last/change summaries lose; B1 remains necessary to show whether any gain is more than a flexible-model artifact. CPU-first is appropriate for aggregation, sparse competing-risk fits and low-dimensional state fitting. The measured audit took 70.8 seconds on 4 CPUs/16 GiB; full feature construction, convergence and 500 bootstrap replicates are unverified. The planned solver envelope is 16 CPUs/256 GiB and up to 28,800 seconds, with GPU optional. If repeated state fitting materially benefits from acceleration, request one allocated A100 and use cuda:0; GPU use is a feasibility choice, not evidence for the hypothesis.

The primary learned/mechanistic deliverable is newly fitted B0, B1 and B2 with locked all-admission probabilities, not a reproduced paper model. A text/image multimodal model is deferred because HCC images and validated time-stamped clinical labels are unavailable. A causal treatment-effect design is deferred because indication, receipt, counterfactual eligibility and adjudicated outcomes are unavailable. A validated liver score is deferred because units/reference ranges are absent. These are dependency-based deferrals.

## Evaluation, falsification and gates

Report held-out multiclass log loss, multiclass Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, observed versus predicted state probabilities, the standardized all-admission P24_72 contrast, the conditional R12 contrast, and B2-minus-B1 changes. Use at least 500 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples with a declared locked-prediction/refit policy. Report flow counts, state support, duplicate/missing/invalid time rates, same-time ties, exact-boundary counts, U-state bounds, and calibration by era/service only when supported.

The solver must emit provenance proving:

- B1/B2 lab timestamps are strictly in [0,12), and no post-12 field enters them;
- Stage 1 contains only R12 admissions with no established pre-12 transition;
- primary procedure states use native Start Time, not End Time or order time;
- no procedure with missing/invalid time is silently converted to no procedure;
- all eligible admissions are present in the Stage-0/all-admission flow.

Falsifications and sensitivity analyses are:

- permute lab times within patient and assay, then values within assay; a physiology-specific gain should attenuate;
- permute terminal states within era/service; gains should disappear;
- compare value features with count/density/missingness-only features; a gain that disappears after opportunity adjustment is observation-process evidence;
- add the forbidden [12,24) lab window as a leakage sentinel only in a labeled diagnostic, never the primary model; any apparent gain from it demonstrates the importance of the lock;
- repeat exact-boundary exclusion, midnight handling and earliest-plausible/no-event U-state bounds;
- remove one procedure string at a time in the gated secondary dictionary and retain X;
- permute K/M/N/U order tiers within era/service;
- use a same-source routine-workflow endpoint as a negative control only if it has adequate support.

Supportive evidence requires coherent joins and time audits, adequate R12 and state support, calibrated B1, positive B1-minus-B0 held-out improvement with uncertainty excluding zero, a stable P24_72 direction, attenuation under timestamp/label permutation, and no dominance by lab density or U states. B2 is supportive only if it is calibrated and improves over B1 with stable posterior state interpretation; complexity alone is not support.

Adverse evidence includes null or opposite score differences, poor calibration, gains persisting after terminal-label or timestamp permutation, equivalence to density-only models, sensitivity to midnight/exact-boundary rules, or a result driven by one procedure string. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse R12/post-buffer states, high unresolved-time mass, unstable lexical dictionaries, nonconvergence, wide intervals, inadequate support across services/eras, or inability to distinguish assay identity from unit differences.

Computational outputs can establish source hashes, schemas, joins, cohort flow, time-safe feature provenance, model fitting, predictions, scores, calibration, uncertainty and falsifications. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for HCC confirmation, stage, tumor burden, resectability, indication, intent, completion, response, adverse events, mortality, outside care, treatment benefit, clinical utility, causal effects and deployment.

## Alternatives retained and actual selection rationale

The transparent B0 is retained because it gives an interpretable admission/pre-admission counterfactual for the information question. B1 is retained because it is transparent enough to diagnose whether gains arise from assay levels, slopes or observation opportunity. B2 is selected as the only substantive alternative because irregular timing and coupled hepatic/renal state are scientifically relevant to this question and can be fitted from the same available inputs. Its compute estimate is planning-level, not measured; a bounded future fit must report convergence and resource use.

The deferred text model would require validated time anchoring and labels in clinical documents; the deferred image model would require unavailable HCC images. The causal model would require missing indication, treatment receipt and counterfactual outcome data. Evidence that would justify revisiting these choices is a time-stamped adjudicated chart sample, validated units/reference ranges, sufficient image capture, or a residual B1 error pattern showing reproducible nonlinear irregular dynamics with adequate event support.

Completion is established only by the frozen cohort/transition definitions, newly fitted B0/B1/B2 models, locked held-out outputs, uncertainty, and falsification reports. A positive result is neither required for completion nor sufficient for clinical adoption.
