# Repeat metabolic change beyond current levels: a UK Biobank prospective cardiorenal trajectory experiment

Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`

## Question, advance and evidence boundary

When a participant has a repeat blood-biomarker assessment, does the within-person change from the first to the second assessment improve prospective identification of a first recorded cardiorenal/metabolic multimorbidity event beyond age, sex and the second/current biomarker levels?

This is a monitoring and risk-triage question: after a current assessment, does retaining the prior value contain decision-relevant information? A positive result would motivate a prospective trajectory-aware monitoring study; a negative result would support current levels alone for this prognostic task. Neither result establishes that ordering a repeat test, changing treatment, or acting at a particular threshold improves outcomes.

The strongest available evidence supports only that the paired field IDs, `eid` joins and outcome code/date arrays exist, plus the inherited measured raw availability bounds. It does not support the claim that these fields are particular analytes, that change is biologically deterioration, or that change predicts events. The unresolved claim is the held-out incremental predictive and decision value of change.

## Prespecified models and evaluation

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold split based on a documented hash of `eid`. All imputation, scaling, feature selection and tuning are training-fold local. No outcome or death field may enter exposure construction.

- M0, the transparent baseline: age, sex and the eight valid instance-1 values; same cause-specific survival model family for target and death.
- M1, the primary longitudinal test: M0 plus the eight standardized differences `instance1 - instance0`; same model family. The joint change block is the primary association test; field-specific coefficients are secondary and multiplicity-controlled.
- M2, the substantive learned alternative: the same inputs as M1 plus prespecified current-by-change interactions, fit with fold-tuned discrete-time gradient-boosted cause-specific hazard models. It produces the same competing-risk cumulative-incidence output. M2 can reveal threshold and discordant trajectory patterns that additive M1 loses; it is not a complexity bonus.

Report held-out Brier, time-dependent AUC, average precision where defined, calibration intercept/slope/plots, joint change-block inference, decision curves and 10-year/domain-specific analogues. Use participant-clustered bootstrap intervals over held-out predictions and report fold heterogeneity. M2 grouped importance is descriptive; only patterns stable across folds and outcome definitions may be discussed.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted M0/M1/M2 models, fixed fold assignments, fold-local preprocessing records, held-out predictions at both horizons, flow/endpoint counts, calibration and decision-curve outputs, uncertainty intervals, grouped M2 pattern summaries, and machine-readable leakage/permutation controls. Readiness or packaging is not a scientific result.

## Falsification and interpretation

Hard checks: every incident date is after 2013-12-31; code/date pairing uses the same array index; semantic and timing gates precede prospective labels; exposure construction cannot read outcome/death columns; splits are participant-level and preprocessing is fold-local; age/sex-stratified shuffling of change vectors removes any M1 increment within uncertainty; and independently breaking code/date position pairing or permuting outcome dates cannot improve the primary result. Audit complete-case missingness and eligible-versus-complete characteristics without changing the primary estimand.

Supportive evidence requires a passed timing/semantic gate, DeltaBS5 95% upper confidence bound below zero, positive DeltaNB5 at both 0.05 and 0.10 without material calibration degradation, and compatible improvement at 10 years or in at least one domain analysis. Stable M2 threshold/discordance patterns strengthen explanation but are not required for the linear change claim.

Adverse evidence is near-null or worse M1 performance, an interval excluding clinically useful improvement, absent decision-curve gain, or disappearance under the age/sex-stratified permutation or code/date leakage controls. This falsifies the added-information claim for this operational panel and favors current values alone.

Results are inconclusive if a gate fails, fewer than 100 primary events remain, follow-up is inadequate, intervals span useful improvement and harm, estimates vary materially across folds or endpoint definitions, or missingness/complete-case handling changes the conclusion. Inconclusive does not mean biomarker trajectories have no clinical value.
