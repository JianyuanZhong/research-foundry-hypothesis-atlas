# Direction-A cystatin-C discordance incremental-information experiment

## Lineage and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`, whose managed execution and repaired independent aggregate audit established the exact selected-repeat-attender cohort and found a positive but strongly attenuated Direction-A association. The parent used an unregularized linear main-effects logistic adjustment. This child asks the clinically sharper next question: does baseline cystatin-C discordance provide reproducible out-of-sample information beyond a deliberately strong, flexible baseline model for creatinine-derived threshold proximity, age, sex, and elapsed time? It therefore replaces adjusted in-sample association as the principal evidentiary target with paired participant-level cross-fitted prediction and prespecified absolute-risk/capacity estimands. It does not change the parent’s UKB snapshot, source joins, Direction-A restriction, inclusive repeat interval, q=60 equations, selected-attender interpretation, or aggregate-only boundary.

The parent’s strongest supported claim remains that baseline discordance is associated with repeat creatinine-derived threshold crossing in this selected cohort under its fixed linear model and computable gates (standardized RD 0.02020; Bonferroni interval [0.00872, 0.03296]), not that cystatin C improves prediction or clinical decisions. The unresolved claim tested here is narrower and falsifiable: among the same selected qualifying repeat attenders, adding the binary baseline cystatin-C discordance to an unfitted-in-advance flexible comparator improves prospective out-of-sample absolute-risk prediction and/or risk concentration at a fixed capacity. Association, CKD, chronicity, measured GFR, causal effects, benefit from testing, and clinical utility remain untested.

## Frozen nested model classes

The model classes and all preprocessing are fixed before outcome evaluation. The target cohort is always exactly `S_A`; the folds do not redefine it. Standardize continuous covariates using training-fold means and standard deviations only. Construct natural cubic spline bases separately in each training fold for baseline eGFRcr, baseline age, and elapsed years, with exactly 4 degrees of freedom each. Boundary knots are training-fold minima/maxima and interior knots are training-fold empirical 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles; if a fold cannot form the prescribed basis, the run fails rather than changing knots. Sex is a binary main effect. There are no post hoc transformations, winsorization, threshold searches, interactions, feature selection, or hyperparameter tuning.

- **M0 (frozen comparator):** penalized logistic regression with intercept, natural-cubic-spline terms for unrounded `eGFRcr_0`, age0, and elapsed years, plus sex. Use one fixed ridge penalty, applied to non-intercept coefficients, selected before analysis and never optimized from these outcomes. A reproducible implementation may use `C=1` after each continuous basis column is training-standardized; it must record the exact solver, convergence rule, and penalty convention. M0 is the prospectively frozen flexible baseline model.
- **M1 (nested discordance model):** M0 plus the single binary term `D`. The same fold-specific preprocessing and fixed penalty are used. M1 is nested by one added prespecified exposure term; no cystatin-C value, eGFRcys magnitude, interaction, or alternative threshold enters the primary analysis.

The fixed penalty protects against separation and optimism but is not evidence that the chosen regularization is optimal. As a computational sensitivity, report an identically cross-fitted unpenalized M0/M1 only if it converges in every fold; it cannot replace the primary fixed-capacity estimand and cannot be selected based on results. Complete/quasi-complete separation, nonconvergence, invalid basis construction, or failed finite predictions is a failure/inconclusive result, not a reason to modify the class.

## Leakage-proof repeated cross-fitting

Generate participant-level, outcome-stratified 5-fold splits using 10 frozen random seeds, yielding 50 held-out predictions per model (each participant has one held-out prediction per repeat). Stratification uses Y only to balance folds and is performed after S_A is frozen; no exposure, covariate, prediction, or outcome-derived tuning is used. For each repeat and fold, fit M0 and M1 only in the other four folds, learn all basis knots/scaling there, and predict the held-out fold. Preserve the paired held-out predictions from the same rows for M0 and M1. Never report apparent/full-sample fitted predictions as evidence of incremental performance.

The primary prediction estimand averages the 10-repeat cross-fitted loss/performance estimates, with participant-level dependence handled by paired repeated-split uncertainty. Because repeated splits are not independent replications, do not multiply the nominal sample size by ten or treat the 50 predictions as 50 independent people.

## Primary estimands and gates

The primary estimand is the paired out-of-sample difference `M1 - M0` in Brier score (negative is improvement) and log loss (negative is improvement), averaged across participants within each repeat and then across repeats. Report the paired difference and a 95% participant-level bootstrap interval. Also report the cross-fitted calibration intercept, calibration slope, calibration-in-the-large and observed event rate for each model; calibration is descriptive and is computed from held-out predictions only. A model with a tiny loss improvement but materially worse calibration fails the primary reproducibility gate.

The clinically interpretable absolute-risk estimand is the held-out mean predicted risk difference `mean(p1-p0)` and the corresponding paired mean absolute change `mean(abs(p1-p0))`, with bootstrap intervals. These quantify changed estimated absolute risk, not a treatment effect or a claim that any person should be tested. For risk concentration at fixed capacity, within each held-out fold and repeat, rank only held-out predictions and select exactly the top 10% of S_A (primary capacity) and top 5% (prespecified secondary capacity; report both, not choose one after seeing results). For each model/capacity report selected fraction, observed Y risk, event capture, and risk ratio versus the unselected remainder. The incremental capacity estimand is the paired difference in observed held-out risk and event capture between M1 and M0 selections, with intervals. This is a capacity/triage-information analysis, not clinical utility: no treatment benefit, harm, cost, or patient preference is available.

Discrimination (cross-fitted ROC AUC with bootstrap interval) is secondary and cannot establish clinical value. Do not use NRI, IDI, an outcome-dependent risk threshold, or a post hoc cutpoint. If a probability threshold is desired for an absolute-risk illustration, freeze it externally before execution; none is available here, so capacity rather than an arbitrary risk threshold is primary.

## Uncertainty, multiplicity, and support rule

Use a participant-level paired nonparametric bootstrap over `eid` clusters, resampling complete S_A participants and carrying their paired held-out predictions within each repeat. Use at least 2,000 successful replicates for the primary loss differences and 1,000 for secondary calibration/capacity summaries; if full-procedure refitting is computationally feasible, repeat the entire fixed split-and-fit procedure in each bootstrap replicate and state that explicitly. Otherwise, the prediction-bootstrap interval is conditional on the frozen splits/model fits and must be labeled so; it does not cover all model-training variation. Never publish rows, IDs, predictions, weights, or notes.

Control the primary two-loss family (Brier and log loss) at two-sided alpha .05 with Holm; treat the fixed 10% capacity comparison as a separately labeled secondary family and adjust within that family. Predeclare directionality: improvement means lower loss, non-worsened calibration, and positive 10% held-out risk concentration/event capture. A primary claim of incremental information requires both loss metrics to improve in the same direction with Holm-adjusted evidence and no clinically material calibration deterioration, plus the 95% interval for the 10% held-out selected-risk difference not crossing zero. If these gates are too stringent for the event count, the result is inconclusive rather than downgraded to an association claim.

For transparency, report point estimates and intervals even when gates fail. A null or adverse loss difference, unstable signs across repeats, calibration deterioration, or failure of capacity concentration falsifies the claim that D adds reproducible predictive/decision information under this protocol, even if a coefficient or in-sample likelihood-ratio test is positive. The parent association is not reinterpreted as incremental prediction.

## Falsification and interpretation matrix

- **Supportive:** M1 improves both held-out primary losses under Holm, retains acceptable calibration, and increases held-out 10% risk concentration with an interval excluding zero. This supports reproducible incremental information for this protocol-derived repeat-threshold outcome in selected attenders only. It does not support cystatin-C testing utility, CKD diagnosis, chronicity, causality, measured GFR, or nonattender risk.
- **Adverse:** M1 worsens either primary loss, materially worsens calibration, has repeat-inconsistent effects, or fails to improve fixed-capacity risk concentration. This would indicate that the parent association is adequately represented by flexible creatinine proximity/age/sex/time or is not useful out of sample; it would actively falsify a claim of incremental predictive information here.
- **Inconclusive:** split/model failures, insufficient successful bootstrap replicates, intervals spanning practically relevant values, or discordant metrics (for example, AUC improvement with worse calibration or a tiny loss change with no capacity gain). This preserves uncertainty and does not convert a nominal coefficient p-value into a positive finding.

A negative result remains scientifically valuable because it distinguishes marker discordance association from incremental information. Any clinically meaningful minimum improvement must be specified by domain experts in a later study; this dataset supplies no validated decision threshold or harm/benefit trade-off, so no claim of net benefit is computable.
