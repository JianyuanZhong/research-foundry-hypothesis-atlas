# Repeat cystatin-C/creatinine discordance and subsequent inpatient-coded AKI: a gated landmark study

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`
Status: prospective observational hypothesis; no fitted result is claimed.

## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

Creatinine-based filtration estimates can appear reassuring when cystatin-C-based estimates are lower, but it is unresolved whether a *new* discordance on repeat testing identifies future kidney vulnerability in community-dwelling adults after an initially reassuring assessment. The decision relevance is limited but real: if the finding is reproducibly associated with later AKI beyond the prior phenotype, it could justify confirmatory cystatin C and medication/nephrotoxin review in a narrowly defined high-risk group. It would not by itself establish that testing or a management change improves outcomes.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UK Biobank participants with baseline eGFRcr >=60 and eGFRcys >=60, alive and free of coded AKI/CKD through a valid paired repeat assessment, participants with repeat eGFRcr >=60 but repeat eGFRcys <60 have a higher adjusted 5-year cumulative incidence of a first inpatient-coded AKI (N17.x) after the repeat date than participants with repeat eGFRcr >=60 and eGFRcys >=60. The contrast is larger among participants with low baseline grip strength.

The primary estimand is this conditional risk contrast in the repeat-observed landmark cohort. The hypothesis is not that cystatin C is causal, that discordance measures low muscle mass, that an AKI code is adjudicated AKI, or that a repeat-test strategy is clinically effective.

## Evidence, distinction from established claims, and advance

I inspected Haines et al., “Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness,” at `references/expert-seeds/papers/kidney-function/article.readable.txt` and its PDF. In 38 mechanically ventilated ICU patients, serial creatinine/cystatin C, iohexol clearance, and rectus-femoris ultrasound supported the narrower physiological claim that prolonged critical illness and muscle loss can make creatinine-based eGFR overestimate measured kidney function and diverge from cystatin-C-based estimates. That study does not establish future AKI prediction in UKB, the value of repeat testing, or interaction with community grip strength.

A current Europe PMC search was frozen as source `[source checksum]`; it returned heterogeneous related literature and did not provide a clean full-text UKB replication. It is used only as a novelty prompt, not as proof. The substantive advance is a temporal, code-bound test of whether *change to discordance* after an initially concordant-preserved result adds prognostic information, with explicit conditioning on repeat observation and a primary estimand that cannot be mistaken for treatment effect.

## Population and time boundaries

Use baseline instance 0 only. Baseline time zero is recorded assessment date `53-0.0`, not row order or an assumed calendar date. Require nonmissing baseline age, sex, assessment date, creatinine, cystatin C, left/right grip, and primary covariates.

The dynamic landmark cohort requires:

1. baseline eGFRcr >=60 and eGFRcys >=60;
2. no aligned inpatient N17.x, N18.x, or N19.x diagnosis on or before baseline;
3. `53-1.0` strictly after `53-0.0`, valid paired repeat creatinine `30700-1.0` and cystatin C `30720-1.0`;
4. survival through the repeat date and no qualifying N17/N18/N19 diagnosis on or before that repeat date.

The interval from baseline to repeat is not counted as outcome follow-up in the landmark analysis. Events in that interval are reported in the flow/selection audit and make a person ineligible for the post-repeat landmark analysis. This prevents immortal time from being assigned to a repeat-exposure category. The estimand is explicitly conditional on reaching a valid repeat assessment, not a population-wide effect of offering a repeat test.

Follow from `53-1.0` until first qualifying event, death, last available outcome date, or 2023-03-31, whichever comes first; the primary horizon is 5 years. Diagnosis dates must be strictly after the landmark. A 365-day post-repeat washout is a sensitivity estimand for reverse causation, not a replacement primary outcome.

## Exposure construction

After unit verification, convert creatinine from µmol/L to mg/dL and calculate the sex-specific 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine and cystatin-C equations in ml/min/1.73 m2, recording the exact formula and constants. Do not use race adjustment. Reject impossible/nonpositive values only under the verified field-specific rule and report counts.

Among baseline-concordant-preserved participants, define:

- reference: repeat eGFRcr >=60 and repeat eGFRcys >=60;
- primary exposure: repeat eGFRcr >=60 and repeat eGFRcys <60 (new discordant-low);
- secondary transition: repeat eGFRcr <60, described separately and never pooled into the primary contrast.

A continuous secondary exposure is the repeat-minus-baseline change in (eGFRcys - eGFRcr), using a prespecified restricted cubic spline. Grip is the maximum valid baseline `46-0.0`/`47-0.0`, with sex-specific 20th percentile defined once in the eligible baseline cohort; it is a reserve proxy, not muscle mass. Repeat grip is exploratory only.

## Outcomes and analysis

The primary outcome is the earliest aligned inpatient date strictly after repeat time zero whose paired code begins `N17`. Secondary outcomes are first `N18`, all-cause death, and AKI-or-CKD composite. They cannot replace an infeasible primary AKI analysis.

First report a CONSORT-like flow: baseline count, baseline biomarker validity, baseline-preserved count, repeat-date availability/order, exclusions for interval and pre-landmark diagnoses, exposure-cell counts, follow-up, deaths, and primary events. Compare eligible repeat-observed versus eligible-but-not-observed people on baseline variables and show baseline-to-repeat interval distribution.

The primary model is a cause-specific Cox model for first N17.x AKI with death as a competing event. Include repeat exposure, baseline age spline, sex, BMI, systolic/diastolic BP, HbA1c, self-reported diabetes, baseline eGFRcr/eGFRcys, baseline grip, and exposure-by-grip interaction. Derive standardized 5-year cumulative-incidence risks and risk difference/ratio with 95% uncertainty intervals; show cause-specific HR and cumulative-incidence curves. Use Fine-Gray only as sensitivity. Use participant bootstrap for standardized contrasts and assess proportional-hazards violations.

The baseline-only comparator is fitted in exactly the same landmark cohort using only baseline values. The dynamic comparator adds repeat eGFR values/change terms. Incremental calibration, Brier score, optimism-corrected discrimination, and decision-curve net benefit are secondary descriptive checks. No AUC or net-benefit threshold is a success criterion for the etiologic/prognostic hypothesis. A bounded elastic-net Cox model may be fit with a fixed eid-hash 70/15/15 split, all preprocessing inside training folds, and the same baseline/dynamic feature sets; it is optional and CPU-feasible, not a reason to use a GPU.

Address informative repeat observation by (a) keeping the primary estimand conditional, (b) publishing the selection flow, and (c) repeating with inverse-probability-of-repeat weights from baseline variables, prespecifying truncation and reporting weight diagnostics. Weighting does not remove unmeasured selection bias. Complete-case and imputed analyses must keep imputation and tuning inside training folds.

## Falsification and decision rules

Support requires all of the following: at least 100 primary N17 events overall and at least 10 in each primary exposure cell; a positive clinically nontrivial adjusted 5-year contrast in the prespecified direction; uncertainty not simultaneously compatible with clinically trivial and harmful effects; broadly consistent direction after 365-day washout, 1–6-year interval restriction, and repeat-observation weighting; and no equal-or-larger association for the fixed negative-control inpatient outcome of traumatic injury (S00–T14). A calibration/AUC improvement alone is not support.

Adverse evidence is an opposite AKI contrast, a null contrast with adequate event support, loss under washout/interval restriction/weighting, or an association as large or larger for traumatic injury. This argues against kidney-specific hidden vulnerability. A failure of the baseline phenotype in the same landmark cohort means the repeat result cannot rescue that premise.

Inconclusive evidence is any failure to verify units/coding or align code/date fields; fewer than the event thresholds; fewer than 10 people in a primary cell; unstable weights; materially violated time ordering; or intervals too wide to distinguish clinically relevant benefit from harm. Do not substitute CKD, death, or predictive metrics for an infeasible AKI analysis.

Computation can establish availability, filtering, temporal ordering, code-based inpatient associations, competing-risk estimates, calibration, and whether gates are met. It cannot establish AKI stage, outpatient AKI, measured GFR, cystatin-C specificity, muscle mass, causality, representativeness, medication safety, or clinical utility. Those claims require adjudicated AKI/CKD, linked outpatient laboratory trajectories, measured GFR, inflammation and muscle measures, treatment/medication data, expert review, external validation, and a prospective testing-and-management study.

## Demonstration and seed dispositions

- The natural-history transformer demonstration was read in its available article text (with XML/PDF available). It motivates longitudinal ordering, competing morbidity, and calibration. A concrete adaptation is this repeat-date landmark/update analysis; reproducing a generative transformer is not justified by the structured UKB fields.
- The cancer demonstration’s main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable. I inspected its supplement and metadata only. Its multimodal imaging/treatment-response setting is not pursued because this snapshot has no raw imaging, verified narrative extraction, staging, treatment records, or validated cancer labels.
- The Bayesian longitudinal EHR/genetic demonstration was read in its article text and available supplement/reporting materials. It motivates time-aware likelihoods, leakage control, selection adjustment, and calibration. This proposal adapts those principles transparently, but does not import its genetic claims because local `genomics` is derived metadata, not a validated PRS or sequence bundle.
- UKB seed 03 is the direct parent question and is retained but repaired: “severe AKI” becomes first inpatient-coded N17.x, grip is not treated as muscle mass, and the repeat/landmark estimand is explicit. Seeds 01, 02, 04–10 were assessed as imported hypotheses; their needed proteomics/organ-age, imaging, accelerometry, CHIP/sequence, validated PRS, or treatment/staging dependencies are not available as verified modalities for this exact experiment, so they are preserved as non-use directions rather than forced into this child.
