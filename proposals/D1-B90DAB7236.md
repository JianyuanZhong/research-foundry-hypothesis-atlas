# Provisional repaired eICU monitoring-gap hypothesis

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a continuous eICU stay who are physiologically unstable at a prespecified hourly index time, a reduction in laboratory-monitoring intensity during the immediately preceding 12 hours may be associated with a higher risk of a time-local clinical deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond measured current severity and recent support. This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim.

The key rival is that reduced testing reflects end-of-life/de-escalation, discharge/transfer, or sparse documentation rather than a monitoring-process signal. The study must distinguish these explanations where the available data permit it.

## Evidence-supported versus untested

The parent and catalog support the existence of longitudinal eICU tables joined by `patientunitstayid`, with candidate laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, care-plan, discharge, mortality, and hospital fields. This supports a bounded feasibility study, not the hypothesis. No cohort analysis has been run. Exact source headers and representative rows, a clinically defensible deterioration endpoint, competing-transition rules, and exactly three inspected public works remain to be completed in this repair.

## Population and time

Use adult `patient` stays with a valid unit interval and `unitdischargeoffset` greater than 18 hours. Restrict the primary cohort to continuous ICU stays with no transfer or early discharge before the outcome window. Index at hourly times from 6 through `unitdischargeoffset - 12` hours. Require at least one valid vital and one laboratory observation in the preceding 24 hours, while retaining an explicit documentation-missing indicator rather than treating missingness as zero testing.

Exposure: testing reduction in `(t-12,t]` versus `(t-24,t-12]`, using laboratory result times. Count distinct laboratory episodes/analytes, with revisions not counted as new tests unless their result time is distinct. Primary reduction is zero current distinct-analyte count after a positive prior count, or at least a 50% reduction when the prior interval has at least two episodes. Continuous change and episode-count analyses are secondary.

## Endpoint and competing transitions

The primary endpoint is the first qualifying deterioration in `(t,t+12]`: new or escalated respiratory support from `respiratoryCare`/`respiratoryCharting`, or new/escalated vasoactive or other clinically relevant infusion support from `infusionDrug`, with the exact drug/status/rate rules to be frozen after the header and representative-row audit. Death, discharge, and transfer before the event are competing transitions, not ordinary non-events. `apachePatientResult` mortality fields are secondary outcomes only. The endpoint will be defined as an observed transition, not as adjudicated clinical deterioration; clinical adjudication is unavailable and must remain an explicit limitation.

## Rival interpretation

Use `carePlanEOL` timestamps and `activeupondischarge` to define prespecified EOL-context strata and a sensitivity analysis excluding documented EOL plans before `t`. Sparse or absent EOL documentation is not evidence of absent intent. Also model discharge/transfer context and recent support de-escalation. A monitoring-process interpretation requires reproducible held-out-hospital association after state and transition handling, with null negative controls; it does not establish causality or a recommendation to increase testing.

## Baseline and alternative

The simple baseline is an adjusted discrete-time logistic model at the same hourly index times, with competing-event censoring, hospital fixed or clustered effects, and clustered uncertainty, using only information available at or before `t`: recent vitals, respiratory state, recent infusion/support, age, sex, unit type, and hospital.

The substantive alternative is a longitudinal recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard model with time-varying testing intensity and physiologic trajectories. It can reveal whether any association is transient, persistent, or preceded by a trajectory that the single-index baseline loses. It is selected for the scientific uncertainty about timing and persistence, not predictive gain alone. Both use the same hospital-held-out validation, calibration, discrimination, absolute risk difference, and uncertainty intervals. Negative controls include within-hospital permutation of testing intervals preserving stay length/outcome, a post-index testing exposure that should not predict prior deterioration, and a non-laboratory documentation-timestamp control where available.

Supportive evidence would be a reproducible held-out-hospital association after state and EOL/discharge handling with null negative controls. Adverse evidence would be attenuation, instability, or disappearance after explicit rival adjustment. Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals, sparse events, or inability to distinguish interface interruption from clinical intent. None establishes causality.
