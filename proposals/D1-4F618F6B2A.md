# Discordant Recovery After Acute Kidney Injury: Urine Output vs Creatinine Trajectories in MIMIC-IV

## Scientific Question

In critically ill patients with AKI, does creatinine-based recovery (falling creatinine) without concurrent urine output recovery (persistent oliguria) identify a subgroup at substantially higher risk of in-hospital death and renal replacement therapy (RRT) initiation than creatinine-based recovery alone?

## Clinical Importance

- AKI affects 20-50% of ICU patients; incomplete renal recovery is a major driver of mortality and long-term kidney failure.
- Current clinical practice and most risk scores rely on creatinine as the primary marker of renal recovery.
- Creatinine is confounded by muscle mass loss, fluid status, and tubular secretion in critical illness [K1]. Haines et al. (2023) showed eGFR-creatinine overestimates true GFR by 2-fold in prolonged ICU stays [K1].
- Urine output is a direct, real-time measure of renal filtration that is not confounded by muscle mass. KDIGO guidelines include urine output as an independent AKI criterion, but recovery assessments rarely incorporate it.
- If discordant recovery (creatinine improving, urine output not) carries excess risk, this would:
  1. Identify patients who appear to be recovering but are not, warranting closer monitoring or earlier RRT.
  2. Inform the design of AKI recovery trials and risk stratification tools.
  3. Challenge the assumption that creatinine recovery is sufficient to declare renal recovery.

## Unresolved Claim

The strongest existing evidence shows:
- Creatinine-based AKI is associated with mortality (well-established).
- Urine output is an independent predictor of AKI severity and mortality [K2][K3]. Tang et al. (2026) showed urine output is an independent predictor of CRRT initiation in sepsis-associated AKI [K2]. Yuan et al. (2026) showed oliguria is one of the most significant predictors of 30-day mortality in AKI patients [K3].
- Creatinine overestimates kidney function in critical illness (K1: Haines et al. 2023).

What is NOT established:
- Whether the *discordance* between creatinine recovery and urine output recovery carries independent prognostic information beyond either measure alone.
- Whether "creatinine-recovered but oliguric" patients have worse outcomes than "creatinine-recovered and euvolemic" patients, after adjusting for illness severity.
- The magnitude of this discordance effect and whether it is clinically actionable.

## Hypothesis

Among ICU patients with AKI (KDIGO stage ≥1) who achieve creatinine recovery (≥25% decline from peak or return to baseline) by day 7, those with persistent oliguria (urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥24h in the 48h before assessment) have higher in-hospital mortality and higher RRT initiation rates than those with concurrent urine output recovery (urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥24h), after adjusting for age, sex, APACHE II/SOFA, vasopressor use, and mechanical ventilation.

## Study Design

### Population
- Adult ICU patients (age ≥18) in MIMIC-IV 3.1.
- AKI defined by KDIGO: ≥0.3 mg/dL rise in creatinine within 48h, or ≥1.5× baseline within 7 days, or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6h.
- Baseline creatinine: earliest value in the 72h before AKI onset, or first ICU value if no prior.
- Exclusions: ICU stay <48h (insufficient time for recovery assessment), pre-existing dialysis (no baseline creatinine), age <18.

### Time Zero
- AKI onset: time of first creatinine meeting KDIGO criteria (or first oliguria episode if urine-output-defined).
- Recovery assessment window: 72h to 168h (day 3-7) after AKI onset.

### Exposure (Recovery Phenotype)
At the recovery assessment window (day 3-7):
1. **Creatinine recovery**: ≥25% decline from peak creatinine OR return to ≤1.2× baseline.
2. **Urine output recovery**: median urine output in the 48h before assessment ≥0.5 mL/kg/h (using outputevents itemid 226559 Foley, 226560 Void, 226561 Condom Cath).
3. **Phenotypes**:
   - Concordant recovery: creatinine recovered AND urine output recovered.
   - Discordant recovery: creatinine recovered BUT urine output NOT recovered (persistent oliguria).
   - No recovery: creatinine NOT recovered (regardless of urine output).
   - Urine-output-only recovery: urine output recovered but creatinine NOT recovered (exploratory).

### Outcomes
- Primary: In-hospital mortality (admissions.deathtime not null, or hospital_expire_flag=1).
- Secondary: RRT initiation (procedureevents or chartevents for hemodialysis/CRRT within 7 days of recovery assessment), ICU length of stay, 28-day mortality.

### Covariates
- Age, sex (patients table).
- APACHE II or SOFA at ICU admission (chartevents, labevents).
- Vasopressor use: norepinephrine, dopamine, epinephrine, vasopressin (inputevents itemids 221906, 221662, 221289, 222315).
- Mechanical ventilation (chartevents).
- Fluid balance: net fluid input (inputevents crystalloids/colloids) minus output (outputevents) in the 48h before assessment.
- Lactate (labevents itemid 50813, 52442, 53154).
- MAP (chartevents itemid 220052).

### Analysis
1. **Descriptive**: Compare baseline characteristics across the four recovery phenotypes.
2. **Primary analysis**: Multivariable logistic regression for in-hospital mortality, with phenotype as exposure, adjusting for covariates. Report odds ratios with 95% CIs.
3. **Secondary**: Cox proportional hazards for time to RRT initiation (censored at ICU discharge or death).
4. **Sensitivity**:
   - Restrict to patients with ≥48h of urine output data.
   - Restrict to septic patients (Sepsis-3 criteria from ICD codes + vitals).
   - Vary the urine output threshold (0.3, 0.5, 0.7 mL/kg/h).
   - Vary the recovery window (day 2-5, day 4-7).
5. **Interaction**: Test whether the discordance effect is modified by AKI stage, vasopressor use, or mechanical ventilation.

### Falsification Criteria
- The hypothesis is falsified if:
  - Discordant recovery (creatinine recovered, oliguria) shows NO significant difference in mortality or RRT vs concordant recovery (adjusted OR <1.2 or >0.8, 95% CI crossing 1).
  - The discordance effect is fully explained by fluid overload (i.e., disappears after adjusting for net fluid balance).
  - The effect is driven entirely by a small subgroup (e.g., <10% of patients) with extreme values.

### Method Comparison: Baseline vs Alternative

**Baseline (fixed-time creatinine recovery)**:
- Define recovery as a single creatinine value at a fixed time point (e.g., day 7).
- Compare mortality between "recovered" and "not recovered" groups.
- Limitation: ignores the trajectory, ignores urine output, ignores the timing of recovery.

**Alternative (joint creatinine + urine output trajectory)**:
- Define recovery phenotype using BOTH creatinine and urine output over a window.
- Captures the discordance that the baseline misses.
- Reveals whether urine output adds independent prognostic information beyond creatinine.
- This is the scientifically substantive alternative: it tests whether the *relationship* between the two measures (concordant vs discordant) carries information that either measure alone does not.

**What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses**:
- The baseline treats "creatinine recovered" as a binary state. The alternative distinguishes "creatinine recovered with urine output" from "creatinine recovered without urine output."
- If the discordant group has worse outcomes, this shows that creatinine recovery is an incomplete marker of renal recovery, and that urine output provides independent prognostic information.
- If the discordant group does NOT have worse outcomes, this suggests that creatinine recovery is sufficient, and urine output adds no incremental value in this context.

- No future information: all covariates measured before or at the recovery assessment time.

### Limitations
1. **No measured GFR**: Without iohexol clearance or cystatin C, we can only identify *suspected* discordant recovery. [K1] shows creatinine overestimates GFR, but we cannot quantify the true GFR.
2. **Urine output measurement**: Foley catheter output is the primary source, but not all patients have continuous monitoring. Patients without urine output data are excluded from the urine-output-based phenotypes.
3. **Fluid balance**: Net fluid balance is a proxy for volume status, not a direct measure. Patients with fluid overload may have low urine output for reasons unrelated to kidney function.
4. **Selection bias**: Patients who survive to day 7 are a selected population. The analysis is conditional on survival to the recovery window.
5. **Single center**: MIMIC-IV is a single-center dataset. External validation is needed.

### Key References

[K1] Haines RW, Fowler AJ, Liang K, Pearse RM, Larsson AO, Puthucheary Z, Prowle JR. "Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness." Clin J Am Soc Nephrol. 2023;18(8):997-1005. DOI: 10.2215/CJN.0000000000000203. PMID: 37256861.
- Claim supported: eGFR-creatinine systematically overestimates kidney function after prolonged critical illness; cystatin C better estimates true GFR.
- Limitation: Small single-center study (n=38); does not test the discordance hypothesis.

[K2] Tang Y, Zhang S, Wang Q, Zhang Q, Ji L, Li R, Fang M. "An interpretable machine learning model for early prediction of subsequent observed CRRT initiation in patients with sepsis-associated acute kidney injury." Clin Exp Med. 2026;26(1):345. DOI: 10.1007/s10238-026-02305-1. PMID: 42814147.
- Claim supported: Urine output is an independent predictor of CRRT initiation in sepsis-associated AKI, alongside creatinine, AKI stage, SOFA, lactate, RDW, and SpO2.
- Limitation: Predicts CRRT initiation, not mortality; does not test the discordance hypothesis.

[K3] Yuan M, Zhong L, Min J, Ni M, Yao Y. "Development and validation of machine learning models to predict 30-day mortality in patients with cardiac arrest complicated by acute kidney injury." Clinics (Sao Paulo). 2026;81:101129. DOI: 10.1016/j.clinsp.2026.101129. PMID: 42710354.
- Claim supported: Oliguria is one of the most significant predictors of 30-day mortality in AKI patients (SHAP analysis), alongside APACHE II, lactate, anion gap, RDW, and norepinephrine.
- Limitation: Specific to cardiac arrest patients; does not test the discordance hypothesis.
