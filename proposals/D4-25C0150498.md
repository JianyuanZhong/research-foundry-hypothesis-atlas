# Episode 2 alternative direction: first-CBC cancer-type signal versus repeat-measurement selection

Status: independent UKB branch; proposed experiment, not an executed result  
Parent context: [prior hypothesis]  
Expert seed: [starting question], references/expert-seeds/cards/ukb-23.md (feasibility_required)  
Dataset snapshot: ukb-completed-subset-20261002

## Question and clinical importance

The seed and parent ask whether haemoglobin falling while platelets rise is more CRC-like than hematologic-cancer-like, whereas broader multi-lineage abnormality may be more hematologic. The parent correctly makes a second-CBC landmark and two-year delayed entry central. The unresolved problem is that a second CBC in UK Biobank is not a routine clinical repeat process: it selects people who survived, remained cancer-free, attended another assessment and were not lost to follow-up. The apparent longitudinal contrast could therefore combine biology, timing and observation selection.

This branch asks:

> Among adults with a complete first CBC and no registered cancer at that first assessment, does a first-CBC phenotype provide a reproducible CRC-versus-hematologic cancer contrast, and does the corresponding second-CBC change contrast persist after an explicit repeat-observation stress test and across near-diagnostic versus longer-latency windows?

This could change the next clinical-research decision. A signal already present at the first CBC and stable after the repeat check would justify external validation of whether one CBC can prioritize CRC-oriented versus hematology-oriented evaluation. A signal only after the selectively observed second CBC, or only in the 0--2-year window, should be treated as timing/selection evidence rather than a longitudinal clinical rule.

The hypothesis is descriptive and predictive, not causal. It does not claim occult bleeding, inflammation, nutritional deficiency, clonal haematopoiesis, or treatment benefit.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

[K1] Hamilton et al. found clinically relevant near-diagnostic information in haemoglobin/anaemia for CRC in UK primary care, with risk varying by haemoglobin, age and sex. It is CRC-only case-control evidence and does not establish a repeated-CBC or CRC-versus-hematologic contrast.

[K2] Sala et al. reported pre-diagnostic CBC trajectories up to 24 months before CRC diagnosis, including haemoglobin decline and inflammatory-index increases associated with metastatic presentation. It is a retrospective CRC-only Spanish cohort and calls for independent validation; it does not test cancer-type specificity or selective observation.

[K3] Hampton et al.'s systematic review found that CRC models can discriminate but often have incomplete calibration and limited validation. That supports temporal holdout, calibration and decision restraint here; it is not evidence that the UKB contrast is clinically useful.

The strongest supported claim is that haemoglobin and longitudinal CBC measures can associate with CRC-related outcomes in selected datasets. The unresolved claim is that the platelet-versus-haemoglobin pattern differs between CRC and hematologic cancer in a first-CBC population and that its repeat-derived version is not created by repeat selection or a near-diagnostic window.

## Population, timing and outcomes

### First-CBC population

Include nonmissing eid, 53-0.0, and all four instance-0 CBC values, with parseable first date T0. Exclude anyone with any qualifying cancer date on or before T0. Do not require a repeat CBC.

Primary follow-up starts at T0 and ends at the first event, T0+2 years, or 2022-12-31. Secondary follow-up is T0+2 through T0+5, with the same fixed administrative boundary. Death is competing. The first post-T0 event is CRC, hematologic cancer, other primary cancer, death or censoring.

### Repeat landmark

R=1 when 53-1.0 is parseable, strictly later than T0, and all four instance-1 CBC values are nonmissing. R=0 is otherwise eligible at first CBC. R is defined without future outcomes.

For the repeat analysis require R=1, no qualifying cancer on/before T1=53-1.0, and no death on/before T1. Follow from T1 to T1+2 and then T1+5. This is explicitly conditional on being re-observed and event-free, not a recovered version of the first-CBC estimand.

### Features

Within each development split, standardize each CBC variable using instance-0 development means and standard deviations, separately by sex or with sex as a prespecified covariate. Apply those quantities to instances 1 and 2.

First-CBC features are z_WBC0, z_Hb0, z_RDW0 and z_platelet0. The prespecified platelet-haemoglobin contrast is the CRC-minus-hematologic coefficient contrast beta_platelet0 - beta_Hb0; positive means relatively higher platelets/lower haemoglobin is more CRC-associated.

Repeat features are the four baseline z values and d_j=z_j,1-z_j,0 for each cell line. The primary repeat contrast is beta_d_platelet - beta_d_Hb. A secondary fixed instability summary is D_ML=mean(abs(d_WBC), abs(d_Hb), abs(d_RDW), abs(d_platelet)). Do not include all four changes and D_HP=d_platelet-d_Hb as separate linear predictors: they are collinear. Include T1-T0 as a covariate and report its distribution. CRP 30710-1.0 is sensitivity-only.

Cancer classes are CRC C18/C19/C20; hematologic C81--C96; other primary malignant C00--C97 excluding those families and secondary C77--C79. C77--C79 are not primary target events. Take the earliest event across cancer classes and 40000-0.0 death. Report denominators, missingness, gap quantiles, event counts/person-time and exclusions before each landmark.

## Selection and timing estimands

The first-CBC estimand is the cause-specific CRC-versus-hematologic coefficient contrast for the platelet and haemoglobin baseline terms in all first-CBC-eligible adults. Its 0--2-year version is near-diagnostic; 2--5 tests persistence beyond likely work-up.

The repeat estimand is the corresponding contrast for the four change coefficients in the T1 landmark. Fit unweighted and with stabilized inverse-probability-of-repeat weights. Estimate P(R=1|X0) using only first-CBC information: baseline CBC, sex, age, BMI, smoking, CRP, first-assessment year, and eligibility indicators. Use a numerator based on sex and first-assessment year, trim at fixed 1st/99th percentiles, and report untrimmed sensitivity. Show balance before/after weighting.

Weights address measured baseline predictors of repeat observation only. They cannot repair cancer/death before T1, unmeasured healthcare use, symptoms, treatment, or the fact that 53-1.0 is a research-assessment measurement. The weighted repeat analysis is a selection stress test, not a causal correction.

Prespecify a 0.20 log-hazard equivalence margin (about a 22% hazard-ratio ratio) for weighted-minus-unweighted change. Call measured-selection robustness compatible only if the point change is within the margin and a 90% bootstrap interval is wholly inside it. If sparse events make that impossible, report inconclusive.

## Methods compared before selection

### A. Transparent cause-specific baseline

Fit separate cause-specific Cox models for CRC and hematologic cancer. Other cancer and death are competing events in the risk sets. The first model uses four baseline CBC values plus age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP and first-assessment year. The repeat model uses the same baseline covariates, four changes and assessment gap, with the repeat weight in the weighted version. Estimate the prespecified CRC-minus-hematologic coefficient contrasts with robust 95% intervals and time-varying-effect diagnostics. Use penalization only for documented separation/convergence failure and record it.

Estimate Aalen--Johansen cumulative incidence at 2 and 5 years for CRC, hematologic cancer, other cancer and death. Fine--Gray can be secondary, never a replacement for the cause-specific estimand. This baseline is primary because the estimand is auditable; it loses nonlinear thresholds, cross-line interactions and time-varying hazards.

### B. Substantive learned alternative

Fit a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model using exactly the same eligible people and raw information. Expand each person into yearly intervals from the relevant landmark through event/censoring/horizon. Predict CRC, hematologic cancer and other cancer/death hazards; include interval number. No post-landmark registry field or future CBC may be a predictor.

The first feature set is exactly the baseline feature set. The repeat set is exactly baseline CBC, four changes, gap and covariates. A small fixed grid is tuned in rolling development folds. This alternative can reveal nonlinear cell-line ranges, low-Hb-by-platelet interactions and time-varying type-specific hazards that A loses; it is not a mechanism detector.

Fit the repeat-observation head P(R=1|X0) within each development fold to generate weights. Do not use future R as a first-CBC risk predictor. In the repeat model R=1 defines the conditional population, not a predictor.

Compare B with A on a final temporal holdout using target-specific calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, 2- and 5-year cumulative-incidence Brier/log scores and participant-bootstrap intervals. Time-dependent AUC/Uno C-index is secondary. A small AUC gain without calibration or a stable cancer-type pattern is not a scientific advance. Feature-effect summaries are descriptive and frozen without test-outcome peeking.

### Split, uncertainty and compute

Sort participants by the relevant landmark date; earliest 70% is development and latest 30% is final test. Participants stay intact. Rolling development folds fit standardization, repeat weights, tuning and calibration. No test outcome, date, normalization, imputation or tuning choice enters fitting. If the held-out hematologic count is below 50, label the learned result imprecise and do not switch to a random split.

Use robust model intervals and 500 participant bootstrap replicates for held-out predictions, weighted estimates and weighted-minus-unweighted differences. Preserve failures and convergence warnings. Complete-case CBC features are required; missing is not normal.

The audit used short CPU scans; full fitting is unmeasured. inputs.json permits up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds for the future solver. Request CPU (8--16 CPUs, 32--64 GiB), estimated 2--4 hours. The modest gradient-boosted model does not require GPU; no GPU feasibility claim is made.

## Falsification and interpretation

The primary direction is relatively higher platelets/lower haemoglobin being more CRC-associated. It is a hypothesis, not an observed result.

Supportive evidence requires: (1) the first-CBC contrast has the prespecified direction; (2) repeat change contrast has the same direction unweighted and weighted, with selection change compatible with the margin; (3) the effect is not confined to 0--2 years, or is explicitly labelled near-diagnostic only; (4) learned nonlinear patterns are stable across folds and improve held-out calibration or cumulative-incidence scores; and (5) the contrast is more specific to CRC versus hematologic cancer than to all cancers.

Adverse evidence is a reasonably precise reversal, a large weighted/unweighted change, disappearance after two years, or the same signal for other cancers. That revises/abandons type-specific discordance while leaving generic CBC associations possible.

Inconclusive evidence is fewer than 50 usable held-out hematologic events, an equivalence interval wider than the margin, unstable gap/instance-2 sensitivity, convergence failure, or discrimination without calibration. A wide null does not refute the hypothesis.

Required checks:

- Audit every date/code pair, duplicate rows, implausible dates and fixed administrative censoring.
- Report 0--2 and 2--5 windows; 5--10 is exploratory only.
- Report repeat-gap strata and baseline R=1 versus R=0 balance.
- Use other primary cancers as a specificity control.
- Permute cancer type among development event dates while preserving timing; interaction should collapse toward zero.
- Use instance 2 only as a small sensitivity cohort, not dense trajectory evidence.
- Compare complete-CBC participants with the eligible denominator; never impute missing CBC as normal.

A supportive result establishes only a temporally ordered descriptive association useful for external-validation design. It does not establish diagnostic accuracy in symptomatic patients, a referral threshold, mechanism, stage, treatment benefit or clinical utility. The source lacks ferritin/iron, reticulocytes, FIT, symptoms, medications, transfusion context, smear, marrow studies, pathology adjudication, stage, work-up, lab indication/source and complete healthcare-contact history. 53 is a research assessment date, not a clinical test-order date. Clinical use requires expert code/lab review, external clinical validation and a prospective decision-linked study.

## Alternatives and deliverable

The transparent cause-specific model is selected as primary because it directly tests the claim and is auditable with sparse hematologic events. The gradient-boosted model is retained because it tests substantive nonlinear and time-varying information, not because complexity earns merit. A latent GP/state-space model or transformer is deferred: only two broadly complete CBC assessments exist, instance 2 is small, and repeat-landmark hematologic events are sparse. A first-CBC model using future R is rejected as leakage. Cross-namespace augmentation is rejected because the catalog has distinct namespaces and no authorized crosswalk.

The solver must newly fit/estimate: first-CBC and repeat cause-specific models; unweighted/weighted repeat selection; both temporal-holdout models; competing-risk curves; calibration/Brier/log scores; coefficient contrasts; 500-bootstrap uncertainty; gap/window sensitivities; and the cancer-type permutation negative control. Completion requires cohort/missingness/event tables, selection balance, split manifest, fitted outputs/predictions, uncertainty and a conclusion tied to those outputs. Readiness or a fit alone does not establish the claim.
