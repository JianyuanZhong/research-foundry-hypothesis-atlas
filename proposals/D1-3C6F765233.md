# Pre-repeat deterioration as a marker of immediate biochemical perturbation at the same repeat-TACE event

## Decision and substantive advance

This proposal is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` (principal parent: exact pre-repeat laboratory trajectory) and `[prior hypothesis]` (complementary parent: event-anchored immediate perturbation). It preserves, without modification, the frozen common-TACE2 cohort, TACE2 time zero, entry-era and day-14 observation rules, the days 1–14 pathway ontology, medication semantics, ambiguity exclusions, and the first strict repeat-TACE event on TACE2 days 15–90. It links the two parental questions at the same later procedure rather than replacing either one.

The substantive advance is a falsifiable vulnerability-marker experiment: among patients selected for a recorded repeat TACE, does deterioration (or improvement) from the exact pre-TACE2 albumin/bilirubin state to the exact pre-repeat panel predict the immediate post-repeat panel, and does that association differ by the frozen early systemic-record pathway? The design explicitly addresses the key defect that a raw change-on-change regression is mathematically coupled: the pre-repeat value appears with a positive sign in the trajectory and a negative sign in the post-minus-pre perturbation. Therefore the primary estimand models the post-repeat level while separately adjusting for pre-repeat and pre-TACE2 levels; change-on-change results are secondary diagnostics only.

A complete-source, value-blinded audit of snapshot `[source checksum]` found 40 systemic-record and 118 TACE2-only complete three-panel observations under the strict common-timestamp definition. This supports a bounded exploratory association, not a definitive clinical prediction model or causal claim. The audit selection and window rules were fixed without using assay values or directions.

## Clinical question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

### Clinical importance

A patient’s hepatic laboratory trajectory before a repeat locoregional procedure may identify a clinically vulnerable state in which the same procedure is followed by a larger short-term biochemical perturbation. If reproducible, this could motivate prospective pre-retreatment monitoring and adjudication of procedural and supportive-care decisions. It could also show that an apparent immediate perturbation is largely explained by the pre-event state rather than by a uniform event response. Either adverse, null, or selection-sensitive findings would be clinically informative for deciding whether this database can support a monitoring hypothesis.

The question is deliberately narrower than “does systemic therapy protect the liver,” “is repeat TACE safe,” or “does TACE cause injury.” The database records medication and procedure rows, not verified administrations or technical details, and the assay table has no unit column. Thus the computable target is a selected, observed association between a frozen pathway label, pre-repeat trajectory, and assay levels around a recorded event.

### Strongest claim already supported by available evidence

The source can reconstruct the frozen cohort and event clocks exactly. The exhaustive audit scanned 105,044 encounter, 1,810,646 diagnosis, 338,040 procedure, 4,097,517 medication, and 28,159,928 laboratory rows. It reproduced 78,067 strict textual TACE matches, 72,273 strict matches with valid start time, 38,742 patient-calendar-day episodes, 6,589 earliest adjacent qualifying pairs, 6,586 HCC/adult pairs, 2,992 era/day-14-observed patients, 1,107 prior-systemic exclusions, 319 systemic-record patients, 1,491 TACE2-only patients, 8 bevacizumab-only ambiguities, and 67 generic-procedure ambiguities.

Among the frozen arms, a first strict repeat TACE on TACE2 days 15–90 was recorded for 159/319 systemic-record patients and 526/1,491 TACE2-only patients. The selection fractions were 49.84% and 35.28%, respectively, with median event day 55 in both arms. The exact same-timestamp panel audit found the following arm-specific counts among selected events: pre-TACE2 joint baseline 88/293; exact pre-repeat joint panel within 72 hours 54/206; exact post-repeat joint panel within 72 hours 120/384; baseline plus pre-repeat 55/186; baseline plus post-repeat 65/198; and complete baseline plus pre-repeat plus post-repeat 40/118. These are availability and timing facts, not evidence of assay direction or clinical effect.

All 685 selected event rows linked to encounter admission and discharge timestamps by the composite key (`patient master index`, `visit number`). The pre panel preceded the event by a median 11.22 hours in the systemic arm and 8.78 hours in the TACE2-only arm; the post panel was selected near the 24-hour target, with median event-to-panel times 32.52 and 32.52 hours. The exact assay source contains 476,846 rows across the full laboratory table. No laboratory unit field exists.

### Unresolved falsifiable hypothesis

For each assay separately, conditional on a first recorded strict repeat TACE on TACE2 days 15–90 and complete observation of the locked three-panel sequence, the pre-TACE2-to-pre-repeat trajectory is associated with the post-repeat assay level after accounting for the pre-repeat level and the pre-TACE2 baseline. The trajectory association may differ by frozen pathway arm.

A prespecified clinically favorable pattern would be that greater pre-repeat deterioration is not accompanied by a larger adverse post-repeat shift in the systemic-record arm, while a larger post-repeat albumin decrease and/or bilirubin increase is adverse. Because units, reference ranges, fluid/albumin exposure, acuity, and clinically meaningful margins are unavailable, these labels are interpretive branches requiring clinical review; they are not automatic safety conclusions.

The primary scientific test is the trajectory-by-arm interaction in a level-adjusted model. A main trajectory association, if present in both arms, is a prognostic/selection association. An interaction indicates effect modification of the recorded event-proximal assay relationship by the frozen pathway, but does not identify a medication effect. Neither result establishes causation.

## Event and three-panel selectors

### Repeat event

Within each frozen arm, select the first strict TACE patient-day episode whose normalized calendar delta from TACE2 is 15–90 inclusive. Use the earliest valid `Start time` on that selected episode as the exact event timestamp; if multiple rows share it, use a deterministic encounter-string tie-break after auditing duplicates. Event selection must be independent of laboratory availability and assay values. Patients without an event remain in the full frozen denominators and event-selection models but not in the event-conditioned three-panel estimand. Repeat events in calendar 2025 remain eligible when TACE2 occurred in 2024 because the entry era, not event year, is frozen.

### Numeric parsing

For each exact assay, trim whitespace, remove commas, and accept `Quantitative Result` only when the complete remaining string consists of an optional leading `<`, `>`, `≤`, or `≥` and a signed decimal/scientific numeric literal. Preserve the inequality marker and numeric value; reject partial strings and all other text. Do not infer units, harmonize assay names, calculate ALBI, or apply external thresholds. Report parsing attrition, inequality frequencies, specimen types, duplicate same-assay timestamps, and discordant duplicate sensitivities.

### Pre-TACE2 baseline

For each assay separately, choose the latest valid numeric result in TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1 inclusive. TACE2 day 0 is excluded. The primary baseline requirement is one valid albumin and one valid total-bilirubin value; they need not share timestamp. Record whether they share timestamp, encounter, and calendar day. A same-timestamp baseline is a locked sensitivity, not a replacement.

### Exact pre-repeat panel

Restrict to the selected event’s exact encounter key (`Patient Master Index`, `Visit Number`). Consider laboratory timestamps in `[event time − 72 hours, event time)`, excluding the event timestamp. Pivot by exact timestamp and retain only timestamps containing valid numeric values for both `Albumin` and `Total Bilirubin`; select the latest such common timestamp. If duplicate values for an assay share a timestamp, retain a deterministic final source-order value only after reporting duplicates; exclude discordant duplicates in a sensitivity.

### Exact post-repeat panel

Again restrict to the selected event encounter. Consider timestamps in `(event time, event time + 72 hours]`, excluding the event timestamp. Retain only common timestamps with both assays. Choose the timestamp minimizing absolute distance to event +24 hours; on an exact distance tie choose the earlier timestamp. Report the selected event-to-panel hours and repeat locked alternatives: first eligible post timestamp, nearest event +48 hours, and calendar days +1–3 excluding day 0.

Define, for assay `a`,

* `trajectory_a = pre_repeat_a − pre_TACE2_a`;
* `acute_a = post_repeat_a − pre_repeat_a`.

These differences are descriptive and secondary for inference. The three-panel complete-case cohort is defined before reading or modeling assay values, using only validity, timing, encounter, and panel completeness.

## Primary estimand and anti-coupling analysis

The primary estimand is the arm-specific difference in the trajectory slope for post-repeat level among patients with the selected repeat event and complete three-panel observation. For assay `a`, fit a prespecified parsimonious model of the form

`post_a = β0 + β1 pre_repeat_a + β2 pre_TACE2_a + β3 trajectory_a + β4 arm + β5 (trajectory_a × arm) + γ^T Z + ε`.

Because `trajectory_a = pre_repeat_a − pre_TACE2_a`, the model’s interpretation must be stated carefully: do not interpret `β3` as an independent biological effect while algebraically redundant terms are included. The implementation should use one nonredundant equivalent parameterization as the primary computation, for example

`post_a = β0 + θ1 pre_repeat_a + θ2 pre_TACE2_a + θ3 arm + θ4 (pre_TACE2_a × arm) + γ^T Z + ε`,

where `θ4` is the prespecified test of whether the baseline-to-pre-repeat relationship differs by arm conditional on the two observed levels. A secondary parameterization may report predicted post values over prespecified trajectory contrasts within the observed overlap. The raw change-on-change regression

`(post_a − pre_repeat_a) ~ (pre_repeat_a − pre_TACE2_a) × arm`

must be labeled coupling-sensitive and cannot be the primary result.

For each assay separately, report:

* adjusted post-repeat predictions and arm contrasts at prespecified observed covariate summaries;
* the conditional association of pre-TACE2-to-pre-repeat trajectory with post-repeat level;
* the trajectory-by-arm interaction or equivalent arm-specific slope contrast;
* raw distributions of baseline, pre-repeat, post-repeat, trajectory, and acute change;
* robust 95% confidence intervals, denominators, and patient-level influence diagnostics.

Keep the model parsimonious because only 40 systemic complete triplets are available. Use baseline overlap weights from all 1,810 frozen-arm patients, with systemic weight `1−p` and TACE2-only weight `p`, where `p` is estimated from information available at TACE2. Candidate baseline covariates are age, sex, TACE1–TACE2 interval, entry year/month, department, prior 30/90/180-day encounter counts, prior 90/180-day procedure counts, prior resection/ablation/transplant flags, encounter-timed cirrhosis/portal hypertension/ascites/encephalopathy/variceal bleeding/portal-vein invasion/metastasis proxies, and closest pre-TACE2 exact albumin, bilirubin, INR, platelet, creatinine, and sodium values with missingness indicators. No grace-period detail, later event, post-TACE2 value, or post-event assay enters this baseline model.

Fit separate five-fold cross-fitted logistic selection models for (1) repeat TACE by day 90 among all frozen-arm patients and (2) complete three-panel observation among selected repeat-event patients. The observation model may use only baseline covariates, arm, event day/year/month, and encounter/admission facts known before the relevant panel; it must not use panel values or post-panel information. As a locked sensitivity, multiply overlap weights by inverse predicted event and observation probabilities, both untruncated and truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles. Report calibration, AUC, probability support, weight distributions, arm and total effective sample sizes, and all weighted standardized differences. These models diagnose measured selection; they do not remove collider bias or identify a causal all-cohort effect.

Use raw-scale outcomes as primary, with rank and 20% winsorized checks. Inequality-bearing results and discordant duplicate panels are excluded in locked sensitivities. Do not use an unvalidated albumin-bilirubin composite or external clinical cutoff.

## Required selection, observation, and regression-to-mean diagnostics

Repeat-TACE selection is post-baseline and plausibly depends on reserve, tumor response/progression, imaging, clinician preference, access, tolerance, outside care, and the frozen pathway. Requiring three observed panels is an additional post-baseline selection mechanism. Report, by arm, event and observation fractions, event day/year distributions, baseline availability, panel availability at 24/48/72/168 hours, admission/discharge linkage, and observed versus unobserved selected-event covariate distributions. Keep non-event patients in the event model denominator.

Regression to the mean is a central alternative explanation. The baseline and pre-repeat assays may contain measurement noise, and a low or high pre-TACE2 value can make subsequent movement appear to predict a later rebound or worsening. Required diagnostics are:

* models with each level and missingness indicator rather than only differences;
* same-assay duplicate/timestamp and inequality-bearing-value exclusions;
* same-timestamp baseline sensitivity;
* alternate pre-repeat windows (24, 48, 72, and 168 hours) and first-versus-latest eligible panel;
* a negative-control pre-event interval, comparing the closest valid panel in repeat-event days −14 to −8 with the pre-repeat panel under the same baseline weights;
* arm-specific plots/tables of baseline level versus trajectory and trajectory versus post level, without selecting a favorable display;
* leave-one-out or influence diagnostics, rank-scale and winsorized analyses.

If a comparable arm contrast appears in the negative-control pre-event interval, the interpretation as a newly event-associated perturbation is falsified. A null negative control does not prove exchangeability or causation.

6. Repeat after excluding selected events after 2024 and after excluding any later non-bevacizumab ontology record between TACE2 day 15 and the event. These are selected-population sensitivities, not as-treated effects.
7. Require common propensity support and report weighted standardized differences. If any core post-weight absolute standardized difference exceeds 0.10, or weights are unstable, limit conclusions to descriptive results.
8. Require systemic complete-triplet effective sample size at least 30 for any adjusted interaction claim; with fewer than 50 systemic complete triplets, classify all inferential estimates as exploratory. The observed 40 systemic triplets therefore make precision a warning, not a pass.
9. Require combined-weight systemic ESS at least 25 and no more than 20% change under locked truncation for a selection-adjusted interpretation. A direction reversal or large change is selection-sensitive, not evidence for either arm.
10. Require both assays to be directionally coherent for a favorable/adverse branch. A favorable albumin pattern with a clearly adverse bilirubin pattern is mixed/inconclusive.
11. If the trajectory-by-arm association reverses across level-adjusted and coupling-sensitive change models, report coupling instability and do not promote a biological interpretation.
12. If the negative-control pre-event contrast is comparable, reject a newly repeat-TACE-associated interpretation. If timing selectors or duplicate/inequality checks reverse direction, report methodologically unstable results.
13. Because the complete recovery triplets are only 40/118, no post-discharge recovery, reversibility, chronic injury, preserved reserve, safety, or survival conclusion is permitted.

## Interpretation branches

* **Supportive but bounded:** all reconstruction, clock, overlap, selection, influence, and timing checks pass; greater pre-repeat deterioration predicts a coherent post-repeat pattern, and the arm interaction is stable in the level-adjusted model without a comparable negative-control signal. Conclude only that the recorded pre-repeat trajectory is associated with immediate post-repeat assay behavior in selected and observed repeat-TACE patients, with a pathway-specific association if the interaction is supported.
* **Adverse:** greater deterioration predicts a larger albumin decrease and/or bilirubin increase, with coherent checks. This supports prospective vulnerability monitoring research, not a contraindication, toxicity attribution, or medication harm claim.
* **Null:** intervals are compatible with no detectable association in both assays. Report a raw-scale null in this selected cohort; do not claim equivalence, safety, or absence of vulnerability, especially because units and clinically meaningful margins are missing.
* **General trajectory rather than event-specific:** a comparable association is present in the negative-control pre-event interval or other locked non-event context. Reject a newly repeat-TACE-associated interpretation and report a broader selected-care/measurement pattern.
* **Selection-sensitive:** event/observation reweighting materially changes the estimate, calibration or positivity is poor, or the complete-case cohort differs sharply by arm or year. State that post-baseline selection governs inference; do not choose the more favorable estimator.
* **Coupling/regression-to-mean sensitive:** the level-adjusted model is null or opposite while raw change-on-change is strong, or duplicate/inequality/baseline-shift checks materially alter results. Treat the apparent change-on-change signal as methodological, not biological.
* **Mixed or inconclusive:** albumin and bilirubin disagree, effective sample size is low, intervals span materially favorable and adverse directions, units are unavailable, or a few observations drive the result. No clinical direction should be promoted.
* **Recovery unavailable:** the source observes an acute event episode but cannot support recovery or longitudinal hepatic safety; state this explicitly.

## Evidence limits, required additional data, and verifier scope

The snapshot does not provide laboratory units or analyzer/reference-range validation, complete fluid balance or exogenous albumin administration, reliable acuity, medication administration/adherence/intent, repeat-TACE rationale, technical selectivity or embolized territory, drug/contrast dose, adjudicated complications, complete imaging/tumor response, performance status, complete Child-Pugh components, outside-care capture, or reliable mortality. Procedure linkage proves a recorded procedure row, not that TACE caused an assay change. Medication linkage proves a record/order, not exposure. Institutional observation does not prove survival.

Clinical importance requires unit validation and expert-defined margins. Causal claims require an additional design with time-varying severity and treatment-decision covariates, reliable administration data, and explicit longitudinal estimands. Stronger safety or toxicity claims require interventional-radiology notes, inpatient medication/fluid/albumin records, complication adjudication, serial outpatient laboratories, imaging/response, retreatment rationale, outside-care linkage, and mortality ascertainment.

An automatic verifier can check source hashes and row counts, frozen flow, joins, event and panel clocks, numeric parsing, denominator/attrition accounting, selection models, weights, balance, effective sample sizes, robust intervals, negative controls, sensitivity consistency, and whether reported conclusions follow the computed branch. It cannot determine whether drugs were administered, whether the procedure caused injury, whether changes are clinically important, why repeat TACE occurred, or whether any pathway is safe, effective, beneficial, or appropriate.
