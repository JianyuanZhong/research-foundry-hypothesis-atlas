TITLE
Measured liver state at operation versus elapsed post-TACE time: does residual bilirubin/INR injury add prognostic information for day-5 hepatic dysfunction after HCC resection?

SUBSTANTIVE ADVANCE AND CLINICAL QUESTION
The selected parent established that an exact continuous last-TACE-to-resection interval can be studied in 846–847 selected recorded resections, with 594 paired perioperative bilirubin/INR outcomes and 82 moderate joint events. Its principal weakness is clinical: elapsed time is only a proxy for recovery. This child asks the directly useful, falsifiable next question: among adults with recorded HCC who ultimately undergo first recorded liver resection >14 to <=180 exact days after their last preoperative TACE, does the latest measured bilirubin/INR state immediately before operation explain or improve on elapsed time in predicting postoperative day-5-to-7 hepatic biochemical dysfunction? More specifically, does within-person residual injury relative to the pre-TACE state add information beyond the absolute latest preoperative bilirubin and INR, or are pre-TACE/acute anchors unnecessary once current values and case mix are known?

This is not a scheduling study. It compares prognostic representations among selected operations that occurred. A positive result would support prospectively measuring and externally validating physiologic readiness rather than relying on calendar delay; it would not establish that waiting changes risk, identify a safe operation date, or justify delaying cancer treatment.

CLAIM LADDER
Strongest evidence-supported claim: routine bilirubin and INR are biologically relevant to hepatic reserve; increased INR and hyperbilirubinemia on/after postoperative day 5 form the laboratory basis of the ISGLS definition, although clinical grade requires management evidence. The frozen HCC source contains dense, dated perioperative bilirubin/INR measurements. The new resource-managed scan ([research job]) read all 338,040 procedures and 28,159,928 lab rows and directly demonstrates feasible current-state and paired-outcome windows.

Unresolved claim tested: a two-marker liver-state representation fixed before incision—latest bilirubin and INR, optionally expressed as residual change from last-pre-TACE values—provides clinically meaningful, internally validated prognostic improvement over exact elapsed time and ordinary preoperative case mix for a postoperative biochemical dysfunction signal. A secondary nested question tests whether the acute post-TACE maximum adds any information beyond pre-TACE-to-operation residual injury.

Stronger unavailable claims: that residual injury is “readiness,” that changing operation time changes outcomes, that any threshold is safe, that the endpoint is true ISGLS PHLF or grade A/B/C, or that results generalize to TACE recipients who never undergo recorded resection. Those require clinical adjudication, complete treatment/death capture, external/prospective validation and, for scheduling benefit, a causal or randomized study.

Report the parent-compatible >28–90-day subset and a >28–180 sensitivity, but do not define the primary claim by 60 days.

TEMPORAL VALIDITY: PREDICTORS FIXED BEFORE OUTCOME
Operation time O is 手术.开始时间; last preoperative TACE time T is its 开始时间. All predictors must have 检验时间 < O. No postoperative lab, document, complication, length of stay or outcome-derived scaling/selection may enter predictors.

Current-operation state uses the latest numeric value for each marker in [O-7 days,O), with exact assay label, specimen, encounter and measurement time retained. Seven days is primary because the full scan found 594 complete current-state/outcome pairs, versus 539 for [O-5,O), 391 for [O-3,O), 262 for [O-2,O), and 149 for [O-1,O). Median recency was 2.01 days for bilirubin and 2.12 for INR; 90th percentiles were 4.59 and 4.65 days. The [O-5,O) sensitivity is the stricter clinically preferred window; [O-3,O) is a freshness stress test. Never carry values from >7 days as “at operation.” Require both markers within the same prespecified window, but not the same timestamp; report their time separation and a <=24-hour-pair sensitivity.

Last-pre-TACE baseline uses the latest marker in [T-7 days,T). Acute maximum uses [T,T+7 days), with right endpoint excluded; it is guaranteed preoperative by the >14-day population boundary. The pre-TACE and acute anchors are histories, not current readiness measures.

Crucially, the primary postoperative endpoint is absolute rather than defined as a change from the same latest preoperative predictor. This avoids mechanical predictor–outcome coupling. The parent's paired relative composite—postoperative bilirubin >=1.5 times current bilirubin AND postoperative INR >=current INR+0.20—is retained only as a secondary sensitivity and must not be used to claim incremental value of current bilirubin/INR, because current values are mathematically embedded in that outcome.

PREDICTOR REPRESENTATIONS AND NESTED BASELINES
All transformations, winsorization bounds and robust scaling are learned inside each training fold, without postoperative outcomes.

M0 timing-only baseline: exact interval_days modeled with restricted cubic spline at empirical 10th/35th/65th/90th percentiles, plus age, sex, calendar year/service, admission-to-operation time and number of prior TACE records.

M1 conventional latest-lab baseline: M0 plus log latest preoperative bilirubin and latest preoperative INR as separate continuous splines, albumin, creatinine, platelets, ALT and hemoglobin when unit/assay validated, baseline diagnoses, measurement recency/intensity, preoperative imaging tumor case mix and validated operation extent/approach. This is the clinically decisive baseline: if historical recovery adds nothing beyond M1, the most recent levels—not trajectory reconstruction—are sufficient in this source.

M2 pre-TACE residual-injury model: M1 plus two separate residual-history terms, log(B_operation/B_preTACE) and INR_operation−INR_preTACE. Do not collapse them into an outcome-trained score and do not require directions to be identical. Absolute current levels remain in M2, so residual terms test whether knowing where the patient started adds information beyond where the patient is now. Include time from each anchor measurement to T/T-to-O and assay-type agreement. A sensitivity replaces change terms with the paired (pre-TACE,current) raw values and interactions to test functional-form dependence.

M3 acute-history extension: M2 plus log(B_operation/B_acute_max) and INR_operation−INR_acute_max, with acute maximum defined in [T,T+7 days). This is secondary. Its added value must be evaluated against M2 in the same 423-person complete subset, and M2 itself must be re-estimated there; it is invalid to attribute performance differences caused by differing samples to the acute anchor.

The primary estimand is incremental prognosis, not a coefficient interpreted causally: optimism-corrected change from M1 to M2 in (a) scaled Brier score, (b) log loss, (c) calibration slope/intercept, and (d) AUC, plus predeclared risk reclassification across a 15% predicted-risk threshold. The 15% threshold approximates the observed 82/594 event frequency and is a decision-analytic test threshold, not a surgical cutoff; also report decision-curve net benefit across 10–20%. Report adjusted partial effects and standardized predicted risks for representative current values and residual histories only if support is adequate. Secondary compare M0→M1 and M2→M3.

OUTCOMES WITHOUT MECHANICAL COUPLING
Primary continuous outcomes, modeled jointly but reported separately: log maximum total bilirubin in [O+5 days,O+8 days) and maximum INR in the same window. Their absolute values do not contain the latest preoperative predictor mathematically. Current lab prediction is supportive only if both components are directionally coherent; a bilirubin-only gain is component-specific, not general hepatic recovery.

Primary binary outcome is a unit/reference-range-gated absolute postoperative dysfunction signal. Before model fitting, a laboratory director must map each assay/era/specimen to institutional upper limits of normal (ULN). Event = postoperative maximum bilirubin >=2×ULN AND postoperative maximum INR >=1.20 on days [5,8). If ULN/reference ranges cannot be recovered, the binary primary is unavailable and the two absolute continuous components become primary; do not substitute a data-derived percentile and do not call either ISGLS PHLF. A descriptive screen using an assumed bilirubin ULN 17.1 (threshold 34.2) and INR 1.20 produced only 36/594 events and is not asserted as a validated endpoint; accordingly penalization and an event gate are required.

Secondary outcomes: (1) parent's paired relative composite, explicitly labeled mathematically coupled and used only to test robustness of outcome definition, never incremental current-lab prediction; (2) postoperative albumin absolute/change after units are verified; (3) day-1-to-3 hemoglobin nadir/change as negative-control outcome; (4) clinician-adjudicated true ISGLS PHLF/grade, encephalopathy, new/worsened ascites, ICU escalation, reoperation, infection/sepsis, bile leak, transfusion and in-hospital death. Documents lack row timestamps and management evidence is incomplete, so automated Harbor cannot establish true grade.

ANALYSIS AND VALIDATION
Freeze a complete-case comparison cohort separately for M1 versus M2 and M2 versus M3. Never impute the postoperative outcome or pre-TACE/acute anchor. Use multiply imputed ordinary preoperative covariates only after reporting complete-case results; include outcome-observation and anchor-observation models as sensitivities.

Use nested patient-level bootstrap validation (>=500 resamples) or repeated stratified 10-fold cross-validation with all preprocessing performed inside folds. Because there are only 70 relative events for M2 and potentially about 36 validated absolute events, use ridge/elastic-net logistic and penalized spline degrees of freedom; no high-dimensional interactions. Prespecify a maximum effective parameter count based on event yield and shrinkage. If the validated absolute endpoint has <40 events, binary incremental modeling is exploratory/inconclusive and the continuous component models carry the principal test. Calibration must be reported; AUC alone is insufficient. Bootstrap the paired M1−M2 and M2−M3 performance differences on identical patients. Do not select predictors or thresholds on full-data outcomes.

Case-mix adjustment follows the parent but is explicitly nested: structured model, then validated tumor/procedure model. Two blinded reviewers validate >=200 stratified preoperative imaging reports and >=200 procedure names for diameter, multiplicity, macrovascular invasion, extrahepatic disease, rupture, response, major/minor extent and approach. Automated use requires both sensitivity and PPV >=0.90; otherwise fully adjudicate or label residual confounding unresolved. Operation extent is after the interval but fixed before postoperative outcome; include it for prognosis, while reporting omission to show whether the physiologic signal merely tags surgical selection. It cannot support a causal timing estimate.

FALSIFICATION AND CONTROLS
1. Mathematical-coupling audit: automatically verify that no preoperative value appears algebraically in the primary absolute outcomes. Refit against the parent's relative endpoint. A strong current-lab effect confined to that coupled endpoint and absent for both absolute components falsifies incremental physiologic interpretation.
2. Preoperative pseudo-outcome: predict entirely preoperative late values [O-3,O) from earlier values [O-7,O-3), timing and history, without using postoperative data. If timing or residual-history “predicts” this pseudo-outcome at an effect/performance increment equal to or larger than postoperative prediction, the result is compatible with monitoring/channeling rather than postoperative vulnerability and is downgraded.
3. Hemoglobin negative-control outcome: day-1-to-3 nadir/change adjusted for preoperative hemoglobin and validated operation extent. If M2 adds >=0.02 scaled Brier/R-squared, standardized effect >=0.20, or p<0.01 in the same pattern, generic illness/operative-complexity selection is implicated.
4. Negative-control exposure: TACE day of week after year/service adjustment. Omnibus p<0.01 or standardized effect >=0.20 on either hepatic component flags scheduling/service structure.
5. Anchor placebo: replace true pre-TACE anchor with a pseudo-anchor sampled from [T-30,T-14) where available. Similar or greater improvement than M2 falsifies a specific post-TACE recovery interpretation and suggests stable patient history/utilization.
6. Clock and repeat-TACE: use last TACE primary, earliest TACE sensitivity, and single-recorded-TACE restriction. A result only under the biologically inappropriate earliest clock is adverse. No lab after an intervening TACE may be attributed to the earlier episode.
7. Timing benchmark: compare M0 with and without M1/M2. If timing appears associated in M0 but attenuates after M1, this supports “current state explains the timing signal” prognostically, not mediation or causal explanation. Display 55/60/65-day cuts only as placebos; isolated significance at one cut is unstable.
8. Case-mix stress tests: omit/include operation extent; restrict to validated minor resections, planned/conversion TACE, routine assays and fresh [O-5,O). Disappearance after validated extent/tumor adjustment indicates channeling rather than robust liver-state prognosis.

CLINICALLY MEANINGFUL SUPPORT, ADVERSE, NULL AND INCONCLUSIVE BRANCHES
Supportive for current measured state over time: on the same validated cohort, M1 versus M0 improves optimism-corrected scaled Brier score by >=0.05 (or absolute Brier by >=0.005), lowers log loss, preserves calibration slope 0.8–1.2 and intercept near zero, increases AUC by >=0.03, and adds positive decision-curve net benefit across at least 10–20% risk; both absolute bilirubin and INR component predictions are coherent; timing's partial association materially attenuates (>=25% reduction in the prespecified 30-versus-60-day risk contrast or spline contribution); all gates and controls pass. This supports replacing calendar-only risk assessment with fresh measured labs for prospective validation, not changing surgery dates.

Supportive for residual injury beyond latest labs: M2 versus M1, on identical patients, improves scaled Brier >=0.05 or absolute Brier >=0.005, AUC >=0.02, log loss and net benefit, with calibration preserved; residual bilirubin and INR histories contribute coherent component-specific information; paired bootstrap 95% CIs exclude no improvement; the gain persists with [O-5,O), validated case mix and routine assays; controls pass. This supports collecting a pre-TACE reference in a future readiness study. It still does not validate a threshold or causal delay.

Supportive for acute anchor: M3 meets the same M2-added-value thresholds against M2 on the identical 423-person subset and remains stable. Anything less means acute maxima do not justify their attrition; latest plus pre-TACE values are preferred.

Adverse/opposite: worse measured current/residual liver state predicts lower postoperative bilirubin/INR or improvement is driven by only one discordant component; acute history degrades calibration/performance; or timing gains strength after proper current-state adjustment. This rejects the proposed physiologic-risk representation and suggests selection, assay artifacts or model misspecification. No early-surgery safety inference follows.

Precise null: paired 95% CIs exclude absolute Brier improvement >=0.005, scaled Brier >=0.05 and AUC gain >=0.02 for M2 over M1, calibration is adequate, controls pass, and both components agree. This supports that pre-TACE residual history adds no clinically material prognosis beyond latest preoperative labs and case mix in observed complete selected resections. If M1 also precisely fails versus M0, measured routine labs do not outperform timing in this source; this is not proof elapsed time is causal.

Inconclusive: intervals include both no and clinically meaningful improvement; validated absolute events <40; M2 <400 or <50 paired events; M3 <350 or <50 paired events; [O-5,O) does not reproduce [O-7,O); component directions conflict; calibration is poor; missingness/positivity/unit/case-mix/intent gates fail; or any control fails. Report the feasible cohort and do not claim readiness.

Falsifying the simple recovery interpretation: M2 adds nothing beyond M1; M3 adds nothing beyond M2; only the mathematically coupled endpoint is positive; pseudo-anchor performs as well as the true pre-TACE anchor; hemoglobin/pseudo-outcome/day-of-week controls reproduce the pattern; or the apparent signal disappears after validated operation/tumor adjustment. These outcomes are scientifically useful: they would indicate that current labs suffice, that historical anchors are noise, or that calendar/monitoring selection—not residual TACE injury—drives associations.

SELECTION, EVIDENCE LIMITS AND CLINICAL ADJUDICATION
The cohort is conditioned on undergoing recorded resection and having postoperative labs. It cannot represent patients who progressed, died, were never resectable, underwent outside treatment, or lacked institutional follow-up. Missing essentials include complete outside TACE/resection/death, longitudinal resectability and intent, ECOG/BCLC, raw imaging/mRECIST, future-liver-remnant volume, ICG clearance, portal pressure, standardized Child-Pugh/ALBI unless units are verified, embolization selectivity, anesthesia/blood loss/transfusion detail, and reliable timestamped management/complications. Hepatobiliary and interventional-radiology review is required for TACE intent/emergency status, resection validity/extent, tumor response/case mix and true ISGLS PHLF/grade. Automatic verification cannot establish them.
