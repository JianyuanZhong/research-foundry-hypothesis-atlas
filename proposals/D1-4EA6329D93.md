# Episode 5 child: scalar hypotension summaries versus temporal recovery trajectories

Status: proposed substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The schema/binding audit is complete; the full creatinine vocabulary/scale audit is a readiness diagnostic, not a cohort result. No clinical event rate or model performance is claimed.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The selected parent asks whether cumulative systemic MAP time below 65 mmHg in ICU minutes 0–360 has reproducible prognostic information for a later creatinine rise beyond nadir and recorded process/treatment variables. The remaining decisive uncertainty is whether that question can be computed with a reproducible creatinine label and whether a scalar burden really captures the clinically relevant temporal pattern.

The strongest inspected evidence supports that hypotension duration/trajectory summaries can be prognostic in a different, surgical cohort [K1]; abstract evidence shows that KDIGO-criteria AKI can disagree substantially with clinical coding and depends on baseline/measurement choices [K2]; and randomized BP-target evidence does not turn an observational association into a pressor benefit or harm [K3]. None establishes an eICU creatinine scale, an eICU transportable signal, a mechanism, or a treatment effect.

The falsifiable estimands are deliberately noncausal:

1. Within first adult eICU stays with common support, does early MAP<65 duration add held-out prognostic information beyond early severity, BP ascertainment, baseline creatinine, and MAP nadir?
2. Conditional on that same information and scalar duration/nadir, does the ordering of low MAP, recovery, oscillation, and observation/treatment coupling add transportable prognostic information for a subsequent creatinine event?

The clinical advance would be to determine whether a compact duration summary is transportable enough to justify external validation, or whether prognosis depends on temporal recovery patterns that a scalar duration loses. Either a robust null or an artifact-dominated result would prevent a misleading duration-based interpretation. These are prognostic/descriptive claims, not evidence that hypotension causes renal injury or that raising MAP improves outcomes.

## Matched scalar baseline versus temporal alternative

All models target the same three-interval competing-risk prediction task and use identical hospital splits, censoring weights, and feature normalization rules. The baseline comparison is:

- **B0 (severity/process baseline):** regularized discrete-time cause-specific regression with age, gender, ethnicity, hospital/ICU type, baseline creatinine, admission APS meanbp/creatinine where available, initial MAP/heart rate/respiration, pre-index creatinine/BP counts, BP coverage/missingness/modality/gaps, and prespecified <=360-minute EOL/process markers. It contains no early MAP duration, nadir, or trajectory shape.
- **B1 (scalar estimand model):** B0 plus covered minutes MAP<65, low-time fraction, area, episode count, first-low offset, recovery slope, and restricted spline of duration, with fixed-nadir bands and common-support restrictions. The primary contrast B1-B0 estimates incremental prognostic value of the early duration/nadir summary, not a causal effect.
- **M (temporal alternative):** a one-layer GRU with hidden width 32 and dropout 0.1, with one sigmoid/softmax head for the same three follow-up intervals. It receives the same 72-bin sequence of MAP, heart rate, respiration, modality, observation mask, time-since-last-observation, and early recorded-pressor indicators only in a treatment-inclusive ablation; the primary M masks treatment inputs. It receives B0 baseline covariates through the head. It is trained only in training hospitals, early-stopped/calibrated in validation hospitals, and evaluated once in held-out hospitals.

M is scientifically substantive because two stays can have the same duration and nadir but differ in repeated isolated lows versus one persistent episode, order of recovery, oscillation, and whether a low precedes renewed observation. B1 discards that order; M can test whether the distinction changes later risk after matching scalar burden. To prevent a small predictive gain from becoming the scientific claim, report an order-destroying sensitivity that permutes complete observed bin tuples (value, mask, modality, time-since-last-observation, treatment proxy) within each stay while retaining the scalar summaries. If original and permuted M perform similarly, any gain is not evidence for temporal ordering.

Training target: discrete-time three-state prediction for each interval (creatinine event, death/competing endpoint, no event), with inverse-probability-of-censoring/observation weights fitted in training hospitals only. Fit B0, B1, and M with the same weighted proper loss; use the same risk-set definition. M is not fit if the label gate or minimum event/hospital-support criteria fail.

Use deterministic whole-hospital splits: hash `episode5|hospitalid` with SHA-256, assign sorted hashes 60% train, 20% validation, 20% test; every `uniquepid` and all its stays remain in one split. If the prespecified minimum hospital/event counts make one split infeasible, use five-fold grouped-hospital cross-validation with one untouched fold for final evaluation and report that deviation before viewing test performance. Fit scalers, vocabulary, model selection, calibration, nuisance models, and censoring/observation weights on training data only.

Primary uncertainty and scores: hospital-cluster bootstrap (at least 1,000 resamples) of held-out differences in Brier score and log loss at 720, 1440, and 2880 minutes, calibration intercept/slope, calibration curves, and cumulative-incidence risk curves. AUROC/AUPRC are secondary. Report hospital, stay, event, competing-event, censoring, and label-readiness counts. No threshold is selected from test results.

## Process/treatment rivals and falsification

Fit a separate observation-process model for any valid post-360 creatinine measurement and time to first measurement, using the same frozen vocabulary without the rise requirement. Strong duration-to-testing association is evidence compatible with ascertainment/clinical-concern coupling; it is not proof of bias. Apply observation weights only when their positivity/support diagnostics pass.

Use a pre-index negative-control event: among stays with valid creatinine in -48 to -24 and a distinct result in -24 to 0, apply the same +0.3 rule before the exposure window. A comparable or stronger association is adverse evidence for baseline severity/measurement process, not causal proof. Use common-support strata fixed before test evaluation: nadir band, covered count, baseline creatinine band, initial vitals, BP modality/missingness, and S/O/T propensity overlap. Refit all nuisance models in each training fold; never residualize or select strata with test information.

The infusion table supports only conservative recorded-pressor indicators/timing and possibly raw-field summaries after a name/field audit; it has no verified stop time, dose unit, or treatment indication. Compare treatment-masked primary models with treatment-inclusive ablations. Loss after treatment inclusion is compatible with treatment-response/recorded-severity coupling; persistence does not identify a causal effect.

Use `carePlanEOL.cpleolsaveoffset` and `cpleoldiscussionoffset` only when <=360 for index predictors. Never use post-index EOL rows or `activeupondischarge` as predictors; post-index EOL may be descriptive censoring context only. A broken-linkage negative control may permute complete early BP trajectories within hospital x nadir band x coverage stratum while keeping outcomes/B0 fixed; the incremental temporal signal should disappear.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive scalar result: label gate passes; B1 improves held-out Brier/log loss over B0 with hospital-cluster uncertainty excluding no improvement; duration-risk is coherent within fixed-nadir/common-support strata; the gain survives density thinning, modality and threshold sensitivities, process weighting, and hospital holdout; and no comparable pre-index negative-control association is present. This supports an independently useful prognostic scalar summary only.

Supportive trajectory result: M improves the same held-out scores over B1, is calibrated across hospitals, and original-versus-order-permuted M shows a prespecified loss of temporal information. This supports useful ordering/recovery information beyond duration/nadir. If M adds no stable information while B1 is stable, the scalar summary is adequate for this question. If M adds information but B1 does not, the duration estimand is insufficient and the next study should focus on trajectory descriptors. Neither outcome establishes renal hypoperfusion, causality, a MAP target, or treatment benefit.

Adverse result: label readiness fails; B1's gain is matched by the observation-process model or pre-index control; gain disappears after observation weighting, density thinning, common-support restriction, hospital holdout, or recorded-treatment adjustment; M learns only masks/treatment; or original and order-permuted M are equivalent. These favor ascertainment, selection, treatment-response, severity, or non-temporal explanations.

Inconclusive result: missing units leave scale unresolved, eligible events are too few, hospital support is inadequate, censoring/observation positivity fails, intervals are wide, or model rankings reverse across prespecified splits. Do not call an imprecise null a refutation. Obtain clinical laboratory/nephrology adjudication, validated treatment and urine/dialysis semantics, and independent external data before stronger claims.

The verifier can check snapshot/source hashes, joins, exact windows, first-stay selection, binning, no future EOL leakage, split integrity, training-only preprocessing, held-out predictions, uncertainty, and conclusion-to-output linkage. It cannot adjudicate laboratory units/analyte identity, BP modality validity, clinical intent, residual unmeasured severity, mechanism, or causal benefit. Those require expert review or another study.

## Alternatives, resource budget, and deliverable

A simple scalar regression is retained because it directly estimates the parent’s duration-versus-nadir question and is auditable. The GRU is retained because it can test a distinct ordering/recovery hypothesis on the exact same inputs and target, not because it is more complex. A temporal convolution of two width-3 layers with 32 channels is the preregistered implementation substitute if GRU library/runtime support fails; it receives the identical sequence and loss. A latent state-space model is deferred because perfusion/congestion/renal biomarkers and validated state labels are unavailable. A transformer is deferred because it adds capacity without a distinct scientific uncertainty. A causal vasopressor model is deferred because dose units, stops, indications, and assignment are unavailable. Revisit these choices only with validated units/intent, adjudicated labels, richer physiologic data, or external validation.
