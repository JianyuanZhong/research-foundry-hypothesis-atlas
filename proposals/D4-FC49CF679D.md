# Direction-A incremental-information experiment: cystatin-C threshold discordance beyond a flexible creatinine comparator

## Lineage, unresolved question, and substantive advance

This is a prospective design child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. It does not rerun or reinterpret the parent's adjusted association. The parent established, in the frozen selected complete-biomarker first-repeat-attender cohort, a Direction-A model-standardized association between baseline cystatin-C threshold discordance and repeat creatinine-derived eGFR below 60. Direction A contained 16,372 selected attenders and 309 repeat events; its cells `(E0Y0,E0Y1,E1Y0,E1Y1)` were `(15,769,255,294,54)`. The adjusted risk difference was 0.02020 (Bonferroni simultaneous interval 0.00872 to 0.03296), but this was an in-sample estimate from a model that represented baseline eGFRcr, age, sex, and elapsed time only as linear main effects. Direction B was inconclusive and is not retested here.

The strongest evidence-supported claim remains narrow: among the selected Direction-A repeat attenders, baseline `eGFRcys_0 < 60` was associated with repeat `eGFRcr_1 < 60` after the parent's fixed linear adjustment. The unresolved and falsifiable claim is stronger but still noncausal: **does the binary baseline cystatin-C discordance indicator add reproducible out-of-sample information beyond a prospectively frozen, regularized, nonlinear comparator based only on baseline eGFRcr, baseline age, sex, and the observed first-repeat interval?** The principal advance is to replace an adjusted in-sample coefficient/RD question with a paired cross-fitted prediction comparison, calibration gates, and an exactly capacity-matched event-concentration estimand.

A supportive result would show incremental protocol-biomarker information in this selected cohort. It would not show CKD, chronicity, measured GFR decline, renal specificity, causality, benefit of ordering cystatin C, treatment or referral benefit, clinical utility, risk in nonattenders, or transportability.

## Frozen prediction algorithms

The prediction unit is a participant in `S_A`; the only outcome is that participant's first qualifying repeat value. Both models are fit without class weights and produce absolute probabilities.

### Comparator M0

`M0` is ridge-penalized logistic regression using only baseline `eGFRcr_0`, baseline age, sex, and elapsed time. To make threshold proximity and time dependence a strong comparator rather than a linear straw model, its design matrix contains:

- an unpenalized intercept;
- sex;
- a restricted cubic spline (RCS) basis for `eGFRcr_0` with five knots at training-set quantiles 0.05, 0.275, 0.50, 0.725, and 0.95 (four basis columns including the linear component);
- RCS bases for baseline age and elapsed time, each with four knots at training-set quantiles 0.05, 0.35, 0.65, and 0.95 (three basis columns each);
- all 12 products of the four eGFRcr basis columns with the three elapsed-time basis columns;
- four products of sex with the eGFRcr basis columns.

Use the standard restricted-cubic truncated-power basis of Harrell, with the first and last listed knots as boundary knots. Estimate knots only in the applicable training partition, then apply those knots unchanged to validation/test rows. Within every applicable training partition, center and scale every nonbinary design column using training means and standard deviations; apply those values unchanged outside that partition. Zero-variance or coincident-knot failures invalidate that fit.

### Extended model M1

`M1` contains every `M0` term and adds exactly two cystatin-derived terms: `E` and `E*(eGFRcr_0-60)/10`. The interaction permits a parsimonious variation in the discordance contribution by creatinine-threshold proximity without giving the cystatin model an unrestricted smooth or access to repeat cystatin C. No continuous baseline cystatin value, alternative discordance definition, combined-marker equation, post-baseline biomarker, additional covariate, feature screening, or outcome-guided transformation is permitted.

All non-intercept coefficients, including sex and the two added terms, receive the same L2 penalty. For each model separately, tune lambda over the fixed grid `10^(-4), 10^(-3.5), ..., 10^(4)` using inner cross-validation and unweighted Bernoulli log loss. Choose the largest lambda whose mean inner-fold loss is within one standard error of the minimum (the one-standard-error rule); ties choose the larger lambda. Preprocessing, knots, scaling, and penalized fitting are repeated from inner-training data inside every inner fold. This independently tuned but otherwise nested pair compares two fully frozen learning algorithms, not two coefficients from one full-data fit.

## Primary estimands and fixed testing hierarchy

Both primary contrasts are paired on the same participants and their two averaged cross-fitted probabilities.

1. **Absolute-risk accuracy gain:** `Delta_Brier = 1000 * [mean((Y-p0)^2) - mean((Y-p1)^2)]`. Positive values are fewer squared probability-error units per 1,000 selected attenders. This proper-score contrast is the first test of incremental information.
2. **Fixed-capacity event-yield gain:** let `K=ceil(0.10*N)` (expected K=1,638). For each model, select exactly the K participants with highest averaged cross-fitted risk. Resolve exact probability ties with ascending SHA-256 of the UTF-8 string `"direction-A-capacity-v1|" + eid`; the hash is used locally and never published. Define `Delta_yield10 = 1000 * (events_in_topK_M1 - events_in_topK_M0)/K`, the additional observed repeat eGFRcr-threshold events per 1,000 slots at exactly equal 10% capacity. Also report capture fractions, top-K observed risks, the number promoted/displaced by M1, and the event counts among promoted and displaced participants. These are protocol-performance summaries, not a test-ordering recommendation or utility/net-benefit analysis.

Use a fixed-sequence family: test positive `Delta_Brier` first; test positive `Delta_yield10` only if the first passes. Each step uses a two-sided 95% interval and symmetric null-centered two-sided bootstrap p-value at 0.05; fixed sequence controls the two-claim family without post hoc metric selection. The incremental-information claim requires both steps. AUC difference and paired log-loss difference may be reported as descriptive diagnostics with bootstrap intervals but cannot rescue a failed primary step and are not separately labeled confirmatory.

## Calibration, support, and event-scarcity gates

Before any supportive incremental interpretation, all of the following must hold:

- exact parent cohort/cell reconstruction and all source/join checks pass;
- all four `E x Y` cells occur in every outer training and test partition and every inner training partition; every outer test fold has at least 50 total outcomes and at least 8 `E=1,Y=1` outcomes;
- every penalized fit converges, has finite coefficients/probabilities, and fewer than 1% of predictions require clipping for log loss;
- for M0 and M1 separately, pooled calibration-in-the-large is represented by observed/expected event ratio in `[0.90,1.10]`, and the logistic recalibration slope from `Y ~ 1 + logit(p)` is in `[0.80,1.20]`; report bootstrap intervals for both rather than treating the point gates as proof of calibration;
- M0 cross-fitted Brier score is better than the intercept-only prevalence predictor, so the comparator itself has nontrivial signal;
- at least 8 of the 10 outer repeats have positive repeat-specific `Delta_Brier`, and, if the capacity step is reached, at least 8 have positive repeat-specific `Delta_yield10` using that repeat's out-of-fold probabilities;
- at least 2,000 successful bootstrap replicates are retained, and failed replicate counts and reasons are reported.

Report ten equal-count calibration bins for each model with only aggregate N, outcomes, mean prediction, and observed risk. No bin with fewer than 20 participants may be published. Also report model-specific top-K counts and aggregate outcomes; do not publish participant predictions, fold assignments, ranks, hashes, coefficients linked to participants, or `eid`.

## Exact bootstrap hierarchy and multiplicity

After all ten repeated nested cross-fitting runs are complete, freeze the two 10-column out-of-fold prediction matrices. Use a paired participant-level nonparametric bootstrap with replacement from the N unique selected attenders. In each replicate, carry together a participant's outcome, exposure, covariates, and all ten paired M0/M1 predictions; average the ten predictions within participant; then recompute calibration, Brier contrast, AUC/log-loss diagnostics, and both top-capacity lists on the bootstrap sample. Repeated copies of a participant remain one resampled cluster and receive multiplicity weight. For the capacity calculation, rank unique sampled participants by prediction and fixed hash, accumulate multiplicity weights, and allow only the boundary participant's final multiplicity to be fractionally counted so total capacity is exactly `ceil(0.10 * sum(weights))`; use the same rule for outcome totals. This avoids splitting duplicate copies across development/test sets because models are not refit in the bootstrap.

Use a fixed seed of 260921 and continue until 2,000 successful replicates are obtained, with a maximum of 2,500 attempts. Report percentile 95% intervals. For each contrast, compute the plus-one symmetric null-centered two-sided p-value as `(1 + #{b: |Delta_b-Delta_hat| >= |Delta_hat|})/(B+1)`. The bootstrap is conditional on the ten frozen cross-fitting algorithms and seeds: it quantifies participant-sampling uncertainty in the paired out-of-fold performance, while the ten-repeat sign gate addresses split instability. It does not fully quantify algorithm-development uncertainty from refitting models in new cohorts; that limitation must accompany any result.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

A result is **supportive** only if every integrity, support, event-scarcity, comparator-signal, and calibration gate passes; `Delta_Brier > 0` with its 95% interval excluding zero and p<0.05; the fixed sequence advances; and `Delta_yield10 > 0` with its 95% interval excluding zero and p<0.05. The permitted conclusion is that baseline cystatin-C threshold discordance adds reproducible out-of-sample information and improves event concentration at the frozen 10% capacity within this selected complete-biomarker repeat-attender protocol cohort.

A result is **adverse** if a primary contrast has a 95% interval wholly below zero, if M1 materially fails calibration while M0 passes, or if the repeat signs reproducibly favor M0. This would argue that the parent's adjusted association does not translate into useful out-of-sample incremental information under the frozen comparator, or may degrade risk concentration.

A result is **inconclusive** if either interval includes zero; the first test fails and the capacity test is therefore not confirmatory; proper-score and capacity results conflict; calibration/support gates fail; split signs are unstable; event/swap counts are too sparse for stable estimation; or bootstrap completion fails. An inconclusive result must not be reframed as equivalence or absence of all cystatin-C information.

Even a supportive result cannot establish clinical utility because the outcome is one protocol-derived creatinine threshold at an attendance-selected visit, repeat timing is conditioned upon, the population already has baseline and repeat cystatin C observed, and no consequences of testing/action are represented. External validation in a separately recruited population, a genuinely baseline-deployable horizon, assay traceability, repeated measurements establishing chronicity, measured or otherwise clinically adjudicated kidney function, missing-follow-up evidence, and decision-impact or randomized testing would be required for CKD, ordering, referral, treatment, nonattender, or benefit claims.
