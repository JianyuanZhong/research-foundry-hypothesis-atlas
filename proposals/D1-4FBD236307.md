# Paired hepatic-reserve change after TACE2 with an explicit laboratory-observation experiment

## Unresolved clinical question and advance

Among adults with recorded HCC who have already undergone a second TACE, does a systemic HCC-directed medication record during TACE2 days 1–14 identify a different subsequent biochemical trajectory from an ambiguity-excluded TACE2-only recorded pathway, or is the apparent difference in day-30–90 laboratory levels explained by pre-existing liver state, calendar/clinical selection, and differential laboratory surveillance?

This is clinically important because clinicians may interpret lower post-TACE albumin or higher bilirubin as evidence that one treatment strategy preserves or damages hepatic reserve. In this source, that interpretation is especially vulnerable: the systemic-record arm is concentrated in later years and has a different pre-index care history; exact baseline albumin and bilirubin already differ; and follow-up laboratory recording is pathway selective. A fixed follow-up level alone cannot distinguish biochemical change from baseline severity or selective testing.

The substantive advance over the parent is therefore not a new treatment-effect claim. It is a bias-resistant, falsifiable experiment that:

1. preserves TACE2 as the common time zero and the day-14 exposure landmark;
2. makes within-person exact-assay change, rather than a follow-up level, the primary outcome;
3. compares several locked baseline and follow-up selection rules without choosing a favorable result;
4. treats baseline, follow-up, and paired laboratory observation as outcomes in their own right;
5. incorporates assay-specific inverse-probability-of-observation sensitivity analyses;
6. uses pre-exposure change in the same assay as the primary negative control for residual channeling or temporal leakage; and
7. explicitly treats the current hepatic-outcome yield as a precision-limited boundary case rather than silently relying on a non-reproducible count threshold.

### What is already supported

A complete-source audit supports computability, not the clinical hypothesis. Under the independently implemented strict ontology it found 319 systemic-record and 1,491 TACE2-only patients after the day-14/2018–2024 and ambiguity rules, with 99 versus 387 paired exact `白蛋白` and `总胆红素` measurements using days -30 to -1 and days 30–90. Baseline and follow-up timing were similar among paired patients, but observation was selective: paired albumin/bilirubin was observed in 99/319 versus 387/1,491, an unadjusted risk difference of 0.0508, and a cross-validated pre-index observation model had AUC 0.673. The systemic-record arm was later-calendar (year SMD 0.393), had a shorter TACE1–TACE2 interval (SMD -0.198), and had more prior procedures (90-day SMD 0.248). Baseline albumin SMD was -0.149 and baseline bilirubin SMD -0.214. These facts support the need for the experiment; they do not support benefit, harm, or causality.

The audit also found that the parent’s stated 317/1,492 arms and 100/393 paired outcomes are not reproduced exactly by an independent implementation. The discrepancy is small at the arm level but crosses the parent’s exposed-pair gate. Therefore cohort reconciliation is a mandatory first output, and current evidence must be described as precision-limited feasibility.

### Untested hypothesis

Conditional on the day-14 recorded pathway and adequate measured overlap, immediate systemic recording will be associated with a more favorable within-person hepatic-reserve profile from pre-TACE2 baseline to days 30–90: less albumin decline (or greater increase) without a larger bilirubin increase than the TACE2-only recorded pathway. This is an association hypothesis about records, not a treatment-effect hypothesis.

### Primary paired outcome

For each assay separately:

* baseline: latest timestamp in TACE2 days -30 through -1;
* follow-up: observation closest to day 60 within days 30–90, breaking equal-distance ties toward the earlier calendar day and then the latest timestamp on that day;
* outcome: follow-up minus baseline on the exact assay’s observed scale.

Albumin and bilirubin remain separate outcomes. The directional profile is favorable only when albumin change favors the systemic-record arm and bilirubin change does not indicate a larger increase. Do not collapse them into an unvalidated composite.

### Locked timing sensitivities

Repeat both exact assays with baseline windows -14 to -1 and -60 to -1. Repeat follow-up selection using earliest and latest measurement in days 30–90, closest to day 60 in days 45–75, and closest to day 90 in days 60–120. Report all estimates in a single matrix; no window may replace the primary because it is more favorable. Empirically, the narrower days 45–75 window has only 68 exposed pairs and days 60–120 has 81, so these are precision-limited sensitivities.

### Repeated measurements

Repeated-measure trajectories are secondary and descriptive. For albumin/bilirubin, 107/319 and 427/1,491 patients had at least two day-30–90 albumin measurements (108 and 428 for bilirubin), but only 44 and 190 had at least three. Fit a mixed model using all exact-assay numeric observations from days 30–90 only if there are at least two measurements in both arms and convergence/positivity diagnostics pass: assay value as the outcome, continuous day centered at 60, recorded arm, arm-by-day interaction, baseline assay value, pre-index adjustment variables, and a patient random intercept. Report the number of patients and observations and the highly selected repeated-measure subset. This model cannot supersede the paired primary estimate and must not be interpreted as a population treatment trajectory.

## Estimands

1. **Primary biochemical estimands:** overlap-weighted adjusted mean differences between recorded pathways in within-person albumin change and, separately, bilirubin change from the -30/-1 baseline to target-nearest day-60 measurement in days 30–90, conditional on belonging to the day-14 institutional-observation population and having the relevant measurements. Report complete-case and observation-weighted versions.
2. **Baseline-state estimands:** adjusted differences in follow-up assay level with baseline assay entered flexibly, used as a sensitivity to the change-score parameterization; and arm differences in baseline assay values/availability.
3. **Observation-process estimands:** arm-specific risks and adjusted risk differences for (a) baseline observation, (b) follow-up observation, and (c) paired observation for each assay; arm-specific counts of follow-up measurements; and assay/timing distributions.
4. **Negative-control estimands:** adjusted arm difference in same-assay pre-exposure change from the observation closest to day -45 in days -60 to -31 to the latest observation in days -30 to -1. This tests measured channeling/temporal leakage, not exchangeability.

All are recorded-pathway associations. No estimand is an intention-to-treat, per-protocol, or causal treatment effect.

## Analysis

### Baseline and calendar selection

Describe age, sex, TACE1–TACE2 interval, TACE2 year and month, department, prior encounter counts over 30/90/180 days, prior procedure counts over 90/180 days, prior resection/ablation/transplant label indicators, encounter-timed diagnosis proxies for cirrhosis, portal hypertension, ascites, encephalopathy, variceal bleeding, portal-vein invasion and metastasis, and closest pre-TACE2 exact albumin, bilirubin, INR (`国际标准化比值`), platelets (`血小板计数`), creatinine (`肌酐`), sodium (`钠`), and AFP if its exact label is catalog-verified. Use values determined no later than TACE2, with missingness indicators. Report standardized mean differences and missingness by arm.

Fit a prespecified logistic propensity model for the systemic-record pathway using only pre-TACE2 variables, flexible calendar terms, and no post-index laboratory, later TACE, or medication information. Use overlap weights. Report propensity distributions overall and by year, arm-specific weight distributions, covariate SMDs before/after weighting, and effective sample size.

For each primary assay, fit overlap-weighted linear regression of change on arm with robust patient-level sandwich uncertainty, additionally including baseline assay and any covariate remaining |SMD| >0.10 after weighting. Report raw arm means, raw mean difference, adjusted mean difference, 95% confidence interval, and exact denominators. Because bilirubin and liver enzymes are heavy-tailed in the audit, add prespecified median/quantile-regression and percentile-winsorized sensitivity estimates while retaining the raw mean as primary; disclose the percentile rule and never select among them by significance.

### Laboratory-observation experiment

For each assay, fit separate observation models in the full eligible cohort for baseline observed, follow-up observed, and paired observed. Predictors must be available by TACE2: recorded arm, age, sex, TACE interval, year/month, department, prior encounter/procedure intensity, diagnosis proxies, available baseline covariates and missingness indicators. Report five-fold cross-validated AUC and calibration, arm coefficient/risk difference, predicted-probability distributions, and observed fractions.

Estimate inverse probabilities of paired observation and combine these with overlap weights for an assay-specific sensitivity. Prespecify truncation at the 1st/99th percentiles and additionally show untruncated weights. Report the combined-weight range and ESS. Compare unweighted complete-case, overlap-weighted complete-case, and overlap-plus-observation-weighted estimates. This analysis addresses selection on measured pre-index data only; it cannot correct unmeasured reasons for testing, outside testing, death, or loss of institutional contact.

The audit demonstrates the observation problem is substantive. For albumin/bilirubin, baseline availability was essentially equal (172/319 versus 805/1,491; risk difference -0.0007), while follow-up availability was higher in the systemic-record arm (169/319 versus 683/1,491; risk difference 0.0717) and paired availability differed by 0.0508. For more frequently recorded assays, paired-observation differences were even larger: 0.128 for hemoglobin, 0.166 for ALT, and 0.141 for INR. These are observation-process findings, not biological outcomes.

### Negative controls and ancillary assays

The primary falsification outcome is pre-exposure same-assay change. It is feasible for albumin in 89 versus 350 patients and bilirubin in 89 versus 351. The unadjusted pre-exposure mean-change differences were -0.11 for albumin and -1.46 for bilirubin; these descriptive values require the same overlap adjustment and uncertainty before interpretation.

Do not call creatinine, sodium, hemoglobin, platelets, ALT, AST, or INR post-exposure biological negative controls merely because they are frequent or co-observed. TACE, systemic therapy, liver disease, bleeding, hydration, renal injury, and surveillance may affect them. Their same-day co-observation can diagnose panel-ordering and surveillance: among patients with any day-30–90 albumin, exact same-day counts ranged from 166–169 systemic and 650–683 comparator across these assays. Biological negative-control status would require clinical adjudication external to this computation. Analyze these assays only as exploratory panel/surveillance outcomes with multiplicity and claim limits stated.

## Gates and falsification criteria

1. **Cohort reconciliation:** report the exact cause of the 317/1,492 versus 319/1,491 discrepancy. If arm membership or direction materially changes across reasonable locked interpretations, classify the study as ontology-unstable/feasibility-only.
2. **Paired-outcome precision:** the independent audit found 99 exposed pairs, one below the parent’s stated >=100 gate. Do not round, borrow another window, or change the era. Unless exact reconciliation legitimately restores the locked gate, label primary hepatic estimates precision-limited. Regardless of count, report confidence intervals and ESS rather than treating 100 as proof of adequate power.
3. **Positivity/calendar:** require visible pooled and within-year overlap, post-weighting |SMD| <=0.10 for core covariates, no extreme concentration of weight, and report arm ESS. A year without overlap is not repaired after results by silently changing the era. The 2020–2024 subset is prespecified sensitivity only.
4. **Observation stability:** report untruncated/truncated weights. Extremely small predicted observation probabilities, major ESS collapse, poor calibration, or material direction conflict between complete-case and observation-weighted analyses makes the biochemical result inconclusive.
5. **Negative control:** a clearly non-null adjusted pre-exposure same-assay contrast in a direction/magnitude capable of explaining the post-index result falsifies the claim that the post-index contrast is newly emergent. A null control does not establish exchangeability.
6. **Timing:** report assay-day distributions and estimates under all locked selection rules. A direction reversal across first, last, target-nearest, or baseline windows indicates timing-dependent ascertainment and prevents a stable clinical interpretation.
7. **Ontology:** repeat strict/broad TACE and placebo/bevacizumab-partner variants. A material direction reversal or large unexplained arm change falsifies robustness.
8. **Outcome coherence:** a favorable albumin association accompanied by clearly worse bilirubin, or vice versa, is mixed/adverse rather than supportive. No composite may hide discordance.
9. **Repeated-measure selection:** failure to converge, insufficient repeated observations, or strong differences between the repeated subset and full cohort makes trajectory modeling descriptive-only; it never invalidates the paired analysis mechanically.

## Interpretation branches

* **Supportive:** after cohort reconciliation, adequate overlap, stable observation weights, and no material pre-exposure signal, the adjusted paired analysis shows less albumin decline (or greater increase) and no larger bilirubin increase in the systemic-record pathway, with compatible directions across timing, era, and ontology sensitivities. This supports only a more favorable *recorded-pathway association* among observed patients.
* **Adverse:** robustly greater albumin decline and/or bilirubin increase in the systemic-record pathway across the same gates. This argues against assuming a liver-preserving association; it does not show medication-caused toxicity or harm.
* **Precise null:** confidence intervals are narrow on the observed assay scales around no arm difference, and all gates pass. Because units and clinically validated margins are unavailable, call this no detectable recorded-pathway difference, not clinical equivalence or equal safety.
* **Falsified:** the post-index contrast is plausibly reproduced before exposure, reverses under locked ontology/timing rules, lacks calendar positivity, or depends on unresolved exposure ambiguity. Report the failed premise, not a treatment conclusion.
* **Inconclusive:** exposed paired count/ESS is too small, intervals are wide, observation weights are unstable, albumin and bilirubin conflict, sensitivity directions differ, or essential adjudication is missing. This is the expected designation if the current 99-pair reconstruction persists without sufficient precision; no favorable window may be promoted.

## Claims that computation cannot establish

Medication records cannot establish administration, adherence, dose, combination delivery, indication, or intent. Institutional observation is not survival. Diagnosis timing is inherited from encounters. The source lacks validated laboratory units, complete outside-care capture, reliable performance status and Child-Pugh components, treatment rationale, adjudicated TACE refractoriness, imaging response/progression, toxicity attribution, and linked vital status. Automatic verification can check cohort construction, timestamps, assay selection, numerical parsing, weighting, diagnostics, and whether reported conclusions follow from computed uncertainty. It cannot establish clinical benefit, toxicity causation, response, progression, survival, or treatment superiority. Those stronger claims require medication-administration evidence, clinician review, validated units and outcomes, outside-care/vital-status linkage, and preferably an external or prospective study.
