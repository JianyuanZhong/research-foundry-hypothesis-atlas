# eICU repair design: laboratory-monitoring reduction during persistent instability

## Provisional hypothesis

Among adults with a continuous ICU stay who are physiologically unstable at a prespecified index, a reduction in laboratory testing intensity during the immediately preceding 12 hours is associated with a higher risk of a clinically defined deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond contemporaneous measured severity and recent support. The association should attenuate when end-of-life care-plan documentation and discharge/transfer context are explicitly handled; a residual association in held-out hospitals would be a monitoring-process signal, not proof that changing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

## Evidence-supported versus untested claims

The available catalog and table metadata support only that eICU has longitudinal, timestamped laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, patient, and hospital records, with `patientunitstayid` as the within-dataset join key and `hospitalid` for hospital-level stratification. The parent expert seed supplies the opening that testing frequency/missingness may encode hospital practice and may transport poorly. It does not establish a clinical deterioration association. The unresolved claim is whether a *dynamic* laboratory-testing reduction, conditional on current instability, predicts near-term deterioration beyond current state.

## Population and time

Use adult `patient` stays with a valid `unitdischargeoffset` greater than 18 hours and a unit interval. Restrict the primary analysis to continuous ICU stays (no transfer/early discharge before the outcome window); handle discharge, transfer, and death as competing events rather than silently coding them as non-deterioration. Index at hourly times from 6 through `unitdischargeoffset - 12` hours. Require at least one valid vital observation and one laboratory observation in the preceding 24 hours; label missingness separately from zero testing.

## Exposure

At index t, count distinct `labname` values and distinct laboratory episodes in (t-12,t], using `labresultoffset`; do not count a revised result as a new test unless its result time is distinct. Compare the prior interval (t-24,t-12] with the current interval. Primary reduction: current distinct-analyte count is zero after a positive prior count, or falls by at least 50% when the prior interval has at least two episodes. Secondary analyses use continuous change and episode count. This is an exposure measured before the outcome window.

## Outcome and competing events

Primary deterioration is a prespecified binary event in (t,t+12]: new or escalated respiratory support from `respiratoryCare` (`respcarestatusoffset`, `airwaytype`, `ventstartoffset`, `ventendoffset`, `highironoxlimit`) or new/escalated vasopressor/infusion from `infusionDrug` (`infusionoffset`, `drugname`, `drugrate`, `infusionrate`, `patientweight`). Use the first qualifying event; do not use post-index laboratory values. Death before the event is a competing event. Discharge/transfer before the event is also a competing event, with a separate sensitivity analysis excluding those stays. `apachePatientResult` mortality fields (`actualicumortality`, `actualhospitalmortality`) are secondary outcomes, not substitutes for time-local deterioration.

## Contemporaneous severity and rival explanations

Adjust only for measurements available at or before t: `vitalPeriodic` (`observationoffset`, temperature, SaO2, heart rate, respiration, systemic pressures), respiratory state from `respiratoryCharting` (`respchartoffset`, `respchartentryoffset`, type/value labels), recent infusion/support, age, sex, unit type, and hospital. Do not use future values or post-index labs.

End-of-life is a rival, not a nuisance to be hidden. Use `carePlanEOL` (`cpleolsaveoffset`, `cpleoldiscussionoffset`, `activeupondischarge`) to define prespecified EOL-context strata and a sensitivity analysis excluding documented EOL plans before t. Also model discharge/transfer status/location and recent support de-escalation. These fields are sparse and documentation-dependent; missing EOL documentation is not equivalent to no EOL care.

## Analysis and falsification

Fit a prespecified discrete-time logistic baseline with the same index times, competing-event censoring, hospital fixed/clustered effects, and clustered uncertainty. Compare it with a longitudinal recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard model with time-varying testing intensity and physiologic trajectories. The latter can reveal whether the signal is transient, persistent, or preceded by a trajectory; it is not justified merely for predictive gain.

Use hospital-held-out validation, calibration, discrimination, absolute risk difference, and uncertainty intervals. Negative controls are: (1) testing intervals permuted within hospital while preserving stay length and outcome; (2) a post-index testing exposure, which should not predict the prior deterioration; and (3) a documentation-timestamp control from non-laboratory charting where available. A result that disappears with EOL/discharge adjustment or negative controls supports a documentation/selection explanation.

Supportive: reproducible held-out-hospital association after state and EOL/discharge handling, with negative controls null. Adverse: null, instability, or attenuation after explicit rival adjustment. Inconclusive: wide intervals, sparse events, or inability to distinguish interface interruption from clinical intent. None establishes causality or a recommendation to increase testing.

## Exact bindings and limitations

All joins are within eICU by `patientunitstayid`; hospital by `hospitalid`. Sources are read-only:
- `patient.csv.gz`: patient, hospitalid, age, gender, unitadmitoffset, unitdischargeoffset, unitdischargestatus/location, unittype, unitvisitnumber.
- `lab.csv.gz`: labid, patientunitstayid, labresultoffset, labname, labresult, labresulttext, labresultrevisedoffset.
- `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`: patientunitstayid, observationoffset, temperature, sao2, heartrate, respiration, systemic pressures.
- `respiratoryCare.csv.gz`: patientunitstayid, respcarestatusoffset, airwaytype, ventstartoffset, ventendoffset, highironoxlimit.
- `respiratoryCharting.csv.gz`: patientunitstayid, respchartoffset, respchartentryoffset, respcharttypecat, respchartvaluelabel, respchartvalue.
- `infusionDrug.csv.gz`: patientunitstayid, infusionoffset, drugname, drugrate, infusionrate, patientweight.
- `carePlanEOL.csv.gz`: patientunitstayid, cpleolsaveoffset, cpleoldiscussionoffset, activeupondischarge.
- `apachePatientResult.csv.gz`: patientunitstayid, actualicumortality, actualhospitalmortality, actualhospitallos.
- `hospital.csv.gz`: hospitalid and hospital descriptors.

The full source headers and a bounded row sample still need to be inspected before execution; the catalog metadata confirms the listed columns. No full cohort analysis has been run. Missing evidence includes clinical adjudication of deterioration, direct interface-interruption flags, staffing/bed capacity, complete pre-ICU history, and reliable EOL intent. A future solver may fit the baseline and longitudinal alternative on CPU; GPU is not required.
