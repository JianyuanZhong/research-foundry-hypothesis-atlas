# Successor A: does late (12–24 h) lactate nonclearance still identify risk after early vasopressor exposure?

## Decision and unresolved claim

The Episode-25 four-hour question is stopped exactly where it was: its frozen 2–4-hour comparison has only three exposure-balanced hospitals and 62 people, so it estimates neither the intended association nor an adverse result. This proposal does not change any of those gates.

The clinically important unresolved claim is different in time: among adults with an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed row and lactate at least 2 mmol/L, who have survived to a 24-hour reassessment landmark, does less than 20% lactate clearance measured during hours 12–24 identify at least a 10-percentage-point higher subsequent hospital-mortality risk than at least 20% clearance? The clinical decision is whether a patient who remains in the hospital at 24 hours should trigger a renewed perfusion/source-control review even when the initial 2–4-hour lactate experiment is not estimable.

This is a prognostic association, not a treatment effect, proof of tissue hypoperfusion, or evidence that acting on lactate improves outcome. Existing EICU evidence supports only that the separate selected eight-hour phenotype had a positive, noncausal association with mortality, while its prespecified 10-point magnitude was unresolved. A current full-text study (Yang et al., DOI 10.3389/fmed.2026.1880921, acquired source [source checksum]) similarly reports that an early perfusion contour was recoverable in eICU only in measurement-dense complete cases and does not establish transportability of the full phenotype or bedside utility. Neither supports the later 12–24-hour claim. The advance is a fixed later landmark that tests whether lactate retains decision-relevant prognostic meaning after the early measurement window, without selecting a more favorable gate after seeing the four-hour result.

## Frozen population and temporal boundaries

Use the complete EICU snapshot [source checksum], with sources read-only and no sampling.

1. From adult patient rows, map age >89 to 90 and retain age >=18. Identify candidate t0 for each stay as the minimum infusionDrug.infusionoffset in [0, 1440] whose case-folded drugname contains norepinephrine or levophed. For each uniquepid, retain the lexicographically smallest (t0, patientunitstayid). This is the parent’s deterministic early-exposure rule; it is not a dose claim.
2. Require hospitaldischargestatus exactly Alive or Expired, hospitaldischargeoffset >= L24, where L24=t0+1440, and retain D=1 only for Expired. Because EICU has no exact death time, the discharge-offset landmark rule is the only computable survival-to-landmark rule; do not infer cause or time of death.
3. Define baseline B using lab rows with case-insensitive labname='lactate', labmeasurenamesystem='mmol/L', numeric labresult in [0.2,30], labresultoffset in [t0-360,t0], and labresultrevisedoffset <= t0+60. At each collection offset keep the greatest eligible revision; invalidate the collection if eligible values disagree; identical duplicates use smallest labid. Choose the latest valid collection and require B>=2.
4. Define late follow-up F24 from the same strict analyte/unit/range/conflict rules, with collection offset in [t0+720,t0+1440] and revision by L24. Select the collection nearest t0+1080, then earlier collection offset, then smallest labid. Define X=1 (late nonclearance) when (B-F24)/B < 0.20; X=0 otherwise.
5. All variables used to adjust the association must be available by t0+720, before the late-lactate window begins. The outcome is read only after the population, lab, and hospital exposure-balance gates are frozen.

A complete-source feasibility scan applying the above baseline and late-window rules found 1,468 measured people (957 X=0, 511 X=1) in 96 hospitals. An outcome-blind site gate of >=5 people in each exposure group retains 33 hospitals and 1,198 people (764 X=0, 434 X=1; 244 and 250 deaths, respectively). These are feasibility counts, not an estimate of the hypothesis; the final executable analysis must reproduce them from the source files before reading outcome values for site selection.

## Analysis and fixed falsification rules

The primary estimand is the standardized hospital-mortality risk difference RD = P(D=1|X=1) - P(D=1|X=0) in the measured-overlap target, conditional on surviving to L24. Fit one prespecified L2 logistic model (C=10) with X, the listed pre-12-hour covariates, missing indicators, and hospital indicators. Winsorize and scale continuous covariates using pooled 1st/99th-percentile rules frozen before outcome modeling. Use normalized overlap weights (1-e for X=1 and e for X=0), report standardized risks and RD in percentage points, and report crude risks separately. This is association estimation; it is not a g-formula treatment effect.

Require before interpretation: >=12 exposure-balanced hospitals, >=500 people, >=50 deaths in each exposure group, propensity scores in [0.02,0.98], group effective sample size >=40, maximum person weight share <=5%, hospital weight share <=35%, every model-column absolute SMD <=0.10, >=95% convergence across 5,000 copied-hospital-then-copied-uniquepid bootstrap replicates, and finite estimates in at least 80% of leave-one-hospital-out refits. Use seed 20260924. Hospital eligibility is exposure-count-only and is frozen before reading any death field for site selection.

- Supportive: every gate passes; the 95% bootstrap interval for RD has lower bound >=+10 percentage points; and at least 80% of finite leave-one-hospital-out RDs are positive.
- Adverse to the clinically relevant +10-point claim: every gate passes but the interval’s upper bound is <+10 points.
- Opposite direction: every gate passes and the interval’s upper bound is <0.
- Inconclusive: any population, event, balance, convergence, or site-deletion gate fails; the estimate is unstable; or the interval contains +10 points.

Supportive or adverse results remain prognostic associations only. A decision-impact study is required to show that acting on the 24-hour phenotype improves care. Randomized or credible target-trial data with administered vasopressor dose, fluids, source control, organ support, specimen type/indication, liver function and treatment limitations are required for causal treatment claims.

## Evidence limits and verification

EICU cannot establish true norepinephrine administration, dose, treatment intent, arterial versus venous specimen, assay indication, fluids, beta-agonists, hepatic clearance, source control, tissue perfusion, treatment limitations, exact death time/cause, or utility. Clinical adjudication and another prospective study are required for those claims.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/headers, deterministic t0 and person selection, landmark survival, lactate revision/conflict/tie rules, exposure counts, outcome-blind site gate, model inputs, and interpretation of synthetic supportive/adverse/opposite/inconclusive outputs. The verifier must reject a correct feasibility count paired with an unsupported clinical or causal conclusion. Reference execution establishes feasibility and computation, not scientific truth.
