# Normal-albuminuria stress test of threshold-nearest, N18-specific grip allocation

## Decision, evidence boundary, and unresolved hypothesis

This is a substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. The parent asks which 20% of adults with creatinine eGFR (eGFRcr) 60–89 should receive cystatin C and requires raw-grip policy G to beat equal-budget baseline, generic-function and lowest-eGFRcr policies B, F and T. Its remaining clinical-comparator threat is that the apparent gain may be confined to people who already have albuminuria, an established kidney-damage signal. In that setting the service already has a clinically important kidney marker, and the case that grip uncovers otherwise hidden filtration impairment is weaker.

The strongest evidence available before this experiment supports association and clinical relevance, not the new policy claim. Chen et al. (Kidney Medicine 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.xkme.2024.100796; PMCID PMC10986041; frozen full-text XML `[source checksum]` inspected in this campaign) showed in UK Biobank that grip and other characteristics predict creatinine–cystatin eGFR differences; it did not test a fixed-budget policy among people with definitely normal albuminuria. The ERBP commentary on KDIGO 2024 (DOI 10.1093/ndt/gfae209; PMCID PMC11792658; frozen full-text XML `[source checksum]` inspected) states that cause, GFR and albuminuria remain the cornerstone of CKD assessment. Zanetti et al. (Hypertension 2020, DOI 10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.119.14028; PMCID PMC8032218; frozen full-text XML `[source checksum]` inspected) analyzed spot urine albumin adjusted for creatinine in UK Biobank and supports the relevance and availability of the marker, not a cystatin allocation rule.

The unresolved falsifiable claim added by this child is:

> Among the parent population with baseline albumin-to-creatinine ratio known, without imputation, to be below 3 mg/mmol (definite A1), the already-frozen G policy will retain replicated equal-20%-budget superiority over B, F and T for combined-equation eGFR <60 yield and marker-discordance yield. A gain in the full parent population that disappears in definite A1 is not evidence that grip finds low-filtration reclassification beyond conventional albuminuria information.

This tests complementary biochemical allocation among people without demonstrable albuminuria. It does not establish measured GFR, persistent CKD, that creatinine is biased, that grip measures muscle mass, that albuminuria was prospectively available before an order, or that cystatin testing changes management or outcomes.
