## 1. Falsifiable hypothesis and clinical decision relevance

The parent’s strongest supported claim is limited: in linked eICU records, a pre-360 observation residual can be associated with a later strict recorded respiratoryCare interval-start after adjustment for measured physiology, state/process blocks, hospital monitoring regime, and continuation/observation selection. The parent does not establish treatment delivery, physiologic deterioration, indication, or mechanism. Pollard et al. [K1] support the concern that ICU monitoring generates abundant data while only a small portion of bedside streams is archived as documentation. Rajkomar et al. [K2] and Tang et al. [K3] support temporal, structured-EHR feature construction, but neither resolves provenance.

The unresolved rival is generic documentation/monitoring: a patient whose vital, laboratory, treatment, and routine chart activity becomes dense may be more likely to have any later recorded event, including respiratoryCare, without a respiratory-specific opportunity. The alternative explanation predicts the same pre-360 signal for a non-respiratory chart endpoint and predicts that a generic-process block absorbs the respiratory residual. A respiratory-specific opportunity explanation predicts a residual from respiratory sources that remains for the respiratoryCare endpoint after generic process is adjusted, while its association is materially weaker for the source-swapped lab endpoint.

Hypothesis (noncausal, falsifiable):

> Among the parent’s adult first-ICU S6 stays in respiratoryCare starting strata B/C, a pre-landmark respiratory-source residual has a larger held-out-hospital association with a strict documented respiratoryCare interval-start than with a first valid post-landmark laboratory-result event, after outcome-blind generic-process intensity, hospital regime, measured physiology/state, and continuation/observation selection are accounted for.

This matters because the next clinical validation decision differs. If respiratory specificity survives the generic negative control, a source-adjudication study should compare respiratory records with device/order/bedside timing to determine whether the signal reflects genuine respiratory opportunity. If generic process explains both endpoints, the observed signal should be treated as workflow/ascertainment and not used to motivate respiratory monitoring or treatment intervention. Neither result creates a bedside rule.

The exact primary differential estimand is the held-out-hospital, selection-standardized difference:
`Delta_spec = A(R_start_doc, S_resp | G) - A(L_start, S_resp | G)`,
where `A` is the 720-minute cumulative-incidence or discrete-time log-risk contrast comparing a training-fold-frozen 90th versus 10th percentile of the respiratory-specific dynamic residual `D_resp`, conditional on the same physiology/state, generic process `G`, hospital component, and competing exits. Report the two endpoint-specific contrasts separately; no causal interpretation is attached.

## 2. Outcome-blind generic-process features and negative control

All predictors are constructed without using endpoint labels, post-360 rows, `actualicumortality`, APACHE predictions, or any endpoint timing. Use the parent’s 12 bins of 30 minutes over inclusive [0,360], with no forward fill.

### Generic process block `G`

For each stay, source, and bin, compute only process/opportunity quantities:

- `vitalPeriodic`: finite-row count, occupied-bin flag, count of distinct `observationoffset` values, first/last finite offset, inter-observation gap summaries, duplicate-offset flag, and missingness mask. Do not use temperature, sao2, heartrate, respiration, or systemicmean in `G`; those remain in the inherited physiology block.
- `vitalAperiodic`: the analogous count/occupancy/offset/duplicate/missingness features for `noninvasivemean`; the value remains physiology, not `G`.
- `lab`: finite-result row count and occupied bins, first/last `labresultoffset`, inter-result gaps, duplicate-row flag, and revision-process counts/delays from `labresultrevisedoffset`. Do not use `labresult`, `labname`, or analyte values in `G`. Apply the inherited finite-result and revision-validity rule.
- `treatment`: row count, occupied bins, first/last `treatmentoffset`, inter-row gaps, duplicate/string-offset flags, and active-row count. Do not use `treatmentstring` content in `G`; the parent’s respiratory/non-respiratory treatment vocabulary and timing audit remain separate inherited process blocks.
- `carePlanEOL`: save/discussion row count, occupied bins, first/last save/discussion offset, and active-row flag. This is retained as a separately reported EOL/selection block, not interpreted as generic nursing documentation.
- Define `G_total` as source-standardized counts/occupancy/missingness and the source-specific `G_vital`, `G_lab`, `G_treatment`, and `G_EOL) summaries. Standardization and any thresholds are fit within training folds only.

For each generic source and bin, calculate the parent-style outcome-blind hospital reference `H_G(h,g,b)` from other adult first-ICU prefixes at hospital `h), with alpha=beta=1 binomial smoothing, at-risk denominators, retained reference counts, and leave-one-stay-out for index stays. Define `D_G=X_G-H_G`, plus last-three-bin mean, first-to-last change, and masks. In each held-out outcome hospital, `H_G` may use other unlabeled stays in that hospital; the strict training-hospital-only sensitivity is reported if identifiable. Define `H_resp,D_resp` analogously for respiratoryCharting and pre-360 respiratoryCare process features, keeping respiratory-specific source provenance visible. These are recorded observation quantities, not severity or treatment scores.

The primary nested blocks are:

- B0: inherited physiology/state and fixed baseline/stratum variables.
- B1: B0 + generic `G` and `H_G,D_G`.
- B2: B1 + respiratory-specific `H_resp,D_resp` and inherited respiratory treatment/process blocks.
- B3: B2 + the parent’s continuation/observation selection standardization `W6` and decomposition-specific `W6^G`/`W6^{G+resp}` sensitivities.
