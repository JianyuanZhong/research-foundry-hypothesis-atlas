# Episode 72 successor: is optional repeat-lactate review value transportable across testing processes?

## Unresolved question, strongest supported claim, and clinical importance

The leading EICU proposal makes a necessary correction: people without a repeat lactate remain in the primary risk set, and repeat-lactate availability, measurement age, and value are separated. Its unresolved clinical question is whether the apparent fixed-capacity mortality-review gain is transportable across hospitals whose lactate-testing processes differ, or whether a model learns local testing practice and fails where repeat testing is less common.

This matters because a hospital cannot safely adopt a review queue from a pooled performance estimate if the gain is confined to hospitals that order or release repeat lactates frequently. A positive result that survives both high- and low-availability settings would justify prospective external validation of an optional-result queue. A process-concentrated result would instead call for workflow redesign and prospective measurement of ordering and display before clinical use.

The strongest evidence available before this experiment is narrower. Baysan et al. (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750, PMCID PMC9444407; acquired full-text XML source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) reported a modest increase in recalibrated C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64 after adding 24-hour lactate to APACHE IV in a MIMIC sepsis validation cohort. That supports possible prognostic information from late lactate, not a fixed-workload queue benefit, transportability across multiple hospitals, a recommendation to order a repeat test, or patient benefit.

The unresolved claim tested here is therefore:

> In adults who reach a fixed 24-hour landmark after an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed entry and have a valid baseline lactate, adding an optionally observed repeat-lactate process and value to a calibrated mortality-review model captures at least 3 additional percentage points of eventual hospital deaths in a within-hospital top-10% queue, and this increment is not confined to hospitals above the outcome-blind median repeat-lactate availability.

The 3-point margin is a prespecified decision-relevance anchor for the modeled queue, not a validated clinical-utility threshold. This is a prognostic and transportability experiment, not a treatment-effect study.

## Estimands and transportability contrast

For each eligible hospital h, let n_h be the number in the complete baseline/L24 population, k_h=ceil(0.10 n_h), and Q_full,h and Q_base,h be the k_h highest calibrated predicted risks under the full and baseline models, respectively. Ties are resolved by ascending patientunitstayid. Let D=1 when patient.hospitaldischargestatus is exactly `Expired`, and D=0 when it is `Alive`.

The primary estimand is the pooled within-hospital death-capture difference:

`Delta10_full = [sum_h sum_{i in Q_full,h} D_i - sum_h sum_{i in Q_base,h} D_i] / sum_h sum_i D_i`.

A secondary fixed 20% queue uses ceil(0.20 n_h) and cannot rescue the primary result.

Before seeing outcomes, calculate each hospital's repeat-availability proportion as the number with a valid F divided by the complete baseline/L24 population in that hospital. Freeze the high-availability stratum as hospitals at or above the population median and the low-availability stratum as hospitals below it. This uses only the availability frame, never mortality. Report stratum-specific Delta10_full and a hospital-level paired contrast for every hospital.

The transportability requirement is prespecified: the full-model increment must be nonnegative in both availability strata (neither stratum's 95% copied-hospital interval may have an upper bound below zero), and at least 70% of finite leave-one-hospital-out deletions in each stratum must be positive. These are stability requirements, not proof of causal transportability. The primary support label additionally requires the pooled 95% lower bound to be at least +3 points, both models to pass held-out calibration, at least 80% of all finite hospital deletions to be positive, and a non-opposite 20% result.

Decompose the primary contrast:

- R_base contains no repeat-lactate availability, age, or value.
- R_process adds F_available, follow-up measurement age (L24-F_offset)/720 when F exists, and a missing-age indicator.
- R_full adds ln(F) and ln(F)^2, with an availability indicator and training-only imputation if F is absent.
- R_full versus R_process is the measured-overlap numeric-value contrast, reported only as a decomposition.
- R_process versus R_base is the observed process-information contrast.

If the pooled full increment is supportive but the process increment is at least as large, or the numeric-value contrast is nonpositive, classify the result as process-dependent. Such a result supports only an association with the observed testing process, not a repeat-testing policy.

## Hospital-held-out fitting, calibration and uncertainty

Use hospital leave-one-out prediction on the fixed primary hospital set. For every outer held-out hospital, fit each R model on all other hospitals using unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1, `lbfgs`, max_iter=10000, tol=1e-8, seed 20260972. Winsorize continuous predictors at training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize using training means and population SDs, and mean-impute missing continuous predictors after scaling to zero. Do not scale indicators; no feature may be dropped for a fold.

Within each outer training set, generate inner hospital-out-of-fold predictions and fit a two-parameter Platt recalibrator separately for each model using only those predictions: logit(p_cal)=a+b logit(p_raw). Clip finite probabilities to [1e-15,1-1e-15] before logits. A failed or separated fit is Not computable; no held-out outcome may affect preprocessing, calibration, quotas, availability strata or features. Apply the calibrator only to the outer held-out hospital.

Require each model's pooled held-out calibration-in-the-large absolute risk difference <=0.05, calibration slope 0.80-1.20, and at least 80% of hospitals with finite predictions having mean predicted risk within 0.10 of observed mortality. These are probability-interpretation guardrails; queue capture is primary. Report threshold DCA at fixed thresholds 0.30 through 0.50 only as secondary context.

Use 2,000 copied-hospital refit bootstrap replicates, seed 20260972. ## Falsification and interpretation

Apply computability gates first.

- **Not computable:** any source/header/hash mismatch; altered flow; missing required columns or keys; incorrect revision/conflict/tie resolution; time leakage; failed hospital gates; incomplete held-out predictions; failed calibration; nonfinite fits; or fewer than 2,000 valid refits. Do not substitute random splits, pooled calibration, raw probabilities, the measured-overlap population, altered windows or post hoc strata.
- **Supportive and transportable:** all gates pass; both models pass calibration; pooled Delta10 lower bound >=+3 points; >=80% of all finite deletions positive; both availability strata are not wholly negative and each has >=70% positive finite deletions; and the 20% contrast is not opposite.
- **Supportive but process-dependent:** pooled support passes, but process increment >= full increment or numeric-value contrast is nonpositive. This does not support ordering or a value-only policy.
- **Calibration/operational failure:** pooled interval meets +3 but either model fails calibration, site stability or fixed-quota requirements.
- **Opposite:** pooled Delta10 upper bound <0, or both availability strata are wholly negative. This opposes optional repeat-lactate information for this comparator and quota.
- **Adverse to materiality:** pooled interval is nonnegative but its upper bound is <+3, with transportability and calibration otherwise adequate.
- **Inconclusive:** all other computable outcomes, including an interval spanning +3, unstable stratum direction, discordant 20% behavior, or process/value disagreement without a clean process-dependent branch.

A supportive result establishes only robust modeled re-ranking under a fixed workload and the recorded EICU processes. It does not establish that a repeat lactate should be ordered, that results were visible at the bedside, that lactate measures occult hypoperfusion, that review changes treatment, or that mortality, safety, workload, cost or equity improves. An adverse or opposite result rejects only the locked margin or direction in this snapshot. Inconclusive results require more data rather than favorable-point interpretation.

## Evidence limits and required follow-up

The configured data contain structured values, ICU offsets, discharge status and hospital clustering, but no adjudicated pressor administration/indication, specimen identity, assay quality, result display timestamp, clinician review action, utility weights, treatment assignment, counterfactual, cause or time of death. Public EICU narrative sections are removed; structured note fields are not assumed to be genuine narrative and no text is used. VitalPeriodic is not a waveform.

The availability strata test robustness to observed hospital testing frequency, not transportability to an unseen health system. A genuinely independent cohort, synchronized ordering/result/display timestamps, clinical adjudication of assay and visibility semantics, and stakeholder-derived false-positive/false-negative costs are required before use. Any policy changing repeat-lactate ordering or treatment requires a prospective impact study, ideally randomized for causal patient benefit.
