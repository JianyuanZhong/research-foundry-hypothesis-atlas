# Is post-ICU harm from nighttime discharge concentrated in patients with residual instability?

## Status, scientific opening, and deliverable

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` and a planned eICU experiment, not an executed clinical result. It repairs the seed by defining a bounded endpoint, exact data contracts, comparable methods, rival explanations, and an explicit clock-validity gate.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is that out-of-hours ICU discharge is associated with higher in-hospital mortality and ICU readmission across heterogeneous observational cohorts, but the literature cannot separate patient differences from differences in care [K1]. Mixed-methods review of selected U.K. cases identifies plausible, co-occurring pathways—premature discharge with physiological instability, deficient handover, reduced nighttime staffing/skill mix, and failed escalation—but does not quantify their relative contributions or transport them to U.S. eICU hospitals [K2]. Objective discharge physiology is clinically relevant, yet a simple established score transported poorly (SWIFT AUROC 0.581), showing why “instability” should not be equated with a single inherited score [K3].

**Unresolved hypothesis.** Among adult first ICU stays ending alive in lower-acuity care at the same hospital, the excess 7-day cumulative incidence of post-ICU in-hospital death for nighttime versus daytime discharge is larger at objectively high residual instability than at low instability. Formally, the prespecified primary contrast is the standardized additive interaction
[
I=[R_{N,high}(7)-R_{D,high}(7)]-[R_{N,low}(7)-R_{D,low}(7)].
]
The claim is supported only if (i) the simultaneous 95% interval for (I) excludes 0 positively for both a transparent baseline and admissible temporal model, (ii) the result survives the clock, destination, documentation, severity/EOL, and hospital-practice gates below, and (iii) the excess is clinically nontrivial: lower 95% confidence bound above **1 absolute death per 100 discharges**. The threshold is a decision-relevance criterion, not a claimed minimal important difference.

**Actual scientific deliverable.** A future solver must newly construct a preregistered cohort and frozen hospital/person splits; estimate baseline and temporal latent instability using only pre-discharge data; fit competing-risk outcome models on identical splits; output four standardized 7-day risks, (I), hospital heterogeneity, calibration/transport metrics, clock-mapping bounds, rival analyses, and uncertainty. Completion means these artifacts exist and are traceable to source hashes; it does not mean the hypothesis is confirmed.

If real local time cannot be established and the multi-mapping analysis is not invariant, the correct deliverable is a clock-invalid/inconclusive result—not a nighttime claim.

## Why this could change understanding or a clinical decision

The actionable decision is not merely “avoid all night discharges.” If harm is concentrated in objectively unstable patients, hospitals could prioritize postponement, step-down placement, or enhanced handover/monitoring for that subgroup. If the same night excess occurs among stable patients, ward support or handover is a stronger target. If the association vanishes within hospitals after destination, admission severity, EOL status, and measurement practice are controlled, timing is likely a marker rather than an actionable patient-state interaction. These competing explanations predict observably different patterns:

- **Residual-instability vulnerability:** positive (I), monotone night excess across instability quintiles, temporal trajectory information improves held-out instability/outcome calibration, and the effect is not abolished by documentation balancing.
- **Reduced ward support/handover:** night excess persists even at low instability, may be larger for Floor than Telemetry/SDU, and cannot be attributed to measured physiology.
- **Severity or treatment-limitation selection:** adjustment/exclusion for admission APACHE, active EOL records, active therapies, and short time-to-death materially removes the contrast.
- **Destination selection:** association is concentrated in Floor versus monitored destinations or changes after standardizing destination.
- **Documentation artifact:** apparent instability or interaction attenuates after conditioning on observation density/source availability, inverse-observation weighting, and matched complete-case analysis.
- **Hospital practice:** within-hospital interaction is weak while hospital night-discharge propensity explains between-hospital differences.
- **Clock artifact:** sign or threshold crossing changes across compatible raw/UTC-to-local mappings.

This advances the closest work by testing a prespecified effect-modification claim across many hospitals, separating physiology from observation process, and comparing a transparent state summary with a temporal representation. It remains associational; no measured staffing, bed occupancy, transfer delay, discharge-readiness decision, or ward response exists.

## Population, index, time, and outcomes

### Population and index

Use eICU 2.0. One index row per `uniquepid`, selected before outcomes:

1. age >=18 (map public `"> 89"` to 90 only for eligibility/modeling);
2. `unitvisitnumber == 1` to target the first recorded ICU visit of the hospitalization and avoid selecting on a prior ICU return;
3. `unitdischargestatus == "Alive"`;
4. `unitdischargelocation` in `{"Floor","Telemetry","Step-Down Unit (SDU)","Acute Care/Floor"}`;
5. nonmissing/coherent unit and hospital discharge offsets; `hospitaldischargeoffset >= unitdischargeoffset`;
6. ICU duration >=24 h, so every patient has the same potential 24-hour feature window;
7. if a `uniquepid` still has multiple eligible health-system stays, retain the earliest deterministically by `patienthealthsystemstayid`, then `patientunitstayid`; report exclusions.

The index is the ICU discharge at `unitdischargeoffset`. Inputs are strictly earlier than this offset. All event/entry clocks must be <= index; events entered after discharge are excluded even if their event time is earlier, preventing post-index documentation leakage.

A bounded audit before feature extraction found 150,386 broad eligible rows across 208 hospitals, 6,072 subsequent deaths, and 4,550 deaths within 7 days. With `unitvisitnumber==1`, there were 118,105 rows, 4,631 deaths, and 3,538 <=7-day deaths before the >=24-hour and one-person restrictions. These are feasibility counts, not hypothesis results. Among 116,008 first-visit adult lower-acuity discharges with >=24 hours in ICU (before final one-person and outcome restrictions), last-24-hour coverage was 97.9% for vitalPeriodic, 96.7% for vitalAperiodic, 96.0% for lab, and 96.2% for nurseCharting; this measured scan took 342 seconds on 8 CPUs/32 GiB. Coverage of individual instability domains still requires execution-time reporting.

### Exposure and the clock gate

Candidate night is local 21:00-06:59; day is 07:00-20:59, matching a common prior definition while avoiding post-hoc cutpoint selection. `unitdischargetime24` is internally coherent: in all 200,859 patient rows it equals `unitadmittime24 + unitdischargeoffset` modulo 1,440 minutes. But public evidence available here does not establish whether Time24 is hospital-local or UTC; exact timezone and date/DST are absent.

Therefore:

1. seek an authoritative eICU data-owner/dictionary statement before execution;
2. regardless, enumerate a finite exposure set: raw Time24 plus every plausible U.S. UTC offset for the hospital's `region` (Northeast: -5/-4 h; Midwest: -6/-5/-4 h; South: -6/-5/-4 h; West: -8/-7/-6 h; missing region: all);
3. primary robust comparison uses **certain night** (night under every admissible mapping including raw) versus **certain day** (day under every mapping); ambiguous rows enter mapping-specific sensitivity analyses;
4. report identified bounds across mappings. Any reversal of \(I\), confidence-bound crossing of zero/1%, or fewer than 20 deaths in any night/day × high/low cell across fewer than 10 informative hospitals makes the nighttime hypothesis inconclusive.

A pre-restriction conservative audit including raw and regional mappings yielded 7,532 certain-night rows (323 7-day deaths), so the robust contrast is feasible overall but cell-level precision remains unresolved until instability and >=24-hour restrictions are applied. Without validated local time, the study may establish non-identifiability rather than a timing effect.

### Outcome and estimand

Primary outcome is **post-ICU in-hospital death within 7 days**, using `hospitaldischargestatus=="Expired"` and elapsed time `hospitaldischargeoffset-unitdischargeoffset`. Alive hospital discharge before day 7 is a competing event; retained hospital stay through day 7 is administrative survival. Use a discrete-time competing-risk model (daily intervals 0-1,...,6-7) and standardized cumulative incidence at day 7. Do not censor live discharge as noninformative.

Secondary outcomes:

- death before hospital discharge at any observed time (descriptive competing-risk analysis);
- death within 48 hours (severity/EOL stress test);
- remaining hospital length of stay among survivors, as descriptive only.

eICU does not reliably identify ward-to-ICU readmission after the index because `unitvisitnumber` and multiple unit rows do not distinguish all planned/internal critical-care transfers and the source snapshot lacks clinical adjudication. Readmission is not an endpoint. Mortality after hospital discharge is unavailable.

## Instability construct and comparable methods

### Shared input and target contract

Both methods receive identical 24 hourly bins \(t=-24,...,-1\) relative to ICU discharge and identical static admission/destination variables. Physiological channels are robustly winsorized and standardized using training hospitals only. For each channel/hour include median, min/max where relevant, last value, time since last observation, count, and source indicator. Include absolute state and trajectory: HR, respiratory rate, temperature, SpO2, MAP, oxygen support/FiO2, GCS/RASS, glucose, pH/PaCO2, lactate, creatinine/BUN, bilirubin, sodium/potassium, WBC, hemoglobin, platelets, vasopressor/inotrope observation, ventilation, dialysis, and active treatment. Missingness is retained explicitly, never silently normal-imputed.

Freeze the **primary objective residual-instability phenotype before outcome modeling**, using only the final 6 hours and six clinically interpretable domains:

1. circulation: MAP <65 mmHg, repeated HR >110/min, or observed vasoactive infusion;
2. respiration: repeated RR >24/min, SpO2 <90%, FiO2 >=0.40/O2 flow >=6 L/min, or invasive/noninvasive ventilatory support;
3. neurologic: last GCS <14 or RASS outside -1 to +1;
4. renal/metabolic: lactate >2 mmol/L, pH <7.32, or creatinine rise >=0.3 mg/dL over the prior 24 hours;
5. temperature/inflammation: temperature <36 or >38.3 C, or WBC <4 or >12 x10^9/L;
6. ongoing organ support: active dialysis, ventilation, or a treatment/infusion explicitly active at discharge.

A domain is abnormal if any listed criterion is met after prespecified artifact/unit checks. It is assessable if at least one required value/support source is present. **High instability** is >=2 abnormal domains; **low instability** is zero abnormal domains with >=4 assessable domains. One abnormal domain or <4 assessable domains is indeterminate and excluded from the primary high/low interaction but retained in continuous-domain-count and missingness analyses. Thresholds are an operational, outcome-independent phenotype—not validated discharge criteria—and require ICU-clinician sign-off before execution. Report every domain's prevalence, coverage and site variation; no outcome-guided threshold changes are allowed.

For the temporal alternative only, fit an **outcome-blind representation** in training hospitals by masking the final 6 hours and predicting their observed physiological states/support plus whether each channel is observed; no death, discharge-time exposure, destination, or fixed high/low label enters pretraining. Freeze the encoder, then fit the outcome model. Both B0 and T1 estimate the same fixed high-versus-low interaction above. A learned continuous vulnerability score and night interaction may be reported as exploratory only, with its definition frozen on validation data.

### B0: interpretable hierarchical discrete-time baseline

Use prespecified summaries over last 6 and 24 hours: last value, worst value, linear slope, proportion beyond clinically reviewed ranges, organ-support flags, and observation counts/time-since-last. Fit a hierarchical discrete-time multinomial logistic model for death versus alive discharge versus remaining hospitalized, with hospital random intercepts and random night slopes. Nonlinear continuous effects use restricted cubic splines; estimate night × fixed high-instability and night × abnormal-domain-count interactions. Static adjustment includes age, sex, admission diagnosis/source, unittype, unit LOS, APACHE admission severity, hospital bed/teaching/region, destination, active EOL, and prior-hospital time. This baseline exposes which state/trajectory/measurement summaries drive estimates.

### T1: substantive temporal alternative

Use a small GRU-D (or time-aware GRU) over the same hourly tensor, with value, mask, and time-since-observed inputs, 1-2 layers and <=128 hidden units. Outcome-blind masked-final-6-hour training forces it to learn multivariate persistence, recovery, oscillation, and asynchronous deterioration that B0 compresses into independent extrema/slopes. Freeze the encoder and fit the same hierarchical discrete-time competing-risk head, adjustment set, and fixed high/low interaction as B0. Fit an observation-only GRU comparator and value-permuted-within-mask negative control.

T1 is scientifically useful only if it improves the masked-final-6-hour target over last-value/persistence and linear-extrapolation baselines, improves hospital-held-out calibration/Brier score for the final outcome over B0, preserves the fixed interaction across hospitals, and is not reproduced by observation-only inputs. A small AUROC gain alone is irrelevant. Attention/gradient attribution is descriptive, not mechanistic explanation.

### Split and fitting

Construct one immutable split manifest before feature fitting:

- whole hospitals assigned 60% development, 20% validation, 20% final test, stratified by region, size, event count, and night support;
- all rows for a `uniquepid` remain together; because hospitals are held out, cross-hospital people are assigned wholly to the earliest hospital's partition or excluded from later partitions and reported;
- hyperparameters, feature-label mapping, artifact/unit rules, imputation/scaling, and probability calibration use development/validation only; the clinical instability thresholds above remain fixed;
- test is opened once after the analysis lock. Bootstrap resamples hospitals (>=1,000 replicates) and repeats person grouping; use simultaneous intervals for the primary baseline/temporal interactions and clock mappings.

For held-out hospitals, prediction uses the population random-effect distribution with no test-hospital outcome fitting; report both marginal estimates and conditional estimates only for development hospitals. Hospitals lacking both exposure levels or <5 deaths contribute descriptive estimates and partial pooling, not standalone interaction claims. Report effective hospital/event counts.

## Analysis and rival-discrimination gates

1. **Clock:** raw and every region-compatible mapping, certain-night/day restriction, and 16:00-07:59 / 22:00-05:59 sensitivity. Clock dependence => inconclusive.
2. **Severity/EOL:** sequential models add APACHE, active EOL, active therapies, and exclude active EOL or death <=48 h. Large attenuation is evidence for selection, not mediation.
3. **Destination:** report Floor, Telemetry, SDU/Acute Care separately; standardize to common destination; night×instability×destination is exploratory with multiplicity control.
4. **Documentation:** report channel/site coverage, mask-only prediction, chart-entry delay, complete-case matched analysis, inverse probability of observation, and exclusion of low-interface hospitals. If mask-only models reproduce (I) or value-shuffling leaves it unchanged, physiological interpretation fails.
5. **Hospital practice:** within-hospital contrasts and random slopes; estimate hospital night-discharge propensity from clock only without outcomes and test whether between-hospital propensity accounts for the aggregate signal. Do not call propensity an instrument.
6. **Continuity/placebo:** plot adjusted risk by each clock hour; test false cutpoints shifted by 6 and 12 hours and a negative-control interaction using admission-time rather than discharge-time clock. Abrupt effects only at a chosen coding boundary or comparable placebo interactions falsify a specific nighttime interpretation.
7. **Positivity:** hospital- and instability-specific exposure probabilities; truncate weights at 1st/99th percentiles if weighting is used, report standardized differences, and refuse inference outside common support.

Main estimand is descriptive standardized association, not a causal effect. Avoid conditioning on variables caused by discharge timing; all covariates are pre-index or fixed destination.

## Results and interpretation contract

**Supportive:** both B0 and admissible T1 show positive, >=1%-point robust (I) with simultaneous lower 95% bound >0 across clock mappings and gates; the continuous curve is monotone; mask-only/placebo tests fail to reproduce it; and hospital-held-out estimates are directionally consistent. This supports the bounded claim that measured residual instability identifies a subgroup in which nighttime discharge is associated with greater 7-day in-hospital mortality. It does not prove delaying discharge saves lives or whether staffing, handover, bed pressure, or residual illness causes harm.

**Adverse:** (I<=0), the excess is similar/larger at low instability, it disappears after defensible severity/destination/clock handling, it is reproduced by observation density, or effects exist only between hospitals. This refutes the proposed concentration claim in this data and redirects action toward general nighttime support, selection/measurement validation, or hospital practice rather than an instability-specific policy.

**Inconclusive:** wide intervals include clinically important benefit and harm; insufficient certain-night events; positivity failure; clock mappings disagree; B0/T1 materially conflict without a prespecified diagnostic resolution; physiological coverage is too sparse/site-dependent; or EOL/destination classifications cannot support the contrast. An imprecise null is not refutation.

A robust null with 95% CI excluding |1%-point| for (I), adequate support, and passed measurement/clock gates would falsify the clinically consequential interaction at that scale.

## Computable versus adjudication-limited claims

An automatic verifier can check cohort logic, leakage boundaries, hashes, split isolation, table/column presence, event counts, competing-risk calculations, model outputs, bootstrap intervals, calibration, clock-mapping invariance, and whether reported conclusions obey the interpretation contract. It must test supportive, adverse, and inconclusive synthetic/result fixtures and reject unsupported causal language even when computation is correct.

It cannot establish that a transfer was premature, that a patient was “ready,” that death was avoidable, that the displayed clock is local absent authoritative metadata, that ward staffing/handover/bed pressure caused harm, or that a policy would improve outcomes. Those claims require data-owner clarification, ICU/ward clinician review, local timezone/DST, staffing and occupancy, transfer-request/actual-transfer timestamps, ward observations and escalation, adjudicated treatment limitations, validated ICU readmission, and ideally prospective/quasi-experimental evaluation.

## Method/resources comparison and selection record

- **B0:** exact inputs above; target is the three-state daily hazard and standardized interaction. Estimated 16 CPU, 64-96 GiB, 2-4 h including bootstrap after feature extraction. Chosen because coefficients/splines and hospital slopes directly test the hypothesis.
- **T1:** same rows/splits/channels, outcome-blind masked-6-hour target then frozen competing-risk head. Estimated 1 A100 80 GiB, 8 CPU, 64 GiB, 2-6 h for bounded tuning plus CPU/hospital bootstrap; estimate is unmeasured but within the 28,800-s solver envelope. GPU is a speed tradeoff, not scientific merit. Chosen because multivariate ordering/persistence may reveal residual instability erased by summaries.
- **Deferred:** gradient-boosted trees can improve nonlinear prediction but add little specific temporal information; revisit only if B0 underfits nonlinearities and T1 fails optimization. A transformer is deferred because 24 bins and this channel count do not justify extra complexity; revisit if longer-history sensitivity reveals useful long-range structure. Joint causal mediation is rejected because staffing, bed pressure, handover, discharge decision, and ward response are missing.

No GPU pilot was needed to choose the method; hardware availability is verified independently. Future execution should use the cached GPU image and explicitly request one allocated GPU; ordinary shell CUDA visibility is not evidence.
