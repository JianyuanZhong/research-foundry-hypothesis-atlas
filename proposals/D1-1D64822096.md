# Hospital transportability of measurement-process signals in early ICU risk prediction

## Deliverable and unresolved clinical question

The deliverable is a newly fitted, hospital-held-out comparison of two early ICU mortality models, with site-level discrimination, calibration, decision-curve net benefit, process-feature ablation, case-mix standardization, prevalence recalibration, and uncertainty. No solver result is claimed in this discovery episode.

The unresolved question is whether laboratory measurement frequency, inter-measurement timing, and missingness are transportable indicators of patient state or mainly encode a hospital's observation process. This matters because a model that looks accurate where it was developed can become miscalibrated or unsafe in an ICU that orders tests differently; the consequential choice is whether to remove process features, recalibrate locally, or require site-specific validation.

The strongest inspected evidence supports only that informative missingness can be predictive but may fail when the missing-data mechanism changes [K1]; that external and prospective evaluation is needed before an EHR mortality model is treated as implementable [K2]; and that dated longitudinal representations can add information beyond an age/sex baseline under strict external testing [K3]. None establishes that eICU process features cause transport degradation.

Hypothesis: among adult eICU stays with an auditable first-six-hour window, a process-aware model will lose more discrimination, calibration, and decision-curve net benefit on untouched hospitals than an aligned physiology-only model, after prespecified case-mix standardization and target-hospital prevalence recalibration. This is a predictive-transportability hypothesis, not a causal claim that hospitals or testing practices cause death.

The advance over existing evidence is a paired hospital-held-out decomposition of transport loss into physiology, observation process, case mix, outcome prevalence, and calibration. A null result would weaken the claim that process features are the main explanation for transport failure.

## Models and analysis

Construct one patient-level feature table, with all preprocessing and imputation fitted only in training hospitals and identical patient-level hospital partitions for both models.

Baseline: penalized logistic regression using prespecified first/last/median/minimum/maximum and simple slope summaries for the named vitals and selected labs, plus age, gender, admission source, and timing-safe admission severity. It excludes measurement counts, intervals, and missingness indicators.

Substantive alternative: a modest time-aware/process-aware model using the same physiology values plus per-lab measurement count, first-to-last interval, time since last measurement, and missingness indicators in the fixed window. A hierarchical discrete-time logistic model with splines and hospital effects is the CPU fallback; a small temporal model is permitted only if readiness supports it. The alternative can reveal whether irregular trajectories and the observation process explain shifts that aggregate summaries erase. Complexity is not itself evidence.

Primary estimand is the paired process-aware-minus-physiology-only performance difference on untouched target hospitals, summarized across targets with hospital-cluster bootstrap intervals and a target-hospital prediction interval. Report AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, and decision-curve net benefit over a clinically reviewed risk range.

Prespecified decompositions are: within-hospital random versus hospital-held-out splits; process-feature ablation from the same fitted alternative; target intercept recalibration using a declared calibration subset, or an explicit unavailable result; standardization/reweighting to the training case mix using age, gender, admission source, timing-safe severity, and hospital descriptors; and site-specific process-feature distributions. These are designed to separate workflow dependence from case mix and prevalence without treating hospital preference as an instrument.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence is preferential loss of process-aware discrimination, calibration, and net benefit on held-out hospitals, attenuation after process ablation or target recalibration, and broadly consistent direction across target sites. This supports workflow dependence as a transportability explanation only.

Adverse evidence is equal or smaller process-aware degradation, a difference present equally in within-hospital splits, loss fully explained by case mix/prevalence, no attenuation after process ablation/recalibration, or a result driven by unstable lab-name mapping or endpoint coding. This rejects or bounds the proposed explanation but does not prove universal transportability.

Inconclusive evidence is inadequate events/sites, irreconcilable lab identity or timestamps, wide site-level intervals, or strongly heterogeneous effects without a prespecified interpretation. Computationally checkable outputs are cohort counts, window integrity, split membership, predictions, metrics, calibration, decision curves, ablations, recalibration, standardization, and uncertainty. Clinical adjudication is still required for any non-mortality deterioration label, clinically chosen alert threshold, or claim that changing testing improves care. This retrospective study cannot establish a biological mechanism, causal treatment effect, or deployment safety.

## Alternatives, compute, and deferred directions

The transparent logistic baseline is CPU-feasible. The process-aware temporal/hierarchical alternative is scientifically substantive because it retains irregular timing and observation-process information; its CPU fallback is predeclared. The solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs, and 28800 seconds. These are planning estimates, not measured full-study runtimes. No GPU is required by the question; use one allocated GPU only if readiness demonstrates that the chosen temporal fit needs it, with `cuda:0` inside the allocation.

The natural-history demonstration motivated the temporal alternative, but its UKB/Danish modalities are unavailable. The Bayesian demonstration's genetic inputs are unavailable, so no reproduction is claimed. The cancer demonstration's main article and complete STAR Methods remain unavailable; its accessible supplement does not justify reproducing the multimodal study. The true parent is eICU-02. eICU-03 hypotension is deferred because it asks a different physiologic/AKI question and would need careful kidney-outcome harmonization; eICU-08 reduced testing overlaps this branch but is more confounded by discharge, interface interruption, and end-of-life limitation. Vasopressor, hyperoxia, ventilatory-burden, nighttime-discharge, transfer, hypoglycemia, and recovery-speed seeds remain deferred pending timing, treatment-confounding, or adjudication checks.
