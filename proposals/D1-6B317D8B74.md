## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance
After circulatory stabilisation in sepsis/septic shock, some patients recover kidney and lung
function together, others recover one organ bed while the other stays impaired ("cross-organ
recovery asynchrony"). The parent (ep-11) replaced a 7-day composite outcome with an
organ-MATCHED vs CROSS-MATCHED contrast: does the slower-recovering limb predict failure of ITS OWN
organ (asymmetry > 0), or does it merely reflect ONE shared severity/observation trajectory sampled
unevenly (asymmetry ~ 0)? The parent admitted two gaps: (a) renal predictor and renal outcome still
shared the RRT/creatinine measurement family, and (b) **cohort power/prevalence was never
established in discovery**. This episode keeps the parent's science and closes (b) with a bounded
feasibility probe, and sharpens the estimator so the contrast is compilable.

Clinical importance: if asynchrony carries organ-specific information, serial organ-recovery
trajectory becomes a decision variable (target the lagging bed) rather than a severity restatement;
if not, it is another expression of one severity axis and adds nothing to standard SOFA monitoring.
A well-powered adverse result is itself consequential — it bounds the value of cross-organ
recovery monitoring.

## 2. Leading claim vs strongest rival
- H1: asynchrony carries organ-specific information — matched-limb predicts its OWN organ's 7-day
  failure more than the cross-limb (matched-minus-cross asymmetry > 0).
- H0 (strongest rival): a single shared severity/observation trajectory sampled unevenly predicts
  both beds about equally (asymmetry ~ 0); any residual renal signal is creatinine/RRT
  measurement coupling. Distinguished by the sign/magnitude of the asymmetry after holding a
  shared inflammatory axis (M2*, K1) and observation intensity (M3).

Interpretation: the sepsis cohort is large enough for a two-limb contrast, BUT the orthogonalised

## 6. Analysis and estimand
- Primary estimand: matched-minus-cross difference in holdout AUROC (and in standardized logistic
  coefficient) for own-organ 7-day failure, adjusting M0 + M2* + M3; 1000-sample bootstrap 95% CI.
- Method ladder on the SAME question:
  - B0 (baseline): interpretable logistic regression, prespecified covariates.
  - A1 (alternative): joint latent-variable / dynamic-factor model that explicitly separates an
    organ-specific residual factor from a shared severity factor; only A1 can attribute the
    asymmetry to organ-specific vs shared variance. B0 shows whether a simple additive model loses
    that decomposition. CPU-only, tabular extraction + logistic/EM factor fit, < 1 h; no GPU needed.
- Negative control: permuted-limb labels must yield asymmetry CI covering 0.

## 7. Falsification criteria (prespecified)
- Supportive (H1): matched-minus-cross asymmetry >= 0.02 AUROC, 95% bootstrap CI excluding 0 on the
  locked holdout, for >= 1 organ bed, reproduced under the orthogonalised renal outcome.
- Adverse (H0): asymmetry <= 0, or CI within +/-0.01 equivalence margin of 0 for both beds.
- Inconclusive: wide CI, effect driven by M3, or a non-null permutation control.

## 8. What each outcome means / limits
Supportive → organ-specific recovery information beyond shared severity (not causal, not mechanistic).
Adverse → asynchrony reducible to one shared trajectory at this resolution (bounds monitoring value).
Inconclusive → underpower/measurement limit; requires the composite outcome or a larger multicentre
sample. Renal creatinine limb remains muscle-mass biased; cystatin C is UNAVAILABLE in MIMIC-IV, so
no marker-independent renal truth standard exists (K3). Single-centre retrospective; no causal claim.
These are computationally checkable for the estimand; the mechanistic/organ-specific interpretation
requires clinical adjudication and independent validation.
