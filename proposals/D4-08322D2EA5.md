# Rising HbA1c with weight loss as a prodromal pancreatic cancer signal: mechanism-discriminating extension with site-resolved outcomes and a paired inflammatory-trajectory moderator

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (episode 1), itself evolved from expert seed UKB-40.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The parent's own falsification criteria anticipate an inconclusive primary result: 35 post-index pancreatic cancers and a 0/208 event cell for the pre-specified rise+loss contrast. The consequential unresolved uncertainty is therefore not the point estimate but **mechanism separation**: does the glycemic-rise-with-weight-loss trajectory behave like occult-tumor catabolism (large, short-latency, cancer-site-specific, inflammation-linked) or like ordinary obesity/metabolic progression (graded, baseline-adiposity-driven, non-specific, not linked to concurrent inflammation)? [K1] established recent-onset diabetes + weight loss as a risk marker in US cohorts; [K3] quantified the baseline obesity-metabolic pathway in this same biobank; [K2] shows dynamic trajectories remain outside EHR-coded risk models. None separates the two mechanisms. Resolution matters: a site-specific, inflammation-linked trajectory signal supports enrichment of early-detection trials around a prodromal window; a non-specific, baseline-adiposity-graded pattern does not.

## Hypothesis (falsifiable)

Among adults whose HbA1c rises between two assessments (>= +5 mmol/mol), concurrent >=3% weight loss predicts pancreatic cancer in the 0-3 y window (parent's primary claim), AND the weight-loss excess is (a) concentrated in pancreatic and other digestive-tract cancers rather than non-digestive cancers (lung/breast/prostate), and (b) stronger among participants whose CRP also rose between assessments. The obesity/metabolic rival predicts no site gradient beyond digestive sites, no CRP moderation, and risk that tracks baseline BMI.

## Design (Harbor experiment, CPU-only)

- **Population / index / follow-up / exclusion**: as in parent (paired assessments, exclude pre-index pancreatic cancer and insulin/sulfonylurea users; metformin-only sensitivity). Index = visit-1 date; follow-up to first outcome cancer of the group under analysis, death, or administrative end.
- **Exposure**: dHbA1c and dWeight% between instances, categorical (>= +5 mmol/mol; <= -3%) and continuous splines, as in parent; **added moderator**: dCRP (>= +1 mg/L pre-specified; continuous spline sensitivity).
- **Outcomes (pre-specified hierarchy)**:
  1. Primary: incident pancreatic cancer (C25), 0-3 y vs >3 y windows (parent's estimand, unchanged).
  2. **Site-resolved specificity panel (new)**: cause-specific analyses for other digestive cancers (C15-C17, C18-C22, C26; 182 events) and non-digestive comparators — lung (63), breast (180), prostate (270) — same exposure contrast and windows. Digestive vs non-digestive contrast has ~1,595 non-pancreatic events, estimable where the pancreatic contrast alone is not.
  3. **Mechanism moderator (new)**: primary contrast re-estimated stratified by / interacted with concurrent CRP rise among the 10,956 with paired CRP (25 of 35 pancreatic events retain CRP data). Pre-specified as mechanism-level secondary evidence, precision-bounded.
- **Primary analysis**: Firth-penalized cause-specific Cox (Fine-Gray sensitivity for competing risks) adjusted as in parent (age, sex, baseline BMI, smoking, baseline HbA1c, interval; + baseline CRP for moderator models).
- **Baseline method (simple)**: exact Poisson incidence rate ratios per exposure cell per site group — assumption-light, but discards covariate structure, timing and moderation.
- **Substantive alternative (chosen)**: penalized Cox with splines + CRP interaction. What the alternative adds that the baseline loses: (i) timing/latency structure; (ii) dose-response shape (sharp non-linear occult-tumor excess vs graded metabolic risk); (iii) the mechanism moderator itself. Neural/deep models are not scientifically useful at 35 primary events (documented, not banned); site-group models with 182-1,595 events could support them, but no substantive scientific uncertainty identified here that a neural model resolves better than penalized splines.
- **Resource budget**: CPU only, minutes per model set (probe ran in 11 s); no GPU required. Estimated <30 min total solver compute.

## Falsification criteria (power-aware, pre-specified)

- **Supportive**: pancreatic-site weight-loss excess (HR >= 2, lower 95% CI > 1, 0-3 y) AND materially weaker or absent at non-digestive sites AND point-estimate concentration among CRP risers (moderator direction consistent; CI may be wide — the moderator is precision-bounded evidence, not a required gate).
- **Adverse**: no weight-loss-specific excess at ANY site (point HR <= 1, upper 95% CI < 1.5 at all sites/windows) with risk tracking baseline BMI — supports the obesity/metabolic interpretation and would drop the prodromal-enrichment rationale in this data.
- **Inconclusive**: pancreatic-site CI spans 1 without excluding HR 2-3, or site-specificity direction inconsistent across groups. Wide-CI null is inconclusive, not refutation.
- **Interpretation limits (pre-stated)**: an association surviving adjustment is not a mechanism; the CRP moderator distinguishes inflammation-linked from non-inflammation-linked weight loss, not tumor from non-tumor causes. Stronger conclusions need external repeat-assessment replication, primary-care prescribing linkage, and registry adjudication (not in this snapshot).

## Competing explanations and handling

As in parent (intentional weight loss, glucose-lowering treatment, other cachexia, healthy-volunteer repeat subset, registry completeness), plus: **inflammation from non-tumor causes** (chronic infection, autoimmune disease, smoking) could mimic the CRP-riser stratum — addressed by covariate adjustment, smoking stratification, and the site-specificity prediction (non-tumor inflammation should not produce digestive-site-specific excess); **inverse-causation of CRP itself** (subclinical illness raises CRP and causes weight loss) — this is partly the point (prodromal catabolic signature) but is labeled as detection-of-occult-illness, not cancer-specific mechanism, unless site specificity holds.
