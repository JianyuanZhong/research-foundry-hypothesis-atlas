# Capacity-frontier test of all-offered UACR-first versus immediate cystatin-C allocation

## Clinical decision, evidence boundary, and advance

A health service with constrained cystatin-C capacity must decide whether to (1) attempt UACR in every eligible adult, wait until a usable result or a protocol-defined failure, use a frozen fallback after failure, and allocate cystatin C with the all-O AG score; or (2) allocate cystatin C immediately to all-O candidates with the UACR-free G score. The inherited experiment answers this only when capacity is exactly 20% of adults aged 40–69 whose baseline 2021 race-free creatinine eGFR (eGFRcr) is 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m². The consequential unresolved question is whether that ranking advantage is stable when capacity is lower or higher. Each comparison gives the two strategies the same cystatin count, but the strategies do not receive equal total resources.

The strongest evidence already supported is narrower. Chen et al. (Kidney Medicine 2024; DOI 10.1016/j.xkme.2024.100796; PMCID PMC10986041; frozen Europe PMC full-text XML source [source checksum], 126,592 bytes, [source checksum]; bibliographic record and abstract claims cross-checked in the available XML) studied 468,969 UK Biobank participants, developed clinical and enriched models in 375,175, internally validated them in 93,794, and reported validation AUC 0.75 for the enriched model. This supports association and internal predictability of large creatinine–cystatin eGFR differences from demographic, clinical, laboratory and lifestyle characteristics. It does not establish a fixed-capacity testing strategy, incremental grip value, a UACR-first workflow, capacity robustness, measured-GFR accuracy, chronicity, treatment benefit, cost-effectiveness, or external transportability.

The unresolved claim tested here is stronger than the inherited 20% claim: the intention-to-obtain-UACR-first analog identifies materially more prespecified biochemical targets per 1,000 cystatin assays than immediate UACR-free allocation at 10%, 20%, and 30% capacity in both untouched locks, while retaining everyone offered UACR in every denominator and candidate pool. Policies can exchange rank order as a budget crosses their score distributions; therefore a finding at one cutoff does not establish a portable allocation rule. The substantive advance is a prespecified capacity frontier around the inherited 20% anchor. The 10% and 30% bookends represent lower and higher constrained assay availability, not empirically optimized or cost-derived thresholds. The mature executable parent's population, recruitment time zero, outcomes, score definitions, v3 splits, model fitting, 20% internal comparator gates and evidence limits are unchanged. V remains a secondary usable-result analysis only.

This is a retrospective strategy analog, not an emulation of observed UKB ordering. “Intention-to-obtain” is an imposed hypothetical protocol assignment: it does not mean UKB participants were offered testing, that UACR was available before cystatin allocation, or that future collection would succeed. The experiment compares biochemical allocation under two deterministic information strategies; it does not estimate causal benefit.

## Supportive, adverse, inconclusive meanings and stronger evidence needed

A supportive result means only that, in both internal UKB locks under the frozen snapshot/fallback mapping and equal cystatin counts at each of three prespecified capacities, AG selected more equation-defined H/D targets than G by the prespecified margins, with N18-coded enrichment only if every q-specific information and effect gate passes. It demonstrates internal capacity-robust allocation yield across 10%–30% at the three tested points and justifies prospective comparison. It does not establish dominance at untested capacities, an optimal capacity, a preferred workflow, lower cost, better care or true CKD.

An adverse result means adequately precise evidence against the broad capacity-robust claim at a named q or against an inherited 20% internal claim. A q-specific AG:G result below materiality shows that the added UACR-first mapping is not materially superior at that operating point, even if it succeeds at another; a sign reversal is evidence of capacity-dependent ranking harm. Failure of G's inherited internal comparators questions the immediate strategy at 20%; failure of W1_ITO internal gates questions the UACR-first score at 20%. None proves that the other workflow improves patient outcomes.

An inconclusive result means the data cannot establish or refute robustness across all three frozen capacities because of uncertainty at one or more q, lock disagreement, missing labels, sparse events/swaps, instability or failed computation. Point-specific support may still motivate another study but is not evidence for the full 10%–30% frontier. Inconclusive is not equivalence and cannot be repaired by the 20% anchor, V-only or sensitivity evidence.

The exact additional data needed to test actual collection success and implementation are participant-level UACR offer/order, collection, result release, invalid/failure reason, retry, protocol deadline and cystatin list/order timestamps. Choosing an optimal absolute or fractional capacity additionally requires site-specific eligible volume, laboratory throughput, marginal assay and urine-workup costs, waiting time, burden, downstream actions, harms and utilities. Persistent albuminuria/reduced filtration requires repeated measurements; kidney truth requires measured GFR and clinical adjudication; complete prognosis requires outpatient events, emigration/censoring and chart review; transportability requires external validation; benefit requires a prospective implementation or randomized testing-strategy study with patient-centered outcomes.
