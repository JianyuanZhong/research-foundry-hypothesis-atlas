# Does hypotension burden add information beyond minimum MAP for subsequent AKI?

## Decision question and falsifiable hypothesis

In adult eICU unit stays with adequate early creatinine and blood-pressure observation, does the duration/burden of MAP below 65 mmHg during ICU hours 6–24 identify subsequent creatinine-defined acute kidney injury (AKI) during hours 24–72 after conditioning on minimum MAP, baseline kidney function, admission characteristics, and hospital?

Primary hypothesis: among patients with the same minimum observed MAP during hours 6–24, greater monitored-time hypotension burden is associated with higher risk of subsequent creatinine-defined AKI, and adding burden to a minimum-MAP model produces reproducible incremental prognostic information in hospitals held out from model fitting.

This is an observational conditional-association/prognostic estimand. It is not a treatment effect, a causal effect of allowing or preventing hypotension, or a recommendation that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## What is known and what remains unresolved

The expert seed `[starting question]` proposes the question but explicitly says that its bindings, novelty, and feasibility are unvalidated. I inspected the local eICU documentation page and the actual catalog schemas. A relevant public full-text study (Li et al., *J Clin Monit Comput*, 2026, DOI 10.1007/s10877-026-01410-6; PMCID PMC13194229) reports associations between several intraoperative hypotension metrics and postoperative AKI in 508 emergency cesarean patients, and notes that these associations do not establish causality or an intervention threshold. That population and perioperative exposure are different from the multicenter ICU question here. The evidence therefore supports the narrower claim that duration, event count, AUC, or time-weighted measures can be associated with AKI in at least one selected surgical cohort.

It does not establish that, in general ICU patients, duration or area-below-threshold adds clinically useful information after minimum MAP, nor that hypotension causes AKI. The substantive advance is a direct incremental test of the duration/burden-versus-minimum distinction, with hospital-held-out validation and explicit measurement-density sensitivity.

## Population and temporal design

Index unit stay is `patientunitstayid`; all offsets below are minutes relative to ICU admission, as documented in `datasets/eicu/metadata.json`.

Primary population:

- adults with a numeric age of at least 18 in `patient`; the eICU string value `> 89` is coded as 90 for age adjustment;
- one first eligible unit stay per `uniquepid` in the primary analysis (all eligible stays clustered by `uniquepid` are a prespecified sensitivity analysis);
- unit stay lasting through offset 72 (at least 72 hours), so the primary 24–72-hour outcome has an opportunity to be observed;
- at least one valid numeric creatinine in hours 0–6;
- at least two valid MAP observations in hours 6–24 and at least 50% monitored coverage after the gap rule below;
- no dialysis/RRT at or before index if this can be identified unambiguously from the available structured fields; otherwise retain the patient and report this as an unavailable exclusion, with a sensitivity analysis using `apacheApsVar.dialysis` and auditable dialysis rows.

The primary cohort is deliberately narrower than “all ICU patients”: requiring a 72-hour stay and follow-up laboratory measurement improves ascertainment but limits generalizability and can select a sicker subgroup. A secondary analysis includes shorter stays with censoring at ICU discharge/death and treats an unobserved outcome as unknown rather than as no AKI.

Exposure window: offsets 360 through 1,440 minutes (hours 6–24). The first six hours are reserved for pre-exposure baseline measurements; no exposure feature may use data after offset 1,440.

Outcome window: offsets 1,440 through 4,320 minutes (hours 24–72). This is a subsequent outcome window, not a claim of complete KDIGO surveillance.

## Variable construction

### MAP and hypotension burden

From `vitalAperiodic`, normalize numeric `noninvasivemean`; reject missing, non-finite, or physiologically implausible values outside 20–200 mmHg. This is a measurement-quality rule, not a diagnosis. Sort by `observationoffset` and take the median when duplicate offsets remain.

For adjacent valid observations in the exposure window with a gap of at most 60 minutes, linearly interpolate MAP. Integrate the piecewise-linear trace, splitting intervals at 65 mmHg:

- minimum MAP: minimum valid observed `noninvasivemean`;
- primary duration/burden: minutes for which the interpolated MAP is below 65 mmHg;
- normalized duration: burden minutes divided by monitored minutes;
- secondary depth burden: area below 65, `integral max(65 - MAP, 0) dt`, in mmHg-minutes;
- secondary event count: contiguous below-threshold episodes, with a gap over 60 minutes ending an episode.

Intervals separated by a gap over 60 minutes contribute neither exposure nor monitored time. The primary cohort requires at least 50% of the 18-hour exposure window to be monitored by these valid intervals; record the exact monitored minutes for every patient. Use raw burden and normalized duration separately, never silently treating missing BP as normotension.

The primary model uses minimum MAP as a restricted cubic spline and adds normalized duration (or, in a separately fitted model, area below threshold). Do not put duration and AUC into the same primary model, because they are different parameterizations of the same trace and would make the incremental estimand unstable. Refit using `vitalPeriodic.pamean` as a modality sensitivity; the catalog describes it as five-minute summary observations, not waveforms.

### Kidney baseline and outcome

Identify creatinine rows only after inspecting normalized `labname` and the two available measurement-system/interface fields. Use numeric `labresult`; do not infer a number from free text when `labresult` is absent. Retain only a validated mg/dL-compatible creatinine representation and plausible values (0.1–20 mg/dL); ambiguous units are excluded from the primary outcome and counted.

Baseline creatinine is the earliest valid value at offsets 0–360. The primary creatinine-defined AKI proxy is met if any valid follow-up value in offsets 1,440–4,320 satisfies either:

1. an increase of at least 0.3 mg/dL with the follow-up no more than 48 hours after the baseline measurement; or
2. a value at least 1.5 times baseline within offsets 1,440–4,320.

The first rule is a strict 48-hour component; the second uses the available 72-hour ICU window and is not a claim to have the complete seven-day KDIGO baseline. Report the two components separately, the composite, and the number of eligible patients with no follow-up creatinine. A primary analysis requiring follow-up creatinine would be described as “among patients with observed follow-up creatinine,” not as an incidence estimate for all ICU admissions.

As a secondary, contingent endpoint, audit `intakeOutput.celllabel`, `cellvaluenumeric`, `outputtotal`, `intakeoutputoffset`, and `intakeoutputentryoffset` for an unambiguous urine-output-in-mL representation and interval semantics. Only if those semantics are validated should an oliguria analysis be attempted, using `admissionweight`; otherwise state that a urine-output AKI criterion is unavailable rather than treating `outputtotal` as hourly urine automatically. `dialysistotal` is a sensitivity signal, not a complete RRT adjudication.

### Baseline covariates and treatment context

The clinical baseline includes age, gender, ethnicity, hospital admission source, unit type, admission weight, baseline creatinine, hospital characteristics, and first-six-hour summaries of available MAP and heart rate. Use `patient` and `hospital` fields only, plus measurements strictly before offset 360. Do not use `apacheApsVar` in the primary model: its physiology is a first-24-hour summary and can overlap the exposure window. Add it only in a labeled severity/leakage sensitivity analysis.

Use `infusionDrug` to flag a pre-exposure vasopressor record in offsets 0–360 and to describe (not causally adjust away) vasopressor exposure during 360–1,440. Normalize `drugname` with a prespecified lexicon for norepinephrine/noradrenaline, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, and dopamine. Do not interpret a row as a complete start-stop dose history without validating eICU infusion semantics. Treatment during the exposure window is a possible mediator and time-varying confounder; the primary estimand intentionally does not claim to adjust it away.

## Estimand, baselines, and analysis

The primary estimand is the adjusted difference in predicted probability of the creatinine-defined AKI proxy between the 75th and 25th percentiles of normalized hypotension duration, holding minimum MAP and baseline covariates at their observed values and averaging over the cohort. The model is a logistic regression with hospital fixed effects (or a hierarchical hospital intercept if the implementation supports it), restricted cubic splines for minimum MAP and duration, and a duration-by-minimum-MAP interaction prespecified as exploratory. Report the marginal risk difference, odds ratio per interquartile-range duration, 95% confidence interval, and calibration.

Fit the following nested baselines:

- B0: baseline covariates only;
- B1: B0 plus minimum MAP;
- B2: B1 plus normalized duration (primary incremental test);
- B3: B1 plus area below threshold (separate secondary incremental test);
- B4: B1 plus raw duration and monitored minutes (recording-density sensitivity).

Use grouped five-fold cross-validation by hospital, with all preprocessing, spline knots, and imputation parameters learned inside training folds. Hospitals are never split across folds. Compare B1 with B2/B3 using out-of-fold Brier score, calibration slope/intercept, AUROC as descriptive discrimination, and standardized marginal risk difference. Decision-curve/net-benefit analysis is exploratory because this dataset provides no validated AKI treatment threshold. A small AUROC increase alone is not evidence of clinical usefulness.

Use complete-case results only as a transparent primary ascertainment analysis; do not impute an unmeasured AKI outcome as negative. Model the probability of having a follow-up creatinine using baseline variables and pre-exposure measurement intensity, apply inverse-probability-of-observation weights as a sensitivity analysis, and provide worst/best-case bounds over unobserved outcomes. Report testing intensity from `lab` without using future testing as a causal covariate.

Uncertainty is clustered at hospital for the transport analysis and at `uniquepid` for repeated-stay sensitivity. Use a hospital-level bootstrap for confidence intervals and report the number of hospitals, patients, events, and held-out hospitals in every analysis. Prespecify a clinically interpretable signal as an adjusted risk difference whose 95% interval excludes zero and whose point estimate is at least 2 percentage points across the IQR of duration; this is an operational decision benchmark, not an externally validated minimal clinically important difference. If the event count or hospital support is inadequate, return an inconclusive result rather than relax the design.

## Falsification and robustness

The duration claim is falsified, or downgraded to unsupported, if its incremental coefficient/risk difference is null with useful precision and B2 does not improve held-out calibration/Brier performance over B1. Treat a signal as measurement-dependent rather than supportive if it disappears under any of the following prespecified checks: 30-minute rather than 60-minute maximum gap, stricter 30–180 mmHg artifact bounds, requiring 75% coverage, robust minimum MAP in place of the raw minimum, or `vitalPeriodic.pamean`.

Permute duration/AUC within hospital, minimum-MAP decile, and coverage decile while preserving each patient’s minimum MAP and outcome. The permuted incremental estimate should be near null; a persistent signal indicates leakage, residual hospital/measurement structure, or a coding error. Fit a temporal negative-control analysis in which the outcome is an early creatinine change occurring wholly in offsets 0–360 and the exposure remains 360–1,440. A strong “future burden predicts prior injury” result is evidence of temporal leakage or severe selection/confounding and invalidates a straightforward supportive interpretation. This negative control is diagnostic, not proof that all confounding is absent.

Check heterogeneity by hospital, admission source, pre-exposure vasopressor status, baseline creatinine, and BP modality. Do not call heterogeneity causal. If the signal is present only in hospitals with dense laboratory testing or only in one BP interface, the appropriate conclusion is limited transportability or ascertainment bias.

## Interpretation

Supportive results would mean that, conditional on observed baseline information and minimum MAP, burden/duration is a reproducible prognostic marker for subsequent observed creatinine injury and may be worth carrying into risk stratification or trial enrichment. They would not show that reducing hypotension prevents AKI, identify a safe threshold, or distinguish hypotension from the illness and treatment processes that produced it.

Adverse results would mean that minimum MAP is at least as informative under this operational outcome, or that apparent duration information is not transportable after recording-density and hospital-held-out checks. This is clinically useful negative evidence against replacing a minimum-BP summary with duration in this setting.

Inconclusive results would include too few patients with 72-hour follow-up, too few observed AKI events, wide hospital-to-hospital variation, substantial missing/ambiguous creatinine units, or unstable estimates across reasonable artifact rules. The response is to preserve uncertainty and obtain better data, not to select a more favorable threshold or model.

## Unavailable evidence and limits on claims

This public eICU snapshot has no pre-ICU baseline creatinine sufficient for reliable CKD adjudication, no complete seven-day creatinine surveillance, and no guaranteed urine-output interval semantics. It lacks raw arterial waveforms and provides noninvasive/summary vital observations rather than continuous physiology. Public narrative note sections are removed. Pre-ICU hypotension, exact bedside BP method, clinician intent, fluid responsiveness, detailed nephrotoxin exposure, contrast, chronic comorbidity severity, and complete RRT adjudication are unavailable or incomplete.

Consequently, the experiment can check source binding, cohort construction, temporal ordering, measurement-density effects, nested-model computation, uncertainty, and whether a reported predictive association survives falsification checks. It cannot automatically establish KDIGO AKI in every patient, adjudicate underlying kidney disease, establish a causal hypotension effect, infer a treatment target, or establish clinical benefit. Those stronger conclusions require expert clinical adjudication of kidney injury and measurement validity, richer pre-ICU and medication/fluid data, and prospective or quasi-experimental validation.
