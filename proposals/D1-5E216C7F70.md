# Branch A — residual instability before ICU discharge

Status: candidate-ready proposed Harbor experiment; no cohort scan, fitted model, or scientific result was run.

Parent/seed: repair of [prior hypothesis] (expert seed expert-20260913-mimic-10), with [prior hypothesis] as contextual evidence only. This is a distinct discharge-instability direction, not a continuation of the selected AKI-recovery question.

## Scientific opening and claim boundary

The expert seed asks whether persistently high oxygen requirements or fluctuating vital signs before ICU discharge predict ICU return or in-hospital death. The closest inspected evidence supports a narrower opening:

- [K1] reports that ward-detected vital/laboratory flags were associated with ICU readmission or death within 48 hours, but the abstract describes a single-center surgical post-transfer monitoring study and says its modeling was inferential rather than a diagnostic/screening model.
- [K2] reports, from an abstract-only inspection, a 48-hour ICU-readmission model in a sepsis subset of MIMIC-IV in which heart rate in the 24 hours before discharge was one predictor.
- [K3] was inspected from full-text XML. It defines same-hospital ICU return after ward transfer and reports a MIMIC-IV temporal/AI model for readmission or death within seven days, with external KNUH validation. It does not establish that an oxygen/vital phenotype is causal or that a model trained here would generalize.

Thus the strongest supported claim is that post-ICU vital abnormalities and pre-discharge variables can be associated with early adverse outcomes in selected cohorts. The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults discharged alive from an ICU to a non-ICU hospital destination with adequate pre-discharge observation, does a prespecified, recurrent oxygen/vital-instability phenotype in the final 24 hours independently mark higher risk of ICU readmission or in-hospital death within 48 hours, and does the ordering/trajectory of those measurements add information beyond transparent summaries?

This is prognostic/descriptive, not causal. A positive association would not show that keeping the patient in ICU, changing oxygen treatment, or increasing ward monitoring would improve outcomes.

Clinical importance: the decision at stake is whether the final ICU-to-ward transition is accompanied by a measurable residual instability signal that could justify targeted handoff or monitoring research. A short, well-defined window is actionable for a future discharge process, while the 48-hour endpoint distinguishes immediate transition failure from longer post-ICU recovery.

## Actual scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly construct the leakage-safe cohort and produce:

1. A reproducible cohort-flow file with counts at every exclusion, destination class, event support, missingness, and treatment-limitation documentation.
2. A locked phenotype table for every included index discharge: oxygen burden, vital-instability burden, urine/documentation support, treatment-intensity markers, destination and code-status fields, all derived only from data at or before the index ICU outtime.
3. A transparent baseline analysis estimating standardized 48-hour risks and risk differences for unstable versus observed-stable discharges, plus separate ICU-readmission and death cumulative-incidence estimates.
4. A patient-split masked temporal model on the same inputs and index population, evaluated on the same held-out test set, with AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration, and decision-curve summaries. It must also report the model's held-out risk contrast for the prespecified instability phenotype, not only a predictive score.
5. Predeclared artifact and falsification checks, with conclusions linked to saved computed outputs. Readiness or a favorable coefficient is not completion.

## Population, index landmark, and time boundaries

Dataset: MIMIC-IV core 3.1 snapshot [source checksum].

Primary population:

- adults with hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18;
- an eligible ICU stay in icu/icustays with non-null outtime;
- discharge alive from that ICU stay: no hosp/admissions.deathtime at or before icustays.outtime; retain hospital_expire_flag as an audit field and exclude any stay whose deathtime is at or before the landmark;
- an immediately observed next hospital transfer to a non-ICU destination, identified from hosp/transfers as the deterministic transfer row nearest the landmark (see below);
- one index discharge per subject_id, hadm_id: the earliest eligible ICU discharge to a non-ICU destination. This prevents correlated multiple ICU discharges within one admission. A sensitivity analysis may use all eligible discharges with subject-clustered uncertainty, but it is not the primary estimand;
- at least three of four six-hour pre-landmark bins contain a core vital measurement, and at least two bins contain an oxygen-status measurement (pulse oximetry or oxygen flow). Discharges failing these support rules are not silently called stable: they form an insufficient-observation diagnostic stratum and are excluded from the primary stable-versus-unstable contrast.

Index time is tD = icustays.outtime. The feature window is [tD - 24 hours, tD), using event/clinical time, never a row's future storage time. No chartevents, inputevents, outputevents, procedureevents, or other clinical record with charttime, starttime, endtime, or storetime after tD may enter a feature. storetime is retained only for a documentation-delay sensitivity analysis. The dataset guide states that subject-specific timestamps are shifted but within-subject intervals are preserved; no cross-subject calendar comparison is used.

The immediate destination join is:

1. For the selected subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, search hosp/transfers for rows with intime in [tD - 1 hour, tD + 6 hours]; prefer eventtype == "transfer" when present and choose the smallest absolute intime - tD, breaking ties by transfer_id.
2. Record the raw careunit, eventtype, intime, and outtime. Destination is ICU if the transfer interval is matched to an ICU stay by the same subject_id, hadm_id and overlapping/starting within 6 hours; otherwise it is non-ICU. The primary cohort requires non-ICU.
3. Preserve the raw careunit and an audited grouped destination (general floor, stepdown/intermediate, procedural/PACU, other). The grouping must be made from the distinct observed labels before outcome modeling and frozen with the run; no labels may be collapsed after looking at outcomes. Missing/no-match destinations are excluded from the primary cohort and reported in the flow and a missing-destination sensitivity analysis.
4. hosp/admissions.discharge_location is the eventual hospital discharge destination. It may describe information after the ICU landmark and is never a primary model feature. It is used only descriptively to expose post-landmark destination/selection artifacts.

## Auditable physiologic phenotype

The item dictionary inspection produced work/branches/ditems-core.tsv (hash recorded separately). The primary labels and IDs are:

- Heart Rate: chartevents 220045.
- Arterial Blood Pressure systolic/diastolic/mean: 220050/220051/220052; Non Invasive Blood Pressure systolic/diastolic/mean: 220179/220180/220181.
- Respiratory Rate: 220210; Respiratory Rate (spontaneous/total): 224689/224690.
- O2 saturation pulseoxymetry: 220277.
- O2 Flow: 223834; O2 Flow (additional cannula): 227287.
- Temperature Celsius/Fahrenheit: 223762/223761.
- Urine output: outputevents Foley 226559, Void 226560.
- Code Status: 223758; Code Status. (MD Progress Note): 228687; Code Status (Intubation): 229784.
- Treatment/procedure labels used only in sensitivity markers: inputevents Norepinephrine 221906, Epinephrine 221289, Dopamine 221662, Dobutamine 221653, Vasopressin 222315; procedureevents Intubation 224385, Extubation 227194, Dialysis - CRRT 225802, CVVHD 225803, CVVHDF 225809, SCUF 225955, and Hemodialysis 225441.

Numeric cleaning is locked before outcome inspection: retain numeric values with recognized units, remove impossible values using a published physiological range table stored with the run, retain one duplicate item/time value by deterministic last-row order only for exact duplicate source rows, and never use text labels to create numeric physiology. For each six-hour bin in [tD-24h,tD), derive medians and counts for HR, MAP (prefer arterial mean, otherwise NIBP mean), RR, SpO2, temperature, and O2 flow; retain event counts and last observation time.

Primary residual-instability phenotype:

- oxygen_high = 1 if a numeric value from O2 Flow 223834 or O2 Flow (additional cannula) 227287 is at least 4 L/min in at least two distinct six-hour bins. The raw maximum, bin count, and source label are also retained. This is supplemental-flow documentation, not FiO2 or ventilator support.
- vital_abnormal = 1 if at least two distinct six-hour bins contain any of: HR >110 or <50 bpm; MAP <65 mmHg using the available arterial/NIBP mean item; RR >24 or <8/min; or SpO2 <92%. Multiple readings in one bin count once. These thresholds are fixed before fitting and are not optimized to the outcome.
- residual_instability = 1 if oxygen_high or vital_abnormal; observed_stable = 0 only when neither criterion is present and the observation-support rules are met.
- Temperature is retained as a continuous trajectory and sensitivity feature, not folded into the primary composite because its measurement and treatment dependence make it a weaker discharge-stability discriminator.
- A sensitivity phenotype uses continuous oxygen burden plus the count of abnormal vital bins; another removes all observations in [tD-2h,tD) to test whether the result is a discharge-process artifact.

Observation support is explicit: count the four bins with at least one core-vital event (HR/BP/RR/SpO2) and the four bins with an O2-status event (SpO2 or O2 flow). Include those counts and per-item missingness indicators in the enriched analysis. Do not impute unobserved oxygen as stable. Report the insufficient-observation stratum separately and repeat the contrast using inverse-observation-probability weighting only if the fitting sample supports a prespecified observation model.

Urine is not part of the primary instability definition. It is a physiologic/documentation rival: sum numeric Foley/Void output value by six-hour bin and report ml/hour only as a recorded-output proxy, with count of output rows and bins. The primary output excludes 226566 irrigation, 227489 GU irrigant, 226627 OR urine, 226631 PACU urine, and text-only 226713; a sensitivity adds them with separate source indicators. No true renal function, fluid balance, measured oxygen fraction, waveform, or clinical adjudication is claimed.

Treatment limitation and treatment intensity are not treated as innocuous confounders:

- Capture the latest pre-tD code-status item from 223758, 228687, or 229784 and a documentation indicator. A locked mapping of observed values to full/limited/comfort/unknown must be reviewed by a clinician before interpretation; until then use raw categories/unknown and do not call lexical categories definitive.
- Capture presence and interval overlap of vasopressors, ventilation/extubation, dialysis/CRRT, and intubation procedure items above. These may lie on the pathway from illness to discharge and are therefore an enriched/sensitivity model, not an adjustment that proves an independent physiologic mechanism.
- Record admissions.discharge_location only after the outcome-free primary analysis as a post-index selection audit; never use it as a predictor.

## Outcomes and competing events

Follow-up starts exactly at tD and ends at tD + 48 hours or the earliest available hospital end/death boundary. The primary outcome is the composite:

Y48 = 1 if either (a) a subsequent icustays.intime for the same subject_id, hadm_id occurs after tD and no later than tD+48h, or (b) admissions.deathtime is after tD and no later than tD+48h.

ICU readmission is a subsequent ICU stay, not merely a transfer row labelled ICU. Keep the subsequent stay_id, intime, and outtime in the outcome file. Death requires a timed deathtime; hospital_expire_flag==1 without a usable time is an ascertainment diagnostic and is not silently assigned to the 48-hour window. patients.dod is a supplementary mortality signal only. If the observation horizon ends before 48 hours because of hospital discharge, label the subject administratively censored for the component that cannot be observed; do not treat ward discharge as absence of readmission. Because the MIMIC core has hospital transfers and ICU stays but no guaranteed post-hospital surveillance, this is an in-hospital 48-hour estimand, not 48-hour all-setting mortality.

Secondary outcomes are (i) ICU readmission by 6, 24, and 48 hours; (ii) timed in-hospital death by 6, 24, and 48 hours; (iii) first event type in a mutually exclusive competing-event analysis; and (iv) a broad same-admission ICU return before admissions.dischtime as descriptive follow-up. Death before ICU readmission is a competing event for the readmission cause-specific analysis. For a tie at the same timestamp, death is the first event; retain raw timestamps and perform a tie sensitivity. A composite can be driven by either cause, so all claims must report components and not call a readmission mechanism from a death-only signal.

## Analysis, uncertainty, and comparison

Primary association estimand: among adequately observed adult ICU-to-non-ICU discharges, the standardized 48-hour risk difference and risk ratio for residual_instability=1 versus observed stable, standardized over prespecified pre-landmark covariates. This is an associational estimand, not a treatment effect.

Transparent baseline, fit on the same patient-level split as the learned model:

- logistic regression for Y48, with residual-instability indicator as the focal term; restricted continuous oxygen burden, abnormal-vital-bin count, age, sex, ICU LOS, first/last ICU careunit, admission type/location, and pre-landmark observation support as prespecified covariates;
- a second enriched specification adds destination careunit, code-status category/unknown, vasopressor/ventilation/dialysis markers, and urine-recording support to test rival explanations. Destination and treatment coefficients are descriptive, not causal;
- use a fixed regularization choice selected only on the training/validation partition, standardized predictions and bootstrap confidence intervals or influence-function intervals with the split frozen. Report calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC, and marginal standardized risks/risk differences, not odds ratios alone;
- fit cause-specific logistic/time-to-event models or cumulative-incidence estimators for ICU readmission and death separately, treating the other event as competing.

Substantive learned alternative: a small masked GRU-D-style temporal model over four six-hour bins, using the same raw item groups, bin summaries, missingness indicators, and time-since-last-observation. The model must use only records before tD, explicitly mask missingness, and output Y48; no free text or eventual discharge fields. Use patient-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits with a fixed seed, so admissions from one patient cannot cross partitions. Select architecture/early stopping on validation only. Evaluate on the untouched test set with the exact baseline metrics, calibration, bootstrapped uncertainty, and the prespecified unstable-versus-stable risk contrast. Use permutation/feature-ablation checks for oxygen, vital trajectory, missingness, and destination features; feature attribution is descriptive and does not establish mechanism.

Why this alternative is scientifically substantive: a static baseline retains counts and extrema but loses whether instability is persistent, improving, oscillatory, newly emergent, or merely measured more often near discharge. The masked temporal model can reveal whether ordering, recovery trajectory, and observation cadence contain information beyond summaries. It is selected to test that scientific uncertainty, not to chase a small score increase. A high-capacity transformer is deferred because this question needs an auditable four-bin trajectory and the available endpoint is a bounded internal study; revisit it only if the GRU-D alternative shows stable incremental information and enough events. A causal treatment model is deferred because treatment assignment, goals of care, and clinical indication are not adequately measured in these tables.

Compute planning is prospective and unverified: the logistic baseline is expected to fit on 4 CPU cores and <=16 GiB RAM in minutes; the four-bin GRU-D model is expected to fit with 1 allocated A100, 4-8 CPU cores and 32-64 GiB RAM in under two hours including repeated seeds and test evaluation. These are future solver estimates, not executed runs and not a GPU requirement. The deployment hardware instructions were followed: ordinary shell CUDA visibility is not used to infer availability; a future GPU job must request gpus=1 and use the allocated cuda:0. If a small readiness probe is needed, it may test imports/device allocation only and must not be reported as a study result.

## Rival explanations and falsification criteria

1. Residual severity: unstable patients are simply sicker. The instability association should attenuate after pre-landmark severity/treatment markers; a remaining association is still not mechanism. Report both core and enriched estimates rather than declaring adjustment proof.
2. Discharge destination/selection: clinicians send unstable patients to different units or monitor them more. Require within-destination estimates, destination-stratified event counts, and an analysis restricted to the modal non-ICU destination. If the result exists only in one destination or only after adding destination, describe selection rather than physiologic instability.
3. Observation process: more measurements create more opportunities to detect a flag and are themselves a proxy for concern. Require the support strata, event counts, missingness indicators, documentation-time sensitivity, and [tD-2h,tD) exclusion. If the association disappears under these checks or is predicted by measurement density without raw abnormality, the observation explanation is favored.
4. Treatment limitation: code status, hospice-oriented care, or comfort-focused treatment can increase death and reduce escalation independently of physiology. Report unknown and limited-status strata and code-status documentation rates; have a clinical reviewer adjudicate the observed value mapping before interpreting. A death-only association confined to limited/unknown status is not evidence of a physiologic discharge mechanism.
5. Competing death: a composite may hide whether readmission or death drives the result. Require cause-specific and first-event competing-risk outputs. A result confined to death cannot support a claim about ICU-return prevention.
6. Discharge-process/reverse-timing artifact: last-hour charting may reflect transfer workflow. Repeat using [tD-24h,tD-2h) and separately compare early versus late bins. A signal present only in the final two hours is evidence against a stable residual-physiology interpretation.
7. Leakage: assert no feature timestamp is >= tD, no eventual discharge_location enters a model, no post-index ICU rows enter features, and no split contains the same subject in more than one partition. A failed audit invalidates the computation.
8. Null/negative controls: report association with a pre-discharge feature window shifted earlier, [tD-48h,tD-24h), as a timing-control analysis; it is not assumed to be a true negative control because severity can persist. Permuting outcomes within the test set must remove predictive performance, validating the evaluation pipeline rather than the clinical hypothesis.

## Interpretation rules

Supportive result: a graded oxygen/vital burden or trajectory association with the 48-hour composite and at least one component, surviving destination, observation, treatment-limitation, competing-death, and final-two-hour checks, with a calibrated temporal model showing prespecified incremental held-out information. This supports a robust prognostic signal and motivates external/clinical validation; it does not establish a mechanism or that delayed discharge/extra monitoring helps.

Adverse result: adequate-precision null association, or association that vanishes after observation/destination/treatment-limitation checks, or a composite effect driven solely by death. This weakens the claim that a physiologic residual-instability phenotype independently marks ICU-to-ward failure and supports revising the phenotype or abandoning a discharge-monitoring direction.

Inconclusive result: sparse 48-hour events, wide intervals, inadequate observation or code-status ascertainment, unstable model calibration, or discordant components. Do not call this a refutation; report the information limit and identify whether a larger/more complete cohort, clinical adjudication, or prospective monitoring is needed.

Computationally checkable claims are the joins, timestamp exclusions, phenotype construction, cohort counts, event labels, split integrity, model metrics, uncertainty, and whether conclusions match those outputs. Clinical adjudication is required for code-status categories, destination semantics, and whether an oxygen/vital pattern represents true physiologic instability rather than care-process documentation. External validation, prospective monitoring benefit, treatment effects, and causal/mechanistic claims require another study.

## Alternatives considered and revisit conditions

- Chosen: transparent logistic summary versus masked GRU-D temporal alternative. Same cohort, target, split and held-out evaluation make the comparison scientifically interpretable.
- Deferred: a transformer/generative sequence model. It may represent longer histories, but four six-hour bins and a 48-hour endpoint do not currently justify its additional complexity. Revisit if the GRU-D adds reproducible trajectory information and event support is ample.
- Deferred: a causal effect of ICU delay or ward monitoring. The available records do not encode a credible intervention assignment, clinician intent, complete treatment limitation, or ward monitoring exposure.
- Deferred: a full external validation. No external MIMIC-compatible ICU-discharge dataset is configured in this branch; it is required before a clinical tool claim.
- Evidence that would change the choices: clinically adjudicated code status/destination, validated treatment-intensity and ward-monitoring fields, adequate event counts for competing-risk precision, and an external hospital cohort with the same item definitions.
