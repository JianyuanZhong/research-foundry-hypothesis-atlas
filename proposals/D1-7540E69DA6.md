# Do early laboratory recording practices transport poorly after measured acuity is accounted for?

Status: Lead episode-2 repair; proposed child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an adaptation of expert seed `[starting question]`, not a reproduction of any demonstration paper.

## Scientific deliverable and unresolved claim

The newly fitted deliverable is a set of leakage-safe, hospital-held-out predictions and uncertainty intervals from paired static logistic and temporal GRU models, estimating whether adding early recorded-laboratory process information changes transport performance beyond the same measured clinical values. Completion requires fitted models, fold assignments, predictions for every eligible test stay, hospital-level discrimination/calibration summaries, the acuity/workflow decomposition, competing-event sensitivity, and falsification outputs. The feasibility audit is not a clinical estimate.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is narrower: the local eICU snapshot contains ICU stays, hospital and person identifiers, ICU-relative laboratory/vital observations, and a heterogeneous recorded laboratory process. The prior audit found 140,801 eligible adult initial stays across 207 hospitals, 2,339,035 early laboratory rows, and 117,368 stays with at least one eligible early laboratory row. It did not fit a model, establish mortality prediction, or show transport failure.

The unresolved claim is:

> In adults who remain observable at a six-hour ICU landmark, a model using early recorded laboratory-testing process features in addition to the same measured values has a larger development-site-to-new-hospital transport loss than its values-only counterpart; this excess is not fully explained by measured acuity and is reproducible when temporal order is modeled.

This is an observational prediction/transport hypothesis. It does not claim that testing causes death, that process variables identify clinician workflow, or that either model should guide care.

Time zero is ICU-relative minute 360. Only information whose clinical observation/result offset is in [0,360] and whose result is available by minute 360 enters the predictor. The primary outcome is ICU death within the next 48 hours, namely `unitdischargestatus='Expired'` and `360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240`. Death after 3240 minutes is not a primary event. An alive ICU discharge before 3240 is a non-event for this explicitly defined “death while still in this ICU within 48 hours” outcome, not a censored observation; it is separately labelled as a competing terminal state. Patients alive and still in the ICU at 3240 are the third state.

The primary estimand is the marginal 48-hour ICU-death probability at minute 360 and the paired transport contrast below, not a causal effect and not hospital mortality. The mandatory competing-event sensitivity fits the same predictor variants to the three mutually exclusive states: (D) death in (360,3240], (C) alive ICU discharge in (360,3240], and (R) alive and remaining in ICU at 3240. It reports cause-specific one-vs-rest logistic predictions and multinomial state probabilities, state-specific Brier scores and cumulative incidence by hospital. If discharge fields or offsets are insufficient to construct these states, the primary analysis stops with a prerequisite failure rather than silently treating discharge as ordinary right censoring.

## Process-versus-acuity design

The central threat is that more testing may simply mean a sicker patient. Therefore “process” is an operational recorded-testing proxy, never a claim of workflow.

For each canonical lab, derive from eligible rows only: any-result indicator, row count, distinct result-timestamp count, minutes to first result, and minutes from last result to 360. Also derive total eligible lab rows and number of canonical analytes observed. Do not use `labid`, interface labels, unselected analytes, or hospital ID as predictors.

Fit and report these predeclared blocks:

1. **Values-only clinical-state block (V):** context plus the value summaries from the vital and canonical-lab channels. No masks, counts, observation times, or hospital features.
2. **Raw process block (V+P):** V plus the laboratory process variables.
3. **Acuity-adjusted process block (V+P-resid):** in each outer training set, standardize P and fit a fixed-alpha ridge multi-output regression of P on the full V design matrix; compute residual process variables for training/validation/test using only that fitted nuisance model. Fit the outcome model on V plus these residuals. No outcome information enters residualization.
4. **Monitoring-control block (V+P-vital):** replace laboratory process variables with analogous recorded-observation features from `vitalPeriodic` and `vitalAperiodic` (per-channel observed indicator, count, first/last offset, and total count) while retaining the same measured values. This is a descriptive control for general surveillance intensity, not a claim that vital recording equals workflow.

Also report process increments within low/middle/high tertiles of the outer-training V predicted risk. These strata are descriptive effect-heterogeneity checks, not causal standardization. A process effect that shrinks after P-residualization and within V-risk strata supports “measured-acuity/observability proxy” as an explanation. Persistence of a lab-specific effect after those checks, alongside hospital-to-hospital shifts in P and attenuation under within-hospital rank normalization, is consistent with a workflow/documentation proxy. Neither pattern proves clinician intent: eICU does not record complete orders, cancellations, interface failures, turnaround, staffing, or bedside rationale.

## Two comparable model alternatives

The exact scientific comparison is held constant across models: input block V versus V+P, target D, and the hospital-held-out estimand. The model family does not earn scientific credit by being harder to fit.

### A. Static penalized logistic regression: primary, auditable baseline

Use a fixed L2 logistic model (C=1, deterministic solver), one-hot encoding, and training-set median/mode rules. Fit V and V+P in every outer fold; fit V+P-resid and V+P-vital as prespecified interpretation checks. This baseline is transparent and gives an auditable conditional process contrast, but loses order, slope, recovery/worsening shape, and irregular timing.

### B. Temporal GRU: substantive alternative

Discretize [0,360] into twelve 30-minute bins. For each value channel and bin, use the mean and the last eligible within-bin value/time. Values-only sequences receive outer-training medians and no observation masks or counts. The process sequence adds lab masks, within-bin counts, time-since-latest-result capped at 360, and the fixed early process totals; context is appended to the final hidden state. Fit a one-layer width-64 GRU, dropout 0.1, Adam 1e-3, batch 256, weighted binary cross-entropy, maximum 30 epochs, patience 5, and one fixed seed per outer repetition (20260913–20260917). Use the same folds and preprocessing isolation as logistic regression. The GRU can reveal trajectory/order and irregular timing that static summaries lose; it is not being used to chase a small predictive gain.

Run both families on the same five repetitions of five whole-hospital folds. The logistic and GRU outputs are paired by fold and patient. The GRU has two variants (V and V+P), 50 bounded fits total; if the declared CPU budget is exceeded or convergence fails, preserve all completed outputs, record the exact failure, and label only the GRU branch computationally deferred. Do not replace it with a different model or alter the hypothesis. The static deliverable remains mandatory.

The approved limits are 7,200 science seconds, concurrency 2, and no mandatory GPU. Feature extraction is a sequential read of the four bound tables, dominated by the compressed `lab` (~0.5 GB) and `vitalPeriodic` (~1.8 GB) sources. Request at most one CPU job with 8 GiB RAM for extraction/logistic work and a bounded CPU/GPU job only if the executor reports that GRU fitting needs it; GPU choice is a resource tradeoff, not evidence. Persist feature manifests, split assignments, fitted preprocessing, checkpoints, predictions, and logs.

## Hospital-held-out estimand and uncertainty

Before fitting, emit cohort counts by hospital, event/competing-state prevalence, canonical name/unit coverage, value availability, process distributions, and the number of hospitals meeting support. Retain hospitals with at least 200 eligible stays and at least 10 primary deaths; retain the same fixed hospital set for every model. If fewer than 20 hospitals qualify, report the prerequisite failure and descriptive estimates only, without a definitive transport conclusion.

For each of five repetitions, assign whole hospitals to five folds with fixed seeds and approximate balance of stay count and deaths. The external set for a fold is all eligible stays in its held-out hospitals. The internal comparator is a patient-grouped 20% holdout within each outer-training hospital set, using only the outer-training hospitals; no test-hospital row affects fitting, imputation, residualization, GRU stopping, or threshold selection.

For each metric m, report hospital-macro means and pooled out-of-fold values. For AUROC, larger is better; for Brier, smaller is better. Define the primary process transport penalty:

- `T_AUROC = (I_AUROC(V+P)-I_AUROC(V)) - (E_AUROC(V+P)-E_AUROC(V))`;
- `T_Brier = (I_Brier(V)-I_Brier(V+P)) - (E_Brier(V)-E_Brier(V+P))`.

Here I is the internal patient-held-out performance and E is the new-hospital performance, computed from the same fold-trained model family. Positive T means the process increment is less preserved in new hospitals. Report E(V), E(V+P), the direct external increments, and I values separately; T is not interpreted as a causal effect and I is explicitly an optimistic development-site comparator. The primary clinical interpretation is external calibration, not T alone.

For calibration, report Brier, calibration intercept and slope, fixed-bin curves, and absolute deviation of slope/intercept from ideal in external hospitals. Report AUROC, AUPRC, and decision curves at predeclared 5%, 10%, and 20% thresholds. Decision curves are descriptive because eICU supplies neither action definitions nor benefit/harm utilities. Use hospital-cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples of complete hospital prediction sets, preserving all repetitions and V/V+P pairing) for 95% percentile intervals. Report hospitals with single-class outcomes separately; they contribute Brier/calibration but not hospital AUROC.

The primary direction is T_AUROC>0, with T_Brier and calibration as corroboration. Directional support requires a 95% interval entirely above zero; a lower bound above 0.02 AUROC/Brier units is the predeclared clearly non-trivial threshold. Robust support additionally requires the same direction in the GRU and logistic families and no failed integrity check. A GRU “win” without a larger process transport penalty is not support for the hypothesis.

For temporal scientific information, separately report GRU(V) minus logistic(V) on external hospitals with the same bootstrap and calibration outputs. This is not a superiority claim: evidence that the GRU adds useful trajectory information requires a reproducible external calibration/Brier improvement, not a tiny AUROC change. The primary question remains process transportability.

## Falsification, adverse findings, and uncertainty

Run these without changing the primary estimand:

1. Permute D within hospital, refit, and repeat evaluation. AUROC should approach 0.5 and AUPRC the permuted prevalence; persistent process-specific transport signal indicates leakage or split failure.
2. Re-run a deliberately permissive lab feature build ignoring `labresultrevisedoffset`. It is invalid and never a result. Material differences from the cutoff-safe build trigger a leakage investigation.
3. Permute each patient’s P vector among patients within hospital, leaving V and outcome unchanged. The process increment should collapse toward V if it is patient-linked; persistence indicates redundancy or a hospital proxy.
4. Shuffle eligible lab offsets within stay while preserving values and counts. Timing-dependent GRU effects should attenuate while static count effects may remain. Failure to separate these patterns weakens the temporal interpretation.
5. Compare raw P with P-resid, V-risk strata, and the vital-monitoring control. This is the acuity/workflow falsification pattern, not proof of workflow.
6. Verify no post-360 row, revision-after-360 result, identifier, discharge/APACHE outcome, or outer-test preprocessing object enters features. Verify each patient appears in only one split role per fit.
7. Report a random patient split with hospital overlap only as an optimistic benchmark. If process gains appear there but not in hospital holdout, that is evidence of site dependence, not a deployable result.

Supportive: T is positive with uncertainty excluding zero, external process-augmented calibration/Brier is worse or less stable than V despite a nonnegative internal increment, the pattern is directionally reproduced in both families, and integrity checks pass. If P-resid/stratified estimates persist, the narrow conclusion is “recorded process contains transport-unstable information beyond measured state,” with workflow only a plausible explanation.

Adverse: T is at or below zero with a sufficiently narrow interval, or V+P is no better internally and no worse externally, or process effects disappear after acuity adjustment and vital-monitoring controls. This weakens/falsifies the stated transport hypothesis for this panel and cohort; it does not prove process is universally safe or transportable.

Inconclusive: fewer than 20 supported hospitals, inadequate event/state support, canonical unit failure, unstable site metrics, wide intervals spanning zero and 0.02, disagreement between families, or GRU convergence failure. Preserve estimates and failure reasons; do not select the favorable point estimate.

## Clinical adjudication and unavailable evidence

Computationally checkable: cohort and time rules, source joins, revision cutoff, split isolation, feature construction, model fits, predictions, AUROC/AUPRC/Brier/calibration, decision-curve arithmetic, bootstrap intervals, competing-state labels, and permutation outcomes.

Not computationally established here: whether a missing lab means no order, unrecorded order, interface loss, delayed result, or a deliberate clinical decision; whether P-resid truly removes acuity; whether a process-dependent model changes triage, escalation, transfer, bed allocation, treatment, or patient outcomes; and whether any threshold has acceptable clinical utility. These require expert review of data provenance and measurement semantics, order/cancellation and turnaround data, staffing/bed-capacity context, a prospective silent deployment, and an independent health system. Hospital ID is not an instrument, and this design cannot make a causal testing-policy claim. Removed calendar dates prevent calendar drift analysis; removed narrative and absent raw waveforms prevent text or waveform claims.
