# Discordant creatinine recovery after AKI: a severity-, treatment-intensity-, and RRT-stratified MIMIC-IV study with KDIGO urine-output sensitivity analysis

Status: active draft, not yet a registered hypothesis. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Among adults in the ICU with KDIGO stage 2 or 3 acute kidney injury (AKI), does apparent creatinine recovery at 48 hours coexisting with persistently low urine output or positive fluid balance identify a worse 7-day ICU prognosis than creatinine recovery alone, after accounting for current severity and treatment intensity at the 48-hour stabilization decision point?

The parent proposal established a prespecified discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype and addressed the strongest confounder—current severity and treatment intensity at the decision point—with SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and renal replacement therapy (RRT) status as adjustment variables. However, RRT status is not merely a continuous treatment-intensity covariate: it marks a fundamentally different renal recovery phenotype and outcome trajectory. Patients on RRT at the 48-hour decision point have already had their native kidney function bypassed, and creatinine recovery may reflect RRT clearance rather than intrinsic renal recovery.

The strongest remaining limitation is that creatinine is a surrogate for measured kidney function and its pharmacokinetic properties can distort the apparent timing of recovery. Creatinine is produced at a rate that depends on muscle mass, and its clearance depends on GFR and tubular secretion. In the setting of AKI, creatinine can lag behind true GFR changes, and its concentration can be influenced by fluid balance, muscle mass, and RRT. A prespecified sensitivity analysis using KDIGO urine-output criteria for renal recovery directly tests whether the discordance signal is robust to the definition of recovery or is an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The inspected literature reports that creatinine-based AKI staging and recovery are associated with ICU outcomes, but that creatinine is a surrogate for measured kidney function and can be influenced by factors other than GFR [K1]. The parent proposal's discordance phenotype—creatinine recovery with persistently low urine output or positive fluid balance—is a clinically useful signal, but its prognostic value may be an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.

The unresolved claim is whether the discordance phenotype's prognostic signal persists when renal recovery is defined by KDIGO urine-output criteria rather than creatinine, and whether the two definitions identify different patient subgroups. If the signal persists, it is more likely to reflect a genuine discordance in renal recovery rather than an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties. If the signal disappears, it is more likely to be an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.

## Study design

### Population

Adults (age >= 18) in the ICU with KDIGO stage 2 or 3 AKI, defined by a creatinine increase >= 2x baseline or >= 3x baseline, or by urine output criteria. The index time is the 48-hour stabilization decision point, defined as the first time at which the patient has been in the ICU for at least 48 hours and has a creatinine measurement available.

### Primary analysis

The primary analysis uses the parent proposal's discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype: creatinine recovery at 48 hours (creatinine at or below baseline) coexisting with persistently low urine output (UO < 0.5 mL/kg/hour over the 24 hours preceding the 48-hour recovery assessment) or positive fluid balance (fluid balance > 0 over the 24 hours preceding the 48-hour recovery assessment). The outcome is 7-day ICU prognosis, defined as ICU death or ICU discharge with a new need for mechanical ventilation or RRT.

### Sensitivity analysis: KDIGO urine-output criteria

The sensitivity analysis uses the same population, index time, RRT stratification, severity adjustment, temporal design, method comparison, and falsification criteria as the primary creatinine-based analysis. The only difference is the definition of recovery: urine-output recovery rather than creatinine recovery.

Urine-output recovery is defined as urine output at or above 0.5 mL/kg/hour over the 24 hours preceding the 48-hour recovery assessment. Urine-output non-recovery is defined as urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour over the 24 hours preceding the 48-hour recovery assessment.

The discordance phenotype in the sensitivity analysis is urine-output recovery at 48 hours coexisting with persistently low creatinine clearance (creatinine above baseline) or positive fluid balance. This tests whether the discordance signal is robust to the definition of recovery.

### UO extraction logic

Urine output is extracted from `icu/outputevents.csv.gz` with the following item mapping:
- `itemid` 226508: Urine Output (ml)
- `itemid` 226510: Urine Output (ml) (alternative)
- `itemid` 226511: Urine Output (ml) (alternative)

The time window is the 24 hours preceding the 48-hour recovery assessment. If UO data are sparse or unavailable, the analysis falls back to `icu/chartevents` with the same item mapping.

### RRT stratification

The analysis is stratified by RRT status at the 48-hour decision point. RRT status is extracted from `icu/procmap.csv.gz` with the following item mapping:
- `itemid` 221289: RRT (hemodialysis)
- `itemid` 221290: RRT (CRRT)
- `itemid` 221291: RRT (peritoneal dialysis)

The stratum-specific estimands are the hazard ratios for 7-day ICU prognosis in the discordant phenotype versus the concordant phenotype, within each RRT stratum. An interaction test is prespecified to test whether the discordance signal differs by RRT status.

### Severity adjustment

The analysis adjusts for SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and RRT status at the 48-hour decision point. SOFA component scores are extracted from `icu/labevents.csv.gz` and `icu/chartevents.csv.gz`. Vasopressor use is extracted from `icu/medicationevents.csv.gz`. Mechanical ventilation is extracted from `icu/chartevents.csv.gz`.

### Temporal design

The index time is the 48-hour stabilization decision point. All covariates are measured at or before the index time. The outcome is measured after the index time. No future information is used in the covariates.

### Method comparison

The primary analysis uses a Cox proportional hazards model with the discordance phenotype as the exposure. The sensitivity analysis uses the same model with the urine-output-based discordance phenotype as the exposure. A propensity-score or overlap-weighted secondary analysis is prespecified to adjust for the strongest confounders.

### Falsification criteria

The study is falsified if:
1. The discordance signal is not present in the primary creatinine-based analysis.
2. The discordance signal is not present in the sensitivity analysis using KDIGO urine-output criteria.
3. The discordance signal is present in both analyses but is not robust to RRT stratification.
4. The discordance signal is present in both analyses but is not robust to severity adjustment.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive results

### Supportive results

The discordance signal is present in both the primary creatinine-based analysis and the sensitivity analysis using KDIGO urine-output criteria, and is robust to RRT stratification and severity adjustment. This would support the claim that the discordance phenotype is a genuine signal of renal recovery discordance rather than an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.

### Adverse results

The discordance signal is present in the primary creatinine-based analysis but not in the sensitivity analysis using KDIGO urine-output criteria. This would suggest that the discordance signal is an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties rather than a genuine signal of renal recovery discordance.

### Inconclusive results

The discordance signal is present in both analyses but is not robust to RRT stratification or severity adjustment. This would suggest that the discordance signal is confounded by RRT status or severity, and that the study cannot distinguish between a genuine signal and a confounded signal.
