# D2-T2: MerTK-inhibitor-associated fibroblast activation versus macrophage-linked protective state

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (D2-T2 seed)
Status: concrete hypothesis-stage proposal; no full analysis executed yet.

## Question and target

Does the observed association between MerTK-inhibitor treatment and a harmful fibroblast program persist when the comparison is restricted within donor and contrasts a macrophage-linked protective state with a non-protective or pathogenic state?

Named target/pathway: MerTK and the downstream MerTK signaling axis, interpreted as an intervention-associated pathway rather than a proven direct target. The experiment tests whether pathway-associated expression changes can distinguish reduction of a fibroblast activation signature from loss of a macrophage-linked protective signature.

Clinical importance: fibrosis treatments that suppress fibroblast activation may also suppress macrophage states that support repair or containment of injury. A donor-aware comparison can identify whether an intervention-associated expression pattern is compatible with benefit without erasure of a protective state, or whether it is indistinguishable from broad suppression. This could prioritize MerTK-pathway modulation for targeted perturbation and patient-specific safety review.

## Evidence versus untested claim

Inspected public evidence supports that macrophage–fibroblast communication and MerTK signaling are relevant to fibrotic injury and that MerTK inhibition can reduce fibroblast activation and profibrotic macrophage–fibroblast crosstalk in preclinical models [K1]–[K3]. These studies do not establish that the expression changes in the available GEO datasets are caused by the named intervention, that MerTK is the direct causal node, or that a protective macrophage state is preserved. The current datasets are observational/associational expression datasets; no causal or selectivity claim is permitted from expression alone.

The unresolved claim is specifically: among donor-matched fibroblast and macrophage-linked samples, the intervention-associated contrast will show a reproducible decrease in a predefined fibroblast activation score without a corresponding decrease in a predefined macrophage-linked protective score, while a donor-unaware contrast will be unstable or donor-driven.

## Population and comparison

Population: all samples in the three prepared views that have a donor identifier and a cell-type or condition field compatible with fibroblast or macrophage-linked states. Exclude samples without a donor identifier. Preserve source-specific sample namespaces; do not join across datasets merely because an ID matches.

Primary comparison: within each donor, compare the intervention-associated state (e.g., inhibitor or LPS-associated state, if the registry supports it) with the donor-matched baseline or control state. The primary estimand is the donor-averaged difference in a predefined fibroblast activation score and the donor-averaged difference in a predefined macrophage-linked protective score. The comparison is observational and is not a causal treatment effect.

Secondary comparison: compare the intervention-associated state with a macrophage-linked protective state and with a non-protective or pathogenic state, within donor, to test whether the intervention-associated expression pattern is compatible with preservation of the protective state.

## Estimand

The primary estimand is the donor-averaged difference in the fibroblast activation score between the intervention-associated state and the donor-matched control state, and the donor-averaged difference in the macrophage-linked protective score between the same two states. The secondary estimand is the difference in the fibroblast activation score and the macrophage-linked protective score between the intervention-associated state and the macrophage-linked protective state, within donor.

The estimand is an association, not a causal effect. It is not a treatment effect, a selectivity claim, or a proof of direct target action.

## Baseline versus learned alternative

Simple donor-aware baseline: a donor-averaged difference in the predefined scores, with donor as a fixed effect or a grouping variable for within-donor contrasts. This baseline is transparent and directly tests the primary estimand.

Substantive learned alternative: a donor-aware linear model that includes donor, cell type, condition, treatment, and time as covariates, and estimates the association between the MerTK pathway score and the fibroblast activation score and the macrophage-linked protective score. This alternative can reveal whether the association is driven by a specific covariate or by a combination of covariates, and can provide a more precise estimate of the association.

The learned alternative is not a neural network or a GPU-intensive model. It is a linear model that can be fit on CPU. The alternative is useful because it can reveal whether the association is driven by a specific covariate or by a combination of covariates, and can provide a more precise estimate of the association.

## Falsification criteria

The hypothesis is falsified if:
- The donor-averaged difference in the fibroblast activation score between the intervention-associated state and the donor-matched control state is not negative (i.e., the intervention-associated state does not show a reduction in fibroblast activation).
- The donor-averaged difference in the macrophage-linked protective score between the intervention-associated state and the donor-matched control state is negative (i.e., the intervention-associated state shows a loss of the macrophage-linked protective state).
- The within-donor contrast is unstable or donor-driven, i.e., the donor-averaged difference is not consistent across donors.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive outcomes

Supportive: the donor-averaged difference in the fibroblast activation score is negative and the donor-averaged difference in the macrophage-linked protective score is not negative, and the within-donor contrast is consistent across donors. This would be compatible with the hypothesis that the intervention-associated expression pattern is associated with a reduction in fibroblast activation without loss of the macrophage-linked protective state.

Adverse: the donor-averaged difference in the fibroblast activation score is not negative, or the donor-averaged difference in the macrophage-linked protective score is negative, or the within-donor contrast is unstable or donor-driven. This would be adverse to the hypothesis and would suggest that the intervention-associated expression pattern is not compatible with a reduction in fibroblast activation without loss of the macrophage-linked protective state.

Inconclusive: the donor-averaged differences are not significantly different from zero, or the within-donor contrast is not consistent across donors, or the sample size is too small to estimate the differences with precision. This would be inconclusive and would require additional data or a different analysis.
