# Provisional child hypothesis: is the eICU monitoring-gap signal a monitoring process or end-of-stay de-escalation?

Status: provisional and untested. This is a substantive design revision of [prior hypothesis], not a result.

## Scientific opening and proposed relationship

The parent observes a plausible opening: among adults who remain physiologically unstable, a reduction in laboratory-testing intensity might precede a time-local deterioration. The strongest rival is not a monitoring mechanism. Clinicians may reduce testing because the patient is recovering, is being transferred/discharged, is dying, or is in a low-intensity end-of-life care trajectory. In those situations, sparse end-of-stay documentation can make a gap look like a signal, while escalation or death may be a competing transition rather than a clean deterioration outcome.

The revised hypothesis is therefore conditional and falsifiable:

> In continuous adult ICU stays, after restricting to a prespecified “stable monitoring” context and explicitly modeling death, discharge/transfer, and documented end-of-life/de-escalation as competing transitions, a laboratory-monitoring reduction at time t will have a smaller and less reproducible association with new respiratory or vasopressor escalation in the next 12 hours than the parent association. A residual association only in patients with active support, no documented EOL/de-escalation context, and adequate documentation would support a monitoring-process interpretation; an association concentrated near discharge/transfer, death, or EOL documentation would support the rival.

This is an association hypothesis about a monitoring process, not a causal claim that increasing laboratory orders prevents deterioration.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use adult eICU stays with a valid unitdischargeoffset greater than 18 hours and a single continuous unit interval. The primary index times are hourly from 6 hours after unit admission through unitdischargeoffset minus 12 hours. Require at least one valid vitalPeriodic observation and at least one laboratory observation in the preceding 24 hours. Keep missingness distinct from zero testing.

Define a documentation-adequate context using only information available by index t:

- at least one vitalPeriodic observation in (t-6,t];
- at least one laboratory result in (t-24,t-12] or (t-12,t];
- no unitdischargestatus or unitdischargelocation indicating an imminent transition before t;
- no carePlanEOL row with cpleolsaveoffset or cpleoldiscussionoffset at or before t and activeupondischarge indicating an active plan;
- no infusionDrug or respiratoryCare record showing a prespecified support discontinuation in (t-12,t] unless it is explicitly retained in a de-escalation stratum.

The primary “active-support” cohort additionally requires a respiratoryCare record or infusionDrug record with a support timestamp at or before t and no documented support discontinuation in (t-12,t]. This is a process stratum, not a claim that support is causal.

## Exposure

At index t, use lab.csv.gz and count distinct analytes and distinct laboratory episodes in two half-open intervals:

- prior: (t-24,t-12]
- current: (t-12,t]

Use labresultoffset as the observation time. Do not count a revised result as a new test unless its result time is distinct; retain labresultrevisedoffset for a sensitivity analysis that distinguishes original-result timing from revision timing. The primary reduction indicator is a positive prior count followed by zero current distinct analytes. Secondary exposures are a >=50% fall from a prior count of at least two episodes and continuous log(1+current)-log(1+prior). A gap with no prior testing is not called a reduction.

## Outcomes and competing events

The primary outcome is the first qualifying new or escalated respiratory or vasopressor support in (t,t+12]:

- respiratoryCare.csv.gz: a new/escalated airwaytype, a new ventstartoffset, or a new highironoxlimit record after t;
- infusionDrug.csv.gz: a new drugname or an escalation in drugrate/infusionrate after t, restricted to a prespecified list of vasopressor/infusion names.

Do not use post-index laboratory values as the primary outcome. Death before a qualifying event, unit discharge/transfer before the event, and documented EOL/de-escalation before the event are competing transitions, not ordinary non-events. Use a cause-specific or Fine–Gray-style competing-risk formulation with the first event in the outcome window; report cumulative incidence for deterioration, death, discharge/transfer, and EOL/de-escalation. The exact death timestamp is not available in the listed patient fields, so death is handled through unitdischargestatus and apachePatientResult mortality fields as a secondary competing outcome, with the limitation stated explicitly.

## Rival-focused discriminating test

Fit the same prespecified landmark models in three contexts:

1. active-support, documentation-adequate, no EOL/discharge context;
2. explicit de-escalation/EOL context;
3. near-transition context (index within 12 hours of unitdischargeoffset, or a discharge/transfer location/status indicating transition).

Compare the monitoring-reduction coefficient and absolute risk difference across contexts, with hospital-held-out validation and hospital-clustered uncertainty. The rival is supported if the association is materially stronger in de-escalation/near-transition/EOL contexts and attenuates or disappears in active-support documentation-adequate contexts. A monitoring-process signal is supported only if a reproducible association remains in the active-support context, is not explained by the rival strata, and passes negative controls.

Negative controls:

- permute laboratory testing intervals within hospital while preserving stay length, documentation density, and outcome;
- use a post-index testing interval as exposure for the prior deterioration, which should not predict the earlier event;
- use a non-laboratory documentation timestamp control from respiratoryCharting or vitalPeriodic availability where available, rather than treating any charting absence as a clinical event;
- repeat the analysis after excluding the last 12 hours of each stay and after requiring two consecutive non-gap landmarks.

A residual association should be interpreted cautiously because unmeasured severity, interface interruption, staffing, and clinical intent remain.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is a discrete-time logistic model for the first qualifying deterioration, with competing-event censoring, hospital fixed/clustered effects, and the same index times, exposure, and context strata. It estimates the landmark association and uncertainty but loses persistence and the order of transitions.

The substantive alternative is a longitudinal recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard model with time-varying laboratory intensity, vital/respiratory/infusion trajectories, and explicit competing transitions. It can reveal whether the signal is a transient landmark artifact, a persistent trajectory, or a transition process; it is included because it tests the rival explanation, not because complexity or a small predictive gain is desirable.

Both models use the same available inputs and the same held-out-hospital split. Report calibration, discrimination, absolute risk difference, and uncertainty intervals. A future solver may fit these on CPU; GPU is not required.

## Interpretation and falsification

Supportive for the monitoring-process hypothesis: a reproducible held-out-hospital association in active-support, documentation-adequate, no-EOL/near-transition contexts; null or weak negative controls; and no concentration in de-escalation strata.

Adverse to the monitoring-process hypothesis: attenuation after explicit context separation, stronger effects only near discharge/transfer/death/EOL documentation, or failure of negative controls. This would support de-escalation or documentation as the leading explanation.

Inconclusive: wide intervals, sparse events, inability to distinguish interface interruption from clinical intent, or contradictory context-specific estimates. An imprecise null is not decisive refutation.

No result would establish that more testing causes better outcomes, identify a biological mechanism, or justify a treatment recommendation. Clinical adjudication of deterioration, direct interface-interruption flags, staffing/bed capacity, complete pre-ICU history, and reliable EOL intent are unavailable or incomplete in the verified bindings.
