# Episode 47: sex-stratified safety qualification of the frozen grip-after-FFM policy

## Lead decision and substantive repair

A child is justified from the assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent remains the correct aggregate experiment and is incorporated without alteration. This child repairs its most consequential unresolved decision weakness: the parent can call AFG1 beneficial overall and in observed-low-UACR participants even if switching from AF1 to AFG1 loses equation-defined cases in one sex.

This is not a hypothetical compositional concern. An outcome-blind audit of the immutable rosters shows that AFG1 replaces 31 AF1 participants with 31 others, but the replacement is sex-asymmetric: AFG1-only contains 26 field-31 females and 5 males, whereas AF1-only contains 14 females and 17 males. AFG1 therefore reallocates 12 of 466 assay slots from males to females. No outcome was opened. Since grip distributions are strongly sex-patterned and kidney information from creatinine versus cystatin can differ by sex, an aggregate gain cannot by itself justify retaining the pooled grip term for a prospective allocation study.

The repaired unresolved hypothesis is:

> Conditional on the entire inherited hierarchy supporting AFG1, switching from frozen AF1 to frozen AFG1 does not obtain its aggregate H1/D1 gain by a clinically material loss of detected H1 or directionally concordant D1 cases among either field-31 sex stratum.

The smallest nonzero roster event is 1/466×1,000 = 2.1459 per 1,000 assays. The noninferiority margin is therefore exactly -2 per 1,000 fixed assays for each sex-by-endpoint contribution: it is just smaller in magnitude than one observed event. Support additionally requires a nonnegative sharp contribution in every component. This provides a stringent sex-concordance qualification without refitting a sex-specific model or claiming broad fairness.

## Remaining nonidentifiability and next evidence

Even support cannot show that grip improves measured GFR classification, causes creatinine-cystatin discordance, identifies sarcopenia, or benefits either sex. The binary field cannot evaluate people outside its two recorded categories or gender-related workflow effects. The 31 swaps and visit-1 repeat-attender cohort may be too sparse or selected for transportable subgroup inference. Race/ethnicity and intersectional safety are not answered by this child.

Stronger conclusions require measured GFR; repeated clinically timed serum/urine measurements; verified collection/order/result/action timestamps; complete outpatient and kidney-replacement capture; chart/nephrologist adjudication; device/workflow/cost/harms review; external validation with broader sex/gender and ethnicity measurement; and a prospectively powered implementation or randomized testing-strategy study with prespecified subgroup safety and patient-centered outcomes.
