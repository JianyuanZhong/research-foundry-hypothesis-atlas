# Can laboratory observation behavior change ICU escalation decisions after clinical-state standardization?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must construct one leakage-safe six-hour eICU stay ledger and newly estimate whether laboratory-observation behavior changes a clinically consequential 10% escalation decision after standardizing to the same measured clinical-state distribution. It must also determine whether any process dependence is attributable mainly to (a) count/presence of testing, (b) timing/revision metadata, or (c) temporal alignment with a latent state, and whether it transports across hospitals.

The parent’s deployment estimand is retained unchanged:

`D_NB10 = [NB(B1)-NB(B0)]_development - [NB(B1)-NB(B0)]_heldout`,

where B0 is clinical-only and B1 adds the prespecified laboratory-process block. The substantive new estimand is the state-standardized process sensitivity at threshold 0.10:

- Freeze ten development-only strata of out-of-fold B0 risk, with cut points learned in development and applied unchanged to held-out hospitals. These are measured clinical-state strata, not biological truth.
- For each stratum k, estimate the paired difference in threshold net benefit between B1 and B0, `d_k = NB(B1)-NB(B0)`, separately in development and held-out hospitals.
- Standardize to the pooled development stratum weights `w_k`: `Psi_state = sum_k w_k d_k`.
- Report `Psi_state` and `D_state = Psi_state(development)-Psi_state(heldout)`, with patient-weighted and equal-hospital versions.
- Fit nested process blocks B0, Bc (count/presence only), Bt (timing/revision only), and Bct (both). Report `Psi_c`, `Psi_t`, and the prespecified two-way interaction on the net-benefit scale `Psi_int = Psi_state(Bct)-Psi_state(Bc)-Psi_state(Bt)+Psi_state(B0)`. These are decomposition quantities, not causal mediation effects.

Completion requires cohort flow and exact source audit; accepted analyte and measurement-system rules; duplicate, offset, and revision audit; outer split and ten-stratum audit; out-of-fold development and frozen held-out predictions for B0/Bc/Bt/Bct and matched S0/S1; the parent D_NB10 and threshold-crossing contribution; the new state-standardized effects and channel decomposition; hospital heterogeneity and transport; 500 whole-hospital paired bootstrap intervals; and a conclusion explicitly labelled supportive, adverse, or inconclusive. No model has been fitted in discovery and no clinical result is claimed.

## Unresolved question, evidence boundary, and clinical importance

The available evidence establishes only that this frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative lab-result and revision offsets, physiologic observations, hospital identifiers, admission severity fields, and documented ICU-unit-exit status that can be joined by the specified keys. It does not establish that a lab row records an order, clinician intent, test availability, turnaround time, assay comparability, or true physiologic missingness.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults still observable at ICU minute 360, does laboratory-observation behavior add decision-relevant information for documented expired ICU-unit exit by minute 2880 after conditioning on the same measured clinical state, and is that increment a portable patient-state surrogate or a hospital-specific documentation artifact?

This matters because a process-sensitive model can allocate escalation review or monitoring to patients according to a hospital’s testing regime. A state-standardized increment that is stable across held-out hospitals would indicate that process behavior contains reproducible information not represented by the observed values; an increment concentrated in timing/revision channels, high in hospital heterogeneity, and lost under cross-hospital transport would warn against treating local documentation behavior as patient risk. The result can inform feature quarantine, site adaptation, or additional data collection, but cannot establish that testing caused death or that an escalation policy improves outcomes.

The strongest supported claim before fitting is data availability and a computable noncausal design—not laboratory-practice dependence, mortality prediction, biological AKI, causality, or clinical benefit.

## Population, temporal boundary, and outcome

Use each eICU ICU stay as one observation; never concatenate later stays. Include `patient.age >= 18`; parse age values >89 as 90 and retain an age-censored flag. Require nonmissing `patient.unitdischargeoffset > 360`. The landmark is ICU minute 360. No predictor may use a row, revision, or derived quantity whose relevant time exceeds 360.

The primary endpoint is documented expired ICU-unit exit by minute 2880. Set event time to `patient.unitdischargeoffset` only when `patient.unitdischargestatus=Expired`. An alive unit exit before 2880 is a competing exit, not a negative expired event. A known-status stay with `unitdischargeoffset > 2880` is event-free at the administrative boundary. NULL/other status is unknown-status censoring, never recoded as alive. Exclude exits at or before minute 360.

For transparent prediction, the binary target is expired exit by 2880 among known-status stays eligible at the landmark. The temporal model uses the same target and retains alive exit as a competing state, yielding an expired cumulative-incidence function through 2880. This is documented ICU-unit exit, not exact biological death, all-hospital mortality, post-ICU outcome, or treatment harm. Any all-hospital-discharge mortality analysis is a separately labelled sensitivity and cannot replace the primary endpoint.

## Leakage-safe ledger and process channels

A primary lab row is available only if `0 <= labresultoffset <= 360` and, when nonblank, `labresultrevisedoffset <= 360`. Clinical values for each accepted analyte and vital are median, minimum, maximum, interquartile range, last value, and least-squares slope over 0--360; fewer than two points yields a missing slope. Development medians impute missing summaries without missingness flags. Vitals are `temperature, sao2, heartrate, respiration, systemicmean`. All accepted pairs, imputation, scaling, and clipping are development-only.

The process variables are partitioned before fitting:

- `P_count`: total lab rows, numeric lab rows, number of distinct case-folded analytes, 12 panel-presence indicators, and counts in [0,120] and (120,360].
- `P_timing`: first and last `labresultoffset`, median gap across distinct offsets, fraction with explicit system/interface, and count/fraction with nonblank `labresultrevisedoffset <=360`.
- `P_joint`: both blocks together. No process block contains hospital ID, outcome, discharge fields, or post-landmark rows.

B0 contains demographics/admission descriptors, the four admission severity values, accepted lab values, and vital summaries, but no process counts, presence indicators, missingness flags, gaps, or revision metadata. Bc adds only `P_count`; Bt adds only `P_timing`; Bct adds both. This makes the channel estimand auditable and prevents a generic “all missingness” claim.

## Matched temporal/latent alternative

S0/S1 use the identical cohort, accepted values, outcome, outer split, inner folds, and development preprocessing, with the same 12 half-hour bins over 0--360. Each bin contains imputed accepted lab and vital values and their temporal derivatives defined only from values available by the bin. S0’s latent three-dimensional state evolves from these value channels and admission covariates; its hazard heads receive only the state and admission covariates. S0 receives no lab masks, presence indicators, counts, observation times, gaps, revision indicators, or hospital ID. This constraint is essential: S0 is the state-only comparator, not a disguised observation model.

S1 adds the prespecified process channel `P_count/P_timing` as a separate input to the hazard/observation heads while retaining the same value-driven state. It may model observation behavior, but hospital identity remains excluded from deployment scoring; any development-only hospital random intercept is a diagnostic and cannot be used for individual predictions. Both produce expired/alive cause-specific hazards and the 48-hour expired CIF. Use fixed state dimension 3, deterministic initialization, regularization, and a development-fold stopping rule. Fit at least five deterministic initializations and retain the predeclared selection rule, never the run most favorable to the hypothesis.

The temporal model is substantive because it can distinguish a process signal aligned with state evolution from a marginal summary association. The state-standardized logistic decomposition is the auditable decision baseline; S0/S1 test whether temporal ordering and an explicit observation channel explain any residual process dependence. Both families must report the same threshold estimands, not merely predictive scores.

## Falsification and gates

1. Permute outcomes within development hospitals before fitting. AUROC should approach 0.5 and net benefit should disappear; persistence indicates leakage or split failure.
2. Within each hospital and B0 risk stratum, permute complete process vectors across stays while leaving clinical values and outcomes fixed. The patient-specific process increment should disappear. Persistence indicates leakage, overly coarse strata, or a process block proxy not controlled by the declared state strata; it is not supportive evidence.
3. Circularly shift each stay’s 12-bin process sequence by one bin in S1, preserving its process burden but breaking its alignment with the value-driven state. A large unchanged S1 increment indicates that temporal alignment is not explaining the effect; a large collapse supports alignment but not causality. This is a diagnostic, not an automatic acceptance rule.
4. Fit Bc and Bt separately. If only `P_timing` drives the effect, repeat the revision-conservative and revision-ignored analyses. A material reversal indicates unresolved result-availability semantics.
5. Independently rebuild exposure summaries from `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` in `vitalAperiodic` (schema `datasets/eicu/table-72ace5b89971196b.json`, schema [source checksum], source [source checksum]) only as a measurement sensitivity; never pool it with periodic MAP. If the conclusion changes materially, label it measurement-inconclusive.
6. Re-run exact vital deduplication, second split namespace, and `uniquepid` cluster sensitivity. A positive process conclusion must not depend on one split or duplicate rule.
7. Deliberately attempt to add `actual*`, `unitdischargestatus`, `unitdischargeoffset`, or any post-360 row to the predictor ledger; the pipeline must reject them before fitting. A post-360 probe is an audit only and cannot enter a result.
8. S0/S1 must pass convergence, five-start latent-state stability, hazard calibration, adequate paired support, and held-out transport gates. If not, report the transparent results and mark the temporal mechanism comparison inconclusive; do not select the favorable family.

## Interpretation and missing evidence

Supportive of a process-artifact deployment warning requires all of: a development process increment meeting the prespecified 0.005 net-benefit margin; a state-standardized held-out increment adverse by at least 0.005 or with a 95% hospital-cluster interval excluding zero in the adverse direction; adverse crossing-in versus crossing-out event composition; reproducibility of the direction in S1/S0, the second split, and revision sensitivity; and at least 75% of evaluable held-out hospitals showing nonnegative process-model transport loss. The parent D_NB10 and the new `D_state` must be reported together. This supports a deployment warning or site adaptation, not causality.

Supportive of a portable state-surrogate interpretation requires the process increment to shrink materially after S0 state standardization, the within-hospital/state-stratum permutation to remove the patient-specific increment, and the S0 decision utility/calibration to transport at least as well as S1. This is evidence that observed testing behavior may be a surrogate for unmeasured or temporally represented patient state; it does not prove the latent state is biological or complete.

Adverse evidence for process dependence is no material B1/Bct increment and no held-out transport loss, with stable null process-channel falsifications. Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 10 held-out hospitals or 25 events; inadequate state-stratum overlap; sparse or unstable accepted analytes; materially different revision/measurement sensitivities; wide intervals spanning both zero and the clinical margin; process-permutation failure; temporal non-identifiability; or discordant model families. No clinical deployment recommendation follows from an inconclusive result.

Computationally checkable claims include source hashes and headers, ordinary gzip status, joins, offset/revision filters, duplicate handling, analyte acceptance, split integrity, stratum cut points, feature ledger, model fits, predictions, threshold actions, CIFs, bootstrap intervals, and permutation/shift results. Clinical adjudication or additional data are required for lab order/intent/availability/turnaround, assay and specimen comparability, true physiology, clinician decisions, action costs, exact biological death timing, post-ICU outcomes, and whether any escalation helps or harms. The public release lacks these; expert review, prospective evaluation, and another database are required before policy change.

## Alternatives, selection, and compute

The transparent B0/Bc/Bt/Bct logistic family is selected as the primary baseline because its nested channel effects, state-standardized net-benefit contrast, crossing decomposition, and held-out transport are directly auditable. It uses the same inputs, target, preprocessing, folds, threshold, and uncertainty procedure.

The substantive S0/S1 alternative is retained because the baseline discards temporal order, state persistence, and process/state alignment. S0’s value-only latent state can reveal whether the observed process increment is absorbed by a temporally coherent clinical representation; S1 tests whether process metadata adds beyond that state. This is a scientific mechanism comparison, not a contest for a small predictive gain. It is deferred if the stated identifiability and calibration gates fail.

A generic boosted tree is not selected: it could expose nonlinearities but would not separate process channel from state and would make a predictive rather than mechanistic comparison. A transformer is deferred because the six-hour structured sequence is short and no evidence currently indicates that a larger representation is needed. Revisit either only if prespecified residual diagnostics show a clinically interpretable nonlinearity or the latent model is non-identifiable; do not replace the estimand with a more complex model.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted nested process models and a value-only latent state/observation model, producing `Psi_state`, channel components, `D_NB10`, `D_state`, threshold crossings, CIFs, transport contrasts, uncertainty intervals, and falsification outputs. Discovery fitted none of these and reports no fitted result.

Verified resource facts are the configured discovery limit of 7200 science seconds, two concurrent jobs, eight A100-SXM4 80-GB devices, and available cached CPU/GPU images.
