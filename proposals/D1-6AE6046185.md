# Does early ventilatory burden predict documented liberation failure or adverse respiratory transition?

## Scientific deliverable and clinical decision

The future solver must newly construct and fit an auditable, hospital-transportable eICU respiratory trajectory study. Its primary deliverable is an uncertainty-quantified estimate of whether early ventilatory burden, measured during ICU-relative minutes 0-360, predicts a later **documented ventilator-off transition by minute 2,880** among adult ICU stays that are documented as ventilator-supported at minute 360. The primary adverse respiratory transition is **documented ventilator-off followed by a documented ventilator restart within 720 minutes**. These are database-observable respiratory-care transitions, not adjudicated extubation, successful liberation, or patient-centered recovery.

Completion requires:

1. a frozen source/header/schema audit and cohort flow;
2. a minute-level/30-minute respiratory ledger with coverage, values, missingness, and transition provenance;
3. out-of-fold development and hospital-held-out predictions from a transparent scalar tidal-volume baseline and a matched mechanics-aware transition alternative;
4. standardized 48-hour cumulative-incidence contrasts for documented ventilator-off transition and competing unit death/alive unit exit;
5. a conditional 12-hour restart risk among documented off transitions;
6. calibration, Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC as secondary summaries, clustered uncertainty, and hospital transport;
7. prespecified falsification and documentation-process analyses; and
8. a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse, or inconclusive and linked to computed outputs.

Discovery fitted no model and establishes no clinical result.

## Strongest supported evidence versus unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported before fitting is only that this frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains time-stamped respiratory charting labels and values, respiratory-care offsets, ICU-relative unit-discharge boundaries, admission weight/height, hospital identifiers, and admission/severity descriptors that can be joined by the stated keys. Direct source-header inspection found 20,168,176 respiratoryCharting rows and 194 labels. Relevant labels include FiO2 (3,075,308 rows), PEEP (1,464,808), Tidal Volume (set) (1,034,549), Plateau Pressure (372,398), Pressure Support (371,161), Tidal Volume Observed (VT) (50,564), SBT Method (1,435), and RT Vent On/Off (60,101). A bounded full-file audit found 1,939 stays with a pre-landmark RT Vent On/Off supported state and 1,170 with all four primary mechanics labels (FiO2, PEEP, Tidal Volume (set), Plateau Pressure) in minutes 0-360. These counts are availability audits, not effect estimates.

The unresolved claim is whether a patient’s early ventilatory burden contains clinically relevant prognostic information beyond a scalar set tidal volume and baseline risk because the combination of size-normalized volume, plateau pressure/PEEP, FiO2, pressure support, and persistence of ventilator support identifies a distinct respiratory-mechanical trajectory. The competing explanation is that apparent liberation differences are driven by documentation intensity, unit length/exit, or hospital-specific respiratory charting rather than patient respiratory state.

Primary hypothesis: among eligible adult ICU stays documented as ventilator-supported at minute 360, higher early mechanical burden—prespecified as the joint pattern of higher valid set tidal volume per admission weight, higher plateau pressure and driving-pressure proxy (plateau pressure minus PEEP), and persistent high FiO2/support—will be associated with a lower 48-hour cumulative incidence of a documented ventilator-off transition and a higher conditional probability of documented restart within 12 hours. The hypothesis is only prognostic and observational. It does not claim that reducing tidal volume, plateau pressure, or FiO2 causes liberation or improves outcome.

Clinical importance: a transportable signal could support a future bedside review trigger for patients whose early support trajectory suggests delayed liberation or restart risk, and could motivate a prospective liberation-readiness study. A null result, or a result that disappears after charting-process and hospital-held-out falsification, would argue against treating a simple early tidal-volume measure as a portable liberation target.

## Population, time origin, and endpoints

Use eICU 2.0 snapshot `[source checksum]`. The unit of analysis is one ICU stay keyed by `patientunitstayid`; include age >=18, parsing age `> 89` as 90 with an age-censored indicator. Repeated ICU stays remain separate observations, but all stays sharing `uniquepid` are assigned to one split and a uniquepid-clustered sensitivity is required.

Time zero is ICU admission. The exposure window is [0,360] minutes and the landmark is minute 360. The primary at-risk cohort requires:

1. at least one `respiratoryCharting` row with `respchartvaluelabel = RT Vent On/Off`, `respchartvalue` in case-insensitive {Start, Continued, Suspended}, and `respchartoffset <= 360`;
2. the last RT Vent On/Off record at or before minute 360 is not Off/off;
3. the stay reaches minute 360 without a valid unit-discharge boundary before the landmark;
4. at least one numeric value in [0,360] for each primary mechanics label: `FiO2`, `PEEP`, `Tidal Volume (set)`, and `Plateau Pressure`; and
5. valid, positive admission weight in kilograms or a predeclared unit/value map sufficient to identify kilograms. If weight is unavailable or implausible, retain the stay for an unnormalized-mechanics sensitivity but do not use it in the primary size-normalized estimand.

The primary unit-observation boundary is `end_i = min(2880, unitdischargeoffset_i)` when `unitdischargeoffset_i > 0); otherwise end at 2,880 minutes only if the stay remains in the source with no earlier unit boundary. Do not infer respiratory observation after ICU-unit discharge from later hospital fields.

Primary outcome is the first valid `RT Vent On/Off = Off/off` at an offset in (360, end_i]. The primary estimand is the 48-hour cumulative incidence of this documented off transition before competing unit death or alive unit discharge/transfer/observation loss. A documented off transition is labelled **sustained-off** if no subsequent `Start`, `Continued`, or `Suspended` occurs in (off offset, min(off offset + 720, end_i)]. The adverse respiratory endpoint is the 12-hour restart indicator among stays with a documented off transition and adequate 720-minute observation; report restart as a conditional secondary estimand, not as proof of extubation failure.

A competing unit-death state is a unit boundary with expiration/death status or death location before an off transition. Alive unit discharge/transfer or unknown-status observation loss is a separate competing exit. Stays with no off record by minute 2,880 while still observable are administratively censored for the transition analysis and are also summarized as documented non-liberation by 48 hours; this distinction must be explicit. Report value maps for `unitdischargestatus` and `unitdischargelocation`. Do not call any endpoint extubation success, liberation success, ventilator-associated complication, or mortality benefit.

### Derived burden and trajectory

For each stay, compute:

- scalar baseline: median valid `Tidal Volume (set)` and its 25th/75th percentile over [0,360];
- size-normalized volume: tidal volume divided by admission weight, retaining the unit audit and excluding implausible weights;
- mechanics: median and maximum plateau pressure, median PEEP, plateau-minus-PEEP driving-pressure proxy, and pressure-support level;
- oxygen/support: median and maximum FiO2, proportion of bins with FiO2 >= 60%, and proportion of bins with a non-Off ventilator state;
- persistence: longest consecutive supported-bin run, number of support interruptions, and first documented SBT Method time;
- observed-versus-set concordance: absolute difference between observed VT and set VT where both occur in the same bin;
- charting process: number of respiratory rows, number of distinct labels, number of respiratory charting bins, and time since last chart at each risk interval.

The primary mechanics burden is a prespecified standardized composite of weight-normalized set VT, plateau-minus-PEEP, plateau pressure, and high-FiO2/support persistence, with equal-direction z-scored components frozen using development hospitals only. Do not use a post-landmark value to predict an earlier event. A secondary analysis estimates each component separately; collinearity, support, and missingness must be reported.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

Both approaches use the identical cohort, 30-minute risk intervals, endpoint definitions, splits, source-derived ledger, competing exits, hospital-held-out evaluation, and uncertainty procedure.

### Transparent baseline B0-B2

Fit regularized cause-specific pooled-logistic models at 30-minute intervals from minute 360 to 2,880:

- B0: age, sex, ethnicity, admission source/type, unit type, baseline Apache sensitivity variables, hospital context, respiratory-charting coverage, and baseline supported-state indicators;
- B1: B0 plus median set tidal volume and weight-normalized set tidal volume;
- B2: B1 plus the prespecified mechanics/oxygenation burden components and early support persistence.

The key baseline contrast is the standardized 48-hour CIF difference for documented off transition between the 25th and 75th percentiles of size-normalized set VT at fixed B0 covariates. The key incremental contrast is B2 versus B1. Also report conditional restart risk among observed off transitions. Use cause-specific hazards and Aalen-Johansen CIFs with unit death and alive unit exit as competing states.

### Mechanistic alternative M1-M2

Fit a piecewise-exponential multi-state model on the same 30-minute ledger with states:

0. ventilator-supported at landmark;
1. documented ventilator-off transition;
2. documented off with 12-hour restart;
3. alive unit exit/observation loss;
4. unit death.

Transitions from state 0 to 1, 3, or 4 use time-varying burden and trajectory segments; the transition from state 1 to 2 is evaluated only within 720 minutes and uses post-off charting/support records. An observation submodel estimates the probability of receiving an RT Vent On/Off or mechanics chart in each interval from prior charting intensity and baseline covariates. It is used for an observation-weighted sensitivity, not as a claim about an unobserved true ventilator state. M1 uses linear burden components and prespecified interactions (plateau-minus-PEEP by FiO2, and burden by support persistence); M2 adds a constrained latent state for low, intermediate, and high support trajectory, with monotone transition-time effects and no post-event inputs.

This alternative can reveal whether two patients with similar tidal volume have different liberation trajectories because pressure load, oxygen requirement, and support persistence differ, and whether the observed off/restart endpoint is partly an interface/documentation process. A scalar tidal-volume model loses those clinically relevant distinctions. The alternative is selected for that mechanistic decomposition, not for a small predictive gain. If sparse transitions or nonidentifiability prevent M1/M2 estimation, retain B0-B2 as the prespecified primary analysis and label the mechanistic comparison inconclusive; do not silently replace it with a more flexible model.

## Splitting, uncertainty, and falsification

Partition hospitals into development and held-out hospitals before label-map confirmation, value-range decisions, imputation, scaling, z-score construction, feature selection, or model fitting. Use grouped inner validation by hospital in development data. Keep all `uniquepid` stays in one partition. For held-out hospitals, use the training-population intercept and no hospital-specific fitted effect. Report hospital count, stay count, patient overlap, and minimum events per held-out hospital.

Use hospital-clustered bootstrap intervals for standardized CIF and restart contrasts, with a uniquepid-clustered sensitivity. Report calibration intercept/slope and Brier score for transition probability; AUROC/AUPRC are secondary and never determine scientific selection. Require a predeclared clinically meaningful margin of 2 absolute percentage points for the 48-hour CIF contrast, subject to event support and uncertainty.

Falsification and artifact tests:

1. Recompute the primary contrast using `respchartentryoffset` rather than `respchartoffset`, and using respiratoryCare interval concordance; a large reversal indicates timing/interface sensitivity.
2. Repeat with no carry-forward, 30-minute carry-forward, and complete-case mechanics; report how much of the contrast is explained by coverage.
3. Repeat using alternative exact label strata (`FiO2` versus `FIO2 (%)`/set-FIO2 labels) without merging them post hoc.
4. Test whether burden predicts respiratory charting density, SBT documentation, and RT Vent On/Off documentation before interpreting it as physiology. A strong process-only association with weak endpoint specificity is adverse for a clinical-mechanics interpretation.
5. Use a negative-control timing endpoint: respiratory charting in the final 60 minutes before an alive unit exit. A strong burden association restricted to this documentation window suggests impending-exit/care-process bias.
6. Refit after restricting to stays with >=8 of 12 mechanics-observed bins and separately to stays with invasive-airway concordance from `respiratoryCare.airwaytype`; these are sensitivity populations, not assumptions that airwaytype means successful extubation.
7. Repeat with unit-discharge boundary shortened by 60 minutes and with unknown-status unit exits censored rather than treated as alive exits.
8. Require direction and calibration to persist in held-out hospitals; report hospital-stratified estimates rather than averaging away heterogeneity.
9. Permute burden features within hospital and support-density strata as a negative control. Any comparable signal after permutation indicates leakage or process confounding.
10. For the restart endpoint, require adequate observation through 720 minutes and report the distribution of restart documentation; do not count absence of a restart record as proof that the patient remained extubated.

Supportive evidence requires a >=2-percentage-point standardized burden contrast for documented off transition or restart, directionally coherent component effects, uncertainty that excludes the null margin, preserved calibration/direction in held-out hospitals, and no disappearance under the main documentation/coverage falsifications. It supports a transportable database-observable risk signal only.

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversed contrast, instability after label/timing/coverage sensitivity, signal restricted to chart density or impending exit, poor held-out calibration, or a burden effect explained by support persistence and documentation process. This rejects the corresponding prognostic interpretation but does not prove low burden causes liberation or that ventilator management is safe.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few events/hospitals, sparse mechanics support, nonconvergence, collinearity of burden components, intervals spanning the 2-percentage-point margin, or inability to distinguish documented-off from observation loss. An inconclusive documentation endpoint must not be relabelled as evidence of clinical liberation.

## Unavailable clinical evidence and interpretation boundary

The configured snapshot does not establish extubation time, successful spontaneous breathing trial, post-extubation respiratory status, reintubation adjudication, sedation depth, neurologic readiness, cough/airway protection, clinician intent, ventilator mode semantics for every row, or complete patient-centered outcomes. It lacks raw ventilator waveforms and a reliable universal tidal-volume unit/weight timing audit. Plateau pressure and mechanics are present for subsets, but their clinical measurement context and plateau-hold quality are not adjudicated. `respiratoryCare.ventendoffset` is not used as the primary outcome because the source audit showed mixed/limited endpoint semantics. The analysis cannot establish treatment effects, optimal tidal volume, causal liberation strategy, or mortality benefit. Those claims require expert respiratory adjudication, richer bedside data, and a prospective or causal study.

The eICU documentation source read for dataset provenance is the frozen local readable extraction of the PhysioNet eICU Collaborative Research Database v2.0 page at `references/expert-seeds/papers/eicu-crd/page.readable.txt`; it describes a multi-center 2014-2015 ICU database and warns that interfaces differ by unit. A current Europe PMC search was frozen as source `[source checksum]`; it supports the broad clinical relevance of individualized mechanical-ventilation monitoring but is not evidence that this eICU hypothesis is true. No unavailable full paper or supplementary method is claimed to have been inspected.
