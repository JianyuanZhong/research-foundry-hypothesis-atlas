# Finite-population policy dominance across governance loss regions

## Decision question and substantive advance

The frozen decision is whether any exact MIMIC pulmonary-opportunity atom can be nominated for a prospective **within-site silent validation** of the inherited admission routes `AUTO`, `RECON`, `TIMING`, and `DEFER`. This proposal does not authorize an alert, copied recommendation, order, referral, referral scheduling, or patient-facing action. The estimand remains text-defined reproducibility and contamination, not guideline correctness, clinician performance, safety, benefit, follow-up, or causal effect.

The unresolved governance question is not whether one investigator's chosen utility threshold makes a route look preferable. It is whether, under every stakeholder loss-weight vector in a transparent predeclared region, one finite set of observable routing policies is provably no worse than another after accounting for route-specific active actionability, explicit supersession, unsafe contamination, missed opportunities, and bounded review workload. A policy that is optimal only for an arbitrarily selected point utility is not a release candidate. The substantive advance is a finite-population, partial-identification decision analysis: report joint adverse-compatible loss-difference bounds, robust dominance regions, and the set of policies that remain potentially optimal. Unknown or shared-wrong labels widen the feasible set; they cannot be assigned a favorable utility or resolved by extra unbounded adjudication.

Available evidence supports construction of a first-eligible, all-opportunity admission frame from linked radiology/discharge artifacts, reciprocal chronology, service/ICU workflow proxies, and stored timestamps. It does not establish that a document was viewed, finalized, physically available at departure, communicated, assigned to a responsible team, clinically appropriate, acted upon, safe, beneficial, or causally effective. The testable claim is narrower: given the inherited observable atoms and the fixed blinded actionability/supersession challenge, can any routing policy be robustly non-dominated over a predeclared loss-weight region? The primary hypothesis is that at least one support-qualified policy/atom pair will be robustly non-dominated in the full governance simplex while at least one less-conservative policy will be robustly dominated by a safer alternative. Both parts may be falsified. If no policy is robustly non-dominated, the correct result is governance indeterminacy and no nomination, not a new review layer.

## Observable outcomes reported before policy comparison

For each exact final atom, inherited route, and policy action, report design-weighted estimates and simultaneous adverse-compatible finite-population bounds for:

1. route-specific active actionability: the mass of units compatible with `A1_ACTIVE_PORTABLE`, and separately `A2_ACTIVE_RECONCILE`;
2. explicit supersession: `A3`, `S1`, `S2`, `S3`, and `S4` masses, with unknown/contradictory states retained;
3. unsafe contamination: for a propagation-permitting action (`AUTO` or `TIMING` under the locked route semantics), an admission is unsafe-compatible if any unit can be `A3`, `S2`, `S3`, `S4`, or `A6`/other unresolved adverse state; no favorable consensus narrows it. `RECON` and `DEFER` are not called safe; they simply do not incur the same direct-propagation indicator under this operational loss definition;
4. missed opportunity: for `DEFER`, an admission is missed-compatible when an eligible unit can be `A1_ACTIVE_PORTABLE` and no inherited exclusion applies; the observable claim is only text-defined missed action structure;
5. review workload: the number of fixed one-pass review packets required by each action (`AUTO` and `DEFER` use zero additional review; `RECON` and `TIMING` use their predeclared one-pass review unit), reported in packet and physician-time units from the frozen ledger, with nonresponse and infeasibility as adverse components.

These are separate estimands, not collapsed into an investigator-chosen clinical utility. For each policy, also report route discordance, support/effective sample size, missingness, challenge inclusion, and worst-admission aggregation. A policy never receives credit for downstream benefit, avoided harm, execution, or clinician acceptance, because those variables are not in MIMIC.

## Finite policy set and governance loss regions

Define a policy as a deterministic map from the exact pre-label final atom and inherited observable eligibility flags to one of `AUTO`, `RECON`, `TIMING`, or `DEFER`. To avoid an unbounded policy search, freeze the following finite policy library before opening labels:

* `P0_INHERITED`: the parent locked route;
* `P1_ALL_DEFER`: `DEFER` for every atom;
* `P2_SUPPORTED_AUTO`: inherited route except support-qualified atoms satisfying all inherited and actionability/supersession gates use `AUTO`, and all others use `DEFER`;
* `P3_SUPPORTED_RECON`: the same support-qualified set uses `RECON`, with all others `DEFER`;
* `P4_SUPPORTED_TIMING`: the same support-qualified set uses `TIMING`, with all others `DEFER`;
* `P5_ROUTE_CONSERVATIVE`: preserve the inherited route only for support-qualified atoms whose adverse-compatible unsafe bound is zero; otherwise `DEFER`;
* `P6_ATOMWISE_BEST`: the deterministic atomwise policy selected only after analysis by the predeclared dominance algorithm from the allowed action set, reported as a decision rule rather than treated as an independent confirmatory candidate.

`P6` may not add atoms, alter chronology, change gates, call an unlisted reviewer, or use an outcome to redefine the population. If the library itself is infeasible because an atom lacks positive challenge probability or required support, that atom is forced to `DEFER` for every policy and the unsupported policy comparison is reported, not repaired.

For each admission `i`, action `a`, and outcome-compatible world, form a loss vector in nonnegative observable units
`ell_i(a)=(U_i(a), M_i(a), W_i(a), Q_i(a))`, where `U` is unsafe-contamination, `M` missed-opportunity, `W` fixed review workload, and `Q` route/actionability uncertainty or unresolved-control burden. `Q` is reported separately and may be omitted from a stakeholder region; it is never silently treated as safety. The primary governance region is the full simplex
`R_full={w: w_U,w_M,w_W,w_Q>=0, sum(w)=1}`.

Before labels, also freeze a transparent display grid of rational weights with denominator 20 over `R_full`; the grid is descriptive only. Exact claims use linear programming over the full simplex, not the grid. A stakeholder-supplied region may be analyzed only if received and timestamped before label unsealing, expressed as a nonempty closed polytope `R={w>=0, sum(w)=1, A w<=b}`; the protocol must publish its exact inequalities and cannot tune them after seeing outcomes. If no stakeholder region is supplied, `R_full` is the sole primary region. No point weight, cost ratio, or numerical clinical threshold is asserted as authoritative. The units are explicitly governance bookkeeping units, and stakeholders would need to elicit their interpretation in a separate process.

## Partial identification and dominance estimand

For every sampled admission, retain the Cartesian product of all panel-compatible category sets. Let `Z` denote a finite compatible label world after applying only the frozen precedence rules. For policy `p`, atom `g`, and world `z`, estimate the finite-population loss vector with the inherited design weights; retain admission-level clustering and all-unit worst-case aggregation. Sampling uncertainty is handled by exact finite-population inversion for binary components and 20,000 Rao-Wu replicates with all admission labels kept together. Construct one simultaneous 95% max-statistic family over component totals, route-specific actionability, supersession, unsafe contamination, missed opportunity, workload, every pairwise policy difference, and all atom/policy endpoints.

For policy pair `(p,q)`, define the loss difference `Delta_pq(w,z)=w^T[L_p(z)-L_q(z)]`, where lower loss is preferred. Do not subtract independently maximized marginal intervals. Compute joint adverse-compatible bounds for every `w` and every declared region using the same compatible worlds, finite-population weights, and simultaneous calibration. Policy `q` **robustly dominates** `p` on region `R` only if the simultaneous upper 95% bound on `Delta_pq(w,z)` is strictly below zero for every `w` in `R`, with a preregistered numerical tolerance of exact zero only (no clinically meaningful margin is invented). Equivalently solve the linear-programming supremum over the region and compatible loss set; a nonnegative or unsupported bound means no dominance claim. If intervals include equality, classify as tied/indeterminate rather than choosing a winner.

A policy is **potentially optimal** on `R` only if it is not robustly dominated by any library policy and there exists a feasible compatible world and weight in `R` for which its lower-bound loss is no worse than every competitor's upper-bound loss after the simultaneous uncertainty set is respected. If that existence test is empty, label the policy `not-supported-as-potentially-optimal`; if multiple policies overlap, report the entire nondominated set. Report the normal-cone/weight subregion where dominance or potential optimality is certified, using exact polytope vertices and inequalities rather than a single score. A policy can remain potentially optimal only because data are too imprecise; call this `precision-limited`, not evidence of benefit. The algorithm must fail closed when positivity, challenge coverage, finite-population effective sample size, simultaneous precision, packet integrity, or control validity is unsupported.

Primary release output is therefore a table with one row per exact atom and library policy containing the five outcome families, joint lower/upper bounds, policy-pair dominance status over `R_full`, exact supported weight regions, potential-optimality status, workload, and first failing reason. Also publish a machine-readable compatible-world ledger and a deterministic LP audit trail. No policy is released merely because it has the smallest point estimate, because a boundary gate was met, or because a reviewer consensus was favorable.

## Falsification, fixtures, and interpretation

The design is falsified or returns no nomination if any of the following occurs: route is computed before label sealing; a packet leaks route, atom, stratum, weight, outcome, or another roster's label; an inherited control is made after labels; a route-invariant control flips; a route-decisive control violates its locked transition; a positive inclusion probability, join, reciprocal chronology, canonical DS, clock, or all-unit worst-case check fails; unsupported atoms are not forced to `DEFER`; a pairwise dominance claim depends on a point utility, a favorable unknown assignment, marginal rather than joint bounds, a post-label policy/library change, or an unbounded adjudication round; or the simultaneous upper bound does not certify the stated dominance region.

Required computation fixtures include shared-wrong labels (high agreement but wide unsafe-compatible bounds), pooled-safe/domain-unsafe atoms, nuisance route-invariant perturbations, one-field decisive perturbations, missing high-weight labels that widen rather than narrow intervals, unsupported signatures mapping to `DEFER`, latent-class nonidentifiability leaving primary bounds unchanged, duplicate restatement versus distinct-unit precedence, a policy tie at a simplex boundary, and a policy that appears best at one point weight but is not robustly dominant over the simplex. The verifier must test correct calculations with unsupported conclusions, including the erroneous claims that a non-dominated policy is safe, beneficial, clinically preferable, or ready for live use.

Supportive results mean only that a stated policy/atom is not robustly dominated and, where applicable, is potentially optimal for a published governance region, with all exact outcome and precision gates passed. They nominate a prospective silent validation of the exact observable signature; they do not establish utility, safety, appropriateness, execution, benefit, or causal effect. Adverse results mean an atom or policy is robustly dominated, has an upper unsafe/missed/uncertainty bound crossing the declared region's comparison, or fails a control; this excludes that nomination but does not prove patient harm. Inconclusive results include overlapping loss regions, no robust dominance, sparse challenge coverage, wide bounds, nonresponse, failed positivity, broken seals, or workload infeasibility; they are not evidence that `DEFER` is clinically safest or that review is beneficial.

MIMIC cannot establish actual viewing, signature or finalization semantics, physical departure visibility, communication, responsibility, patient preference, outside plans, execution of orders/referrals, scheduling, appropriateness, follow-up, malignancy, harm, utility, benefit, or generalization. Stakeholder loss weights require explicit governance elicitation; workflow burden requires prospective measurement; and any claim about safety or patient benefit requires expert adjudication plus prospective actual-workflow evidence. A separately approved prospective silent bridge must measure viewing, edits, signatures, transmission, responsible team, orders/referrals, scheduling, outside plans, review time, and outcomes before any live or causal policy claim.
