# Cardiac Work Versus Structural/Metabolic Cost

## Unresolved question
Among UK Biobank adults with cardiac MRI and repeat assessment, does lower LV ejection fraction and a larger LV end-diastolic volume at the same LV mass and workload indicate worse subsequent cardiovascular prognosis than the same measured cardiac output achieved with higher LV mass and ejection fraction?

## Clinical importance
LV ejection fraction and LV mass are common descriptors of cardiac structure and function, but they do not directly quantify how much work the heart performs relative to its structural and metabolic cost. A low-efficiency phenotype could identify patients at elevated cardiovascular risk who are not captured by ejection fraction or mass alone, informing earlier specialist evaluation and follow-up intensity.

## Evidence and closest work
The literature establishes that LV mass, ejection fraction, and chamber geometry each carry prognostic information, but the available abstracts do not establish that their combination identifies a distinct low-efficiency phenotype with incremental prognosis.

## Design
Use UK Biobank cardiac MRI fields for LV mass, LV end-diastolic volume, LV ejection fraction, and workload-related measures where available. Restrict to adults with repeat assessment and no prior cardiovascular diagnosis at baseline. Align measurements to the assessment date and follow from the later measurement for incident cardiovascular outcomes. Compare a simple adjusted survival baseline with a substantive learned temporal alternative only if it reveals information about workload and structural cost that the baseline loses.

## Falsification
A null or adverse association after adjustment for workload, structural cost, and relevant confounders invalidates the claimed incremental prognostic value. If the signal appears only in a selected subgroup or after post-baseline leakage, the result is inconclusive.
