# Temporal Hypotension Burden and Acute Kidney Injury Risk in Critical Illness

**Episode**: 7
**Dataset**: eICU-CRD (eicu)

**Parent**: None (restart - new direction)

## Hypothesis to Test

**Primary Hypothesis**: After adjusting for baseline severity (Apache III score), the cumulative temporal burden of hypotension (defined as systolic BP < 90 mmHg sustained for ≥30 minutes) is an independent predictor of acute kidney injury (AKI) development within 48 hours of ICU admission, beyond what is explained by minimum systolic blood pressure alone.

**Secondary Hypothesis**: The risk of AKI associated with hypotension burden is modified by the presence of vasopressor use, with a stronger association observed in patients receiving vasopressors.

- All adult patients (age ≥18) admitted to ICU with continuous hemodynamic monitoring

## Hypothesis Specification

### Definition of Hypotension Burden

**Hypotension Event**: A 30-minute continuous period where noninvasivesystolic < 90 mmHg

**Burden Metrics**:
1. **Duration Burden**: Total minutes of hypotension per patient
2. **Frequency Burden**: Number of distinct hypotension episodes
3. **Peak Burden**: Maximum duration of any single hypotension episode
4. **Weighted Burden**: Duration-weighted by severity (systolic < 90, 80-89, <80 mmHg)

### Definition of AKI

Using RIFLE or AKI-NET criteria based on creatinine changes:
- **RIFLE criteria**: Increase in serum creatinine ≥1.5× baseline within 48 hours OR increase ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours
- **AKI-NET criteria**: Increase in serum creatinine ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours OR urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours

### Primary Outcome
Development of AKI within 48 hours of ICU admission

### Covariates (for adjustment)
- Apache III score (saps3today)
- Age
- Gender
- Admission source
- Initial creatinine (baseline)
- Vasopressor use (yes/no)
- Type of vasopressor used (if applicable)

## Scientific Significance

### What This Addresses

1. **Limitation of Static Measures**: Most prognostic models use minimum blood pressure or mean arterial pressure as proxies for hypotension burden. This study directly measures temporal hypotension burden, which may capture clinically relevant information that static measures miss.

2. **Clinical Decision Making**: Understanding the independent contribution of hypotension burden could inform:
   - Early warning systems for AKI risk
   - Vasopressor titration targets
   - Monitoring frequency in high-risk patients

3. **Mechanistic Insight**: Hypotension may cause AKI through:
   - Direct renal ischemia
   - Activation of neurohormonal pathways
   - Immune activation

### Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

**Existing Approaches**:
- Static measures (minimum BP, lowest MAP)
- Time-weighted averages
- Simple duration counts

**Advantage of This Approach**:
- Captures both **intensity** (how low BP goes) and **persistence** (how long it lasts)
- Accounts for **recovery patterns** between episodes
- Can distinguish between **transient fluctuations** vs. **sustained hypotension**

**Key Question**: Does temporal burden capture information that static measures cannot?

**Rationale from Literature**:
As noted in Panwar et al. (2025) [K1], "inadequate RPP tends to overwhelm renal autoregulation capacity earlier than anticipated, leading to tissue hypoperfusion and increased risk of AKI." However, existing studies typically use static BP measures, which may not capture the temporal dynamics of how renal autoregulation is overwhelmed in individual patients.

## Method Comparison Rationale

**Scientific Question**: Does temporal dynamics of hypotension provide prognostic information beyond what is captured by static measures?

**Baseline Approach**: Using Apache III score and minimum BP as predictors
- **Pros**: Well-established, simple to implement, widely used in clinical practice
- **Cons**: May miss important temporal patterns; static measure may not reflect clinical reality of how renal autoregulation is overwhelmed

**Proposed Approach**: Using temporal hypotension burden metrics
- **Pros**: Directly measures clinically relevant information; captures both intensity and persistence; aligns with mechanistic understanding of renal autoregulation failure [K1]
- **Cons**: More complex; requires careful handling of temporal data; needs validation of temporal analysis methods

**Why This Comparison Matters**:
This is not merely a technical comparison of model performance. The fundamental scientific question is whether the *temporal pattern* of hypotension matters clinically. If temporal burden adds significant prognostic information beyond minimum BP, it suggests that:
1. Current monitoring practices may be missing important risk information
2. Treatment targets may need to be more nuanced (e.g., avoiding prolonged periods of low BP, not just avoiding extreme lows)
3. Early warning systems could benefit from temporal analysis

**Information the Alternative Reveals**:
- Whether transient hypotension (short episodes) vs. sustained hypotension (long episodes) have different risks
- Whether recovery patterns between episodes matter (e.g., does repeated recovery and re-hypotension increase risk?)
- Whether intensity and duration interact (e.g., is a very low BP for a short time worse than a moderately low BP for a long time?)

**Selection Rationale**:
The baseline approach (static measures) is well-established and clinically relevant. The proposed approach (temporal burden) is more aligned with mechanistic understanding and has the potential to reveal important patterns that static measures miss. This comparison tests a substantive scientific uncertainty about whether temporal dynamics matter clinically, not just a marginal predictive gain.

## Computational Design

### Analysis Approach
1. **Data Preprocessing**:
   - Extract patient-stay records from admissionDx
   - Join with vitalAperiodic for BP measurements
   - Join with lab for creatinine measurements
   - Join with infusionDrug for vasopressor identification
   - Join with apachePredVar for baseline severity

2. **Temporal Analysis**:
   - For each patient, identify hypotension episodes (30-min continuous periods with SBP < 90)
   - Calculate burden metrics (duration, frequency, peak)
   - Handle gaps in monitoring appropriately (e.g., use longest gap as minimum episode duration)

3. **Statistical Analysis**:
   - Primary analysis: Multivariable logistic regression
   - Secondary: Time-dependent Cox regression for AKI time-to-event
   - Model comparison: AUC, calibration plots, decision curve analysis
   - Subgroup analysis: With/without vasopressors, different severity groups

4. **Validation**:
   - Internal validation: Bootstrap resampling (100 iterations)
   - Hospital-level consistency: Check if results hold across different hospitals

- **CPU**: 4 cores, 8GB RAM (data preprocessing and statistical analysis)
- **GPU**: 0 (no machine learning model training required)

## Falsification Criteria

### Hypothesis Will Be Supported If:
1. Hypotension burden metrics remain significantly associated with AKI after adjusting for Apache III score (p < 0.05)
2. The coefficient for hypotension duration is positive and statistically significant
3. The proposed model shows improved discrimination over the baseline model (AUC difference > 0.02, p < 0.05)
4. Results are consistent across hospitals

### Hypothesis Will Be Refuted If:
1. Hypotension burden metrics are not significantly associated with AKI after adjustment
2. The baseline model (using minimum BP) performs as well as or better than the burden model
3. Results are inconsistent across hospitals (suggesting confounding by hospital practice)

### Inconclusive Results Would Indicate:
1. Weak or inconsistent association (p > 0.05)
2. Significant confounding by hospital-level factors
3. Need for additional data (e.g., renal perfusion measures, hemodynamic monitoring frequency)

## Limitations and Unresolved Questions

### Data Limitations
1. **Monitoring Frequency**: Not all patients have continuous monitoring; gaps in data may bias temporal burden estimates
2. **Artifact Handling**: Need to handle sensor artifacts and measurement errors
3. **Baseline Creatinine**: May not be available for all patients, limiting AKI-NET calculation

### Clinical Limitations
1. **Causality**: Observational design cannot establish causation
2. **Confounding**: Unmeasured confounders (e.g., infection severity, comorbidities) may affect both BP and AKI
3. **Generalizability**: Results may not apply to different ICU populations or monitoring practices

### Unresolved Questions
1. What is the optimal threshold for defining hypotension?
2. Does the relationship differ by etiology of hypotension (vasopressor-induced vs. spontaneous)?
3. How does hypotension burden interact with other organ dysfunction?
