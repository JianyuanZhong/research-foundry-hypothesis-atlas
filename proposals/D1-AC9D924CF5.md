# Admission-anchored early physiology and documented HCC pathway states

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly:

1. freeze the first-observed adult HCC-coded inpatient cohort, the exact pathway/procedure dictionaries, the assay list and all duplicate, unmatched, invalid-time and missingness rules;
2. construct the two time-ordered documented pathway windows P0=[0,24) and P1=[24,72), without requiring continued hospitalization or a later qualifying order;
3. assign every primary-cohort admission to one mutually exclusive observed-care state S, C, M, N or D at 72 hours;
4. fit a transparent competing-state baseline and an interpretable irregular longitudinal alternative on the identical patient split and targets;
5. produce locked held-out state probabilities, state-specific six-hour hazards or equivalent competing-risk probabilities, calibration, standardized lab-information contrasts and paired patient-bootstrap uncertainty;
6. test whether any signal survives observation-process, dictionary, timestamp, procedure-time and state-label falsifications; and
7. provide a claim-to-output table separating computational findings from claims requiring clinical adjudication or another study.

The scientific deliverable is a new fitted/estimated, admission-anchored competing-state analysis—not a reproduction of any demonstration paper and not a claim that the solver has established treatment benefit or clinical utility.

## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

The available evidence supports that this HCC snapshot contains encounter-level diagnoses, native-timestamped laboratory results, non-drug orders and procedure records. The parent design additionally establishes a useful observable question: whether the first-day record and subsequent record contain a documented pathway transition. The data do not establish that a recorded order is intent, recommendation, appropriateness or completion, and they do not establish pathology-confirmed HCC stage, tumor burden, resectability, performance status, complications, death, transplant or complete outside-hospital care.

The unresolved question is:

Among adults with a first observed HCC-coded inpatient admission and one clearly represented initial documented pathway family in the first 24 hours, does numeric hepatic/renal laboratory information observed in [0,24) provide reproducible information about the later recorded pathway state in [24,72), beyond admission/pre-admission context, the initial pathway record and early documentation intensity, when early discharge and absent later documentation are retained as competing states?

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

A worsening or coupled hepatic/renal laboratory trajectory in [0,24) is associated with a higher standardized probability of a documented pathway change or multi/unassignable pathway state (C or M) by 72 hours, and the lab-augmented model remains calibrated after adjustment for early observation intensity. It must not merely predict N (no later qualifying documentation) or D (early discharge). A scientifically useful irregular model should reveal stable, calibrated information about the competing state composition beyond fixed first/last/change summaries, especially when assay measurements are irregularly timed.

This is a recorded-workflow association hypothesis. It does not assert that physiology caused a pathway change, that a clinician intended a pathway, or that a change was appropriate or harmful.

Clinical importance and substantive advance

A pathway record that changes, becomes mixed, or disappears after an early physiologic signal is a consequential point for prospective review: it may identify cases in which pathway communication, repeat assessment or documentation capture deserves attention. A study that incorrectly treats only observed later orders as the denominator can confuse “no documentation” and “early discharge” with stability and can make a documentation artifact look like a clinical transition. The repaired estimand directly measures the joint composition of change, persistence, missing documentation and early discharge from admission.

The substantive advance over the parent’s primary analysis is not a more elaborate model. It is an admission-anchored estimand that does not condition on surviving locally observed through 72 hours or on having an assigned P1. It preserves the parent’s two-window documented-pathway question while making the competing observed states explicit. The irregular alternative is useful only if it shows whether timing, coupling and uncertainty in the irregular measurements carry information that the transparent summaries lose; a small predictive gain alone is not a scientific advance.

## Population and exact temporal eligibility

Use HCC snapshot [source checksum].

Construct the cohort from encounters joined to diagnoses on (patient master index, encounter number), normalizing only whitespace/encoding. Include:

- age >=18;
- a valid native admission time and discharge time;
- nonnegative admission-to-discharge interval;
- native admission in [2011-01-01, 2026-01-01) and native discharge before 2026-01-01;
- an inpatient encounter as determined by the encounter record/service convention, with the convention and counts reported; and
- at least one diagnosis name satisfying a frozen fitting-data HCC lexical rule.

The diagnosis table has no diagnosis time. Therefore “HCC-coded” means an encounter-level diagnosis match, not a temporally adjudicated onset or pathology diagnosis. Report diagnosis multiplicity and Diagnosis Type; do not use a diagnosis string as stage or disease confirmation.

Select the earliest eligible encounter per patient master index by native admission time, then encounter number. Do not replace an invalid earliest encounter with a later one without reporting that flow. Require at least one clearly represented P0 pathway family in [0,24) and no procedure with a known valid start in [0,24). A P0 order must occur before the encounter’s discharge time when discharge is before 24 hours. Admissions with P0=M/N, no P0, invalid times or known pre-24 procedure are excluded from the primary clear-P0 estimand but remain in cohort-flow and sensitivity reports. This is baseline ascertainment, not downstream conditioning on P1.

All windows are measured from native admission time and are half-open:

- P0 documented pathway window: [0,24) hours;
- P1 documented pathway window: [24,72) hours, truncated at native discharge for the later-order search;
- secondary procedure outcome window: [72,144) hours.

A procedure beginning in [24,72) is retained in the pathway audit and is an exclusion-bound sensitivity, not a primary procedure outcome. A known procedure start before 24 hours excludes that admission from the primary clear-P0 cohort. Missing procedure starts cannot be safely classified; report their frequency and perform the prespecified missing-time bounds.

## Recorded pathway states: no missing-window conditioning

Freeze a reviewable exact-string dictionary in fitting data from orders.Orders (non-drug), using native Order time for all state windows. Map only explicit, reviewable pathway terms to:

- E: therapeutic hepatic-artery embolization/interventional pathway;
- A: liver-lesion ablation pathway;
- S: liver resection pathway.

Routine nursing, diagnostic imaging, cancellations and unrelated services are not pathway families. Composite or unassignable liver-directed terms are retained as audit strings and can generate M; they are not silently dropped. Order Status, Order Duration, Frequency, Start Time and End Time are audit/sensitivity fields, never evidence of intent or completion. Deduplicate within encounter by exact order string and opening time, and emit pre/post-dedup counts.

P0 is E/A/S only when exactly one family is present in [0,24). If more than one family, a composite/unassignable pathway term, or no qualifying term is present, retain P0=M/N descriptively and do not silently call it a clear plan.

For each clear-P0 admission, search P1 only among orders in [24,72) that occur before discharge if discharge is earlier. Assign one mutually exclusive state:

- S (stable recorded pathway): later qualifying documentation contains only P0 and no composite/unassignable pathway term;
- C (changed recorded pathway): later qualifying documentation contains exactly one family different from P0 and no second family or composite/unassignable term;
- M (mixed/unassignable recorded pathway): later documentation contains multiple families or a composite/unassignable pathway term;
- D (early discharge without later qualifying documentation): native discharge is before 72 hours and no qualifying/composite later pathway documentation occurs before discharge;
- N (no later qualifying documentation): no qualifying/composite later pathway documentation occurs in [24,72), and discharge is not before 72 hours.

If a later qualifying pathway document exists before an early discharge, S/C/M takes precedence and the discharge is reported as an accompanying audit field; the state is not reclassified as D. Thus every clear-P0 admission with valid discharge has a state without requiring observation through 72 hours, and N is not treated as stability. Report all P0-to-P1 transitions, P1 state counts, discharge timing, order density, order statuses, missing times, duplicates and the fraction of P1=N and D.

The primary recorded-instability estimand is the joint state distribution and the event-specific contrast for C+M versus S, with N and D retained as competing states. The solver must also report the separate C and M probabilities and the contrast of C+M against N+D. This prevents an apparent “pathway change” signal from being accepted if it is actually a missing-documentation or early-discharge signal.

## Physiologic inputs and leakage controls

Use only numeric quantitative results from exact fitting-selected assay strings in labs with native test time in [0,24). The candidate assay set is exact-string albumin, total bilirubin, creatinine, platelets and one fitting-selected prothrombin/coagulation assay. The coagulation choice is made using fitting data only, frozen before selection evaluation, and reported with its exact string. There is no units or reference-range column: do not pool unlike assays, claim a validated liver score, or interpret raw magnitude as a clinical threshold.

For every assay, preserve native time, values, count, distinct-time count, missingness, gap/spacing, first/last value, change, elapsed time, and slope only when at least two distinct valid times exist. Normalize or robust-scale within assay using fitting data only. Same-assay same-time duplicates must be counted and handled by a frozen rule; report sensitivity to keeping the first, retaining the median or retaining all as repeated measurements. Values are not forward-filled across an unobserved interval.

Predictors may include only information available by the relevant prediction origin: age, sex, admitting encounter department, admission-era descriptors, diagnosis-type descriptors, pre-admission encounter/procedure summaries, P0, and documentation-intensity variables from before or within [0,24) such as early order count, distinct opening times and early lab-observation count/gaps. Do not use P1-window order counts, later discharge time, later procedure labels, clinical-document text, pathology text or post-24 labs as predictors. Early observation intensity is included symmetrically in the models and also examined by stratification; it is not a proxy for clinician intent.

## Models, matched comparison, split and uncertainty

B_base is a nested transparent no-lab cause-specific six-hour pooled-logistic state-hazard model for S/C/M/N/D. It uses the admission, pre-admission, P0 and [0,24) observation-intensity covariates above. It is an information-increment reference, not the principal physiological model.

B0 is the matched transparent baseline: the same model and records, adding prespecified assay summaries (first, last, change, elapsed time, slope where defined, count, distinct-time count, missingness, gaps and density). Use regularized coefficients selected in the fitting/selection buckets only. Report coefficients or marginal standardized state probabilities so the model remains auditable.

B1 is the scientifically substantive alternative: an interpretable irregular continuous-time coupled latent-state model using the same raw assay observations, patient covariates, P0 and early observation process. It has assay-specific offsets/noise, patient-level shrinkage, irregular-time updates and an observation-intensity component, with competing heads for S/C/M/N/D and the secondary procedure states. “Hepatic reserve” and “renal stress” are model labels, not diagnoses. The model’s scientific value is the possibility of identifying coupled movement, nonlinear timing and uncertainty under irregular sampling that first/last summaries lose. It must not use a more favorable patient set or target.

Assign all records by patient master index to fitting buckets 0–59, selection 60–69 and locked test 70–79 under participant-sha256-80-20/v1. Buckets 80–99 are inaccessible and are not external validation. Freeze dictionary, assay choice, state definitions, preprocessing, model class and hyperparameters before the single locked test. Use held-out multinomial Brier score, multiclass log loss, calibration intercept/slope and observed-versus-predicted state risks as primary evaluation. AUROC/PR-AUC are secondary and must not replace calibration. Report paired patient-bootstrap intervals with at least 500 resamples for:

- standardized P(C+M), P(C), P(M), P(N), P(D), and P(S);
- the B_base-to-B0 lab information increment;
- the B0-to-B1 representation increment;
- the C+M versus N+D contrast;
- procedure-family cumulative-incidence estimates; and
- subgroup descriptions by P0, service and admission era.

A prespecified relevance threshold is an absolute 0.01 risk-point lab information increment for C+M, with a two-sided interval excluding zero, but this threshold is not clinical utility. Support for B1 requires calibrated improvement and stable state contrasts beyond B0; a small Brier change without stable state interpretation is not substantive support.

## Secondary recorded procedure outcome

For every primary clear-P0 admission, irrespective of P1 state, construct the first native procedure start in [72,144). Exact-string procedure labels from procedures.Surgery are E, A, S or X (mixed/other/unassignable). If discharge occurs before the first qualifying procedure and before 144 hours, label D. If no qualifying procedure or D occurs by 144 hours, administratively censor. Use Start Time, never End Time; report Surgery Source, missing starts and duplicate rows.

This outcome is a secondary recorded-workflow endpoint, not proof that a pathway was completed, recommended or clinically appropriate. Report P0/P1 jointly with E/A/S/X/D. Analyze procedures in [24,72) as a prespecified sensitivity/exclusion bound; they cannot contaminate the primary [72,144) outcome. A separate analysis may include P0=M/N descriptively, but it must not be presented as the primary clear-P0 estimand.

## Falsification and robustness criteria

The solver must emit the following before interpretation:

- native-time leakage proof showing each predictor timestamp precedes the P1 state and secondary procedure window;
- lab timestamp permutation preserving values, assay identity, counts and patient membership;
- addition/removal of early measurement count, missingness, gaps, density and order-observation covariates;
- duplicate and same-assay/same-time handling variants;
- leave-one-assay-out and leave-one-order-family/procedure-family-out analyses;
- frozen dictionary variants reviewed without using the locked test to revise terms;
- permutation of P0/P1 labels within service/era and observation-volume strata while preserving order volume;
- negative-control workflow endpoints based on routine, central-line or venipuncture order families, where exact source strings support a prespecified dictionary;
- naive no-competing-risk analysis compared with the primary state analysis;
- midnight/boundary checks and missing procedure-start bounds; and
- sensitivity excluding any procedure beginning in [24,72).

A physiology-specific interpretation is falsified if the contrast persists under lab-time or state-label permutation, is driven by one order string, assay, service or era, disappears after early observation-process adjustment, or is unstable under duplicate/time rules. If B1’s advantage disappears under timestamp permutation or can be reproduced by negative-control workflow endpoints, it does not establish a physiologic signal. If C+M is not distinguishable from N+D, report that the data support only a documentation/observed-care association, not pathway instability specifically.

If fewer than 100 C+M and 100 S observations are present in the locked test, or if any state is too sparse for stable calibration, the primary result is inconclusive. Dominant P0=M/N, dominant P1=N/D, invalid times, inadequate numeric assay support, nonconvergence or intervals too wide to distinguish the relevance threshold are also inconclusive, not adverse confirmation.

## Supportive, adverse and inconclusive results

Supportive evidence requires adequate locked support for all relevant competing states; a prespecified lab increment for C+M meeting the 0.01 relevance threshold with an interval excluding zero; calibrated B0 improvement over B_base; separation of C+M from N+D; stability across observation-intensity, assay, dictionary, timestamp, service and era checks; and, separately, stable calibrated information beyond B0 from B1. Support would justify a prospective chart-review or documentation-intervention study to determine what the recorded transition represents.

Adverse evidence is a null or opposite C+M increment, poor calibration, a signal explained by observation intensity, dominance by N/D, dependence on one string or service, survival of label/timestamp permutation, negative-control association, or no stable B1 information beyond B0. Adverse evidence would argue against using this EHR pattern as a reliable recorded-pathway review trigger in this snapshot.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse states, missing or invalid times, inability to distinguish C/M from N/D, nonconvergence, inadequate assay support, or intervals too wide to distinguish the prespecified relevance threshold. Inconclusive results do not distinguish absence of association from insufficient ascertainment.

## Evidence boundaries and deferred alternatives

The source cannot establish pathology confirmation, stage, burden, vascular invasion, resectability, performance status, intent, recommendation, scheduling, reason for pathway change/discharge, laboratory units/reference ranges, complications, death, transplant, recurrence, progression, organ failure, mortality or outside-hospital capture. The local lexical extraction capability is unvalidated and limited; no clinical-document or pathology text is promoted into the primary endpoint because those tables lack native acquisition time. Images and waveforms are absent. The study cannot establish causal effects, benefit, harm or clinical utility. Expert chart adjudication and prospective or external validation would be required for those claims.

The natural-history demonstration supports evaluation of dated structured histories and held-out calibration, but its UKB/Danish validation setting is unavailable and this is not a reproduction. The Bayesian demonstration motivates an EHR-only irregular latent adaptation; its genetic inputs are unavailable and are not inferred here. The accessible cancer supplement supports modality-ablation thinking, but the main article/full STAR Methods and HCC images are unavailable; no multimodal claim is made.

A GRU/temporal-attention model is deferred because the immediate scientific uncertainty is whether interpretable irregular timing/coupling adds information beyond summaries after accounting for missing documentation. It should be revisited only as a new child if B1 residuals show structured timing error and the same competing-state estimand remains clinically meaningful. Text, image, multimodal and validated clinical-score branches are deferred for the documented time, image, unit and semantic limitations above.
