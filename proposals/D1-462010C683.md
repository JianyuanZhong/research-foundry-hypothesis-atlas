# Hypotension duration versus nadir for early AKI in eICU

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in eICU with a qualifying ICU stay and at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during the first 24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of new or worsening acute kidney injury during the next 48 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Clinical importance and unresolved question
A single nadir may be transient or artifact-prone, whereas sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved question is whether duration and trajectory add clinically meaningful information about subsequent AKI beyond the nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, measurement frequency, or treatment response. Resolving this could inform whether early AKI risk assessment should use duration/trajectory rather than a single minimum value.

## Evidence supporting the opening
The closest inspected work reports that duration/recurrence-based intraoperative hypotension trajectories were associated with major postoperative complications and improved prediction beyond conventional hypotension measures [K1]. A recent ICU AKI review emphasizes that AKI is heterogeneous and should be considered with urine-output trajectories, hemodynamic phenotyping, and contextual measures rather than as a single creatinine event [K2]. A methods paper warns that covariate-driven observation times in electronic health records can create spurious exposure-outcome associations if not explicitly modeled [K3]. These works motivate, but do not answer, the proposed eICU duration-versus-nadir test.

## Population, timing, exposure, and outcome
Qualifying population: adults (age >=18 at admission) in a single qualifying ICU admission with a valid ICU start time and at least one valid systolic BP <65 mmHg in the first 24 hours. Exclude records lacking the required admission identifier or with impossible/ambiguous timestamps. The primary exposure window is ICU time 0–24 hours; the primary outcome window is 24–72 hours, so the outcome is assessed after the exposure window is complete.

Define baseline as the last valid creatinine before or at ICU time 0 when available; otherwise use the first creatinine in a prespecified pre-exposure window, with missingness reported rather than silently imputed as normal. Define hypotension burden as cumulative minutes with systolic BP <65 mmHg in 0–24 hours using piecewise-linear interpolation between valid measurements, with no extrapolation beyond the first/last observed measurement. Report sensitivity analyses using no interpolation, a shorter interpolation gap, and adjustment for measurement count/observation intensity. Exclude or flag physiologically implausible values using a prespecified range and do not let a single artifact dominate the exposure.

Primary outcome: new or worsening AKI by KDIGO-compatible creatinine criteria, assessed from timestamped creatinine in 24–72 hours: creatinine increase >=0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours from baseline, or >=1.5 times baseline if the rise is documented within the outcome window. Use the earliest qualifying event and retain time to event. A secondary urine-output AKI outcome is included only if `intakeOutput` item semantics, weight, and timing are verified; otherwise report it as unavailable rather than substitute a proxy.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative
The simple baseline is a prespecified hospital-stratified logistic model using nadir systolic BP in 0–24 hours, age, sex, baseline creatinine, and available baseline severity/vasopressor context. The substantive time-aware alternative adds cumulative hypotension minutes, time below threshold in prespecified intervals, last BP before window end, and a simple recovery/trajectory feature, while retaining nadir. The alternative can reveal whether sustained exposure, recovery after the nadir, or observation density carries information that a single minimum discards. It is scientifically useful only if those features are stable and not merely proxies for measurement intensity or treatment; otherwise the baseline remains preferred.

Evaluate both models on the same rows, with a hospital-held-out split and the same outcome definition and uncertainty procedure. Report duration effect estimates and confidence intervals, calibration, and hospital-held-out discrimination as secondary descriptive outputs. Compare direction and uncertainty across hospitals. A complex sequence model is deferred because the available data cannot distinguish hypotension-mediated kidney injury from severity, treatment, and observation artifacts; revisit it only if a bounded diagnostic shows that trajectory information is materially unavailable to the simpler representation.

## Falsification and interpretation
The hypothesis is supported if duration adds reproducible information beyond nadir with a prespecified direction and uncertainty, and the result is not explained solely by observation density or a single hospital. It is adverse if duration adds no information, reverses after prespecified adjustment, or is confined to artifact-prone measurements. It is inconclusive if AKI ascertainment, timestamp validity, or sampling is too sparse to estimate the comparison reliably. Any causal interpretation, treatment threshold, or mechanism requires additional clinical adjudication, richer treatment/etiology data, and prospective or quasi-experimental evidence.

## Scientific deliverable and limitations
The actual deliverable is a newly fitted baseline and time-aware analysis with a frozen cohort, exposure/outcome timing, uncertainty, hospital-held-out evaluation, and interpretation tied to computed outputs. No full study result has been computed in discovery. Clinical adjudication of AKI, reasons for missing measurements, interface interruptions, discharge/transfer, and end-of-life treatment limitations are not assumed available.
