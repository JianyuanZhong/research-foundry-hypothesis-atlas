# HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B validation with a verified discharge/transfer availability audit and fail-closed downstream care-process bridge

## Targeted successor

This is a substantive two-parent successor of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the frozen adult HCC repeat-TACE population, repeat-event construction, assay-specific B/P/event/Y clocks, annual forward validation, locked nested ridge estimand, outcome-blind support funnel, assay-separated training-range partial-identification bounds, fixed-prediction patient bootstrap, and strict noncausal limits. It does not change cohort membership, event selection, model covariates, fitted rows, endpoint, denominator, or uncertainty target.

The clinically important remaining gap is whether the post-event recording process can be interpreted as a decision-relevant care signal. Before adding any such signal, this proposal performs a source-availability audit of discharge, death, transfer, and downstream records. The source audit is itself falsifiable: the analysis must demonstrate which fields are present, which have valid timestamps, and which can be ordered relative to event, Y deadline, and downstream windows. It must fail closed rather than treating a nominal table or a narrative field as an outcome.

The primary question remains exactly:

> Among selected repeat-TACE HCC episodes with remote baseline assay B and contemporaneous pre-repeat assay P, does P improve held-out prediction of subsequent same-assay laboratory value Y beyond B and the locked pre-event core?

The added operational question is narrower:

> Conditional on a BP-eligible episode, does assay observation and later *recorded* system activity vary by an outcome-blind, temporally valid care-record state, including the selected encounter's recorded discharge timing, and can this be distinguished from the absence of any time-valid transfer/death field?

This can establish only a reproducible recording/availability pattern. It cannot establish discharge disposition, death, transfer of care, completed assay, treatment delivery, clinical appropriateness, patient-important outcome, utility, or benefit.

## Evidence boundary and unresolved claim

The retained full-source audit supports feasibility of a selected short-horizon prognostic comparison and an outcome-blind observation audit. It found one locally supported 2022 block but severe later Y loss and sparse systemic-record support. It does not establish robust incremental prediction, missing-at-random observation, assay clinical interpretability, transport, or benefit. The parents' partial-identification calculations are design contracts unless and until downstream execution produces results.

The unresolved claim tested here is not that discharge or a care record causes assay completion. It is that the observable record process can be described without confusing administrative closure, death, transfer, or outside care. A useful result would show which states are actually identifiable: for example, an episode with a valid recorded discharge before the Y deadline, an episode remaining in the selected encounter through the deadline, or an additional time-valid encounter after the deadline. A null or contradictory pattern is equally informative and must not be promoted to equivalence of care.

## Availability audit: what can and cannot be called discharge, death, transfer, or care

Run this audit before constructing any downstream care result and serialize an exact per-field availability table containing source, table, column, nonempty count, valid timestamp count, min/max valid timestamp, duplicate-key count, and semantic status.

1. `encounters.Discharge Time` is available and time-valid only when it parses as a finite timestamp and is not earlier than the linked `Admission Time` when both are valid. It defines `recorded_discharge_time` for that encounter. It is an administrative encounter-close timestamp, not a discharge disposition, death indicator, alive status, or proof of complete care.
2. `encounters.encounter time` is the timestamp for an encounter record. An additional encounter in a downstream window is `recorded_encounter_capture`; it is not a completed visit or follow-up.
3. There is no configured death field or death table. A missing encounter after an event is not death. A document text field, including `discharge status` or `discharge diagnosis`, is not mined for death or outcome and has no native time.
4. `transfers` is identifier-only. It cannot provide transfer timing, destination, disposition, or evidence that a patient transferred. Any nominal key match is reported only as `transfer_payload_unavailable`, never as a transfer event.
5. The only downstream time-valid recorded proxies are encounter `visit time`; lab `test time`; examination `start time`; order `order time`/`start time`/`end time`; medication `start time`/`end time`; and procedure `start time`/`end time`. They are source-record timestamps, not proof of completion, administration, indication, or benefit.
6. Documents have no native timestamp. A nonempty document linked to an additional encounter can be labeled `encounter_timed_document_presence` only using that encounter's `encounter time`; it cannot establish when the document was authored or when care occurred.

The audit must fail closed if a required header is absent, a schema/source hash mismatches, a timestamp parser changes the declared valid-count semantics, or a supposed death/transfer/disposition field is inferred from text or an identifier-only table.

## Outcome-blind discharge and opportunity states

For every assay-specific BP-eligible event, define deadline `D = event_time + 72 hours` and build these states without inspecting target Y values, target-lab rows, or downstream rows after D:

* `discharge_before_event`: selected encounter has valid discharge time < event time;
* `discharge_in_Y_window`: valid selected-encounter discharge time in (event time,D];
* `discharge_after_Y_window`: valid selected-encounter discharge time > D;
* `discharge_missing_or_invalid`: no valid selected-encounter discharge timestamp.

These states are administrative descriptors, not clinical outcomes and do not exclude or censor BP rows. A discharge timestamp in the Y window does not prove that Y was impossible; a discharge after D does not prove inpatient care; missing discharge does not imply death, transfer, or ongoing care. Report counts and completion fractions by state only after states are frozen. Preserve the inherited mutually exclusive pre-Y opportunity strata (`exam+followup`, `exam_only`, `followup_only`, `document_only`, `none`) using valid examination/encounter timing and presence-only documents; do not replace them with discharge states.

Also report `nominal_transfer_key_present` versus `transfer_payload_unavailable` from the identifier-only transfer table, but never stratify as if transfer occurred. Report `death_status = unavailable` globally and by block; never create a death-negative category.

## Leakage-safe downstream recorded-care audit

For all BP rows, after Y selection/attachment is complete but without using downstream rows in any model or bound, define two disjoint windows:

* D1 = (event_time + 72 hours, event_time + 7 calendar days);
* D2 = [event_time + 7 calendar days, event_time + 30 calendar days].

The first interval is open at the Y deadline and the second begins at the exact D1 boundary. For each source and window, report the source-specific timestamp, first valid time, row count, and binary `recorded_*` flag. Use additional encounter keys only for encounter capture; the selected event encounter is excluded.

* `recorded_encounter_capture`: an additional encounter with valid `Encounter Time` in the window.
* `recorded_lab_capture`: any linked lab row with valid `Test time`; separately report exact albumin and exact total bilirubin rows, with no pooling and no requirement that a generic row be finite.
* `recorded_exam_capture`: an examination row with valid `Start Time`; narrative result fields are not read.
* `recorded_order_capture`: an order row with valid `order time`, `start time`, or `end time`, using fixed precedence for the first timestamp; this is a recorded order only.
* `recorded_medication_capture`: medication row with valid start or end time; recorded medication only, not administration.
* `recorded_procedure_capture`: procedure row with valid start or end time; recorded procedure only, not delivery or intent.
* `recorded_document_capture`: nonempty document row linked to an additional encounter with valid encounter `Encounter Time` in the window; it is encounter-timed presence, not a document timestamp.
* `recorded_any_capture`: OR of the above; always retain component flags and component count, and never call the OR a clinical endpoint.

All source rows are deduplicated before counts. Do not use these rows to fill B, P, or Y. A missing row means no record observed under that source definition, not no care.

### Administrative horizon and discharge-aware validity

For every source and event, calculate whether `event_time+30 days` is no later than that source's maximum valid timestamp and whether the source has a valid timestamp field. Call this necessary condition `source_horizon_reaches_window`; it is not proof of patient-level complete follow-up. If false, mark that source/window `administratively_incomplete` and do not report absence as a zero. The global maximum timestamp is only a snapshot-level coverage check; it cannot establish capture of external care.

Discharge timing is used only as an outcome-blind descriptive state and a stratification variable. It must not censor, reweight, impute, or alter source-horizon status. A discharge is not a substitute for an encounter, transfer, or death. Transfer identifiers and discharge fields are never combined into a synthetic disposition variable.

For each assay, origin/block, arm, Y-observed state, inherited opportunity stratum, and discharge state, report N, valid source-horizon N, recorded fraction among administratively observable episodes, and Wilson 95% interval. Mark cells below five and require at least ten BP rows for a formal between-state contrast. Keep all BP rows in the denominator for primary partial identification. For source-incomplete cells report `inconclusive/administratively_incomplete`, not no-care. Use only descriptive two-sided risk differences and prevalence ratios when defined; no adjustment, weighting, mediation, or causal wording.

## Falsification, baselines, and fail-closed interpretation

The audit has prespecified baselines: (a) the overall and arm-specific Y-completion funnel without discharge/care stratification; (b) inherited exam/follow-up/document opportunity strata; (c) discharge-state-specific completion fractions; and (d) source-specific downstream recording fractions with all-BP denominators. A care-process result is not compared against an invented expected clinical rate.

The compiler/verifier must test synthetic and boundary fixtures for:

1. discharge exactly at event, event+72 hours, event+7 days, and event+30 days under the declared open/closed rules;
2. Y exactly at event and event+72 hours, proving Y and downstream D1 cannot overlap;
3. an identifier-only transfer row never becoming a timed transfer;
4. absence of all encounters never becoming death;
5. document text (including `discharge status`) never becoming a timestamped death/discharge outcome;
6. malformed or pre-admission discharge timestamps becoming invalid descriptors, not exclusions;
7. source horizon failure producing administrative incompleteness, not a no-care zero;
8. selected encounter exclusion from additional-encounter capture;
9. generic lab, order, medication, exam, and procedure rows remaining recorded proxies rather than target assay completion or treatment administration;
10. albumin and bilirubin having separate rows, denominators, windows, and summaries;
11. all-BP denominator preservation and no downstream variable entering model fits, bounds, bootstrap, or event selection; and
12. positive, adverse, selection-sensitive, and inconclusive branches.

Interpretation precedence is strict:

* `unsupported` if source/schema/hash/header/clock/join assertions fail, or any unavailable death/transfer field is silently inferred;
* `inconclusive` if BP/arm gates fail, cells are sparse, predictions/range/SD/bootstrap are non-estimable, or source horizon is incomplete;
* `adverse_primary` if the adequately supported frozen primary gain is nonpositive, repeated M1 degradation occurs, or the global missing-Y bound is wholly nonpositive; no downstream recording pattern rescues it;
* `selection_sensitive` if observed-case gain is favorable but the global bound crosses zero, observation/discharge gradients are material, source patterns disagree, or calendar/arm heterogeneity is strong;
* `supported_recording_description` only for a finite, gate-passing, reproducible component vector with complete administrative horizons. It remains a recording result, not support for benefit, completed care, or actionability.

A larger downstream record fraction among Y-missing episodes may indicate delayed testing, in-system reconciliation, or a recording difference. A smaller fraction may indicate no captured contact, outside care, or incomplete source coverage. The data cannot distinguish these mechanisms. A discharge-state contrast cannot be called a mortality, readmission, safety, or outcome contrast. A null contrast cannot prove equivalent care.

For the frozen primary result, supportive language remains allowed only when the inherited observed-case estimand is estimable, support gates pass, no material observation warning exists, and the global all-BP Delta interval is strictly positive throughout the declared training-range scenario. Even then the claim is only selected short-horizon prognostic association in that calendar mixture. A positive recording audit is appended as an operational descriptor and cannot alter that label.
