# Provisional HCC trajectory hypothesis (episode 4)

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with an exact documented hepatocellular carcinoma diagnosis and an inpatient encounter in the HCC snapshot, a prespecified 7-day time-aware representation of liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker laboratory measurements may add held-out information about a subsequent inpatient escalation-of-care proxy beyond a static admission-baseline laboratory snapshot, age/sex, admission department, treatment/procedure indicators, and documentation intensity. The direction and magnitude are not assumed; this is not a causal or clinical-harm claim.

## Why it matters
A trajectory could distinguish evolving decompensation from a stable high-risk baseline and could inform whether an early monitoring signal merits expert review. The strongest claim currently supported is only that the local HCC files contain longitudinal encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, procedure, medication, and document fields that can be joined by the documented patient/admission keys. It does not establish that any trajectory predicts deterioration, that the proxy is valid, or that a treatment effect exists.

Join all detail tables to encounters on (`患者主索引`,`就诊号`), preserving source-specific identifiers and never joining across datasets. Define admission time from `入院时间`, restrict to encounters with valid admission and discharge times, and use a prespecified post-baseline outcome window. The primary outcome is a procedure-derived escalation proxy; its dictionary and clinical validity must be frozen before model fitting. Secondary outcomes are transfer and discharge-document indicators only if semantics are verified.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative
Baseline: logistic regression or a prespecified penalized model using admission snapshot labs, demographics, department, procedure/medication indicators, and documentation intensity. Alternative: a time-aware model (for example, grouped trajectory features or a regularized sequence model) using lab values and timestamps from admission through day 7, with the same covariates and held-out patient-level split. Compare calibration, discrimination, and decision-curve/net-benefit at prespecified thresholds with bootstrap confidence intervals. The alternative can reveal persistence, slope, and cross-marker ordering that a snapshot discards; it does not establish mechanism.

## Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: the time-aware model has a prespecified, statistically and clinically meaningful improvement in held-out performance and calibration, with stable direction across bootstrap and subgroup checks, and the improvement is not explained by documentation intensity or a small number of assays. Adverse: no improvement, worse calibration, or improvement only after leakage/documentation artifacts, weakening the trajectory claim. Inconclusive: too few events, unresolved assay units, unstable outcome dictionary, or wide uncertainty; this motivates adjudication or another dataset rather than refutation.

## Missing evidence and limits
The local catalog does not provide validated death, ICU admission, clinical deterioration adjudication, or assay-unit/reference-range metadata. Procedure-derived escalation is a proxy and may reflect treatment decisions, case severity, or documentation practice. Exact cohort counts, event support, assay mapping, units, and the three inspected key references are not yet established here. A full study must report these gates and must not claim clinical harm or causality without adjudication and additional data.
