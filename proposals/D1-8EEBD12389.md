## 1. Unresolved question

After circulatory stabilization in sepsis/septic shock, some patients recover kidneys and lungs
together while others recover one organ bed while the other stays impaired ("asynchronous organ
recovery"). Does **cross-organ recovery asynchrony** at a pre-specified decision time t* carry
prognostic information **beyond the single-snapshot severity of the same patient at t***?

## 2. Observed vs conjectured
- Observed [K3]: sepsis = static >=2-point SOFA rise; septic shock = vasopressor need + lactate > 2 mmol/L. Snapshots define the syndrome but say nothing about inter-organ recovery asynchrony.
- Observed [K1]: acute inflammatory recovery follows a universal trajectory (exponential WBC decay, delayed platelet recovery) across trauma/ischaemia/infection. Supports "recovery is a dynamic, informative process"; not shown at organ level in ICU sepsis.
- Observed [K2]: latent trajectory classes over longitudinal temperature in MIMIC-IV/eICU improve discrimination beyond single measurements. Closest prior work; still does not test whether *asynchrony between two organ beds* adds information over a snapshot at t*.
- Conjecture to test (unsupported): asynchrony is a distinct predictive state, not merely "sicker at t*".

## 3. Clinical importance
Bedside de-escalation is currently snapshot-driven ("SOFA/numbers look better"). If asynchrony predicts 7-day deterioration independent of snapshot severity, de-escalation should weigh *which* organ recovered. If it does not, the seed's premise is refuted — a useful negative that spares trajectory-monitoring effort.

## 7. Baselines and the substantive alternative
- Baseline (simple, primary falsification test): snapshot severity at t* only — SOFA(t*), lactate(t*), vasopressor dose(t*), age, ICD comorbidity count (`hosp/diagnoses_icd.csv.gz`) — penalized logistic/Cox.
- Substantive alternative: add the two-axis recovery state + asynchrony indicator, estimated with time-varying-covariate Cox or a small GRU over longitudinal vitals/labs on the same inputs/split. Information the baseline loses: whether the *pattern* of organ recovery, not its level, carries prognosis.
- Discriminating question: does asynchrony add discrimination/calibration beyond SOFA(t*)? Simplicity preferred unless that question is tested.

## 8. Analysis and falsification
Temporal split: fit 2008-2016 by `admittime`, hold out 2017-2019. Prespecified covariates; adjusted HR and AUROC with bootstrap CIs; incremental value by delta-AUROC and LR test vs baseline. Negative control: permuted asynchrony labels (should give null).
- Supportive: asynchrony improves delta-AUROC CI excluding 0 and HR CI excluding 1 vs baseline, permuted control null.
- Adverse: no incremental value / HR ~1 — refutes the seed's premise.
- Inconclusive: wide CIs, sparse events, or confounding by measurement density.
