# HCC longitudinal recurrence/progression proposal (evolved)

## Status
Lead working draft after evolving [prior hypothesis] and inspecting a bounded feasibility scan. This is a proposal, not a completed scientific result.

## Question
Among patients with a dated first recorded HCC-related diagnosis or HCC-directed procedure, does a longitudinal pattern in routine liver-related laboratory values and HCC-directed treatment events during a pre-index window predict a subsequent recorded HCC-related event within 365 days, beyond what is captured by the latest available lab values and encounter intensity?

## Why it matters
HCC recurrence and progression can change surveillance and treatment decisions. The primary scientific value is testing whether temporal routine-data patterns contain information lost by current-state summaries. A negative result would argue against adding a temporal model for this endpoint in this dataset.

## Evidence inspected
- HCC catalog and table metadata confirm compatible `患者主索引` and `就诊号` identifiers, 105,044 encounters, 43,815 patients, and explicit encounter timestamps.
- Labs have `检验时间`, procedures have `开单时间`, diagnoses have `诊断时间`, and encounters have `就诊时间`.
- A bounded feasibility scan found 43,815 patients with an HCC-related event, 42,697 with a subsequent recorded HCC-related event within 365 days, but only 11 patients with any pre-index lab and none with a pre-index encounter or HCC-directed procedure under the current broad text/event definitions.
- The feasibility scan used broad HCC text matches and did not adjudicate recurrence, progression, treatment response, AFP, imaging findings, pathology, death, or ICU status.

## Proposed population and time boundaries
1. Identify the earliest dated HCC-related diagnosis or HCC-directed procedure as the index event.
2. Require at least one dated encounter and at least one liver-related lab in the 180-day pre-index window.
3. Exclude patients whose index event is not dated, whose pre-index data cannot be separated from the index admission, or who have no follow-up through 365 days after the index event.
4. Define the primary endpoint as any subsequent recorded HCC-related diagnosis or HCC-directed procedure within 365 days, with a sensitivity analysis excluding events within 30 days of the index admission.

## Variables and estimand
- Baseline features: latest available liver-related lab values, lab count, encounter count, and HCC-directed procedure count in the pre-index window.
- Temporal features: ordered lab values and treatment-event indicators over the pre-index window.
- Primary estimand: the incremental predictive information of the temporal pattern for the 365-day recorded HCC-related event, conditional on the baseline current-state features.
- The outcome is a recorded HCC-related event, not adjudicated recurrence or progression.

## Baseline versus temporal comparison
- Baseline: a transparent logistic model using latest lab values and encounter/procedure counts.
- Temporal alternative: a bounded recurrent or sequence model using ordered pre-index lab and treatment events.
- Training split: time-ordered split, with no future information used to train the model.
- Evaluation: calibration, discrimination, uncertainty intervals, and incremental information relative to the baseline on the same target.
- The comparison tests whether temporal patterns add information beyond current-state summaries; it does not establish causality or clinical utility.

## Feasibility and limitations
- The bounded scan shows that the current broad definitions do not yield an eligible cohort with pre-index data. This is a feasibility finding, not a negative scientific result for the hypothesis.
- Exact HCC text terms require clinical validation.
- Event-stream density and pre-index data availability must be rechecked after tightening the index and endpoint definitions.
- No death, ICU, imaging finding, AFP, treatment response, or pathology adjudication is available.

## Interpretation
- Supportive result: the temporal model improves prediction beyond the baseline with stable uncertainty, suggesting routine-data temporal patterns may contain clinically relevant information.
- Adverse result: no incremental information, arguing against adding a temporal model for this endpoint in this dataset.
- Inconclusive result: insufficient eligible patients, unstable estimates, or inability to separate recorded events from true recurrence/progression.
