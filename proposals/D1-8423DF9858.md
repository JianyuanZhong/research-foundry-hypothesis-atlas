# Episode 76 successor: does optional repeat lactate improve a seven-day mortality-review queue?

## Unresolved question and advance

A repeat lactate may be informative at a 24-hour reassessment, but the parent’s primary endpoint, eventual hospital-discharge death, is distal to a review decision and mixes deaths soon after reassessment with deaths after prolonged hospitalization. The unresolved question is whether the optional repeat-lactate process changes a fixed-capacity mortality-review queue for a prespecified near-term endpoint: death before or at seven days after the landmark. This is a prognostic allocation experiment, not a treatment-effect study.

The strongest available evidence supports only that lactate and observation patterns can be associated with mortality. Directly inspected full text for Baysan et al. (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750, PMCID PMC9444407) reports modest incremental prognostic discrimination from 24-hour lactate in a measured sepsis cohort; it does not establish fixed-capacity queue value, optional-test workflow value, or patient benefit. Directly inspected Sisk et al. (2021, DOI 10.1093/jamia/ocaa242, PMCID PMC7810439) supports representing whether and when information is observed, but not that these proxies measure clinician attention. The cancer demonstration’s main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable; no claim is made that they were read. The three demonstrations are methodological exemplars, not evidence for this hypothesis.

Primary hypothesis: among adults in the locked baseline/L24 EICU risk set who have an observed seven-day hospital outcome under the rule below, replacing the hospital-held-out R_attention top-10%-within-hospital queue with equal-capacity R_full captures at least 3 additional seven-day hospital deaths per 100 review slots, with a 95% lower bound at least +0.03, and with nonnegative lower bounds in both prespecified repeat-availability strata and the equal-hospital macro estimand.

This advances the parent by testing a clinically nearer, explicitly time-bounded endpoint while retaining fixed workload, a surveillance-matched comparator, and the process-versus-numeric decomposition. It does not claim that seven-day death is preventable or that reviewing a patient changes outcome.

## Interpretation and falsification

Apply one ordered hierarchy:

1. Not computable: source/schema/hash mismatch, time leakage, wrong revision/conflict/tie rule, failed outcome-evaluable flow or hospital gate, insufficient finite fits/replicates, incomplete predictions, or changed features.
2. Supportive for near-term fixed-slot prognostic value: pooled Delta7 lower bound >=+0.03, both availability-stratum lower bounds >=0, macro lower bound >=0, deletion stability passes the parent’s 80% pooled / 70% stratum / 70% macro rules, and the 20% pooled upper bound is >=0.
3. Adverse, opposite direction: pooled upper bound <0 or both availability-stratum upper bounds <0.
4. Adverse, surveillance-explained: naive R_full-R_state lower bound >=+0.03 but primary R_full-R_attention upper bound <+0.03.
5. Adverse, below materiality: primary upper bound <+0.03.
6. Adverse, internally nonportable: pooled lower bound is material but a prespecified stratum, macro, deletion, or 20% stress test has a directionally adverse interval.
7. Inconclusive: every other computable pattern, including intervals crossing the margin, failed stability without adverse direction, or substantial censoring that leaves the estimand technically computable but clinically narrow.

Support means only that the locked EICU snapshot supports an incremental near-term prognostic queue signal at equal modeled workload. It does not establish test-ordering benefit, clinician visibility, prevented deaths, treatment response, safety, causality, external transport, or patient benefit. Adverse results falsify this locked materiality claim, not every possible role for lactate. Inconclusive results require a larger or more complete cohort, better follow-up, or prespecified prospective validation, not post hoc horizon or threshold search. Determining whether flagged deaths were preventable, whether review changes care, and whether any outcome benefit exists requires expert adjudication, workflow data, and a prospective impact study.
