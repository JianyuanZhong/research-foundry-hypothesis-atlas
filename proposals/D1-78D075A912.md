# Observation-adjusted renal/feed signal for safer insulin-infusion monitoring

Status: episode-5 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process study, not a causal treatment-effect, administration, or alert-benefit study.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe six-hour eICU person-period cohort on the frozen snapshot, fit an observation model for whether glucose is recorded, and estimate whether renal worsening plus loss of qualifying documented nutrition predicts a newly detected low glucose during a recorded insulin infusion after accounting for the glucose-observation process.

Completion requires:

- a frozen cohort/data dictionary, temporal audit, flow counts and source/codebook audit;
- counts of eligible decision periods, observed-glucose windows, detected low-glucose events, death/discharge/EOL censoring, label coverage, units/interfaces and hospitals;
- fitted B0/B1 coefficients or standardized risk contrasts, observation weights/positivity diagnostics and clustered uncertainty;
- held-out-hospital predictions, calibration, Brier score, fixed-alert-burden comparisons and temporal-model ablations;
- all falsification, negative-control, revision, unit, label and future-value timestamp checks.

These outputs establish that the computation was completed. They do not establish that the hypothesis is true, that a low glucose was clinically symptomatic, or that an alert or insulin/feeding change improves care.

## Unresolved question and evidence boundary

The strongest claim supported by the inspected local evidence is that this eICU snapshot contains timestamped ICU-relative structured records: recorded insulin-infusion updates; laboratory glucose and creatinine rows with units/interfaces and revision offsets; structured intake/output labels; EOL offsets; vital, severity, diagnosis and hospital/stay identifiers. The bounded audit observed the exact infusion label `Insulin (units/hr)`, glucose/bedside-glucose and creatinine labels, nutrition-related labels including P.O. and tube-feeding/flush, and medication-order insulin labels.

Those records do not prove insulin administration, delivered calories, true feed interruption, symptomatic or clinically confirmed hypoglycemia, KDIGO AKI, treatment intent, or benefit/harm from a monitoring intervention. No fitted result is asserted here.

The falsifiable claim is:

> Among adults in their first ICU stay who have a recent valid glucose and a numeric recorded insulin-infusion update, renal worsening in the preceding 24 hours plus no qualifying documented nutrition delivery in the preceding 12 hours predicts a higher probability of a newly observed glucose <70 mg/dL in the next six hours, beyond insulin intensity, recent glucose history and glucose-testing history; the joint contrast remains meaningful after observation adjustment and transport to held-out hospitals.

The primary outcome is the first valid glucose <70 mg/dL in the prospective interval. Secondary outcomes are <54 mg/dL and recurrent low-glucose burden. A window with no glucose result is not a negative hypoglycemia outcome. The observation-adjusted estimand is a detected-event risk contrast under the explicit assumption that, conditional on recorded history through the decision time, the observation model captures relevant selection into glucose measurement. It is not incidence of unobserved hypoglycemia and is not a causal insulin effect.

The clinical advance is a transportable, observation-aware test of whether an interpretable renal/documented-feed context should trigger prospective chart review or intensified glucose surveillance. It cannot justify a dose change, feed restart, or monitoring interval from this retrospective dataset.

## Population and native ICU-relative clock

Use the frozen eICU v2.0 snapshot `[source checksum]`. Dataset metadata states that longitudinal offsets are minutes relative to ICU admission.

The verified `patient` schema has `hospitaladmitoffset` and `unitdischargeoffset`, but no `unitadmitoffset`. Therefore:

- ICU admission is the native time origin: `offset = 0`; no offset is subtracted from any table;
- `hospitaladmitoffset` is retained only as an admission-context variable (it may be negative or positive relative to ICU admission), never as a unit-admission correction;
- all table time fields below are compared directly on this common ICU-relative minute scale;
- clinical predictor windows are restricted to offsets in `[0,t]` in the primary analysis, so no pre-ICU record can enter an ICU decision.

Select one first ICU stay per `uniquepid`: retain rows with a valid numeric `unitvisitnumber` and `unitdischargeoffset > 0`; choose the smallest `unitvisitnumber`, breaking a tie by smallest `patientunitstayid`. Require a valid age, identifier, and discharge offset. Adults are age >=18; encode documented age `> 89` as 90. A secondary analysis keeps later stays with `uniquepid`-clustered uncertainty and labels the estimand as multi-stay.

Decision times are exactly t = 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54, 60 and 66 hours after ICU admission (1,440 through 3,960 native minutes). A decision time is eligible only if:

1. `t < unitdischargeoffset`;
2. at least one numeric `Insulin (units/hr)` infusion update occurs in `[max(0,t-360), t)`;
3. at least one valid glucose result occurs at an offset in `[0,t]`;
4. the first-stay, adult and identifier rules hold.

The index infusion requirement is a recorded update, not proof of administration. Summarize the most recent rate, mean/max rate, update count and rate change using only updates before t. Do not carry an infusion across an unobserved gap without marking the gap; a sensitivity can use a prespecified maximum carry-forward gap, but the primary exposure is the observed update requirement.

All predictors are frozen at t. Use `labresultoffset`, not `labresultrevisedoffset`, as clinical event time. Deduplicate exact/revised laboratory records before constructing history, and report a revision sensitivity. The first valid qualifying glucose after t is the primary event. For an interval ending at discharge/death, discharge/death takes precedence at its recorded `unitdischargeoffset`; glucose records at or after that offset are not outcomes. If discharge/death occurs at d in (t,t+6], it is a competing event and follow-up ends at d. If d <= t, the period is ineligible. If d >= t+6, it does not compete within the six-hour window.

For EOL, define e as the earliest non-null of `cpleolsaveoffset` and `cpleoldiscussionoffset`. Exclude a decision time with e <= t in the primary analysis. If e occurs in (t,t+6], censor follow-up at e before an outcome; a sensitivity retains these periods with an EOL-at-index indicator and a separate EOL censoring analysis. This uses future EOL only for prespecified follow-up censoring, never as a predictor.

## Prospective variables and outcomes

Renal worsening is the latest valid creatinine in `[max(0,t-360),t]` minus the latest valid creatinine in `[max(0,t-1800),max(0,t-1080)]`, requiring one value in each interval. Report the continuous change as primary and >=0.3 mg/dL and >=1.5-fold changes as sensitivity definitions. This is a recorded creatinine change, not adjudicated AKI.

Qualifying documented nutrition is a positive numeric P.O./oral, enteral/tube-feeding, parenteral or TPN delivery row after a frozen, audited codebook. Exclude flushes, medication/insulin volume and dextrose-only rows. Define documented-feed-loss at t as no qualifying delivery in `(max(0,t-720),t]) after at least one qualifying positive delivery earlier in the same ICU stay. Keep no-interface-evidence and nutrition-coverage categories separate. Never label this true feeding cessation.

Valid glucose requires an audited glucose or bedside-glucose label, numeric result and explicitly supported unit/interface. Convert mmol/L glucose by 18.0182 and micromol/L creatinine by 88.4 only where the interface is explicitly supported; retain direct mg/dL rows and interface strata. Pre-t glucose history includes last value, minimum, mean, slope, count and time since last measurement. Define glucose observation before inspecting its value as any valid qualifying glucose in (t,t+6]. Model bedside versus laboratory interface and test density/gap history.

Covariates known by t include recorded insulin rate/update density, prior glucose trajectory and gaps, renal/chemistry history, demographics, weight, admission source, ICU type, `hospitaladmitoffset`, early severity/support, vasopressor context, hospital grouping, nutrition/glucose coverage and index EOL status where defined. Exclude discharge status, future values, mortality/outcome fields and any value whose clinical time exceeds t.

## Baseline, enriched comparison and temporal alternative

Freeze the cohort and all unit/label codebooks before outcome fitting.

B0 is a transparent pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific model for detected low glucose, with separate glucose-observation, death/discharge and EOL-censoring handling. It contains insulin intensity/update density, glucose history/testing gaps, demographics, weight, admission/ICU context, early severity/support, vasopressor context and coverage variables, but no renal/feed block.

B1 adds continuous renal change, documented-feed-loss, separate coverage categories and a prespecified renal-change x documented-feed-loss interaction. Report unweighted and stabilized inverse-probability-of-glucose-observation weighted detected-event estimates, with truncation and positivity diagnostics. Standardize the joint-versus-neither six-hour detected-risk contrast over the empirical development covariate distribution, reporting risk differences, ratios and interaction on logit and risk-difference scales. Compare calibration, Brier score, sensitivity/PPV and alerts per 100 decision times at a development-frozen alert burden; this is a policy-analysis parameter, not a clinical cutoff.

The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over 12 one-hour bins before t, with hidden size 32 or 64 selected only in development. It uses the same clinical variables plus explicit missingness/observation channels and static context, with heads for detected low glucose, glucose observation, death and discharge/EOL censoring. Full, physiology-only and observation-only ablations test whether temporal ordering changes the renal/feed contrast and held-out calibration. The alternative can reveal whether B1’s signal is a stable trajectory pattern or a timing/surveillance artifact that summaries lose. A small AUROC gain alone is not a scientific success.

Split whole hospitals approximately 60/20/20 into development, validation and held-out transport folds, keeping all stays for a `uniquepid` together. Fit preprocessing, observation models, weights and alert thresholds only in development; early-stop on validation Brier score. Use patient-clustered bootstrap for development estimates and hospital-aware bootstrap for transport. If hospital/event support, weight positivity or temporal convergence is inadequate, report B0/B1 as the primary descriptive/prognostic analysis and mark the learned comparison inconclusive rather than altering the question.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required checks are:

- permute insulin update times within stay while preserving rates;
- shuffle renal/feed histories within hospital;
- use a pre-index placebo low-glucose window excluding prior lows;
- compare with glucose-density/gap adjustment, laboratory-only and bedside-only strata;
- repeat strict prior-delivery, complete-observable nutrition, direct-unit and no-carry-forward definitions;
- analyze insulin-free analogous renal x documentation periods;
- audit every predictor and revision timestamp;
- compare leave-one-hospital-out results with and without observation-process channels;
- use newly observed non-glucose laboratory testing as a negative-control outcome.

Support requires adequate units/labels and event support; a monotone joint-versus-neither contrast above a prespecified two-percentage-point six-hour risk-difference target; a nonzero interaction with 95% uncertainty excluding zero; added calibration/alert-burden value beyond B0; agreement of the temporal profile with B1; no single-hospital dominance; acceptable weights/transport calibration; and failure of placebo, permutation and negative-control falsifications. This supports a transportable prognostic monitoring signal for chart-reviewed prospective evaluation only.

Adverse evidence is a null below the target, reversal, disappearance after observation weighting or strict labels, poor held-out calibration, one-interface/one-hospital confinement, similar prediction of the negative-control observation outcome, or reproduction in placebo/insulin-free periods. This refutes the measurable marker in this snapshot or identifies surveillance/documentation artifact; it does not prove insulin or feeding interruption harmless.

Inconclusive evidence is inadequate paired periods/events, unreliable units/labels, unstable weights, unsupported hospital folds, intervals spanning both null and target, competing-event miscalibration, or temporal nonconvergence. It triggers data-quality or prospective-study work, not a forced conclusion.

The available data cannot establish true insulin administration, delivered calories, feed cessation, rescue treatment, clinician intent, symptomatic/confirmed hypoglycemia, adjudicated AKI, causal treatment effects, or alert benefit. Medication-administration and calorie/feed-stop records, point-of-care/chart adjudication, and a prospective or emulated-intervention study are required for those stronger claims.

## Alternatives, seed consideration and deferral

The eICU-09 seed supplies the clinical premise, while eICU-02 and eICU-08 motivate explicit testing-process adjustment. eICU-02 alone could support a useful hospital-transport study of testing-dependent prediction, and eICU-08 a reduced-testing/deterioration study, but neither has a more verified, specific safety endpoint for this episode; deterioration and interface/end-of-life explanations would also require additional adjudication. eICU-03’s hypotension-duration question is clinically useful but requires artifact handling and an adjudicated AKI endpoint. These are deferred alternatives, not reasons to change the current target.

A mechanistic insulin-glucose model is deferred because insulin administration, calories, rescue treatment and validated physiologic parameters are unavailable. A latent longitudinal model is also deferred as primary because the prespecified B0/B1 versus temporal comparison directly tests the monitoring question; it can be revisited if event support is adequate and a mechanistic target becomes observable. The unavailable multimodal cancer demonstration is not reproduced: local eICU data lack its imaging modality, and a lab-only model would not answer this observation-adjusted insulin question.
