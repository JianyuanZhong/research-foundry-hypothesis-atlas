# Hospital heterogeneity in recorded liberation from invasive ventilation

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must produce an audited cohort and a fitted, uncertainty-quantified competing-risks analysis of recorded liberation from invasive mechanical ventilation after early ICU ventilation onset. Completion requires: cohort flow and field-level missingness/observation audit; patient-level, hospital-held-out fits of a transparent hierarchical discrete-time competing-risk model and a mechanistic latent support-state model; standardized hospital-specific 48-, 72-, and 168-hour cumulative-incidence estimates under a common measured case mix; between-hospital heterogeneity and uncertainty; held-out calibration and observed-versus-predicted recovery curves; and all prespecified falsification and sensitivity analyses. Discovery has not fitted these models and claims no direction or size of hospital differences.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The seed asks whether hospitals differ in speed of liberation from organ support. The strongest pre-fitting claim supported by inspected material is only that the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-stay-linked ventilator start/end fields, airway labels, longitudinal respiratory observations, patient disposition and hospital identifiers. It does not establish that end offsets equal successful extubation, that documentation is complete, or that any hospital is better.

Primary observational hypothesis:

> Among adult ICU stays with a first recorded invasive-ventilation episode beginning within ICU hours 0–24, after standardizing measured admission severity and onset context, hospitals exhibit non-zero heterogeneity in cumulative incidence of recorded ventilation end by 72 hours; a longitudinal support-state model will explain more of that heterogeneity as differences in active-support-to-weaning-to-liberation transitions than the transparent onset-only model.

Prolonged invasive ventilation is clinically consequential because it is associated with sedation exposure, ICU bed use and complications, while liberation is a clinically meaningful decision endpoint. This study can identify whether observed cross-hospital variation is concentrated in support trajectories or is mostly case mix and documentation. It cannot identify hospital quality, a superior weaning protocol, or a causal treatment effect.

## Population, index, horizon and competing states

Use one ICU-stay row per patientunitstayid, restricted to adult records with usable age, hospital ID and unit-discharge status. Exclude invalid identifiers and stays without a determinable first qualifying onset. The primary analysis uses the first eligible ICU stay per uniquepid; all eligible stays clustered by uniquepid are a sensitivity analysis.

A qualifying episode is the earliest respiratoryCare record with airwaytype equal to Oral ETT or Tracheostomy and a numeric, nonzero ventstartoffset in [0,1440] minutes. Empty, nonnumeric and zero offsets are treated as unknown/placeholder in the primary analysis because the direct audit found zero-filled offsets to be overwhelmingly common; a zero-offset sensitivity analysis is reported only if respiratoryCharting independently corroborates active ventilation near ICU admission. Require a numeric, nonzero ventendoffset at or after onset to call a recorded liberation. For overlapping records take the earliest qualifying start and first end at or after it; report contradictions. The endpoint is recorded ventilation end, not confirmed extubation or ventilator-free breathing.

Follow in six-hour intervals through 168 hours after onset or earliest exit; primary horizon 72 hours, secondary 48 and 168. First events are:

1. recorded liberation: valid ventendoffset at or after onset within horizon;
2. death before liberation: unitdischargestatus = Expired and unitdischargelocation = Death, with unitdischargeoffset first;
3. alive ICU exit before liberation: unitdischargestatus = Alive and unitdischargeoffset first, including floor, home, another ICU/hospital and transfer destinations; report destination strata;
4. administrative/observation censoring: still in unit with no qualifying end by horizon or ambiguous disposition timing.

Missing respiratory charting is never liberation. If end and discharge share a minute or ordering is ambiguous, use death first, alive exit second, liberation third, flag ties, and repeat with ties excluded. Liberation may be truly present but uncoded; an end may be chart closure.

## Matched methods

Both methods use identical cohort, six-hour grid, source universe, competing states, hospital-held-out split and uncertainty procedure; neither uses future rows.

Transparent baseline: fit a pooled cause-specific discrete-time complementary-log-log model (logistic only if selected before results) with interval terms, onset context, demographics, admission source/unit, APACHE fields, pre-index vital/lab summaries, missingness and observation indicators. Add hospital random intercepts by cause; add hospital-by-interval slopes only if identifiable. For held-out hospitals set unseen effects to zero. Standardize every hospital to the same measured case-mix distribution and estimate 48/72/168-hour recovery, death and alive-exit CIFs plus between-hospital SD/range, with patient- and hospital-clustered uncertainty. This is interpretable but loses within-episode ordering and irregular support intensity.

Mechanistic alternative: fit a constrained semi-Markov latent support-state model on the same six-hour grid. States are active invasive support, transitional/weaning, recorded liberated, death and alive exit. The first two emit time-aligned respiratory settings/care values, respiratoryCare fields and contemporaneous SaO2/respiratory rate; a separate observation-intensity component models chart counts and gaps so sparse documentation is not recovery. Explicit respiratory-care end offsets anchor observed liberation while the latent state represents pre-end weaning dynamics. Hospital random effects enter transition hazards, not emissions. Fit within training hospitals in each outer fold and report standardized hospital differences in active-to-transitional, transitional-to-liberation and competing-exit transitions. This reveals whether heterogeneity is in support-state progression or observation intensity, which onset-only baseline cannot show. Treat it as non-informative if occupancy or transitions are unstable across initializations, emissions too sparse, or transition calibration does not improve without exploiting observation density.

## Splits, uncertainty, resources and falsification

Outer folds hold out hospitals, with a prespecified minimum eligible count and transparent reporting of small/excluded hospitals. Inner patient-grouped splits tune within training hospitals; no post-index or cross-fold feature leakage. Primary checks are CIF calibration and Brier score at 48/72/168 hours; AUC is secondary. Use repeated held-out hospital folds and a hospital-cluster bootstrap for CIFs, between-hospital variance and method differences.

Planning estimate, unmeasured: transparent model 1–3 CPU hours, 8–16 cores, up to 32 GB after interval construction; latent model 4–12 CPU hours, 16 cores, 64–128 GB. GPU is optional; if matrix-heavy repeated latent fitting is the bottleneck, one allocated A100 may reduce runtime, but actual allocation/runtime must be reported. Future solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs and 28800 seconds. No scientific fit was launched during discovery.

Support requires non-negligible, precise standardized 72-hour hospital spread after baseline adjustment; persistence in held-out hospitals and after observation-density adjustment; and reproducible transition-level heterogeneity. Falsification/adverse evidence is near-zero adjusted spread with useful precision, disappearance after observation and discharge censoring handling, failure to transport, or dependence on one hospital, airway label or offset convention. Inconclusive means sparse/contradictory fields, inadequate hospital support, wide intervals or non-identifiable states; report that limitation, not a substitute endpoint.

Computationally checkable: counts, joins, onset/end construction, event ordering, split integrity, fitted parameters/CIFs, calibration and uncertainty. Clinical adjudication is needed to determine whether end offsets represent extubation, ventilator-free time, weaning trials, sedation intent or clinician rationale. Reliable waveform data, spontaneous-breathing-trial results, extubation confirmation, treatment indication and external validation are unavailable. Quality, protocol superiority, causal treatment and patient-benefit claims require adjudication and another study.

## Deferred alternatives and limits

A high-capacity sequence model is deferred because the scientific uncertainty is hospital transport and interpretable transition decomposition, not a small predictive gain. Revisit only if both specified models pass endpoint audits but leave reproducible nonlinear long-range residuals across held-out hospitals, using the same inputs, outcomes, calibration and uncertainty. Treatment-effect analysis is deferred because indication, intent and time-varying confounding are not adequately observed. The incumbent hypotension proposal is fallback context and is not combined with this estimand.

This is an observational study of recorded ICU documentation. Hospital heterogeneity is descriptive conditional on measured variables and recording process, not ranking, quality measurement or causal inference. No fitted result is claimed.
