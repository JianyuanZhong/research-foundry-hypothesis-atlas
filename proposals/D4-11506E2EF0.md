# Baseline cystatin-C/creatinine discordance and protocol-measured renal decline at repeat assessment

## Question and substantive advance

The clinically consequential unresolved question is whether discordance between baseline cystatin-C and creatinine identifies people whose subsequent **protocol-measured** renal function declines more by the UK Biobank repeat assessment than would be identified by either marker alone. This matters because creatinine-based and cystatin-C-based filtration estimates can disagree for reasons related to muscle mass, inflammation, body composition, and early kidney dysfunction; a discordant result may change whether clinicians repeat testing, intensify risk-factor management, or seek confirmation with a second filtration marker.

The strongest claim currently supported by the local evidence is only data availability and temporal structure: the UKB rectangular export contains baseline and repeat instances of fields `30700` and `30720`, and protocol assessment dates in fields `53` and centers in `54`; the first source rows show baseline renal values and a later assessment date where available. The unresolved claim is prognostic and incremental, not established by the field presence: baseline discordance predicts a larger protocol-measured decline at repeat assessment beyond baseline creatinine, cystatin-C, age, sex, and baseline eGFR. No causal, screening, treatment-benefit, or population-transportability claim is proposed.

## Falsification, interpretation, and gates

Falsification tests are: (a) permute baseline discordance within sex and baseline eGFR strata while preserving outcomes; (b) reverse the discordance sign, which must reverse the coefficient and leave the two-sided evidence unchanged; (c) use baseline-to-baseline duplicated fields or a date-order violation audit as negative-control checks; and (d) compare the association with the non-renal repeat marker change only as an exploratory negative-control outcome, never as a renal conclusion. Every result must be linked to a frozen cohort hash, field audit, unit/equation audit, and independent aggregate recomputation.

Support requires verified units/equations, cross-table agreement, adequate paired sample and support in all discordance groups, a prespecified clinically meaningful interval excluding zero for the primary incremental estimand, concordant direction across cystatin-C and creatinine-based decline outcomes, and successful falsification tests. A small p-value alone is insufficient. Adverse evidence is a precisely estimated effect near zero or in the opposite direction with model and measurement checks passing; it would argue against clinically useful incremental discordance information in this repeat-attender setting. Inconclusive evidence includes unresolved units/equation validity, poor repeat coverage, broad intervals, missing-not-at-random sensitivity, or discordant outcomes; it must not be described as equivalence.

If baseline or repeat cystatin-C/creatinine units, field identities, or eGFR equation applicability cannot be verified, do not produce eGFR decline as the endpoint. Authorized pivot: estimate protocol-measured longitudinal biomarker changes directly (standardized cystatin-C and creatinine changes separately), retaining the same repeat-attender/time-order design and treating cross-marker discordance as a standardized residual exposure. This pivot answers a weaker biomarker-dynamics question and cannot be called renal-function decline.

## Evidence limits

The export lacks an adjudicated CKD endpoint, urine albumin/protein, medication start/stop chronology, indication for repeat attendance, renal imaging, and a verified external registry of renal outcomes. Repeat attendance is a selected population and can induce selection bias; baseline discordance can reflect non-GFR determinants of both biomarkers. Automated computation can verify joins, dates, transformations, model arithmetic, uncertainty, and falsification behavior. It cannot establish clinical adjudication, assay comparability, biological mechanism, causal effect, treatment utility, screening performance, or transportability to non-attenders. Those claims require assay documentation, expert review, and an independent prospective or clinically adjudicated study.
