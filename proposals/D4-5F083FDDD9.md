# UKB seed-39 comparison branch: does a low-ApoB/low-albumin pattern mark near-term liver cancer rather than generic illness?

Status: independent comparison branch developed from the admissible, not-blocked expert seed [starting question]. This is a proposed experiment; no hypothesis result has been fitted. The seed's suggested liver-PDFF extension is deferred because the exact selected table does not contain field 21088.

## Scientific opening and claim boundary

The clinical uncertainty is whether an apparently low-lipid result is a benign metabolic trait or a context-dependent signal of reduced hepatic synthetic/metabolic reserve and impending serious disease. A low ApoB/low albumin combination could matter clinically because it might identify people who warrant timely liver assessment, but it could also be a nonspecific consequence of inflammation, nutrition, medication, or already developing cancer. The available evidence does not tell us whether the combination is specific to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), whether it is merely near-diagnostic reverse causation, or whether it predicts later liver disease independently of those alternatives.

[K1] reports that UK Biobank plasma profiling measured ApoB and albumin among a broad panel and that biomarker associations span many disease families, with reported robustness to exclusion of the first two follow-up years. That supports measurement feasibility and the possibility that the two markers carry health information; it does not establish the joint low-ApoB/low-albumin phenotype or HCC specificity. [K2] emphasizes confounding and reverse causation in observational biomarker-cancer work and reports heterogeneous biomarker/cancer relationships, including a genetic association for gamma-glutamyl transferase with liver cancer but not the proposed measured combination. [K3] describes suboptimal current HCC surveillance and says emerging blood biomarkers and risk scores are not sufficiently validated for routine use. Together, the strongest supported claim is that circulating biomarkers can correlate with broad disease risk and that HCC risk stratification remains clinically incomplete. None establishes the claim below.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among adults without recorded cancer at their first UKB assessment, concurrent low ApoB and low albumin is associated with a greater incidence of registry-coded HCC (ICD-10 C22) than would be expected from either low marker alone, with a larger association in the 0–2-year than the 2–5-year window; the joint contrast should be more HCC-specific than it is for biliary, other primary cancer, or death outcomes.

This is a descriptive association and timing/specificity test. It is not a claim that low ApoB or albumin causes cancer, that the pattern reflects a hepatic mechanism, or that it is ready for screening. The leading explanation is a preclinical liver-cancer/liver-reserve signal. The strongest rivals are: (1) registry/diagnostic proximity, which predicts a large 0–2-year association that attenuates after a two-year lag; (2) generic illness, inflammation, nutrition, medication, or healthcare selection, which predicts similar associations for other cancers and death and attenuation after CRP/ALT/BMI/medication sensitivity; and (3) coded pre-existing liver disease, which predicts attenuation after excluding prior K70–K77 inpatient diagnoses. These explanations can remain observationally indistinguishable if symptoms, indication, viral hepatitis, fibrosis, imaging, pathology, or stage are missing.

A supportive result would justify a clinically characterized external validation and liver-work-up study. It would not justify a surveillance threshold. A near-diagnostic-only or pan-cancer result would redirect the work toward illness/ascertainment rather than liver-specific screening. A precise null would weaken the proposed joint marker direction; an imprecise result would be inconclusive.

## Population, exposure, outcomes, and estimands

### Primary population

Include unique nonmissing eid with valid parseable 53-0.0 and complete instance-0 ApoB, albumin, ALT, and CRP. Exclude any invalid/unpaired registry event record and any participant with a valid paired 40005/40006 cancer date on or before T0. Do not require a repeat assessment. Record all exclusions and missingness denominators.

Define low ApoB and low albumin using development-only 25th-percentile cutpoints, then freeze them for the temporal test. The primary exposure contrast is the four-level pattern: neither low, ApoB-only low, albumin-only low, and both low. Continuous standardized ApoB, albumin, their product, ALT, CRP, age, sex, BMI, calendar year of T0, and a medication-code availability indicator are retained for sensitivity models. The primary clinical comparison is the joint-low group versus neither-low, with the two single-low groups showing whether the joint pattern adds information.

### Outcomes and time

For each participant, pair every 40005-i.0 with 40006-i.0 for i=0,…,21, discard invalid dates/codes, and use the first valid qualifying event after T0. The primary event is HCC, ICD-10 C22. Secondary cancer families are biliary C23–C24 and other primary malignant disease C00–C97 excluding C22–C24 and secondary C77–C79. Other cancer and death are competing events. The optional inpatient liver-disease outcome is the first paired 41270/41280 K70–K77 event.

Follow from T0 to the first event, death, administrative censoring at 2022-06-01, or the window boundary. Report two prespecified windows separately: 0–2 years (near-diagnostic timing) and 2–5 years (delayed timing). The primary estimand is the HCC cumulative-incidence/risk contrast at two years for both-low versus neither-low, with cause-specific log-hazard interaction and 95% uncertainty as the interpretable model summary. Report the same contrast for ApoB-only and albumin-only, for 2–5 years, and for biliary/other cancer. A joint-low association that exists only in 0–2 years is a timing result, not evidence of a stable preclinical phenotype.

A prespecified lag sensitivity excludes the first year and then compares 1–2 and 2–5 years. A sensitivity cohort excludes prior K70–K77 inpatient diagnosis when valid paired dates are available; this is not the primary population because incomplete inpatient ascertainment could create selection.

## Falsification and analysis

1. Fit HCC, biliary, other-cancer, and death cause-specific models with the four-level exposure, age, sex, BMI, ALT, CRP, T0 year, and medication-code availability. Use Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence with competing events. Standardization, imputation, cutpoints, and any penalization are learned only in development.
2. Test registry/diagnostic proximity by comparing 0–2 with 2–5 and the one-year-lag sensitivity. A large early-only estimate is adverse to a durable marker interpretation and compatible with reverse causation.
3. Test generic illness/selection by repeating the joint contrast for other primary cancer and death, by CRP/ALT/BMI and medication-availability strata, and after excluding the first year. Similar effect directions across outcomes or disappearance after these measured adjustments are adverse to HCC specificity. These adjustments cannot recover clinical indication.
4. Test liver-family specificity by reporting HCC versus C23–C24 rather than pooling them; a similar biliary result is not a failure of all liver biology but weakens HCC-specific interpretation.
5. Perform a cancer-type permutation negative control that preserves each participant’s event date, censoring, and event count while permuting HCC versus other eligible primary-cancer labels within development data. The joint-low-by-type contrast should collapse toward zero. This tests implementation and outcome-type specificity, not biological truth.
6. Audit pair integrity, duplicate eid, invalid dates, date ties, pre-T0 cancers, values outside plausible laboratory ranges, and death ties. Never use a code from one registry slot with a date from another.
7. Do not call an association mechanistic. A pattern that persists after measured adjustment remains an association and may still be caused by unmeasured liver disease, healthcare use, or selection.

Supportive evidence requires: a positive joint-low versus neither-low HCC contrast in 0–2 years with a reasonably informative interval; a directionally smaller or clearly different pre-specified 2–5 contrast consistent with the stated timing claim; stronger HCC specificity than the other-cancer negative control; no implementation failure under pair/permutation checks; and no complete reversal that is explained by the measured covariate/medication or prior-liver-disease sensitivity. If HCC events are too sparse for the delayed subgroup or held-out calibration, label that component inconclusive rather than switching windows or randomizing the split.

Adverse evidence is a precise null/reversal, a comparable pattern for other cancers/death, persistence only in an unlagged near-diagnostic window when the intended claim is delayed, or dependence on a code/date mismatch. Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals, fewer than 50 HCC events in a relevant final temporal comparison for the learned model, failed convergence, unusable date/code pairing, or missingness that changes the estimand. An imprecise null does not refute a liver-reserve hypothesis.

## Methods compared before narrowing

### A. Interpretable baseline

Use separate cause-specific Cox models for HCC, biliary cancer, other primary cancer, and death, with the four-level joint exposure and the covariates above. Fit separate 0–2 and 2–5 risk-set models or explicitly model window interactions; do not assume proportional hazards across both windows. Report joint-versus-single contrasts, cumulative-incidence curves, calibration of absolute risks, robust 95% intervals, event/person-time tables, and sensitivity results.

This baseline directly answers whether the combined phenotype adds HCC-specific and time-specific information. It makes the clinical contrast auditable but may lose nonlinear thresholds, interactions, and nonproportional time patterns.

### B. Substantive learned alternative

Fit a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time competing-risk model using exactly the same participants, predictors, registry-paired outcomes, competing events, and 0–2/2–5 windows. Expand follow-up into yearly intervals with separate HCC, biliary, other-cancer, and death hazards plus interval indicators. Tune a small fixed grid only in rolling development folds. No future event, repeat assessment, or outcome-derived feature enters the baseline predictor set.

B can reveal nonlinear ApoB/albumin thresholds, interactions with ALT/CRP/BMI, and time-varying HCC-versus-other-cancer hazard shapes that the additive Cox model compresses. It cannot reveal liver mechanism or repair unavailable indication, viral, imaging, pathology, or treatment information. A small AUC gain without stable calibration or a stable HCC-specific contrast is not a substantive scientific advance.

Sort participants by T0: earliest 70% for development and latest 30% for final temporal testing, keeping each participant intact. All cutpoints, transforms, imputation, tuning, and calibration are development-only. Evaluate both methods on the final holdout using HCC/biliary/other-cancer target-specific calibration intercept and slope, 2- and 5-year cumulative-incidence Brier and log scores, calibration plots, and participant-bootstrap uncertainty; discrimination is secondary. Use 500 participant bootstrap replicates for contrasts and held-out metrics while preserving failed fits. If a relevant final-window HCC count is below 50, label that learned window and its calibration comparison inconclusive rather than changing the split or endpoint.

The baseline is primary because the scientific deliverable is an interpretable joint-versus-single HCC timing/specificity contrast. The learned model is retained because it tests whether clinically relevant nonlinear or time-varying information is lost by the baseline; it is not selected to chase a small prediction gain. A future solver is complete only when it emits the cohort and pair-integrity audit, split manifest, fitted baseline and learned outputs, window/family-specific contrasts, competing-risk curves, held-out calibration/Brier/log scores, bootstrap intervals, permutation and other-cancer falsifications, and a bounded conclusion labelled supportive, adverse, or inconclusive.

Approximate future solver resources are 8–16 CPU cores, 32–64 GiB RAM, and 1–3 hours for selected-column reading and tabular fits plus bootstrap; this estimate is unverified. The configured planning envelope permits up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, and 28,800 seconds. GPU is not required for this tabular experiment; available A100 capacity is not a scientific merit criterion.

## Alternatives, selection, and missing evidence

Selected: this seed-39 biochemical adaptation because the exact table has a large complete baseline cohort and enough audited HCC events to make a bounded timing/specificity analysis possible, while its uncertainty is materially different from the CBC cancer-type branch. The question is about a metabolic/liver-risk phenotype and registry timing, not CBC pattern specificity or repeat-CBC selection.

Deferred: the seed's liver-PDFF validation using field 21088, because 21088-0.0 and 21088-1.0 are absent from the exact ukb671626 schema. Do not substitute a field from ukb672073 or another namespace without a documented crosswalk. Revisit only if a same-namespace liver-fat source is supplied.

Deferred: a serial ApoB/albumin-change hypothesis, because only 15,593 complete instance-1 biomarker/date records were audited and repeat observation is highly selected; this branch does not make a repeat-landmark claim.

Not chosen: a transformer or latent longitudinal model. The scientific uncertainty is a small, interpretable joint phenotype and timing/specificity, and the available repeat biomarkers are sparse. Extra model complexity cannot identify clinical indication or mechanism. Revisit only if repeated same-namespace biochemical measurements and a clinically adjudicated outcome source become available.

Essential missing evidence includes symptoms, reason for the laboratory test, primary-care/outpatient records, viral hepatitis, alcohol exposure, fibrosis/cirrhosis stage, imaging or PDFF, AFP, pathology, stage, treatment, nutritional status, medication indication and timing, and clinical adjudication of registry codes. Expert liver review, external validation in a clinically characterized cohort, and a decision-linked prospective study would be required before any screening, referral, causal, mechanistic, or treatment conclusion.
