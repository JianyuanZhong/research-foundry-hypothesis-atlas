# Patient state versus hospital testing workflow in transportable ICU mortality prediction

Status: episode-12 substantive successor to [prior hypothesis]. This is an observational prediction and measurement-process study, not a causal effect of testing, a hospital quality ranking, or evidence of clinical benefit.

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must newly freeze a leakage-safe six-hour landmark cohort and laboratory codebook; fit paired state-only and observation-aware competing-risk models; and estimate the held-out-hospital change in calibration and Brier performance caused by adding patient-level laboratory observation history. Completion requires cohort flow, unit/revision audit, fold assignments, predictions, calibration, uncertainty, ablations, falsification outputs, and an interpretation trace linking each conclusion to computed files.

The deliverable is a transportability estimate, not the best AUROC: does patient-level testing history carry reproducible information beyond patient state, and does that information survive a genuinely new hospital? Hospital workflow descriptors are a secondary decomposition, not primary patient-risk evidence.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

Existing evidence supports that EHR laboratory collection is informative rather than random and that models can learn data-collection processes. The eICU expert seed [starting question] proposes that models relying on testing frequency and missingness lose performance when transported across hospitals. That is an untested hypothesis. The parent’s outcome-blind feasibility audit found 135,680 eligible adult first stays, 2,470 recorded ICU-death events by 1,800 minutes, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, 2,209,118 landmark-known laboratory rows, and 111,655 episodes with at least one row. These counts establish support only, not an association or model result.

The falsifiable claim is:

> In adult first ICU stays, adding patient-level pre-landmark laboratory observation history changes held-out-hospital prediction of recorded ICU death beyond a state-only model; any gain that disappears after training-fold normalization or within-hospital process permutation is a hospital-workflow fingerprint rather than robust transportable patient-level information.

This matters because a warning model may look accurate by recognizing who gets tested at its development hospital and become miscalibrated where ordering and interfaces differ. Conversely, removing all observation information may discard clinically useful surveillance information. The result could inform whether deployment needs workflow auditing and recalibration, a state-only model, or prospective review. It does not justify changing test ordering.

## Population, time and outcome

Use the first adult ICU stay per person in the frozen eICU snapshot: age at least 18 (age greater than 89 treated as 90), positive numeric unitdischargeoffset, smallest valid unitvisitnumber per uniquepid, ties broken by patientunitstayid, and unitdischargeoffset greater than 360. Landmark time is 360 ICU-relative minutes. Predict using only information known by that landmark through 1,800 minutes.

Primary outcome: first patient.unitdischargestatus normalized exactly to Expired with unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800]. The primary estimand is cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death by 1,800 minutes; alive ICU discharge before 1,800 is a competing event. A secondary cause-specific hourly analysis keeps only episodes at risk in each hour. This is not all-hospital mortality and cannot recover death after alive ICU discharge. Evaluate calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, integrated/cause-specific Brier score and patient-clustered uncertainty first. Discrimination and fixed-alert-burden summaries are secondary. Never call the target a causal testing effect or unrecorded mortality risk.

## Models, alternative and split

Freeze eligibility, analyte/unit/revision rules, target, competing events and folds before fitting.

B_state, the simple baseline, is an elastic-net pooled discrete-time cause-specific logistic model for death and alive discharge over hourly rows from 360 through 1,800. Inputs are age, sex/ethnicity where valid, weight, admission source, stay type, and last lab values, early slopes/ranges and prespecified threshold flags. Training-fold medians and one global availability flag handle missing state values. Exclude test counts, elapsed test times, detailed missingness and hospital ID.

B_state+patient-process is the primary substantive comparison. It uses identical episode-hour rows, targets, folds and state inputs, adding only patient-level masks, result counts, time-since-test, total testing density and interface/revision channels, normalized using development data. Compare held-out calibration, Brier and cumulative-incidence risk, not only discrimination.

B_state+site is secondary: add hospital descriptors and development-derived site workflow summaries to decompose a site fingerprint. A gain confined to this model is not transportable patient-level evidence. Any encoding or normalization is fit in development hospitals only.

T_temporal is a secondary one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour pre-landmark bins containing the same values, masks, elapsed-time channels, counts and interface/documentation channels. Heads predict death, alive discharge and next-bin lab observation. It tests whether order, persistence and co-movement add information beyond the transparent process-aware model. It is useful only if held-out calibration/Brier improve beyond B_state+patient-process, survive value-only versus process-only ablations, and pass all support gates.

Use three whole-hospital folds (development 60%, validation 20%, transport 20%), stratified by eligible and event counts, keeping every stay of a uniquepid together. A patient-random split is secondary and measures optimism from ignoring workflow. If any transport fold lacks event and competing-discharge support, report it as unsupported and make no transport claim.

## Support, falsification and interpretation

Before fitting require at least 100 total events, 10 event-bearing hospitals in every retained evaluation fold, 20 eligible episodes per retained hospital, positive value/missingness overlap across folds, and no post-landmark revision leakage. These are computational gates, not clinical thresholds.

Supportive evidence requires the patient-process model to reduce held-out-hospital integrated Brier and calibration error relative to B_state in at least two transport folds, with prespecified relative Brier improvement of at least 10% and bootstrap uncertainty excluding no improvement; persistence under strict unit/revision handling and removal of site descriptors; process-channel ablations and within-hospital process permutation showing the gain is not a site fingerprint; no unacceptable fixed-alert-burden increase; and no domination by one hospital or one lab family. This supports only that patient-level measurement process carries reproducible transport-relevant predictive information in this snapshot.

Adverse evidence is no process-aware transport gain, reversal after strict codebooks, worse calibration or alert burden, collapse after process permutation, or gain only from B_state+site. This refutes the proposed transportable process advantage here, not the clinical relevance of testing.

Inconclusive evidence includes insufficient event-bearing hospitals, no common testing-process support, unstable fold-specific calibration, target leakage, fold reversals, or failure to separate alive discharge from death. Report counts and descriptive distributions only; do not choose by AUROC or confirm the hypothesis.

Harbor can check source bindings, leakage control, cohort counts, fits, fold assignment, calibration, uncertainty, ablations and whether conclusions follow outputs. It cannot determine why a test was ordered, whether a result is physiologically correct, whether an alert changes care, or whether associations are causal. Those require chart review, clinical adjudication, external hospitals and prospective impact evaluation.

## Method choice, resources and deferred branches

The transparent competing-risk model is primary because its standardized risks and coefficients make the state/process decomposition auditable. The GRU/temporal convolution is a substantive alternative only for ordering/persistence information beyond summaries; a small predictive gain without transportable calibration is not sufficient. A mechanistic causal testing model is deferred because clinician intent, a complete order process and intervention assignment are unavailable. CPU tabular fitting is appropriate; the temporal model may use one allocated GPU only if support passes. Planning estimate for the future solver is 4-8 CPUs and 16-32 GiB for preprocessing/elastic-net, and one GPU with about 4 CPUs and 16 GiB for a small GRU/TCN. These are unmeasured planning estimates, not discovery results; no proposer/solver weight training is authorized.

Demonstration dispositions: Delphi/natural-history motivates the temporal alternative, but the original UKB trajectories and external Danish validation are unavailable, so this is an eICU adaptation. ALADYNOULLI’s genetic inputs are absent and its latent genetic analysis is deferred. Oncoformer’s full paper methods remain unavailable and local chest-X-ray images are absent; no multimodal reproduction is claimed, while a lab-only variant would duplicate this question.

Seed dispositions: eICU seed 02 is the direct conceptual parent. Seed 08 requires a separate dynamic deterioration/end-of-life estimand and is deferred. Seed 09 remains clinically important but the measured conservative support had only three joint episodes and zero detected lows, so it is deferred rather than rescued by treating missing nutrition as interruption. Seeds 03 and 10 require separate blood-pressure/organ-support adjudication and are not substitutes for this transport question.

This child repairs the parent by making patient-level process the primary estimand, site workflow a diagnostic decomposition, and permutation/calibration-recalibration necessary before interpreting transport. No fitted clinical result is claimed.
