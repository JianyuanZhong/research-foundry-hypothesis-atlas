# Provisional successor: duration versus nadir for early ICU AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with a qualifying eICU ICU admission and at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with a higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, beyond the admission nadir. The association is expected to be reproducible after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, observation intensity, and hospital.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target. The key unresolved claim is whether duration adds information beyond nadir after timing and ascertainment constraints are made explicit.

## Evidence and uncertainty
The parent candidate supplies three inspected, abstract-only works: [K1] reports that arterial waveform morphology did not improve postoperative AKI prediction beyond time-weighted MAP below 65 mmHg and baseline covariates, but does not establish duration beyond nadir in ICU admissions; [K2] describes ICU AKI as heterogeneous and dynamic, with creatinine-centered diagnosis necessary but insufficient and urine output nonspecific without clinical context; [K3] cautions that missing-data patterns can affect dynamic EHR prediction and transportability. These works motivate the question and its safeguards; they do not validate the local eICU fields or estimate the proposed association. No computed eICU effect is claimed.

## Population, exposure, outcome, and analysis
Qualifying population: adults (`age >=18`) in a single qualifying ICU admission with valid ICU start time and at least one valid systolic BP <65 mmHg in ICU time 0–24 hours. Exclude records lacking the required admission identifier or with impossible/ambiguous timestamps.

Exposure: derive valid systolic BP from both vital tables, cumulative minutes below 65 mmHg, nadir, and measurement counts in 0–24 hours. Use prespecified physiologic-range filtering and piecewise-linear interpolation between valid measurements, with no extrapolation beyond first/last observed measurement. Sensitivity analyses use no interpolation, shorter interpolation gaps, and observation-density adjustment.

Primary outcome: a creatinine-defined event in 24–72 hours. Define baseline as the last valid creatinine at or before ICU time 0 when available; if unavailable, use the first valid creatinine in a prespecified pre-exposure window and report missingness rather than imputing normal. Define the event as the earliest creatinine increase >=0.3 mg/dL from baseline within 48 hours, or >=1.5 times baseline if the rise is documented within the outcome window, retaining time to event. Do not use a post-exposure creatinine as baseline. If the required pre-exposure baseline or outcome-window measurements are absent, classify the endpoint as unavailable/indeterminate rather than silently treating it as negative.

Secondary urine-output endpoint: retain only after semantic and timing validation of `intakeOutput` labels, numeric/text values, entry versus event offset, duplicate/cumulative behavior, and weight/availability. If semantics cannot be established, report urine-output AKI as unavailable rather than substitute a proxy.

The simple baseline is a prespecified hospital-stratified logistic model using nadir systolic BP in 0–24 hours, age, sex, baseline creatinine, and available baseline severity/vasopressor context. The substantive time-aware alternative adds cumulative hypotension minutes, time below threshold in prespecified intervals, last BP before window end, and a simple recovery/trajectory feature while retaining nadir. Evaluate both models on the same rows with a hospital-held-out split, the same outcome definition, and the same uncertainty procedure. Report duration effect estimates and confidence intervals, calibration, and hospital-held-out discrimination as secondary descriptive outputs. A complex sequence model is deferred because the available data cannot distinguish hypotension-mediated kidney injury from severity, treatment, and observation artifacts; revisit only if a bounded diagnostic shows trajectory information is materially unavailable to the simpler representation.

## Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: duration adds reproducible information beyond nadir with a prespecified direction and uncertainty, and the result is not explained solely by observation density or a single hospital. Adverse: duration adds no information, reverses after prespecified adjustment, or is confined to artifact-prone measurements. Inconclusive: creatinine ascertainment, timestamp validity, or sampling is too sparse to estimate the comparison reliably; an imprecise null is not decisive refutation. Any causal interpretation, treatment threshold, or mechanism requires additional clinical adjudication, richer treatment/etiology data, and prospective or quasi-experimental evidence.
