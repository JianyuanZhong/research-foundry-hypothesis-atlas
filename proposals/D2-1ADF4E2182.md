# Does remission-macrophage "protection" of RA fibroblasts buy inflammatory and protease restraint at the price of a matrix-depositing fibroblast program — and are the two separable?

Domain 2 (Therapeutic Target Prioritization) · Episode 1 · Lead working proposal
Parents: expert seeds D2-T2 (`[prior hypothesis]`) and D2-T1 (`[prior hypothesis]`), both assessed `repairable`; operation = targeted-repair crossover.

---

## 1. The scientific opening

The opening is an observation in the frozen source itself, not a conjecture I imported.

`E-MTAB-8873.idf.txt` (Experiment Description, verbatim) states that in FLS–synovial-tissue-macrophage (STM) co-culture:

> "upon co-culture with MerTK/CD206neg an additional fifth cluster (FLS5) emerged with inflammatory properties In contrast to inflammatory effect of the MerTK/CD206neg STMs on FLS, the MerTK/CD206pos population, especially that isolated from biopsies of RA patients in sustained disease remission, induced repair response of FLS as manifested by increased expression of collagens (e.g. COL1A) and TGFb response genes (e.g. TGFBI)"

Two different transcriptional changes are reported and then merged into a single valence word, "protective": (i) *absence/reduction* of the inflammatory FLS program, and (ii) *presence/induction* of a collagen-high, TGFβ-response-high program. The authors read (ii) as repair.

But a collagen-high / TGFβ-response-high synovial fibroblast state is also the molecular description of the **fibroid (pauci-immune) synovial pathotype**, whose clinical valence is genuinely unsettled.

**A correction I have to make against my own first draft.** I initially wrote that the fibroid pathotype is associated with *poorer* DMARD response, which is the version of this claim in common circulation. On inspection of the actual text [K3], that is not what the cohort evidence says: Humby et al. 2019 (PEAC) state verbatim that "Patients with predominant pauci-immune pathology showed **less severe** disease activity and radiographic progression." What the inspected evidence does support is narrower and differently shaped — DMARD *responsiveness* tracks an immune-cell-rich (lympho-myeloid) synovium, so a stroma-dominant synovium is not a responder signature; Lliso-Ribera et al. 2019 points the same way, with escalation to biologics predicted by the lympho-myeloid rather than the fibroid pathotype. The widely repeated "fibroid = multidrug-resistant" claim derives from R4RA/STRAP, which I could **not** inspect (the retrieval tool's 16,000-character cap from document start never reached the abstract), so it is not cited and is not treated as established here.

This correction does not weaken the opening — it sharpens it. The valence of the collagen-high state is unresolved **in both directions**: it is neither established repair nor established harm. That is precisely why the *decomposition* question is the right one to ask first. If the matrix arm cannot be separated from the inflammatory arm, its valence must be settled before the mechanism can be safely pursued, because therapy would deliver both. If it can be separated, the mechanism can be tuned toward whichever arm turns out to be beneficial, and the valence question becomes answerable downstream rather than blocking.

**This is the gap.** It is not "an unstudied variable combination". It is a specific, load-bearing interpretive ambiguity — a single valence word, "protective", covering two transcriptionally distinct changes — in evidence currently being used to prioritise a drug mechanism.

## 2. Why resolving it matters (the consequential decision)

MerTK/CD206⁺ (TREM2⁺, LYVE1⁺) synovial macrophages are the headline cellular correlate of sustained RA remission, and the corresponding therapeutic programmes — MerTK agonism, TREM2 agonism, GAS6/ProS1-axis restoration, pro-resolving lipid mediator supplementation, and strategies that expand or recruit this subset — are all justified by the claim that this subset *restrains* pathogenic fibroblasts.

- If restraint and matrix induction are **strictly coupled** (one axis, same MerTK-dependence, same dose-response), then a MerTK-agonist strategy cannot deliver inflammatory control without simultaneously driving fibroblasts toward the collagen-high state. There is no therapeutic window for the mechanism alone; the programme needs a downstream node that separates the two, and trials of the mechanism should carry a structural/stiffness endpoint, not only a disease-activity endpoint.
- If they are **separable** — different MerTK-dependence, or different EC50 along the MerTK⁺ macrophage fraction — then a window exists, it is quantifiable now, and the separating node (receptor, mediator class, or dose) is the actual target to prioritise. A low-EC50 inflammatory response with a linear matrix response would mean *partial* MerTK⁺ restoration is the therapeutic goal and full restoration is counterproductive.

A second, sharper possibility arose from my own exploratory probe and then failed its own feasibility test — I record both halves because the sequence matters. The probe (§3.1) suggested the MerTK axis also sustains fibroblast **TNFSF11/RANKL**, which would have replaced an ambiguously-valenced collagen state with a quantity of known harmful direction (osteoclast-mediated bone erosion) and an existing countermeasure (RANKL blockade), implying an erosion endpoint, a RANKL:OPG safety biomarker, and an anti-RANKL combination hypothesis. **But RANKL is effectively absent from the targeted panel of the confirmatory dataset (57 molecules in total; §6.2.1), so this proposal cannot test it.** It is carried as a named prediction with a named missing dependency, not as a result or a testable arm. Promoting it would require data this island does not contain.

Either answer to the *testable* question changes the next research decision. A null/inconclusive answer with honest bounds also changes it, by telling us the available human ex vivo material cannot adjudicate and that the question needs a dedicated, adequately replicated co-culture series with matrix and stiffness readouts.

## 3. Strongest claim the existing evidence already supports (NOT what I am testing)

Established, and I will not re-litigate or rebrand it:
1. MerTK⁺CD206⁺ STM subsets are enriched in sustained RA remission and are depleted in active disease [K1].
2. MerTK⁻CD206⁻ STMs induce an inflammatory FLS state; MerTK⁺CD206⁺ STMs do not, and the MerTK⁺ effect is MerTK-dependent (UNC1062 arms) [K1, and the frozen IDF text above].
3. Fibroblast inflammatory and tissue-destructive effector functions are anatomically and functionally separable in arthritis [K2].
4. DMARD responsiveness in early RA tracks an **immune-cell-rich (lympho-myeloid)** synovium; a stroma-dominant synovium is not a responder signature [K3]. Note what this does **not** say, per §1: the inspected text reports pauci-immune pathology with *less* severe disease activity and radiographic progression. "Fibroid = worse outcome" is **not** established background here.

### 3.1 An exploratory Lead observation that sharpened the hypothesis — NOT a result

While checking whether E-MTAB-8316 measures the modules at all (and whether its `cell_type:"monocyte"` label is wrong — §6.3), I computed per-pair log2 fold changes. **This is a feasibility probe, not an executed experiment, and it is reported here because it changed my design and because concealing a peek would be worse than disclosing it.** Script and raw output are attached: `work/lead-diagnostic/emtab8316_marker_and_direction_probe.py`, `..._probe_output.txt`.

What it is: 6 paired transwell experiments (UNC106238 250 µM vs none, both arms LPS-pretreated), 4 FLS donors (SA049 used twice, SA114 used twice), median-of-ratios normalisation over 18 179 genes positive in all 12 samples (size factors 0.885–1.151). Gene-level. **No model, no donor random effect, no multiple-testing control, no independent-filtering of low counts.**

Direction of change on MerTK **blockade** (median log2FC, pairs with increase / 6):

| module | genes | direction on blockade |
|---|---|---|
| A inflammatory | CXCL8 +2.27 (6/6), IL6 +1.19 (6/6), CXCL1 +2.08 (5/6), CCL2 +0.52 (5/6) | **UP, large** |
| C destructive protease | MMP3 +3.39 (5/6), MMP1 +2.08 (5/6), MMP13 +0.99 (4/6) | **UP, large** |
| B matrix | TGFBI −0.45 (0/6), FN1 −0.74 (1/6), POSTN −0.51 (1/6), COL1A2 −0.49 (1/6), COL1A1 −0.41 (1/6); COL3A1 +0.04 | **DOWN, small but directionally consistent** |
| D osteoclastogenic | TNFSF11/RANKL −1.42 (1/6), TNFRSF11B/OPG −0.54 (1/6) | **DOWN** — but TNFSF11 is low-abundance |

Read in the direction of MerTK *signalling* (the therapeutic direction): MerTK signalling on macrophages appears to **suppress** fibroblast inflammation and MMPs while **sustaining** fibroblast matrix genes and, notably, fibroblast **TNFSF11/RANKL**.

Why this mattered enough to change the design: RANKL from synovial fibroblasts is the proximal driver of osteoclast-mediated bone erosion in RA and is a validated drug target (anti-RANKL reduces erosive progression). Unlike the collagen-high state, whose clinical valence is genuinely ambiguous, a RANKL increase has an unambiguous direction of harm. So the candidate liability of the MerTK axis is now **concretely nameable and separately measurable**, and `TNFSF11` and `TNFRSF11B` are both present in the E-MTAB-8873 445-gene panel, so the same readout exists in the independent contact system.

Honest limits of this probe, all of which the formal test must survive:
- **TNFSF11 is low-abundance** (max 5.7 CPM; ≈0.0–0.1 CPM in both SA049 experiments, i.e. single-digit counts). Its apparent consistency rests on the 3 donors with usable counts. A count-based model with independent filtering could well dissolve it. I am pre-registering it as a *prediction that can fail*, not reporting it.
- Pair SA049/1463 is a near-complete non-responder on almost every gene; donor heterogeneity is real and must be modelled, not averaged away.
- 6 pairs over 4 donors are **not** 6 independent donors (domain guide limit).
- The matrix-module effect sizes (|0.4–0.5| log2) are ~5× smaller than the inflammatory ones (|2–3| log2). That asymmetry is itself the quantitative argument for partial separability — but it equally admits the dull explanation that module B is simply a weaker readout of one coupled axis. Only a model that compares *shapes*, not magnitudes, can tell these apart (§7 Step 2–3).
- Both arms are LPS-pretreated healthy monocyte-derived macrophages in transwell. This is **not** the patient-derived STM contact system, and MerTK blockade of a healthy macrophage is not the same manipulation as comparing MerTK⁺ to MerTK⁻ patient STM subsets.

**Where this sits relative to the literature I inspected (abstracts, Europe PMC, retrieved 2026-10-14).** The established direction of the FLS–macrophage–RANKL axis is *inflammatory*: Li et al. 2026 (Front Immunol, PMID 42389538) show IL-34-driven FLS–macrophage crosstalk raises RANKL and osteoclastogenesis, abolished by denosumab — more inflammation, more RANKL, more erosion. The prevailing therapeutic framing (Wei & Xu 2026, PMID 42769565) is that restoring the pro-resolving/efferocytic macrophage compartment is the goal in difficult-to-treat RA. My probe points the other way: the *pro-resolving* MerTK axis appears to sustain FLS RANKL. If real, that is a counter-directional result against the consensus framing, which is exactly why it is worth a prespecified test — **and exactly why I distrust it**. Counter-directional findings in low-abundance genes are where technical artefacts live. Treating it as a hypothesis to be attacked, not a discovery, is the only defensible posture. I did not find work testing whether the MerTK⁺ STM effect on fibroblasts is one axis or several; shorter Europe PMC queries return the general RANKL-crosstalk literature and the general MerTK/remission literature but not this decomposition. **Absence of hits does not establish novelty** and I have inspected abstracts, not exhaustive full text.

**Consequence for inference integrity.** Because I have now looked at E-MTAB-8316 for this contrast, E-MTAB-8316 is **hypothesis-generating only**. The confirmatory test of the dissociation is prespecified in the *independent* system E-MTAB-8873 (different macrophage source — patient STM subsets, not inhibitor-treated healthy MDM; different configuration — contact, not transwell; per-cell rather than bulk; plus a dose axis that E-MTAB-8316 does not have). The E-MTAB-8316 analysis is still run formally, but it is reported as exploratory-origin re-estimation with that circularity stated.

### 3.2 Executed feasibility diagnostics that constrain this proposal — including three results against it

Two managed compute jobs ran in this episode (`[research job]`, 34.5 s, 4 CPU; `[research job]`, 17.5 s, 4 CPU). Full record: `work/methods/method-comparison.md`. These are **executed diagnostics**, not the experiment. Four findings change the proposal, and three of them cut against my hypothesis.

**(a) Disclosure: E-MTAB-8873 is no longer a blinded confirmatory dataset at module level.** To establish feasibility, the diagnostic computed paired Mpos−Mneg module contrasts on the very dataset I had designated for prespecified confirmation. I authorised the feasibility work; the unblinding is my responsibility, not the worker's. Consequence: **this proposal cannot claim a prespecified confirmatory module-level test.** I state below what was seen, and I relocate the genuinely untested object to something that was *not* run.

**(b) The module-level dissociation is NOT cleanly present (against my hypothesis).** Paired Mpos−Mneg deltas on z-scored modules across the 4 clean pairs: module A = +0.153, −0.412, −0.121, +0.004 (mean −0.094, SD 0.240 — **sign-inconsistent**); module B = +0.063, +0.079, +0.033, −0.014 (mean +0.040, SD 0.041 — 3/4 consistent but very small). The predicted "A down, B up" pattern is not reliably there. Worse for the coupling framing: r(A,B) across all 33 samples is −0.649, but **+0.032 once the LPS positive controls are excluded** (−0.300 donor-centred). The apparent reciprocity between inflammation and matrix is largely an artefact of including extreme LPS-stimulated arms. Module C tracks inflammation, not matrix (r(A,C)=+0.70, r(B,C)=−0.73), which does at least confirm the A/C grouping in §6.2.1.

**(c) The baseline's inference layer is provably empty (hard feasibility limit).** With 4 donor-matched pairs, the exact two-sided sign-flip permutation test has minimum attainable p = 2/16 = **0.125**. Simulated power at α=0.05 is **0.000 at every effect size for n=4 and n=5**; it first becomes non-zero at n=6 (0.35 at 1.0 SD) and reaches 0.64 at n=8. Observed p-values were A 0.75, B 0.25, C 0.875, interaction 0.50. **My earlier falsification criterion — "95 % interval excluding 0" — is unattainable by permutation in this design and has been rewritten (§9).** This is the single most consequential thing I learned this episode.

**(d) The dose axis (H1b) is largely unidentified.** The SA263R series contains a second 0 % arm using the other macrophage donor; the donor swap alone accounts for **55 % (module A) and 70 % (module B)** of the apparent 0→100 % dose effect. The SA258R series is **perfectly collinear** (MerTK⁺ fraction = SA267R-donor fraction) and is formally unidentified. The two dose donors also disagree on shape: SA258R shows the inflammatory axis ~94 % suppressed already at 25 % with a flat matrix axis (the therapeutic-window pattern), SA263R shows graded inflammation with monotonically rising matrix. n=2 cannot adjudicate. H1b is demoted from a test to a partially-identified description with the measured confound bound attached.

**(e) One finding in my favour:** unsupervised NMF at k=6 **does** resolve separable axes — an inflammatory factor correlating r=+0.915 with module A and +0.099 with module B, and a collagen factor correlating +0.501 with B and −0.287 with A, cell-level usage correlation −0.208, reproduced at k=5,7,8. (Subject to the initialisation caveat below and the panel-artefact control in §9.)

### 3.3 The gating check, which reverses §3.2 and is the most consequential result of this episode

I commissioned the gating diagnostic independently of the method branch, and the two disagree. **The independent check wins, and it goes against the method branch and against my own convenience.** Record: `work/bindings/binding-facts.json`, `work/bindings/out/gate_summary.json`, `work/bindings/out/gate_percell.tsv`.

The method branch concluded from 3 samples that the deposited matrices were "already FLS-restricted" and that gating was "nearly a no-op". Across **all 33 samples** with a 12-feature immune score, a 10-feature stromal score and an FLS-only reference calibration (3,684 reference cells), that is wrong. A Gaussian-mixture fit on the 16,878 co-culture cells is decisively **bimodal** — ΔBIC(2−1) = **−13,178**, with a minor component of weight 0.057 at mean +0.79 against the major component at −10.31 (SDs 1.73 and 1.01). A well-separated macrophage population is present and is cleanly gateable. The method branch's 3 chosen samples all came from run R2, which happens to be the *least* contaminated run.

**And the contamination covaries with the exposure of interest** — median macrophage-like fraction per gated sample:

| arm | n samples | median | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| FLS-only | 5 | **0.08 %** | 0.00–1.12 % |
| MerTK⁻ (Mneg) | 11 | **10.3 %** | 1.50–22.6 % |
| MerTK⁺ (Mpos) | 16 | **2.3 %** | 0.87–26.8 % |

Mneg co-cultures carry roughly **4–5× more macrophage content than Mpos co-cultures**, and FLS-only arms essentially none. This is the worst possible confounding structure for the pseudobulk estimand, because macrophages are themselves high in exactly module A (CXCL8, IL6, CCL2, IL1B) and low in collagen. **A large part of the published "Mneg induces an inflammatory FLS state while Mpos induces collagen" contrast is therefore recoverable as a pseudobulk composition artefact rather than an FLS state change.** H2 is not a weak rival to be dismissed — on this evidence it is the *leading* explanation at pseudobulk level, and Step 0 is restored to a genuine go/no-go gate rather than the confirmation step I had downgraded it to twenty minutes earlier.

Two consequences I must not soften:
1. **Every module-level number in §3.2(b) is uninterpretable as an FLS effect** until recomputed on gated FLS cells. I am not retracting those numbers — they stand as a description of the pseudobulk — but they cannot be read as fibroblast biology, and the direction of the artefact (more macrophages in Mneg ⇒ apparently more inflammatory Mneg) is the same direction as the hypothesis, so the artefact flatters the published claim.
2. **A new rival that gating alone does not fix:** differential **ambient/soup RNA**. With 10–22 % macrophages in Mneg wells and ~2 % in Mpos wells, the free-mRNA background differs systematically between arms, so even correctly gated FLS cells will carry more macrophage-derived ambient counts in Mneg arms. Gating removes macrophage *cells*, not macrophage *mRNA in FLS droplets*. This must be handled by ambient-RNA estimation from the empty-droplet/low-count distribution or a SoupX-style correction, with the macrophage-like fraction retained as a per-sample covariate and a sensitivity analysis restricted to the low-contamination run R2. If the dissociation survives only in uncorrected data, it is not a finding.

**(f) A reframing that matters clinically.** Relative to the FLS-only arm, Mpos **raises both** module A (+0.154, 4/4 donors) and module B (+0.039, 4/4). MerTK⁺ macrophages do not restore the unstimulated fibroblast state. "Protective" is a statement purely *relative to MerTK⁻ macrophages*, not an absolute anti-inflammatory effect. Any therapeutic reading of this axis must carry that qualifier, and the original "repair response" language obscures it.

**(g) Corrected data facts.** The deposit has **20,562 cells, not the 32,141 FLS** claimed in the IDF (runs R3/R4 are absent). The 3 technical replicates per condition were **pooled before Sample-Tag labelling and are not recoverable**, so my plan to average them is moot — they are already pooled, which removes that pseudo-replication risk but also removes a variance estimate. E-MTAB-8316 has **no FLS-alone arm and no Mpos/Mneg contrast** at all.

**What this does to the proposal.** It converts it from "test a likely dissociation" into something more defensible and, I think, more useful: **the module-level version of the question is under-powered to the point of being unanswerable in this material, and the measured reciprocity is largely an LPS artefact — so the well-posed remaining question is about the *rank and per-cell structure* of the response, not the sign of two prespecified scores.** The genuinely unexecuted object is stated in §7 Step 2 and §11.

### 3.4 Completed gating diagnostic: gating is feasible, and it removes the matrix arm while sharpening the inflammatory arm

The bindings branch finished after §3.3 was written and delivered per-gene gated results (`work/bindings/gating-diagnostic.md`; jobs `[research job]`, `[research job]`, `[research job]`, `[research job]`). Four findings, two of which cut against this proposal harder than anything before them.

**(h) Gating verdict: CLEAN, with a correction to my own §3.3 reasoning.** The prepared pseudobulk column is the *exact* row-sum of the matching `R*_*_RSEC_MolsPerCell.txt.gz` for 33/33 samples (`max_abs_diff = 0.0`) — no FLS selection was ever applied, so per-cell gating is mandatory, not optional. Per cell the two populations are essentially non-overlapping (k=2 components 11.1 log2 units apart, >6 SD), the donor-anchored and pooled-reference gates agree (1,042 vs 1,023 macrophage-like calls), false-positive macrophage rate on the five no-macrophage tags is 0.00–1.12 %, and 195 cells (0.95 %) are doublet-like and are excluded rather than assigned. **But ΔBIC is the wrong criterion and my §3.3 use of it was loose:** a 2-component model wins in all 33 samples, yet in only **17** does the high component sit where macrophages actually are (μ_hi ∈ [−0.71, +1.42], 3.8–17.7 SD separation, 0.967–0.997 agreement with the quantile gate). In the other 16 it splits the FLS continuum (μ_hi ∈ [−10.6, −6.2], agreement 0.27–0.56). The correct criterion is the **location** of the high component. Those 16 are exactly the samples with <2.5 % macrophages, including all five FLS-only tags and all of run R2 — absence of macrophages to find, not gate failure. 19,325 gated FLS against the 32,141 in the IDF, because runs R3/R4 were never deposited.

**(i) The composition confound is confirmed at matched-pair level.** Recovered macrophage-like % in the four donor-matched pairs — SA256A/SA259A 16.30 vs 20.37; SA257A/SA266A 0.78 vs 2.27; SA266A/SA277A 5.66 vs 10.58; SA263R/SA278R 2.42 vs 8.86 — i.e. **4/4 pairs carry more macrophages in the Mneg arm** (ratio 1.25–3.66). Cell-intrinsic specificity within the panel: MMP9 +8.78, SPP1 +7.64, TNF +6.87, IL1RN +5.13, IL1B +4.26, MMP12 +3.46, CXCL5 +1.15, CXCL8 +1.11 log2 macrophage-over-FLS; MMP9 and SPP1 shift >30 % after gating in 4/4 pairs and **flip sign in 3/4**. Composition-safe and FLS-specific: COL3A1 −3.82, COL1A1 −3.76, COL1A2 −2.93, MMP2 −2.05, FN1 −1.75, TIMP1 −1.54, TGFBI −1.32. Each module A/C gene must now be labelled composition-exposed or composition-safe, and the exposed ones cannot carry a dissociation claim.

**(j) The matrix arm does not survive gating — and my §3.2(b) "consistent module B increase" was a denominator artefact.** The matrix module is **~58 % of all panel molecules** (log2 module-share 12.2–12.7), a ceiling with almost no dynamic range. Per-gene gated Mpos−Mneg deltas across the four pairs: COL1A1 [0.11, 0.15, −0.06, −0.10], COL1A2 [0.01, 0.22, 0.01, 0.07], COL3A1 [0.35, −0.28, −0.06, −0.13], TGFBI [−0.09, 0.06, −0.03, 0.00], TGFB1 [−0.27, 0.47, 0.02, −0.11] — **near zero, sign-inconsistent**. The all-positive module-B delta I reported in §3.2(b) (+0.019, +0.126, +0.003, +0.032) is a ceiling artefact of the panel-share denominator and **is not evidence of collagen induction**. I withdraw that reading of my own number. The primary metric changes to **per-gene CPM-within-panel on gated FLS cells**, with module shares demoted to secondary.

**(k) The inflammatory arm is directionally inconsistent after gating, but a sharper and unanticipated candidate appears.** Gated inflammatory module-share deltas: +0.681, −2.689, +0.093, −0.938 log2 — only 2/4 show restraint. However the most *consistent* restrained genes are not IL6 or CXCL8 but the **CXCR3 ligands: CXCL11 negative in 4/4 gated pairs, CXCL10 and CXCL9 in 3/4**. That is a specific, interferon-axis-shaped candidate rather than a general cytokine shutdown, and it is one that neither the module definitions nor [K1]'s "repair response" language anticipated. **It is a four-pair observation on single-tag point estimates with no replicate at the level of the contrast, generated after I had already looked at these data; it is a hypothesis for independent confirmation, not a result.** It also carries a caveat the branch flagged: the −2.689 pair's own gate reference is impure (`R2_SA257A_FLS_only` immune-fraction p99 = 0.0909 vs 0.0014–0.0106 for the other four donors), so its donor-anchored threshold is ~60× looser and that pair requires the pooled threshold as a sensitivity analysis.

**(l) Dose arm: not verifiable as a measured dose.** Designed MerTK⁺ fraction vs recovered macrophage %: R2 (SA258R) 0→1.50, 25→2.05, 75→1.22, 100→0.99 (r = −0.739); R5 (SA263R) 0→22.22, 25→27.14, 100→2.42, no 75 % point (r = −0.908). The only series with a 75 % point spans ~one percentage point of recovered content and moves the wrong way. Four non-separable explanations, in order of ordinariness: capture/depth (macrophage-like cells carry **half** the panel molecules of FLS, median 2,577 vs 5,102, so they sit near the BD cell-calling threshold and recovery varies by run), Sample-Tag labelling efficiency differing by subset, differential STM survival/adherence over 48 h (a biological effect), and low upstream sort yield. **Consequence:** the mixture arms may be used only as *designed-dose (intention-to-treat)* contrasts on gated FLS, and recovered macrophage fraction must never be presented as a measured dose. This independently confirms the H1b withdrawal in §3.2(d) and extends it.

**Net effect on the proposal.** Gating is feasible, so the primary estimand survives — but the *matrix* half of the originally framed dissociation does not survive gating at per-gene level, and the inflammatory half relocates onto the CXCR3-ligand axis. The question is therefore respecified in §4 away from "A down / B up" toward **rank and per-cell structure of the gated FLS response, with a prespecified CXCR3-ligand confirmation and an explicit null expectation for collagen**. This is a substantive narrowing driven by measurement, not a convenience.

### 3.5 A measurable replacement for the withdrawn module D — and a literature result that breaks one of my controls

The reference branch commissioned on module D (`work/refs-rankl/candidate-refs.json`, `module-d-assessment.md`; 7 inspected works, 15 verbatim public queries recorded, no dataset rows or donor IDs sent) returned three things that change the design. **Module D as formulated stays withdrawn: TNFSF11 is unmeasurable here (57 molecules, §6.2.1) and is not reinstated.** What follows is a *different* readout with a different claim.

**(m) OPG (TNFRSF11B) is measurable and discriminates two rival mechanisms.** TNFRSF11B is a bare panel row at 4.31 molecules per 10k (≈48,000 molecules across the deposit, §6.2.1) — three orders of magnitude above TNFSF11. Two inspected works make **opposite, directional, falsifiable predictions** about it in a TGF-β-responding fibroblast:
 - Hase et al. 2008 [A2, abstract_only]: in **RA** synovial fibroblasts TGF-β *reduced* OPG production (with decreased pSmad2, increased Smad7), while in **OA** fibroblasts the same signal *dose-dependently increased* it. Under this reading a TGF-β-response-high FLS state is OPG-low and erosion-permissive.
 - McDowell-Sanchez et al. 2025 [A6, full_text]: in human **dermal** fibroblasts TGF-β1 *induced* OPG (80-cytokine array, confirmed by ELISA/WB/qPCR), and OPG was not a bystander — an OPG-neutralising antibody reduced TGF-β1-induced α-SMA and fibronectin, recombinant OPG alone induced them, and both were blocked by αVβ3 inhibitors. Under this reading the matrix state is OPG-**high**, ratio-low, bone-protective, and OPG is part of the fibrotic program rather than a readout of it.
 These differ on an observable I can measure: gated-FLS TNFRSF11B as a function of MerTK⁺ exposure and of per-cell matrix-program usage. I prespecify it **two-sided with no favoured direction**, as an **exploratory secondary** arm: it was selected after the data were partly inspected, it is a transcript in a targeted panel, and **without TNFSF11, RANKL/OPG protein, or an osteoclastogenesis assay no erosion or ratio claim is permitted** — OPG can neutralise RANKL post-translationally and the ratio is the quantity that matters. What is permitted is "the pro-resolving macrophage input does / does not move fibroblast OPG, in the direction predicted by [A2] or by [A6] or neither".
 Why this matters clinically: the separability of inflammation from erosion is *established*, not conjectural — Danks et al. 2016 [A3] deleted *Tnfsf11* in Col6a1⁺ cells including synovial fibroblasts and abolished osteoclast formation and bone erosion **while the inflammatory joint score was unchanged**, and T-cell deletion did not protect. So an FLS-side bone axis that moves independently of inflammation is exactly the kind of thing a "protective macrophage" therapy could silently shift.

**(n) [K3] breaks my inhibitor specificity control, and I am recording that rather than burying it.** Ryu et al. 2024 [K3, full_text] is the only inspected work measuring an OPG:RANKL ratio as a function of MerTK: *Mertk* knockout **raised** the Opg/Rankl ratio in whole femur and in calvarial osteoblast precursors, Mertk-KO calvarial cells failed to support osteoclast formation from either WT or KO marrow macrophages (interaction p = 0.003), and the MerTK inhibitor **UNC 569 (20 nM) added to WT calvarial-cell/BMM coculture both reduced osteoclast number and raised the calvarial Opg/Rankl ratio**. The inhibitor therefore acts on the *stromal* cell's own bone axis. **Design consequence:** if RA FLS express MERTK, UNC1062 in E-MTAB-8873 is **not** a clean blockade of macrophage→FLS signalling, and the `FLS-only + inhibitor` arm stops being a specificity control and becomes the **decisive arm** — which is bad news, because that arm exists in **run R1 only** (§6.1). My own measurements say FLS MERTK is low but non-zero: 0.32 molecules per 10k in the 8873 panel audit (§6.2.1) and ~0.1–6.7 CPM in the E-MTAB-8316 fibroblast probe (§3.1). Low but non-zero is the worst case for this control — too little to model confidently, too much to dismiss. Any inhibitor-reversal estimate is therefore reported **conditional on the R1-only controls**, with direct FLS action explicitly listed as an unexcluded alternative (§8 item 13), and H1a is demoted from "pharmacological separability" to "pharmacological separability, confounded by possible direct FLS target engagement".

**(o) Novelty phrasing, corrected by the branch.** The branch's bounded one-index search (15 queries, recorded verbatim) found **no** inspected work linking MerTK signalling or a MerTK⁺/CD206⁺/TREM2⁺/LYVE1⁺ synovial macrophage input to fibroblast RANKL or a fibroblast RANKL:OPG ratio; `TITLE:MERTK AND TITLE:(fibroblast OR synoviocyte)` returned one irrelevant hit. **This does not establish novelty.** [K1] was inspected at abstract level only, and absence of RANKL from a ~150-word abstract does not show it was never measured. The correct phrasing, which I adopt: *not reported as an outcome in the inspected abstract of [K1]*, pending the paper's supplementary differential-expression tables, which were not retrievable. Likewise the review Wei & Xu 2026 [A5, full_text] advocates MerTK-axis agonism as one of five therapeutic axes and contains no passage considering a liability of restoring the pro-resolving compartment — an absence in one review is an observation about that review, not proof that the gap is unoccupied.

## 4. The unresolved claim this experiment tests

**Respecified after §3.4.** The original "A down / B up" framing is retained below for provenance, but the primary test is now the rank and per-cell structure of the **gated** FLS response, with per-gene composition-labelled readouts, an **explicit null expectation for collagen** (§3.4(j) measured gated deltas ≈ 0), and the CXCR3-ligand axis as the prespecified confirmation target rather than IL6/CXCL8.

**H1 (dissociation).** The MerTK⁺CD206⁺ STM effect on RA FLS is *not* a single "less activated" axis. It has components that move in opposite directions — an inflammatory/chemokine module (A) and a matrix-degrading protease module (C) that go DOWN, versus a matrix-deposition module (B: collagens, TGFBI, FN1, LGALS1, TIMPs) that goes UP — and these components are separable, i.e. at least one of the following holds:
 - **H1a (pharmacological separability):** MerTK inhibition (UNC1062 on the STMs) reverses one module substantially more than the other.
 - **H1b (dose separability):** across the 0 / 25 / 75 / 100 % MerTK⁺ fraction mixture series, the two modules have different dose–response shape or half-maximal fraction.
 - **H1c (mediator-class separability):** the two modules behave differently in the transwell (soluble-mediator-only) system E-MTAB-8316 versus the contact system E-MTAB-8873.

**H0 (coupling).** The two modules are a single latent axis: they move with proportional magnitude, with the same MerTK-dependence, the same dose–response shape, and the same contact/soluble behaviour. Under H0 the effect is adequately described by one factor and no window exists.

**H2 (nuisance/alternative, must be excluded before H1 is interpretable).** The apparent FLS "response" is a cell-composition artefact: the E-MTAB-8873 co-cultures are pooled FLS + sorted STMs, so a pseudobulk Mpos-vs-Mneg difference can reflect the macrophage transcriptome and doublet content rather than any FLS state change. This is tested directly and is the gate on the whole primary estimand (§7, §8).

**H3 (per-cell structure).** The Mpos effect is a *composition* shift (a responsive FLS subpopulation plus a refractory residue — which would predict ceiling-limited efficacy) rather than a graded shift of all FLS. This distinguishes "the drug works partially on everyone" from "the drug works on some fibroblasts and leaves a pathogenic residue", which is a different clinical failure mode.

### 6.3 E-MTAB-8316 metadata reconciliation (a repair, not a convenience)

`E-MTAB-8316.design.json` labels all 12 samples `cell_type: "monocyte"`. ## 7. Analysis plan

### Step 0 — gating gate (go/no-go for the primary estimand)
**This is a genuine go/no-go gate and it is load-bearing — see §3.3.** Macrophage content is bimodal and separable (ΔBIC −13,178) but is 4–5× higher in Mneg than Mpos arms (medians 10.3 % vs 2.3 %, FLS-only 0.08 %), in the same direction as the hypothesis. Required outputs: (a) per-sample macrophage-like, doublet-like and gated-FLS fractions for all 33 samples; (b) all downstream analyses on gated FLS cells only, never mixed pseudobulk; (c) macrophage-like fraction retained as a per-sample covariate; (d) **ambient/soup RNA correction** (empty-droplet background estimation or SoupX-style), because gating removes macrophage cells but not macrophage mRNA in FLS droplets, and the ambient background differs systematically between arms; (e) a sensitivity analysis restricted to run **R2**, the least-contaminated run (1–2 %), as the cleanest available test; (f) a stated verdict on whether the effect survives correction. If the dissociation appears only in uncorrected data, it is reported as a composition artefact, not a finding. Note that PTPRC (0.52 per 10k) and MERTK (0.32) are too sparse to gate on alone (§6.2.1); the verified 12-feature immune score is in `work/bindings/out/gate_summary.json`.

From the per-cell `*_RSEC_MolsPerCell.txt.gz` matrices, classify each cell as FLS, STM, or ambiguous/doublet using an immune score (PTPRC, CD14, ITGAM, C1QA, C1QB, FCER1G, MERTK, MRC1, CD163, ITGB2, HLA-DRA, CD74) versus a stromal score (COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, FN1, TIMP3, MMP2, THBS1, TGFBI, LGALS1), with a prespecified ambiguity band. Report per-sample macrophage fraction and doublet fraction, and compare the gated FLS total with the authors' reported 32,141 FLS. **All primary analyses use FLS-gated cells and FLS-gated pseudobulk, never the mixed pseudobulk.** If gating is not separable, H1 cannot be tested in E-MTAB-8873 and the study reduces to E-MTAB-8316 plus a reported negative feasibility finding — that outcome is to be reported, not worked around.

### Step 1 — baseline (prespecified modules)
Per-sample module A/B/C/D scores on FLS-gated pseudobulk (library-size normalised, log1p, gene-centred within dataset). Fit a crossed mixed model with random intercepts for FLS donor, STM donor and run (R1/R2/R5), fixed effects for subset (Mpos/Mneg/none), inhibitor, LPS, and module; the **primary test is the module × subset interaction** on the standardised scale. Two prespecified contrasts: **(A+C) vs B** — the benefit-vs-liability contrast, the primary — and **A vs C** as an internal coherence check (if inflammation and proteases do not group together, the two-axis picture is already wrong and that is reported). Module D is not tested (§6.2.1); OPG and SPP1 are reported descriptively with their low counts stated, and carry no inferential claim. Technical replicates (3 per condition in the protocol) are averaged within condition before inference, per the registry limit; the ordinary donor t-test is not used. Inference by donor-label permutation and by leave-one-FLS-donor-out, reporting intervals, not only p-values.

### Step 2 — learned alternative (what the baseline loses)
A per-cell latent-factor decomposition (consensus NMF / NB factor model) on FLS-gated cells with donor and run covariates, learning response axes de novo. **This is now the primary analysis, not an alternative** — see the selection reasoning below. Deliverables: the number of factors needed to explain the Mpos effect (one axis ⇒ H0; two near-orthogonal axes with opposite signs ⇒ H1); the loading of module A/B/C genes on those factors; the §9 panel-artefact negative control; and a per-cell mixture analysis separating **composition shift** from **graded shift** (H3).

**Mandatory implementation constraints, learned the hard way (§3.2(e)):** use **random initialisation** with ≥100 restarts and cNMF-style consensus. The episode probe's `nndsvdar` initialisation produced matched cosine of exactly 1.000 across all 5 seeds and identical reconstruction error to 2 d.p. — that is near-determinism of the initialiser, **not** robustness of the solution, and reporting it as stability would have been a real error. Rank selection must use held-out **donors**, not held-out cells within donors, or the dependence structure leaks.

**Why this method and not the baseline — the comparison, decided on evidence.** Both were developed on the same question at matched specificity and both were actually run (§3.2). The baseline (prespecified module scores + crossed mixed model, measured 17.5 s) is cheap, interpretable and fully specified, and it is **retained as a prespecified secondary readout**. But it was selected against for two measured reasons, not aesthetic ones. First, it *assumes* the answer: the scientific question is literally the **rank** of the response — one coupled axis or several — and a prespecified two-module score imposes rank 2 by construction rather than estimating it. Second, its inference layer is empty at the available n (min permutation p 0.125; power 0.000 at α=0.05 for n=4–5), so it cannot reject anything, whereas the factor model's deliverable is a stability/structure statement that remains interpretable at n=4 because it borrows strength across ~19,000 cells and ~440 features rather than across 4 donors. What the baseline loses that the alternative recovers: a pseudobulk score falls identically whether every cell drops 20 % or 20 % of cells switch state, and those imply different clinical ceilings (partial efficacy in everyone vs a refractory residue). **Deferred, with the evidence that would revisit it:** genome-wide module *definition* from E-MTAB-8316 (revisit if the panel-artefact control fails, which would make the targeted panel unusable as a discovery substrate); and any VAE/scVI elaboration (revisit only if cell counts grow by an order of magnitude — at 19,179 × 440 there is nothing for a deep generative model to earn).

### Step 3 — mechanistic dose-response
Hierarchical Hill/saturating vs linear vs threshold models for each module (and each learned factor) across the mixture series, partially pooled across the 2 FLS donors and across genes. Compare half-maximal MerTK⁺ fraction and shape between the benefit modules (A, C) and the liability module (B). The clinically decisive quantity is whether the benefit modules saturate at a *lower* MerTK⁺ fraction than the liability modules — that would make partial restoration the therapeutic goal and full restoration counterproductive. **Demoted to a partially-identified description after §3.2(d).** The SA258R series is **formally unidentified** (MerTK⁺ fraction is perfectly collinear with the SA267R-donor fraction) and the SA263R series' donor swap alone accounts for a measured 55 % (A) and 70 % (B) of the apparent 0→100 % effect. The two donors also disagree on shape — SA258R shows the window pattern (inflammation ~94 % suppressed at 25 %, matrix flat), SA263R shows graded inflammation with rising matrix. With n=2 and that confound, no EC50 is identifiable. Deliverable: the two donor-specific curves shown separately, the measured confound bound attached, and EC50 reported as an interval **or withheld entirely**. H1b cannot be tested here; the honest statement is that a dose-separability test requires a dose series in which macrophage donor-of-origin is held fixed across doses, which this deposit does not contain.

### Step 4 — MerTK-dependence and specificity
Mpos ± UNC1062 (3 FLS donors) on modules A, B, C separately; the ratio of reversal between the benefit set (A, C) and module B is the H1a readout. The directional prediction transferred from §3.1 is demanding and easily falsified: MerTK blockade should move A and C *up* while moving B *down*, i.e. three modules in two opposite directions within a single arm, in a *different* system (patient STM subsets, contact) from the one that generated the prediction. Interpretation is conditioned on the R1-only specificity controls (FLS+inhibitor alone; Mneg+inhibitor). If FLS-only + inhibitor itself moves the modules, direct fibroblast action of UNC1062 is not excluded and H1a is reported as uninterpretable rather than supportive.

### Step 5 — independent-system and in vivo anchoring
(a) E-MTAB-8316: genome-wide differential expression (paired by experiment, 6 pairs, FLS-donor random effect), module A/B/C behaviour, and genome-wide discovery of the response axes; tests H1c (soluble vs contact). (b) E-MTAB-8322: do macrophages from remission vs naive/resistant RA differ in expression of the candidate mediator genes implicated in Steps 2/5a? Programme-level only; `SA149/SA149P` excluded from inference per the registry; no donor join. (c) GSE95588: descriptive comparator only — does any DMARD in the panel move modules A and B in opposite directions (i.e. is module dissociation pharmacologically achievable at all)? Donor mapping is unresolved, so this is exploratory with no inferential claim and no use as validation of GSE57723-derived data.

## 8. Competing explanations and what distinguishes them

1. **Cell-composition / doublet artefact** (H2). Distinguished by Step 0 gating, by reporting macrophage and doublet fractions per sample, and by repeating Step 1 on FLS-gated cells only and on a doublet-excluded subset. If the apparent effect disappears after gating, the "protective" signal in pseudobulk was compositional.
2. **Technical-replicate pseudo-replication.** The protocol used 3 technical replicates/condition; treating them as independent would manufacture significance. Averaged within condition before inference.
3. **STM donor identity, not subset.** The Mpos and Mneg arms sometimes come from different donors; only the 4 same-donor pairs enter the primary test.
4. **Donor disease state of the STM source** (active vs remission) and **prior DMARD exposure of remission donors** — a carry-over drug effect could masquerade as a subset property. Addressed descriptively by the 3 within-FLS Mpos(A) vs Mpos(R) pairs; drug histories are not in the metadata, so this rival cannot be excluded and is recorded as requiring clinical adjudication.
5. **FLS-intrinsic refractoriness** (FLS donor state modifies the response). 3 active vs 1 remission FLS donor in the clean pairs — too few; reported as a direction with an interval, not a conclusion.
6. **Sorting purity and survival/viability differences** between Mpos and Mneg co-cultures (more dead FLS ⇒ apparent score shifts). Checked via per-cell QC (molecules/cell, detected genes) and by including cell-count and depth covariates.
7. **Panel truncation.** 445 features cannot see most of the matrix programme; an apparently "selective" effect could be panel-limited. E-MTAB-8316's 58,302 features are the corrective, with the caveat that it is a different system.
8. **Culture artefact.** Passaged FLS drift; 48 h contact co-culture is not synovium. In vivo anchoring (E-MTAB-8322) is correlative only and cannot establish the co-culture result transfers to tissue.
9. **Batch/run** confounded with FLS donor (R1/R2/R5). Run is modelled; the confounding with FLS donor is partial and reported.
10. **Low-count instability of module D.** TNFSF11 is near-zero in 2 of 6 E-MTAB-8316 pairs, so its apparent consistency could be a low-count artefact of the ratio estimator. Addressed by count-based modelling with independent filtering, by reporting detection rates per sample, and by the prespecified unmeasurable verdict in §9 rather than a forced estimate.
11. **RANKL transcript ≠ erosion.** No osteoclasts, no bone and no RANKL protein measurement exist in any of these datasets, and OPG can neutralise RANKL post-translationally. An FLS *TNFSF11* increase is a mechanistic hypothesis about erosive potential, not evidence of erosion. The RANKL:OPG ratio partially addresses the balance at transcript level only. Osteoclastogenesis co-culture assays and protein measurement are required and are explicitly out of scope (§10).
12. **Soluble-vs-contact mediator identity.** E-MTAB-8316's effects are necessarily soluble-mediated; E-MTAB-8873's may be contact-mediated. A module that dissociates in one configuration and not the other (H1c) is informative, but a failure to replicate across them is ambiguous between "contact-dependent mechanism" and "different macrophage source". Both readings are reported; neither is asserted.

13. **UNC1062 acts directly on the FLS, not only on the macrophage** — the control-breaking rival of §3.5(n). [K3] showed a MerTK inhibitor applied to a stromal cell changed that cell's own OPG/RANKL balance, and FLS MERTK here is low but non-zero (0.32 per 10k in panel, ~0.1–6.7 CPM in E-MTAB-8316). The `FLS-only + inhibitor` arm is the only discriminator and exists in **run R1 only**. Unexcludable with this deposit; every inhibitor-reversal number is reported conditional on that single-run control, and H1a is labelled confounded.
14. **Module-share denominator / ceiling artefact.** The matrix module is ~58 % of panel molecules and the inflammatory module 0.3–3 %, so a share-based metric is near-saturated for the former and volatile for the latter, and a Mpos-vs-Mneg module-share difference can be produced by movement in the *other* module alone. This explained my own §3.2(b) result (§3.4(j)). Addressed by per-gene CPM-within-panel as primary, and by reporting both metrics.
15. **Differential cell capture/recovery rather than differential biology.** Macrophage-like cells carry half the panel molecules of FLS (median 2,577 vs 5,102) and so sit near the BD cell-calling threshold, with run-level variation in gated-FLS depth (R1 5,676 / R2 4,464 / R5 5,672). Recovered macrophage fraction is therefore a capture quantity as much as a biological one — the direct reason the dose arm cannot be read as a measured dose (§3.4(l)). Sample-Tag labelling efficiency differing between MerTK⁺ and MerTK⁻ STMs, and differential STM survival/adherence over 48 h, are not separable from it in this deposit.
16. **Disease state of the macrophage donor, not MerTK subset.** 3 of 4 matched pairs use active-RA macrophage donors; only SA278R is a remission donor (§6.4). Anything framed as a "remission macrophage" property rests on one pair here and must be reported as such.
17. **Post-hoc selection of the CXCR3-ligand axis.** CXCL9/10/11 were not prespecified; they emerged from gated data I had already inspected (§3.4(k)). The honest status is a hypothesis for independent confirmation, and the prespecified confirmation route is §9.
18. **OPG direction is not predicted by this proposal.** Two inspected works predict opposite signs in a TGF-β-responding fibroblast ([A2] down in RA FLS, [A6] up in dermal fibroblasts, where OPG is itself pro-fibrotic via αVβ3). A two-sided test is therefore informative either way, but a null is uninformative at 4.31 molecules per 10k without a detection-power statement.

## 9. Falsification criteria and interpretation

Prespecified before fitting:

**Rewritten after §3.2(c).** The original criteria assumed α=0.05 significance was attainable. It is not: with 4 matched pairs the minimum permutation p is 0.125 and power at α=0.05 is measured at 0.000. Criteria that cannot discriminate are worse than none, so the primary criteria are now **rank-based and stability-based**, with effect sizes and intervals reported throughout and **no significance claim at module level**. Any criterion below that still refers to a module-level contrast is explicitly *descriptive*, because that contrast has already been inspected (§3.2(a)).

- **Supportive of H1 (dissociation) — primary, on the unexecuted object.** Consensus NMF with **random initialisation** (not `nndsvdar`) over ≥100 restarts on FLS-gated cells yields ≥2 factors whose Mpos-vs-Mneg usage shifts have **opposite sign**, with |cosine| < 0.3 between their gene loadings, and this holds in **≥3 of 4 leave-one-FLS-donor-out refits** and under **donor-label permutation** (observed opposite-sign structure outside the permutation distribution's central 80 %, reported as an exceedance rate, not a p-value). Additionally: a rank-2+ response is preferred over rank-1 by held-out reconstruction on left-out donors.
- **Supportive of H1 (dissociation) — secondary, descriptive.** Inhibitor reversal ratio for (A,C) vs B differing by ≥2-fold with the R1 specificity controls showing no direct FLS effect. Reported as a measured ratio with a bootstrap interval and the n=3 caveat, never as a test. The dose-shape criterion that appeared in my earlier draft is **withdrawn**: §3.2(d) showed the mixture series is unidentified, so no dose-shape comparison can support or refute anything here.
- **Adverse to H1 (supports H0, coupling).** Consensus NMF under random init yields a single factor carrying the Mpos effect, with any second factor's usage shift unstable across leave-one-donor-out refits or indistinguishable from the permutation distribution; and rank-1 is not beaten on held-out donors. This would be a *useful negative*: the mechanism cannot be tuned along this axis and MerTK-axis therapeutics should carry structural endpoints.
- **Adverse to the whole direction — and already partly observed.** §3.2(b) found module A sign-inconsistent across the 4 pairs and r(A,B)≈0 once LPS arms are excluded. If the per-cell factor analysis also returns no stable opposite-sign structure, the honest conclusion is that **the "protective" effect in this material is not decomposable with the data that exist**, and the direction should be parked pending a larger co-culture series. I commit to that conclusion if the stability criteria fail, rather than reporting the k=6 factor correlations from §3.2(e) as if they were the answer — they came from a near-deterministic initialisation (matched cosine 1.000 across all 5 seeds, reconstruction error identical to 2 d.p.), which is a **negative methodological result about the probe, not evidence of robustness**.
- **Inconclusive.** Intervals wide enough to include both proportional and opposite movement; or gating fails; or specificity controls show direct FLS action of UNC1062; or the two module definitions (prespecified vs E-MTAB-8316-derived) disagree. An imprecise null does not refute H1 — with 4 clean pairs this is a realistic outcome and must be reported as such.
- **Module D (RANKL): already resolved as UNMEASURABLE, not refuted.** I prespecified a detection threshold and then measured 57 TNFSF11 molecules across ~36,000 cells (§6.2.1). The arm is withdrawn before testing. I am leaving the record of its rise and withdrawal in this document deliberately: it is the clearest illustration that the attractiveness of a hypothesis and the measurability of its readout are independent, and it names the single most valuable follow-up acquisition.
- **Panel-artefact check, required before any dissociation claim is believed.** The 445-feature panel is 399 immune genes plus 46 FLS genes, so an unsupervised decomposition is predisposed to resolve a richly-sampled chemokine axis and a thin matrix axis **whatever the biology**. The dissociation claim is therefore conditioned on a negative control: refit the factor model on **feature-count-matched random subsets** (sample module-B-sized gene sets from the immune block) and show the opposite-sign two-factor structure does *not* arise at the observed strength by construction. If the structure reproduces under matched random subsets, the apparent dissociation is a panel artefact and H1 is not supported in this assay. This is the check most likely to kill the result, which is why it is prespecified rather than left to a reviewer.
- **H2 confirmed instead.** If the effect is composition-driven, report that the pseudobulk-level "protective signature" is not demonstrably an FLS state change in these data.

**Added after §3.4 and §3.5.** Three criteria are new and two earlier ones are now partly pre-empted by measurement.

- **Matrix arm: the null is now the prior, and refuting it requires more than a module score.** Gated per-gene deltas for COL1A1/COL1A2/COL3A1/TGFBI/TGFB1 are already measured at ≈0 and sign-inconsistent (§3.4(j)). A matrix-induction claim is supported only if a per-cell factor carrying collagen/TGFBI loadings shows a Mpos-vs-Mneg usage shift that is (i) consistent in ≥3 of 4 leave-one-FLS-donor-out refits, (ii) outside the donor-permutation central 80 %, and (iii) **reproduced on per-gene CPM-within-panel**, not only on module share. If (iii) fails the claim is a denominator artefact and is reported as refuted. I record now, before fitting, that the measured evidence currently points to **no collagen induction in gated FLS**, and that this is the single most likely way the originally framed hypothesis dies.
- **CXCR3-ligand axis: prespecified confirmation, not a free win.** Supported only if CXCL9/CXCL10/CXCL11 restraint (a) holds in ≥3 of 4 pairs under the **pooled** gate threshold as well as the donor-anchored one — mandatory because the impure `R2_SA257A_FLS_only` reference makes one pair's threshold ~60× looser; (b) survives ambient-RNA correction, since CXCL9/10/11 are interferon-inducible and expressed by macrophages too; (c) is not reproduced at the observed strength by **feature-count-matched random gene subsets** drawn from the immune block; and (d) appears as coherent loading on a single per-cell factor rather than three independently drifting genes. Failure of any of (a)–(d) means the axis was an artefact of my having looked at the data, and I will say so.
- **OPG (TNFRSF11B): two-sided, with a prespecified uninformative-null clause.** Report the gated-FLS Mpos-vs-Mneg TNFRSF11B estimate with its interval and its per-sample detection rate. Direction consistent with [A2] (down) or with [A6] (up) in ≥3 of 4 pairs, surviving ambient correction, is a substantive mechanistic observation in either direction and discriminates two published mechanisms in the tissue where they are reported to invert. **A null is declared uninformative, not supportive of coupling,** unless the measured detection power excludes an effect of the size [A2]/[A6] report. **No RANKL:OPG ratio, osteoclastogenesis or erosion claim is permitted under any outcome** — TNFSF11 is unmeasurable here (§6.2.1) and protein/functional assays are absent.
- **Inhibitor arms: pre-emptively demoted.** Because [K3] makes direct FLS target engagement live (§3.5(n)) and the specificity controls exist in run R1 only, no inhibitor-reversal result can by itself support H1a. The strongest permitted statement is a measured reversal ratio with an interval, explicitly conditional on R1, with direct FLS action listed as unexcluded. If the `FLS-only + inhibitor` arm in R1 shows any FLS-intrinsic transcriptional response, H1a is withdrawn outright.
- **Remission-donor framing: conditioned.** Any statement about *remission* macrophages rests on one matched pair (SA263R/SA278R) in this deposit (§6.4) and is reported as a single-pair observation. The subset-vs-disease-state distinction cannot be settled here.

## 10. What each outcome would and would not establish

| Outcome | Establishes | Does NOT establish |
|---|---|---|
| H1 supported (stable opposite-sign factors surviving the panel-artefact control) | In this ex vivo human system the MerTK⁺ STM effect on FLS has rank ≥2, i.e. inflammatory/protease restraint and matrix deposition are separable components, with a candidate separation handle (MerTK inhibition) | That the matrix programme is harmful or beneficial in patients; that the separation transfers to tissue or any clinical endpoint; any dose/EC50 claim (unidentified, §3.2(d)); any RANKL/erosion claim (unmeasurable, §6.2.1); any claim about a specific pro-resolving mediator |
| H1 supported but panel-artefact control fails | Nothing about the biology — the apparent separation is attributable to panel composition | Any dissociation claim whatsoever in this assay |
| H0 supported | The effect behaves as one axis in these data; no tunable window via MerTK fraction or MerTK blockade in this system | That no downstream node could separate them; sufficiency of this panel to see a separation |
| Inconclusive | The available material and replication are insufficient; quantifies how much replication would be needed | Nothing about the biology |
| H2 confirmed | The pseudobulk signature is composition-driven | That there is no FLS state change — only that these data cannot demonstrate one |

**Requires additional data / expert adjudication, explicitly out of scope here:** whether the collagen-high state corresponds to the clinically fibroid pathotype (needs matched histology/pathotype calls, absent here); donor DMARD exposure histories (absent); protein-level matrix deposition, stiffness and invasion assays (wet-lab); **RANKL/OPG protein measurement and an FLS-driven osteoclastogenesis co-culture**, without which the module-D result stays a transcript-level mechanistic hypothesis (wet-lab); any claim about patient outcomes (no clinical outcomes in these datasets); mediator identification (needs lipidomics/secretomics, absent).
