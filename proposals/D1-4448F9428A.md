# Treatment Sequencing After Curative Hepatectomy for HCC: Does Initial Post-Operative Modality Predict Time to Next Escalation, and Does Liver Function Reserve Modify This Effect?

## 1. Unresolved Claim and Clinical Consequence

**Claim under test:** After curative-intent hepatectomy for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the initial post-hepatectomy treatment modality (TACE, ablation, re-hepatectomy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, or observation) differentially predicts time to the NEXT treatment escalation (second or later procedure within 365 days), and this relationship is modified by pre-treatment liver function reserve (albumin, total bilirubin, platelet count).

**Clinical consequence:** If the effect of initial post-hepatectomy treatment on time to next escalation depends on liver function, then the optimal treatment sequence is not universal—it is patient-specific. A patient with preserved liver reserve (high albumin, normal bilirubin, adequate platelets) may derive greater benefit from early aggressive local therapy (TACE, re-hepatectomy) than a patient with compromised reserve, for whom observation or systemic therapy may be more appropriate. This directly informs the multidisciplinary tumor board decision of "what to give next after hepatectomy" and supports the emerging concept of "therapeutic time migration" [K1], where treatment timing is a dynamic therapeutic biomarker rather than a fixed stage-dependent sequence.

**Why this is unresolved:** Current evidence establishes that (a) recurrence rates after curative hepatectomy exceed 70% [K3], (b) treatment sequencing matters—systemic therapy introduced earlier may improve outcomes in selected patients [K1], and (c) patient characteristics modify treatment benefit (e.g., sarcopenia abolishes the survival benefit of triple therapy over TACE alone [K2]). However, no study has tested whether the *type* of initial post-hepatectomy treatment differentially predicts time to *next* escalation, and whether liver function reserve (as measured by standard, readily available labs) is the effect modifier. The IMbrave050 trial showed that adjuvant immunotherapy benefit is not universal [K3], but did not test liver function as an effect modifier of treatment sequencing.

## 2. Rival Explanations

| Rival | Prediction | How to distinguish |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Clinician selection bias** (sicker patients get more aggressive therapy) | Treatment modality is a proxy for disease severity; apparent treatment effects are confounded by unmeasured tumor burden | Adjust for AFP, tumor size (if available), and recurrence diagnosis codes; sensitivity analysis with IPTW |
| **Stage/burden confounding** | Patients with larger/more numerous tumors get TACE or re-hepatectomy; smaller tumors get observation | Include AFP and recurrence diagnosis as covariates; stratify by time to first recurrence |
| **Surveillance intensity** | Patients receiving more aggressive initial therapy are monitored more closely, detecting recurrences earlier (lead-time bias) | Compare encounter frequency pre- and post-treatment; sensitivity analysis excluding very early recurrences (<30 days) |
| **Immortal time bias** | Patients who survive long enough to receive treatment are healthier | Use time-dependent exposure coding; landmark analysis at 30 days post-hepatectomy |

## 4. Population and Temporal Boundaries

### Inclusion criteria
1. Patient has ≥1 hepatectomy procedure (any of the hepatectomy keywords) in the procedures file
2. HCC diagnosis present (any HCC keyword in diagnoses file, any encounter)
3. First hepatectomy date defined (earliest hepatectomy 开始时间)
4. Age ≥ 18 years (from encounters file, 年龄 column)

### Exclusion criteria
1. No HCC diagnosis in any encounter
2. Hepatectomy performed before 2010-01-01 (data quality; earliest records)
3. No follow-up data (no encounters after hepatectomy)

### Temporal boundaries
- **Index date:** First hepatectomy 开始时间
- **Pre-treatment window:** 0–90 days BEFORE index date (for liver function labs)
- **Follow-up window:** 0–365 days AFTER index date
- **Exposure window:** 0–365 days post-hepatectomy (first qualifying procedure)
- **Outcome window:** 0–365 days post-hepatectomy (time to second or later procedure)

## 5. Variable Definitions

### Exposure: Initial post-hepatectomy treatment modality
- **Definition:** The first procedure (or medication) occurring after the index hepatectomy within 365 days, classified into one of six categories:
  1. TACE (first TACE procedure)
  2. Ablation (first ablation procedure)
  3. Re-hepatectomy (second hepatectomy procedure)
  4. Immunotherapy (first ICI medication or 抗肿瘤免疫治疗 procedure)
  5. Targeted therapy (first TKI medication or 分子靶向治疗 procedure)
  6. Observation (no qualifying procedure/medication within 365 days)
- **Coding rule:** If multiple modalities occur in the same encounter, the procedure with the earliest 开始时间 determines the category. If a procedure and medication occur on the same day, the procedure takes precedence.
- **Time of exposure:** 开始时间 of the first qualifying event (0 = observation, coded at 365 days)

### Outcome: Time to NEXT treatment escalation
- **Definition:** Time from index hepatectomy to the SECOND or LATER qualifying procedure (any modality) within 365 days
- **Event:** Second or later procedure of any type (TACE, ablation, re-hepatectomy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy)
- **Censoring:** 365 days post-hepatectomy (administrative), or last known follow-up if < 365 days
- **Note:** This is NOT the time to first procedure (which defines the exposure). The outcome is the time to the NEXT escalation after the initial treatment.

### Effect modifier: Pre-treatment liver function reserve
- **Albumin:** Most recent 白蛋白 value in the 0–90 day pre-op window (定量结果, g/dL)
- **Total bilirubin:** Most recent 总胆红素 value in the 0–90 day pre-op window (定量结果, mg/dL)
- **Platelet count:** Most recent 血小板计数 value in the 0–90 day pre-op window (定量结果, ×10⁹/L)
- **Composite liver function score:** ALBI grade (albumin-bilirubin grade) computed from albumin and bilirubin:
  - ALBI score = ln(bilirubin) + ln(albumin) × (−1.511) + 0.081
  - Grade 1: score < −2.6; Grade 2: −2.6 ≤ score < −1.39; Grade 3: score ≥ −1.39
- **Stratification:** For interaction analysis, patients stratified by ALBI grade (1, 2, 3) or by tertiles of each individual lab value

### Covariates
- Age (years, from encounters)
- Sex (from encounters)
- AFP (most recent 甲胎蛋白 in pre-op window, log-transformed)
- ALT, AST (most recent in pre-op window)
- Recurrence diagnosis at index encounter (binary)
- Number of prior hepatectomies (0 = first, ≥1 = repeat)

## 6. Analysis Plan

### Primary analysis: Cox proportional hazards model
- **Model:** Cox PH with time-to-next-escalation as outcome
- **Exposure:** Treatment modality (categorical, 6 levels; observation as reference)
- **Effect modifier:** ALBI grade (categorical, 3 levels)
- **Interaction:** Treatment modality × ALBI grade (primary hypothesis test)
- **Covariates:** Age, sex, log(AFP), ALT, AST
- **Assumption check:** Proportional hazards verified with Schoenfeld residuals; if violated, use time-varying coefficients or stratified Cox
- **Output:** Hazard ratios (HR) with 95% CI for each modality vs observation, overall and within each ALBI stratum; p-value for interaction

### Secondary analyses
1. **Stratified Cox:** Separate Cox models within each ALBI grade, comparing treatment modalities
2. **Landmark analysis:** Restrict to patients alive at 30 days post-hepatectomy; re-estimate treatment effects
3. **IPTW sensitivity analysis:** Inverse probability of treatment weighting to address selection bias
4. **Competing risk:** Fine-Gray model with death as competing event (if death data available from encounters)

### Learned alternative: Cox + Gradient Boosting Survival
- **Model:** XGBoost survival (or equivalent gradient-boosted survival model) with:
  - Features: Treatment modality (one-hot), ALBI grade, albumin, bilirubin, platelet, AFP, ALT, AST, age, sex
  - Target: Time to next escalation (censored)
  - Evaluation: Concordance index (C-index), time-dependent AUC at 90/180/365 days
- **Comparison:** C-index of GBM vs Cox (baseline); feature importance for treatment modality × liver function interactions
- **What the learned model reveals that the baseline loses:**
  - Non-linear interactions between treatment modality and continuous liver function values (not just ALBI grade)
  - Higher-order interactions (e.g., treatment × AFP × albumin)
  - Individual-level risk prediction vs population-level hazard ratios
  - Potential identification of subgroups where the treatment effect reverses (not just attenuates)

### Falsification criteria

| Result | Interpretation | Action |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| **Supportive:** Significant treatment × ALBI interaction (p < 0.05); HRs differ across ALBI strata in a clinically meaningful direction (e.g., TACE protective in ALBI-1 but not ALBI-3) | Liver function modifies treatment sequencing effect; supports personalized treatment selection | Continue to external validation; develop clinical decision rule |
| **Adverse:** No significant interaction (p > 0.20); treatment effects consistent across ALBI strata | Liver function does NOT modify treatment sequencing effect; the "what to give next" decision is not liver-function-dependent in this cohort | Abandon liver-function-modified sequencing hypothesis; refocus on treatment modality main effects only |
| **Inconclusive:** Interaction p between 0.05 and 0.20; or significant interaction in one stratum but inconsistent direction | Insufficient evidence to confirm or refute; may be underpowered for interaction | Increase follow-up window; add more effect modifiers; consider external validation cohort |

### What would require additional data
- **Causal claim:** This is an observational study; treatment assignment is not random. A causal interpretation of "TACE reduces time to next escalation" requires assumptions of no unmeasured confounding, which cannot be verified. RCT data or instrumental variable analysis would be needed.
- **Mechanistic claim:** Whether liver function modifies treatment *efficacy* (biological) vs *tolerability* (safety-driven treatment selection) cannot be distinguished without toxicity data or treatment intensity measures.
- **Generalizability:** Single-center data; external validation required before clinical implementation.

## 7. Method Comparison: Baseline Cox vs Cox+GBM

| Dimension | Baseline Cox | Cox + GBM |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Scientific question answered** | Does treatment modality predict time to next escalation, and does ALBI grade modify this effect? | Same, plus: what non-linear patterns exist? Which individual patients are most/least likely to benefit? |
| **Assumptions** | Proportional hazards; linear log-hazard effects; additive interactions | No PH assumption; flexible functional form; captures non-linear and higher-order interactions |
| **Interpretability** | HRs with CIs; clinically actionable (e.g., "TACE HR 0.6 in ALBI-1 vs HR 0.9 in ALBI-3") | Feature importance; partial dependence plots; less directly translatable to clinical guidelines |
| **What it reveals that the other loses** | Clear, pre-specified interaction test with formal p-value; robust to small sample sizes in strata | Non-linear treatment × liver function interactions; individual-level risk scores; potential identification of "sweet spot" liver function ranges where a given treatment is most effective |
| **Risk** | Misses non-linear effects; ALBI grade is a coarse stratification | Overfitting risk with 6,505 events; harder to validate; may find "interactions" that are noise |
| **Compute** | Trivial (CPU, <1 min) | Moderate (CPU, <10 min for XGBoost on ~25K rows) |

**Selection rationale:** The Cox model is the primary analysis because it directly tests the pre-specified hypothesis (treatment × liver function interaction) with interpretable outputs. The GBM is a sensitivity/extension analysis that tests whether the Cox model's linear, additive assumptions are adequate. If GBM reveals substantial non-linear interaction patterns not captured by Cox, this would be a scientifically important finding (justifying more complex modeling for clinical decision support). If GBM adds little beyond Cox, the simpler model is preferred for clinical translation.

## 8. Limitations

1. **Observational design:** Treatment assignment is by clinician choice, not randomization. Unmeasured confounding (tumor size, number, vascular invasion) cannot be fully addressed.
2. **Single center:** Generalizability to other populations (different etiology mix, different treatment protocols) is uncertain.
3. **Procedure coding:** Treatment modality is derived from free-text procedure names; misclassification is possible (e.g., TACE coded as 肝动脉造影 vs 肝动脉栓塞).
4. **Outcome definition:** "Time to next escalation" is a surrogate for recurrence; it does not directly measure survival or quality of life.
5. **Liver function measurement:** Pre-op labs may not reflect the liver function at the time of the post-hepatectomy treatment (which may occur weeks to months later).
6. **Small subgroups:** Immunotherapy (n=155) and targeted therapy (n=46) groups are small; interaction tests within these subgroups will be underpowered.
7. **No imaging data:** Tumor size, number, and vascular invasion status are not directly available; AFP is an imperfect proxy.
8. **Immortal time bias:** Patients who receive early treatment must survive to receive it; landmark analysis partially addresses this.
