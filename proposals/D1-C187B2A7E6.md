# Candidate: Which vasopressor should be withdrawn first after dual-agent shock stabilization?

## Clinical decision, supported evidence, and unresolved claim

**Decision.** An adult ICU patient has received concurrent norepinephrine (NE) and arginine vasopressin (AVP), is now on low doses with stable pressure, and is ready to begin liberation. Should the team withdraw NE first or AVP first?

**Strongest claim already supported.** Guidelines establish NE as first-line and AVP as an adjunct in septic shock, but do not establish withdrawal order. Earlier observational studies often associated AVP-first withdrawal with more hypotension, while a 2025 single-center overlap-weighted cohort of 524 patients found no adjusted difference in 24-hour hypotension (19% AVP-first versus 21% NE-first; P=0.59). A separate 2026 cohort of 100 ventilated septic-shock patients reported outcome differences but had extreme mortality and substantial baseline differences. These data establish genuine uncertainty and plausible short-term hemodynamic consequences; they do not establish causal equivalence, mortality benefit, or an optimal rule.

**Unresolved falsifiable claim.** Among decision-ready adult ICU patients receiving only low-dose NE plus AVP after at least 6 hours of dual therapy, an NE-first liberation strategy reduces 24-hour clinically significant hemodynamic failure by at least 5 absolute percentage points compared with AVP-first liberation, while increasing 7-day mortality by less than 3 percentage points.

This advances prior work by anchoring eligibility *before* withdrawal, emulating strategies with a grace period rather than conditioning baseline on an observed post-choice cessation, using continuous infusion/MAP trajectories, requiring both benefit and mortality safety, and testing transport to a large US critical-care record. It is not a claim that MIMIC alone can dictate practice.

## Target-trial protocol

### Population and time zero

Use the first eligible ICU stay per `subject_id`. Age is `anchor_age + year(intime) - anchor_year`; include age >=18.

Reconstruct infusion episodes separately for NE item 221906 and AVP item 222315 from `icu/inputevents`. Keep positive-rate records not marked `Rewritten`; normalize NE to mcg/kg/min and AVP to units/hour (convert the three observed units/min records by ×60; quarantine the two NE mg/kg/min records unless concentration-based validation is possible). Merge adjacent records for the same `stay_id,itemid,linkorderid` when gaps are <=30 minutes, retaining dose changes. Audit overlap with an episode construction that ignores `linkorderid`.

At hourly boundaries during an active ICU stay, time zero `t0` is the earliest boundary meeting all criteria using only data at or before that boundary:

1. Both reconstructed infusions have been continuously active for >=6 hours.
2. Current NE rate is >0 and <=0.10 mcg/kg/min and AVP rate is >0 and <=2.4 units/hour (0.04 units/min).
3. No epinephrine (221289, 229617), phenylephrine (221749, 229630, 229632), dopamine (221662), or angiotensin II (229709, 229764) is active in the prior 2 hours.
4. At least three valid MAP measurements occur in `t0-2h`; the median is >=65 mmHg, no value is <55, and no two successive measurements are <60. Prefer invasive MAP 220052; use noninvasive MAP 220181 only when no invasive measure exists within ±15 minutes. Valid range is 20–180 mmHg.
5. Neither infusion has a pause >30 minutes in `t0-2h`; the NE dose did not increase >25% and AVP did not increase >0.6 units/hour over that interval.
6. Patient is alive and in the ICU at t0.

Exclude pregnancy-related admission, age <18, active ECMO/IABP, any third vasopressor at t0, missing weight when NE rate cannot be normalized, or a comfort-measures-only order documented before t0. Current-admission discharge diagnosis codes are never used for eligibility. The primary population is treatment-defined recovery from shock; a prespecified likely-infection subgroup requires a blood culture collection and systemic antibiotic administration in the 48 hours before dual-pressor initiation.

### Strategies and assignment

Clone each eligible patient at t0 into both regimes:

- **NE-first:** reduce NE to zero within 6 hours after t0 while AVP remains active until NE cessation; subsequent care is unrestricted.
- **AVP-first:** reduce AVP to zero within 6 hours after t0 while NE remains active until AVP cessation; subsequent care is unrestricted.

Dose tapering within the 6-hour grace period is permitted. Artificially censor a clone at deviation: the nonassigned drug reaches zero first, both reach zero within 30 minutes, a third pressor starts before assigned cessation, or the assigned drug has not ceased by t0+6h. Death before assignment completion remains an outcome, not a deviation. Treat gaps <=30 minutes as uninterrupted; a >30-minute gap defines cessation. A sensitivity analysis uses 15- and 60-minute gaps and 3- and 12-hour grace periods.

The primary estimand is the per-protocol 24-hour post-cessation risk difference (NE-first minus AVP-first) in clinically significant hemodynamic failure under the two grace-period strategies. Each strategy permits cessation at any time through t0+6h, so the estimand marginalizes over strategy-specific cessation timing. Count death between t0 and assigned-drug cessation as primary failure; otherwise observe the failure window from assigned-drug cessation through cessation+24h (no later than t0+30h). The safety estimand is the 7-day all-cause mortality risk difference from t0 through t0+7d. Estimate both in the full eligible population and report the likely-infection subgroup as prespecified heterogeneity, not a replacement primary cohort.

### Outcomes

Primary failure is death from t0 through 24 hours after assigned-drug cessation, or the following coupled hypotension-and-rescue event occurring from assigned-drug cessation through cessation+24h:

- at least two successive MAP values <60 mmHg within 60 minutes (or MAP <55 once), and within the following 2 hours one of:
  - restart of the stopped agent after a >30-minute gap;
  - >=25% increase in the remaining agent’s rate from the rate at cessation, sustained >=30 minutes; or
  - initiation of epinephrine, phenylephrine, dopamine, or angiotensin II.

Death within 24 hours is included as failure. This conjunction reduces labeling of isolated noisy MAPs or routine titration as clinically significant failure. Report each component, 24-hour MAP area below 65, hours alive without either pressor through day 7, new stage 2/3 AKI by day 7, new RRT, ICU-free days to day 7, and 7-day mortality. A sensitivity outcome adds crystalloid >=500 mL or albumin >=25 g within 2 hours, only after a dictionary-vetted bolus item list is frozen.

Mortality uses `admissions.deathtime` and `patients.dod`; discharge alive before day 7 is counted alive unless `dod` falls within the horizon. Report the fraction whose post-discharge status depends on this assumption. AKI uses creatinine 50912/52546 relative to the minimum from hospital admission through t0; stage 2 is >=2× and stage 3 is >=3×, >=4.0 mg/dL, or new RRT. Urine-output AKI is secondary because complete weight-normalized windows are not assured.

## Analysis, baselines, and diagnostics

Fit pooled logistic artificial-censoring models in 30-minute intervals through the grace period, with stabilized inverse-probability weights. Baseline/time-updated features include current and 2/6-hour NE/AVP rates and slopes, MAP summaries and slope, heart rate, lactate/creatinine level and trend, urine output/fluid balance, ventilation, FiO2, RRT, corticosteroids, antibiotics, sedatives, ICU unit, time since ICU/pressor initiation, prior comorbidity, age, sex, race, admission type, and missingness indicators. Do not use data after each interval to predict censoring at that interval. Estimate standardized risks with weighted pooled logistic models, cluster-robust variance by patient, and bootstrap the complete pipeline by patient as primary uncertainty. Truncate weights at 1st/99th percentiles and report untruncated estimates.

Baselines are unadjusted observed first-withdrawal groups; a conventional observed-cessation propensity-overlap analysis; and a simpler t0 landmark analysis among patients adhering within 6 hours. Report cohort attrition, adherence by strategy, event counts, missingness, standardized differences, propensity overlap, weight maxima, effective sample size, and site-era/ICU-unit heterogeneity. Do not compare exact shifted calendar dates across patients; use `anchor_year_group`.

A full archive profile found 459,800 NE and 37,163 AVP input rows; 459,798 NE rows used mcg/kg/min and 37,160 AVP rows used units/hour. A deliberately permissive row-overlap proxy found 5,032 stays with any dual therapy and 1,745 with at least one >=6-hour overlapping record segment. Among those proxy segments, 1,086 appeared NE-first, 283 AVP-first, and 376 near-simultaneous. These are feasibility—not cohort or outcome—counts: linked-record reconstruction, readiness, clinical exclusions, and full strategy classification can materially reduce or change them. The quick record-level “restart” proxy is invalid for outcome inference because continuous titrations create later records; the compiler must use reconstructed episodes.

## Falsification and interpretation

Feasibility gates: >=150 adherent patients and >=30 primary events per arm after weighting; effective sample size >=75 per arm; >=80% of each arm with sufficient MAP sampling; all key SMDs <0.10; no severe positivity failure; and a clear treatment contrast in which the assigned agent reaches zero before the other in >=90% weighted clones. Failure is inconclusive.

Negative controls: after weighting, assigned strategy must not predict pre-t0 2-hour MAP area below 65 or pre-t0 2-hour pressor escalation (absolute standardized effect <0.10). A positive association blocks causal wording. Sensitivities include gap/grace definitions, invasive-MAP-only, alternative dose/readiness cutoffs, exclude cardiac-surgery/cardiogenic phenotype using pre-t0 procedures and prior diagnoses, likely infection, complete-case versus imputation, alternate weight truncation, and quantitative bias analysis for an unmeasured clinician “readiness” preference.

- **Supportive:** NE-first minus AVP-first primary RD <= -5 points, two-sided 95% CI excludes 0, and the upper 95% bound for 7-day mortality RD is <+3 points, with passed diagnostics and directionally concordant major sensitivities.
- **Adverse:** the primary RD point estimate is >=+5 points and its lower 95% bound is >0; or the 7-day mortality RD lower 95% bound is >0 (with >=+3 points denoting harm beyond the prespecified margin). A short-term hemodynamic benefit accompanied by supported mortality harm is adverse for choosing NE-first.
- **Evidence against a clinically important benefit:** a sufficiently narrow interval excludes a 5-point NE-first reduction while mortality is noninferior. This supports little short-term advantage in this decision-ready population, not equivalence in all shock.
- **Inconclusive:** all other patterns, including an upper mortality bound >=+3 points without a lower bound >0, wide intervals, low adherence, poor MAP coverage, failed negative controls/balance/positivity, or discordant episode definitions. Failure to establish noninferiority is uncertainty, not evidence of harm.

Harbor can verify source hashes and headers, joins, timestamp ordering, episode construction, cohort flow, strategy adherence/censoring, outcomes, weights, diagnostics, estimates/intervals, and whether a reported interpretation follows this matrix. It cannot establish that a charted end time equals the bedside decision, that undocumented boluses or pressure episodes did not occur, that the treatment-defined population has a particular shock etiology, exchangeability, transportability, or a clinical recommendation. Critical-care pharmacist/intensivist review of code lists and blinded manual review of at least 100 reconstructed weaning episodes per arm are essential. A prospective multicenter trial is required before a practice mandate.
