# Asynchronous Organ Recovery in ICU: A Falsifiable Test of Recovery Patterns and Mortality

## Scientific Question

Does the temporal pattern of organ recovery (kidney-first, respiratory-first, synchronous, or discordant) after circulatory stabilization predict subsequent ICU mortality and organ support escalation better than single-point assessments like SOFA or creatinine alone?

## Hypothesis to Test

After circulatory stabilization in critically ill patients, those whose kidney and respiratory function recover asynchronously (one system recovers while the other remains impaired) have distinct mortality risks compared to:
- Patients with synchronous recovery (both systems recover within 72 hours of each other)
- Patients whose recovery stalls in one system (discordant recovery)

**Primary hypothesis**: Discordant recovery (kidney recovers without respiratory improvement, or vice versa) is associated with higher 28-day mortality than synchronous recovery, even after adjusting for baseline severity.

## Clinical Importance

Current ICU practice relies heavily on single-timepoint assessments (e.g., SOFA score) to guide decisions about organ support de-escalation. However, SOFA captures only a snapshot and may miss dynamic recovery patterns. Understanding asynchronous recovery could:
1. Identify patients at high risk of deterioration despite apparent improvement
2. Inform timing of organ support de-escalation
3. Reveal that "recovery" in one system may signal instability in another

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

- **Beyond single-point assessment**: SOFA and creatinine-only recovery fail to capture the temporal dynamics of multi-organ recovery
- **Beyond simple lag analysis**: This tests whether the *pattern* of recovery (asynchronous vs. synchronous vs. discordant) has independent predictive value, not just the time difference
- **Mechanistic insight**: Asynchronous recovery may reflect underlying pathophysiology (e.g., inflammatory cascade affecting one system before another) rather than just measurement noise

## Study Population and Main Data

**Population**: MIMIC-IV ICU patients
- **Inclusion**:
  - ICU stay ≥ 48 hours
  - At least one organ support intervention (vasopressors, mechanical ventilation, or renal replacement therapy)
  - ≥ 3 serial measurements within 72-hour windows for:
    - Kidney: creatinine, urine output
    - Respiratory: PaO2/FiO2 ratio, ventilator settings
  - Vital signs available (SBP, HR, SpO2)
- **Exclusion**:

**Scientific question**: Does the recovery pattern add predictive value beyond baseline severity?

## Analysis Plan

1. **Define recovery windows**:
   - Baseline: Day 0 = first circulatory stabilization (vasopressor initiation or SBP ≥ 90 for 2 hours)
   - Recovery window: Days 0-7 post-stabilization
   - Follow-up: Days 0-28 for mortality

2. **Define recovery patterns**:
   - **Synchronous**: Both kidney and respiratory recovery complete within 72 hours of each other
   - **Kidney-first**: Kidney recovers, respiratory continues to improve or worsens
   - **Respiratory-first**: Respiratory recovers, kidney continues to improve or worsens
   - **Discordant**: One system recovers while the other deteriorates or stalls

3. **Recovery completion criteria**:
   - Kidney: 2 consecutive creatinine measurements showing ≥30% decrease from peak, or urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours
   - Respiratory: PaO2/FiO2 ≥300 for 24 hours with FiO2 ≤40%, or spontaneous breathing trial success

4. **Statistical analysis**:
   - Primary: Cox proportional hazards model with recovery pattern as main predictor
   - Secondary: Logistic regression for 28-day mortality
   - Adjustments: Age, sex, admission SOFA, comorbidities, admission diagnosis, baseline organ support

5. **Falsification criteria**:
   - **Supportive**: Discordant recovery has HR > 2.0 for mortality (95% CI excludes 1.0)
   - **Adverse**: No association (HR ≈ 1.0, 95% CI includes 1.0)
   - **Inconclusive**: Discordant recovery has HR < 1.5 but > 1.0 (insufficient power or effect size too small to be clinically meaningful)

## Limitations

1. **Data availability**: Serial measurements may be selectively obtained; patients with frequent monitoring may have different outcomes
2. **Recovery definitions**: Recovery criteria are pragmatic, not physiological gold standards
3. **Confounding**: Time-varying confounding by indication (e.g., worsening organ function may lead to more intensive monitoring)
4. **External validation**: Not performed in this study; requires independent dataset

## Required Clinical Adjudication

- Definition of "circulatory stabilization" may need expert review
- Recovery criteria should be validated against clinical practice
- Causal interpretation requires RCT evidence; this is observational
