# Pre-TACE-targeted test of early hepatic reserve after first TACE

## Hypothesis and clinical advance

In adults with HCC undergoing a first qualifying TACE, among those with at least one numeric pre-TACE primary reserve assay, does the observed early post-TACE trajectory from T0+1 through T0+7 add prognostic information for a new coded hepatic-decompensation encounter on T0+8 through T0+90 beyond pre-TACE state/history and the post-TACE testing process?

The falsifiable hypothesis is that an adverse trajectory—lower albumin or platelets and/or higher total bilirubin or INR—improves held-out proper scoring and has an adverse-direction association with the broad endpoint after accounting for post-TACE test availability, count and timing. The primary estimand is incremental prognostic information in the pre-TACE-observable target population, not a causal TACE effect.

The substantive child repairs the parent’s post-treatment selection problem. The parent required a numeric primary assay in both windows; this child requires pre-window observability only and treats post-window testing as an informative care process. Patients without a post assay remain in the target, with missingness-aware A1 predictions and prespecified sensitivity bounds. The old complete post-assay landmark is retained as a selection diagnostic. This changes the population boundary explicitly while preserving the first-TACE reserve question and outcome window.

The evidence-supported claim is only that dated local records can operationalize this prognostic comparison. The unresolved claim is whether post-TACE assay values contain information beyond pre-state/history and monitoring intensity. No conclusion about true liver failure, causality, mortality or clinical utility is licensed.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use only discovery buckets 0–79 from the catalog rule SHA-256(`ehr-hypothesis-discovery-v1` + NUL + `hcc` + NUL + patient key) mod 100. Buckets 80–99 are inaccessible and are not external validation. Split and bootstrap by `Patient Master Index`, never by row.

1. Read `procedures` and link qualifying rows to `encounters` by `(Patient Master Index, Encounter Number)`. A qualifying row has nonmissing `Start Time` and `Procedure` equal case-insensitively to TACE or containing `chemoembolization`. Deduplicate same-patient/same-encounter procedures and set T0 to the earliest qualifying `Start Time` per patient. T0 is procedure start.
2. At the T0 encounter require age `age` >=18. Require an HCC diagnosis whose `diagnosis name` contains `hepatocellular carcinoma` or `liver cancer` either on the T0 encounter or on an encounter with `admission time` in [T0−180,T0). Preserve `diagnosis type` strata. Diagnoses has no temporal column; this is explicitly a record-time proxy.
3. Exclude any broad prior-decompensation term (`ascites`, `liver failure`, `hepatic failure`, `decompensation`, `hepatic encephalopathy`, `hepatic coma`) on an encounter with `admission time` in [T0−180,T0). Exclude a procedure containing `resection`, `ablation`, `radiofrequency` or `microwave` with `start time` in [T0−7,T0+7]. These are window exclusions, not lifetime exclusions.
4. For `labs`, use exact names `albumin`, `total bilirubin`, `international normalized ratio`, `platelet count`; parse only numeric `quantitative result`, use `test time`, never infer units. Pre-window is [T0−14,T0), post-window is (T0,T0+7]. Same-day records are excluded. Context assays are `creatinine`, `sodium`, `alpha-fetoprotein` and are descriptive/sensitivity only.
5. Primary target eligibility requires >=1 numeric observation from any primary assay in the pre-window. It does not require any post assay. For each assay/window retain median, count, median time, parse failures and missingness. Patients with no post primary assay receive no observed post value; they remain in the target and are not coded as post-test negatives. A complete four-assay pre/post cohort is a prespecified selection-bias diagnostic only.
6. All one-to-many tables are aggregated by `(patient master index, encounter number)` before any join. No post-day-90 row, follow-up anchor, future diagnosis or future treatment enters a day-7 predictor or observation model.

## Outcomes and observation

The primary broad outcome joins `diagnoses` to `encounters` on `(Patient Master Index, Encounter Number)`. A positive is the first encounter diagnosis containing any broad term above with `Admission Time` in (T0+7,T0+90]—equivalently day 8 through day 90—and with no matching term in [T0−180,T0). Event time is the linked admission-time proxy. A negative requires no event through day 90 and an encounter with `Admission Time` or `Discharge Time` >=T0+90. Positive patients remain positive without a later anchor. No-event/no-anchor is unknown, never a negative; unknowns are excluded from the primary binary score only with inverse-probability-of-follow-up sensitivity and pattern-mixture bounds.

Prespecified endpoint tiers are refit with the same split and model rules:

- severe-term: `Liver Failure`, `Hepatic Failure`, `Hepatic Encephalopathy`, `Hepatic Coma`; report `Ascites` and `Decompensation` separately;
- repeat-confirmed: broad terms on at least two distinct encounter IDs separated by at least one day within day 8–90;
- care-concordant: broad event plus a medication name containing `Spironolactone`, `Furosemide`, `Torsemide`, `Lactulose` or `Rifaximin` on the event encounter or a patient-linked encounter from event day through +7 days, with medication `Start Time` in that interval. This is context only: no administration, dose or indication is inferred. If exact strings are absent, the tier is unavailable.
- diagnosis-type and admission-versus-discharge time-proxy strata are reported separately.

## Baseline and alternative

B0 is rare-event penalized logistic regression from pre-state/history: pre-window assay medians, per-assay missingness/count/timing, age, sex, prior encounter count, prior coded liver/decompensation history, diagnosis-type stratum, index department and pre-index treatment-context indicators. B1 adds post-window per-assay availability, count and median timing. B1 is the primary comparator because it holds monitoring intensity constant while testing value of observed assay change.

A1 is an anchored assay-specific irregular random-effects reserve model fit on actual lab times. Training-fold robust medians/IQRs standardize values; assay residual variances and latent pre/post reserve states are fit on train/tune. Albumin and platelets load positively, bilirubin and INR negatively, with albumin loading fixed to +1. The model yields a reserve change, posterior/empirical-Bayes uncertainty and discordant recovery. If a post assay is missing, its latent state is predicted from observed pre-state and the training-fitted phase transition; B1’s post-test indicators preserve the missingness/care-process information. A1 is B1 plus reserve change, uncertainty and discordance.

A2 is a train/tune-only weighted signed change score with adverse signs fixed as above. It tests whether a simple mechanistic summary suffices, while A1 tests whether irregular timing, measurement error and discordance reveal additional information. Both are fit on the same target, split and outcomes. A black-box sequence/image model is deferred because HCC images are absent and event support is sparse; this is a data-identifiability decision, not a blanket method exclusion.

## Estimand, fitting, uncertainty and compute

Use deterministic 60/20/20 patient-level train/tune/test folds within buckets 0–79. Fit all transforms, imputations, reserve parameters, rare-event penalties, availability models and follow-up models using train/tune only. Produce predictions for every target patient with an ascertainable outcome; never let the outcome or follow-up anchor enter the feature or post-observation model.

The primary estimands are: (i) A1 adverse-IQR reserve-change odds ratio; and (ii) paired held-out log-loss and Brier-score differences A1−B1, with calibration intercept/slope and reliability. AUROC/AUPRC are descriptive. Use 200 patient-level bootstrap replicates, refitting fold-safe transformations as feasible, for intervals. Report unweighted results and stabilized inverse-probability-of-follow-up results. Post-test availability weights are a sensitivity analysis for the observed-trajectory component, not a license to discard non-tested patients. Report weight truncation, positivity and effective sample size.

For missing post assays, use a prespecified training-fold multiple-imputation/latent-prediction procedure (10 draws or the equivalent A1 posterior) and pool patient predictions and uncertainty. Add a pattern-mixture sensitivity shifting every unobserved post primary assay by ±1 training-fold robust SD in the adverse direction; do not choose the direction after seeing test results. The primary conclusion is inconclusive if the claim depends on an untestable missingness assumption.

A CPU job with 2 CPUs and 8 GB RAM is expected to require <=1,800 seconds after source parsing for aggregation, three/four fold-safe model fits, latent recovery simulations and 200 bootstrap metric calculations; it fits within the 7,200-second science budget. No GPU is required. The actual deliverable is newly fitted B0/B1/A1/A2 parameters and transforms, reserve loadings/residuals/posterior uncertainty, missingness and weighting diagnostics, predictions, bootstrap intervals and all prespecified endpoint/falsification outputs.

## Falsification, no-go thresholds and interpretation

Run: (1) post-window process-only model; (2) A1/B1 with post counts/timing removed; (3) within-fold permutation of post-lab values across patients while preserving assay, phase and count; (4) parent complete-post-assay cohort versus the pre-assay target; (5) unweighted versus follow-up-weighted and pattern-mixture results; (6) any-primary versus strict all-four coverage; (7) diagnosis-type, severe, repeat and care-concordant tiers; (8) admission versus discharge proxy; and (9) simulated irregular observations with observed assay/window patterns for parameter sign, scale and recovery.

A confirmatory supportive result requires >=30 broad events overall, >=8 test events, >=40 target patients with >=1 numeric post-primary assay, >=5 events among post-observed patients, acceptable weight positivity/ESS, successful A1 recovery, adverse-direction A1 association, improvement in both proper scores over B1 without material calibration harm, no comparable process-only or permuted gain, and no material reversal under endpoint or ±1-SD missingness checks. These are no-go gates for a confirmatory interpretation, not arbitrary evidence of biology.

Supportive means incremental prognostic information for this locally coded outcome in this pre-assay target under the stated observation assumptions; concordant endpoint tiers strengthen interpretation. Adverse means null/reversal, proper-score or calibration harm, process-only/permutation replication, a materially different complete-case result indicating selection, or loss on specificity tiers. Inconclusive means sparse events/tiers, insufficient post support, failed recovery, poor positivity, wide intervals, or missingness bounds spanning materially different conclusions. No category supports a monitoring or treatment recommendation.

Computationally checkable claims are parsing, cohort flow, temporal joins, fold isolation, fit parameters, recovery, held-out scores, calibration, weights, endpoint tiers and falsification results. Whether labels represent true hepatic failure, medications were administered for that indication, death was missed, or a result improves care requires blinded clinical adjudication, linkage, expert review, an independent cohort or prospective study. The reserved buckets are inaccessible and not external validation; causal TACE toxicity, mortality, clinical utility and transport remain untested.
