# Does discordant AKI recovery add prognostic information beyond observation intensity?

## Unresolved question and clinical value

A fall in serum creatinine during critical illness may indicate filtration improvement, but also reduced creatinine generation during muscle loss or dilution with fluid accumulation. Urine output is a partly independent clinical signal, yet its measurements depend on fluid/diuretic treatment, catheter practice and charting completeness. The unresolved claim is that, among adults with incident creatinine-defined AKI who survive and remain in ICU without KRT to a fixed 48-hour landmark, falling creatinine with valid persistent oliguria identifies greater subsequent mortality risk than concordant improvement beyond measured severity and observation intensity. If robust, this would motivate prospective measurement validation before interpreting creatinine decline as recovery; it would not itself justify a treatment change.

**Evidence already supported:** a 38-patient prospective ICU study with iohexol comparison found creatinine-based eGFR overestimated measured function as critical illness lengthened, plausibly with muscle loss [K1]. PICARD reported fluid accumulation with delayed creatinine-based AKI recognition and lower urine output, while causal direction remains unresolved [K2]. A recent Research Square v1 study in cirrhosis examined joint creatinine/urine-output criteria at a 24-hour landmark and subsequent kidney progression; eligibility denominator and ascertainment affected estimates [K3]. These works motivate the question and bound its novelty; none establishes this post-AKI discordance association.

## Hypothesis, rival and discriminating result

**Hypothesis:** Among the defined 48-hour landmark survivors, observed creatinine decline paired with clinically validated persistent oliguria during the preceding 24 hours is associated with higher conditional 7-day in-hospital death risk than concordant improvement, after accounting for pre-landmark acuity, fluid/treatment proxies and observation intensity.

**Strongest rival:** severity, fluid dilution, diuretics, selective measurement, ambiguous urine-event semantics and survival/ICU-retention selection jointly create apparent discordance and risk. Attenuation after pre-landmark acuity and observation checks, or concentration of the estimate in poorly observed windows, favors this rival. Residual association is prognostic only: adjustment cannot establish true GFR recovery, mechanism or causality.

## Population and time boundaries

Use MIMIC-IV 3.1 only. Read-only archive `[internal dataset path]`, [source checksum]; source snapshot `[source checksum]`. No cross-dataset identity joins.

- Include adults (`hosp/patients.anchor_age >=18`; >89 recorded as 91), first ICU stay per hospital admission, and first incident creatinine-defined AKI during that ICU stay. Onset is first creatinine rise >=0.3 mg/dL within 48 h or >=1.5x the lowest available prior value in 7 d, using only values available by each event time; this is not full KDIGO staging.
- Time zero is AKI onset. Landmark is onset +48 h. Include only patients alive, still in ICU, and not on KRT then. Exposure window is onset +24 h through the landmark. Preserve prespecified creatinine-decline and urine-output thresholds; do not tune to outcomes. If numeric thresholds, weight rule, coverage denominator and clinically negligible risk-difference margin are not frozen before outcomes, do not fit.
- Follow from landmark to in-hospital death, live discharge, 7 days or end of valid observation. Primary endpoint is 7-day death cumulative incidence with live discharge competing. `hospital_expire_flag` is a cross-check, not an event-time substitute. KRT is not a primary endpoint; its use for landmark exclusion requires separately validated codes/items.

## Feasibility gate and analysis

Before any outcome fitting, source/clinical reviewers must freeze urine item list and interval interpretation, duplicate/overlap rules, contemporaneous weight, rate/duration, coverage denominator/cutoff, creatinine-change cutoff and negligible-effect margin. An outcome-blind cohort flow must enumerate all ICU AKI onsets, landmark exclusions (early death/discharge/KRT), missing measures, invalid/unclassifiable windows, exposure groups and event support. If clinical/source evidence cannot establish timed urine volumes and coverage, stop; do not impute or substitute a new label. This missing evidence is a real possibility, not a problem a model can solve.

If gates and sample/event support pass, fit the prespecified simple baseline: separate daily cause-specific logistic hazards for death and live discharge through day 7; derive cumulative incidence and standardized discordant-versus-concordant risk difference/risk ratio with patient-level bootstrap intervals. Use only pre-landmark age/sex, creatinine history, dictionary-validated severity/vitals, reviewed treatment/fluid-input proxies, and observation counts/lags. Keep all stays for one `subject_id` within one 70/15/15 development/validation/test split; freeze cutoffs/mappings without outcome access, tune only on development/validation, and evaluate once on held-out subjects. Report calibration, event support, overlap and full flow, not just discrimination.

Substantive alternative, deferred unless valid phenotype and event support pass: one compact GRU on the same cohort/endpoint and same subject-level split. Encode eight 6-hour bins over the pre-landmark 48 h with validated creatinine/urine summaries, observed-versus-missing/coverage masks, time since last observation, mapped severity/vitals and input/treatment summaries; predict separate death/discharge hazards. No post-landmark inputs and no missing-as-zero urine. Compare competing-risk integrated Brier score, calibration, held-out risk contrasts and patient-bootstrap uncertainty with the matched baseline. Ablate observation masks/density and report valid-coverage strata. The GRU could reveal whether temporal ordering or measurement-process patterns lost by one-row summaries carry prognostic information; it cannot resolve whether recorded urine values are physiological or establish mechanism. A predictive gain alone is not scientific success.

**Interpretation and falsification:** Support requires valid measurement, adequate support/precision, adverse-direction risk difference outside the prespecified negligible range, and robustness across high-coverage, acuity and measurement-intensity checks. This supports only conditional prognostic value and prospective validation. A precise null excluding material excess or precise opposite direction under valid measurement weighs against this association in the selected population. Wide intervals, sparse events, poor overlap, unstable fits or failed measurement gates are inconclusive; do not call these a refutation. Concentration in sparse/irregular records or strong attenuation under observation/severity checks favors the rival. Code-level joins, time ordering, flow, models, estimates and uncertainty are computationally checkable. Urine semantics, KRT mapping, thresholds, clinical meaning, direct filtration and causal/mechanistic claims require expert review or another prospective study with protocolized urine/fluid collection and direct GFR measures.
