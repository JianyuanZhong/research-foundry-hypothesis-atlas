# Early inpatient laboratory deterioration and prolonged hospitalization in HCC

## Scientific question and hypothesis

Among adults with a first observed HCC-coded inpatient encounter in the local HCC snapshot, does the pattern of exact, dated laboratory measurements during the first 48 hours after admission predict a prolonged observed local hospitalization (length of stay at least 14 days), beyond information available at admission and in the pre-admission record? A secondary question is whether any incremental signal is stable across admitting-department families and early recorded procedural pathways.

The prespecified hypothesis is that a coupled early deterioration pattern—worsening bilirubin/coagulation or creatinine, and non-improving albumin or platelets—will be associated with greater risk of a stay of at least 14 days and will improve held-out calibration and risk stratification over an admission/pre-admission baseline. The scientific deliverable is newly fitted admission baseline and early-laboratory models, a locked patient-level evaluation, estimated assay-specific/latent trajectory associations with uncertainty, and a conclusion table that maps each claim to a computed output. A solver must not merely report a readiness check or reuse a precomputed answer.

This is a fresh estimand relative to the parent proposal. The parent begins at first observed TACE and predicts an observed subsequent inpatient encounter after discharge. This proposal begins at the first observed HCC-coded admission, includes patients regardless of TACE, and asks about early stabilization and prolonged index hospitalization. It does not refine the parent’s post-discharge return endpoint.

## Evidence boundary and unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence is source availability and a bounded read-only scan: the HCC snapshot contains encounter admission/discharge times, diagnosis-linked HCC proxies, exact laboratory names/results/times, departments, and procedure times. A scan of encounters and diagnoses provisionally found 42,691 adults with a first completed HCC-coded encounter and 7,559 stays of at least 14 days (17.7%); these counts are feasibility diagnostics and must be regenerated after dictionaries, eligibility and catalog partitioning are frozen. They do not establish that laboratory deterioration predicts prolonged stay.

The unresolved claim is incremental prognostic value: do early, irregular, coupled laboratory changes add reliable information beyond admission context and pre-admission measurements? Even a positive result would describe an association with observed local length of stay. It would not establish hepatic failure, acute kidney injury, treatment toxicity, mortality, causal effect of a procedure, or a policy for escalation or discharge. Length of stay is influenced by care capacity, planned procedures, discharge practice and documentation; it is a consequential care-pathway outcome, not a validated physiologic complication label.

This question matters because a reproducible signal available by 48 hours could inform prospective testing of earlier multidisciplinary review, discharge planning or resource forecasting for patients likely to require a prolonged admission. Those decisions require a future clinical and operational evaluation; this retrospective computation only tests the prognostic premise.

## Population, landmark, outcome and temporal boundaries

Use the configured participant partitioner and discovery catalog buckets 0–79 only. Buckets 80–99 are not accessible and are not external validation. The HCC snapshot is `[source checksum]`; all listed HCC sources are ordinary files, with no archive members.

1. Build encounter keys from the exact pair (`患者主索引`, `就诊号`). Include an encounter with valid native `入院时间` and `出院时间`, `出院时间 >= 入院时间`, parseable adult `年龄 >= 18`, and a diagnosis row joined on the same pair whose `诊断名称` contains the frozen HCC lexical cohort rule (`肝细胞癌` or `肝癌`). Report counts by `诊断类型`, but do not call this an adjudicated HCC diagnosis.

2. The index is the earliest eligible HCC-coded inpatient encounter per `患者主索引`, ordered by native `入院时间` and then `就诊号`. Require admission on or after `2011-01-01` and discharge strictly before the local snapshot boundary `2026-01-01 00:00:00`. This is first observed HCC-coded admission, not first-ever cancer admission. If a patient’s earliest row has missing or ongoing discharge, report it as excluded rather than silently substituting an unverified later episode. Audit duplicate encounter keys, overlapping admissions and admission/discharge inconsistencies.

3. The primary binary outcome is `prolonged_local_stay = 1` when native `出院时间 - 入院时间 >= 336 hours` (14 days), otherwise 0. The index length of stay is never a primary feature because it is the outcome. Report length of stay as a continuous secondary outcome, and repeat the binary analysis at 7 days (168 hours) as a prespecified threshold sensitivity. Do not label either outcome readmission, mortality, organ failure, or treatment complication.

4. The prediction landmark is native `入院时间 + 48 hours`, truncated at native `出院时间` for a stay shorter than 48 hours. Primary early-laboratory features use only measurements with native `检验时间 >= 入院时间` and `检验时间 <= min(入院时间 + 48 hours, 出院时间)`. No laboratory result, procedure, order, medication, diagnosis, document, examination, or length-of-stay-derived feature after this landmark may enter the primary models. A same-timestamp or duplicated measurement is retained and audited rather than treated as independent physiology without a sensitivity rule.

5. Pre-admission features use only native lab times in `[admission - 90 days, admission)` and prior encounters/procedures in the same lookback. The 48-hour laboratory window is intentionally separated from the pre-admission baseline. Patients with no valid early numeric lab are not dropped from the primary cohort; missingness is represented and availability is reported. Qualitative results are not converted to numeric abnormalities.

## Baseline, alternative and scientific comparison

### B0: admission/pre-admission baseline

Fit a training-only standardized elastic-net logistic regression for the same 14-day outcome. Inputs available at admission are age, sex, admitting department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, HCC diagnosis-type indicators and pre-admission lexical proxies for liver disease, cirrhosis/portal hypertension, ascites, portal-vein thrombosis, metastasis and major comorbidity categories. Add prior encounter count, prior inpatient count and prior TACE/liver-directed procedure count in the 180 days before admission. For each frozen exact lab assay in the 90-day pre-admission window, include nearest numeric value, days since measurement, count, missingness and a within-assay slope only when at least two valid dated values exist. Fit imputation, scaling, dictionary expansion and regularization on fitting data only. Do not include index length of stay or any post-admission result.

### B1: simple early-summary comparator

To make the scientific comparison fair, also fit a training-only elastic-net model that adds to B0 the first numeric value, last numeric value, within-48-hour difference and elapsed time, number of measurements and missingness for each frozen assay. B1 is the simple trajectory baseline. It tests whether any signal is captured by clinically transparent summaries before crediting a more complex model.

### Selected substantive alternative: irregular latent organ-state model

Fit an interpretable continuous-time two-state model on the same eligible patients, target and split. A latent hepatic-reserve state and latent renal-stress state evolve over the first 48 hours with patient-level random intercepts/slopes and shrinkage. Each exact assay has its own observation model, offset/time since admission and observation noise; the coagulation channel is included only if its assay dictionary passes the frozen audit. The model receives every valid dated numeric token in the early window, assay identity, elapsed time, qualitative/missingness indicator and measurement density. A regularized logistic head predicts the same 14-day endpoint from the state at 48 hours, recent state slope and posterior uncertainty, with B0 admission covariates retained. The joint observation/outcome fit is confined to the fitting partition; state dimension, process noise, shrinkage and regularization are selected on the selection partition.

B1 and the state model therefore test the same unresolved claim on the same patients, outcome, early window and split. The state model can reveal whether coupled movement, irregular timing and uncertainty contain information that first/last/difference summaries lose. It may also reveal that apparent deterioration is an observation-process artifact. Latent states are data-derived summaries, not validated hepatic or renal physiology, especially given absent units.

A one-layer GRU or temporal-attention model over the identical early tokens is recorded but deferred. Reconsider it only if the state model fails convergence or locked residual analyses show reproducible nonlinear timing interactions that are scientifically important. A text model is not substituted: a validated local examination/document labeler and clinical gold-standard annotations are unavailable, examination/document timing and context are incomplete, and the HCC metadata explicitly describes local lexical extraction as non-validated. The cancer demonstration’s main article and complete STAR Methods were unavailable; only its supplement was inspected, so no reproduction claim is made. The natural-history and Bayesian demonstrations motivate dated sequence and latent longitudinal modelling but do not establish this HCC hypothesis.

## Split, analysis, uncertainty and falsification

Use deterministic participant-level catalog buckets 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for selection and 70–79 for the locked internal test. No patient, encounter, lab, procedure or diagnosis-derived feature may cross partitions. A latest-era chronological stress test is secondary and is not external validation.

Before locked evaluation, write the frozen assay and HCC dictionaries, cohort flow, diagnosis/procedure multiplicities, invalid-time counts, overlap audit, early measurement availability and density, and pathway-stratum counts. Report how many complete discharge rows, missing/ongoing discharge rows and overlapping admissions were excluded. Do not discard no-early-lab patients from the primary cohort.

On the same locked test patients, report AUROC, PR-AUC, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, calibration plots and observed-versus-predicted risk bins for B0, B1 and the state model. Use paired patient-bootstrap 95% intervals with at least 500 resamples for B0–B1 and B1–state differences; all refitting remains within fitting/selection data. Report continuous-LOS secondary association with uncertainty, but do not let it replace the prespecified binary endpoint. Treat decision curves as exploratory, not as a discharge or escalation policy.

Falsification and robustness checks are prespecified:

- add/remove measurement count, gap, missingness and observation-process terms; a state-model gain that disappears after measurement intensity adjustment is surveillance evidence, not organ-state evidence;
- permute lab timestamps within patient and assay while preserving values and assay identity; a timing-dependent result should attenuate, whereas unchanged performance implicates value/density rather than trajectory timing;
- use pre-admission lab timestamps only as a negative timing control and audit that no early-window value precedes admission or exceeds the 48-hour landmark;
- stratify by admitting department family, calendar era, assay availability, measurement density and early recorded procedure pathway; report interactions descriptively and do not interpret them causally;
- repeat with a 7-day threshold, continuous LOS and an all-HCC-encounter sensitivity, while retaining first-admission results as primary;
- exclude same-timestamp duplicates and repeat with a prespecified aggregation rule; report whether conclusions depend on duplicate handling;
- include index length of stay only in a clearly labelled post-outcome leakage demonstration, never in the primary model, to show the direction of the leakage risk.

Supportive results require: (i) directionally coherent, prespecified deterioration associations; (ii) B1 or state-model improvement over B0 and, for the substantive claim, state-model improvement over B1 in held-out calibration and at least one prespecified metric; (iii) an absolute Brier improvement of at least 0.002 for the state-vs-B1 comparison or a prespecified calibrated risk-stratification improvement of comparable magnitude; (iv) paired uncertainty that does not make the clinically relevant direction implausibly broad; (v) calibration that is acceptable by reported intercept/slope and plots; and (vi) persistence across measurement-intensity, timestamp-permutation, department and era checks. Support would justify prospective/external validation of an early prolonged-stay risk marker, not a clinical policy or physiologic diagnosis.

Adverse results are a null/opposite deterioration association, no meaningful state-vs-B1 improvement, poor calibration, instability by department/era/assay availability, or disappearance after measurement-intensity adjustment. They argue against claiming that the proposed early coupled trajectory adds useful information for prolonged-stay forecasting. A result that survives only through early recorded procedure pathway is adverse for a general physiologic interpretation and may indicate care selection.

Inconclusive results include fewer than 100 primary outcome events after the frozen exclusions, inability to freeze exact assay semantics, no repeated/dated support for the selected channels, dominant invalid/overlapping time fields, nonconvergence, incomplete discharge ascertainment, or intervals too wide to distinguish meaningful gain from harm. Inconclusive means the data cannot resolve the question; it is not evidence for or against the hypothesis.

## Interpretation limits and required additional evidence

A supportive computation can establish only a reproducible local prognostic association with an observed >=14-day hospitalization among the defined first HCC-coded admissions. It cannot establish hepatic decompensation, renal injury, treatment toxicity, cancer progression, mortality, transplant eligibility, readmission risk, preventability, or benefit from acting on a score. Those claims require expert adjudication of diagnoses and complications, laboratory units/reference ranges, radiology and staging, death/transplant linkage, treatment intent, complete multi-hospital capture, external validation and, for an action recommendation, a prospective clinical/operational or causal study. Examination and document text would require timestamped expert labels and validated context handling before serving as a clinical endpoint or feature.

## Solver-ready deliverables and compute

The solver must newly produce: source and schema hashes; frozen HCC and exact-assay dictionaries; cohort flow and all exclusions; join-key multiplicity and timestamp/overlap audits; provisional counts regenerated under the frozen rules; bucket assignment; B0/B1 coefficients and preprocessing; state-model loadings, state/slope contrasts, posterior uncertainty and convergence diagnostics; locked predictions; AUROC, PR-AUC, Brier and calibration outputs with paired bootstrap intervals; threshold, department, era, assay, measurement-density, timestamp-permutation, duplicate and procedure-pathway sensitivities; and a claim-to-output table marking claims requiring clinical adjudication or another study.

The bounded admission/diagnosis scan used read-only source files and was measured at about four seconds in an ordinary shell. The full-lab discovery scan is managed separately; its runtime and exact repeated-assay support are not assumed by this proposal until the job completes. The future solver planning envelope from `inputs.json` is up to 16 CPUs, 256 GiB RAM and 28,800 seconds. CPU is appropriate for the elastic-net models and low-dimensional state-space fit; a GPU is not required. A deferred neural sequence model could use one allocated A100-SXM4-80GB (`cuda:0` inside its allocation), but no GPU feasibility or duration is claimed and no GPU is required for the selected study.

The proposal is a scientific plan, not a solved result. Readiness checks cannot establish clinical truth.
