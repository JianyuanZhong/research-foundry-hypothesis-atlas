# Working Proposal — Episode 3 (HCC island)

## Scientific question
After TACE for hepatocellular carcinoma, do the EARLY (first post-procedure day, day 1)
laboratory changes — relative to the pre-procedure baseline — predict 90-day readmission,
beyond what is explained by baseline MELD score?

## Rationale and opening
- TACE is the most common treatment in this cohort (20,151 patients, 109,202 TACE-related
  procedure rows). Post-embolization syndrome (PES) is common (55-68% in published studies)
  but prior work has focused on symptom-based definitions (pain, fever) and procedural
  predictors (tumor size, number of tumors, embolization technique).
- The unresolved question: does the early laboratory trajectory (bilirubin rise, ALT rise,
  platelet drop, fibrinogen rise) carry independent prognostic information for 90-day
  readmission beyond baseline MELD? This would identify patients at risk of hepatic
  decompensation who should be monitored more closely or considered for alternative
  treatment strategies.
- Discovery probes (this episode) found:
  * n=16,030 patients with both pre- and post-TACE labs in the index encounter
  * 90-day readmission: 5,292 events (33.0%)
  * MELD alone: AUC 0.517 (weak)
  * MELD + 6 lab deltas (bili, ALT, PLT, fib, HGB, ALB): AUC 0.561
  * Individual deltas show graded association with 90-day readmission (e.g., d_bili
    highest quartile 34.9% vs lowest 30.2%; d_alt highest 39.5% vs lowest 29.4%)
  * These are exploratory; the formal experiment requires proper train/test split,
    calibration, and comparison against a well-specified baseline.

## Hypothesis
In HCC patients undergoing TACE, the relative change in total bilirubin, ALT, platelet
count, and fibrinogen between the last pre-procedure lab (day <0) and the first
post-procedure lab (day >=1) is independently associated with 90-day readmission,
after adjusting for baseline MELD, age, sex, and pre-procedure AFP.

## Population
- All patients in the HCC dataset with:
  * At least one TACE-related procedure (手术 contains 'TACE' or '栓塞')
  * A TACE-containing index encounter (admit <= t0 <= disch)
  * At least one lab measurement in the index encounter before TACE (day <1)
  * At least one lab measurement in the index encounter on/after TACE (day >=1)
  * 90-day follow-up: either a readmission within 90 days of t0, or last observed
    encounter/lab beyond 90 days (right-censoring)
- Exclusions: missing t0, missing encounter boundaries, no labs in either window

## Temporal boundaries
- t0 = first TACE procedure time (开始时间) for the patient
- Pre-window: admit <= t < t0 (last lab before TACE)
- Post-window: t0 <= t <= disch (first lab on/after TACE)
- Outcome window: t0 < admit_next <= t0 + 90 days
- Censoring: last observed lab/encounter time if no readmission within 90 days

## Variables
### Baseline (pre-TACE, last measurement in pre-window)
- 总胆红素 (total bilirubin), 肌酐 (creatinine), 国际标准化比值 (INR) → MELD
- 白蛋白 (albumin), 血小板计数 (platelet count), 丙氨酸氨基转移酶 (ALT)
- 甲胎蛋白 (AFP), 血红蛋白 (hemoglobin), 纤维蛋白原 (fibrinogen)
- 年龄 (age), 性别 (sex) from encounters

### Early post-TACE (first measurement in post-window)
- Same analytes as above → compute relative deltas:
  d_bili = (bili_post - bili_pre) / max(bili_pre, 1)
  d_alt  = (alt_post  - alt_pre)  / max(alt_pre,  1)
  d_plt  = (plt_post  - plt_pre)  / max(plt_pre,  1)
  d_fib  = (fib_post  - fib_pre)  / max(fib_pre,  1)
  d_hgb  = (hgb_post  - hgb_pre)  / max(hgb_pre,  1)
  d_alb  = (alb_post  - alb_pre)  / max(alb_pre,  1)

### Outcome
- y90 = 1 if any new admission (入院时间) occurs in (t0, t0+90d], else 0
- Censoring time = min(last lab time, last encounter discharge, t0+90d)

## Analysis plan
1. **Cohort construction**: Build the index cohort as described above.
2. **Baseline model (MELD only)**: Logistic regression with MELD as sole predictor.
3. **Extended model (MELD + deltas)**: Logistic regression with MELD + 6 lab deltas +
   age + sex + log(AFP).
4. **Gradient boosting alternative**: HistGradientBoostingClassifier (handles NaN natively)
   with the same features; compare AUC and calibration.
5. **Train/test split**: 70/30 by patient (no patient appears in both).
6. **Evaluation**: AUC, calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, decision curve analysis.
7. **Falsification criteria**:
   - SUPPORTIVE: Extended model AUC > baseline AUC + 0.02 in test set, with at least one
     lab delta having a significant coefficient (p<0.05) in the logistic model.
   - ADVERSE: Extended model AUC <= baseline AUC + 0.02, or no lab delta is significant
     after adjusting for MELD.
   - INCONCLUSIVE: AUC difference between 0.01-0.02, or significant deltas but poor
     calibration, or event rate in test set too low for stable estimation.

## Baseline vs learned alternative
- **Baseline**: MELD score (established, interpretable, clinically used).
- **Learned alternative**: Gradient boosting with lab deltas. This can capture
  non-linear interactions (e.g., high baseline bilirubin + large relative rise may be
  more dangerous than either alone) and heterogeneous effects that a linear model
  would miss. If the boosting model substantially outperforms the linear model, it
  suggests non-linear dose-response relationships in the lab trajectory.
- **What the alternative reveals**: Whether the prognostic signal in early lab changes
  is linear or has threshold/interaction effects; whether the signal is driven by a
  few extreme values or is a graded phenomenon.

## Limitations
- Single-center (HCC dataset is one hospital); generalizability uncertain.
- Readmission as outcome is a proxy for hepatic decompensation; not all readmissions
  are due to liver-related causes (no ICD-coded cause of readmission available).
- No mortality data (no death records in diagnoses table).
- Lab timing: "first post-procedure lab" may be 1-5 days after TACE depending on
  hospital practice; the day-1 window is approximate.
- No imaging data to confirm tumor response or progression as competing risk.
- PES is not directly measured (no symptom scores); the lab trajectory is a surrogate.

## What a supportive result would establish
- Early laboratory changes after TACE carry independent prognostic information for
  90-day readmission beyond baseline MELD.
- This would support using early post-TACE labs as a triage tool for closer follow-up.

## What an adverse result would establish
- Baseline MELD captures essentially all the prognostic information available from
  early post-TACE labs; the lab trajectory is not an independent predictor.
- This would argue against adding early post-TACE labs to risk stratification.

## What an inconclusive result would mean
- The signal is weak or confounded; further work would need a larger cohort,
  cause-specific readmission coding, or a different outcome (e.g., mortality with
  external linkage).
