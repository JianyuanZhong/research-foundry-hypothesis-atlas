# ICU-recorded IV furosemide or bumetanide after post-shock stabilization

## Unresolved question, evidence boundary, and advance

Positive fluid balance after resuscitation is associated with poor outcomes, but association does not show that removing fluid improves outcomes. The indexed abstract of a 2017 systematic review (Silversides et al.; DOI `10.1007/s00134-016-4573-3`; current frozen Europe PMC search `[source checksum]`; full paper not inspected) reports that 11 heterogeneous randomized conservative/deresuscitative trials (2,051 patients) increased ventilator-free days by 1.82 (95% CI 0.53–3.10) without a precise mortality benefit (RR 0.92, 95% CI 0.82–1.02). RADAR-2 was a 180-patient randomized feasibility trial of a bundled conservative-fluid/active-removal strategy, not isolated loop initiation. Its indexed report (DOI `10.1007/s00134-021-06596-8`; frozen source `[source checksum]`; full text not inspected) reports lower day-3 balance (-840 versus +130 mL), but clinical outcomes were underpowered. A 2026 full-text RADAR-2 secondary analysis (McMullan et al.; DOI `10.1097/CCE.0000000000001404`; full Europe PMC XML inspected, frozen source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) found no statistically detectable differences in lactate, AKIRisk, urinary cystatin-C, or vascular-injury markers and explicitly states that modest sample size and imprecision cannot exclude clinically important effects. The 2026 Orieux et al. review (DOI `10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100083`; full Europe PMC XML inspected from frozen source `[source checksum]`, source [source checksum]) calls ICU renal congestion hypothesis-generating, without validated diagnostic criteria, therapeutic targets, or outcome-driven interventional evidence.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is therefore that heterogeneous conservative/deresuscitative bundles can reduce recorded balance and may improve ventilator-free days; RADAR-2 did not detect biomarker kidney/perfusion harm but is too imprecise to establish safety. Evidence does not identify the effect of initiating actual IV furosemide/bumetanide at this stable landmark, exclude mortality/RRT harm, validate an EHR congestion phenotype, or justify standard care. The substantive advance is a falsifiable emulation of that narrower bedside choice with actual ICU administration, fixed pre-treatment time zero, treatment-confounder feedback adjustment, patient-centered benefit and separate safety gates, and an honest route/observation boundary.

Hypothesis: **among invasively ventilated adults at ICU hour 48 after vasopressor-treated shock, currently stable off vasopressors for six hours and with at least 50 mL/kg documented positive ICU balance, initiating or re-initiating ICU-recorded IV furosemide or bumetanide within 12 hours increases 28-day days alive and free of invasive ventilation versus withholding every loop diuretic for 24 hours, without more than 3 percentage points excess 28-day mortality or 5 points excess 7-day new RRT.** A +1-day point estimate is the clinical-relevance gate; superiority requires its 95% CI to exclude zero. A secondary hypothesis is a larger effect when a pre-t0 result-available chest report describes pulmonary edema; that report is not validated renal congestion.

## Population, time zero, and exact eligibility

Freeze participant sampling as `bucket = int(SHA256(ASCII canonical base-10 subject_id),16) mod 100`; use buckets 0–79 and every available record for those subjects. Set `t0 = icu/icustays.intime + 48 hours`. Eligibility uses only data clinically recorded by t0; being alive and in the ICU at t0 is inherent to a landmark trial, but no later stay duration is required.

## Documented balance and actual treatment

“Documented positive balance” is not total-body water, fluid overload, or congestion. From `icu/inputevents`, use positive numeric `amount` with exact case-normalized unit mL or L and observed delivered-segment `statusdescription` in {FinishedRunning, ChangeDose/Rate, Stopped, Paused, Bolus}, retaining `starttime,endtime,storetime,itemid,orderid,linkorderid`. Exclude nonpositive amounts, Reported/unknown statuses, other units, and only exact duplicate rows; do not collapse distinct sequential order segments. Count completed boluses once; for continuous segments crossing a boundary, prorate `amount` by overlap duration, with zero-duration boluses assigned by `starttime`. Never sum both amount and rate. From `icu/outputevents`, use nonnegative numeric `value` in recognized mL/L units at `charttime`; absence is missing, not zero. Freeze urine to `itemid` 226557, 226558, 226559, 226560, 226561, 226563, 226564, 226565, 226566, 226567, 226584, 226627, 226631, 226713, and 227489, with a sensitivity excluding mixed urine/irrigant 226566/227489 and perioperative 226627/226631. Item 227488 is GU irrigant input and is added to input, never urine output. Divide net input minus output by admission weight (`chartevents` 226512, then first valid daily weight 224639, then median pre-t0 `inputevents.patientweight`). Primary eligibility requires valid output in each pre-t0 12-hour block; report input/output capture by six-hour block and repeat with valid input and output in all eight blocks, 30/100 mL/kg thresholds, and continuous balance. Freeze units/statuses before outcomes.

For all ICU medication and vasopressor exposure rules, the same delivered-segment status set applies; Stopped, Paused, and ChangeDose/Rate rows represent delivered segments rather than cancellations. The source-wide audit found no Rewritten status for the selected pressor/loop items, so the compiler must report rather than silently invent status categories.

The two strategies are:

- A: first positive, non-rewritten ICU `inputevents` administration of furosemide (221794 or 228340) or bumetanide (229639) starts in `(t0,t0+12h]`, with no oral loop before it; subsequent care is unrestricted after hour 12.
- B: no furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide, or ethacrynic acid by any route in `(t0,t0+24h]`; subsequent care is unrestricted after hour 24.

This is deliberately not an “all IV loop” intervention. A full-source audit found 570,280 loop-named eMAR keys, but `emar_detail` dose and route are stored sparsely across multiple rows: 1,311,042 positive-dose detail rows had blank route, while PO route generally appeared on separate rows. Requiring positive dose and IV route on one row is invalid; blank route cannot be assumed IV. ICU `inputevents` supplies actual IV furosemide/bumetanide administrations, including 4,086 source-wide bumetanide 229639 rows. No ethacrynic-acid eMAR names were found and torsemide was predominantly represented only in eMAR.

For washout, B-arm deviation, and concordance, reduce `hosp/emar_detail` to one event per `subject_id,emar_id,emar_seq`, then join to `hosp/emar` on those keys and `hadm_id`. Normalize exact medication names and accept actual-administration `event_txt` values (`Administered`, `Delayed Administered`, `Partial Administered`, `Administered in Other Location`, or a `Started/Restarted` event with positive event-level dose); reject Not Given, Hold, Confirmed/order-only, Stopped, and Not Started. Route is any nonblank detail-row route after event aggregation; blank is unknown. Deduplicate same-drug eMAR/input events within 15 minutes. Report ICU/eMAR overlap and discordance; sensitivity requires eMAR corroboration for ICU events and separately adds only eMAR events with explicit event-level IV route. Orders and prescriptions never define treatment.

Clone every eligible person to both strategies at t0. Censor the A clone at the first nonprotocol oral loop, ICU discharge before qualifying initiation, or hour 12 if untreated; censor B at the first loop or alive discharge with unknown medication during the 24-hour grace period. Death during either grace period is retained in both clones as an outcome (VFD=0), never censored as nonadherence. Estimate inverse-probability weights for this artificial censoring. The estimand is the per-protocol mean VFD28 difference A minus B in the eligible start/restart population, conditional on consistency, positivity, correct measurement, and no unmeasured confounding.

## Outcomes and observation boundaries

Primary VFD28 runs from t0 through day 28. Construct invasive-ventilation intervals from `procedureevents.itemid=225792` across all ICU stays and observed readmissions for the subject. Death known by day 28 gives zero. Otherwise count calendar days alive and without invasive ventilation. The primary carries an alive discharge forward as alive/unventilated unless later MIMIC ventilation or death contradicts it; label it **VFD28 with alive-discharge carry-forward**, not complete post-discharge VFD. Mandatory sensitivities are hospital-observed VFD, alive/extubated days while observed, liberation for 48 continuous hours by day 7 with early discharge unknown, and ICU/hospital-free days. Death uses earliest `admissions.deathtime`, `patients.dod`, or `hospital_expire_flag`; analyze date-only DOD boundary uncertainty.

Safety outcomes are separate 28-day death and new RRT by day 7. For RRT, use first post-t0 start of the six procedure IDs; treat death before RRT as a competing event and report cause-specific cumulative incidence rather than coding death as no RRT. Secondary renal/hemodynamic outcomes are KDIGO creatinine AKI through day 7, creatinine and potassium change, K<3.0, new vasopressor use, MAP<55, 24/48h documented balance, and urine output. Map creatinine/potassium through `hosp/labevents` and `hosp/d_labitems`, using `charttime` for clinical time and `storetime` for result availability. Post-discharge missing labs are unknown, not normal. Balance/urine responses are process checks, not patient benefit.

For the exploratory modifier, select the latest chest radiology report with `charttime in (t0-12h,t0]` and `storetime<=t0`. Freeze a section- and negation-aware classifier for explicit pulmonary vascular/interstitial edema, isolated effusion, neither, or unknown. Report charttime-only sensitivity. Two blinded clinicians must review at least 200 stratified reports, with third adjudication and edema PPV >=0.80, before any clinical imaging claim. ## Baselines, causal analysis, and falsification

Report attrition and unadjusted landmark contrasts. Create clone-hour rows through hour 24. Estimate stabilized inverse-probability adherence/censoring weights with pooled logistic models using only information available by each hour: treatment history; lagged MAP, vasopressor status, urine output, balance, creatinine, lactate, potassium, FiO2, PEEP, oxygenation, respiratory support, sedation, crystalloid/albumin, and result-available imaging. These may be treatment-affected predictors of later treatment; use marginal structural weighting rather than ordinary post-treatment outcome adjustment.

Baseline adjustment includes age, sex, race, admission type/year, first unit/service, prior completed-admission comorbidity, weight, balance, prior loop dose/time, KDIGO stage, pressor dose/duration/time since cessation, MAP, heart rate, temperature, oxygenation, lactate, electrolytes, sedation, fluids, and baseline radiology. Index-discharge diagnoses and post-t0 notes are prohibited. Primary analysis estimates weighted arm means and A-minus-B VFD difference with subject-clustered robust 95% intervals and 500 subject-level bootstrap replicates. Report unadjusted, baseline overlap-weighted, and clone-censor estimates; standardized differences, missingness, adherence probabilities, 1st/99th weight truncation, arm ESS, outcome capture, and calendar interaction. A naive treated-within-12h comparison cannot be called causal.

Required sensitivities: never-treated new users; 6/12h washouts; 30/100 mL/kg and continuous balance; strict six-hour accounting; 6/24h initiation windows; exclude cardiac surgery and heart failure; baseline-KDIGO strata; no albumin co-initiation; ICU/eMAR concordance; hospital-observed outcomes; first ICU per hospitalization; calendar era; and quantitative bias analysis for an unmeasured indication.

Temporal falsification tests whether eventual strategy predicts already observed pre-t0 six-hour creatinine, urine-output, or MAP slopes after the same baseline adjustment. A material standardized difference >0.10 or multiplicity-controlled P<0.05 forces the causal interpretation to inconclusive. Expected more-negative 24-hour balance and greater urine output under A are process-positive controls; failure raises exposure/strategy concern but cannot prove inefficacy.

Parent counts—1,122 broad landmarks and 212 furosemide initiators by 12h—preceded the final MAP, urine, RRT, complete pressor, 12h all-loop washout, bumetanide, and accounting rules and are not effect estimates. A source execution that does not emit each sequential attrition stage and observed A/B grace-period counts has not established feasibility. Before outcome modeling declare infeasible/inconclusive if either observed adherent arm has <100 people, ESS<75, >10% adherence probability <0.01, ICU/eMAR discordance >20%, or material balance/outcome missingness. Do not relax population, windows, estimand, outcomes, or gates mechanically.

## Result logic

Supportive requires all of: A-minus-B VFD point estimate >=+1 day with lower 95% bound >0; upper 95% bound for excess 28-day death <+3 percentage points; upper bound for excess 7-day new RRT <+5 points; expected balance/urine process direction; and all feasibility, positivity, exposure, missingness, and falsification gates passed. This supports external validation and a pragmatic randomized trial, not clinical benefit as fact or practice change.

Adverse is VFD upper 95% bound <=0, mortality-harm lower bound >=+3 points, or new-RRT-harm lower bound >=+5 points. Favorable balance cannot rescue respiratory or safety harm. Inconclusive/mixed is every other pattern: wide intervals, estimated benefit without both safety margins excluded, conflicting VFD sensitivities, nonpositivity, route/exposure failure, residual pre-t0 imbalance, missing follow-up, or an unvalidated text interaction. Nonsignificance is neither equivalence nor safety. An opposite or null validated imaging interaction falsifies only the modifier, not the primary hypothesis.
