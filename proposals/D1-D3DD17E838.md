# When does recorded hyperoxia signal short-term ICU risk? Pulmonary-status interaction in eICU

## Episode 7 successor and clinical decision

This is a substantive child of the assessed eICU lines `[prior hypothesis]` (recorded-event safety question), `[prior hypothesis]` (time-ordered kidney-risk and ascertainment design), and `[prior hypothesis]` (hospital-held-out transportability). It develops expert seed `[starting question]`, Hyperoxia exposure.

The clinical decision is whether an ICU oxygen-surveillance rule should treat sustained high recorded FiO2 as a risk marker even when oxygenation is initially adequate, and whether that signal differs by pulmonary reserve. The result could inform which patients merit review of oxygen prescription and blood-gas monitoring, but this dataset cannot establish an oxygen target or a benefit/harm of changing therapy.

This is an observational prognostic experiment about recorded oxygenation and short-horizon ICU death. It does not estimate the causal effect of oxygen, ventilation, or FiO2 titration.

## Evidence-supported claim versus unresolved hypothesis

The strongest claim supported before execution is structural and feasibility-related. The frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-stay and hospital identifiers and discharge status/offsets in `patient`; timestamped FiO2 chart values in `respiratoryCharting`; timestamped SpO2 and vital summaries in `vitalPeriodic`; timestamped respiratory-support fields in `respiratoryCare`; and timestamped `paO2` laboratory rows with result and unit/interface fields in `lab`. A read-only header and representative-row audit found `FiO2` in respiratory-flow settings/care data and `paO2` examples with `mm Hg` units. This does not establish complete blood-gas surveillance, true delivered FiO2, hyperoxemia for patients without PaO2, pulmonary diagnosis, or treatment intent.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults with an adequate baseline PaO2, greater recorded FiO2 burden during ICU minutes 120–360 is associated with higher risk of ICU death during minutes 360–1,440, and this association is stronger among patients with lower baseline PaO2/FiO2 pulmonary status, after accounting for early physiology, support context, hospital, and observation density.

The primary statistical test is the prespecified FiO2-burden-by-pulmonary-status interaction in a hospital-held-out model for ICU death in the 6–24-hour window. The null is no reproducible interaction and no held-out improvement over the same model without the interaction. It is not a causal effect-modification hypothesis.

## Population, temporal boundaries and variables

Use one `patientunitstayid` as the index unit. Retain `uniquepid` only for a person-clustered sensitivity analysis; do not collapse or join repeated ICU stays through it. Include adults with numeric `patient.age >= 18`, report missing-age exclusions and the eICU `> 89` convention, and require `patient.unitdischargeoffset >= 360`.

The landmark is ICU minute 360. Define a baseline oxygenation window as minutes 0–120 and an exposure window as 120–360. This separation prevents the exposure from defining baseline pulmonary status.

Eligibility for the primary complete-case estimand requires:

1. at least one valid numeric `paO2` in `lab` at minutes 0–120 with an audited oxygen-pressure unit (`mm Hg`, preserving the exact encountered unit strings before locking the accepted map);
2. baseline PaO2 >=80 mm Hg, the prespecified “adequate” oxygenation condition;
3. at least one valid FiO2 value in `respiratoryCharting` in minutes 0–120 and at least two valid FiO2 values in minutes 120–360, after the locked value and label rules; and
4. ICU follow-up opportunity through minute 1,440, unless the stay reaches an explicitly reported competing death/discharge state earlier.

For each baseline PaO2, pair the nearest FiO2 chart observation within 30 minutes (prefer a FiO2 at or before the gas; tie-break by absolute time then `respchartid`). Convert numeric FiO2 percentages such as 21, 40 and 100 to fractions 0.21, 0.40 and 1.00. Do not use text-only FiO2, implausible values, or carry a value over a gap greater than 30 minutes. If no valid paired FiO2 exists, retain the stay in the cohort-flow/unknown table but exclude it from the primary interaction estimand. The baseline pulmonary-status variable is PaO2/FiO2 from this pair, continuous as the primary variable and categorized at <200 versus >=200 as a prespecified interpretability sensitivity. The continuous interaction is primary; do not choose cutpoints using outcomes.

The primary exposure is exposure-window hyperoxia burden: minutes carried at FiO2 >=0.60 under the 30-minute gap rule, divided by covered exposure minutes. Secondary exposure summaries are time-weighted FiO2 as a continuous fraction, proportion at >=0.80, maximum FiO2, and number of FiO2 observations. Carry forward only to the next chart observation when the gap is <=30 minutes; report covered fraction, observation count, maximum gap, and source chart type. Do not call charting a delivered dose.

Early recorded context is derived only from minutes 0–360: `vitalPeriodic` `sao2`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, `temperature`, and pressure summaries; `respiratoryCharting` support/settings labels; and `respiratoryCare` support fields. Use FiO2 and PaO2 observation density, gaps and unit/interface as explicit process covariates. A sensitivity may use `respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset`, `ventendoffset`, `airwaytype` and `setapneafio2`, but these fields do not prove invasive ventilation or continuous support. Do not use Apache first-24-hour aggregates in the primary model because they can overlap the landmark.

Pulmonary-status sensitivity strata may use a prespecified respiratory-support-recorded indicator, not an inferred diagnosis. Admission source, unit type, gender, ethnicity, and hospital are context covariates. Structured diagnosis fields may be used only in a labeled sensitivity for recorded pulmonary diagnoses by minute 360; they are not complete pulmonary adjudication.

## Outcomes and ascertainment states

The primary outcome is ICU death by minute 1,440, identified from `patient.unitdischargestatus` and `unitdischargeoffset` when death occurs after the landmark and before 1,440. The primary analysis is a 6–24-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazard/logistic model with bins 360–720 and 720–1,440. Report the exact status mapping and audit unexpected discharge-status values before analysis.

Alive ICU discharge before 1,440 is a competing state, not a negative or normoxic outcome. Stays remaining in the ICU without death through 1,440 are non-events if outcome ascertainment is otherwise complete. Report:

1. ICU death in the outcome window;
2. alive ICU discharge before the window ends;
3. still in ICU without death at 1,440; and
4. unknown/ineligible oxygenation ascertainment due to missing/ambiguous PaO2, FiO2, units, or insufficient coverage.

No post-ICU death is available and must not be inferred. A secondary outcome is death by 720 minutes; it is not a separate discovery hypothesis.

All otherwise eligible adults must appear in a flow table before complete-case restrictions, including no baseline PaO2, no paired FiO2, inadequate baseline PaO2, insufficient exposure coverage, early death/discharge, and unknown status. Missing PaO2 is not normal oxygenation.

## Baselines, analysis and uncertainty

Use five grouped hospital folds, with every stay from a hospital held in one fold. All label maps, unit rules, preprocessing, imputation, tuning and cutpoint-free transforms are learned or fixed within training folds. Do not use a random patient split as the primary validation.

Fit the following nested transparent models:

- M0: demographics, admission/unit context, early recorded physiology, baseline PaO2 and FiO2, oxygen/PaO2 observation density, and recorded respiratory-support context;
- M1: M0 plus exposure-window FiO2 burden and its density variables;
- M2: M1 plus continuous baseline PaO2/FiO2 pulmonary status and the prespecified FiO2-burden × pulmonary-status interaction;
- Mchart-sensitivity: FiO2 values separated into `respFlowSettings` and `respFlowCareData`, without assuming they have identical semantics;
- Mlab-sensitivity: laboratory `FiO2` rows paired with `paO2` where available, never pooled silently with chart-derived FiO2.

The primary estimand is the interaction coefficient and the held-out incremental performance of M2 versus M1. Report fold- and hospital-level Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC, precision-recall area, interaction estimate with 95% uncertainty, and absolute predicted-risk contrasts at prespecified FiO2 burdens and PaO2/FiO2 values. Use hospital-cluster bootstrap or robust hospital-level uncertainty and a `uniquepid`-clustered sensitivity. Report direction and calibration by hospital; do not rank hospitals.

Because death/discharge and FiO2/PaO2 observation are informative, include a competing-risk sensitivity for death versus alive discharge and an inverse-probability-of-observation sensitivity based only on pre-landmark demographics, hospital, unit/admission context, baseline vital density, and whether/when a gas was recorded. Show weight positivity and truncation. Weighting cannot repair unmeasured clinical reasons for ordering a blood gas or changing oxygen.

A feasibility gate precedes interpretation: report counts and coverage for PaO2, paired FiO2, valid units, baseline PaO2 >=80, exposure coverage, hospitals, respiratory-support strata, and each outcome/unknown state. If accepted-unit concordance, paired-gas coverage, hospital overlap, or event counts are inadequate, classify the study as inconclusive and do not relax thresholds after seeing outcomes.

## Falsification and interpretation

Prespecified falsifications are:

- permute exposure-window FiO2 burdens within hospital, broad baseline PaO2/FiO2, baseline PaO2, and observation-density strata;
- shift FiO2 exposure 240 minutes earlier to predict an outcome interval that precedes the true exposure, and verify that any interaction disappears;
- prohibit all post-landmark values from features and audit that no `labresultrevisedoffset`, discharge field, or later respiratory row enters M0–M2;
- repeat with 15- and 60-minute carry-forward gaps, FiO2 thresholds 0.50 and 0.80, and continuous FiO2, with definitions fixed before outcome comparison;
- test chart settings versus care-data labels separately and compare, without conflating them with delivered FiO2;
- test whether the interaction is attenuated by FiO2/PaO2 counts, covered time, gap summaries, hospital, or respiratory-support context;
- use a pre-exposure oxygenation measure as a temporal negative-control target after conditioning on its baseline value; a post-exposure exposure association with an already recorded baseline quantity indicates leakage or construction error; and
- run a labeled Apache/leakage sensitivity only as a diagnostic, never as a primary result.

Supportive results require a directionally coherent interaction, clinically interpretable absolute-risk contrast, lower held-out Brier score or better calibration for M2 than M1, and stability across hospitals, chart-source definitions, reasonable gap rules, and the 0.50/0.80 thresholds. This supports a reproducible marker of short-term recorded ICU-death risk in the selected PaO2-observed population and suggests that pulmonary status matters for risk stratification. It does not support oxygen toxicity, a FiO2 treatment target, or benefit from changing oxygen.

Adverse results include a null or sign-reversing interaction, no held-out improvement, a signal confined to one hospital or sparse-gas subgroup, disappearance after density adjustment/observation weighting, failure under exposure permutation or temporal shifting, or disagreement between chart-source definitions. These weaken the claim that the recorded interaction is transportable in this snapshot; they do not show that hyperoxia is biologically harmless.

Inconclusive results include inadequate paired PaO2/FiO2 coverage, ambiguous units, nonpositive observation weights, death/discharge dominating the eligible window, insufficient hospitals/events, or major disagreement between settings and care-data labels. The next requirement would be validated delivered-FiO2 and ventilator data, continuous arterial oxygenation, indication/treatment-intent data, and clinical adjudication—not a post hoc threshold or imputation of absent blood gases.

Computationally checkable claims are source integrity, exact joins, label/unit counts, FiO2 conversion and gap formulas, temporal ordering, cohort flow, competing-state accounting, model predictions, held-out performance, uncertainty, and falsification permutations. Clinical claims requiring expert review or another study are whether FiO2 chart rows represent delivered oxygen, whether PaO2 samples are arterial and clinically comparable, whether pulmonary disease or support was present, whether death was oxygen-related, whether the interaction is causal, and whether an oxygen-surveillance or titration policy improves outcomes. Clinical utility requires decision thresholds, harms/costs, clinician review, prospective validation and an intervention study.

## Demonstration and seed dispositions

I inspected the available local readable material within the stated availability limits. These demonstrations motivate rigor but do not establish this hypothesis:

Its temporal sequence modeling, external validation and bias awareness motivate grouped hospital-held-out validation here. eICU lacks its lifetime/genetic population and this is not a reproduction.
2. *Advancing cancer detection and treatment using longitudinal routine clinical data* (DOI `10.1016/j.cell.2026.07.009`): the README states the main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable. Only local metadata and the supplement were inspected; no unavailable main-text result is used. Its oncology/multimodal dependencies are absent from eICU, so it is not pursued.
3. *A Bayesian framework for longitudinal EHR and genetic discovery* (DOI `10.1038/s41586-026-10780-5`): the local article, supplement and reporting material are available and the article text was inspected. Its longitudinal selection/uncertainty discipline motivates explicit observation weighting and uncertainty; eICU has no configured germline data and this is not a genetic study.

Expert seed 04 is developed here with a narrower, auditable recorded FiO2/PaO2 interaction. Seed 05 was already developed in a valid prior eICU candidate around recorded ventilatory burden and liberation, but its sparse/ambiguous liberation semantics remain a limitation. Seeds 02, 03 and 09 were developed in prior valid lines; their measurement-process, hypotension and recorded insulin/low-glucose questions are not silently treated as evidence for hyperoxia. Seeds 01, 06, 07, 08 and 10 remain non-primary because treatment intent, organizational mechanisms, complete ascertainment, or transfer/recovery semantics are not identified here. The ten imported eICU cards are expert hypotheses, not conclusions; MIMIC, UK Biobank and HCC data are not cross-joined.
