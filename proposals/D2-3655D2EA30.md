## Hypothesis and clinical significance

The unchanged hypothesis is that MERTK inhibition may reduce inflammatory macrophage-to-FLS signaling in an inflammatory context but impair resolution-associated macrophage efferocytosis or FLS repair; any therapeutic rationale therefore depends on cell context and selective delivery. The clinical-development decision is whether to pursue cell-restricted testing, broad inhibition, or defer pending functional evidence. The strongest supported evidence is published association between MerTK-positive synovial tissue macrophage states and remission/stromal repair context [K1], broader homeostatic and immunoregulatory roles [K2], and the importance of matrix/niche remodeling in immune patterning [K3]. These do not establish MERTK causality, a therapeutic window, or clinical benefit. The strongest rival remains inhibitor off-target action, unverified exposure, toxicity/carry-over, or donor/state/measurement artifacts. Drug-associated RNA alone cannot distinguish these explanations.

The unaltered Harbor estimand is narrower: paired inhibitor-minus-vehicle difference in the two predeclared FLS transcript scores across six archived E-MTAB-8316 experiments. It is an archival RNA robustness audit, not the functional hypothesis test. The expression-direction support/adverse/inconclusive rules and all protocol boundaries remain those of the parent candidate. Transcript scores do not measure secretion, matrix repair, efferocytosis, viability, target engagement, or patient outcome.

**Alternative assessed, but deferred for inferential use:** for each score, model the same pair contrast as `delta[pair, score] = beta[score] + b[FLS donor, score] + error[pair, score]`. The potential target is the same two average paired score contrasts; `b` is a partially pooled FLS-specific treatment slope. A weakly regularizing prior on the FLS-slope SD and a robust pair residual could stabilize the estimate; correlated slope/covariance terms should only be attempted if prespecified, with prior-scale sensitivity and posterior predictive checks. This could add a shrinkage-based mean and a cautious indication of FLS response heterogeneity beyond the unpooled six contrasts. It cannot create additional biological replication.

With four FLS clusters, two repeated and two singleton, the slope variance is weakly informed and can be dominated by its prior or residual assumptions. Each monocyte donor appears in one pair, so monocyte-specific variance is not separable from pair residual. A random FLS intercept largely cancels in the matched contrast. A multi-gene mechanistic model is not an adequate rescue: genes are not donor replicates, and there are no measured exposure, target-engagement, or functional labels to identify an on-target mechanism.
