# Discordant AKI recovery: separating true renal recovery from creatinine artifacts

## Question

Among ICU patients with KDIGO stage 2-3 acute kidney injury, does creatinine recovery without concurrent recovery in urine output or fluid balance identify a discordant-recovery phenotype with worse subsequent outcomes than concordant recovery?

## Why it matters

Creatinine is the standard marker for AKI recovery, but it can be affected by muscle mass, fluid shifts, and measurement timing. Urine output and fluid balance provide complementary information about renal perfusion and function. If discordant recovery (creatinine improves but urine output/fluid status does not) carries higher mortality or longer ICU stay, clinicians should not rely on creatinine alone to declare recovery. This could change how recovery is assessed and when supportive care is escalated.

## Evidence base

[K1] Haines et al. (2023) demonstrated that eGFR creatinine systematically overestimated kidney function after prolonged critical illness, with a posterior mean difference of 33 ml/min/1.73m² at ICU discharge. Cystatin C better estimated true kidney function because it was unaffected by muscle loss. This supports the biological plausibility that creatinine-based recovery can be misleading after critical illness.

[K2] The KDIGO clinical practice guideline for AKI defines AKI stages using creatinine and urine output, and emphasizes that both criteria should be considered. It provides the clinical framework for defining recovery and for recognizing that a single marker is insufficient.

[K3] The MIMIC-IV documentation describes the available ICU tables, including creatinine, urine output, fluid balance, and vital signs, with exact field names and time fields. This establishes the data resolvability of the proposed design.

## Strongest rival explanation

The leading claim is that discordant recovery reflects true incomplete renal recovery. The strongest rival is that creatinine recovery is an artifact of fluid shifts or measurement timing: dilution from fluid administration can lower creatinine concentration without improving glomerular filtration, and the timing of creatinine draws can create apparent recovery that does not reflect a sustained change in kidney function.

## Discriminating comparison

To separate these explanations, the analysis will define two discordant-recovery phenotypes:

1. **Creatinine-defined discordance**: creatinine falls to within 20% of baseline by 48 hours, but urine output remains below 0.5 ml/kg/h for at least 12 hours and cumulative fluid balance is positive.
2. **Urine-output-defined discordance**: urine output remains below 0.5 ml/kg/h for at least 12 hours despite a positive fluid balance, regardless of creatinine trajectory.

If the leading claim is correct, both definitions should identify similar risk profiles, with creatinine-defined discordance carrying worse outcomes. If the rival is correct, creatinine-defined discordance will show no excess risk after adjustment for fluid balance and measurement timing, while urine-output-defined discordance will retain prognostic value.

## Method comparison

### Baseline

A Cox proportional hazards model with prespecified covariates: age, sex, ICU admission, KDIGO stage, fluid balance at 48 hours, and urine output at 48 hours. The outcome is 28-day mortality. This model tests whether discordant recovery (defined by creatinine) is associated with higher mortality after adjustment for fluid status.

### Substantive alternative

A time-to-event model with recurrent event handling (e.g., Andersen-Gill or pooled-episode model) that treats each ICU admission as a separate episode and allows for within-patient correlation. This model can reveal whether the prognostic signal of discordant recovery persists across multiple admissions, which the baseline Cox model cannot capture. It can also estimate the time-varying hazard of mortality, which may show that discordant recovery carries a higher early hazard that attenuates over time.

### What the alternative reveals

The baseline Cox model assumes independent episodes and a constant hazard ratio. The recurrent-event model can reveal whether the prognostic signal is driven by a single admission or persists across admissions, and whether the hazard ratio is time-varying. This information is lost in the baseline model and is relevant to the clinical question of whether discordant recovery is a stable phenotype or a transient artifact.

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must:
1. Fit the baseline Cox model and report the hazard ratio for creatinine-defined discordant recovery, with 95% confidence intervals.
2. Fit the recurrent-event model and report the hazard ratio for creatinine-defined discordant recovery, with 95% confidence intervals.
3. Compare the two models and report whether the prognostic signal persists across admissions.
4. Report the number of patients in each discordance phenotype and the number of events.

Completion is established by:
- A fitted baseline Cox model with reported hazard ratios and confidence intervals.
- A fitted recurrent-event model with reported hazard ratios and confidence intervals.
- A comparison table showing whether the prognostic signal persists across admissions.
- A summary of the number of patients and events in each phenotype.

## Falsification criteria

- **Supportive**: creatinine-defined discordant recovery is associated with higher 28-day mortality (HR > 1.2, 95% CI excluding 1) after adjustment for fluid balance and measurement timing, and the signal persists in the recurrent-event model.
- **Adverse**: creatinine-defined discordant recovery is not associated with higher mortality (HR < 1.2, 95% CI including 1) after adjustment, while urine-output-defined discordance retains prognostic value. This would support the rival explanation that creatinine recovery is an artifact.
- **Inconclusive**: the confidence intervals for the hazard ratio include both 1 and 1.2, or the number of events is too small to distinguish the two explanations.

## Limitations

- MIMIC-IV does not contain measured GFR or cystatin C, so the analysis can only identify suspected discordant recovery.
- Fluid balance and urine output are selectively measured, which may introduce selection bias.
- The analysis cannot establish causality; it can only identify associations.
