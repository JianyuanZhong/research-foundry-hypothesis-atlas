# Support-aware successor of the lagged discordant-AKI design

Status: proposed clinical-methods child; design only. No cohort scan, model fit, or scientific result was run.

Parent: [prior hypothesis]. This child preserves its six-hour exposure lag, first-eligible-ICU and t0 rules, component-specific hospital-death/ICU-recorded-RRT estimands, competing-event clocks, exact MIMIC bindings, and fixed-summary versus sequence comparison. It adds a prespecified, time-ordered treatment/support rival and a support-conditioned estimand.

## 1. Scientific opening and hypothesis

The inherited design asks whether recovery discordance—creatinine recovery accompanied by low direct urine output and/or positive recorded balance—predicts short-term outcomes after a six-hour lag. Its remaining clinically consequential ambiguity is that the same observed contrast may be selected or produced by support delivered before the landmark: vasopressors and ventilation mark shock severity, administered fluids create positive recorded balance, diuresis changes urine output, and RRT both changes the renal state and ends eligibility. MIMIC records that support was charted/administered, but not the clinician's intent, indication, bedside completeness, or counterfactual effect.

The strongest inspected evidence supports bounded premises, not the hypothesis. [K1] shows that creatinine can overestimate measured kidney function during prolonged critical illness as muscle is lost. [K2] shows that urine-output window and persistence choices change AKI classification and mortality prediction. [K3] reports mortality associations with vasoactive drugs, mechanical ventilation, and positive recorded balance while stating that KRT initiation was discretionary and recorded fluid balance omitted insensible losses. These observations motivate both a renal-recovery interpretation and a support/severity-selection rival; none establishes this MIMIC phenotype or causality.

Hypothesis:

> Among adults who reach the prespecified 24-, 48-, or 72-hour post-t0 landmark alive, in the first eligible ICU stay, and without incident pre-landmark ICU-recorded RRT, does a lagged recovery/urine/recorded-balance discordance predict seven-day hospital death and ICU-recorded RRT within observed pre-landmark support strata, and does the contrast persist when support is documented before the first renal-defining observation?

Leading interpretation: a reproducible component-specific prognostic association of renal recovery discordance. Strongest rival: observed support selection or treatment-driven measurement—support intensity and its timing identify severe patients, alter administered input/urine recording, or precede the discordance, so the apparent renal contrast is largely a support-process contrast.

The rival makes different observable predictions. If support selection dominates, support-first patients and support-intensity strata will carry much of the outcome gradient, renal-state contrasts will attenuate within supported strata, and a process/support-only diagnostic will resemble the renal contrast. If a renal-recovery prognostic signal remains, component contrasts should persist in well-supported support strata, in support-absent and support-present strata where both are observable, and across lagged landmarks; support-first versus renal-first ordering should not fully account for the contrast. Either result remains prognostic association. Neither identifies treatment effects, a mechanism, true GFR, or clinician intent.

## 2. Actual scientific deliverable and decision rule

The solver must newly estimate, for L in {24, 48, 72} hours:

1. A deterministic flow from first eligible ICU stay and t0 through the lagged assessment interval, six-hour washout, and landmark risk set.
2. Mutually exclusive renal states: concordant recovery, low-urine-only, positive-recorded-balance-only, both, unknown/insufficient opportunity, and no recovery; retain binary discordant = low urine OR positive recorded balance as a sensitivity.
3. A support block with exact presence, duration/opportunity, composition, and clinical ordering for vasopressor, ventilation, administered-volume, diuresis, and RRT signals.
4. Separate seven-day Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidences and standardized risk differences for hospital death and ICU-recorded RRT, with support-stratum estimates, support-by-renal-state interaction, events, competing events, person-time, and cluster/bootstrap uncertainty.
5. A support-only process diagnostic, support-first versus renal-observation-first diagnostic, current-window sensitivity, complete-opportunity sensitivity, and six-hour lagged primary result.
6. A fixed-summary competing-risk baseline and a support-aware sequence alternative on identical held-out subjects.
7. A manifest that links every estimate to source fields, item definitions, interval boundaries, risk set, competing events, adjustment/stratum, model, counts, uncertainty, and interpretation.

Completion requires saved flows, support audit tables, endpoint-specific estimates and intervals, sparse-cell/unknown-state reports, held-out metrics for both models, and conclusions linked to those outputs. A readiness probe, model coefficient, or favorable result is not completion.

Continue the direction if at least one component has adequate support/opportunity and a clinically meaningful, directionally consistent contrast across two or more landmarks that is not explained by support-first strata or documentation density. Revise to a narrower component/landmark if only one component or one support stratum is estimable. Defer/abandon the renal interpretation if support-only gradients match it and renal contrasts disappear in support-complete strata; retain the result as evidence about ascertainment/selection. Call the result inconclusive, not negative, when cells are sparse, unknown opportunity is large, or uncertainty spans clinically important effects.

## 3. Population and temporal boundaries

Use MIMIC-IV adults with `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`. Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, retain valid `intime < outtime) stays overlapping the admission interval, sort by `intime, stay_id`, and retain exactly the first valid ICU stay within each hospital admission. A later ICU stay cannot replace a first stay lacking a qualifying t0. A subject can contribute multiple admissions, but all admissions for a subject stay in one train/validation/test split.

Use hours from the retained ICU `intime`. Primary creatinine is `hosp/labevents.itemid=50912`; `52546` is a sensitivity. A valid row has finite `valuenum`, compatible `valueuom`, and clinical `charttime` inside the retained ICU interval. Duplicate valid primary values at one `charttime` use their median. A sensitivity item is used only when no primary value exists at that time. `storetime` never moves an observation earlier. Blank `hadm_id` lab rows are matched to the subject only when clinical time lies in the retained admission/ICU interval and cannot be assigned across overlapping admissions; otherwise they are excluded.

t0 is the earliest eligible creatinine `charttime) in [`intime`, `intime+72h`) that is either at least 0.3 mg/dL above a valid value in the immediately preceding 48 hours or at least 1.5 times the valid minimum in the preceding seven days. The preceding values use same `subject_id/hadm_id`, clinical `charttime`, and no value after the candidate. A separately labeled admission-baseline sensitivity may use the minimum valid creatinine in [`admittime`, t0) only when no prior window supports t0. Exclude established RRT before t0 using the procedure definition below; distinguish no baseline, no qualifying t0, invalid time, and pre-t0 RRT.

For each L, define Ltime = t0 + L and the primary lagged assessment interval:
`A_L = [max(t0, Ltime-24h), Ltime-6h)`.
It contains three six-hour bins under the inherited design. The six-hour washout is `W_L=[Ltime-6h,Ltime)`. Primary renal features cannot use observations in W_L or after Ltime. The primary risk set R_L contains subjects alive, still in the retained ICU, without incident ICU-recorded RRT before Ltime, and with a classifiable or explicitly unknown renal state. Report all losses rather than assigning a later state.

For treatment/support timing, define:
- `H_L=[t0, max(t0, Ltime-24h))`, antecedent support history before A_L (empty at L=24);
- `S_L), support documented or administered in A_L;
- `W_L), support in the washout, reported as attrition/diagnostic only and never used to adjust the primary contrast.

The primary support-conditioned prognostic comparison uses support in H_L and baseline/t0 variables. The support-order diagnostic uses time-resolved S_L without treating ordering as treatment assignment. At L=24, H_L is empty, so the support-order result is explicitly less informative and relies on t0 status plus within-A ordering.

## 4. Renal exposure and component outcomes

Use the most recent valid creatinine in A_L, peak creatinine from t0 through the end of A_L, and recovery if peak minus lagged creatinine >=0.3 mg/dL; peak/lagged ratio <=0.8 is a sensitivity. Primary direct urine uses `icu/outputevents` itemids 226559 Foley, 226560 Void, 226561 Condom Cath, 226563 Suprapubic, and 226567 Straight Cath, with `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, charttime, itemid, value, valueuom`. Numeric zero is observed zero; an empty bin is unknown. Exclude 226564/226565 nephrostomy, 226627/226631 OR/PACU, 226566/227489 mixed GU-irrigant, 226713 text estimate, and 227519 Apache-derived output from primary phenotype.

Convert urine to mL/kg/h using the nearest valid weight at or before the lagged endpoint within 48 hours, from `icu/chartevents` itemids 224639, 226512, 226531, with units verified via `icu/d_items`. Primary urine classifiability requires valid primary urine information in at least two of three A_L bins; report all-three and at-least-one sensitivities. Low urine uses the prespecified rate threshold from the inherited design; the threshold and bin rule are frozen before fitting and are not optimized on outcome.

For recorded balance, use `icu/inputevents` rows whose `starttime,endtime` intervals overlap A_L, with `itemid, amount, amountuom, totalamount, totalamountuom, rate, rateuom`. Never add `amount` and `totalamount` from one row. Convert only compatible mL quantities, retain item-level composition and unresolved/non-mL fractions, and calculate recorded input minus direct primary urine. It is a documentation proxy, not true total-body balance. A balance state requires valid input and direct-urine opportunity.

The mutually exclusive states are concordant recovery, low-urine-only, positive-recorded-balance-only, both, unknown/insufficient opportunity, and no recovery. Unknown is never imputed. The parent current-window [L-24h,Ltime) phenotype is a sensitivity to quantify boundary co-temporality.

Hospital death D_L,7 is the first valid `hosp/admissions.deathtime` in [Ltime,Ltime+7d) before an alive `dischtime), with `hospital_expire_flag` only a consistency check. Alive discharge is a competing event; alive and hospitalized at day 7 is administrative censoring. Missing or inconsistent death times are exceptions. If `deathtime=dischtime`, classify death only when the death flag/time is internally consistent.

ICU-recorded RRT R_L,7 is the first `icu/procedureevents` row with itemid in {225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809, 225955}, valid `starttime` in [Ltime,Ltime+7d), strictly before `icustays.outtime), while alive and in the retained ICU. ICU discharge alive and hospital death before RRT are competing events; ICU discharge wins when first and death wins when earlier or tied. Start exactly at outtime/deathtime is not an RRT event and is a tie exception. This is not all-hospital RRT. The union death-or-ICU-RRT is exploratory only.

Estimate seven-day Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence and risk difference (discordant minus concordant), standardized over the observed R_L. Use six-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards as an implementation check, not as a replacement for the competing-event estimand. For D, causes are death and alive discharge. For R, causes are RRT, alive ICU exit, and death. Use subject-clustered bootstrap or influence-function uncertainty across landmarks and report all person-time and exceptions.

## 5. Exact support/treatment rival block

All support variables are observed-record indicators, not proof that an intervention was intended or delivered completely.

Vasopressor exposure uses `icu/inputevents` with `itemid` in the prespecified MIMIC-IV core set: 221906 norepinephrine, 221289 epinephrine, 222315 vasopressin, 221662 dopamine, and 221749 phenylephrine. Verify each label and `linksto` in `icu/d_items` before freezing. Use `starttime,endtime,rate,rateuom,amount,amountuom,totalamount,totalamountuom,storetime,statusdescription`. Primary variables are any observed administration, hours with a valid interval, and a drug-class indicator; dose is not pooled across incompatible units. A sensitivity uses presence only.

Mechanical ventilation uses `icu/procedureevents` joined to `icu/d_items` on `itemid`, with the exact dictionary labels “Invasive Ventilation” and “Non-invasive Ventilation” (expected procedure itemids 225792 and 225794, frozen only after dictionary verification); extubation label/item (expected 225795) terminates an interval when present. Use `starttime,endtime,itemid,value,valueuom,statusdescription,ordercategoryname,storetime`. If the local dictionary disagrees, the dictionary label and verified itemid—not an invented substitute—control the analysis. Primary variables are any invasive ventilation, any non-invasive ventilation, and support hours before L.

Administered-volume support uses every compatible `icu/inputevents` row with valid `starttime,endtime` and mutually exclusive `amount/totalamount` volume accounting. Retain `itemid` and `icu/d_items.label` composition, total mL, mL/kg when a contemporaneous valid weight exists, and the fraction unresolved because units are not mL. Do not call this a fluid bolus, resuscitation, or treatment effect. A descriptive fluid-support subclass may be formed only from dictionary-verified crystalloid/colloid/blood labels frozen before fitting; the all-input total is primary.

Diuresis uses `icu/inputevents` dictionary-verified furosemide/loop-diuretic rows (the primary furosemide item is expected as itemid 221794, frozen after `d_items` verification), with `starttime,endtime,amount,amountuom,rate,rateuom,itemid,statusdescription`. Primary variables are any observed diuretic and unit-compatible exposure duration; cumulative dose is secondary and is not combined across incompatible formulations. This records an administered order/input, not intent or response.

RRT uses the exact procedureevents item set above, with `starttime,endtime,itemid,ordercategoryname,statusdescription`; pre-t0 RRT excludes the primary cohort, pre-L RRT is a risk-set loss, and post-L RRT is the component endpoint.

Observation opportunity is separate from treatment exposure. In each six-hour bin count distinct valid renal observations from `labevents.charttime`, `outputevents.charttime`, and `inputevents.starttime/endtime`; count support opportunity as the presence of a valid row/interval and report missing `endtime`, unit-invalid rows, and ICU-exit truncation. Count non-renal `labevents` and non-weight/CVP `chartevents` observations using `charttime`; `storetime` is a labeled documentation sensitivity. No row absence is interpreted as no treatment.

For each L and support class report:
- H_L support absent in the observed record, one support class, multiple support classes, support duration/opportunity, and unknown/invalid timing;
- S_L support-first if the first valid support start precedes the first renal-defining observation in A_L, renal-observation-first if the reverse ordering is observed, simultaneous/indeterminate if tied or missing, and neither if no support is recorded;
- support in W_L, but only as washout attrition/description;
- support-only outcome risks among subjects with classifiable renal state and renal-state outcome risks within support strata.

The first renal-defining observation is the earliest valid creatinine or primary urine observation that contributes to the A_L state. Positive balance is interval-derived, so its ordering is labeled indeterminate unless the contributing input interval starts before that first renal observation. This ordering check separates temporal compatibility from causal identification.

## 6. Primary comparison and adjustment

The primary estimand is the component-specific prognostic risk difference between discordant and concordant renal states at seven days, separately for D and R, standardized within the observed R_L and reported overall and within support strata. Strata are prespecified as:

- no observed H_L support versus any observed H_L support;
- support class: vasopressor, ventilation, administered-volume, diuresis, multiple support, or none;
- support-first versus renal-observation-first within S_L;
- adequate versus inadequate renal and input opportunity.

At L=24, the H_L stratum is structurally empty and will not be overinterpreted. Use interaction estimates only where each cell has adequate events; otherwise report counts and an inconclusive result.

The fixed prognostic adjustment model includes age, sex, admission type, baseline creatinine, AKI stage at t0, t0-to-L timing, ICU timing, pre-A renal summaries, H_L support indicators/durations, renal/output/input opportunity masks, and non-renal documentation density. The H_L block is antecedent to A_L when nonempty. S_L support variables are not silently treated as confounders: support-conditioned estimates and support-order estimates are descriptive diagnostics for selection/timing, not causal adjustment. Never adjust for post-L values, outcome-derived strata, or W_L support in the primary outcome model. Report unadjusted and standardized estimates so attenuation is visible.

Falsification/interpretation checks are fixed before fitting:

- If the renal contrast persists in both observed-support strata, across at least two landmarks, and is not matched by a support-only or documentation-density gradient, this supports a reproducible prognostic renal-discordance association.
- If support-first strata and support-only variables explain most of the contrast, or renal contrasts disappear when opportunity/support is restricted, this supports treatment/support selection or measurement-process rivalry and weakens renal interpretation.
- If death shows a contrast but ICU-RRT does not, do not call it a renal-support signal; if ICU-RRT shows a contrast only while ICU observation is retained, do not generalize beyond recorded ICU RRT.
- If the current-window sensitivity is positive but the six-hour-lagged interval is not, boundary co-temporality is favored.
- If contaminated output item definitions or storetime ordering alone create the contrast, the primary phenotype is not supported.
- A support gradient is not a negative-control outcome and cannot prove that documentation causes death.
- Sparse cells, large unknown states, inconsistent event times, or intervals spanning clinically important effects are inconclusive, not refutations.

## 7. Baseline versus learned/mechanistic alternative

Primary baseline: a fixed-summary, six-hour cause-specific discrete-time competing-risk model using the same A_L and H_L inputs: creatinine level/peak/recovery/slope and AKI stage; three-bin urine values, low-urine state and masks; input/output composition and recorded balance; weight and exploratory CVP; vasopressor class/presence/hours; ventilation mode/hours; administered-volume total/composition/unresolved fraction; diuretic presence/duration; pre-L RRT; ICU timing, admission type, age, sex, and non-renal documentation density. Fit separate heads for hospital death/alive discharge and ICU-RRT/ICU exit/death. Standardize held-out cumulative incidence and risk differences; report calibration, Brier score, events, support-stratum calibration and cluster-bootstrap intervals.

Substantive alternative: a support-aware temporal sequence model on the identical six-hour bins from t0 to Ltime-6h, with separate channels and missingness masks for creatinine, urine, input/output, weight/CVP, vasopressor, ventilation, administered-volume composition, diuresis, RRT, and observation counts. Use a causal-time mask so no W_L or post-L value is visible. Separate cause-specific heads predict D, alive discharge, ICU-RRT, and ICU exit. Include an auxiliary ordering/persistence output—support-first, renal-first, simultaneous/unknown and persistent/resolving renal discordance—only as a descriptive trajectory target, not as a causal mechanism label. This alternative can reveal whether timing, persistence, or support transitions lost by fixed summaries distinguish the two interpretations; it cannot recover treatment intent or true renal physiology.

Use a subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split with all admissions for a subject in one split. Evaluate endpoint-specific held-out calibration, cumulative-incidence calibration, time-dependent Brier, cause-specific discrimination, and support-stratum/ordering-stratum risk contrasts with bootstrap uncertainty. The sequence model is not selected for a small AUROC gain. Revisit it only if event support is adequate and its held-out trajectory/order outputs change the renal-versus-support interpretation or calibration; otherwise retain the transparent baseline.

Planning envelope is CPU-default: 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, up to 28,800 seconds for the future solver, with a bounded readiness probe before fitting. The estimate is inherited planning, not a measured full-study runtime. An allocated A100 may accelerate repeated sequence fitting but is not required; ordinary shell CUDA visibility is not evidence of hardware absence, and this discovery branch does not launch GPU work.

Alternatives not chosen: a causal treatment effect or inverse-probability support analysis is deferred because intent, complete administered exposure, treatment indications, and post-ICU RRT are unavailable; a single black-box model as primary is rejected because prediction cannot resolve ascertainment; the mixed death-or-ICU-RRT union remains exploratory; post-L support adjustment is rejected as leakage; external validation is deferred to a cohort with adjudicated treatment and renal measures.

## 9. Evidence limits and interpretation

Supportive evidence can establish only a reproducible prognostic association for the component and support/opportunity strata that replicate. It cannot establish that support caused the phenotype, that renal dysfunction caused death, that recorded balance is total-body balance, or that ICU-recorded RRT is all hospital RRT.

Adverse evidence is support-first dominance, attenuation after opportunity restriction, a support-only gradient as large as the renal contrast, a current-window-only result, reversal across landmarks, contaminated item dependence, or a death/RRT component mismatch. These weaken the leading renal interpretation but do not prove normal renal function or absence of treatment effect.

Inconclusive evidence is sparse RRT or support cells, wide intervals, inconsistent times, large unknown states, nonidentifiable support order, or a sequence gain without reproducible component/ordering interpretation.

Computationally checkable claims are source/member/schema binding, joins, item dictionary verification, first-stay/t0 filters, lag/washout, support intervals, state construction, competing-event counts, estimates, uncertainty, split integrity, held-out metrics, and falsification outputs. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for true GFR, muscle mass, treatment indication/intent, complete fluid capture, insensible losses, complete ward RRT, goals of care, causal effects, and transportability.
