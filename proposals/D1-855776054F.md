# Hypotension duration, renal reserve, and observed renal deterioration: an eICU successor

## Scientific question and advance

The parent tests whether density-aware minutes of MAP below 65 mmHg add transportable information beyond minimum MAP for a subsequent creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal. This successor asks: **does that information differ by pre-landmark renal reserve, and does the interaction survive prespecified renal-phenotype and MAP-observation sensitivities?**

The strongest claim supported before execution is only that eICU contains ICU-relative MAP observations, creatinine-labelled laboratory rows, admission physiology, and hospital identifiers. No effect, AKI rate, or transport result has been computed. The unresolved claim is that, among patients with comparable observed minimum MAP, low-MAP duration has a larger association with a future creatinine deterioration signal in a low-reserve phenotype, reproducibly across hospitals and plausible ascertainment rules.

This is an observational risk-marker/effect-modification study, not a causal treatment analysis. A positive interaction could motivate targeted prospective monitoring or renal adjudication; it cannot justify a MAP or pressor target. The advance over the parent is a clinically interpretable duration-by-renal-reserve estimand and an outcome-robustness panel, rather than only a population-average duration coefficient. A null interaction is informative because it would argue against using measured pre-landmark reserve to personalize interpretation of the duration signal.

## Population, landmark, exposure and reserve

Retain the first ICU stay per person: adult (age >=18, mapping >89 to 90 and reporting other nonnumeric ages), unitvisitnumber=1, nonmissing hospital and numeric discharge offset, and at least 36 hours between offset 0 and discharge. If a person has multiple unitvisitnumber=1 rows, retain the earliest valid stay and report the conflict. Candidate landmarks are t = 12, 18, ..., 72 hours after ICU admission. Require at least six hours before t and at least 24 hours after t; exclude EOL-proxy landmarks at or before t.

Primary exposure is constructed in [t-360,t) from valid numeric vitalPeriodic.systemicmean in [-50,300]. Each observation represents min(5, next observation offset - current offset) minutes only; no carry-forward across a gap and no interval beyond t. Require >=80% observed coverage, with 70% and 90% sensitivity gates. Derive covered minutes, low-MAP minutes below 65, low-MAP fraction, minimum MAP, and episode count descriptively. The primary duration contrast is >60 versus 0-60 low-MAP minutes within minimum-MAP strata (55-64, 45-54, <45 where supported), with a continuous 60-minute contrast secondary. Repeat thresholds 60 and 70 mmHg, the aperiodic MAP source, and a periodic-first/aperiodic-second source rule.

Define renal reserve at t using the last two numeric, label-audited creatinine results with labresultoffset <= t, labresultrevisedoffset <= t, and each result in [t-48h,t]. The primary reserve measure is the last value, categorized by prespecified quantiles computed in training data; a secondary measure is pre-t slope (last minus first divided by elapsed hours). A separate sensitivity uses admission apacheApsVar.creatinine after a feasibility audit. Do not combine values across unverified units. If fewer than two comparable results exist, retain a one-result reserve sensitivity but do not call it longitudinal reserve.

## Outcomes and estimand

The primary outcome is a creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal in (t,t+24h]: the first future valid creatinine at least 0.3 above baseline or at least 1.5 times baseline, where baseline is the last pre-t value. Require a valid future result; missing future testing is not a negative. Analyse three prespecified outcome versions: (1) the primary first-future-value rule, (2) a last-pre-t baseline rule, and (3) absolute-only and relative-only decompositions. If label/system/interface review cannot establish comparable units and a plausible range, stop interpretation and report inconclusive.

The primary estimand is the standardized 24-hour risk-difference interaction: compare the >60 versus 0-60 minute low-MAP contrast between low- and high-reserve strata, conditional on minimum-MAP support and the observed eligible-landmark distribution. Report stratum-specific risks and risk differences, the risk-difference interaction with a patient-clustered 95% interval, and a prespecified duration-by-reserve-by-minimum-MAP check. ICU death and discharge/transfer before 24 hours are competing events; landmarks with no future creatinine are observation-censored, never event-free. Report cause-specific and cumulative-incidence estimates for renal signal and competing events, plus future-creatinine observation probability by exposure and reserve.

Covariates are the parent’s time-safe pre-window MAP summaries and coverage, heart rate, respiration, SpO2, creatinine level/slope, age, sex, ethnicity, time since admission, admission Apache fields/scores, current documented ventilation and pressor-record indicators, EOL exclusion and missingness indicators. Hospital is a stratification/holdout variable. No adjustment removes unmeasured severity, treatment intent, or testing selection.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The transparent baseline is a duration-free regularized pooled-logistic competing-risk model B_min with spline minimum MAP, reserve stratum, their interaction, pre-window physiology, admission covariates, and observation/missingness indicators. The duration-added model B_dur adds low-MAP minutes and the prespecified duration-by-reserve interaction. Fit on training hospitals with patient-level internal validation and evaluate untouched hospitals. Standardize predictions to the eligible landmark distribution. The primary comparison is the duration-by-reserve risk-difference interaction and uncertainty, not AUROC.

The substantive alternative is a bounded joint temporal model J_shape on the same outcome and split. Construct six one-hour bins for MAP values/masks, low-MAP minutes, gap length/coverage, creatinine values/masks and slope, pressor/ventilation records, and admission covariates. Fit a small temporal-convolution model with a reserve-conditioned duration representation and a discrete-time competing-risk head for renal signal, death and discharge; include an observation head predicting whether a future creatinine is observed. Compare to an otherwise identical duration-free J_min. This can reveal whether recovery shape, repeated gaps, and timing of creatinine observation modify the reserve-duration relationship—information discarded by summary minutes and one reserve value. The observation head is a diagnostic/weighting component, not proof that missingness is ignorable.

Use patient-level separation and hospital-held-out evaluation. Tune only on training hospitals using an internal patient split; never use hospital ID as a feature. Quantify uncertainty with hospital-stratified patient bootstrap for baseline models and three fixed seeds plus patient bootstrap for the temporal model. Compare interaction estimates, standardized risks, calibration, Brier score, cumulative-incidence calibration and decision curves at frozen thresholds. AUROC is secondary.

Budget under inputs.json (science_seconds=7200): two CPUs, up to 8 GiB; 20-40 minutes extraction/support audit, <20 minutes per regularized model, and 60-120 minutes for both small temporal models with early stopping. GPU is deferred because it does not identify the reserve interaction or solve the missing gold-standard MAP problem. If label, exposure-strata, observation support, or hospital holdout fails, stop at feasibility and do not substitute an easier endpoint.

## Falsification and interpretation

1. Require comparable creatinine labels/units, both duration levels within reserve/minimum-MAP support, future-outcome support, MAP coverage, and the prespecified number of eligible hospitals before interpreting the interaction.
2. Repeat 70/80/90% coverage, MAP thresholds 60/65/70, periodic versus aperiodic MAP, absolute versus relative outcome, one-result versus two-result reserve, and first versus last future creatinine. A finding present under one interface or threshold is weak.
3. Permute the order of MAP observations within each pre-t window while preserving values, count and coverage. A duration/recovery-shape effect should attenuate; minimum MAP and coverage should remain comparable.
4. Apply the creatinine-rise rule as a pre-t placebo in [t-24h,t), using only timestamps before t. Similar placebo and future associations indicate reverse-time severity or documentation bias.
5. Model future-creatinine observation as an outcome and report exposure-by-reserve observation rates. If the interaction exists only among frequently tested patients or disappears under inverse-probability observation weighting/complete-case sensitivity, classify it as observation-process sensitive.
6. Exclude landmarks within 12 hours of discharge/transfer, EOL-proxy landmarks, and abrupt loss of all MAP coverage; repeat with one random landmark per stay and patient-clustered inference.
7. Test adjustment for documented pressor/ventilation records, but do not call this treatment confounding control because infusion end times and intent are unavailable.
8. Require replication of the sign and clinically nontrivial magnitude in held-out hospitals; do not rank hospitals.

Supportive results are a duration-by-low-reserve interaction with a 95% interval excluding zero, consistent direction across outcome and MAP-source sensitivities, no strong pre-t placebo, and reproducible held-out-hospital calibration/interaction estimates. This supports a transportable observational risk-stratification signal and motivates prospective renal adjudication or targeted monitoring research.

Adverse results are a precise null/negative interaction, disappearance under source/coverage/observation checks, a strong pre-t placebo, or failure in held-out hospitals. This weakens the claim that measured renal reserve modifies duration information; it does not prove duration is harmless or that minimum MAP is a treatment target.

Inconclusive results include noncomparable units, sparse paired creatinine or future observation, no exposure contrast in reserve strata, too few hospitals, unstable labels, or wide intervals. No clinical recommendation follows.

## Evidence limits and deliverable

Computationally checkable are source hashes and headers, exact filters/joins, ICU-relative timestamps, exposure arithmetic, reserve/outcome construction, support by hospital, fitted parameters, interaction estimates, competing-event/observation metrics, uncertainty, splits, calibration and falsification outputs. Not established are adjudicated KDIGO AKI, urine-output criteria, outpatient baseline renal function, true continuous MAP between observations, artifact-free pressure, complete vasopressor dose/stop times, clinician intent, treatment effects, or benefit from acting on a prediction. These require clinical adjudication, richer measurement/treatment metadata, or a prospective study.

Completion requires a machine-readable provenance/support manifest; newly fitted B_min, B_dur, J_min, and J_shape; reserve-stratified standardized risks and duration-by-reserve interaction intervals; observation-process and competing-event outputs; held-out-hospital results; and every falsification sensitivity. No computed estimate is claimed here.

Alternatives not chosen: a latent “true MAP” state-space correction is deferred because eICU lacks a gold-standard waveform/reference channel, so its measurement-error scale would be unidentified; revisit only with a validated duplicate/reference MAP source. Hyperoxia, insulin-after-treatment, and hospital recovery-speed seeds remain deferred because required longitudinal exposure, administration, nutrition, or practice-adjudication fields are not guaranteed. A pure deep sequence predictor without a reserve-conditioned estimand is deferred because predictive gain alone would not answer the clinical question.
