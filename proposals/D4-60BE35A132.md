# Sparse CBC trajectories with an ascertainment-safe CRC specificity fallback

## Substantive evolution from the assessed parent

This child evolves assessed parent `[prior hypothesis]` without weakening its central coverage safeguard. The parent correctly makes a release-matched cancer/death coverage certificate a prerequisite for population-level prognostic, censoring, calibration and capacity claims. That certificate could not be established from the local catalog, the exact rectangular export, the available cancer supplement, or generic public field pages. The new contribution is an explicitly separate, event-anchored fallback that remains identifiable when endpoint completeness is unresolved: a conditional comparison among participants who have a valid, recorded malignant diagnosis, rather than an analysis that silently labels everyone without a recorded event as cancer-free.

The certified population analysis remains the preferred and unchanged estimand. If the certificate and field semantics pass, it tests whether prespecified sparse CBC trajectories improve five-year CRC prognosis after a day-366 landmark. If coverage remains uncertified but the cancer-field semantics gate passes, the fallback tests whether those trajectories distinguish recorded CRC from recorded non-CRC malignant cancer among eligible recorded cases. The fallback cannot estimate population risk, incidence, survival, hazard, non-events, PPV, calibration, capacity yield or clinical utility. If field semantics cannot be verified, only the raw audit is permitted.

## Clinical question and falsifiable hypotheses

The primary certified question is: among UK Biobank repeat attenders aged 40–75 with paired protocolized CBC measurements, alive and free of malignant cancer through day 365 after the second assessment, do prespecified annualized changes in haemoglobin, MCV and platelets add prognostic information for CRC recorded during days 366–1825 beyond age, sex and the second-assessment CBC values, and is the increment greater for CRC than for first non-CRC malignant cancer?

The fallback question is narrower: among eligible participants with a first valid recorded malignant diagnosis during the outcome window `[E,H]`, where `E=L+366` and `H=L+1825`, do the same prespecified CBC trajectories distinguish recorded CRC from recorded non-CRC malignant cancer beyond age, sex and instance-1 CBC values? Its estimand is the conditional recorded-case contrast

`P(recorded CRC | recorded malignant cancer in [E,H], eligible history and observed fields)`.

It is not a claim about `P(CRC)` in the source population. The fallback hypothesis is that the trajectory terms provide incremental case-case discrimination, and specifically that the incremental CRC-versus-non-CRC discrimination is better than a prespecified age/sex/current-CBC comparator. The null is no incremental case-case discrimination. An adverse result is a precise null or deterioration, not evidence that CBC trends cannot have value in a complete, clinically timed population.

The strongest evidence currently supports exact source availability, a full-row feasibility audit, and the general plausibility of longitudinal routine-data cancer prediction. It does not support complete follow-up for this `ukb672073` rectangular export, authoritative units for all CBC fields, or the untested incremental CRC-specific claim. The available cancer demonstration supplement is used only for contextual support about longitudinal routine-data prediction and washout; its unavailable main article and STAR Methods are not treated as inspected.

## Cohorts and temporal rules

Use calendar arithmetic: `E=L+366`; washout is `[L,E)`, days 0 through 365, and follow-up includes `E` through `H=L+1825`. A diagnosis or death on `E` is a follow-up endpoint or competing event, not washout.

The certified primary cohort requires age 40–75 at `L`, finite paired primary CBC values, `365 <= L-t0 <= 2922` days, no malignant diagnosis before `L`, alive at `L`, and no CRC, non-CRC malignant cancer or death in `[L,E)`. It additionally requires `E` to be inside the authoritative common cancer/death coverage interval. Left truncation is at `E`. The alternate cohort retains non-CRC malignancy during washout but excludes washout CRC and death; it is a cancer-experienced survivor estimand and cannot be used for the paired first-CRC versus first-non-CRC specificity contrast.

Cancer parsing accepts only syntax confirmed by the semantics gate, including three-character, undotted child-code and dotted child-code forms as applicable. Malignancy is `C00–C97`; CRC is `C18`, `C19` or `C20`, including children; non-CRC is malignant excluding CRC. Collapse exact `eid/date/code` duplicates. If both CRC and non-CRC codes occur on one date, classify that date as CRC while retaining every underlying code. Exclude diagnoses after earliest death from observable endpoint analyses and audit them. Same-day cancer/death is death-first primarily because within-day order is unavailable; cancer-first is a sensitivity rule.

The recorded-case fallback uses the same base/history and temporal construction, but does not require absence of an event to define follow-up. It includes only participants with a valid, suffix-paired malignant diagnosis in `[E,H]`, with the first eligible recorded malignant date determining the case label. Participants with no recorded case are not controls and are not assigned a negative outcome. Participants with both CRC and non-CRC codes on the first date receive the prespecified CRC date-level label and are reported separately in an ambiguity audit. Cases after death are excluded from the primary fallback, and same-day death cases are retained only under the prespecified death-first rule. The fallback therefore estimates recorded-case specificity conditional on the ascertainment process, not incidence or prognosis in all eligible participants.

## Variables and models

For each primary marker `m`, define

`slope_m = (m_1-m_0) / ((L-53-0.0)/365.2425)`.

The prespecified trajectory terms are falling haemoglobin, falling MCV and rising platelets. Keep exactly three continuous one-degree-of-freedom slope terms; do not choose markers, windows, transformations or thresholds using outcome data. The comparator contains age at `L`, sex, and instance-1 haemoglobin, MCV and platelets. RDW and its slope are a separately labeled secondary extension requiring four-marker completeness.

For the certified branch, fit the locked low-dimensional ridge-penalized cause-specific Cox model with left truncation at `E`; estimate CRC cumulative incidence with Aalen–Johansen and death as a competing event; use Fine–Gray only as sensitivity. Use identical participant-level repeated outer splits for comparator and trajectory models. Scaling, imputation, censoring estimation and penalty fitting must be fold-local. Report paired out-of-fold time-dependent AUC/Uno C-index, IPCW integrated Brier change and horizon-specific calibration only with adequate event and effective-sample support. Refit the identical pipeline for first non-CRC malignancy on the same eligible people and splits and report `D = incremental CRC AUC - incremental non-CRC AUC` with participant-bootstrap intervals. Capacity results at fixed 1%, 2% and 5% are descriptive and require certified censoring and competing-risk information.

For the fallback, fit a locked penalized logistic or multinomial-free binary case-case model among recorded cases only, with CRC versus non-CRC as the label. Use the same comparator, the comparator-plus-three-slopes trajectory model, identical participant-level repeated outer splits, and fold-local preprocessing. The primary fallback estimand is the paired out-of-fold change in case-case log loss and AUC, with bootstrap intervals clustered at participant level. Report calibration only as calibration within recorded cases and only when class support is adequate; do not translate it to risk, PPV, population sensitivity or a review-capacity yield. The case-case model is not a survival model and must not use time-to-event methods to manufacture non-case person-time. A prespecified secondary analysis stratifies recorded cases by diagnosis lag from `E` (for example, 0–365 versus 366–1,459 days) only if each stratum meets support gates; it is descriptive and cannot establish lead time or incident status.

## Event gates and decision hierarchy

For the certified branch, at least 60 coverage-valid CRC events permits the locked association, prediction and descriptive capacity analyses; 30–59 permits parsimonious association and descriptive censoring-aware analyses but no flexible model or strong calibration claim; fewer than 30 permits flow, event audits, cumulative incidence and at most the simplest prespecified penalized association, with incremental prediction inconclusive.

For the fallback, require at least 50 recorded CRC cases and 100 recorded non-CRC cases for the locked case-case increment. With 25–49 CRC cases or 50–99 non-CRC cases, permit only the locked parsimonious association/descriptive case-case comparison with uncertainty explicitly labeled imprecise. Below either minimum, report case flow, code/date audits and class counts only; incremental discrimination is inconclusive. These thresholds govern precision and do not convert recorded counts into coverage-valid population events.

The branch is selected before model performance is inspected:

1. semantics and release-matched coverage pass: certified population analysis is primary; fallback is an ascertainment sensitivity analysis;
2. semantics pass but coverage is unresolved: no population-level inferential or operating-characteristic claims; run only the recorded-case fallback and raw audit;
3. semantics fail: raw audit only, with no clinical cancer classification claim.

## Falsification and interpretation

Permute endpoint labels within sex and five-year age strata and rerun all fold-local steps. Any systematic retained increment in either branch indicates leakage, dependence left unaccounted for, or implementation error. For the certified branch, attempt prediction of pre-`L` CRC using the post-`L` pipeline as an impossible-temporal-outcome check. For the fallback, permute CRC/non-CRC labels within diagnosis-lag and age/sex strata; above-null performance after permutation indicates leakage or coding contamination. Unit tests must cover identical-suffix pairing, ICD syntax, exact day-366 inclusion, same-day precedence, post-death exclusion, left truncation, no-case-as-control handling, fold isolation and training-fold-only preprocessing/weight estimation.

Support for the certified hypothesis requires adverse-direction joint slope association with uncertainty excluding the null, positive paired out-of-fold prediction increment without Brier or calibration deterioration, and clean falsification checks. CRC specificity additionally requires paired `D>0` with an interval excluding zero and compatible Brier findings. Support for the fallback is limited to reproducible incremental case-case discrimination with adequate class support and clean permutation tests; it means CBC trajectories differ in recorded CRC versus recorded non-CRC cases, not that they identify CRC among the general population.

A precise null or adverse result in the certified branch discourages sparse CBC trends as CRC-prioritisation evidence in this risk set. A precise null or adverse fallback result weakens the recorded-case specificity hypothesis but cannot refute population-level value under different ascertainment. Broad intervals, unstable weights, low event support, discordant metrics, failed semantics, or unresolved coverage are inconclusive. Computation can verify cohort construction, pairing, timing, model fitting, resampling and reported uncertainty. It cannot establish registry completeness, incident-versus-correction status, clinically appropriate units, within-day ordering, transportability or a safe action threshold.
