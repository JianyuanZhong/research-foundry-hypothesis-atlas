# Decision-relevant monitoring value of early hepatic-reserve change after first TACE

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

In adults with HCC who undergo a first recorded transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does early post-procedure change in routine hepatic-reserve assays identify patients who should receive intensified near-term reassessment, beyond pre-TACE reserve, prior history, index-procedure context, and the observation process?

The strongest claim supported by existing evidence and this record is that baseline hepatic reserve and disease/procedure context are prognostically relevant, and that longitudinal laboratory change is biologically plausible. Recent published trajectory work is in other cohorts and does not validate this HCC snapshot's endpoint. The unresolved claim is narrower: whether an early adverse trajectory provides reproducible *incremental prognostic and monitoring-stratification information* for a subsequent local-record hepatic-decompensation signal.

Primary hypothesis: among the prespecified first-TACE target population, adding an adverse early trajectory (albumin or platelets falling and/or total bilirubin or INR rising from the pre-index window to the early post-index window) will improve patient-held-out proper scoring and calibration over a pre-TACE state/history plus procedure/observation baseline for a new coded decompensation encounter in T0+8 to T0+90. The trajectory coefficients/contrast must be adverse-direction and remain directionally similar in prespecified endpoint-specificity tiers and observation/follow-up sensitivity analyses. A secondary decision-analytic hypothesis is that the trajectory model yields more useful risk stratification for a hypothetical action of repeat hepatic assessment within 48 hours, measured by decision-curve net benefit over a predeclared plausible threshold range; this does not estimate benefit from actually taking that action.

This is not a causal estimate of TACE toxicity, a treatment effect, an adjudicated liver-failure rate, a mortality estimate, or a clinical threshold. It is not evidence that monitoring changes outcomes.

Death capture is incomplete and is not silently included.

## Baseline, alternative, fitting and split

B0 is penalized logistic regression using pre-TACE assay levels, missingness/count, prior 180-day coded history, age/sex and broad encounter history. B1 adds the index procedure-context bundle (procedure-string category/source, duration when valid, same-encounter procedure counts), post-index observation features (assay availability/count/timing) and 90-day follow-up ascertainment features. These are the clinically relevant current-state/care-process baselines.

A1 is a constrained irregular longitudinal reserve model on the same inputs: assay-specific robust pre/post summaries plus a monotone signed trajectory score, observation intensity and missingness indicators, with regularization and no cross-assay unit pooling. Its target is the held-out binary endpoint via penalized logistic likelihood; no unobserved trajectory is imputed as observed. A2 is a substantive learned alternative: a small patient-level, fold-fitted temporal model (regularized additive/spline or shallow sequence model) that maps the dated assay values, gaps and procedure/observation context to the same 90-day endpoint. It is useful only if it reveals nonlinear timing, recovery-versus-worsening patterns or interactions that A1 loses; its comparison is scientific, not a search for a tiny metric gain.

Fit all preprocessing, assay mapping, imputation parameters, feature selection and model parameters inside each patient-held-out fold. Use repeated grouped cross-fitting by `患者主索引`; no patient appears in both train and test. Estimate out-of-fold Brier/log loss, calibration slope/intercept and calibration plots, AUROC/AUPRC as descriptive, and decision curves for the hypothetical reassessment action. Report bootstrap or cross-fitting uncertainty, event counts, effective sample size and positivity/observation diagnostics. The actual deliverable is newly fitted out-of-fold risk estimates, trajectory contrasts with uncertainty, calibration/proper-score differences, endpoint-tier and sensitivity results, and a frozen feature/assay audit—not merely an input audit.

Budget: CPU-first, up to 8 cores and 32 GB RAM for at most 2 hours; use streaming reads for the large labs/medications files and declare source files plus derived audit/prediction outputs. A small GPU is optional only if the learned alternative demonstrably needs it; do not force it.

## Falsification and interpretation

Falsify the primary hypothesis if the prespecified trajectory contrast is not adverse-direction, or if its held-out proper-score improvement over B1 is non-positive with uncertainty excluding the prespecified clinically meaningful margin, or if apparent gains vanish in severe/repeat-confirmed tiers, observation-weighted analyses, or follow-up ascertainment sensitivity. A negative-control exposure formed by permuting early trajectories within patient/eligible index strata must not reproduce the primary gain; a negative-control pre-index outcome window must not be predicted by post-index data.

Supportive results require adverse-direction contrast, reproducible out-of-fold score/calibration improvement over B1, stable endpoint tiers, and no severe optimism or observation-only explanation. This supports a local prognostic/monitoring-stratification signal and justifies external and clinical validation, not a monitoring policy. Adverse results suggest early trajectory adds little beyond current state/care process or is too observation-dependent. Inconclusive results include sparse events, poor assay support, unstable units/name mapping, nonpositivity, wide uncertainty, or discordant endpoint tiers; they should trigger no clinical claim.

Clinical adjudication is essential to determine whether coded/narrative terms represent true decompensation, whether the hypothetical reassessment action is appropriate, and whether any threshold has acceptable harms. Exact tumor burden/stage, imaging, validated text extraction, treatment dose/technical TACE details, complete mortality and external follow-up are unavailable. Those limitations require chart review, linkage to adjudicated outcomes, or an independent external cohort before clinical deployment or causal interpretation.
