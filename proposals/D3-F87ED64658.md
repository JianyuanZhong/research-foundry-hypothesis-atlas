# Donor-aware macrophage-to-FLS communication in E-MTAB-8873

## Provisional hypothesis and clinical importance

In the frozen E-MTAB-8873 targeted expression experiment, co-culture of RA type B synovial cells (FLS) with MerTK/CD206-positive macrophages may induce a reproducible, condition-specific transcriptional response that is distinguishable from FLS-only and MerTK/CD206-negative macrophage co-culture after accounting for contributing donors and repeated context measurements. Resolving this would determine whether the available paired conditions support a testable stromal–myeloid mechanism or whether apparent effects are better explained by donor, composition, treatment, batch, or assay artifacts.

This is a hypothesis, not a computed result. The inspected literature motivates reciprocal macrophage–FLS communication and failed resolution in RA synovium [K1], supports the relevance of preserving macrophage-containing synovial interactions in experimental models [K2], and describes MerTK-associated resolution pathways [K3]. None of these works establishes a response in E-MTAB-8873, direct ligand-receptor action, causality, or clinical benefit.

## Falsifiable experiment

Population: all 33 records with verified exact joins and defensible contributing-donor representation. Primary contrasts, restricted to `treatment=none`, are:

1. MerTK/CD206-positive macrophage co-culture versus not co-cultured;
2. MerTK/CD206-positive macrophage co-culture versus MerTK/CD206-negative co-culture.

Use a donor-aware paired/stratified contrast or contributing-donor random-effects model in log-expression space, with the contributing donor/context as the uncertainty unit. Use condition/donor-preserving permutation inference. Report feature effects, uncertainty, permutation results, donor counts, and negative-control behavior. Pre-LPS and inhibitor records are mechanistic context, not independent causal replicates. Prespecify direction, effect-size and donor-level error thresholds before inspecting results.

Supportive results require a condition-specific signature with donor-aware uncertainty, separation from both controls, and no comparable signal in the negative-control contrast. Adverse results include no separation, reversal, or failure under donor/condition-preserving permutations. Inconclusive results include unresolved donor mapping, insufficient independent donor contexts, low detectability, or uncertainty too large to distinguish communication from composition or technical artifacts.

## Baseline, alternative, and deliverable

The simple baseline is the transparent donor-aware feature contrast above. A substantive alternative is a hierarchical/regularized model across the 445 measured features, borrowing information across features to reveal coordinated pathway-level structure that single-feature contrasts lose. It cannot establish direct ligand-receptor action or causality and is deferred unless metadata integrity and baseline signal are established. Both methods use the same inputs, contrasts, donor-stratified split/permutation, outcome, and uncertainty evaluation.

The scientific deliverable is a fitted donor-aware contrast table and prespecified condition-specific signature test, with estimates, uncertainty, permutation results, negative-control behavior, and an explicit supportive/adverse/inconclusive interpretation. Screening is planned; no job result is claimed.

## Limits and competing explanations

The assay is targeted and cannot support claims about unmeasured genes. Repeated context measurements and reused donors do not make cells independent biological replicates. Donor identity, age/sex, macrophage composition/ratio, treatment, batch, cell count, and targeted-panel measurement are competing explanations. Expression association cannot establish direct target action, ligand-receptor causality, clinical transition, or treatment benefit; those require perturbation, independent experimental evidence, or another study. The anomalous feature identifier and donor conflict are limitations, not biological findings.
