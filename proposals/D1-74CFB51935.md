# Provisional hypothesis: reduced laboratory testing during persistent instability

Status: provisional and untested. This is a substantive repair of [prior hypothesis], not an estimated result.

## Proposed relationship

Among adult eICU unit stays with a measurable laboratory-testing history and persistent physiologic instability, a prespecified abrupt reduction in laboratory observation intensity over the preceding 6 hours is associated with a higher probability of a new physiologic deterioration event during the subsequent 6 hours, compared with no reduction, after adjustment for the immediately preceding clinical state, hospital, and time from admission.

The primary estimand is an adjusted observational association at a time-varying index, not a causal effect of withholding tests. The unresolved scientific claim is whether the association reflects information loss during instability, rather than current severity, end-of-life care, discharge/transfer, or observation-practice artifacts.

For each candidate index time t, define the exposure window as [t-6h,t), using only lab rows with labresultoffset in that half-open interval. Define the prior reference window as [t-12h,t-6h). Use labresultoffset, not labresultrevisedoffset, for the exposure time; the revised offset is retained only as a data-quality/sensitivity field. Count distinct labid values as the primary testing count and distinct labname values as a secondary count. A zero count means no recorded result in the window, not proof that testing stopped.

Require at least one recorded laboratory result in [t-12h,t) and a valid patientunitstayid. Exclude t values within 6 hours of unitdischargeoffset or hospitaldischargeoffset, and exclude windows after the earliest recorded EOL timestamp (cpleolsaveoffset or cpleoldiscussionoffset). These exclusions are primary safeguards, with sensitivity analyses relaxing them one at a time. Do not use discharge, mortality, EOL, or post-index values to construct the exposure.

## Deterioration outcome

At index t, define the outcome window as [t,t+6h), half-open and strictly after exposure. The primary outcome is a binary new/worsening physiologic event based only on vitalPeriodic and vitalAperiodic rows in that window: (i) systemic mean or noninvasive mean below a prespecified hypotension threshold, (ii) sustained tachycardia, (iii) hypoxemia, or (iv) a prespecified worsening from the last valid pre-index vital. The exact numeric thresholds and the required number of consecutive observations must be fixed after a single descriptive audit and frozen before outcome modeling; no threshold is asserted here. The outcome must be constructed without using laboratory values from the outcome window, to avoid making the exposure and outcome share the same observation process.

Secondary outcomes are new abnormal laboratory trajectories in [t,t+6h), actual ICU/hospital mortality from apachePatientResult, and discharge status from patient. These are not substitutes for the primary physiologic outcome. A mortality/discharge analysis is explicitly vulnerable to ascertainment and end-of-life selection.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Baseline: a prespecified hospital-stratified or hospital-random-intercept logistic model for the primary outcome, with exposure as a continuous change in 6-hour lab count (and a prespecified abrupt-reduction contrast), prior 6-hour vital summaries, prior laboratory summaries, age, hospital, time from admission, and missingness indicators. Use patient-level or hospital-held-out splitting, bootstrap/hospital-cluster uncertainty, calibration, and discrimination.

Alternative: a time-aware recurrent or discrete-time sequence model using ordered 1-hour bins of vital observations, laboratory observations, test identity, elapsed time since last observation, and explicit missingness. Fit it to the same index times and outcome, with the same hospital-held-out split and uncertainty procedure. This alternative can reveal whether the signal is concentrated in evolving trajectories or particular test-gap patterns that a count baseline loses. It is scientifically useful only if it changes the interpretation of the exposure/outcome relationship or materially improves calibration/discrimination; a small predictive gain alone is not an advance. CPU is appropriate for the baseline; a bounded recurrent model may use CPU or one allocated GPU, with resource use decided after a feasibility audit.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: the adjusted association is directionally consistent across hospitals, persists after EOL/discharge exclusions and current-state adjustment, and is not explained by the alternative's explicit observation-timing features. This supports a monitoring-alignment signal for prospective validation, not causality.

Adverse: the association disappears after current-state adjustment, is confined to EOL/discharge windows, reverses across hospitals, or is reproduced by a negative-control exposure (for example, a similarly defined non-clinical observation count if available). This favors severity, observation practice, or selection explanations.

Inconclusive: sparse testing, unstable event prevalence, wide uncertainty, or inability to separate missingness from true no-testing makes the result non-informative. It would justify measurement validation or another study, not a clinical recommendation.

## Missing evidence and limits

The available eICU data do not establish interface downtime, unrecorded orders, clinician intent, staffing, bed capacity, or treatment-limitation decisions. The exact thresholds, row counts, missingness, and event prevalence remain unprofiled.
