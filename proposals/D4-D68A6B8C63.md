# Fixed-combined-eGFR profiles with incidence-matched K30 and a recording-process stress test

Status: prospective substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. No P−/P0 renal or broad-outcome association has been inspected or fitted. This child preserves the parent's age-40–69 population, fixed-combined-eGFR profiles, day-30 origin, common N17/N18-free risk set, 10-year renal estimand, support/matching/censoring gates, competing-death framework, uncertainty, and clinical limits. Its only scientific change is to replace an invalid universal unseen-code binomial-trials interpretation with a discovery-day/bundle-size recording process after an outcome-only audit established that K=30 is feasible and incidence-commensurate.

## Opening, evidence, unresolved claim, and advance

The strongest inspected evidence supports only these bounded claims. Large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, this difference tracked frailty and broad adverse outcomes; using the combined equation may lose prognostic information [K2]. Administrative AKI coding can be insensitive and subgroup-dependent relative to serum-creatinine KDIGO AKI [K3]. These works do not establish that equal-combined-eGFR negative discordance selectively predicts incident biological AKI, that UKB N17/N18 first-appearance fields are complete, or that a K-th new code is biologically exchangeable with N17.

The unresolved hypothesis is: among supported P− and P0 profiles at the same combined GFR G, P− carries more information about first recorded N17 than first recorded N18 or an incidence-matched broad first-code endpoint, after measured baseline history and broad recording-process differences are addressed. The strongest rival is not merely generalized morbidity. Stable frailty/contact propensity may create more dates with newly recorded diagnoses, while high-intensity admissions may create larger same-day bundles; differential code salience may then leave N17 more profile-associated than B30 even if recording rather than renal biology drives the contrast.

The advance is a bounded selectivity test with three distinct comparisons on the same people and clock: N17 versus N18 first transition, N17 versus frozen incidence-matched 30th new nonrenal group, and transparent B30 versus a learned baseline-history plus discovery-day/bundle-size process. Support would justify external encounter/laboratory validation, not diagnosis, causality, mechanism, or clinical use.

## Outcome-only audit: observed feasibility, not a profile result

A vectorized full-file audit read no creatinine, cystatin C, grip, combined-eGFR profile, P−/P0 label, or marker association. It used age, sex, baseline date, all-position ICD-10 first-appearance pairs, and death dates to construct the parent clock and broad common pre-S renal-history state. For bounded feasibility only it required S+10 years<=2022-10-31, the observed all-position maximum; that date is not proof of completeness.

The audit processed 502,370 outcome rows and retained 498,473 broad common-history-free participants with provisional 10-year eligibility. On a 70/15/15 participant split frozen with seed 20260924 and stratified by sex and aggregate renal transition class, training mean crude N17/N18 risk was 2.563%. Risks of reaching K={1,3,5,10,15,20,30} before death were 70.79%, 54.96%, 41.57%, 21.46%, 11.62%, 6.49%, and 2.18%; K=30 minimized the absolute incidence gap. Without reselection, validation and test K30/mean-renal risk ratios were 0.831 and 0.861. Test counts were approximately 1,962 N17, 1,857 N18 and 1,644 K30.

The frozen global universe had 1,938 nonrenal three-character groups; every audited participant had at least 1,852 globally unseen groups. This resolves no-admissible-K and mathematical structural-zero concerns in the broad audit frame, but also falsifies the intended meaning of U as participant-specific binomial trials: many globally possible groups are not realistic future diagnoses for an individual. U is retained only as a technical QA bound. Final assay/profile-cohort K selection, event counts and profile opportunity remain uninspected and must be rerun.

## Falsification and interpretation

All interpretations require verified units/code semantics/C, support/matching/K/event gates, convergence, held-out process calibration, joint uncertainty, and no material tie/principal reversal.

Supportive for bounded recorded-N17 selectivity requires theta17 lower 95% bound >0, D lower bound >0, matched/principal directional agreement, QK lower bound >0, and agreement of calibrated recording-process simulation with the transparent B_K contrast. The pattern is strengthened if neither discovery-day nor bundle-size contrasts explain the N17 advantage. It warrants an external encounter/laboratory-adjudicated study only.

Adverse to selectivity/generalized-recording rival supported if thetaK lower bound >0 and QK upper bound <=0 in baseline and calibrated process analyses; or a stable NMF phenotype predicts N17, discovery days, bundle size, B_K and multiple families similarly with no precise N17 advantage. A bundle-only contrast is compatible with coding/admission intensity, not generalized incident disease. D upper bound<=0 is adverse to acute-versus-chronic selectivity; similar precise theta17/theta18 supports shared renal information. Both renal theta upper bounds<=0 are adverse to both profile claims.

Inconclusive includes unverified export completeness; no final-cohort K; profile support/matching/event failure; QK spanning clinically material alternatives; inadequate NMF stability/process calibration; <90% bootstrap convergence; death/CIF/cause-specific discordance; principal/tie reversal; or method disagreement without an identifiable calibrated recording pathway. An imprecise null does not refute the hypothesis. Do not rescue results by changing K, families, profiles, population, horizon, ranks or exclusions.

Biological AKI/CKD and coding sensitivity require record-level admissions, serial creatinine/cystatin C/albuminuria and clinical adjudication. Complete opportunity requires provider/nation/contact and export provenance. True filtration requires measured GFR/error data. Transportability and clinical benefit require external validation and prospective decision-impact/intervention study.
