## Unresolved question and substantive advance

When a person has two valid paired biomarker panels, does retaining the earlier panel add calibrated prospective risk information beyond the current panel and the fact and spacing of repeat assessment—and is that incremental value different at older versus younger ages?

This is clinically consequential because a repeat-assessment workflow may preserve historical values for review, but a pooled history benefit could be misleading if it is actually a consequence of who returns, how long the interval is, or older participants having higher baseline risk. The proposed comparison is not a study of whether ordering a repeat panel, treatment, monitoring or a clinical policy improves outcomes.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among participants with a valid paired panel and a qualifying five-year recorded landmark outcome, the incremental value of prior-panel values beyond current-panel values, verified assessment spacing, age, sex and calendar year differs between adults aged 65 or older and those younger than 55. This age contrast remains directionally present after outcome-blind common-support standardization for baseline risk, spacing, calendar year, sex and repeat-selection propensity, and after calibration assessment.

The primary age-modifier estimand is the common-support standardized difference in history-related Brier improvement:

[
d_g = E_{g,w}{BS(I1)-BS(B0+L)},qquad
H_{mathrm{std}}=d_{ge65}-d_{<55}.
]

Here (g) is a prespecified age stratum, (B0+L) is current values plus spacing, (I1) adds the prior values, and (E_{g,w}) averages paired held-out participant-level losses using weights defined without held-out outcomes. Negative d_g means prior values reduce prediction error in that stratum; a negative H_std means the reduction is larger in the older group. The unweighted H_raw, the 55–64 stratum, the pooled contrast, and a continuous age interaction are required secondary reports. They cannot be replaced by a significant interaction p-value.

The substantive advance over the parent is an interpretable age contrast on a common baseline-risk/spacing/selection scale, rather than treating a larger raw Brier difference in an older, higher-risk, differently observed group as evidence of age modification. The candidate-38 nested decomposition remains the validity anchor: B0 to B0+L measures information in repeat spacing, while B0+L to I1 tests prior-value information.

## Population, selection and transport estimands

The population anchor is unique `eid` in `population`, joined one-to-one to the five analytic tables above. Require agreement of overlapping age/sex fields; retain a disagreement table and fail closed rather than choosing a source.

The primary estimand is conditional on having a valid paired panel. It is not transported to people without a repeat panel. To quantify that limitation:

1. Build a flow from age/sex-valid population participants to T0-complete participants, observed valid T1, 16-cell complete paired panel, timing-eligible panel, and final outcome categories.
2. Among T0-complete participants, define (S=1) as a valid paired T1/panel. Fit a fold-local repeat-observation model using only pre-T1 information: T0 biomarker fields, age, sex and T0 calendar year. Add another field only after its dictionary semantics pass. Do not use T1 values, post-T1 fields or outcomes.
3. Estimate stabilized inverse-probability-of-repeat weights for the paired cohort, truncate only at a predeclared 1st/99th percentile, report overlap, maximum weight and effective sample size by age stratum, and fail the transport sensitivity if positivity or ESS is inadequate. This estimates a measured-selection transport sensitivity toward the T0-complete population under a missing-at-random assumption; it does not remove unmeasured selection or establish a causal testing effect.
4. Define the primary common-support age weights without held-out outcomes. For each outer fold, obtain training-only or inner-cross-fitted B0+L predicted target/death risks, interval (L), current assessment calendar year, sex and the repeat propensity. Fit a binary age propensity for `>=65` versus `<55` in the training data using prespecified splines for the risk vector and (L), then use overlap weights (1-p_{
m old}) for older and (p_{
m old}) for younger. The 55–64 group is reported descriptively and in a three-level sensitivity.
5. Multiply age-overlap weights by the repeat-selection weights, normalize within age group, and calculate (H_{mathrm{std}}). The age-overlap and repeat weights use no held-out outcome or I1 prediction. A second sensitivity uses covariate-balancing weights on the same predeclared variables. If overlap is poor, report (H_{mathrm{std}}) as inconclusive rather than extrapolating.

This construction repairs scale and transport without pretending that the paired cohort is a random sample. It compares history increments among comparable baseline-risk/spacing/selection-support participants and separately reports what changes when targeting the measured T0-complete repeat population.

## Models and held-out analysis

Use a single deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on `eid`, published with its hash algorithm and manifest. Every model, imputation, scaling, age-overlap propensity, repeat propensity, calibration map, tuning operation, bootstrap and permutation uses the same participant membership. No person may occur in multiple folds.

### Transparent models

- **B0:** current position-1 panel, verified age, verified sex, and current assessment calendar year.
- **B0+L:** B0 plus (L=T1-T0), only after the assessment-time gate. This is the current-plus-spacing baseline.
- **I1:** B0+L plus all eight position-0 panel fields, fit as a regularized additive multinomial probability model. Preprocessing, imputation and regularization are training-fold-only.
- **A1:** I1 plus centered age in years per decade interacted with each prespecified prior-minus-current contrast, with shrinkage. This is a pooled interpretable age-modification sensitivity; it does not create cutoffs from held-out outcomes.

The primary age-stratum losses compare the same held-out participants and predictions from I1 and B0+L. Preserve one row per eligible `eid` containing fold, age, fixed age stratum, sex, T0, T1, L, endpoint category, B0+L probabilities, I1 probabilities, A1 probabilities, repeat propensity, common-support weights and the B0+L target-risk band. Bands are assigned using training-fold B0+L thresholds (20th/80th percentiles) and never by I1 or held-out outcomes.

### Nonlinear alternative

Use the M2 alternative and rationale recorded in `work/ukb-age-modifier-alternatives-v2.md`: a small gradient-boosted multinomial probability model with exactly I1 inputs and the same participants, endpoint and folds. Prespecify a small depth/regularization grid and training-fold-only early stopping or calibration. M2 may reveal nonlinear age-by-history or current/prior discordance regions that A1 averages away, but cannot replace the transparent (H_{mathrm{std}}) estimand.

### Scale and calibration evaluation

Report, in this order:

1. (H_{mathrm{std}}) as the primary common-support age contrast in paired multiclass Brier improvement, its two component (d_g) values, the raw (H_{mathrm{raw}}), and the repeat-selection-weighted sensitivity.
2. A normalized history improvement ((BS(I1)-BS(B0+L))/BS(B0+L)) within each target population, clearly labelled secondary; normalization is descriptive and cannot rescue a failed primary result.
3. Multiclass log loss as a proper-score sensitivity and Brier resolution/reliability decomposition. A gain that is only an intercept/calibration correction must be distinguished from a gain in refinement/resolution.
4. Outer-test calibration intercept/slope and observed-to-predicted summaries for target and death probabilities by age stratum, risk band and interval regime.
5. Calibration-robust sensitivity: inside each outer training fold, fit a prespecified multinomial recalibration map using inner cross-fitted predictions for B0+L and I1, then apply it once to the outer test fold. Do not recalibrate on held-out outcomes. If the history contrast disappears only after recalibration, report that the apparent age difference was calibration, not added resolution.
6. Fixed illustrative target-risk thresholds 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10% and 15% for the recorded five-year target probability. Report counts and weighted event fractions moving upward/downward from B0+L to I1, with uncertainty. These are not validated action thresholds.
7. Exploratory decision-curve net benefit for target versus all other recorded categories at the same thresholds, with paired uncertainty, by age stratum and current-risk band. Death remains a recorded competing category; this is not a treatment utility or a recommendation.

The Brier and log-loss uncertainty intervals are paired participant bootstrap intervals over retained outer-fold predictions, with weights recomputed according to the predeclared training-only procedure. Also report fold instability and a sensitivity bootstrap stratified by age and endpoint. Do not use a subgroup claim if a stratum has fewer than 200 eligible participants or 30 target events; call it sparse/inconclusive.

The practical margin must be declared before fitting (for example, an absolute Brier difference of 0.001, plus a threshold-crossing/calibration criterion), and the conclusion must report the estimate and interval rather than only whether an interval excludes zero. A negative result can be scientifically valuable.

A significant interaction alone is not support. Since H_std is defined as older minus younger loss improvement, all direction rules in this proposal are negative for the hypothesized larger older-group benefit; a positive estimate is adverse, not supportive.

Inconclusive evidence includes any failed semantic/timing gate, fewer than 100 target events, sparse cells, nonpositive overlap, low effective sample size, unstable weights, endpoint ambiguity, broken code/date integrity, materially different conclusions under prespecified missingness/endpoint sensitivities, or insufficient observation evidence. Inconclusive is not evidence against the hypothesis.

## Successor advance: temporal transport of the age contrast

The parent answers the age-modifier question under a participant-random five-fold split. That split is appropriate for internal prediction but leaves a consequential validity question unresolved: would the age-specific prior-panel increment survive a later assessment/ascertainment era, rather than reflect calendar-dependent measurement, follow-up, or return patterns? The successor therefore adds a prespecified temporal stress test while retaining the parent’s random-split estimand as the primary common-support analysis.

The added falsifiable claim is:

> Among the same valid paired-panel population, the direction and practical magnitude of the common-support age contrast in prior-value Brier improvement are retained when models are trained only on participants whose verified current assessment T1 is on or before 2012-12-31 and evaluated, without refitting, on participants with T1 from 2013-01-01 through 2013-12-31.

This is a transport/generalization claim about recorded prognostic information, not evidence that calendar year causes risk, that ascertainment is complete, or that a clinical policy transports. The parent’s H_std remains primary; the late-era estimate H_late,std is a prespecified consequential-validity secondary. Report H_late,std - H_std with a paired bootstrap interval and do not call a temporal result supportive if the late era lacks support.

After all inherited semantic gates and the identical population, timing, prior-record exclusion, endpoint and T1 <= 2013-12-31 rules pass, define the temporal training set as T1 <= 2012-12-31 and the temporal test set as 2013-01-01 <= T1 <= 2013-12-31. These boundaries are fixed before outcome inspection. There is no participant overlap. The existing deterministic five-fold eid hash split remains in force within the temporal training set for preprocessing, tuning, calibration and fitting; the late set is never used to select hyperparameters, calibrate, fit age-overlap or repeat-selection models, or choose thresholds.

Fit B0, B0+L, I1 and A1 on the early set and the bounded M2 alternative using the exact inputs already specified: current position-1 fields, verified age/sex, current assessment calendar year, verified spacing L, and—only for I1/A1/M2—the eight literal position-0 fields. Apply the frozen early-fitted transformations and models to the late set. The late test uses the same recorded three-category endpoint. No time-varying or undocumented field is added. For the late-era age standardization, fit the age propensity and repeat-observation propensity only in the early training data, using the parent’s outcome-blind covariates and spline specification, then predict them in the late test. It reports raw and standardized d_g, H_late,std, log loss, calibration intercept/slope, reliability/resolution, threshold-crossing summaries, fold-free paired late-test bootstrap intervals, and an unweighted sensitivity. A late-era result is supportive for temporal robustness only when the inherited gates pass, all late support checks pass, the late age contrast has the predeclared negative direction, its uncertainty interval is entirely below -0.001 Brier units (or the exact practical margin declared before fitting), and it is not explained by spacing or calibration. It is adverse when the late contrast reverses, is practically negligible, or disappears after standardization/calibration despite a random-split signal. It is inconclusive for sparse late events, drift-driven non-overlap, timing/semantic/ascertainment failure, or materially incompatible endpoint/missingness results.

The primary bootstrap remains the parent’s participant bootstrap over held-out random-fold predictions with weights recomputed fold-locally. For the temporal estimate, resample late-test participants within age stratum and endpoint, holding the early-fitted models and weights fixed; this quantifies test-sample uncertainty, not early-training model-selection uncertainty. Add an early-training perturbation sensitivity by refitting the complete temporal pipeline in 200 prespecified bootstrap resamples of the early set if the solver budget permits; otherwise label that component uncomputed rather than presenting the fixed-model interval as full uncertainty.

### Interpretation and revisit rule

Because d_g = BS(I1)-BS(B0+L), improvement is negative; therefore the hypothesized older-group advantage has H_std < 0. Temporal robustness is supportive only if H_std and H_late,std both have the predeclared negative direction and agree within the predeclared 0.001-Brier practical margin. If H_std is negative but H_late,std is null, positive, or practically incompatible, the age-specific claim is not transport-robust; the result supports only the original paired-cohort internal association and motivates recalibration or new data. If both are null, retaining prior values has no demonstrated age-specific information for this recorded endpoint. A failed temporal gate is inconclusive, not evidence against the biological or prognostic hypothesis. None of these outcomes supports repeat-testing, treatment, monitoring, threshold, causal, mechanism or external-population claims.

Revisit the temporal branch only with evidence of an additional validated assessment era or observation endpoint, a field-level date/assay dictionary, or external patient-level validation. Do not revisit because M2 wins a small score margin or because a GPU is available.
