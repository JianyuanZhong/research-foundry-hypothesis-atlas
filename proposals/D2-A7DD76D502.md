# MerTK signaling mediates the protective macrophage-fibroblast interaction in rheumatoid arthritis

## Seed
D2-T2 (Protective versus pathogenic states)

## Domain
Therapeutic Target Prioritization

## Question
Does MerTK inhibition of the protective (MerTK/CD206pos) macrophage context abolish the protective
FLS program in E-MTAB-8873, and is this replicated in E-MTAB-8316?

## Background
In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue macrophages (STMs) play a central role in shaping the
synovial microenvironment. FACS-sorted MerTK/CD206pos STMs from RA patients in remission induce a
protective (repair) program in synovial fibroblasts (FLS), characterized by increased expression of
collagens (COL1A1, COL1A2) and TGFβ response genes (TGFBI). In contrast, MerTK/CD206neg STMs from
patients with active RA induce a pathogenic (inflammatory) FLS program (FLS5 cluster).
[E-MTAB-8873 repository.json]

A MerTK inhibitor (UNC1062, 250 nM) was applied to MerTK/CD206pos STMs before co-culture with FLS
in E-MTAB-8873. The original study (Otto lab, Glasgow) reported that MerTK/CD206pos STMs, especially
from patients in sustained remission, induced a repair response in FLS. However, the effect of MerTK
inhibition on this protective response was not the primary focus of the original analysis.

[K1] Kuo et al. 2019 (GSE95588) showed that HBEGF+ inflammatory macrophages in RA promote
fibroblast invasiveness in an EGFR-dependent manner, and that most RA medications targeted these
macrophages. This established the principle that macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk is a key driver of
RA pathology and that medications can redirect macrophage states.

[K2] Mosallanejad et al. 2026 reviewed the role of macrophages in tissue repair, emphasizing
efferocytosis and metabolic rewiring as key drivers of inflammation resolution. MerTK is a key
efferocytosis receptor, and its signaling is thought to be part of the resolution program that
macrophages use to induce repair in surrounding cells.

[K3] Wei & Xu 2026 proposed a "failed-resolution switch" in difficult-to-treat RA: imprinted
pathological fibroblasts and a macrophage compartment stripped of its pro-resolving, efferocytic
subsets work against resolution. Their reciprocal, feed-forward signaling assembles a
self-sustaining stromal-myeloid circuit. This framework motivates testing whether MerTK is a key
mediator of the protective effect.

## Hypothesis
MerTK signaling in MerTK/CD206pos macrophages is necessary for the induction of the protective
FLS program (COL1A1, COL1A2, TGFBI up) in E-MTAB-8873. If MerTK is inhibited, the protective
program will be abolished or significantly reduced.

## Rival hypothesis
The protective FLS program is driven by other macrophage-derived factors (e.g., TGFβ, IL-10, SPMs)
that are not affected by MerTK inhibition. In this case, MerTK inhibition would not affect the
protective program, and the protective effect would persist.

## Data
- E-MTAB-8873: 33 samples, 445 features (BD Rhapsody targeted panel).
  - Mpos (protective) context: 8 samples (none) + 4 samples (Inhibitor) + 1 sample (preLPS).
  - Mneg (pathogenic) context: 6 samples (none) + 4 samples (preLPS) + 1 sample (Inhibitor).
  - FLS only: 6 samples.
  - Mixed Mpos+Mneg: 3 samples.
  - Key paired contrasts (Mpos_Inhibitor vs Mpos_none, same FLS donor):
    - SA256A: R1_SA256A_FLS_SA259A_Mpos_Inhibitor vs R1_SA256A_FLS_SA259A_Mpos
    - SA256A: R1_SA256A_FLS_SA260R_Mpos_Inhibitor vs R1_SA256A_FLS_SA260R_Mpos
    - SA257A: R2_SA257A_FLS_SA266A_Mpos_preInhibitor vs R2_SA257A_FLS_SA266A_Mpos
    - SA266A: R5_SA266A_FLS_SA277A_Mpos_preInhibitor vs R5_SA266A_FLS_SA277A_Mpos
  - Baseline: FLS_only for the same FLS donors.
- E-MTAB-8322: 27 samples, 33694 features (10X scRNA-seq of macrophages).
  - Healthy controls: HC0547, HC0572, HC0701, HC0732.
  - Naive: SA131, SA138, SA146, SA172.
  - Remission: SA132, SA160, SA161, SA166.
  - Resistant: SA144, SA145, SA151, SA154, SA159, SA168, SA174.
  - UPASA: SA149, SA153.
  - SA149P: SA149P (relatedness to SA149 unresolved).
  - Use: define the protective (remission) vs pathogenic (active/resistant) macrophage state
    reference. Identify genes up in remission macrophages and down in active macrophages.
- E-MTAB-8316: 12 samples, 58302 features (bulk RNA-seq of FLS in transwell).
  - 4 FLS donors × 2 macrophage-donor contexts × {LPS, LPS+MerTK inhibitor}.
  - Use: independent validation of the MerTK inhibitor effect on FLS.

## Analysis
1. Load E-MTAB-8873 counts (445 features × 33 samples).
2. Define the protective FLS program: genes up in Mpos_none vs FLS_only (across donors, log2FC > 0.5,
   padj < 0.05).
3. Define the pathogenic FLS program: genes up in Mneg_none vs FLS_only (across donors, log2FC > 0.5,
   padj < 0.05).
4. Test the MerTK inhibitor effect: for each FLS donor, compare Mpos_Inhibitor vs Mpos_none.
   - Primary outcome: does the protective FLS program (step 2) decrease with MerTK inhibition?
     Use a donor-aware paired analysis (e.g., linear model with donor as a random effect, or a
     simple paired t-test on the log2-fold changes).
   - Secondary outcome: does the pathogenic FLS program (step 3) change with MerTK inhibition?
5. Cross-reference with E-MTAB-8322: identify the protective (remission) macrophage state genes.
   Check whether the protective FLS program genes are correlated with the protective macrophage
   state genes.
6. Validate with E-MTAB-8316: check whether the same FLS genes are affected by MerTK inhibition
   in the bulk RNA-seq data.

## Falsification
- Supportive: MerTK inhibition significantly decreases the protective FLS program (e.g., COL1A1,
  COL1A2, TGFBI) in E-MTAB-8873, and this is replicated in E-MTAB-8316.
- Adverse: MerTK inhibition does NOT decrease the protective FLS program in E-MTAB-8873, and the
  effect is not replicated in E-MTAB-8316.
- Inconclusive: The effect is inconsistent across FLS donors, or the sample size (4 paired
  Mpos_Inhibitor vs Mpos_none contrasts) is too small to draw a conclusion.

## Clinical importance
- RA patients in remission have a protective macrophage population (MerTK/CD206pos) that induces
  repair in FLS. Understanding what mediates this protective effect could inform strategies to
  preserve or enhance it.
- MerTK is a known therapeutic target in other contexts (e.g., autoimmune diseases, cancer). If
  MerTK is necessary for the protective effect in RA, then MerTK inhibitors could be harmful in
  patients with active disease (by abolishing the protective effect) or beneficial in patients with
  excessive protective signaling (by reducing fibrosis).
- This question directly informs the therapeutic decision of whether to use MerTK inhibitors in RA.

## Limitations
- The E-MTAB-8873 data is a targeted 445-gene panel; unmeasured genes may be important.
- The sample size for the Mpos_Inhibitor vs Mpos_none contrast is small (4 paired contrasts).
- The MerTK inhibitor may have off-target effects.
- The E-MTAB-8316 data uses a different system (bulk RNA-seq of FLS in transwell with
  M-CSF-differentiated macrophages) and may not be directly comparable to E-MTAB-8873 (scRNA-seq
  of FLS co-cultured with FACS-sorted STMs).
- The original publication for the E-MTAB datasets was not found via Europe PMC search; the
  repository.json descriptions are used as the primary source for the experimental design.
