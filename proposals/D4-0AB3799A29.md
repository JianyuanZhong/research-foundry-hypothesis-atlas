# Scientific Proposal: Temporal Proteomic Recovery Phenotypes Predict Long-Term Organ-Specific Outcomes After Infection

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question**: Do distinct temporal proteomic recovery trajectories following hospitalization for infection predict long-term cardiovascular and renal outcomes, and do these trajectories differ based on the organ system most severely affected during the acute infection?

**Secondary Question**: Can machine learning models identify proteomic recovery "phenotypes" that stratify patients by risk for long-term organ dysfunction, outperforming traditional clinical risk scores?

## Clinical Importance

### 1. Early Identification of High-Risk Patients
Current post-infection care relies heavily on clinical scores (SOFA, APACHE) that may not capture individual recovery trajectories. Identifying patients with aberrant proteomic recovery could enable:
- Early intervention strategies to prevent long-term organ damage
- Targeted monitoring and follow-up
- Personalized rehabilitation programs

### 2. Understanding Recovery Mechanisms
Distinct proteomic recovery trajectories may reflect different biological pathways:
- **Pro-inflammatory phenotype**: Sustained inflammatory cytokine elevation, impaired resolution
- **Fibrotic phenotype**: Early markers of tissue scarring and remodeling
- **Metabolic dysregulation**: Impaired metabolic recovery affecting organ function
- **Successful resolution**: Rapid normalization of immune and metabolic markers

### 3. Bridging Acute and Chronic Care
This research bridges the gap between acute infection management and long-term chronic disease prevention, addressing a critical gap in clinical practice.

## Hypothesis

### Primary Hypothesis
Among patients hospitalized for infection, distinct temporal proteomic recovery trajectories (measured during the first 30 days post-discharge) predict long-term cardiovascular and renal outcomes. These trajectories differ based on:
1. The organ system most severely affected during acute infection
2. The specific proteomic pathways involved in recovery

### Formal Statistical Statement
Let T(t) be the proteomic trajectory at time t (days post-discharge) for patient i, and O_i be the organ system most severely affected during acute infection. We test whether:

1. **Trajectory-Outcome Association**: The area under the proteomic recovery curve (AUC) for inflammatory markers, fibrotic markers, and metabolic markers is significantly associated with incident cardiovascular disease (CVD) and chronic kidney disease (CKD) within 5 years post-discharge.

2. **Organ-Specific Effect Modification**: The association between recovery trajectories and long-term outcomes is modified by the organ system affected during acute infection (e.g., respiratory infection → lung recovery trajectory; cardiovascular infection → cardiac recovery trajectory).

3. **Phenotype Prediction**: Machine learning classifiers trained on proteomic recovery trajectories achieve AUROC > 0.75 for predicting high-risk long-term outcomes, outperforming traditional clinical risk scores (e.g., REIN, SOFA-derived scores) by at least 5% in AUROC.

## Study Population and Data

### Population
UK Biobank participants who:
- Were hospitalized for infection (ICD-10 codes starting with J, B, A) between 2010-2015
- Had proteomic measurements from the Assessment Center (measured 2010-2015)
- Were followed for hospitalization for CVD or CKD within 5 years post-discharge
- Did not have prior CVD or CKD at baseline

### Main Data Tables
1. **biological_samples**: Proteomic measurements (proteomics tables with protein identifiers and concentrations)
2. **health_outcomes**: Hospitalization records with ICD-10 diagnoses (infection, CVD, CKD)
3. **population**: Participant baseline characteristics (age, sex, BMI, comorbidities)
4. **assessment**: Assessment center visit timing

### Temporal Framework
- **T0**: Baseline assessment center visit
- **T1**: Index hospitalization for infection
- **T2**: Discharge from infection hospitalization
- **T3**: Follow-up proteomic measurements (if available, or use baseline as proxy)
- **T4**: Long-term outcomes (CVD, CKD hospitalization) within 5 years post-discharge

## Statistical Approach

### Baseline Approach
**Multivariate Cox Proportional Hazards Regression**
- Outcome: Time to CVD or CKD hospitalization
- Covariates: Proteomic features at T0, demographic factors, traditional risk factors
- Adjustment: Age, sex, BMI, baseline comorbidities, smoking status, socioeconomic status
- Primary outcome: Hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals

### Substantive Alternative: Machine Learning Approach
**Random Forest and Gradient Boosting Models**
- Feature engineering: Trajectory-based features (slope, curvature, area under curve)
- Cross-validation: 5-fold stratified CV
- Performance metrics: AUROC, AUC-PR, calibration slope
- Comparison: Against traditional clinical risk scores

### Organ-Specific Analysis
- Stratified analysis by affected organ system
- Interaction terms: Proteomic features × organ system
- Mediation analysis: Does organ system affect outcome through specific recovery pathways?

## Falsification Criteria

### Hypothesis Will Be Falsified If:
1. **No Trajectory-Outcome Association**: Proteomic recovery trajectories show no significant association with long-term outcomes after adjustment for traditional risk factors (p > 0.05 for all trajectory features)
2. **No Organ-Specific Effect**: The association between recovery trajectories and outcomes is not modified by the affected organ system (interaction p > 0.05)
3. **No Phenotype Prediction**: Machine learning models do not outperform traditional clinical scores (AUROC difference < 0.05, p > 0.05)

### Supportive Evidence
- Significant trajectory-outcome associations (p < 0.01) for at least 2-3 proteomic pathways
- Organ-specific effect modification (interaction p < 0.05)
- Machine learning models outperform traditional scores by ≥5% AUROC

### Inconclusive Results
- Weak associations (p > 0.05) that may reflect limited statistical power
- Mixed results across pathways (some significant, some not)
- Need for larger sample size or longer follow-up

## Method Comparison

### Baseline: Multivariate Regression
- **Advantage**: Interpretability, clear effect estimates
- **Limitation**: May miss non-linear relationships, interactions
- **What it reveals**: Main effects of proteomic features on outcomes

### Substantive Alternative: Machine Learning
- **Advantage**: Captures non-linear relationships, interactions, high-dimensional patterns
- **Limitation**: Less interpretable, risk of overfitting
- **What it reveals**: Complex proteomic patterns that traditional methods miss

### Why Both Are Needed
The baseline approach tests whether proteomics has *any* predictive value beyond traditional factors. The ML approach tests whether proteomics provides *additional* predictive value through complex patterns. Only comparing both can determine if proteomics is truly informative or just capturing traditional risk factors.

## Scientific Advance Over Existing Knowledge

This proposal advances beyond UKB-01 in three key ways:

1. **Temporal Dynamics**: Instead of static baseline proteins, we test *trajectories* during recovery
2. **Organ-Specificity**: We explicitly test whether recovery trajectories differ by affected organ system
3. **Machine Learning**: We test whether ML can identify complex patterns beyond traditional statistical methods

The existing UKB-01 work tests whether pre-illness proteins differ between infection onset and organ damage. This proposal tests whether *dynamic changes during recovery* predict long-term outcomes - a fundamentally different and more clinically relevant question for post-acute care.
