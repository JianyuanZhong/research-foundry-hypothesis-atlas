# Proposal: outcome-ascertainment-aware early-transition test of post-TACE laboratory pathways

## Episode, parent, and scientific deliverable

This is an Episode-26 successor to assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent’s all-index population, early-transition/observation-selection design, first encounter-constrained timed TACE-like index, L=14 and L=45 information boundaries, component-resolved AFP/albumin/total-bilirubin profile, temporal split, competing recorded states, patient-clustered uncertainty, and recorded-care evidence boundary.

The distinct remaining ambiguity is outcome ascertainment, not landmark selection. The parent’s primary downstream endpoint is a procedure-lexical proxy. Even with strict-versus-broad tiers, a procedure row may be a coding artifact, a planned-but-not-completed action, or a technical/diagnostic record; a same-window order can also be a plan rather than completion. Thus “later recorded therapeutic state” is not yet a sufficiently explicit measurement model for a clinically consequential monitoring claim.

The actual future deliverable is newly fitted and estimated, not a presumed positive result:

1. An index, early-transition, observation, lab-profile, and outcome-evidence manifest for every eligible all-index patient.
2. Frozen procedure-only, procedure-plus-order, and optional imaging-workflow outcome tiers with source/time concordance diagnostics.
3. Full-index transition probabilities through day 45 and L=14/L=45 post-boundary cumulative incidence contrasts through day 365.
4. Transparent competing-risk baseline fits and a pre-specified multi-source latent outcome/transition model, each with locked predictions, calibration, uncertainty, overlap, and falsification outputs.
5. An interpretation file that maps every conclusion to an exact computed output, interval, model, boundary, endpoint tier, split, and frozen criterion.

Completion requires these artifacts and a conclusion that distinguishes a robust association with a recorded-care evidence tier from claims that require clinical adjudication. It does not require a supportive result.

## Evidence, unresolved question, and hypothesis

The strongest available evidence is operational. The HCC snapshot contains dated procedure, encounter, non-drug-order, examination, and laboratory records that can be joined by patient/encounter keys. A read-only audit found 338,040 procedure rows, 106,915 rows matching the parent’s TACE-like lexical screen, and timed procedure records extending to 2005; the encounter table begins at 2010-07-27 in the inspected snapshot. These facts establish data availability and temporal heterogeneity, not clinical validity, treatment completion, or an association.

The unresolved question is:

> After retaining all eligible index patients and modeling early recorded transitions and observation loss, does a favorable early component profile predict a later *concordant recorded therapeutic episode*—a strict TACE-like procedure temporally corroborated by a same-encounter TACE-like non-drug order—beyond measurement opportunity and local-care capture? Or does the parent association occur mainly in procedure-only or ambiguous records, indicating endpoint coding/workflow rather than a stable pathway signal?

The primary hypothesis is directional but noncausal: favorable early profile (AFP decrease with non-worsening albumin and total bilirubin) will be associated with a lower 320-day probability of the concordant recorded therapeutic episode than the discordant profile, after standardization to the same pre-index history/capture distribution. The design margin is a 3 percentage-point absolute contrast with a patient-level 95% interval excluding zero; this is a decision threshold, not an expected or observed result. The scientific repair is successful even if the result is adverse or null, provided endpoint discordance is quantified correctly.

Clinical importance: if a component profile predicts a corroborated downstream-care record and the association is stable across endpoint tiers, it can motivate a prospective study of whether early laboratory monitoring should trigger adjudicated reassessment. If it predicts only procedure-only records, clinicians and researchers should not treat the parent endpoint as evidence of response or progression. No result from this snapshot establishes treatment indication, intent, technical success, radiologic response, viable tumor, recurrence, progression, liver failure, treatment benefit, mortality, survival, or utility.

## Population, index, and temporal boundaries

Use one first-index episode per patient, preserving the parent definitions.

1. From `procedures`, form primary 24-hour within-patient/within-encounter episodes using `Surgery start time`. The candidate index is the earliest timed episode containing literal `TACE`, `transarterial chemoembolization`, or both `hepatic artery` and `embolization`. Hepatic angiography alone is not therapeutic index. Retain tied rows, source labels, and missing/invalid-time rows in an audit.
2. Require age >=18 and nonmissing `sex` from linked `encounters`. Require a `diagnoses` row with `diagnosis name` containing `hepatocellular carcinoma`, linked to an encounter whose `visit time` is in [t0-180 days, t0+7 days]. Diagnosis has no native timestamp, so this is an encounter-time ascertainment proxy, not disease onset.
3. Require t0 <= 2025-01-01. Do not infer death, survival, outside care, or discharge as clinical endpoints.
4. Include every eligible index patient in the all-index population, including patients with early events, incomplete laboratory trajectories, and no later local record. At each information boundary, report who reaches the boundary; never silently condition away early events or observation loss.
5. Preserve the parent’s day-45 continuity risk set (no primary-ontology liver-directed therapeutic episode in (t0,t0+45 days]) as a continuity analysis only. The primary early-transition-aware analysis retains those early events as absorbing competing states.

Use two prespecified information boundaries:

- L=14: last eligible pre-index component observation and first numeric value in days 7–14.
- L=45: last eligible pre-index component observation and first numeric value in days 7–45.

All predictors stop at L. The primary outcome window starts after L and ends at 365 days after t0. The full-index multi-state analysis models first transitions from t0 through day 45. Report cumulative incidence at 30, 90, and 320 days after t0 and conditional post-boundary estimates through day 365.

## Outcome ascertainment ontology

Construct mutually exclusive first post-boundary states, with the parent’s strict/broad/diagnostic/non-liver/administrative hierarchy retained:

- **C2 concordant recorded therapeutic episode (primary new endpoint):** a strict TACE-like procedure episode with a valid `surgery start time`, linked by (`patient master index`, `visit number`) to a non-drug order whose `non-drug medical order` passes the same training-frozen TACE-like order screen, with `order time` or `start time` within [-24,+24] hours of the procedure episode time. The order is corroborating documentation, not proof of intent or completion.
- **C1 procedure-only strict episode:** a strict timed TACE-like procedure episode not meeting C2. This is the parent-compatible recorded procedure endpoint and must be reported separately.
- **C0 broad/ambiguous/mixed liver-directed episode:** a timed liver-directed record that does not meet strict criteria, including ambiguous or mixed labels.
- Diagnostic/technical-only, non-liver procedure, and administrative local-observation-loss states remain competing states.
- A secondary workflow-supported tier adds a liver/upper-abdominal examination from `examinations` in the preceding 30 days, using only `Examination` and `Start Time` for primary screening; `Examination Findings` and `Examination Diagnosis` are sensitivity-only narrative evidence. This tier is not radiologic adjudication.

Aggregate rows before any join and preserve row-level provenance. If multiple source rows fall within one 24-hour episode, retain all source IDs/labels and define the episode time as the earliest valid procedure start. Matching is evaluated within the same patient/encounter key; cross-encounter matches are a sensitivity analysis, not a primary event. Run order time-window sensitivities of ±6, ±24, and ±72 hours, and status-sensitive analyses using exact observed `order status` values. Do not put the order used to define C2 into a predictor or “plan” covariate for that same outcome.

The parent’s recorded-plan P proxy remains a descriptive early-window/context output only. For a post-L C2 endpoint, P is not an adjustment variable when it contributes endpoint evidence. The parent’s reassessment R proxy remains an outcome-context descriptor and sensitivity analysis, never clinical confirmation.

## Estimand and analysis

Let k be C2, C1, C0, diagnostic/technical, non-liver, or observation-loss state. For L in {14,45}, estimate the predictive standardized contrast

`Delta^L_k(h) = P(first recorded state k by h | favorable profile, L, history/capture) - P(first recorded state k by h | discordant profile, L, history/capture)`

using the same pre-index demographic, diagnosis, prior recorded-care, and observation-opportunity distribution in both profile groups. Report h=30, 90, 320 and the post-boundary through-day-365 values. The primary estimand is `Delta^L_C2(320)`; C1-C0 contrasts quantify ascertainment dependence. Report both absolute risk differences and risk ratios only when denominators are stable.

The full-index estimand is the vector of transition probabilities from t0 through day 45, including early C2/C1/C0/diagnostic/non-liver/observation-loss transitions. The day-45 continuity estimand is reported for parent comparison, not substituted for the all-index target. No contrast is causal, a treatment effect, or a recovered effect for patients with an early event.

Use 2010–2022 fit, 2023 tuning, and 2024–2025-01-01 locked test when date/event support permits; otherwise use the deterministic patient-hash split declared before fitting. No patient crosses splits. Fit vocabulary, assay rules, profile thresholds, standardization, weights, and sparsity/common-support rules on training only. Use patient-clustered bootstrap or an equivalent patient-level resampling procedure for intervals.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

All models use the same index patients, boundaries, endpoint tiers, split, locked test, and uncertainty procedure.

**Transparent baseline B_evidence.** Fit regularized discrete-time cause-specific hazards/multi-state models for the mutually exclusive evidence tiers. Predictors are age, sex, index encounter timing/department, diagnosis ascertainment, pre-index procedure burden and timing, pre-index encounter/capture intensity, medication/order/examination availability, and the L=14 or L=45 AFP/albumin/bilirubin values, changes, exact delays, qualitative/inequality flags, counts, and missingness. Early transitions are absorbing competing states. The order that supplies C2 endpoint evidence is excluded from predictors for that endpoint. Report coefficients, standardized contrasts, calibration, Brier score, dynamic log score, and clustered intervals.

**Mechanistic alternative M_multisource.** Fit an irregular-time, multi-view latent evidence model. For each 24-hour post-boundary episode, a latent recorded-therapeutic-care state generates source-specific observations from procedures, same-encounter orders, and optional examination workflow, with source-specific timing kernels, missingness/capture intensity, and false-positive/sensitivity parameters. Couple that latent state to the same competing-transition hazards and component profile. Fit the model to the same patient training split, with pre-specified weakly informative priors or penalization; perform prior-sensitivity analyses because no adjudicated gold standard identifies source sensitivities. The model’s target is the joint likelihood of observed source streams and held-out source indicators, not clinical response.

B_evidence is transparent and directly tests whether tightening the endpoint changes the profile contrast. M_multisource can reveal graded source discordance, timing dependence, and whether a hard C2/C1 threshold is driving the estimate—information lost when each episode is deterministically tiered. It is scientifically useful only if it converges, improves held-out source prediction/calibration or materially clarifies uncertainty, and gives stable profile contrasts under prior sensitivity. It cannot turn the latent state into a validated clinical endpoint.

CPU is the default: the baseline is tabular and the latent model is a modest repeated likelihood fit. Future solver envelope: at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, and 28,800 seconds. These are planning limits, not measured runtimes; no model was fitted in discovery. GPU use is not required and should not be used as a scientific merit criterion. If repeated latent fitting is materially faster on an allocated device, a solver may request one A100 and use `cuda:0`, but a convergence or infrastructure problem must be reported rather than replaced by an easier question.

Alternatives not selected: a transformer over clinical text is deferred because the clinical-document table has no row-level time and no validated outcome labels; ALBI/MELD or other reserve scores are deferred because lab units, INR, and validated thresholds are unavailable; image/multimodal modeling is deferred because HCC image files are absent; causal treatment-policy and survival analyses are deferred because intent, assignment, completion, mortality, and outside-care ascertainment are absent. Revisit these only with validated timestamps/labels, units and INR, image files, or linked adjudicated outcomes respectively. A generic neural model would not resolve this endpoint-measurement ambiguity and is deferred unless new labels justify it.

## Falsification and interpretation criteria

Required falsifications and sensitivity checks:

1. **Parent continuity:** reproduce the parent day-45 procedure-only strict contrast, plan/context summaries, and early-transition accounting. Failure indicates a definition or packaging problem, not evidence for or against C2.
2. **Endpoint specificity:** compare C2, C1, C0, broad, and workflow-supported estimates. A materially different C2 estimate, or an effect concentrated in C1/C0, directly supports outcome-ascertainment ambiguity.
3. **Time-concordance placebo:** permute eligible order times within patient while preserving order names and encounter structure, and rederive C2. A similar C2 association after permutation is adverse to the endpoint interpretation.
4. **Window/source sensitivity:** repeat C2 at ±6/24/72 hours, same-encounter versus cross-encounter matching, and exact status/non-canceled sensitivities. Instability or universal absence of matches is adverse or infeasible, not a clinical null.
5. **Negative endpoint controls:** test diagnostic/technical and non-liver states with the same profile and capture adjustment. Similar contrasts for unrelated workflow endpoints weaken a disease-specific pathway interpretation.
6. **Temporal profile placebo:** permute assay values to eligible times within patient, preserving assay/specimen and measurement counts, and use pre-index placebo windows. Preserved effects favor timing/capture artifact.
7. **Early transition/observation sensitivity:** retain explicit absorbing early states; compare L=14 and L=45, inverse-observation-weighted and unweighted fits, complete numeric pairs, and qualitative/inequality-as-missing versions. No analysis may silently discard early events or missing follow-up.
8. **Latent-model integrity:** require convergence, posterior/predictive checks, held-out source log score/Brier/calibration, and prior-sensitivity intervals. Nonconvergence or prior-dominated contrasts are inconclusive and cannot justify preferring M_multisource.

Supportive evidence requires parent continuity; a directionally compatible and practically meaningful C2 contrast at L=14 and L=45; incremental value beyond capture-only B_evidence; weaker or null unrelated endpoint/placebo contrasts; adequate overlap and calibration; stable time/status sensitivities; and a converged M_multisource whose intervals do not depend materially on arbitrary priors. This supports only a robust association with a corroborated locally recorded-care evidence tier and motivates prospective adjudication.

Adverse evidence includes a C2 null/reversal while procedure-only signal persists; effect concentration in C1/C0, canceled/ambiguous statuses, or order-window artifacts; similar negative-control/placebo effects; poor overlap/calibration; or latent-model instability. These favor coding, workflow, measurement, or selection explanations.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse C2 cells, no valid time-concordant matches, missing timestamps, unstable lexical vocabulary, insufficient follow-up, positivity failure, temporal-split failure, latent nonidentification/prior domination, or intervals crossing the design margin. Inconclusive does not establish absence of biological response, absence of treatment benefit, or absence of disease progression.

The verifier can check paths, schema IDs, headers, key aggregation, time windows, lexical freezing, endpoint tier construction, patient splits, leakage, transition/CIF calculations, held-out predictions, calibration, clustered uncertainty, permutations, and output-to-conclusion links. It cannot adjudicate intent, completion, technical success, imaging/pathology response, viable tumor, recurrence/progression, liver failure, death, survival, benefit, appropriateness, utility, or external validity. Those require expert chart/radiology/pathology adjudication, linked mortality and outside-care data, and prospective or external validation.
