# Episode 11: a bounded continuous-PaO2 test of the pH-conditioned SpO2 blind spot

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and substantive advance

When released monitor SpO2 is 96–97%, does acidemia correspond to a clinically material upward shift in the arterial oxygen-tension distribution among observations with the same hospital and similar measured oxygenation context, or was the episode-10 binary PaO2>120 signal too sparse to distinguish such a shift?

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis:** in the inherited measured-overlap population, the symmetric-overlap-weighted acidemic-minus-normal difference in mean PaO2 winsorized to 60–150 mm Hg is at least **+10 mm Hg**.

This is a concurrent retrospective measurement association. It is not an effect of changing pH, FiO2, SpO2, or oxygen therapy. A material and robust shift would justify a prospective study with synchronized, adjudicated measurements to test whether pH should alter oxygen assessment. A smaller or imprecise difference would argue against promoting the same-panel association into extra blood-gas testing or oxygen-titration decision support.

The +10 mm Hg threshold and 60–150 mm Hg bounds were fixed before this continuous-outcome execution. The bounds are inherited clinically recognizable hypoxemia and severe-hyperoxemia cut points and prevent a few extreme released PaO2 values from dominating the mean. The threshold is a proposed material distributional shift, not a guideline threshold; clinical experts must adjudicate its decision importance before any prospective protocol. This successor does not redefine success after seeing the result.

The inspected full-text XML of Delgado et al. (Antioxidants 2026; DOI 10.3390/antiox15020235; PMCID PMC12938712; frozen source [source checksum], [source checksum]) describes a retrospective 2017–2022 single-tertiary-center cohort of 21,406 adults and 717,064 paired measurements. It aligned arterial gases to preceding five-minute SpO2/FiO2 summaries and reports higher PaO2 in acidotic states at comparable SpO2, FiO2 modification, and generally physiologic PaO2 at SpO2 96–97%. This supports plausibility and one-center association. It does not establish the EICU +10 mm Hg contrast, measured-overlap robustness, multi-hospital transportability, specimen/device validity, a pre-draw alert, causality, toxicity, or treatment benefit.

Episode 10 correctly estimated a +2.955-point PaO2>120 risk difference with a −7.875 to +15.535-point interval from only ten overlap events, leaving the +10-point claim unresolved. The substantive advance here is not a new clinical claim: it uses every PaO2 value in the same outcome-blind overlap population, caps leverage at prespecified clinical bounds, preserves the exact measured-context comparison and copied-cluster uncertainty, and asks a distinct magnitude question in mm Hg.

## Outcome-blind overlap target and estimand

Before inspecting PaO2, define a typed cell from exact:

- `hospitalid`;
- released median SpO2 (96, 96.5, or 97);
- `floor(FiO2_percent/10)`;
- `floor(PaCO2/20)`, with missing PaCO2 its own typed level;
- `floor(draw/1440)` for ICU day 0, 1, or 2.

Do not use string concatenation as the implementation identity. Retain cells containing at least one observation from each pH group. Reconcile 237 observations, 197 people, 198 stays, 57 cells, and 9 hospitals: 110 acidemic and 127 normal. Sites are 122, 183, 184, 318, 394, 420, 443, 458, and 459.

For cell s with n1s acidemic and n0s normal observations, assign acidemic rows weight n0s/(n1s+n0s) and normal rows weight n1s/(n1s+n0s). Normalize within exposure group. Define `Y*=min(150,max(60,PaO2))`. The primary estimand is the weighted mean Y* among acidemic rows minus that among normal rows in this measured site/oxygenation-overlap population. It is not the all-705 population, a within-person effect, a causal effect, or a hyperoxemia risk difference.

Report group weight sums; weighted means and difference; ESS `(sum w)^2/sum(w^2)`; maximum normalized row weight; original-group exclusion fractions; each site's weight fraction; counts clipped below 60 and above 150; and weighted FiO2, PaCO2/missingness, draw, age, sex, unit type, and admission source.

## Uncertainty and diagnostic gates

Use 5,000 deterministic hierarchical bootstrap replicates, seed 20260909, conditional on the frozen 237-row source:

1. sample nine hospitals with replacement;
2. within each sampled hospital copy, sample its `uniquepid` clusters with replacement, drawing the observed person count and carrying all rows;
3. assign new hospital-copy and person-copy IDs;
4. rebuild typed cells using the new hospital-copy ID, discard cells lacking either pH group, recompute symmetric weights, and recompute Y* means/difference.

Report defined/failed replicates and percentile 95% interval. Pooling copied hospitals under the original ID is an error. More than 10% undefined replicates, observed ESS<50 in either group, any original site>50% of total observed weight, or a sign reversal in equal-person, earliest-person, or leave-one-site-out analyses forces an inconclusive interpretation regardless of the nominal interval. Tighter-cell non-overlap is reported as a transport limitation, not silently ignored.

## Reference execution and strongest supported claim

Complete-source execution reproduced 705 eligible and 237 overlap observations. In the overlap rows, 2/127 normal and 5/110 acidemic PaO2 values were below 60; 1/127 and 3/110 were above 150. The acidemic maximum was 556.4 mm Hg, which demonstrates why the unbounded mean is not primary.

The symmetric-overlap Y* means were 86.514 mm Hg (acidemic) and 80.309 (normal), difference **+6.205 mm Hg**. The copied-hospital/person bootstrap had 5,000/5,000 defined replicates and a percentile interval **−2.712 to +13.345 mm Hg**. ESS remained 87.7/105.7 and maximum site weight 45.3%. Equal-person and earliest-person differences were +6.590 and +6.607 mm Hg. Every leave-site-out primary contrast remained positive (+3.025 to +7.331).

Sensitivity estimates were raw-mean difference +10.774 mm Hg (interval −2.300 to +40.229), geometric-mean ratio 1.090 (0.974–1.222), median difference +8.4 mm Hg (−2 to +14), 75th-percentile difference +6 (−8 to +20), and 90th-percentile difference +7 (−19 to +43.05). Their wider or scale-dependent intervals cannot override the primary.

The strongest computational claim supported is: **the point direction was positive and stable under the specified person/site deletions, but the EICU overlap data remain inconclusive about a +10 mm Hg material shift because the primary interval includes both zero and +10.** This is neither support for the hypothesis nor evidence of no association. It does exclude neither a small null/opposite difference nor the prespecified clinically material difference. Reference execution establishes computation on this snapshot, not clinical truth.

## Falsification and meaning of results

- **Supportive:** primary lower 95% bound >=+10 mm Hg and every diagnostic gate passes. Allowed conclusion: “A >=10-mm Hg concurrent restricted-mean PaO2 difference persisted in the prespecified measured-context overlap population.” This justifies prospective synchronized validation, not treatment advice.
- **Adverse to +10:** primary upper bound <+10. If the interval is entirely above zero, it supports a smaller positive association while falsifying the material-magnitude claim. If upper bound <0, it supports an opposite association in this estimand. Neither proves an SpO2-only rule is safe.
- **Inconclusive:** interval contains +10 or any gate fails. The observed +6.205 (−2.712,+13.345) result is inconclusive. A positive point estimate is not support when the interval crosses the threshold.
- **Discordant:** binary and Y* conclusions differ. Report both target populations/scales; do not attribute discordance uniquely to confounding or biology.

Even a supportive result cannot establish that pH caused the shift, that any PaO2 was harmful, that another ABG would help, or that lowering oxygen improves outcomes. An adverse result cannot validate universal SpO2 targets. An inconclusive result means this snapshot cannot resolve the magnitude and informs sample-size/design planning for another study.
