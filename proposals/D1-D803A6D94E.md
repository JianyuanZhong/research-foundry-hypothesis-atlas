# Hypothesis Proposal: Different Meanings of Persistent Hyperlactatemia in Critically Ill Patients

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question:** In critically ill patients with persistently elevated lactate levels, does the underlying pathophysiology—specifically whether elevated lactate reflects impaired microcirculatory perfusion versus metabolic dysfunction without perfusion impairment—predict differential responses to fluid resuscitation and vasopressor therapy, and ultimately influence 28-day mortality?

**Secondary Question:** Can we identify a clinical phenotype that distinguishes patients with "lactate-elevated with improving perfusion" from those with "lactate-elevated with worsening perfusion," and do these phenotypes have divergent prognoses and treatment trajectories?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Precision Resuscitation Strategy**: Current sepsis guidelines (Sepsis-3) recommend targeting lactate clearance, but the ANDROMEDA-SHOCK trial (Hernández et al., JAMA 2019) demonstrates that targeting peripheral perfusion (capillary refill time) may be more effective than lactate-focused strategies for reducing organ dysfunction. However, EHR data rarely captures capillary refill time. This hypothesis addresses the clinical reality that clinicians must interpret persistent hyperlactatemia in the absence of perfusion measures.

2. **Avoiding Misclassification**: Elevated lactate has multiple causes:
   - **Perfusion-related**: Tissue hypoperfusion with anaerobic metabolism (requires hemodynamic optimization)
   - **Metabolic-related**: Increased production (e.g., mitochondrial dysfunction, sepsis-associated mitochondrial impairment) without proportional perfusion impairment
   - **Consumption-related**: Increased clearance failure (e.g., liver dysfunction)

   Treating these as a single category may lead to inappropriate interventions.

3. **Treatment Response Prediction**: Patients with different underlying mechanisms may respond differently to:
   - Fluid resuscitation (beneficial in perfusion-related, potentially harmful in metabolic-related)
   - Vasopressors (beneficial in perfusion-related, may be less effective in metabolic-related)
   - Source control and antimicrobial therapy (beneficial in both)

4. **Resource Optimization**: Identifying which patients need aggressive hemodynamic management versus metabolic support could optimize ICU resource allocation.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Current literature establishes:
- **ANDROMEDA-SHOCK trial**: Targeting peripheral perfusion (capillary refill) vs. lactate levels showed no mortality benefit but reduced organ dysfunction at 72 hours (Hernández et al., JAMA 2019)
- **Biphasic lactate kinetics**: Lactate may rise initially during shock (perfusion-related) then fall as perfusion improves, but persistently elevated levels may indicate ongoing issues (Bakker et al., Crit Care Resusc 2025)
- **Pediatric shock management**: Lactate-targeted strategies show variable outcomes compared to broader physiological approaches (García-Pineda et al., Pediatr Crit Care Med 2022)

**What remains unresolved:**
- How to interpret persistent hyperlactatemia when perfusion measures (capillary refill) are unavailable
- Whether lactate elevation with "improving" clinical signs differs from "worsening" signs in terms of mortality and treatment response
- How to operationalize these distinctions in EHR-based clinical decision support

This study addresses the practical clinical question: When we cannot measure capillary refill time, what EHR-derived proxy best distinguishes patients whose elevated lactate requires hemodynamic optimization from those whose elevated lactate requires metabolic or source-control interventions?

## Study Population and Main Data

**Population**: Adult patients (≥18 years) in MIMIC-IV ICU who:
- Have at least 3 consecutive lactate measurements >2.0 mmol/L during a single ICU stay
- Receive either fluid resuscitation or vasopressors during the ICU stay
- Have complete follow-up (discharge, death, or transfer out of hospital)

**Exclusions**:
- Known chronic liver failure (cirrhosis) at admission
- Severe burns or trauma (alternative mechanisms for lactate elevation)
- Patients without any vasopressor or fluid administration during ICU stay

**Main Data Sources** (MIMIC-IV):
- `icu/icustays`: ICU admission, discharge, death, transfer out
- `icu/labevents` + `hosp/d_labitems`: Lactate measurements with timestamps and units
- `icu/chartevents`: Clinical signs (perfusion status, consciousness level)
- `icu/inputevents`: Fluid resuscitation (units, timing)
- `icu/ingredientevents`: Vasopressor administration (drug, dose, timing)
- `hosp/admissions`: Demographics, admission diagnosis, length of stay
- `note/discharge`: Discharge disposition

**Key Variables**:
- **Lactate phenotype definition**: Based on trajectory of lactate levels and concurrent clinical signs
- **Perfusion proxy**: Derived from chartevents containing "capillary refill", "extremity perfusion", or "perfusion status" (coded as present/absent)
- **Fluid response**: Change in lactate after fluid administration (percentage decrease)
- **Vasopressor response**: Change in lactate after vasopressor initiation (percentage decrease)
- **Outcomes**: 28-day mortality (from icustays), organ dysfunction (SOFA score at discharge), length of ICU/hospital stay

## Methodological Design

### Baseline Approach
Compare outcomes between patients with:
- **High lactate + poor perfusion** (defined by documented poor perfusion signs or rapid lactate decline after vasopressors)
- **High lactate + improving perfusion** (defined by documented improving perfusion signs or sustained lactate elevation despite vasopressors)

Statistical analysis:
- Logistic regression for 28-day mortality
- Cox proportional hazards for time-to-death
- Multivariate adjustment for: admission SOFA, age, comorbidities, admission lactate, sepsis diagnosis, organ support at admission

### Learned Alternative Approach
Develop a supervised learning model to classify patients into lactate phenotypes based on:
- Temporal patterns of lactate measurements
- Concurrent vitals (MAP, heart rate, respiratory rate)
- Chartevent-coded clinical signs
- Fluid/vasopressor response patterns
Training: 80% of patients, validation: 20%
Evaluation: AUC-ROC, calibration, decision curve analysis

### Mechanistic Alternative Approach
Test the "perfusion-lactate dissociation" hypothesis using instrumental variables:
- **Instrument**: Admission SOFA score (captures baseline severity but not treatment response)
- **Outcome**: Phenotype assignment and subsequent mortality
- **Test**: Does the association between lactate trajectory and mortality persist after adjusting for baseline severity?

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **falsified** if:
1. No significant difference in 28-day mortality between lactate phenotypes after adjusting for baseline severity (p > 0.05)
2. Fluid resuscitation and vasopressors show no differential effects on lactate clearance or outcomes based on phenotype
3. The learned phenotype classifier performs no better than random classification (AUC < 0.55)
4. The "perfusion-lactate dissociation" hypothesis is not supported (instrumental variable analysis shows no effect modification)

## Expected Supportive Results

If the hypothesis is **supported**, we would expect:
1. Patients with "lactate-elevated + worsening perfusion" have significantly higher 28-day mortality than those with "lactate-elevated + improving perfusion" (hazard ratio > 1.5, p < 0.01)
2. Fluid resuscitation shows greater lactate clearance in the "worsening perfusion" phenotype
3. Vasopressor use shows greater mortality benefit in the "worsening perfusion" phenotype
4. A machine learning model can reliably distinguish phenotypes (AUC > 0.75)
5. Instrumental variable analysis confirms phenotype-specific treatment effects

## Adverse Results and Interpretation

If results show **no phenotype-specific effects**:
- Elevated lactate may represent a unified pathophysiological process in critically ill patients
- Treatment strategies should focus on lactate clearance regardless of underlying mechanism
- The clinical utility of phenotyping may be limited; current lactate-guided resuscitation is appropriate

If results show **conflicting patterns**:
- Some lactate phenotypes may benefit from fluid resuscitation, others from vasopressors alone
- Treatment decision trees may need to incorporate phenotype-specific algorithms
- Further research into mechanistic biomarkers may be warranted

## Required Clinical Adjudication

**Unavailable Evidence**:
- **Capillary refill time**: Not available in MIMIC-IV EHR; must be proxied by chartevent codes
- **Microcirculatory assessment**: Not routinely measured; requires clinical judgment
- **Organ perfusion imaging**: Doppler ultrasound, NIRS not available in standard MIMIC-IV

**Mitigation Strategies**:
- Use chartevent-coded clinical signs as proxy for perfusion status
- Validate phenotype definitions against clinical expertise
- Acknowledge limitations in interpretation and discussion
