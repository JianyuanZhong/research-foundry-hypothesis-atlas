# Temporal origin of recovery-context mismatch after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable and claim boundary

The solver must newly construct and fit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 study of whether the *temporal origin* of a recovery-context mismatch changes the early post-ICU pathway. The parent candidate ([prior hypothesis]) asks whether a final-six-hour mismatch between recorded support withdrawal and domain-matched physiology is associated with ICU return versus death. This child preserves that topology and observation discipline but makes a different unresolved distinction primary:

> Among adults with a recent recorded respiratory or vasoactive support withdrawal at the first ICU outtime, does persistent domain-matched physiologic instability across the preceding and final six-hour halves have a different competing-event profile from instability that emerges only in the final six hours, after comparison with recorded recovery and adjustment for observation/process intensity?

This is a prognostic association about the meaning of a recorded transition. It is not a causal effect of withdrawing support, a determination of discharge readiness, or a clinical policy.

Completion requires newly fitted/estimated held-out outputs, not a readiness check:

1. run_manifest.json with the catalog and snapshot hashes, exact source paths and archive members, schema hashes, item-label assertions, locked cohort/window/support/physiology/q/topology/tie rules, split seeds, software versions, resource use and output hashes.
2. cohort_audit.parquet with one row per subject’s earliest eligible ICU stay, all eligibility and temporal-integrity checks, support-domain and physiology coverage, trajectory-class eligibility, and exclusion reasons.
3. hourly_features.parquet with twelve one-hour rows in [outtime-12 hours, outtime), raw chart/interval evidence references, respiratory and hemodynamic summaries, support states, masks, row counts and process indicators.
4. trajectory_ledger.parquet with the preceding-half and final-half domain statuses, continuous instability burdens, trajectory class for each domain, joint classes, q recodings and every ambiguity flag.
5. transfer_outcome_audit.parquet containing all later ICU candidates through the 48-hour/discharge boundary, all candidate bridge rows, careunit topology, event ordering and L/U labels.
6. bound_cif.parquet with group-specific 6-, 24- and 48-hour first-event CIFs for documented-bridge ICU return L, any later ICU return U, death before return D and alive discharge before return A; standardized contrasts, compatible L/U contrast intervals and widths.
7. Locked patient-level test predictions from (a) the transparent competing-risk baseline and (b) the observation-aware coupled dynamic state-space/hidden semi-Markov alternative, with calibration, IPCW Brier scores, subject-bootstrap intervals, split stability and dynamic-state/lag summaries.
8. falsification_outputs.parquet and interpretation_map.parquet linking every conclusion to an estimate, horizon, event count, overlap/ESS, uncertainty interval, L/U width, sensitivity result and explicit evidence limitation.

The actual scientific deliverable is the estimated temporal-origin contrast and its uncertainty, plus the fitted dynamic model’s persistence/lead-lag parameters and locked predictions. Completion is established only when both models are fitted on training/validation data, test predictions are locked before test evaluation, observable CIFs and uncertainty are computed, and the prespecified interpretation gates are populated. No fitted result is claimed in this proposal.

## What is supported versus untested

The available parent evidence supports a narrower feasibility and measurement claim:

- A first adult ICU outtime can be defined using patients, admissions and icustays.
- Respiratory support, vasoactive exposure, charted physiology and observation intensity can be represented in dated pre-outtime bins.
- Later ICU return can be retained as two topology endpoints: a documented non-ICU bridge followed by return is a lower-bound event L, while any later ICU return is an upper-bound event U.
- Inherited feasibility checks found 52,591 eligible adult first outtimes, 2,661 later ICU returns within 48 hours, 2,060 with a qualifying bridge and 601 without one, including 294 returns with multiple bridge candidates. These are inherited feasibility/topology counts, not exposure associations and not evidence that any return was unplanned, preventable or clinically necessary.

The expert MIMIC-10 seed asks whether persistently high oxygen requirements or fluctuating vital signs before ICU discharge predict ICU return or death and flags treatment limitation, destination and competing death. The MIMIC-01 seed similarly proposes asynchronous organ recovery, but its kidney definitions were not adopted because validated creatinine/urine-output recovery mappings and sufficient event support have not been established in this design. The seeds are untested research inputs, not evidence for the hypothesis.

What remains untested is not merely whether final instability is associated with outcome. It is whether two patients with the same recorded support transition and final-hour mismatch have different risks because one was unstable throughout the preceding six hours and the other became unstable only near discharge. A positive final snapshot alone cannot resolve that temporal ambiguity. The primary claim is falsifiable if persistent and late-emergent mismatch have compatible null/reversed contrasts, if trajectory adds no information beyond final-half burden, or if apparent differences disappear after observation/process adjustment or topology sensitivity.

## Population, landmark, split and follow-up

Use one earliest valid ICU stay per subject across all admissions, tie-breaking by smallest stay_id. Require:

- patients.anchor_age >= 18; age 91 remains the MIMIC top-coded value;
- non-null valid subject_id, hadm_id and stay_id;
- icustays.intime < icustays.outtime < admissions.dischtime;
- non-null admissions.dischtime;
- no admissions.deathtime <= icustays.outtime;
- a complete 12-hour pre-outtime window beginning after the index ICU intime;
- for a primary domain-specific trajectory contrast, valid support evidence and at least two usable physiology-observation bins in each six-hour half for that domain.

The landmark is icu/icustays.outtime. Predictors are restricted to [outtime-12 hours, outtime). Charted observations use charttime; interval exposures and procedures use starttime/endtime. storetime is never a clinical-time predictor. Follow-up is [outtime, min(outtime + 48 hours, admissions.dischtime)). Use the first event among later ICU return, in-hospital death before return and alive hospital discharge before return. Resolve a death/return tie within one minute in favor of death, and a discharge tie in favor of the non-discharge event; repeat key estimates with 0- and 5-minute tie tolerances.

Split by subject, never by row, ICU stay, note or event. Use fixed 70/15/15 train/validation/test partitions with seeds 17, 29 and 43. Fit and tune all preprocessing, q choices, thresholds, hazard parameters and dynamic-state parameters on train/validation. Lock all test predictions and the complete prespecified sensitivity grid before test evaluation. If a class is too sparse, retain it in the audit and report the prespecified sparse-cell rule; do not merge classes after inspecting test results.

## Exposure and trajectory definition

Preserve the parent’s support ledger. Construct respiratory and vasoactive evidence separately in the preceding half P = [outtime-12 hours, outtime-6 hours) and final half F = [outtime-6 hours, outtime).

Respiratory-positive evidence is any valid charted O2 flow >2 L/min (itemid 223834), FiO2 >0.21 (223835), PEEP >0 (220339), or a corroborating intubation, extubation or NIV procedure event. Procedures corroborate a transition only; they are not treated as continuous ventilation.

Vasoactive-positive evidence is valid interval overlap in icu/inputevents for norepinephrine 221906, epinephrine 221289 or 229617, dopamine 221662, phenylephrine 221749, 229630, 229631 or 229632, or vasopressin 222315. Use starttime/endtime overlap and statusdescription validity. Do not use storetime as exposure time.

For each domain and half classify positive evidence, observed non-positive evidence, uninformative absence, contradiction or invalid evidence. Require at least two respiratory and two MAP observation bins per half for the primary complete-observation population. Recode only uninformative absence over q in {0, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00}; never recode an observed non-positive value. Retain inadequate, active, discordant and unclassifiable records in the audit.

A domain is recently withdrawn if it has positive support evidence in P and no positive evidence in F, subject to the q scenario and adequate-evidence rule. Durable recorded-off requires no positive evidence in either half with adequate evidence in both halves. The primary trajectory analysis is restricted to recently withdrawn domains so that it does not silently mix an active-support patient with a patient already off support. Durable-off status is a prespecified secondary analysis and does not replace the primary estimand.

For physiology, use chart-time one-hour bins. Respiratory instability in a half is the fraction of valid bins with SpO2 <90% (itemid 220277) or respiratory rate >=30/min (220210). Hemodynamic instability is the fraction of valid bins with MAP <65 mmHg, using arterial MAP 220052 and non-invasive MAP 220181. If both MAP sources are present, use a prespecified arterial-first rule and retain source masks; do not count missing as stable. Store numerator and denominator for each burden. The primary abnormal-bin threshold is more than one of six bins, with any abnormal bin and more than two bins as locked sensitivities.

For each recently withdrawn domain d, define the mutually exclusive domain-specific trajectory class from (P instability, F instability):

- recovery-concordant: P unstable and F stable;
- persistent mismatch: P unstable and F unstable;
- late-emergent mismatch: P stable and F unstable;
- stable-transition: P stable and F stable;
- unknown/contradictory: any inadequate, contradictory or otherwise unclassifiable support/physiology evidence.

The primary contrast is persistent mismatch versus late-emergent mismatch, with recovery-concordant as the clinically interpretable reference. Stable-transition is reported as a secondary descriptive comparator, not silently pooled with recovery-concordant. Respiratory and hemodynamic classes are estimated separately, and a joint secondary ledger records the Cartesian pair when both domains are recently withdrawn. No post-outtime variable enters the exposure or model.

This coding makes the new uncertainty explicit. Persistent mismatch represents instability that was already present before the recorded transition and remains present. Late-emergent mismatch represents a new final-half deterioration despite the same recent support-withdrawal definition. The analysis does not claim that either class is a true physiologic state: support and physiology are recorded proxies with missingness and process dependence.

## Outcome topology and estimands

For every index stay, search later ICU stays with later.intime > index.outtime within the follow-up boundary.

Define L as a later ICU return with at least one qualifying documented non-ICU bridge. Search all valid hosp/transfers rows with non-null careunit, intime < outtime, interval start no earlier than index outtime minus five minutes and end no later than later ICU intime plus five minutes. Clip each candidate interval to [index outtime, later ICU intime], require at least 30 minutes, and require its careunit not equal to the later stay’s first_careunit or last_careunit. Retain every candidate and label L if any qualifies. Define U as any later ICU return irrespective of bridge documentation. These are topology endpoints, not adjudicated clinical escalation labels.

Prespecify endpoint sensitivities: 0/5-minute bridge endpoint tolerance; minimum bridge durations of 0, 15, 30 and 60 minutes; careunit-only versus interval logic; exclusion of discharge events; and ICU-unit matching. Repeat death/return and discharge ties at 0 and 5 minutes. Keep all candidate bridge rows in transfer_outcome_audit.

At horizon h in {6, 24, 48 hours}, estimate standardized absolute first-event CIFs for L_h, U_h, D_h and A_h. The primary contrasts are:

- persistent respiratory mismatch minus late-emergent respiratory mismatch;
- persistent hemodynamic mismatch minus late-emergent hemodynamic mismatch;
- each mismatch class minus domain-specific recovery-concordant.

Standardize to the common eligible population or, for the primary recent-withdrawal contrast, to the common support/physiology-observed recent-withdrawal population. Report the standardization population, event counts, overlap, positivity and effective sample size.

Because the latent clinical meaning of a return is not observed, do not select a midpoint between L and U. For groups A and B, report the nonparametrically compatible interval for the return-compatible contrast:

[L_A,h - U_B,h, U_A,h - L_B,h].

Retain the operation inside every subject-bootstrap replicate. Report bound width, and report D and A directly as competing events rather than censoring them. A positive U contrast with a null L contrast supports only an aggregate return association, not the stronger documented post-non-ICU escalation interpretation. A death contrast does not establish treatment limitation, delayed rescue or discharge error.

## Transparent competing-risk baseline

Fit pooled one-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazard models for L return, U return, death and alive discharge, with a separate aggregate-return model. Use the identical feature table and the following prespecified nested models:

1. trajectory-only: domain, support-transition class, trajectory class, and hour;
2. physiology/support: continuous P and F burdens, trajectory class, support ledger and their prespecified domain interaction;
3. observation/process-only: masks, valid-bin counts, chartevent row counts, support/procedure availability, ICU LOS, transfer density and discharge-process variables;
4. full model: the prespecified union, plus age, gender, anchor-year group, admission_type, first_careunit, last_careunit and support-by-trajectory interactions.

Use regularized logistic discrete hazards only with the penalty locked by validation; retain coefficient tables, design matrix manifest and standardized predictions. The baseline is intentionally auditable: every trajectory effect is visible and can be decomposed into preceding burden, final burden and measurement intensity. It directly estimates observable CIFs and respects L/U partial identification. Its limitation is that it summarizes the sequence into two halves and cannot represent within-half ordering, persistence duration or latent measurement error.

Evaluate locked test predictions with group-specific CIFs and absolute contrasts at 6/24/48 hours, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier scores, event counts, overlap/ESS and subject-level bootstrap 95% intervals. The primary scientific comparison is the standardized trajectory contrast, not a predictive leaderboard gain.

## Scientifically substantive alternative: coupled observation-aware dynamic model

Fit a coupled two-domain observation-aware state-space/hidden semi-Markov model on the same twelve hourly bins and the same raw-derived inputs. The model has:

- latent respiratory and hemodynamic recovery states with explicit duration distributions and transition persistence;
- a directed one-hour lag term allowing physiology instability to precede or follow recorded support withdrawal;
- support transitions as noisy, observed process indicators rather than truth;
- emissions from the same SpO2, respiratory-rate and MAP measurements and the same support/procedure variables;
- a separate observation model using per-bin masks, valid-bin counts, chart row counts, source-channel indicators and support/procedure availability;
- a post-landmark topology component with ICU, documented non-ICU and unknown-location states, aligned to observable L, U, death and discharge hazards.

Fit state definitions, lag parameters, duration distributions, regularization and any state-number choice on train/validation only. Produce posterior probabilities of persistent and late-emergent latent instability, posterior transition/lag summaries, state entropy, and standardized outcome CIF predictions. Use the same subject partitions and q/topology sensitivity grid as the baseline. Any model-based latent transition-compatible return output is a sensitivity quantity only: it must be reported with its assumptions, checked against [L,U], and never called ground truth or substituted for the compatible interval.

The alternative is scientifically informative because the baseline’s two-half categorical exposure loses the order and duration within those halves. The dynamic model can test whether persistent mismatch is a stable latent state, whether late-emergent physiology follows the support transition, and whether an apparent trajectory effect is explained by missingness or channel-specific recording. It can also reveal a clinically meaningful disagreement: a strong baseline contrast but high latent-state entropy suggests a fragile proxy, while stable latent persistence and agreement on observable CIFs support a trajectory interpretation. It is not selected for complexity or a small score improvement.

Use CPU for extraction, ledger construction, baseline fitting, bootstrap and primary bounds. The current run’s discovery compute limit is 7,200 science seconds. The future solver planning envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM, 8 allocated GPUs and 28,800 seconds. The baseline and bounds estimates inherited from the parent (roughly 10–30 minutes extraction and 30–60 minutes baseline/bounds) are planning estimates; the 2,661-return and 2,060-bridge counts are measured inherited feasibility evidence. The dynamic model’s 1–4 hour estimate is unverified. A bounded A100 profile may be used only if dynamic optimization materially benefits from it, requesting one allocated GPU and explicitly using cuda:0 inside that allocation; ordinary CPU CUDA absence is not evidence about hardware. No GPU is required by the scientific question and no proposer/solver weight training is authorized.

Selection reason: the coupled dynamic model is retained because temporal origin is the scientific question, and explicit duration/lag parameters can distinguish a transient recording discontinuity from persistent or ordered instability. Deferral reason: a neural sequence model without interpretable state duration/lag or observation modeling would test only prediction and would not resolve this question; it is deferred. A kidney-specific latent organ model is also deferred until verified renal item mappings and event support exist. A simple Cox model is not selected as the primary baseline because the landmark has competing discrete first events and bounded return topology; it may be a secondary sensitivity, not a replacement.

## Falsification, sensitivity and interpretation gates

Lock and preserve:

- a timestamp audit proving no predictor has charttime, starttime or endtime after outtime, and a subject-leakage audit;
- within-window time permutation of support/physiology and a landmark-shift negative control;
- time reversal of the twelve bins and a P/F swap negative control; the temporal-origin claim should attenuate if ordering is destroyed;
- storetime substitution/negative control: delayed documentation may be diagnosed, but storetime cannot be used as clinical time;
- physiology/support-only, observation/process-only and full-model comparisons;
- removal of procedure corroboration and separate respiratory support-channel analyses;
- q, coverage, instability-threshold, six-versus-twelve-hour and bridge/topology grids;
- transfer careunit permutation and a +6-hour transfer-time shift;
- pre-landmark pseudo-return/topology negative control;
- discharge-as-competing-event versus discharge-as-censoring sensitivity;
- first-careunit, admission-type, anchor-year, ICU-LOS and transfer-density strata;
- class event counts, overlap/ESS, bound widths and dynamic state entropy/duration stability;
- whether dynamic latent return quantities remain within the observed L/U envelope.

The temporal-origin hierarchy is falsified when either domain’s persistent-versus-late-emergent worst-case compatible interval is null or reversed at the prespecified primary horizon(s), when the result is explained by final-half burden with no residual trajectory contrast, when it collapses after observation/process adjustment, when it depends on one support channel or one bridge rule, or when transfer/topology permutation preserves it. A signal that survives time reversal, P/F swapping, storetime substitution or transfer permutation is evidence of coding, documentation or topology confounding, not supportive clinical evidence. If only U is positive and L is null, reject the documented-escalation interpretation.

Supportive evidence requires adequate events and overlap; a prespecified directional compatible interval with a subject-bootstrap 95% interval excluding zero; stability at 6, 24 and 48 hours and across q/topology frontiers; attenuation under time reversal/permutation controls; and concordance between the auditable baseline and dynamic model on observable outcomes. Even this supports only a retrospective association that may inform monitoring prioritization or discharge-review hypothesis generation. It does not establish clinical utility or a discharge intervention.

Adverse evidence includes reversal after observation adjustment, a U-only signal, dependence on a single channel or topology rule, persistence under temporal permutation, non-positivity, or dynamic latent outputs outside [L,U]. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse trajectory cells, wide intervals, large L/U widths, high latent entropy, unstable durations/lag signs, poor overlap, or baseline/dynamic disagreement without a prespecified resolution. Inconclusive results do not confirm or refute the clinical mechanism.

## Unavailable evidence and deferred branches

The files do not provide clinician intent, treatment-limitation orders/goals of care, explicit ICU-discharge readiness, ward/intermediate-care monitoring intensity, oxygen-device detail, bedside adjudication of deterioration, reason for ICU return, planned versus unplanned transfer, causal treatment assignment, source control, or result-availability time. Raw waveforms, MIMIC-CXR images and external validation are unavailable in this configured snapshot. The discharge note text and detail fields are available as rows, but no validated local method makes them reliable labels for readiness, intent or deterioration; therefore they remain audit-only.

Consequently, even supportive results cannot establish that persistent mismatch caused return/death, that discharge was inappropriate, that a return was preventable, that monitoring was inadequate, or that a treatment should be continued. Expert adjudication, richer ward/transfer data, external validation and preferably a prospective or causal study would be required for those claims. Revisit the kidney/asynchronous-recovery branch only if a future snapshot supplies validated creatinine and urine-output item mappings plus adequate coverage/events. Revisit antimicrobial de-escalation only if indication, source control and result-availability timing are adjudicated. New imaging, waveform or ward-monitoring inputs would justify a separate study, not an unannounced expansion.
