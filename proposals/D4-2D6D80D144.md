# Earlier natural menopause and cardiac structural remodelling beyond measured haemodynamic load

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank women with self-reported natural menopause, does earlier age at menopause remain associated with left-ventricular myocardial mass and left-atrial structure/function after adjustment for age, body composition, and measured blood-pressure load?

The clinically important unresolved question is whether menopause timing marks a subgroup with earlier subclinical cardiac structural change, or whether the apparent association is explained by residual haemodynamic load and shared metabolic ageing. This matters because menopause timing itself is not modifiable, but if it identifies a subgroup with earlier cardiac remodelling, it could motivate earlier cardiovascular surveillance and risk-factor control.

The leading explanation is that earlier natural menopause is associated with higher cumulative blood pressure and/or adverse body composition, which in turn drives left-ventricular hypertrophy and left-atrial remodelling. The strongest rival is a sex-hormone-related biological transition: loss of oestrogen may independently alter cardiac structure. A third rival is that menopause age is a proxy for broader ageing or health behaviours, so the association disappears after measured load adjustment.

## Estimand and adjustment hierarchy

Primary estimand: the adjusted mean difference in each cardiac phenotype per 5-year earlier natural menopause age.

Model 0: age only.
Model 1: Model 0 plus ethnicity, smoking, physical activity.
Model 2: Model 1 plus BMI, weight, body fat percentage, and visceral fat volume.
Model 3: Model 2 plus systolic and diastolic blood pressure.
Model 4: Model 3 plus repeat blood-pressure availability and prespecified HRT strata, where feasible.

The primary result is Model 4. Model 3 is the principal test of whether measured haemodynamic load explains the association. If Model 4 is unavailable because repeat-BP coverage is too sparse, report Model 3 as primary and label the result as not fully adjusted for repeat measurement.

## Competing explanation check

If the association is present in Model 0 but attenuates substantially in Model 3, the data support residual haemodynamic/body-composition explanation rather than an independent menopause-timing association. If the association persists in Model 4 and is stable in the repeat-BP subset, this is supportive evidence for an association beyond measured load, but not proof of a hormonal mechanism. If the association is absent in Model 4 or unstable across sensitivity analyses, the result is adverse or inconclusive.

## Method comparison

Baseline: linear regression with the Model 0–4 hierarchy, robust standard errors, and prespecified missingness handling.

Learned alternative: a gradient-boosted regression model trained on the same baseline features, with menopause age as the exposure of interest. Evaluate held-out R-squared improvement over the linear baseline and inspect the partial dependence of menopause age. The learned model may reveal nonlinear or interaction effects that the linear baseline loses, but it cannot establish causation or a hormonal mechanism.

Selection criterion: if the learned model improves held-out R-squared by more than 0.01 and shows a stable, monotonic menopause partial dependence, report it as supportive of a nonlinear association. If it does not improve or the partial dependence is unstable, retain the linear result.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: a 5-year earlier menopause is associated with higher LV myocardial mass and adverse LA structure/function in Model 4 with CI excluding the null, and the association is stable in the repeat-BP subset.

Adverse: the association attenuates to null in Model 3 or Model 4, or is absent in the repeat-BP subset.

Inconclusive: wide CIs, materially different results across sensitivity analyses, or insufficient repeat-BP coverage.

This study cannot establish causation, a hormonal mechanism, or clinical benefit of HRT. Those claims would require longitudinal hormone measurements, causal inference methods, or a clinical trial.
