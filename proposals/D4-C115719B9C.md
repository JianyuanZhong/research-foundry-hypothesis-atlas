# Hypothesis: Differential Biomarker-Disease Association Patterns in CRM Comorbidity Progression

## Scientific Question

**Among UK Biobank participants, do biomarker profiles show differential association patterns with single versus multiple cardio-renal-metabolic (CRM) comorbidity progression?**

Specifically, we test whether the set of biomarkers showing significant independent associations with CRM disease progression differs between:
- Participants progressing to single CRM disease (cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, or type 2 diabetes)
- Participants progressing to multiple CRM diseases (two or three of the above)

## Background and Rationale

Recent work in UK Biobank (Lin et al., 2025) demonstrated that biological aging strongly predicts progression to CRM comorbidity, with specific proteins mediating these associations. However, this study treated CRM progression as a homogeneous endpoint and did not examine whether the proteomic risk profile differs when predicting progression to single versus multiple CRM diseases.

This unresolved question is clinically important because:
1. **Personalized risk stratification**: Different protein profiles may identify distinct high-risk subgroups warranting different interventions
2. **Mechanistic insight**: If certain proteins predict only single-disease progression while others predict multi-disease progression, this suggests distinct biological pathways
3. **Therapeutic targeting**: Proteins mediating multi-disease progression might be better candidates for broad-spectrum prevention

## Hypothesis

Among UK Biobank participants without baseline CRM disease, the proteomic profile associated with progression to multiple CRM diseases differs from that associated with progression to single CRM disease. Specifically:
- Proteins predicting single-disease progression include those involved in single-organ dysfunction pathways
- Proteins predicting multi-disease progression include systemic inflammatory and metabolic regulators that coordinate multiple organ systems

## Study Population

- **Source**: UK Biobank (UKB) dataset
- **Inclusion**: Participants without cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, or type 2 diabetes at baseline
- **Exposure**: Baseline proteomics measurements (2911 plasma proteins)
- **Outcome**: Progression to CRM comorbidity over follow-up
- **Stratification**: Progression to first CRM disease vs. progression to multiple CRM diseases

## Data Bindings

The experiment will use the following UKB tables:
- `ukb_population`: For baseline demographics (age, sex, ethnicity)
- `ukb_main`: For proteomics measurements (columns in format X-Y.Z where X is protein ID, Y.Z represents time points)
- `ukb_health_outcomes`: For CRM disease endpoints and progression status

### Key Columns
- `eid`: Participant ID (join key)
- Proteomics columns: Pattern X-Y.Z (e.g., "12345-0.0", "12345-1.0")
- Outcome columns: Disease status codes for cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes

## Temporal Boundaries

- **Start**: Baseline assessment (first available proteomics measurement)
- **End**: Latest follow-up with outcome data
- **Follow-up period**: Variable, determined by last outcome assessment date

## Primary Outcome

Time-to-event to first CRM disease, stratified by:
1. Progression to single CRM disease (one of the three)
2. Progression to multiple CRM diseases (two or three)

## Analysis Plan

### Baseline Model (Reference)
- Cox proportional hazards model with age, sex, and key covariates as confounders
- Hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals for proteomic profiles

### Alternative Model (Hypothesis)
- Multivariate Cox model with interaction terms between proteomic profiles and disease state
- Mediation analysis to identify proteins that specifically mediate multi-disease progression
- Machine learning classifier trained on proteomic profiles to distinguish single vs. multi-disease progression

### Falsification Criteria
- **Hypothesis supported**: Proteins show differential effect sizes for single vs. multi-disease progression; machine learning classifier achieves significant AUC (>0.70) in distinguishing progression pathways
- **Hypothesis refuted**: No differential proteomic effects across disease states; machine learning classifier performs no better than random chance (AUC < 0.60)
- **Inconclusive**: Mixed results with limited statistical power; requires larger sample size or different biomarker panel

## Scientific Significance

This study would advance understanding of:
1. **Biological heterogeneity in disease progression**: Whether CRM comorbidity progresses through distinct biological pathways
2. **Protein biomarker utility**: Which proteins are best for predicting single vs. multi-organ disease
3. **Clinical implications**: Development of stratified prevention strategies based on proteomic profile

## Limitations

1. **Cross-sectional proteomics**: Baseline measurements cannot establish temporal causality
2. **Disease coding accuracy**: Administrative codes may not capture disease severity or stage
3. **Confounding**: Unmeasured lifestyle and environmental factors may influence both proteomics and disease risk
4. **Population**: UK Biobank participants are not fully representative of the general UK population
