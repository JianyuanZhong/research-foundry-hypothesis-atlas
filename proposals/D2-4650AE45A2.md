# MerTK-dependent macrophage–fibroblast context hypothesis

Seed D2-T1; parent [prior hypothesis]. This is a planned hypothesis, not an executed result.

## Question and clinical importance

In RA synovium, reciprocal macrophage–fibroblast signaling is proposed to sustain a failed-resolution state [K1]. The unresolved question is whether an intervention-associated transcriptional response in fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) depends on the identity of the macrophage partner, and whether that dependence is selectively altered when MerTK signaling is perturbed. This matters because a target that changes only a subset of macrophage–fibroblast contexts would predict selective rather than universal therapeutic effects; it also distinguishes a context-gated response from a generic inflammatory response.

The strongest evidence currently supports only that the available experiments contain matched macrophage–FLS co-culture conditions and MerTK-inhibitor perturbations, and that MerTK/CD206-positive and negative macrophage populations are experimentally represented. It does not establish that the inhibitor acts directly on MerTK in every cell, that transcriptional changes are causal, or that a clinical treatment effect exists.

## Hypothesis

Among FLS co-cultured with macrophages, the intervention-associated transcriptional response will be larger and more reproducible in the MerTK/CD206-positive macrophage context than in the MerTK/CD206-negative context, and the difference will be attenuated by MerTK inhibition. The primary estimand is the context-by-intervention interaction in log2 fold change of FLS transcript abundance, not a claim of direct MerTK target action.

A falsifiable prediction is that the interaction contrast
(response in Mpos context - response in Mneg context)
is positive for a prespecified inflammatory/activation response and decreases toward zero after MerTK inhibition. A secondary prediction is that the interaction is not explained by a generic LPS effect: the same direction should not appear in FLS-only controls.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Baseline: a prespecified donor-aware paired analysis of the targeted panel. For each gene, calculate log2 pseudobulk fold changes for the intervention and context contrasts, then estimate the interaction with a linear model with donor as the repeated unit and FLS-only control as an offset/negative control. Use permutation within donor and a false-discovery-rate threshold; report effect sizes and uncertainty, not only significance.

Substantive alternative: a joint hierarchical interaction model across the measured FLS–macrophage context pairs, with donor and partner-context random effects, gene-specific context slopes, and a shared inflammatory response factor estimated from the prespecified panel. Fit the model to the same E-MTAB-8873 pseudobulk inputs and evaluate by donor-held-out prediction of the context-by-inhibitor contrast and by calibration of the interaction posterior/credible intervals. The alternative can reveal whether a coherent context-gated program is shared across donors and whether the apparent interaction is driven by one donor or one partner; the baseline loses that structure and may overstate isolated gene effects. If the hierarchical model is unstable because of the small donor count, defer it and retain the baseline as the primary result.

## Outcomes and interpretation

Supportive: a reproducible positive context-by-inhibitor interaction in the targeted assay, with FLS-only controls near null, uncertainty excluding the prespecified effect region, and consistency in the E-MTAB-8316 bulk check. This would support context-dependent intervention-associated transcription and justify a perturbation study, but not direct MerTK causality.

Adverse: no interaction, an interaction of opposite sign, or a large FLS-only control effect. This would argue against the proposed context gate or indicate that the observed change is generic to LPS/inhibitor exposure, donor selection, or measurement.

Inconclusive: wide intervals, failed matched-pair availability, unstable hierarchical fitting, or discordant assays. This would not refute the hypothesis; it would identify the need for more donors, better matched conditions, or direct target validation.

## Limits and next test

The data contain intervention-associated transcription, not proof that UNC106238 directly blocks MerTK in the relevant cells, not clinical outcomes, and not whole-tissue or complete transcriptome coverage. The next test is a donor-matched perturbation with direct MerTK engagement readout (for example, validated target occupancy/phosphorylation or a genetic MerTK perturbation), paired with the same FLS-only and macrophage-context controls. Clinical relevance would additionally require patient-level outcomes or expert adjudication of synovial disease state.
