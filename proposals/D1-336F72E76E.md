# Strict prior-encounter history beyond current laboratories for all-admission HCC care transitions

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

This proposal is an independent successor of `[prior hypothesis]`. It retains the incumbent's clinically honest scope—prediction of a locally recorded care transition—and repairs a concrete validity ambiguity: a row linked to the anchor encounter can have a native timestamp before admission without proving that it was available as prior history. Such rows may reflect registration, retrospective documentation, or peri-admission workflow. The primary exposure is therefore restricted to records linked to encounters whose native `admission time` is strictly before the anchor `admission time`; anchor-linked rows are excluded from the primary history model even when their event timestamp is earlier.

The future solver must newly produce:

1. a frozen all-admission adult first-observed HCC-coded cohort and patient-level partition;
2. an exhaustive admission-anchored recorded-transition label audit;
3. locked predictions for `A`, `L`, `H_P`, `S_P`, and a mandatory process-only `S_{P,process}` on exactly the same admissions, labels, split, and calibration policy;
4. a primary all-admission incremental estimate for strict prior-encounter history `H_P-L`, with statewise calibration and patient-clustered uncertainty;
5. a prespecified comparison of strict prior history with an anchor-linked pre-admission sensitivity, so the source of any gain is empirically testable;
6. source-ablation, history-availability, time-order and cross-patient-history falsifications; and
7. a claim-to-output table that separates recorded-care computation from active-HCC, treatment, causal and clinical-utility claims.

Completion requires the data/key/time audit, fitted models, locked predictions, proper scores, calibration intervals and falsification outputs. A null increment, a process-only increment, or an inconclusive endpoint is a valid scientific result.

## Unresolved question, hypothesis and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence supports only that this HCC snapshot contains timestamped prior encounters, assays, procedures, orders and medications, and that procedure starts and discharge can define a locally recorded transition. It does not establish active HCC, diagnosis timing, tumor burden, stage, resectability, indication, intent, scheduling, receipt, completion, response, toxicity, mortality, outside-care completeness or benefit. The incumbent's provisional counts are feasibility evidence only, not this study's result.

The unresolved predictive question is:

> Among all eligible adult first-observed HCC-coded inpatient admissions, does strictly prior-encounter local history from the preceding 730 days add calibrated information about the complete recorded transition through 72 hours beyond admission context and current-admission assays observed in [0,12) hours?

The primary hypothesis is that `H_P` improves held-out full-vector log loss and/or multiclass Brier score over `L`, with no material calibration deterioration, because prior encounters contain recency and trajectory information unavailable in the first 12 hours. A clinically useful secondary hypothesis is that the increment is not limited to rows linked to the anchor encounter; if it is, the apparent “pre-admission history” is more plausibly peri-admission documentation than portable prior-history information. This is an association/prediction hypothesis, not a treatment or causal hypothesis.

The clinical importance is bounded but consequential: before a team can rely on an electronic history summary alongside current tests for early coordination, it matters whether the historical signal survives a strict availability definition and whether it adds information when current laboratory observation is sparse. The result could justify collecting prior records, show that current assays suffice, or show that apparent history value is only an observation-process artifact. It cannot by itself justify a treatment decision or a bedside threshold.

## Population and temporal estimand

Use HCC snapshot `[source checksum]`, catalog [source checksum], and all source files read-only. Every source is an ordinary CSV; no archive member is used.

Normalize Unicode and whitespace in keys and diagnosis text. Join `diagnoses` to `encounters` on the exact pair (`patient master index`, `visit number`). The cohort rule is normalized exact `hepatocellular carcinoma` or case-insensitive substring `hepatocellular carcinoma` in `diagnosis name`. Print the regex, all matched strings, diagnosis-type overlap, duplicate keys and unmatched keys. This is a record-coded cohort, not confirmation of active HCC.

Require age >=18, parseable native `Admission Time` and `Discharge Time`, nonnegative stay, and anchor admission `[2011-01-01,2026-01-01)`. Keep the earliest eligible HCC-coded encounter per `Patient Master Index`, ordered by native `Admission Time` then `Encounter Number`; do not replace an invalid earliest record with a later one. Set (t_0) to anchor `Admission Time`. Exclude direct identifiers (`Name`, `National ID Number`, `Mobile Phone Number`, `Medical Insurance/Visit Card Number`, `Hospitalization Number`) from features.

Use half-open elapsed-time windows:

- strict history: prior encounters with native `admission time < t_0`, and event rows linked to those prior encounter keys only, with native event time in `[t_0-730 days,t_0)`;
- current labs: `[t_0,t_0+12h)`;
- primary recorded transition: `[t_0,t_0+24h)` followed, when unresolved, by `[t_0+24h,t_0+72h)`.

A strict-history event is eligible only if (a) its exact (`patient master index`, `encounter number`) key joins an encounter with native `admission time<t_0`, (b) its primary native event time is parseable and strictly less than (t_0), and (c) it lies in the 730-day window. The prior encounter need not itself carry an HCC diagnosis. Anchor-linked pre-(t_0) rows are excluded from `H_P` and `S_P`, counted separately, and used only in a frozen sensitivity. Rows with unparseable times are counted by source/key/window and are not coded as absence. Prior-encounter admission before the left edge produces a left-truncation indicator; records older than the window are not used as features.

The unchanged primary terminal outcome is exhaustive over every eligible admission. Use procedure presence and valid native `procedures.Start Time` only:

- `P0`: valid procedure start in `[0,24)` before discharge;
- `D0`: discharge at or before 24 hours before any valid early procedure;
- `U0`: a missing/invalid procedure time whose unknown position could change early procedure-versus-discharge or boundary ordering;
- `R24`: no established `P0`, `D0` or `U0`;
- among `R24`, `P1`: first valid procedure start in `[24,72)` before discharge;
- `D1`: discharge in `[24,72)` before any valid later procedure;
- `U1`: unresolved procedure timing that could change later ordering;
- `N72`: no established later transition by 72 hours.

The terminal vector is `[P0,D0,U0,P1,D1,U1,N72]`, with later states marginalized through `R24`. The primary all-admission estimand is the mean terminal probability vector over all eligible admissions, not the conditional `R24` distribution. Discharge wins exact procedure/discharge ties in the primary analysis; procedure-wins and conservative/optimistic unresolved-time policies are prespecified sensitivities. A procedure start before admission or after discharge is audited and cannot silently define an anchor outcome. Missing procedure time never becomes “no procedure.” Procedure text and route families are descriptive secondary analyses only, not the primary endpoint.

## Information sets, inputs and matched alternatives

All models use the same eligible admissions, terminal labels, patient-level SHA-256 split, preprocessing fit policy, regularization selection, calibration and target.

`A` is admission context: age, sex, admitting department (`Encounter Department`), calendar era, admission time-of-day and missingness indicators. It excludes diagnoses, procedures, orders, medications, documents, pathology and examinations.

`L` is `A` plus assay-specific current observations in `[0,12)`, using `labs.Test`, `Qualitative Result`, `Quantitative Result`, `Specimen Type`, `Test Time`. Numeric and qualitative results remain separate because the catalog states there is no lab unit/reference-range column. For each assay use first/last valid numeric value, change, elapsed time, count, density/missingness and a slope only with at least two distinct native timestamps. Qualitative results remain assay-specific categorical indicators. No `[12,24)` lab enters `L`.

`H_P` is a transparent strict-prior summary added to `L`, using only `[t_0-730d,t_0)` rows linked to prior encounters:

- prior encounters: count, inpatient days, days since prior admission and discharge, distinct departments and left-truncation flag;
- labs: assay-specific counts, distinct prior encounters, first/last numeric values, change, recency, span, supported slope, and qualitative/missingness indicators;
- procedures: recorded occurrence count, distinct prior encounters, recency and inter-event gaps, without interpreting `surgery`;
- orders: count, distinct prior encounters, recency, gap/density and time-missingness using `Order Time` as the primary process timestamp;
- medications: count, distinct prior encounters, recency, gap/density and time-missingness using `start time` as the primary process timestamp.

Names, doses, frequency, route and status are not used to call treatment or intent. Every count/recency feature has a “no observation” indicator. A source-ablation table compares prior encounter+lab+procedure blocks against those blocks plus order/medication process blocks. All assay vocabulary, clipping, imputation and scaling are fit on buckets 0–59 only and selected on 60–69; save a Chinese-column/time-rule feature manifest.

`S_P` is a low-dimensional irregular continuous-time latent state model on the exact same strict-prior event rows as `H_P`, not a richer cohort or later data. Each event carries source/type, native time relative to (t_0), permitted assay identity plus numeric or qualitative payload, generic encounter/procedure/order/medication process tokens and missingness. It excludes diagnosis text, clinical-document text, pathology, examination narratives, direct identifiers and every anchor-linked row. Assay values are standardized within assay using fit-only parameters.

Between events, a latent state decays with elapsed time; at events, an observation-mask-aware source/type-specific update is applied; a coupled head predicts the same terminal vector. Fit supervised negative log likelihood, with a masked-event reconstruction term optional only if fixed before selection and tuned on bucket 60–69. Use at most two latent coordinates unless a reproducible predeclared selection rule supports more. Label them latent coordinates, never “liver reserve,” “renal reserve,” or validated disease scores. `S_P` can reveal order-sensitive, nonlinear recency patterns and uncertainty from irregular sampling that first/last/count summaries lose.

Mandatory `S_{P,process}` uses the same event times, sources, encounter/procedure/order/medication counts and missingness but removes all assay values and assay-result tokens. If its increment reproduces `S_P` or `H_P`, interpret the result as documentation/observation intensity rather than physiology.

The scientific comparison is therefore matched:

- baseline: `A`;
- current-lab comparator: `L`;
- primary transparent increment: `H_P-L`;
- learned representation increment: `S_P-H_P` and `S_P-L`;
- observation-process control: `S_{P,process}-L`.

The baseline and learned alternatives answer the same target with the same split and uncertainty. `H_P` is selected as primary because it is auditable and directly tests availability of prior records. `S_P` is retained because it can reveal irregular order/recency patterns that summaries discard. A transformer is deferred: no local evidence establishes that a higher-capacity model is needed, and it would add complexity without a new scientific contrast. Revisit only if the low-dimensional model shows stable incremental value and a subsequent question specifically concerns long-range event order.

## Split, estimand, evaluation and uncertainty

Assign each patient by SHA-256 of normalized `Patient Master Index` modulo 100: buckets 0–59 fit, 60–69 preprocessing/hyperparameter/calibration selection, 70–79 locked test, and 80–99 inaccessible. All rows for a patient follow that assignment. Freeze cohort, strict-history eligibility, labels, dimensions, vocabulary and calibration before opening bucket 70–79 labels.

Primary held-out outcomes on every eligible admission are paired differences `H_P-L` in multiclass log loss and multiclass Brier score for the full seven-state terminal vector. Report calibration intercept/slope, reliability curves, observed-versus-predicted probability for each state and statewise probability contrasts as secondary diagnostics. Report `S_P-H_P`, `S_P-L` and `S_{P,process}-L` with the same metrics. Use at least 1,000 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples of locked predictions (500 only with a documented time-budget fallback), preserving all admissions from each patient. Calibration is fit-only and selected on 60–69, never fit on 70–79.

The main result is an all-admission predictive increment, not a causal effect, policy value, treatment-selection effect, threshold rule or net benefit. Report availability strata as diagnostics: no strict prior history, 1–2 prior encounters, >=3; current-lab none/sparse/dense using a locked count rule; and strict-prior versus anchor-linked pre-admission availability. Do not interpret these strata causally. The primary all-admission score must remain the unstratified cohort score.

## Falsification and interpretation gates

Freeze these before locked evaluation:

1. **Anchor exclusion audit:** compare primary strict-prior history with an anchor-linked pre-(t_0) sensitivity. A gain confined to anchor-linked rows does not support portable prior-history information.
2. **Cross-patient history permutation:** permute complete strict-prior histories within each fit/selection/test partition while preserving history length. Any persistent gain suggests leakage, duplicate patients or split failure.
3. **Within-patient time shuffle and reverse order:** preserve values/counts but destroy temporal ordering. Attenuation of `S_P` but not necessarily count summaries supports order information; no attenuation means do not claim trajectory information.
4. **Process-only control:** compare `S_{P,process}` and a process-only `H_P`. If they reproduce the full increment, attribute it to observation/documentation intensity.
5. **Forbidden-window sentinels:** verify no anchor-linked row, current `[12,24)` lab, post-discharge event, or outcome-derived feature enters history. A sentinel using current ([12,24)) data to predict the already-started `[0,24)` label must flag any apparent gain as leakage.
6. **History-window sensitivity:** repeat 30-, 180- and 730-day strict-prior windows and report left truncation. A result dependent only on an observation-system boundary is not a stable long-history claim.
7. **Endpoint timing robustness:** audit invalid/missing procedure times, midnight starts, before-admission and after-discharge rows, exact boundaries and tie precedence; rerun conservative/optimistic unresolved rules. Material reversal is inconclusive.
8. **Label and partition negatives:** permute labels within calendar era and admitting department and verify no patient crosses buckets.
9. **Source ablation:** compare strict prior encounter+lab+procedure history with and without order/medication process blocks. This tests whether the increment is driven by documentation volume.
10. **Anchor-date placebo:** for admissions with a prior encounter, move the history cutoff to a pre-anchor pseudo-cutoff at a locked offset while keeping the actual outcome fixed only as a negative-control data-flow test; any use of rows after the real anchor or other temporal inconsistency invalidates the affected pipeline. This placebo is not interpreted as a clinical effect.

Supportive evidence requires a nonzero 95% patient-clustered interval for `H_P-L` in at least one proper score without material calibration deterioration, reproducibility across fit seeds and not only one history-availability stratum, attenuation after cross-patient history permutation, and no forbidden-window or label-permutation artifact. A clinically meaningful trajectory interpretation additionally requires attenuation after time shuffling/reversal and failure of process-only history to reproduce the gain.

Adverse evidence is no improvement, worse calibration, a gain fully reproduced by process-only inputs, gain only from anchor-linked rows, persistence after history permutation, or a forbidden-window/split artifact. This rejects the strict prior-history increment for this recorded endpoint while leaving the local-care description valid.

Inconclusive evidence includes dominant `U0/U1`, too few later transitions, wide clustered intervals, severe dependence on timestamp ties or history window, unstable calibration, strong left truncation, or inability to distinguish assay/history signal from observation intensity. Inconclusive is not confirmation.
