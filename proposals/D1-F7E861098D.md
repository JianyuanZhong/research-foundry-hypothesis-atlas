# Admission-anchored, label-robust heterogeneity of early laboratory information in HCC-coded care

## Scientific deliverable and substantive successor

This is a substantive scientific child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent asks whether a 12-hour laboratory increment differs across four untimed documentary strata, but its primary model conditions on reaching hour 12. That leaves two residual vulnerabilities: early discharge/procedure is not represented by the laboratory comparison, and the A/P/T/U contrasts can be unstable or be mistaken for validated clinical phenotypes.

The new deliverable is an **admission-anchored discrete-time competing-transition analysis**. It will newly fit, on the same patient split:

1. a transparent no-current-laboratory baseline (B0);
2. a matched assay-specific interval-summary model (B1); and
3. an irregular latent laboratory state-space model (B2).

It will estimate the cumulative 72-hour laboratory information gain for **every eligible first observed HCC-coded admission**, while estimating heterogeneity as a documentation-robust analysis quantity rather than as a claim about active HCC biology.

Completion requires frozen cohort and lexical rules, an all-admission flow diagram, interval-specific locked predictions, the marginal 72-hour terminal distribution under B0 and B1, the label-stratified and label-robust contrasts with uncertainty, B2 comparison, and all prespecified falsifications. A positive hypothesis is not required for completion.

### Strongest supported claim versus unresolved claim

Available evidence supports only the following recorded-data claims:

- the HCC diagnosis source contains 1,810,646 rows and, in the parent audit, HCC-core text on all 105,044 encounter keys;
- the encounter source supplies admission/discharge times, the procedure source supplies a native procedure start time for most rows but not all, and the laboratory source supplies a native laboratory time;
- provisional A/P/T/U counts in the parent audit were A=73,415, P=696, T=2,134 and U=28,799 among 105,044 HCC-core encounter keys.

These are snapshot and documentation facts. They do not establish active HCC, recurrence, pathology confirmation, stage, tumor burden, treatment status, or procedure indication. Diagnosis rows have no native time; narrative and pathology fields do not provide validated time-linked adjudication.

The unresolved claim is whether laboratory observations available before each prediction boundary add calibrated information about subsequent **recorded-care transitions**, and whether that information gain differs reproducibly by documentation context after early transitions, sparse strata, and lexical uncertainty are handled. The study will not call A, P, T or U biological phenotypes.

## Hypothesis and estimands

The eligible population is all adults with a first observed eligible inpatient encounter carrying the frozen HCC-core lexical rule. The primary hypothesis is:

> In the all-admission population, adding assay-specific laboratory history available before each discrete-time prediction boundary improves held-out prediction of the first recorded procedure, discharge, unresolved-procedure state, or no transition through 72 hours; the improvement differs between encounter-context documentation (A) and the pooled non-A documentation context, and the direction of this contrast is stable under prespecified lexical encodings.

This is an information/transportability hypothesis, not a treatment-effect hypothesis. The directional claim is deliberately weaker than the parent's A-versus-U/T claim: P, T and U remain visible, but sparse groups are not required to support separate clinical interpretations.

### Admission-anchored time-safe target

Set t0 to native `admission time`. Use the half-open grid [0,6), [6,12), [12,24), [24,72), assigning exact boundaries to the later interval.

For each admission, define the first recorded transition using only source-native time:

- P: a procedure with parseable `start time`, within [0,72) after (t_0) and before native `discharge time`;
- D: native discharge before a valid procedure;
- Uproc: procedure evidence has missing/invalid `start time` that can change the ordering, and the transition cannot be assigned to P or D;
- N72: no P, D or Uproc before 72 hours.

Within each interval, the solver must retain the sequence of the first applicable transition. Exact same-time ties, discharge/procedure precedence, invalid times, and procedures after discharge must be tabulated. Missing procedure time is never silently treated as no procedure. Conservative and optimistic Uproc bounds must be reported; the primary rule must be frozen before locked evaluation.

A discrete-time cause-specific hazard is estimated at each boundary among admissions still without an established P/D/Uproc transition. This risk-set conditioning defines a transition hazard only. The primary estimand is the **product-integrated all-admission 72-hour terminal distribution**, initialized with every eligible admission at time zero; early transitions are therefore retained rather than discarded.

For patient i, let S_i^0 and S_i^1 be the held-out proper scores for the product-integrated terminal distribution from B0 and B1. Choose one sign convention before evaluation (positive means improvement), and define the laboratory information gain as the paired score difference delta_i = S_i^1 - S_i^0. The primary all-admission estimand is:

`delta_all^q = sum_g pi_g^q E(delta_i | G_i^q = g)`.
where (G^q) is a frozen documentation encoding and (pi_g^q) is its observed proportion in the full eligible population—not the proportion among an hour-12 risk set.

The primary heterogeneity estimand is:

`H^q = E(delta_i | G_i^q = A) - E(delta_i | G_i^q != A)`.

Also report the B0/B1 difference in all-admission cumulative probability of P by 72 hours and the complete terminal vector. These predicted probabilities are not counterfactual treatment probabilities and must not be described as effects of ordering or performing a procedure.

## Models, split and all-admission fitting

Use one deterministic participant split based on the catalog's participant hashing: buckets 0-59 for fitting, 60-69 for prespecified preprocessing/regularization selection, and 70-79 for locked evaluation. The reserved 80-99 partition is inaccessible and is not external validation. All encounters and modalities for a patient remain in one bucket. Label rules, assay inclusion, state dimension, and hyperparameters must be frozen before test predictions.

At every boundary b in {0, 6, 12, 24}, B0 is a transparent multinomial cause-specific hazard model for the next interval. It uses age, sex, admitting department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, prior encounter count, and pre-admission assay-specific nearest/count/recency/missingness summaries. B0 excludes raw diagnosis text, diagnosis type, Q labels, orders, medications, procedure text, discharge time after the boundary, and any future lab.

B1 has exactly the same targets, patient split, preprocessing, regularization and model family. At each boundary it adds only assay-specific laboratory history available strictly before that boundary: first/last valid result, change, elapsed time, count, missingness/density and a within-assay slope when at least two times support it. Current-encounter rows are joined through the encounter key and restricted by native `test time`; an admission that already transitioned is not later reintroduced. B1 therefore tests incremental laboratory information rather than documentation-label information.

B2 is the substantive alternative. Fit an assay-specific irregular state-space model on exactly the same laboratory rows and split: latent hepatic-reserve and renal-stress coordinates, assay-specific offsets/noise, irregular observation times, shrinkage, posterior state uncertainty, and coupled competing-hazard heads. It may represent nonlinear level/slope coupling and informative observation timing that B1's first/last/change summaries lose. It must not be described as a validated physiologic score. A separate count/density-only model identifies observation-process information; B2 is supportive only if it improves held-out terminal Brier/log score and calibration over B1 and its latent trajectories are stable under time and label falsifications.

The hierarchical heterogeneity analysis is applied to locked patient-level paired score differences, not to an unvalidated label as a clinical predictor. Fit a prespecified robust partial-pooling model with group effects for Q_full, Q_type and Q_modifier; report raw support, shrunken means, `delta_all^q`, `H^q`, and 95% bootstrap intervals. The primary bootstrap resamples patients (not rows) and preserves each patient's complete risk-set sequence. Use at least 500 paired clustered resamples; 1,000 is preferred if the solver budget permits. Report both locked-prediction bootstrap intervals and, as a sensitivity, refit-on-bootstrap intervals. Do not use the locked test outcomes to select the dictionary, assay list, state dimension or primary endpoint.

## Evaluation and falsification

Primary held-out outputs:

- terminal-state multiclass Brier score and log score for the all-admission product-integrated distribution;
- interval-specific cause-specific calibration and all-admission calibration at 72 hours;
- `delta_all^q`, `H^q`, full Q_full A/P/T/U score differences, and their uncertainty;
- all-admission predicted P/D/Uproc/N72 probabilities and observed frequencies;
- B2-minus-B1 score/calibration changes, latent-state uncertainty and support by assay/interval.

Prespecified falsifications and sensitivity analyses:

1. Shuffle laboratory times within patient and assay, then separately shuffle values within assay. A physiology-linked gain should attenuate; persistence points to assay identity or observation process.
2. Compare B1 values/trajectories with count/density/missingness-only features. If the gain is explained by testing intensity, the laboratory-value hypothesis is adverse or inconclusive.
3. Permute terminal transition labels within calendar era and admitting department. A gain that survives should be treated as a pipeline or documentation artifact.
4. Use a forbidden-window sentinel by allowing [12,24) information to predict an earlier interval. It must not improve a properly time-safe target.
5. Re-run exact-boundary, midnight, same-time-tie, and conservative/optimistic Uproc rules. Large changes make the all-admission estimand inconclusive.
6. Permute Q labels within supported era/department cells while preserving group sizes. Heterogeneity should attenuate.
7. Recompute Q_full, Q_type and Q_modifier without letting any raw untimed diagnosis text enter a prediction feature. A sign change across encodings is lexical fragility, not biological heterogeneity.
8. Report the parent's R12-only analysis as a sensitivity and compare its weighted result with the admission-anchored result. It may not be called an all-admission estimate.
9. Report a strict A-only and an unstratified all-HCC analysis, but do not substitute either for the primary population.
10. Keep order/examination/procedure-family concordance secondary. Orders cannot establish intent, scheduling, receipt or appropriateness; examination/pathology text cannot establish imaging/pathology-confirmed HCC.

Supportive evidence requires coherent source joins and temporal flow, an uncertainty-supported positive `delta_all` and `H` that are stable across Q encodings, calibrated B1, attenuation under time/terminal-label/Q permutation, and no dominance by density or unresolved-time handling. B2 is supportive only if it adds reproducible calibrated information over B1.

Adverse evidence includes a null or opposite H, gain surviving time or terminal permutations, equivalence to density-only features, large boundary/Uproc sensitivity, or a result driven by one lexical/procedure string. This refutes the directional documentation-heterogeneity hypothesis or implicates process artifact; it does not prove that laboratory data have no clinical value.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse P/T/U groups, unstable full-label precedence, wide paired intervals, high Uproc mass, inadequate repeated assays, nonconvergence, or disagreement among Q encodings. Inconclusive is a valid study result and must not be converted into a positive clinical claim.

## Clinical advance and evidence limits

The substantive advance is an estimand that can answer whether a laboratory-based recorded-care signal is transportable across documentation contexts without pretending that a downstream hour-12 risk set represents all admissions. It also separates a real laboratory trajectory contribution from testing intensity and from the arbitrary use of diagnosis strings. A stable result could motivate prospective work asking whether an adjudicated, time-stamped active-HCC cohort needs separate calibration or review pathways; an adverse result could prevent overinterpreting documentary subgroup differences.

Computationally checkable outputs include source hashes and cohort flow, rule/matched-string audits, interval event construction, valid laboratory provenance, locked predictions, scores, calibration, partial-pooled contrasts, all-admission integration and permutation results. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for active HCC confirmation, recurrence, stage, tumor burden, resectability, liver-function grading, procedure indication/intent/receipt/completion, response, toxicity, mortality, outside-hospital care, clinical benefit, causal effects, and clinical utility. This snapshot has no validated diagnosis timing, pathology timing, imaging ground truth, units/reference ranges, tumor measurements, treatment semantics, or complete external-care capture. No deployment, treatment, referral, or monitoring recommendation follows from a supportive result.

## Method alternatives and revisit record

The learned/mechanistic B2 was selected because irregular timing and coupled assay trajectories could reveal information that first/last/change summaries discard; its value is tested against the matched B1 and is not a complexity bonus. The transparent B0/B1 pair remains necessary to distinguish laboratory information from baseline composition and observation intensity.

Deferred alternatives are:

- raw diagnosis/document text as a real-time predictor: deferred because diagnosis and document tables have no native time or validated scope/temporality; revisit only with a time-stamped adjudicated ontology;
- imaging/pathology confirmation: deferred because no imaging files are available and pathology has no time field or adjudicated active-HCC label; revisit with time-linked ground truth;
- causal treatment-effect analysis: deferred because indication, receipt, completion, counterfactual eligibility and outcomes are unavailable;
- a neural sequence model: not prohibited, but not selected as primary because the scientific uncertainty is irregular laboratory state and documentation transportability, and B2 is a more interpretable matched alternative. Revisit if B2 is inadequate and a bounded sequence representation has a prespecified clinical target, same split, calibrated evaluation and measured resource need.
