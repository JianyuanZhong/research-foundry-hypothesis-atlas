# Proposal: INR-corrected, pathway-safe reassessment after TACE

## Episode, parent, and substantive repair

This is a successor to assessed valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent’s validated estimand: at a day-45 landmark after a first recorded TACE-like procedure, among patients still free of a liver-directed procedure, prognose the first subsequently recorded liver-directed action during days 46–365 in the local HCC record.

Direct source inspection exposed a clinically important assay-identity error in the parent: it prohibits calling the available coagulation measure INR and uses `Prothrombin Time Ratio`, yet the laboratory source contains the exact assay `International Normalized Ratio` with 297,731 nonempty numeric rows, alongside 297,726 nonempty numeric `Prothrombin Time Ratio` rows. The substantive repair is to use literal `International Normalized Ratio` as the primary hepatic synthetic-function input, keep `Prothrombin Time Ratio` as a separately named legacy sensitivity, and audit their co-reporting and discordance rather than pooling or treating them as aliases. INR is dimensionless, but no other assay units are inferred from the schema.

The successor also prevents a favorable aggregate boundary from hiding selective failure for one recorded liver-directed pathway. The combined liver-directed contrast remains primary and directly comparable with the parent, but a boundary is not promoted as a prospective reassessment candidate unless it preserves sensitivity for both repeat TACE-like and alternate liver-directed families and shows no analogous gain for the non-liver capture control. The learned alternative has separate cause-specific hazards so it can reveal whether irregular biomarker shape is associated with one recorded pathway rather than another.

This is a prognostic study of recorded local care. It does not estimate treatment effects, radiologic response, recurrence, viable tumor, or the benefit of triggering reassessment.

## Evidence, unresolved claim, and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported by the available local evidence is narrower than a clinical response claim. The HCC snapshot contains linkable dated encounter, procedure, laboratory and examination records. A source audit measured 105,044 encounter rows, 338,040 procedure rows, 1,810,646 diagnosis rows, 28,159,928 laboratory rows, and 419,996 examination rows in 215.27 seconds with 8 CPUs and 32 GiB. The procedure table has 95,674 distinct encounter keys but 242,366 additional rows beyond one per key; labs have 95,188 distinct keys and 28,064,740 additional rows. This establishes that the study is computable only after table-level aggregation and does not establish biological response or complete care capture. It found nonempty numeric rows for literal `alpha-fetoprotein` (182,935), `albumin` (238,289), `total bilirubin` (238,553), `international normalized ratio` (297,731), `prothrombin time ratio` (297,726), and `platelet count` (253,588). These are all-source row counts, not patient counts or paired-window availability. The local procedure vocabulary includes abundant TACE-like records and distinct resection/ablation terms, but raw names are not adjudicated indications.

The parent’s prior cohort audit measured 19,496 patients with a first TACE-like index, 15,605 with another procedure during days 0–45, 1,468 with a later first repeat-TACE-like procedure, 1,491 with a later first alternate liver-directed procedure during days 46–365, and 4,524 with a joined encounter at least 365 days after index. These are feasibility and ascertainment measurements, not results for the hypothesis. They also motivate retaining the day-45 risk set while reporting early action as a separate pathway.

The unresolved primary claim is:

> In adults with structured HCC and a first recorded TACE-like procedure who remain free of any liver-directed procedure through day 45, adding the prespecified early AFP/hepatic trajectory to a capture-adjusted transparent model reduces the alert fraction by at least 10 absolute percentage points at a predeclared 90% sensitivity for the first recorded liver-directed action during days 46–365, on a later locked time period, without a comparable gain for a non-liver capture-control outcome.

The source-correction claim is that the primary INR-based result is not an artifact of conflating coagulation assays. Report the legacy PT-ratio trajectory on the identical cohort, split and outcome, but never include both values as one assay or choose between them using locked-test performance. If the two assays are nearly always co-reported and numerically equivalent, the correction may leave predictions unchanged while still repairing nomenclature. If they are discordant or have different availability and conclusions depend on substitution, the biological trajectory claim is adverse or inconclusive.

The pathway-safety claim is:

> The same trajectory-based boundary does not trade away clinically different recorded liver-directed pathways: at its locked-test operating point, its cause-specific sensitivity is no more than 10 percentage points below the baseline for both repeat TACE-like and alternate liver-directed outcomes, using the declared uncertainty rule.

The 10-point safety margin is a research design convention, not a clinical non-inferiority margin or a treatment-utility claim. If the data cannot support enough events for pathway-specific uncertainty, the pathway claim is inconclusive and the aggregate result is not promoted.

A secondary model-information hypothesis is that an irregular-time latent model improves the pathway-safe operating boundary over the transparent trajectory model by at least 5 percentage points in alert fraction at the same combined sensitivity, while maintaining the pathway-safety and capture-control checks. This asks whether asynchronous shape, discordance between AFP and hepatic proxies, and the measurement process contain information lost by a thresholded phenotype—not whether a more complex model is intrinsically better.

This matters because a prospective silent reassessment workflow needs to know both how many patients it would flag and which recorded care pathways it would fail to surface. A result that is aggregate-specific but pathway-blind would not support a clinically interpretable reassessment study.

## Population, temporal rules, and outcomes

Use one index per patient. In `datasets/hcc/table-d5eae16f8f8093d9.json` / the ordinary source `[internal dataset path]`, define a TACE-like procedure from `Surgery` by a frozen, case-insensitive rule: contains `TACE`, contains `hepatic arterial chemoembolization`, or contains both `hepatic artery` and `embolization`. Require a diagnosis name containing literal `hepatocellular carcinoma` in `Diagnosis Name` from `datasets/hcc/table-12710723c3df0c99.json` / `[internal dataset path]`, joined to the encounter on (`patient master index`, `encounter number`) whose `Encounter Time` is from 180 days before through 7 days after the index. Diagnoses have no independent time field; their time is the joined encounter time. Include index starts through 2025-01-01 only when the entire day-365 outcome horizon can be observed in the available local record; otherwise censor and label the index as not full-horizon eligible according to the prespecified censoring rule.

The primary day-45 landmark risk set excludes a repeat-TACE-like or alternate liver-directed procedure after index +24 hours through day 45. Report these early actions as a separate pathway; do not code them as non-events or response. Alternate liver-directed names contain `resection`, `ablation`, `radiofrequency`, or `microwave`, excluding TACE-like rows. The complete pre-landmark pathway table must include early TACE-like, early alternate, both, and no early liver-directed action. This makes clear what population the day-45 estimand conditions on.

For days 46–365, define the first recorded outcome as:

1. repeat TACE-like procedure;
2. alternate liver-directed procedure;
3. first non-liver procedure, used only as a capture-control outcome; or
4. no recorded event before censoring or day 365.

Report the combined liver-directed outcome as the primary endpoint, the two liver-directed families separately as co-secondary causes, and the non-liver procedure separately. A non-liver action is not a competing biological endpoint. If liver-directed and non-liver procedures have exactly equal first `Start time`, mark a same-time tie, exclude it from the ordered primary contrast, and repeat analyses assigning ties to each side. If multiple rows represent one procedure episode, de-duplicate only by a frozen patient, normalized family, and 24-hour start window; preserve raw rows and counts.

The primary estimand is the locked-test difference in alert fraction between B2-INR and B0 at the smallest tuning-set predicted cumulative incidence/risk threshold achieving at least 90% IPCW sensitivity for the combined first liver-directed outcome, among the eligible day-45 risk set. The pathway-safety estimand is the corresponding cause-specific sensitivity difference for repeat TACE-like and alternate liver-directed outcomes. These are local recorded-care estimands conditional on observed eligibility and local follow-up.

## Matched baseline and irregular-time alternative

All models use identical cohort, day-45 risk set, outcome coding, censoring, patient-level split, tuning rule and locked test.

B0 is a transparent regularized discrete-time cause-specific hazard model. Inputs are age, sex, encounter department, pre-index diagnosis and procedure-family counts, pre-index assay summaries and missingness, and day-45 observation/capture features. It has separate hazards for repeat TACE-like, alternate liver-directed, and non-liver procedures, and produces cause-specific cumulative incidences and combined liver-directed risk. It is the capture-adjusted reference.

B2-INR is B0 plus the corrected frozen paired AFP/hepatic trajectory using literal `International Normalized Ratio`, continuous component changes, measurement intervals, trajectory completeness and missingness. No information after day 45 is used. B2-INR versus B0 is the primary scientific comparison and retains the parent’s 10-point aggregate alert-burden criterion. B2-PT is the otherwise identical legacy sensitivity substituting literal `Prothrombin Time Ratio`; it diagnoses assay dependence and cannot replace the primary model after seeing results.

M3-INR is a scientifically substantive irregular-time low-rank state-space model with AFP-informed activity and hepatic-function states using literal INR, patient random intercepts/slopes, assay-specific observation-intensity submodels, and separate competing hazards for the three recorded outcome families. Fit states using actual measurement times through day 45 and output posterior state/slope uncertainty, predicted cause-specific hazards/CIFs, and pathway-specific operating points. On the paired analysis, use observed trajectories; on the full-index analysis, make missingness an explicit state/category and never impute an unobserved response. M3 can reveal asynchronous shape, discordance, and informative measurement timing that the B2 category and summary changes lose. It cannot recover unmeasured tumor burden, treatment intent, outside care, access, or biological response. A matched M3-PT sensitivity is optional only after the transparent INR/PT audit; it must not consume the primary tuning decision.

The comparison is substantive: it tests whether a pathway-safe recorded-care boundary is driven by a reproducible trajectory signal rather than by generic contact intensity, and whether longitudinal shape helps distinguish recorded pathway families. A transformer adaptation is deferred because the remaining uncertainty is endpoint/capture specificity and the HCC site has no images or validated narrative labels; extra sequence capacity would not repair those identification limits. A transformer may be revisited only if M3 shows stable shape information and the task gains an independent adjudicated endpoint.

## Splits, boundary, uncertainty, and falsification

Use an index-date split: 2010–2022 fit, 2023 tuning, and 2024–2025-01-01 locked test, with no patient in more than one split. If exact date support or split event counts fail, use a deterministic participant-hash fallback and label it internal temporal replacement, never external validation. Freeze vocabulary, parsing, feature construction, imputation, model settings, censoring model and boundary rules before locked-test evaluation.

On tuning data, for B0, B2-INR and M3-INR choose the smallest predicted combined liver-directed CIF/risk threshold with IPCW sensitivity >=90%; ties go to the higher threshold only if sensitivity remains >=90%. Repeat at 80% and 95% as secondary operating points. Threshold selection never uses locked-test outcomes. B2-PT uses the same procedure but is reported as a frozen sensitivity, not an alternative selected by test performance. On the locked test report combined sensitivity, cause-specific sensitivity for each liver-directed family, competing CIF, alert fraction, PPV, false-alert fraction, flags per observed liver-directed event, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, Brier score and log score. Use patient-clustered bootstrap intervals, repeating tuning/threshold selection within bootstrap resamples or showing nested fixed-threshold sensitivity.

Primary support for H1 requires: B2-INR reaches the locked-test sensitivity target under the declared uncertainty convention; B2-INR reduces alert fraction versus B0 by >=10 points with a 95% interval excluding zero; the reduction is stable in complete-follow-up and literal-vocabulary sensitivities; and neither non-liver capture control nor pre-index placebo shows a comparable trajectory-specific gain. Report B2-INR minus B2-PT contrasts and paired assay agreement, but do not require INR to outperform PT: scientific correctness of the exact assay identity is the repair, while predictive equivalence is a valid result.

Pathway-safe support additionally requires, for both repeat TACE-like and alternate liver-directed outcomes, B2-INR’s cause-specific sensitivity difference from B0 to have a lower 95% bound above -10 points at the matched operating point, or an equivalent predeclared one-sided 95% bound, and requires enough events to make that bound estimable. If either family is too sparse, the pathway claim is inconclusive rather than silently dropped. The same safety rule is applied to M3-INR versus B2-INR when testing the secondary model-information hypothesis. A >=5-point alert-fraction improvement by M3-INR is required for a substantive alternative-model claim; smaller differences are exploratory.

Falsification includes: comparable B2-INR gain for non-liver procedures or the pre-index placebo; aggregate gain with a >10-point loss for either liver-directed family; attenuation after observation features or censoring adjustment; availability-only prediction without paired-trajectory improvement; reversal in complete follow-up; unstable weights or thresholds; poor calibration; raw-name or tie ambiguity; M3-INR nonconvergence; or a large pathway-mix change between fit and test. Material INR/PT discordance, lack of paired INR in the landmark population, or a conclusion that changes only because the two assays were pooled is adverse to the corrected trajectory claim. A result concentrated in early actions does not support the day-45 estimand. If there are too few pathway events, high loss to local follow-up, or insufficient assay overlap, report inconclusive.

## Interpretation and unavailable evidence

Supportive computation would establish only that, in this HCC source and time split, a day-45 EHR rule can reproducibly stratify the probability of a later recorded liver-directed action at a quantified alert burden while retaining sensitivity across the two observed action families. M3 support would additionally indicate that irregular timing or discordance contains useful recorded-care information. These findings could justify a prospective silent reassessment and clinician-adjudication study.

Adverse results would favor capture intensity, endpoint heterogeneity, unstable local documentation, or insufficient signal over a trajectory-specific reassessment boundary. An aggregate-positive but pathway-unsafe result is specifically a failed repair, not success. An inconclusive result cannot distinguish these explanations.

The dataset lacks image files, validated radiologic response labels, explicit treatment intent, reliable tumor burden/number/size, death linkage, outside-care capture, access or eligibility reasons, another institution for external validation, and separate laboratory units. Examination narratives are available but unvalidated and are not a substitute for imaging review. Procedure names may represent administrative recording rather than a completed or clinically intended treatment; `Operation Source` is audited but does not solve intent. Stronger claims require expert review of raw procedures and examinations, validated assay units, radiology/pathology adjudication, mortality/outside-care linkage, external validation, and a prospective impact study with clinician-selected harms and benefits. The 90% operating point is a research boundary, not a clinical decision threshold.
