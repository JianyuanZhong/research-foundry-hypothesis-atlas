## Unresolved question, supported evidence, and hypothesis

The expert seed eICU-07 asks whether risk differences between interhospital-transfer and directly admitted patients shrink after accounting for arrival organ injury. The audited eICU snapshot supports only that the relevant source labels, ICU-relative observations, disposition, hospital identifiers, APACHE fields, and stated joins are available. In the parent-like frame there are 12,688 adult first ICU admissions: 9,771 Direct Admit and 2,917 Other Hospital. A prior audit found 425 recorded unit deaths and 6,473 alive exits by 48 ICU-relative hours; these are feasibility counts, not evidence of a source effect. The audit also found APACHE physiologic coverage but no variable-level APACHE time field.

The unresolved claim is narrower and temporally valid:

> Among eligible adult first ICU admissions that are alive and still under ICU-unit observation at minute 60, how much of any standardized post-minute-60 difference in recorded ICU-unit death between `Other Hospital` and `Direct Admit` is explained by the opportunity to record early arrival physiology, and how much remains after the values and their ordered early trajectory are represented?

Primary hypothesis: in the minute-60 landmark risk set, the source-only post-landmark death CIF contrast is positive; adding only the opportunity to observe early physiology attenuates it; adding valid early values attenuates it further; and any remaining contrast is concentrated in prespecified worsening/persistence versus recovery states rather than a source-label main effect. This is an observational, database-observable prognostic/measurement-process hypothesis, not a causal effect of transfer, transport delay, referral quality, treatment, or organ injury.

The repair changes interpretation materially. The study no longer estimates mortality from ICU admission including the period used to define eligibility and features. It estimates prognosis conditional on reaching a documented, observable minute-60 landmark. Early deaths and early exits remain clinically relevant selection evidence, but are not silently treated as post-landmark outcomes. A positive landmark result cannot be generalized to patients who die or leave the ICU unit before minute 60.

A supportive result requires all of: (a) a prespecified positive source-only CIF contrast; (b) at least 50% attenuation by B2 versus B0, with a 95% interval compatible with that criterion; (c) the opportunity-only B1 step reported separately; (d) the same qualitative direction/attenuation in held-out hospitals; and (e) M localizing residual risk to interpretable post-landmark worsening/persistence or recovery transitions. The minimum clinical interpretation margin is 2 percentage points absolute; an interval spanning that margin is inconclusive even with a small p-value.

Adverse evidence is a reproducible null/reversal, no attenuation, attenuation attributable only to source-label adjustment, failure in held-out hospitals, or residual risk not localized by M. This rejects the corresponding arrival-information interpretation, not transfer harmlessness. Source-label permutation within hospital that reproduces the result, or an effect confined to dense-measurement hospitals, falsifies the intended physiologic interpretation. Inconclusive evidence includes insufficient arm overlap/support after landmarking, invalid source/status/time dictionaries, unstable missingness or units, wide cluster intervals, poor calibration, or a mechanistic fit unable to distinguish states.

## Population, landmark, time, and outcomes

The unit is one ICU stay keyed by `patientunitstayid`. Include rows from `patient` with parsed age >=18 (parse `> 89` as 90 and retain an age-censored flag), `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitstaytype=admit`, nonmissing `hospitalid`, and `hospitaladmitsource` exactly `Other Hospital` or `Direct Admit`. Repeated stays sharing `uniquepid` are assigned to one split; they are not deduplicated across the cohort unless the frozen inclusion rule says so. `unitadmitsource` is reserved for concordance sensitivity and cannot replace the primary exposure.

Time zero is ICU admission. All offsets are raw minutes relative to ICU admission. Construct early features from valid observations/results with time in `[0,60]), using result/observation time. Negative offsets, sentinel values such as -1, nonnumeric values, and invalid units are missing. For each domain retain first valid value, count, distinct valid timestamp count, coverage/opportunity indicator, and the exact earliest/latest valid time. `labresultrevisedoffset` is not used to backdate a value in the primary analysis; it is a prespecified revision-time sensitivity.

Landmark eligibility is deterministic and must be evaluated before outcomes or model fitting:

- `unitdischargeoffset > 60` and finite, with `unitdischargestatus` exactly `Alive` or `Expired`, means the patient is observed alive and in the ICU unit at minute 60 for the primary risk set. The strict `>60` rule avoids treating a boundary at minute 60 as post-landmark observation.
- A valid `Expired` or `Alive` disposition at offset `<=60` is excluded from the primary landmark risk set and counted separately as an early event/exit by source arm.
- Missing/invalid status or offset is excluded as unknown boundary and counted separately.
- The primary post-landmark ledger starts immediately after 60 and ends at 2,880 minutes after ICU admission. Event 1 is recorded ICU-unit death: `unitdischargestatus=Expired` with valid offset in `(60,2880]). Event 2 is recorded alive ICU-unit exit: `unitdischargestatus=Alive` with valid offset in `(60,2880]), regardless of location. A stay observed without either event through 2,880 is administratively censored at 2,880; unknown/invalid status or offset is unknown censoring. The endpoint is recorded unit disposition, not overall hospital mortality.

The flow must report, by source arm and overall, the parent-eligible count, early expired count, early alive-exit count, early unknown-boundary count, landmark count, and post-landmark event/censor counts. The direct source audit currently yields 12,688 parent-like eligible stays, 17 early expired (14 Direct Admit, 3 Other Hospital), 189 early alive exits (162, 27), one later unknown-status boundary, and 12,481 landmark-eligible stays (9,595, 2,886). The solver must recompute these from the frozen files and flag any discrepancy; discovery counts are not a substitute for the solver artifact. It must also report arm-specific landmark retention and compare early exclusion rates. No inverse-probability correction for pre-landmark exclusion is part of the primary estimand; a prespecified inverse-probability-of-landmark sensitivity may be reported only as a different, explicitly labelled target and cannot restore unobserved clinical information.

## Matched baseline and substantive mechanistic alternative

All methods use exactly the same minute-60 landmark cohort, outcome ledger, inputs, hospital split, transformations, and uncertainty protocol. The exposure contrast is standardized over the landmark cohort, not over excluded early deaths/exits.

B0 is a transparent pooled 30-minute cause-specific hazard model for outcomes strictly in (60,2,880]. It contains source label (`hospitaladmitsource`), age, gender, ethnicity, `unittype`, hospital context, and post-landmark time-bin indicators. B1 adds only early measurement opportunity from `[0,60]`: per supported vital/lab domain, opportunity indicator, count, distinct timestamp count, and coverage/earliest-time features, with no measured values. B2 adds valid early measured values and prespecified missingness indicators to B1, using only records timestamped no later than 60. Report standardized death and alive-exit CIFs for B0/B1/B2; the primary opportunity attenuation is B0-B1 and the value-added attenuation is B1-B2, with total B0-B2 and a 2-percentage-point scale.

M is a mechanistic ordered, piecewise-exponential multi-state model on the same 30-minute post-landmark bins and the same B2 early inputs plus a prespecified time-ordered early trajectory ledger. Freeze thresholds, transformations, and state definitions on development hospitals only. States at landmark are: observed/stable, partially recovered, worsening/persistent instability, and missing/insufficiently observed; outcome states are recorded unit death and alive unit exit/observation loss. State membership uses clinically bounded, source-supported thresholds and early slopes/persistence, never post-landmark values. Permit stable-to-worsening, worsening-to-recovery/stable, and each observed state to death or alive exit; estimate source-label transition effects conditional on opportunity and state. The multi-state estimand is the standardized death CIF and transition contribution, not an assertion of biological mechanism.

M adds information B2 loses: the order, spacing, persistence, and direction of early observations (for example, whether a low value was followed by recovery, remained low, or was observed only after a delay). The alternative therefore tests whether residual source risk is an ordered path/state phenomenon rather than a scalar source effect. Its extra complexity is scientifically justified by localization of the opportunity-versus-value claim, not by a predictive leaderboard.

A small learned irregular-sequence alternative is deferred, not prohibited. Revisit only if M shows stable held-out state structure, adequate timestamp/analyte support, and a preregistered small RNN/transformer can use the identical cohort, B2 event target, hospital split, calibration, process ablation, and uncertainty evaluation. No current evidence establishes that a learned model would separate physiology from observation behavior better than M; missing images and narrative text also preclude multimodal reproduction. The Delphi, Bayesian, and Oncoformer demonstrations were read through the local README/method guide with their stated availability limits; they motivate dated-trajectory comparison only, not reproduction.

Fit cause-specific hazards or equivalent competing-risk models with robust hospital-clustered uncertainty, with a `uniquepid`-clustered sensitivity. Standardize predictions to the landmark cohort. Report 95% confidence intervals (or bootstrap percentile intervals) for source contrasts, B0-B1, B1-B2, B0-B2, death/alive CIFs, transition contributions, calibration, Brier/proper scores, and transport differences. Decision-curve/net-benefit results are secondary and only permitted if a clinically justified action threshold and action consequence are supplied; predictive discrimination alone is not clinical utility.

Required falsifications/sensitivities are source-label permutation within hospital; restriction to hospitals with at least 20 patients per arm; leave-one-large-hospital-out; `unitadmitsource` concordance; lab result-time versus revision-time ordering; alternative first-valid tie rules; APACHE-free versus APACHE-secondary fits; measurement-density strata; exclusion of extreme `hospitaladmitoffset`; and separate death versus alive-exit CIFs. A source-permutation signal, or a result only in high-density sites, fails the intended interpretation. A sensitivity that includes early events is labelled a different time-zero target and cannot be presented as validation of the landmark estimand.

## Clinical interpretation and evidence limits

Supportive evidence would show that among patients who remain observable at minute 60, early arrival ascertainment and/or recorded physiology explain a material, transportable part of the later recorded unit-death contrast. It could motivate prospective evaluation of structured arrival reassessment and measurement capture. It does not justify a transfer policy, causal transfer or treatment claim, bedside threshold, hospital ranking, or inference about patients excluded before minute 60.

Adverse evidence rejects the corresponding portable arrival-information hypothesis; it does not show equivalence or absence of transfer harm. Inconclusive evidence produces a data-quality/selection report and no directional clinical recommendation.

Computationally checkable claims include source and schema hashes, actual headers, row counts, disposition maps, joins, cohort/landmark flow, early exclusion audit, feature timestamps, dictionaries, event ordering, split integrity, fitted parameters, predictions, CIF contrasts, uncertainty, calibration, transport, state transitions, and falsification outputs. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for true transfer route/timing, pre-arrival treatment, indication, adjudicated AKI/organ injury, treatment effects, overall mortality, and bedside benefit/harm. The release lacks images, raw waveforms, complete narrative notes, and validated clinical text extraction; no automatic verifier can establish those claims.
