# Remote pre-TACE2 hepatic state beyond the contemporaneous pre-repeat panel for a jointly adverse next-panel pattern

## Substantive two-parent evolution and decision question

This proposal is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the first parent's frozen repeat-TACE population, pathway ontology, event clocks, exact laboratory selectors, complete-source provenance, and trajectory cautions. It preserves the second parent's clinically relevant reverse nesting: ask whether remote information adds prediction after the contemporaneous pre-repeat state is already known. The substantive advance is to replace a single-assay next-observed level with a tightly event-anchored, jointly directional next-panel endpoint:

> Among selected patients with a first recorded repeat TACE and complete common-timestamp albumin/total-bilirubin panels, does the remote pre-TACE2 panel improve out-of-fold prediction of the next observed jointly adverse pattern—albumin lower and total bilirubin higher than the contemporaneous pre-repeat panel—beyond the contemporaneous panel and a compact decision-time core?

This is clinically sharper because albumin decrease and bilirubin increase together are more coherent with worsening hepatic laboratory state than either unitless assay level alone. It is still only a prognostic experiment for an observed laboratory pattern. It does not establish hepatic decompensation, treatment toxicity, procedural injury, safety, benefit, response, actual drug administration, or a reason to change repeat-TACE decisions.

## Claim ladder and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim supported before outcome modeling is feasibility. A no-sampling complete-source reconstruction produces 319 systemic-record and 1,491 comparator patients, 159 and 526 first strict repeat-TACE events, and 40/118 systemic/comparator patients with strict common-timestamp `B`, `P`, and `Y` albumin/bilirubin panels. A bounded audit of the prespecified endpoint, without inspecting any predictor-outcome association, finds 26/40 systemic and 71/118 comparator joint events, hence 97 events and 61 non-events among 158 patients. This supports a pooled, parsimonious nested prediction comparison; it does not support an arm interaction or outcome conclusion.

The unresolved primary hypothesis is:

> Conditional on the contemporaneous pre-repeat albumin/bilirubin panel and the frozen compact decision-time core, adding the remote pre-TACE2 albumin/bilirubin panel yields reproducible improvement in cross-fitted prediction of the jointly adverse next observed panel.

The null is no reproducible incremental prediction. The alternative is direction-agnostic with respect to coefficients: no sign of a remote albumin or bilirubin coefficient is prespecified. Only the endpoint directions are prespecified from the assay identities. No model, window, feature, or threshold may be selected from predictor-outcome association or performance.

## Endpoint and empirical feasibility decision

For patient `i`, define the primary binary endpoint

`C_i = 1[(Y_albumin < P_albumin) AND (Y_total_bilirubin > P_total_bilirubin)]`.

Ties are non-events. This endpoint uses within-person directions and no unit-dependent cut point. Its descriptive label is “jointly adverse next observed panel,” not clinical hepatic decompensation. Same-label/unit consistency is not guaranteed because HCC has no laboratory-unit column; therefore report specimen types, value ranges, and within-patient discontinuities, and mark the result inconclusive if the exact labels appear to mix incompatible scales or specimen contexts. Do not infer severity from magnitude.

The complete-source endpoint audit scanned every relevant source row and examined only endpoint prevalence and support, not any association between `B` (or any predictor) and `C`, not model performance, and not alternative directions or thresholds:

- strict common-panel complete cases: 40 systemic, 118 comparator, 158 total;
- joint events: 26 systemic, 71 comparator, 97 total;
- non-events: 14 systemic, 47 comparator, 61 total;
- marginal directional counts among joint-complete patients: albumin down 31/40 and 79/118; bilirubin up 34/40 and 107/118.

The binary endpoint is retained because both classes have adequate pooled support. The primary design is pooled with pathway arm as a covariate; no arm interaction or arm-specific performance claim is primary. A naive unstratified hash split produced one systemic fold with no non-event, so it is rejected as a feasibility warning before predictor associations are examined. The locked outer-fold construction instead sorts a SHA-256 hash within each `arm × C` stratum and allocates round-robin to five folds. This yields, up to fold-label permutation, systemic event counts 6/5/5/5/5 and non-event counts 3/3/3/3/2, and comparator event counts 15/14/14/14/14 and non-event counts 10/10/9/9/9. Thus every fold has both classes in each arm. Outcome-stratified allocation is used only to stabilize class support; preprocessing, tuning, and fitting remain training-only.

Secondary endpoints, which cannot rescue a failed primary, are: (1) ordinal directional burden `S=1[Y_albumin<P_albumin]+1[Y_bilirubin>P_bilirubin]` with levels 0/1/2; and (2) the continuous bivariate vector `(Y_albumin-P_albumin, Y_bilirubin-P_bilirubin)` analyzed assay-wise after training-fold standardization. These retain information if binary conclusions are imprecise but cannot be described as clinical severity because units and clinically important thresholds are absent.

## Nested models and primary estimand

Use exactly the same 158 expected complete-panel patients and folds in both models. The compact pre-event core is available by `P` time:

- contemporaneous `P_albumin` and `P_total_bilirubin`;
- frozen pathway arm `A`;
- age and sex at the latest encounter no later than TACE2;
- TACE2-to-repeat interval in calendar days;
- event-minus-`P_time` lead in hours.

The history addition is exactly `B_albumin` and `B_total_bilirubin`. Do not add `P-B`, slopes, observation counts, recency, interactions, broad laboratory vectors, departments, post-event variables, or a feature selected from association. `P-B` is algebraically derived from the same information and is not a separate discovery.

Fit nested ridge-logistic models with an unpenalized intercept:

`M0: C ~ P_albumin + P_total_bilirubin + A + age + sex + interval_days + P_lead_hours`

`M1: C ~ P_albumin + P_total_bilirubin + B_albumin + B_total_bilirubin + A + age + sex + interval_days + P_lead_hours`.

Use penalty grid `{0.01,0.1,1,10,100}`. Within each outer training fold, impute missing age/sex only under a locked rule, create any missingness indicators identically in M0/M1, standardize continuous predictors, and select penalty by inner stratified cross-validation minimizing log loss. No held-out outcome enters preprocessing or tuning. If a fold is rank deficient before penalization, has a single training class, or cannot produce finite predictions, the primary is computationally inconclusive; do not change features or folds.

The primary estimand is paired cross-fitted relative Brier improvement:

`Delta_Brier_rel = [Brier(M0)-Brier(M1)] / Brier(M0)`.

Report absolute paired `Delta_Brier`, log-loss difference, AUC difference, calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, prediction ranges, selected penalties, fold-level metrics, and arm-level performance descriptively. The design relevance margin is a 5% relative Brier reduction; it is not a clinical minimal-important difference.

Use 2,000 fixed-seed patient-level bootstrap replicates of paired held-out predictions, stratified by `arm × C`, for the primary 95% interval. Report the estimable fraction; fewer than 95% estimable replicates fails the uncertainty gate. A 200-replicate full-pipeline bootstrap and 20 fixed hash repetitions are partition/optimism sensitivities, not replacements for the primary split.

## Baselines, falsification, and selection diagnostics

1. **Current-state baseline:** M0 is the mandatory comparator. A B-only model is descriptive and cannot support the reverse-nested claim.
2. **History-linkage destruction:** within every outer partition, permute the paired `(B_albumin,B_total_bilirubin,B_time)` patient assignments 500 times within arm, leaving P, C, and other covariates fixed; rerun the identical pipeline. If observed improvement is typical of this linkage-destroyed distribution, patient-specific remote history is not demonstrated.
3. **Temporal leakage:** automatically assert that B precedes TACE2 day 0, P strictly precedes the repeat event, Y strictly follows it, all P/Y rows belong to `event_encounter`, stage timestamps are common across assays, and no duplicate/many-to-many join creates observations.
4. **Timing stability:** rerun all inherited locked selectors and count empirically distinct selected panels. No favorable selector may be chosen. Dependence on only the calendar-day or assay-specific alternative is timing-sensitive/inconclusive.
5. **Observation/selection sensitivity:** report complete-case construction at every stage. Cross-fit repeat-event and panel-observation models using only information available before each relevant observation; report positivity, standardized balance, maximum/concentration of weights, and arm-specific effective sample sizes. Weighted/unweighted conflict diagnoses selection sensitivity but cannot recover unobserved outcomes or remove collider bias from conditioning on repeat TACE and observed panels.
6. **Influence and partition stability:** report leave-one-patient-out metric changes, leverage/Cook/DFBETA from an unpenalized transparency fit when estimable, all 20 hash repetitions, and most-influential-patient deletion. A few patients cannot establish utility.
7. **Coefficient and calibration transparency:** report standardized coefficients and intervals as descriptive, never as causal effects. Material calibration worsening blocks a supportive branch even if discrimination rises.
8. **Assay-scale gate:** report `标本类型`, empirical ranges, abrupt scale discontinuities, and stage-specific distributions without selecting exclusions by outcome association. Evidence of incompatible scales/units makes the joint endpoint inconclusive; it cannot be repaired by post hoc thresholds.
9. **Multiplicity:** the binary endpoint is the sole primary endpoint. Ordinal and continuous analyses are secondary. No broad “hepatic reserve” statement may be based on selectively favorable secondary results.

## Locked result branches

Apply in this precedence order:

1. **Reconstruction/ontology falsified:** source hash, exact cohort/event/common-panel count, parser, duplicate, common-timestamp, or temporal assertion failure yields `inconclusive—reconstruction failure`; no outcome inference.
2. **Scale/meaning inconclusive:** incompatible assay scales/specimens, inability to defend within-label directions, or unit/context ambiguity beyond a directional comparison yields `inconclusive—assay meaning` and activates only clearly labelled assay-specific continuous descriptive outputs.
3. **Computationally inconclusive:** failed class/fold support, training-only preprocessing, finite fitting, or bootstrap estimability yields `inconclusive` without model redesign.
4. **Selection-sensitive:** failed positivity/ESS, severe weight concentration, or material weighted/unweighted conflict yields `selection-sensitive/inconclusive`, regardless of a favorable unweighted point estimate.
5. **Adverse degradation:** if the primary 95% interval for relative Brier improvement lies wholly below zero, or M1 materially worsens locked calibration/log loss, remote B degrades prediction beyond P under this design.
6. **Supportive:** only if all prior gates pass, `Delta_Brier_rel >=0.05`, its two-sided 95% interval excludes zero, log loss is non-adverse, calibration is non-adverse, linkage destruction does not reproduce the gain, and timing/influence/partition sensitivities do not reverse the branch. This supports reproducible prediction of the next observed joint panel only.
7. **Precise null:** if gates pass and the upper 95% bound is below 0.05, report no detected improvement of at least the prespecified 5% relative Brier margin in this selected cohort. This is not equivalence, no biology, or no clinical value.
8. **Ordinary null/inconclusive:** an interval spanning meaningful degradation and the 5% margin, linkage-placebo comparability, selector reversal, influence dependence, or discordant secondary endpoints yields `no demonstrated remote increment` or `inconclusive`, as appropriate.

Arm-specific coefficients, arm interactions, or subgroup utility are prohibited as primary claims. With only 40 systemic patients and 14 systemic non-events, any interaction is exploratory and cannot change the primary branch.

## Interpretation and evidence boundaries

A supportive result means that the remote pre-TACE2 panel contains patient-specific information about the next observed joint laboratory direction after the current panel is known, within patients selected for recorded repeat TACE and complete panels. It would justify external validation against an adjudicated clinical endpoint. It would not show that remote history should alter TACE decisions, that TACE caused the changes, that the systemic pathway modified them, or that any therapy was beneficial or harmful.

An adverse result means the remote panel worsened locked predictive performance or calibration. A precise null weakens the proposed monitoring increment at the prespecified design margin. A selection-sensitive result indicates that surveillance/event selection may explain apparent gain. A broad interval, unstable folds, incompatible scales, or sparse effective sample is inconclusive rather than evidence of absence.

The HCC tables lack laboratory units/reference ranges, validated Child-Pugh/ALBI context, performance status, adjudicated decompensation, tumor burden and imaging response, transfusion/albumin infusion and fluid balance, infection/bleeding, TACE rationale/technical success/complications, verified medication administration/adherence/dose, outside care, and complete mortality. Diagnoses are lexical encounter-linked proxies. Conditioning on a repeat procedure and complete panels may select on disease, access, clinician choice, surveillance, and survival. Expert chart/imaging/procedure adjudication and a prospective or external study with verified units, exposures, clinically meaningful outcomes, calibration, and decision-curve/net-benefit analysis are required for clinical-policy claims.

An automatic verifier can check source hashes, full row scans, joins, cohort/event/panel counts, parser and duplicate rules, exact clocks, endpoint arithmetic, fixed stratification, training-only preprocessing, ridge tuning, paired out-of-fold predictions, metrics, bootstrap and permutations, sensitivity selectors, diagnostics, and branch precedence. It cannot establish clinical importance, causal effects, administration, safety, mechanism, or decision utility.
