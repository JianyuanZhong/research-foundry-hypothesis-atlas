The clinical decision is whether a cystatin-C allocation rule that appears to enrich subsequent kidney-coded events—especially among people with low observed albuminuria—has a competing mortality signal that would make that enrichment unsafe or difficult to interpret. Mortality is also a clinically important outcome and a potential source of informative survival: censoring N18 at death can make two lists look different because one list dies before a kidney code is recorded.

The new falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among the exact frozen O1_hold population and the exact n1=466 AG1 and G1 lists, the AG1 list does not have a materially higher nine-year all-cause death yield than G1; specifically, the one-sided 95% upper confidence limit for the fixed-denominator contrast is below +10 deaths per 1,000 slots.

This is a prognostic safety/interpretation hypothesis about two fixed lists, not a claim that ordering cystatin C causes or prevents death. The +10/1,000 boundary is a prespecified operating margin (about five whole deaths at n1=466), not a validated patient-utility threshold. The parent’s direct N18 and low-UACR claims remain primary and cannot be rescued by a mortality result.
