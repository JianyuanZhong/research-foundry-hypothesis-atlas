# Episode 2 lead repair: repeated residual instability versus a last-value snapshot at ICU discharge

## Research question and falsifiable hypothesis

The unresolved clinical question is whether a patient who appears acceptable on a single last vital sign but has repeatedly abnormal physiology or substantial oxygen support during the final day of ICU care is at higher near-term risk after ICU discharge. This matters because ICU-to-ward transfer is a consequential monitoring decision: a reproducible, calibrated signal could motivate targeted ward surveillance or a prospective discharge-readiness study, whereas a null or charting-sensitive result would argue against using isolated final-window values as a discharge marker.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only data/provenance: the frozen MIMIC-IV 3.1 core snapshot contains linked admissions, ICU stays, timestamped ICU chart events, transfers and in-hospital outcomes, and its item dictionary contains the specified vital and oxygen labels. The prior candidate's clinical claim that residual instability predicts failure remains untested; the earlier aggregate audit did not produce usable event/coverage counts.

Primary hypothesis, stated as a prognostic incremental-value claim rather than a treatment claim:

> Among adults discharged alive from an ICU during an ongoing MIMIC-IV hospital admission, a prespecified repeated-instability summary measured in the 24 hours before ICU outtime will show a positive, reproducible association with first ICU return and/or death before ICU return within 7 days, and will improve held-out calibration/Brier score over a model using baseline context plus only the last observed values.

This is falsifiable. It does not claim that discharge caused the event, that delaying discharge helps, or that oxygen flow/FiO2 proves actual delivered oxygen or work of breathing.

## Population, index, and temporal boundaries

Use every available anchor-year group in the snapshot, but do not treat deidentified anchor years as a calendar trend because timestamps are subject-specific shifted. Include age >=18 using patients.anchor_age; report the MIMIC-coded value 91 as >89 rather than exact age.

Build admissions from hosp/admissions and ICU stays from icu/icustays, joining on (subject_id, hadm_id). Require a non-null ICU intime and outtime, outtime < admissions.dischtime, and no admissions.deathtime <= outtime. The primary index is the first eligible ICU discharge per (subject_id, hadm_id), selected before calculating outcomes. This prevents multiple correlated opportunities in one admission and makes a later ICU stay an outcome. A secondary all-eligible-stay analysis clusters uncertainty by hadm_id.

Time zero is the index icustays.outtime. The exposure window is the half-open interval [outtime - 24 hours, outtime), using chartevents.charttime, never storetime; rows at or after outtime and rows from other stays are excluded. Six non-overlapping backward 4-hour blocks are anchored to outtime. Follow-up ends at the earliest of outtime + 7 days, admissions.dischtime, and the first event.

The primary competing events are (1) first later icustays.intime > index outtime for the same (subject_id, hadm_id) within the follow-up horizon (ICU return); and (2) admissions.deathtime strictly after outtime and before any ICU return, within the horizon (death before ICU return). Discharge before either event is censoring. Death after ICU return is a secondary outcome, not death-before-return. Report the exact gap between ICU stays and a sensitivity requiring at least 2 hours. A secondary analysis follows to hospital dischtime. hosp/transfers is used to audit broad versus strict ward-separated return definitions (transfer_id,eventtype,careunit,intime,outtime); it is not used as an exposure.

## Covariates, baselines, and estimands

The context baseline B0 uses only information available at outtime: patients gender,anchor_age,anchor_year_group; admissions admission_type,admission_location,insurance; index stay first_careunit,last_careunit,los; time from admittime to outtime; and number of earlier ICU stays in the admission. Add missingness indicators. Do not use deathtime, dischtime, hospital_expire_flag, discharge_location, future ICU rows, discharge notes, or post-outtime measurements as predictors.

The last-value baseline B_last is B0 plus the last valid value of each prespecified vital/oxygen numeric domain and observation indicators. The repeated model B_repeat is B_last plus the repeated burden, valid-block/measurement-density features, and oxygen maxima. Fit no more than the declared feature set; no outcome-driven cutpoint or automated feature search.

For the primary analysis, estimate 7-day cumulative incidence of each competing event with Aalen-Johansen and cause-specific Cox models from outtime. Report cause-specific hazard ratios per training-cohort SD of the continuous burden and the prespecified joint phenotype with 95% intervals; these are associations, not causal effects. For incremental prediction, compare B0, B_last and B_repeat using subject-grouped held-out evaluation: Brier score, calibration slope/intercept and observed-versus-predicted risk are primary; AUROC/AUPRC and decision-curve net benefit at predeclared thresholds are secondary. ## Feasibility, leakage, missingness, and falsification

Pre-fit gates are fixed: at least 1,000 eligible first-index stays, at least 100 primary composite events, at least 50 first ICU returns and 50 deaths-before-return (or explicitly mark the corresponding event model infeasible), and at least 60% of eligible indexes with >=4/6 covered vital blocks. If a gate fails, report the actual audit and stop inferential fitting for that endpoint; do not alter windows or manufacture events.

The pipeline must assert no charttime >= outtime, no post-index or later-stay rows in features, no storetime used for temporal inclusion, exactly one admission per index, exactly one index stay per primary admission, and no prohibited outcome/future fields in B0/B_last/B_repeat. It must audit admission/stay key mismatches and impossible chronology. Patient-level resampling is required for confidence intervals.

Missing charting is informative. Report coverage by outcome, unit and exposure group. If a repeated-instability association disappears after density adjustment, or occurs only in densely charted patients, interpret it as ascertainment/acuity evidence rather than robust residual physiology. Code status 223758 can quantify availability but not classify DNR/DNI/comfort intent without clinician-reviewed mapping. Do not use note/discharge, note/discharge_detail, note/radiology or note/radiology_detail in the primary model; narrative text is post-index or not a validated classifier. The archive's radiology files are reports, not images, and raw waveforms are unavailable.

Supportive results require a prespecified positive burden gradient or joint-phenotype contrast, agreement of cause-specific and Aalen-Johansen estimates, improved locked-test calibration/Brier score of B_repeat over B_last, and no material collapse in the strict-return, density-adjusted, MAP-source and 12/48-hour sensitivity analyses. A small AUC gain without calibration or absolute-risk separation is not clinically useful evidence.

Adverse results are a consistently null/negative burden association with a sufficiently narrow interval, worse or unchanged calibration/Brier score versus B_last, or a signal that reverses after competing death, coverage adjustment, strict-return definition, or source sensitivity. These findings would argue that repeated charted instability adds no reliable information or that transfer/measurement patterns explain it.

Inconclusive results include insufficient events/coverage, wide intervals, unstable estimates across prespecified windows or sources, failed leakage/permutation checks, or inability to separate charting density from physiology. A failed permutation negative control invalidates computation; it is not evidence against the clinical hypothesis. A placebo window shifted 24 hours earlier and an earlier-window measurement-density negative control are required. Subject-level outcome permutation must remove predictive performance. A similar or stronger placebo signal means the feature is likely general severity/trajectory rather than discharge-proximal residual instability.

Computationally checkable claims are cohort counts, item availability, temporal ordering, event definitions, model estimates, uncertainty, calibration, negative controls and within-snapshot held-out performance. The study cannot establish causation, premature discharge, benefit from delaying ICU discharge or changing oxygen/monitoring, true delivered oxygen, unrecorded physiology, ward staffing, functional/cognitive readiness, goals of care, readmission reason, external validity or clinical utility. Those require clinician adjudication, richer prospective data, external validation and ultimately an intervention or target-trial design.
