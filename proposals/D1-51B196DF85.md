## 1. Unresolved question

AKI "recovery" is almost always declared by serum creatinine returning toward baseline [K2]. Yet creatinine is a poor measure of true kidney function during critical illness: in a prospective study with iohexol clearance, eGFR-creatinine overestimated measured GFR roughly two-fold after prolonged ICU stay, diverging progressively with muscle loss [K1]. This creates a specific, testable mechanism for a clinical blind spot: creatinine may fall (signalling "recovery") while true kidney function and urine output remain impaired. The expert seed asks whether patients whose creatinine falls without improvement in urine output remain at high risk. No standardized definition of renal recovery exists [K2], and current large-cohort AKI work in MIMIC-IV focuses on predicting RRT initiation rather than on the recovery stage [K3].

**Unresolved claim to test (H1):** Among ICU patients who meet creatinine-defined AKI, those whose creatinine has returned to near-baseline at a fixed landmark but who remain oliguric ("discordant recovery") have higher risk of subsequent in-hospital death, post-landmark RRT initiation, and hospital length of stay than patients with concordant recovery (creatinine-recovered AND non-oliguric), after adjustment for severity, fluid balance and diuretic exposure.

**Leading rival explanations (must be addressed):** (a) residual confounding by illness severity (sicker patients stay oliguric; severity not fully captured by measured covariates); (b) diuretic use masking oliguria in the concordant group or causing creatinine rise in others; (c) fluid overload lowering creatinine by dilution independent of kidney function; (d) measurement artifact: urine output charting gaps interpreted as oliguria; (e) reverse causation (pre-terminal oliguria). The design below distinguishes these as far as the data allow and labels what it cannot.

**What each outcome would mean.** Supportive (discordant group has clearly elevated adjusted risk): creatinine-only recovery assessment is unsafe as a stand-alone reassurance signal; recovery phenotyping should incorporate urine output — changes how recovery is defined in AKI research [K2] and how clinicians weigh "recovered" AKI at handoffs. Adverse (no excess risk after adjustment): persistent oliguria after creatinine recovery is benign tolerance/hemodynamic state rather than occult kidney dysfunction — also useful, since it would justify not escalating monitoring for these patients. Inconclusive (small discordant group or wide CIs): no change to current practice; methodological note only.

**Clinical importance:** AKI affects a large fraction of ICU patients; post-AKI decisions (monitoring intensity, fluid/liberalization, discharge readiness, nephrology follow-up) currently key off creatinine. A cheap, universally charted marker (urine output) that flags a high-risk "recovered" subgroup would directly inform monitoring decisions. What this study cannot do: MIMIC-IV has no measured GFR or cystatin C, so we identify *suspected* discordant recovery only [K1]; causal claims about intervening on oliguria require prospective/experimental data.

## 3. Design

**Population:** adult (≥18) ICU stays with KDIGO creatinine-defined AKI within the first 7 ICU days (creatinine rise ≥0.3 mg/dL/48h or ≥1.5× baseline; baseline = lowest creatinine from 7 days before admission through AKI onset). Exclusions: RRT before landmark, <48h ICU stay, missing urine-output charting (no UO records in 24h before landmark), metastatic-cancer-only stays not excluded (sensitivity analysis).

**Landmark (t=0):** 72h after first AKI criterion. Exposure classified using only data ≤t0:
- Creatinine-recovered: creatinine at t0 ≤ baseline +0.3 mg/dL AND ≤1.25× baseline (alternative threshold ≤1.5× in sensitivity).
- Persistent oliguria: mean urine output <0.3 mL/kg/h over the 12h before t0 (weight from admissions/echo charted weight; kg available in chartevents — enumerate at run time; if missing, use recorded weight else exclude).
Groups: A = concordant recovery (recovered, not oliguric); B = discordant recovery (recovered, oliguric); C = non-recovered (reference, any UO status; analyzed separately as expected high-risk).

**Outcomes (after t0 only):** primary = in-hospital mortality (admissions.hospital_expire_flag / deathtime after t0); secondary = 28-day mortality (patients.dod), post-landmark RRT initiation (procedureevents/procedures_icd), ICU and hospital LOS, and a kidney composite (RRT or recurrent creatinine-based AKI stage ≥1 after t0).

**Analysis:** multivariable logistic regression for in-hospital death (and Cox for time-to-event outcomes with day-level granularity) comparing B vs A, adjusted for: age, sex, baseline creatinine, peak AKI stage (creatinine and UO criteria separately), SOFA-proxy components available at t0 (vasopressor exposure from inputevents, mechanical ventilation from procedureevents/chartevents, bilirubin/platelet/paO2 labs), sepsis (diagnoses_icd ICD-9/10 codes at run time), cumulative fluid balance and loop diuretic dose 0–72h, and charting density (UO record count, to address artifact (d)). Report adjusted OR/HR with 95% CI. **Falsification criteria:** supportive if adjusted OR for death (B vs A) has lower 95% CI >1.15 with ≥250 discordant-recovery patients; adverse if CI excludes meaningful harm (upper CI <1.15) on both primary and the kidney composite; inconclusive otherwise (n<250 or CI spanning 0.8–1.5). No effect of the *dilution* control (see below) on the direction of the estimate.

**Method comparison (baseline vs substantive alternative):**
- Baseline: single-window phenotype + logistic/Cox as above. Simple, prespecified, directly interpretable.
- Alternative 1 (substantive, mechanistic-descriptive): group-based trajectory modeling of hourly urine output from AKI onset to t0 (and to t0+72h in a descriptive extension). This answers something the baseline loses: whether risk tracks *cumulative/trajectory* oliguria burden rather than current status — i.e., whether "discordance" is a state or a process. If trajectory classes (recovering vs persistent-low UO) predict outcomes better than the single-window phenotype, the marker is the dynamics, not the cross-section; if not, the simpler phenotype suffices.
- Alternative 2: inverse-probability-of-treatment weighting for loop diuretic exposure to address rival (b).
Selection: primary analysis stays with the baseline phenotype (interpretability, prespecification); trajectory modeling and IPTW are secondary, exploratory analyses in the same cohort, CPU-only (sklearn/lifelines/statsmodels; no GPU needed; estimated <2 CPU-hours on the prepared Parquet views).

**Leakage control:** all exposure variables computed strictly ≤ t0; outcomes strictly > t0; baseline creatinine window ends before AKI onset; one ICU stay per hospitalization selected by earliest intime with cohort-eligibility to avoid within-admission duplication (sensitivity: all stays with robust SE).

## 4. Claim scope and limits

- MIMIC-IV is single-center (Beth Israel); results may not transport.
- No measured GFR/cystatin C → "suspected" discordant recovery only [K1]; the mechanistic reading (muscle-loss-driven creatinine fall) is plausible but not directly verifiable here.
- Urine output charting gaps and catheter timing are measurement limitations; mitigated by the charting-density covariate and exclusion rule, not eliminated.
- This is an observational association; it cannot establish that treating oliguria in "recovered" patients improves outcomes — that needs prospective/experimental evidence.
- Novelty is bounded by a non-exhaustive literature search this episode; [K2] shows recovery definition is unsettled and [K3] shows MIMIC-IV AKI work concentrated on RRT prediction; a dedicated oliguria-after-creatinine-recovery cohort study in MIMIC-IV was not identified in the bounded search.
