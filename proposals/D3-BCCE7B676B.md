# Directional macrophage-to-fibroblast communication in rheumatoid arthritis

## Question

Does macrophage-derived inflammatory signaling causally drive fibroblast activation in rheumatoid arthritis, or is the observed macrophage-fibroblast co-expression pattern better explained by a shared disease state, donor-specific biology, or fibroblast-autonomous activation?

## Clinical importance

Fibroblast activation is central to rheumatoid arthritis (RA) pathogenesis, driving synovial inflammation, cartilage destruction, and bone erosion. Macrophages and fibroblasts communicate bidirectionally in the synovial microenvironment. If a macrophage-conditioned state has a robust, donor-aware fibroblast response, it supports a testable intervention point for macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk. If the signal is donor-specific or absent after paired comparison, it argues against treating observed macrophage-fibroblast co-expression as a directional mechanism.

The clinical consequence is significant: if macrophage-derived inflammatory signaling is a primary driver of fibroblast activation, then targeting macrophage-fibroblast communication could provide a new therapeutic strategy for RA, particularly for patients who do not respond to current anti-IL-6 or anti-TNF therapies.

## Evidence and hypothesis

The available datasets provide three complementary views of macrophage-fibroblast interaction:

1. **E-MTAB-8316**: Four FLS donor clusters with six nested monocyte donors and twelve condition samples. This dataset supports donor-aware paired comparisons of macrophage-conditioned versus unconditioned fibroblast states.

2. **E-MTAB-8873**: Targeted assay with 445 measured feature columns. Provides macrophage and fibroblast expression in a targeted panel, enabling ligand-receptor expression analysis.

3. **GSE95588**: Coculture expression data with duplicate and date-like gene labels that require feature-map resolution before naming targets.

The hypothesis is that macrophage-derived inflammatory signaling drives fibroblast activation in a donor-robust manner. The strongest rival explanation is that the observed co-expression pattern reflects a shared disease state or donor-specific biology rather than a directional signal.

## Falsifiable test

Donor-aware paired comparison of macrophage-conditioned versus unconditioned fibroblast states in E-MTAB-8316, with replication in E-MTAB-8873 and GSE95588.

- **Supportive**: Consistent donor-level direction and effect size across datasets.
- **Adverse**: No consistent direction or donor-specific reversal.
- **Inconclusive**: Insufficient donor overlap or assay-level differences.

## Limitations

The data cannot establish causality at the ligand-receptor level. The observed macrophage-fibroblast co-expression pattern may reflect a shared disease state, donor-specific biology, or fibroblast-autonomous activation rather than a directional macrophage-to-fibroblast signal.
