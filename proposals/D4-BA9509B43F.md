# Repeat metabolic change beyond current levels: a UK Biobank prospective cardiorenal trajectory experiment

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (valid; selected parent)
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot [source checksum]
Status: frozen research proposal; no scientific fit or result has been completed

## Scientific question and clinical advance

When a participant has a repeat blood-biomarker assessment, does the within-person change from the first to the second assessment improve prospective identification of people who will develop a first recorded cardiorenal/metabolic multimorbidity event, beyond age, sex and the second/current biomarker levels?

The clinical decision relevance is a monitoring question: after the repeat assessment has occurred, would trajectory information reclassify people for whom intensified prevention or closer clinical review might be considered? The study tests information available after the repeat assessment; it does not test whether ordering a repeat test changes outcomes, and it cannot recommend a monitoring interval or treatment.

The unresolved claim is incremental information in change conditional on current levels. Existing local/public evidence supports that current cardiometabolic measurements can stratify future disease, but does not establish that their within-person difference adds transportable information, nor that a predictive increment is clinically actionable. A negative result is clinically useful: current levels alone may be adequate for this operational use, while repeating assays may add noise, cost and false alerts. A positive result would justify a separately designed clinical-impact study with real measurement dates, treatment context and external validation.

Pre-specified hypothesis:

> In UKB participants with complete verified values for both field instances of the eight-marker panel and no recorded pre-index endpoint domain, adding instance-1 minus instance-0 change to age, sex and instance-1 current levels will reduce prospective 5-year prediction error for the first post-index two-domain endpoint, with a consistent direction across clinically defined domains and sensitivity cohorts.

The hypothesis is not causal and does not require all markers to move in one direction.

## Evidence boundary and semantic/timing gates

The local catalog is the binding data evidence. Its UKB convention states that field-instance arrays encode field-instance, not elapsed time; per-field labels, units and missing-value meanings are not locally supplied, and negative numeric codes must not be globally recoded. Only age field 21022 and sex field 31 have approved local metadata. Therefore no field is treated as an analyte, unit-bearing measurement, or disease-free status until the execution gate below passes.

Before any outcome-based model is fit, the solver must create a machine-readable semantic manifest for each of the eight field IDs, recording field ID, exact field label, unit, allowed value range/negative codes, instance meaning and source URL/version/hash. It must verify from a frozen UKB Showcase/dictionary source that fields 30690, 30700, 30720, 30740, 30750, 30760, 30780 and 30870 are the intended repeated blood-biochemistry measures and that instance 0 and instance 1 are comparable assays. If labels, units, instance meaning, or code handling cannot be verified, the run stops and reports “inconclusive—semantic dependency unavailable”; it must not silently substitute guessed labels or claim an analyte-specific result.

The local snapshot has no participant-level blood-draw date columns. The primary temporal interpretation is consequently administrative: current exposure is the instance-1 value, change is instance 1 minus instance 0, and prospective follow-up begins at the fixed boundary 2014-01-01. This boundary is after the reported repeat-assessment era but is not an individual draw date and must not be described as one. Sensitivity analyses may use an explicitly declared alternative boundary only as robustness checks; they do not alter the frozen primary estimand. Participants whose qualifying endpoint date is on or before 2013-12-31 are excluded from the primary incident analysis.

## Cohort, endpoint and follow-up

1. Read only the specified columns from the three required source files and join by eid. Assert one row per eid and agreement of any overlapping fields.
2. Apply the semantic manifest and field-specific missing-value policy.
3. Keep eids with all 16 exposure cells and valid age/sex.
4. Construct three diagnosis domains from each paired 41270/41280 record:
   - metabolic: ICD-10 E10, E11, E12, E13, E14;
   - renal: N18, N19;
   - cardiovascular: I20, I21, I22, I23, I24, I25, I50.
5. Exclude anyone with any qualifying domain recorded on or before 2013-12-31. This is “no recorded pre-index endpoint domain,” not disease-free status.
6. For each remaining participant, order valid post-index qualifying records by date (ties are handled deterministically as the same date). The primary event time is the earliest date by which two distinct domains have been observed cumulatively. A participant with only one domain is a non-event for the composite unless a later second domain appears.
7. Follow from 2014-01-01 to the primary event, death, the last defensible outcome observation, or 2018-12-31 for the 5-year estimand, whichever comes first. The 10-year endpoint is secondary and uses 2023-12-31 if outcome observation supports that boundary. A date after the administrative boundary but before the index is impossible under valid parsing and is a data-quality failure.
8. For the primary 5-year binary target, define Y5=1 when the composite occurs by 2018-12-31; death before the composite is a competing event and therefore Y5=0 for the event-incidence target, while administrative loss of outcome observation is censored and handled with prespecified inverse-probability-of-censoring weights if estimable. The primary time-to-event model is cause-specific with death as a competing event; report a Fine-Gray/cumulative-incidence sensitivity only if death dates pass semantic and date gates. Do not call this an all-cause risk or a causal effect.

Secondary outcomes are first post-index event in each domain, first single-domain event, and 10-year composite risk. A cancer endpoint using 40005-0.i/40006-0.i is optional negative-control analysis only after semantics pass verification.

## Estimand and clinical decision relevance

The primary estimand is the population-average incremental 5-year prediction error of adding change to current levels:

Delta_Brier = E[(Y5 - pM1)^2 - (Y5 - pM0)^2]

where Y5 is the prespecified 5-year event-incidence indicator (death before the composite is a competing non-event and administrative loss is handled by prespecified censoring weights), pM0 is risk from current levels plus age/sex, and pM1 is risk from the same inputs plus the eight changes. Negative values mean lower error with change. Estimate it only with strictly out-of-fold predictions from a participant-level five-fold split fixed before fitting, and report a 95% participant-cluster bootstrap interval. The primary hypothesis test is whether Delta_Brier is below zero, accompanied by calibration; statistical significance without meaningful calibration improvement is not treated as clinical value.

The clinically interpretable decision analysis is secondary but required: compare M0 and M1 net benefit at predeclared 5%, 10% and 20% predicted-risk thresholds for considering intensified prevention/review. These are analytical thresholds, not validated UK clinical action thresholds. Report threshold-specific net-benefit differences and bootstrap intervals, and require clinical expert review to map any threshold to a real action before a monitoring recommendation. Also report calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, time-dependent AUC and average precision. Do not use reclassification indices as the primary evidence.

The survival estimand is secondary: adjusted joint association of the change block in a cause-specific Cox model conditional on current levels, age and sex, with robust uncertainty and a prespecified joint test. It is an association in an administratively defined cohort, not a causal effect.

## Models, alternatives and fold-safe analysis

All models use exactly the same eligible participants, index, outcomes and fixed five-fold assignment, generated from a documented hash of eid. The split is patient-level and created before preprocessing. All imputation, transformations, standardization, tuning and feature selection occur inside training folds; out-of-fold predictions are never used to fit another model or choose a threshold.

M0 — simple current-level clinical baseline:

- Inputs: age 21022-0.0, sex 31-0.0, and the eight instance-1 fields.
- Fit a prespecified-ridge logistic model for 5-year risk and a cause-specific Cox model for time-to-event. Standardization uses training-fold parameters.
- This estimates what can be learned from current repeat-assessment values, without trajectory information.

M1 — simple longitudinal increment:

- Inputs: all M0 inputs plus eight changes, field-1.0 minus field-0.0, with field-specific verified units and training-fold standardization.
- Fit the same model family and regularization protocol as M0.
- The primary inferential object is the joint change block, not eight uncorrected marker claims. Report marker-level coefficients descriptively with multiplicity control.
- This is the minimum scientifically adequate test of the hypothesis and remains the primary comparison.

M2 — substantive learned alternative:

- Inputs: M0 inputs, eight changes, age and sex, plus only predeclared current-by-change interactions; no outcome-derived variables.
- Fit gradient-boosted logistic risk models for 5- and 10-year outcomes inside each training fold, with hyperparameters tuned only by an inner training split or nested cross-validation. Use monotonic constraints only if justified by verified measurement semantics; otherwise do not impose them.
- Evaluate with the same held-out Brier, calibration, AUC, average precision and decision-curve metrics as M0/M1. Use grouped feature importance for current versus change, and predeclared accumulated-local-effect summaries for trajectory patterns. These summaries are descriptive and must be generated without inspecting held-out outcomes.
- Treat M2 as scientifically useful only if a nonlinear or discordant trajectory pattern is stable across folds and at least one domain-specific endpoint, with uncertainty—not merely because its AUC is slightly higher.

The alternative was selected because additive M1 can miss threshold effects and discordance, such as worsening renal markers at stable glycaemia or lipid deterioration despite a stable current value; those patterns bear directly on whether a clinician would regard change as actionable. A latent Gaussian-process/transformer trajectory model is not selected: with only two locally encoded instances and no participant-level dates, it would add assumptions about smooth timing that this snapshot cannot identify. A mechanistic signed-deterioration score is retained as a future sensitivity branch, not a primary method, because two points and missing assay metadata do not establish turning points or biological comparability.

## Falsification and interpretation

Hard checks:

- semantic manifest and field-specific missingness must pass before fitting;
- exact eid joins, paired diagnosis/date positions and date parsing must pass;
- no qualifying endpoint date on or before 2013-12-31 enters incident follow-up;
- no outcome field enters exposure preprocessing;
- fold assignment, imputation, standardization and tuning are training-fold-only;
- permuting change vectors within age/sex strata while preserving current levels should remove the claimed incremental signal;
- a negative temporal control asks whether nominal “future” change predicts a pre-index domain after current levels; a material signal indicates temporal/selection leakage or a non-incident phenotype;
- repeat the primary comparison with a verified missingness-aware sensitivity and a competing-risk sensitivity, without changing the primary cohort after seeing results.

Supportive result: Delta_Brier is below zero with a reproducible interval, M1 improves calibration and/or decision-curve net benefit at a prespecified threshold without deterioration elsewhere, the joint change association has stable direction, and M2 reveals replicated nonlinear/discordant patterns. This supports incremental prognostic information for this operational repeat-assessment phenotype. It does not establish that a repeat test should be ordered, that an intervention improves outcomes, or that the pattern transports outside UKB.

Adverse result: Delta_Brier is near zero or above zero, calibration/net benefit does not improve, the joint change block is unstable, the permutation control retains the apparent increment, or the negative temporal control predicts pre-index disease. This falsifies the added-information claim for the specified phenotype and favors current levels alone for this use.

Inconclusive result: semantic verification fails; field units or assay comparability remain unknown; the final endpoint count is sparse; censoring is not estimable; intervals are wide; complete-case selection changes the result materially; folds or domain definitions disagree; or the administrative timing boundary cannot be defended. This snapshot then cannot resolve the question. It is not evidence that biomarker trajectories lack clinical value.

## Missing evidence and claim limits

The experiment cannot establish individual blood-draw dates, the true interval between instances, assay batch/quality, fasting status, primary-care or self-report disease history, medication/treatment changes, adjudicated CKD/heart-failure status, acute kidney injury episodes, urine output, clinical decision thresholds, benefit from repeat testing, causality, or external transportability. Recorded inpatient diagnosis arrays are proxies for coded events, not adjudicated disease onset. Clinical adjudication is required to interpret diagnosis validity and to decide whether a risk threshold corresponds to a real monitoring or prevention action. An external cohort and prospective impact study are required for stronger clinical claims.
