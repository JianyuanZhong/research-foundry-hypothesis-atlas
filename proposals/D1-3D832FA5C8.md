# Testing-practice dependence of early ICU mortality models across eICU hospitals

## Status and scientific deliverable

This is a fully specified observational prediction/transport experiment, not an executed model result. It is a substantive repair of expert seed 02, parent `[prior hypothesis]`. The prior MAP/AKI candidate is transition context only and is not ancestry.

At the 24-hour ICU landmark, the future solver must newly: (1) construct and audit the eligible eICU cohort; (2) fit a simple physiology-value-only model (P0), an otherwise matched observation-process-augmented model (P1), and a masked learned temporal model (T1); (3) evaluate all three on identical internal patient holdouts and previously unseen hospitals; (4) run controlled value-preserving process perturbations and synthetic missingness shifts; and (5) report discrimination, calibration, clinical net benefit, heterogeneity, uncertainty, and result-linked interpretations.

Completion requires the declared cohort flow, feature/unit audit, frozen split manifest, fitted-model manifests, hospital-held-out predictions, per-hospital and pooled metrics, paired hospital-bootstrap intervals, calibration and decision curves, perturbation/ablation outputs, subgroup diagnostics, and a machine-readable claim-to-output table. Confirmation of the hypothesis is neither required nor rewarded.

## Scientific opening, strongest supported claim, and unresolved claim

The strongest claim already supported by inspected evidence is that EHR laboratory observation is not a neutral window onto physiology: laboratory presence, frequency, and timing carry outcome-associated healthcare-process information [K1]. Across eICU hospitals, mortality models have also shown variable discrimination and calibration under hospital-to-hospital transfer [K2]. A learned recurrent model can explicitly use masks and elapsed times and improved mortality prediction in MIMIC-III/PhysioNet experiments, but that work did not test whether the added missingness signal transports across eICU hospitals [K3].

These findings do **not** establish that testing practice causes death, that missingness-aware models are necessarily less transportable, or that a model relying on observation-process signal is clinically harmful.

The unresolved primary hypothesis is:

> Among adult first-unit ICU stays alive and still hospitalized at hour 24, laboratory presence/timing/count information will improve prediction of death in hospital during the next seven days on patient-held-out data from represented hospitals, but the gain will attenuate or reverse—and calibration or net benefit will worsen—when the same frozen model is applied to whole unseen hospitals, beyond the change observed for an otherwise matched value-only model.

The leading explanation is **shared clinician concern**: clinicians test sicker patients more often, so observation-process features add a genuine, broadly transportable proxy for latent severity. The strongest rival is **hospital-specific measurement policy**: local ordering sets, batching, interfaces, staffing, and workflows create predictive shortcuts that look useful internally but shift across hospitals. The explanations make different predictions. Shared concern predicts stable P1/T1 gains after whole-hospital transport and sensitivity mainly to within-hospital patient-level process shuffling. Local policy predicts a positive transport penalty for process-aware models, hospital identifiability from process features, and prediction changes under cross-hospital process swaps or hospital-template missingness even when the value representation is held fixed.

The substantive advance over inspected work is a paired decomposition on the same eICU landmark population: value-only versus explicit process signal versus learned temporal value/process structure, evaluated at the hospital—not random row—transport boundary and challenged with prespecified process-channel interventions. This is a predictive/descriptive study of reliance and transport, not a causal effect of testing.

## Clinical decision, population, landmark, and outcome

**Decision use-case.** At ICU minute 1,440, identify patients whose risk of death in hospital over the next seven days could justify closer senior review, monitoring, or goals-of-care assessment. Because alerts consume clinical attention, calibration and decision-curve net benefit are co-primary clinical evaluations; AUROC alone cannot establish usefulness.

**Unit of analysis.** A qualifying first ICU unit visit within a hospital encounter (`unitvisitnumber == 1`). Multiple encounters for the same `uniquepid` may remain but are always assigned together. Patients appearing in more than one `hospitalid` are excluded before hospital eligibility and splitting, preventing patient leakage across the hospital boundary.

**Inclusion.**

- Age at least 18; map the documented category `"> 89"` to 90 for eligibility and modeling.
- Nonmissing `hospitalid`.
- `unitvisitnumber == 1`.
- Alive and still in the indexed ICU strictly after minute 1,440: `unitdischargeoffset > 1440`.
- Still in the hospital strictly after minute 1,440: `hospitaldischargeoffset > 1440`.
- Known `hospitaldischargestatus` in `{"Alive","Expired"}`.
- Hospital has at least 500 qualifying stays and at least 20 primary events after removal of cross-hospital patients.

No complete-laboratory requirement is imposed in the primary cohort because that would condition on the process under study.

**Exposure window.** ICU offsets ([0,1440)) minutes only. Rows at or after minute 1,440 are unavailable to predictors. Negative pre-ICU offsets are excluded.

**Primary outcome.** Seven-day post-landmark in-hospital death: `hospitaldischargestatus == "Expired"` and `hospitaldischargeoffset` in ((1440,11520]), where 11,520 is 24 hours plus seven days from ICU admission. Alive hospital discharge before the horizon is a non-event; death after the horizon is a non-event for this horizon. This label does not capture death after discharge.

**Secondary horizons/outcomes.**

- Death in hospital in ((1440,4320]), the next 48 hours.
- Eventual in-hospital death after the landmark, explicitly labeled as a different, discharge-dependent endpoint.
- Competing-event description of alive hospital discharge by 48 hours and seven days; a cause-specific/Fine–Gray sensitivity may describe death versus alive discharge, but it cannot replace the fixed primary binary estimand.

## Feature construction and prevention of process leakage

Use only training-fold statistics for unit rules, clipping, scaling, imputation, tuning, and calibration.

**Shared static block:** age, gender, ethnicity, admission weight, ICU type, and admission source. Missing categorical values receive an explicit `unknown` level shared by all models.

**Physiology value tensor:** map the 12 labs into four fixed six-hour bins and the four periodic vitals into 24 fixed hourly bins. For duplicate values in a bin, use the median after unit harmonization. Empty cells are multiply imputed from training-hospital value distributions conditional on static variables and available value channels, without giving the imputer or downstream P0 a mask, count, timestamp, hospital identifier, unit-interface string, or outcome. Average P0 predictions over five fixed imputation draws. This avoids the exact training median acting as an implicit missingness flag. P0 receives prespecified first/last, minimum, maximum, and robust slope summaries of the dense imputed trajectories plus the static block. The imputed tensor is frozen before any process-channel perturbation.

**Observation-process block:** for each lab, presence in each six-hour bin, total count, first and last offset, time from last result to landmark, median/maximum inter-result gap when defined, and repeated-test indicator; total lab rows, unique lab names, number of co-ordered panel members in each 15-minute batch, and panel-completeness pattern. Features from revised offsets, interface names, raw hospital IDs, or hospital metadata are excluded from primary predictors. P1 adds this block to the exact P0 design matrix.

A mask-leakage audit must test whether the imputed P0 tensor predicts original masks. If the held-out mask AUC materially exceeds 0.55, repeat with posterior predictive draws and remove distributional artifacts before outcome fitting. This is a preprocessing validity check, not a favorable-result search.

## Three models on identical frozen splits

### P0 — simple value-only baseline

Ridge-penalized logistic regression on the shared static block and dense value summaries. Continuous predictors are standardized and truncated with training-only rules; nonlinear age and value terms use prespecified three-knot restricted cubic splines. The ridge parameter is selected by development-set log loss. No observation mask, count, recency, timestamp, interface, hospital identifier, or APACHE-derived score enters P0.

### P1 — explicit observation-process augmentation

The same ridge-logistic specification, target, rows, imputation draws, static/value terms, tuning criterion, and split as P0, plus the observation-process block. The paired P1–P0 contrast isolates the incremental predictive reliance on testing behavior within the limits of residual severity and measurement error.

### T1 — substantive masked temporal alternative

A modest GRU-D-style model on the 24-hour trajectory: 24 hourly bins, the same standardized lab/vital values, binary masks, and elapsed-time-since-observation channels, with a 64-unit recurrent layer, 0.2 dropout, and a static-feature branch. Train binary cross-entropy with Adam, early stopping on validation log loss, class-balanced minibatches without changing probability calibration, and five fixed seeds; ensemble predictions by arithmetic mean before calibration.

T1 is scientifically substantive because P1 collapses order, timing, and nonlinear co-ordering into hand-built summaries. T1 can reveal whether an ordered clinician-response trajectory—such as repeated measurements following unstable values—is shared across hospitals or whether temporal mask dynamics are more site-specific. Required T1 ablations freeze the dense value tensor and remove or swap masks/time-gap channels; this tests information content rather than celebrating a small predictive gain. A larger transformer is deferred because 24 bins and approximately 77,000 stays do not establish a scientific need for greater capacity. Revisit it only if T1 underfits reproducible long-range structure after the prespecified ablations.

## Frozen hospital-held-out evaluation

Create five deterministic outer hospital folds, balanced without outcomes at the patient level using hospital `region`, `teachingstatus`, `numbedscategory`, and qualifying-stay count; record the exact hospital IDs and seed in `split-manifest.json`. Every hospital is an untouched external test site exactly once.

For each outer fold:

1. Hold out all stays from its hospitals.
2. In the remaining hospitals, assign all encounters for each `uniquepid` by stable hash to development (70%), calibration (15%), or internal test (15%), preserving representation from each training hospital.
3. Tune and fit only on development patients. Use calibration patients for one shared logistic recalibration protocol per model (intercept and slope). Do not recalibrate on held-out hospitals.
4. Evaluate calibrated probabilities on the internal patient test and external hospital test. The same eligible rows and outcome labels are used for P0, P1, and T1.
5. Do not inspect outer-test outcomes for feature selection, thresholds, model choice, or early stopping.

Primary metrics are mean log loss and its paired P1–P0 and T1–P0 changes. Report Brier score, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, integrated calibration index, AUROC, AUPRC, and decision-curve net benefit at 5%, 10%, and 20% risk thresholds. The 5% and 10% thresholds are primary decision thresholds; 20% is a high-risk secondary threshold. Report sensitivity, alerts per 100, positive predictive value, and deaths captured at each threshold.

Estimate 95% intervals with 1,000 paired bootstrap replicates resampling hospitals, then patients within hospital, recomputing metrics from frozen predictions. Report per-hospital effects and random-effects heterogeneity; do not hide adverse hospitals in a pooled average. Sex, age band, and recorded ethnicity calibration/false-negative differences are safety diagnostics, not claims of biological group differences.

## Primary estimand and controlled perturbations

Let (Delta_m^I = LL_m^I-LL_{P0}^I) and (Delta_m^E = LL_m^E-LL_{P0}^E), where lower log loss is better and (I/E) denote internal-patient and external-hospital tests. The primary reliance/transport estimand for P1 is:

[
D_{P1}=Delta_{P1}^E-Delta_{P1}^I.
]

Positive (D) means the incremental process-model performance deteriorates more at the hospital boundary than the value-only baseline. Compute the analogous (D_{T1}) as secondary.

Run every perturbation only after the unperturbed analysis is frozen:

1. **Within-hospital process shuffle (value preserving):** within external hospital, P0-risk decile, ICU type, and primary outcome-blinded strata, permute the complete process feature/mask-gap channels across patients while keeping static data, dense imputed values, outcomes, and hospital marginals fixed. A large decrement indicates patient-specific clinician-concern signal but does not identify causality.
2. **Cross-hospital policy swap (value preserving):** match donors from another external hospital on P0 risk, age band, ICU type, and sex, then swap only process/mask-gap channels. Values and outcomes remain fixed. Greater sensitivity than within-hospital shuffle supports site-specific policy dependence.
3. **Hospital-policy normalization (diagnostic):** map each held-out hospital's process features to pooled training-hospital quantiles using its unlabeled process distribution. This is an oracle/transductive diagnostic, not the zero-shot deployment model. Attenuation of (D) suggests process-distribution shift; it does not prove which workflow caused it.
4. **Complete-panel restriction:** repeat on stays with all 12 fixed labs observed at least once in ([0,1440)). Presence is then fixed and only repeat frequency/timing/co-ordering vary. This addresses the rival that results merely reflect which physiology was measured.
5. **Controlled MCAR deletion:** using fixed masks shared across models, delete 10% and 30% of observed lab cells in the external set, re-impute from training rules, and update process channels. This benchmarks generic robustness to information loss.
6. **Hospital-template MNAR deletion:** estimate per-lab/per-hour deletion propensities from a donor hospital's mask distribution within P0-risk deciles, apply those masks to another hospital with fixed random seeds, and update all models identically. Excess P1/T1 degradation relative to P0 distinguishes policy-shaped missingness sensitivity from generic missingness.
7. **Interface/unit and severity rivals:** exclude hospitals with unresolved unit/range anomalies; add the APACHE APS block to all models in a matched sensitivity; exclude/stratify first-day EOL records. Conclusions must be revised if process reliance disappears after severity adjustment or is confined to EOL/interface artifacts.

Synthetic perturbations are model probes, not interventions on clinical care, and cannot estimate what would happen if clinicians ordered fewer tests.

## Prespecified interpretation and falsification

The hypothesis receives **support** only if all of the following hold:

1. P1 improves internal log loss over P0 by at least 0.002 and its paired 95% CI excludes no improvement.
2. (D_{P1}) is at least 0.005 and its paired hospital-bootstrap 95% CI is entirely above zero.
3. On unseen hospitals, P1 is clinically adverse relative to P0 at 5% or 10% risk: either net benefit is lower by at least 0.005 with a CI excluding zero (0.5 net true-positive equivalents per 100 patients), or calibration slope moves at least 0.10 farther from 1 with a CI excluding no worsening.
4. Cross-hospital process swap or hospital-template MNAR deletion perturbs P1/T1 more than P0, and the effect is larger than the matched within-hospital shuffle or attenuates with process normalization.
5. The direction is not confined to one hospital, EOL documentation, unresolved units, or the APACHE severity sensitivity.

This would support the bounded claim that observation-process reliance harms zero-shot transport for this endpoint and era; it would not show that missingness-aware modeling is always harmful or that changing testing changes mortality.

Evidence is **adverse to the hypothesis** if P1 has no internal gain, or if its gain and calibration/net benefit remain stable on unseen hospitals with (D_{P1}<0.005), while cross-hospital process perturbations are no more damaging than within-hospital shuffles. Stable T1 gains after process-channel ablation would favor transportable temporal physiology; stable gains that vanish after within-hospital process shuffle would favor shared clinician concern. Either adverse result is scientifically useful.

Evidence is **inconclusive** if intervals include both the prespecified supportive and adverse regions; fewer than 30 outcome-supported external hospitals survive final QC; process/value models cannot be kept on identical rows; imputation leaks masks after repair; decision thresholds yield too few alerts/events; or results are dominated by EOL, unit, discharge, or interface artifacts. An imprecise null does not refute process dependence. No alternate subgroup or outcome may replace the primary analysis after test inspection.

## Bounded full-data feasibility evidence

These are executed support audits, not fitted model results.

A full scan of `patient.csv.gz` found 109,064 adult first-unit stays alive/in ICU and hospital after hour 24 with known hospital status, 6,447 next-seven-day deaths, and 207 hospitals before cross-hospital patient and hospital-support restrictions. Removing patients recorded at more than one hospital, then requiring at least 500 stays and 20 events, leaves **76,765 stays, 4,733 events, and 64 hospitals** (minimum retained hospital support: 512 stays and 20 events).

A full `lab.csv.gz` scan in the closely matched audited landmark cohort found 4,493,949 first-day lab rows; median 45 rows and 32 unique lab names per stay, with 1,034 stays having no lab row under the audit's slightly broader status rule. In the final cross-hospital-patient-excluded, 64-hospital population, **60,169 stays, 3,952 events, and 61 hospitals** had all 12 fixed labs observed at least once, supporting the complete-panel perturbation. Among those stays, 37%–54% had repeat measurements for each panel analyte. Common system units were directly observed (for example creatinine/BUN/glucose in mg/dL, sodium/potassium/chloride/bicarbonate in mmol/L, Hgb in g/dL, Hct in %, and platelet/WBC counts in K/mcL), while interface variants justify explicit harmonization.

An independent full scan of `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz` in a broader eligible cohort found first-day heart rate in 98.5%, SaO2 in 97.8%, respiration in 91.2%, but invasive systemic mean pressure in only 26.2%, motivating its sensitivity-only status. The managed audit used four CPUs and 32 GiB and completed in 182 seconds for patient/lab/vital support. These counts establish computability and guide QC; they do not establish any model performance or scientific conclusion.

## Limits and claims requiring more evidence

The experiment can establish predictive reliance, transport performance, calibration, net benefit under specified thresholds, and sensitivity to synthetic observation-process shifts. It cannot show that ordering tests causes outcomes, identify clinician intent, rank hospital quality, prove a biological mechanism, validate safe deployment, or estimate the effect of changing a testing policy. Hospital identity may proxy patient mix, staffing, vendor configuration, or unrecorded treatment. eICU reflects 2014–2015 participating hospitals and one tele-ICU ecosystem; modern external validation is required. Clinical experts must adjudicate variable ranges, EOL semantics, alert thresholds, and whether observed harms are actionable. A prospective silent trial and then an impact study would be required before clinical use.
