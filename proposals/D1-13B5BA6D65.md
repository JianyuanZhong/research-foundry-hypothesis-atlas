# Baseline-severity-conditioned robustness of time-weighted hypotension burden for early AKI

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP), after controlling for measurement density, baseline illness severity, and after showing that the signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined)?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density. The strongest rival is that the apparent burden signal is an artifact of recording density, measurement timing, or baseline illness severity, or that it is specific to one arbitrary choice of blood-pressure summary. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Clinical importance

Early AKI in the ICU is common, associated with worse outcomes, and may be preventable if at-risk patients are identified before creatinine rises. MAP is the standard bedside summary, but it collapses duration and timing of hypotension. If a time-weighted burden measure adds prognostic information beyond MAP after controlling for severity and measurement artifacts, it could inform earlier monitoring or intervention decisions. If it does not, the simpler MAP summary remains sufficient and avoids unnecessary complexity.

## Evidence base and closest prior work

[K1] The eICU-CRD 2.0 documentation describes the available patient, vital, lab, infusion, and hospital fields and the temporal structure of the data. It supports the feasibility of constructing first-24-hour blood-pressure summaries and AKI outcomes, but it does not establish that duration adds prognostic value beyond MAP.

[K2] Jain et al. (2015) describe the epidemiology and outcomes of AKI in the ICU, including the association between AKI and mortality. This supports the clinical importance of early AKI identification but does not test whether a time-weighted hypotension burden adds information beyond MAP.

[K3] Lipman et al. (2017) review the pathophysiology and management of AKI in the ICU, including the role of hypoperfusion. This supports the biological plausibility that hypotension contributes to AKI but does not establish that duration and time-weighting add prognostic information beyond MAP after controlling for severity and measurement artifacts.

## Study population and temporal boundaries

- Population: adult ICU patients (age >= 18) in eICU-CRD 2.0 with at least one systolic and one diastolic blood-pressure measurement in the first 24 hours of ICU care.
- Temporal window: first 24 hours of ICU care for exposure; AKI within 48 hours of ICU admission for outcome.
- Exclusions: patients with fewer than 3 blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours (measurement-density threshold); patients with missing baseline creatinine; patients with missing APACHE IV and SOFA scores (sensitivity analysis includes them).

## Variables and data bindings

- Blood pressure: `vital` table, columns `systolic_bp`, `diastolic_bp`, `timestamp`, joined to `patient` on `patient_id`.
- AKI outcome: `lab` table, columns `creatinine`, `timestamp`, joined to `patient` on `patient_id`; AKI defined by KDIGO criteria (creatinine >= 2x baseline or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for >= 6 hours).
- Baseline severity: `patient` table, columns `apache4`, `sofa`, `age`, `sex`, `ethnicity`, `admission_type`, `admission_source`.
- Vasopressor exposure: `infusion` table, columns `infusion_type`, `rate`, `timestamp`, joined to `patient` on `patient_id`; vasopressor use defined as any norepinephrine, epinephrine, or vasopressin infusion in the first 24 hours.
- Hospital: `patient` table, column `hospital_id`.
- Measurement density: number of blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours, computed from `vital` table.

## Primary analysis

1. Construct the 24-hour MAP, systolic, diastolic, and combined summaries, and the prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden (e.g., area under the curve of (MAP < 65) over the first 24 hours).
2. Fit a within-hospital logistic regression model for AKI within 48 hours, with the following predictors:
   - Baseline: age, sex, ethnicity, admission type, admission source, APACHE IV (with SOFA fallback), baseline creatinine, vasopressor use.
   - Exposure: 24-hour MAP (primary) or time-weighted hypotension burden (alternative).
   - Measurement density: number of blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours.
   - Hospital: fixed effects or hospital-level random intercepts.
3. Compare the incremental AUC and calibration (e.g., Brier score, calibration-in-the-large, calibration-slope) of the model with time-weighted hypotension burden versus the model with 24-hour MAP, after adjustment for the above predictors.
4. Repeat the analysis for each blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined) to test robustness to the choice of summary.
5. Perform severity stratum analysis: repeat the primary analysis in low (APACHE IV < 20), moderate (APACHE IV 20-30), and high (APACHE IV > 30) severity strata.
6. Perform missing-severity sensitivity analysis: repeat the primary analysis excluding patients with missing APACHE IV and SOFA scores.

## Falsification criteria

- The primary result is considered adverse if the time-weighted hypotension burden does not improve AUC or calibration beyond the 24-hour MAP after adjustment for baseline severity, measurement density, vasopressor use, baseline creatinine, and hospital.
- The result is considered adverse if the signal is absent in the prespecified low- and moderate-severity strata, suggesting that the apparent burden signal is driven by baseline illness severity.
- The result is considered adverse if the signal is absent after excluding patients with fewer than the required measurement-density threshold, suggesting that the apparent burden signal is an artifact of recording density.
- The result is considered inconclusive if the signal is present in only one blood-pressure summary, suggesting that it is specific to an arbitrary choice of summary.

## Solver deliverable and uncertainty reporting

- Required fitted models: within-hospital logistic regression models for AKI within 48 hours, with and without time-weighted hypotension burden, adjusted for baseline severity, measurement density, vasopressor use, baseline creatinine, and hospital.
- Confidence intervals: 95% confidence intervals for AUC, Brier score, calibration-in-the-large, and calibration-slope.
- Calibration metrics: Brier score, calibration-in-the-large, calibration-slope.
- Hospital-level analysis: hospital-level AUC and calibration metrics, with a summary of heterogeneity across hospitals.

## Limitations and future work

- eICU-CRD is a retrospective observational study; causal inference is not possible.
- Measurement density is a proxy for recording practices and may not fully capture measurement timing artifacts.
- APACHE IV and SOFA scores are not available for all patients; the missing-severity sensitivity analysis addresses this but may reduce power.
- A positive result would support prospective validation of a more complex burden measure for early AKI risk stratification; a null result would weaken the claim and favor the simpler MAP summary.
