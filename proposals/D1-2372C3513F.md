# Provisional HCC hypothesis: post-operative liver injury after hepatectomy

Status: provisional draft for episode 1; not a completed study or clinical conclusion.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults undergoing liver resection in the HCC snapshot, the early post-operative trajectory of liver-related laboratory abnormalities will be associated with a clinically important deterioration signal in the subsequent 30 days, beyond baseline disease severity and operative context. The unresolved claim is whether a reproducible trajectory (rather than a single post-operative value) identifies a distinct recovery/deterioration pattern that could inform surveillance or escalation research.

This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated effect. The available evidence establishes that the snapshot contains longitudinal clinical data and that the source explicitly lacks separate lab units, validated diagnosis extraction, images, waveforms, and complete front-page/transfer content. It does not establish the proposed association or a causal mechanism.

## Clinical importance and advance

A result could distinguish a potentially actionable early warning pattern from the simpler explanation that late deterioration merely reflects baseline illness or measurement frequency. The substantive advance over a one-time post-operative marker would be a test of whether the direction and persistence of change carries information about recovery, while explicitly separating predictive association from mechanism or treatment recommendation.

## Strongest supported claim versus unresolved claim

Supported by the inspected dataset documentation: the HCC source provides encounter-linked laboratory, diagnosis, medication, procedure, examination, pathology, and clinical-document tables, with local encounter dates and patient/encounter keys. It also documents that lab numeric results have no separate unit column, vitals/transfers are nominal identifier tables, and images/raw waveforms are unavailable.

Unresolved and to be tested: whether a pre-specified early post-operative laboratory trajectory is associated with a later deterioration outcome, and whether that association survives adjustment for baseline severity, treatment, observation intensity, and competing explanations.

## Proposed computable experiment

Population: patients in the HCC snapshot with a qualifying liver-resection procedure and at least one pre-operative and one early post-operative laboratory record. The exact procedure and laboratory inclusion rules must be frozen after inspecting the table dictionaries and source headers; no cross-dataset joins are permitted.

Temporal boundaries: index procedure date/time from the procedure table; pre-operative window from the documented encounter dates; early post-operative trajectory over days 0–7; outcome window days 8–30. Use only records occurring before each prediction horizon. If the source lacks sufficient timestamps to define these intervals, the result is inconclusive rather than silently using a looser window.

Variables:
- Exposure: trajectory features from repeated laboratory results, including level, slope/change, and number of observations, with assay-specific handling because units are unavailable.
- Outcome: a prespecified deterioration signal defined from available post-index clinical records, with a transparent operational definition and sensitivity definitions. It must not be derived from future records or from the same row used as exposure.
- Covariates: baseline laboratory and diagnosis/medication/procedure context available before the exposure window, plus observation intensity and calendar/encounter structure where identifiable.
- Missingness and measurement: report assay-specific availability and do not pool incompatible assays.

Baselines and alternative:
- Simple baseline: a prespecified single post-operative marker or a regularized regression using baseline covariates and the latest pre-specified value.
- Substantive alternative: a trajectory model (for example, a time-aware model or latent trajectory analysis) fitted on the same patients and target, with patient-level temporal split and leakage-controlled validation. The alternative can reveal persistence, direction, and within-patient change that a single marker loses; it is not justified by predictive gain alone.
- Evaluation: discrimination/calibration and uncertainty on held-out patients, comparison against the baseline, and prespecified sensitivity analyses for observation intensity, assay availability, treatment, and competing diagnoses.

Falsification and interpretation:
- Supportive: the trajectory model improves the prespecified held-out evaluation and the association is directionally consistent across assay-specific sensitivity analyses, with uncertainty reported.
- Adverse: no incremental information, unstable direction, or association explained by observation intensity/selection; this would favor the single-marker or severity explanation.
- Inconclusive: insufficient repeated measures, inadequate timestamps, unresolved assay comparability, or imprecise estimates. This does not refute the hypothesis.
- A supportive association would not establish a liver-injury mechanism, causal effect, or treatment benefit. Those claims require adjudicated outcomes, validated units/assays, richer clinical context, and prospective or external validation.
