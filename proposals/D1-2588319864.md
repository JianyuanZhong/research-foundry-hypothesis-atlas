# Time-safe pathway-specific early physiology in HCC-coded inpatient care

## Audit finding and substantive successor

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent repaired the major selection problem by integrating every eligible admission into an all-admission 72-hour competing-transition estimand. Its strongest remaining scientific flaw is endpoint heterogeneity: a single procedure state `P` combines catheter-based embolization, resection/transplant, ablation, biopsy, drainage, line placement, endoscopy, and unrelated operations. A laboratory increment that predicts this composite cannot answer the clinically sharper question of whether early physiology is associated with which recorded HCC-care pathway follows.

A second, related limitation is temporal: the parent permits laboratory information up to each prediction boundary, while the procedure source contains many coarse midnight starts. Merely reporting a midnight sensitivity does not create a clean decision-to-event separation.

This child changes both the estimand and the endpoint. It tests whether assay-specific laboratory information available in the first 12 hours, with a 12-hour buffer, improves prediction of the first pathway-specific recorded transition during hours 24–72. It retains every admission by modeling early procedure/discharge/unresolved-time states explicitly, then marginalizes the later pathway distribution back to the complete eligible population. It does not interpret procedure text as treatment intent, completion, indication, or benefit.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitting and evaluating, on a frozen participant split:

1. an admission-anchored sequential competing-risk baseline (B0);
2. a matched transparent early-laboratory model (B1); and
3. an assay-specific irregular latent-state alternative (B2).

Completion is established by the frozen cohort/rule audit, exhaustive early and late state construction, locked patient-level predictions, pathway-specific cumulative incidence and proper scores with clustered uncertainty, and the prespecified falsifications. A null or inconclusive result is a valid completion; confirmation of the hypothesis is not required.

## Strongest supported claim and unresolved claim

The available snapshot supports recorded-data facts only. The encounters table has native admission/discharge times; the procedures table has `surgery`, `start time`, `end time`, and `surgery source`; the labs table has assay/result fields and native `test time`. In the bounded read-only audit using a simple HCC lexical rule and first eligible patient admission, 42,692 patient-level admissions were identified. Among their procedure rows, 6,518 patients had at least one invalid or missing procedure start and 85,337 rows had a midnight start. These figures are provisional feasibility evidence, not locked cohort results.

The procedure source also contains repeated, recognizable local strings such as `Transcatheter hepatic arterial embolization`, `arterial chemoembolization`, `TACE`, hepatic resection names, and ultrasound-guided microwave/radiofrequency ablation names. This supports testing an auditable text-defined pathway endpoint, but not a validated clinical endpoint. The source does not establish active HCC, diagnosis timing, stage, tumor burden, resectability, liver function, procedure indication, consent, scheduling, receipt, completion, response, toxicity, mortality, or outside-hospital care.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults with a first observed HCC-coded inpatient admission, does valid assay-specific laboratory history in [0,12) improve calibrated prediction of the first recorded pathway in [24,72)—embolization/TACE-like, resection/transplant-like, ablation-like, other procedure, discharge, unresolved procedure time, or no transition—beyond admission and pre-admission information, and does it add pathway discrimination rather than only procedure-occurrence prediction?

This is a prognostic recorded-care information hypothesis. It is not a treatment-effect, causal, active-disease, or clinical-utility hypothesis.

### Time origin and two-stage target

Set (t_0) to native `Admission Time`. All intervals are half-open and in elapsed hours from (t_0):

- Early window: [0,24).
- Laboratory window for the primary increment: [0,12), using only rows with parseable native `Test time < t_0+12h).
- Primary pathway window: [24,72).
- Exact boundary times belong to the later window; this convention is frozen before test prediction.

The early stage is needed to preserve the all-admission estimand. For every eligible admission, define the first early state in [0,24):

- `E_PROC`: a valid procedure start in [0,24) before discharge;
- `E_DISCH`: discharge at or before 24 hours before a valid early procedure;
- `E_UPROC`: a missing/invalid procedure start that could alter the early ordering;
- `R24`: no established early procedure, discharge, or unresolved procedure-time state.

Same-time procedure/discharge ties, multiple same-time procedures, starts before admission, starts after discharge, invalid dates, and procedures after discharge must be tabulated. The primary policy is conservative: an invalid procedure row is `E_UPROC` when its unknown time could fall before 24 hours or change ordering; an optimistic bound treats it as outside the relevant window when that is logically possible. No missing procedure time is silently coded as no procedure.

Only `R24` admissions enter the later risk set. The later state is the first applicable event in [24,72), with discharge and unresolved timing retained as competing states:

- `E`: embolization/TACE-like dictionary family;
- `S`: resection/transplant-like dictionary family;
- `A`: ablation-like dictionary family;
- `M`: one procedure text matching more than one family;
- `O`: a valid procedure in the window not matching E/S/A/M;
- `D`: discharge before a valid later procedure;
- `U`: unresolved procedure timing that could change the later ordering;
- `N72`: no established state through 72 hours.

A valid non-family procedure is `O`, not absence of care. A family event after discharge is not a later procedure event. For a valid same-time family combination, retain `M` rather than assigning an arbitrary family. If `M` is too sparse for a stable estimate it remains visible and is descriptive/inconclusive; it is never silently folded into a favorable pathway.

The primary all-admission estimand is the mean, over every eligible admission at time zero, of the sequential 72-hour terminal distribution. The early-state distribution is estimated for all admissions. The later conditional distribution is estimated among `R24`, then multiplied by the estimated all-admission probability of reaching `R24`. Report the full vector for B0, B1, and B2 and the paired B1-minus-B0 changes for `E`, `S`, `A`, `O`, `D`, `U`, and `N72`. Also report the conditional pathway distribution among `R24` as a secondary estimand.

The primary test is the paired held-out proper-score improvement for the full later competing distribution and the pathway-discrimination component: B1 must improve multiclass log score/Brier score over B0, with particular attention to whether improvement remains when `E` is distinguished from `S/A` rather than only from `O/D/N72`. Probability differences are predictions of recorded transitions, not counterfactual probabilities of receiving treatment.

## Frozen procedure-family dictionary

The dictionary is outcome labeling only; procedure text is never a prediction feature.

Use these prespecified rules:

- E: text contains `TACE`, `TAE`, `embolization`, or `chemoembolization`, and also contains `liver`, `hepatic artery`, `hepatic`, `liver`, or `artery`;
- S: text contains liver/hepatic context and `resection`, `transplant`, `resection`, or `transplant`;
- A: text contains liver/hepatic, tumor, or lesion context and `ablation`, `radiofrequency`, `microwave`, `cryotherapy`, or `ablat`;

These are deliberately auditable local lexical families, not clinical adjudication. A supportive result cannot be promoted to a claim about treatment selection.

## Matched models, split, and uncertainty

Use the catalog participant hashing and keep all encounters/modalities for a patient in one partition: buckets 0–59 fit, 60–69 preprocessing/regularization selection, 70–79 locked evaluation; 80–99 are inaccessible and are not external validation. Freeze cohort rules, dictionary, assay inclusion, transformations, state dimension, hyperparameters, and primary endpoint before test predictions.

B0 is a transparent sequential multinomial cause-specific hazard model. At the early stage it uses age, sex, admitting department, calendar era, admission time-of-day, prior encounter count, and pre-admission assay-specific nearest/count/recency/missingness summaries. At the later stage it uses the same features plus the fact of reaching `R24`; it excludes current-window laboratories, raw diagnosis text/type, procedure text, orders, medications, clinical documents, pathology, and any post-boundary field. It predicts early states and the late E/S/A/M/O/D/U/N72 states.

B1 has the identical split, targets, preprocessing, regularization and model family. It adds only the assay-specific history in [0,12): first/last valid result, within-assay change, elapsed time, count, missingness/density, and a slope when at least two native times support it. It uses no result at or after 12 hours and no outcome-derived selection. The primary estimand is the paired B1-minus-B0 score difference for the marginal all-admission terminal vector and the conditional `R24` pathway vector.

B2 is the substantive learned/mechanistic alternative. Fit an assay-specific irregular state-space model on exactly the same rows and split, with assay offsets/noise, irregular observation times, shrinkage, posterior state uncertainty, and coupled competing-risk heads for the same early/later targets. Permit two low-dimensional latent coordinates representing reserve/stress-like trajectory structure only as model parameters; never name them a validated liver or renal score. B2 can reveal nonlinear level/slope coupling and time-of-observation structure that B1's summaries lose. A count/density/missingness-only model is an additional process-information diagnostic. B2 is scientifically informative only if it improves held-out multiclass log/Brier score and calibration over B1 and its latent contribution is stable under time and label falsifications.

Compute patient-level held-out multiclass log score, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope or reliability bins, pathway-specific cumulative incidence, and observed-versus-predicted terminal vectors. Use at least 500 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples (1,000 if within budget), preserving each patient's complete sequence; report 95% intervals. Locked-prediction bootstrap is primary; refit-on-bootstrap is sensitivity. Do not use the test partition to select dictionaries, assays, latent dimension, or thresholds. Report effective support for E/S/A/M/O/D/U/N72 and mark sparse contrasts inconclusive.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Prespecify:

1. Shuffle lab times within patient and assay, then shuffle values within assay. A physiology-linked gain should attenuate; persistence points to assay identity or observation process.
2. Compare B1 with count/density/missingness-only features. A gain fully explained by testing intensity is adverse or inconclusive for laboratory physiology.
3. Permute early/later state labels within calendar era and admitting department. A surviving gain indicates pipeline leakage or documentation artifact.
4. Run a forbidden-window sentinel in which [12,24) information predicts an earlier target. It must not improve a valid time-safe target.
5. Repeat exact-boundary, midnight, same-time-tie, and conservative/optimistic unresolved-time rules. Large changes make pathway estimates inconclusive.
6. Recompute the pathway dictionary using the frozen stricter rule (requiring explicit liver/hepatic context for every family) and a frozen broad rule. A large route reversal, unmatched-string concentration, or family-overlap instability is lexical fragility.
7. Report pathway-specific results with and without `O`; `O` is never deleted from the primary competing-state vector. If removal changes the conclusion, the route result is inconclusive.
8. Compare the full early-stage all-admission distribution with the parent’s single-composite `P` result. This is a descriptive bridge, not a replacement of the new endpoint.
9. Recompute the parent's Q_full/Q_type/Q_modifier documentation contrasts only as secondary, partial-pooled, untimed documentary sensitivities. They cannot be used as admission phenotypes or prediction features.
10. Use orders, examinations, clinical documents, pathology, and medications only for secondary concordance audits. They cannot establish intent, scheduling, receipt, completion, imaging/pathology confirmation, or treatment response.

Supportive evidence requires coherent joins, valid native-time flow, an uncertainty-supported B1 improvement in the pathway vector over B0, calibrated predictions, pathway discrimination not limited to procedure-occurrence, attenuation under timestamp/label permutations, and no dominance by density or unresolved-time handling. B2 is supportive only if it adds reproducible calibrated information over B1.

Adverse evidence includes no B1 gain, gain explained by density, persistence under time/label permutation, strong dependence on midnight/unknown-time policy, or route differences driven by one lexical family. This rejects the directional recorded-care information hypothesis or identifies process/lexical artifact; it does not show that laboratory data have no clinical value.

Inconclusive evidence includes high unresolved-time mass, sparse A/M cells, unstable family dictionary, wide intervals, nonconvergence, inadequate repeated assays, or disagreement between strict and broad dictionaries. It must not be relabeled as support.

## Clinical advance and limits

The advance is a clinically sharper estimand: it asks whether early laboratory trajectories carry information about distinct locally recorded pathways, rather than about an uninterpretable aggregate procedure flag. The 12-hour-to-24-hour separation directly addresses coarse procedure timestamps, while explicit early states preserve the admission population and prevent survivor-only results from being presented as all-admission effects. A stable association could justify prospective work on time-linked pathway adjudication and whether pathway-specific review or data collection is warranted. It cannot justify assigning a treatment, triaging a patient, or claiming a benefit.

Computationally checkable claims are source/header/schema/hash provenance, joins and multiplicities, cohort flow, exact regex/dictionary audits, native-time ordering, leakage exclusion, locked predictions, proper scores, calibration, cumulative incidence, uncertainty, and falsification behavior. Additional expert review or another study is required for active-HCC confirmation, diagnosis timing, recurrence, stage, tumor burden, resectability, liver-function grade, procedure indication/intent/receipt/completion, pathology or imaging ground truth, clinical outcomes, causal effects, external-care capture, transportability, and clinical utility.

No automatic verifier can establish those clinical claims. No deployment, treatment, referral, or monitoring recommendation follows from a supportive result.
