# Renal worsening and documented feed loss during recorded insulin infusion

Status: episode-2 substantive child of the two valid episode-1 insulin-hypoglycemia proposals. This is a prognostic monitoring study in eICU, not a causal treatment-effect study.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and audit a leakage-safe six-hour person-period cohort from the frozen eICU snapshot, then fit and compare two prespecified models on exactly the same periods, outcomes, covariates, hospital split and censoring rules:

1. a transparent cause-specific pooled-logistic model estimating the renal-worsening by documented-feed-loss interaction and standardized six-hour risk contrasts; and
2. a compact time-aware GRU or temporal convolution model over the preceding 24 hours, with explicit measurement-process channels, competing-event heads, held-out-hospital predictions, calibration and feature-block ablations.

Completion requires a frozen cohort/data dictionary and flow counts; insulin/nutrition/glucose unit and label-audit tables; fitted coefficients and uncertainty for the interaction; held-out predictions and calibration for both models; risk contrasts and alert-burden summaries; hospital influence/transport estimates; renal/nutrition/observation ablations; and every prespecified falsification analysis. Completion is not a positive hypothesis test.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

Insulin-associated hypoglycemia is a consequential ICU safety event. The strongest available evidence supports only that this snapshot contains timestamped insulin-infusion updates, glucose and creatinine laboratory rows, and structured intake labels, and that renal dysfunction and loss of carbohydrate delivery are clinically plausible contexts for low glucose. The raw fields do not establish that an infusion update was administered continuously, that undocumented intake means feeding stopped, that a low lab glucose was clinically confirmed, or that any association is causal.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among adults in their first ICU stay who have a recorded insulin infusion update and at least one recent pre-index glucose, the combination of renal worsening during the preceding 24 hours and no qualifying documented nutrition delivery during the preceding 12 hours is associated with a higher probability of an incident documented glucose <70 mg/dL in the next six hours, conditional on the recent recorded insulin rate and baseline illness context; the joint contrast is greater than additivity and remains directionally transportable to hospitals not used for fitting.

The primary estimand is a standardized observational six-hour risk difference and risk ratio for the joint profile (renal delta >=0.3 mg/dL, or continuous delta in the primary model, plus no documented delivery) versus neither feature, evaluated at the empirical distribution of insulin intensity and prespecified covariates. The interaction is reported on the logit and risk-difference scales. This estimates a risk marker among periods with recorded infusion updates; it does not estimate the effect of giving insulin, stopping feeds, changing insulin dose, or increasing glucose monitoring.

The substantive advance over the seed and routine static associations is a timestamp-safe, multicenter test of whether change in renal state plus observed nutrition documentation identifies a near-term safety state at a fixed treatment-record context, while quantifying how much of the signal is explained by the fact that clinicians measure glucose or record intake differently. A positive association could justify a chart-reviewed prospective alert evaluation; it cannot set a dose or monitoring interval.

## Population, time origin and temporal boundaries

Use the eICU v2.0 frozen snapshot, with offsets in minutes from ICU admission.

Include valid adult (age >=18; retain the documented >89 category as 90) first ICU stays, choosing the smallest valid unitvisitnumber per uniquepid, with valid patientunitstayid, hospitalid, unitadmitoffset, unitdischargeoffset and unitdischargestatus. A sensitivity analysis includes later stays with patient-clustered uncertainty. Decision times are t = 24, 30, ..., 66 hours after ICU admission. A period is eligible only when an insulin infusion update occurs in [t-6,t) and at least one valid glucose result occurs on or before t. This avoids relying on early incomplete baseline physiology and ensures a recent exposure record.

End a period at the earliest of t+6, ICU discharge, or death. Death and alive discharge before the six-hour window are competing events, not hypoglycemia non-events; periods without adequate follow-up are censored. Exclude periods with an end-of-life discussion/save recorded at or before t in the primary analysis and repeat with an EOL indicator. Exclude periods within 12 hours of ICU discharge in a sensitivity analysis because disposition and reduced measurement can mimic feed loss.

No predictor may use an event after t. Do not use future glucose, future infusion updates, post-index rescue treatment, post-stay Apache outcomes, or labresultrevisedoffset as the clinical event time. The first qualifying low glucose in (t,t+6] is the primary outcome; glucose <54 mg/dL in the same window and recurrent low-glucose burden are secondary outcomes. Report the number and timing of glucose measurements in every period so ascertainment is not confused with event occurrence.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative

Freeze cohort construction, codebooks, units, split and outcome definitions before outcome modeling.

The transparent baseline is a pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific model with hospital fixed effects (or a documented random intercept if fixed effects fail), patient/stay-clustered robust uncertainty and separate death/discharge heads. Predictors are recorded insulin rate/intensity and update density, continuous creatinine delta, documented-feed-loss indicator, their prespecified interaction, prior glucose trajectory and count, diabetes/age/weight/sex/ethnicity, admission source, unit type, early severity/organ support, vasopressor context, EOL/coverage flags and time since ICU admission. Restricted splines are allowed only with knots fit in development data. Standardize to obtain the primary six-hour interaction and joint-vs-neither risk contrast, not AUROC alone.

The learned alternative is one compact one-layer GRU (hidden size 32 or 64 selected only in development) or temporal convolution over 12 one-hour bins immediately before t. It receives the same clinical sources and no future data: insulin rate/update count, glucose/creatinine/BUN/potassium and vital summaries, nutrition delivery/coverage, time-since-measurement, explicit missingness and static covariates. It has three next-window heads for hypoglycemia, death and alive discharge. Fit with patient-level splitting by uniquepid, development-hospital preprocessing only, early stopping on validation Brier score and one validation calibration step. The scientific comparison is whether ordering, lag, nonlinear renal change and observation-process information add a stable calibrated renal/feed risk contrast across held-out hospitals. A small predictive gain without this contrast or transportability is not a substantive advance.

A mechanistic glucose-insulin/renal-clearance model was considered but deferred. It could reveal latent insulin sensitivity and carbohydrate balance, but this snapshot lacks reliable administered dose, concentration and stop times, calories, dextrose rescue administration and validated physiology. A latent model fit to these proxies would not identify the proposed mechanism. Revisit it only with medication administration, feeding-stop/calorie, rescue-treatment and clinical adjudication data.

## Splits, uncertainty and analysis

Assign entire hospitals to approximately 60% development, 20% validation and 20% held-out transport folds, using only eligible-period counts and ensuring all stays for a uniquepid remain in one partition. If hospital counts are too small, report descriptive coverage and do not fabricate stable hospital estimates. A secondary within-development patient split is not a substitute for hospital holdout.

Report cohort flow, eligible periods, patients, hospitals, event counts, competing-event counts, glucose measurement density, insulin-unit composition, nutrition-label coverage and missingness by hospital before fitting. Primary metrics are standardized six-hour interaction/risk contrasts, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope and prediction intervals; AUROC/AUPRC, sensitivity, positive predictive value and alerts per 100 decision times are secondary. Use hospital-aware bootstrap for transport and patient/stay-clustered bootstrap for fitted contrasts, with the number of resamples and any small-hospital limitation recorded.

## Falsification criteria

1. Permute insulin update times within stay while preserving rates and update counts. A time-aligned association should attenuate; persistence is adverse evidence for temporal coding/trend artifact.
2. Shuffle renal/nutrition histories across decision times within hospital while preserving broad coverage. Loss of the interaction is required; persistence suggests leakage or patient-selection structure.
3. Test a pre-index placebo outcome (new <70 in (t-6,t]) after excluding prior lows. A strong effect weakens prospective interpretation.
4. Repeat with bedside-glucose-only versus laboratory-glucose-only rows and model glucose measurement count/gaps. Large disagreement or an effect fully explained by density/interface is adverse evidence for a clinical risk interpretation.
5. Repeat with strict prior-delivery requirement, complete-observable nutrition only, direct units/hr only, no carry-forward and with periods near discharge/EOL excluded. Directional stability is required for support.
6. Fit insulin-free periods with an analogous renal-by-documentation exposure placebo. Reproduction outside insulin-exposed periods is adverse.
7. Leave one hospital out at a time and compare held-out calibration with/without observation-density features. One-site dominance or failure after removing density features limits the claim to local recording practice.
8. Audit every feature timestamp and deliberately run a future-value leakage diagnostic separately; any apparent gain confined to the diagnostic cannot enter the research result.

## Interpretation and falsification of the clinical claim

Supportive evidence requires adequate label/unit coverage, a positive and reasonably monotone renal/feed contrast in the transparent model, a positive learned-model observed-profile contrast, a joint effect exceeding the prespecified additive contrast, 95% uncertainty excluding zero and preferably exceeding a two-percentage-point six-hour risk-difference threshold, direction not driven by one hospital, and acceptable held-out calibration after observation-density removal. This supports a transportable prognostic monitoring signal and motivates chart-reviewed prospective evaluation.

Adverse evidence is a reproducible null below the minimum important difference with a narrow interval, a reversed interaction, loss under strict prior-delivery or unit sensitivities, failure to transport, or reproduction in insulin-free/placebo periods. It refutes the proposed measurable marker in this snapshot or shows that the available records cannot support it; it does not show that insulin or nutrition interruption is harmless.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few paired periods or <54-mg/dL events, unreliable nutrition labels, unresolved glucose units/revisions, wide intervals spanning both null and the minimum important difference, severe hospital exclusion, poor competing-event calibration, or inability to distinguish EOL/discharge/interface loss from true observation. The solver must return inconclusive with diagnostics rather than force a binary conclusion.

The data cannot establish actual administration, calories delivered, a true feed interruption, clinician intent, symptomatic or confirmed hypoglycemia, adjudicated KDIGO AKI, or benefit/harm from an alert or dose change. Those claims require medication administration and nutrition records, point-of-care confirmation, clinical chart adjudication, and a prospective or emulated-intervention study.
