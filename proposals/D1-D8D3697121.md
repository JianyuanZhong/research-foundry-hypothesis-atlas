# Provisional hypothesis: reduced laboratory monitoring during persistent instability

Status: provisional, untested; literature and several exact field checks are still pending.

## Proposed relationship

Among eICU patients who remain clinically unstable at an index time, a dynamic reduction in the number and breadth of laboratory tests over the preceding 12 hours is associated with higher risk of near-term physiologic deterioration in the following 6 hours, beyond contemporaneous vital signs, respiratory support, and recent laboratory values. The association should attenuate when the reduction is explained by a care transition, discharge/transfer, or documented end-of-life limitation.

This is an observational association hypothesis, not a causal claim that stopping tests causes deterioration. The clinically consequential unresolved question is whether a monitoring gap identifies patients whose instability is being under-observed, or merely reflects lower observed severity, an ICU transition, or documentation practice.

## Opening and rivals

The expert seed reports an untested idea that abrupt reductions in testing while unstable may predict deterioration and vary across hospitals. The available catalog confirms longitudinal eICU laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, patient, and hospital tables, but no result has yet been computed.

Three materially different explanations must be compared:

1. **Monitoring-gap explanation:** among patients with similar contemporaneous and recent severity, a fall in testing frequency/breadth precedes deterioration, suggesting delayed detection or insufficient surveillance.
2. **Severity/transition explanation:** the same reduction is a proxy for a change in care state (for example, transfer, discharge, or a transition to less intensive observation), so it loses predictive information after accounting for recent trajectory and transition indicators.
3. **End-of-life/documentation explanation:** reduced testing reflects goals-of-care limitation, non-recoverable illness, or documentation/interface practice. eICU carePlanEOL and care-plan data can identify some explicit limitations, but absent or delayed documentation, family preferences, and interface interruptions are not fully observed.

A prespecified comparison should distinguish these explanations by showing whether the monitoring signal persists after severity and transition adjustment, and whether it is concentrated in patients without explicit EOL limitation. A null result after adjustment would support the rival explanation rather than prove that monitoring is irrelevant.

## Population and time design (to be finalized after field validation)

Use adult eICU patient-unit stays with at least two distinct laboratory observation windows before an index time and sufficient post-index follow-up. Define index times at fixed intervals (for example every 6 hours) while the patient is in the ICU, excluding times within 12 hours of ICU discharge/transfer and times with an explicit EOL limitation in the primary analysis. Require a prespecified instability definition based on contemporaneous and recent vital/respiratory measurements; the exact thresholds must be fixed before outcome analysis and reported as a sensitivity analysis.

Exposure: compare the 12-hour pre-index laboratory testing pattern with the immediately preceding 12-hour pattern, using both count and distinct labtypeid/labname breadth from `lab`. A reduction is defined only when the earlier window has adequate testing, to avoid interpreting absence of a baseline as a gap.

Outcome: near-term deterioration in the next 6 hours, defined from `vitalPeriodic`, `vitalAperiodic`, and `respiratoryCharting` using prespecified worsening of blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, respiration, or respiratory support. The outcome must be computed only from records after the index time. Secondary outcomes should be new escalation of respiratory support or vasopressor/infusion treatment when exact infusion fields and timing permit.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is a prespecified logistic or discrete-time hazard model for the 6-hour deterioration outcome using contemporaneous severity, recent vital/respiratory trajectories, demographics, and hospital fixed effects, without the pre-index monitoring reduction. It answers whether a conventional severity model captures the risk.

The substantive alternative is a longitudinal model that represents the prior 12-hour laboratory process as a sequence of test counts, distinct analyte categories, and time gaps, with contemporaneous severity and transition indicators included. It can reveal whether the pattern and timing of monitoring loss add information beyond a single current severity summary, and whether the signal differs by hospital or care state. A recurrent model or a carefully regularized gradient-boosted sequence model is an implementation option, not a scientific requirement.

Both models use the same patient-level temporal split, with hospitals held out for validation where feasible. Evaluate discrimination, calibration, and the incremental association of the monitoring component, with uncertainty intervals and hospital-clustered resampling. Do not interpret a small predictive gain as clinical utility.

## Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive:** the monitoring-reduction component remains associated with deterioration after adjustment, is temporally prior to the outcome, and is less strong or absent in patients with explicit EOL limitation or a care transition; the effect is reproducible in held-out hospitals. This supports a monitoring-gap signal, not causality.
- **Adverse:** the association disappears after adjustment for contemporaneous/recent severity, transition, or EOL documentation, or is confined to periods near discharge/transfer. This favors the severity/transition/documentation rival.
- **Inconclusive:** wide uncertainty, insufficient eligible windows, unstable hospital-level estimates, or inability to distinguish explicit EOL documentation from undocumented goals of care. This does not refute the hypothesis and should motivate a smaller or different study.
- **Falsification probes:** permute monitoring windows within patient while preserving hospital and time structure; compare a post-index-only monitoring feature (which should not be used for prediction) as a leakage check; and repeat with a negative outcome window or with testing reductions among stable patients. These are design checks, not evidence yet.

## Missing evidence and next checks

No numerical cohort size, outcome prevalence, effect estimate, or fitted result is claimed. The exact patient, hospital, carePlanEOL, and infusionDrug schemas and field semantics still need inspection. The three required hypothesis-specific public works and frozen UTF-8 evidence snapshots have not yet been attached. Clinical adjudication of “persistent instability,” “deterioration,” goals of care, and interface interruption is unavailable or incomplete in eICU and must be stated as a limitation.

The smallest next check is a bounded schema-and-coverage audit of the four tables above, followed by a fixed instability/outcome definition and a patient-level feasibility count. If coverage is inadequate, revise the population or defer the branch rather than inventing a result.
