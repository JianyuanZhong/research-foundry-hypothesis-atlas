# Discordance at fixed two-marker filtration and the first hospitalized kidney transition

Status: prespecified prospective study design; no exposure–outcome association has been fitted. This substantive child resolves the parent's strongest remaining interpretation rival. The parent compared `eGFRcys−eGFRcr=-15` with 0 while fixing eGFRcr at 90, which necessarily lowered eGFRcys and therefore also lowered aggregate two-marker filtration. Here the primary comparison fixes race-free 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine–cystatin C eGFR (`G`) at 90 and changes only the marker discordance coordinate (`delta=eGFRcys−eGFRcr`) from 0 to −15. Population, day-30 landmark, release gate, first-transition states, censoring, code-position and tie sensitivities, participant-level joint uncertainty, and clinical-adjudication boundaries are preserved.

## Scientific opening, evidence, and falsifiable claim

The strongest inspected evidence supports—not tests—the following. Large creatinine–cystatin C eGFR differences are common in UK Biobank and reflect multiple renal and non-GFR correlates [K1]. In SPRINT, discordance was associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes; its authors explicitly noted that a combined equation may improve GFR estimation while losing discordance information [K2]. In CRIC participants with established CKD, baseline and time-updated discordance carried information about adjudicated ESKD and death [K3]. None establishes whether negative discordance in UK Biobank adults with observed eGFRcr at least 60 preferentially precedes N17-first rather than N18-first after holding the combined information in both filtration markers fixed.

**Strongest supported claim:** discordance is a prognostic/phenotypic signal with kidney and non-kidney determinants. It cannot be assumed to represent true GFR, acute renal reserve, occult CKD, muscle mass, or inflammation.

**Unresolved primary hypothesis:** in the common day-30 risk set, at `G=90 mL/min/1.73m²`, changing `delta` from 0 to −15 is associated with a larger standardized 10-year relative-risk increase for N17-first than for N18-first. Formally, `I_A=log RR10(N17_first; delta -15 vs 0 | G=90)`, `I_C=log RR10(N18_first; delta -15 vs 0 | G=90)`, and `S=I_A−I_C>0`. The prespecified low-grip secondary hypothesis is that `S` is larger at the sex-specific 25th than 75th grip percentile: `M=S_low−S_high>0`.

The leading explanation is discordance-specific enrichment for an acute coded kidney event beyond aggregate filtration. The strongest rival is residual chronic/shared renal burden or non-GFR frailty: it predicts an equal or larger N18-first contrast, or similar N17/N18 contrasts. Coding opportunity remains a rival that these fields cannot fully separate. Resolving this matters because an N17-selective pattern would justify external peri-illness/nephrotoxin-risk validation, whereas an equal/larger N18 pattern would redirect validation toward CKD confirmation and surveillance. Neither pattern demonstrates mechanism or treatment benefit.

## Exposure equations and fixed-coordinate estimand

Convert creatinine from µmol/L to mg/dL by division by 88.4. Compute the parent's race-free 2021 CKD-EPI eGFRcr and 2012 CKD-EPI eGFRcys. Define `delta=eGFRcys−eGFRcr`. Compute race-free 2021 CKD-EPI combined eGFR:

`G = 135 × min(Scr/k,1)^a × max(Scr/k,1)^−0.544 × min(Scys/0.8,1)^−0.323 × max(Scys/0.8,1)^−0.778 × 0.9961^age × sex_factor`,

where for women `k=0.7`, `a=−0.219`, and `sex_factor=0.963`; for men `k=0.9`, `a=−0.144`, and `sex_factor=1`. No race term is used. Unit and equation tests against frozen worked examples are mandatory.

The two standardized prediction worlds are `(G=90, delta=−15)` and `(G=90, delta=0)`, separately at the cohort-fixed sex-specific 25th and 75th grip percentiles, while each participant's other baseline covariates remain observed. Do not include eGFRcr, eGFRcys, or raw markers in the outcome models once `G` and `delta` are included. This is an adjusted model-based contrast between marker configurations, not an intervention on biomarkers, true GFR, or a causal effect.

The diagnostic inspected 461,523 adults meeting age/sex/positive-marker/observed-eGFRcr criteria before grip and temporal exclusions. At combined eGFR 85–95, 9,990 had delta within ±2.5 of 0 and 16,799 within ±2.5 of −15. Exact common-risk support must be rechecked by sex, age band, grip level, and covariate pattern before fitting. At `G=90`, numerical inversion produced positive marker pairs for both contrast states at ages 40, 60, and 80 in both sexes; for example, at age 60 the female pairs were (creatinine 69.35 µmol/L, cystatin C 0.8768 mg/L) for delta 0 and (63.98, 0.9276) for delta −15. These checks establish coordinate feasibility only, not an outcome result. If either coordinate lacks overlap in a material stratum, do not extrapolate: report the unsupported stratum and classify that estimand inconclusive.

Use maximum valid left/right grip and sex-standardize it. Preserve covariates: age, sex, raw categorical ethnicity, smoking, diabetes, center, assessment year, BMI, waist, averaged valid BP replicates, CRP, glycemia, and albuminuria only if urine units are frozen. Keep unknown codes as explicit levels; never globally treat all negative values as missing. Impute covariates only within each training/bootstrap sample; never impute markers, exposure coordinates, outcomes, or dates.

## Transparent primary baseline

For each transition fit a cause-specific Cox model from the common initial state, treating every other transition as competing. Include restricted cubic splines with cohort-frozen 5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th percentile knots for `G`, `delta`, grip, age, BMI, waist, BP, CRP, glycemia, and verified albuminuria; include the prespecified `delta×grip` interaction on linear terms and the remaining categorical covariates above. Do not select covariates by outcome association. Check proportional hazards with scaled Schoenfeld residuals and prespecified time interactions if violated; if estimates materially change, report both and classify model dependence.

G-compute transition cumulative incidence by combining the four fitted cause-specific hazards over the empirical common-risk population under each fixed coordinate. Aalen–Johansen curves are unadjusted descriptions; Fine–Gray models are sensitivities. Report endpoint-specific 10-year CIF, risk difference, risk ratio, `I_A`, `I_C`, `S`, and `M`. Absolute risks convey magnitude; only the joint relative-scale contrast `S` tests selectivity because N17 and N18 baseline risks differ.

Use one participant-level bootstrap (target 1,000 successful replicates; minimum 500 after convergence) that resamples participants once per replicate, repeats imputation, knot/grip-percentile estimation, all transition fits, g-computation, and every derived contrast. Return percentile 95% intervals and the joint covariance of `I_A`, `I_C`, both grip-specific `S` values, and `M`. Failure to reach 500 successful replicates is inconclusive. Preserve first-year exclusion, complete-case, primary-position, tie assignment, Fine–Gray, PH, eGFRcys/eGFRcr ratio and percent-difference exposure, and sequential adjustment-block sensitivities. For ratio/percent-difference sensitivities, hold `G=90` and use coordinate values corresponding to the primary biomarker pairs; do not revert to fixing eGFRcr.

The parent's original `delta −15 versus 0 at eGFRcr=90` analysis remains a labeled bridge sensitivity, not evidence for filtration-independent discordance. Its original cohort, death competing risk, joint uncertainty, and 0.5-percentage-point clinical-utility threshold remain unchanged.

The scientific comparison is a task-specific-head network versus an ablation forcing the `delta` and `delta×grip` contributions to be shared across N17 and N18 heads. The alternative can reveal nonlinear, endpoint-specific marker-discordance structure that the proportional-hazard baseline compresses; it earns retention only if both kidney heads are acceptably calibrated (absolute calibration-in-the-large ≤0.02 and slope 0.8–1.2), at least one prespecified Brier or clinically relevant decision-utility metric improves without worsening the other endpoint by more than 5%, and the signs of `I_A`, `S`, and `M` are stable across all three seeds. A small AUC gain is insufficient. Cox remains the primary inferential model. Material baseline/learned disagreement with no calibrated winner makes the interpretation inconclusive.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Rules apply only after release, unit, pairing, support, event-count, convergence, bootstrap, and calibration gates pass.

- **Supportive of filtration-independent N17 selectivity:** at a grip level, the lower 95% bounds for both `I_A` and `S` exceed 0, and their signs survive primary-position and all tie sensitivities. This supports prognostic enrichment for coded N17-first beyond aggregate two-marker filtration; it does not establish acute susceptibility or mechanism.
- **Supports discordance information but not acute selectivity:** `I_A>0` and `I_C>0` are each supported, but the interval for `S` includes 0 with adequate precision. Discordance predicts both coded kidney transitions after fixing `G`; the acute-versus-chronic interpretation remains unresolved.
- **Adverse to acute selectivity / favors chronic or shared burden:** the upper 95% bound for `S` is at most 0 while `I_C` is positive, or the upper 95% bound for `I_A` is at most 0. This redirects the next study toward CKD confirmation/shared-risk explanations.
- **Adverse to residual discordance prognostic information:** both endpoint 95% intervals lie wholly within the prespecified equivalence band `log(0.90)` to `log(1.10)`. This does not prove the markers biologically interchangeable.
- **Low-grip modification supported:** the lower 95% bound for `M` exceeds 0 and the result survives transition-definition sensitivities. A similar low-grip amplification for both endpoints supports frailty/shared reserve, not an acute mechanism.
- **Inconclusive:** any hard gate fails; `S` crosses 0 without equivalence; N18 precision is inadequate; fewer than 20 events occur per effective parameter or either endpoint has fewer than 200 first events; tie convention changes the sign of `S`; all-position support disappears in primary-position analysis; PH correction materially changes the claim; or baseline and learned results materially disagree without a calibrated winner. An imprecise null does not falsify the hypothesis.

Adequate precision for selectivity means the 95% interval width for `S` is no more than `2×log(1.25)`; wider estimates are reported but labeled inconclusive. No result is promoted merely because it confirms the hypothesis.

## Clinical boundaries and additional evidence

This study can establish a reproducible association between a model-defined marker configuration and first inpatient codes, with competing-risk uncertainty. It cannot establish true GFR, renal reserve, occult CKD, AKI stage/KDIGO criteria, biological mechanism, causality, benefit of cystatin-C testing, or a clinical action. N18 may be prevalent but first coded, and N17 may be incidental or co-coded.

Essential noncomputable requirements are renal-clinician approval of N17/N18 and kidney-failure code sets; release-linked censoring documentation; verified semantics for fields 191, 41270/41280, and 41202/41262; urine units; encounter-level admissions and laboratory trajectories to distinguish principal/incidental coding and testing opportunity; repeated outpatient creatinine/cystatin C/albuminuria or measured GFR; and external validation before any decision trial.
