# UKB metabolite trajectory and incident type 2 diabetes

## Question and competing explanations

In UK Biobank participants with two metabolite measurements and no prevalent type 2 diabetes (T2D), does the change in circulating branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs: leucine, isoleucine, valine) and/or glycine from baseline to a later metabolite visit identify incident T2D beyond baseline glucose/HbA1c and standard metabolic risk?

The strongest competing explanation is that the change is a marker of already deteriorating metabolic state, regression to the mean, medication or weight changes, or differential ascertainment. A precursor explanation predicts that metabolite change adds information after controlling for the current glucose/HbA1c level and standard risk factors, especially among people whose baseline glycemic state is not already diagnostic. A deterioration/regression explanation predicts that the association weakens or disappears after adjustment for baseline glycemic state, baseline metabolite level, and contemporaneous risk factors, or that it is concentrated among people with unstable measurements or recent medication/weight changes.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Include adults with a baseline metabolite visit and a later metabolite visit, no T2D diagnosis or diabetes medication before the baseline visit, and a defined follow-up endpoint. Exclude participants whose first incident diabetes diagnosis occurs before the follow-up metabolite visit if that diagnosis would make the follow-up metabolite post-outcome; retain them only in a sensitivity analysis with censoring at diagnosis. Use the earliest post-baseline metabolite measurement as the follow-up measurement, requiring a minimum interval (for example 1 year) and a maximum interval (for example 5 years) to be specified after inspecting the date distribution. Follow-up for incident T2D starts at the baseline visit and ends at first T2D diagnosis, death, loss to follow-up, or end of available follow-up.

Prevalent diabetes is excluded using diagnosis codes and medication fields available in the Health_Related_Outcomes and Additional_Exposures tables. Incident T2D is defined by a first diabetes diagnosis after baseline, using the diagnosis date field in the Health_Related_Outcomes table. If the diagnosis field cannot distinguish T2D from other diabetes types, use available medication and clinical context fields to restrict to probable T2D and label the remaining uncertainty explicitly.

## Exposure and adjustment hierarchy

Define change as follow-up minus baseline concentration, or as a standardized slope when the interval is variable. Use both raw change and change standardized by baseline level and interval length in sensitivity analyses. The primary exposure is a joint BCAA change score and glycine change, with individual leucine, isoleucine, valine, and glycine changes reported separately.

Model 0: unadjusted Cox regression for incident T2D.
Model 1: adjust for age, sex, ethnicity, and assessment center.
Model 2: add baseline BMI/weight, smoking, family history, and physical activity if available.
Model 3: add baseline glucose/HbA1c and baseline BCAA/glycine levels.
Model 4: add diabetes medication use and other available baseline metabolic covariates.

The key comparison is Model 3 versus Model 4, or baseline-only versus trajectory models, because the hypothesis is specifically about information beyond baseline glucose/HbA1c.

## Methods

Simple baseline: Cox proportional hazards regression with baseline glucose/HbA1c, age, sex, ethnicity, BMI/weight, smoking, family history, and diabetes medication. This tests whether standard metabolic risk explains the outcome.

Learned alternative: a gradient-boosted survival or classification model using the same baseline covariates plus metabolite changes, fitted with time-based train/validation/test splits and no participant overlap across splits. The alternative can reveal nonlinear associations and interactions between baseline glycemic state and metabolite change that a linear Cox model cannot represent. It is not a substitute for the Cox comparison; it is a secondary analysis to test whether the trajectory signal is robust to flexible modeling.

Evaluation: concordance index, integrated discrimination improvement, and calibration for survival; hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals for the Cox models. Report uncertainty using bootstrap or cross-validation confidence intervals. Resource estimate: CPU-only is feasible for tabular Cox and gradient boosting on the UKB subset; a GPU is not required unless a much larger repeated-bootstrap analysis is needed.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive result: metabolite change remains associated with incident T2D after adjustment for baseline glucose/HbA1c and standard risk, with a stable direction and adequate event support, and the learned model does not reverse the signal.

Adverse result: the association disappears after adjustment for baseline glucose/HbA1c, baseline metabolite level, or medication/weight changes, or the direction is inconsistent across BCAAs and glycine. This would favor the current-state/regression explanation over a precursor signal.

Inconclusive result: wide confidence intervals, low event counts, missing follow-up metabolites, or unstable results across sensitivity analyses. This would not refute the hypothesis but would require a larger cohort or better outcome adjudication.

Falsification criteria: a negative-control exposure unrelated to glucose metabolism should not show a comparable association; the association should not be explained entirely by baseline glycemic state; and the signal should not be driven by participants with diabetes diagnosis before the follow-up metabolite visit.
