# Episode 2 selection/timing repair: UKB CBC discordance

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`

This branch keeps the parent’s scientific question and repairs its most consequential interpretation problem: a second CBC is not a neutral observation. It is available only for a selected subset, and the observed repeat interval is long enough that requiring a second measurement already selects on survival, healthcare contact and possibly evolving disease.

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

The question is whether repeated pre-diagnostic CBC discordance contains cancer-type-specific information: a relative platelet rise with haemoglobin decline should be more CRC-associated than hematologic cancer, while broader multi-lineage instability should be relatively more hematologic-associated.

The strongest available evidence supports narrower claims. [K1] reviews CRC/neoplasia risk models and finds that discrimination can be useful but calibration and external validation are limited. [K2] reports, from a retrospective CRC-only Spanish cohort, longitudinal haemoglobin and inflammatory-index changes up to 24 months before diagnosis, but calls for prospective external validation. [K3] reports post-diagnosis/prognostic RDW associations and explicitly notes inconsistent timing and thresholds. None establishes a repeated CBC contrast between incident CRC and hematologic cancer, nor does any resolve who receives a second measurement.

The falsifiable claim is:

> Among UKB participants with a complete baseline CBC, the repeated-CBC contrast D_HP = change in standardized platelets minus change in standardized haemoglobin has a larger cause-specific association with incident CRC than with incident hematologic cancer, and that contrast remains directionally similar after measured repeat-selection standardization and in the post-two-year window.

This is a descriptive association, not a causal effect, diagnostic rule, or mechanistic claim. A result confined to the first two years after T1 is evidence of detection/timing or evolving occult disease, not evidence of a stable long-latency signature. A result that changes materially after selection weighting is evidence that the unweighted repeat-CBC contrast is not transportable even within the measured baseline covariate distribution.

## Bounded availability diagnostic

The read-only diagnostic used the selected columns above and is saved at `diagnostic_selection.json`; its script is `diagnostic_selection.py`. It was a measured feasibility/availability check, not a fitted scientific result.

Observed availability:

- 478,033 participants had complete CBC0 and date.
- 19,409 had complete CBC1 and date.
- 5,862 had complete CBC2 and date.
- 18,377 had complete CBC0 and CBC1 with `53-1.0 > 53-0.0`.
- 16,071 of those had no nonmissing registry cancer date by T1.

The repeat gap is not near one or two years: among the 18,377 repeaters, the 1st/5th/25th/50th/75th/95th/99th percentiles are 916/996/1329/1606/1799/2052/2168 days. All observed gaps exceed 730 days. Therefore the parent’s two-year delayed entry begins after a second CBC that is itself typically 4.4 years after baseline, and it is a severe survivor/measurement-selection gate.

For orientation, the event audit found, in the complete baseline cohort over T0 to T0+2 years, 861 CRC, 620 hematologic cancer, 8,735 other cancer and 1,497 deaths. In the 16,071 cancer-free repeaters over T1 to T1+2 years, there were 44 CRC, 27 hematologic cancer, 401 other cancer and 29 deaths. The instance-0/2 sensitivity cohort had 5,623 complete pairs and 4,855 cancer-free at T2, with only 7 CRC and 10 hematologic events in its first two years. This is a sparse robustness check, not a second powered primary cohort.

Baseline measured selection differences are present: among baseline-complete participants, repeaters versus non-repeaters had mean age 57.21 versus 56.52 years, BMI 26.86 versus 27.44, WBC 6.64 versus 6.89, smoking code 0.468 versus 0.547, and CRP 2.32 versus 2.61; missingness also differed modestly. These are descriptive selection signals, not proof of healthcare-use bias.

Date audit found two records with T1 <= T0, 167,852 dated cancer entries, 15,256 cancer dates without a paired code, no code without a date, latest cancer date 2022-06-01 and latest death date 2022-12-19. The solver must exclude invalid T1<=T0 from repeat eligibility, report unmatched dates, and use the fixed administrative boundary 2022-12-31.

## Population and estimands

### Baseline selection-free anchor

Define the baseline cohort as all participants with nonmissing `eid`, `53-0.0`, all four CBC0 fields, and no nonmissing cancer date on or before T0. Exclude invalid dates and death on/before T0. This cohort does not require a second CBC.

Define baseline standardized values using training-set quantities only. The anchor exposure is baseline platelet-minus-haemoglobin level `B_HP = z_platelet0 - z_haemoglobin0`, with WBC, haemoglobin, RDW and platelets also entered separately. Follow from T0 to the first CRC, hematologic cancer, other cancer, death or censoring at T0+7 years or 2022-12-31, with separate 0–2 and 2–7 year windows.

The anchor estimand is the CRC-minus-hematologic difference in the cause-specific association for B_HP. It is not a substitute for the repeated-change question; it establishes whether an apparent repeat-change contrast is already present in a large cohort before repeat measurement is required.

### Repeated-CBC cohort and selection-standardized estimand

Define T1 = `53-1.0`. Require complete CBC0, complete CBC1, `53-1.0 > 53-0.0`, and no nonmissing cancer date on or before T1. Do not require survival without an event for two years after T1 in the primary analysis.

Fit a repeat-observation model among the entire baseline-complete, T0 cancer-free cohort:
`S=1` if a valid complete CBC1 and T1 are observed, otherwise `S=0`. Predict S from only pre-selection fields: sex, age, BMI, smoking, CRP0, four CBC0 values, T0 calendar year, and missingness indicators for the same fields. Fit this model separately within each training fold. Use a stabilized inverse-probability-of-observation weight among S=1, with numerator `P(S=1 | T0 calendar year)`. Truncate at the prespecified 1st and 99th percentiles, report the untruncated distribution and effective sample size, and declare the weighted analysis non-identifiable if positivity is poor or the weight coefficient of variation is extreme. This weighting standardizes measured baseline differences; it cannot recover people who died, developed cancer, or never returned before T1, and it does not measure healthcare utilization.

The repeated estimand is the weighted cause-specific CRC-versus-hematologic coefficient contrast among observed repeaters who are cancer-free at T1, with the target explicitly labelled “baseline-covariate-standardized observed-repeat cohort.” It is not an all-baseline-cohort causal estimand. The unweighted contrast, weighted contrast, and their difference are all required outputs.

Let `d_j=z_{j,1}-z_{j,0}` for j in WBC, haemoglobin, RDW and platelets. Define:

- `D_HP = d_platelet - d_haemoglobin`.
- `D_ML = mean(abs(d_WBC), abs(d_haemoglobin), abs(d_RDW), abs(d_platelet))`.

Fit z-scores and any imputation/normalization quantities inside each training fold. Include baseline CBC levels and changes separately so the contrast cannot hide level effects or regression to the mean. Prespecified adjustment is age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP0, the CBC gap in days, and T1 calendar year. CRP1 is a sensitivity feature, never required for the primary complete-CBC cohort.

Follow all repeaters from T1, without conditioning the primary cohort on surviving to T1+730. Model two prespecified intervals: 0–730 days and day 731 through the earlier of T1+7 years and 2022-12-31. The first interval is the near-diagnostic/reverse-causation stress test; the second is the primary long-latency window. A secondary strict delayed-entry analysis may start at T1+730, but it must be labelled a survivor-selected sensitivity analysis, not the primary repair.

For timing, report continuous gap effects and fixed gap strata 730–1460 days, 1461–1825 days, and >1825 days. Do not create outcome-selected gap bins. The solver must print each stratum’s n, person-time and CRC/hematologic event counts before inference. Given the observed diagnostic, the first stratum may contain few or no participants; that is a result and must not be rescued by changing cut points.

## Outcomes and event handling

For each landmark, the first event is CRC, hematologic cancer, other qualifying primary cancer, death before cancer, or censoring. Other cancers and death are competing events for cumulative incidence; cause-specific Cox risk sets treat them as censoring after their occurrence. Report event counts, person-time, earliest date derivation, and missingness/audit exclusions.

Primary outcome contrast: the difference between CRC and hematologic cause-specific coefficients for D_HP. D_ML is a prespecified secondary contrast. Report coefficient differences, hazard ratios, robust 95% confidence intervals, and time-window interactions. Use Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence at 2 and 5 years as descriptive support; Fine–Gray is secondary and cannot replace the cause-specific estimand.

## Transparent baseline method

The primary fitted method is a weighted and unweighted cause-specific Cox analysis with the same covariates in both target models. Use a piecewise time interaction for 0–730 versus 731+ days rather than deleting early events. Fit separate CRC and hematologic models and test the cause-by-D_HP interaction, equivalently the coefficient difference. Use penalization only for separation/convergence, recording penalty and convergence status. Check proportional-hazard assumptions and report time-varying-effect diagnostics.

Run the baseline-only T0 anchor with the analogous transparent model. Its purpose is selection diagnosis: if the repeated-change contrast disappears when compared with B_HP in the large T0 cohort, the increment from repeat measurement is not established; if it persists after measured selection weighting and is absent from the anchor, it is more informative but still not causal.

## Learned alternative

The substantive learned challenge is a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model, fitted on the same repeat population and raw information. It receives the eight CBC levels at instances 0 and 1, four changes, gap, CRP0 (and CRP1 only in a marked sensitivity run), age, sex, BMI, smoking and calendar year. It never receives cancer dates or post-landmark variables.

Expand each participant into one-year intervals from T1 through seven years, stop at first event/censoring, and use four interval outcomes: CRC, hematologic cancer, other cancer/death, or no event. Fit weighted and unweighted versions; the observation weights are constant across that participant’s rows. Derive target-specific cumulative-incidence predictions from interval hazards.

Compare:

- M0: baseline clinical/selection variables without CBC trajectory.
- MA: M0 plus the prespecified CBC levels, changes, D_HP and D_ML in the transparent specification.
- MB: M0 plus the identical raw CBC levels and changes in the learned model.

Use the earliest 70% of T1 dates for development and latest 30% for final temporal holdout, keeping participants intact. The T0 anchor uses the analogous T0-date split. Within development, use rolling participant-level folds for selection weights, standardization, imputation and hyperparameter tuning. No held-out outcomes or dates may influence fitting. Report target-specific calibration intercept/slope, Brier/log scores and cumulative incidence at 2 and 5 years, with time-dependent AUC/Uno C-index secondary. Use 200–500 participant bootstrap replicates on held-out predictions, refitting the selection model inside each bootstrap where feasible. If the held-out hematologic count is below 100, call the learned hematologic result imprecise feasibility evidence, not a model validation result.

The learned model is scientifically informative only if it identifies stable nonlinear ranges, interactions or timing patterns that explain why the linear D_HP/D_ML contrast loses information. A small discrimination gain without calibration or a reproducible cancer-type timing pattern does not justify preferring it.

## Splits, uncertainty and computational deliverable

The actual deliverable is newly fitted evidence, not a readiness check:

1. An audited baseline T0 anchor and repeated-T1 cohort with exact denominators, exclusions, gap distribution, selection-model overlap and event support.
2. Unweighted and selection-weighted cause-specific estimates for CRC versus hematologic cancer, separated into 0–2 and 2–7 year windows.
3. The strict T1+730 delayed-entry sensitivity estimate, labelled as survivor-selected.
4. A temporal-held-out learned competing-risk model compared against M0 and MA with calibration, Brier/log scores, cumulative-incidence predictions and uncertainty.
5. Falsification outputs, including type-label permutation and all gap/landmark sensitivity tables.

Planned CPU resources are 8–16 CPUs, 32–64 GiB RAM and approximately 2–4 hours for feature construction, weighted Cox fits, rolling folds and 200–500 bootstrap replicates. This is a planning estimate; the measured diagnostic used 4 CPUs, 16 GiB and 90.4 seconds for the selected-column scan. No GPU is needed for the specified HistGradientBoosting model. A future solver may substitute a documented GPU model only after measuring its benefit; GPU use is not a scientific requirement.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires the prespecified positive CRC-minus-hematologic D_HP contrast in the 731+ day window, with compatible direction in unweighted and weighted analyses, acceptable selection overlap, and no major collapse across fixed gap strata. The learned model must show stable held-out calibration and a reproducible nonlinear/timing pattern, not merely AUC gain. This supports a cancer-type-specific descriptive association worth external clinical validation.

Adverse evidence is a directionally opposite contrast with an interval excluding the prespecified direction; a large weighted-versus-unweighted change; a contrast confined to 0–730 days; a similar association for other cancers; failure under the strict delayed-entry sensitivity; or learned-model instability/poor calibration. These outcomes support reverse causation, measurement/healthcare selection, generic illness or registry timing as better explanations and should narrow or abandon the discordance claim.

Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals from the 27 hematologic early events or sparse later events, poor positivity, unstable weights, insufficient held-out event support, or divergent instance-0/1 and instance-0/2 directions. It supports deferral to a larger repeatedly sampled clinical cohort; it is not a positive finding.

Prespecified falsification checks are: (i) permute cancer type among events while preserving event dates in development; the type interaction should collapse toward zero; (ii) compare 0–2 with 2–7 years and the strict delayed-entry sensitivity; (iii) compare fixed gap strata and continuous gap; (iv) use other primary cancers as a specificity control; (v) report complete-case repeaters against the full baseline-complete denominator; (vi) repeat normalization/change construction with fixed winsorization and gap adjustment; and (vii) repeat the minimal instance-0/2 analysis without treating its sparse result as confirmation.

The UKB table lacks pathology adjudication, stage, symptoms, iron/ferritin, reticulocytes, FIT/colonoscopy, smear, marrow studies, medication/transfusion data, infection timing and measured healthcare utilization. Thus no computed result can establish occult bleeding, inflammation, nutrition, clonal hematopoiesis, a clinical referral threshold, or causal benefit. Clinical adjudication and external prospective validation remain necessary.

## Alternative methods considered

The transparent cause-specific model was selected as primary because its cancer-type estimand, timing interaction and uncertainty can be audited and directly expose selection-weight sensitivity. The learned gradient-boosted model is retained because it can reveal nonlinear CBC ranges and cross-lineage interactions that D_HP/D_ML lose; its target, inputs, temporal split, calibration evaluation and CPU budget are specified above.

A latent Gaussian-process trajectory model was considered with the same CBC0/CBC1 inputs and a competing-risk target, but deferred: only two broadly complete CBC assessments exist, instance 2 has 5,862 complete rows, and the data do not support a dense trajectory or a mechanistic latent-state claim. A transformer was also deferred because it would add capacity without adding observed longitudinal information. These are deferrals, not blanket exclusions; revisit them only if additional dated CBCs and sufficient hematologic events become available. The baseline-only T0 anchor is retained as a scientific selection diagnostic, not as a replacement for the repeated-measure question.
