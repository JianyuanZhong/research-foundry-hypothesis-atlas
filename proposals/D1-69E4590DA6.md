# Episode 10 lead successor: fully observed transfer-proximal worsening after ICU exit

Parent: [prior hypothesis], assessed valid and selected by the Lead.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

At an adult ICU exit, does worsening in observed vital-abnormality burden over the preceding 24 hours add prognostic information beyond the final recorded state and measurement opportunity for an immediate subsequent ICU re-entry or in-hospital death?

This matters because ICU exit removes continuous monitoring and rescue capacity. The substantive advance over the parent is an index population with a complete within-stay 48-hour trajectory window, explicit exclusion of overlapping ICU episodes, and a 48-hour primary horizon aligned to immediate post-transfer surveillance. It tests a narrower, more interpretable claim than “residual instability” or “safe discharge.”

Primary hypothesis: among adults at the first qualifying ICU exit of a hospital admission, a larger change Delta = F - P in observed covered-bin vital-abnormality burden from the preceding 24-hour window (P) to the final 24-hour window (F), conditional on F, final last values, and measurement-opportunity features, is associated with higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of a subsequent same-admission ICU stay or in-hospital death before that stay, and improves locked-test prediction beyond the final-state model.

This is an observational recorded-data prognostic hypothesis. It is not a causal effect of transfer, a recommendation to delay transfer, a claim about delivered oxygen, or a clinical-utility/discharge-readiness claim.

## Exact source and population contract

Use only the frozen snapshot and read-only source ZIP in the audit ledger. Parse every datetime as timezone-naive and use within-subject/admission intervals.

Join icu/icustays to hosp/admissions on (subject_id, hadm_id); hosp/patients on subject_id; icu/chartevents on (subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id); and icu/d_items on itemid for labels only. Use hosp/transfers only for aggregate chronology audit; never infer a ward taxonomy or endpoint from careunit.

Construct the candidate index universe before reading feature rows or future outcomes. A candidate must have:
1. anchor_age >=18;
2. nonmissing admittime, intime, outtime, dischtime;
3. admittime <= intime < outtime < dischtime;
4. outtime - intime >=48 hours, so the full A window is within the selected ICU stay;
5. deathtime missing or outtime < deathtime <= dischtime;
6. no other ICU stay in the same admission whose interval overlaps it under other.intime < outtime AND other.outtime > intime.

Duplicate identity keys, impossible chronology, missing anchors, ICU/death timestamp ties, and overlap candidates are retained in an aggregate audit and excluded. Do not repair from hospital_expire_flag, discharge_location, dod, or notes.

Within each (subject_id, hadm_id), sort remaining candidates by intime then numeric stay_id and retain exactly the first. This first qualifying non-overlapping ICU exit is selected without chart values, feature coverage, or future outcome. Preserve all candidate rows and exclusion reasons. Report candidate order and single- versus multi-ICU-admission strata.

Set t0 = outtime. Primary windows are half-open six-bin windows: P=[t0-48h,t0-24h), F=[t0-24h,t0), six contiguous 4-hour bins each. t0-24h belongs to F and t0 is excluded. Only rows from selected stay_id qualify; charttime, never storetime, controls membership. Keep the all-index manifest before measurement gates.

The paired primary estimand requires at least 4 of 6 bins covered in each window, where coverage is at least one valid HR, MAP, RR, SpO2 or temperature observation. It is explicitly conditional on recorded coverage. The secondary all-index prediction analysis uses development-fitted imputation and missing indicators and is not the paired trajectory association. A complete-6-of-6 sensitivity tests denominator/coverage selection.

## Outcomes and competing events

Primary horizon H48=t0+48h; H168=t0+7d is prespecified secondary. For each horizon use the frozen index and earliest qualifying event.

A re-entry is a different stay_id in the same admission, with nonmissing intime >t0, intime <=H, and intime <=dischtime. A later stay beginning before or at t0 that overlaps the index is an anomaly/tie audit, not a primary event. Select earliest by intime then numeric stay_id.

Cause 1: subsequent ICU re-entry at r, before any death and at or before H.
Cause 2: in-hospital death at d with t0<d<=min(H,dischtime), before any re-entry.
Cause 3: alive hospital discharge at q with t0<q<=H, no cause 1/2 at or before q, and deathtime missing or strictly after q.
If q occurs before H, it is terminal cause 3 because later ICU stay would be a different admission; it is not ordinary censoring. If no cause occurs by H, event-free at H. Death after re-entry belongs to cause 1. Equal-time r/d/q ties are indeterminate and excluded from primary fit, with fixed sensitivity priority re-entry, death, discharge. Report overlap, exact-time and missing-event-time anomalies. Never use discharge location or notes to infer alive status, clinical reason, avoidability or readiness.

The primary target is CIF1(H48)+CIF2(H48), with cause 3 terminal competing event, estimated by Aalen-Johansen and predicted by three cause-specific models. H168 is secondary. This is not outpatient ICU use, another admission, adjudicated readmission, or clinician intent.

## Baselines, models and estimands

Use identical preprocessing and one subject-level SHA-256 split: 60% development, 20% validation, 20% locked test, all records for one subject together. anchor_year_group is categorical in every model or omitted from all; never a calendar split.

B0: gender, anchor_age, admission_type, admission_location, insurance, first/last careunit, ICU LOS, hours from admission to t0, earlier ICU-stay count, missing indicators.
B_last: B0 plus final last HR, separate/combined MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, oxygen flow/FiO2 and observed indicators.
B_density: B_last plus coverage counts/indicators, total valid/invalid rows, per-domain valid/invalid counts, log1p counts, oxygen observation and missingness indicators. No abnormality vectors, abnormal counts, burden or abnormal indicators.
B_final: B_density plus F and final per-domain abnormal indicators.
B_traj: B_final plus Delta only.

Fit identical cause-specific ridge Cox models for causes 1-3 at H48, selecting penalty only by inner development CV using composite Brier. Use validation once for an intercept-only calibration correction; lock the test. The primary incremental estimand is paired locked-test Brier(B_traj)-Brier(B_final) at 48h, binary target cause 1/2 versus cause 3/no event at H48. Report cause-specific CIFs, calibration and standardized predictions. Repeat at H168 secondarily.

Use development P10/P90 Delta for adjusted locked-test standardized composite-risk contrast and report within development-derived final-burden quartiles. The 2-percentage-point separation is a reporting threshold, not a clinical cutoff. Use 1,000 fixed-seed subject bootstrap resamples for paired Brier, calibration, coefficients and standardized contrasts; no secondary p-value overrides the primary locked comparison.

Gates: >=1,000 paired indexes, >=100 primary composite events, >=60% of all eligible indexes passing paired coverage, >=20 locked-test composite events, and >=10 locked-test events of each cause before cause-specific inference. If gates fail, label sparse/infeasible and do not alter thresholds. Descriptive estimates may be reported with uncertainty.

## Falsification and interpretation

Fail closed for duplicate index, post-t0 feature, wrong stay, storetime windowing, overlap treated as re-entry, inconsistent ties, subject leakage, test-tuned preprocessing, or conclusions not linked to output.

Required checks:
1. Earlier placebo P0=[t0-72h,t0-48h), F0=[t0-48h,t0-24h), same frozen index/outcome/split; comparable or stronger placebo weakens transfer-proximal interpretation.
2. Machine-check B_density has no abnormality-derived field; compare B_last, B_density, B_final, B_traj.
3. Evaluate Delta within development-only F quartiles; marginal-only association is not evidence beyond final state.
4. Compare complete-6 coverage and all-index imputation; divergence is coverage-selection sensitivity.
5. Report first qualifying index versus all qualifying exits in a secondary subject-clustered analysis, and single- versus multi-ICU strata; never replace frozen primary after results.
6. Fixed MAP source, abnormal-row fraction, 2-hour post-t0 re-entry exclusion, exact ties, 12/48-hour and 7-day horizons.
7. Locked-label permutation preserving class counts; failure is a computation failure, not evidence against the hypothesis.
8. Coverage, invalid-rate and burden audits by cause, care unit, anchor-year group and partition.

Supportive requires positive compatible Delta, B_traj improvement over B_final or P90/P10 risk separation >=2 points with interval excluding zero, persistence after density adjustment and final-state strata, no similar placebo, stable key definitions, and valid permutation. This supports only reproducible prognostic information in recorded MIMIC fields.

Adverse means null/reversed Delta, no incremental locked performance, stronger placebo, attenuation with density/complete coverage, disappearance in final-state strata, or definition instability. It argues against this recorded trajectory adding reliable information; it does not establish safe transfer.

Inconclusive means failed gates, sparse strata, conflicting horizons/competing risks, unresolved chronology, or failed integrity/permutation. These are not positive or negative clinical findings.

Computationally checkable: source/schema availability, index counts/exclusions, window membership, item validity, feature construction, event/tie labels, CIFs, model metrics, bootstrap/permutation outputs and conclusion linkage. Requires other evidence: bedside instability, actual oxygen delivery, treatment limitation, functional readiness, transfer causality, clinical reason/avoidability of re-entry, transportability, equity, utility and a safe discharge policy. These require adjudicated prospective data, external validation and/or an interventional or target-trial study.
