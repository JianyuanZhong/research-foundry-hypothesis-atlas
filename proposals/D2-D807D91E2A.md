# Donor-aware protective-versus-pathogenic target selectivity

Seed: D2-T2. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Does an intervention-associated transcript pattern preferentially suppress a pathogenic fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state, such that a target ranked from an averaged signal is misleading? This matters because macrophage and fibroblast programs can have opposing roles in fibrotic or inflammatory disease: an intervention-associated change in one compartment may reflect a protective response, a pathogenic program, or simply differential cell composition and stimulation. Resolving the question would change whether a target is pursued as context-specific, requires a cell-type-resolved intervention, or is deprioritized.

## Design

Fit a donor-aware linear mixed model on the four configured sources, with donor random effects and condition fixed effects, testing whether the intervention-associated pattern separates protective and pathogenic states. Include a negative-control gene set and a stimulation control to model composition and stimulation artifacts.

- Population: the donors and condition samples recorded in the four configured sources.
- Temporal boundaries: the recorded condition time points in each source; no cross-sectional transition is assumed.
- Variables: transcript expression from the prepared counts matrices; donor identity and condition from the design metadata.
- Outcomes: the intervention-associated transcript pattern for the protective macrophage-linked state and the pathogenic fibroblast program.
- Baselines: an averaged intervention-associated expression ranking; a donor-stratified ordinary comparison.
- Analysis: donor-aware linear mixed model with donor random effects and condition fixed effects; negative-control gene set and stimulation control.
- Falsification: the selectivity pattern disappears or reverses after donor-aware adjustment, or the negative-control set shows the same pattern.

## Competing explanations

- Donor composition: donor-specific baseline differences may drive the apparent selectivity.
- Stimulation: a general stimulation response may mimic a protective-versus-pathogenic pattern.
- Measurement: the targeted assay measures only 445 feature columns; unmeasured genes are unavailable.
- Selection: the datasets are not independent validation sets; coculture r1/r2 includes earlier GSE57723 data.

## Method comparison

The donor-aware linear mixed model is the primary method because it accounts for nested-donor dependence and condition effects. An averaged intervention-associated expression ranking is the main competing strategy: it is simpler but can be misleading if protective and pathogenic programs move in opposite directions or if donor composition/stimulation drives the signal. A fully mechanistic perturbation experiment would be stronger but is not available in the configured datasets; the proposed experiment is the smallest feasible computational check that could change whether a target is pursued as context-specific. A donor-stratified ordinary comparison is an alternative but ignores nested-donor dependence and is less suitable for the small donor structure.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive outcomes

- Supportive: the selectivity pattern is preserved after donor-aware adjustment and controls.
- Adverse: the pattern disappears or reverses after adjustment, or the negative-control set shows the same pattern.
- Inconclusive: the donor structure or assay limits prevent a stable estimate, or the effect is present but too imprecise to distinguish from the controls.

## What the experiment would and would not establish

The experiment would establish whether the intervention-associated transcript pattern separates protective and pathogenic states after donor-aware adjustment and controls. It would not establish direct target action, wet-lab validation, or clinical benefit. A supportive result would justify pursuing the target as context-specific; an adverse result would justify deprioritizing it; an inconclusive result would justify collecting additional donors or using a different assay.
