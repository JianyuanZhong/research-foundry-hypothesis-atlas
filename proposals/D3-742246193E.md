# Directional macrophage-to-fibroblast communication in OA-like cocultures

Seed: D3-M2
Domain: Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses
Status: hypothesis-stage proposal; no confirmatory analysis has been executed.

## Question and clinical importance

In osteoarthritis-like tissue, macrophages and fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) can reinforce one another through paracrine signaling. The consequential unresolved question is whether a measured FLS response is better explained by directional macrophage-to-FLS communication or by an alternative, including endothelial-cell input, donor-specific baseline state, or simply changes in measured cell composition.

Resolving this matters because it changes the next mechanistic experiment and could inform whether anti-inflammatory strategies should target macrophage signaling, FLS intrinsic state, or tissue composition. It is not a treatment recommendation.

## Evidence opening

The seed points to paired coculture and inhibitor conditions in E-MTAB-8316 and E-MTAB-8873, plus GSE95588 coculture data. The available source catalog lists condition-specific RSEC matrices and design metadata, including macrophage-positive/negative and inhibitor conditions. These are the relevant comparisons, but the assay registry and protocol must be reconciled before any cell-specific claim.

Closest inspected work: the seed itself and the frozen source catalog. No exhaustive novelty search has been performed. The remaining question is whether the available condition contrasts can distinguish directional macrophage signaling from composition or donor effects.

## Hypothesis

In the available OA-like coculture assay, macrophage-positive versus macrophage-negative FLS responses show a donor-aware, condition-specific transcriptional signature that is attenuated by the available inhibitor condition more than expected from donor or composition differences alone.

This is a hypothesis, not an established result. The hypothesis is directional and perturbation-oriented, but it does not claim causality until the assay-level perturbation is properly interpreted.

## Competing explanations

1. **Composition artifact:** macrophage-positive samples may contain a different proportion of measured cell states, making apparent FLS changes a bulk composition effect.
2. **Donor baseline:** donors with intrinsically higher inflammatory FLS state could drive the observed differences.
3. **Endothelial contribution:** if endothelial cells are present or influence the culture, their signals could mimic macrophage-to-FLS communication.
4. **Measurement/assay artifact:** targeted panel coverage, RSEC normalization, or duplicate/date-like gene labels could create spurious contrasts.
5. **Selection bias:** the available donors and conditions may not represent the full OA disease spectrum.

## Estimand and analysis

Primary estimand: the donor-aware mean difference in a predefined FLS response signature between macrophage-positive and macrophage-negative conditions, with donor as a blocking factor.

Baseline analysis: donor-aware linear model or paired contrast on the measured FLS response genes, with donor fixed effects and condition as the treatment variable. This is the minimum feasible check.

Substantive alternative: a donor-aware regularized regression or small mechanistic model that predicts FLS response from macrophage state features and composition covariates, compared against the donor-aware baseline. The alternative could reveal whether macrophage state features carry information beyond composition and donor baseline, and which features contribute.

Training/fitting target: donor-aware FLS response signature. Split: donor-level split to avoid leakage. Outcome/uncertainty evaluation: prediction error with donor-level bootstrap confidence intervals, and comparison of baseline versus alternative on the same donor-level split.

Resource/time budget: CPU-only, small tabular fits, expected under 1 hour on 4 CPUs and 16 GB RAM. No GPU required.

Selection reason: the donor-aware baseline is sufficient for the primary estimand; the alternative is retained as a sensitivity analysis to test whether macrophage state features add information beyond composition. If the alternative does not improve donor-level prediction or reveal a coherent feature set, the baseline result is the primary interpretation.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: the macrophage-positive versus macrophage-negative FLS response signature is reproducible across donors and is attenuated by the inhibitor condition beyond what donor or composition differences predict.

Adverse: the signature is not reproducible across donors, or is fully explained by donor baseline or composition, or is not attenuated by the inhibitor condition.

Inconclusive: the signature is present but donor-level uncertainty is too large, or the inhibitor condition is not a clean perturbation, or the assay registry does not support the claimed cell-specific contrast.

A supportive result would justify a targeted perturbation experiment. An adverse result would redirect toward donor-specific or composition-driven explanations. An inconclusive result would require additional donors or a cleaner perturbation assay.
