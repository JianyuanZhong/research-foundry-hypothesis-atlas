# Trajectory persistence and unsafe ICU exit

## Unresolved clinical question

ICU discharge decisions usually compress the preceding course into a terminal assessment. The unresolved claim is whether persistent or fluctuating physiologic instability during the final 24 hours before ICU exit identifies patients at risk of an early ICU return or death beyond the information in the final observation. Existing evidence and the MIMIC seed support that ICU readmission and death are observable outcomes and that time-stamped physiologic data exist; they do not establish that a particular residual-instability phenotype is valid, clinically actionable, or causal.

This matters because a reliable trajectory signal could support a discharge checklist, targeted ward monitoring, or prospective testing of a delayed-discharge/monitoring strategy. The substantive advance is a falsifiable comparison of persistence and recovery dynamics against a terminal-state baseline, with clinically interpretable instability burden, rather than reporting only a generic risk score.

## Hypothesis and estimand

Among adults with an index ICU stay who are alive at ICU outtime and transition to non-ICU acute care within the same hospital admission, greater residual instability burden in the 24 hours immediately before outtime is associated with a higher cause-specific hazard and 48-hour cumulative incidence of ICU readmission or in-hospital death. The primary incremental hypothesis is that trajectory burden and recovery features improve calibrated 48-hour risk over the terminal-state baseline.

Index cohort:
- MIMIC-IV icu/icustays: select the first ICU stay per hadm_id with intime and outtime; retain stay_id, subject_id, hadm_id, first_careunit, last_careunit, and los.
- Join hosp/admissions on subject_id and hadm_id to obtain admittime, dischtime, deathtime, hospital_expire_flag, admission_type, admission_location, and discharge_location.
- Restrict anchor_age >= 18 using hosp/patients.subject_id and anchor_age, and exclude records where deathtime <= outtime. Use transfers (subject_id, hadm_id, eventtype, careunit, intime, outtime) to require the next disposition after the index ICU outtime to be a non-ICU acute-care location; if the next disposition cannot be classified, exclude from the primary cohort and retain it for a missing-disposition sensitivity analysis. Do not use hospital discharge location as a proxy for ICU disposition.
- If there is no next ICU stay and the patient exits hospital alive before 48 hours, treat the alive hospital discharge as censoring rather than an unobserved event. Follow-up is from outtime to the earliest of ICU readmission, death, hospital discharge alive, 48 hours, or dischtime; report IPCW and a sensitivity analysis restricted to patients observable in hospital for the full 48 hours.

Outcome:
- ICU readmission is a subsequent icustays.intime for the same hadm_id with intime > index outtime and intime <= index outtime + 48 hours.
- Death is admissions.deathtime > outtime and deathtime <= outtime + 48 hours; hospital_expire_flag is a consistency check, not a substitute for an event time.
- Primary endpoint is the first of these events within follow-up. Secondary endpoints are ICU readmission alone, death alone, and any in-hospital death through dischtime.
- Report cause-specific hazards and 48-hour cumulative incidence with alive hospital discharge treated as censoring/competing observation loss; do not call the result a causal effect of discharge timing.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Baseline is a prespecified transparent regression using only terminal values from the last six hours before outtime, measurement-availability indicators, age/sex, first/last care unit, ICU LOS, admission type, and disposition context. Do not use SOFA as if it were present; if a derived SOFA-like summary is included, it must be separately documented and cannot replace the baseline.

The mechanistic alternative uses hourly bins in the final 24 hours. For each domain it computes observed-bin proportion below/above threshold, longest consecutive run, first-to-last slope, and variability; it retains observation coverage and missingness indicators. Core instability domains are MAP <65 mmHg, FiO2 >=0.40, SpO2 <92%, HR >110 bpm, RR >24/min, and active vasopressor or ventilation support. Thresholds are frozen before test evaluation; no measurement is treated as normal solely because it is absent. Fit penalized logistic and cause-specific Cox models with the same covariates as the baseline. The alternative could reveal transient versus sustained instability and recovery direction, information the terminal snapshot loses.

Use deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/tuning/test splits. Fit preprocessing and any penalty/threshold decisions only on training/tuning data. Estimate uncertainty with subject-clustered bootstrap on the held-out test predictions (at least 1,000 resamples if feasible). Primary comparisons are 48-hour IPCW Brier score, calibration slope/intercept and observed-versus-predicted cumulative incidence; AUROC/AUPRC and likelihood-ratio/deviance difference are secondary. Report absolute risk differences at prespecified risk strata with 95% CIs, not only p-values.

## Falsification and interpretation

Falsification checks:
1. Recompute all predictors with a strict two-hour exclusion buffer before outtime; a large loss of effect suggests discharge-time recording leakage.
2. Fit a reverse-time control using equal-length bins ordered backward. If reverse ordering performs as well as forward recovery features, claims about recovery direction are not supported.
3. Shuffle trajectory rows within subject while preserving marginal values and observation count. If the trajectory advantage persists, it is likely an aggregation/missingness artifact rather than temporal information.
4. Compare against a last-24-hour aggregate without persistence/run/slope features. The mechanistic claim requires incremental value beyond both terminal state and non-temporal aggregate.
5. Repeat after excluding stays with any invasive/non-invasive ventilation or vasopressor support in the final six hours and after excluding missing-disposition cases.

Supportive result: consistent higher cumulative incidence with greater burden, calibrated incremental improvement over both baselines, and loss of the trajectory advantage under row-shuffling/reverse-time controls. This supports prognostic evidence that persistence/recovery dynamics contain information for discharge-risk stratification.

Adverse result: no association, worse calibration, or no incremental value over the terminal baseline. This argues against using the proposed trajectory phenotype for discharge-risk stratification; it is a valuable negative finding.

Inconclusive result: inadequate event support, excessive missingness, unstable estimates, non-identifiable disposition, or contradictory sensitivity results. This yields a data-quality/feasibility report and no clinical recommendation.

Even a supportive Harbor result cannot establish that delaying discharge, changing monitoring, or treating any physiologic variable improves outcomes. Clinical adjudication and another prospective or external study are required for causal and implementation claims. Expert review is needed for whether thresholds represent clinically meaningful instability and whether treatment limitations or clinician rationale explain residual risk.

## Completion deliverable

The experiment is complete only when it produces: (1) a frozen cohort/outcome audit with counts and missingness by item/domain; (2) fitted terminal-state and trajectory models with held-out predictions, uncertainty, calibration and cumulative-incidence outputs; (3) prespecified falsification/control outputs; and (4) a conclusion explicitly tied to those outputs, including supportive, adverse or inconclusive status and the claims that remain outside computational verification.
