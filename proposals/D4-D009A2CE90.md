# Cystatin-C/creatinine discordance and grip strength as a marker of future kidney events

## Hypothesis and clinical decision

Among UK Biobank adults free of recorded renal disease at the biomarker assessment, a positive difference between creatinine-based and cystatin-C-based eGFR (eGFRcr - eGFRcys), particularly when grip strength is low, is associated with higher risk of a subsequent hospital-coded acute kidney injury (AKI). The clinical question is whether a patient who appears reassuring by creatinine but has a cystatin-C signal and low physical reserve merits intensified kidney surveillance. The study estimates prognostic association and incremental information; it cannot determine whether ordering cystatin C or changing treatment improves outcomes.

The strongest evidence available is the small critical-illness study in references/expert-seeds/papers/kidney-function/article.readable.txt: it used serial creatinine, cystatin C, iohexol clearance and muscle ultrasound in 38 mechanically ventilated patients and found creatinine-based eGFR could overestimate measured dysfunction as muscle was lost. That supports biological plausibility, not the UKB hypothesis. The unresolved claim is whether a similar discordance-plus-grip pattern predicts future coded events in a community cohort.

## Analysis and alternatives

The primary estimand is the cause-specific hazard ratio for AKI per training-set SD increase in D, its interaction with low grip, and the absolute 5-year risk difference between prespecified low/median/high grip profiles. Fit a transparent Cox model with a restricted cubic spline for mean kidney function, linear standardized D and grip, D×grip, age, sex, BMI, weight and other covariates only where field coding is verified. Report the nested comparison that omits D and grip. Use cause-specific Cox for AKI and CKD, Fine-Gray as a sensitivity analysis with death competing, robust uncertainty, and multiplicity-controlled primary interaction/secondary outcome reporting.

The substantive alternative is a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time survival model using exactly the same baseline inputs, outcome definitions and participant-level split. Shallow trees and early stopping are sufficient; it tests whether clinically meaningful nonlinear thresholds and D-by-grip heterogeneity are missed by the transparent model, rather than rewarding a tiny discrimination gain. Hyperparameters use only tuning data. Produce held-out 5-year risk, time-dependent Brier score, calibration slope/intercept and integrated calibration error, plus accumulated-local-effect curves for D at low/median/high grip and bootstrap uncertainty. A patient-level SHA-256 split of accessible catalog discovery buckets 0-79 assigns 60% train, 20% tuning and 20% test; eid is never a feature. Reserved buckets 80-99 are inaccessible and are not external validation. Expected fitting budget is <=4 GB RAM and <=20 minutes after extraction on CPU; no GPU is needed.

Before fitting, perform a bounded feasibility audit: counts, missingness, valid ranges, number of eligible AKI/CKD events, date ordering, and overlap of biomarker and grip participants. If AKI support is sparse or date/instance correspondence is unusable, downgrade the study to an explicitly inconclusive feasibility result rather than silently substitute an easier outcome.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence is a positive D-by-low-grip association with a clinically non-negligible estimate whose uncertainty is compatible with the prespecified direction, persists under primary-code and pre-existing-renal-disease exclusions, and has stable nonlinear structure in held-out data with improved calibration over the nested baseline. This supports incremental prognostic association only.

The hypothesis is adverse if the interaction is null or reversed, confidence/credible intervals include clinically negligible effects, or the signal disappears under primary-diagnosis or renal-code sensitivity. Poor held-out calibration also argues against a surveillance use. Inconclusive results include sparse events, wide uncertainty, severe missingness/selection, or unstable alternative-model curves; these do not establish absence of risk.

Computational verification can establish cohort counts, variable construction, date ordering, split integrity, model fits, uncertainty intervals and calibration/Brier outputs. It cannot establish true AKI severity, measured GFR, causality, treatment benefit, transportability, or a recommendation to order cystatin C. Those require clinical adjudication, measured-GFR anchors, treatment/exposure data, independent validation and/or a prospective clinical study.
