# When creatinine-cystatin-C discordance predicts a coded acute rather than chronic renal phenotype

## Episode-7 successor and unresolved claim

This is a substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. The parent asks whether positive creatinine-based minus cystatin-C-based eGFR discordance (D), especially with low grip, is more associated with a first hospital-coded N17 event than N18. The remaining threat is ascertainment: an N17/N18 code can reflect documentation intensity, pre-existing disease, or a secondary diagnosis rather than acute kidney injury or chronic kidney disease.

The strongest directly relevant evidence inspected is Haines et al., Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness, locally available at references/expert-seeds/papers/kidney-function/article.readable.txt. In 38 mechanically ventilated adults, serial creatinine, cystatin C, iohexol clearance and rectus-femoris ultrasound supported creatinine-based overestimation during muscle loss. That establishes biological plausibility in a selected ICU cohort, not the meaning of low grip or future N17/N18 coding in community UK Biobank participants. Current public evidence also supports discordance as a higher-risk phenotype, but remains observational and does not establish AKI specificity or benefit from cystatin-C-triggered surveillance.

The falsifiable unresolved claim is:

> In UK Biobank adults without recorded N17, N18 or N19 diagnoses at biomarker assessment, the association of positive D and low grip with a first post-index N17-only coding phenotype is larger than its association with an N18-only coding phenotype, and this acute-versus-chronic contrast persists after accounting for pre-index hospital-diagnosis contact burden and is stronger when the renal code is a primary inpatient diagnosis.

This is a claim about coded phenotypes, not adjudicated AKI/CKD. The clinical decision it can inform is whether discordance should prompt evaluation for acute-injury vulnerability versus chronic-kidney-disease assessment. It cannot determine whether either action improves outcomes.

## Estimands and statistical analysis

The primary estimand is the cause-specific hazard and five-year Aalen-Johansen cumulative-incidence contrast for N17-only versus N18-only, comparing the 10th-to-90th percentile D profile and low-versus-median grip profile. Report hazard ratios, absolute risks, risk differences and participant-bootstrap intervals. Clinical materiality is prespecified before fitting as at least a 0.5 percentage-point N17-minus-N18 risk differential in the low-grip/high-D profile, with uncertainty not compatible with reversal. This is an information criterion, not surveillance benefit.

Fit separate cause-specific hazards for N17-only, N18-only and mixed/other. Death is censored for cause-specific hazards and retained in Aalen-Johansen. Fine-Gray is sensitivity. The nested models are:

- M0: age, sex, BMI, weight, grip and eGFRcr.
- M1: M0 plus eGFRcys (or D; never include eGFRcr, eGFRcys and D together).
- M2: M1 plus prespecified continuous D-by-grip interaction and restricted cubic splines for age and eGFRcr.
- M3: M2 plus log1p(pre_contact_days) and pre_primary_contact, the ascertainment-adjusted analysis.

The primary claim requires the acute-versus-chronic contrast to be directionally larger in M3 and in the primary-inpatient analysis than the corresponding N18 contrast, not merely that M2 predicts more renal codes. A separate negative-control analysis models first post-index nonrenal diagnosis-date contact (any valid 41280 date with no N00-N99 code on that date) as an outcome using M0/M2 exposures. A large analogous D-by-low-grip effect on this contact outcome weakens an acute-specific interpretation and is reported as evidence of generalized frailty or healthcare-contact association, not as proof of no kidney relevance.

## Falsification, interpretations and evidence limits

Supportive evidence requires all unit/date/join gates to pass; adequate N17-only and N18-only events; a prespecified D-by-low-grip association that is materially larger for N17-only than N18-only; persistence after M3 contact adjustment, broad-history exclusion and primary-inpatient ascertainment; and a concordant learned-model endpoint contrast with calibrated held-out risks. A negative-control contact effect should not be as large as the renal phenotype contrast. This supports an acute-coded-versus-chronic-coded prognostic distinction, not adjudicated AKI.

Adverse evidence is a larger N18 association, a null/reversed N17-minus-N18 contrast, attenuation after contact adjustment or broad-history/primary-inpatient ascertainment, a similarly large nonrenal-contact association, or learned-model calibration that fails to reproduce endpoint-specific risks. This argues against calling discordance acute-injury-specific but does not prove cystatin C has no value.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed unit verification, duplicate or non-one-to-one joins, invalid/unpaired dates, impossible administrative boundary, sparse events, severe missingness, data leakage, unstable bootstrap, or model nonconvergence. Do not replace failed N17/N18 analysis with an easier all-renal endpoint. If the primary-inpatient event count is sparse, report it as inconclusive and retain the any-diagnosis estimate only as a nonconfirmatory sensitivity.

QA can verify joins, formulas, code/date pairing, contact proxy construction, risk sets, splits, calibration, and whether conclusions follow from computed outputs. It cannot establish AKI severity, CKD stage, measured GFR, urine output, inflammation, muscle mass, causality, treatment effect, surveillance utility, transportability or clinical appropriateness. Those require adjudicated laboratory trajectories/urine output, measured-GFR and body-composition anchors, treatment and encounter context, expert review, and independent/prospective validation.
