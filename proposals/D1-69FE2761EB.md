# Proposal: A landmarked, boundary-aware target trial of vasopressor discontinuation order

## Substantive repair and clinical advance

This child retains the parent’s bedside question but changes the estimand from a future, chart-derived weaning episode to a sequence of explicitly eligible decision landmarks. The parent’s time zero was described as the end of a first drug’s final active segment while also requiring future coverage and no restart; those future conditions can select on early outcome and create immortal-time or collider bias. The repaired design defines eligibility using only information available before a documented first-stop action, treats restart as an outcome rather than an exclusion, uses the same boundary rule for either drug, and makes the primary outcome observable when a patient dies or leaves the ICU.

The strongest public evidence is directional but not definitive. The 2019 individual-patient meta-analysis (six studies, 957 patients) reported clinically significant hypotension in 60.7% after vasopressin-first versus 43.3% after norepinephrine-first, pooled OR 0.22 (95% CI 0.07–0.68), with no detectable short-term mortality or length-of-stay difference and high heterogeneity for hypotension (I2 87%) (Europe PMC search source `[source checksum]`, PMID 30893494, DOI 10.1002/phar.2265). A second meta-analysis found one RCT and seven retrospective cohorts, OR 0.30 (0.10–0.86), but trial sequential analysis judged evidence insufficient (required information size 11,821) (source `[source checksum]`, PMID 31008869, DOI 10.1097/SHK.0000000000001345). Conversely, the recent open-access 2025 single-center cohort of 524 patients found no weighted difference in hypotension (19% vasopressin-first vs 21% norepinephrine-first; P=0.59) (frozen full text source `[source checksum]`, PMC12487481, DOI 10.1186/s40560-025-00823-w). Thus the supported claim is an association with possible short-term hemodynamic instability, not a proven mortality benefit or universal superiority.

The unresolved claim is: among ICU adults on concurrent low-dose norepinephrine and vasopressin at a documented, clinically stable first-stop decision, does stopping norepinephrine before vasopressin reduce 6-hour clinically significant hemodynamic failure by a clinically important amount? MIMIC can estimate a carefully bounded observational contrast; it cannot establish exchangeability, clinician intent, or a practice recommendation.

## Target trial emulation

### Population and phenotype

Use the first ICU stay per adult hospital admission and the first qualifying dual-infusion stop decision in that stay. Adult status is derived from `hosp/patients.anchor_age + year(admissions.admittime) - patients.anchor_year`, with `anchor_age >89` treated as 91 per the MIMIC convention. Require concurrent active IV norepinephrine and vasopressin for at least 6 hours before the candidate stop, with valid rates and a patient weight for norepinephrine normalization. At the candidate stop, require both agents to be at or below norepinephrine 0.20 mcg/kg/min and vasopressin 0.04 U/min, no epinephrine, dopamine, or phenylephrine active in the preceding 2 hours, and no ICU discharge or death before the candidate time.

Eligibility must be assessed from records with timestamps <= candidate time. Require a 2-hour pre-index window with at least three valid MAP observations, median MAP >=65 mmHg, no MAP <55 mmHg, and no upward trend in either infusion over the last 30 minutes. Lactate is a prognostic covariate, not a required criterion: if two pre-index values exist, require the most recent not to exceed the prior by >0.5 mmol/L; otherwise retain the patient and flag lactate missing. This avoids excluding patients merely because lactate was not obtained and avoids using post-index stability.

The primary population is all such dual-agent shock episodes, not a falsely adjudicated septic-shock population. Prespecify an exploratory vasodilatory-proxy stratum based only on pre-index information: no active dobutamine/milrinone, no mechanical circulatory support or RRT procedure documented before index, and concurrent dual vasopressors. A sepsis-likely subgroup may be reported using early cultures/antimicrobial administration when timestamped, while discharge ICD coding (`hosp/diagnoses_icd`) is used only for description/sensitivity. Shock phenotype, perfusion, and clinician intent are not fully recoverable from MIMIC and remain limitations requiring expert adjudication.

### Time zero and treatment strategies

Create candidate stops from reconstructed administration intervals in `icu/inputevents` for norepinephrine item 221906 and vasopressin item 222315. A stop event is the first end of an active interval after eligibility in which the drug has no subsequent active interval beginning within 10 minutes. Use the same 10-minute gap rule for either agent. Use `statusdescription` (including `Stopped`, `Paused`, and rate-change records) to classify but do not trust status alone; retain a boundary-quality flag. The comparator must remain active at the stop time. Exclude only simultaneous stops whose recorded stop times differ by <=5 minutes, using this symmetric rule for both agents. The exposure is the drug stopped at this first documented stop; no future restart or future comparator behavior may be used to define the arm.

The primary estimand is an intention-to-treat analogue: the 6-hour risk contrast associated with the observed first stop of norepinephrine versus vasopressin at the qualifying decision. Follow-up starts at the stop time, so there is no immortal pre-assignment period. A 30-minute boundary sensitivity analysis shifts the start of physiologic MAP ascertainment to 30 minutes after the stop, but does not exclude early restart; early restart is a failure component. A separate per-protocol analysis clones each episode to the two strategies at the observed stop, censors a clone at symmetric deviation (restarting the stopped drug, stopping the comparator before 2 hours, or adding another vasopressor), and uses 15-minute adherence weights. It is secondary and is declared inconclusive if overlap or weight diagnostics fail.

### Outcomes and estimands

The primary outcome is clinically significant hemodynamic failure by 6 hours after stop, defined as the first of: (1) restart of the stopped drug; (2) an increase in the remaining norepinephrine rate by >=0.05 mcg/kg/min sustained in a subsequent valid interval; (3) initiation of epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine; (4) two valid MAP values <60 mmHg separated by 5–120 minutes, with at least one value in each of two 30-minute bins; or (5) death before 6 hours. Components are reported separately. ICU discharge before 6 hours is not silently treated as a normal observed MAP trajectory: it is a competing event. It is counted as failure-free at 6 hours only when both agents remain off, no rescue occurs, and the patient is alive at discharge; otherwise it is censored and handled with inverse-probability-of-observation weights and best/worst-case bounds. This defines a proximal clinical endpoint that remains interpretable under death and discharge while exposing ascertainment assumptions.

MAP is assembled from `icu/chartevents` item 220052 (arterial) and 220181 (non-invasive), preferring arterial values when concurrent. Exclude `warning != 0` and impossible values. Do not carry a MAP forward more than 15 minutes, interpolate only across a gap <=15 minutes for a secondary MAP area-below-65 measure, and report arterial-only and observation-density-restricted analyses. Calculate MAP observation coverage in each 30-minute bin; differential monitoring by arm is a diagnostic and a reason to downgrade conclusions.

Secondary outcomes are MAP area below 65 over the observed ICU follow-up to 6 hours; alive and free of all vasopressors at 24 hours; vasopressor-free hours to 24 hours; 24-hour lactate change; new or persistent invasive ventilation at 24 hours; RRT initiation by 48 hours; AKI stage 2/3 by 48 hours when creatinine and urine-output coverage permit; ICU/hospital discharge; and in-hospital mortality. `patients.dod` is used only to ascertain death where compatible with the admission; outpatient mortality completeness is not assumed. A 28-day mortality analysis is not primary and is reported only when the shifted MIMIC dates permit unambiguous ascertainment.

Estimate risk differences and risk ratios for the primary 6-hour composite, with 95% confidence intervals clustered by `subject_id`. The clinically important primary margin is 5 absolute percentage points: a result is supportive of norepinephrine-first only when the adjusted point estimate favors it by at least 5 points, the confidence interval excludes no difference, components are directionally coherent, and there is no clinically concerning increase in death, rescue, or RRT. A confidence interval spanning -5 to +5 percentage points is inconclusive for a practice-changing difference. Because mortality and RRT are uncommon and confounded, prespecify no claim of mortality benefit from this study; report absolute estimates and uncertainty.

## Confounding, observation, and falsification controls

All baseline covariates are measured strictly before stop: age, sex, race, first/last care unit, admission type, ICU time, hospital time, ventilation and RRT status, MAP level/variability/observation density, heart rate, lactate and creatinine trajectories, urine output and fluid balance, both infusion rates and slopes, cumulative dual-agent duration, preceding crystalloid/albumin, steroid exposure where timestamped, and calendar/shift indicators. Use admission diagnoses only as proxy covariates and never as time-zero shock adjudication. Weight-normalized NE and vasopressin dose must be calculated from `rate`, `rateuom`, `patientweight`, and `amountuom`; reject impossible units and retain a conversion audit.

The primary adjustment is a parsimonious propensity model plus augmented inverse-probability weighting; cross-fitted boosted models are a sensitivity analysis, not a substitute for transparent diagnostics. Trim to propensity 0.05–0.95, report arm-specific counts, standardized mean differences (<0.10 target), effective sample size, weight tails, and overlap plots. Require at least 200 eligible episodes and 40 primary events per arm for a practice-relevant analysis; otherwise report feasibility/descriptive estimates only. Multiple imputation may be used for covariates and missingness indicators, never for exposure boundaries or outcome events.

For the per-protocol analysis, use 15-minute time-varying adherence models with pre-bin MAP, dose, urine output, lactate, fluids, ventilation, rescue treatment, and monitoring intensity. Do not condition on post-stop measurements in the baseline propensity model. If ESS <30% of eligible episodes, positivity falls outside the trim, or >1% of stabilized weights exceed the predeclared 99th-percentile truncation threshold, label the per-protocol estimate inconclusive and privilege the observed-stop analogue as descriptive.

Falsification/diagnostic tests are: (a) pseudo-index 6 hours before the actual stop while both agents remain active, testing for an apparent arm association in pre-index MAP/lactate; (b) differential MAP measurement frequency and arterial-line availability by arm; (c) an outcome requiring future MAP 12–18 hours after both agents are off among survivors, interpreted only as a selection/monitoring negative control; (d) leave-one-care-unit and anchor-year-group analyses; (e) alternate gap rules of 5 and 15 minutes, simultaneous-stop windows of 2 and 10 minutes, and MAP thresholds of 55 and 65; (f) a boundary-adjudication audit of a blinded sample of timelines using inputevents rows, rate continuity, statusdescription, and charted MAP. Reversal across reasonable boundary rules, strong pseudo-index effects, or arm-linked monitoring invalidates a causal interpretation.

## Interpretation limits

Supportive results establish, at most, a reproducible association in an explicitly defined MIMIC decision population and justify a prospective protocol or randomized discontinuation trial. Adverse results (vasopressin-first lower failure, or norepinephrine-first lower transient failure but more rescue/RRT/death) falsify the directional hypothesis and should prevent a simplistic norepinephrine-first recommendation. Inconclusive results preserve equipoise when overlap, event counts, observation coverage, or boundary adjudication fail, or when reasonable definitions reverse the result. Automated verification can check source paths, row-level chronology, cohort counts, endpoint component counts, balance, weights, uncertainty, and whether claims match computed outputs. It cannot verify true drug administration, clinician intent, exchangeability, shock phenotype, or clinical importance without blinded expert review and preferably prospective randomization.
