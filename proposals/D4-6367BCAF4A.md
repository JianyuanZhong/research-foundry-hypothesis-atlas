# UKB 34: Fat distribution and differences in hormone-related cancers after menopause

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

At similar BMI, the combination of visceral fat and low SHBG is more strongly associated with endometrial cancer than with colorectal cancer.

## Study population and key data

Postmenopausal women without the corresponding cancer; abdominal MRI, biochemistry, HRT, and cancer registry.

## Basic Approach

Follow participants from MRI onward, comparing the standardized associations of the same exposure with the two cancer types, and consider hysterectomy and HRT.

## Significance of the Research

Test whether fat-related risk exhibits endocrine specificity.

## Main Challenges

Hormone levels and MRI may not be contemporaneous; differences by cancer type cannot directly establish hormone mediation.

## References

Relevant background or data description, not evidence that the hypothesis is valid or novel: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60058-z; field definitions: https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=21085; https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=21001; https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=30830

## Life Sciences

Sex, hormones, and metabolic redistribution

## Competing Explanations

Screening, surgical history, and general metabolic abnormalities may cause differences between cancer types.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

First verify the population with an intact uterus and the number of events; if the differences are driven only by the distributions of surgery or HRT, this does not support specificity.
