# Recovery-aware ICU discharge readiness and post-ICU hospital death

## Question, evidence boundary, and hypothesis

The assigned parent established that the temporal order of low-MAP exposure may matter beyond minimum MAP and total burden, using a subsequent creatinine-defined renal deterioration signal. This successor asks a different, clinically consequential decision question:

> Among adults discharged alive from the ICU, does failure of MAP to recover during the final six hours before ICU discharge identify higher 48-hour in-hospital death risk after ICU discharge, after accounting for terminal MAP, total burden, admission severity, support and discharge timing?

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that the frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative vital observations, ICU and hospital discharge offsets/statuses, hospital identifiers, admission severity fields, and structured respiratory/infusion records. Neither the parent, expert seed 06, nor the demonstrations establishes that nighttime or physiologically incomplete ICU discharge causes harm, that MAP 65 is a treatment target, or that a retrospective risk marker is useful for deciding discharge.

The falsifiable hypothesis is that, among eligible ICU survivors with adequate final-window MAP coverage, late-persistent low MAP (MAP <65 in the final two hours) has a positive standardized 48-hour cumulative-incidence difference for hospital death after ICU discharge versus early-recovered low MAP, conditional on comparable minimum MAP, total low-MAP burden, terminal MAP and measured severity. A temporal model should reveal additional recovery patterns only if they reproduce in hospitals held out from fitting. This is an observational prognostic study, not a causal estimate of delaying discharge.

The substantive advance over the parent is a new transition-of-care estimand: it tests whether recovery shape is relevant at the ICU-exit decision and predicts a hard hospital outcome, rather than only a future laboratory documentation signal. It also tests whether the apparently actionable recovery signal survives conditioning on terminal state and the selection of patients who were discharged alive.

## Population and time

Retain the first ICU stay per person: age >=18 after mapping age >89 to 90, `unitvisitnumber=1`, nonmissing hospital, numeric ICU discharge offset, and at least six hours of ICU observation before discharge. Resolve duplicate valid first-visit rows per `uniquepid` by earliest valid admission and report conflicts. Restrict to `unitdischargestatus` indicating alive discharge; retain only records with `hospitaldischargeoffset > unitdischargeoffset` and a nonmissing hospital discharge status. The primary cohort requires at least 48 minutes of documented hospital follow-up or a hospital outcome sufficient to classify the 48-hour endpoint; the support audit must report how this is operationalized and never treat missing outcome observation as alive.

For each stay define t = `unitdischargeoffset`. Use MAP observations in [t-360,t), with each valid observation covering only `min(5, next_valid_offset-current_offset)` minutes, clipped to the window, no carry-forward across gaps, plausible numeric systemicmean in [-50,300], and >=80% covered minutes (70% and 90% sensitivities). Require at least 80% coverage in the final two hours for the primary recovery-state classification.

Primary exposure states are: recovered, with early low-MAP minutes >0 in [t-360,t-120) and zero covered low-MAP minutes in [t-120,t); versus persistent, with low MAP in the final two hours. Retain minimum MAP, total low-MAP minutes, depth area, last MAP, final-two-hour mean/coverage, and early/late low minutes. The primary contrast is within prespecified minimum strata (55-64, 45-54, <45 when supported) and total-burden bands (0-60, >60-120, >120 minutes when supported). A continuous secondary contrast estimates a standardized risk difference per 60 late-low minutes at fixed total burden/minimum MAP with a prespecified spline. Patients with no low MAP are a descriptive reference, not substituted for the recovered-versus-persistent contrast. Repeat with MAP 60/70 and with `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` separately.

The landmark occurs at ICU discharge, so no post-discharge measurements are predictors. All admission covariates and pre-discharge support summaries are timestamp-safe by construction. Exclude EOL proxy offsets <=t and report an exclusion sensitivity rather than claiming EOL intent.

## Outcome and estimand

The primary outcome is hospital death within 48 hours after ICU discharge, identified from `hospitaldischargestatus` and `hospitaldischargeoffset`: death event if hospital status is a prespecified death encoding and hospital discharge offset is in (t,t+48h]. Alive hospital discharge in (t,t+48h] is a competing event; discharge after 48h without death is administratively censored at 48h; missing/invalid hospital outcome is censoring and never a negative. Report all-cause hospital death through eventual hospital discharge as a secondary outcome when the status/offset is valid.

The primary estimand is the standardized 48-hour cumulative-incidence risk difference for persistent versus recovered recovery shape, standardized over the eligible cohort covariate distribution and adjusted for terminal MAP, burden and admission severity. Fit a cause-specific pooled-logistic model with patient clustering (one primary record per stay) and hospital-stratified intercept; bootstrap hospitals for 95% intervals. Require at least 20 hospitals with outcome/exposure support for transport summaries and report hospital-level intervals without ranking sites.

A positive observational result does not show that delaying ICU discharge prevents death. Post-ICU ward care, readmission, goals of care, bed availability, handoffs and unmeasured clinical judgment are unavailable.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The transparent baseline is a regularized cause-specific pooled-logistic model using age, sex/ethnicity, time/era proxies available in the snapshot only if non-identifying, admission Apache variables/scores, terminal MAP min/last/mean/SD/coverage, total low-MAP minutes/depth, final-window heart rate/respiration/SpO2 summaries and slopes, ventilation/pressor-record indicators, ICU length of stay, and missingness indicators. Hospital is stratified, not a feature. The nested comparator removes recovery shape while retaining terminal state and burden. The primary incremental estimate is recovery shape’s standardized risk difference, calibration and Brier contribution.

The substantive alternative is a small order-aware temporal-convolution model over six one-hour bins before t, using the same admission covariates and hourly MAP, heart rate, respiration, SpO2, ventilation-record and pressor-record values, masks and coverage/gap channels. Fit a shape-free terminal/burden variant and a full-order variant to the same 48-hour competing-risk target. It can detect partial recovery, recurrent low-MAP episodes and irregular monitoring patterns that a binary final-two-hour indicator loses. Use patient-contained hospital splits: fit on patients from discovery hospitals, validate on a fixed patient-level split, and evaluate untouched hospitals; never use hospital ID. Use three fixed seeds and hospital bootstrap uncertainty. Compare standardized risks, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score and prespecified decision curves; AUROC is secondary.

CPU is preferred because the baseline is expected under 30 minutes and the small temporal models under 60-90 minutes with two CPUs and <=8 GiB, within the 7,200-second science budget. GPU is deferred because no different clinical question requires it. If the temporal alternative fails, the baseline discharge-transition estimate remains the promised primary fitted deliverable; the compiler must not replace it with an audit-only task.

## Feasibility, falsification, and interpretation

Before fitting, report first-stay and alive-ICU-discharge counts, outcome-status encodings, ICU-to-hospital offset validity, 48-hour support, MAP coverage/density, recovery-state/burden support, EOL exclusions, support-record availability, and each hospital’s exposure/outcome support. A failed status audit, sparse death events, absent within-stratum contrast, inadequate MAP coverage, or insufficient hospital support yields an inconclusive result, not a new endpoint or uncomputed claim.

Falsification checks:

1. Permute MAP observation order within each final six-hour window while preserving values, gaps and coverage. The recovery-shape contrast should attenuate while terminal/burden summaries remain comparable.
2. Apply an earlier-window placebo shape [t-12h,t-6h) with the same construction; a comparable association suggests general severity or discharge selection rather than final recovery.
3. Refit after excluding discharges within 12 hours of hospital discharge, EOL proxies, and abrupt loss of all source coverage.
4. Repeat 70/80/90% coverage, MAP source, 60/65/70 threshold, one-stay/duplicate resolution and minimum/burden strata.
5. Leave one hospital out and require transport summaries only for hospitals with prespecified support.
6. Add/remove documented pressor and ventilation rows; interpret shifts as documentation sensitivity, never treatment adjustment.
7. Negative-control outcome: a post-ICU event impossible before ICU exit, such as hospital death recorded at or before t, must be absent after valid temporal filtering; any leakage invalidates the computation.

Supportive results require a positive persistent-versus-recovered risk difference with a 95% interval excluding zero, stability across primary timing/source/coverage checks, attenuation under order permutation, weak earlier-window placebo, and either calibrated incremental value in held-out hospitals or a reproducible temporal pattern beyond the binary baseline. This supports an observational discharge-transition risk marker and prospective adjudication/monitoring research only.

Adverse results include a precise null/negative result, disappearance after terminal-state adjustment, a strong placebo, no attenuation under permutation, or failure in held-out hospitals. That would indicate the apparent association reflects general severity, selection or documentation rather than recovery timing.

Inconclusive results include invalid hospital status semantics, inadequate post-ICU observation, sparse deaths, no supported recovered/persistent contrast, unstable site transport, or intervals too wide to separate supportive from adverse. No discharge-policy recommendation follows.

Computationally checkable claims are snapshot/source hashes, exact joins and ICU/hospital offset arithmetic, filters, MAP coverage/exposure calculation, outcome encoding, split integrity, model fitting, standardized risks, uncertainty, calibration, transport and falsification. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to establish true perfusion, adjudicated cause of death, ward-level deterioration/readmission, discharge readiness, clinician intent, staffing/bed-capacity mechanisms, and whether changing discharge timing improves outcomes.

The earlier demonstrations are methodological context, not a topic list.
