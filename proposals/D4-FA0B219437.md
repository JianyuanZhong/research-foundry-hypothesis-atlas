FROZEN QUESTION, ESTIMANDS, AND CLAIM BOUNDARY
P1 asks whether baseline cystatin-C unexpectedly high conditional on baseline creatinine predicts higher repeat creatinine conditional on baseline creatinine among the eligible repeat attenders. P2 asks the reciprocal question: whether baseline creatinine unexpectedly high conditional on baseline cystatin-C predicts higher repeat cystatin-C conditional on baseline cystatin-C in the identical participants.

All four renal analytes are natural-log transformed. Fold-specific nuisance OLS generates held-out residual exposures, standardized by the corresponding training residual SD. One stacked OLS per direction uses the common complete-cohort mean and sample SD for the follow-up marker. Beta is follow-up-marker SD per training-fold residual SD of unexpected baseline cross-marker level. Final-model spline knots are fixed from the original cohort; nuisance knots and scales and outcome mean/SD are recomputed inside every participant-bootstrap replicate. Original eid-derived folds are retained in every replicate.

The strongest numerical claim supported is positive bidirectional observable cross-marker persistence among these eligible repeat attenders. It is not evidence of latent GFR decline, CKD progression, causality, clinical utility, renal specificity, or transportability. Two waves cannot separate stable renal level from trajectory or stable marker-specific non-GFR effects, assay variation, correlated error, or selection.
