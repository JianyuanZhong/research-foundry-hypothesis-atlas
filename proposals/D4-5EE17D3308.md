## Clinical question, supported claim, and untested hypothesis

Repeated routine full blood counts may contain a weak prediagnostic pattern of falling haemoglobin, falling mean corpuscular volume (MCV), and rising platelets. If that pattern adds CRC-specific prognostic information beyond the current blood count, it could motivate independent evaluation of longitudinal rather than single-time-point laboratory features. However, selected-case discrimination is not the same as identifying CRC in a population, and a rectangular phenotype export cannot establish outcome completeness by its observed event dates.

The strongest claim currently supported is limited:

1. the exact required fields are present in the current UKB source headers and schemas and can be joined horizontally on `eid`;
2. the corrected primary platelet field is 30080 and the secondary RDW field is 30070;
3. later analyses of 998 selected future recorded-malignancy cases found no supported incremental or subtype-localizing signal under their frozen methods, while preserving substantial uncertainty; and
4. this local export lacks a release-matched cancer/death coverage certificate, so the present population gate is `FAIL_UNCERTIFIED`.

The unresolved primary hypothesis is:

> Among UKB repeat attenders aged 40–75 with paired protocolized haemoglobin, MCV, and platelet-30080 measurements and adequate certified cancer/death observation, adverse annualized trajectories from baseline to repeat assessment improve prediction of a first recorded CRC in days 366–1825 after repeat assessment beyond age, sex, and repeat-assessment CBC levels; the incremental discrimination is greater for CRC than for first non-CRC malignancy.

This hypothesis is not supported merely by field availability, biological plausibility, the existing selected-case analyses, or the cancer research demonstration. The demonstration's main article and STAR Methods were unavailable and are not claimed to have been inspected; it is not used as evidence of this UKB effect.

## Branch A: coverage-certified population experiment

### Population

Include participants with:

1. valid `B` and `L`, with `365 <= L-B <= 2922` days;
2. completed age 40–75 at `L` and valid sex coding;
3. finite paired primary values for haemoglobin 30020, MCV 30040, and platelet 30080;
4. certified endpoint observation from an approved cancer-history start `A_i` through at least `E`, with `A_i <= B`;
5. no malignant diagnosis recorded during certified history `[A_i,L)` and no recorded death before `L`;
6. no CRC, non-CRC malignancy, or death in washout `[L,E)`; and
7. `E` within certified cancer/death observation.

This defines a cohort with no *recorded malignancy during certified history*, not proof of lifetime cancer absence. Follow each person from delayed entry `E` to the earliest of first CRC, first non-CRC malignancy, death, certified administrative end `C_i`, or `H`. A participant can be considered event-free only while certified observation remains active. Do not substitute the empirical maximum event date for `C_i`.

### Exposures and comparator

For marker `m`, define annualized change:

`change_m = (m_1-m_0) / ((L-B)/365.2425)`.

Define adverse-oriented slopes:

- `S_Hb = -change_30020` (larger means more rapidly falling haemoglobin);
- `S_MCV = -change_30040` (larger means more rapidly falling MCV);
- `S_PLT = +change_30080` (larger means more rapidly rising platelet count).

The primary comparator includes completed age at `L`, sex, repeat haemoglobin `30020-1.0`, repeat MCV `30040-1.0`, and repeat platelet count `30080-1.0`. The trajectory model adds exactly the three continuous one-degree-of-freedom adverse-oriented slopes. No outcome-driven marker, threshold, window, transformation, or interaction selection is allowed. RDW 30070 and `S_RDW=change_30070` are a separately labeled four-marker secondary analysis and cannot replace platelet 30080.

### Outcomes and estimands

The primary event is first CRC in `[E,H]`. First non-CRC malignancy and death before CRC are competing events. For the paired specificity analysis, first non-CRC malignancy is the target and CRC/death are competing events. Administrative censoring uses only certified observation boundaries.

Fit cause-specific Cox models with delayed entry at `E` for each cause and derive individual CRC and non-CRC cumulative incidence by `H`; report nonparametric Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence. Fine–Gray is sensitivity only. The prediction pipeline uses low-dimensional ridge regularization and identical participant-level repeated outer splits for comparator and trajectory models. All standardization, permitted imputation, penalty tuning, censoring estimation, weight truncation, and calibration fitting are training-fold-local. No test-fold information may affect preprocessing.

Frozen primary estimands are:

- `Delta_AUC_CRC`: paired out-of-fold change in cumulative/dynamic discrimination for CRC by `H`, trajectory minus comparator;
- `Delta_IBS_CRC`: paired out-of-fold change in IPCW integrated Brier score over `[E,H]`, trajectory minus comparator, where negative favors trajectories;
- `D = Delta_AUC_CRC - Delta_AUC_nonCRC`, calculated on the same eligible participants and split schedule.

Report participant-bootstrap 95% intervals for paired changes and `D`. Also report horizon calibration intercept/slope and observed-to-expected summaries only when certified censoring, event, and effective-sample-size gates pass. Cause-specific slope coefficients and a three-degree-of-freedom global slope-block test are secondary association summaries; positive adverse-oriented coefficients must not be called causal. Fixed 1%, 2%, and 5% predicted-CIF quantile summaries are descriptive distributions only and must not be called capacity yield or clinical utility.

### Event and support gates

- At least 60 coverage-valid CRC events: execute locked prediction, association, competing-risk, and calibration analyses.
- 30–59 CRC events: execute only parsimonious association and descriptive censoring-aware cumulative-incidence analyses; incremental prediction and strong calibration claims are inconclusive.
- Fewer than 30 CRC events: report flow, events, competing events, Aalen–Johansen curves, and at most the simplest prespecified association with prominent imprecision; do not interpret incremental prediction.

In every tier, require finite fold predictions, no empty event folds, adequate censoring-weight effective sample size, positive-definite covariance, convergence, and clean leakage tests. A support failure cannot be repaired after outcome inspection by changing folds, markers, windows, penalties, or endpoints.

`P(recorded CRC | recorded malignant diagnosis in [E,H], eligible recorded history, observed covariates and ascertainment selection)`.

This is a selected-case case-type contrast. It is not population CRC probability, incidence, prognosis, survival, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, lead time, screening performance, or referral utility. Conditioning on future recorded malignancy can induce collider bias.

## Decision rules and meanings

### Supportive

Branch A is supportive only if all semantic, coverage, event-support, censoring/weight, calibration, numerical, and falsification gates pass; the adverse-oriented slope block is directionally coherent; `Delta_AUC_CRC>0` with a 95% interval excluding zero; `Delta_IBS_CRC<0` without calibration deterioration; and CRC-specificity `D>0` with a 95% interval excluding zero. This would support incremental prognosis in this certified repeat-attender risk set, not screening utility or benefit.

Branch B support, in an independent approved study, would require reproducible positive case-case increment, adequate class support, acceptable selected-case calibration, and clean falsification. It would support only a conditional recorded-case difference.

### Adverse

A precise Branch A interval excluding a prespecified meaningful benefit and showing no Brier/calibration gain would discourage these sparse protocolized trajectories for this defined risk set. Because no clinical equivalence margin is currently justified, simple non-rejection is not equivalence. A precise deterioration may be adverse for the frozen model but not proof that all serial CBC use is futile.

A precise Branch B deterioration would weaken the selected-case specificity hypothesis only. Failed nuisance calibration, positivity, coverage, or semantic gates are **uncertified/inconclusive**, not adverse biological evidence.

### Inconclusive

Broad intervals, low event support, discordant AUC/Brier/calibration, unstable weights, failed falsification, unresolved coverage, semantic failure, or calibration failure are inconclusive. Existing selected-case results fall in the non-supportive/inconclusive-or-uncertified category. No result may be relabeled after model inspection to favor the hypothesis.
