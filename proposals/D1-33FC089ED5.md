# Asynchronous Organ Recovery: Persistent Respiratory Failure After Circulatory Stabilization and Risk of Circulatory Re-Escalation

## Expert Seed
[starting question] (asynchronous organ recovery)
Parent: [prior hypothesis]

## Scientific Question

After circulatory stabilization (>=24h without any vasopressor inputevents row, following >=1h of vasopressor documentation), does persistent invasive ventilation (invasive ventilation continuing >=24h after stabilization without extubation) identify patients at higher risk of subsequent circulatory re-escalation (new vasopressor) and in-hospital death than patients with respiratory recovery (extubated within 24h of stabilization), after severity adjustment?

## Clinical Importance

In critical care, organ support is weaned independently: vasopressors are discontinued when hemodynamics stabilize, and mechanical ventilation is weaned when respiratory mechanics and gas exchange permit. These processes are often asynchronous. A patient whose vasopressors have been stopped for 24h but who remains on the ventilator may have unresolved pulmonary injury, ongoing sepsis, or critical illness myopathy that will eventually manifest as circulatory decompensation. Conversely, a patient extubated promptly after vasopressor discontinuation may have a more favorable trajectory.

No prior work inspected here defines a circulatory-stabilization index time (t0) and tests whether the respiratory state at t0 predicts subsequent circulatory re-escalation. The closest prior work [K1] conditions on the extubation event and asks about outcomes of extubation while on vasopressors, not the reverse temporal order. [K2] establishes that prolonged mechanical ventilation (days to weeks) is prognostic, but does not test a 24h post-stabilization window. [K3] validates vasopressor-free days as a trial endpoint but does not examine re-escalation risk stratified by respiratory status at a prespecified index time.

## Leading Explanation and Rival Hypotheses

**Leading explanation (asynchronous recovery):** Persistent ventilation at t0 reflects unresolved respiratory injury or ongoing systemic inflammation that will independently drive circulatory decompensation. The ventilator is a marker of a disease process that has not fully resolved, and the circulatory system will eventually fail despite initial stabilization.

**Rival 1 (severity confounding):** Patients who remain ventilated at t0 are simply sicker overall (higher SOFA, more comorbidities, more severe sepsis), and the apparent association between persistent ventilation and re-escalation is entirely explained by baseline severity. If severity adjustment eliminates the association, the leading explanation is weakened.

**Rival 2 (treatment/selection artifact):** Clinicians may keep patients on the ventilator longer when they anticipate hemodynamic instability (i.e., ventilation is a proxy for clinician expectation of decompensation), or may stop vasopressors earlier in patients who are already expected to fail. This would create a spurious association between ventilation status at t0 and subsequent re-escalation.

**Observation that distinguishes them:** If the leading explanation is correct, the association between persistent ventilation and re-escalation should persist after adjusting for measured severity (age, sex, HR, SBP, SpO2, lactate, vasopressor duration, ventilation time before t0). If Rival 1 is correct, the association should attenuate substantially or disappear after adjustment. If Rival 2 is correct, the association should be concentrated in specific subgroups (e.g., patients with very short vasopressor-free windows, or those with very high baseline severity) and should not be robust to sensitivity analyses that vary the stabilization window.

**Population rules:**
- Adult: age >= 18 at admission (patients.anchor_age >= 18)
- First ICU stay only (earliest intime per subject_id)
- ICU LOS >= 2 days (icustays.los >= 2.0)
- Valid stabilization time t0 before ICU outtime

**t0 definition (stabilization time):**
- Requires >= 1h total vasopressor documentation before t0 (sum of overlap of vasopressor inputevents intervals with (-inf, t0))
- t0 = start of the first >=24h gap in vasopressor administration that ends before ICU outtime
- Multiple vasopressors: any of the 11 itemids counts; gaps are computed on the union of all vasopressor intervals
- Gaps < 24h: do not qualify as stabilization; the next gap is considered
- Re-escalation before window completes: if a new vasopressor row appears within the 24h gap, that gap does not qualify; t0 is not defined for that stay (or the next gap is used if it qualifies)
- Ventilation started after t0: does not affect t0 definition; a patient who is not ventilated at t0 but is intubated at t0+12h is classified as "novent" (not ventilated at t0)

**Phenotype (respiratory state at t0):**
- **failure**: invasive ventilation (procedureevents itemid 225792) active at t0 (interval overlaps t0) AND no extubation event (itemid 227194, 225468, or 225477) in (t0, t0+24h]
- **recovery**: invasive ventilation active at t0 AND extubation event in (t0, t0+24h]
- **novent**: invasive ventilation NOT active at t0 (reported separately, excluded from primary analysis)

**Outcomes:**
- **Primary: circulatory re-escalation** — any new vasopressor inputevents row (any of the 11 itemids) with starttime > t0
- **Co-primary: in-hospital death** — admissions.deathtime not null for the stay's (subject_id, hadm_id)
- **Secondary: ICU mortality** — admissions.deathtime <= icustays.outtime (proxy; MIMIC-IV does not have a direct ICU-death flag)

## Analysis Plan

### Primary analysis
1. **Unadjusted**: re-escalation rate and in-hospital death rate in failure vs recovery groups, with 95% CIs (Wald). Risk difference and risk ratio with 95% CIs.
2. **Severity-adjusted**: logistic regression for each outcome (re-escalation, in-hospital death), with covariates:
   - age (anchor_age at admission)
   - sex (gender)
   - HR at t0 (last chartevents 220045 value in [t0-2h, t0])
   - SBP at t0 (last chartevents 220179 value in [t0-2h, t0])
   - SpO2 at t0 (last chartevents 220277 value in [t0-2h, t0])
   - lactate at t0 (last labevents lactate value in [t0-24h, t0])
   - vasopressor duration before t0 (hours, from probe1)
   - ventilation time before t0 (hours, from probe2)
   - Report adjusted odds ratios with 95% CIs.
3. **Unadjusted vs adjusted gap**: compare unadjusted RR with adjusted OR; if the adjusted OR attenuates by >50% from the unadjusted RR, this supports Rival 1 (severity confounding).

### Sensitivity analyses (5 prespecified)
1. **No-support window 12h**: t0 defined as start of first >=12h vasopressor-free gap (instead of 24h). Tests whether the stabilization window length matters.
2. **No-support window 48h**: t0 defined as start of first >=48h vasopressor-free gap. Tests whether a longer stabilization window changes the phenotype distribution or outcome association.
3. **Exclude ventilated >72h pre-t0**: exclude patients with invasive ventilation duration before t0 > 72h. Tests whether the association is driven by very long pre-existing ventilation.
4. **All stays, not just first**: include all ICU stays (not restricted to first stay). Tests whether the first-stay restriction is driving the result.
5. **Require >=2 vasopressor types**: require at least 2 distinct vasopressor itemids before t0. Tests whether the result is driven by single-agent (typically norepinephrine) cases vs multi-agent shock.
6. **Exclude re-escalation within 2h of t0**: exclude patients whose first post-t0 vasopressor row occurs within 2h of t0. Tests whether the result is driven by immediate re-escalation (possibly a documentation artifact or borderline stabilization).

### Method alternatives record

**Chosen: unadjusted rates + severity-adjusted logistic regression.**
- Baseline: unadjusted rates with 95% CIs. This is the simplest possible analysis and establishes the raw association.
- Learned/mechanistic alternative: severity-adjusted logistic regression. This adds the ability to test whether the association is explained by measured severity (Rival 1). The logistic model is interpretable (odds ratios), appropriate for binary outcomes, and computationally trivial for N ~ 8,000.
- **Deferred: ML (gradient boosting, random forest) or Fine-Gray competing-risk models.** Justification: (a) the primary outcome is a binary event (re-escalation yes/no), not a time-to-event with competing risks, so Fine-Gray is not the natural framework; (b) ML would add predictive performance but not mechanistic insight into whether the association is severity-driven; (c) the sample size (~8,000) is adequate for logistic regression with 8 covariates; (d) the scientific question is about the magnitude and robustness of the association, not about building a prediction tool. If the logistic model shows a strong, robust association, a subsequent study could explore ML for prediction.

## Falsification Criteria

- **F1 (supportive)**: Adjusted OR for re-escalation in failure vs recovery >= 1.5 with 95% CI not crossing 1.0, AND the association is present in at least 4 of 5 sensitivity analyses. Interpretation: persistent ventilation at t0 is an independent marker of circulatory re-escalation risk, supporting the asynchronous-recovery hypothesis.
- **F2 (adverse)**: Adjusted OR for re-escalation in failure vs recovery < 1.2 OR 95% CI crosses 1.0 in the primary analysis. Interpretation: persistent ventilation at t0 does not independently identify higher re-escalation risk after severity adjustment; the unadjusted association (if any) is explained by severity. The asynchronous-recovery hypothesis is not supported by this data.
- **F3 (inconclusive)**: Adjusted OR is 1.2-1.5 with 95% CI crossing 1.0, OR the association is present in only 1-2 of 5 sensitivity analyses. Interpretation: the data are insufficient to distinguish the leading explanation from severity confounding; a larger or more precisely phenotyped study is needed.
- **F4 (phenotype feasibility)**: N_failure < 500 OR N_recovery < 200 after all exclusions. Interpretation: the phenotype is too rare to support a powered analysis; the study design needs revision (e.g., different stabilization window, different ventilation definition).
