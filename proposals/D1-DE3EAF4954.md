# Recorded renal trajectory, nutrition observability, and detected hypoglycemia after insulin in eICU

## Status and scientific deliverable

This is a data-bound, noncausal eICU study proposal. It is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`, which imported expert seed `[starting question]`. The seed's hypothesis is preserved, but its language is narrowed to what this snapshot can observe.

The future solver must newly construct a frozen, auditable insulin-exposure event ledger and fit two matched models. Completion requires:

1. a cohort-flow and field-semantics audit, including insulin-label, glucose-unit, creatinine-measurement, nutrition-label, and observation-coverage tables;
2. patient-level and hospital-level split manifests;
3. the six-hour detected-hypoglycemia cumulative-incidence contrasts and their uncertainty;
4. calibration and transport results for both methods;
5. an observation-process decomposition (glucose observed versus not observed, and low versus non-low among observations);
6. the pre-specified falsification and sensitivity analyses; and
7. a conclusion explicitly bounded by the recorded-data estimand.

No fitted result is asserted here.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The unresolved claim is:

> Among ICU stays with a qualifying recorded insulin-infusion signal, does a recent upward trajectory in recorded creatinine, particularly when no qualifying nutrition event is documented before the exposure, identify higher six-hour risk of *detected* hypoglycemia at comparable recorded infusion intensity?

The primary interaction hypothesis is that the renal-trajectory contrast is larger in the “no documented nutrition event” stratum than in the “documented nutrition event” stratum, after standardization over the recorded pre-exposure history and insulin intensity. The estimand is an observational association in the eICU recording process. It is not an insulin-treatment effect, a causal effect of fasting, a diagnosis of acute kidney injury, or evidence that any event was preventable.

“Worsening kidney function” means an upward change in serial, valid, comparable recorded creatinine measurements. “No documented nutrition event” means that the qualifying nutrition lexicon is absent from the specified lookback window. It does not mean that the patient was fasting, that feeds stopped, or that intake was zero.

### What the available evidence supports

The local eICU documentation supports that:

- the database contains time-offset longitudinal data linked by `patientunitstayid`;
- `medication` contains medication orders and start/stop offsets, but orders do not necessarily mean administration;
- continuous-infusion titration is represented in `infusionDrug`;
- different care units can have different interfaces, so absent rows can reflect absent observation rather than absent physiology.

The imported expert card supports only an untested clinical question. It supplies no effect size, cutoff, validated nutrition label, or administration guarantee. The local research-ambition demonstrations justify considering a time-aware alternative and strict temporal validation, but they do not establish this eICU hypothesis. The available materials do not constitute a systematic novelty review.

The substantive advance is an observation-aware, multi-center test that separates (i) recorded infusion exposure, (ii) recorded renal trajectory, (iii) nutrition documentation opportunity, and (iv) glucose ascertainment. Existing seed wording collapses those processes. The study can therefore determine whether a signal is reproducible as a documented safety pattern and whether a dynamic representation adds clinically meaningful information beyond a transparent landmark summary.

## Population and temporal design

The unit of analysis is the ICU stay’s first eligible recorded insulin-infusion episode. The primary population is:

- an adult stay with numeric `age >= 18`;
- a valid `patientunitstayid`, `uniquepid`, and `hospitalid`;
- an ICU observation endpoint in `unitdischargeoffset`;
- a first qualifying insulin row in `infusionDrug` between 24 and 72 hours after unit admission;
- at least six hours between the index `infusionoffset` and `min(unitdischargeoffset, index+360)`;
- a numeric, auditable infusion-intensity value from `drugrate` or `infusionrate`, with the selected field and unit/formulation rule frozen after the source audit.

The 24-hour lower boundary supplies a lookback for renal trajectory and avoids mixing a very early exposure with an incomplete history. A secondary all-time analysis starts at six hours and marks missing renal history rather than silently excluding it. A recurrent-episode sensitivity uses later infusion episodes separated by at least six hours, with patient-clustered uncertainty; it is not the primary estimand.

A qualifying infusion name is created from an auditable, versioned lexical dictionary of insulin formulations. The dictionary is frozen after listing all matched and unmatched names. Non-insulin drugs, ambiguous strings, and rows with no defensible rate are excluded from the primary intensity analysis and reported in the flow table. The actual numeric meaning and unit of `drugrate` versus `infusionrate` must be established from observed values and documentation; if no common dose unit can be verified, intensity is represented only as a within-formulation standardized recorded rate, and no unit-based dose claim is permitted.

The `medication` table is not primary administration evidence. Its `drugstartoffset` and `drugstopoffset` can define an order/start-record sensitivity, with `drugordercancelled`, `routeadmin`, `dosage`, and `frequency` retained. That sensitivity must be labeled “insulin order/start signal.” A pattern found only in orders cannot support a claim about administered insulin.

All source rows used at a time t must satisfy the appropriate ICU-relative event offset <= t. For `intakeOutput`, `intakeoutputoffset` is the event/measurement time and `intakeoutputentryoffset` is retained for a delayed-entry sensitivity. For `lab`, `labresultoffset` is the primary result time; a result whose `labresultrevisedoffset` is after the prediction time is not allowed into that prediction’s features. No post-index value is used in the time-zero baseline.

## Exposure, predictors, and missingness safeguards

### Recorded renal trajectory

Within [-24 hours, index time], identify creatinine rows using a frozen audit of `labname`, `labmeasurenamesystem`, and `labmeasurenameinterface`. Use `labresult` only after numeric and physiologic-range checks and after confirming comparable measurement labels/interfaces. The index value is the most recent valid result before t0. The prior reference is the minimum valid result in the preceding 24 hours, requiring at least two values separated by six hours. Define:

- `creatinine_latest`;
- `creatinine_delta = latest - prior_minimum`;
- `creatinine_trend_observed`;
- a sensitivity flag `creatinine_delta >= 0.3` only if the unit and timing audit support that threshold.

The 0.3 rule is an operational sensitivity, not a KDIGO diagnosis. No urine-output or dialysis criterion is substituted. The `apacheApsVar.creatinine` value is not silently merged with time-stamped lab values.

The primary renal contrast is a prespecified contrast between stable/non-increasing and upward recorded trajectory within the trend-observable cohort, expressed per prespecified standardized delta and additionally by quantiles determined in training data. Episodes without adequate serial creatinine are retained in the all-exposure flow and missingness sensitivity; they are not called stable.

### Nutrition documentation and observability

The primary nutrition signal is built from `intakeOutput`, not from absence of rows. Construct a frozen lexicon from `cellpath`, `celllabel`, and `cellvaluetext) for explicit oral meal/diet, enteral/tube feeding, and parenteral/TPN nutrition. A qualifying event requires a timestamp in [-6 hours, 0] and either a defensible positive `cellvaluenumeric`/total or an explicit active/provided status after manual rule review. The audit must publish matched labels, counts, value distributions, and examples locally.

The primary nutrition variable has three levels:

1. documented nutrition event in the six-hour lookback;
2. no documented nutrition event but adequate intake-output interface coverage for the stay/window;
3. nutrition status unobserved or label coverage inadequate.

The second level means “no qualifying documentation found,” never “fasting.” A secondary gap phenotype requires a qualifying nutrition event in [-12,-6] and no event in [-6,0], but remains a documented gap, not cessation. `intakeoutputentryoffset` and `nurseCharting` fields are used in delayed-entry and documentation-augmentation sensitivities. If no stable nutrition lexicon exists in the actual values, the nutrition hypothesis is not estimable; the solver must report only an observability analysis rather than invent a label.

### Glucose outcome and observation process

Identify glucose results from `labname`, `labresult`, `labresulttext`, `labmeasurenamesystem), and `labmeasurenameinterface). The primary endpoint is the first result <70 mg/dL in (t0, t0+360] before unit exit, with <54 mg/dL as a severe-hypoglycemia sensitivity. The threshold is admitted only after a source-specific unit audit; the schema does not contain a universal unit column. Unknown or mixed-unit results are excluded from the primary endpoint and reported. If units cannot be verified, the primary clinical threshold analysis is inconclusive.

A low result is a detected low result, not proof of all hypoglycemia. No glucose row is not a non-low glucose. For every episode report:

- any glucose observation in the six-hour window;
- first detected low and severe low;
- first observed non-low;
- no glucose observed before unit exit;
- time to first glucose observation and number of tests.

Unit death/expired status and other unit discharge are competing exits for the event-history analysis. A stay ending before six hours is censored/competing according to its recorded `unitdischargestatus`; the solver must not carry observation beyond `unitdischargeoffset`.

Pre-index predictors include last/minimum/maximum glucose and test count in [-6,0], time since last glucose, the renal variables, the nutrition documentation level, recorded infusion intensity, prior insulin signals, age, sex, ethnicity, unit type, admission source, admission diagnosis, and hospital characteristics. Missingness indicators and “not observed” categories are explicit. Static APACHE fields are a sensitivity only after timing audit.

## Matched method comparison

Both methods use the same episode ledger, predictors’ source fields, 30-minute outcome bins, hospital-held-out split manifests, training-only preprocessing, and detected-hypoglycemia/competing-exit outcomes. The comparison is scientific: does preserving order, persistence, changing infusion rate, documentation opportunity, and glucose-observation timing reveal a clinically interpretable risk pattern that a time-zero summary loses?

### Transparent landmark baseline

Fit a prespecified pooled-logistic landmark model for the six 30-minute bins after t0, with an additive logit and only a limited set of clinically interpretable terms:

- recorded infusion intensity and formulation stratum;
- current creatinine and recorded delta;
- nutrition documentation level;
- pre-index glucose level/history and testing intensity;
- age, sex, ethnicity, unit type, admission source, and selected pre-index insulin history;
- renal-delta × nutrition-documentation interaction;
- flexible but prespecified restricted-linear/spline terms for glucose, creatinine and intensity;
- bin-time indicators and competing unit-exit indicators.

The model reports standardized six-hour detected-low cumulative incidence and the renal-by-nutrition contrast. It is transparent enough to inspect coefficient direction, missingness categories, calibration intercept/slope, and partial-standardized contrasts. Penalization, if needed for separation, is selected in the training folds only and disclosed.

This baseline compresses the pre-index history to summaries and treats a six-hour risk as one landmark outcome. It does not say whether a low result follows a rate escalation, whether risk persists after a transient creatinine rise, whether nutrition documentation occurs before or after a glucose test, or whether a “no event” episode simply had no glucose measured.

### Dynamic observation-aware multi-state alternative

Fit a hierarchical discrete-time multi-state model on the same 30-minute bins. The clinical states are at-risk/no detected low, detected low, severe detected low, and unit exit; severe low is nested/reported separately rather than double-counted. A companion observation process models whether a glucose result is recorded in each bin, with past testing intensity, unit type, nutrition documentation, and prior state available at that bin.

At every bin, update:

- insulin on/off and the most recent recorded infusion rate from `infusionDrug`;
- time since the last infusion record and rate change;
- last observed glucose and time since measurement;
- latest creatinine and its running change when newly measured;
- new intake-output nutrition documentation and time since the last qualifying event;
- time since index, unit-exit risk, and missingness/coverage state.

Use ordered transitions and a hospital random effect estimated in training hospitals, with shrinkage or a pooled intercept for held-out hospitals. The model may use maximum likelihood or a documented approximate Bayesian fit; uncertainty must be quantified by hospital-cluster bootstrap or posterior intervals, not by treating rows as independent.

This alternative reveals transition timing, persistence, rate-change windows, renal trajectory updating, and the difference between “not tested” and “tested non-low.” It can show whether the baseline’s interaction is a stable process pattern or an artifact of one time-zero summary. It does not identify unrecorded hypoglycemia or causal mechanisms. A neural sequence model is deferred: the dynamic state model directly tests the scientific uncertainty with greater interpretability and lower dependency risk. It may be revisited only if the event ledger is sufficiently dense and the state model fails a prespecified adequacy check.

### Common estimand and comparison

For each method, standardize over the training-fold empirical covariate distribution and report:

- six-hour detected-low cumulative incidence;
- the renal-trajectory contrast at each nutrition-documentation level;
- the interaction contrast (difference of renal contrasts);
- the analogous severe-low result;
- glucose-observation probability and detected-low risk conditional on at least one glucose observation;
- unit-exit competing incidence;
- calibration intercept, slope, Brier score, reliability curve, and event-rate-stratified calibration;
- paired method differences in the primary interaction contrast and in held-out calibration.

The primary result is not “the dynamic model wins.” A method is scientifically useful only if it changes the interpretation of the renal/nutrition association, improves calibration or observation-process decomposition materially, and remains transportable. A small discrimination gain without a clearer clinical process interpretation is not a substantive advance.

## Split, transport, uncertainty, and analysis

Use hospital-held-out validation. Hash-sort unique `hospitalid` values with a published fixed seed into five folds. In each rotation, hold one hospital fold out for final evaluation; split the remaining hospitals into training and validation by a second fixed hash, keeping every `uniquepid` and all its stays in one partition. Fit lexicons, unit maps, quantile cutpoints, preprocessing, regularization and model parameters using training data only. No hospital identifier is used as a transferable patient feature in the held-out score.

Primary inference uses hospital-clustered uncertainty, with patient-level resampling inside the training data where appropriate and a hospital bootstrap for the transport interval. Report event counts and effective hospitals in every fold. Sparse strata, separation, or fewer than a prespecified minimum number of supported hospitals are reported as instability, not repaired by changing the estimand after seeing results.

The primary analysis is a model-based standardized association. Do not call it an adjusted causal effect, treatment effect, mediation, preventable fraction, or recommendation for insulin adjustment. A directed causal interpretation would require reliable administered dose, nutritional delivery/interruption, medication timing, renal physiology including urine output/dialysis, and clinical confounders that this snapshot does not establish.

## Falsification and sensitivity criteria

Run these before interpreting supportive results:

1. **Exposure-order falsification.** Repeat the pipeline with medication-table insulin start/order signals, explicitly not administration. A strong result confined to orders and absent from infusion records is evidence of exposure misclassification or confounding, not support for administered-insulin risk.
2. **Temporal permutation.** Permute eligible infusion start offsets within stay among comparable exposure records, preserving hospital, stay length and exposure count. The primary renal-by-nutrition interaction and time-local transition pattern should attenuate toward the null; failure indicates time-structure leakage or strong shared scheduling artifacts.
3. **Nutrition timestamp shift.** Shift qualifying nutrition timestamps by a fixed post-index offset in a diagnostic sensitivity while preserving labels. The pre-index interaction should attenuate if it depends on correct temporal ordering.
4. **Observation decomposition.** Compare complete observed-glucose episodes, observation-weighted estimates based only on past coverage, and the explicit no-observation competing category. A result that disappears after accounting for testing opportunity is a documentation/ascertainment finding.
5. **Creatinine definitions.** Compare continuous delta, the unit-verified 0.3 sensitivity, latest creatinine alone, and a stricter two-measure definition. Do not label any version AKI without the missing clinical criteria.
6. **Nutrition-source sensitivity.** Compare intake-output only with a pre-specified augmentation from `nurseCharting`. If nurse labels are not stable or are entered after the event, retain the intake-output result as primary and call the augmentation infeasible.
7. **Leakage checks.** Re-run with revised lab rows excluded whenever `labresultrevisedoffset` exceeds prediction time, and verify no feature uses rows after each 30-minute prediction boundary.

A falsification failure does not prove the clinical hypothesis false; it invalidates the corresponding interpretation of the signal.

## Interpretation rules

Supportive evidence requires a nontrivial, uncertainty-quantified renal-by-nutrition interaction in the detected-low estimand, stable direction across the renal definitions and observation analyses, no material temporal-permutation signal, acceptable calibration, and at least some hospital-held-out transport. It would support a reproducible recorded-data safety association and justify expert review of renal monitoring, nutrition documentation, and insulin administration workflows.

Adverse evidence is a precisely estimated null or opposite interaction with adequate event support, valid glucose units, and stable held-out calibration. This would weaken the proposed recorded association in this eICU snapshot; it would not prove that renal impairment or absent nutrition never alters true insulin-related hypoglycemia.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse low events, unverified glucose units, no stable nutrition lexicon, inadequate creatinine pairs, inability to distinguish infusion from order records, severe calibration failure, or very wide transport intervals. In that case the correct output is a data-availability/observability result and a next-study specification, not a directional claim.

The study cannot establish true unmeasured hypoglycemia, that nutrition was absent, insulin was administered at the recorded dose, renal clearance changed, an event was preventable, or a safe dose adjustment. Those claims require clinical adjudication and/or another study with medication administration records, insulin units and delivery timestamps, feed delivery and interruptions, point-of-care glucose measurements, validated renal outcomes including urine output and dialysis initiation, bedside confounders, and prospective or external validation.

## Alternatives not chosen and revisit evidence

- A medication-order-only study was not chosen because local documentation explicitly says orders do not necessarily reflect administration. It remains a measurement-error sensitivity.
- A broad “no intake rows = fasting” phenotype was rejected because interface absence and delayed documentation make that biologically invalid. Revisit only if feed-delivery timestamps and a validated nutrition adjudication lexicon are obtained.
- A full AKI endpoint using KDIGO criteria was deferred because the available binding does not guarantee a clinical baseline, urine-output interval semantics, or dialysis initiation timing. Revisit with validated creatinine baseline, urine output and renal-support adjudication.
- A neural recurrent model was deferred in favor of the dynamic multi-state model because the current scientific uncertainty is temporal ordering and observability, not marginal prediction. Revisit if event density supports a pre-registered incremental-information test after the state model, with the same split/outcome and calibration criteria.
- A causal insulin-dose analysis was not chosen because administered dose and treatment assignment are not identified. Revisit only with reliable administration records, dose units, feed interruptions, and a credible causal design.
- An early-exposure cohort was not primary because the renal lookback and six-hour follow-up are incomplete. It is a secondary all-time analysis with explicit missingness.
