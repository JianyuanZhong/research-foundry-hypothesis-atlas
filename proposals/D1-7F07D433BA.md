# Does observed arrival organ injury explain the 48-hour ICU-unit death risk contrast of interhospital admissions?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly build an auditable eICU arrival-risk experiment and estimate whether the observed risk contrast between adults admitted to the ICU from Other Hospital and otherwise comparable adults labelled Direct Admit is attenuated after accounting for organ injury observed at or immediately after ICU arrival.

The primary deliverable is the hospital-transportable standardized difference in cumulative incidence of recorded ICU-unit death by 2,880 minutes after ICU admission between the two observed source groups, first source-only and then adjusted for a prespecified arrival injury vector. Report alive ICU discharge/transfer as a competing exit and unknown/missing status as censoring. The experiment is not a causal effect of transfer.

The advance over the expert seed is a fixed landmark, exact source-bound exposure, explicit organ domains and measurement opportunity, competing exits, hospital-held-out transport, and a mechanistic trajectory alternative. It tests whether observed transfer status remains informative after early physiologic case-mix alignment, and whether any residual contrast is concentrated in early worsening or failure to recover.

## Evidence boundary and hypothesis

Before fitting, the strongest supported claim is data availability: the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains hospital admission-source labels, ICU-relative discharge offsets/statuses, hospital identifiers, APACHE fields, and time-stamped ICU measurements joinable on patientunitstayid. eICU documentation says interfaces differ across units, so missing measurements do not prove that they were not obtained.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

Among adult first ICU admissions labelled Other Hospital or Direct Admit, the standardized 48-hour cumulative incidence of recorded ICU-unit death is higher for Other Hospital in a source-only comparison, but the absolute contrast is materially attenuated after adjustment for the prespecified 0–60-minute arrival organ-injury vector and measurement opportunity.

This is observational and prognostic. It does not claim transfer, delay, referral quality, or treatment causes death. A null, reversal, no attenuation, loss after missingness/interface adjustment, or failure to transport is adverse. Sparse overlap, inadequate event ascertainment, or unstable timing semantics is inconclusive.

## Population, exposure, time, and outcome

Population: patient rows with parsable age >=18, unitvisitnumber=1, unitstaytype=admit, nonmissing hospitalid, and hospitaladmitsource exactly Other Hospital or Direct Admit. This excludes later ICU transfers and readmissions. The local audit found 12,688 adults: 9,771 Direct Admit and 2,917 Other Hospital, across 179 hospitals.

Index time is ICU admission, the zero point for ICU-relative offsets. Do not use hospitaladmitoffset as the index; it can be negative relative to ICU admission. Record it descriptively and use it only for a sensitivity describing pre-ICU hospital time.

Exposure is patient.hospitaladmitsource. unitadmitsource is retained for concordance diagnostics and is not substituted for the primary exposure. Labels are recorded categories; no exact transport route or pre-arrival course is inferred.

Primary arrival window: raw ICU-relative offset 0 through 60 minutes. For multiple rows, sort by source time and source row ID, use the first valid value, and preserve value-present and measurement-count/opportunity indicators. Treat -1 and equivalent sentinels as missing. Numeric lab use requires a frozen name/unit rule; labresulttext is audit-only. The 0–60-minute window is pragmatic, not proof of pre-transfer physiology.

Outcome ledger from patient: event 1 is unitdischargestatus=Expired and 0 <= unitdischargeoffset <= 2880; competing event 2 is unitdischargestatus=Alive and 0 <= unitdischargeoffset <= 2880, regardless of location; administrative censoring at 2,880 for a stay still in the unit; unknown-status censoring for missing/invalid status or offset. An expired discharge after 2,880 is not a 48-hour event. Do not use hospital-discharge status as the primary outcome. The local audit found 425 death events and 6,473 alive exits by 2,880; these counts are not an analysis result.

## Analysis, uncertainty, and transport

Freeze transformations before outcome comparison and produce cohort flow, arm counts by hospital, source concordance, missingness, measurement opportunity, and APACHE duplicate handling.

Reserve approximately 20% of hospitals as held-out test hospitals using a fixed seed, stratified by size and presence of both arms. No held-out patient may inform fitting, imputation, feature selection, or calibration. Report test hospitals without both arms rather than extrapolating silently.

Fit B0 and B1 as pooled 30-minute cause-specific hazards through 2,880 minutes with death as event, alive exit as competing event, and unknown/missing as censoring. Use hospital-clustered uncertainty or hospital-block bootstrap. Standardize predictions to the same target population and report risk differences/ratios with 95% intervals, calibration, Brier score, and decision-curve net benefit at a predeclared triage-review threshold if event support permits. Discrimination is secondary. The central result is B0-to-B1 attenuation.

The primary injury vector uses only 0–60-minute features. A secondary dynamic model may use strictly lagged 30-minute history through 360 minutes to test early worsening/recovery. M1 uses identical stays, features, outcome, split, and standardization; predeclare state thresholds from clinical units/source values, assign missing rather than outcome-derived states, and gate sparse transitions.

Falsifications: permute source labels within hospital; repeat with unitadmitsource only as a concordance exposure; restrict to hospitals with >=20 adults in each arm; test single-hospital dominance; stratify/exclude extreme hospitaladmitoffset; use lab revision time and alternative first-valid rules; compare death with alive-exit incidence and hospital-discharge status only as endpoint diagnostics; report APACHE-free and APACHE-secondary versions. No placebo is interpreted as a clinical outcome.

## Interpretation and evidence limits

Supportive evidence requires a directionally consistent source-only contrast with a predeclared 2-percentage-point margin, at least 50% attenuation after arrival adjustment with uncertainty compatible with that threshold, calibrated held-out direction, and (if supported) M1 localization to prespecified worsening/recovery states. These support an observational arrival-risk pattern, not efficacy.

Adverse evidence is reproducible null/reversal, no attenuation, or loss in held-out hospitals. It does not prove transfer is harmless. Inconclusive evidence is sparse overlap, missing outcome/status, unstable timing, wide intervals, or failed state support.

A supportive result could motivate prospective triage tool design or chart review. It cannot establish transfer causality, treatment delay, referral quality, pre-arrival treatment, full hospital mortality, adjudicated organ injury, or clinical utility/harms of acting on a threshold. Those require clinical adjudication, richer transport/pre-arrival data, or another study. The eICU documentation read locally is database documentation, not evidence that this hypothesis is true.

Discovery fitted no model and claims no clinical result.
