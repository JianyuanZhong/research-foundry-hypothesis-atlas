# Proposal: Close the EHR enrichment branch and validate whether a charted restart is a true hemodynamic failure

## Clinical question, advance, and evidence boundary

This successor preserves both registered parents' negative feasibility findings and tests the weak link in the inherited 137-episode branch rather than relaxing any gate. Among 6,040 MAP-eligible repeated landmarks, the assigned actions remained rare: 211 NE-first actions (3.49%) and 167 VP-first actions (2.76%). Both are below the frozen 5% gate, and the 5,638 no-action landmarks are not controls. In the observed-first-stop cohort, there are only 84 NE-first and 53 VP-first episodes, with 49 and 24 determinate failures respectively; these counts still fail the inherited direct-comparison arm/event gates. Nothing in this child reopens an assigned-strategy emulation, estimates a causal order effect, or recommends which pressor to stop first.

The strongest claim supported by the newly completed no-sampling audit is narrower. The chart-derived six-hour endpoint is computable, but it is dominated by restart records: 59 of 73 failures include restart and 36 failures are restart-only. A deliberately narrow automated documentation-transition screen identifies 13 of 59 restarts, including 6 restart-only failures; removing only those six changes total failures from 73 to 67. Thus a plausible transition artifact does not explain most endpoint events, but it affects a clinically nontrivial subset and cannot be distinguished from true resumed drug delivery in MIMIC. Separately, all 137 episodes have complete values for the frozen six-variable pre-stop model, yet that model has essentially no formal out-of-sample Brier improvement and adverse later-period transport. Adding documented first-stop order contributes far less than the frozen 0.01 Brier increment. The prognostic/enrichment branch therefore fails under its own rules.

The unresolved clinically consequential hypothesis is now an endpoint-validity hypothesis:

> **Among prospectively observed adults meeting the frozen dual-low-dose-pressor stop phenotype, an `inputevents`-analog positive interval for the stopped pressor beginning within six hours has positive predictive value at least 0.80 for true resumed pump delivery undertaken for hemodynamic support, and PPV differs by less than 0.10 between NE-first and VP-first episodes.**

“True resumed pump delivery” requires pump-native start/stop and flow data plus blinded adjudication of reason and intent; it is not established by an EHR order boundary. The PPV and arm-symmetry gates are inherited, clinically interpretable, and falsifiable. This endpoint-validation target is a substantive advance over another underpowered association or score: it determines whether the dominant component of the outcome intended for a future randomized discontinuation-order study measures rescue rather than bag/syringe change, order reconciliation, transfer continuation, or chart segmentation.

The current Harbor computation is the retrospective diagnostic phase and has been executed. It tests whether the compact score or an automated restart-cleaning rule is sufficient to advance prospective enrichment. Both are falsified. The next phase is a prospective endpoint-validation study whose required data are specified below. MIMIC supplies the sampling-frame logic and EHR-side candidate event; pump logs, clinician intent, and adjudication are explicitly unavailable and must not be imputed.

Public evidence cited by the parents supports at most heterogeneous short-term hypotension associations, not a universal stopping order. This child does not extend that literature claim. The research-ambition papers remain methodological demonstrations only; no unavailable paper text or supplement is claimed as inspected.

## Frozen retrospective population and temporal boundaries

Use the inherited cohort exactly, without resampling or post-result eligibility repair: all 137 adults at their first qualifying non-simultaneous documented stop in their first ICU stay. Time zero is the end of the reconstructed active interval for the first stopped agent, after at least six hours of concurrent NE and VP, while the comparator remains active. Eligibility was established at or before time zero: alive and in ICU; NE `>0` and `<=0.20 mcg/kg/min`; VP `>0` and `<=0.04 U/min`; neither dose increasing in the prior 30 minutes; no active epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine in the prior two hours; and the inherited pre-stop MAP coverage/stability rule. Future restart, comparator persistence, MAP coverage, ICU residence, and survival are outcomes or ascertainment variables, never eligibility criteria.

Dose normalization remains symmetric and limited to supported units. NE is represented in mcg/kg/min, with mcg/min divided by a plausible recorded weight of 30–300 kg; normalized values above 5 mcg/kg/min are rejected. VP in units/hour is divided by 60, units/min is retained, and values above 0.2 units/min are rejected. Positive intervals require positive rate and `endtime > starttime`. `orderid` and `linkorderid` indicate documentation continuity but are not treated as proof of uninterrupted pump administration.

The inherited outcome window is `(time zero, min(time zero + 6 hours, ICU exit, death)]`. The raw composite remains positive at the first recorded stopped-agent restart, competing/rescue pressor initiation, confirmed NE escalation, repeated MAP below 60 mmHg, death, or pressor-active ICU exit. One inherited episode is indeterminate. This child does not silently replace that endpoint. It reports the raw endpoint and a prespecified diagnostic sensitivity that removes only transition-compatible restart-only events.

## Phase 1: completed Harbor endpoint and prognosis audit

### Restart semantics

For every inherited episode, scan all supported positive rows for the stopped agent beginning after time zero and no later than six hours. The first such row is the raw restart. Preserve its `starttime`, gap from time zero, normalized dose, `rateuom`, `orderid`, `linkorderid`, and `statusdescription`, as well as the corresponding boundary row.

A restart is **transition-compatible** only when all three conditions hold:

1. the restart gap is at most 60 minutes;
2. the boundary and restart share `orderid` or `linkorderid`; and
3. normalized restart dose is within 20% of the boundary dose.

This is intentionally a high-specificity screen for possible chart/order continuity, not a label of bag change and not a claim that the clinical endpoint is false. The diagnostic sensitivity endpoint removes a transition-compatible restart only when restart is the sole raw failure component. Death, competing pressor, NE escalation, repeated low MAP, active-pressor exit, and non-transition-compatible restart remain failures. The raw composite remains primary for describing inherited data.

The completed audit used all 137 inherited rows and found:

- raw failures: 73;
- stopped-agent restarts: 59;
- restart-only failures: 36;
- transition-compatible restarts: 13;
- transition-compatible restart-only failures: 6; and
- sensitivity failures after removing only those six: 67.

For NE-first episodes, 46/84 restarted; median gap was 60 minutes (IQR 31.25–106.75), 24 were within 60 minutes, 24 shared an order chain, 29 were dose-concordant, and 10 met all transition criteria. For VP-first episodes, 13/53 restarted; median gap was 43 minutes (IQR 28–81), 9 were within 60 minutes, 6 shared an order chain, 12 were dose-concordant, and 3 met all criteria. These arm-specific counts are documentation diagnostics only. They cannot establish a causal order effect or even differential artifact rates without pump-ground-truth adjudication.

### MCS status

Freeze the broad `d_items` expression `ecmo|extracorporeal|iabp|intra.?aortic|impella|lvad|rvad|ventricular assist|balloon pump|mechanical circulatory|centrifugal pump|durable vad`. The audit identified 179 dictionary rows: 164 chart item IDs, including 42 selected functional/activity item IDs, 9 datetime item IDs, and 4 procedure item IDs. A computable positive requires either a nonblank/positive functional MCS chart signal in the six hours before time zero or a dictionary-confirmed procedure interval active at time zero. Datetime rows are retained as chronology evidence but do not by themselves prove that a device remains active. No episode in the inherited 137 met this positive rule.

Crucially, absence of such a record is not device absence. Every non-positive episode is classified `MCS-unknown`, yielding 137 unknown and zero validated negative episodes. The earlier inherited proxy screen had already removed one selected proxy-positive case from 138 pre-MAP candidates, but that exclusion does not create a validated MCS-negative cohort. No MCS-restricted prediction, association, safety claim, or future enrollment rule advances from these data.

### Compact prognostic models

On the 136 determinate episodes, compare:

- **M0:** training-set outcome prevalence;
- **M1:** age, log NE dose, log VP dose, log overlap duration, two-hour pre-stop MAP median, and pre-stop MAP OLS slope per minute; and
- **M2:** M1 plus the documented first-stop indicator.

All six M1 features are complete in all 137 inherited rows. Fit L2-penalized logistic regression after training-set standardization. Select `C` only inside the training data by leave-one-anchor-year-group-out Brier loss over a frozen grid. Train once on 2008–2016 determinate episodes (`n=79`), formally test 2017–2019 (`n=36`, 20 raw events), and use 2020–2022 only as a descriptive sparse stress test (`n=21` determinate, 12 raw events). No test data select features, transforms, penalty, or endpoint definition.

For the raw endpoint in the formal 2017–2019 holdout:

- M1 Brier score was 0.248077 versus M0 0.248251, improvement 0.000174 with bootstrap 95% interval -0.000884 to 0.001235; AUROC was 0.566.
- M2 Brier score was 0.247966, only 0.000111 better than M1 and far below the inherited 0.01 incremental-information gate; AUROC was 0.578.
- Calibration slopes were extreme and unstable in the small holdout and therefore do not support transport.

In the 2020–2022 stress test, M1 was worse than M0 by 0.000507 Brier points and AUROC was 0.380; M2 was worse than M0 by 0.000261 and AUROC was 0.472. Calibration slopes were negative. Under the transition-screen sensitivity endpoint, formal M1 improvement remained only 0.000146 and M2 improved on M1 by only 0.000088; both models again worsened Brier score and discriminated below chance in the later stress test. Uncertainty intervals include no improvement throughout.

These results fail the inherited model-transport and observed-order incremental-information gates. No low-risk threshold is selected, no subgroup is called safe, and no score is advanced for prospective enrichment. This is an adverse result for the prognostic hypothesis, not evidence that pre-stop physiology is generally useless or that either drug order is harmful.

## Phase 2: prospective endpoint-validation experiment

### Population and enrollment

At one or more ICUs capable of exporting synchronized smart-pump logs, screen consecutive adults at the bedside using the same forward-time phenotype: concurrent supported NE and VP for at least six hours; NE `>0` to `0.20 mcg/kg/min`; VP `>0` to `0.04 U/min`; no increase in either during the prior 30 minutes; no epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine active in the prior two hours; at least three MAP observations across three of four prior 30-minute bins including the final bin; median MAP at least 65 mmHg and minimum at least 55 mmHg; alive and in ICU. Record but do not infer actual MCS state. Episodes enter the endpoint-validation sample when a clinician documents or executes a non-simultaneous first stop while the comparator remains active. This is observational endpoint validation; treatment is not assigned.

Enroll consecutively until there are at least 50 raw EHR restart candidates in each observed-stop arm, unless a simulation based on blinded pilot prevalence justifies a larger target before any PPV result is unblinded. This event-based minimum supports an approximate PPV standard error no worse than about 0.057 near PPV 0.80, but it is not a definitive clinical trial size. Also retain all eligible stop episodes without restart to estimate sensitivity and negative predictive value; PPV alone cannot validate the full endpoint.

### Exact prospective data required

For every eligible episode, preserve synchronized timestamps and source identifiers for:

1. **Pump-native delivery:** channel and pump identifiers; drug/library identity; concentration; programmed and delivered rate; infusion start, stop, pause, resume, bolus, alarm and channel-association events; seconds-level timestamps; and downtime/export gaps.
2. **Consumable and workflow transitions:** bag/syringe identifier when available; replacement, empty-container, line change, channel handoff, pump replacement, transfer reconciliation and concentration-change flags.
3. **EHR medication record:** the same fields used from MIMIC `inputevents`—drug/item, `starttime`, `endtime`, `storetime`, rate/unit, amount/unit, weight, `orderid`, `linkorderid`, status, continuation to next department—and medication administration/order audit trails when locally available.
4. **Clinician intent:** timestamped stop/restart decision, intended permanent discontinuation versus temporary pause/titration, reason for resumption, rescue intent, and whether the event followed hypotension, perfusion concern, procedure, transport, or documentation correction. A missing reason remains unknown.
5. **Physiology and rescue:** continuous or high-frequency MAP with arterial/non-invasive source and data-quality flags; all vasoactive pump channels; death and ICU transfer; lactate and bedside perfusion markers as descriptive context, not required to manufacture a restart label.
6. **MCS truth:** actual device type, insertion/removal time, active/standby state, flow or support setting, and source confirmation for IABP, Impella, ECMO, LVAD/RVAD or other temporary support.
7. **Clock quality:** mapping among pump, bedside monitor, EHR and adjudication clocks, including synchronization offsets and periods of missing export.

### Reference standard and blinded adjudication

Construct a candidate packet around every EHR restart and a stratified sample of apparent non-restarts. Two blinded critical-care/pharmacy reviewers independently inspect de-identified pump traces, transition flags, time-local physiology, and medication audit trail without seeing stop-arm aggregate outcomes. They classify:

- true resumed delivery for hemodynamic support;
- uninterrupted delivery split by documentation or bag/syringe/channel transition;
- brief technical pause without clinical rescue;
- transfer continuation;
- EHR timestamp mismatch;
- no delivered drug; or
- indeterminate.

Require adjudicator agreement to be reported and resolve disagreement by a third reviewer. The primary reference-positive event requires delivered stopped agent after a genuine pump-off interval plus adjudicated hemodynamic-support intent. Report a secondary pump-only reference that does not require intent, so disagreement about intent is visible rather than silently absorbed into the label. Prespecify clinically meaningful minimum off-duration and delivered-dose rules after a blinded technical pilot, before arm-specific PPV is opened; report sensitivity to 1-, 5-, 10-, 30- and 60-minute gaps.

### Estimands and analysis

The primary estimands are raw EHR-restart PPV against the adjudicated reference within each observed-stop arm and the absolute arm difference in PPV. Report exact or score 95% intervals. The endpoint passes only if:

- PPV is at least 0.80 in each arm with the lower confidence bound reported;
- absolute PPV difference is below 0.10 with its interval;
- indeterminate adjudication is below 10% in each arm; and
- conclusions remain stable across prespecified gap definitions and after excluding clock/export failures.

Secondary estimands are sensitivity, specificity and negative predictive value using all consecutively enrolled stops; confusion matrices by boundary `statusdescription`; error fractions attributable to bag/syringe change, order-chain continuation, transfer, and clock mismatch; and performance of the retrospective three-part transition screen. Estimate whether adding pump/EHR metadata can identify artifact without using post-event clinical deterioration, but any classifier is exploratory and must be evaluated in site- and time-held-out data before use.

MCS classification is separately validated against device-native or registry state. EHR MCS status may be called negative only if the site-specific observation process is shown to capture active devices with sufficiently high sensitivity and the episode lies within that validated coverage. Otherwise it remains unknown.

### Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive endpoint-validity result:** both arms meet PPV and symmetry gates, indeterminate records are uncommon, sensitivity is acceptable, and errors do not concentrate by arm, site, unit, year, pump system or transition type. This supports using the restart component in a later prospective discontinuation-order study. It does not validate the entire composite, prove clinical importance, or identify a superior order.

**Adverse result:** either arm has PPV below 0.80, the PPV difference is at least 0.10, bag/syringe or order transitions account for a material share of reference-negative restart-only failures, or sensitivity is poor. The raw restart component is then falsified as a sufficiently valid symmetric outcome. Future studies must use pump-native delivery plus adjudicated rescue, not the raw EHR interval rule; inherited MIMIC arm comparisons and scores remain non-advancing.

**Inconclusive result:** confidence intervals are too wide; more than 10% are indeterminate; clocks cannot be synchronized; pump export is selectively missing; MCS state remains unresolved; or sites use incompatible pump/EHR workflows. The remedy is better prospective capture or a larger validation sample, not a lower PPV gate or retrospective relabeling.

Only chronology, interval reconstruction, transition features, model metrics, sampling counts and prospective confusion-matrix calculations are automatically checkable. Whether a pump event represents clinical rescue, whether clinician intent is credible, whether MCS is truly inactive, the clinically important difference for a future trial, safety, and ethical equipoise require clinical adjudication, expert agreement, and another study.

## Decision-relevant planning after the negative score result

Because no enrichment score passed, planning uses transparent baseline-risk/effect scenarios rather than a post hoc “low-risk” threshold. Illustrative equal-allocation, two-sided alpha 0.05, 90%-power calculations with 10% attrition require per arm:

| Baseline event risk | 5-point reduction | 10-point reduction | 15-point reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 30% | 1,859 | 434 | 177 |
| 40% | 2,187 | 529 | 225 |
| 50% | 2,328 | 576 | 252 |

These are scenarios, not a trial recommendation. A prospective endpoint pilot must estimate the adjudicated event risk and misclassification rate. Clinicians and trialists must define the minimum clinically important difference, safety outcomes, attrition, site/pump clustering, competing events, stopping rules, and whether randomization is ethical. The current MIMIC branch cannot answer those questions.

## Frozen conclusion

The compact prognostic/enrichment hypothesis is not supported: formal Brier improvement is effectively zero, the observed action term misses its incremental gate by roughly two orders of magnitude, and later-period stress performance is adverse. The assigned-strategy positivity and observed-arm/event gates remain failed. The endpoint is computable but restart-dominated; six restart-only labels meet a narrow transition-artifact screen, while most restarts remain unresolved. All 137 MCS states remain unknown rather than negative.

Accordingly, the branch advances neither a score nor a stopping-order recommendation. The decision-relevant next experiment is prospective validation of the dominant restart component against synchronized pump-native delivery, workflow-transition metadata, actual MCS state, and blinded clinical intent. Stronger claims about causal order, bedside benefit, endpoint clinical importance, safety, or randomization require that prospective evidence and expert review.
