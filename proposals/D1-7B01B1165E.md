# Dynamic hepatic reserve after first TACE and 90-day coded decompensation: repaired, observation-aware HCC experiment

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

For adults with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) receiving a first recorded transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does the direction and rate of early liver-function change after TACE identify patients at higher risk of a subsequent coded hepatic-decompensation encounter beyond their current pre-treatment laboratory levels?

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is: among patients without a coded hepatic-decompensation diagnosis in the 180 days before index TACE, a prespecified worsening trajectory (albumin decreasing and/or total bilirubin or INR increasing from days -14:-1 to days +1:+7) is associated with a higher probability of a new coded decompensation encounter on days +8:+90, and a model containing that trajectory has better held-out proper scoring and calibration/net benefit than a model using pre-TACE levels and history only.

This is a prognostic and monitoring-information hypothesis. It is not a claim that TACE causes decompensation, that the coded diagnosis equals adjudicated liver failure, or that a model establishes a treatment policy.

## What is established versus unresolved

The strongest evidence available to this proposal supports only the narrower premise that baseline hepatic reserve, tumor burden and treatment context are clinically relevant after TACE. The inspected frozen external TACE evidence reported associations of baseline tumor burden, portal-vein tumor thrombosis, Child-Pugh class, INR and AFP with survival in a cohort with standardized imaging, procedural details, clinical adjudication and follow-up. Those richer variables and adjudication are not available in this HCC snapshot.

It remains unresolved whether routine local longitudinal measurements contain an early, reproducible within-person reserve signal beyond current levels, and whether that signal is sufficiently calibrated to identify a group for closer post-procedure monitoring. The substantive advance is a prespecified comparison of current state/history versus early response, including discordance and measurement uncertainty, rather than treating a single baseline lab as the whole reserve phenotype.

## Features and models

For every assay and window, calculate the within-patient median, number of observations, and days from index to the median. Standardize each exact assay using training-fold median and IQR only. Define trajectory as early minus baseline robust-standardized value, with worsening direction prespecified for albumin versus bilirubin/INR. Keep platelets, creatinine, sodium and AFP as assay-specific context, not as an invented liver score. Report test-count and missingness features separately as possible care-process signals.

The simple baseline is a train-fold-fitted penalized logistic regression for the primary outcome using pre-TACE assay medians, age, sex, prior encounter count, prior coded liver disease/decompensation history, and pre-index treatment-context indicators. Missingness is handled by a train-fold-fitted multiple-imputation or explicit missingness-category rule, with complete-case sensitivity. This benchmark asks whether current measured state and history are sufficient.

The substantive alternative is a Bayesian assay-specific latent reserve state-space model. Each exact assay has its own robust scale and measurement-error term; a shared latent reserve state has a pre-index level and a post-index transition with irregular observation times, separate assay loadings and a patient-level recovery/worsening slope. Fit the transition, loadings and outcome link using training patients only. Use the posterior mean and 95% interval of day-7 latent change, plus its uncertainty, to predict the day +8:+90 coded outcome. This alternative can reveal discordant recovery, rate and uncertainty that the baseline discards. It is selected for scientific information, not complexity or a small predictive gain.

Use a deterministic patient-level 60/20/20 train/tune/test split inside discovery buckets 0-79, generated by a second documented hash fold. Standardization, imputation, model selection and priors are fit only on train/tune. Touch the test set once for final comparison. If the state-space model is unstable, nonidentified, or cannot converge within the declared CPU budget, preserve its diagnostics and classify the comparison inconclusive; do not replace it with an easier endpoint.

## Estimands, uncertainty and falsification

The primary association estimand is the adjusted odds ratio and patient-bootstrap 95% interval for an IQR increase in latent worsening trajectory, with assay-specific trajectory associations as secondary. The primary incremental-information estimand is paired test-set change in log loss and Brier score for latent-trajectory versus baseline predictions. Also report AUROC/AUPRC, calibration intercept and slope, reliability plots, and decision-curve net benefit at prespecified reporting thresholds 5%, 10% and 20% (not treatment targets). Bootstrap resampling is by patient, not by repeated lab row.

Supportive results require: (a) directionally adverse trajectory association with a reasonably precise interval; (b) held-out log-loss/Brier improvement with uncertainty excluding no improvement; (c) no material calibration deterioration; and (d) net-benefit improvement at a prespecified monitoring threshold, with similar direction after removing test-count/missingness features. This supports incremental prognostic information for the coded endpoint only.

Adverse results are a precise null or reversed trajectory association, no held-out proper-score improvement, worse calibration, or an apparent gain that disappears when care-process features are removed. This would falsify the claim that the early trajectory adds reliable information in this snapshot, not prove that biological liver reserve is irrelevant.

Results are inconclusive if primary assay/event support is sparse, complete follow-up is inadequate, diagnosis-type or observation rules materially change the result, the latent model is unstable, or intervals remain too wide. Missingness is not converted into normality, and no clinical recommendation is made from an inconclusive result.

Negative controls are: a pre-index-only model; within-patient permutation of post-index lab times preserving assay values; and a test-count/missingness-only model. A large trajectory effect that survives only in the missingness model or vanishes under valid time permutation indicates leakage or care-process bias.

The verifier can check exact joins, partitioning, windows, model fitting, held-out predictions, uncertainty, scoring and whether conclusions match outputs. It cannot establish that the lexical diagnosis is clinically adjudicated decompensation, determine whether TACE caused an event, resolve missing death capture, or show that a monitoring policy benefits patients. Those claims require expert chart adjudication and an independently validated cohort.

## Compute and scientific deliverable

The deliverable is newly fitted patient-level baseline and latent-trajectory models, not merely an input audit. It must include cohort flow and actual filtering counts; assay/event/follow-up support; frozen split hashes; fitted parameters and posterior trajectory summaries; held-out predictions; patient-bootstrap intervals; calibration, proper-score and net-benefit comparisons; negative-control outputs; and a conclusion labeled supportive, adverse or inconclusive.

The bounded plan uses streaming extraction over the read-only source CSVs, 2 CPU cores, up to 8 GiB memory and approximately 2 hours of science time after queued execution. Baseline fitting should be under 10 minutes after extraction; the latent model is expected to require roughly 30-120 CPU minutes. No GPU is required. If these limits are exceeded, the unmet computation is reported rather than replaced by an uncomputed-result envelope.
