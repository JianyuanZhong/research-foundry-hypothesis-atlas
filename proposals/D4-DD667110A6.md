## Opening, evidence, unresolved claim, and clinical value

The strongest inspected evidence supports only these claims. Large cystatin-C versus creatinine eGFR differences are common in UK Biobank and have kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, eGFRcys−eGFRcr tracked frailty and broad adverse outcomes, so a combined equation may discard prognostic information [K2]. Administrative AKI coding can be insensitive and subgroup-dependent relative to creatinine-defined AKI [K3]. These works do not establish that the frozen equal-combined-eGFR profiles differ in biological AKI, that UKB first-appearance dates are contacts, or that adjusted associations identify a mechanism.

The unresolved hypothesis is: among supported P− and P0 participants at equal combined eGFR, the ten-year profile contrast is larger for first recorded N17 than for first recorded N18, so D=theta17−theta18>0, and the contrast remains materially positive when a support-aware marked first-appearance model represents measured persistent discovery-date frequency and conditional bundle density.

The leading explanation is bounded recorded acute-renal selectivity: discordance contains information lost by combined eGFR that is expressed more strongly in recorded acute than chronic renal transition. The strongest rival is generalized morbidity/recording: P− participants may have more persistent broad discovery dates or denser same-day bundles, making N17 more likely to be recorded. The explanations differ observably. The leading claim predicts positive D under the transparent joint competing-risk baseline and no broad process contrast commensurate with the renal result. The rival predicts transported profile differences in recurrent discovery-date frequency/density and attenuation of D under calibrated population integration. Neither pattern identifies contact, severity, testing, coding intent, or biological onset.

Clinical importance is a decision about whether this marker discordance deserves external encounter/laboratory adjudication as an acute-risk signal. A well-supported positive result justifies that next study; precise absence or recording-process dependence argues against treating the marker profile as selective acute reserve evidence. An imprecise result does neither.

A bounded diagnostic was preregistered before its result was inspected in `precision-preregistration.md`.

The diagnostic:

1. constructs the frozen provisional-C cohort and outcome-free propensity model;
2. applies exact sex, five-year age and assessment year, |G|<=1.5 and a 0.2 pooled-SD propensity-logit caliper;
3. withholds observed profile-specific event counts and all observed effects;
4. pools N17/N18 events across each retained pair, then performs 5,000 independent within-pair label swaps (seed 20260924);
5. reports only match retention/balance, pooled event support, and null-information widths for theta17, theta18 and D.

Continue only if >=1,000 pairs remain, neither profile loses >50%, reported continuous/propensity |SMD|<=0.10, pooled matched N17>=150 and N18>=100, and each random-label 95% half-width is <=log(1.5). The log(1.5) threshold asks whether a large contrast is distinguishable and is not a clinically actionable effect threshold. Also report <=log(1.25) as an aspirational sensitivity; failing only that tighter target prohibits fine-discrimination claims but does not make a large-effect question uninformative. Categorical balance, verified C, raw-assay local support, final competing-risk standardization and bootstrap remain solver gates.

The diagnostic is not a hypothesis test: because labels are swapped within fixed pairs, it measures information under a null assignment while deleting observed direction. Its crude paired-risk width can differ from final standardized competing-risk uncertainty.

## Falsification and interpretation

All substantive interpretation requires verified C and field semantics, renal-clinician code review, raw-assay/support/matching/balance gates, diagnostic continuation, final precision, process calibration, >=90% bootstrap success, and sensitivity agreement.

Supportive for bounded recorded-N17 selectivity requires lower joint 95% bounds of theta17 and D>0 in the transparent analysis; matched and principal-position direction agreement; D lower bound>0 after calibrated population-integrated process adjustment; and no calibrated broad date-frequency or conditional-density profile contrast commensurate with the renal contrast. The two D estimates must differ by less than log(1.25). This supports external adjudication, not biological AKI or clinical action.

Adverse to acute-versus-chronic selectivity is D upper 95% bound<=0 in either calibrated prespecified renal analysis, or a precise interval for D wholly within [−log(1.25),log(1.25)]. Both theta upper bounds<=0 are adverse to both profile claims.

Adverse to robustness is adequate transport/calibration plus a material broad frequency/density profile contrast and attenuation of D to an interval wholly within ±log(1.25), especially when transparent-minus-alternative D excludes zero. This is compatible with stable morbidity/recording propensity but proves no latent cause.

Inconclusive includes unverified C; unrecoverable raw-assay gate; support/matching/categorical balance failure; diagnostic half-width >log(1.5); final intervals spanning materially positive, null and adverse values; failure of the stricter 1.25 target when only fine discrimination is claimed; process transport/calibration/identifiability failure; marginal/conditional disagreement without a calibrated path; death, principal or tie reversal; or <90% bootstrap success. An imprecise null never refutes the hypothesis.

Stronger claims require additional evidence: admissions and serial creatinine/cystatin C/albuminuria plus clinician adjudication for biological AKI/CKD; provider/nation/encounter/testing and export provenance for observation opportunity and C; measured GFR for filtration; external validation for transportability; and a prospective decision-impact study for benefit.
