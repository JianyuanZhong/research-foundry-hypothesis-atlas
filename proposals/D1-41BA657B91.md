# Early hypotension burden beyond minimum MAP for later observed kidney deterioration

## Episode-3 successor and clinical decision

This substantive successor evolves the episode-2 highest-mu valid eICU line ([prior hypothesis] and [prior hypothesis]). It preserves the six-hour landmark, hospital transport test, density controls, and causal limits, while sharpening the clinical meaning of the outcome: a later *observed creatinine-rise phenotype* with a separately reported kidney-support/dialysis observation audit, rather than an unqualified claim of adjudicated AKI. The practical decision is whether an early risk summary should include time-under-threshold information in addition to minimum recorded MAP when deciding who merits intensified kidney surveillance.

### Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

The strongest claim supported before execution is structural, not clinical: the frozen eICU snapshot and its catalog expose patient-stay and hospital identifiers, ICU-relative offsets, noninvasive MAP observations, creatinine-labeled laboratory fields, infusion timing descriptors, and structured intake/output fields. The exact source files, table schemas, joins, and temporal columns are available in the read-only catalog. The prior candidate and seed establish a plausible research question, but do not establish its association in this snapshot, a complete AKI label, a treatment effect, or clinical benefit. No row-level event or coverage count is claimed here: the attempted managed source audit remained queued and was canceled under the phase budget.

The unresolved claim is: among adult ICU stays still present at a six-hour landmark, conditional on the minimum observed MAP, greater time-weighted early hypotension burden predicts a higher risk of a later observed creatinine rise and improves transportable prognostic performance. The null is no residual association and no reproducible held-out improvement after observation-density, hospital, competing-event, and temporal-leakage checks. This is an observational prognosis hypothesis; it is not a claim that hypotension caused AKI, that MAP 65 is a treatment target, or that increasing MAP would improve outcomes.

## Population and temporal estimands

Use snapshot `[source checksum]`; all dates remain removed and times are ICU-relative offsets. The index is one `patientunitstayid`; repeated stays are not collapsed by `uniquepid`.

Include adults with numeric `patient.age >= 18`, coding eICU `> 89` as 90 and reporting missing-age exclusions; stays with `unitdischargeoffset >= 360`; at least three valid primary MAP observations in minutes 0–360; and at least 60 covered minutes after the prespecified gap rule. The primary prognostic analysis requires at least one valid baseline creatinine in minutes 0–360, but all otherwise eligible stays must remain in an ascertainment flow table so absence of a baseline or follow-up measurement is visible rather than treated as normal.

Exposure is complete at minute 360: valid `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` values in 0–360, excluding missing and values outside 20–200 mmHg. Minimum MAP is the minimum. Carry each value to the next observation only for a gap <=30 minutes, clipped to 0–360. Duration is covered minutes with carried MAP <65; secondary depth burden is \`sum(max(0,65-MAP_i)*delta_t_i)\` in mmHg-minutes. Retain MAP count, covered minutes/fraction, median and maximum accepted gap, and fraction below 65 as measurement-process variables. Repeat with thresholds 60/70, gaps 15/60, and at least six observations. Analyze `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` or `pamean` alone as modality sensitivity, never pooled with primary noninvasive MAP. These are structured summaries, not continuous raw waveforms.

Follow-up begins only after minute 360 and ends at the earliest of ICU discharge, ICU death, minute 2,880, or last usable creatinine observation. Define baseline creatinine as the earliest valid creatinine at 0–360, tie-broken by `labid`. Define the primary event as the first later numeric creatinine at 360<`labresultoffset`<=2880 that is >=0.3 mg/dL above baseline. Report four states: observed creatinine rise; death/discharge before a qualifying test; follow-up creatinine without the rise; and no post-landmark creatinine (unknown ascertainment). Also report number/timing of tests and maximum rise. A parallel descriptive audit uses `intakeOutput.dialysistotal`, `outputtotal`, `celllabel`, and `cellvaluenumeric` to identify possible dialysis/urine records, but these do not become a composite clinical AKI endpoint unless a prespecified semantic audit establishes a valid event definition.

## Analysis, baselines, and uncertainty

Fit pooled discrete-time cause-specific hazards for first observed creatinine-rise event over 6–48 hours; ICU death and discharge before a qualifying test are competing absorbing states. Keep unknown laboratory ascertainment explicit. Report a complete-follow-up analysis only as secondary, plus inverse-probability-of-observation weighting based solely on pre-landmark variables (baseline creatinine availability/value, early MAP summaries and density, demographics, admission source, unit type, hospital, and early lab counts). Show weight positivity/diagnostics; weighting does not repair unmeasured reasons for ordering creatinine.

Use nested models with all preprocessing confined to training hospitals:

- M0: baseline creatinine, age, gender, ethnicity, admission source, unit type, early heart-rate/temperature/respiration summaries when available, and hospital effect.
- M1: M0 plus minimum MAP.
- M2: M1 plus early hypotension duration (primary comparison).
- M3: M1 plus depth burden, a separate secondary comparison.

Use a transparent linear-logit version in interpretable units and a prespecified spline version with fixed knots; never choose a functional form or label using outcomes. Use five grouped hospital folds with every stay from a hospital in one fold; do not split a `uniquepid` across folds in the person-cluster sensitivity. Report held-out AUROC, area under precision-recall, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, fold-level M2–M1 differences, duration association and 95% uncertainty (hospital-cluster bootstrap or robust standard errors), event/competing/unknown counts, and direction by hospital. A small predictive gain is not clinical utility; decision thresholds, net benefit/costs, clinician review, and prospective impact evidence are outside this dataset.

## Falsification and interpretation

Computationally adverse/falsifying evidence includes: duration association null or directionally unstable across density-adjusted, gap, threshold, modality, and hospital-held-out analyses; M2 not improving held-out calibration/discrimination over M1; apparent gain limited to one hospital, sparse observations, long-gap carry-forward, complete follow-up, or leakage variables; comparable signal in a pre-landmark creatinine-change negative control; exposure permutation within hospital/minimum-MAP/coverage strata failing to remove the signal; or attenuation after adding MAP count, covered time, gap summaries, and early lab intensity. Poor overlap of duration at a given minimum MAP, too few observed events/hospitals, nonpositive observation weights, or ambiguous creatinine labels/units makes the result inconclusive rather than negative.

Supportive results require a positive, clinically interpretable duration association conditional on minimum MAP, stable direction across prespecified definitions, maintained calibration, and reproducible held-out incremental performance across multiple hospitals. They support only a transportable prognostic marker for an observed creatinine-rise phenotype. Adverse results would favor minimum MAP alone for this operational endpoint or show non-transportability, not show that hypotension is harmless. Inconclusive results require a better cohort or prospective monitoring data; they do not justify relaxed windows or selective exclusions.

The computation can verify source integrity, formulas, cohort flow, time ordering, competing/unknown accounting, nested-model performance, uncertainty and falsification. It cannot establish complete KDIGO AKI, pre-ICU renal reserve, urine-output semantics, dialysis indication, BP artifact validity, treatment intent, causality, or clinical benefit. Those require expert adjudication, richer external ICU data, and ultimately prospective or quasi-experimental evaluation.
