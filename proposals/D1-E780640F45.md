# Successor proposal: urine-output trajectory versus treatment, observation, and endpoint timing

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The strongest evidence currently supports only that urine output is a clinically important but imperfect observable: creatinine-based estimates can overestimate kidney function during prolonged critical illness [K1], urine output can be retained in predictive models for observed CRRT initiation without establishing indication or benefit [K2], and AKI classification depends on the measurement strategy used [K3]. These findings do not establish that a falling or recovering urine-output trajectory in MIMIC-IV is physiologic deterioration rather than treatment response, observation intensity, or endpoint-initiation timing.

The falsifiable successor hypothesis is: among adult ICU stays at a prespecified ICU-hour-12 landmark, a prespecified 12-hour urine-output trajectory adds held-out information for a first mutually exclusive 12-hour deterioration event beyond current state, explicit treatment exposure, and documentation intensity. The leading rival is that any apparent signal is generated by diuretic/fluid/vasopressor response, selective charting, or the timing of RRT/ventilation initiation. The experiment is predictive association, not a causal treatment effect or mechanism.

## Population, landmark, and estimand

Use MIMIC-IV 3.1 adult ICU stays with valid age >=18, ICU duration >=24 hours, and no endpoint before L = icustays.intime + 12 hours. Eligibility and predictors use only information available at or before L. The primary estimand is the 12-hour risk of the first mutually exclusive event in (L, L+12 hours] among new RRT, new invasive ventilation, or death, censored at ICU outtime when earlier. Report component-specific risks and competing-risk cumulative incidence; repeated procedure rows are not independent observations. The endpoint is observed initiation, not adjudicated deterioration.

## Baseline, alternative, and analysis

B0 is regularized logistic prediction using current/last urine value, current vital signs, demographics, admission type, care unit, and preceding treatment summaries. B1 adds observation intensity. B2 adds explicit fluid, diuretic, and vasopressor exposure summaries and timing. T1 is a prespecified hourly urine trajectory/persistence model with slope, recovery, and explicit missingness, compared with B2 on the same landmark, split, endpoint, and uncertainty procedure. T1 can reveal ordered decline/recovery and persistence that latest-value summaries lose; this is the substantive scientific contrast, not a small predictive-score contest. A neural sequence encoder is deferred unless T1 is feasible and the artifact-adjusted contrast remains unresolved.

Use subject-level train/validation/test splits, repeated-split or bootstrap uncertainty, AUROC/AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope, and decision-curve/net benefit at prespecified alert thresholds. Include trajectory-shuffling and pre-landmark charting-gap negative controls. Supportive results require held-out incremental value beyond B2, stable calibration, persistence in high-coverage strata, and no negative-control reproduction. Adverse results include no incremental gain, gain confined to low coverage, disappearance after treatment/documentation adjustment, or negative-control reproduction. Inconclusive results include inadequate eligible landmarks, sparse urine coverage, unstable mapping, or wide uncertainty; they do not refute the hypothesis.
