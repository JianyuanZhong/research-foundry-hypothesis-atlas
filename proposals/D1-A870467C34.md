# Calibration of ventilator-setting documentation against explicit RT Vent On/Off transitions

## Substantive child and unresolved question

This child combines the assessed-valid parents `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves their adverse/inconclusive findings: the eICU structured-setting signal is highly prevalent, dominated by recurrent setting families, entangled with respiratory documentation workflow, and cannot be called active invasive ventilation. The new question is narrower and falsifiable:

> In an independently defined, all-stay event cohort, does the airway-independent signal (at least two inspected ventilator-setting families in a fixed six-hour bin) rise after an explicit structured `RT Vent On/Off=Start` event and fall after `RT Vent On/Off=Off` or `Suspended`, relative to its own pre-event bin, sham times, and the opposite transition?

A time-locked rise/fall would establish temporal concordance with a structured RT transition and improve calibration of the signal as a **documentation-state marker**. It would not establish invasive ventilation, tube presence, device connectivity, continuous support, or clinical extubation. Persistence around sham times, weak directionality, or large hospital instability would further falsify the useful interpretation that setting activity tracks explicit RT transition documentation.

## Evidence and clinical importance

The eICU Collaborative Research Database description supports that the database contains high-granularity, routinely collected ICU monitoring, treatment, and care-plan documentation, but does not validate any particular respiratory field as a bedside gold standard. The present experiment therefore treats the eICU `RT Vent On/Off` field as a structured comparator whose relationship to active ventilation remains unverified. The public evidence supports database availability and multi-center provenance, not the semantic equivalence being tested here.

Clinically, this distinction matters because ventilation duration, liberation surveillance, and retrospective cohort denominators can be badly biased if copied or setting-only fields are treated as true ventilator occupancy. Explicit RT transitions are the strongest available structured temporal comparator in this snapshot, but they too are workflow documentation. The advance is a direct temporal calibration rather than another cross-sectional comparison of airway-lookback groups.

## Population, temporal design, and deduplication

The primary population is every eICU stay represented in `respiratoryCharting` with at least one qualifying transition. After parsing the complete source, there were 2,734 raw `Start`, 1,018 raw `Off`, and 368 raw `Suspended` transition records. Same-direction records within 30 minutes in a stay were deduplicated by retaining the earliest; this removed 14 On and 3 Off records, yielding 4,103 event episodes: 2,720 On and 1,383 Off (Off combines `Off` and `Suspended`). No patient join was missing for these events. The deduplication rule prevents dense duplicate rows from dominating but does not infer a true state machine; alternating transition records remain separate episodes.

For each event time `T`, independently reconstruct setting activity from source rows in half-open bins:

* pre-event: `[T-360, T)` minutes;
* post-event: `[T, T+360)` minutes.

There is no carry-forward of a previous setting or transition. Events within the same stay may contribute multiple episodes, so stay-clustered uncertainty is primary. Hospital-clustered results are a documentation-system sensitivity. A 12-hour observation flag is `unitdischargeoffset >= T+720`; it is descriptive and does not remove events from the primary analysis.

A deterministic sham time is the first feasible of `T-720` or `T+720` with at least 360 minutes of observation and no explicit transition within 720 minutes. Sham estimates are not presumed null: they test whether apparent setting movement is simply a general respiratory-charting rhythm. The source-derived artifact records whether a sham was available.

## Estimands, baselines, and falsification gates

The principal estimands are (i) the paired change in setting positivity, `P(post)-P(pre)`, separately for On and Off events, and (ii) the On-minus-Off difference in post-event positivity. Report raw rates, paired stay-cluster bootstrap intervals, hospital-cluster intervals, and common-hospital rates. Compare each transition change with its sham change. The baseline is the event's own pre-window; the sham is an adjacent-time negative control; the opposite transition is a directional control.

Supportive calibration requires all of the following: On has a positive pre-to-post change and Off a negative change; both changes exceed their sham changes in the expected direction; stay- and hospital-cluster intervals exclude zero; and common hospitals show the same direction without a single hospital determining the result. Even then, the conclusion is only that the setting signal is temporally concordant with RT transition documentation.

Adverse calibration is triggered by a direction opposite to the transition, a sham change of comparable magnitude, hospital-cluster intervals crossing zero despite a large pooled contrast, or strong hospital-specific reversals. Inconclusive calibration is appropriate when event counts or site coverage are sparse, when event timing is too coarsely documented to separate bins, or when transition semantics cannot be separated from care workflow. Neither adverse nor supportive output adjudicates active ventilation.

## Executed result

The bounded resource-managed job was `[research job]`, with script [source checksum]. Results SHA-256 is `[source checksum]`; event rows SHA-256 is `[source checksum]`.

* On events: setting positivity was 7.76% pre and 94.19% post, a +86.43 percentage-point paired change; 2,382 stay clusters; the 2,000-replicate stay-cluster percentile interval was +84.92 to +87.87 points. A sham was available for 1,849 On events, where positivity changed -6.60 points; the observed-minus-sham change was +94.27 points.
* Off events: setting positivity was 64.14% pre and 11.71% post, a -52.42 percentage-point paired change; 1,045 stay clusters; the stay-cluster percentile interval was -57.13 to -47.99 points. A sham was available for 1,185 Off events, where positivity changed +2.03 points; observed-minus-sham was -55.53 points.
* Post-event On-minus-Off positivity was +82.48 points, with stay-cluster interval +77.34 to +85.22 (922 common stay clusters in the bootstrap comparison) and hospital-cluster interval +80.93 to +88.17 (12 hospital clusters). Nine hospitals had at least 10 events of each type; post-On rates ranged 92.62–100.0%, while post-Off rates ranged 0–21.43%.
* Continued observation through 12 hours was available for 92.02% of On and 88.79% of Off events. This is not a complete validation sample because discharge timing can correlate with the transition.

These results are strongly supportive of **temporal concordance**: explicit Start and Off/Suspended documentation are followed by, respectively, high and low setting-family activity, with directional sham and opposite-transition controls. They do not upgrade the setting signal to active invasive ventilation. The result could reflect a common respiratory documentation workflow in which the RT transition and settings are entered together, and Off/Suspended may represent a documentation action rather than actual cessation. The 9-hospital common-site rates remain broadly concordant, but site heterogeneity and the small number of hospitals mean transportability is not proven.

## Mapping to the frozen 3,788-row landmark cohort

The frozen parent cohort remains unchanged and was not used to define this calibration. A separate mapping artifact checked its exact 3,788 unique `(sid, landmark)` rows against all 4,103 deduplicated event episodes (2,720 On and 1,383 Off) and their event times, with the inherited groups `unbounded_only` 1,148, `retained_24h_only` 1,462, and `retained_6h` 1,178 and landmarks 1,440, 2,880, 4,320, 5,760, and 7,200 minutes. No explicit Start, Off, or Suspended event fell in either the present `[L-360,L)` or future `[L,L+360)` bin of any frozen row. This is an important availability result: direct event calibration cannot be claimed as a within-landmark validation of those 3,788 rows. The all-stay calibration is a source-level semantic audit, not a redefinition or outcome analysis of the frozen cohort.

## What is and is not established

The strongest supported claim is that in this eICU snapshot, the airway-independent setting-family signal is temporally aligned with explicit RT Vent On/Off transition documentation. This is stronger than cross-sectional co-occurrence and provides a useful falsification against a completely transition-insensitive setting trace. It remains a structured documentation proxy. It does not establish invasive airway status, active mechanical ventilation, extubation, liberation, SBT delivery, continuity between chart rows, clinician intent, care quality, causal effects, or benefit.

Essential missing evidence includes bedside ventilator/device connectivity logs, exact ventilator mode and airway modality at the transition, tube insertion/removal times, respiratory therapist narrative, waveform or machine audit trails, and expert adjudication of whether each Start/Off/Suspended event denotes bedside state versus a documentation workflow. The next study should stratify blinded adjudication across hospitals and event types, oversampling discordant setting/transition episodes, and estimate PPV, NPV, sensitivity, specificity, and inter-rater agreement against a prespecified bedside gold standard. Only that study could support clinical-state use.

## Interpretation gates

Automatically checkable: source hashes, transition value mapping, deduplication counts, event counts, half-open windows, setting-family mapping, bin rates, sham construction, cluster bootstrap outputs, common-hospital rates, and the zero-event mapping inside the frozen landmark bins. Not automatically checkable: whether RT `Start` means active invasive ventilation, whether `Off` or `Suspended` means extubation or cessation, whether settings were device-connected or copied, and whether the signal transports beyond this eICU snapshot. Any compiler or verifier must reject conclusions that turn temporal concordance into a bedside ventilation or causal claim.
