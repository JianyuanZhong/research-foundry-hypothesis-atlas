# Decision-relevant outcome triangulation for the frozen eICU respiratory-label transport design

## Status, question, and substantive advance

This is a substantive successor of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the Lead-frozen scientific schema exactly: 540 person-landmark rows, 443 distinct first qualifying ICU stays/persons, and 74 hospitals; the first qualifying stay per `uniquepid`; landmarks `L ∈ {1440, 2880, 4320, 5760, 7200}` minutes after unit admission; the first exact target label in `[L,L+360]`; decision time `D = cplitemoffset`; inherited pre-landmark exclusions; exact literal labels; fixed B/E/T windows, parser, and raw thresholds; an independent three-class respiratory trajectory; five mutually exclusive all-row states; repeated-landmark clustering by `patientunitstayid`; and descriptive, noncausal interpretation.

The strongest evidence currently supported by this lineage is limited to a structured-chart association question: the labels are sparse and hospital-heterogeneous, and apparent respiratory-chart increments may arise from observation opportunity, exits, site composition, or persistence across landmarks. Existing design work does not establish that either literal label denotes a bedside spontaneous breathing trial, extubation evaluation, clinician intent, readiness, delivered settings, treatment, benefit, harm, quality, or a causal intervention.

The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable: **does the exact current-landmark label add reproducible information about clinically consequential, database-defined eventual ICU outcomes, beyond pre-`D` respiratory state and documentation opportunity, and does any such increment persist across hospitals and after the label/trajectory association is stress-tested against exits, ascertainment, and repeated-stay persistence?** The outcome triangulation is not intended to convert a label into a treatment or to prove bedside semantics. It asks whether the label contains information that is relevant to an outcome that matters for ICU decision-making, while keeping the respiratory trajectory as the independently charted primary construct.

The substantive advance is a prespecified, executable **outcome-and-utility triangulation** using exact eICU fields that are not part of the respiratory endpoint: (1) eventual ICU mortality (`actualicumortality`), (2) eventual invasive-ventilation duration (`actualventdays` and, where valid, `unabridgedactualventdays`), and (3) eventual ICU length of stay (`actualiculos`). These are database outcome fields, not adjudicated bedside endpoints. The proposal tests whether the current label improves held-out prediction of these outcomes, while retaining every 540 landmark row in the respiratory five-state accounting. A decision-relevant threshold/utility analysis is prespecified only when event prevalence, both-arm support, outcome completeness, calibration, and utility parameters are estimable; otherwise it is reported unavailable/inconclusive rather than forced. The utility exercise is a model decision analysis (flag versus do-not-flag for a defined database outcome), not a bedside treatment recommendation.

## Outcome triangulation: exact outcome definitions and temporal boundary

### Outcome sources and exact joins

Join each frozen row through `patientunitstayid` to the read-only eICU tables `apachePatientResult` and `apachePredVar`; join `patient` for the discharge and identity fields already used by the frozen design. Enforce one-to-one joins at the table's declared identity level before attaching outcomes. `apachePatientResult` is one row per `patientunitstayid` in its schema; if a source scan finds duplicates, conflicting values, or no unique record, mark that outcome unavailable for the affected stay and fail the corresponding outcome analysis closed rather than selecting a row by order. The primary frozen respiratory row remains in the all-row state table regardless of outcome availability.

The outcome fields are:

1. **ICU mortality:** `apachePatientResult.actualicumortality`, used only after a source audit establishes its observed value set and a deterministic binary mapping. Do not infer mortality from discharge status. Values outside the audited binary mapping, missing values, and conflicting duplicate records are `outcome_invalid_or_missing`.
2. **Ventilation duration:** `apachePatientResult.actualventdays`, with `unabridgedactualventdays` as a prespecified sensitivity. These are eventual stay-level database fields. Use the first field as primary only if its source audit confirms a numeric nonnegative duration and a stable observed unit/encoding; otherwise the ventilation-duration analysis is unavailable. Define prolonged ventilation as `actualventdays >= 7` only if at least 20 positive events and at least 20 outcome-available rows in each exact arm are present after the frozen join; otherwise do not estimate the binary prolonged-ventilation utility. Do not reinterpret this field as continuous bedside ventilator exposure or as an adjudicated liberation outcome.
3. **ICU length of stay:** `apachePatientResult.actualiculos`, used as a secondary descriptive continuous outcome only after a finite, nonnegative, stable-unit audit. A prespecified binary prolonged-ICU-stay sensitivity uses `actualiculos >= 7` days only if the same event/support requirements pass. If units or encoding are not auditable from the source, report this outcome as unavailable.

The aggregate Apache outcomes are not timestamped in these schemas. Therefore they cannot establish that the outcome was known at `D`, cannot support a real-time causal decision claim, and cannot be treated as a time-to-event endpoint. The analysis explicitly labels them **eventual stay-level outcomes attached to a landmark row**. A row can be respiratory `competing_exit` while still having an eventual Apache outcome; this does not retroactively classify the respiratory endpoint. The analysis never uses an eventual outcome, discharge field, actual length of stay, actual ventilation duration, or mortality field as a pre-`D` feature. Outcome availability and missingness are reported by arm, hospital, landmark, K state, and exit timing; no complete-case outcome analysis is allowed to replace the primary 540-row denominator.

For an outcome-available secondary analysis, retain the frozen row and add a status among `outcome_observed`, `outcome_missing`, `outcome_invalid`, and `source_record_missing`. Report the number and fraction available for every exact arm and landmark. If outcome availability differs materially by arm or hospital, model/report availability as an ascertainment diagnostic using only pre-D predictors; do not inverse-weight away competing exits or impute missing outcomes. The outcome model is descriptive prediction conditional on the defined outcome being observed, with availability and all-row respiratory states shown alongside it.

### Pre-D predictors and leak prevention

The principal outcome models compare:

* `OM0`: pre-`D` respiratory state and opportunity features from the frozen design, no current label;
* `OM1`: the identical feature vector plus the current exact two-level label;
* `OM2` (secondary triangulation): `OM1` plus the independently constructed primary respiratory trajectory `K` only in a post-outcome-independent, outcome-available analysis; this is not a real-time model and must be labelled a decomposition, not a deployable predictor.

The decision-relevant comparison is `OM1` versus `OM0`. `OM2` is included only to quantify whether any outcome increment is statistically/operationally redundant with the later respiratory chart construct; it cannot be used to claim that the label caused or represented a bedside action. Never include `K`, E/T values, post-D source density, future exit, actual mortality, actual ventilation duration, actual ICU LOS, discharge status/location, or any post-D field in OM0/OM1. The `OM2` result does not replace OM1 and is not interpreted as available at D.

In addition to the frozen pre-D respiratory features and opportunity features, predeclare two source-bound predictor blocks:

* `APACHE-admission/day1 block`: from `apachePredVar`, use only `gender`, `age`, `admitdiagnosis`, `admitsource`, `ventday1`, `oobventday1`, `oobintubday1`, `day1pao2`, `day1fio2`, and, where finite and explicitly marked as day-1/admission in the source audit, `day1meds`, `day1verbal`, `day1motor`, `day1eyes`, `day1creatinine`, and `day1ejectfx`. Do not use `diedinhospital`, `dischargelocation`, `visitnumber`, or any field whose timing cannot be established as pre-D. For the 24-hour landmark, include this block only after a conservative audit confirms that day-1 values are determined no later than the landmark; otherwise omit it for that landmark and mark the block unavailable, not approximately aligned. At later landmarks it remains fixed admission/day-1 information.
* `Apache-physiology block`: from `apacheApsVar`, use `intubated`, `vent`, `dialysis`, `eyes`, `motor`, `verbal`, `meds`, `urine`, `wbc`, `temperature`, `respiratoryrate`, `sodium`, `heartrate`, `meanbp`, `ph`, `hematocrit`, `creatinine`, `albumin`, `pao2`, `pco2`, `bun`, `glucose`, `bilirubin`, and `fio2` only if the source audit documents that these are admission/early Apache predictors available by the relevant `D`. If timing is not auditable, this block is not used as a real-time predictor. Report field-level availability and explicitly preserve an `unavailable_timing` status.

These Apache blocks are not needed to redefine the frozen respiratory outcome. Their purpose is triangulation: if the label increment persists after basic, independently sourced early severity predictors, it is more decision-relevant within this snapshot; if it disappears, the label may mainly proxy baseline severity or documentation. A persistent increment still does not establish a clinical label meaning.

All column manifests are frozen before model fitting. Each predictor's source table, timing status, missingness handling, and inclusion/exclusion reason is recorded. Preprocessing, imputation, scaling, regularization, calibration, and any outcome-availability model are fit within the training hospitals/folds only. ## Outcome estimands and analysis

For each supported binary outcome `Y` (primary ICU mortality; secondary prolonged ventilation and prolonged ICU stay when estimable), report by exact arm and landmark:

1. outcome availability and missingness, with the frozen 540-row count retained;
2. event rate among outcome-observed rows, with exact denominators and no interpretation when availability/support gates fail;
3. hospital-held-out OM0 and OM1 predictions for every outcome-observed row, with no hospital predictor or held-out target calibration;
4. paired held-out log loss, Brier score, area under the precision-recall curve only when event support is sufficient, calibration intercept/slope or reliability summaries only under finite support, and discrimination as a secondary metric;
5. the paired incremental value `OM1 - OM0` with a stay-clustered confidence interval and per-hospital direction; and
6. a decomposition by respiratory K state and by early/late competing exit, always labelled descriptive and outcome-available rather than a causal effect.

The primary outcome-triangulation estimand is the held-out predictive increment in proper scoring loss, not an arm risk difference. For a binary outcome, define the loss increment with a fixed orientation before fitting: `I = loss(OM0) - loss(OM1)`, so positive I means OM1 predicts better. Report both micro row-weighted and equal-hospital macro increments; repeated landmarks are clustered by stay and never treated as independent. The frozen arm contrast in eventual outcome rates is descriptive only and is not a treatment comparison.

Do not claim that improved prediction of eventual mortality, actual ventilation duration, or ICU LOS proves the label is a respiratory intervention. A positive increment establishes at most that the exact structured label contains incremental information about the specified eventual database outcome under the frozen sampling and outcome-availability mechanism.

### Competing exits and outcome availability

Retain the five-state K accounting as the primary analysis. For outcome triangulation, cross-tab outcome availability and predictions by `K`, exit timing `< D+720` versus `(D+720,D+2160]`, `unitdischargestatus`, and `unitdischargelocation`. Do not treat a discharge/transfer row as a negative respiratory outcome, and do not censor it in the primary endpoint. A secondary outcome signal concentrated in `competing_exit` rows, in rows with unusually complete Apache records, or in one exit type is adverse to the interpretation that the current label carries general respiratory-state information. It may indicate severity, admission selection, or administrative availability instead.

If an outcome is available for nearly all stays but the label increment exists only in rows that are classifiable for K, report that as a conditional triangulation and do not generalize it to all rows. If outcome availability cannot be audited or is strongly arm/site dependent with insufficient overlap, report outcome triangulation inconclusive even if an unadjusted rate difference is numerically present.

## Prespecified decision-relevant utility and threshold analysis

A threshold analysis is permitted only for the binary outcome `Y` after all of the following are passed: (a) a fully specified outcome definition and stable source encoding; (b) at least 20 events and 20 non-events overall among outcome-observed rows; (c) at least 10 events and 10 non-events in each exact label arm, unless the arm-specific analysis is explicitly marked unsupported; (d) at least five hospitals with outcome-observed rows in both arms and at least 10 outcome-observed rows per arm in each eligible hospital; (e) finite held-out probabilities and calibration support; (f) no evidence that outcome availability or utility results are confined to competing exits, one landmark, or one site; and (g) fixed utility parameters specified before viewing results.

The default utility is a transparent **risk-flagging utility**, not a treatment utility. Let `Y=1` mean the audited binary eventual outcome is present. At threshold `t`, a model flags `p >= t`. Define benefit `B=1` for a correctly identified event and harm/cost `C=1` for a false-positive flag, with a predeclared net benefit `NB(t) = TP(t)/N - FP(t)/N * C/B`. Report a sensitivity grid `C/B ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4}` and thresholds `t ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.50}` only as a fixed policy-analysis grid. Do not select a favorable threshold or cost ratio after inspecting label results. Because the dataset contains no observed resource cost, patient preference, clinician action, downstream harm, or intervention efficacy, the grid is a sensitivity analysis of classification value, not a clinical utility estimate.

For every grid point, report OM0 and OM1 net benefit, paired difference, uncertainty by stay-cluster bootstrap, number flagged, event/non-event support, and hospital-stratified direction. Use held-out predictions only. A threshold/utility result is computationally supportive only if OM1 exceeds OM0 at the prespecified threshold grid in at least two non-adjacent thresholds and at least one cost ratio, the paired interval for the selected prespecified grid summary excludes zero, and the gain is not confined to one hospital, one landmark, one K state, competing exits, or availability strata. The selected grid summary is fixed before outcome results as the mean paired net-benefit difference across all supported thresholds and cost ratios, not the maximum cell.

If any utility gate fails, report `utility_unavailable_or_inconclusive` and retain proper-scoring outcome increments if those are supported. A positive risk-flagging utility does not justify flagging patients, changing ventilation, extubating, delaying care, or interpreting the label as treatment intent. Clinical decision utility would require an intervention, action threshold, calibrated real-time outcome, patient-centered harms/benefits, resource consequences, and prospective impact evaluation, none of which are present here.

## Fail-closed supportive, adverse, and inconclusive gates

### Invalid computation

The proposal is invalid and no scientific estimate may be interpreted if any of the following occurs: inherited artifact/hash mismatch; wrong source or schema hash; duplicate or non-one-to-one frozen joins; wrong exact labels or landmarks; boundary inequality error; parser integrity failure; use of post-D data in OM0/OM1; use of actual outcomes or discharge fields as predictors; care-plan fields outside exposure verification; failure to assign exactly one K state to all 540 rows; exit precedence violation; outcome source duplicates/conflicts resolved by arbitrary row selection; undocumented Apache timing assumptions; data-dependent threshold or utility selection; or row-level resampling.

### Respiratory feasibility and transport

Respiratory feasibility is supportive only if at least 40% of all 540 rows are classifiable and at least 10 hospitals have both exact labels among classifiable rows. Below 20% classifiable or below five such hospitals is adverse feasibility; intermediate is inconclusive. No unobservable row becomes a negative clinical outcome. No transported patient-state description is allowed unless at least five hospitals have at least 10 classifiable, non-competing rows per arm, represent at least 50% of supported rows, no site contributes more than 25%, and row-weighted and equal-site held-out increments agree. First/repeated landmark disagreement is heterogeneity, not a license to select one result.

### Outcome triangulation

A computationally supportive outcome-triangulation finding requires all of: (1) source-valid, timing-audited outcome encoding; (2) at least 80% outcome availability overall or an explicitly justified lower availability with adequate overlap and no arm/site availability reversal; (3) at least five eligible hospitals with both arms and minimum outcome support; (4) OM1 improves both micro and equal-hospital macro held-out proper scoring loss over OM0; (5) the stay-cluster interval for the prespecified aggregate increment is favorable; (6) the increment is not confined to one landmark, one site, one exit state, K-classifiable rows, or a documentation mask; (7) the opportunity/persistence label null does not make it typical; and (8) the result is directionally consistent in at least two fixed outcome definitions when more than one outcome is estimable. Failure of one outcome definition does not invalidate another, but prevents pooling or broad clinical claims.

The result is **adverse** to a label-specific decision-information interpretation if OM1 adds no proper-scoring value, the increment reverses across hospitals/landmarks, is comparable under the opportunity/persistence null, is confined to outcome availability or competing exits, or disappears under the fixed Apache-severity adjustment. This does not prove that the label lacks bedside meaning; it means this snapshot cannot show robust incremental outcome information beyond the specified predictors.

The result is **inconclusive** if outcome support, event counts, timing audit, hospital overlap, missingness overlap, calibration, permutation feasibility, or clustered uncertainty is inadequate. A mortality-only result with too few events is inconclusive, not negative. A ventilation-duration field with unknown unit or timing is unavailable, not silently converted. A utility analysis that fails its gates is unavailable/inconclusive and cannot downgrade a supported proper-scoring result.

### Utility gate

Only if the threshold/utility prerequisites pass may the analysis state that OM1 has greater fixed-grid risk-flagging value than OM0 for the named database outcome. This establishes no bedside utility, benefit, harm, treatment effect, or recommendation. If utility prerequisites fail, the proposal reports only the supported predictive increment and its limits.

## Interpretation boundary and required next evidence

Supportive output establishes only incremental prediction of one or more specified eventual eICU database outcomes under a frozen landmark sampling and outcome-availability mechanism. It does not establish that the labels are SBT/extubation evaluations, that they are clinically actionable, that they improve care, or that they cause mortality, ventilation duration, or ICU LOS. An adverse outcome triangulation means that this label did not add robust out-of-sample information beyond the specified pre-D state/opportunity and Apache blocks in this snapshot; it does not show that clinicians never evaluated extubation or that the labels are meaningless.
