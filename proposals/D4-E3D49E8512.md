# Do shared systemic-inflammatory/aging axes explain "disease-specific" plasma-protein signals? (UKB-Olink, episode 3)

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (episode 2, valid, mu 29.4). Operation: evolve.
Status: design + data bindings verified; no association results claimed.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance
Plasma-proteomic screens repeatedly report single proteins (e.g. GDF15, IL-6/CRP family) as strong
predictors of one incident disease. It is unresolved whether such protein-disease associations mark a
**disease-specific preclinical process** (A) or a **shared systemic "sickness/inflammation/aging" axis**
elevated before many diagnoses, partly via reverse causation (subclinical disease at blood draw) (B).
This decides whether a circulating protein is a plausible disease-specific early-detection target or only
a generic risk/severity correlate. Episode 2 left the temporal contrast **conditional** because diagnosis
date fields were unconfirmed; that dependency is now resolved below. [K1] shows protein patterns encode
many health states at once; [K2] shows broad shared age structure across 2,925 plasma proteins; [K3]
defines the UKB-PPP platform/scale our test runs on.

- Covariates: age `n_21003_0_0`, sex `n_31_0_0` (dta parquet).

## Design: baseline vs substantive alternative (same question)
- Baseline: per-protein logistic/Cox association with each incident disease, adjusted for age+sex, with
  BH-FDR across proteins. Yields "disease-specific" hits.
- Learned/mechanistic alternative: jointly estimate a low-rank **shared axis** across diseases --
  a multi-task / reduced-rank (or factor-model) decomposition in which one latent factor is shared across
  all outcomes and each protein retains a disease-specific loading. This separates shared variance from
  disease-specific variance in one fit, which the per-protein baseline cannot do (it can only condition
  on a single pre-computed PCA score). Evaluation: same held-out disease outcomes; primary estimand is the
  attenuation of protein-disease log-odds after removing the jointly estimated shared component, and
  whether residual disease-specific loadings survive.
- Baseline justification: simple, interpretable, and the current standard; the latent-axis alternative is
  scientifically necessary because it tests whether specificity is multivariate structure or single-protein noise.
- Approximate budget: 53k participants x 2,923 proteins -> CPU feasible (matrix ops ~O(53k x 2923));
  a bounded pilot is ~minutes; no GPU required for the primary fit. Larger repeated fits may use 1 GPU slot.

## Split and analysis
- Derive the shared axis on a training fold (or with out-of-fold scores) and evaluate associations on held-out
  participants to avoid leakage; report uncertainty (bootstrap CIs) and multiplicity control.
- Report the fraction of nominal (baseline-significant) protein-disease pairs that (i) attenuate below the
  analysis threshold, (ii) lose significance, (iii) persist, after shared-axis adjustment and after the
  washout restriction.

## Falsification criteria
- H_shared predicts: after shared-axis adjustment AND after excluding events within the washout, most
  nominal protein-disease associations attenuate substantially (e.g. >50% attenuation) or lose significance.
- H_specific predicts: attenuation is small (e.g. <20%) and a majority of disease-specific associations
  persist at FDR control in the washout-restricted set.
- Supportive (for H_shared), adverse (association specificity persists -> supports H_specific), and
  inconclusive (wide CIs / no power for a given disease due to under-ascertainment) outcomes are all
  pre-specified. An imprecise null does not refute specificity.

## What would NOT be established
Association persistence is not mechanism; a shared-axis attenuation is not proof of reverse causation.
Confirming either direction to clinical utility would require outpatient/GP outcome ascertainment,
external replication, and expert clinical adjudication.
