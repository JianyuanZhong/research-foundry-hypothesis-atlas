# Bone-muscle discordance and incident fracture risk: a two-pathway hypothesis

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank participants with low bone mineral density (BMD), does low grip strength identify a subgroup with higher incident fracture risk beyond what BMD alone predicts, and is this excess risk partly explained by falls?

The clinically important unresolved question is whether frailty in the context of low BMD represents a single latent process (where muscle weakness is merely a marker of overall biological ageing) or two separable pathways: (1) bone fragility driving fracture independent of muscle, and (2) muscle weakness driving falls that then cause fracture. This distinction has direct implications for screening and prevention: if the two pathways are separable, then dual anti-resorptive + resistance-exercise interventions would be more effective than bone-targeted therapy alone for the discordant subgroup.

The leading explanation (single latent process) is that low BMD and low grip strength are both downstream of a shared ageing/frailty trajectory, so their co-occurrence adds no independent fracture risk beyond what either measure alone predicts after adjustment. The strongest rival (two-pathway hypothesis) is that muscle weakness contributes an independent fall-related fracture pathway that is not captured by BMD, so the discordant subgroup (low BMD + low grip) has a multiplicative or super-additive fracture risk.

- **Population**: Participants aged ≥50 at baseline assessment with valid DXA (L1-L4 BMD field 23204 and/or femoral neck BMD field 23208), valid grip strength (fields 46/47), and no prior fracture diagnosis in the 2 years before baseline.
- **Baseline**: Date of attending assessment centre (field 53).
- **Follow-up**: From baseline to first incident fracture diagnosis (ICD10 field 41270, date field 41280), censoring at study end, loss to follow-up, or death. Maximum follow-up 10 years.
- **Exposure groups** (pre-specified):
  - Group A: Low BMD only (T-score ≤ -1.0 at L1-L4 or femoral neck, normal grip strength)
  - Group B: Low grip only (grip strength in lowest quartile, normal BMD)
  - Group C: Low BMD + low grip (discordant subgroup)
  - Group D: Neither (reference)

## Primary outcome

Incident fracture: first ICD10 code in field 41270 matching fracture codes (S02, S12, S22, S32, S42, S52, S62, S72, S82, S92, M48.31, M80-M84) with date in field 41280 after baseline.

## Competing explanations and falsification

**Single latent process (null hypothesis)**: After adjustment for age, sex, BMI, and falls, the interaction between low BMD and low grip strength on fracture risk is not significantly different from 1.0 (HR interaction ≈ 1.0, p > 0.10 for interaction).

**Two-pathway hypothesis (alternative)**: The interaction HR is significantly > 1.0 (p < 0.05, 95% CI lower bound > 1.0), indicating super-additive risk. Additionally, falls (field 2296) partially mediate the excess risk: the interaction attenuates by ≥30% after adjustment for falls.

**Inconclusive**: Interaction HR > 1.0 but 95% CI includes 1.0, or attenuation by falls is < 30%.

## Method comparison

### Baseline: Cox proportional hazards regression
- Model 1: Cox with BMD (continuous, per SD) + grip strength (continuous, per SD) + age + sex + BMI
- Model 2: Cox with BMD × grip strength interaction term
- Model 3: Cox with falls (field 2296) added
- Estimand: HR for the interaction term (low BMD × low grip)

### Learned alternative: Gradient-boosted survival model
- Algorithm: XGBoost with survival objective (or gradient-boosted trees with Cox partial likelihood)
- Features: BMD, grip strength, BMD × grip interaction, age, sex, BMI, falls, plus up to 10 additional covariates (smoking, alcohol, physical activity)
- Training: 80% train / 20% held-out test split (stratified by fracture status)
- Evaluation: Concordance index (C-index) on held-out data; comparison with baseline Cox C-index
- Additional: Partial dependence plots for BMD and grip strength to visualize interaction

### What the learned model reveals that the baseline loses
The Cox model assumes proportional hazards and a specific parametric form for the interaction. The gradient-boosted model can capture non-linear and higher-order interactions between BMD and grip strength that a two-way Cox interaction cannot. If the boosted model shows substantially better held-out C-index and the partial dependence plots reveal a non-rectangular interaction pattern, this would suggest the two-pathway hypothesis is more complex than a simple multiplicative interaction.

## Falsification criteria (summary)

| Result | Interpretation |
|--------|---------------|
| Interaction HR > 1.0, 95% CI > 1.0, falls attenuation ≥ 30% | Supports two-pathway hypothesis |
| Interaction HR ≈ 1.0, 95% CI includes 1.0 | Supports single latent process |
| Interaction HR > 1.0 but CI includes 1.0, or attenuation < 30% | Inconclusive; needs larger sample or different mediation approach |
| Boosted C-index > Cox C-index by > 0.02 with non-rectangular PD plots | Suggests complex interaction beyond multiplicative model |
