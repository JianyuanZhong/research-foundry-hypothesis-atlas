# Scientific Proposal: Proteomic Signatures Distinguishing Infection Onset from Infection-Associated Organ Damage

## Refined Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis**: Among UK Biobank participants without prior infection at baseline, pre-illness proteomic profiles that predict infection onset (any severity) differ fundamentally from those that predict infection-associated organ damage (severe outcomes with organ involvement). We test whether:

1. The set of proteins significantly associated with infection onset (any severity) has **less than 50% overlap** with proteins significantly associated with infection-associated organ damage (severe outcomes with organ involvement)
2. Proteins uniquely associated with infection onset represent pathways involved in pathogen detection, initial immune activation, and early inflammatory responses
3. Proteins uniquely associated with organ damage represent pathways involved in tissue injury, immune dysregulation, and host vulnerability to severe disease

**Formal Statistical Statement**: Let P_onset be the set of proteins with p < 0.05 in a multivariate logistic regression predicting infection hospitalization (any severity), and P_damage be the set of proteins with p < 0.05 in a multivariate logistic regression predicting infection-associated organ damage (SOFA score increase ≥2 due to infection). We test whether |P_onset ∩ P_damage| / |P_onset ∪ P_damage| < 0.5, indicating distinct biological pathways rather than a continuum of severity.

## Clinical Importance

### 1. Prevention vs. Treatment Target Identification
If proteins differ between infection onset and organ damage, this suggests:
- Proteins predicting infection onset could be targets for **prevention** strategies (antibiotics, antivirals, vaccines)
- Proteins predicting organ damage could be targets for **treatment** strategies (anti-inflammatories, organ-protective therapies)
- Current empiric treatment may miss prevention opportunities by focusing only on severe cases

### 2. Early Risk Stratification
Distinguishing onset from organ damage enables:
- Earlier intervention before irreversible organ damage occurs
- Targeted monitoring of patients at risk for progression
- Personalized treatment intensification based on proteomic phenotype

### 3. Understanding Disease Mechanisms
The differential proteomic signatures could reveal:
- Distinct biological pathways driving infection initiation vs. organ injury
- Potential therapeutic targets for organ-protective strategies
- Biomarkers for monitoring treatment response and predicting outcomes

## Study Population and Main Data

**Population**: UK Biobank participants (n ≈ 500,000)
- Exclusion criteria: prior infection history, severe comorbidities, missing proteomic data
- Inclusion: participants without prior infection at assessment center, with proteomic measurements

**Data Tables**:
1. `ukb.main` - proteomic measurements (protein identifiers, concentrations)
2. `ukb.health_outcomes` - infection hospitalizations with severity (SOFA scores)
3. `ukb.population` - baseline demographics and characteristics
4. `ukb.assessment` - assessment center visit dates and timing

**Temporal Boundaries**:
- Baseline: assessment center visit (2006-2018)
- Follow-up: through 2021 (last available outcome data)
- Exposure window: pre-illness proteomics (baseline only, as UKB measures pre-illness samples)

**Comparison Groups**:
- Group A: Participants with infection hospitalization (any severity)
- Group B: Participants with infection-associated organ damage (SOFA increase ≥2)
- Group C: Controls without infection (for baseline adjustment)

## Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis: Protein Overlap Test
1. Fit multivariate logistic regression for Group A vs. Group C:
   - Outcome: infection hospitalization (any severity)
   - Predictors: all measured proteins (with covariates for age, sex, BMI, ethnicity)
   - Extract proteins with p < 0.05 (FDR-adjusted) → P_onset

2. Fit multivariate logistic regression for Group B vs. Group C:
   - Outcome: infection-associated organ damage
   - Predictors: all measured proteins (with same covariates)
   - Extract proteins with p < 0.05 (FDR-adjusted) → P_damage

3. Calculate overlap statistic: θ = |P_onset ∩ P_damage| / |P_onset ∪ P_damage|
   - Null hypothesis: θ ≥ 0.5 (proteomes are largely overlapping)
   - Alternative hypothesis: θ < 0.5 (proteomes are distinct)
   - Test: hypergeometric test or permutation test

### Secondary Analyses
4. Pathway enrichment analysis for proteins in P_onset only
5. Pathway enrichment analysis for proteins in P_damage only
6. Machine learning approach: train classifiers to distinguish onset vs. damage using proteomic features
7. Sensitivity analyses: different FDR thresholds, different covariate sets

### Baseline Methods
- Logistic regression with LASSO for feature selection
- Permutation tests for p-value calibration
- Multiple testing correction (Benjamini-Hochberg FDR)

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **falsified** if:
1. The protein overlap statistic θ ≥ 0.5 (proteomes are largely overlapping)
2. Proteins in P_onset are enriched for pathways related to organ damage (e.g., inflammation, tissue injury)
3. Proteins in P_damage are enriched for pathways related to pathogen detection (e.g., immune recognition)
4. Machine learning models perform no better than chance distinguishing onset from damage

The hypothesis will be **supported** if:
1. The protein overlap statistic θ < 0.5 (proteomes are distinct)
2. Proteins in P_onset are enriched for pathogen detection/early immune response pathways
3. Proteins in P_damage are enriched for tissue injury/immune dysregulation pathways
4. Machine learning models significantly outperform traditional clinical scores in predicting organ damage

## Limitations and Mitigations

1. **Reverse Causation**: Baseline proteomics may reflect subclinical disease rather than true pre-illness state
   - Mitigation: Restrict to participants without prior infection; sensitivity analysis with lag time

2. **Proteome Coverage**: Limited protein panel may miss key biomarkers
   - Mitigation: Use comprehensive proteomic platform; report protein coverage

3. **Outcome Misclassification**: SOFA-based organ damage may not capture all severe infections
   - Mitigation: Use multiple severity definitions; sensitivity analysis with alternative outcomes

4. **Population Representativeness**: UK Biobank may not represent all infection populations
   - Mitigation: Acknowledge limitations; suggest validation in other cohorts

## Substantive Alternative Approach

**Machine Learning with Protein Signatures**:
- Train supervised classifiers (random forest, gradient boosting) to distinguish onset from damage
- Compare classifier performance against traditional clinical risk scores
- Identify protein signatures that predict organ damage beyond onset status
- This approach could reveal non-linear relationships and interaction effects missed by logistic regression

**Information Gained**:
- Logistic regression tests the overlap hypothesis directly but assumes linearity and additive effects
- ML approach can capture complex patterns and may identify proteins that are important only in specific contexts
- ML provides a framework for personalized risk prediction based on proteomic phenotype
