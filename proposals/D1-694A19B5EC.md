# Provisional HCC trajectory-versus-care-process hypothesis (episode 7)

## Proposed relationship
Among adults admitted with a documented HCC diagnosis, an ordered 7-day laboratory trajectory will add held-out information about a subsequent non-routine inpatient procedure beyond an admission snapshot and explicit treatment/documentation covariates. The procedure is a computable escalation proxy, not a validated death, ICU, harm, or clinical-deterioration endpoint.

The clinically important unresolved claim is whether temporal laboratory information reflects evolving patient state rather than routine HCC treatment, care intensity, or documentation practice. A positive result would justify expert review of temporal monitoring; it would not establish causality or clinical benefit.

## Falsifiable test
Primary cohort: exact HCC diagnosis, nonempty admission time, and one of 肝肿瘤内科, 肝胆肿瘤与肝移植外科, 肝胆肿瘤内科, 肝肿瘤外科. Feature window is admission through admission+7 days and no later than discharge. The primary endpoint is the first post-day-7, pre-discharge procedure not in a frozen routine-HCC dictionary (TACE/arterial embolization/arterial chemoembolization, hepatic resection, ablation, transplantation, and documented compound variants); the broader procedure proxy is secondary. Untimed procedures are excluded and reported.

Compare a penalized logistic snapshot baseline (age, sex, department, first assay values, time-zero treatment/medication indicators, documentation counts) with a patient-level ordered sequence/state-space alternative using the same covariates plus within-assay values, time since admission, specimen type, and missingness. Split by 患者主索引, use nested time-aware validation and repeated bootstrap uncertainty. Evaluate held-out discrimination, calibration, and incremental difference. Use post-outcome measurements and within-patient order permutation as negative controls.

Supportive: nontrivial held-out trajectory advantage for the non-routine endpoint with uncertainty excluding a trivial margin, stable across department/documentation/missingness/assay sensitivities, and absent in the timing-shifted control. Adverse: no improvement, degradation, advantage confined to routine procedures, disappearance after process adjustment, or a positive negative control. Inconclusive: sparse events, unstable dictionary, missing timing, imprecise intervals, or inability to adjudicate clinical meaning.

## Uncertainties and missing evidence
The routine-procedure dictionary and exact event counts are not frozen. Laboratory units are absent, so cross-assay pooling is not assumed. The source lacks validated death/ICU fields and independent clinical adjudication of whether a procedure represents deterioration. No model has been fitted and no effect is claimed. The solver must freeze the dictionary, fit both models, estimate uncertainty, and link conclusions to outputs. Clinical experts are required to interpret endpoint meaning and any potential monitoring implication.

## Method comparison
The snapshot baseline is simple, auditable, and tests whether any incremental signal exists. The sequence/state-space alternative can reveal persistence, ordering, and cross-marker interactions that a snapshot loses. A larger neural sequence model is deferred because event support and runtime are not yet established; CPU is appropriate for the bounded tabular/sequence comparison. This is an adaptation, not a reproduction of any demonstration.
