# Provisional hypothesis: macrophage context induces a reproducible within-FLS state response

Seed context: D3-M1, narrowed to the verified E-MTAB-8873 assay scope.

## Proposed relationship

In the targeted human synovial type B synovial cell (FLS) assay E-MTAB-8873, exposure to MerTK/CD206-positive versus MerTK/CD206-negative macrophages produces a reproducible, donor-aware change in the measured FLS transcript state. The unresolved claim is that this response is a cell-intrinsic/context-dependent FLS state change rather than being adequately explained by recorded contributing-cell counts or sample construction. This is a hypothesis, not an executed result.

Restrict analysis to verified FLS samples and matched FLS-only/Mpos/Mneg context units supported by the design. The primary deliverable is a fitted context contrast and decomposition, not a mechanism claim.

Supportive evidence: a reproducible within-FLS context contrast remains after donor and recorded context adjustment, with stable uncertainty across held-out donor/condition units and a coherent state pattern rather than isolated features. Adverse evidence: no contrast, or the contrast disappears/is explained by recorded cell-count or sample-construction variables. Inconclusive evidence: insufficient independent donor units, unstable estimates, failed feature-map reconciliation, or inability to distinguish context from batch/treatment.

## Uncertainties and missing evidence

The panel measures only 445 features; unmeasured genes cannot be inferred. The design does not provide an independent whole-tissue cell-abundance measurement for the same samples, so this assay cannot establish a whole-tissue composition explanation. Treatment, age, sex, batch, macrophage phenotype, and sample construction may confound the response. Cross-sectional coculture does not establish a causal pathway or clinical treatment mechanism. Exact matched contrasts, sample-to-donor mapping, and feature-map reconciliation still require direct source inspection.
