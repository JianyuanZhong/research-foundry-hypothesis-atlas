# Renal and nutrition context as a safety signal during recorded insulin infusion

Status: proposed repaired child for [prior hypothesis], with methodological safeguards borrowed from the hypotension and observation-process parents. This is an observational risk/monitoring study, not a treatment-effect claim.

## Scientific question and hypothesis

The actionable unresolved question is whether a bedside monitoring rule should become more intensive when a patient receiving a recorded continuous insulin infusion has both recent renal deterioration and no recently documented nutritional delivery.

The falsifiable primary hypothesis is:

> Among adult first ICU stays with a recorded insulin infusion and a recent glucose measurement, at the same recent recorded insulin infusion rate and baseline illness context, a 24-hour rise in creatinine and absence of documented nutrition delivery in the preceding 12 hours jointly predict a higher probability of a new glucose <70 mg/dL in the next 6 hours. The joint association is larger than the sum of the two main associations and is reproducible across held-out hospitals.

The estimand is an adjusted, person-period association: the 6-hour risk difference and risk ratio for the joint renal-worsening/low-documented-nutrition profile versus neither feature, evaluated at the median recent insulin rate and prespecified baseline covariates. It does not estimate the causal effect of insulin, withholding nutrition, or an intervention.

What is already supported is narrower. Renal dysfunction and interrupted enteral nutrition are clinically plausible risk contexts for insulin-related hypoglycemia, and this eICU release contains timestamped insulin-infusion updates, glucose/creatinine laboratory rows, and structured intake labels. The eICU medication documentation explicitly warns that medication rows are orders and do not necessarily mean administration; continuous-infusion titration is represented in infusionDrug. Neither the seed nor the public literature search establishes the proposed interaction, its size, or that missing intake documentation means nutrition stopped.

The substantive advance is a time-aligned, multicenter test of an actionable interaction and its transportability, rather than a static association of insulin orders with hypoglycemia. A positive result would motivate prospective glucose/nutrition interruption surveillance; it would not by itself set an insulin dose or monitoring interval.

## Population, time origin, and exclusions

Use the frozen eICU v2.0 source snapshot. The unit is a 6-hour person-period within a patient ICU stay.

- Include age >=18 (parse the eICU age field, retaining the documented > 89 category as 90), first ICU stay by uniquepid using the smallest valid unitvisitnumber, and a valid patientunitstayid, hospitalid, unitdischargeoffset, and hospitaldischargestatus.
- Include periods at t = 24, 30, ..., 66 hours after ICU admission, because the first-day severity fields can then be used without allowing their later values to leak into early periods. A period requires a recorded insulin infusion update in [t-6,t) and at least one valid glucose measurement before t. The primary analysis uses the earliest eligible stay per uniquepid; a sensitivity analysis includes subsequent stays with patient-clustered uncertainty.
- Stop follow-up at the earliest of t+6, ICU discharge, or death. Discharge alive and death are competing events, not negative hypoglycemia outcomes. Do not create a no-event label when the patient leaves the ICU before the outcome window.
- Exclude a period if a structured end-of-life discussion in carePlanEOL.cpleoldiscussionoffset is at or before t; repeat analysis retaining these periods with an EOL indicator. This is a safeguard against interpreting reduced monitoring near a limitation-of-treatment decision as a nutrition mechanism.
- Do not use any value whose event time is after t for prediction. In particular, do not use labresultrevisedoffset as an event time, future discharge fields except for censoring/competing-event definition, apachePatientResult post-stay outcomes, or any future insulin/glucose/nutrition row.

Periods are not a causal start-insulin cohort: infusionDrug has rate-update observations, and the record does not prove that every order or update was administered continuously between updates.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The same six-hour risk periods, outcomes, split, covariates, and competing-event rules are used for both models.

Simple prespecified baseline: a pooled logistic cause-specific model with one row per period, patient-clustered standard errors, hospital fixed effects in development and hospital-clustered uncertainty. Predictors are recent insulin rate (restricted to prespecified linear/quartile terms), creatinine delta, documented-nutrition flag, their single interaction, prior glucose trajectory, baseline diabetes/age/weight/sex, admission source, unit type, baseline severity/organ support, vasopressor context, and an indicator for glucose measurement coverage. Separate cause-specific heads model hypoglycemia, death, and alive discharge; no discharge-before-outcome period is labeled as a hypoglycemia non-event. The primary scientific output is the adjusted interaction risk difference/RR, calibration, and hospital-specific shrinkage estimates—not AUROC alone.

Substantive learned alternative: a small time-aware GRU (or temporal convolution if GRU is unavailable) consuming the preceding 24 hours as four six-hour bins. Inputs are the same clinical sources, with channels for insulin rate/update density, glucose/creatinine/BUN/potassium/vital summaries, nutrition delivery and missingness, vasopressor support, and static patient/hospital covariates. It has three competing-event output heads for next-6-hour hypoglycemia, death, and alive discharge. Train with patient-level splitting by uniquepid; reserve entire hospitals for outer transport testing (for example, five hospital folds, with hospital-stratified development/validation). Standardize numeric features using development data only, mask missing measurements explicitly, early-stop on validation cause-specific Brier score, and calibrate once on validation data. Report the model’s risk contrast under observed profiles at matched insulin-rate strata and a transparent time-ablation/feature-permutation analysis. The learned alternative can reveal nonlinear thresholds, delayed renal trajectories, and whether missingness/observation timing carries transportable signal that the additive baseline loses; it cannot turn observational data into a treatment effect.

Primary comparison is scientific: concordance and transport of the renal-by-nutrition interaction and risk calibration across hospitals. A small AUROC gain without a stable, clinically interpretable interaction or calibration improvement is not a substantive advance.

## Analysis, uncertainty, and falsification

Fit the baseline and learned model only after freezing the insulin, glucose, creatinine, nutrition codebooks and period construction. Use patient-level bootstrap or influence-function uncertainty with hospital-aware resampling; report event counts, effective number of patients, number of hospitals, missingness, and calibration-in-the-large/slope. Do not select the hypothesis after inspecting held-out outcomes.

Supportive evidence requires all of: (1) the baseline interaction and learned observed-profile contrast point in the prespecified positive direction; (2) the adjusted joint-vs-neither risk difference is at least 2 percentage points over 6 hours and its 95% interval excludes zero; (3) direction is not driven by one hospital and remains after the strict prior-delivery sensitivity; (4) transport calibration is acceptable and the interaction survives removal of observation-density features. The 2-point threshold is a decision-relevance threshold, not a claim that smaller effects are biologically absent.

Adverse evidence is a reproducible reverse interaction, or a sufficiently precise null whose interval excludes the 2-point minimum important difference, with codebook and falsification checks passing. If the learned model shows a signal but the baseline interaction does not, interpret that as unresolved nonlinear/heterogeneous association, not confirmation. If the baseline is positive but the learned model fails transport, interpret the finding as site-specific or observation-process dependent.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few qualifying insulin periods or <54-mg/dL events, unreliable nutrition-label coverage, wide intervals spanning both null and the minimum important difference, poor competing-risk calibration, or inability to separate discharge/EOL from observation loss. The solver must return these diagnostics rather than force a binary conclusion.

Falsification and robustness tests:

- Randomly permute insulin-update times within each stay while preserving the patient’s rate values and update count. A claimed time-aligned signal should attenuate; persistence indicates temporal trend or coding artifact.
- Repeat the model in insulin-free periods with an analogous recent-insulin placebo exposure; the renal-by-nutrition interaction should not reproduce the insulin-specific claim.
- Test a pre-index placebo outcome, glucose <70 in (t-6,t], using only covariates available at t. A strong association here is compatible with reverse causation and weakens a prospective interpretation.
- Repeat using only bedside-glucose rows and only laboratory glucose rows, and report the glucose testing count. Large disagreement indicates measurement-process dependence.
- Refit excluding periods with any recorded dextrose/glucose rescue order and excluding periods near ICU discharge; do not interpret these as causal adjustment for administered rescue.
- Use hospital-held-out evaluation and a leave-one-hospital-out influence analysis. No hospital ranking is reported.

## Evidence boundaries and clinical deliverable

The deliverable is newly fitted baseline and learned competing-risk models, a frozen cohort/feature manifest, adjusted renal-by-nutrition contrasts with uncertainty, hospital-held-out calibration, falsification results, and an auditable count of missing/ambiguous nutrition and insulin records. Completion is established by these computed outputs, not by a positive hypothesis test.

This dataset cannot establish actual insulin administration for medication orders, exact nutrition interruption, bedside symptoms, confirmatory point-of-care testing, adjudicated AKI, clinician intent, or whether changing monitoring or insulin dose prevents harm. Those claims require medication administration records, nutrition-order/feeding-stop times, chart review/clinical adjudication, and a prospective or emulated-intervention study. Structured eICU note fields are retained but the dataset catalog states that public narrative sections are removed; no text model should infer rationale from absent narrative.

Current-public and local references are hypothesis context only: the local eICU-CRD v2.0 documentation and medication page were read; the expert seed is explicitly untested; and a Europe PMC search (source frozen in the episode) returned related glycemic/nutrition observational work but does not establish this interaction. The three research-ambition demonstrations were read through their available local materials and methods guide; they motivate careful temporal sequence evaluation, baseline comparison, and bounded adaptation, not reproduction or a mandatory model choice.
