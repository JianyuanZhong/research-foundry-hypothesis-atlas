# Episode 16 — posterior-thigh MFI, later falls report, and response selection

## Unresolved question and clinical value

Among UK Biobank `ukb672073` participants with visit-2 body MRI, is higher bilateral posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) associated with a classifiable self-reported fall at visit 3, at least 366 days later, after adjustment for visceral adiposity, thigh muscle distribution, baseline falls and measured function? Is that association materially sensitive to measured selection into later return and response, among people who already have visit-2 MRI/MFI?

Falls can cause injury, fracture, disability and loss of independence. If a reasonably precise positive association survives measured function and a follow-up-selection sensitivity, the result would justify prospective replication with event-validated falls; a precise null/adverse result would lower the priority of MFI as a falls phenotype. It would not support clinical screening, treatment, or a causal claim. The new secondary analysis specifically asks whether the observed complete-case association is fragile to measured follow-up response patterns, not whether MRI nonattendance can be corrected.

## Evidence already supported vs. hypothesis

Three inspected works delimit the question. The Europe PMC abstract for a large UKB thigh-MFI analysis reports broad mortality and disease associations, not this lagged falls contrast [K1]; only its abstract was inspected. The closest overlap reports cross-sectional UKB associations between posterior/anterior thigh-volume ratio and falls in the last year [K2], so novelty is not claimed for thigh MRI or falls generally. The inspected myosteatosis review summarizes age-related evidence but notes unresolved age versus disease/disuse explanations and remaining longitudinal gaps [K3]. These works motivate and constrain the question; none establishes the proposed posterior-MFI-to-later-falls association or resolves response selection.

**Hypothesis under test:** within the prespecified analysis population, one SD higher visit-2 mean bilateral posterior-thigh MFI has a positive adjusted association with any classifiable visit-3 falls report, and this estimate does not materially change after weighting for measured visit-2 predictors of timely return and classifiable response.

**Strongest rival:** pre-existing frailty, illness/disuse, prior falls, health and differential recall/reporting can influence both MFI and later response/report; healthy participation/return selection may distort the observed association. Adjustment and weighting probe only measured proxies. Neither attenuation nor persistence identifies a mechanism or eliminates latent confounding, reverse causality, MNAR response, measurement error or selection into MRI attendance.

## Estimands and analysis

**Primary respondent-conditional association:** fit the parent’s prespecified adjusted logistic model for any falls report with mean bilateral MFI per SD, measured VAT, nonredundant thigh-muscle distribution, prior falls, age, sex, BMI/body fat as prespecified, smoking, activity, walking pace and grip. Avoid algebraically redundant volume/ratio terms. Report the MFI log-odds coefficient, odds ratio, 95% interval, absolute predicted-risk contrast standardized over a declared analysis-cohort distribution, complete-case flow and event counts. Use participant-level bootstrap uncertainty. The matched shrinkage-GAM alternative uses the same participants and outcome, a regularized MFI smooth and prespecified measured-function interaction/shape checks; compare nonlinear shape/heterogeneity with the logistic baseline rather than optimizing small predictive gains. Complexity is justified only if it reveals a materially different MFI pattern or identifies that the association is concentrated in a measured-function stratum.

**Secondary measured follow-up-selection sensitivity:** define the denominator as unique participants with visit-2 date, both numeric visit-2 MFI sides, and the complete prespecified baseline covariate set needed for the outcome model. This is the *MRI/MFI-observed cohort*, not all UKB participants, not MRI invitees, and not people without MRI. Within it, define `R=1` if a valid visit-3 date is at least 366 days after visit 2 and the visit-3 falls item is classifiable (1/2/3); otherwise `R=0`, while separately tabulating no recorded visit-3 date, shorter-gap visit, refusal, other/missing answer, and other exclusion reasons. Audit predictor missingness/special codes, same-namespace sex joins and uniqueness; do not silently remove rows or use post-visit-2 predictors.

Fit a modest ridge-logistic model for `P(R=1 | X_visit2)` on the full MRI/MFI-observed denominator, using five-fold `eid`-grouped cross-fitting for out-of-fold response probabilities. Report Brier/log loss, calibration, response rates by MFI and covariate strata, overlap of propensity distributions, weight quantiles/extremes, effective sample size and measured-covariate balance before/after weighting. The sensitivity estimand targets the MRI/MFI-observed complete-covariate source cohort only under correct response model, positivity and MAR conditional on measured `X`; it is distinct from, and does not replace, the respondent-conditional primary estimand. MRI-attendance selection is unobserved and uncorrected. A numerical MNAR tipping point is deferred because outcomes for nonresponders and defensible external shift parameters are unavailable.

## Interpretation and falsification

- **Supportive:** adjusted primary MFI association is positive with uncertainty excluding the null; the weighted sensitivity has adequate overlap/effective sample size and remains directionally consistent with no material paired shift. This supports a robust association within stated sampled populations and motivates event-validated prospective replication—not mechanism, causality, or clinical use.
- **Adverse to the primary hypothesis:** a sufficiently precise adjusted estimate is null or adverse, or a stable weighted sensitivity reverses/attenuates the association with adequate overlap and materially informative uncertainty. Report whether this weakens the respondent association or shows sensitivity to measured selection; do not call the selection model proof of bias or a protective MFI effect.
- **Inconclusive:** sparse valid cases/events, wide intervals, unverified codes, poor propensity overlap/calibration, low effective sample size, strong truncation dependence, or unstable bootstrap. Do not treat an imprecise null as refutation or an unstable weighted estimate as evidence. Report the diagnostic and defer/revise.

For the secondary “material shift,” report the paired bootstrap distribution of weighted minus unweighted log-odds coefficient per SD; prespecify a statistical flag if its 95% interval excludes zero and absolute shift is at least half the absolute unweighted estimate, or if sign reverses. This is not a clinical threshold and is unstable if the parent estimate is near zero; in that case report continuous difference and uncertainty without a binary materiality claim. The primary positive claim is weakened only by a precise null/adverse estimate after data gates pass. An association persisting after adjustment is still not causal.

## Unavailable evidence and next decision

The listed sources cannot adjudicate whether a fall truly occurred or its date, identify visit-3 invitation/eligibility, quantify MFI among MRI nonattenders, remove nonresponse MNAR, validate MRI quality thresholds/scale, or externally validate the estimate. These stronger claims require event-level records or clinical adjudication, MRI QC/scale documentation, follow-up/nonrespondent data, and independent prospective replication. If gates pass and measured-selection sensitivity is estimable, continue to externally validated event ascertainment; if support/positivity fails, retain the primary as respondent-conditional descriptive evidence and revise/seek better data; if results are precise and adverse, deprioritize this MFI-falls direction; if uncertain, defer rather than infer a null.
