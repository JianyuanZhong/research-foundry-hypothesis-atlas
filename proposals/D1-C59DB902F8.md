# Early lactate trajectory and 28-day mortality with frozen ICU strata and fail-closed estimation

## Child status and substantive repair

This is the final targeted child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the clinically anchored prognostic question: among adults entering an ICU with an initial blood lactate at least 2.0 mmol/L, does a second lactate obtained two to six hours later provide incremental prognostic information for 28-day mortality beyond the initial lactate and admission covariates? It remains an observational association experiment. It does not estimate the effect of lactate clearance, treatment benefit, safety, or the value of a clinical alert.

The preceding candidate froze the source bindings, dictionaries, exact ICU strata, null controls, and fail-closed analysis. This final child resolves only three operational ambiguities: one timestamp convention for date-only versus timestamped death evidence, explicit known-survival versus unknown censoring after discharge, and a paired out-of-sample log-loss criterion in place of the undefined test-set likelihood-ratio p-value. No new causal estimand, treatment comparison, clinical decision rule, source, variable, stratum, null control, or association scope is introduced.

The evidence-supported claim is only that serial EHR measurements may contain prognostic information and that this MIMIC snapshot contains ICU, laboratory, vital-sign, medication-event, and mortality fields. The untested claim is the narrow, falsifiable incremental association stated above under the exact measurement windows, item dictionaries, strata, covariate set, and censoring rules below. A supportive result would justify external/prospective validation of a prognostic marker, not lactate-guided intervention.

## Frozen item dictionaries and unit rules

### Lactate and creatinine

The primary blood-lactate inventory is exactly the rows in `hosp/d_labitems` with `label == "Lactate"` and `fluid == "Blood"`: item IDs **50813, 52442, 53154**. Retain a labevent only if `itemid` is one of those IDs, `valuenum` is finite, and `valueuom == "mmol/L"` exactly after trimming surrounding whitespace. No unit conversion, keyword matching, alternate IDs, `value` parsing, or result from another fluid is allowed. The eligibility threshold is `L0 >= 2.0` mmol/L.

The primary creatinine inventory is exactly the rows with `label == "Creatinine"` and `fluid == "Blood"` and `itemid` in **50912, 52546**. Retain only finite `valuenum` with exact `valueuom == "mg/dL"`; the one observed empty-unit row is excluded and counted. No serum/urine/body-fluid synonym expansion or conversion is allowed. The dictionary/source audit must report counts and units by ID before mortality is merged.

Within an episode and item inventory, results sharing the same `charttime` are collapsed to the arithmetic median of finite retained values. The median is computed across all retained IDs at that exact time, and the number of contributing rows and IDs is retained. If two retained rows have equal time but one is marked abnormal, `flag` does not override the numeric value. Ties in selecting the earliest time are therefore resolved by this median rule, not file order or `labevent_id`.

### MAP

The exact primary MAP inventory is the two `icu/d_items` rows whose observed dictionary values are frozen as follows: item **220052**, label `Arterial Blood Pressure mean`, abbreviation `ABPm`, `linksto == chartevents`, unit `mmHg`; and item **220181**, label `Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean`, abbreviation `NBPm`, `linksto == chartevents`, unit `mmHg`. Retain finite `chartevents.valuenum` with `valueuom == "mmHg"` exactly, using `charttime`. Same-time values across these two IDs are collapsed to the median; if both invasive and non-invasive values exist, neither is preferred. The first such median in the baseline window is `MAP0`. No systolic/diastolic calculation, APACHE score, alarm, waveform, text value, or label keyword is allowed.

### Vasopressor exposure

The early vasopressor indicator is included, conservatively, as a binary covariate. Its exact `inputevents.itemid` set, frozen from `icu/d_items` with `linksto == "inputevents"`, is **221289 (Epinephrine), 229617 (Epinephrine.), 221906 (Norepinephrine), 222315 (Vasopressin), 221749 (Phenylephrine), 229630 (Phenylephrine (50/250)), 229631 (Phenylephrine (200/250)_OLD_1), 229632 (Phenylephrine (200/250)), 221662 (Dopamine), and 221653 (Dobutamine)**. An episode is exposed if at least one retained input-event row has an exact `starttime` in `[intime, intime + 6 hours]`, an item ID in this set, and a nonmissing/nonempty `statusdescription` that is not an explicit `Cancelled` or `Rewritten` value after case-insensitive trimming. The indicator does not use amount, rate, endtime, order status inferred from another field, pharmacy orders, ingredient events, or notes. If `starttime` is missing, the row is excluded and counted. This is exposure documentation, not a dose or treatment effect.

### Ventilation omission

Mechanical ventilation is **omitted** from every primary and secondary prognostic model. The reason is not absence of ventilation-related labels: the dictionary contains text ventilator items and a checkbox, and process information exists in `procedureevents`; rather, this protocol does not claim that those heterogeneous fields form a validated episode/state dictionary. No ventilation proxy, free-text inference, procedure substitution, or post hoc inclusion is permitted. The model is explicitly described as not adjusted for ventilation.

## Population and time windows

1. Read all `hosp/admissions` and all `icu/icustays`, validate the declared schema and keys, and inner-join on `(subject_id,hadm_id)`.
2. For each hospital admission, select the ICU stay with the earliest nonmissing `intime`; ties are broken by smallest numeric `stay_id`. Retain only adults with `anchor_age >= 18`, nonmissing `intime` and `outtime`, and `outtime >= intime`. The index is the first ICU stay within that hospital admission. Admissions remain distinct at this stage.
3. Define the baseline lactate `L0` as the first collapsed blood-lactate time in the closed interval `[intime, intime + 6 hours]` and with value at least 2.0. If no value meets this definition, exclude the episode from the eligible denominator and record the exclusion reason.
4. Define `L1` as the first collapsed blood-lactate time in `(L0_time + 2 hours, L0_time + 6 hours]` and with `charttime <= outtime`. If there is no L1, retain the episode in the eligible denominator with `L1_missing = 1`, but do not code it as zero, normal, stable, or alive.
5. Define `delta6 = L1 - L0` and `relative_delta = (L1-L0)/L0` only when L1 exists. The primary exposure is continuous `delta6` per 1 mmol/L. A secondary descriptive trajectory has `decrease >=20%`, `stable/decrease <20%`, or `increase >=20%`; the exact boundary belongs to the first category for `relative_delta <= -0.20`, middle category for `-0.20 < relative_delta < 0.20`, and third for `relative_delta >= 0.20`.
6. Baseline covariates are age (`anchor_age`), sex (`gender`), `admission_type`, `admission_location`, `L0`, first qualifying creatinine in `[intime,intime+6h]`, first qualifying `MAP0` in `[intime,intime+6h]`, exact `first_careunit` stratum, and the early vasopressor indicator. Missing categorical values receive a literal `MISSING`; missing numeric values receive a missingness indicator and median imputation calculated from the development partition only. No variable uses a timestamp after L1, except the exposure definition and the prespecified ascertainment of whether L1 exists. The availability indicator `L1_missing` is not included in the primary complete-case prognostic model; it is analyzed separately as a selection/availability outcome and included in the observation-weighted sensitivity model only through the observation model.
7. Primary complete-case modeling requires L0, L1, creatinine, MAP0, and outcome status. A secondary model drops creatinine and/or MAP0 only in a clearly labeled reduced-covariate sensitivity; there is no automatic per-row covariate deletion.

## Deterministic 28-day mortality, censoring, and unknowns

The target time origin is ICU `intime`. Parse every valid timestamp as an ISO-like **UTC-naive** timestamp without timezone conversion; timestamps with an explicit offset are invalid rather than shifted. Let `H28 = intime + 672 hours`, preserving the time of day. Parse `admissions.deathtime` at its recorded date and time. Because `patients.dod` is date-only in this snapshot, map every valid `YYYY-MM-DD` value deterministically to `YYYY-MM-DD 00:00:00.000000` UTC-naive. This midnight mapping is the sole date-imprecision convention: do not truncate `H28` or `deathtime` to dates, append noon/end-of-day, or apply a half-day correction. Let `Tdeath` be the minimum valid mapped timestamp across `deathtime` and `dod`, retaining both raw values, mapped values, and provenance. If both valid sources disagree, use their minimum and report the discrepancy. Thus a date-only `dod` on `H28`'s calendar date is an event because mapped midnight is no later than `H28`; an exact `deathtime` later than `H28` on that same date is not an event unless the mapped `dod` supplies earlier evidence. `hospital_expire_flag` never overrides these comparisons.

Assign outcome status using only explicit, orderable death-date evidence:

* `Y28 = 1` if at least one valid death source exists and `Tdeath <= H28`, including exact equality.
* `Y28 = 0` (known survival through 28 days) if at least one valid death source exists, every valid death-source timestamp is strictly after `H28`, and no valid source is on or before `H28`. A later recorded death is explicit evidence of survival through the horizon.
* `Y28 = UNKNOWN_CENSORED` if neither death source supplies a valid timestamp. This remains unknown after ICU or hospital discharge at any time, including `dischtime > H28`, because discharge, continued hospitalization, `outtime`, and `hospital_expire_flag == 0` are not death-registry follow-up and are never used to infer survival. If a raw nonmissing death field cannot be parsed or ordered, assign unknown unless the other valid source independently establishes `Y28 = 1`; a later valid source cannot establish survival when another nonmissing source is invalid because an earlier death cannot be excluded.
* `hospital_expire_flag == 1` is retained as a separate in-hospital mortality sensitivity endpoint only. It is never a proxy for 28-day death, and `hospital_expire_flag == 0` is never proof of 28-day survival.

QC must tabulate the four source patterns (both valid, `deathtime` only, `dod` only, neither valid), invalid raw fields, source disagreements, events determined by mapped date-only `dod`, and cases whose classification differs if `dod` were mapped to end-of-day; that last count quantifies precision sensitivity but does not alter the primary convention.

The primary outcome analysis includes only known `Y28` values, reports separately the known-event, known-survival, and unknown/censored fractions, and compares known versus unknown baseline distributions. Unknowns are not imputed as alive or dead. The inverse-probability-of-observation sensitivity estimates the observation probability from baseline variables and exact ICU stratum only, fit in development data; weights are stabilized, truncated at the prespecified 1st/99th percentiles learned in development, and applied only to known outcomes. If positivity fails (any development cell with fewer than 10 observed outcomes or an estimated probability outside `[0.02,0.98]`), the weighted sensitivity is `NOT_ESTIMABLE`, not repaired by changing the model.

## Analysis, baselines, event policy, and separation

Use one fixed chronological split by `intime`: earliest 70% of eligible episodes for development, next 15% for calibration/threshold-free model checking, and latest 15% as locked test. The split cutpoints are the order statistics of eligible `intime`, ties assigned deterministically by `(intime, subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id)`. The test set is never used for dictionary selection, imputation, spline knots, penalty selection, thresholding, or stratum pooling. A sensitivity analysis keeps only the earliest eligible admission per `subject_id`, selected by `(intime, hadm_id, stay_id)`.

The primary complete-case comparison uses the same rows, split, encoding, imputation, and outcome for:

* `M0`: age, sex, admission type, admission location, exact ICU stratum, L0, creatinine, MAP0, and vasopressor indicator.
* `M1`: `M0 + delta6`.
* `Mfirst`: L0 alone, descriptive reference.
* `Mtime-null`: `M0 + interval_hours`, where `interval_hours = L1_time - L0_time` and the observed delta6 is permuted only within exact ICU stratum and baseline-L0 decile, using seed `20260912`; it is not a surrogate exposure and is not interpreted causally.

The primary continuous M1 coefficient is linear. A prespecified secondary M1 uses a restricted cubic spline for delta6 with knots at the development-only 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles; if a knot is nonunique or the development exposure has fewer than 100 complete rows, the spline is `NOT_ESTIMABLE` and the linear model remains primary.

The primary estimator is ordinary maximum-likelihood logistic regression with an intercept. It is fit only if the design matrix has full column rank, both outcome classes occur, and every coefficient has finite estimates with convergence status `success` under a fixed tolerance of `1e-8` and maximum 200 iterations. Deterministic separation handling is:

1. Before fitting, run an exact rank and cell check on every categorical level and each binary covariate.
2. Fit the unpenalized model with the specified solver and deterministic column order.
3. If any coefficient is nonfinite, the solver reports perfect/quasi separation, the Hessian is singular, or convergence fails, mark that model `MLE_SEPARATED_OR_NONESTIMABLE`; do not drop a level, collapse strata, alter the event threshold, or switch silently to a penalized model.
4. A fixed Firth logistic fit (same rows and columns, deterministic iteration and step-halving) is a sensitivity estimate only when MLE is nonestimable. It cannot satisfy the primary evidence gate. If Firth also fails, report no coefficient.

The minimum descriptive event policy is 10 events and 10 nonevents in each development, calibration, and test partition for any overall model. Below that, the model is `LOW_EVENT_COUNT` and the corresponding discrimination/calibration gate is inconclusive. No oversampling, synthetic event creation, or outcome-dependent exclusion is allowed. Confidence intervals for coefficients and performance differences use 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap resamples with seed `20260912`; a resample that has no event, no nonevent, or a failed fit is retained as a failed replicate and the failure count is reported. If more than 10% of bootstrap replicates fail, the interval and gate are `NOT_ESTIMABLE`. AUC uses the deterministic midrank convention for ties.

Report test AUROC, Brier score, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, the paired out-of-sample log-loss improvement and its deviance-scale equivalent for M1 versus M0, the likelihood-ratio statistic as a descriptive in-sample fit quantity only, odds ratio and 95% interval for delta6, and absolute AUROC/Brier/calibration changes. For each locked-test complete case `i`, obtain the single prespecified probabilities `p0_i` and `p1_i` from models fit in development, clip each to `[1e-15, 1-1e-15]` only for scoring, and define

`ell_mi = -[Y_i log(p_mi) + (1-Y_i) log(1-p_mi)]`,

`d_i = ell_0i - ell_1i`, `D_pair = 2 * sum_i(d_i)`, and `DeltaLL = mean_i(d_i) = D_pair/(2*n_test)`. Positive values mean that M1 has lower out-of-sample log loss. The primary paired criterion is `n_test` identical for both models, with identical rows/outcomes and no test fitting: require finite predictions for every case, `DeltaLL > 0`, and the two-sided 95% percentile subject-cluster bootstrap lower confidence bound for `DeltaLL` (1,000 resamples, seed `20260912`, resampling whole subjects and retaining all their test episodes) to be strictly greater than 0. The bootstrap uses the same paired resample for M0 and M1; a replicate with no event, no nonevent, or a nonfinite score is failed, and if more than 10% fail the paired criterion is `NOT_ESTIMABLE`. The corresponding deviance improvement `D_pair` and its bootstrap interval are reported, but the decision is made on `DeltaLL` so it is invariant to test-set size. No likelihood-ratio reference distribution or test-set likelihood-ratio p-value is calculated or used. Calibration parameters are evaluated without recalibration on the locked test set; the 15% partition is used only to choose no model threshold and to verify the frozen encoding.

## Numerical null-control criterion and primary gate

Generate exactly 1,000 null permutations of `delta6` within exact ICU stratum and L0 decile, with independent streams from master seed `20260912` and permutation index `0..999`. The interval covariate is not permuted. Refit `Mtime-null` for each permutation using the locked M0 fit procedure and calculate its test-set incremental AUROC (`AUC_null - AUC_M0`) and likelihood-ratio statistic (`LR_null`). Failed null fits are reported; if more than 10% fail, the null comparison is `NOT_ESTIMABLE`.

Define `q975_AUC_null` and `q975_LR_null` as the empirical 97.5th percentiles using the nearest-rank rule. The true trajectory is considered distinguishable from the temporal null only if both:

`AUC_M1 - AUC_M0 > q975_AUC_null + 0.005`

and

`LR_M1 > q975_LR_null + 1.0`.

A null control is declared to perform “as much as” the true trajectory, and the incremental hypothesis fails that control, if either inequality is false. The 0.005 AUROC and 1.0 LR margins are fixed utility margins for this experiment, not clinical safety thresholds. A numerical null pass cannot establish causation; it only rules out this prespecified timing/measurement-intensity explanation under the permutation design.

The primary supportive gate requires all of the following on the locked test set: (i) M1 has a finite, nonseparated fit; (ii) the paired out-of-sample log-loss criterion above is estimable and its 95% subject-cluster bootstrap lower bound is strictly greater than 0 (equivalently, the reported deviance improvement is positive with the same paired interval); (iii) AUROC increase at least 0.02 with bootstrap lower confidence bound above 0; (iv) absolute calibration-in-the-large change no greater than 0.05 and no calibration slope deterioration below 0.90; and (v) both numerical null inequalities above. These are experiment utility gates, not validated clinical thresholds. If test events or any required fit are insufficient, the result is inconclusive rather than adverse.

## Falsification and interpretation

The protocol fails computationally if any dictionary row, unit rule, exact ICU label, archive member, schema hash, time boundary, tie rule, seed, split, or mortality rule changes after outcome access; if post-L1 values enter baseline covariates; if duplicate handling depends on file order; if a ventilation proxy is introduced; if missing outcomes are coded alive; if models use different rows or encodings; if separation triggers analyst-selected collapsing or penalization; if null permutations cross strata or deciles; if test outcomes tune any component; or if the paired score is computed from separately selected rows, test-refit probabilities, an unpaired bootstrap, or a likelihood-ratio reference p-value.

A supportive result means that, in this exact MIMIC population with known 28-day status, delta6 has an incremental association that meets the locked discrimination, calibration, fit, and null-control criteria and is not wholly confined to an unestimable ICU stratum. It supports only the modest statement that early lactate trajectory contains incremental prognostic information in this source population. It does not show that lactate clearance causes survival, that clinicians should target lactate, or that an alert or treatment improves outcomes.

An adverse result means a finite, adequately powered analysis does not meet the incremental gate, the null timing control is comparably informative, or the association is unstable across exact ICU strata. This supports not assuming that a second early lactate adds reliable information beyond the initial lactate and baseline fields in this setting; it does not prove that lactate is biologically uninformative or that another interval has no value.

An inconclusive result means low L1 availability, high or differential unknown mortality, sparse events, nonpositivity, separation, failed bootstrap/null fits, missing strata, or materially discordant complete-case and observation-weighted results. It identifies a measurement or follow-up limitation and requires protocolized serial sampling and ascertainment before a stronger claim.

MIMIC does not supply assay calibration across institutions, fluid balance, source control, exact treatment dose, clinician intent, bedside reassessment, a complete post-discharge death registry, or causal intervention assignment. Expert adjudication is not needed to compute the stated association but is required to interpret whether a prognostic signal is clinically actionable. External multicenter validation and, for any treatment or alert claim, a prospective interventional study are required.
