## Interpretation limits and exact additional evidence required

Support would establish only that, in this UKB snapshot, the frozen UACR-plus-grip ordering retains equation-defined biochemical yield in both available recorded-sex strata and higher fixed-denominator low-UACR/recorded-N18 yield when both policies receive the same proportional sex quotas. It would strengthen the rationale for prospective evaluation of that constrained roster rule.

It would not establish sex or gender fairness, nondiscrimination, calibrated CKD probability, true or persistent low-albuminuria CKD, measured filtration accuracy, complete event capture, mechanism, causal benefit from testing, safety, workflow feasibility, cost-effectiveness, or transportability. Equal quotas can itself be ethically or clinically inappropriate if need and benefit differ; the experiment estimates a tradeoff and cannot choose the normative policy.

Adverse biochemical evidence would show that proportional constraint removes a prespecified component of the frozen ranking advantage. Adverse N18 evidence would argue against material constrained-list low-UACR N18 enrichment. Neither proves G1 improves health. Inconclusive evidence leaves the constrained policy unresolved and is not equivalence.

Stronger claims require repeated clinically timed eGFR/UACR, measured GFR, outpatient and primary-care kidney outcomes, kidney-replacement dates, individual linkage censoring and emigration, assay order/result timestamps, downstream decisions and medications, costs, burden and harms, chart/nephrologist adjudication, gender identity and broader sex representation, ethnicity/socioeconomic intersectional analysis, patient and equity-expert review, external validation, and a prospective implementation or randomized testing-strategy study with patient-centered outcomes.
