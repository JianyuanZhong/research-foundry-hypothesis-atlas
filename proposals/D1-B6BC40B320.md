## 1. Unresolved question and opening

Clinicians treat a lactate that stays high as "ongoing hypoperfusion" and escalate support. But the same achieved
lactate can mean two different states: (i) persistent hypoperfusion driving production, or (ii) a
non-hypoperfusion/stress (type-B) hyperlactatemia driven by impaired clearance / metabolic stress. K1 states
hyperlactatemia can occur despite adequate oxygen delivery; K2 states lactate is a context-dependent marker whose
meaning varies with timing/perfusion. Neither establishes that the *observed direction* of perfusion change at a
fixed achieved lactate level modifies outcome, nor that it does so beyond lactate level and clearance. That is the
gap this experiment tests.

## 2. Evidence vs. untested claim

- Supported by prior evidence: higher and more persistent lactate is associated with worse outcome; hyperlactatemia
  can be non-hypoperfusion in mechanism (K1/K2); dynamic trajectory carries prognostic signal (K3).
- UNTESTED claim (this experiment): at the SAME achieved t* lactate and clearance, the DIRECTION of perfusion change
  over (t*-6h, t*] modifies 7-day deterioration risk.
- Not claimed: causality; that the perfusion-context signal is a mechanism; that escalation helps.

## 3. Competing explanations (falsifiable separation)

- (C) context-independent: t* lactate level + early clearance suffice; perfusion direction adds nothing.
- (R-hypo) context is real perfusion: worsening MAP/vasopressor-load/urine-output trajectory at fixed lactate
  predicts worse 7-day deterioration than improving trajectory.
- (R-measure) any apparent "context effect" is measurement-selection / observation-intensity / severity confounding:
  sicker, more-monitored patients get more labs/vitals and more escalation, producing a spurious context-risk link.

Distinguishing comparison: M0 (lactate + severity) < M1 (adds perfusion-context trajectory features) vs a **rival
observation-intensity feature set R3** (counts/rates of lactate draws, MAP charts, output records in the window) and
a **negative control R-neg** (perfusion-context labels permuted within strata of t* lactate). If M1 gain is
reproduced by R3 or by permuted labels, R-measure is favoured; if M1 beats both R3 and R-neg reproducibly,
R-hypo/C separation is supported.

  vasopressor escalation (inputevents rate increase/new agent) OR new RRT (`icu/procedureevents.csv.gz` itemids
  225441,225802,225803,225805,225809,225955), within 7 days of t*. Baseline severity covariates from labs/vitals at

  `hosp/d_icd_diagnoses.csv.gz` (exploratory stratum, not the primary population).

## 5. Method comparison (M0 < M1 < M2)

- M0 snapshot baseline: t* lactate + lactate clearance + severity covariates (interpretable logistic/Cox).
- M1 dynamic perfusion-trajectory features: adds MAP slope, vasopressor-load slope, urine-output slope (+ window
  means) — interpretable; directly tests the seed's DIRECTION hypothesis.
- M2 substantively different alternative: a small regularized/shared-latent **perfusion-context factor** fit over the
  (t*-24h, t*] trajectory (e.g., a low-rank / mixed-effects latent state summarising the joint MAP–vasopressor–urine
  path). What it reveals that M0/M1 lose: M0/M1 impose hand-crafted slopes and treat limbs independently; a shared
  latent factor can capture *coherent vs incoherent* multi-limb recovery and detect a context effect expressed
  through limb covariance rather than any single slope.
- R3 rival observation-intensity feature set and R-neg permuted-label negative control as in §3.
- Compute: all CPU-feasible. Cohort ~ tens of thousands of stays; per-patient windows small. M0/M1 are logistic/Cox
  with a few features; M2 is a low-rank/mixed-effects factor or a small ridge/1-hidden-layer model (n<<features
  controlled), fitting in minutes on a few CPUs. No GPU needed; a GPU would not change the scientific question.
  Estimated discovery-to-solver compute: < ~2000 CPU-seconds + bootstrap; well inside solver_planning_limits.

## 6. Analysis and falsification (prespecified)

Locked temporal/patient split (earliest 70% derivation / latest 30% confirmation by icustays.intime) or grouped CV;
report ΔC-index / ΔAUROC of M1 vs M0 and M2 vs M1 with bootstrap 95% CI, plus calibration (slope/reliability).
- Supportive (for R-hypo / against C): M0→M1 ΔAUC>=0.02 with 95% CI excluding 0 in confirmation, AND M1 gain
  exceeds R3 and R-neg gain (both < 0.01), calibration acceptable. Establishes perfusion-context direction adds
  discrimination beyond level+clearance; does NOT establish mechanism or treatment benefit.
- Adverse (for C): ΔAUC <= 0.01 (within +/-0.01 equivalence) — context direction adds nothing beyond level/clearance.
- R-measure favoured: M1 gain is matched by R3 (observation intensity) or collapses under R-neg permutation.
- Inconclusive: point estimate favourable but CI includes 0, or M2 unstable / not estimable; an imprecise null does
  not refute the claim. Secondary: continuous lactate-at-t* adjustment to ensure "same achieved level" conditioning.

Clinically/mechanistically NOT checkable here (require adjudication or another study): whether a context effect is
true microcirculatory hypoperfusion; whether escalating vs not improving a patient's trajectory changes outcome.
