# Is the laboratory signal specific after accounting for documentation opportunity?

Status: Episode 46 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test-ordering or treatment study, a physiologic-truth study, a hospital-quality ranking, or evidence that changing documentation, support, or laboratory surveillance improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest supported claim before this experiment is limited: the frozen eICU snapshot can link adult first ICU stays to revision-aware laboratory observations, recorded vital trajectories, documented organ-support activity, nursing/respiratory charting, hospital identifiers, and recorded ICU disposition. The parent established a clinically meaningful challenge from recorded support activity and transitions. No fitted result in the parent or this discovery episode establishes independent laboratory information, biological deterioration, clinician intent, or benefit.

The unresolved claim is:

> At ICU-relative minute 360, does revision-safe laboratory-observation history add a transportable, recorded-death-specific signal after adjustment for recorded physiology, documented organ-support context, and timestamp-safe broad nursing/respiratory documentation opportunity?

The advance is an interpretation test. A laboratory-process feature can look useful because a patient is being observed intensively in general, not because laboratory surveillance contains information beyond the rest of the record. Nursing and respiratory charting are an available, clinically adjacent measurement-opportunity challenge. If the laboratory increment persists after this challenge, it is a more credible recorded-data association; if it disappears or is reproduced by documentation permutations, it should be treated as a generic workflow fingerprint. Either result informs whether a future silent laboratory-review study is worth pursuing. Neither result establishes a biological mechanism or clinical benefit.

## Population, time and outcome

Use snapshot `[source checksum]`.

Predictors are known by the inclusive ICU-relative window [0,360] minutes. The outcome is in (360,1800]. Cause 1 is `unitdischargestatus = Expired` with `unitdischargeoffset` in that interval. Cause 2 is `unitdischargestatus = Alive` with that offset. A stay without either event by 1,800 is administratively censored. Missing/other disposition is reported and excluded under a frozen rule. Death after alive ICU discharge and undocumented death are outside the estimand.

Fit 12 cause-specific discrete-time hazards for (360,420], ..., (1740,1800] and convert them to death and alive-discharge cumulative-incidence functions. The primary estimand is the macro-hospital mean held-out difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score and death-CIF calibration between the documentation-adjusted model with and without laboratory process. Report patient-weighted and fold estimates secondarily, with 95% hospital-cluster bootstrap or a prespecified hierarchical interval.

## Models and method comparison

Use identical rows, folds, outcomes and inputs in nested transparent models:

- `B_v`: admission context, vital state/trajectory and EOL channels.
- `B_vS`: `B_v` plus recorded treatment/respiratory support activity and transitions.
- `B_vSD`: `B_vS` plus timestamp-safe nursing/respiratory documentation opportunity.
- `B_vSDL`: `B_vSD` plus revision-safe laboratory state and process channels. The primary scientific increment is `B_vSDL - B_vSD`; `B_vSD-B_vS` quantifies broad documentation opportunity, and `B_vS-B_v` quantifies support context.
- Paired result-time, clinical-offset-only, respiratory-only and nursing-only sensitivities use identical retained rows where applicable.

Each is pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazard modeling with 12 interval indicators. Preserve the parent’s laboratory process-by-recorded-trajectory interaction. Report integrated Brier, death-CIF calibration at 600/1200/1800, alive-discharge CIF calibration, fold/hospital counts, and fixed death-CIF review thresholds (0.01/0.02/0.05/0.10) only as retrospective label-relative diagnostics.

The scientifically substantive alternative is a one-layer six-bin GRU with separate death/discharge heads using exactly the same patient-level admission, vital, EOL, support, documentation, laboratory state/process, and missingness inputs. Compare with a parameter-matched non-temporal MLP and within-patient bin-order shuffle. The GRU is informative only if an uncertainty-compatible held-out death calibration/Brier improvement over the matched MLP is lost under order shuffle and is stable across held-out hospitals. Otherwise the transparent model remains primary and the learned branch is negative or inconclusive.

## Splits, falsifications and interpretation

Hold out approximately 20% of hospitals in five outer folds; keep `uniquepid` groups together. Within each development fold use a 75/25 hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Require global row overlap, >=20 hospitals, >=500 episodes and >=200 recorded deaths for any support/documentation contrast; report sparse cells as inconclusive. Use >=1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples or the prespecified hierarchical alternative.

Falsifications are mandatory: reassign lab histories and documentation histories within hospital/risk strata; shuffle six-bin order separately for labs and documentation; mask (300,360] and early bins; compare recorded death with alive discharge; compare `B_vSD-B_vS` and matched vital/support process increments; repeat nursing-only, respiratory-only, common-label, delayed-entry, result-time and clinical-offset sensitivities; leave out hospitals by outcome-free surveillance regime; audit every predictor for post-360, invalid-revision, `activeupondischarge`, hospital-ID and target-fold preprocessing leakage. A lab increment unchanged by patient-link or time-order permutation is adverse.

Supportive evidence requires all gates, positive uncertainty-compatible death-specific `B_vSDL-B_vSD`, smaller/null discharge increment, attenuation under lab permutation, no comparable generic documentation increment, stability after delayed-entry/common-label/masking/regime checks, and (for the learned branch) order-sensitive GRU improvement. Adverse evidence is null/reversed lab increment, permutation persistence, documentation-only or discharge-sized increment, one-site/interface concentration, entry-time leakage dependence, or order-shuffle-surviving GRU gain. Inconclusive evidence includes failed coverage/overlap/event gates, unstable folds, sparse categories, intervals spanning the prespecified meaningful effect, or nonconvergence.

## Evidence limits and revisiting alternatives

The snapshot lacks specimen collection/order times, laboratory units, raw waveforms, delivered-dose start/stop/dose, complete treatment intent, reliable continuous support states, complete goals-of-care documentation, unrecorded tests/deaths and external validation. The experiment can establish only a held-out association in recorded eICU data. Stronger claims require timestamped orders/collections, unit-aware assays, administration records, waveform or expert support-state adjudication, an external system, and prospective silent-mode/impact or randomized evaluation.

The nursing/respiratory extension is deferred if the bounded audit shows inadequate overlap or delayed-entry dominance. A mechanistic state-space model is deferred for the missing dependencies above. A larger transformer or multimodal reproduction is deferred because the demonstrations’ required UKB lifetime/genetic data, local images, complete Oncoformer text/STAR Methods, and verified genetics are unavailable. The compact GRU is a bounded EHR adaptation, not a reproduction. Seed eICU-02 motivates the testing-practice challenge, eICU-05 the respiratory field caution, and eICU-10 the non-ranking support-context caution; all ten imported eICU seeds were assessed as untested source material, not evidence or priority.
