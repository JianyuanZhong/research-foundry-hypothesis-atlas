## Clinical question and evidence boundary

The parent asks whether an earlier panel adds information for a composite two-domain transition. That is useful for overall triage, but it loses the clinically different question faced after a person already has one recorded cardiometabolic domain: is the next recorded progression more likely to be cardiovascular or renal/metabolic? Those pathways imply different review, prevention, and specialist-referral priorities. A model that only predicts “any second domain” cannot establish whether historical information changes that choice.

The strongest available local evidence supports a bounded statistical study: the snapshot has two assessment-date fields, two numeric instances for an eight-field biological panel, and position-matched inpatient diagnosis code/date arrays. The local catalog does not establish the biomarker identities, units, specimen collection times, disease onset, outpatient completeness, death-field semantics, individual observation end, or clinical validity of a codebook. The accessible abstract of Xue et al. (PMID 42289689; DOI 10.1186/s12933-026-03255-4) reports baseline-index cardiometabolic multimorbidity associations and multistate progression analyses in UK Biobank, but does not establish that a repeated panel adds information about the next domain in this locally available paired-panel cohort. This proposal therefore tests conditional prediction of a recorded next-domain category, not biological onset, causal progression, treatment benefit, or clinical utility.

Public-literature provenance retained for that statement: Europe PMC full-text XML source ID `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]. Only the bibliographic record and relevant abstract-level evidence were used; no claim is made that supplementary methods or every analysis of that paper was inspected.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among UKB participants with exactly one recorded C/R/M domain at the second valid panel assessment, and whose current-only risk of a new domain is ambiguous, does the prior-to-current panel trajectory improve held-out prediction of whether the next new recorded domain is cardiovascular or renal/metabolic, beyond the current panel, baseline state, age, calendar year, and repeat spacing? Is that information stable when trained through 2012 and applied without refitting in 2013?

Here C is cardiovascular (ICD-10 I00–I99), R is renal (N00–N29), and M is diabetes/metabolic (E08–E13), including subcodes after a prespecified normalization. These are computational domains, not adjudicated disease labels.

## New estimand and endpoint

For each participant, derive the earliest valid same-position date for each domain from all 259 literal pairs:

- `41270-0.0` through `41270-0.258`;
- `41280-0.0` through `41280-0.258`.

For domain d, F_d is the minimum valid paired `41280` date among its `41270` codes. Codes and dates must never be independently sorted. Any nonmissing C/R/M code with a missing, malformed, or out-of-approved-range paired date makes the participant unknown-time and excludes them from the primary estimand; the count is reported.

Use participants with exactly one finite domain date at or before T1 and the other two domain dates either finite and strictly after T1 or absent. Exclude zero-domain participants, prior two-domain participants, and participants with indeterminate code/date pairing. This creates a clinically interpretable single-domain state at the repeat assessment, with starting state S1 in {C, R, M}.

Let H = T1 + 5 years. Among domains not already present at T1, the first valid domain date strictly after T1 through H determines:

- C-next: C is the first newly observed domain;
- K-next: R or M is the first newly observed domain;
- joint-next: C and R/M first occur on the same date;
- D: a verified death date precedes the new-domain event, or occurs before H when no new-domain event occurs;
- N: no qualifying recorded new-domain or death event through H.

If a death and a new-domain date tie, death has precedence. If F_d is beyond H, it cannot define the endpoint. The endpoint is a mutually exclusive recorded category. N means no qualifying record in the available source, never disease-free. If death-field meaning or complete observation through H cannot be verified, the solver must fail closed for the primary claim rather than treat missing follow-up as N. Joint-next is retained as a nuisance class; if it lacks prespecified support, the outcome-specific claim is inconclusive rather than silently discarding it.

The primary decision stratum is the central 60% of current-only risk of any new domain: obtain early-only out-of-fold (r_i=P(C	ext{-next}mathbin{lor}K	ext{-next}mathbin{lor}joint	ext{-next})) from the current-only model, define q20 and q80, and use L = r < q20, A = q20 <= r < q80, U = r >= q80. Cut points are calculated before late outcomes and never from the trajectory model. The primary estimand is the paired held-out change in multiclass Brier score and log loss in A between the current-plus-spacing baseline and the full trajectory model, with prespecified one-vs-rest C-next and K-next scores and probability calibration as outcome-specific secondary estimands. State-stratified estimates for S1=C, R, M test whether the information is concentrated in a clinically different starting state; they are not interpreted if support is inadequate.

The main hypothesis is supported only if the full trajectory model improves both the overall proper score in A and the prespecified C-next/K-next probability information, with no material deterioration for D/N, and the direction is retained in the no-refit 2013 test. It is not enough for a learned model to improve a generic prediction score while failing to distinguish C-next from K-next.

## Selection and leakage control

Create a participant flow from population to T0-complete, T1-complete, complete paired panel, valid one-domain state, valid endpoint, early/late era, and L/A/U band. Report every exclusion, all three starting-state counts, each endpoint count, unknown-time counts, and all source/join integrity checks.

Among T0-complete people, define S=1 as a valid T1 and complete second panel. Fit an early-only regularized observation propensity (p(S=1|X_0)) using only T0 panel values, age, sex, and T0 year. Use stabilized weights (P(S=1)/p(S=1|X_0)), truncated at early 1st/99th percentiles, only as a selection-transport sensitivity. This is not causal correction and does not claim that repeat attendance or testing causes benefit. Report overlap, maximum weight, standardized differences, and weighted ESS by era, band, and starting state.

Use a deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on `eid`. All imputation, scaling, regularization, tuning, propensity fitting, and calibration are training-fold-only. The early M0 gives out-of-fold probabilities to define q20/q80. For late evaluation, apply all five early fold models and average their class probabilities; use the frozen early q20/q80 and frozen preprocessing. No late outcomes define a band.

## Matched baseline, trajectory model, and learned alternative

The alternatives test the same scientific uncertainty with the same people, endpoint, folds, bands, and uncertainty procedure:

1. **B0 current-only baseline:** regularized additive multinomial model for {C-next, K-next, joint-next, D, N} using the eight T1 values, age, sex, T1 year, and one-hot starting state.
2. **Bspace baseline:** B0 plus unannualized assessment spacing L. The nested Bspace-versus-B0 contrast quantifies repeat-spacing information.
3. **Bprior:** Bspace plus all eight T0 values. Bprior-versus-Bspace is the transparent prior-information contrast, matched to the parent.
4. **Btrajectory (primary trajectory representation):** Bspace plus the eight T1-T0 differences and their interactions with the starting state, with shrinkage. It is a statistical trajectory representation, not a biological deterioration score unless units, direction, and specimen timing are verified. The primary trajectory estimand compares Btrajectory with Bspace; Bprior is the value-of-history sensitivity and tests whether any gain is specifically carried by differences or merely by raw prior levels.
5. **Glearn:** a small gradient-boosted multinomial model with exactly Btrajectory inputs and the same fold-local preprocessing and prespecified depth/learning-rate/regularization grid. It can reveal nonlinear thresholds, cross-field discordance, and state-specific interactions that additive Btrajectory averages away. It is not selected by the best late score.

A mechanistic latent organ-reserve model is explicitly deferred: biomarker identities, units, assay quality, specimen timing, and validated organ mappings are unavailable, so assigning fields to kidney, cardiovascular, or metabolic biology would fabricate mechanism. A disease-history transformer is also deferred because the catalog has no verified event chronology beyond paired first-recorded diagnosis dates and no documented sequence-token semantics. The learned tabular alternative is retained because it can answer a real nonlinear/state-interaction question without pretending to recover missing biology.

## Outcomes, uncertainty, and falsification

For early cross-fitted and late no-refit predictions, report by era, L/A/U band, and starting state when supported:

- multiclass Brier and log loss for all five categories;
- paired changes Btrajectory-Bspace and Bprior-Bspace, with 95% intervals;
- one-vs-rest Brier, calibration intercept/slope, and observed-versus-predicted summaries for C-next and K-next;
- conditional C-next versus K-next log loss among participants with either event, labelled secondary because it conditions on an observed event;
- state-specific contrasts, threshold-free probability movement, and fixed descriptive movements at 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10%, and 15%; thresholds are not clinical utilities;
- selection-weighted sensitivity, early-to-late change, and weighted ESS/overlap diagnostics.

Use participant bootstrap resampling within fixed era-by-band cells, retaining fold, state, endpoint, predictions, and weights. For state-specific intervals, resample within state as well and report support failures. The primary interval is conditional on fitted models, frozen bands, and weights; a prespecified sensitivity refits the early pipeline in early bootstrap samples to include training uncertainty. Do not recalibrate on late data.

Integrity/falsification tests:

- permute endpoint labels within fold and era; all trajectory advantage should disappear within uncertainty;
- permute T0 panel values within era, starting state, band, interval, missingness, and selection strata while retaining T1 values and frozen bands; this tests whether the claimed gain depends on true person-level history;
- deliberately break code/date position pairing; the gate must detect it;
- compare all-259-array parsing with the retired 0–29 truncation only as an audit, never as the primary endpoint;
- compare valid-date exclusion with an unknown-time report, never date imputation;
- compare complete-case and prespecified fold-local missingness handling;
- permute early/late era labels and inspect fold instability, calibration, and overlap;
- report whether Glearn’s nonlinear shape is stable across early folds and the late no-refit data.

Minimum primary support is 200 participants and 30 C-next or K-next events in each early/late by L/A/U cell, at least 30 C-next and 30 K-next events in the ambiguity band overall, and at least 20 joint-next events overall. If any required cell or outcome class is below these thresholds, the corresponding primary or state-specific claim is inconclusive; no band or outcome definition is changed to rescue it. These are feasibility gates, not observed results.

## Interpretation and falsification criteria

Supportive evidence requires: verified field identities/units and assessment-date comparability; expert review of the C/R/M codebook; verified death semantics and observation through H; no unresolved code/date pairing; all support gates; no leakage or integrity failure; negative Btrajectory-Bspace and Bprior-Bspace Brier/log-loss changes in A with 95% intervals excluding zero in the favorable direction; improvement for both C-next and K-next one-vs-rest probability scores without material D/N calibration deterioration; stable direction in the 2013 no-refit evaluation; and no reversal under selection weighting or label permutation. Glearn’s stable nonlinear/state-specific shape would strengthen the scientific interpretation but is not required to support the transparent primary trajectory contrast.

Adverse evidence is a non-improving or worsening trajectory contrast, improvement only for one endpoint class with worsening of the other, a gain that disappears when spacing is included or T0 values are permuted, calibration deterioration for D/N, era reversal, unstable fold-specific shape, poor overlap/ESS, or an advantage attributable only to Glearn without the transparent nested contrast. This argues against retaining two assessments for this recorded next-domain discrimination; it does not establish absence of value in other populations or decisions.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed semantic, timing, ascertainment, death, codebook, or support gates; too few C-next/K-next/joint events; unknown-time excess that changes the estimand; sparse state cells; nonidentifiable bands; wide intervals crossing the prespecified null; broken pairing; or materially conflicting missingness/endpoint sensitivities. Inconclusive is not evidence against the hypothesis.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes, column presence, joins, parsing, flow counts, endpoint construction, split manifests, model fits, held-out predictions, proper scores, calibration, bootstrap intervals, weighting, and permutation behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for biological disease onset, biomarker interpretation, specimen timing, outpatient completeness, causal progression, treatment/referral benefit, action utilities, external transport, and prospective clinical policy evaluation.

## Preserved and deferred branches

The parent’s composite two-domain endpoint, central-60% overall-risk band, nested current/spacing/prior decomposition, selection weighting, early-through-2012/2013 no-refit test, full 259-position pairing, and fail-closed evidence boundaries are preserved as a secondary audit so the child’s outcome-specific result can be compared with the parent.

The following branches are deliberately not chosen:

- broad or single-domain composite endpoints, because they do not answer which next pathway is implicated;
- biomarker-specific kidney discordance, because field identities and cystatin/creatinine validation are unavailable;
- latent organ-age or organ-reserve models, because proteomic identities, organ mappings, and assay metadata are unavailable;
- raw imaging, genetic-risk, CHIP, infection-onset, sleep-regularity, and disease-history-transformer branches, because the required local modalities or verified chronology are unavailable;
- causal claims about repeat testing, treatment, referral, or prevention, because no intervention assignment or utility data exist.

Revisit the learned/trajectory branch only if clinician/coding review, biomarker dictionary and specimen timing, death semantics, and observation-end evidence are obtained. A new outcome, temporal boundary, estimand, or support rule would require a new Lead decision rather than compiler repair.
