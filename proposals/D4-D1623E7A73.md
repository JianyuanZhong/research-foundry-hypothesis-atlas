# Episode 96: two-proxy ascertainment robustness of the fixed-capacity kidney contrast

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]` asks whether the immutable baseline-trained AG1 roster (routine variables, observed/fallback UACR, and grip) contains more first recorded inpatient N18 events before death than the otherwise matched AF1 roster (routine variables, observed/fallback UACR, and whole-body bioimpedance FFM), at exactly 466 cystatin-C slots in the UKB visit-1 holdout. That is the clinically consequential decision: which measurement should be evaluated in a prospective selective cystatin-C strategy.

The strongest available evidence supports plausibility and computability only. Inspected public evidence supports that creatinine-cystatin discordance is common/predictable and that cystatin-based reclassification can be prognostically relevant; it does not support AG1 superiority, measured-GFR validity, incident CKD, or patient benefit. The configured UKB outcome is an administrative inpatient code and the snapshot supplies no encounter identifier, complete outpatient linkage, individual censoring, or specimen/order chronology. Thus a favorable N18 contrast could partly reflect differential opportunity for a diagnosis to be recorded.

The falsifiable successor hypothesis is:

> In the exact frozen O1_hold and fixed 466-person AG1/AF1 rosters, the AG1-minus-AF1 contrast for first recorded inpatient N18-before-death remains materially positive after standardizing to both prespecified, outcome-blind proxies of prior nonrenal recording opportunity: (a) the number of valid nonrenal diagnosis/date positions and (b) the number of distinct calendar dates containing a valid nonrenal diagnosis.

This is a substantive advance over a single density adjustment: it tests whether the conclusion is robust to the representation problem created by repeated codes on the same day. A result cannot prove ascertainment bias is absent; a reversal or attenuation under either proxy is clinically useful because it blocks an unqualified kidney-specific interpretation and prioritizes adjudicated prospective validation.
