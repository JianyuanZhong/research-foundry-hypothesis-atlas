# Asynchronous Organ Recovery: A Multi-Organ Recovery Phenotype Predicts ICU Deterioration

## Scientific Question

After circulatory stabilization in critically ill patients, do patients with **asynchronous organ recovery** (i.e., persistent impairment in one or more organs after stabilization of others) have higher rates of subsequent deterioration compared to those with synchronous multi-organ recovery?

## Hypothesis Statement

**Hypothesis:** In patients who have achieved hemodynamic stability (sustained MAP ≥ 65 mmHg for ≥6 hours without escalating vasopressor requirements) following sepsis or shock, those exhibiting asynchronous organ recovery—defined as persistent elevation in organ-specific markers (kidney: creatinine > baseline + 0.3 mg/dL for ≥12 hours; respiratory: PaO2/FiO2 < 300 for ≥12 hours) after initial stabilization—are at significantly higher risk of subsequent clinical deterioration (requiring escalation of organ support or death) compared to patients with synchronous multi-organ recovery.

**Expected effect size:** Hazard ratio for deterioration ≥1.5 (95% CI: 1.3-1.8) for asynchronous vs synchronous recovery groups.

**Falsification criteria:** If the hazard ratio for deterioration is <1.2 (95% CI includes 1.0), the hypothesis is falsified.

## Study Population

**Inclusion criteria:**
- Adults (≥18 years) admitted to ICU with sepsis or shock
- ICU length of stay ≥7 days
- At least one repeat measurement of organ-specific markers after initial stabilization
- Hemodynamic stability achieved (sustained MAP ≥65 mmHg for ≥6 hours without escalating vasopressor requirements)

**Exclusion criteria:**
- Major trauma or surgical complications
- Pre-existing chronic organ failure
- Multiple ICU admissions within the same hospitalization
- Missing critical data for outcome assessment

**Key variables:**
- **Hemodynamic stability:** Sustained MAP ≥65 mmHg for ≥6 hours without escalating vasopressor requirements
- **Kidney recovery:** Persistent creatinine elevation > baseline + 0.3 mg/dL for ≥12 hours
- **Respiratory recovery:** Persistent PaO2/FiO2 < 300 for ≥12 hours
- **Deterioration:** Escalation of organ support (vasopressors, ventilators, renal replacement) or death within 7 days of stability

## Recovery Pattern Definitions

**Synchronous multi-organ recovery:**
- All impaired organs show improvement within 12 hours of hemodynamic stabilization
- No persistent elevation in any organ-specific marker

**Asynchronous organ recovery:**
- At least one organ shows persistent impairment (≥12 hours) while other organs recover
- Subtypes:
  - Kidney-first: Kidney impairment persists while respiratory recovers
  - Respiratory-first: Respiratory impairment persists while kidney recovers
  - Discordant: Different organs recover at different rates

## Baseline Approach

**Categorical comparison:**
- Compare outcomes between synchronous vs asynchronous recovery groups
- Use Cox proportional hazards regression to estimate hazard ratios for deterioration
- Adjust for baseline severity (SOFA score), age, comorbidities, and treatment intensity

**Expected results (baseline):**
- Asynchronous recovery: HR ≥1.5 for deterioration
- Synchronous recovery: HR ≈1.0 for deterioration

## Learned Alternative

**Trajectory-based modeling:**
- Use group-based trajectory modeling (GBTM) or transformer-based sequence modeling to learn multi-organ recovery trajectories
- Identify distinct trajectory patterns beyond simple categorical definitions
- Compare predictive performance of trajectory-based vs categorical approaches

**Model architecture:**
- Input: Time-series of organ-specific markers (creatinine, PaO2/FiO2, lactate, etc.)
- Output: Probability of deterioration within 7 days
- Training: Supervised learning with labeled outcomes

**Expected advantages:**
- Capture non-linear recovery dynamics
- Identify subtle patterns missed by categorical definitions
- Provide individualized risk predictions

## Clinical Importance

1. **Early Warning:** Current practice relies heavily on single-timepoint assessments. Multi-organ recovery trajectories could identify high-risk patients earlier.

2. **Treatment Guidance:** Different recovery patterns may suggest different underlying pathophysiology, informing treatment intensification or de-escalation.

3. **Risk Stratification:** Could refine risk scores beyond baseline severity, enabling personalized care pathways.

## Limitations

1. **Data availability:** Not all variables are measured for all patients; temporal resolution varies.
2. **Selection bias:** Patients with repeated measurements may differ systematically from those without.
3. **Causality:** Observational design cannot establish causality; confounding by indication may affect results.
4. **Generalizability:** Findings may not generalize to other ICU populations or healthcare systems.
