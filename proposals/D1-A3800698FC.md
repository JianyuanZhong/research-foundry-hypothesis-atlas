# Hypothesis Proposal: Dynamic Lactic Acid Trajectories Predict TACE Outcomes in HCC Patients

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question:** In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) undergoing transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), do serial serum lactic acid measurements capture prognostic information beyond baseline characteristics and tumor burden?

**Secondary Question:** Is the trajectory of serum lactic acid during and after TACE (rising vs. falling vs. stable) predictive of treatment response, progression-free survival, and decompensation risk?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Treatment Response Prediction**: Current TACE decision-making relies on Child-Pugh score, tumor size, and number. A dynamic lactic acid trajectory could identify patients at risk of treatment failure before decompensation occurs.

2. **Early Warning System**: Rising lactic acid during TACE might indicate hepatic hypoperfusion, tumor necrosis complications, or sepsis, allowing earlier intervention.

3. **Risk Stratification**: Patients with persistently elevated or rising lactic acid post-TACE might benefit from alternative therapies (systemic therapy, transplant evaluation) rather than repeat TACE.

4. **Heterogeneity in Response**: Lactic acid dynamics could capture metabolic heterogeneity among HCC patients that static biomarkers miss.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Current literature focuses on:
- **Static baseline biomarkers** (AFP, Child-Pugh components)
- **Tumor characteristics** (size, number, vascular invasion)
- **Treatment timing** (interval from diagnosis to TACE)

This work proposes that **dynamic lactic acid trajectories** provide prognostic information that:
- Captures metabolic stress during treatment
- Reflects hepatic perfusion status
- May predict complications before they manifest clinically

Unlike prior work on TACE timing (which focuses on when treatment should start) or pre-TACE biomarkers (which focus on baseline characteristics), this work examines **temporal patterns in lactic acid** during treatment, which could reveal:
- Treatment-induced metabolic changes
- Early signs of treatment failure or complications
- Patient heterogeneity in treatment response

### Temporal Analysis Strategy

1. **Define TACE onset**: Time of first TACE procedure for each patient
2. **Define lactic acid window**: Serial measurements within ±7 days of TACE
3. **Define trajectory categories**:
   - Rising: Lactic acid increasing over time
   - Falling: Lactic acid decreasing over time
   - Stable: Lactic acid showing no significant change
4. **Outcomes**:
   - Progression-free survival (PFS): Time to tumor progression or death
   - Treatment-related complications: Defined by clinical criteria
   - Decompensation: Liver decompensation events
   - Mortality: In-hospital or 30-day mortality

## Baseline Approach

**Static Baseline Model**: A logistic regression or Cox proportional hazards model using:
- Baseline age, sex
- Pre-TACE lactic acid (single measurement)
- Tumor characteristics (if available)
- Child-Pugh score components (if available)

This tests whether any lactic acid information is available beyond established prognostic factors.

## Learned/Mechanistic Alternative

**Dynamic Phenotype Model**: A machine learning approach (e.g., gradient boosting, random survival forest) that:
- Uses serial lactic acid measurements (pre-TACE, during, post-TACE)
- Captures lactic acid trajectory patterns (rising, falling, stable)
- Integrates with static baseline features (age, sex, pre-TACE labs)
- May use time-dependent covariates to update risk estimates during treatment

This could reveal:
- Non-linear relationships between lactic acid changes and outcomes
- Interaction effects between lactic acid trajectory and tumor characteristics
- Time-dependent risk patterns that static models miss
- Early warning signals before clinical decompensation

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. Serial lactic acid measurements show no association with outcomes after adjusting for baseline lactic acid and static prognostic factors
2. Lactic acid trajectory patterns (rising/falling/stable) show no predictive value beyond baseline lactic acid
3. A dynamic model performs no better than a static model with baseline lactic acid alone (e.g., C-index difference < 0.01)

## Limitations

1. **Language barrier**: HCC data is in Chinese; test name extraction requires careful mapping
2. **Sample size**: Need sufficient HCC patients with TACE and serial lactic acid measurements
3. **Confounding**: Nutritional status, sepsis, and other conditions affect lactic acid
4. **Causality**: Observational design cannot establish lactic acid as causal marker
5. **Test name variation**: 乳酸（急） may not be the only lactic acid test name; need to verify all variants

- Captures temporal patterns and treatment-induced changes
- May reveal early warning signals before clinical decompensation
- More complex but potentially more informative
