# eICU 24-hour landmark: lab-process signal and hospital transport

**Status:** Proposed experiment; no final analytic cohort, model, or outcome comparison has been run. Source files remain read-only. This child retains the assessed parent’s population, landmark, outcome, estimand and falsification question; the addition is a matched, substantive learned temporal alternative and an executable comparison plan.

## Scientific question and claim boundaries

Among adults alive and still in ICU at hour 24, does adding first-day lab-testing process information to available physiology improve prediction of subsequent in-hospital death, and is that incremental information less stable at hospitals withheld from fitting?

The motivating observations are bounded: eICU links patient stays, laboratory records and hospital metadata [K1]; inspected ICU prediction work documents sensitivity of performance/generalizability to institutional composition and validation design [K2]; inspected multi-site work treats laboratory missingness/testing patterns as potentially informative of illness course or clinician concern, while noting site data-definition/mapping issues [K3]. These works motivate the question; they do not establish that eICU testing intensity is a workflow artifact or that it will predict death. The closest question left unresolved here is whether this defined process-feature block has incremental predictive value beyond physiology and whether that increment is reproducible across unseen eICU hospitals.

**Hypothesis:** relative to physiology alone, first-day testing counts, repetition and timing improve prediction of post-landmark in-hospital death under patient-grouped internal validation, but that increment is attenuated on held-out hospitals. **Strong rival:** lab intensity is a robust proxy for genuine latent severity or clinician concern, so it should add information at unseen hospitals after the same physiology adjustment. Other explanations include severity mix, treatment pathways, 24-hour survivor/continued-stay selection, assay/name mapping, site documentation practices and outcome coding. These observational predictions cannot separate workflow from unmeasured severity or establish mechanism.

The primary estimand remains the paired change in patient-level Brier score when adding the lab-process block to physiology, assessed separately in internal and held-out-hospital evaluation, and the difference between those two increments (transport attenuation). Primary outcome remains death after the 24-hour landmark before hospital discharge. Calibration and discrimination are secondary checks, not replacement estimands. The comparison is predictive, not causal, and no clinical utility claim follows without prospective decision-impact evaluation.

## Population, time and outcome

Include one first ICU visit per `uniquepid`; adults with an interpretable adult age; alive and still in ICU at 1,440 minutes; and a later in-hospital disposition ascertainable. Apply an explicit prespecified rule for EICU’s age-obfuscation values before cohort construction; do not silently classify an ambiguous value as an adult. Exclude unclear landmark status or unresolvable outcome from primary analysis and report counts/reasons. This is a conditional cohort of 24-hour ICU survivors who remain in ICU, not all ICU admissions. Define death as `hospitaldischargestatus` indicating expired and discharge after hour 24, based on a pre-fit label dictionary; report alternate/ambiguous status counts and require clinical review of the status mapping. `hospitaldischargeoffset` establishes the temporal ordering. Discharge alive after hour 24 is the non-death class. Label completeness and status semantics must be audited before fitting.

All feature information must be available by minute 1,440; use offsets 0–1,440 inclusive only where an event is recorded by the cutoff. No post-landmark data, future discharge variables as predictors, APACHE-derived scores/predictions, notes, medications, other datasets or cross-database ID joins. The endpoint is later in-hospital death, not a fixed-horizon mortality risk after discharge.

## Matched methods and executable plan

Both method families use the same eligible cohort, outcome, feature definitions, cutoffs, patient/hospital partitions and training target. Each has two nested feature arms: **P** = first-day physiology; **P+L** = the same physiology plus first-day lab-process features. Lab-result values are excluded in both arms so the comparison tests testing process, not a mixed process/value bundle.

**Simple baseline: regularized logistic regression.** For P, extract prespecified summaries for each available vital (median, minimum, maximum, within-window measurement count and missingness; retain consistent units and explicit missingness indicators). For P+L, add per-hour lab-event counts and distinct test-group/repeat counts, plus time-to-first and last-observed time for each frozen group; use a capped/log-count transform defined before fitting. Standardize numeric inputs and fit L2-regularized logistic regression; select penalty using only training-grouped inner validation and optimize log loss. Fit preprocessing and mappings without access to held-out outcomes. This is intentionally interpretable and estimates whether coarse marginal summaries/process counts carry useful risk information.

**Learned temporal alternative: GRU.** Construct a shared 24-hour hourly sequence, hours 0–23, from the same first-day vital observations and lab event records. Vital channels contain within-hour mean and observed/missing mask for the same variables as P; lab channels contain the same frozen-group counts/distinct/repeat events as L. P sees vital channels and masks; P+L sees those plus lab channels. Do not add raw outcome-adjacent fields, patient/hospital identity, future observations, text or lab results. A bounded GRU encoder feeds a single mortality logit; train with binary cross-entropy, early stopping on patient-grouped training validation, fixed regularization/dropout and a small prespecified configuration set. Any hyperparameter selection is nested within training hospitals/people only. Report multiple fixed seeds (at least 3) and seed variability; the same input representation, cohort, training target and partitions apply to both arms.

Hourly sequence order may retain transient deterioration, bursts/repetition, and whether testing preceded/followed a physiologic change that medians/count totals discard. That could reveal incremental temporal predictive information; it cannot identify clinician intent or causality. GRU complexity can also overfit site-specific cadence, making the learned sequence less transportable than the baseline. The model-family comparison is secondary to the same P versus P+L predictive estimand: compare paired changes and their hospital attenuation for each method. Do not declare scientific superiority from a small AUROC gain alone.

**Splits.** Before fitting, designate a deterministic group of hospitals as held out (target 20% of hospitals with adequate eligible-patient support; finalize using cohort counts without inspecting model outcomes, and publish IDs/rule). The hospital test contains these sites. Remove from training any patient (`uniquepid`) appearing at any held-out site, so no person crosses the hospital boundary; report removed and multi-hospital persons. For the internal comparison, assign unique persons from the remaining development hospitals to a fixed patient-grouped 80/20 train/test split, stratified where feasible by hospital and outcome; the same internal test persons evaluate all four fits. Tuning uses grouped inner folds within training persons only. Hospital holdout is the primary transport check; internal test quantifies within-development performance. If too few deaths/sites for stable site-level estimates, do not relax the identity guard or change the population; return imprecision/limited estimability.

**Evaluation and uncertainty.** For each arm/method and each test regime, save held-out patient predictions and report Brier score (primary), calibration-in-the-large and calibration slope, AUROC and AUPRC (secondary), plus observed event prevalence. Estimate paired P+L-minus-P differences on identical persons, with bootstrap resampling by hospital for held-out-site uncertainty (and patient-group bootstrap for internal uncertainty); for GRU include seed-level variation, not as a substitute for sampling uncertainty. Report per-hospital paired scores, event counts and intervals, and their distribution; do not let a large hospital silently dominate a pooled site conclusion. Define sign convention so negative Brier difference favors P+L. Primary transport contrast is `delta_heldout - delta_internal`; a positive value means the P+L improvement is less favorable at held-out hospitals. Show interval estimates and site count, not only p-values.

**Falsification / interpretation.** Support for the stated pattern requires a credibly favorable internal P+L Brier difference, a less favorable held-out difference, and uncertainty narrow enough to distinguish meaningful attenuation from noise; report calibration/site heterogeneity and whether either model family agrees. If P+L is similarly favorable on held-out hospitals, the proposed attenuation is not supported in this test (though universal transport is not established). If P+L is null or worse internally as well, the incremental predictive hypothesis is not supported. If intervals span meaningful benefit and harm or hospital/event support is inadequate, result is inconclusive, not evidence of equivalence. If the GRU changes but logistic does not, or vice versa, describe model-dependent evidence; do not treat the GRU as a rescue for a failed primary contrast. Site-level adverse results may indicate heterogeneity, but cannot attribute it to workflow versus case mix, assay mapping, care pathway, selection or coding.

## Deliverable, limits and decision

Newly fit four outcome models (logistic P/P+L and GRU P/P+L) on the same locked splits. Completion outputs: auditable cohort flow and exclusions; frozen feature/cutoff and mapping specification; per-split identities with no `uniquepid` overlap; fit/configuration records; predictions; paired metrics, calibration, hospital- and seed-level uncertainty; and a conclusion traceable to these files with supportive/adverse/inconclusive label. A computable verifier can check bindings, cohort/time rules, data leakage, group separation, calculations, uncertainty reporting and whether interpretations match computed result fixtures. Clinical review remains required for adult-age handling, label semantics, lab grouping/unit/name mapping and acceptable site support; automatic verification cannot establish mechanism, bedside utility or general external validity. Clinical indication/treatment and chart-adjudicated labels are unavailable in the bound sources; resolving the rival mechanism needs chart review or linked order/indication/workflow data and likely a separate study.

Estimated future solver budget, not measured: 4 CPU cores/16 GiB for one-time cohort/sequence preparation; 2–4 CPU cores for logistic fits, likely under one hour; GRU three seeds and small fixed tuning set, approximately 2–4 CPU cores/16 GiB plus one allocated A100 for up to two hours if materialized sequence volume warrants it. These are planning estimates; wall time depends on final eligible N, sparse feature width and input pipeline and must be recorded. CPU is plausible for logistic; a GPU is justified only for repeated sequence fitting if CPU timing exceeds the solver envelope. Configured solver limits are 9,000 science seconds, concurrency 1, two GPU slots and up to 7,200 seconds reference execution; this does not authorize proposer/solver training. Discovery has not fitted either model.

**Selection/defer rationale:** retain regularized logistic as the primary, lowest-complexity test of the lab-process estimand; include the GRU as a substantive, prespecified alternative because hourly order/bursts may hold information aggregate features discard, and because sequence learning may expose transport fragility rather than merely raise prediction. Compare both on identical estimands/splits, not as a leaderboard. Defer any claim that the GRU is necessary or superior until paired results show stable additional value beyond uncertainty and seed variability. A substantive concern in the parent is that aggregating physiology and test activity can lose clinically relevant order and that site-level performance can be dominated by uneven hospital volume. Hourly GRU inputs and hospital-level uncertainty address these descriptively, but do not resolve unmeasured indication/severity or turn prediction into explanation.
