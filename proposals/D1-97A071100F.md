# Calendar-transportable laboratory result content versus measurement opportunity for subsequent HCC encounters

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

This is a targeted child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent’s clinically bounded P-minus-M question, calendar transport test, exhaustive local-observation states, and matched baseline/transition models. It repairs a measurement-process ambiguity in the parent: specimen type and result missingness were partly included in the alleged assay-payload block, so an apparent P increment could reflect result availability rather than laboratory result content. It also tightens endpoint language so the study does not call an encounter a readmission, planned treatment, or clinical deterioration when the HCC release does not provide those labels.

The future solver must newly produce:

1. a frozen adult, first locally HCC-coded encounter cohort with exact key, duplicate, multiplicity, timestamp, follow-up, and boundary ledgers;
2. a source-bound pre-index feature ledger in which Q captures general observation/capture, M captures laboratory measurement opportunity and availability, and P contains only assay-specific result content;
3. locked random-split and calendar-transport predictions for an exhaustive post-discharge local-observation outcome;
4. paired proper-score contrasts for P-minus-M, calibration and era interaction, with the same contrasts decomposed into early local encounter versus later local observation;
5. measurement-process permutation, boundary, missingness, and coherence audits; and
6. a claim-to-output table separating computationally checkable local-record claims from clinical claims requiring adjudication or new data.

No model was fit and no clinical result is claimed in discovery.

## Unresolved question, evidence, and hypothesis

The available evidence supports only feasibility and provenance: the frozen HCC snapshot contains structured encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, and process tables with the native fields bound below. Direct schema/header inspection confirms the laboratory table has assay name, qualitative result, quantitative result, specimen type, and laboratory time, but no separate laboratory-unit/reference-range column. The snapshot and catalog do not provide a validated active-HCC/stage label, discharge disposition, planned-versus-unplanned status, result-release time, death, outside-care linkage, or clinical adjudication. No local model result supports the hypothesis.

The unresolved question is:

> Among adults with a first locally recorded HCC-coded encounter, does strictly pre-index laboratory result content add calibrated information about a subsequent locally recorded encounter within 30 days after index discharge beyond (i) general capture/process history and (ii) which laboratory measurements were obtained, when they were obtained, and whether their results were available—and does that increment transport to the 2021–2025 calendar era?

Primary hypothesis: in the locked 2021–2025 era, result-content P will improve held-out prediction of the 30-day subsequent local-encounter state over measurement-opportunity M, after Q is retained, with a direction and magnitude compatible with the held-out 2012–2018 estimate calibrated/locked in 2019–2020. A process-specific hypothesis is that P-minus-M is larger for the 0–30-day local encounter state than for later local observation among those without a 0–30-day encounter.

This is an information and transport hypothesis, not a causal, biologic, readmission, recurrence, treatment, safety, or utility claim. A supportive result would show that recorded assay values contain transportable information beyond the opportunity to obtain and document those assays. It could justify prospective validation of a follow-up-risk score; it cannot justify acting on the score or infer that the values represent active tumor biology. An adverse result would show that the result-content increment is null/harmful, calendar-unstable, or reproduced by measurement opportunity. Inconclusive results would reflect inadequate event/assay support, timestamp or unit limitations, missing follow-up, or unreconciled joins.

## Population and endpoint

Use HCC snapshot `[source checksum]`, catalog [source checksum], and the ordinary read-only CSV paths in the binding table.

1. Normalize Unicode/whitespace in the two join keys for audit while retaining raw values. Join diagnoses to encounters on the exact pair (`患者主索引`, `就诊号`). An HCC-coded encounter has normalized `诊断名称` containing `肝细胞癌` or case-insensitive “hepatocellular carcinoma”; report matched strings and `诊断类型`. Diagnoses have no native time and select the cohort only.
2. Include age >=18, parseable nonnegative `入院时间` and `出院时间`, and index admission in [2012-01-01, 2026-01-01). Select the earliest eligible HCC-coded encounter per normalized patient by admission time then `就诊号`; do not substitute a later row for an invalid earliest candidate. Set t0=`入院时间`, td=`出院时间`; require td+90 days < 2026-01-01 for the primary 90-day label.
3. Define encounter identity as the normalized two-key pair. Collapse exact duplicate rows only after reporting duplicate counts; distinct keys are not collapsed because they may represent separate local records. A candidate post-discharge encounter must have a distinct key, parseable `入院时间`, and admission >= td. Records with admission < td are not returns even if their discharge is later. If several distinct keys share the first admission timestamp, label the state as observed and report the tie.
4. Define exhaustive Y90:
   - R30: at least one distinct subsequent local encounter with admission in [td, td+30 days);
   - O90: no R30 and at least one distinct subsequent local encounter with admission in [td+30 days, td+90 days);
   - U90: neither is locally observed.
   The primary endpoint is deliberately named “subsequent local encounter record,” not readmission or unplanned care. Same-day/near-boundary records with admission in [td,td+24 hours) are retained in primary R30 but reported separately; a sensitivity analysis excludes that interval. This exposes administrative-boundary sensitivity rather than silently choosing a clinically loaded interpretation. L=60 and L=180 are sensitivity analyses only when administrative follow-up permits them. U90 means no qualifying local row, not no care, survival, cure, or complete follow-up.
5. The clinical estimand is the paired held-out P-minus-M contrast in proper scores for R30 under the three-state Y90 distribution. The measurement-process estimand is the P-minus-M contrast for O90 versus U90 among Y90 != R30 and the difference between the R30 and O90 contrasts. Future documentation intensity is a negative-control outcome, never a predictor.

Calendar transport is fixed: fit on index anchors in 2012–2018; use 2019–2020 only for fit-only vocabulary/feature selection, calibration, and model locking; evaluate once on 2021–2025. Report the earlier held-out estimate and later-era estimate plus their later-minus-earlier interaction. Do not pool, move boundaries, or redefine the endpoint to rescue a failed era. A patient-hash split (0–59 fit, 60–69 selection/calibration, 70–79 locked evaluation, 80–99 inaccessible) is secondary precision analysis only; split before row expansion and keep all patient-linked rows together.

## Time-safe information blocks and the measurement repair

All predictors stop strictly before t0. A prior row must join to a non-index encounter with parseable admission and discharge, linked discharge < t0 and linked admission < t0. Use native event/availability times in [t0-730 days,t0): labs `检验时间`; procedures `开始时间`; orders `开立时间`; medications `开始时间`; examinations `开始时间`. Exclude index-linked rows even if their event time precedes admission. Publish invalid/missing-time, missing-linked-discharge, overlap, index-linked, post-t0, duplicate, unmatched, and row-inflation counts. This is an availability rule, not proof of clinician awareness or result release.

Exclude direct identifiers (`姓名`, `身份证号`, `手机号`, `医保/就诊卡号`, `住院号`).

- Q, general capture/process: age, sex, department, admission era/time-of-day; prior completed-encounter count, duration and recency; source-specific row counts and distinct linked-encounter counts for labs, procedures, orders, medications and examinations; source-type count; invalid/excluded-time counts; left-truncation and no-history flags. Q contains no assay identity, assay result, specimen type, or procedure/order content.
- M, measurement opportunity/availability: laboratory assay identity, assay-specific counts, distinct encounters, sampling-time count, recency/span, specimen type, result-present/missingness flags, invalid numeric/qualitative parse flags, and assay-specific availability patterns. M may describe whether a value was obtainable and recorded but contains no value magnitude or qualitative result content.
- P, result content only: assay-specific valid quantitative results and valid qualitative results, represented within assay and never pooled across assays; within-assay change/slope only with at least two distinct valid `检验时间` values. P has no specimen type, result-present flag, assay count, sampling count, or missingness indicator. Because no laboratory-unit column is available, quantitative values are not compared across assays; units/reference ranges are not inferred. Numeric parsing/impossible-value rules, assay vocabulary and rare handling are fit-only and frozen before calibration/test. Missing P values are represented as missing/zero only in a way that cannot reconstruct missingness or assay identity; those indicators remain in M.

The decisive contrast is therefore P versus M, not a contaminated “payload” block versus an opportunity block. Report P-minus-Q only as a continuity contrast, never as the result-content estimand.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods use the identical cohort, Q/M/P ledgers, pre-t0 window, eligibility rules, calendar and patient splits, Y90 states, preprocessing, locked cases, and missingness policy. Neither uses documents, pathology, images, vitals, transfers, post-t0 rows, or direct identifiers.

Transparent baseline B fits regularized logistic models for R30 versus non-R30 and multinomial logistic models for Y90, nested as B_Q, B_M (Q+M), and B_P (Q+M+P), with all preprocessing, penalty selection, rare handling, and calibration fit only in allowed partitions. It is interpretable and makes the measurement-opportunity increment auditable, but it loses event timing and the coherent distinction between an early encounter and later observation.

Substantive alternative H fits a matched discrete-time complementary-log-log competing-transition model: R hazards in [0,7), [7,14), [14,30), then O hazards in [30,60), [60,90) conditional on no R30. The same Q/M/P vectors enter every transition with interval offsets and prespecified regularization; derive coherent R30/O90/U90 probabilities. H can reveal whether calendar drift occurs in early encounter hazard, later observation hazard, or both, and whether result content changes timing in a way pooled B cannot show. It is selected for this scientific information, not for complexity or expected predictive gain.

A learned irregular sequence encoder remains deferred, not prohibited. It would consume the same eligible pre-t0 laboratory sequence (time, assay identity, value/missingness, specimen type) but would be revisited only if the locked P-minus-M effect is stable, H residuals show reproducible ordering/nonlinearity, and event/assay support is adequate. A neural model now would confound the measurement-process repair with optimization/calibration variance. If revisited, compare it against B and H on identical splits and proper scores; an allocated A100 using `cuda:0` is an option, not a requirement. No GPU discovery or full solver fit was run.

## Estimation, uncertainty, and falsification

Primary evidence is paired held-out multiclass log loss and multiclass Brier score for Y90, with binary R30 and conditional O90 decompositions; lower is better. Report calibration intercept/slope, reliability by state and era, prevalence, missingness, and later-minus-earlier interaction. Report B_P-minus-B_M and H_P-minus-H_M as the main result-content contrasts, B_M-minus-B_Q/H_M-minus-H_Q as the opportunity increment, and the R30-versus-O90 contrast. Use at least 1,000 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples, preserving all rows and linked history per patient, with 95% intervals and a prespecified clinically negligible score-difference margin. Publish denominators and support gates by era/state/interval; no p-value substitutes for uncertainty.

Mandatory audits/falsifications:

1. permute P values within assay and calendar era while preserving M, times, row counts, and missingness;
2. permute assay identities while preserving values, times, counts, and M;
3. permute specimen type/result-availability labels within the patient split to test whether the M block is carrying an artifact;
4. shuffle complete prior histories within split while preserving Q;
5. shuffle within-patient laboratory times while preserving assay/value identity;
6. permute calendar-era labels for the interaction;
7. repeat endpoint with R30 [td+24h,td+30d) and report immediate-boundary state separately;
8. verify no post-t0, index-linked, future-window, duplicate-key, encounter-overlap, or split-leaked feature;
9. reconcile Y90 scores with R30 and O90 strata and coherent H probabilities;
10. compare future encounter count and future source-specific documentation intensity in [td+30,td+90) among R30 nonreturns; these are outcomes only.

Supportive evidence requires favorable later-era P-minus-M for R30, calibrated predictions, an interval excluding clinically negligible harm, compatible earlier/later direction, attenuation under P permutation, no comparable M/Q-only explanation, and concordance of B and H. Stronger “return-specific” support additionally requires a larger R30 than O90 contrast and stability to the 24-hour boundary sensitivity. Adverse evidence includes null/harmful or reversed later-era P-minus-M, survival after P permutation, reproduction by M, instability under boundary rules, or a signal confined to O90/documentation intensity. Inconclusive evidence includes dominant U90, sparse events/assays, wide intervals, invalid timestamps, parser/vocabulary instability, no units/reference ranges, unreconciled joins, or nonconvergence.

## Clinical evidence limits and completion

Computationally checkable claims are limited to the frozen cohort, source/key/time audits, feature-block separation, held-out predictions, proper scores, calibration, interactions, missingness/boundary analyses, permutation falsifications, and coherent ledger reconciliation. Even favorable results cannot establish active HCC versus historical coding, stage, tumor burden, liver reserve, recurrence, planned/unplanned status, treatment indication or receipt, response, mortality, outside-care completeness, causal effects, safety, or clinical utility. These require expert adjudication, native diagnosis/result-release times, assay units/reference ranges, imaging/pathology, discharge/intent/receipt data, death and outside-care linkage, external validation, and prospective or causal/utility studies.

Completion requires the solver to freeze and report the ledgers, model manifests, locked predictions, score/calibration/uncertainty tables, support gates, all ten falsification outputs, the 24-hour boundary analysis, and a claim-to-output table. Completion is the artifact set and honest interpretation regardless of whether the hypothesis is supported.
