# Discordant renal recovery in MIMIC-IV: a falsifiable, temporally separated prognostic study

## Question and clinical importance

Among adult ICU patients with KDIGO acute kidney injury (AKI), does a discordance between creatinine-based and urine-output-based recovery at a fixed 72-hour post-onset landmark predict worse 28-day in-hospital mortality beyond what is explained by current illness severity?

The clinical decision is consequential: clinicians must decide whether to escalate monitoring, involve nephrology, initiate renal replacement therapy (RRT), or modify fluid management when creatinine and urine output tell different stories about renal recovery. A model that identifies patients whose discordance reflects true residual kidney injury rather than measurement or severity artifacts could support earlier nephrology review and more informed RRT decisions. This study does not claim that discordance itself causes worse outcomes.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

**Already supported by evidence:** AKI is common in ICU patients and associated with increased mortality; creatinine and urine output can disagree in AKI staging and recovery; AKI recovery is an important prognostic marker [K1][K2].

**Unresolved claim:** Whether discordance between creatinine-based and urine-output-based recovery at a fixed landmark predicts worse outcomes beyond current illness severity, or whether it reflects measurement artifacts, selective recording, or severity confounding.

## Study design

### Population
Adult ICU patients (age >= 18) in MIMIC-IV with KDIGO AKI stage 1-3, defined by creatinine and/or urine-output criteria. Exclude patients with missing creatinine at baseline or discharge, and those with <24 hours of ICU stay.

### Temporal boundaries
- AKI onset: first time meeting KDIGO criteria
- Landmark: 72 hours post-onset
- Recovery assessment: at landmark, compare creatinine-based recovery (creatinine returned to baseline or improved >=50%) with urine-output-based recovery (urine output >=0.5 mL/kg/h for >=6 hours)
- Discordance: creatinine recovered but urine output did not, or vice versa
- Outcome: 28-day in-hospital mortality

### Variables
- Exposure: discordance category (concordant recovery, creatinine-only recovery, urine-output-only recovery, no recovery)
- Covariates: age, sex, ICU admission type, SOFA score at landmark, AKI stage, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, fluid balance
- Outcome: 28-day in-hospital mortality (binary)

### Analysis
1. **Simple baseline:** Logistic regression with current severity (SOFA) and AKI stage as covariates; compare discordance categories
2. **Learned alternative:** Gradient-boosted model with temporal features (creatinine trajectory, urine-output trajectory, fluid balance) to capture non-linear interactions; compare discrimination (AUC) and calibration
3. **Falsification:** If discordance does not predict mortality beyond severity, or if the effect is driven by selective measurement (e.g., more measurements in sicker patients), the hypothesis is not supported

### Falsification criteria
- **Supportive:** Discordance predicts worse mortality beyond severity (adjusted OR > 1.2, 95% CI excluding 1.0)
- **Adverse:** No association or inverse association after adjustment
- **Inconclusive:** Wide CIs, small sample, or measurement artifacts dominate

## Limitations
- No measured GFR; creatinine and urine output are surrogate markers
- Selective measurement: sicker patients may have more frequent measurements
- Observational design; no causal claims
- Single-center data (MIMIC-IV); external validation needed
