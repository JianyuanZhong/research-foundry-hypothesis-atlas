# Hypothesis Proposal: Pre-TACE Nutritional Status Predicts TACE Outcomes

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question:** Do pre-TACE serum nutritional markers beyond Child-Pugh components (specifically pre-albumin, total protein, and cholesterol) predict both TACE treatment response (progression-free survival) and treatment-related complications (decompensation, hospitalization, death)?

**Secondary Question:** Can a multivariate nutritional panel improve risk stratification beyond Child-Pugh score alone?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Nutritional Assessment Gap**: Current HCC prognostic models focus on liver synthetic function (Child-Pugh) but may neglect comprehensive nutritional status, which is prevalent in HCC patients and independently predicts outcomes.

2. **Treatment Selection**: Identifying patients with poor nutritional status might benefit from nutritional optimization before TACE or alternative therapies (systemic therapy, transplant evaluation).

3. **Complication Prevention**: Nutritional markers may predict TACE-related complications (jaundice, ascites, coagulopathy) even in patients with "adequate" liver function, enabling proactive interventions.

4. **Early Warning System**: Pre-TACE nutritional profiles could serve as actionable biomarkers for anticipating complications and optimizing monitoring.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Current literature focuses on:
- **Child-Pugh score** as the primary prognostic tool for HCC treatment (5 components: albumin, bilirubin, INR, ascites, encephalopathy)
- **ALBI grade** (albumin-bilirubin) for selected patients
- **Tumor characteristics** (size, number) as prognostic factors

**Gap**: While ALBI grade combines albumin and bilirubin, it does not capture the full nutritional picture. Comprehensive nutritional assessment including total protein (marker of overall protein status) and cholesterol (marker of metabolic health) could provide superior prognostic accuracy.

## Method Comparison

### Baseline: Child-Pugh Score

**Approach**: Calculate Child-Pugh score using pre-TACE labs (albumin, bilirubin, INR) and use it as primary predictor.

**What it tests**: Main effects of liver synthetic function on TACE outcomes.

**Limitations**:
- Only 5 components, may miss important predictors
- Linear scoring may not capture non-linear relationships
- Does not incorporate additional nutritional markers with different biological half-lives

### Alternative: Multivariate Nutritional Panel

**Approach**: Build multivariate model including:
- Child-Pugh components (albumin, bilirubin)
- Additional nutritional markers (total protein, cholesterol)
- Use gradient boosting or similar machine learning to capture non-linear interactions

**What it reveals**:
- Non-linear relationships between nutritional markers and outcomes
- Feature interactions (e.g., albumin × total protein interaction)
- Whether additional nutritional markers provide independent predictive value

**Scientific Information Gained**:
- Whether total protein and cholesterol add independent predictive value beyond albumin
- Whether combining multiple nutritional markers provides superior stratification
- Non-linear threshold effects in nutritional markers

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. Child-Pugh score performs as well as or better than any multivariate nutritional panel (comparable AUC/C-index)
2. No individual nutritional marker (beyond Child-Pugh components) adds significant predictive value (p > 0.05 in multivariate analysis)

The hypothesis would be **supported** if:
1. The multivariate panel demonstrates statistically significant improvement in predictive accuracy (AUC/C-index improvement > 0.02)
2. At least one additional marker (total protein, cholesterol) shows independent predictive value
3. The panel correctly identifies higher-risk patients that Child-Pugh misses

## Expected Impact

- **Clinical**: Provide a more comprehensive prognostic tool for TACE decision-making
- **Research**: Advance understanding of nutritional status in HCC treatment
- **Policy**: Inform guidelines on pre-TACE assessment requirements

## Limitations

1. **Single-center data**: Results may not generalize to other institutions
2. **No direct nutritional scores**: Relying on surrogate markers (albumin, total protein, cholesterol) rather than validated nutritional assessment tools (NRS-2002, PG-SGA)
3. **Treatment selection confounding**: Fitter patients may receive TACE, potentially confounding relationships
4. **Temporal variability**: Pre-TACE labs may not represent true nutritional status
