## Question (falsifiable)
Among RA synovial tissue macrophages, is there a DISTINCT, donor-consistent transcriptional program in patients who are RESISTANT to treatment versus patients in REMISSION — and is it distinct from (not merely a more extreme version of) the NAIVE/undifferentiated state? Does it nominate a therapeutic target / resistance signature?

## Clinical importance
~40% of RA patients respond inadequately to DMARDs; a subset remains refractory after cycling through agents of distinct mechanisms (the EULAR "difficult-to-treat" phenotype) [K1]. Identifying a macrophage-based treatment-resistance signature would (a) predict which patients will fail current therapy and (b) nominate the resistant-state program as a therapeutic target. This directly informs a consequential clinical decision: mechanism-based therapeutic selection for the difficult-to-treat subset [K2]. De Lima 2023 showed that tissue-resident macrophage states and TAM/MERTK signaling are associated with treatment response in a different cohort [K3]; this proposal tests whether a complementary, bulk-pseudobulk macrophage transcriptional contrast on E-MTAB-8322 carries a distinct resistant program.

This is an INCONCLUSIVE result: a partially distinct resistant program is suggested by the distinctness metrics, but the module enrichment does not nominate a specific therapeutic target, and the contrast is underpowered for genome-wide FDR (0 genes).

## Competing explanations (addressed)
1. Cross-sectional confound: Resistant vs Remission are different patients, not a within-patient transition; no causal/temporal claim.
2. "Resistant = more naive/inflammatory": tested by the distinctness test (Jaccard 0.205 low, Spearman 0.606 moderate -> partially distinct, shared axis). The module enrichment shows the M1 enrichment (S100A8/S100A9) is shared with the naive state, not resistant-specific.
3. Donor/age/sex confound: all female (no sex confound); age balanced.
4. Library-size/batch: log2(count+1) used, not a full normalization; a proper pseudobulk normalization could change the picture.
5. Cell-state composition: pseudobulk macrophages only; subclustering could change the picture (future test).
6. Low power: n=6 vs 7 -> 0 FDR<0.1; the distinctness test and module enrichment are the primary informative checks.
7. TAM-MERTK axis: The module enrichment shows NO enrichment of the TAM-MERTK axis (M2) in the top-100 resistant genes. This does NOT support a TAM-MERTK-specific resistant program. The De Lima 2023 finding [K3] was in a different cohort and may not generalize to E-MTAB-8322.

## Falsification criteria (unchanged from parent)
- SUPPORTIVE: >=20 genes FDR<0.1 (or a clear module-level signal); top module enriched for a specific axis (e.g. pro-resolving/TAM-MERTK down in resistant); LOW overlap (<0.3) AND LOW correlation (<0.4) with naive-vs-rem -> distinct resistant program.
- ADVERSE: No FDR<0.1 AND HIGH overlap (>0.6) AND HIGH correlation (>0.7) with naive-vs-rem -> resistant is just more naive/inflammatory, no distinct program; OR group imbalance makes the contrast uninterpretable.
- INCONCLUSIVE: <5 FDR<0.1 (as observed: 0) AND MODERATE overlap (0.2-0.5) AND MODERATE correlation (0.4-0.7) -> partially distinct but underpowered; needs larger cohort or subclustering.
- CURRENT RESULT: INCONCLUSIVE. 0 FDR<0.1, Jaccard 0.205 (low), Spearman 0.606 (moderate). Module enrichment does NOT support a TAM-MERTK-specific resistant program.

## Method alternatives (recorded)
- Baseline (used): donor-mean log2FC + Welch t (proper T-distribution p via scipy) + BH FDR + Jaccard/Spearman distinctness + module enrichment. Simple, transparent, appropriate for n=6/7 donors.
- Alternative (deferred): per-donor subclustering (M1/M2/IFN) then cluster-proportion contrast. More mechanistic but heavier; revisit if the pseudobulk contrast is inconclusive (as it is).
- Alternative (deferred): elastic-net classifier Resistant vs Rem with donor-blocked CV. Overkill for n=13 donors; overfitting risk; not justified now.
- Alternative (deferred): proper pseudobulk normalization (DESeq2/SCT) + donor-level permutation test for exact p-values. Recommended as the next step to resolve the low-power FDR. The permutation test was attempted this episode but was too slow for 16k genes x 1000 permutations in the discovery budget; proper T-distribution p-values were used instead.
