# Proposal: persistence and landmark timing of component-resolved trajectories after a recorded TACE-like index

## Episode, parent, and scientific deliverable

This Episode-23 successor evolves the assessed-valid HCC candidate `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves that candidate's exact read-only HCC bindings, encounter-constrained first recorded TACE-like index, 24-hour procedure episode construction with encounter-only sensitivity, HCC diagnosis ascertainment proxy, day-45 risk set, component-resolved exact-assay AFP/albumin/total-bilirubin rules, strict-versus-broad pathway ontology, coding/capture controls, 2010–2022/2023/2024 patient-temporal split, clustered uncertainty, and evidence limits.

The remaining clinically consequential ambiguity is temporal: the parent reduces all eligible post-index values in days 7–45 to a first post-index component state. A favorable value early in that interval may be transient, whereas a later value may be missing because of care timing or a competing therapeutic episode. A fixed day-45 analysis therefore cannot tell whether its association with a later recorded pathway reflects a persistent trajectory or merely a snapshot taken at a variable clinical opportunity. This matters for the timing of reassessment and surveillance, even though this dataset cannot establish treatment response or treatment benefit.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted, locked estimates of the cause-specific and cumulative-incidence contrast for a prespecified persistent versus transient/nonpersistent three-component trajectory, at the inherited day-45 landmark, plus a predeclared dynamic-landmark sensitivity at days 15 and 30. Discovery has fitted no model and claims no result.

## Evidence, unresolved claim, and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is operational: local dated procedure, encounter and laboratory rows can be linked on `patient master index, encounter number`, and the procedure and laboratory sources contain relevant labels, timestamps and exact assay names. The source does not establish tumor burden, viable tumor, radiologic response, treatment indication or intent, technical completion, laboratory units, death, survival, outside care, utility or external validity.

The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable: within adults in the inherited day-45 risk set, does persistence of the joint component pattern through day 45 distinguish a later coherent recorded retreatment pathway from a transient or unavailable pattern after accounting for prior history and local measurement/capture intensity?

Primary hypothesis: compared with a nonpersistent, discordant or uncertain component trajectory, a prespecified persistent-concordant pattern—AFP direction favorable in both early and late windows, albumin non-worsening in both, and total bilirubin non-worsening in both—has a different cause-specific hazard and 320-day cumulative incidence of the first subsequent `TACE_strict` episode from day 46 through day 365. The contrast should add information to the parent's single post-index snapshot and coding/observation baseline, be directionally compatible on the broad therapeutic endpoint, and be more specific to therapeutic than diagnostic, mixed-ambiguous or non-liver capture states.

This hypothesis is about a recorded pathway, not response. It is falsified if the persistent-versus-nonpersistent contrast is null or reversed after the prespecified capture baseline, is no larger for strict therapy than for diagnostic/non-liver controls, disappears under encounter-only episode construction or the dynamic landmarks, is concentrated in unresolved labels or one assay variant, or is explained by early treatment selection/measurement opportunity. A null or adverse result would challenge the recorded-pathway interpretation; it would not show that a patient biologically failed treatment.

## Estimands and analysis

The primary estimand is the held-out 320-days-after-day-45 difference in cumulative incidence of `TACE_strict` between persistent-concordant and the prespecified reference state (all nonpersistent/discordant/uncertain states), standardized over the full day-45 risk set with unavailable components retained as explicit categories. Also report cause-specific hazard contrasts and CIFs at 30, 90 and 320 days after day 45. The paired-observed estimand, restricted to individuals with supported component pairs, is secondary and must be labeled as such. Report the broad therapeutic, diagnostic, mixed-ambiguous and non-liver endpoints using the same index, state, split and follow-up.

The transparent baseline is a regularized discrete-time cause-specific hazard model with 30-day bins. It includes the parent's age, sex, index year/department, pre-index diagnosis/procedure counts, local contact history, procedure-source mix, episode/coding density, assay availability and measurement counts, plus the inherited single post-index component states. The persistence model adds the prespecified E/L states, time delays and missingness indicators; its incremental CIF contrast over the baseline is the primary transparent test. Fit separate cause-specific models or a multinomial competing-state model with identical preprocessing and patient split; choose one before tuning and preserve the alternative in the manifest.

The substantive alternative is a shared-parameter irregular-time joint model: separate latent, assay-specific AFP, albumin and bilirubin trajectories with robust observation/error models, an observation/contact-intensity submodel, and competing transition hazards for the same recorded states. Fit only observations through the selected landmark. It can reveal asynchronous change, curvature, uncertainty and informative sampling that two window categories lose, and tests whether the persistence result is a latent-trajectory phenomenon rather than a threshold artifact. It is scientifically matched to the same target, not justified by a small predictive gain. Report held-out CIF/log score, calibration, convergence, posterior/parameter uncertainty and observation-model sensitivity. A generic transformer is deferred because the limiting dependencies are clinical intent, validated coding semantics, units, response adjudication, death and outside-care linkage; reconsider it only if those dependencies become available.

Use the inherited patient-temporal split: 2010–2022 fitting, 2023 tuning, and 2024 locked test when event support permits; if a calendar cell is too sparse, declare the deterministic patient-hash fallback before fitting and use it for every model. No patient crosses splits. Freeze assay vocabulary, procedure ontology, episode rule, robust tolerances, state definitions, sparse-cell rule, model hyperparameters, observation-weight truncation and bootstrap count before locked evaluation. Any weighting is predictive standardization for measurement opportunity, not causal adjustment.

Use patient-clustered bootstrap or an equivalent patient-level resampling procedure, never row bootstrap. Report absolute contrasts, cause-specific hazards, CIFs, calibration, overlap/effective sample size, Brier/log score, and intervals. Preserve all availability states in the full-index analysis; report the paired-observed analysis and any observation-weighted analysis separately.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Supportive computation requires: (i) the persistent-concordant versus reference contrast on `TACE_strict` exceeds a prespecified 5-percentage-point absolute CIF association margin, with the lower bound of its patient-level interval above the 5-point margin; (ii) the contrast survives the capture baseline and has adequate positivity/calibration; (iii) strict and broad therapeutic estimates are directionally compatible; (iv) therapeutic specificity is stronger than diagnostic, mixed-ambiguous and non-liver controls; (v) dynamic landmarks do not show an unexplained timing reversal; and (vi) the joint model converges with compatible latent-trajectory contrasts. This supports only a reproducible local recorded-pathway association.

Adverse computation is a null/reversed strict contrast after capture adjustment, a similar or larger diagnostic/non-liver contrast, disappearance when ambiguous labels or one assay variant are removed, strong dependence on episode construction or landmark, poor overlap, or joint-model nonconvergence/observation-model instability. These results favor measurement opportunity, coding, pathway ambiguity or timing explanations.

Inconclusive computation is insufficient event or paired support, missing early/late measurements, positivity failure, sparse strict events, unstable assay vocabulary, inadequate local follow-up, a failed temporal split, intervals failing to exclude the 5-point margin, or inability to distinguish administrative observation loss from a recorded transition. None of these outcomes establishes or rules out biological response.

The verifier can check source paths, schemas, two-key aggregation, index and episode rules, no-leakage landmark construction, exact assay exclusions, competing-state definitions, locked predictions, uncertainty, calibration and whether interpretation statements map to computed outputs. It cannot adjudicate intent, response, viable tumor, technical success, treatment benefit, survival, mortality, appropriateness, utility, outside care or external validity. Those require expert chart/imaging review, validated units and assay harmonization, death and outside-care linkage, and prospective or external outcome ascertainment.
