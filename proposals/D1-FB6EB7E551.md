# Auditable fixed-capacity review allocation for documentary high-grade MVI

## Episode-21 successor and unresolved clinical claim

This is a substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. The decision is whether a scarce multidisciplinary-review queue should prioritize adults before a first eligible HCC liver resection because the eventual resection specimen may contain pathology-documented high-grade microvascular invasion (M2). The action under study is allocation of a fixed number of reviews. It is not a recommendation for resection extent, transplantation, neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy, systemic or locoregional therapy, surveillance, or any other treatment.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is documentary. The HCC snapshot contains patient-linked encounter and procedure records, CT/MRI examination rows with narrative findings/diagnoses, same-encounter pathology narratives, and longitudinal medication/order/diagnosis records. The source does not contain report authored/final/release/view times or version history, pathology time, specimen or slide identifiers, sampling adequacy, raw images, observed multidisciplinary-review workload, clinician action, treatment assignment/effect, recurrence, survival, harms, costs, or patient benefit. The laboratory field `检验时间` has no documented collection/finalization/release semantics, so laboratory values and missingness have no primary decision-time lineage.

The expert-selected demonstrations establish useful design principles rather than evidence for this HCC claim. The inspected full Nature article on longitudinal generative disease modelling demonstrates temporal locking, calibration and external validation as requirements for longitudinal prediction; the inspected full Bayesian EHR/genetic article demonstrates explicit time-varying uncertainty and selection-bias handling. The cancer demonstration's main article was unavailable; only its supplement was inspected and it cannot support claims about the main paper or STAR Methods. None establishes that HCC report semantics improve a clinical review queue, or that an eventual stored report was visible before an operation.

The unresolved claim is therefore:

> Among the fixed, source-reconciled first-resection target frame, at a review capacity fixed by an auditable service contract before outcome review, does an interpretable CT/MRI semantic strategy select more documentary-M2 cases than (i) its identical semantic-mask pipeline and (ii) an otherwise identical laboratory-independent structured baseline, while holding the number of reviews exactly constant across all admissible source states?

The primary claim is an information-value claim about historical stored content under an auditable queue contract. It is not a causal claim, a biological-M2 claim, a report-availability claim, or evidence of patient benefit.

## Capacity contract and hypotheses

Before pathology parsing, semantic extraction, model fitting or test-period counts are inspected, the study team must register a capacity contract containing: (1) an integer review budget K per 100 eligible source-frame patients (or the integer equivalent for the locked 2020–2021 union frame); (2) the calendar/service unit over which K applies; (3) the maximum acceptable queue size and reviewer hours; (4) the minimum clinically material capture margin; and (5) the reviewers and workflow that would receive a flag. K is chosen from a contemporaneous service-capacity audit or a prospectively agreed stakeholder contract, not from HCC outcomes, predicted scores, or the arbitrary result of model tuning. The audit must state whether slots are fungible across the eligible queue and how fractional final slots are handled.

The data do not contain this audit. Therefore K in {5%, 10%, 20%} is a locked computational sensitivity grid inherited from the parent, not a current clinical capacity. A primary clinical-contract conclusion is permitted only if an external audit supplies a pre-registered K and workload/acceptability bounds. If it does not, the experiment can still produce a valid stored-content sensitivity result over the grid, but deployment and workload claims are inconclusive. This explicitly repairs the parent’s use of fixed capacities as a research benchmark without inventing a service capacity.

For each registered K and the inherited sensitivity capacities, fit:

- B: age, sex, report-modality/process and acquisition/accession structure, with no report-body semantics, laboratory values, untimed diagnosis text or post-decision information.
- S: the identical B block plus the prespecified interpretable CT/MRI semantic block.
- S0: the S pipeline with the semantic block masked after fitting; S0 is never separately refit.
- Random, flag-all, flag-none and structured no-feature queues as prespecified reference baselines.

The primary contrasts on the exact same patient queue and capacity are
D_K = captured documentary-M2 cases by S minus S0, and
C_K = captured documentary-M2 cases by S minus B.
The research margin is five additional documentary-M2 cases per 100 review slots, m_K = 0.05K on the target-frame scale, unless the registered service contract specifies a different clinically justified margin before outcome review. The primary contract supports the hypothesis only if simultaneous 97.5% lower confidence limits for both D_K and C_K exceed the registered margin, at the registered K and the 24-hour decision buffer, in every terminal outer outcome state. Report exact queue size, captured positives, precision, recall and false-review burden; equal queue size makes comparisons workload-normalized by construction.

The semantic-attribution hypothesis is that D_K exceeds the margin. The complete-strategy hypothesis is that C_K exceeds the margin. Both are required for the strongest supportive interpretation. A positive C_K with nonpositive D_K means the complete strategy may differ from the structured baseline but does not establish semantic incremental value.

## Analysis, uncertainty and falsification

Use patient-wise worst-admissible-state convex logistic fitting for development, tuning and recalibration. Do not average invented states equally and do not condition on complete cases. Rebuild states, refit, calibrate, rank, queue and recompute every endpoint in a patient-level outer bootstrap. Use one simultaneous 97.5% family covering registered K, the sensitivity capacities, D and C, default contrasts, all buffers, modality/access cells and frontier endpoints. Variable denominators and fractional final slots are explicit. Compute frontier extrema with an exact state-expanded MILP or multiple-choice dynamic program and replay against brute force on small fixtures.

Falsification tests are mandatory:

1. A semantic permutation within frozen year, modality/process and report-cardinality strata must produce a null envelope; observed D_K must exceed it for support.
2. The semantic-mask fixture must produce identical S and S0 scores, ranks and queue identity, with D_K exactly zero.
3. Every queue must contain exactly the registered K (with the frozen final-slot convention) in every state; ties use a frozen patient-ID hash and never pathology.
4. Flag-all/flag-none/random and structured no-feature queues must be reported; a favorable AUC or point estimate without queue capture above margin is not support.
5. State reachability, row identity, no-overlap quarantine, source hashes, exact joins, time windows, report grouping, outcome-envelope arithmetic and zero-lab lineage must be independently checked.
6. The external capacity audit, reviewer-hours bound and report-version/finalization/view audit must be checked as separate evidence gates, not inferred from HCC rows.
7. Re-run the primary result under 12/48/72-hour buffers and modality-pattern states; capacity-dependent sign changes are reported as mixed.

## Interpretation

Supportive stored-content results require valid computation, complete state reachability, parser validation, semantic null rejection, S0 identity, calibration, queue-capacity integrity, zero-lab lineage, simultaneous uncertainty, and both D_K and C_K lower limits above the registered margin at the registered capacity and all terminal outer states. The permitted conclusion is that, in this historical source reconstruction, semantic information selected more documentary-M2 cases at an equal, externally specified review capacity than its same-fit mask and structured baseline. If the capacity audit and report-access audit are present and representative with joint lower bounds satisfying the contract, a separately labeled conditional workflow-feasibility result may be reported. It still does not establish clinical benefit.

Adverse results require valid computation with an upper bound at or below zero (harmful) or at/below the margin (non-material) for either contrast at any primary capacity across the outer envelope, or failure of the semantic permutation null. If S improves C but not D, semantic incremental value is not supported. A favorable adjudicated/core envelope with an adverse outer envelope is not support. Harm at a fixed queue is clinically relevant because the same review capacity would be allocated less accurately.

Results are inconclusive when the interval crosses the margin, the capacity contract is missing for a clinical interpretation, states are incomplete, the optimizer or brute-force replay fails, labels are sparse/ungradable, semantic validation fails, report versions or visibility are mismatched, or capture is not supportive despite a favorable discrimination statistic. Deployment is inconclusive without representative evidence of the report body shown before t_dec, final/release/view timestamps, version matching and coverage at least c*. A positive stored-content result cannot rescue these missing workflow facts.

The automatic verifier can check hashes, headers, row ordinals, duplicate rules, joins, clock states, temporal quarantine, feature lineage, S0 identity, exact queue capacity, frontier arithmetic, bootstrap family and conclusion linkage. It cannot establish operation identity, Chinese clinical semantics, specimen linkage, pathology sampling adequacy, report visibility, capacity-audit representativeness, reviewer acceptability, clinician action, treatment effects, recurrence, survival, cost or patient benefit. Those require expert adjudication, source-system evidence, stakeholder review, prospective workflow linkage or another study.
