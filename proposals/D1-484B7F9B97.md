# Assay payload beyond assay selection: an observation-qualified HCC return study

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

This is a substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent repaired an important observation-process ambiguity by decomposing a 30-day local recorded return into return, later local re-observation, and unresolved no-later-observation states. Its remaining identifiability problem is that its primary E-versus-Q comparison adds two scientifically different things at once: which assays were selected and what those assays measured. A favorable E increment could therefore be entirely a marker of clinician test selection or care intensity, rather than information in the measured biochemical payload.

The new deliverable is a prespecified nested comparison of three information sets:

- **Q**: strict-prior capture intensity and encounter context, with no assay identity or result;
- **M**: Q plus the prior assay menu and sampling structure (assay identities, assay-specific counts, recency, span, missingness and specimen-type availability), but no result values;
- **P**: M plus assay-specific qualitative and valid quantitative results, specimen type, and within-assay change features.

The primary scientific question is:

> Among adults discharged after their first locally recorded HCC-keyed inpatient encounter, does strict prior laboratory payload (P) improve held-out, calibrated prediction of the subsequent 30-day local recorded-return state beyond the same patient’s strict prior capture intensity and assay menu (M), and is that payload increment distinct from a later local-observation process?

The hypothesis is falsifiable and deliberately narrower than a causal or clinical-utility claim:

> After Q and M are held fixed, P will produce a positive, calibrated proper-score increment for 30-day local recorded return and/or the exhaustive observation-qualified state, with a larger return-state increment than later-observation-state increment. If the P-M increment disappears while M-Q remains, the apparent laboratory signal is assay-selection/process information rather than measured content. If P-M is concentrated in later re-observation, it is information about local observability, not return risk.

This advances the parent by making “assay content” identifiable as a nested predictive contrast. It does not identify the causal effect of ordering a test, treatment response, active HCC status, or a true readmission endpoint.

## Evidence-supported versus untested claims

Available evidence supports that the frozen HCC snapshot contains native encounter admission/discharge dates, and that its laboratory table exposes assay identity and result fields with a native laboratory time. It supports construction of local-record observation states and a strict prior-history policy. The inherited feasibility audit reported 42,681 administratively complete 30-day anchors, 4,913 recorded returns, and later local encounters among some 30-day nonreturns; those are inherited feasibility counts, not this child’s model results. The incumbent 42,688/4,916 versus 42,681/4,913 ledger discrepancy remains an explicit pre-analysis reconciliation task.

The untested claim is that measured assay payload contains reproducible information beyond assay selection and capture intensity. A solver must newly construct the nested feature matrices, fit all specified models, evaluate locked predictions, and produce the payload-versus-menu contrasts and observation-process decomposition. No favorable direction is assumed.

Set t0=`入院时间` and td=`出院时间`. For L in {60, 90, 180}, require td+L <= 2026-01-01 00:00 for that risk set. Define O_L=1 when a same-patient encounter begins in [td+30 days, td+L), regardless of R. Later encounters are never predictors and never relabel R. Use L=90 as the primary decomposition and 60/180 as nested, administratively eligible sensitivities; use half-open boundaries.

The primary estimand is the paired locked proper-score increment P-M for R on all 30-day eligible anchors. Secondary estimands are P-M for Z_90, state-specific P-M contrasts for R and N+_90, and the nested L curve. Report M-Q as the assay-menu/process increment and P-Q for continuity, but do not interpret P-Q as payload evidence. Conditional O_L strata are descriptive decompositions, not causal adjustments.

## Strict information sets

All models use identical people, labels, preprocessing, patient split and calibration protocol. Exclude direct identifiers `姓名`, `身份证号`, `手机号`, `医保/就诊卡号` and `住院号`.

A contains admission age, sex, department, calendar era, native admission time-of-day and missingness. A_d is a context sensitivity adding only native LOS td-t0 and discharge calendar/time-of-day; it is not part of Q/M/P’s primary comparison.

Q contains completed prior-encounter count and duration, active days, time since last completed encounter, source-specific row counts and distinct linked-encounter counts for labs, procedures, orders, medications and examinations, observed source-type count, missing/excluded-time counts, left-truncation and no-history indicators. Q has no assay identity, assay result, procedure name, order name, medication name, dose, route, frequency, or examination text.

M adds only laboratory menu/measurement-process features from eligible prior laboratory rows: normalized `检验` identity as categorical indicators or a prespecified hashed vocabulary, assay-specific row/count, distinct linked-encounter count, recency, span, sampling-time count, missingness, and `标本类型` availability/missingness. M must not include `定性结果`, `定量结果`, result-derived abnormality, or any numeric value. To prevent menu leakage through sparse categories, vocabulary construction and rare-category handling occur in fit/selection patients only and are frozen before locked evaluation.

P adds to M, within each assay identity, normalized `定性结果`, valid numeric `定量结果`, `标本类型`, and assay-specific recency/span/count/missingness and within-assay change/slope only when at least two distinct valid `检验时间` values exist. Numeric values remain assay-specific because the HCC metadata confirms no separate laboratory unit column; never pool values across assays. Define valid numeric parsing and impossible-value handling before locked labels/predictions. Result missingness is represented without treating a missing value as a normal measurement.

For every source row, require its (`患者主索引`, `就诊号`) to link to a non-anchor encounter for the same patient with parseable native admission/discharge and linked discharge <t0. The source-specific availability time must be parseable and in [t0-730 days,t0):

- labs: `检验时间`;
- procedures: `开始时间`;
- orders: `开立时间`, never later `开始时间`;
- medications: `开始时间`;
- examinations: `开始时间`.

Exclude anchor-linked rows even when their event timestamp precedes t0. Publish missing/invalid availability, missing linked discharge, open/overlapping linked stay, anchor-linked, post-t0, duplicate and row-inflation counts. Timestamp-only and anchor-linked histories are sensitivity branches, never primary evidence.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is fit-only standardized regularized logistic regression with the same feature matrix and patient partitions:

- B_Q, B_M and B_P for binary R;
- multinomial B_Q, B_M and B_P for Z_L;
- optional incumbent timing endpoint [0,7), [7,14), [14,30), no recorded return for continuity only.

Use fit-only standardization, one predeclared regularization-selection grid, and calibration selected only in fit/selection buckets. The primary contrasts are B_P-B_M and B_M-B_Q, evaluated on the same locked patients. This baseline is transparent and shows whether payload adds information after menu conditioning, but it loses irregular event order, interval-specific hazard changes, and the distinction between return and later observation.

The substantive alternative is a matched discrete-time multistate observation model H_Q, H_M and H_P. Starting at td, create person-state intervals [0,7), [7,14), [14,30) with return as an absorbing transition; for patients without R by day 30, create the later-observation interval [30,L) and a later-observation transition. Fit complementary-log-log cause-specific hazards with interval offsets, the same Q/M/P vectors, and a small predeclared ridge or weakly informative penalty. Convert hazards into coherent R, N+_L and N-_L probabilities. The alternative can reveal whether P-M changes the return hazard, the later-observation hazard, or both—information a single multinomial baseline loses. H is not selected for predictive complexity or a small score gain; it is selected because the remaining scientific uncertainty is temporal and observational.

The baseline and H model receive exactly the same inputs, target, patient split, preprocessing, calibration data, locked patients, proper scores and bootstrap scheme. A model-complexity advantage without a distinct hazard interpretation is not evidence for the hypothesis.

An irregular latent sequence encoder remains deferred. Dated sequences could model nonlinear cross-assay trajectories, as the research-ambition methods guide describes for bounded adaptations of learned disease histories, but that representation would confound the present question with another layer of feature learning before the menu-versus-payload estimand is resolved. Revisit only if P-M remains supported with adequate event support and the future solver envelope allows a preregistered, same-target comparison. A document/pathology neural branch is also deferred: `clinical_documents` and `pathology` have no native time and no validated extraction for this endpoint, and the HCC release contains no images. This is an evidence-bound deferral, not a ban on neural methods.

## Splits, uncertainty and decision relevance

Partition patients by SHA-256(normalized `患者主索引`) modulo 100: 0-59 fit, 60-69 preprocessing/hyperparameter/fit-only calibration selection, 70-79 locked evaluation, and 80-99 inaccessible. Every anchor, prior row, future encounter and interval for a patient remains in one bucket. Freeze the ledger, all labels, windows, feature vocabulary, result parser, feature manifest, model dimensions, penalty, calibration and boundary rules before reading locked outcomes or predictions.

Primary evaluation is paired patient-clustered locked log loss and Brier, lower better: P-M for R (primary), P-M for Z_90, and M-Q as the process/menu comparator. Report calibration intercept/slope, reliability, and state-specific one-vs-state decompositions only as secondary explanatory quantities. Report H versus B on the same targets and locked patients.

Use at least 1,000 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples of locked predictions, retaining all intervals for each patient, for 95% percentile or BCa intervals. Publish paired confidence intervals for P-M, M-Q, P-Q, the R-versus-N+_L contrast, and the H hazard increments. Require declared minimum locked support per state and interval; otherwise mark the affected estimand inconclusive rather than merge states post hoc.

Decision relevance is limited to a potential post-discharge follow-up risk-stratification use: the experiment asks whether measured prior laboratory results add information that could justify a future validated risk score beyond knowing which tests were selected. It does not choose a treatment or claim utility. As a descriptive bridge, report calibration and net-benefit curves for prespecified alert thresholds (for example 5%, 10%, 20%) only if locked event prevalence and support are adequate; thresholds are operational sensitivity analyses, not clinical recommendations. Because intervention harms, capacity, patient preferences, true readmissions and costs are unavailable, no positive net-benefit curve establishes clinical utility. A future prospective study with an intervention, harms, workload and adjudicated outcomes is required.

## Falsification and interpretation gates

Mandatory audits and falsifications are:

1. **Menu-vs-payload nesting:** P-M must be computed directly; P-Q alone cannot support payload interpretation. If P-M is null but M-Q is favorable, reject the claim that measured content adds information.
2. Permute result payload within assay and partition while preserving assay identity, native times, counts and missingness. P-M should attenuate.
3. Permute assay identity while preserving values, times and counts; large changes indicate menu/identity dependence or a broken assay-specific representation.
4. Shuffle complete strict prior histories across patients within each split while preserving Q and labels; increments should attenuate.
5. Shuffle strict event times within patient/source while preserving payload and counts; timing-sensitive increments should attenuate.
6. Fit H_M. If H_M reproduces H_P/B_P for both R and Z_L, the result is process/menu information and the payload interpretation is rejected even if prediction is good.
7. Permute labels within calendar era and department; persistent performance indicates leakage or label artifact.
8. Verify no row at/after t0, anchor-linked primary-history row, current-anchor row, post-discharge feature, future-window feature, outcome-derived O_L feature or split leakage.
9. Publish duplicate-key, multiplicity, unmatched-key and row-inflation audits for every two-column join and reconcile the incumbent 42,688/4,916 versus 42,681/4,913 counts before confirmatory interpretation.
10. Verify coherent H probabilities: hazards in [0,1], state probabilities sum to one, derived R equals the sum of R transitions, and the all-state score equals the O_L-stratum score weighted by observed stratum proportions within tolerance.
11. Repeat L=60,90,180 only after publishing support. A sign reversal or single-window dependence is observation-process-sensitive and not confirmatory.
12. Keep O_L and all future encounters out of predictors.

Supportive evidence requires: favorable calibrated B_P-B_M for R with a confidence interval and clinically interpretable calibration improvement; concordant P-M evidence for Z_90 or a prespecified R-versus-N+_90 contrast; attenuation under payload permutation; H_M not reproducing H_P; no leakage or ledger discrepancy; and directional stability in at least two administratively supported L values. This supports only incremental information in recorded prior assay payload beyond local assay selection, for local recorded-care risk stratification.

Adverse evidence includes null/harmful P-M, calibration deterioration, M-Q explaining P-Q, H_M reproducing H_P, payload-permutation persistence, P-M confined to N+_L, or any leakage. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse assay support or outcome states, dominant invalid-time/result burden, wide clustered intervals, unstable result parsing, boundary sensitivity, unresolved ledger counts, or inability to establish that assay units are comparable. If Z_L improves while R does not, report observation-qualified composition information only, not improved return-risk prediction. If P-M improves R but not Z_L, report the binary local-return increment with no claim about observation-process separation.

The parent’s original E-versus-Q comparison is retained as a continuity analysis but is not sufficient for the payload claim because its contrast conflates assay selection with assay values. The selected M intermediate is the repair.

A latent sequence model is deferred pending a supported P-M increment and adequate event/assay density; revisit it if the hazard and baseline results show robust nonlinear residual structure or if the primary solver finishes within the stated envelope. A document/pathology model is deferred pending native timestamps and validated semantic extraction.
