# Does early laboratory documentation lose transportability beyond measured state and monitoring intensity?

Status: Episode-7 substantive successor of [prior hypothesis]; adaptation of expert seed [starting question], not a reproduction of any demonstration paper. The parent’s eICU cohort, six-hour landmark, revision-safe laboratory rule, hospital-held-out design, and decision-facing operating-characteristic aim are retained. This successor retains the parent’s repairs and further freezes a metric-specific transport estimand, separates prevalence shifts from transport instability, and makes the inferential role of the GRU explicit.

## Unresolved clinical question and substantive advance

Before fitting this experiment, the strongest supported claim is limited to data availability: the frozen eICU snapshot contains hospital-linked ICU stays, ICU-relative vital observations, laboratory results with result and revision offsets, and a heterogeneous recorded laboratory process. The catalog and source audit do not establish a fitted model, transport failure, workflow causation, clinical utility, or prospective alert benefit.

The unresolved claim is:

> In adults who remain in an initial ICU stay at six hours, is adding early laboratory observation-process information to the same measured clinical state and general vital-monitoring process associated with a larger loss of 48-hour ICU-death alert performance when transported to entirely held-out hospitals?

This wording is deliberately associational: the observational snapshot cannot identify an intervention on testing or a causal effect of an alert.

This matters because an escalation, monitoring, transfer, or scarce-resource alert can appear accurate at development hospitals while exploiting local test documentation or interface practice. The scientific advance is a paired operating-characteristic transport experiment that separates laboratory process from both measured state and general vital-monitoring intensity, evaluates a threshold selected without using the internal evaluation set, and treats early discharge as a competing state. It estimates portability of an association. It does not estimate the effect of testing practice, the causal effect of an alert, optimal alert policy, or net clinical benefit.

The analysis unit is one eICU patientunitstayid. From patient, retain rows with numeric age >=18, unitstaytype = admit, unitvisitnumber = 1, unitdischargestatus exactly Alive or Expired, and finite unitdischargeoffset > 360. The strict unitdischargeoffset > 360 condition excludes a stay discharged exactly at the landmark, so the endpoint states are defined at the landmark.

## Exhaustive competing-state endpoint

The primary target is the mutually exclusive state at 48 hours after the landmark, with horizon minute 3240:

- D: unitdischargestatus = Expired and 360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240.
- C: unitdischargestatus = Alive and 360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240.
- R: finite unitdischargeoffset > 3240, regardless of eventual later unitdischargestatus.

Thus R means the stay was still in the ICU at minute 3240; a later death is not retroactively made a D event at the 48-hour horizon. These rules are exhaustive for retained rows with finite discharge offsets: early death is D, early alive discharge is C, and no discharge by 3240 is R. If the implementation finds an unassigned or multiply assigned state, stop the definitive analysis and report prerequisite failure.

The primary model is a three-class softmax estimate of the state distribution, with p_D used only to define the high-sensitivity death alert. The primary alert estimand is the change in held-out-hospital versus development-hospital operating characteristics when X1 is compared with X0 at a development-only threshold. State-vector Brier/log loss and the C/R distribution are co-primary safeguards against interpreting a death-alert metric without its competing-state context. A binary D-versus-not-D logistic sensitivity analysis is secondary and cannot replace the three-state primary analysis.

## Baseline, learned alternative, and fitting design

The transparent baseline is paired three-class multinomial L2 logistic regression (C=1, deterministic solver) for X0 and X1. Numeric imputation/scaling, categorical encoding, temperature scaling, and threshold selection are fit only in the relevant development partitions. Do not use class weighting because the model must produce interpretable state probabilities; report class prevalence and event counts instead.

The substantive alternative is a paired one-layer width-64 GRU with dropout 0.1, Adam learning rate 1e-3, batch size 256, unweighted three-class cross-entropy, at most 30 epochs and patience 5. Use the same X0/X1 bins, target, folds, preprocessing boundary, and threshold rule. The GRU’s early-stopping validation is a fixed 10% subgroup of the model-fit partition. It can test whether trajectory shape and irregular measurement timing reveal information lost by static summaries; it does not turn a process association into a mechanism.

## Episode-7 estimand and interpretation lock

For any metric m in the prespecified alert/calibration set, define the transport loss for representation X as L_m(X) = m_internal(X) - m_external(X) when higher is better, with the corresponding prespecified absolute-calibration or alert-fraction distance when lower is better. The laboratory-process transport contrast is Δ_m = L_m(X1) - L_m(X0), paired within outer fold and repetition. Report each Δ_m with its hospital-cluster bootstrap interval; do not average metrics into a favorable composite or select the metric after seeing results. Positive Δ_m supports the narrow process-specific transport hypothesis only when its direction is concordant across repetitions and the relevant integrity/falsification checks pass.

Observed PPV is reported for the actual hospital prevalence. PPV_std uses the fixed development prevalence p_ref and is the primary prevalence-transport safeguard; a PPV-only change with stable PPV_std is interpreted as prevalence shift. Differences in D/C/R prevalence are descriptive transport context, not evidence that the model or workflow caused outcomes. The GRU is corroborative for the temporal-information question: if logistic and GRU disagree, the result may support only the family-specific estimate and cannot establish a cross-representation conclusion. If the GRU is deferred, the logistic estimate remains a valid bounded test of the primary information-block transport contrast, but all claims about information in temporal trajectories are withheld.

## Primary analysis and uncertainty

For each model family, representation, repetition and outer fold, evaluate the frozen threshold on the disjoint internal-evaluation partition and on every outer held-out hospital. Report pooled and hospital-macro results. Hospitals with one outcome class are omitted only from AUROC diagnostics; they remain in alert counts, PPV/NPV where defined, Brier, log loss and calibration summaries.

The alert table reports D sensitivity, observed PPV, prevalence-standardized PPV, alert fraction, specificity and NPV. Fix p_ref before fitting as the pooled eligible development prevalence and repeat descriptively using pooled eligible prevalence. For each threshold, calculate PPV_std = sensitivity*p_ref / (sensitivity*p_ref + (1-specificity)*(1-p_ref)); report observed prevalence beside every PPV. The primary higher-is-better transport loss for sensitivity, observed PPV and PPV_std is internal minus external; for calibration use change in absolute intercept distance from 0, slope distance from 1, and D Brier/log loss; for alert fraction use abs(external alert fraction - internal alert fraction). For each metric, the process-specific transport contrast is the X1 loss minus the X0 loss. The principal hypothesis is a positive process-specific loss in at least one decision-facing alert metric, especially PPV_std or calibration, with no compensating external calibration improvement, after M is included in X0. An observed-PPV change without a corresponding PPV_std change is a prevalence shift, not evidence of transport failure. Report the actual paired point estimates; do not collapse discordant metrics into a favorable composite.

Also report the full three-state Brier score and multinomial log loss, state prevalence D/C/R by hospital, calibration of p(D), and the parent’s AUROC/AUPRC and binary D-versus-not-D results as secondary diagnostics. A high D-alert sensitivity with a changed C/R mix or poor state-vector score is not treated as evidence of a safe alert.

Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of complete paired hospital prediction sets, preserving folds, repetitions, thresholds and paired X0/X1 predictions, for percentile 95% intervals. This uncertainty is conditional on the fitted threshold pipeline. Report threshold distributions across repetitions and a 500-resample threshold-selection diagnostic within each calibration partition; if thresholds or alert fractions are unstable, say so and do not represent a point estimate as a stable policy. Require concordant direction across the five repetitions for a robust transport claim. The 0.02 lower-interval check is a non-triviality convention, not a clinical-benefit threshold.

## Falsification, adverse results, and stopping rules

Run these prespecified checks:

1. Permute D/C/R labels within hospital and refit. AUROC should approach chance and alert metrics should reflect permuted state prevalence.
2. Permute each patient’s P vector within hospital while preserving the X0 values and P marginal distribution. A process-specific result that survives this check is not credible.
3. Shuffle within-stay observation offsets while preserving measured values and counts. This tests dependence on timing alignment.
4. Compare revision-safe availability with a revision-ignorant diagnostic build. Any material change identifies revision leakage sensitivity, not permission to use the invalid build.
5. Compare raw P with P residualized against an outer-training-only X0 risk score, and stratify by X0 risk. This checks whether a result is only a re-expression of measured acuity.
6. Compare X0 and X1 with and without M as an explicit decomposition. A gain/loss that disappears when M is included is general monitoring dependence, not laboratory-specific transport.
7. Verify no patient/group overlap across fit, calibration, internal evaluation or outer test; no post-360 or post-revision rows; no hospital identifier/descriptors in predictors; and no calibration, threshold or preprocessing operation reading outer-test data.
8. Verify D/C/R exclusivity and exhaustiveness, including later-expired stays with discharge offset greater than 3240 assigned to R.

Supportive results require a positive X1-minus-X0 transport loss in sensitivity, PPV, alert-fraction stability, or calibration with a 95% interval excluding zero, a nonnegative X1 internal increment, concordant direction across the logistic and GRU families and repetitions, and all integrity checks passing. They support only the narrow statement that recorded laboratory process is associated with less portable early ICU-death alerts beyond the measured state and general monitoring process in this eICU snapshot.

Adverse results are no process-specific transport loss, stable or improved X1 external operating characteristics, a loss that disappears after M or X0-risk adjustment, a label/permutation failure, or a result isolated to random patient splits. These weaken or falsify the hypothesis for this panel/cohort; they do not prove universal portability or that workflow never matters.

Inconclusive results include fewer than 20 supported hospitals, state-label failure, threshold/calibration failure, unstable thresholds or alert fractions, intervals spanning zero, model-family disagreement, missing canonical fields, failed integrity checks, or GRU deferral when the conclusion would rely on temporal representation. If only the logistic branch completes, report the transparent model’s bounded result and explicitly withhold the claimed cross-family learned-representation conclusion.

Computationally checkable are the cohort, deduplication, joins, revision/time rules, coverage audit, feature construction, state assignment, split isolation, fits, calibration, threshold isolation, predictions, metrics, bootstrap intervals and permutation behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a missing row represents no order, an unrecorded order, interface loss, delayed result, or de-escalation; whether laboratory process is clinically actionable; whether residualization removes acuity; whether an alert changes care or outcomes; and whether any alert policy has net benefit. This data does not provide validated order intent, cancellations, turnaround, interface logs, staffing/capacity, treatment indications, prospective alert actions, post-discharge mortality, or external validation outside this eICU snapshot.

## Episode-7 method decision and revisit rule

The selected baseline remains paired three-state multinomial L2 logistic regression because it is transparent, directly estimates the X0-versus-X1 information contrast, and makes the incremental laboratory-process coefficients, calibration and threshold behavior auditable. The selected substantive alternative remains the paired width-64 GRU on identical bins, targets, splits and process blocks because it can reveal temporal physiology and irregular observation timing that static summaries lose. Both are task-specific fits, not proposer training, and neither complexity nor GPU use is a merit criterion. A mechanistic workflow model is deferred because this snapshot lacks order intent, cancellation, turnaround, interface logs, staffing/capacity and treatment indication. Revisit it only if those dependencies become available or if reproducible timing signals survive the offset and within-hospital process permutations.
