# Scientific Proposal: Discordant Liver-Renal Recovery Trajectories Predict HCC Decompensation Outcomes

## Unresolved Question

**Clinical Problem:** In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and decompensated cirrhosis undergoing liver transplantation evaluation, current risk stratification relies on single-time-point MELD scores and static laboratory values. However, patients with identical baseline MELD scores exhibit vastly different trajectories of clinical deterioration and transplant progression.

**Key Uncertainty:** Does the *discordance* between liver synthetic function recovery (INR, albumin) and renal function recovery (creatinine) over the first 30 days post-diagnosis predict which patients will experience 90-day outcomes (death, transplant listing, or readmission for decompensation) better than either trajectory alone or static baseline assessments?

**Scientific Gap:** Existing trajectory-based models treat each organ system independently. This proposal tests whether the *pattern of discordance* (liver improving while kidney deteriorates, or vice versa) captures clinically important information that single-organ trajectories miss, based on the principle that multi-organ failure involves coordinated inter-organ interactions.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Transplant Allocation Optimization:** Discordant liver-renal trajectories may identify high-risk patients who need urgent transplant evaluation despite moderate baseline MELD scores.

2. **Prognostic Guidance:** Recognizing discordant patterns (e.g., improving INR but rising creatinine) could signal impending hepatorenal syndrome or multi-organ failure.

3. **Early Intervention:** Discordant trajectories may trigger earlier interventions (diuretics, vasopressors, or transplant evaluation) before irreversible damage occurs.

## Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis:** In HCC patients with decompensated cirrhosis, patients exhibiting discordant trajectories between liver synthetic function (INR, albumin) and renal function (creatinine) over 30 days post-diagnosis have significantly worse 90-day outcomes (death, transplant listing, or readmission) than patients with concordant trajectories (both improving together) or static baseline assessments.

**Formal Definition:**
- Liver synthetic function trajectory: Slope of INR and albumin over 30 days
- Renal function trajectory: Slope of creatinine over 30 days
- Discordance score = |slope_INR - slope_creatinine| + |slope_albumin - slope_creatinine|
- Concordance: Both liver and renal slopes are negative (improving) or both positive (deteriorating)
- Discordance: Liver and renal slopes have opposite signs

**Predictive Claim:** Patients in the highest discordance quartile will have:
- Hazard ratio for 90-day death/readmission ≥ 2.0 compared to lowest quartile
- C-index improvement of ≥ 0.02 over static MELD score
- Independent predictive value after adjusting for baseline MELD

## Baseline Comparisons

1. **Static MELD Score:** Traditional single-time-point MELD using baseline INR, bilirubin, creatinine, albumin.
2. **Liver Trajectory Only:** Model using only INR and albumin trajectories.
3. **Renal Trajectory Only:** Model using only creatinine trajectory.
4. **Concordance Model:** Model identifying concordant vs discordant patterns.

## Substantive Alternatives and What They Lose

| Alternative | What It Tests | What It Loses Compared to Discordance Model |
|------------|---------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Static MELD | Baseline single-time-point prediction | Dynamic information from 30-day trajectory |
| Liver trajectory only | Liver-specific recovery | Renal contribution to multi-organ failure |
| Renal trajectory only | Renal-specific recovery | Liver synthetic function contribution |
| Concordance (binary) | Simple pattern classification | Quantitative discordance score and continuous prediction |
| Independent regression | Each organ separately | Interdependence of organ failure patterns |

**Key Insight:** The discordance model captures the *coordinated deterioration* across organ systems, which may be more predictive than any single organ's trajectory. When liver and kidney functions move in opposite directions, it signals systemic instability that single-organ models miss.

### Step 2: Trajectory Extraction
```python
# For each patient
1. Extract INR, albumin, creatinine values with timestamps
2. Fit linear regression: value ~ time
3. Compute slopes: slope_INR, slope_albumin, slope_creatinine
4. Compute discordance score:
   - If slopes have opposite signs: discordant
   - If both negative (improving): concordant
   - If both positive (deteriorating): concordant (but worse)
5. Compute discordance magnitude: |slope_INR - slope_creatinine|
```

### Step 3: Statistical Analysis

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis is **falsified** if:
1. Discordance score shows no association with outcomes (HR 0.8-1.2, 95% CI includes 1)
2. Concordant patients have better outcomes than discordant patients (supporting "concordance is protective" alternative)
3. Static MELD outperforms discordance model by ≥0.02 in C-index (p > 0.05)

The hypothesis is **supported** if:
1. Higher discordance predicts worse outcomes (HR ≥ 1.8, 95% CI excludes 1)
2. Discordance model outperforms static MELD by ≥0.02 C-index
3. Discordance is independently predictive after MELD adjustment

The hypothesis is **inconclusive** if:
1. Discordance shows weak association (HR 1.2-1.5, p > 0.05)
2. Results vary substantially by subgroup
3. Discordance pattern differs by cirrhosis etiology
