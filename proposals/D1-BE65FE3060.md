# eICU ventilator care-plan label transportability audit: evidence adverse to a common multicenter phenotype

## Status, parents, and clinical advance

This is a substantive transportability child of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves, without modification, the exact 540 person-landmark cohort, five landmarks, first-label rule, decision time D, two literal care-plan labels, and all exclusions. It also preserves two completed parent findings rather than retrying them:

1. Durable liberation cannot be validly ascertained under the locked eICU algorithms: independent liberation signals were only 13/309 versus 4/231 overall, only 1/130 versus 0/173 in the three overlap hospitals, and strict 48-hour observation among union signals was approximately one half. No durable-liberation association is re-estimated.
2. The 12-hour primary setting association was inverse in the three overlap hospitals, but it was dominated by peak-pressure charting; the no-peak result was sparse and attenuated, and sedation-rate results were imprecise and hospital-dependent.

The consequential remaining question is whether `Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation` and `Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial` can be treated as two common multicenter phenotypes with stable operational meaning, as would be required for pooled quality measurement, cohort definition, or decision-support features. The alternative is that their use and correlates are substantially hospital-specific documentation conventions.

The executed audit provides evidence adverse to the common-phenotype hypothesis. In the frozen target-labelled cohort, hospital explains a large descriptive share of binary label variation; only three of 74 represented hospitals have at least ten rows under each label; the apparently stable inverse primary setting contrast disappears into site- and landmark-dependent sparse results after peak pressure is removed; and sedation and robust setting leave-one-hospital-out directions reverse. These findings make unqualified pooling of the labels unsuitable in this snapshot.

The strongest supported claim is deliberately narrower than “the labels are documentation conventions”: their observed use and computable 12-hour mappings fail prespecified transportability gates in this selected eICU cohort. Differences in patient state, clinician intent, device semantics, staffing, or local workflow could also generate the pattern. Determining literal semantics and intended use requires hospital-level data dictionaries, clinician/abstractor adjudication, and external validation.

## Hypothesis and evidence boundary

### Hypothesis under test

Conditional on the frozen cohort and receipt of one of the two exact target labels, a transportable common phenotype should show:

- sufficiently broad within-hospital use of both labels to permit multicenter comparisons;
- limited dependence of label assignment on hospital;
- similar label-to-proxy mappings across hospitals and decision landmarks;
- stability to removal of peak inspiratory pressure, the dominant chart component;
- stable leave-one-hospital-out estimates;
- a post-D pattern distinct from trajectories already present before D; and
- independent outcome evidence adequate to validate the construct.

The falsifying alternative is concentration of label use by hospital together with heterogeneous, component-dependent, landmark-dependent, or leave-one-site-out-unstable operational mappings.

### Claims not tested or authorized

This audit does not establish actual spontaneous-breathing-trial delivery, daily evaluation, extubation, reintubation, ventilator liberation, bedside setting delivery, sedation interruption, appropriateness, treatment effect, quality of care, or patient benefit. A charted numeric change is a documentation proxy. A difference between hospitals is not itself proof of a local documentation convention. The 540 rows are not a denominator for all ventilated eICU patients, so label rates reported here are conditional label mix among frozen eligible target-labelled rows, not hospital prevalence among ventilated patients.

## Estimands and handling of small hospitals

### 1. Conditional label-use transportability

For each hospital h, estimate `P(evaluation label | frozen eligible target-labelled row, hospital h)`, exact label counts, and whether both labels occur. Report Jeffreys-binomial intervals for sparse hospitals only as regularized description; do not use them to manufacture overlap. Report:

- number of hospitals with both labels, at least 5 per label, and at least 10 per label;
- fraction of all 540 rows arising in hospitals with at least 10 per label;
- number and row fraction in single-label hospitals;
- hospital eta-squared for the binary label, weighted by observed hospital row counts;
- label-specific Herfindahl concentration, inverse-HHI effective hospital count, and top-one/top-three hospital shares.

All 74 hospitals contribute to label-use description. Hospital-specific outcome contrasts require at least ten outcome-available rows under each label. Smaller hospitals are displayed with counts/availability but are not pooled through unstable raw contrasts and are not relabelled as evidence of absence. The primary overlap set remains hospitals 248, 252, and 420; this is not a sample of a superpopulation of hospitals.

### 2. Operational mapping and heterogeneity

For each eligible hospital estimate the evaluation-minus-no-trial risk difference for:

- primary setting reduction: any inherited paired component reduction;
- robust setting reduction: the same composite excluding peak pressure;
- same-drug sedation-rate reduction; and
- the pre-D setting trajectory diagnostic.

Summarize fixed-effect inverse-variance heterogeneity using Cochran Q, descriptive I-squared, DerSimonian-Laird tau-squared, site range, and directional agreement. With only three eligible hospitals these statistics are diagnostics, not precise population heterogeneity estimates. The estimand is the observed conditional chart-proxy difference in these hospitals, not a causal average treatment effect.

### 3. Component dominance

Define `peak_only = primary setting reduction AND no reduction in FiO2, PEEP, pressure support, or set rate`. Report peak-only events as a share of all primary composite events by label and overlap hospital. Compare primary and no-peak hospital, landmark, and leave-one-out directions.

### 4. Cross-landmark and within-stay stability

Report conditional label mix at each landmark overall and within the three overlap hospitals. Report operational risk differences separately at all five landmarks without smoothing small strata. Among the 76 stays with at least two eligible landmarks, report adjacent-label persistence and both switching directions. Persistence can reflect repeated charting and switching can reflect appropriate clinical change; neither validates semantics. Their only purpose is to diagnose whether meaning appears stable across decision times.

### 5. Pre-D negative-control trajectory

The negative-control diagnostic is the inherited setting-reduction trajectory from `(D-1440,D-720]` to `(D-720,D]`. Report hospital-specific pre-D risk differences and, among rows observed before and after D, the hospital-specific difference between post-D and pre-D binary changes. This is not a no-treatment control and cannot establish exchangeability; a pre-D difference or site-reversing post-minus-pre pattern is adverse to interpreting the label as a newly initiated common action state.

### 6. Leave-one-hospital-out stability

Within hospitals 248, 252, and 420, omit each hospital in turn and re-standardize each outcome's hospital-specific risk differences by analyzed row count. Direction reversal is a failure of transportability, even if a pooled estimate appears nonzero.

## Frozen supportive, adverse, and inconclusive gates

The gates are semantic-transportability gates, not statistical-significance tests.

1. **Broad overlap**
   - Supportive: at least ten hospitals have at least ten frozen rows under each label and at least 50% of rows arise in those hospitals.
   - Adverse: fewer than five such hospitals or less than 50% of rows arise in them.
   - Inconclusive: five to nine hospitals meet the count while at least 50% of rows arise in them.
2. **Hospital-label dependence**
   - Supportive: hospital eta-squared is at most 0.10 and at most 20% of rows arise in single-label hospitals.
   - Adverse: eta-squared is at least 0.25 or at least 40% of rows arise in single-label hospitals.
   - Inconclusive: between these boundaries.
3. **Operational mapping**
   - Supportive: for both robust no-peak setting and sedation, at least five hospitals have ten outcome-available rows per label, hospital directions agree, I-squared is at most 25%, and leave-one-out direction never reverses.
   - Adverse: either robust outcome has fewer than three estimable hospitals, I-squared is at least 75%, site/leave-one-out direction reverses, or the primary setting direction/magnitude materially changes after peak pressure is removed.
   - Inconclusive: otherwise, especially when intervals are broad enough to include both stable and heterogeneous mappings.
4. **Cross-landmark stability**
   - Supportive: a robust outcome has one direction at all five landmarks and no landmark differs from its pooled risk difference by more than 10 percentage points.
   - Adverse: direction reverses and its landmark range exceeds 20 points.
   - Inconclusive: otherwise, including sparse event counts.
5. **Pre-D diagnostic**
   - Supportive for the literal positive operational hypothesis: the positive post-D robust contrast exceeds the absolute pre-D contrast by at least 5 points without site reversal.
   - Adverse: pre-D differences are comparable/larger or post-minus-pre reverses across sites.
   - Inconclusive: uncertainty includes both a 5-point positive shift and no shift.
6. **Independent validation**
   - Supportive: independent liberation markers occur in at least 10% under both labels in at least five overlap hospitals with adequate 48-hour observation.
   - Adverse: the completed parent ascertainment gates fail due to marker and observation sparsity.
   - Inconclusive: source absence cannot be distinguished from event absence.

Overall support for a common phenotype requires supportive broad-overlap and hospital-dependence gates plus no adverse operational, landmark, pre-D, or independent-validation gate. Any adverse gate blocks unqualified multicenter pooling. An overall adverse decision means “not validated as a common phenotype in this cohort,” not “proven to be a documentation artifact.”

## Executed results

The bounded execution was `[research job]`; it read all 540 cohort rows, all 540 frozen action rows, and all 1,620 endpoint-algorithm rows without sampling.

### Hospital dependence and overlap

The 540 rows span 74 hospitals. Only 18 hospitals contain both labels, four contain at least five of each, and only hospitals 248, 252, and 420 contain at least ten of each. Those three contain 303/540 rows (56.1%). Fifty-six hospitals are single-label hospitals, containing 116/540 rows (21.5%). Thus the broad-overlap gate is adverse because only three hospitals permit the prespecified comparison, despite their containing more than half of rows.

Hospital eta-squared for the binary label is 0.354, crossing the adverse hospital-dependence boundary. Concentration is especially strong for the no-trial label: its HHI is 0.245 (inverse-HHI effective count 4.08 hospitals), its largest hospital supplies 41.6%, and its top three supply 74.9%. Evaluation-label HHI is 0.078 (effective count 12.78), with top-one and top-three shares 19.1% and 42.1%. These are conditional concentration measures, not rates among all ventilated patients.

Overall evaluation-label mix across 24, 48, 72, 96, and 120 hours is 53.5%, 61.9%, 60.4%, 58.4%, and 48.7%. Within the overlap hospitals the same time does not imply the same label mix: for example, hospital 248 ranges from 14.3% to 47.1% evaluation, hospital 252 from 25.9% to 41.9%, and hospital 420 from 56.5% to 82.4% across landmarks. Among 97 adjacent pairs in 76 repeated stays, 69 (71.1%) retain the same label, with 14 evaluation-to-no-trial and 14 no-trial-to-evaluation transitions. This temporal pattern is descriptive and does not distinguish evolving clinical intent from repeated local chart conventions.

### Hospital heterogeneity and component dominance

The primary setting risk difference remains inverse in all three overlap hospitals: -17.6, -10.7, and -5.0 percentage points; diagnostic I-squared is 0%. On its own that could look operationally stable. It is not component-robust. In the overlap hospitals, peak-only events account for 34/39 (87.2%) evaluation composite events and 60/72 (83.3%) no-trial events. Across all hospitals they account for 63/91 (69.2%) and 69/85 (81.2%).

After peak pressure is removed, hospital risk differences are +2.3, -7.7, and +1.2 points, with direction reversal and I-squared 34.0%; the pooled overlap contrast is -3.1 points (95% Wald interval -8.2 to +2.1). Omitting hospital 252 changes the standardized no-peak contrast to +1.7 points, whereas omitting 248 or 420 gives -4.5 and -4.5 points. Therefore the only directionally uniform mapping is the peak-pressure-dominated composite, whose meaning as a delivered ventilator setting is not clinically adjudicated.

Sedation-rate hospital differences are -8.0, +18.3, and -6.3 points, I-squared 59.6%, and the pooled overlap contrast is +0.6 points (95% interval -9.8 to +11.1). Leave-one-out estimates are +10.1, -7.2, and +8.8 points depending on the omitted hospital. This is adverse directional instability with broad uncertainty, not a common operational mapping.

### Cross-landmark and pre-D diagnostics

The peak-dominated primary setting contrast reverses from -22.5, -11.8, -7.2, and -27.8 points at 24-96 hours to +12.4 points at 120 hours, a 40.2-point range. The no-peak contrasts are -7.5, +3.2, -5.1, -7.4, and +1.6 points; they also reverse but span only 10.7 points and contain very few events. Thus primary cross-landmark stability is adverse, while the robust no-peak landmark result is inconclusive rather than proof of heterogeneity.

Before D, hospital setting-trajectory differences are +11.8, +1.1, and -5.0 points. Among rows observed in both periods, hospital post-minus-pre differences are -27.5, -13.9, and +3.6 points; the pooled difference is -16.1 points (95% interval -32.7 to +0.4). The site reversal and lack of a distinct positive post-label shift meet the adverse negative-control criterion, while the broad intervals prevent a precise magnitude claim.

### Independent validation remains failed

The parent endpoint audit remains determinative: independent liberation markers and strict 48-hour observation do not satisfy validation gates. Stratifying the same failed marker artifact by hospital does not create validation; most sites have zero events, and only one independent event occurs in hospital 420 among the three overlap hospitals. No endpoint association, discharge bridge, or causal model is authorized.

## Gate decision and interpretation

- Frozen cohort, labels, D, and artifact joins: pass computationally.
- Broad multicenter overlap: adverse; only 3/74 hospitals have at least ten rows per label.
- Hospital-label dependence: adverse; eta-squared 0.354.
- Operational mapping: adverse; peak-pressure dominance, robust no-peak site/leave-one-out reversals, and sedation site/leave-one-out reversals.
- Cross-landmark stability: adverse for the peak-dominated primary composite; inconclusive for the sparse no-peak composite.
- Pre-D negative-control trajectory: adverse to a positive common action-state interpretation, with imprecise magnitude.
- Independent validation: adverse under the completed parent ascertainment audit.

The common multicenter-phenotype hypothesis is therefore not supported. For this frozen eICU cohort, the exact labels should not be pooled uncritically as a quality metric, implementation-fidelity endpoint, or transportable decision-support feature. If used at all, hospital identity, exact landmark, availability, and component semantics must remain explicit, and conclusions should be limited to documentation within this snapshot.

A stronger conclusion that the labels *are* hospital-specific conventions requires hospital data dictionaries, workflow/templating metadata, clinician or abstractor review of notes and care-plan interfaces, device-linked setting semantics, standardized drug units, and replication in another multicenter dataset containing the same literal labels. A claim about quality or patient benefit requires a separately designed clinical study with validated SBT, extubation/reintubation, and longitudinal outcomes.
