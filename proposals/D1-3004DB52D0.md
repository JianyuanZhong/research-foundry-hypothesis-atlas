# Early Post-TACE Hepatic Deterioration as a Prognostic Marker in HCC

## Question
In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who undergo transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does a composite measure of early hepatic deterioration during the first 7 days after the procedure predict subsequent hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days, after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and post-procedure observation intensity?

## Clinical importance
TACE is a common locoregional treatment for HCC, but a subset of patients experience early hepatic deterioration that may signal a need for escalation to systemic therapy or closer surveillance. Identifying these patients could inform treatment-selection decisions and resource allocation. The unresolved question is whether the early laboratory trajectory itself carries prognostic information beyond baseline liver function, or whether it merely reflects more intensive post-procedure monitoring.

## Evidence and closest prior work
- [K1] Decraecker et al. (2026) review the limitations of Child-Pugh scoring and the potential value of dynamic liver function monitoring and ALBI grade in HCC patients.
- [K2] Yang et al. (2026) describe the AAPS scoring system for predicting post-TACE outcomes, which uses baseline and early post-procedure variables.
- [K3] Tarbaghia et al. (2026) demonstrate that early hemodynamic response to dobutamine in septic shock predicts subsequent outcomes, providing a methodological precedent for using early physiological trajectories as prognostic markers.

## Data bindings
- **Source**: HCC dataset (12 source files)
- **Population**: 20,064 TACE patients
- **Early labs**: ~16,000 with complete early labs (bilirubin, albumin, INR)
- **Outcome events**: 817 with outcome-like diagnoses
- **Early hepatic deterioration composite**: bilirubin, albumin, INR changes over days 0–7 (ALT/AST excluded due to zero early rows)
- **90-day outcomes**: hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death
- **Adjustment**: baseline liver function, tumor burden, post-procedure observation intensity

## Analysis plan
1. **Primary analysis**: Logistic regression predicting 90-day outcomes from the early deterioration composite, adjusted for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and post-procedure observation intensity.
2. **Sensitivity analysis**: Repeat the primary analysis with alternative early deterioration windows (3-day, 5-day, 10-day) to assess robustness.
3. **Decision-curve analysis**: Evaluate the net benefit of using the early deterioration composite to guide escalation to systemic therapy at different threshold probabilities.

## Falsification criteria
- If the early deterioration composite does not improve prediction beyond baseline variables (AUC difference < 0.01), the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the prognostic signal is not robust to alternative windows (3-day, 5-day, 10-day), the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the decision-curve analysis shows no net benefit at any threshold probability, the hypothesis is not supported.

## Prognostic vs. causal distinction
This study estimates a **prognostic association** (early deterioration → subsequent outcomes) and does **NOT** estimate a causal effect of any treatment. The early deterioration composite is a prognostic marker, not a treatment. If the composite is used to inform treatment escalation, the prognostic model's clinical utility must be evaluated separately from causal treatment effects. A causal effect of systemic therapy on outcomes would require a different study design (e.g., randomized trial or causal inference methods).

## Sensitivity analysis on the 7-day window
The primary analysis uses a fixed 7-day window for the early deterioration composite. A sensitivity analysis tests alternative windows (3-day, 5-day, 10-day) to assess whether the prognostic signal is robust to the window choice.

For each alternative window:
1. Recalculate the early deterioration composite using the same variables (bilirubin, albumin, INR) but with the alternative window.
2. Repeat the primary logistic regression analysis.
3. Report the AUC, calibration, and decision-curve analysis for each window.
4. Compare the results across windows to assess robustness.

The sensitivity analysis will handle the resulting changes in sample size and event counts by reporting the number of patients with complete early labs and the number of outcome events for each window.

## Decision-curve analysis
A decision-curve analysis (DCA) evaluates the net benefit of using the early deterioration composite to guide escalation to systemic therapy at different threshold probabilities.

**Treatment strategy**: Escalate to systemic therapy if the predicted probability of 90-day outcomes > threshold.

**Comparison strategies**:
- Treat-all: Escalate to systemic therapy for all patients.
- Treat-none: Do not escalate to systemic therapy for any patient.

**Threshold probabilities to test**: 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50.

**Clinical context for each threshold**:
- 0.10: Low threshold, appropriate when the cost of missing a patient who would benefit from escalation is high.
- 0.20: Moderate threshold, appropriate when the cost of unnecessary escalation is moderate.
- 0.30: Higher threshold, appropriate when the cost of unnecessary escalation is high.
- 0.40: High threshold, appropriate when the cost of unnecessary escalation is very high.
- 0.50: Very high threshold, appropriate when the cost of unnecessary escalation is extremely high.

The DCA will report the net benefit for each threshold probability, comparing the treatment strategy to the treat-all and treat-none strategies.
