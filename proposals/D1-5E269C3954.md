# Prompt FiO2 de-escalation after confirmed hyperoxemia in invasively ventilated adults: a multicenter eICU target-trial emulation

## Decision and unresolved question

The clinical decision is whether to reduce inspired oxygen promptly after an arterial blood gas documents hyperoxemia in an adult who remains invasively ventilated. The unresolved claim is:

> Among invasively ventilated adults with a first PaO2 of 120–600 mm Hg while receiving FiO2 at least 0.40, a protocol-compatible absolute FiO2 reduction of at least 0.10 within 60 minutes, compared with no reduction of 0.05 or more within 60 minutes, reduces subsequent excess-oxygen exposure over hours 1–6 without a clinically important increase in hypoxemia.

This is an event-triggered, proximal effectiveness-and-safety hypothesis. It is not a claim that conservative oxygen targets reduce mortality.

### What is already supported

The strongest directly inspected external evidence supports two narrower claims. First, oxygen targets materially change achieved SpO2 and FiO2. In the 2,541-patient PILOT randomized trial, lower, intermediate, and higher SpO2 target groups had median patient-level SpO2 values of 94%, 95%, and 97% and median FiO2 values of 0.31, 0.37, and 0.45. Second, that trial detected no difference in ventilator-free days (medians 20, 21, and 21; P=0.81) or a clear 28-day mortality difference. It therefore does not establish an outcome benefit from a lower target. A current review also characterizes trial results and optimal targets as conflicting and highlights heterogeneous response. These facts motivate, but do not answer, the present event-level question.

The local data establish computability, not the hypothesis. A prior read-only feasibility pass found 420,762 validly parsed `paO2` rows in 85,769 stays, 3,075,308 respiratory-charting rows labeled `FiO2` in 75,234 stays, and 8,444 candidate first hyperoxemia events with an FiO2 observation in the following two hours; 6,396 had an observed absolute FiO2 decrease of at least 10 percentage points and 2,048 did not. That permissive screen did not enforce the final eligibility and treatment definitions and must not be reported as the final cohort. The final program must emit its own flow counts and label audit.

### Substantive advance

Global target trials answer whether assigning broad SpO2 ranges over an ICU course changes distal outcomes. They do not identify whether, after a clinician has objective evidence of excess arterial oxygen, a rapid and common bedside action actually reduces short-term excess exposure safely across heterogeneous hospitals. This study links a discrete, time-stamped trigger to a modifiable response and high-frequency physiologic follow-up, estimates both benefit and harm, and tests cross-hospital transportability. A supportive result would justify a prospective alert/protocol trial; an adverse result would warn against automatic de-escalation; an inconclusive result would define the required sample and measurements.

## Target trial specification

### Population and time zero

Unit is the ICU stay. Use only the first qualifying event per `patientunitstayid`; a sensitivity analysis uses only the first ICU stay per `uniquepid`.

Eligibility:

1. Adult age at least 18; map `age='> 89'` to 90 only for adjustment and include it as adult.
2. An active invasive-ventilation interval at the event, operationalized from `respiratoryCare`: `ventstartoffset <= labresultoffset < ventendoffset`, plus an artificial airway (`airwaytype` in Oral ETT, Nasal ETT, Tracheostomy, Double-Lumen Tube, or Cricothyrotomy) documented in an overlapping respiratory-care record. The main analysis permits an artificial-airway record anywhere in the same active ventilation episode; a strict sensitivity analysis requires it on the overlapping row. Require corroborating PEEP or ventilator-mode charting within ±120 minutes and publish the exact accepted label list before outcome modeling.
3. First `lab.labname` equal to `paO2` case-insensitively, numeric `labresult` 120–600 mm Hg, at offset at least 0. Values above 600 are treated as implausible, not winsorized.
4. A valid contemporaneous FiO2 at least 40%: the closest eligible chart at or before the trigger in [-30,0] minutes; if none, the closest in (0,+15] minutes. FiO2 labels are limited to `FiO2`, `FIO2 (%)`, and `Set Fraction of Inspired Oxygen (FIO2)`. Parse numeric values 0.21–1.00 as fractions multiplied by 100 and 21–100 as percentages; reject all others.
5. ICU stay has not ended at the trigger. Do not require future survival, continued ventilation, outcome measurement, or a future FiO2 chart for initial eligibility.

Time zero `t0` is `labresultoffset`, minutes from ICU admission. Because eICU cannot prove whether this is specimen, result-entry, or clinician-notification time, the tested exposure is response after the recorded result, not response after confirmed clinician awareness. Report the empirical lag between `labresultoffset` and `labresultrevisedoffset`; do not substitute the revised time.

Exclude only invalid age/time/value records and an already-ended ventilation interval. Do not silently exclude neurologic injury, cardiac arrest, sepsis, COPD, or ARDS; these are prespecified strata because their oxygen goals may differ. Diagnosis-derived strata may use only rows with `diagnosisoffset <= t0`, with code/string lists frozen and published.

### Strategies and grace period

Create two clones at t0 and follow a 60-minute grace period.

- Prompt de-escalation: by minute 60, a valid FiO2 is at least 10 absolute percentage points below baseline; the last valid FiO2 in minutes 45–60 must remain at least 10 points below baseline. A transient isolated low entry followed by reversal does not qualify.
- No prompt de-escalation: no valid FiO2 during (0,60] is 5 or more absolute points below baseline and the last valid value in minutes 45–60 remains within 5 points of baseline.
- Values between these definitions, no follow-up FiO2, or oscillatory patterns adhere to neither complete strategy. Clone-censor each clone when its strategy becomes impossible. Do not drop these stays before cloning.

Within each 15-minute grace-period bin, estimate adherence/censoring probabilities using information available through the previous bin: latest and slope of SpO2, FiO2, PEEP, heart rate, respiratory rate, invasive/noninvasive MAP, pH/PaCO2, and vasopressor infusion status. This aligns treatment, confounder, and eligibility times and addresses the fact that evolving hypoxemia or shock can appropriately prevent de-escalation. Stabilized inverse-probability-of-censoring weights are multiplied across bins and truncated at the prespecified 1st/99th percentiles. Report effective sample size, maximum weight, adherence counts, and positivity by hospital. If either strategy has weighted effective N <500 overall, fewer than 10 hospitals with at least 10 adherent patients per arm, or >5% of weights exceed 10 before truncation, the causal contrast is declared positivity-limited and cannot be supportive.

### Follow-up and outcomes

Outcome follow-up begins at t0+60 and ends at t0+360 minutes.

Primary effectiveness outcome: time-weighted proportion of observed ventilated follow-up with `vitalPeriodic.sao2 >96`. Values outside 50–100 are invalid. Each 5-minute monitor median carries forward to the next observation for at most 10 minutes; uncovered time is missing. The main endpoint requires at least 180 covered minutes, but initial eligibility does not. Use inverse-probability-of-observation weighting based on pre-outcome chart density, hospital, severity, and strategy; also report complete-case and all-available-bin analyses.

Key safety outcome: risk of sustained hypoxemia, defined as at least two consecutive 5-minute `sao2 <90` observations spanning at least 10 minutes during hours 1–6. Additional safety outcomes are proportion of covered time below 90%, any `sao2 <85`, and minimum SpO2.

Secondary mechanistic outcomes are mean FiO2, absolute FiO2 reduction at 6 hours, repeat PaO2 >100 and >120 mm Hg among those remeasured, and repeat-ABG frequency. Extubation, ICU discharge, and death during six hours are reported and handled with IPC weighting; they are not coded as favorable oxygen values. Exploratory unit mortality, hospital mortality, `actualventdays`, and ICU length of stay are reported only as associations, with no mortality-benefit conclusion.

### Estimand and analysis

Primary estimand is the per-protocol average treatment effect among all eligible clones: adjusted mean difference (prompt minus no-prompt) in high-saturation burden over hours 1–6. The safety estimand is the adjusted risk difference for sustained hypoxemia. Fit weighted outcome models with flexible splines and prespecified interactions, hospital fixed intercepts, and patient-clustered robust standard errors; use a doubly robust augmented-IPW estimator as primary. Bootstrap the entire stay-level pipeline 1,000 times, stratified by hospital; report percentile 95% confidence intervals.

Confounders fixed at/before t0 are age, sex, ethnicity, hospital, unit type/source, admission source, hours since ICU admission, baseline PaO2 and FiO2, prior-hour SpO2 median/minimum/slope and measurement count, last PEEP, pH, PaCO2, lactate, hemoglobin, creatinine, heart rate, respiratory rate, invasive/noninvasive MAP, vasopressor indicators/rates, and pre-t0 diagnosis strata. Untimestamped APACHE variables and APACHE outcome predictions may describe the cohort but are excluded from the primary propensity/adherence model because they can incorporate information after an early t0. A sensitivity analysis restricted to t0 after hour 24 may adjust `apacheApsVar` and `apachePatientResult.apachescore`.

Baselines that the proposed estimator must beat in credibility and stability are: unadjusted arm means; covariate-adjusted regression without weights; propensity overlap weighting at the 60-minute landmark; and within-hospital estimates meta-analyzed with random effects. Report absolute standardized differences before/after weighting, overlap plots, hospital-specific estimates, leave-one-hospital-out estimates, and I²/tau². No predictive accuracy metric substitutes for these estimands.

Prespecified effect-modification analyses are baseline PaO2 120–149 versus at least 150, FiO2 40–59 versus at least 60, PF ratio categories when contemporaneous PaO2/FiO2 is valid, neurologic injury/cardiac arrest, COPD, sepsis/ARDS, vasopressor use, and hospital. These are interaction estimates with multiplicity-controlled false-discovery rate and are not treatment rules.

### Falsification and interpretation

The hypothesis is supported only if all hold:

1. adjusted high-saturation burden difference is negative with a two-sided 95% CI excluding 0 and magnitude at least -0.10;
2. sustained-hypoxemia risk is noninferior with the upper 95% CI for risk difference below +0.03;
3. the upper 95% CI for difference in time below SpO2 90% is below +0.01;
4. positivity requirements hold, weighting achieves all absolute standardized differences <0.10, and direction is unchanged in strict ventilation, first-patient-stay, PaO2 ≥150, complete-case, and leave-one-hospital-out analyses.

A result is adverse if prompt de-escalation has a higher sustained-hypoxemia risk with a 95% CI excluding 0, or if high-saturation burden is not reduced and any safety endpoint worsens. Such a result argues against an automatic protocol in the represented population; it does not prove all FiO2 reductions are harmful.

A result is inconclusive if effectiveness crosses 0 or is smaller than 0.10, a safety CI crosses a noninferiority margin, positivity/balance fails, hospital effects materially reverse, or missing coverage differs by >10 percentage points after weighting. Failure to prove noninferiority is inconclusive, not proof of harm. A precisely null effectiveness estimate with adequate overlap falsifies the proposed operational benefit.

Falsification/diagnostic tests:

- Future exposure must not predict pre-trigger (-360 to -60 minute) SpO2 >96 burden after adjustment; an absolute standardized residual difference ≥0.10 flags residual confounding.
- Repeat with pseudo-triggers six hours before eligible ABGs in stays with adequate data; a comparable “effect” flags secular trajectory bias.
- Require consistent direction using PaO2 thresholds 100, 120, and 150 and FiO2 reductions 5, 10, and 15 points, while retaining 120/10 as primary.
- Quantify differential SpO2/FiO2 chart frequency and invalid-value rates by strategy and hospital.
- The manipulation check must show lower measured FiO2 during hours 1–6; absent separation makes clinical outcome interpretation inconclusive.

## Required computation products and verification

Write only derived artifacts to the workspace:

1. `source_manifest.json`: source path, SHA-256, parser options, schema columns, row counts.
2. `label_audit.csv`: every lab/respiratory/drug label considered, raw/valid/invalid counts and normalization rule.
3. `cohort_flow.csv`: counts after each criterion, unique stays/patients/hospitals, plus exclusion reasons.
4. `analysis_dataset.parquet`: one row per clone/bin plus a data dictionary; identifiers remain private.
5. `balance.csv`, `positivity.json`, `missingness.csv`, `hospital_effects.csv`, `sensitivity.csv`.
6. `estimands.json`: point estimates, 95% CIs, margins, effective N, and machine-readable interpretation gates.
7. `report.md`: every substantive sentence linked to a row/key in computed outputs; software versions and random seed.

Automated verification can check hashes, joins, one-trigger-per-stay logic, temporal leakage, FiO2 normalization, clone/censor logic, outcome windows, bootstrap reproducibility, effect/CI arithmetic, and whether the reported label (supportive/adverse/inconclusive) follows the gates. It must include fixtures where (a) effectiveness is favorable but safety crosses its margin, (b) effectiveness is null, (c) hypoxemia is significantly worse, and (d) numerical results are supportive but the narrative claims mortality benefit; only the correctly bounded interpretations pass.

No automatic verifier can establish invasive-ventilation truth in ambiguous charting, clinician awareness of the ABG, correctness of diagnosis phenotypes, pulse-oximeter accuracy across skin pigmentation, adequacy of the safety margins, exchangeability, or a treatment recommendation. These require respiratory/critical-care review, manual adjudication of a blinded sample, and ultimately a prospective multicenter trial. A mortality, neurologic, or long-term benefit requires another study with adequately powered randomized assignment and post-discharge outcomes.
