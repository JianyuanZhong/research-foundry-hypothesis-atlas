# Repeat-TACE pre-event state as an incremental predictor of the next observed hepatic panel

## Decision and substantive advance

Publish this child as a sharper alternative to the two chosen parents. The key advance is to stop calling a change score `P-B` a vulnerability or a predictor of post-repeat perturbation. The primary estimand is instead an explicitly prognostic, incremental-prediction estimand: among patients already selected for a recorded first repeat TACE and for whom all three panels are observed, does the pre-repeat assay level `P` improve prediction of the next observed same-assay level `Y` beyond the pre-TACE2 baseline `B` and prespecified pre-TACE2 covariates? `P-B` is retained only as a descriptive trajectory display and a secondary sensitivity, never as the primary predictor.

This distinction matters because a change-on-change analysis can be driven by mathematical coupling, measurement error, and regression to the mean. A strong association of `P-B` with `Y-P` would not establish an acute repeat-TACE effect. The proposed nested prediction comparison asks a narrower and computable question: whether the contemporaneous pre-repeat state carries reproducible information about the subsequent observed panel beyond the earlier baseline. A positive result supports short-horizon prognostic information in this selected observation process, not toxicity, recovery, treatment response, safety, benefit, or causation. A null result can show that the pre-repeat state adds little predictive information under this exact clock; it cannot show absence of hepatic biology outside observed panels.

## Evidence versus unresolved claim

The complete-source audit already supports, but does not estimate an outcome association, the following facts: the frozen early pathway cohort contains 319 systemic-record and 1,491 TACE2-only patients; first strict repeat TACE on TACE2 days 15–90 occurs in 159/319 and 526/1,491; assay-specific complete triplets are albumin 40/118 and total bilirubin 41/119 (systemic/comparator); common-timestamp triplets are 40/118; and assay-specific timing adds no albumin triplets and only one bilirubin triplet per arm relative to common timestamps. These are availability and timing facts, not evidence that either pathway changes laboratories or that repeat TACE causes a post-event change.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is: conditional on the frozen pathway, a recorded repeat event, and observation of the complete triplet, the exact pre-repeat level contains incremental information about the next observed assay level beyond the pre-TACE2 level and measured baseline context. The direction is not assumed. A pathway difference in this incremental information is exploratory and is not the primary claim.

## Primary estimand and analysis

For each assay `a` separately, define the target as the incremental predictive value of `P_a` for `Y_a` in the observed complete-triplet, repeat-event population. Fit two prespecified nested models with the same folds and baseline covariates:

```
M0: Y_a = alpha0 + alphaB B_a + alphaA A + theta'Z + error
M1: Y_a = alpha0 + alphaB B_a + alphaP P_a + alphaA A + theta'Z + error
```

`A=1` is the frozen systemic-record arm, `A=0` the TACE2-only arm. `Z` is restricted to variables available before panel observation: age, sex, TACE1–TACE2 interval, entry year/month, latest pre-index department, prior encounter/procedure counts and procedures, encounter-timed baseline disease/observation proxies, closest pre-TACE2 albumin/bilirubin/INR/platelet/creatinine/sodium with missingness indicators, and pre-TACE2 values/timing. Do not include `Y`, post-repeat medication, post-event information, or any selection variable generated by the panels. Keep the model parsimonious given 40/41 systemic triplets; prespecify the covariate reduction rule from parent design (weighted absolute SMD >0.10) or use a locked compact core if the compiler cannot support the larger set.

The primary estimand is the out-of-fold improvement in prediction from M0 to M1, reported separately for albumin and bilirubin as change in cross-fitted RMSE and MAE, cross-fitted R-squared increment, calibration slope/intercept, and 95% bootstrap or HC3-compatible uncertainty. Also report the partial `alphaP` association with HC3 interval, but do not equate a statistically nonzero coefficient with clinically useful prediction. Because raw units are unavailable, prediction improvement is reported in native database scale with unit caveat and as a unit-invariant standardized incremental performance (`RMSE/SD(Y)` and `R²` increment). Do not combine assays or invent clinically meaningful thresholds.

Use a fixed five-fold cross-fitting scheme stratified by arm where feasible and frozen before outcome inspection. Each training fold must fit preprocessing, covariate handling, and coefficients; evaluate only in its held-out fold. With sparse arms, report fold-level support and do not claim reliable generalization if any held-out fold has fewer than five observations in either arm or if a fold's outcome variance is zero. A simple linear model is primary; no machine-learning outcome model is needed or permitted as a substitute.

Use baseline-overlap weighting as a sensitivity, not to manufacture an all-cohort estimand. Separately model repeat-event selection among all 1,810 frozen-arm patients and complete-triplet observation among selected repeat events using only pre-TACE2/event-independent information. Report unweighted complete-triplet results first, then cross-fitted overlap/inverse-selection weighted results, positivity, balance, weight concentration, and arm-specific ESS. Weighting diagnoses differential selection; it does not identify the unobserved or all-frozen-population prediction performance and cannot remove collider bias from conditioning on repeat TACE and panel observation.

The explicit trajectory quantities `D=P-B` and `Q=Y-P` are secondary diagnostics. Report correlations and a baseline-adjusted descriptive comparison, but never use `D` as a predictor of `Q` as the primary test. If desired, fit the algebraically nonredundant level model `Y ~ B + P + A` as the confirmatory form; explain that `P-B` is a linear re-expression, while `Y-P` introduces coupling and is not a primary outcome. The central interpretation is persistence/incremental prediction, not immediate procedural perturbation.

## Regression-to-mean and event/observation falsification

The principal regression-to-mean safeguard is comparison of M1 against M0: baseline `B` is retained in both models, and the incremental predictor is the observed pre-event level `P`, not a noisy change score. Stratify or interact descriptively by baseline quintile/standardized baseline and report whether the incremental gain is concentrated among extreme B values. Run a locked measurement-error sensitivity using plausible reliability values only if laboratory duplicate data support them; otherwise state that reliability is unavailable. Do not infer improvement or deterioration from `P-B` alone.

Falsify a claimed event-specific acute or vulnerability interpretation through these locked checks:

1. **Pre-event negative control:** use `P` as the outcome and `B` as predictor in the same selected panel population, with the same baseline/context adjustment. If an arm contrast or apparent “gain” is already present in the pre-event relation, the post-event result cannot be attributed to repeat-TACE context and is reported as serial persistence/selection, not event-linked signal.
2. **Timing stability:** repeat M0/M1 under all locked pre/post timing selectors, first-versus-latest panel choices, and common-timestamp pairing. Reversal or reliance on a single window makes the result unstable.
3. **Selection sensitivity:** compare complete-case, baseline-overlap, and cross-fitted repeat-event-plus-observation weighted estimates. Material change, poor positivity, or arm-specific ESS failure makes inference selection-sensitive. Passing does not remove unmeasured retreatment rationale or conditioning bias.
4. **Post-panel exposure context:** report any later medication/procedure records descriptively, but never adjust for them as confounders of `Y`; they are post-outcome/selection context. Excluding events with a matched non-bevacizumab systemic record between TACE2 and repeat TACE is a locked sensitivity only.
5. **Permutation/placebo clock:** where the event encounter contains enough pre-event observations, assign a pseudo-post clock at a pre-event timestamp using only panels strictly before the true event, and repeat the same incremental comparison. This is a diagnostic for temporal specificity, not a replacement outcome; if a comparable gain appears before the procedure, the post-event claim is weakened to serial measurement predictability. If support is insufficient, mark this check not estimable rather than changing the population.
6. **Influence and optimism:** report leave-one-out influence, fold-level performance, bootstrap optimism correction, leverage/Cook/DFBETA for `alphaP`, and outcome-independent 1st/99th percentile weight sensitivity. A result driven by a few triplets is inconclusive.

Do not label the post-repeat panel “recovery,” “toxicity,” “perturbation,” “response,” or “safety” because the source does not adjudicate fluid/albumin administration, acute illness, complications, or technical success and has no validated units.

## Prespecified result branches

- **Supportive incremental prediction:** M1 improves cross-fitted standardized RMSE/MAE or R-squared over M0 for an assay, with calibration improvement, non-dominant influence, stable direction/size across locked selectors and weighting, adequate overlap/ESS, and no comparable pre-event placebo gain. Conclude only that the pre-repeat level contains reproducible short-horizon predictive information about the next observed same-assay panel among selected, observed repeat-TACE episodes. This is not evidence of a treatment effect, acute TACE perturbation, safety, benefit, or clinically actionable threshold.
- **Adverse to the proposed interpretation:** M1 adds no information, is less stable than M0, or the gain disappears after baseline/selection adjustment; or a comparable pre-event placebo gain, baseline-extreme concentration, timing dependence, or influence concentration appears. Conclude that apparent deterioration/prediction is compatible with regression to the mean, serial persistence, measurement process, or selection, and do not call it event-specific.
- **Selection-sensitive:** performance or `alphaP` materially changes under repeat-event/observation weighting, overlap fails, or event/panel probabilities are poorly calibrated. Report selected-observation dependence and no robust pathway inference.
- **Null with adequate precision:** M1–M0 confidence intervals exclude a prespecified small standardized predictive improvement chosen before outcome inspection, with all computational gates passing. Conclude no detectable incremental prediction of that magnitude in this selected cohort, not equivalence and not absence of hepatic change. Because no clinical unit or validated minimal important difference exists, do not call the null clinically unimportant.
- **Inconclusive:** fewer than the audited complete-triplet counts, failed reconstruction/clocks/joins, sparse fold support, broad intervals, unstable weights, influence dependence, missing unit validation, or non-estimable placebo/timing checks. Preserve the negative or unavailable finding; do not widen windows or substitute a more favorable endpoint.
- **Pathway modification:** only exploratory. Report arm-specific incremental performance and `P×A` interaction descriptively. Do not promote pathway modification unless both assays show stable, same-direction, cross-validated interaction and all support/overlap/influence/timing gates pass; with 40/41 systemic triplets, the likely result is inconclusive. Even a passed interaction is an association with a recorded pathway, not a systemic-treatment effect.

## Evidence limits and required additional data

The HCC source has no laboratory unit field, validated analyzer/reference ranges, reliable outpatient/inpatient assay indicator, adjudicated acute illness, transfusion or exogenous albumin/fluid balance, imaging/tumor burden or response, performance status, complete Child-Pugh context, repeat-TACE rationale, technical details or complications, verified medication administration/adherence/dose, outside-care capture, or reliable mortality. Diagnoses have no native timestamps and lexical matching is not expert adjudication. Conditioning on a recorded repeat TACE and a measured panel is post-baseline selection and may introduce collider bias through disease status, access, clinician decisions, and observation/survival. The +14 institutional-observation rule is not proof of survival.

Clinical interpretation of units, thresholds, retreatment appropriateness, toxicity, recovery, benefit, safety, response, and mechanism requires laboratory metadata and expert record/imaging/procedure adjudication. Causal treatment or policy conclusions require a separate longitudinal design with verified exposure, time-varying severity and decision covariates, repeated untreated comparators, and clinically meaningful adjudicated outcomes.

An automatic verifier can check hashes, row counts, frozen 319/1,491 arms, 159/526 events, 40/118 and 41/119 triplet counts, exact composite joins and clocks, parsing and duplicate handling, fold construction, nested-model computation, cross-fitted performance, uncertainty, weighting, timing/placebo diagnostics, and whether conclusions follow the result branch. It cannot establish clinical importance, causality, administration, toxicity, recovery, safety, benefit, or why repeat TACE occurred.
