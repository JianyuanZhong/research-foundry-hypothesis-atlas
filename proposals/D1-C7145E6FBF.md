# Does discordant AKI recovery add prognostic information beyond observation intensity?

## Unresolved question, clinical value, and evidence boundary

Serum creatinine may fall during critical illness because filtration improves, but also because muscle loss reduces creatinine generation or fluid accumulation dilutes serum concentration. Urine output offers a partly independent signal, but it is affected by diuretics, fluids, catheter practice, and incomplete charting. The unresolved question is whether apparent creatinine improvement paired with documented persistent oliguria at a fixed post-AKI landmark identifies a higher subsequent mortality risk than concordant improvement, beyond measured severity, fluid/treatment proxies, and observation intensity. Clinically, this could determine whether creatinine decline alone is an adequate retrospective marker of recovery and whether a prospective validation study with protocolized urine capture and direct renal-function measurement is justified. It is not a treatment-effect question.

**Already supported:** in a small prospective ICU cohort with iohexol comparison, creatinine-based eGFR overestimated measured kidney function as critical illness lengthened [K1]. PICARD found fluid accumulation associated with delayed creatinine-based AKI recognition and lower urine output, but observational data do not settle causal direction [K2]. A recent Research Square v1 preprint reports an association between the number of positive creatinine/urine-output AKI criteria at a 24-hour ICU landmark and subsequent severe kidney progression in cirrhosis patients in MIMIC-IV/eICU; denominator and ascertainment affected estimates [K3]. These are motivation and boundaries, not evidence that the hypothesis below is true.

**Hypothesis to test:** Among adults with incident creatinine-defined AKI who are alive, still in the ICU, and not receiving KRT at 48 hours after AKI onset, an observed creatinine decline paired with validly measured persistent oliguria during the preceding 24 hours is associated with greater conditional 7-day in-hospital death risk than concordant improvement, after accounting for pre-landmark severity, treatment/fluid proxies, and measurement intensity.

The strongest rival is severity and observation artifact: severe illness drives more testing and charting, fluid accumulation can dilute creatinine, diuretics alter output, urine events may be incomplete or semantically ambiguous, and landmark selection conditions on survival and ICU retention. Attenuation under measured severity/fluid adjustment, or concentration in poorly observed patients, favors this rival. A residual association is still not a renal mechanism or causal effect. The 48-hour survivor/in-ICU estimand excludes earlier death and discharge by definition.

## Population, index, and temporal design

Use MIMIC-IV 3.1 read-only sources only. Include adults (age >=18 based on `hosp/patients.anchor_age`; values above 89 are represented as 91) with a first ICU stay per hospital admission and a first incident creatinine-defined AKI episode during that ICU stay. AKI onset is the first qualifying KDIGO-compatible serum-creatinine rise: at least 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours or at least 1.5 times the lowest available preceding value within 7 days, using only measurements available at the event time. This is a creatinine-defined cohort, not complete KDIGO staging where urine capture is insufficient.

Index time is AKI onset. Landmark is onset +48 hours. Include only patients alive, still in ICU, and not on KRT at that landmark. Classify exposure from the 24 hours immediately before the landmark. Retain the parent's prespecified creatinine-decline and oliguria thresholds/duration unchanged; do not tune from outcomes. The diagnostic design groups retain discordant improvement, concordant improvement (reference), and other prespecified recovery patterns as separate groups rather than collapsing unlike states. Oliguria follows the parent/KDIGO-compatible rate/duration definition only if item, interval, weight and coverage gates below pass. If the parent's numerical coverage cutoff or change threshold is not available as a binding value to the solver, the cohort cannot be fit until those values and the clinically negligible risk-difference margin are frozen in an analysis configuration before outcome access. Do not invent a cutoff or replace it after seeing results.

Follow-up begins at the landmark and ends at the earliest of in-hospital death, live hospital discharge, 7 days, or last valid observed follow-up. Primary endpoint is 7-day in-hospital death cumulative incidence; live discharge is a competing event, not an ordinary control. Death/discharge time fields define events; `hospital_expire_flag` is a cross-check, not a replacement for event time. KRT is secondary only after a separately validated procedure/code mapping; it is not part of the primary endpoint. Report full flow from all ICU AKI onsets through deaths, discharges, KRT, missing measurements, failed exposure gates, landmark eligibility, and analysis inclusion.

## Measurement gate, analysis, and competing methods

Before opening outcome associations, freeze a clinically reviewed exposure configuration: approved urine item whitelist; interval-value meaning (instantaneous vs per-chart interval vs cumulative/revised); duplicate/overlap handling; unit conversion; a contemporaneous weight rule; the parent's unchanged duration/rate/coverage threshold; and a clinically negligible risk-difference margin. Use `charttime` for event ordering, not `storetime` as interval duration. Do not forward-fill gaps, count absent rows as zero, or select a threshold from outcome results. If intervals or adequate coverage cannot be defended or weight is unavailable, those exposure windows remain unclassifiable and the oliguria hypothesis is infeasible in this source. Report coverage flow and missingness before the outcome model. The source table schema itself cannot clinically adjudicate these semantics; clinical/nephrology review is required. No cohort association has been computed in discovery.

**Baseline:** Fit prespecified discrete-time cause-specific logistic hazards on seven daily post-landmark intervals, separate hazards for death and live discharge, with exposure group and mapped pre-landmark covariates (age/sex, creatinine baseline/trajectory, measured severity, validated fluid/diuretic proxies, output/lab/vital counts, lags and recording delays). No post-landmark predictors or data-driven feature selection. Derive seven-day death cumulative incidence with discharge competing; report adjusted risks, discordant-vs-concordant risk difference and ratio with confidence intervals. If event counts cannot support terms, report instability/no estimable contrast rather than changing exposure groups.

**Substantive alternative:** Fit a compact GRU on the same eligible population and outcome, using only pre-landmark 48h in eight six-hour bins: validated creatinine/urine summaries, explicit missingness/coverage masks and time-since-last-measurement, mapped pre-landmark vitals/severity, and validated input/fluid/medication summaries. Predict separate daily death/discharge hazards. No imputation across unobserved urine intervals or post-landmark leakage. It can test whether sequence order/co-evolution or the observation masks are informative under the severity/measurement rival, which one-row regression summaries lose; it does not confer mechanism. Compare the GRU against the regression on patient-level held-out data using identical cohort/split: competing-risk integrated Brier score, calibration, exposure-group cumulative incidence contrasts and bootstrap intervals. Preserve a final untouched subject-ID test set, tune only within development patients, and bootstrap by patient. Falsification checks include adjustment for and stratification by pre-landmark measurement counts/lags; high-coverage-only versus full classifiable cohort; comparison with measurement-intensity markers; and whether the discordant contrast is confined to late/irregular charting. A model improvement alone is not scientific support if the exposure association disappears or is mask-driven.

## Results that would and would not support the hypothesis

Support requires valid preregistered output/weight/coverage gates, adequate landmark flow and event support, a discordant-vs-concordant adjusted mortality difference in the prespecified adverse direction whose uncertainty excludes the predeclared negligible range, and consistency in high-coverage and measurement-intensity sensitivity checks. It would establish only a conditional prognostic association in this retrospective MIMIC landmark population and motivate independent prospective validation; not physiological recovery, treatment benefit, causal mechanisms or a new bedside rule.

Adverse evidence is a precise estimate excluding a clinically material excess, or an opposite association, with valid measurement gates; that would weigh against the prognostic hypothesis for this selected population. Attenuation after measured severity/fluid/observation adjustment or concentration in low-quality chart windows favors the rival, though residual confounding remains. A wide interval spanning important excess and no excess, sparse events, poor overlap, unstable fits, or failed measurement gates is inconclusive, not refutation. Clinical reviewers set the negligible-effect margin before outcomes. Computational checks can verify joins, temporal order, code, counts, estimates, uncertainty and whether interpretation matches those outputs. Only expert review or another study can validate clinical urine semantics, true GFR, unmeasured severity, causality or treatment utility; a prospective protocolized urine/fluid/direct-function study and independent validation would be needed for stronger claims.
