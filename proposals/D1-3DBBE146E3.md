# Residual instability before ICU-to-ward transfer: event-time and competing-state stress test

Status: proposed substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. No prediction model has been fit and no hypothesis result is claimed. The fixed adult first ICU-to-general-ward transfer population, `t0`, final-24-hour exposure, 48-hour primary target, decision-time availability rules, exact MIMIC-IV 3.1 bindings, subject split, and S0-versus-T1 core estimand are unchanged.

## Why this child is warranted

The parent correctly treats live hospital discharge as a competing state and requires component reporting, but its model comparison remains primarily a fixed-horizon classification. That can answer whether T1 improves 48-hour prediction over S0; it cannot show when predicted risk accrues, whether a gain concerns ICU return/death rather than the operational discharge transition, or whether the fixed class is distorted by incompatible event chronology.

A bounded exact-cohort audit now establishes that this is consequential rather than hypothetical. Among the frozen 49,157 transfers, the first observed state by 48 hours was ICU return for 1,999 (4.067%), death for 586 (1.192%), live discharge for 9,950 (20.241%), and continued hospitalization without event for 36,622 (74.500%). Only 3,312 (6.738%) were discharged by 24 hours, but 9,950 were discharged by 48 hours. ICU-return/death first transitions accumulated from 499 by 6 hours to 827 by 12 hours, 1,550 by 24 hours, 2,059 by 36 hours, and 2,585 by 48 hours. Thus discharge is common, strongly time-dependent, and capable of changing the meaning of a fixed 48-hour risk.

The audit also reconciled the parent's 2,607 raw composite events: 2,585 were first ICU-return/death transitions, while 22 records had a recorded live discharge earlier than a later same-admission ICU return or death within 48 hours. These 22 are chronology conflicts for an absorbing-discharge multistate representation; they are not evidence of post-discharge safety or failure. The fixed raw target remains unchanged, and the conflicts become a mandatory sensitivity rather than being silently relabeled.

## Evidence boundary, rival explanation, and hypotheses

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. A MIMIC-IV/external-hospital study found that a longitudinal GRU-D++ model predicted seven-day ICU readmission/death better than SWIFT, while explicitly identifying operational factors, DNR status, and distinct composite-component trajectories as unresolved limitations [K1]. A systematic review found wide variation in ICU-readmission windows and composite definitions, despite advantages from longitudinal predictors and clearly specified populations/outcomes [K2]. A large EHR study showed that measurement timing and frequency encode healthcare processes as well as patient state [K3].

None establishes that final-24-hour trajectory values add decision-time information about the timing and cause of first post-transfer transitions; that a fixed 48-hour gain is attributable to ICU-return/death prediction rather than live-discharge selection; or that any prediction identifies mechanism or intervention benefit.

**Frozen primary hypothesis.** In the parent's fixed cohort and information set, T1's prespecified value-trajectory features improve held-out 48-hour prediction over S0 after enforcing decision-time availability, and measured-value content adds information beyond the observation process. The primary paired estimand remains `Brier(S0)-Brier(T1)`.

**Event-time stress-test hypothesis.** If T1 captures clinically relevant information about observed post-transfer deterioration rather than mainly operational selection, then relative to an S0-matched discrete-time model, a T1-matched discrete-time model will improve calibrated cumulative-incidence prediction for first ICU return or death over 48 hours, with interpretable timing, and the gain will not be explained solely by improved prediction of live hospital discharge.

The strongest rival is operational selection: the same transfer context, destination, service, treatment limitation, charting process, and post-transfer discharge process may determine how long a patient remains observable and whether instability manifests as ICU return, death, or discharge. This rival predicts substantial discharge-hazard signal, destination/service heterogeneity, and possibly a fixed-horizon T1 gain that is not corroborated by time-integrated ICU-return/death prediction. The rival is descriptive, not causal; adjustment does not identify discharge intent or ward-monitoring effects.

## Outcomes and chronology contract

### Frozen fixed-horizon target

Preserve the parent's raw 48-hour outcome:

- deterioration if any same-admission later ICU `intime` or `admissions.deathtime` lies in `(t0,t0+48h]`;
- live hospital discharge by 48 hours without the raw deterioration label;
- otherwise alive and in hospital without deterioration at 48 hours.

S0 versus T1 on this target remains primary so this child does not move the scientific goal after seeing chronology.

### Time-resolved first-transition target

Create eight consecutive six-hour intervals over `(t0,t0+48h]`. Every person begins in state W: alive, in hospital, and without post-`t0` ICU return. At each interval, the first transition is one of:

- W→R: first later same-admission ICU-stay `icustays.intime`;
- W→D: `admissions.deathtime`;
- W→L: live `admissions.dischtime`;
- W→W: no transition in that interval.

R, D, and L are absorbing for this **first-transition** analysis. If multiple endpoints occur, use the earliest exact timestamp. Exact ties are resolved by a frozen rule before fitting: ICU return before death before live discharge, with tie counts reported. The feasibility audit found no ICU/death or live-discharge/deterioration exact ties.

The 22 live-discharge-before-later-deterioration records are flagged `chronology_conflict=1`. Primary fixed-horizon labels remain raw. The discrete-time primary analysis uses literal first timestamp, while two mandatory sensitivities (a) exclude the 22 conflicts and (b) classify them according to the raw deterioration endpoint while reporting that this violates absorbing-discharge chronology. Divergence is an endpoint-validity finding, not a model error.

A later death after ICU return is not modeled as a separate R→D transition: only 56 patients have both events within 48 hours, and this sparse post-return transition is not needed to answer the frozen decision-time question. Report its count and ordering descriptively. Revisit a non-absorbing five-state model only with adequate transition support and a separate clinical question.

Live discharge is an observed end of in-hospital follow-up, not proof of stability and not a post-discharge outcome. The design cannot assess deterioration after hospital discharge.

## Simple baseline and substantive time-resolved alternative

### Fixed-horizon S0/T1 baseline

Retain ridge multinomial S0 and nested T1 with the identical three-state 48-hour target. Their primary incremental estimand and the parent's four interpretation axes remain unchanged. This baseline directly answers the fixed decision question with minimal complexity.

### M0/M1 discrete-time competing-transition models

Reshape each subject into up to eight at-risk six-hour rows. Fit a ridge-penalized pooled multinomial logistic transition model with outcomes R, D, L, or W for each interval. Include categorical interval baseline hazards and all decision-time-frozen covariates:

- M0 uses exactly S0 inputs;
- M1 uses exactly T1 inputs.

Use no post-`t0` time-varying covariates. Select penalty on validation multinomial log loss, calibrate cause-specific cumulative incidences on validation only, and open the same untouched subject-level test set once. Compute coherent individual transition hazards and cumulative incidence functions (CIFs) for ICU return, death, live discharge, and remaining event-free/in-hospital at 6, 12, 24, 36, and 48 hours.

This alternative adds information the fixed baseline loses: when risk accrues; whether T1 improves ICU-return, death, or discharge transitions; whether the same 48-hour probability arises from early versus late hazards; and whether discharge removes a large, operationally selected group from in-hospital risk. It does not discover physiology, estimate the effect of monitoring, or make discharge independent.

A proportional cause-specific Cox model is deferred. It would impose proportional hazards and still require competing-risk transformation; the discrete-time model matches the observed hourly timestamps and fixed 48-hour decision window more directly. A neural multistate model is also deferred because the scientific uncertainty is decomposition and timing, not flexible representation; L1/L2 already test nonlinear longitudinal input structure on the same question.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Event-time corroborated:** T1 improves fixed-horizon Brier over S0 under axis A; M1 improves R+D integrated Brier over M0 with a paired 95% CI above zero; calibration is acceptable at 24 and 48 hours; and the improvement is not confined to L prediction. State whether the gain is early, late, ICU-return-specific, death-specific, or mixed. This supports time-resolved incremental prognostic information only.

**Operational-selection dominant:** any fixed-horizon improvement is concentrated in live-discharge prediction, disappears for R+D integrated Brier, or reverses materially across destination/service/night or treatment-limitation strata. This weakens the clinical-monitoring interpretation but does not prove that physiology is irrelevant.

**Fixed-horizon adequate:** M0/M1 produce calibrated 48-hour CIFs concordant with S0/T1, interval-specific gains add no decision-relevant distinction, and uncertainty rules out a prespecified R+D integrated-Brier improvement of 0.002 averaged over 6–48 hours. Prefer S0/T1 for parsimony; retain event chronology as reporting.

**Endpoint ambiguous:** chronology-conflict exclusion, PACU-proxy exclusion, or treatment-limitation strata materially reverse the R+D conclusion; ICU return and death move in opposing directions; or fixed versus CIF predictions disagree beyond calibration uncertainty. Report prognostic performance but withhold preventability or monitoring-allocation language.

**Inconclusive:** intervals span zero/materiality bounds, death/component support is insufficient, calibration fails, or group sensitivities are too sparse. An imprecise null does not falsify trajectory information.

No outcome permits claims that measured instability caused an event, that a return was planned or preventable, that a postoperative pathway was intended, that live discharge was safe, or that delayed transfer or extra monitoring improves outcomes.

## Ward destination, treatment limitation, postoperative pathways, and operations

Destination, source ICU, current service, transfer time, recent support, and code status remain S0 covariates and prespecified stratifiers. The event-time report must show observed R/D/L CIFs for major strata before interpreting model utility because the audit found large descriptive variation in live discharge (for example, 14.9% for Cardiac Surgery destination versus 30.8% for Medicine/Cardiology) and death (0.062% versus 0.806%). These are observed differences, not adjusted effects.

Do not label cardiac-surgery, CVICU, surgical-service, or PACU-associated records as “planned postoperative.” Report exact recorded categories only. The exact PACU-before-return sensitivity remains an imperfect pathway proxy. True plannedness, discharge intent, expected death, goals of care, ward staffing, occupancy, rapid-response availability, and monitoring adequacy require blinded clinical adjudication or unavailable operational data.
