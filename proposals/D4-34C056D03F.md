# Continuous baseline cystatin-C rescue test after adverse binary discordance result

## Lineage and unresolved claim

This is a prospective, post-parent follow-up to assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. It does not reinterpret or erase that executed adverse result. The parent found that adding the binary baseline discordance flag `D = I(eGFRcys_0 < 60)` to a strong flexible creatinine comparator produced small repeat-stable loss reductions that failed the two-loss Holm gate and worsened prespecified 10% risk concentration. The unresolved question is whether that failure was caused by dichotomization, or whether baseline cystatin-C has no reproducible incremental information in this exact selected repeat-attender setting.

The evidence-supported claim is therefore narrow: under the parent protocol, the binary flag did not establish incremental held-out prediction or improved fixed-capacity concentration. The new hypothesis is narrower and different: **replacing the binary flag with the prespecified continuous baseline cystatin-C measurement, on the identical cohort, outcome, splits, comparator, and evaluation rules, will pass a preregistered incremental-information rescue gate.** This is a prediction experiment among observed qualifying first-repeat attenders, not a test of measured GFR, CKD chronicity, causality, benefit from ordering cystatin-C, clinical utility, or transport to nonattenders.

A positive result would identify avoidable information loss from the binary threshold. A negative result would strengthen the conclusion that the parent’s adverse result is not explained by dichotomization. Either result is clinically useful for deciding whether continuous cystatin-C merits external validation as a monitoring or risk-stratification signal; neither result alone justifies changing care.

## Inference, gates, and multiplicity

Use the parent’s paired participant-level bootstrap: resample participants, carrying all ten repeated predictions for each sampled participant together; do not treat repeated predictions as independent. Run 2,000 successful primary replicates and 1,000 secondary replicates, with the model fits and splits held fixed. Label intervals conditional on frozen fits and splits; this is not full training-variation uncertainty. Use participant-cluster standard errors for paired loss tests. Apply Holm adjustment separately to the two primary loss tests (Brier and log loss) at family alpha 0.05. Apply a separate Holm adjustment to the two primary 10% capacity tests (selected risk and event capture).

The continuous rescue is **supportive** only if all gates pass:

1. reconstruction, field/unit, finite-value, fold-support, paired-coverage, and convergence checks pass;
2. both primary loss differences are negative and both pass the two-test Holm gate, with the 95% interval excluding zero;
3. neither primary 10% selected-risk nor event-capture difference is negative with a 95% interval excluding zero; and
4. calibration does not meet the inherited computational deterioration rule: increase in absolute joint calibration-intercept error >0.10, absolute slope-from-one error >0.10, or absolute observed-minus-predicted risk error >0.002, with the corresponding degradation interval above zero.

The direction in gate 3 is deliberately noninferiority-like for concentration: a continuous rescue must not worsen the parent’s fixed-capacity decision signal. These margins are computational protocol gates, not clinically validated equivalence or utility margins. A positive result must also be stable in the fixed unpenalized convergence sensitivity (`C` removed) without changing the primary classification; the sensitivity is confirmatory stability, not a new opportunity to search.

## Interpretation and evidence limits

Supportive results would mean that, conditional on the observed 2–8-year repeat and selected-attender cohort, a fixed continuous baseline cystatin-C representation adds reproducible held-out information beyond flexible creatinine/age/sex/time and avoids the binary flag’s 10% concentration loss. They would not show that cystatin C improves diagnosis, measured GFR ascertainment, CKD progression detection, medication dosing, outcomes, or patient benefit.

Adverse results would mean that the parent’s binary failure is not credibly rescued by the one prespecified continuous measurement representation; they would strengthen, but not prove universally, the practical conclusion that cystatin-C baseline information is not useful under this protocol. Inconclusive results would mean the available data or computational support cannot distinguish rescue from no rescue, not that the models are equivalent.

The experiment cannot resolve assay traceability beyond the field/unit certificate, biological non-GFR determinants, acute versus chronic kidney status, missingness mechanisms, why participants attended repeat assessment, causal effects, deployment without knowing future repeat time, external transport, or clinical utility. Those claims require expert review, measured GFR/clinical adjudication, richer longitudinal or registry data, and/or prospective external validation.
