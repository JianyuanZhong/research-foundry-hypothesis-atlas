# Discharge-proximal recovery after observed TACE: separating lab-linked deterioration from process-only return in HCC

## Candidate lineage and substantive repair

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`. This episode-9 child preserves the parent's validated primary early post-TACE prognostic estimand, population logic, temporal windows, patient-level discovery partition, chronological test, and non-causal interpretation.

The one substantive repair is a new, prespecified **first-return composition endpoint**. It separates a return accompanied by a reproducible, directionally adverse core-laboratory pattern from a return that is visible only as an encounter/diagnosis or lacks that lab evidence. The discharge model is required to predict the lab-linked category more strongly than the process-only category after adjustment for laboratory observation opportunity and non-medication order intensity. This directly challenges the unresolved alternative that the discharge trajectory is merely a marker of how intensely the patient was observed or how a return was coded.

This remains an observational prognostic experiment. It cannot estimate the effect of TACE, discharge timing, testing, monitoring, or an intervention; it cannot establish a safe discharge threshold; and it cannot prove that a lab-linked pattern is biological hepatic non-recovery. The strongest possible Harbor conclusion is about an operational, lab-linked return phenotype.

## Clinical question and hypotheses

### Preserved primary early estimand

Among adults with HCC who have a first **observed** TACE episode and are discharged, does the within-person laboratory trajectory from the 14 days before TACE to the first 72 hours after TACE add reproducible held-out information about a subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days after discharge beyond baseline hepatic reserve, prior utilization, demographics, and recorded disease history?

Primary early trajectory features remain the three core-assay changes:

- total bilirubin (`total bilirubin`);
- albumin (`albumin`);
- INR (`international normalized ratio`).

### New discharge/composition estimand

In the nested cohort with at least 96 hours from observed TACE start to discharge and complete baseline, early, and discharge core assays, does the discharge-proximal change from the first 72 post-TACE hours to the final 24 hours before discharge add held-out information about the **composition of the earliest 30-day return**—(i) a lab-linked directionally adverse return, (ii) another return without that lab phenotype, or (iii) no observed return—beyond baseline/early values, demographics, prior utilization, recorded history, laboratory observation opportunity, and pre-discharge non-medication order intensity?

The biologically motivated but deliberately bounded claim is:

> If discharge-proximal non-recovery is more than a care-process artifact, the discharge block should provide a larger incremental, chronological-stable signal for the lab-linked deterioration-return category than for an otherwise observed return without that phenotype.

The alternative explanations are prespecified:

- **Observation artifact:** the signal is explained by counts, timestamps, spans, gaps, or order intensity.
- **Arbitrary-return artifact:** the signal predicts any return or a diagnosis-coded return but not a return with a directional lab pattern.
- **Generic temporal artifact:** a non-overlapping pre-TACE placebo trajectory or timestamp-shuffled trajectory performs comparably.

The endpoint is not called hepatic decompensation. A lab-linked return is a computable phenotype requiring expert validation in a future study.

## Evidence boundary and clinical importance

The strongest claim from the external evidence actually inspected is narrower. Sanai et al., *Scientific Reports* 2026 (DOI `10.1038/s41598-026-54466-4`, PMCID `PMC13457603`, frozen source ID `[source checksum]`) reported that, in a four-center cohort undergoing TACE or TARE, post-locoregional-treatment hepatic decompensation was operationalized using ascites, variceal bleeding, or portosystemic encephalopathy, and that baseline ALBI and treatment type were associated with post-treatment decompensation. This supports plausibility that hepatic reserve and post-treatment complications matter.

That evidence does not establish that a routinely recorded, time-ordered laboratory trajectory after an observed TACE episode adds incremental prognostic information in this HCC snapshot. It does not establish that a discharge trajectory identifies true hepatic decompensation, an unplanned return, or patients who benefit from delayed discharge or intensified monitoring. The HCC source has no laboratory-unit column and cannot produce a validated ALBI score.

The unresolved clinical question matters because a signal available at discharge could motivate prospective observation or follow-up. A signal that survives process and return-phenotype challenges would justify prospective validation with units, treatment intent/dose/territory, stage, adjudicated complications, mortality ascertainment, outpatient capture, and a specified care pathway. A signal confined to process-only returns would caution against interpreting routine discharge trajectories as biologic hepatic non-recovery.

The natural-history and Bayesian demonstrations motivate longitudinal comparison in general, but this proposal uses transparent patient-level models and does not claim their generative or genetic methods. The expert-seed README states that no HCC seed was supplied; the 30 UK Biobank/MIMIC/eICU seeds are not joined to this HCC island.

## Laboratory features and process features

For each core assay, use the median of valid numeric `Quantitative result` after verifying the two-key join to an encounter and timing by `Test time`. Do not use `Encounter time` as a surrogate lab time. The early block is early minus baseline. The discharge block is discharge minus early. Keep the three raw differences for audit and descriptive reporting; robust-standardize assay-wise inside training folds using training-fold median/IQR. Expected adverse directions are higher total bilirubin/INR and lower albumin; these are directional conventions, not clinical cutoffs. There is no lab-unit column, so do not infer units, pool assays, or compute ALBI.

- total valid core-lab row count and distinct core-lab timestamp count in [TACE start, index discharge);

- valid order-row count;
- distinct valid `Opening Time` count;
- distinct encounter keys represented.

## Operational return phenotypes

The new phenotype is deliberately computable and deliberately not called decompensation.

For each qualifying return encounter, use only rows joined by `(patient master index, encounter number)` and valid `test time` in [return `admission time`, return `admission time + 24 hours`]. For each of the three core assays, calculate a return-window median. Compare it with that participant's discharge-window median from the index stay. An assay is directionally adverse when:

- total bilirubin return median > discharge median;
- INR return median > discharge median; or
- albumin return median < discharge median.

A **lab-linked deterioration return** is the earliest qualifying 30-day return that has discharge and return values for at least two core assays and is directionally adverse on at least two of those paired assays. Require strict inequality; ties are not adverse. This threshold is an operational rule, not a validated clinical threshold and not a claim about severity.

Define the earliest qualifying 30-day return by `encounters.admission time`. Classify it into exactly one of:

- `first_lab_deterioration_return`: the earliest return meets the lab-linked definition;
- `first_other_return`: the earliest return exists but does not meet it, including no return-window lab, insufficient paired assays, or no two-assay directional deterioration;
- `no_observed_return`: no qualifying return is present in the observable window.

The primary new outcome is this three-level first-return composition. The stricter endpoint sensitivity requires at least two distinct valid lab timestamps per assay contributing to the return phenotype and at least two adverse assays. The broader sensitivity counts any lab-linked deterioration return within 30 days even if an earlier other return occurred; this is secondary because it changes event ordering.

Separately retain the parent's mutually exclusive descriptive first-return liver-coded flag: a first return is liver-coded if any `diagnoses.diagnosis name` row on that return contains one of the frozen terms `ascites`, `peritoneal effusion`, `hepatic encephalopathy`, `hepatic coma`, `esophageal and gastric fundal varices`, or `gastrointestinal bleeding`; otherwise it is non-liver-coded. Never relabel an earlier return using later diagnoses. This flag is a code phenotype only. Report its overlap with the lab-linked phenotype but do not treat either as clinical adjudication.

This composition is a falsification-oriented distinction, not a claim that an `other` return is non-biologic. Missing return labs can reflect outpatient capture, scheduling, clinician choice, or incomplete source capture. Therefore a lab-linked result can support only a lab-anchored operational association; it cannot prove that the other-return category lacks biology.

## Models, baselines, estimands, and uncertainty

### Preserved primary early analysis

Fit patient-level baseline-only and trajectory models for 30-day all-cause subsequent admission `Y_any_return`. Baseline covariates are age, sex, index department, the three baseline core medians, prior 365-day encounter count, and prior recorded indicators. The trajectory model adds the three early-minus-baseline core changes.

Use a prespecified regularized logistic model as primary and a shallow gradient-boosted model only as a bounded functional-form sensitivity. Use patient-level five-fold cross-validation within discovery buckets 0–79, plus a chronological split with 2010–2022 derivation and 2023–2025-12-02 testing. All imputation, robust scaling, feature selection, and tuning must be inside training folds. No GPU is needed.

The primary early estimand is the global likelihood-ratio increment and held-out incremental prognostic information of the early block. Report out-of-fold AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, adjusted odds ratios per training-fold IQR, representative-pattern absolute risk differences, patient-bootstrap intervals, and fold-specific sample/event counts.

### New composition analysis

On the nested discharge cohort, define D0 as baseline covariates plus early core medians. Define D1 as D0 plus the three discharge-minus-early changes. Fit a regularized multinomial model with outcome categories `first_lab_deterioration_return`, `first_other_return`, and `no_observed_return`.

The primary process-controlled comparison is:

- D0+P+Q versus D1+P+Q.

P and Q are in both models. The comparison asks whether discharge change contributes information beyond recorded observation and order intensity, not whether those process variables should be used for care.

Do not use discharge values as separate predictors in D0; D1 contains the fixed discharge-minus-early parameterization. Do not use post-discharge labs, diagnoses, orders, medications, return status, or length of stay as predictors. Use the exact same fold-contained preprocessing in D0 and D1.

Report:

- global multinomial likelihood-ratio increment;
- one-vs-rest held-out log loss and Brier score for the lab-linked and other-return categories;
- class-specific AUROC/AUPRC only when event counts permit;
- calibration intercept/slope by category;
- category-specific coefficient contrasts per training-fold IQR;
- patient-bootstrap 95% intervals, with bootstrap count and seed;
- the held-out incremental log-score improvement for lab-linked versus other-return categories and its paired patient-bootstrap difference.

The primary composition contrast is the difference between these two held-out incremental log-score improvements. This provides the direct test of specificity: a discharge block that only predicts any observed return should not be labeled evidence for lab-linked non-recovery.

Before inferential modeling, require at least 20 first lab-linked deterioration returns and at least 20 first other returns in the nested cohort. If either gate fails, do not claim a multinomial inferential result; report category counts, missing-return-lab frequencies, overlap with liver-coded returns, and descriptive intervals only and label the new composition claim inconclusive. The inherited all-cause nested gate of at least 20 events remains required for any nested D0/D1 inference. Penalization cannot rescue inadequate events.

## Falsification, adequacy, and leakage tests

All tests use participant-level discovery partitions and fold-contained preprocessing.

1. **Process-control challenge.** Compare D1 versus D0 with no process variables, with P only, and with P+Q in both models. A discharge increment that disappears after P+Q is evidence that recorded testing/order intensity explains the signal; it is not evidence of biologic non-recovery. Report selection and process-vector distributions by calendar period and index department.

2. **Return-phenotype specificity.** Compare the lab-linked and other-return category increments. A comparable or larger D1 gain for other returns than for lab-linked returns weakens the proposed biologic interpretation. A result based only on the parent's all-cause or liver-coded outcome is not sufficient for the new claim.

3. **Non-overlapping pre-TACE placebo.** Set placebo anchor TACE start − 28 days. Use placebo baseline [TACE start − 42 days, TACE start − 28 days) and placebo early [TACE start − 28 days, TACE start − 25 days], with the same assays and paired-window rule. Predict the same first-return composition with the same covariates and process controls. A placebo increment comparable to the TACE-anchored increment within bootstrap uncertainty weakens TACE timing. If the placebo cohort fails assay/event adequacy, call timing evidence inconclusive.

4. **Timestamp falsification.** Within participant and assay, permute valid `test time` assignments with a recorded seed while retaining values, labels, and joins; rederive all windows and repeat for the prespecified number of permutations. Also run time reversal. Attenuation is expected for a time-ordered signal; failure to attenuate is adverse to the timing interpretation. This is not a clinical null test.

5. **Density and gap sensitivity.** Repeat under prespecified minimum timestamp and maximum-gap requirements, including the stricter two-timestamp post-TACE analysis. Do not silently replace the primary complete-case cohort or impute missing windows.

6. **Lab-phenotype robustness.** Repeat the return phenotype using the stricter two-timestamp rule and a prespecified training-fold assay-specific magnitude threshold of 0.5 training-fold IQR for each contributing directional change. Do not pool assays or use unverified units. If the primary strict-direction result and stricter version disagree, report both and treat mechanistic interpretation as uncertain.

7. **Procedure and assay audit.** Repeat with the exact frozen TACE dictionary and a narrower label subset only as a sensitivity. Preserve all included/excluded labels and assay availability. If TACE classification or assay meaning is not reproducible, the corresponding claim is inconclusive.

8. **Join and leakage assertions.** Assert exact two-key joins, no row multiplication, valid timestamp ordering, exclusion of index encounter from returns, no post-discharge feature row, no return-window lab entering predictors, and no return diagnosis entering predictors. Audit orders and medications separately for post-discharge leakage.

9. **Administrative censoring.** Report indexes whose 30-day window extends beyond the last observable encounter/date coverage. Do not interpret no observed return as known absence of return. If administrative coverage is insufficient for the declared boundary, fail that analysis gate rather than silently treating censoring as negative.

## Exact interpretation rules

### Supportive

The preserved early hypothesis is supportive only if its inherited criteria are met: global LR p < 0.05; optimism-corrected held-out Brier improvement with a 95% bootstrap interval excluding zero; direction and calibration not reversed in the chronological split; and no explanation by placebo, timestamp, density/gap, or high-capacity-only behavior.

The new discharge/composition hypothesis is supportive only if all of the following hold:

1. both first-return event gates (at least 20 lab-linked and 20 other returns) and the nested all-cause event gate pass;
2. D1+P+Q improves held-out prediction of the lab-linked category over D0+P+Q, with a prespecified 95% patient-bootstrap interval excluding zero for its incremental log-score/Brier improvement;
3. the lab-linked incremental improvement exceeds the other-return incremental improvement, with a 95% paired patient-bootstrap interval for their difference above zero;
4. the direction and calibration do not reverse in the chronological split;
5. the result is not eliminated by the stricter timestamp-density/gap lab phenotype and the time-ordered signal attenuates under timestamp permutation/time reversal;
6. the placebo composition increment is smaller and not comparable within uncertainty; and
7. the conclusion-to-output ledger links every claim to saved held-out predictions, fold metrics, uncertainty intervals, category counts, and falsification outputs.

Even then, the supported statement is: discharge-proximal change contains incremental prognostic information for an operational, lab-linked first-return phenotype conditional on measured observation/order opportunity. It is not evidence of adjudicated hepatic decompensation, a discharge threshold, treatment harm, or benefit from monitoring.

### Adverse/falsified

Call the new specificity claim adverse when the lab-linked event gate passes but D1 has no held-out incremental evidence for lab-linked returns, the lab-linked increment is no larger than the other-return increment, or the contrast reverses chronologically. It is also adverse to a TACE-timed interpretation if placebo is comparable, timestamp permutation does not attenuate, or process adjustment eliminates the apparent discharge increment.

An in-sample AUROC gain with worse held-out Brier/log score or calibration is not supportive. If the preserved early model supports but the composition test is adverse, retain only the narrower early all-cause prognostic conclusion and do not claim biologic non-recovery.

### Inconclusive

Call the new claim inconclusive—not negative—when either category event gate fails; return laboratory availability or administrative follow-up is too sparse; key assay/label mapping is not reproducible; process vectors cannot be computed without unresolved timestamps; chronological or placebo sensitivity lacks adequate events; or the lab-linked and other-return categories cannot be separated with justified uncertainty.

A favorable p-value cannot override an event gate, missing outcome evidence, poor follow-up coverage, or a contradiction between primary and robustness phenotype. Inconclusive means that additional data or adjudication are required.

## Unavailable evidence and next study

This HCC snapshot lacks validated BCLC stage, tumor number/size, vascular invasion, performance status, Child–Pugh score, treatment dose/territory, scheduling intent, mortality ascertainment, reliable outpatient capture, lab units/provenance, and a dependable treatment/discharge pathway. It cannot distinguish scheduled from unplanned returns. A death-coded diagnosis is not a mortality outcome.

`clinical_documents`, `examinations`, and `pathology` contain potentially adjudicable text/findings but are not truth labels: `clinical_documents` has no temporal column, `pathology` has no time-varying outcome field, and examinations are not automatically linked to a validated decompensation definition. A future study should draw a blinded sample of index stays and returns, adjudicate ascites, variceal bleeding, encephalopathy, infection, renal injury, and procedure-related complications from source documentation/imaging, and record lab units, treatment intent, tumor stage, death, outpatient encounters, and planned-return status. It should then prospectively test whether a prespecified discharge rule changes monitoring, return, complications, and patient-important outcomes.

Harbor can verify paths, table IDs, headers, filters, frozen dictionaries, joins, times, windows, process vectors, event counts, models, held-out predictions, uncertainty, and falsification computation. It cannot determine whether a directionally adverse lab pattern is true hepatic decompensation, whether a return was unplanned, whether TACE caused the pattern, whether death occurred, or whether changing care improves outcomes. Those claims require expert adjudication, missing evidence, or another study.
