# Provisional hypothesis: fat distribution separates metabolic from functional/fall risk trajectories

Status: provisional and not yet validated. This is a research design, not an observed result.

## Proposed relationship
Among UK Biobank participants with a second assessment and complete body-composition measures, at similar total adiposity, higher visceral fat will be more specifically associated with subsequent metabolic outcomes, while higher posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) will be more specifically associated with subsequent functional decline or falls. The contrast is between a simple total-adiposity baseline and a prespecified composition-specific model.

## Grounding and evidence status
The inspected evidence supports only that myosteatosis/MFI is associated with metabolic and functional outcomes [K1], that IMAT/MFI spans metabolic, cardiovascular and oncologic settings but remains heterogeneous across imaging and disease contexts [K2], and that UKB abdominal MRI phenotypes including visceral fat improve internal prediction of incident T2D [K3]. These works do not establish the proposed divergence, causality, or clinical utility. No association, causal mechanism, or clinical utility has been computed in this episode. The required clinical adjudication and external validation are not available in the current data.

## Baseline, alternative, falsification, and interpretation
Baseline: adjusted total adiposity plus age, sex, ethnicity, and measurement timing. Substantive alternative: add visceral fat and posterior-thigh MFI, with prespecified outcome-specific contrasts and held-out calibration/discrimination. The alternative reveals whether fat distribution carries information lost by total adiposity alone; it does not prove mechanism. A learned sequence/trajectory model is deferred unless repeated functional measurements and temporal support are verified, because it would add complexity without resolving the primary distribution question if the simpler models are not estimable.

Supportive: prespecified outcome-specific associations with uncertainty excluding the null, stable direction in held-out or sensitivity analyses, and clinically meaningful divergence beyond total adiposity. Adverse: no stable information, reversal, or explanation by baseline severity/timing/selection diagnostics. Inconclusive: insufficient overlap, sparse events, missing dates, wide uncertainty, or unstable calibration. A positive result would not establish that changing visceral fat or MFI improves outcomes. Causal, mechanistic, and treatment claims require stronger longitudinal design, clinical adjudication, and additional evidence.
