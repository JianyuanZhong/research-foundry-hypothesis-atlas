# Does a within-patient change in clinical-panel lab-draw intensity predict subsequent ICU deterioration, or is it a severity/practice fingerprint?

Lead probe was executed on the REAL source files at discovery (numbers in §Probe). **No
reference/solver solution was executed at discovery** — this is readiness, not a solved result.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICU patients are monitored by repeated laboratory draws, and clinicians decide how often to draw.
[K1] Agniel 2018 showed, across 272 lab tests in 669,452 patients, that the *presence and timing* of
a test order — not only its value — carries survival signal (order presence significant in 233/272
tests; order timing beat the value in 118/174). Lab-draw intensity therefore encodes a care process,
not only the patient's physiology. [K3] the WICH-ICU stepped-wedge cluster randomised trial
(NCT07013175) is now testing whether *reducing* routine laboratory testing and arterial blood gas
sampling in ICU patients is safe, with 30-day mortality as the primary outcome and measured testing
intensity per patient-day. The decision is live and consequential: if a fall in a patient's own draw
intensity prospectively signals deterioration, a de-implementation programme that lowers testing
could delay recognition in the very patients who need it; if the association is only a
severity/practice fingerprint, reducing testing should be safe. [K2] a scoping review of ICU
low-value-care interventions (13 routine-blood-testing studies) bounds the prior the other way —
interventions reducing routine ICU testing have not shown a signal of harm. These two bodies of
evidence disagree about the direction of the safety question, so a within-patient, severity-adjusted,
short-window ICU test is the discriminating piece currently missing.

The unresolved claim is **directional and within-person**: after adjusting for current severity, does
a *change in a patient's own* lab-draw intensity over two fixed early ICU windows carry prospective
information about (24,48]h in-ICU deterioration — and if so, is that information (H1) a patient-level
monitoring signal, (R1) a severity/indication artifact, (R2) a between-hospital ordering fingerprint,
or (R3) a disposition/recovery/end-of-life artifact?

## What episode 15 changes (the scientific revision)

Episode 14's own probe found a **non-monotone (inverted-U)** relation between `logR` and (24,48]h
mortality: a single linear "OR per log-unit" coefficient (the parent estimand) is therefore
misspecified, and its headline could be an artifact of that misspecification. Episode 15 makes the
exposure–response **non-linear by construction** and elevates the shape itself to the tested claim:

- The primary exposure remains the **volume-normalised clinical-panel draw-rate ratio**
  `logR_panel = log((d2_panel + 0.5)/(d1_panel + 0.5))`, where `d1_panel` counts pre-specified
  clinical-panel lab rows with `0 < labresultoffset <= 720` and `d2_panel` counts `720 < labresultoffset
  <= 1440` (panel = lactataemia/ABG/WBC/haemoglobin/platelets/creatinine/BUN/electrolytes/glucose/
  bilirubin/coagulation/troponin families, resolved from the exact `lab.csv.labname` strings emitted
  by the probe). Total-draw `logR_all` is retained as the secondary practice contrast.
- **New primary estimand**: a restricted cubic spline (RCS) with 3 degrees of freedom (4 knots at the
  5th/35th/65th/95th percentiles of `logR_panel`) inside a hospital-level multilevel model, replacing
  the linear-in-logR term. The pre-specified shape hypothesis is **inverted-U**: the fitted curve has an
  interior maximum, with higher mortality at **both** extreme de-escalation and extreme escalation and a
  lower mid-range minimum.

## Design (frozen)

- **Data**: eICU-CRD 2.0, read-only, under `datasets/eicu/`. Join key `patientunitstayid` in every
  table; hospital via `patient.hospitalid`.
- **Population**: `patient.csv.gz` rows with numeric `age >= 16` (eICU truncates age at "> 89"),
  `unitdischargeoffset >= 1440` (in ICU at the hour-24 landmark), and decisive
  `unitdischargestatus in {Alive, Expired}`. Restrict the between-hospital component to `hospitalid`
  with >= 200 eligible stays. *Parent-probe baseline*: 132,774 eligible stays, 208 hospitals, 1,860
  events, crude (24,48]h rate 1.40 % (this episode re-runs the recount; see §Probe).
- **Temporal boundaries**: all ICU-admission offsets are in **MINUTES**. Exposure windows (0,720] and
  (720,1440]; landmark t = 1440; outcome window 1440 < `unitdischargeoffset` <= 2880.
- **Outcome (primary)**: `(24,48]h in-ICU mortality` = `unitdischargestatus == 'Expired'` AND
  `1440 < unitdischargeoffset <= 2880`. Observability rule: stays discharged alive before hour 24 are
  not in the risk set; stays discharged alive at/after hour 24 with no recorded in-ICU death are
  censored non-events.
- **Adjustment (hour-12/24 severity)**: `apachePatientResult.apachescore` / `acutephysiologyscore`
  and `predictedicumortality` for `apacheversion == 'IVa'`; hour-12 `vitalPeriodic` vitals
  (`systemicmean`, `systemicsystolic`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, `sao2`) and hour-12 panel-lab
  values. Binding correction inherited from parent: `vitalPeriodic` has no `meanbp`; the MAP field is
  `systemicmean` (`meanbp` exists only in `apacheApsVar`). Confirmed on the source header this episode.
- **Secondary outcome (exploratory)**: new vasopressor start in (24,48]h from `infusionDrug.drugname`
  (`infusionoffset` in MINUTES), substrings norepinephrine/epinephrine/dopamine/dobutamine/
  vasopressin/phenylephrine/milrinone. Drugname strings are **unbound at discovery**; treated as
  exploratory only.
- **Rivals**: R1 severity/indication — the spline shape is absorbed when adjusting for hour-12
  severity; R2 between-hospital ordering habit — modelled explicitly by a **hospital random intercept
  plus random slope on the spline basis**, and by leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO) transport; R3
  disposition/EOL — `carePlanEOL` (`cpleolsaveoffset`) and `treatment` (`treatmentoffset`,
  `treatmentstring`) EOL markers, plus a "comfort-measures-only" sensitivity exclusion.

## Baseline vs substantive alternative (method comparison)

- **Baseline**: fixed-effect logistic regression, single linear `logR_panel` term + hour-12 severity
  covariates. What it *loses*: it can only represent a monotone dose–response, so it cannot distinguish
  a genuine inverted-U (two-sided monitoring signal) from a monotone association, and it forces a single
  "per log-unit" number on a curve the probe already shows is non-monotone.
- **Alternative (chosen)**: multilevel logistic with RCS(3 df) on `logR_panel`, hospital random
  intercept + random slope on the spline basis, LOHO transport. What it *reveals*: whether the
  association is a **threshold/shape** phenomenon (both under-monitoring and over-monitoring extremes
  risky) rather than a monotone dose-responsche — a different, more falsifiable clinical statement with
  a location parameter (the peak) that the baseline literally cannot report.
- Device: **CPU** (logistic/GLMM on ~1.3e5 rows, tens of predictors; no GPU needed). Approximate budget
  well within the configured solver envelope; no GPU requested.

## Falsification criteria (pre-specified)

- **Supportive of H1 (inverted-U)**: RCS likelihood-ratio test vs the linear model is significant
  (p < 0.05 for the 2 extra df) **and** the fitted maximum lies strictly inside the observed
  `logR_panel` range (not at a boundary knot) **and** the hospital random slope for the high-intensity
  region is positive within most hospitals (LOHO point estimates of the same sign in >= 80 % of folds).
- **Adverse / refuting of H1**: the spline collapses to linear (non-linear LR p >= 0.05), **or** the
  inverted-U disappears after adjusting for hour-12 severity + hospital random slope (shape is a
  R1/R2 artifact), **or** the peak migrates to the boundary (monotone dose–response).
- **Inconclusive**: non-monotone pattern present but the peak-location bootstrap CI spans the whole
  range, or >= 20 % of LOHO folds flip sign, or the event count in an extreme bin is too sparse to fit
  stable splines — reported as such rather than as support.
