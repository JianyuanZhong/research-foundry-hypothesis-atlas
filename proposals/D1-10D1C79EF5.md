# When does laboratory observation history support a physiologic-adjusted monitoring review?

Status: Episode-19 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic, transportability, measurement-process, and decision-reclassification study. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment-effect study, or evidence that changing monitoring improves survival.

## Unresolved clinical question and hypothesis

The strongest evidence currently available is an outcome-blind feasibility audit attached to the parent: 135,680 eligible adult first ICU-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by ICU minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows known by the six-hour landmark. The parent also verified the relevant vital tables and required fields. These facts establish that a hospital-held-out experiment is supportable; they do not establish a model result, physiologic correctness, clinician intent, or benefit.

The remaining clinical question is narrower and more consequential than an average score improvement:

> After recorded vital-sign state and availability are measured, does adding patient-level laboratory observation history identify a reproducible, cross-hospital group that would cross a prespecified monitoring-review threshold for recorded ICU death, with lower or equal alert burden and better calibration than the physiologic-state baseline?

Primary hypothesis: at the fixed six-hour ICU-relative landmark, a state-plus-laboratory-process model (Bp) will produce a positive, transportable incremental decision-reclassification signal over the matched physiologic-state model (Bv) at prespecified risk thresholds q = 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, and 0.10. The signal must be present in held-out hospitals, attenuate when laboratory-process channels are permuted within hospital and Bv-risk strata, and not be reproduced by hospital descriptors alone. If the process increment vanishes after vital adjustment or does not improve any supported threshold-level reclassification, the appropriate conclusion is that laboratory surveillance is not independently actionable in these recorded data—even if a small discrimination gain exists.

This is a monitoring-review hypothesis, not a recommendation to change testing. A positive result would justify prospective expert review of an alert design; it would not demonstrate that ordering more tests improves outcomes.

## Feature blocks and models

Freeze all eligibility, codebooks, support decisions, and transformations before reading held-out labels.

- B0 is a diagnostic parent baseline using demographics/admission context, laboratory state summaries, and laboratory availability.
- Bv is the primary state-adequate baseline: B0 plus recorded vital state and vital availability. For each supported physiologic field, use prespecified first/last/median/range/slope summaries and explicit missingness/availability; add no post-landmark information. Separately record vital observation density.
- Bp is the matched transparent process model: exactly Bv plus patient-level laboratory observation process (counts, distinct observation times, first/last time, recency, inter-result gaps, duration/span, total rows, distinct analytes, revision-known status, and interface/system indicators where supported). Exclude hospital ID and descriptors.
- Br is a secondary outcome-free target-site diagnostic in which continuous process variables are replaced by empirical percentiles learned from a fixed calibration subset of eligible predictor-window episodes within each target hospital; require at least 50 calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes, and never use target outcomes.
- T is the substantive learned alternative: a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six hourly bins in [0,360], consuming the same lab state, lab availability, vital state, vital availability, and vital-observation channels as Bp, without hospital ID. Use discrete-time death and alive-discharge heads. Fit only in development hospitals; validation and early stopping remain inside development data.

The primary scientific contrast is Bp minus Bv, because it isolates laboratory observation history after recorded physiology and is auditable. T is retained because summaries can lose temporal ordering, persistence, and co-movement between lab and vital observation; its added scientific value is demonstrated only by better calibrated held-out-hospital performance or more stable threshold reclassification on identical inputs, not by a tiny AUROC increment. If vital support fails, do not silently revert to Bp as the primary hypothesis test: report the vital-adjustment experiment as infeasible/inconclusive and retain the parent analysis only as a labeled secondary feasibility result.

## Splits, uncertainty, and falsification

Use five grouped outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once, stratified by eligible episodes and event counts when feasible. Keep all stays for a `uniquepid` together. All imputation, scaling, panel resolution, percentile transforms, regularization, temporal stopping, risk bands, and alert thresholds are fit within development hospitals. The primary summary is the macro-hospital paired difference; patient-weighted results are secondary.

Use hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical uncertainty, targeting at least 1,000 resamples if feasible, and simultaneous intervals for prespecified threshold cells. Report fold- and hospital-level support, death events, competing discharges, alert counts, and missingness.

Required falsifications are:

1. Permute lab-process channels within hospital and Bv-risk stratum while holding state fixed. Any process gain that survives is evidence against a patient-linked process interpretation.
2. Refit/score after conditioning on vital observation density. A disappearing gain supports the workflow/monitoring-intensity explanation.
3. Repeat with a strict common lab panel and revision-known-only records. Reversal indicates interface, unit, or revision sensitivity.
4. Compare Bp with hospital-descriptor-only predictions and report descriptor contribution. Similar performance indicates a site fingerprint.
5. Report leave-one-hospital-out influence and require no single hospital to dominate.
6. Compare fixed absolute process features with the outcome-free relative-process Br diagnostic. Divergence is evidence of site-scale dependence, not proof of patient biology.
7. For T, use a time-order-preserving block shuffle and a state-matched temporal ablation. A gain that survives time destruction or appears only in interface channels is adverse to a temporal physiologic interpretation.

Supportive evidence requires adequate vital/lab event support and overlap, leakage-free folds, directionally favorable Bp-minus-Bv calibration/Brier performance in held-out hospitals, a threshold reclassification/net-benefit effect with simultaneous uncertainty excluding no improvement at at least one supported prespecified q, attenuation under matched process permutation, and no single-hospital domination. Adverse evidence is no transport improvement, no supported threshold reclassification, reversal after strict revision/unit handling, deterioration in calibration, or persistence under state-matched permutation/site-descriptor controls. Inconclusive evidence includes vital coverage/overlap failure, sparse event-bearing hospitals after complete-case rules, unstable fits, fold reversals, unsupported threshold cells, or target leakage.

Any supportive result establishes only a recorded-eICU prognostic/measurement-process and label-relative reclassification claim. It cannot establish that a test was ordered because of concern, that a recorded vital is physiologically correct, that missed instability was absent, that an alert would be acted upon, or that changing surveillance improves patient outcomes. Those require clinical adjudication/chart review, external hospitals, and prospective impact evaluation.
