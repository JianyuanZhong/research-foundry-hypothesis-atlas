# Observation-selection repair: all-eligible ICU-discharge instability

Status: design-only branch proposal, child of `[prior hypothesis]`. No cohort scan, row count, model fit, Harbor execution, or clinical result was run.

## What changes and why

The parent asks whether persistent multi-domain abnormality in the final 24 hours before ICU discharge is associated with a first 48-hour ICU readmission or hospital death. Its primary exposure-known cohort requires valid core observations in at least three of four six-hour bins. That is a scientifically reasonable measurement-quality sensitivity, but it can also select on severity, destination, staffing, goals of care, or the opportunity to measure. An observed association in that restricted cohort is therefore not the same claim as a discharge-time association among all eligible alive ICU discharges.

This branch makes the all-eligible observed-discharge estimand primary. Patients with insufficient observation are retained as an explicit `unknown/insufficient` exposure state; they are not coded as stable, excluded from the primary flow, or imputed as abnormal. The known-exposure analysis and a pre-landmark inverse-observation-weighted sensitivity remain secondary. This is a substantive estimand and clinical-interpretation improvement because it separates:

1. risk associated with a clinically interpretable observed physiologic state;
2. risk associated with failure to ascertain that state; and
3. the narrower known-exposure contrast that depends on an observation model.

The change does not claim that unknown is a physiologic phenotype. It makes ascertainment itself visible and tests whether the parent’s interpretation survives it.

## Scientific opening, question and claims

[K1] reports an association between post-ICU-transfer vital/laboratory flags and 48-hour ICU readmission or death in a small single-center surgical cohort. It supports a short-term instability opening, but not a MIMIC ICU-discharge phenotype, independence from destination or observation opportunity, or a causal monitoring effect. [K2] compares associative and causal-fused MIMIC-IV readmission models and is useful here as a boundary: prediction and feature realignment do not establish a discharge intervention effect. [K3] associates respiratory independence and post-discharge dependency with one-year mortality in a small cohort of very elderly ICU survivors; it supports keeping support, destination and dependency separate, but does not answer the acute 48-hour MIMIC question.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is therefore only: late or post-transfer physiologic and dependency signals can be associated with adverse outcomes after ICU transition in selected cohorts, and temporal models can describe patterns that fixed summaries lose. The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults reaching an observed alive ICU discharge in their first eligible ICU stay, does the observed final-24-hour state of persistent multi-domain physiologic abnormality have a different 48-hour first-event risk profile from an observed stable state when every eligible discharge, including insufficiently observed discharges, is retained and readmission, hospital death, alive discharge and loss of same-hospital follow-up are separated?

Primary hypothesis: in the all-eligible population, the observed state `>=2 persistent domains` has higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU readmission and/or hospital death than the observed stable state, while `unknown/insufficient` has its own risk profile. The primary claim is prognostic and descriptive, not causal or mechanistic. The strongest rival is observation selection: sparse charting is severity-, destination- or care-process-dependent, so the known-exposure contrast may attenuate or reverse when the unknown state and pre-landmark observation predictors are represented. Other rivals are residual severity, treatment limitation/goals of care, oxygen-support meaning, and post-discharge destination.

The result would change the next decision. A stable, directionally consistent observed-state contrast after ascertainment audit would justify prospective discharge-readiness/monitoring validation. A large unknown-state gradient or a known-exposure contrast that disappears under observation weighting would redirect the work toward measurement and discharge selection. An adverse or inconclusive result would prevent treating the parent’s signal as a discharge phenotype.

## Actual scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly produce:

- an all-adult to first-eligible-stay flow retaining every alive ICU discharge, with counts for stable, one-domain, multi-domain and unknown exposure states;
- an exposure/availability manifest for all four pre-discharge bins, including item resolution, valid counts, charttime/storetime gates, missingness and the exact reason a subject is unknown;
- separate first-event 48-hour cause-specific counts and Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidences for ICU readmission, hospital death and alive hospital discharge;
- a separate loss-of-same-hospital-follow-up flow and risk-set table, never silently folded into alive discharge or event-free censoring;
- unadjusted all-eligible observed-state contrasts, fixed-summary standardized contrasts, known-exposure sensitivity contrasts, and the prespecified inverse-observation-weighted sensitivity with positivity/weight diagnostics;
- destination, prior-severity and observation-density audits;
- matched held-out predictions from the fixed-summary baseline and chronological temporal alternative, with calibration, cause-specific Brier scores, uncertainty and the order-permuted temporal control;
- a conclusion tied to these outputs and classified supportive, adverse or inconclusive.

Readiness, a completed package, or a model fit alone cannot establish completion or a clinical conclusion.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Unit is one hospital admission, using the earliest valid ICU stay for that `subject_id/hadm_id` pair.

1. Read `mimic-iv-3.1/hosp/patients.csv.gz`; require `anchor_age >= 18`.
2. Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, then patients on `subject_id`.
3. Require finite `icustays.intime < icustays.outtime`, and retain the earliest `intime` (tie broken by minimum `stay_id`) within each `hadm_id`). Do not replace a first stay that fails the alive-discharge rule with a later stay.
4. Define the landmark `t_d = icustays.outtime`. Retain only subjects alive at `t_d`: `deathtime` null or `deathtime > t_d`; a death exactly at `t_d` is not an alive ICU discharge.
5. Do not require exposure-known status for the primary cohort. Retain every immediate destination, including ward, step-down/intermediate, hospice/palliative and unknown.
6. The primary exposure window is the half-open interval `[t_d-24 hours, t_d)`, divided into four consecutive half-open six-hour bins. The earlier audit window is `[t_d-48 hours, t_d-24 hours)`.
7. Primary predictors must be available by discharge: `charttime < t_d`; when `storetime` is present, require `storetime <= t_d`. Missing storetime is fail-closed for the primary core exposure. Charttime-only is a prespecified sensitivity, not a replacement.
8. The outcome window is `[t_d, t_d+48 hours)`. No value or note at or after `t_d` is a predictor.

## Exposure and observation state

Use the parent’s fixed, dictionary-audited core items:

- 220045 Heart Rate, bpm;
- 220052 Arterial Blood Pressure mean, mmHg;
- 220210 Respiratory Rate, insp/min;
- 220277 O2 saturation pulseoxymetry, %;
- 223761 Temperature Fahrenheit, converted to Celsius;
- 223762 Temperature Celsius;
- 223834 O2 Flow, L/min;
- 223835 Inspired O2 Fraction;
- 226732 O2 Delivery Device(s), text-only descriptive sensitivity, never coerced to numeric.

Numeric channels require finite `valuenum` and compatible `valueuom`; text-only values are invalid for numeric channels. Fahrenheit and Celsius are collapsed into one temperature channel after conversion. Within each bin use median HR/MAP/RR/SpO2/temperature and maximum O2 flow/FiO2; preserve per-channel masks and valid-record counts. Fail closed if an item is absent, has a changed label, `linksto`, or unit, or collides with another selected item.

Define flags without outcome-derived tuning:

- respiratory/oxygen: SpO2 <92%, O2 flow >0 L/min, or FiO2 >0.21;
- hemodynamic: MAP <65 mmHg, HR >100 or <50 bpm;
- ventilatory pattern: RR >24 or <8/min;
- temperature: Celsius <36.0 or >38.3.

A domain is persistent when flagged in at least two of four bins. The observed four-level exposure `E` is:

- `stable`: no persistent domain;
- `one`: exactly one persistent domain;
- `multi`: at least two persistent domains;
- `unknown`: fewer than three of four bins contain a valid core observation.

A missing bin is unknown, not normal. The primary estimand is the vector of observed-state risks and state-versus-stable contrasts over this four-level state, with the unknown state retained in the all-eligible flow. The primary contrast of clinical interest is `multi - stable`; the unknown-versus-stable contrast is an ascertainment result, not a physiologic result. Domain-specific and oxygen-support components are reported separately.

For a known-exposure sensitivity, require `O=1` (at least three observed bins) and repeat the parent’s fixed trajectory labels: stable, persistent, newly abnormal, resolving and intermittent, with unknown excluded only from that sensitivity. This restricted analysis is explicitly secondary.

## Observation-selection repair and inverse weighting

Define `O=1` exactly when at least three of four primary bins contain a valid core observation under the primary charttime/storetime rule; `O=0` otherwise. The observation model is fit without outcome, immediate destination, post-landmark values, or any final-window exposure summary.

Let `Z` contain only variables known strictly before the primary exposure window ends, using:

- static `patients.anchor_age`, `patients.gender`;
- `admissions.admission_type`;
- `icustays.first_careunit`, `intime`, `outtime`, `los`;
- in the earlier window `[t_d-48h,t_d-24h)`: core-channel medians/masks, core valid-record counts, all-chartevent counts and coverage;
- earlier-window counts and latest valid values for the prespecified laboratory panel: creatinine 50912; lactate 50813; glucose 50809; hemoglobin 50811 and 51222 kept as separate itemids; platelet 51265; WBC 51301, with missingness;
- earlier-window counts from inputevents, outputevents, procedureevents and labevents, which describe opportunity rather than treatment intent.

Fit a training-only logistic observation model `P(O=1 | Z)` on the fixed subject-level training partition. Standardize continuous predictors using training data only. Truncate stabilized weights at the training 1st and 99th percentiles, report the untruncated/truncated distributions and effective sample size, and declare positivity failure if any prespecified state/destination/severity cell has no usable observation support. The weighted known-exposure analysis targets the all-eligible observed-discharge population only under the conditional missing-at-random assumption `O ⟂ (latent E, outcomes) | Z`; that assumption is not testable here. It is therefore a sensitivity, not a correction that proves the unknown subjects’ latent state.

The primary analysis remains the transparent four-level all-eligible state. Do not impute unknown as stable or multi-domain. Do not use `transfers.careunit`, `admissions.discharge_location`, notes, death, readmission or any post-landmark field in `Z`.

## Distinct competing events and follow-up loss

Construct a first-event process beginning at `t_d`). If exact timestamps conflict, emit a data-integrity flag and use the fixed precedence death, ICU readmission, alive discharge; ties are not resolved by exposure or outcome inspection.

- **ICU readmission risk set:** every all-eligible subject alive at `t_d`; event is the first later `icustays.intime >= t_d` and < `t_d+48h` for the same `subject_id/hadm_id`, excluding the index stay. Hospital death, alive hospital discharge and follow-up loss are competing transitions.
- **Hospital-death risk set:** every all-eligible subject alive at `t_d` with an ascertainable same-admission death clock. Event is `admissions.deathtime` in the outcome window, no later than valid `dischtime` when present. `hospital_expire_flag` is a consistency check, never a substitute for a missing time. ICU readmission, alive discharge and follow-up loss precede/censor death as first-event transitions.
- **Alive-discharge risk set:** every all-eligible subject alive at `t_d` with valid `dischtime >= t_d`. Event is alive hospital discharge in the window when no earlier death or readmission occurred. `discharge_location` is descriptive and not used to infer physiology or intent.
- **Loss-of-same-hospital-follow-up risk set:** every all-eligible subject for whom the admission-level endpoint cannot be verified through 48 hours. Define the last verified same-admission timestamp as the maximum valid time, before `t_d+48h`, among the bound `transfers.outtime`, `icustays.outtime`, `labevents.charttime/storetime`, `chartevents.charttime/storetime`, `inputevents.endtime/storetime`, `outputevents.charttime/storetime` and `procedureevents.endtime/storetime`. A loss transition occurs only when `admissions.dischtime` and `deathtime` are both null/invalid, no later bound timestamp exists, and the last verified time is before 48 hours. If MIMIC’s admission endpoint is complete, this flow may be empty; report zero/not estimable rather than inventing loss. Loss is an observability transition, not a clinical adverse outcome.

For all four transitions report denominators, events, competing events, administrative 48-hour censoring and invalid-time exceptions. The primary clinical CIFs are separate first ICU readmission, first hospital death and first alive discharge, with loss shown separately and never merged with alive discharge. Use Aalen–Johansen for descriptive CIFs. For adjusted estimates, use six-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards and standardize to the all-eligible cohort; do not call that causal standardization.

## Destination and severity audits

Immediate destination is the first `hosp/transfers` row for the same `subject_id/hadm_id` with `intime >= t_d`; retain `transfer_id,eventtype,careunit,intime,outtime` and label missing as unknown. Ultimate `admissions.discharge_location` is a separate descriptive field. Destination is post-landmark and therefore excluded from the primary adjustment and observation model. Report state contrasts within destination families only when a prespecified cell has at least 100 patients and 20 component events; otherwise report inconclusive.

Use only pre-landmark severity: age, gender, admission type, ICU timing/LOS, and latest valid values/missingness for the fixed lab panel above in `[t_d-48h,t_d)`. Report observation-density gradients using core/all chartevent counts and labevent/inputevent/outputevent/procedureevent counts. A density gradient comparable to the exposure gradient, or strong attenuation after density stratification, is adverse to a physiologic interpretation. No field here adjudicates goals of care, discharge rationale, chronic oxygen, work of breathing, functional status, clinician consideration, or treatment limitation.

## Fixed-summary baseline versus chronological alternative

Both methods use the identical all-eligible subjects, four-level exposure contract, outcome clocks, loss rules, pre-landmark covariates, and deterministic subject-level split: SHA-256 of `subject_id`, 70% train, 15% validation, 15% test; all admissions for one subject stay in one partition. Scaling, category maps, density cutpoints and observation-model weights are training-only.

**Baseline B (primary, auditable).** Fit separate six-hour cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards for ICU readmission, hospital death, alive discharge and follow-up loss. Features are the four-level `E` state, domain flags/counts and masks, four-bin fixed summaries (median/minimum/maximum/range and flagged-bin counts without order), `O`, pre-window severity, static covariates and observation-density counts. Unknown exposure is an explicit categorical level with masks; no hidden imputation is permitted. Standardize predicted 48-hour CIFs to the all-eligible test risk set. Report component calibration, cause-specific Brier score, observed/predicted counts, risk differences, and subject-cluster bootstrap intervals. The primary scientific comparison is the state-specific risk profile, not a small AUROC gain.

**Temporal alternative T (substantive, same question).** Fit a one-layer GRU with hidden size 32 over the same physiologic and observation-process data in 12 chronological two-hour bins covering `[t_d-24h,t_d)`: seven numeric channels after Fahrenheit/Celsius collapse, per-channel masks, elapsed-time/bin index, core and total observation counts, and domain flags. Static covariates and earlier-window severity enter a static head. Output four cause-specific hazards for the same first-event states over eight six-hour post-discharge intervals. Explicitly encode unknown/insufficient coverage; the model may not infer a missing bin is normal. Use Adam 1e-3, batch size 128, dropout 0, at most 50 epochs, early stop after five validation epochs without improving mean cause-specific Brier score, and seeds {17,29,43}. Report each seed and the prespecified aggregate.

B loses the chronological order of equal-frequency abnormalities and the timing of missingness; T can reveal whether persistence, recency, resolving patterns, or observation bursts explain risk beyond fixed summaries. The scientific test is held-out calibration/Brier and state/trajectory contrasts, plus an order-permuted training control with identical bins, masks and split. Retain T as informative only if its chronology-sensitive calibration or state contrast is stable across seeds and not reproduced by the order-permuted control; a small discrimination gain alone is predictive utility, not a clinical advance. Transformers and causal-fused treatment models are deferred because discharge intent, validated intervention, treatment limitation, complete observation and external validation are unavailable.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results require: (i) the all-eligible multi-versus-stable risk difference for at least one primary clinical component is positive with a 95% interval excluding zero; (ii) component readmission and death estimates are reported separately; (iii) the direction is not confined to one adequately sized destination or observation-density stratum; (iv) the known-exposure estimate, its pre-landmark IPW sensitivity, and the all-eligible state profile are directionally compatible; and (v) persistent/newly abnormal temporal patterns remain distinguishable from stable/resolving patterns on held-out predictions and are not reproduced by order permutation. This would support a reproducible prognostic association conditional on observed discharge context, not a causal discharge policy or mechanism.

Adverse results include: the multi-versus-stable contrast disappears after retaining unknowns or after IPW; unknown has a large risk gradient while known-state contrasts are weak; the signal is restricted to one destination, density or oxygen-support stratum; the earlier-window signal is comparable; or T’s chronology gain vanishes under permutation. These favor observation selection, residual severity, destination, support meaning or generic prediction.

Inconclusive results include: high unknown fraction; weight nonpositivity or unstable effective sample size; fewer than 100 subjects or 20 component events in a required stratum; incompatible clocks; material loss of same-hospital follow-up; dictionary/unit/availability failure; intervals spanning clinically important benefit and harm; or an imprecise null. Do not retune thresholds, merge destinations, redefine unknown or choose a weight truncation after outcome inspection. An imprecise null is not decisive refutation.

Computationally checkable claims are source binding, flow/filter/join correctness, time availability, unknown-state construction, event precedence, risk sets, loss audit, split integrity, model metrics, calibration, bootstrap intervals and weight diagnostics. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for true treatment limitation, discharge rationale, goals of care, chronic oxygen, work of breathing, functional status, clinician intent, causal monitoring/discharge effects, mechanism and transportability.

## Alternatives, resources and deferral

The fixed-summary baseline is selected as primary because it makes unknown exposure, observation density, event precedence and weights auditable. The GRU is retained as a substantive secondary because chronological persistence and the timing of observation bursts are the rival explanation; these are information the baseline intentionally loses. A temporal model that only improves AUROC without changing calibration, state contrasts or observation interpretation is deferred.

Archive scans, materialization, Aalen–Johansen estimates, logistic fits and 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap intervals are planned CPU work under the configured future solver envelope of 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM and at most 28,800 seconds. The GRU is small enough for CPU-first fitting; one allocated A100 is an optional future solver resource if readiness shows repeated sequence fits or bootstrap inference is the bottleneck. Any GPU job must explicitly use allocated `cuda:0`; an ordinary shell CUDA check is not a hardware-feasibility test. These are planning estimates, not measured runtimes. No discovery computation or proposer/solver training was run.

Revisit the direction only with evidence that unknown exposure is negligible, observation weights have good positivity, or clinical adjudication supplies discharge intent/measurement context. Do not revisit by adding model complexity alone.
