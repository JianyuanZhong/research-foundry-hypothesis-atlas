# Episode 24 successor: can an early lactate reassessment be prognostic before the eight-hour landmark?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and substantive advance

The selected Episode-23 parent asks whether lactate nonclearance retains a large mortality association after balancing strictly pre-follow-up renal/hepatic laboratory context and recorded pressor proxies. It uses a late, eight-hour phenotype: adults with an early norepinephrine record, serial lactates, baseline monitor context, and recorded MAP recovery in the final hour before the eight-hour reassessment. That is a clinically coherent phenotype, but it leaves an important timing question unresolved: **does lactate nonclearance become a decision-relevant prognostic signal by about four ICU hours, before the late MAP-recovery/survivor selection of the parent, or is the association visible only in a later selected population?**

This matters because a clinician deciding whether to reassess perfusion, seek source control, review treatment response, or enroll a patient in a protocol cannot wait for an eight-hour phenotype if an earlier signal is useful. Conversely, an early association may be misleading if it is mostly a marker of who survives long enough to receive a late blood gas or of later treatment intensity. Resolving the timing boundary would inform the design and timing of a prospective multimodal reassessment study, without pretending that this retrospective EICU analysis establishes a treatment.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis:** among adults who survive and remain hospitalized to a four-hour landmark after an early recorded norepinephrine start, early lactate nonclearance (<20% relative fall from a valid pre-exposure lactate to a valid 2–4 hour lactate) is associated with at least a **10-percentage-point higher probability of hospital death** than clearance (at least 20%), after standardizing the strictly pre-follow-up measured clinical context in the four-hour target. Full support additionally requires a positive estimate in at least 80% of finite leave-one-hospital-out refits.

This is a four-hour landmark prognostic association, not a causal effect of lactate, norepinephrine, fluids, MAP, or any protocol. The +10-point boundary is a prespecified decision-relevance anchor, not a validated minimum clinically important difference.

The strongest available evidence supports only the parent’s narrower late association and its uncertainty: the parent reconstructed 216 people in ten hospitals, estimated a positive +24.389-point mortality risk difference, and obtained a copied-hospital/person 95% interval of +6.121 to +42.882. That is evidence of a positive association in the selected late phenotype, but it is inconclusive about the prespecified +10-point magnitude and says nothing about earlier actionability. The present experiment tests the unresolved early-landmark claim; it must not be described as a confirmation of the parent, tissue hypoperfusion, or benefit from intervention.

A preliminary outcome-blind source scan for this proposed timing window found 14,289 adult early-norepinephrine stays meeting the broad four-hour discharge-survival screen, 5,719 with a baseline lactate, 1,416 with a 2–4 hour lactate, and 888 with both. Under a deliberately permissive raw MAP-context screen, 262 paired records occurred across 53 hospitals, with 115 deaths and 147 survivors. These are feasibility diagnostics only: they precede the exact revision/conflict, tie, unit, and final covariate rules below and are not inferential results. They are stored in `episode24_early_audit.json` and reproducible from `episode24_early_audit.py`.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use the EICU snapshot and no sampling. All times are integer ICU-relative minutes.

1. Read every row of `patient.csv.gz`. Treat numeric age `>89` as 90, retain age >=18, and use the earliest qualifying stay per `uniquepid`, breaking ties by `t0` and then `patientunitstayid`. Define `t0` as the minimum `infusionDrug.infusionoffset` in [0,1440] for which case-folded `drugname` contains norepinephrine or levophed. Drug rate, amount, or weight does not establish eligibility.

2. Define the four-hour landmark `L4=t0+240`. Require `patient.unitdischargeoffset>=L4`, `patient.hospitaldischargeoffset>=L4`, and exact `hospitaldischargestatus` Alive or Expired. The outcome is `D=1` for hospitaldischargestatus=Expired and 0 for Alive. This is hospital death by discharge among people observed through L4; the released data do not provide an exact death time, so no claim about deaths after the exposure can be made beyond this landmark-survivor definition.

3. Construct the baseline lactate `B` from `lab`: case-insensitive `labname='lactate'`, numeric `labresult` in [0.2,30] mmol/L, collection offset in [t0-360,t0], and `labresultrevisedoffset<=t0+60`. For each collection offset, use the greatest eligible revision. If eligible revisions at the same collection offset disagree, invalidate that collection; identical duplicates retain the smallest `labid`. Select the latest valid collection, then smallest `labid`. Require B>=2 mmol/L, preserving the parent’s high-lactate clinical phenotype.

4. Construct the early lactate `F4` from the same analyte and range in [t0+120,t0+240], with revision completed by L4. Apply the identical revision/conflict rule. Select the valid collection whose collection offset is closest to t0+180, then the earlier offset, then smallest `labid`. Let `E4=F4_offset-t0` and `X=1` if (B-F4)/B < 0.20 (nonclearance), otherwise X=0 (at least 20% clearance). The exposure is defined before outcome status is read and cannot use any result after L4.

5. Require baseline monitor context: at least three unique `vitalPeriodic.observationoffset` values in [t0-60,t0), each with numeric `systemicmean` and `heartrate`. Collapse duplicate offsets by the median. Record baseline median MAP and heart rate. Unlike the parent, do not require final-hour MAP recovery: requiring it would answer a late-survivor question rather than the early-actionability question.

6. Freeze the site gate before reading outcome values: a hospital must have at least five X=0 and five X=1 people after the preceding rules. Retain the target only if at least eight hospitals and 50 people remain. A minimum of 20 hospital deaths in each exposure group is an outcome-feasibility gate checked after the population is frozen, not a reason to select hospitals. If these gates fail, the result is inconclusive rather than a reason to broaden the window.

## Strictly pre-follow-up context

For each person let Q=min(F4_offset,t0+120). Every primary adjustment variable must be observed or become revision-eligible before Q; no post-Q value may enter the primary model, missingness calculation, or cohort selection.

From `lab`, select the latest valid collection in [t0-360,Q) whose revision is <=Q, using the same greatest-revision and conflicting-revision invalidation rule. Require the named unit in `labmeasurenamesystem`: creatinine mg/dL, total bilirubin mg/dL, AST (SGOT) Units/L, ALT (SGPT) Units/L, and BUN mg/dL. Use the maximum of AST and ALT if both are present; otherwise retain missingness. Apply physiologic-range checks fixed before fitting, log1p-transform skewed values, and include an indicator for each missing analyte. This organ-context block is the primary incremental adjustment, with a complete-case analysis as a secondary sensitivity.

From `infusionDrug`, use records with `infusionoffset` in [t0,Q): indicators for any recorded norepinephrine/levophed record, any recorded epinephrine, phenylephrine, vasopressin, or dopamine record, and the number of distinct recorded pressor-name families. These are recorded-name proxies only. Do not convert `drugrate`, `infusionrate`, `drugamount`, `volumeoffluid`, or `patientweight` into dose equivalents.

The fixed baseline adjustment block contains age, gender indicators including missing, B, t0, baseline median MAP and heart rate, early elapsed time E4, and hospital indicators. Add the latest pre-Q organ block and its missing indicators to the primary model; add the pressor-proxy block only as a prespecified secondary incremental model. If available before Q, summarize pre-Q MAP and heart rate using median and fraction of MAP readings >=65, with missing indicators; these are recorded vital signs, not adjudicated perfusion.

## Estimand and analysis

The primary estimand is the standardized risk difference

P(D=1 | X=1) - P(D=1 | X=0)

in the four-hour measured-overlap target after balancing the fixed pre-Q context. It is a conditional retrospective association, not a propensity-score causal effect.

Fit the exposure model using scikit-learn logistic regression with C=10, penalty=l2, solver=lbfgs, max_iter=10000. Winsorize continuous covariates at pooled 1st/99th percentiles and standardize using pooled means and population SDs. Include missing indicators and hospital indicators. Use overlap weights 1-e for X=1 and e for X=0, normalized to mean one within exposure group. Hospital indicators may balance measured site composition but do not establish transportability or remove site selection.

Report, in this fixed order:

- crude early-landmark RD;
- hospital-only overlap RD;
- baseline-context primary RD;
- organ-context-adjusted RD;
- organ plus pressor-proxy secondary RD;
- complete-case organ sensitivity;
- a descriptive model-discrimination check using within-hospital cross-validation, only when every fold has events. Predictive improvement cannot replace the RD estimand or imply utility.

The parent’s eight-hour estimate is a historical reference, not pooled with the four-hour analysis and not used to tune the early protocol. A separate eight-hour replication is not a rescue analysis.

## Feasibility, uncertainty and falsification

Before outcome interpretation, report exact final counts, baseline and early-lactate missingness, revision conflicts, selected collection offsets, unit failures, exposure counts, hospitals retained by the exposure-only gate, organ-block missingness, pressor-proxy prevalence, and the number of people surviving the final context construction. Reconcile the preliminary scan rather than quoting it as the final cohort.

The primary analysis is feasible only if the final target has at least 50 people, eight hospitals, at least 20 deaths in each exposure group, effective sample size >=40 in both exposure groups, propensity support within [0.02,0.98], maximum person-weight fraction <=5%, maximum hospital-weight fraction <=35%, maximum absolute weighted standardized mean difference <=0.10, and at least 95% of bootstrap refits converge. Failure of any required gate is inconclusive.

Use the parent’s hierarchical uncertainty logic: seed 20260924; sample the retained hospitals with replacement as unique hospital-copy IDs, then sample unique `uniquepid` clusters within each copied hospital and exposure group to the original size as unique person-copy IDs. Recompute final preprocessing, revision resolution, context model, weights, and RD in each of 5,000 replicates, retaining repeated copies. Use the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. Report failures, ESS, propensity range, weight concentration, all model-column SMDs, and every leave-one-hospital-out primary estimate.

The predeclared interpretations are:

- **Supportive:** all gates pass, the primary 95% interval lower bound is >= +10 points, and >=80% of finite leave-one-hospital-out estimates are positive. This supports an early, measured-context-adjusted prognostic association of the prespecified magnitude in this four-hour survivor target.
- **Adverse to the early magnitude:** all gates pass and the primary interval upper bound is < +10 points. This falsifies the >=+10 early association, but does not show no prognostic information, equivalence, or safety.
- **Opposite direction:** all gates pass and the primary interval upper bound is <0. This supports an adjusted direction opposite to the hypothesis, but does not show that clearance is protective.
- **Inconclusive:** any population, support, convergence, event, balance, or site-deletion gate fails; the interval contains +10; or the interval/site estimates are undefined or unstable. A positive point estimate alone is not supportive.

A parent-positive/early-adverse result would indicate that the late signal does not transport to the earlier landmark under this measured context, but it would not prove that the late association is caused by survivor selection. A parent-positive/early-inconclusive result would leave timing unresolved. An early-supportive result would justify prospective synchronized validation of an early reassessment marker, not bedside routine use or a treatment recommendation. All interpretations must preserve the landmark-survivor selection boundary.

## What this can and cannot establish

Automatically checkable claims include source hashes and headers, complete-file scan/no sampling, exact stay/person selection, four-hour survival screen, lactate revision/conflict and unit rules, exposure timing, no post-Q variables, joins, overlap weights, balance/ESS, bootstrap IDs and interval, leave-site-out estimates, and deterministic classification.

The EICU rows cannot establish arterial or venous specimen identity, assay indication, true norepinephrine administration or dose, fluid or inotrope treatment, tissue perfusion, treatment intent, exact death time/cause, complete limitations of care, delivered oxygen, or whether a clinician could act on an early result. The outcome is hospital status, not time-to-death. A supportive association cannot justify fluid, vasopressor, inotrope, source-control, lactate-target, or monitoring recommendations. Clinical adjudication is needed for specimen/assay timing, treatment intent, organ-failure definitions, perfusion, and limitations of care. Independent prospective synchronized data are needed for transportability, calibration, timing, and utility; a randomized or credible target-trial design is needed for treatment effects.
