# Support-context matched recovery at the first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe adult first-ICU-discharge cohort, identify a matched low-final-burden population, estimate whether recent recorded withdrawal of respiratory or vasoactive support carries prognostic information beyond the final physiology and the observation/documentation process, and fit two predeclared models on the same underlying data.

Completion requires:

1. a cohort, join, temporal-integrity, coverage, cell-count and competing-event audit;
2. a one-hour, 12-hour pre-outtime feature tensor with separate physiology, recorded-support and observation-process streams;
3. a primary matched estimand comparing durable recorded-off, recent respiratory withdrawal, recent vasoactive cessation and dual recent withdrawal among patients with adequate coverage and low final recorded burden;
4. 6-, 24- and 48-hour standardized cumulative incidences for first ICU return, in-hospital death and alive hospital discharge as mutually exclusive first events;
5. a transparent pooled discrete-time competing-risk baseline and an observation-aware factorized hidden semi-Markov alternative, both trained with patient-level splits;
6. calibration, IPCW Brier score, absolute CIF contrasts, interaction contrasts, bootstrap uncertainty, positivity and latent-state diagnostics;
7. predeclared timing, mask, source-channel, support-threshold, pseudo-landmark and topology falsifications; and
8. an interpretation file mapping every reported claim to an output row, estimate, interval, event count and falsification result, classified as supportive, adverse or inconclusive.

The new scientific object is the fitted support-context prognostic estimand and its uncertainty/sensitivity outputs. No discovery result, model score or clinical policy is asserted here.

## Unresolved question and strongest supported claim

Expert seed 10 asks whether persistent oxygen requirements or fluctuating vital signs before ICU discharge predict ICU return or in-hospital death. The available evidence supports only that MIMIC-IV contains dated ICU observations, interval medication/procedure records, ICU stays, admissions, transfers and hospital outcomes from which a recorded-data estimand can be constructed. It does not support an association, bedside readiness, treatment limitation, planned/unplanned return, or causality.

The unresolved and falsifiable claim is:

> Among adults leaving their first ICU stay whose final six hours show low recorded respiratory and hemodynamic burden with adequate measurement coverage, patients recently withdrawn from respiratory and/or vasoactive support have different 48-hour competing risks of ICU return and death than patients stably recorded off support, even after adjustment for the final physiology, observation intensity and observable discharge-process context.

This is deliberately narrower than asking whether any residual abnormality predicts failure. It tests whether two patients with similarly reassuring final recorded values carry different risk because one has only recently stopped support. A null result after process adjustment would be clinically useful: the apparent distinction may be documentation timing or discharge selection rather than a reproducible recovery trajectory. The estimand is prognostic, not causal; stopping support is not treated as an intervention.

Primary contrasts are standardized absolute CIF differences at 6, 24 and 48 hours for:

- durable recorded-off in both domains (reference);
- recent respiratory withdrawal only;
- recent vasoactive cessation only; and
- recent withdrawal in both domains.

The primary matched population requires at least two valid respiratory-observation bins and two valid MAP bins in each six-hour half, no qualifying respiratory or vasoactive support record in the final half, and a prespecified low final-burden rule. Active/residual, inadequate-coverage, contradictory and discordant states remain in cohort-flow and secondary analyses; they are not silently forced into the primary contrast.

Events are mutually exclusive first events from ICU outtime: later ICU stay in the same hospital admission within 48 hours, in-hospital death before a counted return, or alive hospital discharge before either. Administrative follow-up is the earlier of 48 hours and hospital discharge. A later ICU stay is not labelled planned, unplanned or deterioration. Equal-time ties are locked before fitting (death first when within one minute; non-discharge first when discharge ties another event), with 0- and 5-minute sensitivity analyses.

## Clinical importance and advance

The ICU-to-ward transition reduces monitoring while patients may be physiologically near a support boundary. A persistent signal after matching on final burden and adjusting for observation intensity would justify prospective evaluation of targeted reassessment after recent respiratory or circulatory support withdrawal. A signal that disappears in the observation/process model would warn against using sparse recorded support-free intervals as a discharge-readiness marker. A domain-specific contrast could distinguish whether respiratory or circulatory transition deserves closer review.

The advance is not a small predictive improvement. It separates four explanations that otherwise collapse together:

1. final recorded physiology/support;
2. recent support transition;
3. selective measurement and missingness; and
4. observable discharge context and patient selection.

The primary matched design makes the clinical interpretation more specific than a generic “instability” score. It also preserves the competing alive-discharge event, avoiding the prior error of treating discharge as non-informative censoring.

## Operational recorded states

Materialize physiology, support documentation and observation masks separately. Missing observations are not coded as stable physiology.

Physiology uses hourly medians/robust summaries, slopes, range and final-minus-preceding-half change for HR, arterial/NIBP MAP, RR, SpO2, FiO2, O2 flow, PEEP and temperature. Apply an auditable Fahrenheit-to-Celsius rule and retain source channels.

Respiratory support documentation in an hour is positive if O2 flow >2 L/min, FiO2 >0.21, PEEP >0, an active intubation-to-extubation interval, or active NIV interval is recorded. Vasoactive support is positive when a valid interval for any listed vasoactive item overlaps the hour. These are recorded-evidence rules, not proof of continuous bedside support. Use a final-half low-MAP indicator (<65 mmHg median) and low respiratory burden (<1/3 of available final-half respiratory-support observations) only as operational summaries, not diagnoses.

Primary states require adequate coverage in both halves:

- recent respiratory withdrawal: respiratory support in the preceding half, none in the final half;
- recent vasoactive cessation: vasoactive support in the preceding half, none in the final half, with MAP coverage;
- durable recorded-off: no qualifying support record in either half;
- dual recent withdrawal: both recent indicators.

The primary matched subset additionally requires low final MAP and low final respiratory burden. Report the recorded support-free buffer and all active, unobserved, contradictory and inadequate states descriptively and secondarily. Do not interpret a buffer as continuous absence of support.

Observation/process covariates use only pre-outtime data: observed-bin fractions, maximum gap, chart-row counts, distinct item/source channels, final/preceding coverage, support interval counts, contradictory-time flags, pre-index transfers, first/last careunit, ICU LOS, admission type, age, gender and anchor-year group. These are process/context variables, not latent physiology.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods receive the same 12-hour-by-channel matrix, static covariates and observation masks, with identical patient-level splits (70/15/15; fixed seeds 17, 29 and 43). Item mapping, unit conversion, thresholding, imputation, state definitions and tuning are fit on training/validation data only; the held-out test set is opened once.

The transparent baseline is a pooled discrete-time cause-specific hazard model at 1, 6, 24 and 48 hours for ICU return, death and alive discharge. It includes nested versions: final physiology/support only; observation-only; process/context-only; and the full matched model. Standardize held-out CIFs and report absolute contrasts and respiratory-by-vasoactive interaction, with calibration intercept/slope and IPCW Brier score. The baseline is the primary scientific result because it makes the process-adjustment change visible and is CPU-suitable.

The substantive alternative is an observation-aware factorized hidden semi-Markov model over twelve one-hour bins, with separate respiratory and hemodynamic chains, sparse transitions and dwell distributions. It has distinct emission channels for physiology/support and an observation model for channel availability/intensity. Its added information is ordered persistence and transition timing: it can test whether recent withdrawal plus duration is a reproducible state rather than a fixed summary or a sparse-record artifact. Evaluate posterior entropy, state occupancy, split stability, calibration and CIF contrasts—not merely AUROC. If states are not identifiable or add no stable domain-specific information, report the transparent baseline and call the alternative inconclusive.

A flexible GRU/TCN is deferred: it could improve prediction but would not by itself distinguish physiology from documentation or provide interpretable duration states. Revisit only if the HSMM demonstrates reproducible ordered information that the summary baseline misses. A causal discharge-policy analysis is deferred because intent, ward monitoring, treatment limitations and counterfactual discharge are unavailable. Renal recovery and note-language readiness are deferred because measured GFR, adjudicated renal recovery and validated readiness labels are unavailable.

## Falsification, uncertainty and interpretation

Predeclare:

- shuffle hourly order within patient while preserving values, masks and support totals;
- compare full, mask-only, mask-removed, physiology/support-only and support-removed models;
- replace `charttime` with `storetime) as a negative timing control;
- replace interval-overlap support with last-value coding;
- remove procedure corroboration;
- separate arterial from NIBP MAP and FiO2, O2-flow and PEEP channels;
- vary support thresholds, the one-third burden rule, six- versus twelve-hour windows and buffer rules;
- use a pseudo-landmark six hours before outtime with a newly defined leakage-free outcome window;
- stratify by first careunit and admission type;
- compare aggregate return with topology-confirmed and topology-unconfirmed return;
- compare alive-discharge competing-risk analysis with discharge-as-censoring only as sensitivity; and
- audit invalid/contradictory interval exclusions rather than silently repairing them.

Supportive evidence requires adequate cell counts/positivity, a predeclared domain or interaction contrast with a clinically interpretable direction, calibrated predictions, stable patient-bootstrap intervals, and persistence across observation, source-channel, timing and threshold falsifications. It supports only retrospective prognostic association with recorded support-context patterns.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed contrast, attenuation to the observation/process-only estimate, strong dependence on masks/storetime/last-value coding/procedure corroboration/topology, non-positivity, or an unidentifiable latent state. This argues against treating the recorded transition as a distinct recovery signal.

Inconclusive evidence is sparse cells or events, wide/unstable intervals, poor calibration, contradictory reasonable-window results, high latent posterior entropy, missing topology, or failure of temporal integrity. It must not become a bedside policy.

Even supportive results cannot establish causality, clinical readiness, appropriateness of ICU discharge, preventability, treatment limitation, monitoring benefit or planned/unplanned status. Those require clinician adjudication, richer ward data, external validation and a prospective or causal study.
