# UKB 40: A pancreatic cancer clue involving elevated blood glucose accompanied by weight loss

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

When the HbA1c increase between two assessments is similar, those who lose weight are more likely than those who gain weight to be diagnosed with pancreatic cancer in the subsequent short term.

## Study population and key data

Participants with repeated HbA1c and weight measurements who had no pancreatic cancer at the second assessment; diabetes medication and cancer registry.

## Basic Approach

Follow up from the second joint assessment, compare the interaction between changes in body weight and changes in blood glucose, separate near-term and long-term pancreatic cancer, and account for glucose-lowering treatment.

## Significance of the Research

Distinguish obesity-related worsening of glucose metabolism from metabolic changes associated with occult tumors.

## Main Challenges

This clinical phenomenon has a research basis; the incremental contribution should be in the time window and biochemical context. The onset of diabetes cannot be determined using measurements taken several years apart.

## Life Sciences

Tumor development, host reserve, and hematopoiesis

## Competing Explanations

Intentional weight loss, glucose-lowering treatment, or another wasting disease causes weight loss.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

First check the interval between repeat assessments, medication use, and the number of pancreatic cancer events; if there are not enough near-term events, pause rather than expanding to all cancers to cobble together an outcome.
