# Is the laboratory-surveillance signal specific to impending ICU death?

Status: Episode-24 substantive child of the assessed eICU laboratory-observation line (parents [prior hypothesis] and [prior hypothesis]). This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that an alert or additional testing improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest evidence currently available is an outcome-blind feasibility audit and exact local schema/header inspection, not a fitted result. The inherited audit supports 135,680 eligible adult first ICU stays, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals and 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes. A direct audit of carePlanEOL found 1,433 rows and 1,230 unique stays, with 717 discussion and 249 save offsets in [0,360]; it does not establish complete goals-of-care documentation.

The unresolved claim is: at ICU-relative minute 360, does patient-linked laboratory observation history add a transportable, calibrated signal specifically for later recorded ICU death after recorded physiologic state, vital-observation intensity, documented care limitation and hospital surveillance regime are accounted for? Or does it merely predict that any disposition (including alive ICU discharge) is near because of generic workflow, length of stay, interface conventions, or documentation intensity?

This distinction matters clinically. A mortality alert based on surveillance process could wrongly imply hidden deterioration when it is actually a proxy for short stay, data workflow, or a documented decision not to escalate. Endpoint-specific evidence would narrow whether the result is appropriate for retrospective risk review; it still would not show that changing test frequency or review changes outcomes. The substantive advance over the existing parent is a prespecified specificity test: the same process block must be evaluated for both competing endpoints, with a death-specific contrast rather than a mortality-only gain.

## Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: adding laboratory observation-process history to the recorded-state model improves held-out-hospital calibration and integrated competing-risk Brier score for recorded ICU death by minute 1,800, and its incremental improvement is larger for death than for alive ICU discharge. The primary estimand is the macro-hospital paired difference in death-specific integrated Brier score and calibration between BvP and Bv, plus the specificity contrast D = (improvement for death) - (improvement for discharge), with hospital-clustered uncertainty.

Falsification hypothesis: if the laboratory process increment is generic workflow, it will be similar for death and discharge, be reproduced by vital-observation intensity or hospital descriptors, survive process permutation, or collapse under interface/revision restrictions. Those outcomes refute a patient-linked, death-specific interpretation even if raw mortality discrimination improves.

## Features and fitted models

Freeze analyte/interface/system codebooks, value compatibility and revision treatment outcome-blind in development hospitals. Lab state comprises first/last/median/range and slope when at least two observations, availability masks and supported instability flags. Lab process comprises row counts, distinct result times/analytes, first/last times, recency, spans/gaps, revision-known/stable interface/system indicators and common-panel blocks. Vital state comprises supported first/last/median/range/slope and field masks; vital process comprises observation counts, distinct times, recency, span and six-bin coverage. EOL features are presence and timing only.

Bv is the transparent pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly rows 360–1,800 and separate death and alive-discharge heads, using demographics/admission context, lab state/availability, vital state/masks and no lab/vital process counts. BvP adds patient-level laboratory process. BvV and BpV add vital observation-process summaries as the general workflow placebo. BvP-EOL adds pre-landmark EOL presence/timing; repeat the primary comparison in the no-recorded-EOL-by-360 subset only if support passes.

The substantive alternative T is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins in [0,360], with exactly the supported BpV channels, EOL channels and masks, no hospital ID, and two competing-risk heads. It is fit only in development hospitals, tuned/stopped in development validation hospitals, and assessed in held-out hospitals. It can reveal ordering, persistence and co-movement lost by summaries. It is not selected for complexity: retain it only if it improves held-out endpoint-specific calibration/Brier or fixed-burden outputs over BvP, with order-shuffle and parameter-matched non-temporal controls. CPU elastic-net is expected; one allocated A100 using cuda:0 is optional if repeated temporal fits require it.

## Split, uncertainty and decision diagnostic

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals, keeping all uniquepid episodes together. Development hospitals use a 75/25 fit/validation split. All codebooks, imputation, scaling, tuning, calibration, percentiles and thresholds are development-only. Report macro-hospital paired differences first and patient-weighted results second. Use hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals, at least 1,000 resamples if feasible, with per-hospital calibration slope/intercept, integrated competing-risk Brier, cumulative incidence and endpoint-specific uncertainty.

At q in {0.01,0.02,0.05,0.10}, use the minute-360 predicted cumulative risk for recorded death by 1,800 to report alerts per 100, capture, PPV, false-review burden, number needed to review and the paired Bp/Bv reclassification. Require >=200 held-out episodes, >=20 recorded deaths and >=20 hospitals for each threshold cell. This is a label-relative review diagnostic, not net clinical benefit. Also report each model's discharge-risk calibration and whether death alerts disproportionately select patients whose competing event is alive discharge.

## Support gates and falsification

Require at least 100 hospitals with eligible support, 50 event-bearing hospitals, 1,000 recorded deaths and 1,000 competing discharges overall, approximately 20 hospitals per fold with both events where feasible, per-variable vital coverage of at least 30% and at least 20% with two observations, and overlapping state/process support in at least 80% of held-out hospitals. Require the death and discharge-specific cells to meet the stated support thresholds. Failure makes that claim inconclusive; it does not justify substituting a different endpoint.

Prespecify: (1) complete lab-process permutation within held-out hospital, Bv-risk stratum and vital-coverage stratum, retaining state, EOL and outcomes; (2) compare Bp-Bv with BpV-BvV; (3) compare full, common-analyte, revision-known-only and interface-stratified codebooks; (4) compare hospital-descriptor-only models; (5) leave one surveillance regime or hospital out; (6) order-shuffle and non-temporal controls for T; (7) compare death-specific versus discharge-specific increments and reclassification; (8) prohibit post-360 rows, Apache mortality, target-outcome calibration and target-fold preprocessing. An increment that remains after process permutation, is reproduced by site descriptors, or is concentrated in one interface/hospital is evidence against the patient-linked interpretation.

Supportive evidence requires valid gates, favorable uncertainty-supported BpP-minus-Bv death calibration/Brier, a positive death-versus-discharge specificity contrast, stable held-out review reclassification, attenuation under matched process permutation, no comparable vital/site increment, and no coding reversal. Adverse evidence includes null/worsening after state/EOL adjustment, equal or larger discharge increment, workflow-matched gain, permutation failure, interface/revision reversal, or disappearance at fixed review burden. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse endpoint cells, failed coverage/overlap, unstable fits, intervals spanning clinically meaningful ranges, or unsupported EOL/threshold strata.

## Required deliverable and evidence limits

The solver must newly freeze and save the cohort flow, codebooks, five-fold hospital manifest, feature matrices, cause-specific predictions, Bv/BvP/BvV/BpV/BpV-EOL fits, conditional T fit, endpoint-specific calibration/Brier/CIF outputs, death-specificity contrasts, uncertainty, fixed-burden diagnostics, falsifications and an interpretation trace linking every claim to computed output files. Completion is this uncertainty-bearing fitted experiment, not confirmation of the hypothesis.

Harbor can verify source paths, columns, joins, offsets, leakage, fitting, endpoint arithmetic, uncertainty, falsifications and whether conclusions follow from outputs. It cannot determine whether a laboratory test was ordered because of concern, whether recorded values reflect true physiology, whether absent documentation means no limitation, whether a clinician would review a candidate, or whether changing surveillance improves survival. Those claims require clinical chart adjudication, external health systems, prospective silent-mode evaluation and an impact or randomized study.

The three demonstrations remain adaptations rather than reproductions: Delphi supports only the bounded temporal alternative because the UKB trajectory/genetic setting is unavailable; ALADYNOULLI's genetic inputs and sharper latent target are unavailable so an EHR-only latent model is deferred; Oncoformer's images and main article/STAR Methods are unavailable, so the full multimodal study is not claimed and a lab-only variant would duplicate T. Seed eICU-02 motivates testing-practice transportability and eICU-08 motivates reduced-testing/instability falsification; the other seeds remain deferred pending distinct exposure and outcome support.
