# HCC longitudinal recurrence/progression proposal

## Status
Lead working draft after data-binding inspection; not yet a published candidate.

## Question
Among HCC patients with a first recorded HCC diagnosis or HCC-directed procedure, does a longitudinal pattern in routine liver-related laboratory values and HCC-directed treatment events over a defined pre-index window predict subsequent HCC recurrence or progression, beyond what is captured by the latest lab values and encounter intensity?

## Why it matters
HCC recurrence and progression are consequential clinical transitions that can change surveillance and treatment decisions. A model that identifies patients at elevated risk before a subsequent HCC event could inform earlier review, but the primary scientific value is testing whether temporal routine-data patterns contain information lost by current-state summaries. A negative result would argue against adding a temporal model for this endpoint in this dataset.

## Evidence inspected
- HCC catalog and table metadata confirm compatible `患者主索引` and `就诊号` identifiers, 105,044 encounters, 43,815 patients, and explicit encounter timestamps.
- Labs have `检验时间`, procedures have `开始时间`, medications have `开始时间`/`结束时间`, orders have `开立时间`, and examinations have `开始时间`.
- Diagnoses and pathology lack independent timestamps, so their timing is inferred from the linked encounter.
- Vitals and transfers are identifier-only in this snapshot; no death flag, ICU/ward detail, or standardized coding is available.
- The data therefore support an encounter-anchored longitudinal study but not vital-sign deterioration, ICU escalation, or external mortality claims.

## Closest demonstrations and disposition
- [K1] Nature longitudinal natural-history transformer: demonstrates longitudinal sequence modeling, but its full cohort and external validation are unavailable. We will not reproduce it; a bounded HCC adaptation is possible only if the event stream is sufficiently dense.
- [K2] Nature Bayesian longitudinal EHR/genetic discovery: full article and supplement are available; it supports longitudinal modeling and uncertainty, but its genetic discovery question is not directly transferable.
- [K3] Cell longitudinal routine clinical data cancer work: main article is unavailable; only supplement/metadata were checked. It motivates routine-data longitudinal cancer questions but cannot be cited for full methods.

## Proposed experiment
1. Population: patients with a first HCC diagnosis in `诊断` or an HCC-directed procedure in `手术`, using text-matched HCC terms that will be validated against a bounded sample.
2. Index time: linked encounter `就诊时间` for diagnosis or procedure `开始时间`.
3. Pre-index window: 180 days of labs, procedures, medications, orders and examinations.
4. Outcome: subsequent HCC-related diagnosis, HCC-directed procedure, or imaging finding within 180–365 days, with a prespecified primary composite and sensitivity analyses.
5. Baseline: transparent logistic regression/Cox model using latest pre-index lab values, age/sex, prior HCC-directed procedures and encounter intensity.
6. Alternative: bounded temporal model over ordered event streams using the same inputs and target, designed to test whether sequence/order information adds predictive value.
7. Split: patient-level temporal split; no patient appears in both training and test.
8. Evaluation: calibration, discrimination and uncertainty; compare incremental value over baseline.
9. Falsification: no meaningful incremental predictive value over the baseline, or loss of signal after encounter-intensity adjustment, would not support the temporal-pattern hypothesis.

## Required outputs
- Newly fitted baseline and temporal model (if feasible).
- Patient-level predictions and held-out performance estimates.
- Calibration and uncertainty intervals.
- A decision rule for whether the temporal model adds clinically meaningful information.

## Alternatives and limitations
- Current-state severity may explain most of the signal; the baseline comparison is essential.
- Text-matched HCC diagnosis/procedure definitions require clinical review.
- Absence of death, ICU and external follow-up limits interpretation to recorded HCC-related events.
- If the event stream is too sparse, defer the temporal model and retain a descriptive/structured baseline analysis.

## Unresolved
- Exact HCC text terms and validation sample.
- Event-stream density and whether a temporal model is feasible.
- Whether the outcome composite is clinically meaningful without adjudication.
- Which demonstration claims can be cited after direct inspection.
