# Calendar-stable CBC cancer-type contrast: a competing-burden and ascertainment stress test

Status: proposed substantive successor of `[prior hypothesis]`; planned Harbor experiment, not an executed solver result.

## Decision and scientific opening

The parent’s first-CBC design remains interpretable only as a survivor-conditioned delayed association: among people alive and free of registry-coded cancer at two years, does the baseline platelet/haemoglobin pattern differ between later CRC and hematologic cancer? It cannot be read as a persistent biological phenotype. The remaining consequential rival is that the contrast reflects observation and ascertainment: CBCs obtained in different calendar periods may sit in different registry/testing environments, and a generic cancer burden may produce the same pattern. Clinical indication, symptoms, test ordering, iron studies, FIT, treatment and community contacts are unavailable.

The strongest available evidence supports only that CBC measurements can be associated with CRC before registry diagnosis and that longitudinal blood trajectories can be predictive in selected clinical cohorts [K1]. It does not establish a CRC-versus-hematologic contrast in UKB, its persistence after the near-diagnostic interval, or a mechanism. Prediction-model reviews also show why discrimination without calibration and appropriate validation is not a clinical decision rule [K2], while symptomatic-referral test performance is heterogeneous and cannot be transferred to an unindicated research-assessment CBC [K3].

### Hypothesis

In adults with a complete first UKB research-assessment CBC and no qualifying primary cancer on or before that assessment, the platelet-high/haemoglobin-low contrast will be more associated with CRC than with hematologic cancer during years 2–5 after assessment, will remain materially positive across prespecified first-assessment calendar eras, and will exceed the analogous CRC-versus-other-primary-cancer contrast.

This is a test of a descriptive, cancer-type-specific delayed association. The strongest rival predicts one or more of: a positive 0–2-year contrast but attenuation in years 2–5; similar CBC contrasts for other primary cancers; or calendar-era heterogeneity. A calendar-era check cannot identify clinical indication, and “other cancer” is a competing-burden specificity comparator, not a true negative control: CBC abnormalities may genuinely precede several cancers. A supportive result therefore narrows the ascertainment explanation but does not establish biology.

The clinical advance is a defensible boundary for interpretation. A calendar-stable, CRC-specific delayed contrast would justify external validation in indication-linked clinical data. A near-diagnostic-only, broad-cancer, or era-unstable result would argue against using a single research-assessment CBC as a general CRC-specific rule. Either result is more informative than a pooled association that leaves timing and competing burden conflated.

## Population, time and estimands

Include nonmissing `eid`, parseable T0, and all four instance-0 CBC values. Exclude any primary malignant registry code date on or before T0 using the same paired date/code rule. Do not condition the primary cohort on a future repeat CBC. Report all exclusions, complete-CBC missingness, calendar-era counts, prior-cancer counts and event-support counts before fitting.

Follow each participant from T0 to the first of the mutually exclusive event, T0+5 years, or 2022-12-31. Events are CRC, hematologic cancer, other primary malignant cancer, and death. If distinct cancer classes share the earliest date, assign an auditable `multi-class cancer tie` competing outcome rather than forcing a type contrast. If cancer and death share a date, assign the registry cancer event and report the tie count. This is a prespecified descriptive competing-risk convention, not a causal claim.

The primary delayed risk set begins at T0+2 for participants alive and event-free then and ends at the first event, T0+5 or 2022-12-31. The 0–2 window is a prespecified near-diagnostic context comparison. The primary model is also fit over the full 0–5 risk set with a 0–2/2–5 piecewise interval so that the delayed entry is not mistaken for an unconditional effect.

Within each window (w) and calendar era (e), fit target-specific CBC coefficients. Define:

`Delta_H,w,e = (beta_P,CRC,w,e - beta_P,HEME,w,e) - (beta_Hb,CRC,w,e - beta_Hb,HEME,w,e)`.

Define the competing-burden specificity contrast:

`Delta_O,w,e = (beta_P,CRC,w,e - beta_P,OTHER,w,e) - (beta_Hb,CRC,w,e - beta_Hb,OTHER,w,e)`.

The primary specificity estimand is `Q_2-5 = Delta_H,2-5 - Delta_O,2-5`, standardized to the development-era covariate distribution. Positive `Delta_H` means the platelet-high/haemoglobin-low direction is more CRC-associated than hematologic-associated; positive `Q` means it is more CRC-specific than the broad other-cancer comparator. Also report `D_H = Delta_H,0-2 - Delta_H,2-5`, era-specific `Delta_H`, and the corresponding other-cancer contrasts. Use log-hazard intervals and a prespecified 0.20 log-hazard difference as a material magnitude. The delayed result is explicitly conditional on surviving and remaining cancer-free to two years; it is not a natural-history effect, causal effect, or individual diagnostic probability.

## Methods compared on the same question

### Transparent baseline (primary)

Fit separate cause-specific piecewise Cox models for CRC, hematologic cancer, other primary cancer and death. The risk sets retain other cancer, death and multi-class ties as competing events. Inputs are the four instance-0 CBC z-scores, age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP, calendar era, 0–2/2–5 interval, and prespecified CBC-by-window and CBC-by-calendar-era interactions. Standardization parameters are learned in development only. Penalization is used only after recorded separation/convergence failure.

Estimate `Delta_H`, `Delta_O`, `Q`, and `D_H` by linear contrasts, with robust 95% intervals, proportional-hazard/time-varying-effect diagnostics, event counts and Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence at 2 and 5 years. The primary report uses the delayed 2–5 risk set and calendar-era standardization; the full 0–5 piecewise model reports the timing comparison. This baseline is scientifically preferred because the target contrast and risk-set conditioning are visible and auditable. It loses thresholds, nonlinear CBC interactions and flexible hazards.

### Learned alternative

Fit a discrete-time multitask gradient-boosted competing-risk model with separate hazards for CRC, hematologic cancer, other primary cancer and death, interval indicator, calendar era and exactly the same baseline CBC/covariate inputs. No later CBC, repeat-attendance indicator, registry field, test date, or incompatible table is allowed into this first-CBC model. Tune a small fixed grid only in rolling development folds.

On the final holdout, compute the same standardized `Delta_H`, `Delta_O`, `Q`, and `D_H` contrasts from cumulative-incidence predictions for a prespecified +1 SD platelet/−1 SD haemoglobin profile versus the development-set sex-specific mean profile, separately for 0–2 and 2–5 and within each era when support permits. Report finite-difference marginal effects, not feature-importance stories. The model’s scientific role is to reveal nonlinear CBC combinations or era-dependent thresholds that the Cox average can hide; a small AUC gain without a stable type-specific contrast is not evidence for the hypothesis.

### Split, uncertainty and calibration

Sort by T0 and eid and place the earliest 70% of participants in development and the latest 30% in the held-out test set; participants remain intact. All scaling, missingness rules, tuning, calibration, era standardization and repeat-attendance weights are fitted in development only. Report target-specific calibration intercept/slope and plots, cumulative-incidence Brier and log scores at 2 and 5 years, and time-dependent discrimination only secondarily. Use 500 participant bootstrap replicates for held-out predictions and `Delta/Q/D`; preserve convergence failures. If the held-out hematologic count is below 50, type-specific held-out metrics and `Q` are automatically inconclusive, regardless of point estimates; do not replace the temporal split with a favorable random split.

### Measurement/observation stress test

Define repeat attendance `R=1` only when `53-1.0` is parseable and strictly later than T0 and all four instance-1 CBC fields are present. Repeat analyses exclude cancer and death on or before T1 and use baseline CBC, four CBC changes, assessment gap and the same competing outcomes. Compare unweighted and stabilized baseline-only inverse-probability-of-repeat weighted estimates using baseline CBC, sex, age, BMI, smoking, CRP, eligibility indicators and first-assessment era; trim at fixed 1st/99th percentiles and report balance. This is a measured-selection sensitivity analysis, not causal repair. The parent audit found only 27 early hematologic events at the clean repeat landmark; any repeat analysis with fewer than 50 held-out hematologic events is automatically inconclusive and cannot overturn the first-CBC result. Instance 2 is sensitivity-only.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support for a delayed, cancer-type-specific association requires all of the following:

1. The delayed `Delta_H,2-5` has the prespecified positive direction, an informative interval excluding zero, and a point estimate at least 0.20 log-hazard units.
2. `Q_2-5` is positive with an informative interval excluding zero; the direction is not a generic other-cancer burden signal.
3. Era-specific estimates do not show a reversal or material instability: no supported era differs from the pooled delayed `Delta_H` by more than 0.20 log-hazard units. This is a stability check, not proof that ascertainment is absent.
4. The learned model reproduces the prespecified direction and type-specific pattern on the temporal holdout with acceptable target-specific calibration; improvement in discrimination alone is insufficient.
5. Repeat weighting does not materially change the direction, while its sparse-event limitation is reported.

Evidence supporting the ascertainment/proximity rival is a positive 0–2 `Delta_H` with material attenuation or reversal in 2–5, a broad or comparable `Delta_O`, calendar-era instability, or a large weighted-versus-unweighted repeat change. These results bound the interpretation to near-diagnostic or measured-observation association; they do not prove unmeasured indication bias.

Adverse evidence for the proposed specificity is a precise negative delayed `Delta_H` or `Q`, or a precise delayed pattern stronger for hematologic than CRC. A broad delayed signal shared by other primary cancers is adverse to a CRC-specific interpretation but may motivate a different broad-cancer question. Inconclusive evidence is a wide delayed interval spanning both no meaningful effect and 0.20, fewer than 50 held-out hematologic events, poor date/code support, unstable era strata, failed convergence, or calibration failure. An imprecise null is not a refutation.

Computationally checkable claims are cohort construction, date/code pairing, event timing, competing-risk contrasts, era stability, repeat-weight balance, temporal holdout calibration and uncertainty. The data cannot establish clinical indication, symptoms, test ordering, iron deficiency, occult bleeding, marrow disease, pathology, stage, molecular subtype, causal mechanism, symptomatic-population diagnostic accuracy, or clinical utility. Those require linked primary-care order/indication data, ferritin/iron and other clinical measurements, adjudicated cancer phenotypes, external validation and a prospective decision-linked study.

## Actual deliverable and alternatives

The experiment is complete only after it newly fits the transparent and learned models and produces: a locked cohort/missingness/event table; date/code and tie audit; calendar-era table; split manifest; `Delta_H`, `Delta_O`, `Q`, and `D_H` estimates with 500-bootstrap uncertainty; cumulative-incidence outputs; target-specific calibration/Brier/log outputs; era-stability tables; repeat-attendance balance/weighted sensitivity; and the prespecified other-cancer comparator. Conclusions must point to these computed outputs. Readiness or a fitted model alone is not completion.

The transparent Cox baseline is selected because it directly represents the delayed risk-set estimand and competing-cancer comparator. The gradient-boosted model is retained because clinically meaningful nonlinear CBC combinations and calendar interactions could be hidden by coefficient averaging; it is not retained for a small predictive gain. A neural sequence model or latent GP/state-space model is deferred: the source has only two broadly complete repeat CBC visits, repeat hematologic events are sparse, and more flexible state representations cannot recover clinical indication. Revisit only with richer dated clinical measurements and sufficient repeat target events. A model using future `R`, later CBCs, or `ukb672073` center fields is rejected as leakage or invalid namespace joining. A true disease-free negative control is not available; other primary cancer is explicitly treated as a burden/specificity comparator.

Measured discovery facts are the 4.2-second Parquet schema probe (`[research job]`) and 68.5-second selected-column audit (`[research job]`). Future full fitting is unverified but budgeted at 8–16 CPU cores, 32–64 GiB RAM and approximately 2–4 hours including person-period expansion, two model families, holdout predictions and bootstrap. No GPU is requested: this is a tabular competing-risk study, and GPU capacity is not a scientific requirement.

## Three key references

[K1] Sala et al. reports prediagnostic longitudinal CBC deviations in a retrospective, CRC-only Spanish cohort and explicitly calls for prospective external validation. It motivates dated CBC timing and a learned comparator; this proposal adds cancer-type competing risks, delayed-entry conditioning, calendar stability and other-cancer specificity.

[K2] Hampton et al. finds that CRC prediction studies often omit calibration and have limited validation. It motivates the temporal holdout and target-specific calibration; this proposal does not claim clinical deployment.

[K3] Brands et al. shows heterogeneity in symptomatic-referral test performance. It bounds the interpretation of UKB research-assessment CBCs because indication is unavailable; this proposal uses observable timing and competing burden as stress tests, not as a claim that indication is fixed.
