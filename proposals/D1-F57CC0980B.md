# Does early ventilatory load predict a transportable respiratory-support transition?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly construct an auditable adult eICU cohort and 30-minute respiratory ledger, then estimate whether early body-size-normalized ventilatory burden and respiratory mechanics are associated with a later, directly observed ventilator-to-non-ventilator respiratory-support transition.

Completion requires:

1. a frozen source/header/schema audit and cohort-flow table;
2. a leakage-safe 0–6 hour mechanics ledger;
3. an exact 6–48 hour competing-exit endpoint ledger;
4. out-of-fold development and hospital-held-out predictions from a tidal-volume-only baseline, a transparent mechanics-augmented model, and an ordered trajectory/state model;
5. standardized 48-hour transition cumulative-incidence contrasts, calibration and proper scoring with hospital-clustered uncertainty;
6. endpoint, timing, source-concordance and placebo falsification analyses; and
7. a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive and linked to saved computed outputs.

Discovery fitted no target-cohort model and claims no clinical result.

The expert seed proposes that ventilatory burden adjusted for body size and respiratory mechanics provides more information about liberation failure than tidal volume alone. The substantive adaptation is necessary because the local snapshot does not provide a validated extubation label: `respiratoryCare.ventendoffset` is zero in 865,224/865,381 audited rows. The primary endpoint is therefore explicitly a **recorded respiratory-support transition**, not extubation or ventilator-free days. This makes the question observable while preserving clinical relevance: it asks whether early load identifies patients unlikely to reach a documented lower-support state during the next 48 hours, and whether the association transports across hospitals.

## Evidence and unresolved hypothesis

The strongest claim supported before fitting is limited to field availability. The snapshot contains ICU-relative respiratory charting with `TV/kg IBW`, set and observed tidal volume, plateau pressure, PEEP, pressure support, FiO2, respiratory rate, O2 device and RT ventilator on/off labels; patient height, sex/gender, age, ICU disposition and hospital identifiers; and five-minute summarized vital observations. It also contains 1,511 stays with a broad early `O2 Device=Ventilator` feasibility signal and 764 with a later broad non-ventilator transition in a preliminary probe. These are not effect estimates and do not validate the semantics.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among adult ICU stays with a documented ventilator state during ICU minutes 0–360, adequate early respiratory mechanics coverage, and survival/observation to a six-hour landmark, greater early time-weighted ventilatory burden—especially persistent high `TV/kg IBW` together with high driving pressure (`Plateau Pressure – PEEP`)—is associated with a lower 48-hour cumulative incidence of a strictly defined recorded transition from a recent ventilator device state to a prespecified non-ventilator respiratory-support state, beyond a tidal-volume-only baseline; an ordered persistent-high/recovery trajectory will explain more transportable variation than scalar averages.

This is a prognostic/documentation hypothesis. It is not a claim that changing tidal volume, plateau pressure or PEEP causes liberation, nor that the endpoint is extubation.

Clinical importance: a reproducible early signal could inform when a patient merits a more deliberate respiratory reassessment or closer liberation surveillance. A null or nontransportable result would be clinically useful because it would argue against treating a scalar ventilatory-burden summary as a portable liberation-risk signal. A positive database association would still require chart/order adjudication and prospective impact testing before policy.

## Population, time zero and eligibility

Use eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`. Use one row per ICU stay keyed by `patientunitstayid`; retain repeated ICU stays but assign all stays sharing `uniquepid` to the same split. Primary population:

- adult `patient.age >= 18`, parsing `> 89` as 90 and retaining an age-censored indicator;
- ICU stay has nonmissing, nonnegative `unitdischargeoffset` and reaches minute 360; a missing offset is excluded from the primary analysis and reported in a sensitivity rather than treated as continued observation;
- at least one `respiratoryCharting` row with `respchartvaluelabel=O2 Device`, `respchartvalue=Ventilator`, and `respchartoffset` in [0,360];
- at least two distinct 30-minute bins in [0,360] with valid numeric respiratory-mechanics information, including at least one valid `TV/kg IBW` and at least one valid plateau/PEEP pair for the mechanics-augmented analysis;
- positive `admissionheight` is required for the primary body-size completeness audit, but the primary exposure uses the recorded `TV/kg IBW` field; the height requirement is relaxed only in a declared sensitivity that uses the source-provided normalized field without a height audit.

Time zero for prediction is ICU minute 360. Exposures use only `respchartoffset <= 360` and never `respchartentryoffset` as a clinical measurement time. Rows with negative offsets are excluded from exposure construction. Exact duplicate `respchartid` rows are deduplicated; same-label/same-bin values are summarized by the prespecified median after numeric parsing. Implausible values are excluded by fixed rules recorded before fitting: `TV/kg IBW <= 20`, plateau 5–60 cmH2O, PEEP 0–30 cmH2O, pressure support 0–40 cmH2O, FiO2 21–100 when percentage-like, and respiratory rate 1–80/min. Values such as FiO2 0.4 are converted to 40 only in a predeclared unit rule; ambiguous values are excluded and coverage reported. No last-observation carry-forward is used for exposure construction; a state sensitivity permits at most 60 minutes and reports the change.

## Exact observable endpoint and competing states

Define the eligible non-ventilator device set before fitting as:

`None (Room air)`, `Nasal cannula`, `High flow nasal cannula`, `Heated high flow nasal cannula`, `Bi-PAP`, `CPAP`, `Non-rebreather mask`, `Face mask`, `Aerosol mask`, `Simple mask`, `High flow mask`, `Venturi mask`, `Trach mask/collar`, `T-Piece`, `Face tent`, `Blow-by`, and `Oxyhood`.

Exclude `Ventilator`, `ETT`, `Ambubag`, `Other (Comment)`, blank and unrecognized values from the primary endpoint. The primary event is the first `O2 Device` row in (360, 2880] whose value is in the eligible set and whose immediately preceding nonmissing `O2 Device` row for that stay is `Ventilator`, with a maximum preceding-to-event gap of 240 minutes. This is a recorded ventilator-to-lower-support transition; it is not called extubation. A sensitivity requires a contemporaneous `RT Vent On/Off=Off` or `Suspended` row within ±120 minutes, but the sparse RT-off label cannot define the primary endpoint.

Follow each stay from minute 360 until the first of:

- the primary recorded transition;
- ICU unit discharge with `unitdischargestatus=Expired` or `unitdischargelocation=Death` (recorded unit death);
- alive ICU unit discharge/transfer before transition;
- minute 2880;
- unknown/missing unit disposition or an observation boundary that cannot be defended.

Death and discharge ties at the same minute take precedence over the respiratory transition. The primary estimand is the hospital-standardized 48-hour cumulative incidence of the recorded transition with recorded unit death and alive unit exit as competing exits. A stay with no post-landmark O2-device row is not a negative physiologic finding; it is handled as observation loss/unknown in the competing-state sensitivity and is never silently coded as persistent ventilation.

Secondary, only if support is adequate, is **recorded re-escalation**: after a qualifying non-ventilator transition, a later `O2 Device=Ventilator` row by minute 2880. This is not part of the primary event and does not establish reintubation.

## Matched baseline and trajectory/state alternative

### B0: transparent tidal-volume baseline

Use the identical eligible stays, endpoint, 80/20 hospital-held-out split, covariate set and uncertainty procedure for every model. From the 0–360-minute ledger, calculate:

- median and interquartile range of valid `TV/kg IBW`;
- proportion of observed 30-minute bins with `TV/kg IBW > 8`;
- number of valid bins and respiratory-documentation coverage;
- baseline age, gender, admission height, unit type/admission source and early vital summaries.

Fit a regularized cause-specific discrete-time logistic model on 30-minute outcome intervals from 360 to 2880, or equivalently a pooled complementary-log-log model, with recorded transition as the event and death/alive exit as competing causes. The primary B0 question is whether the scalar TV/body-size signal transports; no tree search or post hoc threshold tuning is permitted.

### B1: transparent mechanics-augmented model

Add only predeclared respiratory mechanics features from the same early bins: median driving pressure from paired plateau and PEEP, proportion of bins with driving pressure >15 cmH2O, median PEEP, median FiO2, median pressure support, median total RR, and their valid-bin counts. Pair plateau and PEEP only within the same 30-minute bin; do not infer a driving pressure from unmatched timestamps. B1 tests the seed’s substantive claim beyond TV/kg IBW and is the primary baseline-to-mechanics comparison.

### M2: ordered respiratory-state alternative

Use the same rows, early window, endpoint and split. In each 30-minute bin, define an observed state:

- low-load: valid TV/kg IBW <=8, driving pressure <=15 and FiO2 <60%;
- high-load: valid TV/kg IBW >8 or driving pressure >15 or FiO2 >=60%;
- missing/uncertain: no valid state or incompatible units.

Fit a prespecified ordered piecewise-exponential/discrete-time state model with initial state, high-state persistence, high-to-low recovery transitions, low-to-high worsening transitions, longest high run, last observed state and coverage. Missing is a state/coverage signal, not “no ventilation.” A sensitivity uses only bins with both TV/kg IBW and paired mechanics. The alternative’s scientific purpose is to distinguish persistent high load from a transient high load with the same mean; B0/B1 cannot retain that order. The learned component, if the solver uses a penalized state-transition model, is restricted to shrinkage of these predeclared states and not free representation learning.

Compare B0, B1 and M2 using hospital-held-out 48-hour CIF, integrated Brier score, log score, calibration slope/intercept, calibration plots, and standardized CIF contrasts. Report the incremental B1-versus-B0 and M2-versus-B1 differences, not only the best score. Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates for primary intervals, refitting within the development role; repeat clustering by `uniquepid` where feasible. Do not recalibrate or tune thresholds on held-out hospitals.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following:

- the prespecified 25th-to-75th early-burden contrast changes the 48-hour transition CIF by at least 2 absolute percentage points in the adverse direction, with a two-sided hospital-cluster interval excluding zero;
- B1 improves held-out proper scoring over B0 without material calibration deterioration, and the mechanics direction is concordant across the development and held-out hospital roles;
- M2 shows a coherent persistent-high/recovery contrast and improves or preserves held-out calibration/proper score over B1, with at least 10 development and 10 held-out hospitals and adequate endpoint support;
- the direction is stable under strict versus permissive O2-device gap rules, entry-time timing, unit-death ties, height completeness, and source-concordance sensitivities.

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversed burden contrast, no held-out improvement, worsening calibration, a state effect that disappears after coverage adjustment, a result driven by one hospital or one label source, or an association that is equally strong for a placebo/non-respiratory documentation endpoint. A positive TV-only score gain without mechanics or trajectory specificity does not support the seed’s substantive claim.

Inconclusive means fewer than 100 strict transitions or fewer than 10 supporting hospitals, unstable bootstrap/convergence, inadequate overlap, unknown-disposition dependence, large sensitivity to the 240-minute preceding-device gap, or inability to distinguish device transitions from documentation loss. Inconclusive is not a null clinical conclusion.

Prespecified falsifications:

1. Permute respiratory measurement times within hospital and early-documentation strata while preserving values and counts. The trajectory/order increment should disappear.
2. Permute endpoint timestamps within hospital and baseline documentation strata. Any persistent effect indicates leakage or hospital workflow artifact.
3. Refit with `respchartentryoffset` as the measurement clock and compare with `respchartoffset`; a large reversal weakens temporal interpretation.
4. Require `RT Vent On/Off=Off/Suspended` near the transition, and separately require a respiratoryCare airway-type change. These are specificity sensitivities, not the primary endpoint, because support is sparse and `ventendoffset` is unreliable.
5. Repeat after replacing the endpoint with first routine alive unit exit; a similarly large association would suggest general discharge/documentation selection rather than respiratory liberation.
6. Restrict to stays with at least one `O2 Device=Ventilator` in [0,360] and a recent pre-event Ventilator row, and report no-post-device observation separately.

Computationally checkable claims include source and schema hashes, live headers, joins, exact label dictionaries, unit filters, binning, exposure time boundary, endpoint predecessor rule, competing exits, split integrity, fitted predictions, scoring, calibration, bootstrap and permutation outputs. Every conclusion must cite a saved output and uncertainty interval.

Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether `TV/kg IBW` is calculated with correct sex/height semantics, whether plateau pressure was measured under static conditions, whether O2-device transition equals extubation or simply a charting change, whether tracheostomy/T-piece states represent liberation, whether missingness means no care, and whether a respiratory-support risk review changes patient outcomes. The configured data lack reliable extubation/reintubation adjudication, ventilator-free days, raw waveforms, complete ventilator order/administration/indication data, patient preferences, post-ICU outcomes, causal treatment assignment and intervention utility. No causal treatment, mortality, extubation, or policy claim may be made from this experiment alone.
