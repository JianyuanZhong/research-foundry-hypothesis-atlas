# Early Post-TACE Hepatic Deterioration as a Prognostic Marker in HCC

## Question
In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who undergo transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does a composite measure of early hepatic deterioration during the first 7 days after the procedure predict subsequent hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days, after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and post-procedure observation intensity?

## Clinical importance
TACE is a common locoregional treatment for HCC, but a subset of patients experience early hepatic deterioration that may signal a need for escalation to systemic therapy or closer surveillance. Identifying these patients could inform treatment-selection decisions and resource allocation. The unresolved question is whether the early laboratory trajectory itself carries prognostic information beyond baseline liver function, or whether it merely reflects more intensive post-procedure monitoring.

## Evidence and closest prior work
- [K1] Decraecker et al. (2026) review the limitations of Child-Pugh scoring and the potential value of dynamic liver function monitoring and ALBI grade in HCC patients. They do not establish a validated early post-TACE deterioration marker.
- [K2] Yang et al. (2026) develop the AAPS (AFP-ALBI-PIVKA-II-serum albumin) scoring system for HCC patients receiving TACE. They do not specifically address early post-TACE hepatic deterioration as a dynamic prognostic marker.
- [K3] Llovet et al. (2022) provide a comprehensive review of HCC management, including TACE and systemic therapy. They do not address the specific question of early post-TACE hepatic deterioration as a prognostic marker.

## Data bindings
- Source: HCC dataset (exact source paths and table schemas in datasets/README.md)
- Population: 20,064 TACE patients, ~16,000 with complete early labs, 817 outcome events
- Early hepatic deterioration composite: bilirubin, albumin, INR (ALT/AST excluded due to zero early rows)
- Outcome: hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death within 90 days
- Adjustment: baseline liver function, tumor burden, post-procedure observation intensity

## Analysis
- Baseline: logistic regression using pre-TACE liver function and tumor burden
- Alternative: time-aware survival model using the early hepatic deterioration composite
- Temporal train/test split, bootstrap uncertainty, calibration
- Sensitivity and negative-control analyses for observation intensity

## Falsification
- Supportive: significant predictive improvement of the time-aware model over the baseline, with calibration and uncertainty estimates
- Adverse: no improvement after adjustment for observation intensity
- Inconclusive: insufficient events or wide confidence intervals
