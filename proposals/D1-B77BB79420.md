# Complementary branch: lagged, component-specific audit of discordant-AKI recovery

Status: candidate-ready proposed Harbor experiment; no cohort scan, model fit, or scientific result was run.

Parent: [prior hypothesis]. This branch is a complementary evolution, not a reproduction of a cited paper and not a claim that the proposed result is already observed.

## 1. Scientific opening

The parent resolves the largest ambiguity in the earlier death-or-ICU-RRT composite by separating hospital death from ICU-recorded RRT. A remaining ambiguity is temporal: a discordant state is constructed from observations ending at the landmark, while the first outcome can occur immediately after that endpoint. The contrast could therefore be a contemporaneous severity/recording marker, a genuinely reproducible short-term prognostic signal, or an artifact of documentation timing and conditioning on survival and continued ICU observation.

The expert seed mimic-08 asks whether a creatinine rise during improving versus persistent congestion has different consequences. Its useful scientific opening is the distinction between recovery as a physiologic pattern and recovery as a severity/measurement mixture. It leaves the MIMIC phenotype, time window, and congestion proxy unresolved. Seed mimic-10 asks a related but different question about residual instability before ICU discharge and later ICU return/death. I inspected both cards and do not import mimic-10 as a second outcome: ICU readmission is a discharge-selection question that would change the estimand and requires a separate branch. Here its caution about discharge destination and competing death is retained as a rival explanation for the ICU-RRT component.

The closest inspected evidence bounds, rather than proves, the question. [K1] reports that creatinine eGFR can overestimate measured kidney function during prolonged critical illness and muscle loss, supporting a creatinine-measurement rival but not this phenotype. [K2] shows that urine thresholds and windows change AKI mortality prediction, supporting explicit opportunity and window sensitivity but not recovery discordance. [K3] associates positive recorded fluid balance with AKI/mortality while noting missing insensible losses and clinician-dependent KRT, supporting a severity/congestion rival but not true volume status.

The unresolved claim is prognostic:

> Among adults with AKI who are alive and in the index ICU at a prespecified 48-hour landmark, does a discordant recovery state measured with a six-hour documentation gap predict seven-day hospital death or ICU-recorded RRT after the gap, beyond a transparent fixed-summary competing-risk baseline and after accounting for pre-landmark severity and observation opportunity?

The primary scientific comparison is the same patients and same component endpoints under (i) a contemporaneous parent window ending at the landmark and (ii) a lagged window ending six hours before the landmark. A component contrast that persists with the lag, across 48- and 72-hour landmarks and supported observation strata, is more consistent with a reproducible prognostic signal than with a last-minute charting artifact. A contrast that exists only contemporaneously, only for ICU-RRT, or only among highly observed index-ICU survivors is weakened and is not re-described as intrinsic kidney dysfunction.

This remains an observational prognostic test. It cannot establish a treatment effect, causal mechanism, true GFR, total-body volume, complete hospital RRT, or transportability.

## 2. Actual scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct, estimate, and save:

1. An auditable flow from AKI onset through 24, 48, and 72 hours: death, ICU exit, ICU-recorded RRT, missing creatinine, urine/input opportunity, lag-gap events, and exposure states.
2. At 48 hours as the primary landmark, and 72 hours as confirmation, contemporaneous and six-hour-lagged exposure definitions evaluated on the identical landmark risk set. The 24-hour result is a prespecified early-landmark sensitivity with a shorter lagged window, not a replacement primary.
3. For each exposure version, separate seven-day cumulative incidence and standardized risk differences for hospital death and ICU-recorded RRT, with their competing events and uncertainty. The mixed death-or-ICU-RRT union is exploratory only.
4. A selection audit showing who is lost before the landmark and during the six-hour lag gap, including discharge destination and the outcome rates of the lost groups where ascertainable.
5. A pre-landmark observation-process diagnostic: non-renal documentation density, urine/input opportunity, charttime/storetime discordance, and the explicit unknown state. No post-outcome documentation is an adjustment variable.
6. A transparent fixed-summary competing-risk baseline and a sequence model using the same pre-landmark information, identical patient-level split, and held-out endpoint-specific calibration/Brier/discrimination and risk-contrast outputs.
7. A manifest mapping every estimate to landmark, lag rule, risk set, fields, event clock, competing-event rule, model, counts, uncertainty, and interpretation.

Completion requires saved computed flow tables, component-specific cumulative-incidence/risk-difference estimates, uncertainty, diagnostics, held-out model outputs, and conclusions linked to those outputs. Readiness, a coefficient, or a favorable prediction metric is not completion.

## 3. Population and temporal estimands

Use adults with `anchor_age >= 18`, the first eligible ICU stay per hospital admission, and `intime < outtime`, joining ICU stay to admission on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`. Select the first valid AKI onset `t0` within the index stay using the parent KDIGO-like rule: a valid creatinine increase of at least 0.3 mg/dL over a prior 48 hours or at least 1.5 times the valid minimum in the prior seven days. Use `hosp/labevents` itemid 50912 as primary and 52546 as sensitivity, numeric `valuenum`, valid blood chemistry units, and clinical `charttime`. When no prior observation supports onset, retain an explicitly labeled admission-baseline sensitivity; do not invent a baseline. Exclude established RRT before `t0`.

For each landmark `L in {24, 48, 72}` hours after `t0`, define the landmark risk set `R_L` as alive, in the index ICU, without incident ICU-recorded RRT before `t0+L`, and with valid admission/ICU timestamps. Keep all people outside `R_L` in the flow; never assign them a later exposure. The primary estimand is the 48-hour survivor/in-index-ICU prognostic estimand, not an onset-cohort or causal estimand. The 72-hour estimand tests temporal replication. The 24-hour estimand is an early timing sensitivity.

### Exposure versions

For the contemporaneous version `C_L`, use observations in `[t0+L-24h, t0+L)`. For the lagged version `G_L`, use observations in `[t0+L-30h, t0+L-6h)`, a 24-hour window ending six hours before the landmark. The window is intersected with the index stay and `t0`; therefore `G_24` has only 18 hours available and is labeled a shortened sensitivity. `G_48` is primary and `G_72` is confirmation. The six-hour interval `[t0+L-6h,t0+L)` is an explicit silent gap: report death, ICU exit, and RRT during it and do not backfill the gap with future values. Both exposure versions are evaluated on the same `R_L`; the comparison is a temporal robustness check, not a causal effect of waiting.

Within each window, define recovery as peak valid creatinine through the window minus the latest valid creatinine in the final six hours of that window being at least 0.3 mg/dL, with a peak/landmark-window creatinine ratio <=0.8 as a sensitivity. If the final six-hour creatinine is absent, label recovery unknown; do not substitute a later value. Define low urine using primary numeric mL output and weight-normalized rate, with a prespecified threshold of <0.5 mL/kg/h averaged across the required bins. Define positive recorded balance as the prespecified recorded-input proxy minus direct primary urine output, retaining its continuous value and a binary threshold selected before looking at outcomes. The primary phenotype is the mutually exclusive state set: concordant recovery, low-urine-only, positive-balance-only, both, unknown/insufficient, and no recovery; the inherited binary discordance is low urine OR positive recorded balance among recovered/classifiable patients. Sensitivities use 2-of-4 and 4-of-4 urine bins and a ratio-only recovery rule. Unknown is never imputed to normal or discordant.

### Outcomes and competing events

All outcome windows are half-open `[t0+L, t0+L+7d)`.

- Hospital death `D_L` is `admissions.deathtime` in the window and before a valid alive `dischtime`; `hospital_expire_flag` is a consistency check, not a substitute event time. Missing or internally inconsistent death times are reported as exceptions. Alive hospital discharge is the competing event for this hospital-death estimand; alive and hospitalized at day 7 is administrative censoring.
- ICU-recorded RRT `R_L` is the first qualifying `procedureevents.starttime` in the window, while alive and before index `icustays.outtime`, using itemids 225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809, and 225955. ICU exit and death before RRT are competing events. This endpoint is not all-hospital RRT: ward-only and post-index-ICU procedures are unavailable.
- The inherited death-or-ICU-RRT union is exploratory and must report both component counts and its mixed ascertainment. It cannot be the sole basis for a renal-support conclusion.

For `D_L`, use Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence for death versus alive discharge and, as a model-based secondary, six-hour cause-specific hazards. For `R_L`, use Aalen-Johansen for ICU-RRT versus ICU exit and death before RRT. A death after ICU exit is not a second competing event for the ICU-RRT clock: ICU exit is the first ineligible state. Report the exact first-event ordering and do not treat ICU exit as ordinary independent censoring.

## 4. Selection, timing, and observation-process audit

Produce a state-flow table at `t0`, each landmark, and each six-hour gap. It must show alive/in-ICU, hospital discharge, ICU exit, death, pre-landmark RRT, missing creatinine, urine opportunity, recorded-input opportunity, and every phenotype state. For the same `R_L`, compare contemporaneous versus lagged exposure counts and unknown rates. A signal that appears only after large losses of high-risk patients or after dropping unknowns is selection-sensitive.

For every landmark/exposure/component, report hospital death, alive discharge, ICU-RRT, ICU exit before RRT, death before RRT, day-7 completion, and death-time exceptions. ICU discharge does not erase later hospital death for `D_L`, but it ends ascertainment of `R_L`. Use patient-cluster bootstrap or an influence-function method for repeated landmarks; the primary 48-hour contrast must not treat the 24- and 72-hour estimates as independent replications.

Observation features are constructed only before the corresponding exposure-window end:

- Primary urine is numeric mL in `icu/outputevents` itemids 226559 Foley, 226560 Void, 226561 Condom Cath, 226563 Suprapubic, and 226567 Straight Cath. Numeric zero is observed zero; an absent six-hour bin is unknown.
- Exclude 226564/226565 nephrostomy, 226627/226631 OR/PACU urine, 226566/227489 GU-irrigant channels, text estimate 226713, and Apache-derived 227519 from the primary phenotype; retain them for measurement-error sensitivity.
- Count distinct six-hour bins with a primary urine record. Require 3 of 4 bins primarily; report 2-of-4 and 4-of-4 sensitivities. Convert to mL/kg/h with the nearest valid weight at or before the window end within 48 hours using `icu/chartevents` items 224639 (kg), 226512 (kg), and 226531 (lb), with units verified in `icu/d_items`.
- For recorded input, use `icu/inputevents` rows overlapping the window. Apply a deterministic mutually exclusive rule: use numeric `amount` when `amountuom` is mL; otherwise use numeric `totalamount` when `totalamountuom` is mL; never sum both fields. For a valid continuous `starttime<endtime`, prorate the selected amount by overlap duration; for an instantaneous row use it once. Keep non-mL, missing, open-bag, and item-composition indicators rather than silently converting them. Recorded balance is a charted proxy, not true fluid balance.
- Count creatinine, urine, input, weight/CVP, and non-renal chart/lab observations by four six-hour bins using `charttime`; `storetime` ordering is a labeled documentation-delay sensitivity, not a claim of true availability. Non-renal density is non-creatinine `labevents` plus non-weight/CVP `chartevents`, with urine/input opportunity counts separate.
- Prespecify a process-only diagnostic: replace kidney discordance by quartiles of pre-window non-renal documentation density and estimate the same two component risks. This is a diagnostic for acuity/recording intensity, not a negative-control outcome and not evidence that documentation causes death. If its gradient is comparable to the kidney contrast and the lagged kidney contrast attenuates after process stratification, the clinical interpretation is weakened.

A second falsification is the six-hour lag test itself. If a strong parent contrast collapses when the final six hours are withheld, this supports co-temporality or late documentation as an explanation. If death and ICU-RRT behave differently, preserve that distinction. A stable hospital-death signal with no ICU-RRT signal is a death-risk finding, not proof of renal support; an ICU-RRT-only signal with strong process/destination dependence is surveillance-sensitive.

## 5. Baseline versus substantive learned alternative

### Transparent fixed-summary baseline

The primary analysis is an auditable fixed-summary competing-risk model fit separately for each landmark and exposure version. Inputs are fixed summaries available no later than the exposure-window end: age, sex, admission type, ICU timing, onset AKI stage, baseline creatinine, peak and latest creatinine, creatinine slope, urine rate and observed-bin count, recorded-input amount and item composition, weight, exploratory CVP, prior support/RRT indicators, pre-window severity summaries, non-renal documentation density, and missingness/unknown indicators. The model uses six-hour cause-specific discrete-time hazards with separate heads for death, alive hospital discharge, ICU-RRT, and ICU exit/death as appropriate; ridge-penalized logistic terms with prespecified splines are acceptable, but model form and penalty selection must be fixed using training data only.

Also report unadjusted state-specific Aalen-Johansen risks and standardized model risks. The estimand is the standardized seven-day cumulative-incidence difference between inherited discordance and concordance, and between each component state and concordance, over the observed `R_L`. Severity adjustment is prognostic standardization, not confounding control. It must not use post-landmark data or model predictions as covariates.

### Learned temporal alternative

Fit a multi-task GRU or temporal convolution to the same six-hour sequence and masks used to make the fixed summaries, from `t0` through the lagged-window end. Channels are creatinine values/masks, primary urine values/masks, item-level input/output components, weight, CVP, support/RRT indicators, non-renal observation counts, and missingness. Separate discrete hazard heads represent hospital death, alive discharge, ICU-RRT, and ICU exit/death; no post-landmark or future outcome information is visible. The learned model’s scientific role is to test whether ordering, persistence, and transitions within the window distinguish the same component estimands after the summary baseline has been specified. It is not evidence of mechanism.

Use a subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split with all admissions for a subject in one split. Freeze exposure definitions and outcome clocks before fitting. Evaluate on the identical held-out test patients: component-specific cumulative-incidence calibration, calibration slope/intercept, time-dependent Brier score, cause-specific discrimination, uncertainty by patient bootstrap, and discordant-versus-concordant and persistent-versus-resolving risk contrasts. Require a prespecified clinically material improvement in calibration or a reproducible lag-versus-contemporaneous trajectory distinction; a small AUROC increase alone does not justify a scientific claim. If event support or sequence completeness is inadequate, defer the learned fit and retain the fixed-summary analysis.

The alternative adds information the baseline discards—whether a creatinine fall preceded or followed the urine/balance change, persistence versus a one-bin spike, and whether missingness itself is temporally patterned. It cannot add unmeasured GFR, adjudicated fluid status, treatment intent, or ward-RRT capture. This is a scientific method comparison, not complexity for its own sake.

## 6. Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires: (a) a clinically material component-specific contrast at 48 hours with directionally concordant 72-hour confirmation; (b) similar direction under the six-hour lag; (c) survival in well-supported severity and observation strata; (d) no domination by unknown-state exclusion; and (e) if a temporal claim is made, persistent discordance having a consistent component pattern beyond resolving discordance. Such evidence supports only a reproducible prognostic association for the named component. It does not establish intrinsic kidney injury, congestion, a harmless creatinine rise, or treatment benefit.

Adverse evidence includes collapse under the lag, reversal across landmarks, a contrast confined to sparse or highly documented cells, an unknown state with materially different risk, process-only gradients as large as the kidney contrast, a union signal driven only by ICU-RRT surveillance, or outcome patterns explained by ICU exit/discharge selection. This weakens the discordant-recovery interpretation but does not prove normal kidney function or no treatment effect.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse RRT or transition cells, wide intervals spanning clinically important benefit and harm, incompatible death-time records, large lag-gap loss, low observation opportunity, no stable concordant comparator, or a learned-model gain too small to alter component interpretation. A lagged null is not evidence that the phenotype has no value if precision is inadequate.

Computationally checkable claims are cohort construction, exact joins, timestamps, item filters, exposure and unknown counts, flow states, competing-event counts, risk estimates, bootstrap intervals, and held-out model metrics. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for true kidney function, total-body volume, bedside urine completeness, treatment intent, goals of care, complete ward RRT, mechanism, causal effects, and external validity.

## 8. Compute and method decision

The future solver planning envelope in `inputs.json` is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds; this is a planning envelope, not an authorization or a measured runtime. A bounded fit can use the same 16 CPUs and memory for a small GRU/temporal-convolution model; an allocated A100 may accelerate repeated fitting, but GPU use is not scientifically required. Ordinary shells have no CUDA allocation; if acceleration is selected, request `gpus:1` through managed job execution and use the allocated `cuda:0`. Measured facts are the configured A100 hardware and solver envelope; branch-specific training time and convergence are unverified. Selection reason: retain the baseline as primary for auditable component inference; retain the learned alternative because temporal ordering/persistence is scientifically relevant to the lag question, not because it is more complex.

Revisit the learned model only if it is calibrated and changes the component/lag interpretation on held-out patients. Revisit a more ambitious causal or latent-physiology model only with adjudicated true GFR, complete fluid collection, treatment intent, ward-RRT capture, and a defensible discharge process. Abandon this direction after a well-powered, observation-complete validation shows no reproducible component or lagged contrast.

## 10. Alternatives not chosen and revisit evidence

- The parent contemporaneous window is retained as the direct baseline, not discarded; the new six-hour-lag version is the complementary temporal audit.
- The 48-hour death-or-ICU-RRT union remains exploratory because its components have different observation clocks.
- Death alone is not sufficient because it would discard the bounded ICU-support question; RRT alone is not sufficient because it is ICU-surveillance-limited.
- Ordinary censoring of ICU exit for RRT is rejected; ICU exit and death before RRT are competing first states.
- Inverse-probability correction for informative discharge is deferred: complete ward-RRT capture, clinician intent, and a defensible discharge model are unavailable.
- A single sequence model as the primary analysis is rejected: prediction cannot resolve measurement or ascertainment, and a small predictive gain would not be a scientific advance.
- The mimic-10 ICU-readmission direction is deferred as a distinct question requiring explicit post-ICU follow-up, destination/treatment-limitation variables, and its own three-work evidence package.
- A causal diuresis/decongestion analysis is not selected from mimic-08 because treatment intent and complete fluid exposure are unavailable; the current study tests phenotype prognosis, not treatment safety.

Revisit the lagged branch if the primary 48-hour cohort has adequate event and observation support, the lag gap is not dominated by exclusions, and the component clocks can be estimated with clinically useful precision. Continue to a learned temporal fit only if event support passes readiness and the fixed baseline leaves a prespecified ordering/persistence uncertainty. Revise toward a pure measurement-validation study if component contrasts disagree or process-only gradients dominate. Defer or abandon if a well-powered observation-complete validation finds no stable component signal.
