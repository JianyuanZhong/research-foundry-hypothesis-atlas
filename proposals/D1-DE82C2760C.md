# Working proposal: discordant kidney recovery after ICU AKI — episode 2 repair

Status: proposed Harbor experiment; no scientific result has been computed in discovery.

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must construct one prespecified, survivor-landmark cohort and produce:

1. A transparent primary analysis estimating the standardized in-hospital 7-day risk difference for discordant versus concordant observed recovery, with a cohort flow, missingness table, component outcomes, bootstrap uncertainty, and prespecified sensitivities.
2. A secondary, substantive temporal alternative trained and evaluated on the same eligible population and outcome, with patient-level splitting, held-out calibration/Brier/AUROC/AUPRC, and a held-out phenotype-specific risk contrast.

Completion requires saved computed outputs linked to the cohort, exposure, model, uncertainty, and falsification files. Readiness, a coefficient, or a favorable prediction score is not completion.

## Question, hypothesis, and clinical importance

After ICU AKI, a falling serum creatinine can be interpreted as recovery. The unresolved question is whether that interpretation is unsafe when urine output and recorded fluid balance do not improve at the same time.

Hypothesis: among adults with a first eligible ICU stay who develop creatinine-defined AKI, survive and remain in the ICU to a prespecified 48-hour post-onset landmark, and have creatinine recovery, those with low primary-set urine output or positive net documented fluid balance at the landmark have higher subsequent in-hospital death or incident kidney-replacement therapy risk than those with both urine recovery and non-positive balance.

The estimand is the standardized prognostic risk difference:
RD = P(in-hospital death or incident RRT within 7 days of tL | discordant) − P(same | concordant),
among eligible creatinine-recovery landmark survivors. This is an association, not an intervention effect, mechanism, measured GFR, or bedside rule. It could change whether creatinine decline alone is considered sufficient for de-escalating renal surveillance or transition planning after ICU AKI, while identifying whether prospective measurement validation is warranted.

The closest competing explanations are: current severity and treatment intensity cause both the phenotype and outcome; fluid administration dilutes creatinine; reduced creatinine generation from muscle loss makes the fall misleading; and urine/weight documentation is selectively denser in deteriorating patients. The design distinguishes these by pre-landmark adjustment, continuous balance/weight sensitivities, observation-density covariates/weighting, negative controls, and strict temporal leakage checks. No remaining association identifies a mechanism.

## Evidence boundary and advance

The strongest inspected evidence supports that creatinine may overestimate measured kidney function during prolonged critical illness in association with muscle loss [K1]; urine-output thresholds and their windows can change AKI prognostic discrimination, including in MIMIC-IV, but that is not this recovery phenotype [K2]; and positive fluid balance around ICU AKI is associated with adverse outcomes in an external prospective cohort, with severity/treatment confounding [K3].

The unresolved claim is narrower: at a fixed post-onset landmark among patients who show a creatinine fall, does low charted urine output or positive charted balance identify a reproducibly higher-risk observed-recovery phenotype than creatinine plus physiologic recovery? This proposal adds a temporally anchored comparison, an explicit risk-difference estimand, exact MIMIC bindings, observation/missingness falsification, and a predeclared test of whether trajectory ordering adds information beyond fixed summaries. It does not reproduce [K1]-[K3], and it cannot establish true filtration, dilution, tubular injury, or treatment benefit.

## Analysis and uncertainty

Fit nested primary models on the same eligible cohort:
A) creatinine-only recovery plus pre-landmark covariates;
B) add urine and balance phenotype;
C) add continuous balance, weight change, pre-landmark severity/treatment, observation density and missingness.
Use standardized predictions to estimate RD on the analysis population, patient-level bootstrap intervals, and calibration. Covariates are age, sex, admission type, baseline creatinine, AKI stage/peak before tL, ICU elapsed time, pre-tL vasopressor/ventilation/diuretic/support summaries, weight change proxies, CVP exploratory value, counts/time-since-last measurements, and missingness. Treat this as prognostic adjustment; do not call it causal identification. Fit imputation, scaling, observation weights and regularization only in training folds when using the learned branch.

Report unadjusted, pre-landmark severity/treatment-adjusted, and observation-weighted contrasts. Use cause-specific models and a cumulative-incidence sensitivity for discharge alive; never hide the death/RRT components behind the composite. The primary claim is supported only if a clinically meaningful excess risk is directionally stable across creatinine assay, baseline, urine source/threshold, weight age, balance scale, missingness, and discharge/competing-event analyses, with adequate cell/event support.

## Baseline versus substantive learned alternative

The simple baseline is primary because it directly answers whether the observed recovery phenotype changes risk and remains interpretable. It uses fixed tL summaries and nested additions above.

The alternative is a masked temporal GRU (or an equivalently specified small sequence model if GRU software is unavailable) over 6-hour bins from t0-24h through tL. Channels are creatinine, urine volume/rate, input/output volume, weight, CVP, BUN/electrolytes/albumin/lactate when available, vital-sign summaries, ventilation/vasopressor/diuretic/RRT indicators, ICU elapsed time, and observation/missingness counts. The target is Y7d; no post-tL information, notes, future diagnoses, or outcome proxies enter. Split by a deterministic hash of subject_id into 70/15/15 train/validation/test, keeping each subject in one split. Compare test Brier, AUROC, AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope, patient-bootstrap intervals, and the held-out standardized phenotype contrast. Ablate creatinine-only, creatinine+urine, creatinine+urine+balance/weight, and full channels. This can reveal whether ordering and joint trajectories, rather than one fixed summary, distinguish the rival explanations; a small AUC gain alone is not a scientific advance.

The learned branch is conditional on audited repeated-measurement support and both phenotype cells having adequate events. If not, defer it and report why; do not change the primary question. Planning estimates are unverified: CPU tabular fit/bootstrap approximately 4–16 cores, 16–64 GiB and 0.5–2 hours after feature extraction; small sequence fit approximately 1–6 hours CPU or 1–3 hours on one allocated A100. Discovery used no GPU and no model was trained. The future solver envelope from inputs is 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs and 28800 seconds; GPU use is optional and must be requested explicitly.

## Falsification and interpretation

A post-tL exposure or outcome-proxy model, cross-row/cross-subject split, or use of post-tL measurements is a computational failure. A strong association with preceding outcomes indicates temporal leakage. A strong discordance-to-future-documentation association, or disappearance after observation weighting, supports ascertainment bias rather than the clinical interpretation. Permuting event timing within subject should remove the prespecified temporal association. Dictionary/unit ambiguity or a guessed item map fails readiness.

Supportive result: stable, nontrivial excess Y7d risk in discordant recovery, adequate held-out calibration/cells, and persistence after severity/treatment/observation checks would support confirmatory prospective validation of enhanced renal monitoring and measurement studies; it would still be prognostic only.

Adverse result: null/reversal or attenuation after observation/severity checks weakens the claim that creatinine-only recovery identifies a clinically important risk state; it does not prove creatinine is valid or fluid/urine management is harmless.

Inconclusive result: sparse cells/events, wide intervals spanning clinically meaningful harm, unstable thresholds, or missing/unreliable weights/balance means MIMIC cannot resolve the question. Do not call an imprecise null refutation.

MIMIC cannot adjudicate measured GFR, cystatin-C discordance, true total-body volume, complete urine collection, muscle mass/creatinine generation, tubular injury, clinician intent/goals of care, RRT indication, or causal treatment effects. These require biomarkers/measured clearance, body composition and fluid validation, expert chart adjudication, prospective synchronized measurement, and external validation.

## Demonstration and seed dispositions

The natural-history/Delphi demonstration was inspected through the available article text and methods guidance; its learned dated disease histories motivate testing temporal order against a fixed baseline, but UKB/Danish reproduction data are not the MIMIC question. The cancer/Oncoformer demonstration has only supplementary material available locally; the main article and full STAR Methods were not inspected, and chest X-rays are unavailable, so no multimodal claim is made. Other MIMIC seeds remain context; treatment-timing seeds require stronger confounding/source-control evidence than this prognostic design.

All four configured dataset guides and the full catalog were inspected. HCC, EICU, and UKB were not substituted into this MIMIC hypothesis: their available modalities and identifier systems do not add measured GFR or an ICU synchronized validation cohort for this question; UKB groups also require documented namespace crosswalks. Their source rows remain read-only and available.
