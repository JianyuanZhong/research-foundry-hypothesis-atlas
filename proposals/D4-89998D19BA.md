# UKB-04: FEV1 × Systemic Reserve Discordance and 30-Day Post-Infection Mortality

## Hypothesis

Among participants with an infection-related hospital admission, those with poor FEV1 but preserved systemic reserve (grip strength and albumin/CRP ratio) have lower 30-day mortality than those with similar FEV1 but poor systemic reserve. The interaction between respiratory impairment and systemic reserve is the key testable claim: if the two are independent, the interaction term is null; if they are synergistic, the interaction is positive.

## Clinical Importance

Clinicians managing acute infections in patients with chronic lung disease face a critical decision: how aggressively to treat, whether to admit to ICU, and how to counsel about prognosis. Current practice relies heavily on FEV1 (GOLD staging) and clinical gestalt. If systemic reserve (muscle mass, nutritional status, inflammatory burden) modifies the mortality risk associated with poor lung function, then a patient with COPD and sarcopenia is not the same patient as one with COPD and preserved muscle mass. This has direct implications for:
- Triage decisions in respiratory emergencies
- Prognostic counseling for patients with combined respiratory and musculoskeletal frailty
- Targeted interventions (nutritional support, physiotherapy) in high-risk subgroups

The question is whether the two dimensions of impairment—respiratory and systemic—are additive, independent, or synergistic in determining acute infection outcomes.

## Unresolved Question

The leading claim is that FEV1 and systemic reserve have **independent** (additive) effects on post-infection mortality, with no meaningful interaction. The strongest rival is that they are **synergistic**: poor lung function in the context of poor systemic reserve creates a disproportionate mortality risk that neither dimension alone predicts. A third possibility is that systemic reserve is merely a proxy for prior illness severity or healthcare utilization, and the apparent interaction disappears after adjustment.

The observation that distinguishes these explanations: the FEV1 × reserve interaction term in a Cox model. If the interaction HR is near 1.0 (0.9–1.1), the additive model holds. If the interaction HR is substantially >1.1, synergy is supported. If the interaction is null but both main effects are strong, the dimensions are independent.

## Population and Temporal Boundaries

- **Cohort**: UK Biobank participants with FEV1, grip strength, CRP, and albumin measured at the assessment center (AC) visit.
- **Inclusion**: Infection-related hospital admission identified by ICD10 codes in field 41270 (first inpatient diagnosis). Infection codes: J00–J99 (respiratory diseases), A00–B99 (infectious/parasitic diseases).
- **Outcome**: Death within 30 days of the first inpatient admission date (41280-0.0). Death date from 40000-0.0.
- **Exclusion**: Death before AC visit; FEV1 measured after infection admission (temporal ordering); missing any of FEV1, grip, CRP, albumin.
- **Follow-up window**: 0–30 days post-admission. Censoring at death or 30 days.

## Variables

### Exposure
- **FEV1** (field 30020-0.0, BioSamples): Forced expiratory volume in 1 second, liters. String type in parquet; requires numeric conversion.

### Interaction
- **FEV1 × grip strength** (continuous product term, both z-scored).
- **FEV1 × albumin/CRP ratio** (continuous product term).
- Sensitivity: quartile-based interaction (FEV1 quartile × reserve quartile).

### Covariates

## Analysis Design

### Primary Analysis
Cox proportional hazards model:
```
hazard(death) ~ FEV1_z + reserve_z + FEV1_z × reserve_z + age + sex + BMI + smoking + comorbidity_count
```
- FEV1 and reserve z-scored within the infection cohort.
- Interaction term is the primary scientific test.
- Proportional hazards assumption checked with Schoenfeld residuals.

### Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Quartile interaction**: FEV1 Q1–Q4 × reserve Q1–Q4, 16-cell table with cell-specific HRs.
2. **Non-infection control**: Repeat the analysis in participants with non-infection hospital admissions (ICD10 codes outside J/A/B). If the interaction is present in both, it reflects general frailty, not infection-specific vulnerability.
3. **90-day mortality**: Extend the outcome window to 90 days.
4. **Stratified by age**: <65 vs ≥65.
5. **Grip-only and albumin/CRP-only interactions**: Separate the two reserve components.

### Falsification Criteria
- **Interaction HR 0.9–1.1** (95% CI excluding 0.7 and 1.3) after full adjustment → **no discordance effect**; the additive model is supported.
- **Interaction HR >1.1** with 95% CI below 1.0 → **synergy supported**; poor FEV1 and poor reserve are not independent.
- **Interaction HR <0.9** → **protective interaction** (preserved reserve offsets poor FEV1 more than expected); biologically surprising, requires careful interpretation.
- **Null main effects for both FEV1 and reserve** → the cohort may be too healthy or the outcome window too short; inconclusive.

### Method Comparison
- **Baseline**: Cox PH with main effects only (FEV1 + reserve, no interaction). This is the standard clinical model.
- **Learned alternative**: Gradient boosting (e.g., XGBoost) with automatic interaction detection, trained on the same cohort. The alternative can reveal non-linear interactions and higher-order terms that the Cox model cannot capture. If the gradient boosting model shows a strong FEV1×reserve interaction that the Cox model misses, this suggests the linear interaction assumption is too restrictive. If both agree, the Cox model is adequate.
- **What the alternative reveals**: Non-linearities (e.g., the interaction only matters at very low FEV1), threshold effects, and whether the interaction is driven by a small subgroup.

## Limitations

1. **41280 is first inpatient diagnosis only**, not a complete admission history. Participants with multiple admissions are only captured at their first. This may undercount infection events and bias toward milder presentations.
2. **FEV1 may be measured years before infection**. The AC visit and the infection admission are not necessarily close in time. FEV1 may have changed substantially between measurement and event.
3. **Single baseline measurement** of grip, CRP, and albumin. No repeated measures to capture trajectory or acute changes.
4. **ICD10 first code only** (41270-0.0). Secondary diagnoses are not captured in the core parquet (they are in the dta parquet with 259 instances, but the core only has the first).
5. **No adjustment for immunosuppressive medications** (e.g., corticosteroids, chemotherapy) which could confound both reserve and infection risk.
6. **Selection bias**: The UKB is a self-selected, healthier-than-average population. Results may not generalize to sicker populations.

## What Supportive, Adverse, and Inconclusive Results Would Mean

- **Supportive** (interaction HR >1.1, CI below 1.0): Poor FEV1 and poor systemic reserve are synergistic. A patient with COPD and sarcopenia has a disproportionately higher mortality risk than expected from either dimension alone. Clinical implication: combined screening for lung function and muscle mass in infection triage.
- **Adverse** (interaction HR 0.9–1.1): The two dimensions are independent. FEV1 and reserve each contribute additively to mortality risk. Clinical implication: standard separate assessment of lung function and frailty is sufficient; no combined risk score needed.
- **Inconclusive** (wide CI, small interaction): The data cannot distinguish additive from synergistic effects. Clinical implication: further research needed; current practice unchanged.
- **Stronger conclusions requiring additional data**: Causal inference (e.g., Mendelian randomization using FEV1 genetic instruments), external validation in a non-UKB cohort, and prospective studies with repeated FEV1 and grip measurements.

## Alternatives Not Chosen

- **Mendelian randomization**: Would require genetic instruments for FEV1 and grip strength. The Genomics parquet ([UKB data file]) has only 22006/22009 fields, not a full GWAS. Not feasible with current data.
- **NMR metabolomics**: Not available in this dataset (olink is proteomics, not NMR).
- **Body MRI for muscle mass**: Not available. Grip strength is a proxy.
- **Complete admission history**: The dta parquet has 259 ICD10 instances, but the core parquet only has the first. Using the dta parquet would require a different join strategy and is deferred.
