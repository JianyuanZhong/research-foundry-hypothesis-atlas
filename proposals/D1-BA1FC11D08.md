# Episode 6 repair handoff: make discharge and documentation selection testable

Status: design-only. I read the inherited reference-complete proposal ([prior hypothesis]) and its exact source-binding summary. I did not read patient rows, execute the full study, fit a model, or claim patient-level results. This branch reuses the parent’s frozen three-work evidence package and source-check.

## Decision

The inherited design is scientifically strong but not yet discriminating enough about two selection processes. It conditions on survival and remaining in the first ICU for at least 48 hours, then on reaching an alive ICU outtime, and it makes a dense urine-documentation restriction. It also defined a pseudo-landmark from future t0 and could treat early hospital discharge as an ordinary no-event. Those choices can make residual severity, discharge selection, or documentation propensity look like persistent renal physiology.

The substantive repair preserves:

- the exact first-ICU adult population and ICU-outtime landmark t0;
- the non-causal, 48-hour prognostic estimand;
- the exact MIMIC-IV 3.1 archive/member bindings and item IDs;
- the coverage ladder and no-absent-as-zero urine rule; and
- the held-out transparent baseline versus masked longitudinal/HMM comparison.

The repair adds an explicit discharge-survivor estimand, a competing hospital-discharge endpoint, a fixed ICU-age pseudo-landmark selection audit, and a pre-final-window observation-process sensitivity.

## Frozen population and time boundaries

Use the parent’s exact source and joins:

The primary population is therefore conditional on surviving and reaching the first ICU outtime alive. It is not an all-ICU prognosis and does not estimate a discharge or treatment effect. The LOS >=48-hour condition is a survivor/continuation restriction; it is not an immortal-time error if every predictor is strictly pre-t0, but the report must not describe early-bin patterns as representative of all ICU admissions.

Primary features remain in [t0-24h,t0); longitudinal bins remain non-overlapping 6-hour bins from icustays.intime through t0, with partial first-bin duration recorded. No charttime, starttime, or endtime at/after the landmark may enter a predictor.

Report a source-frame flow before model fitting:

Separate exclusions by death, ICU exit, linkage/time failure, and documentation threshold. Do not call P a general ICU cohort.

## Replacement primary outcome: account for hospital discharge

Define H=t0+48h. Order the first event in (t0,H], using admissions.dischtime only to identify observed follow-up:

- E: first in-hospital death (deathtime>t0 and <= H) or later ICU admission for the same subject_id,hadm_id with icustays.intime>t0 and <= H. Preserve separate death and ICU-readmission components.
- D: alive hospital discharge before H with no preceding E. If dischtime=t0, classify D at time zero rather than as a 48-hour non-event.
- N: no E or D by H and still hospitalized at H.

If dischtime<t0, or death timing contradicts the alive-at-t0 rule, fail/quarantine as a data-quality exception. A hospital_expire_flag=1 without usable deathtime is not assigned an event time.

The primary estimand is the standardized observational probability of E by 48 hours conditional on P, with D as a competing observed endpoint. Report probabilities for E,D,N; this is not risk after hospital discharge and not a causal discharge estimand.

B0/B1 retain the parent’s exact inputs, patient-level 70/15/15 split, train-only preprocessing, tune-only penalty selection, untouched test set, and 200 patient-bootstrap intervals, but use the three-level E/D/N target as the primary held-out analysis (multinomial landmark model or equivalent cause-specific discrete-time hazards). Report held-out E probability, standardized group risk differences/ratios, calibration for all states, Brier/log loss, and descriptive AUROC/AUPRC. The old binary logistic treatment of D as a no-event is a sensitivity only and must be labeled selection-sensitive. The masked HMM uses the same target/split and remains descriptive, not mechanistic.

## Fixed pseudo-landmark: no future t0 leakage

Replace the parent’s “24 hours before t0” pseudo-landmark with the fixed ICU-age landmark p=icustays.intime+48h. This is a diagnostic, not a second primary endpoint.

From A0, report how many reach p alive and with a valid first ICU record (A48), distinguishing death before p, ICU exit before p, and invalid timing. Among A48, construct the same 24-hour creatinine/urine discordance and 6-hour longitudinal bins in [p-24h,p), using only rows before p. In (p,p+24h], classify the first process outcome as:

- alive ICU exit: outtime in the interval and no death at/before ICU exit;
- ICU death: deathtime in the interval and deathtime <= outtime;
- still ICU/unknown.

Fit a simple held-out multinomial selection model for these process outcomes using early analog features and the same patient split. Use its pre-specified G_p score/stratum only to standardize or stratify the primary repeated-versus-final-only contrast. For primary subjects, G_p uses only observations before p; missing early fields remain missing with indicators. It is not a discharge-decision model. A signal that is comparable for early alive ICU exit/ICU death, or that disappears after G_p stratification, is adverse for the persistent-physiology interpretation.

This audit cannot remove unmeasured survivor selection, discharge rationale, or treatment confounding. It makes their measured component visible.

## Supportive/adverse/inconclusive criteria

Supportive requires: the held-out repeated-versus-final-only E contrast is directionally stable and calibrated in the dense ladder and O_pre-standardized analysis; it survives G_p stratification; no comparable early ICU-exit/death selection signal dominates; HMM persistence is stable and adds a held-out risk contrast beyond B0 at matched final-window discordance; and coverage, source, support, care-unit, 12/48-hour, continuous-creatinine, and permutation checks do not reverse it. This supports only an observed conditional prognostic regularity.

Adverse includes attenuation/reversal after coverage standardization; concentration in sparse documentation, one urine source, heavy support, or a final-window burst; comparable early selection-outcome association; disappearance after G_p; no held-out longitudinal contrast beyond B0/final-only; or a permutation contrast comparable to observed. These favor measurement, residual severity, or survivor/discharge selection explanations.

Inconclusive includes missing/hash/schema/item failures; invalid time order; unresolved death/discharge timestamps; inadequate D_dense/G_p overlap; fewer than 30 primary test events; unstable/calibration-failing models; HMM state failure; small selection strata; or wide uncertainty that cannot distinguish rivals. An imprecise null is not a refutation.
