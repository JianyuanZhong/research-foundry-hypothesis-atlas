# Duration of early ICU hypotension and 72-hour AKI: a data-verified eICU prognostic experiment

Status: working proposal; no cohort analysis or model fitting has been executed.

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among adults with at least one usable blood-pressure observation during the first 24 ICU hours, does the proportion of observed 30-minute intervals that are hypotensive predict KDIGO creatinine AKI during the first 72 ICU hours after adjustment for illness severity and recording density?

The question matters because the minimum pressure is a common bedside summary but discards how long low pressure persists and can be driven by a single abnormal value. If duration adds reproducible prognostic information, it could support earlier risk communication and closer renal-risk monitoring. The study is explicitly prognostic, not causal: it does not test whether treating to a duration threshold prevents AKI.

## Evidence-supported and unresolved claims

The strongest claim supported by the inspected eICU schema is that longitudinal periodic and aperiodic blood pressures, vasopressor infusions, creatinine results, APACHE severity fields, and hospital identifiers are available and can be aligned to ICU admission using `patientunitstayid` and offset fields.

The unresolved claim is whether the 24-hour duration estimand retains a positive, monotonic association with 72-hour AKI after accounting for nadir pressure, APACHE severity, and recording density, and whether that association is stable across held-out hospitals. A second unresolved question is whether a time-aware learned model reveals clinically interpretable duration-nadir or duration-density interactions beyond a transparent severity-adjusted baseline.

## Population and time origin

Use one ICU admission per patient: select the first `patientunitstayid` in `patient.csv.gz` for each `uniquepid`, ordered by `unitadmittime24` where available. Require an adult stay, at least one physiologically plausible systolic or MAP observation in the first 24 hours, and at least one creatinine result by hour 24.

Define ICU admission as the time origin. Use `observationoffset` and `labresultoffset` as minutes from admission. Exclude stays with no usable pressure in hours 0–24, no creatinine by hour 24, or creatinine AKI already established before hour 24. Report all exclusion counts and sensitivity analyses for the first-admission rule.

## Exposure estimand

Use a fixed 24-hour exposure window, hours 0–24. Hypotension is systolic pressure <90 mmHg or mean arterial pressure (MAP) <65 mmHg. Use `vitalPeriodic.systemicsystolic` and `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` together with `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivesystolic` and `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean`; retain only values within prespecified physiologic ranges and do not silently impute missing monitor values.

The primary estimand is the fraction of observed 30-minute intervals in hours 0–24 with at least one hypotensive pressure. A 30-minute interval is counted as hypotensive only when an observed value is hypotensive; intervals without a pressure observation are not counted as normotensive. Report observed interval count, coverage, and the fraction of the 24-hour window with any pressure observation. A secondary estimand is the time-weighted mean pressure deficit below 90 mmHg using the same interval convention. Minimum systolic and MAP are calculated over the same window and serve as baseline exposures.

## Outcome, censoring, and missing-data rules

The primary outcome is KDIGO stage 1–3 creatinine AKI during hours 0–72. Use creatinine from `lab.csv.gz`, mapped by `labname`, `labresult`, `labresultoffset`, `labmeasurenamesystem`, and `labmeasurenameinterface`. Define baseline creatinine as the lowest creatinine observed before hour 24, or admission creatinine when no earlier value exists. AKI is creatinine at least 2 times baseline with absolute creatinine at least 1.2 mg/dL; report stage 2 and stage 3 separately.

Censor at death, ICU discharge/transfer, or 72 hours, whichever occurs first. Death and discharge are secondary outcomes. Missing creatinine after hour 24 is not treated as AKI or non-AKI; report censoring and missingness by hospital. Use a sensitivity analysis requiring a creatinine result after hour 24 for the primary outcome, and report the primary result with explicit uncertainty.

## Strongest rival explanation and discrimination

The strongest rival is that longer hypotension is a proxy for greater illness severity or more frequent monitoring. A sicker patient may have both a lower nadir and longer low-pressure periods; a more monitored patient has more opportunities for a low pressure to be recorded.

The design adjusts for age, gender, ethnicity, APACHE II components available in `apacheApsVar`, APACHE score and predicted mortality in `apachePatientResult`, admission diagnoses in `admissionDx`, vasopressor exposure in `infusionDrug`, nadir systolic/MAP, and a prespecified recording-density measure: number of pressure observations and coverage in the first 24 hours. The primary comparison is within similar nadir-pressure strata, with prespecified interactions for duration by nadir and duration by recording density.

A negative-control comparison asks whether duration predicts a pre-exposure laboratory change that should not be caused by later hypotension. A strong association there indicates residual confounding or measurement bias and weakens the prognostic interpretation.

## Baseline versus learned alternative

Baseline: a prespecified severity-adjusted logistic model for AKI using age, gender, ethnicity, APACHE II components, admission diagnosis, vasopressor use, nadir systolic/MAP, and recording density. This model is transparent and establishes whether duration has any incremental signal.

Alternative: a time-aware gradient-boosted model fitted on the same 24-hour exposure and covariates, with a monotonic constraint on the duration variable and hospital fixed effects or hospital-stratified fitting. The learned model can reveal nonlinear duration-nadir interactions and patient-specific patterns that a linear baseline loses, while the monotonic constraint prevents an implausible protective interpretation of longer hypotension. It is not a mechanistic causal model.

Both models use the same whole-hospital holdout, bootstrap confidence intervals, calibration-in-the-large/separation, AUROC, and decision-curve net benefit at clinically relevant thresholds. The baseline is preferred if it captures the duration signal adequately and is more interpretable; the alternative is preferred only if it materially improves held-out calibration or reveals a clinically plausible nonlinear interaction, not merely a small AUROC gain. A small bounded CPU diagnostic on a subsample can estimate cohort size and fitting time before the full experiment.

## Validation and interpretation

Use whole-hospital holdout: fit on all but one hospital, predict the held-out hospital, repeat across prespecified hospitals, and report calibration and uncertainty rather than treating one split as definitive. Hospital identifiers come from `hospital.csv.gz` and `patient.hospitalid`.

The hypothesis is supported if duration remains associated with AKI after nadir and severity/recording-density adjustment, with a positive monotonic trend and stable held-out-hospital calibration. It is adverse if the association disappears after adjustment, reverses, or is explained by recording density. It is inconclusive if confidence intervals are wide, coverage is too sparse, or hospital performance is unstable. A positive result supports prognostic information, not a treatment target or causal effect.

## Exact data bindings

All joins use `patientunitstayid`; patient-level grouping uses `uniquepid`. Required sources are `patient.csv.gz`, `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`, `vitalAperiodic.csv.gz`, `infusionDrug.csv.gz`, `lab.csv.gz`, `apacheApsVar.csv.gz`, `apachePatientResult.csv.gz`, `admissionDx.csv.gz`, and `hospital.csv.gz`, all ordinary compressed CSV members in the eICU snapshot. Offsets are minutes from ICU admission.
