# Stress Hyperglycemia Ratio (SHR) as an Independent Prognostic Marker Beyond Absolute Glucose in Undifferentiated ICU Patients: A Falsifiable MIMIC-IV Experiment

**Parent:** [prior hypothesis] (expert seed 5: "At the same admission glucose level, patients with a larger increase relative to their prior glycemic level have higher risk.")
**Dataset:** MIMIC-IV 3.1 (frozen archive, [source checksum])
**Status:** Data-bound proposal with verified itemids, prespecified SHR phenotype, executed bounded feasibility probe, and three inspected key references (abstract-only, honestly labeled). No full analysis was run.

---

## 1. Scientific Question

**Unresolved claim (this experiment tests):** In undifferentiated adult ICU patients, the stress hyperglycemia ratio (SHR = admission glucose / estimated average glucose derived from HbA1c) is independently associated with in-hospital mortality *after adjusting for absolute admission glucose and standard severity covariates* — i.e., SHR carries prognostic information beyond what absolute glucose alone provides.

**Why this matters:** Absolute admission glucose is a well-known mortality predictor in critical illness, but it conflates two distinct physiological states: (a) acute stress hyperglycemia in a previously normoglycemic patient, and (b) chronic hyperglycemia in a diabetic patient whose glucose is only mildly elevated. These states may have different pathophysiology and prognosis. SHR standardizes acute glucose against the HbA1c-derived chronic baseline, isolating the "relative" glycemic stress. If SHR adds independent prognostic value beyond absolute glucose, it would support using SHR (rather than absolute glucose alone) for early metabolic risk stratification in the ICU — a consequential decision for glycemic management targets and resource allocation.

**Strongest rival explanation:** SHR's apparent association with mortality is entirely mediated by absolute glucose (i.e., SHR is just a rescaled glucose) or by baseline severity/diabetes status. If SHR adds no incremental discrimination or reclassification beyond a model already containing absolute glucose + HbA1c + severity, the hypothesis is falsified.

## 2. Supported Claim (Prior Literature, Not Re-tested Here)

- **K1 [Wu & Wang 2026, PMID:42666133]:** In 8,835 MIMIC-IV patients with ASCVD, early SHR trajectories (group-based trajectory modeling over 6×12h windows in first 72h) identified a "persistently high" class with independently worse 28-day survival (fully adjusted HR 1.37, 95% CI 1.15–1.64 vs moderate-stable class), validated in NWICU (HR 2.04, 95% CI 1.39–3.01). HbA1c and first-12h max glucose were leading predictors of high-SHR trajectory membership. *Limitation:* restricted to ASCVD; trajectory-based, not a single-admission SHR; did not test incremental value beyond absolute glucose directly.
- **K2 [Zhang et al. 2026, PMID:42323628]:** In 1,221 MIMIC-IV patients undergoing extracorporeal circulation during open-heart surgery, SHR showed a nonlinear threshold-like association with 180-day mortality (inflection ~0.97; HR 4.62, 95% CI 2.67–7.95 above inflection vs below). *Limitation:* restricted to cardiac surgery; exploratory; substantial exclusion due to missing glucose/HbA1c; authors explicitly frame findings as hypothesis-generating.
- **K3 [Shi et al. 2026, PMID:42683027]:** In 1,068 STEMI patients post-emergency PCI, SHR (admission glucose / eAG from HbA1c) independently predicted hospitalization heart failure (adjusted OR 2.31 per unit, 95% CI 1.68–3.18; AUC 0.748 vs 0.728 for absolute glucose; NRI 0.318, IDI 0.035). *Limitation:* single-center; restricted to STEMI; outcome was heart failure, not mortality; did not test in undifferentiated ICU population.

**Gap this experiment fills:** All three prior studies are restricted to specific cardiac/surgical subpopulations. None tests whether SHR adds independent prognostic value beyond absolute glucose in an *undifferentiated* adult ICU cohort — the population where the clinical decision (use SHR vs absolute glucose for risk stratification) is most consequential.

## 3. Experiment Design

### Population
- Adult ICU patients (age ≥ 18) from MIMIC-IV 3.1.
- Must have: (a) ≥ 1 HbA1c measurement (itemid 50852 or 51631) with valid valueuom during the admission; (b) ≥ 1 glucose measurement (itemid 50931 or 50809) with valid valueuom in the first 24h of ICU admission.
- Exclude: ICU stay < 24h; glucose measured > 24h after ICU admission.

### SHR Definition
- **Admission glucose (G):** median glucose value in the first 24h of ICU admission (itemid 50931/50809, mg/dL).
- **Estimated average glucose (eAG):** derived from HbA1c (A) using the standard ADA formula: eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × A − 46.7. HbA1c A is the most recent value before or within 24h of ICU admission (itemid 50852/51631, %).
- **SHR = G / eAG.** SHR > 1 indicates acute glucose above chronic baseline; SHR < 1 indicates below.

### Outcome
- **Primary:** in-hospital mortality (binary, from admissions.death_event).
- **Secondary:** ICU mortality (icustays.death_event).

### Analysis
1. **Baseline model:** logistic regression for in-hospital mortality with covariates: age, sex, ICU type, absolute admission glucose (G), HbA1c (A), comorbidities.
2. **Extended model:** baseline model + SHR.
3. **Comparison:** ΔAUROC, NRI, IDI, adjusted OR per 0.1 SHR, restricted cubic splines for nonlinearity.
4. **Sensitivity:** restrict to SHR ≥ 1; restrict to HbA1c < 6.5% (no diabetes); alternative eAG formula; time-window sensitivity (6h vs 24h).
5. **Learned alternative:** XGBoost with SHR + G + A + severity; compare AUROC and feature importance (SHR vs G). CPU sufficient.

### Falsification Criteria
- **Supportive:** SHR independently associated with in-hospital mortality (adjusted OR > 1.05 per 0.1 SHR, 95% CI excl. 1.0) AND adds incremental discrimination (ΔAUROC > 0.005) or reclassification (NRI > 0.05, p < 0.05).
- **Adverse:** SHR not independently associated (95% CI includes 1.0) OR adds no incremental value beyond G + A + severity.
- **Inconclusive:** borderline incremental value (ΔAUROC 0.001–0.005, NRI 0.01–0.05) — requires external validation.

## 6. Limitations
- HbA1c is selectively ordered → selection bias.
- HbA1c reflects 2–3 month average; may not capture recent glycemic changes.
- Anemia, transfusion, hemoglobin variants can affect HbA1c interpretation.
- Single-center (MIMIC-IV); no external validation in this experiment.
- Retrospective design; no causal inference.
