# Episode 61: does late lactate retain material decision information after contemporaneous laboratory and physiologic context?

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

At a 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine entry, a late lactate may alter perceived hospital-mortality risk. The Episode 59 parent asked whether that value adds material information beyond baseline lactate and the leave-lactate-out laboratory record released by the landmark. Its comparator still omits routinely recorded physiology that often drives the same review decision. The unresolved question is whether late lactate captures a distinct risk signal after contemporaneous hemodynamics, oxygenation, respiratory rate, heart rate and temperature are represented, or merely summarizes physiologic instability already visible at the bedside.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis:** in the inherited age-inclusive EICU 24-hour risk set, adding the released 12-to-24-hour lactate to a fixed model containing pressor-entry lactate, the released leave-lactate-out laboratory snapshot, and prespecified six-hour vital/hemodynamic summaries increases uniformly threshold-averaged decision-curve net benefit over hospital-mortality thresholds 0.30-0.50 by at least **1 net true-positive-equivalent review per 100 people**.

This is a test of incremental modeled information conditional on survival and repeat lactate measurement. It is not an effect of lactate ordering, resuscitation, monitoring, or acting on predictions. Resolving it would distinguish a potentially distinct biochemical warning from redundancy with already recorded organ and physiologic state, informing whether prospective evaluation of repeat lactate is worth pursuing.

## Strongest supported claim versus claim under test

The strongest existing evidence is the executed Episode 58 result: among 1,084 APACHE-complete people in 31 hospitals, adding late lactate to entry lactate and retrospective APACHE-IVa predicted mortality yielded ΔINB +1.982 per 100 (95% interval +0.483,+3.351), positive improvement in all 31 site deletions, and improved discrimination/proper scores. This supports a positive modeled increment against that sparse, partly untimestamped comparator, but not the locked ≥+1 materiality claim because +1 remained inside the interval. It establishes neither ordering utility nor treatment benefit.

The Episode 59 parent is an unexecuted valid design, not positive evidence. It strengthened the comparator with 16 release-time-audited non-lactate laboratory features in the inherited 1,229-person, 33-hospital cohort. The present experiment tests the still stronger and clinically closer claim that late lactate adds ≥1 per 100 after both those labs and contemporaneous recorded physiology.

Baysan et al. 2022 (DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407), previously acquired and inspected as full-text XML, reported modest serial lactate prognostic information in critically ill adults with sepsis, but did not test incremental decision value beyond a released laboratory-plus-physiology record. The current Europe PMC search response is frozen as source [source checksum]; its snippets are discovery evidence only. The research-ambition README and its availability limits were inspected; none of its demonstrations is used as clinical evidence.

## Predictors and temporal boundaries

R0 includes every Episode 59 base predictor: age and age squared; sex indicators; t0 and square; baseline collection lag; ln(B) and square; F measurement age (in both models); and the latest valid [t0,L24] leave-lactate-out values, measurement ages and missing indicators for potassium, sodium, chloride, BUN, glucose, creatinine, bicarbonate, calcium, Hgb, platelets, WBC, albumin, total bilirubin, paO2, paCO2 and pH, using the parent's exact names, units, revision and conflict rules.

R0 additionally contains fixed summaries from observations in [L24-360,L24]:

- vitalPeriodic: for temperature, sao2, heartrate, respiration and systemicmean, the last value, median, minimum and maximum after physiologic range checks (temperature 25-45 C, sao2 40-100%, heart rate 20-250/min, respiration 2-80/min, systemicmean 20-200 mm Hg), plus count and no-valid-observation indicator.
- vitalAperiodic: the same last, median, minimum, maximum, count and missing indicator for noninvasivemean restricted to 20-200 mm Hg.

The last value is ordered by observationoffset then row ID. Tied contradictory values invalidate that timestamp; identical ties use the smallest row ID. Values outside bounds are missing, retained in QC counts, and never winsorized into plausibility. The two MAP sources remain separate because invasive/systemic and noninvasive measures are not interchangeable. No complete-case exclusion occurs. R1 adds only ln(F) and ln(F)^2.

Observation times do not prove clinician-view time: these tables lack entry/release timestamps. The estimand therefore conditions on recorded-by-L24 measurement time, not verified bedside availability.

## Analysis, baselines, estimand and uncertainty

Use hospital-level leave-one-hospital-out prediction. In each training fold, winsorize continuous predictors at training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize with training mean/population SD, and mean-impute after scaling with fixed missing indicators. A feature wholly absent in a training fold makes the analysis noncomputable. Fit unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10000, tol=1e-8 and seed 20260961. Held-out outcomes inform no preprocessing, feature choice or fit.

At p in {0.30,0.31,...,0.50}, NB=TP/N-FP/N*p/(1-p). Compare R1 with R0, treat-none, and treat-all. Primary ΔINB is 100 times the trapezoidal mean integral of NB_R1-NB_R0 over 0.30-0.50.

For uncertainty, sample the 33 hospitals with replacement 2,000 times using seed 20260961. Copies remain the same held-out group; all copies are excluded from that fold's training and multiplicity weights training and evaluation. Refit preprocessing and both models in every replicate; use percentile 95% intervals. Report ΔAUROC, Brier improvement, log-loss improvement, mean absolute probability change, all threshold-specific ΔNB values, calibration slope/intercept, and all 33 fixed-prediction site deletions as non-rescuing diagnostics. Baseline comparisons are: labs-only Episode 59 R0 versus its R1, physiology-augmented R0 versus R1, and treat-all/none. The first is descriptive and cannot determine the primary label.

Gates require exact hashes/headers and flow; ≥1,000 people, ≥30 hospitals, ≥200 deaths and survivors; both outcomes per hospital; maximum site share ≤15%; at least 80% patient coverage for any vital family retained in the fixed primary feature set (otherwise that entire family and this candidate are not computable, not silently dropped); every feature observed in each training fold; finite unique predictions; all thresholds; ≥95% finite converged refits; and ≥80% finite deletions.

## Falsification and interpretation

After gates:

- **Supportive:** ΔINB 95% lower bound ≥+1; R1 calibration slope 0.80-1.20 and absolute calibration-in-the-large ≤0.05; R1 beats the better default at all 21 thresholds; no threshold ΔNB below -0.5 per 100; ≥80% site deletions positive; and both Brier and log-loss point estimates improve.
- **Adverse to materiality:** upper bound <+1 but ≥0 with no opposite branch. This falsifies the locked material increment for this model/setting, not every smaller signal.
- **Opposite:** upper bound <0. The fixed addition worsened modeled decisions; repeat testing did not cause harm.
- **Inconclusive/mixed:** interval includes zero or +1, or the lower bound clears +1 but a guardrail fails.
- **Not computable:** any source, cohort, coverage, feature, fit, bootstrap, threshold or deletion gate fails.

Support would establish only incremental out-of-hospital modeled information among selected repeatedly measured EICU survivors under assumed review thresholds. Adverse/opposite results would weaken this fixed use after physiology; inconclusive results demand independent data, not threshold narrowing or feature removal.

## Verifier and evidence limits

The verifier can check hashes/headers, all selection and conflict rules, source-specific vital features, no leakage, model settings, LOHO predictions, thresholds, refit bootstrap, diagnostics, deterministic label, and whether every reported claim cites a computed output. It must reject numerically correct outputs paired with claims that repeat lactate improves survival, proves occult hypoperfusion, or should be ordered.

Computation cannot establish arterial versus venous lactate; actual norepinephrine delivery/dose; observation or test-order indication; bedside display time; monitor/device validity; treatment changes, source control, liver/renal mechanism, tissue perfusion, limitations of care, death cause, burden/cost, or benefit from acting. Clinical informatics adjudication is required for specimen, device and workflow semantics; clinician/patient review for thresholds and +1 margin; prospective external validation with order/view/action timestamps for transportability; and an impact study, ideally randomized for any ordering/treatment policy, for changed decisions, harms, costs or patient benefit.
