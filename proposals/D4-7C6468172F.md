## Repair decision and affected estimand

The ambiguity band is repaired to the central 60 percent of current-only predicted target risk. The former two-quintile wording is retired. Let q20 and q80 be the early-training 20th and 80th percentiles of the cross-fitted M0 predicted probability of the target category. Use the mutually exclusive, exhaustive labels

- lower tail L: r < q20;
- ambiguity band A: q20 <= r < q80;
- upper tail U: r >= q80.

Thus A contains the central 60 percent (the second, third and fourth quintiles when ties do not alter the empirical proportions), while L and U are the lowest and highest risk quintiles. This is the clinically interpretable decision contrast: retain prior information for people neither clearly low nor clearly high risk under the current panel. It is not a claim that the central 60 percent is a validated clinical threshold.

All references below to middle, middle-risk, or middle-versus-tail are replaced by ambiguity band A or central-60-percent band. The same L/A/U labels and the same frozen q20/q80 cut points are used for the early held-out evaluation, late no-refit evaluation, support gates, bootstrap strata, selection-weighted sensitivity, and interpretation. If q20 or q80 is non-finite, q20 >= q80, or the risk score cannot be defined under the outcome gate, the study fails closed.

## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

After a person has a current blood-biomarker panel, is the earlier panel worth retaining for risk triage when the current-only target-risk estimate is ambiguous, and does that incremental value remain valid when the assessment calendar era changes? This matters because a historical result could change who is referred for review or prevention research, but an apparent benefit could instead reflect the fact that people return for repeat assessment, the spacing between assessments, or an era-specific measurement or ascertainment process.

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among participants with a valid paired panel and a verified five-year assessment landmark, adding prior-panel values to current values plus repeat-spacing metadata improves held-out prediction of a later qualifying recorded target more in the prespecified central-60-percent current-only target-risk ambiguity band A than in the equal-weight average of the lower and upper tails, and this contrast is retained, within a prespecified practical margin, when models fitted only through 2012 are evaluated without refitting in 2013.

The strongest local evidence supports paired field-instance columns, two assessment-date fields, demographic fields, position-matched diagnosis code/date arrays, and death-date fields. It does not establish biomarker identities, units, allowable clinical ranges, missing-value meanings, specimen collection time, disease onset, code-domain mapping, individual observation end, or complete five-year ascertainment. The unresolved claim is therefore conditional prognostic information in this explicitly recorded endpoint, not a causal effect of repeat testing or a validated action threshold.

## Estimands and signs

Let Y have three mutually exclusive recorded endpoint categories: T for the earliest qualifying target after T1 through H, D for death under the prespecified tie and timing rule, and N for no qualifying target or death record through H. If ascertainment is not certified, N means no qualifying record in the available source and is never called disease-free; no incidence or censoring claim is then made.

For participant i, let r_i = p-hat-M0,i,T, the M0 predicted probability of category T. M0 is current position-1 panel, approved age, approved sex, and T1 calendar year. The cut points q20 and q80 are computed only from early-era out-of-fold r_i values in the eligible analysis population, before late outcomes are inspected. For early participants, the out-of-fold r_i assigns L, A, or U. For late participants, the same frozen q20 and q80 assign L, A, or U to predictions from early-fitted models. No late outcomes, late quantiles, M1b predictions, or nonlinear-model predictions may define a band.

For an evaluation set E and band g in {L,A,U}, the multiclass Brier score is

BS(M,g,E) = (1 / sum_i in E,g w_i) sum_i in E,g w_i sum_k in {T,D,N} (1[Y_i=k] - p-hat-M,i,k)^2,

where w_i is 1 for the primary unweighted analysis and the frozen repeat-selection weight for the sensitivity. Define

d_g,e = BS(M1b,g,e) - BS(M1a,g,e),

where e is early or late. Negative d_g,e favors adding prior values conditional on current values and spacing. The primary within-era effect-modification contrast is

D_e = d_A,e - (d_L,e + d_U,e) / 2.

The equal-weight tail average is deliberate: it compares A with the average of the lower and upper tail-specific increments, rather than allowing a larger tail to dominate because of late-era composition. D_e < 0 means the prior-value increment is more favorable in A than in the two tails. D_e is not the overall population Brier improvement.

The no-refit temporal stability estimand is

tau_A = D_late,no-refit - D_early.

The practical transport margin is [-0.002, 0.002] Brier units. The primary transport criterion requires the complete uncertainty interval for tau_A to lie inside this interval, together with no reversal of the direct A-band prior-value direction and no prespecified calibration deterioration. The practical margin is a stability criterion, not a clinical-benefit threshold.

A result cannot establish that repeat testing, monitoring, treatment, a threshold, or referral improves patient outcomes. It can test whether prior values contain conditional information for recorded future categories and whether that information appears robust to the defined calendar-era shift.

## Substantive advance and alternatives

Existing parent evidence supports a selection-aware nested decomposition: M0 to M1a measures repeat-spacing information, and M1a to M1b isolates prior values conditional on current values and spacing. The temporal parent supports an early-fit/late-test stress test, but its primary emphasis is age modification. This repaired proposal makes the clinically direct current-only ambiguity band the transported estimand and fixes its population definition mathematically. A positive result would identify a reproducible prognostic-information target for external validation or prospective selective-monitoring research; a null or era-specific result would warn against carrying the finding into a changed workflow.

The baseline and learned alternative are matched on question, inputs, participant split, outcome, band definition, and uncertainty:

- M0: current position-1 panel, approved age, approved sex, and T1 calendar year.
- M1a: M0 plus verified spacing L.
- M1b: M1a plus all eight literal position-0 panel values; this is the primary prior-value increment.
- M1c: M1a plus eight raw changes (T1 minus T0), only as a representation sensitivity; annualized changes require verified timing and units.
- A1: M1b plus prespecified current/prior discordance contrasts and the fixed L/A/U indicator interactions, with shrinkage. It is interpretive and cannot replace M1b versus M1a.
- M2: a small gradient-boosted multinomial model with exactly M1b inputs and a prespecified depth/regularization grid.
- M2a: the matched M2 model with prior fields removed, retaining current fields, age, sex, year, and L.

M2 may reveal threshold, nonlinear current/prior discordance, and interval interactions that additive M1b averages away. Its target is the same Y and its band evaluation is still based only on frozen M0 target-risk predictions. It is scientifically useful only if the shape is stable across early folds, calibrated, and reproduced in late no-refit data; a small score gain alone does not replace the transparent nested estimand. A mechanistic organ-score model is deferred because field identities, units, assay quality, and specimen timing are not verified; biological organ assignment would be fabricated.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted fold-local M0, M1a, M1b, M1c, A1, M2 and M2a models, one held-out prediction record per eligible eid, early and late estimates of every d_g,e and D_e, tau_A with uncertainty, calibration and selection diagnostics, the gate certificate, and falsification outputs. Completion is either those uncertainty-quantified outputs under passed gates or a reproducible fail-closed audit naming the exact missing dependency. No discovery result is substituted for the future solver experiment.

## Selection control, frozen bands, and no-refit transport

Create a flow from the population anchor through T0-complete, valid paired observation, complete panel, timing window, prior-record exclusion, early/late era, L/A/U band, and T/D/N outcome categories. Report all counts, including the number assigned to each band and the number with target events.

Among T0-complete participants, define S = 1 as valid paired T1 plus complete panel. Fit a regularized repeat-observation propensity using only pre-T1 T0 panel values, age, sex, and T0 calendar year. Fit it in early training data and apply it to late T0-complete people. Define the stabilized weight as w_i = P(S=1) / p-hat(S=1 | X_i), using the early training prevalence in the numerator; truncate at the early 1st and 99th percentiles. Report overlap, maximum weight, standardized differences, and effective sample size. This is a measured-selection transport sensitivity under a missing-at-random assumption, not causal correction and not evidence that returning for assessment causes benefit.

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on eid within early data. For each fold, fit M0 on the other four folds and generate out-of-fold probabilities for every eligible early participant. Define q20 and q80 as the empirical 0.20 and 0.80 quantiles of all eligible early out-of-fold M0 target probabilities, using one prespecified quantile algorithm recorded in the manifest. The quantiles use no early outcomes, late records, M1b predictions, or model-selection results beyond the fold-local M0 predictions. Assign early participants with r < q20 to L, q20 <= r < q80 to A, and r >= q80 to U.

Fit each model separately within each early training fold, with all imputation, scaling, regularization, propensity fitting, tuning, and early calibration training-fold-only. Generate early held-out predictions from the corresponding fold model. For the late no-refit evaluation, apply all five already-fitted early fold models to each eligible late participant and average their predicted class probabilities before scoring; do not fit, calibrate, tune, impute, scale, or alter a model using late data. Apply the frozen q20 and q80 to late M0 probabilities. This keeps the late band definition fixed even if the late risk distribution shifts.

The six primary interaction cells are early-L, early-A, early-U, late-L, late-A, and late-U. Each cell must contain at least 200 eligible participants and at least 30 target events. These are hard support requirements for the primary interaction and transport claim. The overall eligible analysis set must contain at least 100 target events. For every late band, the selection-weighted effective sample size must be at least 200 and at least 50 percent of the corresponding unweighted cell size; otherwise the weighted transport sensitivity and the transport claim are inconclusive. If early-to-late covariate overlap is absent, or any unweighted late cell has fewer than 200 participants or 30 target events, the transport claim is inconclusive. Ties at q20 or q80 are retained using the stated half-open rule and exact cell counts, not silently rebalanced.

## Models and evaluation

Use regularized multinomial probability models for recorded categories. If all timing and ascertainment gates pass, use a common regularized discrete-time competing-risk hazard model and cumulative-incidence predictions as a separately labelled sensitivity; it does not replace the primary recorded-category analysis.

Thresholds are not validated clinical utilities.

Use paired participant bootstrap intervals by resampling with replacement within each of the six fixed era-by-band cells, retaining each participant's fold assignment, frozen band, outcome, predictions, and weight. Compute d_g,e and D_e from the paired predictions in each replicate, and compute tau_A from independently resampled early and late strata. The primary bootstrap therefore measures held-out/test-sample uncertainty conditional on fitted models, cut points, and weights. A permitted sensitivity refits the entire early pipeline in prespecified early bootstrap samples, recomputes the early cut points from that replicate's early out-of-fold M0 predictions, applies that replicate's frozen cut points to late predictions, and reports the additional model-training uncertainty. If not run, state explicitly that model-training uncertainty is not included. A bootstrap replicate that fails a support condition is reported, not repaired by changing the band definition.

For calibration, fit and report one-vs-rest intercept and slope for each endpoint category k in {T,D,N}, for each model and era, using the predicted p-hat-k. For the prespecified no-material-deterioration rule, compare M1b with M1a within each era: M1b is not materially worse only if, for every k, the absolute calibration-intercept shift is no more than 0.05 and the absolute slope shift is no more than 0.10; additionally report whether each slope lies in [0.90, 1.10]. These are analysis quality criteria, not claims of clinical calibration or utility.

## Interpretation and fail-closed criteria

Supportive evidence requires all timing, biomarker, and outcome gates to pass; at least 100 target events overall; all six unweighted cells to meet 200-participant and 30-target-event support; required late weighted ESS and overlap; no integrity or leakage failure; d_A,early < 0 and d_A,late < 0 with a 95 percent upper confidence bound below zero in each era; D_early < 0 and D_late < 0 with the 95 percent upper confidence bound below zero for the prespecified ambiguity-versus-equal-tails contrast; the selection-weighted sensitivity not reversing either D direction; the calibration rule above; and the complete 95 percent interval for tau_A inside [-0.002, 0.002]. No direction reversal means both direct A-band increments remain negative and both D contrasts remain negative; it does not mean that every individual feature or every exploratory threshold must improve. Stable M2 shape across folds and late data is stronger support, not a prerequisite for the transparent primary claim.

Adverse evidence is a non-improving or worsening prior-value increment in A, a gain attributable to M1a spacing rather than M1b prior values, a nonnegative or reversed D contrast, disappearance under selection weighting or permutation, calibration deterioration beyond the declared limits, fold instability, an early-to-late reversal, or tau_A outside the practical margin. This weakens the case for retaining prior values for this recorded-risk task; it does not prove no clinical value in another population.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed semantic, timing, or ascertainment gates; fewer than 100 target events; any sparse era-by-band cell; poor overlap or weighted ESS; unstable weights; q20/q80 nonidentifiability; endpoint ambiguity; broken pairing; fold instability; materially conflicting missingness or endpoint results; or a confidence interval too wide to classify the sign or transport margin. Inconclusive is not evidence against the hypothesis. A small learned-model improvement without a stable transparent nested contrast is also inconclusive for the substantive prior-value question.

Computationally checkable claims are source integrity, flow counts, era and band assignments, model fits, held-out predictions, score and contrast arithmetic, calibration arithmetic, overlap and ESS, uncertainty, and permutation behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for biomarker meaning, specimen timing, disease onset, complete ascertainment, action utilities, treatment benefit, causal repeat-testing effects, mechanism, external transport beyond measured support, and prospective policy evaluation.
