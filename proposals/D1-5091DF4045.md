# Candidate Proposal: Temporal Discordance of Antimicrobial De-escalation and Clinical Improvement in Sepsis

## Scientific Question

**Primary Hypothesis:** In sepsis patients, antimicrobial de-escalation occurring BEFORE clinical improvement (defined by decreasing SOFA/qSOFA scores or improving laboratory markers) predicts better 30-day mortality and readmission rates than de-escalation occurring AFTER or DURING clinical worsening.

## Unresolved Question

While prior studies have examined antimicrobial de-escalation outcomes and SOFA score trajectories separately, no study has systematically analyzed whether the **timing discordance** between de-escalation and clinical improvement predicts outcomes. Specifically:

1. Does de-escalation that occurs **before** clinical improvement indicate a more aggressive/appropriate stewardship approach?
2. Does de-escalation that occurs **after** clinical improvement indicate delayed stewardship that may be associated with worse outcomes?
3. Does de-escalation that occurs **during** worsening indicate inappropriate or reactive stewardship?

The temporal discordance index aims to capture these nuanced relationships that static analyses miss.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Antimicrobial Stewardship Optimization:** Current stewardship programs often focus on whether de-escalation occurs, not WHEN it occurs relative to clinical status. Understanding temporal patterns could inform timing-specific stewardship protocols.

2. **Prognostication:** Identifying patients who de-escalate at suboptimal times could improve risk stratification and early intervention strategies.

3. **Quality Improvement:** Hospitals can use temporal discordance metrics to identify units or providers with systematic timing issues in stewardship practices.

4. **Mechanistic Insight:** Early de-escalation may reduce unnecessary toxicity in deteriorating patients, while late de-escalation may reflect missed opportunities to reduce drug pressure during recovery.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Existing literature [K1][K2][K3] demonstrates:
- Antimicrobial de-escalation is common in sepsis patients [K1]
- SOFA score trajectories are predictive of outcomes [K2]
- Antimicrobial management timing matters for sepsis outcomes [K3]

However, these studies do not:
- Systematically classify de-escalation timing relative to clinical improvement
- Quantify temporal discordance as a continuous or categorical variable
- Test whether this temporal relationship independently predicts outcomes

This proposal advances by:
1. **Temporal Classification:** Creating subgroups based on the temporal relationship between de-escalation and clinical improvement
2. **Discordance Index:** Developing a quantitative measure of timing discordance
3. **Outcome Prediction:** Testing whether temporal discordance predicts mortality/readmission beyond traditional covariates

## Population Definition

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- MIMIC-IV ICU patients with sepsis diagnosis (ICD-9 995.91 or ICD-10 A40-A47)
- At least 2 antimicrobial courses within 14 days of admission
- At least 3 lab measurements within 7 days post-admission
- ICU stay duration ≥ 24 hours
- 30-day follow-up data available for mortality/readmission

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with multiple ICU stays within 30 days
- Patients with missing critical data (SOFA, antimicrobial events, labs)
- Patients with known renal/hepatic failure

**Target Sample Size:** Approximately 2,000-3,000 patients based on MIMIC-IV sepsis cohort characteristics

## Baseline Method: Static Timing Analysis

**Approach:**
1. Identify the last antimicrobial administration before day 7 post-admission
2. Identify the first clinical improvement marker (decreasing SOFA or improving labs) within 7 days
3. Calculate time difference: deescalation_timing = time_to_improvement - time_since_last_antimicrobial
4. Classify into categories:
   - **Before**: deescalation_timing < 0 (de-escalation before improvement)
   - **During**: -24h < deescalation_timing < +24h
   - **After**: deescalation_timing ≥ +24h

**Statistical Analysis:**
- Logistic regression: outcome ~ discordance_category + age + comorbidities + ICU length of stay
- Compare AUC across categories
- Kaplan-Meier survival curves by category

## Alternative Method: Temporal Discordance Index

**Approach:**
1. **Multi-event Analysis:** Track all antimicrobial courses and all clinical improvement markers
2. **Discordance Classification:** For each antimicrobial course, classify based on whether de-escalation occurs before, during, or after clinical improvement
3. **Temporal Discordance Index (TDI):** TDI = Σ(weight_i × discordance_score_i)
4. **Subgroup Analysis:** Low TDI (≤0), Medium TDI (>0, ≤0.5), High TDI (>0.5)

**Statistical Analysis:**
- Multivariable logistic regression with TDI as continuous predictor
- Restricted cubic spline analysis for non-linear relationships
- Time-dependent Cox proportional hazards modeling

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be considered falsified if ALL of the following are true:
1. No association between temporal discordance subgroup and outcomes (p > 0.05)
2. Alternative method performs no better than baseline (ΔAUC < 0.02)
3. Results not robust after adjusting for confounders (age, comorbidities, hospital)
4. No association between temporal discordance and outcomes when controlling for static antimicrobial burden and clinical severity
