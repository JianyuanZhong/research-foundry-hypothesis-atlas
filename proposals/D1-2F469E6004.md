# Laparoscopic versus open hepatectomy in HCC with preoperative portal-hypertension features: hepatic-functional benefit without margin harm

## Question and hypothesis

For resectable HCC in a cirrhotic liver, choosing laparoscopy may reduce operative stress but selection by tumor location/complexity can make routine retrospective comparisons misleading. The unresolved question is whether its short-term hepatic-functional advantage persists specifically in patients with **preoperatively observable portal-hypertension features**, without sacrificing margin clearance.

Existing evidence supports that minimally invasive resection can reduce average perioperative morbidity in selected HCC patients; it does not establish benefit in this high-risk phenotype in this institution. The tested hypothesis is associational: after overlap weighting on pre-incision variables, laparoscopic resection is associated with at least a 30% relative reduction in a prespecified day-5-to-7 hepatic-dysfunction endpoint versus open resection, while the upper 95% confidence bound for excess positive-margin risk is <3 percentage points. It does not claim the approach causes better survival.

## Population and clocks

One row per patient, first pathology-confirmed HCC resection from 2013-2025, age >=18. Index time is `procedures.开始时间`. Require an eligible upper-abdominal CT/MRI report in days -90 to -1 with explicit cirrhosis or portal-hypertension evidence (`肝硬化`, `门静脉高压`, splenomegaly, varices, ascites, or portal collaterals), plus preoperative bilirubin and INR in days -7 to -1 and both measures on postoperative days 5-7. Exclude transplant, emergency rupture/hemorrhage, extrahepatic metastasis, gross main-portal-vein tumor thrombus, prior resection, simultaneous ablation, and missing approach.

Exposure is laparoscopic if same-encounter `手术` contains `腹腔镜` and operative-document `手术名称/手术经过` confirms it; open if operative documentation explicitly states open/laparotomy and no laparoscopic term. Converted cases form a separate descriptive group. Generic procedure codes without confirmatory text are excluded, not silently called open. Two blinded Chinese-reading reviewers adjudicate 200 stratified operations; approach PPV must be >=0.95.

Baseline ends 24 hours before incision. Outcomes begin at incision. No same-day untimed diagnosis/document is a baseline covariate.

## Outcomes

Primary hepatic dysfunction is both (a) postoperative day-5-to-7 INR at least 1.25 times the last preoperative INR and (b) total bilirubin at least 1.5 times the last preoperative value, using identical exact assay names. This is a computable deterioration endpoint, **not** claimed to be ISGLS post-hepatectomy liver failure because lab units/reference limits and clinical grading are unavailable.

Co-primary oncologic safety is pathology text indicating involved/positive resection margin, adjudicated on a blinded 300-record sample. Secondary outcomes: postoperative length of stay; explicit index-encounter diagnoses of liver failure, ascites, bleeding, infection, and renal injury; transfusion/rescue-treatment proxies from medications/orders; 30-day same-system readmission; and continuous peak bilirubin/INR fold change. Mortality, Clavien-Dindo grade, external readmissions, long-term recurrence, and quality of life are unavailable.

## Baselines and analysis

Report crude outcomes; conventional multivariable regression; propensity overlap weighting; and exact/coarsened matching sensitivity. The propensity model uses only pre-incision age, sex, calendar year, preoperative labs, CT/MRI tumor size/count/location, macrovascular language, cirrhosis/portal-hypertension features, prior treatment, and planned resection extent. It cannot include postoperative pathology. Require weighted standardized mean differences <0.10 and effective sample size >=500 per arm; lack of overlap is inconclusive.

Estimate weighted risk ratio/risk difference for hepatic dysfunction and margin risk difference with patient bootstrap CIs. Prespecified sensitivities: pathology-confirmed cirrhosis; explicit imaging-only portal hypertension; minor versus major resection; M0/M1/M2; 2013-2018 versus 2019-2025; complete-case; alternative 1.2/1.5 fold dysfunction definitions; excluding converted cases; and E-value/unmeasured-confounding analysis. Surgeon identity, blood loss, Pringle time, intraoperative pressure, and treatment intent are unavailable, so even balanced results remain adjusted associations.

Support requires hepatic-dysfunction RR <=0.70 with upper 95% CI <1, margin excess upper 95% CI <0.03, overlap/balance gates, and consistent sign across eras/minor-resection sensitivity. Adverse means higher dysfunction or margin excess >=3 points. Falsified means the lower 95% CI for RR is >0.70 or margin noninferiority fails. Inconclusive means poor overlap, failed label adjudication, <500 effective patients/arm, or CIs cross either threshold.

A supportive result justifies prospective multicenter evaluation of approach selection in this phenotype. It does not prove causal benefit, recurrence equivalence, or survival benefit. Those require surgeon/operative detail, validated liver-failure grading, death and external follow-up, clinical review, and preferably randomization.

## Verifier contract

Check source columns/hashes, exact attrition, approach dictionary and adjudication results, pre-incision covariate timestamps, day-5-to-7 lab windows, same-assay ratios, balance/effective sample size, metrics/CIs, and trace every conclusion to outputs. Fail correct computations paired with claims of improved survival, causal superiority, ISGLS liver failure, or clinical deployment. Supportive output permits only an adjusted short-term association; adverse output must warn against benefit; inconclusive output permits no direction claim. Automatic verification cannot establish surgical semantics, margin truth, acceptable tradeoffs, or causal exchangeability.
