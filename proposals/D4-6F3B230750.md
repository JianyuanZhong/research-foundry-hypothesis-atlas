# Baseline creatinine–cystatin C discordance and first hospitalized kidney transition: acute coded AKI versus chronic coded CKD

Status: prospective study design; no exposure–outcome association has been fitted. This child preserves the parent's population, exposure, 30-day landmark, and 10-year hospitalized coded-AKI estimand. Its scientific change is a prespecified same-risk-set N17-versus-N18 first-transition analysis aimed at the strongest remaining interpretation threat: low eGFRcys relative to eGFRcr may identify occult chronic kidney burden rather than preferential susceptibility to acute stress.

## Evidence, unresolved claim, and clinical advance

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. In UK Biobank, large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common and have multiple kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, discordance is associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes [K2]. In CRIC participants who already had CKD, baseline and time-updated discordance carried information about adjudicated ESKD and mortality [K3]. These studies do not establish whether discordance in UK Biobank adults with baseline eGFRcr >=60 preferentially precedes hospitalized coded AKI rather than first hospitalized coded CKD, whether low grip modifies that relative pattern, or whether cystatin-C-guided care improves outcomes.

Unchanged primary hypothesis: among UK Biobank participants with baseline race-free 2021 CKD-EPI eGFRcr >=60 mL/min/1.73m2 and no prior inpatient N17 code, a 15-unit lower 2012 CKD-EPI eGFRcys minus eGFRcr predicts a higher 10-year cumulative incidence of first hospitalized coded AKI after adjustment for eGFRcr and prespecified covariates; the contrast is larger at lower sex-specific grip.

New falsifiable interpretation hypothesis: within the subset free of both N17 and N18 at the same day-30 landmark, the adjusted discordance contrast is larger for N17-first than N18-first on the standardized 10-year relative-risk scale. A larger N17 contrast would favor—but not prove—an acute-event-enrichment interpretation. An equal or larger N18 contrast would favor occult chronic filtration burden or shared renal risk over AKI-selective susceptibility.

Why this advances the question: N18 is not a generic negative control. It is a consequential within-renal rival whose predictions differ from an AKI-selective marker. The comparison can change the next clinical research decision: an AKI-selective pattern would justify external validation focused on peri-illness/nephrotoxin risk enrichment; an equal/larger N18 pattern would redirect validation toward CKD confirmation and longitudinal kidney monitoring. Neither result establishes a mechanism, a treatment effect, or the correctness of either code as a biochemical diagnosis.

## Exposure, covariates, and unchanged primary estimand

Compute race-free 2021 CKD-EPI eGFRcr from creatinine, age, and sex and 2012 CKD-EPI eGFRcys from cystatin C, age, and sex. Let `delta=eGFRcys-eGFRcr`; never include eGFRcr, eGFRcys, and delta simultaneously. Use maximum valid left/right grip, then sex-standardize grip. The intervention contrast remains delta −15 versus 0 at eGFRcr 90, separately at sex-specific 25th and 75th grip percentiles.

Covariates are age, sex, raw categorical ethnicity, smoking, diabetes, center, assessment year, BMI, waist, averaged valid BP replicates, CRP, glycemia, and albuminuria only if urine units are frozen. Continuous main effects use restricted cubic splines with cohort-fixed 5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th percentile knots.

The unchanged primary estimand is the standardized 10-year N17 cumulative-incidence difference and ratio for delta −15 versus 0 at each grip level in the original AKI cohort.

## Baseline same-risk-set competing-transition analysis

Fit cause-specific Cox models from the common initial state for N17-first, N18-first, death-first, and same-day tie. The primary comparison treats each other transition as competing, then g-computes all transition cumulative incidences over the empirical common-risk population. Aalen–Johansen curves are unadjusted descriptions; Fine–Gray models are sensitivities.

Let `theta_A=log RR10(N17_first; delta −15 vs 0)`, `theta_C=log RR10(N18_first; delta −15 vs 0)`, and `D=theta_A-theta_C`. Estimate both grip-specific D values and `M=D_low_grip-D_high_grip`. Use a participant-level bootstrap that resamples participants once per replicate, refits all transition/death models, repeats imputation and standardization, and returns the joint covariance and percentile 95% intervals. Do not compare raw risk differences as evidence of specificity because N17 and N18 baseline risks differ. Report both endpoints' absolute CIF/RD for clinical magnitude, but use D for relative-scale selectivity.

Sensitivity analyses: primary-position N17 and N18 using 41202/41262; tie assignment in both directions; first-year exclusion; complete case; eGFRcys/eGFRcr ratio and percent-difference exposure; Fine–Gray; PH diagnostics; adjustment blocks (demographic, eGFRcr/albuminuria, cardiometabolic/inflammatory, grip/frailty proxies). Sequential attenuation describes measured confounding and never proves mechanism.

## Result-pattern interpretation

The labels below apply only after the censoring, pairing, event-support, convergence, and calibration gates pass.

- **Supportive for the unchanged AKI hypothesis:** the original-cohort AKI risk-difference lower 95% bound is >0. Clinical usefulness additionally requires its lower bound >0.5 percentage points and improved calibrated decision utility.
- **Favors AKI-selective enrichment:** the unchanged AKI hypothesis is supported and the lower 95% bound for D is >0. If N18 has adequate precision and the pattern survives primary-position/tie sensitivities, this supports using discordance as a marker that enriches more for coded acute than coded chronic first transitions. It does not exclude occult CKD, because occult CKD may remain uncoded or first present during an AKI admission.
- **Favors occult chronic/shared renal burden:** the N18 contrast is positive and D's upper 95% bound is <=0, or N18 is clearly positive while the AKI upper bound is <=0. This is adverse to the AKI-selective interpretation and redirects clinical follow-up toward CKD confirmation/monitoring.
- **Shared renal-risk marker, unresolved interpretation:** both N17 and N18 contrasts are positive but D crosses 0. This supports prognostic renal information but cannot distinguish acute susceptibility from chronic burden.
- **Adverse to the primary AKI hypothesis:** the original-cohort AKI risk-difference upper 95% bound is <=0, regardless of N18.
- **Inconclusive:** D crosses 0 with inadequate N18 precision; same-day coding is substantial and tie assignment changes D; all-position effects disappear in primary-position analyses; release linkage is absent; code/date pairing fails; or baseline and learned results materially disagree without a calibrated winner. A precise null for both endpoints is adverse to both prognostic claims, whereas an imprecise null is inconclusive.

Low-grip modification is a separate secondary claim: support requires the relevant joint low-minus-high contrast interval to exclude 0 in the hypothesized direction and stability across transition definitions. A low-grip pattern shared by N17 and N18 supports frailty/shared reserve, not an acute mechanism.

## Clinical decision relevance and claim boundaries

If externally validated, an AKI-selective pattern could identify ambulatory adults with apparently preserved creatinine eGFR for whom peri-illness medication review, hydration/sick-day counseling, or closer creatinine monitoring merits prospective testing. An equal/larger N18 pattern would instead prioritize confirmatory kidney assessment, albuminuria evaluation, and CKD surveillance. This observational study cannot recommend those actions, show benefit, diagnose occult CKD, establish AKI stage/KDIGO criteria, measure true GFR, or attribute discordance to muscle, inflammation, or renal reserve.

Essential additional evidence includes renal-clinician approval of N17/N18 and kidney-failure code definitions; linked release/censoring documentation; urine unit confirmation; encounter-level HES/admission data to distinguish principal versus incidental coding and testing opportunity; repeat outpatient creatinine/cystatin C/albuminuria or measured GFR to adjudicate chronic filtration; and external validation before a clinical trial.
