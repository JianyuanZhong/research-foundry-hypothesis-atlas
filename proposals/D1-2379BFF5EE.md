# Does laboratory observation history transport across ICU surveillance regimes?

Status: Episode-23 adjacent repair of `[prior hypothesis]`. This preserves the parent’s adult first-stay, ICU-relative landmark, competing-risk estimand and primary whole-hospital evaluation. It is an observational prognostic/measurement-process study, not a causal test-ordering, hospital-quality, alert-benefit, or treatment recommendation study.

## Unresolved question and substantive advance

The strongest available evidence is the parent’s outcome-blind feasibility audit and exact local schema/header inspection; it does not establish a fitted laboratory increment, transportability, clinician intent, or clinical benefit. The eICU-02 seed motivates testing dependence on testing practice, while eICU-08 motivates reduced-testing falsification; both remain untested hypotheses.

The unresolved claim is stronger than “BvP works on average in held-out hospitals”: does the laboratory-observation increment remain informative when the target hospital’s pre-landmark surveillance regime differs from the hospitals used for fitting? Random hospital holdout can average over, or inadvertently represent, similar testing cultures. A model that succeeds only in high-cadence laboratories may be a workflow fingerprint and unsafe to interpret as patient-linked information elsewhere.

Hypothesis: after recorded physiology, vital-monitoring intensity, and pre-landmark documented care-limitation history are accounted for, the BvP-minus-Bv incremental recorded-death information is positive and directionally stable in outcome-free, surveillance-regime-blocked held-out hospitals; the increment will attenuate toward Bv in a within-risk, within-regime process permutation. A learned temporal model will add information only if the ordering/persistence of the same lab-process and physiology channels is preserved and its gain survives regime-blocked validation.

A stable result would narrow the claim toward transportable patient-linked prognostic information and justify a prospective silent-mode review study. Failure confined to a surveillance regime would identify a clinically important deployment boundary and support a workflow interpretation. Neither result says that increasing or reducing testing changes mortality.

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must newly freeze:

1. the eligible cohort, analyte/interface/revision codebook, six hourly bins, EOL codebook and regime codebook;
2. paired cause-specific models Bv and BvP, plus the learned temporal T-v on identical episode inputs and hospital folds;
3. zero-shot held-out predictions, competing-risk calibration/Brier and cumulative-incidence outputs, fixed-burden review metrics, hospital-clustered uncertainty, and regime-specific contrasts;
4. a regime-blocked transport table and interaction estimate, process/order/site falsifications, support audit, and an output-linked interpretation trace.

The primary estimand remains the held-out macro-hospital difference in cumulative incidence prediction for recorded ICU death by minute 1,800, with alive ICU discharge competing, BvP minus Bv. The new secondary estimand is the difference in that contrast across outcome-free surveillance regimes and the BvP-minus-Bv contrast when an entire regime is held out. It is not a causal effect.

## Baseline, alternative and regime challenge

Bv is a transparent elastic-net pooled discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly rows from 360 through 1,800 and separate recorded-death and alive-discharge heads. Inputs are demographics/admission context, compatible lab state plus availability/masks, vital state plus masks, and prespecified vital-state summaries (first/last/min/max and slope only with at least two observations). It excludes vital observation counts/gaps from the state baseline.

BvP adds only laboratory-process blocks: counts, distinct observation times, first/last time, recency, span/gaps, revision-known status and stable interface/system indicators. BvP-EOL adds only pre-landmark EOL presence/timing as the parent’s interpretation challenge. BvV is a vital-process placebo.

T-v is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six hourly [0,360] bins, with exactly the supported BvP channels, masks and EOL-history channels, the same outcome heads, preprocessing and hospital splits. It tests whether temporal persistence/co-movement reveals information lost by summary blocks, rather than merely seeking a predictive gain. Required controls are an order-shuffled T-v, process-channel ablation and a parameter-matched non-temporal model.

Primary outer evaluation is five approximately 20% whole-hospital folds, keeping all `uniquepid` episodes together; development hospitals use a 75/25 split. No held-out outcome, post-landmark record or held-out preprocessing enters fitting, coding, imputation, tuning, calibration or thresholds.

For the new policy-shift challenge, calculate each hospital’s outcome-free pre-landmark surveillance profile from eligible development/held-out predictor rows: per-stay lab observation-time cadence, vital observation-time cadence, and lab-to-vital cadence ratio. Use only development-hospital distributions to freeze cutpoints (terciles, with a prespecified fallback to median split if a tercile has fewer than 20 hospitals); assign a target hospital using its predictor rows without target outcomes. Report the parent five-fold results by target regime. If support permits, perform leave-one-regime-out fits, training on two regimes and testing on the third, while retaining whole hospitals and all patient-level joins. This is zero-shot covariate-shift evaluation, not target-outcome adaptation. Hospital descriptors and `hospitaldischargeyear` may be reported as diagnostics; they are not a substitute for surveillance-regime measurement.

Report macro-hospital paired differences first and patient-weighted results second. Use hospital bootstrap or hierarchical intervals (>=1,000 hospital resamples if feasible), per-hospital calibration slope/intercept, competing-risk integrated Brier, cumulative incidence, alert burden, capture and PPV. At q={0.01,0.02,0.05,0.10}, match BvP to Bv’s alert count within held-out hospital using outcome-free top-k selection; label-relative net-benefit arithmetic is a review diagnostic, not clinical utility.

## Support and falsification

The regime branch requires at least 20 hospitals per regime, 20 eligible stays per evaluated hospital, >=2,000 stays and >=30 recorded deaths per evaluated regime, and >=10 event-bearing hospitals; otherwise it is inconclusive and the parent primary result is not replaced. Each retained analyte/interface and vital field needs finite values, per-hospital support and state/process overlap. EOL-specific claims require the parent’s EOL support gates. Failed extraction, positivity, convergence, calibration or finite-metric gates also produce an inconclusive result.

Prespecified falsifications:

- Within held-out hospital and Bv-risk stratum, permute complete lab-process vectors; the BvP increment should attenuate toward Bv. Failure suggests coding or site fingerprints.
- Shuffle six-bin order while preserving each episode’s values and masks; a T-v gain that survives is not evidence for temporal ordering.
- Compare lab-process and vital-process increments, and repeat with a common analyte panel, strict revision-only rows, and interface/system blocks ablated. Gains confined to interface/revision or regime coding support workflow rather than patient-level interpretation.
- Randomly permute frozen regime labels across hospitals as a negative control; regime interaction should disappear.
- Compare BvP, BvP-EOL and the no-recorded-EOL stratum. Attenuation after EOL history supports documented care-limitation workflow; persistence narrows but does not prove a patient-level mechanism.
- Prohibit post-360 rows, actual Apache mortality, target-outcome calibration and hospital-descriptor prediction.

Supportive evidence requires an uncertainty-supported BvP-minus-Bv increment that remains directionally consistent in held-out and regime-blocked results, temporal gain only when order is retained, process-permutation attenuation, no comparable vital/site placebo, stable fixed-burden review outputs, and adequate EOL support for any care-limitation interpretation. Adverse evidence is a reproducible null/worsening, regime-specific collapse, workflow-matched gain, permutation failure or coding reversal. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse regime/event support, unresolved units, no overlap, unstable temporal fits or intervals spanning clinically meaningful ranges.
