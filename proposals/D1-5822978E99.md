# Laboratory-observation history after recorded physiology: an outcome-blind support-gated transport experiment

Status: Episode 21 successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process study. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that an alert or review benefits patients.

## Evidence boundary and unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence is the parent’s outcome-blind audit: 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows available by the landmark. These are feasibility/support counts, not model performance, transportability, physiologic truth, or clinical benefit. The parent explicitly left vital coverage and overlap unverified. The local eICU catalog confirms that the required source members and columns exist, but availability of a column is not adequate support for a transported comparison.

The unresolved claim is:

> At ICU-relative minute 360, after adjustment for the physiologic and laboratory state actually recorded by that time and for general recorded vital-monitoring intensity, does patient-linked laboratory observation history provide incremental, calibrated information about recorded ICU death through minute 1,800 that transports to held-out hospitals and changes a fixed-burden retrospective review classification?

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is that `Bp` (state plus laboratory observation-process history) improves the paired macro-hospital held-out integrated competing-risk Brier score and calibration relative to `Bv` (the same state/availability inputs plus recorded vital state), with a directionally favorable strict intensity-adjusted `BpV-BvV` contrast and at least one support-qualified equal-review-burden threshold comparison. The hypothesis is refuted if the increment disappears after vital-state/intensity adjustment, is not transportable, is explained by hospital/interface descriptors, or survives state- and hospital-stratified process permutation.

This is a stronger question than whether testing frequency predicts death. It asks whether a patient-linked surveillance history adds evidence after recognizable recorded state and general monitoring opportunity have been accounted for. “Review candidate” means a retrospective label-relative risk reclassification; it does not mean a clinician would review it, that testing was ordered because of concern, or that changing surveillance improves outcome.

## The readiness/support repair

All support operations below use only the predictor window and source identifiers/measurement values. They do not inspect outcome labels, discharge status after cohort selection, or post-landmark data when deciding admissibility. The solver must save the exact counts and codebook used.

1. Freeze the cohort and split manifest from `patient`. Assign hospitals once to five approximately equal outer folds, stratified by eligible episode count and, only for reporting/stratification, event support. Keep `uniquepid` intact. Because event support is needed to describe fold readiness, the manifest must be generated once and then frozen; no fold may be rebalanced after seeing model performance.

2. Freeze the laboratory codebook before outcome analysis. The candidate panel is potassium, sodium, hemoglobin, hematocrit, glucose, creatinine, BUN, calcium, WBC, platelets, bicarbonate, FiO2, PaO2, PaCO2, pH, anion gap, HCO3, oxygen saturation, magnesium, and base excess, represented by exact `labname` + `labmeasurenamesystem` + `labmeasurenameinterface` triples. Audit triple-to-name consistency, numeric parse rate, revision timing, and the presence of incompatible apparent units across hospitals. Keep a triple only if its naming/unit interpretation is stable; otherwise mark it audit-only or exclude it from state features. Counts of rows or times may use only a frozen admitted triple, never a newly discovered post hoc name. If the source labels cannot establish a common numeric interpretation, the numeric lab-state contrast is not interpretable and the primary experiment is inconclusive; no silent conversion is allowed.

3. Freeze a common vital panel using the exact schema fields above. A candidate variable is primary only if it has a numeric value in at least 30% of eligible stays, at least two distinct recorded observation times in at least 20% of eligible stays, and at least 10 eligible stays with a value in at least 80% of hospitals having at least 20 eligible episodes. Variables failing this rule remain in the audit table but cannot be selected per fold. This is deliberately stricter than “at least one value in a hospital.” If the complete primary state panel cannot meet this rule, downgrade the affected contrast to inconclusive rather than substitute a different state definition after outcomes are known.

4. For each outer evaluation fold, require at least 80% of its evaluation hospitals to meet the same per-variable support rule and require overlapping ranges/observed-bin support between development and evaluation hospitals. A fold failing this condition produces descriptive predictions only and is not pooled into the primary transport estimate. If fewer than four of five folds are support-qualified, the primary transport claim is inconclusive. Report the support-qualified and failed folds separately.

5. For the process features, require at least 20 eligible stays per hospital for a hospital-level transport diagnostic and preserve zero counts as zero rather than missing. Compute target-hospital percentiles only from an outcome-free calibration subset of eligible predictor-window episodes, with at least 50 calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes per target hospital; otherwise omit `Br` and report that its relative-process diagnostic is unsupported. This does not rescue a failed common numeric state panel.

These gates are readiness criteria, not evidence supporting the hypothesis. They also prevent the solver from choosing a more favorable analyte subset or hospital subset after observing outcomes.

## Features and matched models

The common state features are demographic/admission context from `patient`; lab first/last/median/range and slope when at least two values exist; lab analyte availability; and vital first/last/median/range/slope and missingness for every retained vital field. All continuous preprocessing, imputation, scaling, codebooks, and penalties are frozen without outcome use within the development side of each outer fold, with the common panel itself frozen by the predictor-only protocol above.

The patient-level lab-process block contains, per retained lab triple, total rows, distinct result times, first/last result time, recency to minute 360, inter-result gaps, revision-known status, interface/system indicators, total retained rows, distinct analytes, and total span. Process counts are not interpreted as orders: `lab` has results/revisions, not complete orders or intent.

The vital-process block contains periodic/aperiodic row counts, distinct observation times, last observation, span, and six one-hour-bin coverage indicators. It is kept separate from vital values.

Fit these prespecified models:

- `Bv): pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic heads over hourly rows `[360,1800]`, for recorded ICU death and alive discharge, using demographic/admission context, the frozen lab state/availability block, and the frozen vital state/missingness block. It is the transparent state baseline.
- `Bp): exactly `Bv` plus the frozen patient-level laboratory-process block, excluding `hospitalid` and hospital descriptors. The primary scientific contrast is paired held-out `Bp-Bv`.
- `BvV` and `BpV`: `Bv` and `Bp) plus the frozen general vital-observation-intensity block. The strict process contrast is `BpV-BvV`; it is not claimed to remove all workflow or unmeasured physiology.
- `Br`: a secondary relative-process diagnostic replacing continuous raw process quantities with outcome-free within-target-hospital percentiles, only where the calibration/support rules pass.
- `T`: one compact one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins in `[0,360]`, using the exact frozen `BpV` channels and masks, no hospital ID, and competing death/discharge heads. Fit only in development hospitals; use a development validation subset for stopping/hyperparameters. Compare it with a parameter-matched non-temporal control using the same `BpV` inputs. An order-shuffle ablation tests whether any T increment depends on temporal ordering rather than merely its aggregate inputs.

All models use the same cohort, landmark, input admissibility, outer folds, outcome, and uncertainty plan. `T` is not required to make the primary `Bp-Bv` question answerable.

## Baseline versus learned alternative: why both are scientifically needed

The transparent `Bv/Bp` baseline is selected as primary because coefficient blocks and contrasts are auditable: it can show whether the laboratory-process increment remains after recorded vital state and general vital-monitoring intensity, and whether the effect is concentrated in one analyte, revision class, interface, or hospital. It is a simple baseline that answers the state-versus-process question directly.

The matched temporal `T` is retained because six-bin summaries can lose ordering, persistence, recency patterns, and co-movement—for example, a late cluster of tests after a sustained physiologic trajectory can have the same total count as evenly spaced testing. A temporal improvement over a parameter-matched non-temporal control using identical channels would reveal that the timing structure itself carries information, a substantive measurement-process result rather than a small predictive contest. If T gains only discrimination without calibration or transport support, that does not establish a clinically useful advance.

The method is deferred if common-panel support fails, if the validation fit is unstable, if held-out hospitals lack overlap, or if its order-shuffle/parameter-matched comparison does not test temporal information beyond the transparent features. It is not rejected because it uses a neural architecture or might use a GPU. An EHR-only latent Gaussian-process alternative is deferred because it adds a representation without a sharper identification contrast for this question. A full Delphi reproduction is unavailable because the eICU snapshot lacks the UK Biobank trajectory/genetic/lifestyle scope; the narrow equal-input temporal adaptation is the useful bounded alternative. A full Oncoformer reproduction is unavailable because images and the main article/STAR Methods are unavailable; a lab-only version would duplicate the present temporal test without improving identification.

## Evaluation, fixed-burden decision translation, and uncertainty

Use five grouped outer folds with hospital holdout and person integrity. Report every hospital’s eligible episodes, recorded deaths, competing discharges, retained-variable support, and whether its fold is support-qualified. The primary metric is the paired macro-hospital difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score over hourly `[360,1800]` and calibration of the death cumulative-incidence prediction. CIF at 1,800 is secondary. Use at least 1,000 hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical resamples where feasible, with simultaneous intervals for the four prespecified thresholds and fold-level results.

At minute 360, for each held-out prediction and `q in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}`, report the fixed rule `risk >= q` for recorded death by 1,800. Report Bv/Bp alert rates, incremental cells `(Bv < q, Bp >= q)`, observed competing-event risk and calibration per cell, death capture, PPV, false-review burden, and number needed to review. In addition, construct a development-frozen equal-review-burden comparison: within each held-out hospital and fixed Bv-risk stratum, choose the same top-k review fraction for Bv and Bp, where k is frozen from development hospitals and applied without target outcomes. This ensures that a gain cannot be attributed only to producing more alerts. A q cell or equal-burden cell is interpretable only if it contains at least 200 held-out episodes, 20 recorded deaths, and 20 hospitals; otherwise label it unsupported. These are label-relative retrospective diagnostics, not utility, treatment effect, or clinical benefit.

Primary falsifications are:

- permute lab-process channels within hospital, Bv-risk stratum, and vital-coverage stratum while holding state fixed;
- repeat the strict `BpV-BvV` comparison and equal-burden policy;
- repeat under strict common-panel and revision-known-only lab rules;
- compare hospital-descriptor-only predictions from `hospital`;
- inspect concentration by analyte family, exact codebook triple, interface, and hospital;
- repeat fixed-q and equal-burden reclassification after the matched process permutation; and
- perform temporal order-shuffle and parameter-matched controls for T.

A patient-level interpretation is weakened or refuted if the gain survives state-matched process permutation, is reproduced by hospital descriptors, is confined to one hospital/interface, reverses under stable revision/unit handling, or vanishes after vital-state/intensity adjustment. A gain that appears only in raw process features but not in the strict and relative-process diagnostics is treated as workflow/coverage evidence, not patient-level surveillance evidence.

## Interpretation rules

Supportive evidence requires all readiness gates; leakage-free predictions in support-qualified folds; directionally favorable `Bp-Bv` Brier/calibration results with uncertainty excluding no improvement; a favorable strict `BpV-BvV` direction; at least one support-qualified equal-burden q comparison with improved death capture or PPV without calibration harm; attenuation under matched process permutation; and no single hospital dominating. This supports only transportable recorded-data prognostic/measurement-process evidence in this eICU snapshot.

Adverse evidence is no transport improvement, calibration harm, reversal after strict codebook/revision handling, loss after vital adjustment, persistence under state-matched permutation, site-descriptor replication, or concentration in one interface/hospital. A negative result remains scientifically valuable: it would argue that apparent testing-process signal is not robust evidence beyond recorded state and monitoring intensity.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed vital or lab support gates, fewer than four support-qualified folds, sparse decision cells, non-overlapping support, unstable fits, fold reversals, target leakage, or uncertainty that includes both clinically relevant improvement and harm. Inconclusive is not a positive or negative hypothesis result.

Harbor can verify source paths, headers, codebook logic, frozen splits, no post-landmark leakage, model fitting, held-out predictions, metric arithmetic, uncertainty, falsifications, and whether conclusions follow from saved outputs. It cannot establish that a test was ordered because of concern, that a recorded vital value is physiologically correct, that missing physiology did not cause the signal, that a human would review an alert, the cost of review, or that changing surveillance changes survival. Those stronger claims require expert chart adjudication, external validation across health systems, and a prospective silent-mode followed by impact study.
