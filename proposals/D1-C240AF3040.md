# Residual instability before ICU-to-ward transfer: era- and destination-transport stress test

Status: proposed substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. No outcome model has been fit and no hypothesis result is claimed. The frozen population, t0, final-24-hour information set, raw 48-hour outcome, endpoint-adjudicated first-transition analysis, original subject split, and primary paired Brier estimand are unchanged. This child adds a secondary temporal/destination transport experiment because that uncertainty is computable in MIMIC; plannedness, treatment intent, monitoring benefit, and external-site transport are not.

## Why this is the strongest remaining testable uncertainty

The parent already repairs a consequential endpoint error and distinguishes ICU return/death from valid live discharge. Its remaining central interpretive risk is that a pooled subject split can reward stable local charting, oxygen documentation, transfer thresholds, or destination coding even when T1's value trajectory is not portable across practice eras or ward workflows.

The alternatives are important but not equally identifiable. MIMIC has no reliable structured label for planned return, expected death, ward staffing, rapid-response availability, bed pressure, monitoring uptake, or benefit from monitoring. PACU paths, code status, and notes can support sensitivities or blinded adjudication, but cannot identify those clinical constructs automatically. In contrast, approximate source era, exact destination, service, observation timing, store lag, measurement density, and caregiver counts are available before t0. Therefore temporal/destination transport is the smallest available experiment that can strengthen or sharply narrow the claim.

MIMIC dates are subject-specific shifted dates and cannot be compared directly between people. Temporal era must be reconstructed only as an interval. For a transfer at t0 define offset = year(t0)-patients.anchor_year, era_low = lower(anchor_year_group)+offset, and era_high = upper(anchor_year_group)+offset. These are approximate original-year bounds, not exact calendar years.

A bounded complete-cohort audit, before fitting predictions, found:

- frozen cohort: 49,157 transfers, 40,014 subjects, 2,607 raw ICU-return/death composites;
- strict early: era_high <= 2013, n=17,890, 966 composites;
- bridge/ambiguous: neither strict early nor strict late, n=12,726, 649 composites;
- strict late: era_low >= 2015, n=18,541, 992 composites;
- 655 subjects occur in both strict-era sets, contributing 883 early and 1,027 late rows;
- 11 exact destinations have at least 250 transfers in each strict era, totaling 17,057 early transfers/915 composites and 17,957 late transfers/961 composites;
- strong workflow-label turnover exists: Medical/Surgical (Gynecology), Surgery/Pancreatic/Biliary/Bariatric, and Thoracic Surgery occur only in strict early, whereas Surgery and Oncology occur only in strict late. Cardiac Surgery falls from 3,142 to 916 transfers; Surgery/Trauma rises from 597 to 1,375.

These are support diagnostics, not model results. They show that a naive early/late comparison would conflate era with destination composition.

## Evidence boundary and falsifiable claims

The strongest inspected evidence supports only these claims. A longitudinal ICU-discharge model achieved internal and external discrimination, while explicitly acknowledging that bed occupancy, discharge timing, step-down resources, treatment limitation, and local practice can drive predictions [K1]. A systematic review found advantages for longitudinal modeling in selected, homogeneous populations but substantial variation in outcome windows and composite definitions [K2]. EHR measurement values and measurement processes carry separable but entangled information [K3]. The bounded audit establishes feasible strict-era and destination-overlap samples; it does not establish transportability.

Frozen primary hypothesis: in the fixed cohort and decision-time information set, T1's prespecified final-24-hour value-trajectory features improve held-out raw 48-hour three-state prediction over S0. The primary estimand remains paired test-set Brier(S0)-Brier(T1) under the original subject split.

New secondary transport hypothesis: models fit and calibrated only in the strict-early era retain a positive T1-over-S0 Brier gain on a subject-disjoint strict-late test, including after standardizing to common destination support; a value-history stream also adds late-era information beyond a process/workflow stream. This pattern is compatible with portable patient-state information, but does not identify physiology or mechanism.

Strongest rival: T1's pooled gain is era- or workflow-specific. Under this rival the gain disappears or reverses in strict-late evaluation, is explained by destinations entering/leaving support, or is carried mainly by masks, counts, recency, store lag, caregivers, destination, service, and transfer timing.

## Scientifically substantive learned alternative

Refit the parent's compact two-stream temporal convolutional model on the same 12 two-hour bins, raw target, subject-disjoint strict-early train/validation, and strict-late tests:

- value stream: standardized observed values and forward-filled values, without masks, gaps, counts, store lag, caregiver, destination, or service;
- process stream: masks, time since last measurement, within-bin counts, store-lag summaries from records available by t0, and caregiver counts, without measured values;
- static stream: the frozen S0 context, including exact destination, latest service, source ICU, admission type, transfer night/day, age, sex, ICU LOS, support state, and code status;
- two causal convolution layers per stream, kernel 3, dilations 1 and 2, 32 or 64 channels, dropout 0 or 0.2, three-class head;
- five fixed seeds, Adam at 1e-3 or 3e-4, early-validation log-loss stopping, and early-validation-only temperature scaling.

Required frozen ablations are full value+process+static, process+static, value+static, static only, and a workflow-ablation that removes destination, service, source ICU, admission type, and transfer timing from static inputs. Repeat the parent's within-person pre-endpoint value-bin order permutation with the final bin fixed and its patient-level value-sequence permutation. Apply frozen permutations in both early validation and late evaluation.

This alternative can reveal transportable nonlinear value history that T1 summaries lose, versus reliance on observation and workflow streams. It still cannot label a representation physiological: treatments, clinician responses, device choice, and measurement error can alter values. A domain-adversarial era model is deferred because making era hard to predict does not establish physiology and would add optimization assumptions without a new observable contrast. A recurrent model and rolling landmark remain deferred for the parent's stated sparse-support and changed-decision reasons.

## Falsification and result meanings

Portable trajectory information supported: the frozen pooled primary is supported; Delta_late and Delta_std are positive with paired 95% intervals above zero; E1 has acceptable late calibration; the upper 95% bound for transport_loss after common-support standardization is below 0.002; and the learned full model has a positive late value contribution with interval above zero. This supports within-site temporal portability of prognostic value-history information, not biology, external transport, or intervention benefit.

Workflow-composition explanation favored: pooled gain is supported but all-late gain materially attenuates, while common-support/destination standardization restores it, or destination/workflow ablation materially changes it. This means label/case-mix turnover explains important apparent drift; restrict claims to represented destinations.

Observation-process explanation favored: pooled or early gains are present, but late full-versus-process value contribution includes zero while process/static prediction and process drift dominate. This weakens a physiology-compatible interpretation but does not make process signal clinically useless.

Adverse for transport: Delta_late or Delta_std is negative with interval below zero, late calibration fails despite early calibration, or major endpoint sensitivities reverse direction. Conclude that pooled internal prediction is not transport evidence. Do not conclude that residual instability is biologically absent.

Fixed simple model adequate: E1 transports but the learned full model adds no calibrated value or order information and its 95% upper gain bound is below 0.002. Retain ridge T1 for this question.

Inconclusive: intervals include useful gain and harm/material loss, destination support yields low effective sample size, strict-era bounds are too coarse, calibration is unstable, endpoint sensitivities disagree, or value/process ablations conflict. An imprecise null is not refutation.

No outcome, subgroup, era boundary, destination threshold, or architecture may be changed after opening strict-late results to rescue the hypothesis.
