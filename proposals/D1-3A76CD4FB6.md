## 0. Hypothesis vs already-supported conclusion

Already supported (NOT the novel claim): absolute/stress hyperglycemia is *associated* with
short-term ICU mortality (descriptive regularity across many cohorts). "Relative" or
acute-on-chronic glycemia (stress hyperglycemia ratio, SHR = admission glucose / HbA1c-derived
chronic glucose) is reported to add prognostic information in single-centre studies [K2]; the
counter-claim that glycemic-gap terms add little *discrimination* beyond absolute glucose motivates
an informative-negative test [K3]; lab-test order-presence/timing alone can predict survival, so
observation processes can manufacture an apparent effect [K1].

Unresolved, falsifiable claim this experiment tries to FAIL (H-rel): **at the same absolute
admission glucose, a larger admission-anchored relative-glycemia ratio is NOT associated with higher
28-day mortality once the measurement/observation process (who is tested, when, how often, and
whether an anchor exists) is modelled and matched** — i.e. the observable apparent effect is a
testing-process artifact rather than a within-patient acuity-of-glycemia signal.

## 1. Why it matters / substantive advance over existing knowledge

Many ICU triage and glycemic-control decisions implicitly treat "stress hyperglycemia ratio" as a
severity marker. If the association survives observation-process decoupling and within-patient
discordance, SHR earns a place in risk stratification; if it collapses, clinicians should stop
treating the ratio as an acuity signal. The advance over [K2] is that [K2] self-reports the
measurement-selection gap without resolving it; over [K1] is that we *use* the artifact signature
as a predeclared falsifier rather than only noting it.

## 4. Falsification design (predeclared)

- S1 label-permutation collapse of any SHR coefficient.
- S2 value-free measurement-only negative control (testing-count/timing features only) with
  equivalence margin ΔAUC ≤ 0.005 → if the value-free control matches the SHR model, the ratio adds
  no value.
- S3 negative-control outcome (a diagnosis plausibly unrelated to acute glycemia).
- S4 within-subject discordance among the 16,242 multi-stay subjects: does a within-patient rise in
  the ratio associate with a higher within-patient mortality/outcome, vs a purely between-patient effect?
- Supportive/adverse/inconclusive rules predeclared around ΔAUC and the S2 equivalence margin;
  UNDERPOWERED anchor coverage → INCONCLUSIVE (not a silent negative).
