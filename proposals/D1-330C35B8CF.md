# When does an ICU kidney-risk signal travel? Physiology-only versus testing-process-augmented prediction

## Episode 4 successor and clinical decision

This is a substantive successor to the highest-ranked valid eICU candidate `[prior hypothesis]`, with assessed foundations `[prior hypothesis]` (testing-practice dependence), `[prior hypothesis]` (hypotension duration), and `[prior hypothesis]` (transfer risk). The prior line asked whether early recorded hypotension burden adds information beyond minimum MAP for a later observed creatinine rise. The new unresolved question is whether any such risk signal is transportable when the model is prevented from learning local laboratory-ordering practice.

The clinical decision is whether a future ICU kidney-surveillance alert should be based primarily on portable physiologic information, or whether adding early laboratory values and the pattern of laboratory testing yields a model that looks better locally but fails at another hospital. The study is explicitly a prognostic/transportability experiment. It is not a treatment-effect study, does not estimate the effect of monitoring or ordering tests, and does not establish that hypotension caused kidney injury.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence before execution is structural and methodological:

- The frozen eICU snapshot contains stay-level demographics, hospital identifiers, ICU-relative offsets, noninvasive MAP observations, other periodic vital summaries, timestamped laboratory records, admission diagnoses, and structured discharge status.
- The prior candidate and the expert seed make a plausible clinical case that time under a low recorded MAP may add information beyond a single minimum, while the testing-practice seed motivates concern that missingness and testing frequency can transport poorly.
- The local demonstrations support strict temporal validation, uncertainty/calibration reporting, and explicit recognition of measurement bias; the full natural-history and Bayesian articles were inspected locally, while the cancer README states that its main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable and only its supplement was inspected.

No available evidence establishes the proposed association in this snapshot, the direction of transportability differences, a complete KDIGO AKI phenotype, or clinical benefit.

The unresolved, falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among adult eICU stays with a completed six-hour landmark and an observed baseline creatinine, a model using only pre-landmark admission context and physiologic observations will have no worse and, under the directional alternative, better hospital-held-out calibration and discrimination for a later observed creatinine-rise phenotype than an otherwise matched model that additionally uses early laboratory values and laboratory-testing-process features. The process-augmented model may perform better in randomly mixed or within-hospital validation but will lose calibration or discrimination when tested in hospitals whose testing practices differ.

The null is equal transport performance after uncertainty, or no reproducible difference. A contrary result—process-augmented performance that is at least as good in held-out hospitals without calibration degradation—will be an adverse result for the proposed portability concern, not evidence that testing patterns are biologically valid or that a lab-ordering policy is clinically beneficial.

## Outcome and ascertainment estimands

The primary outcome is deliberately named an **observed creatinine-rise phenotype**, not adjudicated AKI. Baseline creatinine is the earliest numeric `lab.labresult` whose normalized `labname` exactly passes the prespecified creatinine-label audit in minutes 0–360, tie-broken by `labid`. Numeric values are used only after an audit of exact labels, `labmeasurenamesystem`, `labmeasurenameinterface`, and units; retain values within the prespecified plausibility range 0.1–30 mg/dL and do not silently convert ambiguous units.

The event is the first later numeric creatinine at 360 < `labresultoffset` <= 2,880 that is at least 0.3 mg/dL above that baseline. Report the continuous maximum rise and the timing of the first rise as secondary descriptions. Four mutually exclusive ascertainment-flow states are required:

- observed creatinine rise;
- at least one post-landmark creatinine before discharge/death/window end, without the qualifying rise;
- death or discharge before a qualifying follow-up test;
- no post-landmark creatinine before censoring, an unknown ascertainment state rather than a no-event state.

The primary performance analysis must show the full flow. It must not classify unknown ascertainment as “no rise.” The main event-risk analysis is among baseline-creatinine-positive stays with at least one post-landmark creatinine, with a prespecified inverse-probability-of-observation sensitivity analysis using only minute-360 information. A companion two-part analysis predicts (a) post-landmark creatinine ascertainment and (b) the rise conditional on ascertainment. This distinguishes a model that predicts clinicians’ testing from a model that predicts the recorded result.

Report, but do not silently combine into the primary outcome, the descriptive availability of `intakeOutput.dialysistotal`, `outputtotal`, `celllabel`, and `cellvaluenumeric`. The snapshot does not provide complete urine-output semantics, dialysis indication, baseline renal reserve, or expert KDIGO adjudication. These fields can be used for a transparent audit of possible kidney-support observations only.

## Model comparison and baselines

The unit of fitting is the ICU stay, with all preprocessing and hyperparameter choices learned inside training hospitals.

Define four nested prediction specifications:

- **B0, admission/context baseline:** age, sex, ethnicity, admission source, unit type, admission height, broad admission-diagnosis categories, and prespecified end-of-life indicator if included in the sensitivity analysis.
- **B1, physiology-only:** B0 plus early vital summaries, MAP minimum, covered-time and density variables, gap-limited duration below 65 mmHg, and depth-area below 65. A nested B1-minimum model replaces duration/depth with minimum MAP alone, preserving the prior question’s incremental test.
- **B2, process-augmented:** B1 plus only minute-360 laboratory values, lab-test counts/timing/gaps, and permitted lab-family observation indicators. No post-landmark values or outcome-derived variables.
- **B3, process-only diagnostic control:** B0 plus laboratory/process features but no physiologic trajectory, to quantify how much apparent performance is attributable to testing practice alone.

The primary scientific contrast is B2 versus B1 on hospital-held-out performance and calibration. B1 versus B1-minimum retains the earlier hypotension question; B3 is a diagnostic control, not a recommended model. Use an interpretable regularized logistic/discrete-time cause-specific hazard model as the primary implementation, with a fixed-knot spline for MAP burden as a prespecified sensitivity. A bounded tree-based model may be used only as a secondary same-input alternative; model complexity is not itself evidence of clinical value.

Because death and discharge can preclude a qualifying test, use a discrete-time cause-specific hazard representation for observed creatinine rise, death, discharge, and unknown/non-ascertainment states. For the conditional observed-outcome analysis, define the risk set and observation weights before fitting. Never treat discharge as a negative kidney outcome. Show B1/B2 performance for both event risk conditional on observed follow-up and the two-part ascertainment-plus-rise decomposition.

Use five grouped hospital folds, assigning every stay from a hospital to one fold. Within each training fold, reserve a validation subset by hospital or use nested grouped resampling for fixed model choices. A random stay-level split is a calibration diagnostic only and must not be the transportability result. Do not use hospital ID as a feature in held-out scoring. Also report leave-one-hospital-out results where the number of hospitals permits, and a temporal split only if an actual usable time variable is available; the released eICU snapshot removes calendar dates, so no calendar-temporal generalization claim may be made.

Report fold-level AUROC, area under the precision-recall curve, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, observed-versus-predicted risk plots, event/competing/unknown counts, observation-weight diagnostics, and B2-B1 differences with 95% uncertainty from hospital-cluster bootstrap or grouped resampling. Report performance distributions rather than only pooled point estimates. Prespecify a clinically meaningful calibration tolerance with expert input before interpreting a deployment claim; absent that input, report calibration differences without calling them clinically acceptable. Net benefit, alert burden, clinician workflow, and outcome impact are not identifiable here.

## Falsification criteria and result interpretation

Computationally supportive evidence requires all of the following:

1. The primary creatinine-label and unit audit is unambiguous, the cohort/ascertainment flow is reproducible, and the analysis retains unknown follow-up.
2. B1 has stable held-out calibration and discrimination across multiple hospitals and density/gap/modality sensitivity definitions.
3. B2's apparent within-hospital advantage is materially attenuated or reversed in held-out hospitals, with calibration drift aligned to between-hospital testing-process differences; or B1 is at least as transportable as B2 with less drift. The direction and uncertainty must be shown at the hospital-fold level.
4. The observation-process signal is reduced by within-hospital permutation of process features, while the physiology signal remains directionally stable; the result is not explained only by one hospital, long MAP gaps, high testing density, or a leakage variable.
5. A pre-landmark creatinine change negative control, where computable without using the same result twice, does not reproduce the claimed post-landmark transport pattern.

Adverse evidence includes B2 matching or outperforming B1 in held-out calibration and discrimination across hospitals; B2's process features adding no incremental performance even in mixed validation; no association of process features with ascertainment; or B1 failing similarly to B2. These outcomes would weaken the portability hypothesis or show that this endpoint cannot distinguish it, not establish a safe testing strategy.

Inconclusive evidence includes ambiguous creatinine labels/units, too few qualifying events or hospitals, poor duration/minimum-MAP overlap, nonpositive observation weights, unavailable process features, no post-landmark laboratory coverage, unstable predictions, or a large unknown-ascertainment fraction that cannot be separated from discharge/death. Inconclusive results trigger a better-defined kidney outcome or external/prospective monitoring data, not relaxed thresholds or selective exclusions.

Falsification checks are:

- rerun with MAP carry-forward gaps of 15 and 60 minutes and thresholds 60 and 70 mmHg;
- compare primary noninvasive MAP with `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean`/`pamean` without pooling modalities;
- add MAP count, covered fraction, gap summaries, and early lab intensity incrementally to identify density artifacts;
- permute process features within hospital and broad baseline-risk/coverage strata, preserving the outcome and physiologic features;
- permute exposure/features within hospital and minimum-MAP/coverage strata;
- shift the feature window earlier within 0–360 minutes and verify no post-landmark feature leakage;
- use the pre-landmark creatinine-change negative control and report its ascertainment limitations;
- audit the leakage sensitivity using the first-24-hour APACHE aggregates and demonstrate that any improved result is not accepted as primary evidence;
- report stratified results for transfer source, unit type, monitoring density, documented end-of-life planning, and hospital characteristics, without interpreting hospital residuals as quality rankings.

Supportive results justify only the computational claim that a physiology-constrained risk representation is at least as transportable as a process-augmented representation for this recorded eICU phenotype. They do not prove that physiology is biologically sufficient, that a creatinine rise is adjudicated AKI, or that changing an alert or testing policy improves care. A clinical decision would require expert review of creatinine/urine/dialysis semantics, external validation in a new ICU system, prospective impact evaluation, and assessment of alert harms and benefits.

The computation can establish source integrity, exact filtering, time ordering, feature provenance, model contrasts, hospital-held-out uncertainty, calibration, observation flow, and the stated falsification checks. It cannot establish complete KDIGO criteria, pre-ICU kidney function, true MAP artifact status, treatment intent, reasons for testing, dialysis indication, causality, clinical utility, or a treatment recommendation. Those claims require adjudicated chart review, richer physiology and treatment data, expert review, and another study.
