# Kidney-marker discordance at fixed combined eGFR in an outcome-blind supported population

Status: revised prospective study design; no marker–outcome association has been inspected or fitted. This child preserves the parent's kidney-marker question, day-30 clock, common N17/N18-free risk set, competing transitions, fixed-combined-eGFR profiles, uncertainty, censoring and noncausal limits. Its scientific change is an outcome-blind restriction from ages 40–80 to 40–69 because direct source counts falsified the parent's prespecified support gate above age 69. It also distinguishes a sparse primary-position measurement stress test from the adequately supported all-position primary outcome.

## Evidence, unresolved claim, and clinical advance

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. Large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have multiple kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, discordance is associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes; the inspected discussion notes that combining markers may lose prognostic information even while improving GFR estimation [K2]. In CRIC participants with established CKD, baseline and time-updated discordance carried information about adjudicated ESKD and mortality [K3]. These works do not establish whether discordance carries acute-versus-chronic renal transition information after holding a two-marker aggregate equation fixed.

The parent showed why N17-first versus N18-first is the strongest available interpretation rival, but its main contrast fixes eGFRcr at 90. Making delta more negative then necessarily lowers eGFRcys and the combined estimate, so any signal could simply encode lower aggregate filtration.

Primary unresolved hypothesis: among UK Biobank adults aged 40–69 in the common day-30 N17/N18-free risk set, the marker profile with 2021 CKD-EPI combined eGFR `G=90` and `delta=eGFRcys-eGFRcr=-15`, compared with `G=90, delta=0`, has a larger adjusted 10-year relative-risk contrast for N17-first than N18-first.

This is falsifiable and clinically consequential. A positive N17-selective profile contrast would justify external validation of discordance for peri-illness AKI enrichment beyond the aggregate two-marker equation. Disappearance of the parent's fixed-eGFRcr signal at fixed G would redirect interpretation toward lower aggregate filtration. Equal or larger N18 information would redirect validation toward CKD confirmation and longitudinal kidney monitoring.

The estimand is a two-marker profile contrast at equal equation-based aggregate eGFR. It is not an intervention on true filtration or a causal “effect of discordance,” and it cannot identify muscle, inflammation, renal reserve, occult CKD or an assay-specific mechanism.

## Marker coordinates, profiles and estimand

Convert creatinine from µmol/L to mg/dL only after unit verification. Compute race-free 2021 CKD-EPI eGFRcr, 2012 CKD-EPI eGFRcys and race-free 2021 CKD-EPI combined eGFR from baseline creatinine, cystatin C, age and sex. Let `delta=eGFRcys-eGFRcr` and `G=eGFRcr-cys_combined`.

At fixed age and sex, `(G,delta)` is a one-to-one reparameterization of the positive creatinine/cystatin-C pair in the relevant range: each single-marker equation is invertible and combined eGFR increases monotonically with both component eGFRs. Generate profiles separately at each participant's fixed age and sex:

- `P-: G=90, delta=-15`;
- `P0: G=90, delta=0`.

Across the revised ages 40–69, both profiles lie within the previously verified age-40–80 raw ranges: P- eGFRcr 91.4–97.2, eGFRcys 76.4–82.2, creatinine 51.4–88.8 µmol/L and cystatin C 0.905–1.020 mg/L; P0 eGFRcr/eGFRcys 84.1–88.4, creatinine 63.8–96.1 and cystatin C 0.841–0.966. Both profiles retain eGFRcr >=60. These are plausible equation profiles, not evidence of conditional positivity.

Define standardized 10-year transition risks under each profile over the empirical common-risk population. Let:

- `theta17G = log RR10(N17_first; P- versus P0)`;
- `theta18G = log RR10(N18_first; P- versus P0)`;
- `DG = theta17G - theta18G`.

DG is the primary selectivity parameter. Report both endpoints' CIFs, RDs and RRs for magnitude; do not compare raw RDs as specificity evidence. The parent's delta contrast at fixed eGFRcr=90 remains a secondary decomposition diagnostic, not co-primary evidence.

## Transparent baseline and matched corroboration

Primary transparent model: fit cause-specific Cox models from the common initial state for N17-first, N18-first, death-first and same-day tie. Include G and delta as restricted cubic splines plus a prespecified linear `G×delta` interaction, grip and inherited covariates. Treat other transitions as competing and g-compute all CIFs under P- and P0. Aalen–Johansen curves are unadjusted descriptions; Fine–Gray models are sensitivities.

Matched overlap baseline, frozen before outcomes:

- negative-discordance group: `85<=G<=95` and `-18<=delta<=-12`;
- concordant group: `85<=G<=95` and `-3<=delta<=3`;
- 1:1 optimal matching without replacement, exact on sex, 5-year age band and assessment year;
- caliper `|G_i-G_j|<=1.5`;
- propensity caliper 0.2 pooled SD of the logit using inherited nonprofile covariates, center, continuous age and G.

Reject matched inference if either group loses >50%, effective matched pairs are fewer than 1,000, or any prespecified covariate/G standardized mean difference exceeds 0.10. Estimate paired/clustered Aalen–Johansen CIFs and `Dmatch`; repeat matching inside each participant bootstrap. This is an overlap-population band contrast, not the exact P-/P0 estimand, and is used to detect model extrapolation.

Resample participants once per replicate, repeat imputation, matching, all transition models and standardization, and report joint covariance plus percentile 95% intervals for theta17G, theta18G, DG and Dmatch. Freeze at least 500 successful replicates; if fewer than 90% converge, inference is inconclusive rather than reduced post hoc.

## Measurement, collider and selection limits

G and delta reuse the same two noisy assays. Conditioning on G induces inverse dependence between marker-specific non-GFR determinants and assay errors if interpreted causally—a collider-like consequence—but does not invalidate the descriptive joint-profile estimand. Combined eGFR is not measured GFR. Holding it fixed does not equalize true filtration, and moving P0 to P- changes both assays in opposite directions without a known clinical intervention.

Single-measurement error can rotate observations across iso-G contours and create or attenuate local gradients. Do not run SIMEX without validated assay-error variances. Report documented assay-range checks, delta-band sensitivity and complete-case analysis.

The eGFRcr >=60 entry criterion selects on one profile component. Results apply only to that cohort, even though both target profiles satisfy the threshold. Baseline N18-free selection also depends on prior inpatient diagnosis and contact. Future N18 is first inpatient coding, not biochemical CKD onset; N17/N18 differences can reflect surveillance, admission and coding practices. Use participant-specific age/sex inversion; one raw assay pair for all participants is invalid. Field 41259-0.0 is a lifetime HESIN row count that includes post-outcome care and must not be adjusted for. Without dated record-level HESIN, healthcare-contact opportunity remains unmeasured.

## Falsification and result interpretation

Interpret only after release, pairing, support, event-count, convergence, calibration and uncertainty gates pass.

- **Supports profile information beyond aggregate two-marker eGFR and favors N17 selectivity:** theta17G lower 95% bound >0, DG lower bound >0, Dmatch agrees in direction, and primary-position/tie sensitivities do not reverse it. Because the outcome-blind audit found only 248 primary-position N18-first events (versus 1,906 N17-first), lack of primary-position statistical significance alone is not a failure; directional reversal or gross incompatibility is.
- **Lower aggregate filtration explains the parent's signal:** inherited fixed-eGFRcr N17 contrast is positive, but theta17G is precisely null/adverse and DG is not positive. This is adverse to discordance-specific enrichment.
- **Favors chronic/shared renal information beyond aggregate:** theta18G lower bound >0 and DG upper bound <=0, or N18 is positive while N17's upper bound <=0.
- **Shared profile marker, unresolved acute/chronic interpretation:** both profile RRs are positive but DG crosses 0 with adequate precision.
- **Adverse to both profile claims:** both endpoint upper 95% bounds are <=0.
- **Inconclusive:** support/matched balance fails; release linkage or field pairing is unverified; N18 is too sparse; same-day ties materially change direction; all-position results fail primary-position sensitivity; matched and calibrated nonlinear results materially disagree; or intervals cannot distinguish the patterns. An imprecise dual null is inconclusive.

No result diagnoses occult CKD, validates N17 as KDIGO AKI, estimates true GFR, proves a marker mechanism or recommends care.

## Clinical and adjudication boundaries

A validated N17-selective fixed-G pattern could justify another study of peri-illness medication review, sick-day counseling or closer creatinine monitoring. A fixed-G N18/shared pattern would instead prioritize confirmatory kidney assessment and CKD surveillance. This study cannot show those actions help.

Required external evidence remains: renal-clinician review of N17/N18 and kidney-failure code sets; release-linked censoring and field semantics; marker/urine units; encounter-level HES to distinguish principal from incidental coding and testing opportunity; repeated outpatient creatinine/cystatin C/albuminuria or measured GFR; external validation; and a separate intervention study for clinical benefit.
