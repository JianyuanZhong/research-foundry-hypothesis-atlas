# Same fixed repeat-TACE question, with an audited estimable-basis rule rather than a rank-deficient nominal model

## Targeted repair and decision

This child preserves the parent’s exact clinical question, population, pathway ontology, event selection, five read-only HCC sources, parser and duplicate handling, `B/P/H` clocks, fixed common early panel, `O` and `Y`, evidence boundary, estimands, folds, fixed ridge penalty, uncertainty procedures, falsification gates, and interpretation branches. It changes only the mechanical representation of structurally non-estimable nuisance columns.

The unresolved question remains exactly:

> Among adults already selected for a recorded first repeat TACE, do the two prespecified leakage-safe intermediate-to-current within-assay direction indicators predict whether the fixed early post-event panel is observed after matching the intermediate measurement process, and, among episodes with that panel observed, improve out-of-fold prediction of the concordant albumin-down/total-bilirubin-up transition beyond remote-to-current direction, a compact decision-time core, and exactly matched intermediate-panel observation variables?

The outcome remains `Y=1` only when albumin at the selected post panel is strictly lower than its contemporaneous `P` value and total bilirubin is strictly higher; equality on either assay is a non-event. It remains only a concordant adverse-direction laboratory transition, not toxicity, decompensation, safety, complication, treatment effect, or clinically important change.

A new complete-source rank/fold audit found that the parent is not statistically overburdened in effective-complexity terms under its very strong fixed `lambda=1` mean-loss ridge: approximate slope effective degrees of freedom were only 1.08–1.10 for `M_obs`, 1.14–1.17 for `M_hist`, 1.52–1.53 for `G_process`, and 1.56–1.58 for `G_value`. All training and held-out folds retained both classes, and no continuous predictor was constant. However, the nominal design matrices are structurally rank deficient: `sex_unknown` is zero in every fold; the outcome matrices have rank deficit one; observation matrices have rank deficit three in four folds and four in one fold because the two assay-process histories are nearly identical and become exactly identical in one training fold. The parent’s instruction to fix absent sex categories to zero resolves `sex_unknown`, but it does not explicitly resolve exact linear dependencies among process columns. Leaving solver-dependent coefficient allocations in a nominally rank-deficient basis is avoidable and weakens executable inference even though ridge predictions are usually unique.

A one-degree-of-freedom sum of the two numeric history indicators was audited but rejected as the primary repair: it would constrain albumin and bilirubin history to equal weights and therefore would not answer exactly the same two-indicator incremental prediction claim. The valid targeted simplification is instead an outcome-blind, fold-specific estimable-basis rule applied identically to each nested pair. It removes no information from the column space, does not use `O`/`Y`, and does not change any fitted prediction that a correctly optimized ridge model can identify from that column space.

## Targeted frozen estimable-basis simplification

For each training fold and for each model family (`M` or `G`), construct the ordered full-model candidate column list exactly as written above. Before reading the training outcome vector, process columns left-to-right as follows:

1. Apply the parent’s fixed sex-category rule first: if a sex indicator has no training observations, fix its coefficient to zero and record it.
2. Standardize the prespecified continuous columns using training means and population SDs. If a continuous column has zero/nonfinite training SD, fix it to zero under the parent’s existing rule and record it.
3. Starting with the intercept, retain a candidate column only if appending it increases numerical matrix rank, computed by singular-value decomposition with tolerance `max(n,p)*machine_epsilon*largest_singular_value`. Otherwise fix that coefficient to zero and record the exact predecessor columns spanning it.
4. Freeze this retained full-model basis for the nested pair in that fold. Fit the reduced model on the subset of those retained columns belonging to its formula, and the full model on all retained columns. Thus the two models use a common outcome-blind basis decision and differ only by the two `HP` columns when those add independent predictor information.
5. Apply the basis mapping learned from training unchanged to held-out rows. Never recompute rank with held-out data, `O`, `Y`, residuals, fit quality, or optimizer behavior. In bootstrap replicates, keep the original fold-specific retained-column identities fixed; do not let multiplicity resampling reselect a different basis. In linkage permutations and alternate frozen partitions, derive the basis from the unpermuted original predictor matrix for that partition and hold it fixed across permutations.

This is not feature selection: no outcome is consulted, no independent predictor direction is removed, and all fitted linear-predictor information in the training design column space is retained. If either `HP` column is linearly dependent after the shared process basis, report that the corresponding incremental direction is non-identifiable in that fold; do not replace it. If both `HP` columns are dependent in any fold, the same unresolved incremental claim cannot be tested in every held-out fold and the result is computationally inconclusive.

Execution must report, per family and fold, nominal columns, retained columns, fixed-zero columns, dependency witnesses, rank before/after, smallest retained singular value, and class counts. The audited expectation is: `sex_unknown` is fixed to zero in all folds; outcome-model rank deficit is otherwise resolved without dropping an `HP` column; observation-process dependencies involve nearly duplicated assay-history process columns, with one fold showing exact equality for all three paired process terms. Any discrepancy is not automatically failure, but unexplained reconstruction differences stop inference.

## Why this is a material validity improvement

The parent’s nominal coefficient counts look high relative to 104–106 `Y` training episodes and 192–195 `O` training episodes, but fixed strong ridge shrinks local effective slope complexity to roughly 1.1 and 1.5, respectively. Therefore deleting adjustment variables or compressing the two target indicators is not justified by sample size alone and could change the exact unresolved claim. The actual executable defect is narrower: deterministic exact dependencies make nominal coefficients non-identifiable, especially in the paired observation-process block. The frozen estimable-basis rule answers the identical prediction question using the identical predictor column space while making optimization, bootstrap, and permutation execution solver-independent and auditable.

## Claim and verifier limits

The strongest supported claim remains feasibility and now estimable-basis feasibility. Whether numeric intermediate history predicts `O` or improves conditional prediction of `Y` is still untested. A favorable computation could establish only reproducible noncausal prognostic information for the fixed direction-only transition in the selected observed-panel denominator, qualified by the observation-selection gate, component attribution, timing specificity, influence, and partition stability. It cannot establish toxicity, decompensation, clinical deterioration, treatment benefit, treatment harm, monitoring utility, missing-at-random assumptions, or transportability.

A verifier can check hashes/full scans, exact source columns and composite joins, population/event reconstruction, parser/duplicates, `B/P/H` and post-panel clocks, all denominator counts, fold assignment, outcome-blind rank reduction, fixed mapping in resampling/permutations, fixed-penalty fits, estimands, diagnostics, and branch-consistent conclusions. Clinical meaning requires unit-validated assays, reference ranges and analytical variation, supportive-care/exposure context, adjudicated outcomes, expert review, and external or prospective validation.

## Independent bounded successor question

Without changing the frozen construction or relabeling laboratory trajectories, a clinically consequential successor is whether a parsimonious predictor trained on the fixed pre-repeat state can identify which selected patients will *lack* the fixed early panel despite documented observation through +12 hours. This is a care-process forecasting question, not a safety endpoint: it could reveal systematic blind spots in event-aligned laboratory capture and inform prospective data-completeness or monitoring-workflow studies. The present `G_process/G_value` experiment already supplies its feasibility and the narrower numeric-history test. Any actual workflow recommendation would require prospective validation and adjudication of why panels were or were not ordered; it is not licensed by this archive.
