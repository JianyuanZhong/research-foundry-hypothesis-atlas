## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported before fitting is only that the configured snapshot contains HbA1c, acute glucose, insulin and time-stamped ICU/hospital records, and that indexed literature has studied SHR as a prognostic descriptor. The seed's proposed relationship and any insulin-safety value are untested here. The local data do not establish that SHR measures insulin sensitivity, stress physiology, or a causal treatment response.

The falsifiable primary hypothesis is:

> At the first early-ICU paired glucose/HbA1c landmark, among otherwise comparable episodes, the observed association between recorded insulin initiation in the next six hours and first ascertainable hypoglycemia in the following 24 hours differs by acute glucose elevation relative to chronic glycemia; specifically, the high-SHR stratum has a larger absolute observed-risk contrast than the low-SHR stratum after adjustment for absolute glucose, HbA1c, baseline severity and glucose-observation intensity.

The estimand is an observed-risk label contrast, not an average treatment effect. If A denotes insulin initiation in [t0,tau), S denotes low/high SHR and Y denotes first hypoglycemia after tau, estimate in each stratum:

Delta_s = standardized Risk_model(Y | A=1,S=s) - Risk_model(Y | A=0,S=s)

and H = Delta_high - Delta_low, with separate death and ICU-exit competing transitions. Setting A to 1 or 0 in a fitted model is not a counterfactual insulin intervention. The decision-relevant companion estimand compares BSHR versus B0 held-out capture and PPV of Y at a fixed 10% review queue, with 5% and 20% sensitivities. This asks whether the phenotype is useful for insulin-safety review; it does not prove an alert should change care.

A clinically material interaction region is |H| >= 0.05 absolute 24-hour risk, prespecified as an interpretive threshold rather than a known minimal important difference. A continuous restricted-spline SHR interaction is primary; low/high strata use training-derived median cutpoints frozen before validation/test. Quartiles are descriptive only.

## Clinical importance and advance

Insulin is frequently used in critical illness, while hypoglycemia is an immediate, preventable safety concern. Absolute glucose alone can treat a glucose value as equivalent across patients whose chronic glycemic background differs. A reproducible relative-elevation signal could identify a subgroup needing closer glucose surveillance or conservative protocol review, and a null could discourage adding a biologically suggestive but unstable ratio to decision support.

The substantive advance is a phenotype-validity and decision-transport test, not another mortality correlation: it asks whether SHR adds calibrated, fixed-capacity information beyond its component measurements, survives temporal care-practice shift, and remains distinct from glucose-testing/insulin-selection process. The result would inform whether prospective insulin-safety validation is warranted. It cannot determine a safe insulin dose, a treatment threshold, or causal benefit/harm.

## Matched model comparison

Freeze the split before reading test outcomes. B0 is a transparent penalized logistic cause-specific model for Y, D and X with absolute glucose and HbA1c but no SHR term; include prespecified splines and the insulin label A. BSHR is B0 plus the prespecified SHR spline and A-by-SHR interaction; it tests the seed's relative-elevation claim and is the primary model. Standardize predictions over the overlap-eligible test distribution within SHR stratum to obtain Delta_s and H. M1 is the masked chronological GRU described in the supporting note, using the same inputs, target and split. A process-only Bmask removes physiologic values but retains observation masks/counts, care context and A; similar H or queue performance means process selection is a plausible explanation. No model is granted scientific value for GPU use or complexity.

## Support, uncertainty and falsification

Before fitting, require dictionary/unit validity, unique first-stay/t0 keys, no reversed intervals, no post-t0 predictors, complete D/Y/X/N/U flow, and a pre-fit support table. The primary interaction is not estimable unless each A-by-S high/low cell has >=50 episodes and >=20 Y events, each S stratum has >=10 D events, empirical A propensity has support [0.05,0.95], effective sample size >=100 per stratum, and post-tau glucose ascertainment is >=80% in every primary cell. Do not merge cells, alter cutpoints or relax gates after viewing outcomes. Report the complete-case primary population and a separate HbA1c-missing flow; do not impute HbA1c and then claim a measured chronic baseline.

Falsification and sensitivity analyses are fixed in advance:

1. permute A within glucose band, SHR stratum, anchor-year group and split; the A-by-SHR interaction and queue gain should center toward null;
2. permute HbA1c within acute-glucose band, diabetes-context and era strata; a materially unchanged SHR signal indicates the ratio is not using chronic glycemia as claimed;
3. shuffle M1 time-bin order while preserving values/masks; chronological advantage should disappear;
4. predict a pre-index hypoglycemia window using future insulin labels only as a negative-control timing test; strong association indicates time leakage or selection, not support;
5. use the glucose-measurement process (any post-tau glucose test) as its own outcome and compare B0/BSHR/Bmask; large gains concentrated there indicate ascertainment rather than biology;
6. repeat with chemistry-only, blood-gas-only, and ICU-chart glucose sources; require unit-compatible direction, not identical magnitudes;
7. exclude prior PRBC/transfusion-proxy exposure where available and flag anemia-related HbA1c distortion; report this as a limitation because RBC lifespan and clinician indication are not observed;
8. report insulin item/status/route/dose strata only when units and administration semantics are valid; no completed dose, nutrition intake, glucocorticoid exposure or clinician intent is assumed.

## Interpretation and clinical limits

Supportive evidence requires all gates, a simultaneous 95% interval for H outside [-0.05,0.05] in the prespecified direction, compatible BSHR/M1 calibration and fixed-queue improvement over B0, attenuation under A/HbA1c/order permutations, and no comparable Bmask process-only signal. It supports only a reproducible observed-risk phenotype and justification for prospective insulin-safety validation.

Adverse evidence includes H precisely inside the material-null region, reversal across assay/source or era, Bmask equivalence, unchanged HbA1c permutation, strong pre-index negative-control association, or a signal confined to testing intensity. This would argue against using SHR as an insulin-safety stratifier in this snapshot; it would not prove that chronic glycemia is irrelevant or insulin is safe.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed HbA1c coverage, sparse A-by-S cells/events, poor propensity overlap/ESS, high U or missing post-tau ascertainment, incompatible units/status semantics, unstable temporal transport, or broad intervals spanning material benefit-like and harm-like directions. Report “not estimable with this snapshot”; do not relax the design.

MIMIC lacks clinician intent, actual nutrition/feeding, glucocorticoid exposure, continuous glucose monitoring, reliable completed insulin dose, RBC lifespan/transfusion context for HbA1c interpretation, and adjudicated symptomatic/severe hypoglycemia. It also lacks random insulin assignment and external hospital validation. Notes and MIMIC-CXR/raw waveforms are not needed and were not used. A supportive result cannot establish insulin harm/benefit, a dosing threshold, mechanism, or transport outside this MIMIC snapshot. Those stronger claims require adjudicated clinical hypoglycemia, treatment intent/dose, prospective temporal/external validation and ideally a randomized or causally identified study.

## Deferred alternatives

A causal g-formula/TMLE analysis is deferred because clinician indication, feeding, steroid exposure and reliable insulin administration are not adequately observed; adopting it would change the estimand and require a new Lead decision. A mechanistic glucose-insulin model is deferred because insulin sensitivity, nutrition, counter-regulatory hormones and continuous glucose are absent. A full transformer is deferred because the question can be answered by an auditable small GRU and additional complexity would not add a distinct clinical uncertainty. These alternatives should be revisited only if those dependencies become available or if M1 shows stable chronological information that merits a larger preregistered model.
