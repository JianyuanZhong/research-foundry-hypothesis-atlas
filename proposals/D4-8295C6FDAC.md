# Cardiac Work Versus Structural/Metabolic Cost

## Unresolved question

Among UK Biobank adults with cardiac MRI and repeat assessment, does lower LV ejection fraction and a larger LV end-diastolic volume at the same LV mass and workload indicate worse subsequent cardiovascular prognosis than the same measured cardiac output achieved with higher LV mass and ejection fraction?

## Clinical importance

LV ejection fraction and LV mass are common descriptors of cardiac structure and function, but they do not directly quantify how much work the heart performs relative to its structural and metabolic cost. A low-efficiency phenotype could identify patients at elevated cardiovascular risk who are not captured by ejection fraction or mass alone, informing earlier specialist evaluation and follow-up intensity.

## Evidence and closest work

The literature establishes that LV mass, ejection fraction, and chamber geometry each carry prognostic information, but the available abstracts do not establish that their combination identifies a distinct low-efficiency phenotype with incremental prognosis.

## Data and design

Use UK Biobank participants with cardiac MRI and repeat assessment. The primary population is adults with a first cardiac MRI and no prior cardiovascular diagnosis. Define LV ejection fraction, LV end-diastolic volume, and LV mass from the cardiac MRI fields. Define workload from repeated blood pressure and heart rate measurements where available. Follow from the first cardiac MRI for subsequent cardiovascular diagnosis and hospitalization.

## Baseline versus learned comparison

A simpler baseline compares ejection fraction, LV mass, and chamber volume separately or as conventional composite categories. A substantive alternative fits a penalized Cox model and a small gradient-boosting model on the same inputs to test whether nonlinear interactions improve time-to-event prediction and whether the learned model reveals stable interaction patterns.

## Falsification

A null or reversed association after prespecified adjustment and sensitivity analyses would falsify the proposed incremental prognostic claim.

## Limitations

Cardiac MRI is a single time point for many participants, and workload is not directly measured. The design cannot establish causality or prove a metabolic mechanism.
