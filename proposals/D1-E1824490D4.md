# Discordant creatinine recovery after AKI: a severity-, treatment-intensity-, and RRT-stratified MIMIC-IV study with KDIGO urine-output sensitivity analysis

Status: active draft, not yet a registered hypothesis. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Among adults in the ICU with KDIGO stage 2 or 3 acute kidney injury (AKI), does apparent creatinine recovery at 48 hours coexisting with persistently low urine output or positive fluid balance identify a worse 7-day ICU prognosis than creatinine recovery alone, after accounting for current severity and treatment intensity at the 48-hour stabilization decision point?

The parent proposal established a prespecified discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype and addressed the strongest confounder—current severity and treatment intensity at the decision point—with SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and renal replacement therapy (RRT) status as adjustment variables. However, RRT status is not merely a continuous treatment-intensity covariate: it marks a fundamentally different renal recovery phenotype and outcome trajectory. Patients on RRT at the 48-hour decision point have already had their native kidney function bypassed, and creatinine recovery in this group may reflect dialysis clearance rather than true renal recovery.

The UO sensitivity analysis tests whether the discordance phenotype's prognostic signal persists when renal recovery is defined by KDIGO urine-output criteria rather than creatinine, and whether the two definitions identify different patient subgroups. This directly tests whether the discordance signal is robust to the definition of recovery or is an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that longitudinal laboratory and support patterns can distinguish ICU patients with different prognoses. The parent proposal's discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype is a prespecified, falsifiable hypothesis that tests whether apparent creatinine recovery coexisting with persistently low urine output or positive fluid balance identifies a worse prognosis.

The UO sensitivity analysis addresses a major residual limitation: creatinine is a surrogate for measured kidney function, and its recovery may be influenced by factors other than true renal recovery, including fluid balance, muscle mass, and dialysis clearance. If the discordance signal persists when renal recovery is defined by KDIGO urine-output criteria, this would strengthen the claim that the discordance phenotype is a genuine prognostic signal rather than an artifact of creatinine's pharmacokinetic properties.

The unresolved claim is whether the discordance phenotype's prognostic signal is robust to the definition of recovery and whether it identifies a distinct patient subgroup with different prognosis.

## UO sensitivity analysis

### UO extraction logic

- Item mapping: urine output items from icu/outputevents (itemid mapping to d_items)
- Time window: 48-hour window from AKI onset to decision point
- Fallback for sparse data: if UO data are sparse (<50% of patients with UO measurements), report the UO sensitivity analysis as exploratory and interpret with caution

### KDIGO UO criteria

- Stage 1: UO <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6-12 hours or <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥12 hours
- Stage 2: UO <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥12 hours or anuria for ≥6 hours
- Stage 3: UO <0.3 mL/kg/h for ≥24 hours or anuria for ≥12 hours

### Analysis

- Primary analysis: creatinine-based discordance phenotype
- Sensitivity analysis: UO-based discordance phenotype
- Comparison: whether the two definitions identify different patient subgroups and whether the prognostic signal persists

## Falsification criteria

- If the discordance phenotype's prognostic signal is not robust to the definition of recovery, the claim is weakened
- If the UO sensitivity analysis shows no prognostic signal, the claim is refuted
- If the two definitions identify the same patient subgroup, the claim is strengthened
