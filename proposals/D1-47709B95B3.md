# Ascertainment-adjusted renal/nutrition risk during recorded insulin infusion

Status: episode-3 successor to [prior hypothesis]. This is a prospective prognostic/measurement-process study in eICU, not a causal treatment-effect study.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and audit a leakage-safe six-hour person-period cohort, fit an ascertainment model for whether glucose is measured in the next six hours, estimate the renal-worsening × documented-feed-loss contrast with and without inverse-probability-of-observation weighting, and compare the prespecified transparent model with a compact temporal model on identical periods and hospital splits.

Completion requires:

- a frozen cohort/data dictionary and flow diagram; counts of eligible periods, patients, hospitals, observed glucose windows, low-glucose events, competing events, nutrition coverage, insulin-unit composition and missingness;
- an auditable nutrition-label and glucose-unit/revision codebook;
- coefficients, standardized six-hour risk contrasts, stabilized observation weights and uncertainty for the interaction;
- held-out-hospital predictions, calibration, Brier score and alert-burden summaries for both models, with and without observation-process features;
- an ascertainment table showing how renal/nutrition features predict the chance of any glucose observation, and sensitivity analyses under strict labels, glucose interfaces, no carry-forward, discharge/EOL exclusions and placebo windows;
- all prespecified falsification results, including shuffled histories/times, insulin-free placebo periods and leave-one-hospital-out analyses.

Completion is a computed set of estimates and diagnostics, not confirmation of the hypothesis.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest evidence currently available supports only that the frozen eICU snapshot contains timestamped insulin-infusion rows, glucose and creatinine laboratory rows, structured intake labels, and hospital/stay identifiers. Raw documentation does not prove continuous administration, calories delivered, feeding cessation, clinically confirmed hypoglycemia, or adjudicated AKI. The parent proposal established a plausible safety signal but left a consequential ambiguity: a low glucose can only be detected when someone orders or records a glucose, and feed documentation can itself vary by unit and clinical state.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults in their first ICU stay with a recorded insulin infusion update and a recent valid glucose, renal worsening during the preceding 24 hours plus no qualifying documented nutrition delivery during the preceding 12 hours predicts a higher six-hour risk of a newly observed glucose <70 mg/dL at the same recorded insulin context; the joint contrast remains above the prespecified minimum important difference after adjustment for the probability that a glucose is measured and transports to held-out hospitals.

Primary outcome: the first valid glucose <70 mg/dL in (t,t+6]. Secondary outcomes are <54 mg/dL and recurrent low-glucose burden. A window with no glucose measurement is not a negative outcome. The primary estimand is an observation-adjusted detected-event risk contrast under an explicit missing-at-random-given-recorded-history assumption: inverse-probability weights are estimated for any valid glucose result in (t,t+6], using only information available by t. The unweighted detected-event estimand is reported alongside it. Neither estimand is the incidence of unobserved hypoglycemia.

This advances the seed and routine static associations by testing whether the clinically actionable renal/feed interaction survives a measured surveillance process, rather than confusing frequent testing with more disease. It can decide whether a chart-reviewed monitoring intervention is justified for study; it cannot prescribe insulin, feeds or a monitoring interval.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use eICU v2.0 snapshot `[source checksum]`, with all offsets in minutes from ICU admission. Include age >=18 (retain documented >89 as 90), valid identifiers and discharge fields, and the first ICU unit stay (smallest valid `unitvisitnumber`) per `uniquepid`. A secondary analysis includes later stays with patient-clustered uncertainty.

Decision times are t = 24, 30, ..., 66 hours after ICU admission. A period requires an insulin infusion update in [t-6,t) and at least one valid glucose on or before t. Follow-up ends at the earliest of t+6, ICU discharge or death. Death and alive discharge before the six-hour window are competing events, not hypoglycemia non-events; inadequate follow-up is censored. Exclude a care-plan EOL discussion/save at or before t in the primary analysis and repeat with an EOL indicator. Exclude periods within 12 hours of discharge in sensitivity analysis.

No predictor may use a value after t. Use `labresultoffset` as clinical event time, not `labresultrevisedoffset`; suppress exact duplicate/revision rows without making revisions new events. The primary glucose is the first valid result in the outcome window. Glucose observation is defined before examining its value as any valid glucose result in that window. Pre-index glucose history includes last value, minimum, mean, slope, count and time since last measurement, all ending at t.

## Analysis, baselines and alternative

Freeze codebooks and cohort construction before outcome modeling. The transparent baseline is pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific regression with hospital fixed effects or a documented random intercept, patient/stay-clustered uncertainty, and separate death/discharge heads. It includes insulin intensity/update density, renal change, documented-feed-loss and their prespecified interaction, prior glucose trajectory/count/gaps, diabetes/age/weight/sex/ethnicity, admission source/unit, early severity/support, vasopressor context, EOL and nutrition/glucose coverage. Estimate both unweighted and stabilized-IP-observation-weighted versions; truncate only at a prespecified percentile and report weight diagnostics. Standardize the joint-vs-neither six-hour risk contrast at the empirical covariate distribution.

The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over 12 one-hour bins before t, hidden size 32 or 64 chosen only in development. Inputs are the same sources: insulin rate/update count, glucose/creatinine/BUN/potassium and vital summaries, nutrition delivery/coverage, time since measurement, explicit missingness and static covariates. It has hypoglycemia, glucose-observation, death and alive-discharge heads. Compare full, physiology-only (without observation/nutrition documentation channels), and observation-only ablations. The scientific question is whether temporal ordering and observation-process information alter the renal/feed risk contrast and held-out calibration—not whether a black box wins AUROC.

Split entire hospitals approximately 60/20/20 into development/validation/held-out transport folds, with all stays for a `uniquepid` together. Fit preprocessing, ascertainment models and thresholds only in development; early-stop on validation Brier score and use one validation calibration step. If hospital counts or event counts cannot support stable folds, report descriptive coverage and return inconclusive.

Report standardized contrasts, risk ratios, interaction on logit and risk-difference scales, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC/AUPRC, sensitivity/PPV and alerts per 100 decision times. Use patient/stay-clustered bootstrap for fits and hospital-aware bootstrap for transport, recording resamples and small-hospital limitations.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required falsifications are: (1) permute insulin update times within stay while preserving rates/counts; (2) shuffle renal/nutrition histories across decision times within hospital; (3) test a pre-index placebo low-glucose window after excluding prior lows; (4) compare laboratory-only and bedside-only glucose and model glucose density/gaps; (5) repeat strict prior-delivery, complete-observable nutrition, direct-unit, no-carry-forward, discharge/EOL exclusions; (6) fit analogous renal×documentation exposure in insulin-free periods; (7) leave one hospital out and compare with/without observation channels; and (8) run an explicit future-value timestamp audit.

Support requires adequate label/unit coverage; positive, monotone renal/feed contrasts in the transparent model; a learned-model profile contrast consistent with it; a joint effect exceeding the prespecified additive contrast; 95% uncertainty excluding zero and preferably exceeding a 2-percentage-point six-hour risk-difference threshold; no single-hospital dominance; and persistence after ascertainment weighting with acceptable held-out calibration. This supports a transportable prognostic monitoring signal for chart-reviewed prospective evaluation.

Adverse evidence is a narrow null below the minimum important difference, reversal, loss after ascertainment weighting or strict labels, poor transport, or reproduction in placebo/insulin-free periods. That refutes the measurable marker in this snapshot or identifies a documentation artifact; it does not prove insulin or feed interruption harmless. Inconclusive evidence includes too few paired periods/events, unreliable labels/units, wide intervals spanning null and the minimum important difference, unstable weights, poor competing-event calibration, or unrepresentative hospital folds.

The data cannot establish actual administration, calories, true feed interruption, clinician intent, symptomatic/confirmed hypoglycemia, KDIGO AKI, or benefit/harm from an alert or dose change. Those claims require medication administration records, calories/feed-stop data, point-of-care confirmation, chart adjudication and a prospective or emulated-intervention study.

## Demonstration and seed dispositions

- Delphi/natural-history: the locally documented structured dated-sequence method is a credible adaptation for the temporal alternative, but this eICU study does not reproduce UK Biobank trajectories or demographics. Evidence used is limited to the locally read methods summary and accessible natural-history materials.
- Oncoformer/cancer: the main article and complete STAR Methods remain unavailable; only the supplement was inspected. Images are absent from eICU, so the multimodal claim is not pursued. A lab-only adaptation would not answer this observation-process question.
- ALADYNOULLI/Bayesian: the local article/supplement support latent longitudinal modeling as an adaptation, but verified genetic inputs are absent and no genetic claim is made. A mechanistic glucose-insulin model is deferred because administered dose, calories, rescue treatment and validated physiology are unavailable.
- Expert seed eicu-09 supplied the clinical premise; eicu-02 supplied the observation-process threat. The other eICU seeds remain repairable alternatives, not evidence or operational instructions.

## Compute and revisit rule

Discovery feasibility was a bounded CPU streaming audit, not the future solver. The planned solver envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, 8 GPUs and 28800 seconds as listed in `inputs.json`. The baseline is expected to fit CPU; the compact temporal alternative may use one allocated GPU (A100, `cuda:0`) or CPU, with actual runtime measured by the solver. No GPU is required by the scientific question. Revisit the deferred mechanistic model only if administration, nutrition/calorie, rescue-treatment and physiology dependencies become available; revisit the hypothesis if ascertainment-adjusted effects are unstable or label coverage is inadequate.
