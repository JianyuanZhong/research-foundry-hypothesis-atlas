# Episode 21 evolution: stable assay profile versus within-person change

Status: delivery-ready design only. No cohort count, fitted parameter, effect estimate, prevalence, or clinical conclusion is claimed.

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]` (Episode 20). This child targets one issue only: whether the result-content increment persists after separating a stable person-level assay/severity/recording profile from assay changes relative to that person's pre-index history. It preserves the parent's fixed O0-O4 endpoint hierarchy, capture-adequate `O_planproxy_cap` process control, observation-clock versus result-content ablation, capture-latency audit, time windows, temporal patient-held-out split, uncertainty family, and non-adjudication limits.

## Scientific opening, supported claim, and unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported by the inherited evidence is record-level and predictive: in the first dated exact-code TACE index population, assay-result content through day 42 may add held-out information for a later closed-coherent operational TACE record beyond a matched observation/documentation clock, while the process-control contrast tests whether the same information predicts a capture-adequate pre-opened-order/no-strict-coherence pattern. The inherited O0-O4 classes are records, not adjudicated TACE intent, completion, indication, response, progression, survival, benefit, or utility.

The unresolved claim is narrower:

> After the matched clock is held fixed, does content that changes within a patient relative to that patient's pre-index assay history add information for O4 beyond the patient's stable assay profile and stable recording pattern?

The leading explanation is a dynamic within-person signal: a pre-index-calibrated change in recorded assay content carries information about a later O4 record. The strongest rival is a stable-person explanation: patients with persistently severe disease, persistently abnormal assay profiles, or persistently dense documentation are more likely to have both the apparent trajectory and the later record; the apparent increment disappears once their pre-index profile is separated. A second, related rival is residual recording phenotype: the apparent “change” is an artifact of what is repeatedly measured or encoded. The exact comparison on which they differ is the held-out loss of a stable-profile model versus a matched stable-plus-within-person-change model, with identical clock inputs and endpoint labels.

This matters because it determines the next study. Persistence of a within-person increment would justify a blinded study of assay interpretation, procedure intent, and imaging response. A stable-only result would redirect the work toward baseline severity and recording-process measurement, not biological trajectory interpretation. Neither result identifies mechanism or causality.

[K1] supports ordered-record modeling but warns that learned patterns can reflect the data-generating process; this child adds a patient-history decomposition. [K2] motivates explicit observation/selection handling but does not make weighting causal; this child keeps R42/R28 as observation arms and uses a matched ablation. [K3] distinguishes recorded, biological, and decision time; this child makes the pre-index/day-42 boundary and post-procedure capture timing explicit. These are the three inspected works for this proposal; their frozen UTF-8 excerpts and receipts are attached separately.

## Hypothesis and estimands

Primary hypothesis:

> In the inherited HCC index population, the within-person result-change block `Delta42`, defined relative to pre-index assay references, improves untouched-test prediction of fixed O4 beyond the identical observation clock and stable pre-index person profile; this increment is retained in both a transparent elastic-net hazard and a matched two-stream GRU, is directionally stable under ordinary/R42/R28 arms, is not reproduced for `O_planproxy_cap`, and is not confined to one capture-latency band.

This is a descriptive/predictive hypothesis about recorded fields. “Stable profile” and “within-person change” are feature constructions, not validated disease states. No causal effect, biological mechanism, treatment response, clinical utility, or actionability is claimed.

Keep the inherited blocks exactly:

- `A0=M0+B0+P_obs+P_care`;
- `A_snap42=A0+S42`;
- `A_traj42=A0+T42`;
- `D_j=L_w(A_snap42,j)-L_w(A_traj42,j)`, with positive values favoring the inherited trajectory direction;
- `A_clock=A0+C42`, where C42 is the parent’s observation/documentation clock;
- `A_full=A_clock+R42`, where R42 is the parent’s result-content channel;
- `G_j=L_w(A_clock,j)-L_w(A_full,j)`, the parent’s matched result-content increment.

The new nested decomposition is fitted without changing those estimands:

- Pre-index history is exactly `Hpre=[t0-365 days,t0)`; the index observation window is exactly `H42=[t0,t0+42 days]` for R42 and `H28=[t0,t0+28 days]` for R28. No row after `t0+42` is a predictor.
- `P_stable` is a patient-level, assay-specific profile learned only from valid labs in Hpre: last available pre-index content, robust pre-index location where repeated numeric values exist, baseline qualitative mode/transition reference, and pre-index content-availability indicators. Numeric transforms are assay-specific; values are never pooled across assays because units/reference ranges are unavailable. The stable availability indicators are reported separately from content and are not called biology.
- For each H42 lab event with the same assay label/sample type, `Delta42` is a baseline-referenced change: development-fold assay-specific standardized current-minus-pre-index reference for numeric content, or a normalized qualitative transition from the pre-index reference to the current token. It includes an explicit baseline-unavailable mask; it never substitutes zero for an absent baseline and never pools unlike assays. A current result with no pre-index reference is retained only in the predeclared `new-content/baseline-unavailable` sensitivity channel, not interpreted as within-person change.
- `C42) retains event presence, assay label, sample type, parsed lab time, relative/inter-event time, opportunity, value-present/missingness, pre-landmark row counts, distinct source-family counts, encounter-day coverage, and timestamp/status completeness from encounters, examinations, orders, and medications. It contains no qualitative or quantitative result.
- Compare `A_clock`, `A_clock+P_stable`, `A_clock+P_stable+Delta42`, and the unchanged parent `A_full`. Define `G_stable=L_w(A_clock)-L_w(A_clock+P_stable)`, `G_within=L_w(A_clock+P_stable)-L_w(A_clock+P_stable+Delta42)`, and retain parent `G_j) for the full-content check. The primary new estimand is `G_within,O4`; `G_within,O_planproxy_cap` is a process-control estimand, not a clinical outcome.
- The baseline-supported availability of `Delta42`, missing-baseline fraction, and new-content fraction are frozen and reported. If baseline-supported events or paired test predictions are insufficient, the within-person claim is inconclusive rather than replaced by a favorable current-only analysis.

This decomposition asks whether dynamic content adds information after stable person-level profiles have been supplied. It does not claim that conditioning on `P_stable) removes all severity confounding.

- If O4 and the pre-opened-order rule overlap, retain O4 and set an overlap flag. O3-only, closure-unknown, and capture-unknown remain visible. Absence of a downstream row is never evidence of intent.

The parent’s capture-latency audit is retained exactly and is descriptive only: for each O4/O3 candidate report `u_min-tp), where `u_min) is the earliest qualifying post-procedure examination/order/medication time already allowed for O3, and `te-tp). Report unchanged O4 in `[0,24]) hours and `(24,72]) hours, plus missing/invalid closure and unknown-provenance reasons. These post-`tp) fields never enter predictors, never relabel an endpoint, and never alter censoring.

## Comparable baseline and learned alternative

Both methods use the same eligible patients, O0-O4 and `O_planproxy_cap) labels, risk grid, right-censoring masks, R42/R28 weights, latency audit, feature availability masks, split, and uncertainty family. Endpoint and post-`tp) capture fields never enter predictors.

**Transparent baseline.** Fit the inherited elastic-net pooled-logistic discrete-time hazard with intercept and risk-bin indicators. Fit `A_clock`, `A_clock+P_stable`, `A_clock+P_stable+Delta42`, and parent `A_full` for every fixed endpoint. Alpha=0.5; lambda, assay-specific parser, token dictionary, robust scaler, imputation, baseline reference rule, and feature-selection choices are fit only inside grouped development folds. The scientific decomposition is `G_stable), then `G_within), with `G_O4` primary and the parent `G` retained. Report ordinary and R42; repeat the predeclared R28 arm. The baseline is selected because each information block and its missingness can be audited directly.

**Matched two-stream GRU.** Use the parent compact architecture and the same seeds 17/29/41, one GRU layer, hidden size 32, dropout 0.10, 32/16 head, Adam 1e-3, weight decay 1e-4, maximum 50 epochs, and eight-epoch early stopping. The clock stream receives C42. A stable stream receives the patient-level P_stable vector. A within-person stream receives the ordered H42 Delta42 events and baseline-unavailable masks; streams are fused only at the hazard head. Fit clock-only, clock+stable, clock+stable+within, and parent full-content variants on identical folds, masks, and weights. The GRU can represent irregular timing and nonlinear assay combinations that the pooled baseline loses; the matched ablation shows whether that flexibility is specifically in stable profile or within-person change.

A mechanistic response model, transformer, narrative NLP model, imaging model, or causal treatment model is deferred because this snapshot lacks validated assay units/reference ranges, images, intent/response labels, reliable narrative time, outside-care evidence, and patient-important outcomes. This is a dependency-based deferral, not a ban on neural or structured-data methods. Cross-fitted residualization is also deferred as primary analysis because no validated observation model exists; it could make extrapolation look like residual biology. Revisit only after adjudicated outcomes or a validated capture model are available.

Approximate future solver workload: deterministic source/materialization and endpoint/profile audits, 4 CPUs/16 GiB for 1-3 hours; elastic-net, paired bootstrap and within-patient permutations, 4 CPUs/16 GiB for 1-3 hours; three-seed GRU, 4 CPUs/16 GiB for 2-5 hours. These are planning estimates, not measured results. CPU is adequate for the baseline. A GRU may use one allocated A100 only through a managed job with `gpus=1), explicit `cuda:0`, and recorded allocation; ordinary-shell CUDA visibility is not a hardware test. ## Split, uncertainty, falsification, and decision gates

Sort index patients by `t0): earliest 80% development and latest 20% untouched test, with no patient in both. Use five grouped development folds for all parsing, baseline-reference construction, scaling, imputation, hyperparameters, early stopping, and seeds. The untouched test set is not used to alter time windows, endpoint rules, baseline availability thresholds, or gates.

For every method, block, endpoint, weight arm, and supported latency stratum report patient-level paired log loss, Brier score, integrated Brier score, calibration, risk-bin counts, paired availability, and discrimination secondarily. Use 1,000 patient-level paired bootstrap resamples of the untouched test set with one max-|t| simultaneous 95% family covering O0-O4, `O_planproxy_cap`, `D), `G), `G_stable`, `G_within`, ordinary/R42/R28, baseline-supported availability, and fixed falsifiers. Report event/risk-set counts, O3/O4 overlap, closure/capture unknowns, latency support, R42/R28 ESS, weight tails, and distribution of stable-profile and Delta availability.

Frozen falsifiers:

1. Remove all result content while retaining the identical C42 observation clock, as in the parent.
2. Retain P_stable but permute Delta42 within patient among eligible H42 lab events with the same assay label/sample type, preserving event times, missingness, and availability. A persistent within-person increment falsifies dynamic content specificity.
3. Permute the pre-index stable profile across patients within development-only baseline strata while retaining C42 and Delta42; this is a diagnostic of stable-profile dependence, not a claim about exchangeability.
4. Remove late assay rows in `(t0+35,t0+42]) while retaining the earlier clock and content.
5. Compare R42 with R28 and ordinary weighting.
6. Compare unchanged O4 with O0/O1/O2/O3 and `O_planproxy_cap`; do not create latency-specific endpoints.
7. Report `G_within,O4` in `[0,24]) and `(24,72]) capture-latency bands. If a band lacks events or paired availability, latency separation is unresolved.
8. Fit timing/source-only models without assay result values. If they match the full model, the assay-content claim fails even if the inherited snapshot-versus-trajectory contrast is positive.

**Supportive for a within-person record-level increment:** O4 `G_within` has a positive simultaneous lower 95% bound in both elastic-net and GRU; direction is retained under ordinary and R42 and in the R28/late-window controls; P_stable-only does not account for the full increment; `G_within,O_planproxy_cap` has an upper simultaneous bound at or below zero; paired Delta availability and O4 support pass frozen gates; the within-patient Delta permutation and timing-only models do not reproduce it; and O4 is not confined to one supported latency band. A concordant parent `G_O4` is required as a consistency check, but it cannot substitute for `G_within,O4`.

This supportive result establishes only that baseline-referenced recorded result changes add held-out information for a narrower operational record beyond stable recorded profile and measured clock. It does not establish biological change, clinician interpretation, treatment intent/completion, response, causality, benefit, or utility.

**Adverse:** `G_stable` accounts for the parent increment and `G_within` is near zero or adverse; the Delta permutation survives; the timing-only model matches; `G_within,O_planproxy_cap` is positive; the increment disappears with closure or post-source timing audit; it is present in only one method/seed/weight arm; or it is confined to one latency band. These results favor stable severity, recording phenotype, workflow, capture timing, or representation sensitivity. They do not identify which unmeasured mechanism is responsible.

**Inconclusive:** O4 or baseline-supported Delta events/ESS are inadequate; closure/capture unknowns dominate; test availability is unstable; simultaneous intervals are wide; R42/R28 disagree; weight tails are extreme; or methods disagree. Do not rescue the claim by changing baseline windows, endpoint classes, availability gates, subgroups, or seeds. Continue only with better capture, assay calibration, or blinded clinical adjudication.

## Alternatives not chosen and revisit rule

The stable-profile/delta decomposition is the selected alternative because it directly tests the one remaining scientific contrast while preserving the parent’s clock/content comparison. The elastic-net baseline is retained for transparent block accounting; the two-stream GRU is retained for irregular nonlinear interactions and is judged by the same held-out estimand, not by complexity or small predictive gain.

Not chosen: a transformer or latent biological model, because it would add representational capacity without the missing units, response labels, or adjudicated outcomes; narrative NLP and imaging, because the required validated text temporality/images are unavailable; mechanistic TACE-response simulation, because no treatment intent, technical dose, response, or outcome target exists; cross-fitted observation residualization, because no validated capture model settles what should be removed. Revisit these only if a validated capture model, assay calibration, adjudicated intent/response, imaging, or patient-important outcome becomes available. If the within-person result is adverse or inconclusive, obtain that evidence before adding model complexity.
