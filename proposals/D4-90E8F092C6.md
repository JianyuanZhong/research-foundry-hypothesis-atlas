# Seed 03 child: prospective muscle-quality increment beyond thigh quantity

Status: substantive evolution of `[prior hypothesis]`; planned Harbor experiment, not a fitted result. The discovery episode performed literature inspection and a schema/mount diagnostic only. No participant count, overlap count, event count, model output or conclusion is claimed.

## Scientific opening and deliverable

The strongest inspected evidence supports a bounded descriptive claim: muscle quality/composition and strength can diverge from muscle mass and relate to mobility, and a UKB MRI study reports cross-sectional associations of thigh distribution with grip, falls and mortality [K1, K2]. The prospective evidence for a composite sarcopenia/falls interaction is small and inconclusive [K3]. The closest UKB MRI work does not resolve whether a baseline quality/function measure improves prediction of a later mobility change or later reported fall after quantity is held constant.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among UKB adults with an MRI-anchored baseline assessment, baseline grip strength and posterior-thigh fat infiltration add clinically meaningful, reproducible information beyond bilateral thigh fat-free muscle volume for a later worsening in usual walking pace and/or a later reported fall; the increment is not accounted for by pre-existing pain/arthritis, neurologic disease, or the selection of people who return for a later assessment.

This is a prospective association/prediction claim, not a causal claim that myosteatosis causes falls, a diagnosis of sarcopenia, or evidence that an intervention will prevent falls. A supportive result would justify an indication-rich external validation and decision-impact study; it would not justify clinical deployment. An adverse result would narrow the value of MRI quality/function to concurrent description or quantity-adjusted association. An inconclusive result would identify inadequate repeat-outcome support or selection/measurement uncertainty, not refute the biology.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted evidence, not readiness: (1) a locked quantity-only baseline and quantity-plus-quality/function model; (2) an independently evaluated nonlinear alternative using exactly the same participants, variables, timing and outcomes; (3) standardized quantity-matched risk contrasts, calibration/Brier outputs and uncertainty; (4) temporal-ordering, pain/arthritis/neurologic, early-event and repeat-attendance falsifications; and (5) a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive and linked to those computed files.

## Three inspected works and the substantive advance

[K1] McGregor et al. review evidence that composition, fat infiltration and neural/functional factors may matter beyond muscle mass, and that strength can decline faster than mass. It does not test a prospective UKB MRI-to-outcome estimand. This child supplies that test and separates quantity-only from quantity-plus-quality/function information.

[K2] Kim et al. report UKB thigh MRI distribution associations with grip, sarcopenia, mortality and falls, but the inspected Results section describes the 37,004-person functional analysis as cross-sectional. This child adds a date-ordered follow-up outcome, a repeat-assessment selection analysis, and clinical rival falsifications.

[K3] Glavas et al. synthesize prospective osteosarcopenia evidence and find no reliable falls interaction, with wide/inconclusive estimates. This child does not assume that a composite label is useful; it tests a more direct, continuous quantity-versus-quality/function increment and accepts a null or inconclusive result.

These works motivate the opening and bound it; none establishes the UKB hypothesis or its clinical usefulness.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The primary population is adults with a unique `eid`, parseable `53-2.0`, complete Q, M and F at the MRI anchor, and a later assessment date `T1 > T0` with observed `924-T1` and/or `2296-T1`. Define `T0=53-2.0`; do not use a date from a different namespace or infer the MRI date from file order. Require age at T0 >=50 for the primary analysis and report the full eligible age range as a descriptive audit.

For walking pace, require observed `924-2.0` and `924-T1`; define decline as a prespecified worsening in the ordered category (slow < average < brisk) and separately report unchanged and improved transitions. The primary mobility estimand is the standardized probability difference for decline over the observed T0-to-T1 interval. This is not a measured gait-speed change: `924` is a self-reported usual-pace category.

For falls, require `T1-T0 >=365` days so the T1 “last year” recall window is not entirely contemporaneous with the MRI assessment. Define the primary outcome as `2296-T1 >0`, and report the count category separately. A sensitivity requires >=730 days. A secondary hard-outcome sensitivity uses post-T0 paired inpatient codes/dates for W00-W19 only if the audit confirms the arrays and sufficient support; it is not a replacement for the self-reported primary.

- Model Q+F: Q_total plus grip;
- Model Q+MF+F: Q_total plus MFI and grip, with prespecified MFI-by-grip interaction.

## Baseline versus substantive learned alternative

Baseline, selected for interpretability: separate ordinal-logistic/change models for pace decline and logistic/Poisson models for any reported fall, with Q-only and nested Q+MF/Q+F/Q+MF+F specifications. Fit complete-case models after the cohort screen, define all standardization and missingness decisions in development, use robust intervals and report calibration. The baseline exposes the quantity-adjusted coefficient and fixed-profile absolute risk, but it loses nonlinear MFI thresholds, grip-by-quantity interactions and joint risk regions.

Learned alternative, retained because it tests that substantive uncertainty: a CPU gradient-boosted multi-task model with one head for walking-pace decline and one for any fall, using exactly the same baseline inputs, T0/T1 eligibility, outcomes, covariates and missingness policy as the baseline. It can reveal nonlinear quality/function interactions and threshold-like regions that additive models can miss. It cannot reveal pain severity, mechanism, clinical indication or causal effects. Use development-only tuning and a locked temporal holdout split by T0 date (earliest 70% development, latest 30% holdout; one `eid` in one split). No T1 attendance indicator or future variable is a predictor.

On the holdout report calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, Brier and log loss, standardized profile risks, Q-versus-Q+MF+F absolute increments and 500-participant bootstrap intervals or a clearly documented cluster/bootstrap equivalent. If fewer than 100 holdout fall events or 100 pace declines exist, label that endpoint learned comparison inconclusive rather than changing the split or outcome. Compare the learned model scientifically on nonlinear interaction shape and calibrated absolute risk, not on a small AUC gain.

## Interpretation and falsification criteria

Supportive evidence requires: exact same-namespace binding and valid date ordering; sufficient holdout outcomes; a positive, calibrated Q+MF+F increment beyond Q-only on the locked holdout; reproducible direction across walking pace and/or falls sensitivity; lower uncertainty above the descriptive 1-point boundary where the endpoint has adequate support; no diagnosis-clean collapse, recall-lag collapse, or large selection-weight shift; and no learned-only gain without calibrated absolute-risk improvement. This supports external validation, not clinical deployment or mechanism.

Adverse evidence is a precise null or reversal for the incremental contrast, a signal confined to baseline cross-sectional association, collapse after pain/arthritis/neurologic restriction, restriction to the short recall window, strong repeat-selection shift, or comparable performance from Q-only. This weakens the claim that available quality/function fields add clinically meaningful future information but does not prove muscle quality is irrelevant in other populations or with better outcomes.

Inconclusive evidence includes absent exact source/hash, missing baseline fields, no valid ordered repeat outcome, poor positivity, severe missingness, fewer than prespecified holdout event counts, broad intervals spanning both zero and the 1-point boundary, invalid diagnosis pairing, or uncertain outcome timing. An imprecise null is not refutation.

Computationally checkable claims are schema/path/hash compliance, participant and date audits, exposure construction, cohort counts, transitions, model estimates, calibration/Brier/log metrics, bootstrap intervals, interaction shapes and adherence of the interpretation label to the prespecified rules. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for actual fall timing, fall severity, pain and neurologic examination, outpatient disease, gait measurement, MRI segmentation validity, treatment decisions, causal mechanism, transportability and clinical utility. A prospective cohort with adjudicated falls, objective gait testing, indication-rich clinical data and external validation would be needed before a decision rule.

## Alternatives considered and deferral record

The transparent regression/change analysis is retained because it directly estimates the quantity-adjusted risk increment and is auditable under sparse outcomes. The CPU boosted multi-task alternative is retained because nonlinear quantity-quality-function interactions are a substantive scientific uncertainty, not a request for a small predictive gain. A deep image model is deferred: the configured table supplies derived MRI fields, not the image payload, so it cannot test image-level quality without unavailable inputs. A recurrent trajectory model is deferred unless the audit confirms at least one genuinely post-MRI outcome assessment with adequate overlap; two irregular assessments would not identify a muscle trajectory. A causal mediation model is rejected for this question because falls, pain severity and temporal clinical intermediates are not adjudicated. Revisit these branches only if the source audit or a new external dataset supplies the missing evidence.
