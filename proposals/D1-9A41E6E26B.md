# Provisional eICU hypothesis: hypotension duration versus nadir and subsequent AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU admission with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone. This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Why it matters and unresolved question
A single nadir can be transient or artifact-prone, while sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved question is whether duration and trajectory add clinically meaningful information about subsequent AKI beyond the nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, measurement frequency, or treatment response. Resolving this could determine whether early AKI risk assessment should use a burden measure or a single minimum pressure.

## Falsifiable test
Compare a pre-specified baseline model using admission nadir with a model adding cumulative hypotension duration (and, if justified, trajectory) for the 24–72-hour creatinine endpoint. Use hospital-held-out evaluation, uncertainty intervals, and sensitivity analyses for measurement frequency, baseline creatinine availability, and vasopressor timing. Support requires a reproducible incremental association beyond nadir; absence of increment falsifies the added-information claim; wide intervals or inadequate endpoint ascertainment are inconclusive. This provisional draft makes no claim about a computed effect.
