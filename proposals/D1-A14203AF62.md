# Substantive child: observation-opportunity and ascertainment-robust hypotension burden

## Scientific question and advance

The unresolved clinical question is whether time-weighted MAP<65 burden contains reproducible prognostic information beyond MAP nadir, baseline kidney function, and the opportunity to observe MAP. The parent’s strongest supported claim is only that hypotension duration/severity and dynamic MAP features are associated with kidney outcomes in prior cohorts [K1, K2], while an ICU prevention bundle was associated with some kidney outcomes without isolating duration or establishing causality [K3]. The untested claim is narrower: after fixing MAP observation opportunity and separating post-index treatment response and creatinine ascertainment, does burden add a stable, transportable prognostic signal?

This is a substantive advance over the parent because it makes the two most consequential rival explanations directly falsifiable. Observation intensity can create apparent burden through missingness and source mix; vasopressor treatment after the index can be a response to the same shock that generated burden; and creatinine-based AKI can be detected more often when clinicians test more. The design therefore tests a prognostic contrast, not a causal effect of hypotension, treatment, or monitoring.

## Population, index, and temporal boundaries

Include adults with parsed `age` 18–89, the first ICU stay per `patienthealthsystemstayid`, at least one valid MAP in `vitalPeriodic.systemicmean` or `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` during the first 24 ICU hours, at least one MAP<65, and at least six valid MAP observations. Exclude unavailable baseline creatinine, documented pre-existing dialysis/renal replacement therapy, and fewer than 24 hours of follow-up. Define the index as the first contiguous MAP<65 interval after the first qualifying MAP, with a 30-minute grace period for missing observations. Repeat prespecified sensitivity analyses with no grace, 60-minute grace, and thresholds 60 and 70.

Construct a five-minute grid from the first qualifying MAP through 24 hours. Use the last observed MAP without extrapolation beyond 30 minutes. The primary exposure is the fraction of covered time below 65; secondary exposure is area below 65. Retain nadir, median, MAP observation count, covered fraction, median gap, and periodic/aperiodic source mix. The baseline covariate block is fixed before the index: age, sex, ethnicity, hospital, unit type, admission source, baseline creatinine, pre-index MAP and heart-rate summaries, and pre-index observation opportunity. Do not use post-index treatment in the primary estimand.

## Primary estimand and observation-opportunity test

The primary estimand is the conditional prognostic contrast for incident creatinine-based AKI through 72 hours after index in this selected cohort, comparing burden+nadir with nadir-only after the prespecified opportunity block. It is not a causal effect of MAP or treatment.

Fit nested models in the same cohort and same outcome view:

1. Opportunity-only: baseline severity and measurement opportunity, including MAP observation count, coverage, median gap, source mix, and pre-index clinical variables.
2. Opportunity+nadir: add MAP nadir.
3. Opportunity+nadir+burden: add time-weighted burden.

The primary comparison is model 3 versus model 2. Report adjusted OR, likelihood-ratio contrast, calibration, and hospital-clustered bootstrap uncertainty. Also report the opportunity-only versus burden+nadir comparison to show whether burden adds information after the observation process is represented. A prespecified negative-control comparison is an observation-density-only model versus a model with a deliberately nonclinical observation feature (for example, source mix or gap pattern) without burden; this checks whether the apparent burden contrast is merely a proxy for documentation intensity. It is not a substitute for causal identification.

## Post-index treatment decomposition

Treatment is secondary and descriptive. From `infusionDrug`, extract prespecified vasopressor/inotrope rows by normalized `drugname` (norepinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine, epinephrine), using `infusionoffset`, `drugrate`, `infusionrate`, `drugamount`, `volumeoffluid`, and `patientweight`. From `medication`, retain order/start/stop fields and `drugordercancelled`; do not treat an order as administration. From `treatment`, retain `treatmentoffset` and `treatmentstring` as a separately labeled treatment-charting view. Summarize time to first record, class, duration, rate/dose where a numeric field is present, and timing relative to index. Do not combine incompatible namespaces or infer a dose from a missing field.

Repeat the burden contrast in treated versus untreated groups and add treatment descriptors only in a secondary model. Because treatment is post-index and may be a collider, this decomposition can show whether the association is concentrated in treated patients or changes after descriptive accounting; it cannot estimate the effect of withholding treatment, mediation, or a mechanism. If treatment records are sparse or unit-inconsistent, report that limitation rather than imputing exposure.

## Creatinine ascertainment design

The primary outcome is incident creatinine-based AKI through 72 hours after index: an increase of at least 0.3 mg/dL from the minimum creatinine in the prior 24 hours or at least 1.5 times that baseline. Use numeric `labresult` for exact `labname=creatinine`, `labmeasurenamesystem=mg/dL`, and `labresultoffset`; retain `labresultrevisedoffset` for revision auditing. The audit found essentially all creatinine rows in mg/dL, with only two blank interface labels, so no unit conversion is planned. This is not full KDIGO AKI because reliable urine-output adjudication is unavailable.

Define three prespecified views:

- Strict ascertainment view: require at least two post-index creatinine measurements, at least one in 0–24 hours and one in 24–72 hours, and no missingness gap greater than 12 hours within those intervals. This is a sensitivity outcome, not a replacement for the primary endpoint.
- Observed-trajectory view: retain the parent outcome but require the minimum baseline and qualifying post-index measurement to be observed; report the number and timing of creatinine measurements.
- Measurement-intensity strata: stratify by creatinine observation count and median inter-measurement gap, fitting the same burden models within strata.

Missing creatinine is never coded as no AKI. A burden signal that appears only in the observed-trajectory view and disappears in the strict view is evidence of ascertainment dependence, not a biological duration effect. If strict-view events are too sparse, the result is inconclusive; do not relax the criterion after seeing results.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative

The simple baseline is multivariable logistic regression with the fixed opportunity block, nadir, and burden. It provides an interpretable adjusted OR, likelihood-ratio contrast, calibration, and uncertainty. It is the primary scientific test because the question is whether burden adds information, not whether a complex model can improve prediction.

The substantive alternative is a regularized gradient-boosted tree model using the same five-minute summaries and the same feature blocks, with nested hospital-held-out evaluation and time-ordered splits within training hospitals. Compare opportunity-only, opportunity+nadir, and opportunity+nadir+burden. The learned model can reveal nonlinear burden–nadir interactions and heterogeneity by baseline kidney function or treatment timing that the linear baseline loses. It cannot identify a mechanism; a small AUROC gain alone is not scientific success.

A mechanistic discrete-time survival model with cumulative burden and time-varying treatment is deferred because creatinine ascertainment and treatment timing do not support a defensible causal or mechanistic time-to-event estimand. A neural sequence model is not selected because the central uncertainty is decomposition of observation, treatment, and outcome ascertainment; the tabular learned alternative is adequate. Revisit either only if the five-minute representation is shown to lose information needed to distinguish the rivals.

## Split, uncertainty, and solver deliverable

Use hospital-held-out evaluation as the primary transportability test: all stays from a hospital are held out together. Within each training hospital, use a time-ordered split by ICU admission offset or earliest qualifying MAP offset, with no future rows in training. Fit preprocessing, regularization, and model selection inside training folds only. Use hospital-clustered bootstrap intervals for the primary adjusted OR and likelihood-ratio contrast. Report calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, AUROC/AUPRC as secondary summaries, and decision-curve net benefit at prespecified risk thresholds. Do not select a model or threshold on held-out data.

The future solver must newly construct the cohort and five-minute exposure grid; estimate the primary adjusted burden contrast; produce strict and observed creatinine views; produce the treatment-response decomposition; and evaluate the learned alternative with hospital-held-out and time-ordered splits. It must report uncertainty, calibration, decision value, sensitivity analyses, and a conclusion tied to computed outputs. It must not report a causal treatment effect or full KDIGO AKI claim.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results require burden to add prespecified incremental association beyond nadir and observation opportunity, with a 95% CI for the adjusted OR excluding 1 and a likelihood-ratio test favoring burden+nadir; direction should be stable across hospitals, thresholds, grace periods, and observation-density strata; and the signal should persist in the strict creatinine view or be shown not to depend on creatinine measurement intensity. The learned model may improve calibration or decision value, but this is secondary. This would support a reproducible prognostic duration signal, not a treatment target.

Adverse results include no incremental burden information beyond nadir/density, reversal after density adjustment, confinement to one hospital or source, or disappearance in the strict creatinine view. These favor nadir, severity, observation practice, or ascertainment explanations and stop the duration claim.

Inconclusive results include wide intervals, sparse strict-view events, or materially different results under creatinine ascertainment and grace/threshold choices. An imprecise null is not decisive refutation.

Unavailable evidence includes validated full KDIGO urine-output adjudication, reliable pre-ICU baseline creatinine for every stay, clinician-confirmed AKI, complete nephrotoxin exposure, and the causal effect of raising MAP. Clinical adjudication or a prospective/controlled study is required for stronger claims.
