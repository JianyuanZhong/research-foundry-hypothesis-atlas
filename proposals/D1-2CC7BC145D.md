# Transfer-ascertained early failure after first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly fit an auditable, leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study that preserves the parent’s recorded-support/observation-process estimand but repairs the clinical ambiguity of an aggregate “later ICU stay.” It must produce:

1. `cohort_audit.parquet`, one row per eligible adult first ICU stay, with the exact first-stay rule, inclusion/exclusion flags, complete-window and support-coverage flags, temporal-integrity checks, and broad versus primary analysis populations;
2. `hourly_features.parquet`, twelve one-hour rows in `[outtime-12h,outtime)`, containing chart-time physiology, positive support evidence, observed non-positive values, uninformative absence, contradictory/invalid flags, and observation/process covariates with no post-`outtime` predictors;
3. `support_observation_audit.parquet`, with respiratory and vasoactive domain/half-window ledgers, coverage, last recorded-positive times, durable/recent/active/discordant classifications, and every locked `q`-recoding;
4. `transfer_outcome_audit.parquet`, one row per eligible stay, listing the earliest later ICU stay, all candidate transfer rows used to bridge `outtime` to that ICU return, event times, tolerance variants, bridge category, and whether the result is aggregate-return, transition-confirmed return, or immediate/unconfirmed return;
5. locked patient-level test predictions from a transparent cause-specific discrete-time baseline and a same-input two-domain observation-aware hidden semi-Markov alternative for transition-confirmed return, immediate/unconfirmed return, death, and alive discharge;
6. standardized 6-, 24-, and 48-hour competing-event CIFs and contrasts for the parent’s aggregate-return/death/discharge estimand and the repaired topology-refined four-event estimand;
7. bootstrap uncertainty, calibration, IPCW Brier scores, overlap/effective-sample-size diagnostics, the missing-support `q`-sensitivity frontier, topology-rule sensitivity, and a prespecified coverage-transport diagnostic;
8. all falsification outputs and `interpretation_map.parquet`, mapping every reported conclusion to an output file/row, estimate, interval, event count, sensitivity result, and evidence limitation.

Completion means these data products, held-out estimates, uncertainty intervals, endpoint audits, and interpretation mappings have actually been generated by the solver. This proposal asserts no clinical association or model performance.

## Unresolved question, evidence-supported claim, and hypothesis

The parent validly repaired the observation-identifiability problem. It distinguishes positive support evidence, an observed non-positive value, and no qualifying row/interval; it does not equate missing documentation with support cessation; and it requires a locked `q`-grid for unrecorded support. That contribution is preserved exactly.

A separate unresolved problem remains in the outcome. “A later ICU stay in the same admission within 48 hours” is reproducible from `icu/icustays`, but it can combine a clinically concerning ward-to-ICU escalation with a planned/procedural return, an immediate transfer artifact, or a return whose intermediate ward documentation is missing. MIMIC’s structured transfer topology can refine ascertainment, but it does not contain a validated reason-for-return or clinician adjudication.

The strongest evidence currently supports only data feasibility: the configured MIMIC snapshot contains linked ICU stays, admissions, transfer rows, dated support-related ICU observations, and hospital death/discharge times from which both the parent aggregate competing-event estimand and a prespecified record-based topology refinement can be computed. Schema/archive inspection establishes availability, not a support-to-outcome association, clinical deterioration, or a causal effect.

The unresolved claim is:

> At the first ICU outtime in an adult hospitalization, among stays with adequate prespecified support-observation coverage, is recent recorded respiratory and/or vasoactive withdrawal evidence associated with a higher cumulative incidence of a subsequently documented non-ICU-to-ICU transition within 48 hours than durable recorded-off evidence, after observation/process adjustment and across prespecified unrecorded-support assumptions?

The primary endpoint is deliberately “transfer-confirmed return,” defined below as a documented non-ICU interval between the index ICU outtime and the later ICU intime. The exact parent estimand—any later ICU return versus death versus alive discharge—is retained as a secondary comparison. The hypothesis is prognostic and record-based, not causal: the study does not test whether stopping support causes return or whether a clinician should delay discharge.

The primary directional hypothesis is that the standardized 48-hour CIF for transfer-confirmed return is higher for recent recorded withdrawal than for durable recorded-off evidence, with corresponding domain-specific and joint contrasts. The hypothesis is falsified if the prespecified contrast is non-positive or if it disappears under the locked observation, `q`, endpoint-rule, and timing falsifications. A clinically meaningful decision signal is considered plausible only if its absolute-risk contrast remains directionally stable with uncertainty narrow enough to distinguish a prespecified 2-percentage-point absolute difference; this is an analysis gate, not a validated bedside threshold or policy recommendation.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

ICU discharge decisions trade bed capacity against the risk of moving a patient who may require rapid escalation. Aggregate ICU return and death are important but can be difficult to interpret when the return endpoint mixes ward-to-ICU escalation with planned or poorly documented transitions. A topology-refined endpoint can answer a narrower, more decision-relevant question: whether the support pattern identifies patients who subsequently have a documented non-ICU interval followed by ICU re-entry, while showing how much of the apparent association is confined to immediate/unconfirmed returns.

The substantive advance over the parent is endpoint ascertainment, not a cosmetic model change:

- the primary estimand is now a clinically interpretable, explicitly record-based transition-confirmed return;
- immediate/unconfirmed returns are retained as a separate competing event rather than silently combined or discarded;
- the parent aggregate-return CIF remains available for direct comparison, so the child can show whether endpoint refinement changes the scientific conclusion;
- transfer topology, transfer-time tolerance, and care-unit ambiguity are audited and falsified rather than treated as ground truth.

A supportive result would strengthen a retrospective prognostic interpretation for a documented post-transition ICU escalation. It would not establish that the return was unplanned, preventable, caused by support withdrawal, or transportable to another hospital. A null or adverse result is also clinically useful if it shows that an aggregate-return signal was driven by unconfirmed/planned-like topology or by missing-support assumptions.

### Transfer-confirmation rule

The primary rule is frozen before fitting and uses only `hosp/transfers` plus linked ICU-stay fields:

- identify the earliest later ICU stay in the same `subject_id,hadm_id` with `later.intime > index.outtime` and `later.intime < min(index.outtime+48h, dischtime)`;
- inspect all valid `hosp/transfers` rows for the same subject/admission with non-null `careunit`, valid `intime < outtime`, and an interval beginning no earlier than `index.outtime-5 minutes` and ending no later than `later.intime+5 minutes`;
- require at least 30 minutes of recorded interval between clipped transfer `intime` and `outtime`, and require its `careunit` not to equal the later ICU’s `first_careunit` or `last_careunit`. The primary bridge is the candidate with greatest clipped duration; ties use earliest `outtime`, then smallest `transfer_id`;
- the bridge is a “documented non-ICU” record-topology label only. Invalid times, null careunit, or no qualifying bridge do not prove that no ward interval occurred;
- record `eventtype`, careunit, bridge duration, row count, and ambiguity flags. A confirmed return still has unknown clinical reason.

Sensitivities use 0 and 5 minutes for endpoint joins, 0/15/30/60 minutes for minimum bridge duration, careunit-only versus interval-only bridge logic, exclusion of rows with `eventtype='discharge'`, and ICU-unit matching derived from linked ICU intervals. The primary rule is not changed based on results. Aggregate return remains the ascertainment-complete but clinically less specific comparator.

## Recorded-support exposure and observation process

Use the parent’s ledger unchanged. For every domain and half-window classify each hour as positive recorded support evidence, observed non-positive value, no qualifying row/interval (uninformative), contradictory source evidence, or invalid/out-of-window timing.

A positive respiratory flag requires a valid charted value with O2 flow >2 L/min, FiO2 >0.21, PEEP >0, or valid intubation/NIV interval corroboration. A positive vasoactive flag requires valid overlap for a listed inputevent. Procedures corroborate transitions but do not establish continuous support. Keep arterial and NIBP MAP separate and convert Fahrenheit only by an auditable rule.

Primary exposure eligibility requires at least two respiratory observation bins per half and at least two MAP observation bins per half. Define durable recorded-off evidence as no positive respiratory or vasoactive evidence in either half; recent respiratory withdrawal as respiratory-positive in the preceding half and none in the final half; recent vasoactive cessation as vasoactive-positive in the preceding half and none in the final half; and both recent withdrawal as both domain definitions. “No positive evidence” never means continuous absence. Retain active/residual, uninformative, discordant, and inadequate-coverage strata in counts; do not force them into durable recovery.

Use observation fractions, maximum gaps, row counts, source channels, support interval counts, contradictory indicators, pre-index transfer count/last careunit, admission context, first careunit, ICU LOS, age, gender, and anchor-year group. For each domain/half use locked `q_dh in {0,0.10,0.25,0.50,0.75,1.00}` scenarios, with shared-q, domain/half-specific, and higher-vasoactive-q analyses. Only uninformative no-row hours are recoded; observed non-positive respiratory values are not randomized. Report changed labels, effective sample size, overlap, and every CIF contrast. This is an assumption-based sensitivity analysis, not an identified correction.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative

Both methods use the same cohort, ledger, twelve-hour matrix, masks, context, and patient split. Thresholds, imputation, recoding, bridge rules, and tuning are training/validation-only.

**Transparent baseline.** Fit pooled one-hour cause-specific hazards through 48 hours for four refined events (confirmed return, unconfirmed return, death, alive discharge), plus the parent aggregate-return/death/discharge fit. Predictors are exposure states, preceding/final physiology summaries, source-specific masks, observation/process fields, and admission/context covariates. Fit nested recorded-physiology/support, masks-only, process/context-only, and full models. Standardize predictions to the common eligible population for absolute CIFs and contrasts. As a secondary selection diagnostic, fit a pre-landmark model of coverage eligibility and use stabilized inverse-odds weights to standardize eligible-group conditional predictions toward broad eligible first-stay profiles, with locked truncation and positivity reporting; this is not causal.

**Substantive alternative.** Fit a two-domain factorized hidden semi-Markov model over twelve hourly bins. Respiratory and hemodynamic chains have duration distributions and constrained persistence/withdrawal transitions; emissions use the same physiology/support evidence; an explicit observation model uses masks, row counts, source channels, and history; the event head predicts the four refined hazards and the parent aggregate event. Fit on training, select state number/duration/regularization/q handling on validation, and lock test posteriors/CIFs. Refit or marginalize under every fixed q scenario.

The alternative can reveal whether ordered duration and discordant recovery contain information lost by fixed summaries, and whether endpoint-specific risk separates confirmed from unconfirmed returns. It is reportable only if states have reproducible domain/order meaning, split stability, acceptable observation diagnostics, and no high-entropy artifact. A flexible GRU/TCN is deferred because it adds sequence capacity without directly resolving endpoint ascertainment or missing support. Causal policy, note-language, renal, waveform, imaging, and external-validation branches are deferred because intent/readiness labels, validated text, adjudicated endpoints, raw waveforms, images, and another cohort are unavailable.

## Evaluation, uncertainty, and falsification

Primary estimates are standardized 6-, 24-, and 48-hour CIFs and recent-minus-durable contrasts for every refined event and aggregate return. Use patient-bootstrap 95% intervals, resampling subjects rather than rows; report event counts, positivity, effective sample size, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier, and calibration plots. AUROC is secondary.

Predeclare: shuffled hourly time labels; mask-only, support-only, physiology-only, and full ablations; `charttime` replaced by `storetime` as a negative timing control; last-value interval coding; removal of procedure corroboration; separate arterial/NIBP and FiO2/O2-flow/PEEP channels; support/window/coverage/tie/bridge-duration/bridge-careunit/eventtype variants; permutation of transfer careunit labels within admission while preserving ICU times/durations; +6-hour transfer-interval shift; aggregate versus topology-refined outcomes; discharge-as-censoring sensitivity; complete-case/coverage-restricted/coverage-transport-standardized analyses; strata by first careunit, admission type, anchor-year, and transfer density; and a complete audit proving no post-outtime predictor leakage.

Supportive evidence requires a positive confirmed-return contrast with interval excluding zero, adequate overlap/events, stability across observation adjustment and a declared q range, agreement of baseline and HSMM, loss of topology-specific signal after careunit permutation, and no dependence on storetime, one channel, one bridge rule, or one tie tolerance. It supports only retrospective association with documented post-non-ICU ICU re-entry.

Adverse evidence includes reversal/collapse after observation or q adjustment; signal confined to aggregate/unconfirmed return; dependence on one endpoint rule, storetime, last-value coding, or one channel; persistence after topology permutation; non-positivity; or unidentifiable latent states. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse confirmed events, wide intervals, unstable topology, contradictory/missing transfers, poor transport overlap, high state entropy, unstable splits, or a q frontier inside the declared plausible range. Do not substitute the aggregate result for a failed primary endpoint.

No result establishes causal support-withdrawal effects, discharge appropriateness, planned/unplanned intent, preventability, ward surveillance adequacy, goals of care, treatment limitation, or transportability beyond this MIMIC snapshot. Those claims require expert chart/intent adjudication, ward monitoring and rapid-response data, validation of transfer semantics, external validation, and preferably prospective or causal designs.
