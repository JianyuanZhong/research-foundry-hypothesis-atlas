# Cross-assay validation of the MerTK-dependent macrophage-to-FLS signaling signature: targeted panel versus full transcriptome

## Seed
D3-M2 (Directional cell communication) — extension of [prior hypothesis]

## Domain
Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses

## Question
Does the full transcriptome (E-MTAB-8316, 58,302 genes) from the same MerTK/UNC106238 FLS-macrophage co-culture experimental system recapitulate the MerTK-dependent FLS activation signature identified in the targeted 445-gene assay (E-MTAB-8873), and does it reveal pathway-level effects invisible to the targeted panel?

## Scientific opening
The parent candidate ([prior hypothesis]) proposed that MerTK-positive macrophages activate FLS through a MerTK-dependent pathway, using the targeted 445-gene assay (E-MTAB-8873) from the University of Glasgow group. A critical limitation of that analysis is the restricted gene panel: 445 genes cannot capture the full transcriptomic response to MerTK inhibition. The same experimental system was profiled with full bulk RNA-seq (E-MTAB-8316, 58,302 genes, 12 samples) from the same lab, same protocol (LPS-stimulated macrophages ± UNC106238, transwell co-culture with RA FLS, 48h), but with different FLS donors and healthy monocyte donors. No prior candidate in this campaign has used E-MTAB-8316.

The unresolved question is twofold:
1. **Concordance**: Does the full transcriptome show concordant directionality for the core inflammatory genes (IL6, TNF, IL1B, CCL2, CXCL8) and the MerTK pathway genes identified in the targeted panel?
2. **Discovery**: Does the full transcriptome reveal pathway-level effects (ECM remodeling, Wnt signaling, metabolic reprogramming, additional cytokine/chemokine axes) that are invisible to the 445-gene panel?

This question is scientifically important because: (a) if the targeted panel captures the main effects, it validates the panel as a cost-effective tool for monitoring MerTK pathway activity; (b) if the full transcriptome reveals major pathway effects missed by the panel, it identifies specific biological processes that the panel cannot monitor, guiding panel redesign; (c) the cross-assay comparison provides a quantitative measure of assay sensitivity for the MerTK perturbation response.

## Hypothesis
UNC106238 (MerTK inhibitor) reduces FLS activation in a MerTK-dependent manner. We predict:
1. **Concordant core response**: The full transcriptome (E-MTAB-8316) shows concordant directionality (≥70% of core inflammatory genes in the same direction) for the UNC106238 effect compared to the targeted panel (E-MTAB-8873), for the genes present in both assays.
2. **Novel pathway effects**: The full transcriptome reveals significant pathway-level effects of UNC106238 (FDR < 0.05 in gene set enrichment) in biological processes not well-represented in the 445-gene panel, such as ECM remodeling, Wnt/β-catenin signaling, metabolic reprogramming (glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation), or additional chemokine axes.
3. **Donor consistency**: The UNC106238 effect direction is consistent across ≥4 of 6 FLS×monocyte pairs for the core inflammatory genes.

## Clinical importance
RA is characterized by synovial inflammation and joint destruction driven by macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk [K1]. MerTK is a key receptor in the tissue repair circuit, and its dysregulation contributes to autoimmune disease pathogenesis [K2]. Understanding the full transcriptomic consequences of MerTK inhibition in FLS co-culture:
- Identifies the complete set of pathways affected by MerTK blockade, informing therapeutic target selection
- Determines whether targeted panels (cost-effective, clinically deployable) can adequately monitor MerTK pathway activity, or whether full transcriptome profiling is necessary
- Reveals whether MerTK inhibition has off-target pathway effects that could limit therapeutic specificity
- Provides a quantitative framework for cross-assay validation that can be applied to other targeted panels in inflammatory disease [K3]

## Methods
### Analysis 1: Per-gene paired comparison in E-MTAB-8316 (baseline)
1. For each of the 6 FLS×monocyte pairs, compute the log2 fold change (UNC106238 vs none) for each gene
2. For each gene, test the consistency of effect direction across the 6 pairs (sign test or Wilcoxon signed-rank)
3. Report effect sizes (mean log2FC), direction consistency (n/6 pairs), and significance for all 58,302 genes
4. Identify the top 100 most consistently affected genes (by direction consistency and effect magnitude)

### Analysis 2: Cross-assay concordance (key scientific advance)
1. Extract the 445 targeted genes from E-MTAB-8873 that are present in E-MTAB-8316 (by ENSG ID mapping)
2. For each of these genes, compute the UNC106238 effect in E-MTAB-8316 (paired log2FC, as in Analysis 1)
3. For the same genes, compute the MerTK-specific effect in E-MTAB-8873 (FLS+Mpos vs FLS+Mpos+Inhibitor, donor-aware pseudobulk)
4. Compare direction and magnitude:
   - Direction concordance rate: fraction of genes with same sign in both assays
   - Magnitude correlation: Spearman ρ between log2FC values
   - Core inflammatory genes (IL6, TNF, IL1B, CCL2, CXCL8, TGFB1): individual concordance
5. Report concordance rate with 95% CI (Clopper-Pearson for direction; bootstrap for correlation)

### Analysis 3: Pathway analysis on full transcriptome
1. Perform gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA or over-representation) on the UNC106238 effect in E-MTAB-8316
2. Use GO biological process, KEGG, and Reactome gene sets
3. Identify significantly enriched pathways (FDR < 0.05)
4. For each enriched pathway, determine whether it is well-represented in the 445-gene panel (≥3 genes in panel) or invisible (0-2 genes in panel)
5. Report "novel" pathways: significantly affected in full transcriptome but not measurable in the targeted panel

### Analysis 4: Mixed-effects model (alternative)
1. Fit a linear mixed-effects model: gene expression ~ condition + (1 | FLS_specimen) + (1 | monocyte_donor)
2. Fixed effect: condition (none vs UNC106238)
3. Random effects: FLS specimen (6 levels), monocyte donor (4 levels)
4. Test the condition effect for each gene
5. Compare the set of significant genes with Analysis 1 (paired comparison)
6. Report the variance partition: fraction of variance explained by FLS specimen vs monocyte donor vs condition

### Key genes for concordance check
- Core inflammatory: IL6, TNF, IL1B, CCL2, CXCL8, TGFB1
- MerTK pathway: MERTK, AXL, TREM2, APOE, GAS6
- Macrophage markers: CD163, MRC1, CD80, CD86
- ECM/remodeling: MMP1, MMP3, MMP13, TIMP1, COL1A1, COL1A2
- Wnt signaling: WNT5A, WNT10B, LRP5, CTNNB1

## Falsification criteria
- **Supportive**: (a) ≥70% direction concordance for core inflammatory genes between E-MTAB-8316 and E-MTAB-8873; (b) ≥3 significantly enriched pathways in E-MTAB-8316 that are invisible to the 445-gene panel; (c) ≥4/6 pairs show consistent direction for core genes
- **Adverse**: (a) <50% direction concordance for core genes (suggesting the targeted panel captures a different or spurious signal); OR (b) no significant UNC106238 effect in E-MTAB-8316 (suggesting the effect is assay-specific); OR (c) no novel pathways beyond the panel
- **Inconclusive**: (a) Insufficient power (6 pairs) to detect concordance with CI excluding both 50% and 90%; OR (b) conflicting results across FLS donors (e.g., 3 pairs show one direction, 3 show the other); OR (c) the 445-gene panel and full transcriptome use different FLS donors, making direct comparison confounded

## Competing explanations
1. **Different FLS donors**: E-MTAB-8873 uses SA256A-263R; E-MTAB-8316 uses specimens 1462-1602. Donor-specific baseline expression could drive apparent discordance.
2. **Different macrophage donors**: E-MTAB-8316 uses healthy monocyte-derived macrophages (SA049, SA051, SA104, SA114); E-MTAB-8873 uses RA synovial macrophages (Mpos/Mneg sorted). The macrophage phenotype may differ substantially.
3. **Different assay sensitivity**: Targeted 445-gene assay vs bulk RNA-seq have different dynamic ranges, normalization, and detection limits.
4. **Transwell vs direct co-culture**: E-MTAB-8316 uses transwell (0.4 μm pore); E-MTAB-8873 may use direct contact. Soluble vs contact-dependent signaling differ.
5. **UNC106238 off-target effects**: The inhibitor may affect pathways beyond MerTK (e.g., AXL, other TAM receptors).
6. **LPS stimulation**: E-MTAB-8316 uses LPS 1 ng/ml pre-stimulation; the timing and concentration may differ from E-MTAB-8873.

## Limitations
- **Small sample size**: 6 FLS×monocyte pairs (12 samples), 4 unique monocyte donors, 6 FLS specimens from 4 patients. Power is limited for detecting modest effects.
- **Healthy monocyte donors**: Not RA synovial macrophages; the inflammatory state may differ from the E-MTAB-8873 system.
- **Transwell co-culture**: Soluble factor-mediated signaling only; contact-dependent interactions are excluded.
- **Single compound, single dose**: UNC106238 at 250 μM only; no dose-response or control compound.
- **Different FLS donors between assays**: Direct cross-assay comparison is confounded by donor differences.
- **No FLS-only control in E-MTAB-8316**: All samples are co-culture; cannot separate FLS-autonomous effects from macrophage-mediated effects.
- **Cross-sectional**: Single time point (48h); no time course.

## What would change the next decision
- **If supportive**: Proceed to pathway-level mechanistic analysis; identify specific novel pathways for wet-lab validation; propose panel redesign.
- **If adverse (no concordance)**: Re-examine the E-MTAB-8873 analysis; consider that the targeted panel may capture a different signal; investigate whether the effect is donor-specific.
- **If adverse (no effect)**: The MerTK-dependent FLS activation hypothesis is challenged; consider that the effect requires direct cell contact or RA-specific macrophage phenotype.
- **If inconclusive**: Need more donors or a different experimental design (e.g., same FLS donors in both assays).
