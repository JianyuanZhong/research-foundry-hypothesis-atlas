## Hypothesis
People with normal creatinine-based eGFR (eGFR-Cr >= 90 mL/min/1.73m2), lower cystatin-C-based eGFR (eGFR-Cys < 60 mL/min/1.73m2), and poor grip strength (below sex-specific median) have a higher risk of acute kidney injury (AKI) during follow-up than people with concordant normal eGFR estimates.

## Scientific Question
Does discordance between creatinine-based and cystatin-C-based eGFR estimates identify a subgroup of people who appear healthy by routine creatinine testing but are at elevated risk of AKI, and does this risk signal persist after adjusting for grip strength and body composition?

## Clinical Importance
- Routine clinical practice uses creatinine-based eGFR to identify CKD and AKI risk.
- Creatinine is influenced by muscle mass; low-muscle-mass individuals may have falsely "normal" eGFR-Cr.
- Cystatin C is less influenced by muscle mass and may reveal true kidney dysfunction.
- Identifying high-risk individuals who appear healthy by routine testing could inform earlier monitoring and intervention.

## Evidence Base

### Key References (unchanged from parent; reference evidence is unchanged)

**[K1] Estrella MM, Ballew SH, Sang Y, et al. Discordance in Creatinine- and Cystatin C-Based eGFR and Clinical Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis. JAMA. 2025;334(21):1915-1926. PMID: 41202182.**
- Claim: In the CKD-PC, 11% of outpatient participants had an eGFRcys that was at least 30% lower than their eGFRcr. This discordance was associated with significantly higher rates of all-cause mortality (HR 1.69), cardiovascular events (HR 1.35-1.61), and kidney failure (HR 1.29).
- Contribution: This proposal extends the CKD-PC findings to a different population (UK Biobank) and tests whether discordance specifically identifies AKI risk (not just mortality or kidney failure).
- Limitation: The CKD-PC is a cohort of people with CKD, not the general population. The association may not generalize to people without CKD.
- Inspection: abstract_only.

**[K2] Haines RW, Fowler AJ, Liang K, et al. Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness. Clin J Am Soc Nephrol. 2023;18(8):997-1005. PMID: 37256861.**
- Claim: In 38 mechanically ventilated ICU patients, eGFR-Cr systematically overestimated kidney function after prolonged critical illness. Cystatin C better estimated true kidney function because it was unaffected by muscle loss.
- Contribution: This proposal tests whether the same discordance pattern (eGFR-Cr overestimating true GFR) is present in the general population and predicts AKI risk.
- Limitation: The study is in critically ill patients, not the general population. The mechanism (muscle loss) may differ in the general population.
- Inspection: abstract_only.

**[K3] Li C, Ma Y, Yang C, et al. Association of Cystatin C Kidney Function Measures With Long-term Deficit-Accumulation Frailty Trajectories and Physical Function Decline. JAMA Netw Open. 2022;5(9):e2234208. PMID: 36178684.**
- Claim: In 15,949 older adults from HRS and CHARLS, cystatin C, eGFRcys, and eGFRdiff were associated with long-term frailty trajectories and physical function decline (grip strength and gait speed).
- Contribution: This proposal tests whether eGFR discordance and poor grip strength jointly predict AKI risk, extending the frailty/physical function findings to a different outcome (AKI) and population (UK Biobank).
- Limitation: The study is in older adults, not the general population. The outcome is frailty/physical function decline, not AKI.

### Baseline approach

### Learned alternative

- Compare AUROC for AKI prediction: (a) eGFR-Cr alone, (b) eGFR-Cys alone, (c) both + grip strength.

### What the alternative reveals
