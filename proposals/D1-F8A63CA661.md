# Episode 33 synthesis: does 12–24-hour lactate trajectory add material cross-hospital prognosis beyond the late value?

## Clinical question, evidence, and hypothesis

At a 24-hour reassessment, the clinician already sees the selected late lactate. The parent established that a binary <20% clearance phenotype is feasible and associated with a clinically recognizable reassessment decision, but that contrast cannot tell whether trajectory adds anything: clearance is defined by the same follow-up value and an earlier baseline, and nonclearance is therefore strongly coupled to the absolute late value.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is limited. Published selected sepsis/shock cohorts show that both follow-up lactate and percent clearance are prognostic. Ryoo et al. (2018; DOI 10.1097/CCM.0000000000003030; indexed abstract inspected, frozen source [source checksum]) reported better standalone discrimination for 6-hour lactate than clearance (AUROC 0.70 versus 0.65). Lee et al. (2021; DOI 10.1097/MD.0000000000024835; acquired full-text source [source checksum]) similarly reported 0.720 versus 0.656. Chaudhari and Agarwal (2022; DOI 10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23935; acquired full-text source [source checksum]) reported standalone 24-hour AUROCs of 0.948 for lactate and 0.963 for relative clearance in 200 patients. None of these analyses establishes added value from clearance in a model already containing the follow-up value. The 2021 Surviving Sepsis Campaign (DOI 10.1007/s00134-021-06506-y; acquired full-text source [source checksum]) makes only a weak, low-quality-evidence suggestion to guide resuscitation toward decreasing lactate and explicitly requires clinical-context interpretation. It does not establish this prognostic increment or decision benefit.

**Untested primary hypothesis:** in the parent's fixed 24-hour landmark population, adding 12–24-hour percent clearance to a model that already contains the absolute late lactate and fixed admission context increases cross-hospital out-of-sample AUROC for subsequent hospital mortality by at least 0.02. An AUROC increment of 0.02 is the prespecified materiality margin: it means two additional correctly ordered death–survivor pairs per 100 randomly sampled death–survivor pairs. It is an operational prognostic margin, not a guideline-endorsed treatment threshold.

The substantive advance is a nested, paired, cross-site prediction comparison. It can falsify the bedside shorthand that “nonclearance” adds material prognosis once the current value is known. Because (C=100(B-F)/B), a positive result supports only that retaining the earlier value, represented as percent change, improves prediction beyond (F); it cannot show that clearance is a distinct biological state. A prespecified raw-baseline benchmark tests that representation issue.

## Locked paired prediction experiment

Fit three prespecified L2 logistic models with `C=10`; do not tune them against the outcome.

- **M0, current-value model:** age, gender, unittype, unitstaytype, t0, `log(F)`, and indicators (F>=2), (F>=4), (F>=8).
- **M1, trajectory model:** every M0 term plus continuous C and indicators (C<20) and (C<0).
- **M2, raw-history benchmark:** every M0 term plus `log(B)` and indicators (B>=4), (B>=8). M2 is secondary and asks whether any M1 gain is simply the earlier raw value under another parameterization.

Within each training set, one-hot encode categorical predictors with an explicit missing level and ignore unseen held-out levels. Winsorize continuous age, t0, and C at training 1st/99th percentiles, median-impute if needed, add missingness indicators, and standardize using training parameters. B and F cannot be missing by design. Never learn preprocessing from held-out rows.

Perform deterministic leave-one-hospital-out validation across all eligible hospitals: fit each model on all other hospitals and predict every person in the held-out hospital exactly once. Pool the paired out-of-hospital predictions. The primary estimand is
[
Delta AUC=AUC(M1)-AUC(M0)
]
for D. Also report each AUROC, Brier score and difference, log loss and difference, calibration intercept/slope and plots, and hospital-specific AUROCs where a hospital has at least five deaths and five survivors. Report M2-M0 and M1-M2 descriptively. Report decision-curve net benefit differences at risk thresholds 0.20, 0.30, and 0.40 only as threshold scenarios; none is a validated trigger.

Use 2,000 copied-hospital bootstrap replicates with seed 20260933 and refit the complete cross-validation procedure. In each replicate sample the 33 eligible hospital IDs with replacement. For each distinct sampled hospital used as a held-out site, exclude every copy of that original hospital from training, fit M0/M1/M2 on the sampled multiplicities of all other hospitals, relearn one-hot levels, winsorization, imputation and scaling inside that training set, predict the held-out original people once, and repeat those paired prediction rows according to the held-out hospital's sampled multiplicity. Pool predictions only after every represented original site has been held out. A replicate fails if a training or pooled set has one outcome class, a fit does not converge, or a metric is nonfinite. This copied-cluster procedure propagates model fitting and preprocessing uncertainty while ensuring no copy of a held-out original hospital enters training. Report percentile 95% intervals. It remains internal multisite validation, not external or prospective validation.

Interpretation requires: >=30 eligible hospitals, >=1,000 people, >=150 deaths and >=150 survivors, >=20 hospitals containing both outcomes, largest hospital <=15% of people, finite predictions and convergence in every M0/M1 leave-site-out fit, finite ΔAUC in >=95% of bootstrap replicates, M0 pooled AUROC >=0.60, and M1 pooled calibration slope in [0.75,1.25] with calibration intercept in [-0.15,0.15]. If M1 Brier score exceeds M0 by >0.005, prediction quality is discordant and the primary result is inconclusive even if discrimination improves.

## Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive:** every gate passes, the 95% interval lower bound for ΔAUC is >=+0.02, and M1 Brier is no worse than M0 by more than 0.005. The strongest supported conclusion is that prior lactate history expressed as percent clearance materially improves cross-hospital ranking beyond the current late value and fixed admission context in this selected measured EICU population. If M2 performs similarly, do not claim percent clearance is superior to retaining both raw values.
- **Adverse to material incremental value:** every gate passes but the interval upper bound is <+0.02. Continue to report the point estimate. This falsifies the prespecified materiality claim even if a coefficient or likelihood-ratio test is statistically significant.
- **Opposite:** every gate passes and the interval upper bound is <0; adding clearance worsens discrimination under this procedure.
- **Inconclusive:** the interval includes +0.02, any population/event/site/convergence/bootstrap/calibration gate fails, or Brier discordance exceeds 0.005. An interval excluding zero but including +0.02 is statistically compatible with a small increment but still inconclusive—not supportive—about clinical materiality.

Results at the 0.20/0.30/0.40 decision thresholds cannot establish decision benefit because EICU does not identify a validated action, action cost, treatment response, or counterfactual outcome. Supportive prediction would justify prospective validation and workflow design, not automatic escalation. Adverse results would favor displaying the absolute late lactate rather than a redundant clearance alert, subject to prospective confirmation. Inconclusive results justify neither practice.

## Evidence limits and verification

EICU cannot establish true vasopressor administration or dose, treatment intent, sepsis, adequate fluids, specimen type, assay indication, beta-agonist exposure, hepatic/renal contribution to clearance, source control, tissue perfusion, treatment limitations, exact death time/cause, clinician response to either lactate, or benefit/harm from an alert. Clinical review is required to adjudicate phenotype meaning and a prospective multisite study is required for calibration transport; a randomized or credible decision-impact study is required to claim that using clearance changes outcomes.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/headers, t0 and one-person selection, literal `> 89` mapping, landmark eligibility, lactate collection/revision/conflict/tie logic, C and X arithmetic, outcome-blind site gate, model matrices and fold isolation, one prediction per person per model, paired metrics, full-refit copied-hospital bootstrap, gates, and whether the submitted conclusion matches supportive/adverse/opposite/inconclusive synthetic and actual outputs. A verifier must reject: (1) correct computation followed by a causal, physiologic, or decision-benefit claim; (2) a positive coefficient or P value called supportive when the ΔAUC margin is not met; (3) an adverse or inconclusive result described as “no association”; and (4) a supportive M1 result described as unique value of percent clearance when M2 is equivalent. Clinical meaning, action thresholds, unmeasured causes, and treatment benefit are not automatically verifiable.
