TITLE
Laboratory-adjudication-gated reciprocal eGFR<60 prediction with direct same-marker construction avoided

PARENT AND SUBSTANTIVE EVOLUTION
Parent: [prior hypothesis].

This proposal evolves the parent from reciprocal continuous biomarker persistence to a clinically interpretable, falsifiable threshold experiment. It preserves the parent's eligible baseline pool (468,887), first-repeat cohort (16,546), initial assessment instance 0, first repeat instance 1, inclusive elapsed-time window 2.0 through 8.0 years, repeat-attender estimand, measured-baseline attendance sensitivity, and noncausal evidence boundary. It does not reinterpret the parent's positive persistence coefficients as CKD progression.

The substantive advance is a prespecified test of whether one baseline filtration marker identifies subsequent below-threshold status on the opposite marker, beyond the opposite marker's own baseline value. Each primary direction uses a single-marker exposure and a different single-marker outcome, thereby avoiding direct same-marker exposure-outcome construction. This is narrower and more honest than calling a two-marker discordance score coupling-free. It also introduces a hard laboratory-adjudication gate: until equation-compatible analyte calibration is documented, results may be computed only as protocol-defined biomarker reclassification and may not be labeled clinically standard eGFR or CKD status.

UNRESOLVED CLINICAL QUESTION
Among UK Biobank participants with valid baseline measurements and a first repeat assessment 2 to 8 years later, does baseline cystatin-C-derived filtration below 60 predict repeat creatinine-derived filtration below 60 beyond baseline creatinine-derived filtration, and does the reciprocal relation hold?

Clinical importance is tied to the commonly consequential eGFR 60 boundary, where discordance can affect whether a patient receives confirmatory testing, medication review, or closer monitoring. The experiment does not test whether a participant has CKD, because one below-60 observation and two measurements years apart do not establish the required chronicity or kidney-damage evidence.

EVIDENCE BOUNDARY
Already supported by the parent: positive reciprocal observable persistence among repeat attenders, including measured-baseline attendance-weighted positive P1 and P2 simultaneous bootstrap intervals; P1 discriminated from matched LDL and vitamin-D controls, while P2 did not. Those findings support persistence of observed biomarkers, not renal decline.

Untested claim: baseline opposite-marker threshold status provides incremental information about future target-marker threshold status after accounting for baseline target-marker value, age, sex, and elapsed time.

The experiment cannot establish latent measured-GFR decline, CKD onset or progression, a treatment effect, causality, correction of MNAR repeat selection, clinical utility, or transportability.

with kappa 0.7 female or 0.9 male and alpha -0.219 female or -0.144 male.

Before any clinically standard interpretation, an independent laboratory reviewer must document that the UKB creatinine results are traceable in the relevant assay period to an IDMS-compatible reference system and that the UKB cystatin-C results are traceable to the standardized ERM-DA471/IFCC system, or provide a validated assay-specific conversion. The official UKB documents currently establish platform, method, units, ISO quality systems, internal QC, and EQA, but do not by themselves establish those exact traceability claims. If the gate fails, the study remains a valid protocol-biomarker threshold analysis but the clinical eGFR label and clinical decision interpretation are stopped.

Freeze before computation: conversion constant 88.4; no intermediate rounding; classify raw calculated values strictly as below 60 when eGFR < 60. Output units are mL/min/1.73 m^2. Do not add <45 or <30 thresholds after inspecting results.

PRIMARY ESTIMANDS AND COUPLING CONTROL
Both directions are co-primary and require simultaneous inference.

Direction A (cystatin C to creatinine):
- Exposure: baseline eGFRcys below 60 versus at least 60, constructed solely from baseline cystatin C, baseline visit-specific age, and sex.
- Outcome: repeat eGFRcr below 60 versus at least 60, constructed solely from repeat creatinine, repeat visit-specific age, and sex.
- Comparator/adjustment: baseline eGFRcr as a prespecified continuous spline or, in a secondary clinically tabulated model, baseline eGFRcr below 60; plus baseline age, sex, actual elapsed years, and prespecified interactions only.
- Primary estimand: model-standardized risk difference for repeat eGFRcr <60 comparing baseline eGFRcys <60 with >=60 over the observed baseline covariate distribution.

Direction B (creatinine to cystatin C):
- Exposure: baseline eGFRcr below 60 versus at least 60, constructed solely from baseline creatinine, baseline visit-specific age, and sex.
- Outcome: repeat eGFRcys below 60 versus at least 60, constructed solely from repeat cystatin C, repeat visit-specific age, and sex.
- Comparator/adjustment: baseline eGFRcys as a prespecified continuous spline or secondary baseline-threshold term; plus baseline age, sex, actual elapsed years, and prespecified interactions only.
- Primary estimand: model-standardized risk difference for repeat eGFRcys <60 comparing baseline eGFRcr <60 with >=60 over the observed baseline covariate distribution.

The exposure and target outcome in each direction share no analyte value directly. This avoids direct same-marker mathematical construction, but does not eliminate common latent GFR, regression to the mean, correlated measurement error, biological confounding, treatment, or conditioning on attendance. Do not call the analysis coupling-free. The baseline target-marker comparator is adjustment, not part of the opposite-marker exposure.

ANALYSIS PLAN
Fit prespecified weighted binomial or logistic outcome models among first-repeat attenders using the parent's measured-baseline attendance weights, with the same ten-fold cross-fitted attendance model, held-out probabilities, stabilized numerator, and 1st/99th percentile truncation. Refit all nuisance components within each participant bootstrap replicate. Use restricted cubic splines with knots at probabilities 0.05, 0.275, 0.50, 0.725, and 0.95 for continuous baseline target-marker adjustment and elapsed years. Use actual elapsed days divided by 365.25. Preserve participant folds under the bootstrap and use multiplicity rather than copying rows.

Report, for each direction, unweighted and weighted event counts, event prevalence, effective sample size, overlap by exposure group, calibration intercept and slope or observed-to-expected calibration, Brier score, risk difference with simultaneous 95% interval, and risk ratio only if event support is adequate. Use penalized or Firth fallback only if frozen before analysis; otherwise declare separation a computational failure rather than silently change the estimand. Apply a prespecified multiplicity procedure to the two co-primary directions. Threshold classifications must use raw eGFR before rounding.

Minimum support gates are mandatory: at least one event and one nonevent in each exposure group; no unsupported covariate extrapolation; finite and positive weights; and a predeclared effective sample-size threshold. If a gate fails, report the direction as not estimable rather than collapse categories or change the threshold.

ATTENDANCE AND MISSINGNESS
Repeat attendance is a selected post-baseline subset. The attendance model can address only measured baseline predictors included in it. The 0.005 individual probability diagnostic remains a diagnostic, not a positivity pass; probability flooring may be used only as the inherited prespecified sensitivity and cannot be called MNAR correction. Report complete-case and attendance-weighted results, the inherited floor sensitivity, weight distributions, and support diagnostics. Do not generalize repeat-attender risks to the full baseline population without additional assumptions.

FALSIFICATION AND INTERPRETATION
Supportive: both co-primary directions have adequate support, simultaneous intervals exclude zero in the prespecified direction, calibration is acceptable, and findings are stable under the inherited measured-baseline attendance sensitivity. This supports incremental prediction of opposite-marker repeat threshold status among comparable repeat attenders, not CKD progression.

Asymmetric supportive result: only Direction A is positive and it exceeds prespecified nonrenal controls while Direction B does not. Interpret as marker-specific evidence limited to Direction A; do not claim bidirectional renal specificity. This is plausible given the parent's P1/P2 asymmetry.

Adverse: intervals include zero, reverse sign, fail under attendance sensitivity, or one direction is no better than negative controls. This falsifies the corresponding incremental threshold-prediction claim; it does not show no renal biology exists.

Inconclusive: sparse events, separation, unsupported overlap, unresolved laboratory traceability, materially divergent weighted and unweighted results, or failure of the attendance model diagnostics. Preserve the parent and report the threshold analysis as nonclinical protocol evidence.

A threshold result is not CKD diagnosis, onset, progression, or decline. Stronger claims require repeated measurements meeting chronicity requirements, urine albumin or other kidney-damage evidence, measured GFR, treatment and comorbidity data, clinical adjudication, laboratory calibration documentation, external validation, or prospective clinical-impact testing.
