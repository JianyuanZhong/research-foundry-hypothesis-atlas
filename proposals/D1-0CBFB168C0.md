# HCC child working note: response trajectory versus surveillance/observation bias

## Status and scientific opening
This is a substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. The parent asks whether post-treatment response adds prognostic information for recurrence/progression. Its strongest remaining ambiguity is not model capacity: whether an apparent response signal is response-driven information or a marker of who receives adequate surveillance and treatment selection. The child makes that ambiguity the primary estimand and tests it with prespecified surveillance-stratified and observation-process analyses, while retaining the parent’s exact HCC bindings and three inspected references.

## Question and claims
Among adults with HCC and a first documented locoregional treatment, does an explicit early response trajectory add prognostic information for subsequent recurrence/progression after accounting for the timing, frequency, and adequacy of post-treatment surveillance and treatment-selection proxies?

The evidence-supported claim is narrower: prior work supports that baseline tumor burden, AFP, liver reserve, treatment heterogeneity, and imaging features are prognostic or informative in retrospective HCC cohorts [K1]–[K3], but does not establish incremental value of an EHR-derived response trajectory in this local cohort. The unresolved claim is incremental prognostic information conditional on surveillance and treatment-selection proxies. This remains prognostic, not causal: adjustment cannot prove residual viable tumor or treatment mechanism.

## New observation-process estimand
For each patient, derive from examinations and encounters, without using post-response outcome text:
1. adequate-response surveillance: at least one liver imaging report in T0+30–T0+90 with an explicit response category; record date, modality, number of reports, and whether the report is complete/partial/indeterminate.
2. surveillance intensity: number and timing of liver imaging reports in T0–T0+90 and T0+90–T0+365, plus number of linked encounters and the interval from T0 to first post-response imaging.
3. observation opportunity: dates of any encounter and imaging, with time-varying censoring/observation indicators; do not treat absence of a report as absence of disease.
4. treatment-selection proxies: treatment category/intensity at T0, subsequent qualifying procedures, orders, and medication exposure only when available and temporally valid; these are prognostic/selection descriptors, not causal treatment effects.

The primary incremental estimand is the held-out change in 1- and 2-year recurrence/progression risk and integrated Brier score when adding explicit response trajectory to a model containing baseline variables plus the prespecified surveillance/observation covariates. The key contrast is response trajectory among patients with adequate response-window surveillance versus patients without it; a secondary analysis restricts to adequate surveillance and reports the unadjusted versus surveillance-adjusted association. A negative-control/observation audit compares recurrence ascertainment and follow-up duration across response categories and surveillance strata. These analyses distinguish a signal that persists with adequate observation from one that is concentrated in intensively observed patients, but they cannot fully identify mechanism.

## Baseline and substantive alternative
Baseline: penalized Cox or pooled logistic model using only pre-T0 variables (age, sex, treatment, tumor-burden proxy, liver disease, AFP, liver function, time since diagnosis), with missingness indicators. Fit on earlier index years, tune on a middle validation year, evaluate on held-out later years; never split by row. Report integrated Brier score, time-dependent AUC, calibration intercept/slope, risk differences, and bootstrap intervals.

Alternative: patient-level gradient-boosted survival model adding response category and longitudinal summaries (AFP slope/level, liver-function change, imaging count/timing, residual-disease indicators) from T0 through the response window, plus the surveillance/observation covariates defined above. The alternative is scientifically useful because it tests whether temporal response information adds what baseline and observation intensity lose; it is not selected for complexity. A latent residual-versus-de-novo mechanistic model is deferred because local data cannot distinguish etiologies without validated imaging adjudication, pathology/genomics/ctDNA. CPU is appropriate; GPU is not needed. Planned solver envelope: 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, 2 GPUs, 28800 seconds; no model has been fitted.

## Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: response remains associated after surveillance adjustment and the response-augmented model improves held-out calibration/Brier score with stable direction across treatment strata, calendar periods, and adequate-surveillance strata; recurrence ascertainment is not materially different across response groups after accounting for observation opportunity.

Adverse: association disappears after surveillance adjustment, is confined to one modality or one observation stratum, reverses across periods, or the alternative is worse than baseline; this favors burden, selection, or ascertainment explanations.

Inconclusive: sparse adjudicated response/recurrence, wide uncertainty, poor calibration, or excessive missingness; this does not refute biology. A null in poorly observed patients is not evidence that response has no biological meaning.

Computation can check joins, dates, leakage, surveillance definitions, model fitting, and conclusion/output consistency. It cannot establish mechanism, treatment benefit, residual viable tumor, or an action threshold. Expert radiology/clinical adjudication of response, recurrence, tumor burden, and treatment intent is essential; prospective validation and ideally imaging/pathology or molecular residual-disease data are needed for stronger claims.
