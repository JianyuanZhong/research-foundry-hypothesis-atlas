## Unresolved question and advance

After a current biomarker assessment, does retaining the preceding panel change a fixed-capacity review list enough to find more people with a later recorded cardiovascular, renal, or metabolic event? The parent tests pathway-specific score heterogeneity. This successor makes the decision estimand explicit: a service has a fixed number of reviews, so a model that improves a proper score but does not improve event yield among the same number of reviewed people does not yet show triage value.

The falsifiable primary hypothesis is:

> In the eligible paired-panel cohort, adding the prior eight-field panel to current values, age, sex, current-assessment year, and repeat-assessment interval increases held-out capture and positive predictive value for any first qualifying cardiovascular/renal/metabolic recorded event among a prespecified fixed number (K) of reviewed participants, compared with current values plus interval metadata; the gain is not fully explained by a change in pathway mix.

The primary policy is a single pooled review list, not separate pathway-specific quotas. (K=lfloor0.10 n_{A,mathrm{early}}
floor), where (A) is the fold-local central 60% of current-only target risk and (n_{A,mathrm{early}}) is its early-fit population. The identical absolute K is applied to the late no-refit cohort and to every model on the same eligible people. Ties use eid hash. This prevents changing total workload or hiding a capacity tradeoff by allocating separate slots. Secondary analyses report capture and PPV separately for cardiovascular, renal, and metabolic events and the selected-list pathway composition; they do not replace the pooled primary estimand.

A positive result would justify a prospective study of whether a history-aware review policy is worth evaluating. It would not show that ordering repeat tests, reviewing selected people, treating them, or using these thresholds improves clinical outcomes.

## Selection audit and estimand

From all people with a valid T0 panel, model selection into a valid T1 panel within each training fold using only T0 values, age, sex, and T0 calendar year. Report repeat rate, covariate balance, overlap, effective sample size, and inverse-probability-of-repeat sensitivity. No T1 value or future outcome enters this model. This is a generalizability sensitivity for the paired cohort, not a causal correction.

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold split, fixed by a published hash of eid and shared by every model and capacity list. Fit preprocessing, imputation, scaling, regularization, calibration, tuning, and weights within training folds only. The primary contrast is:

- M1a: current instance-1 values, age, sex, T1 calendar year, and L.
- M1b: M1a plus the eight instance-0 values.

M0 (current values, age, and sex only) is a descriptive continuity baseline. M1b versus M1a isolates prior values from repeat spacing. For each model, rank only by the held-out probability of any C/R/M event, apply the fold-local early-defined band and fixed K, and report capture@K, PPV@K, event yield per 100 reviewed, list overlap, reclassified people, and pathway composition. Compare paired bootstrap confidence intervals and a prespecified permutation distribution. The primary estimand is the held-out difference in pooled capture@K and PPV@K under identical K; Brier score and log loss remain secondary proper-score checks.

The substantive learned alternative is M2: gradient-boosted multiclass probabilities on exactly M1b inputs, with prespecified current-by-prior discordance, current-by-L and prior-by-L interactions and smooth threshold terms. It can reveal nonlinear pathway-specific discordance or thresholds lost by the transparent nested models. It is evaluated on the same participants, folds, K, outcomes, calibration, uncertainty, and falsification checks. M2 cannot replace M1b versus M1a as the causal-to-the-model attribution of prior history.

Require at least 100 total target events, 50 each in C/R/M, and 200 participants per reported capacity/pathway cell; otherwise mark that claim inconclusive. Use participant bootstrap preserving folds and event labels, calibration intercept/slope, pathway Brier scores, log loss, discrimination as secondary, and exploratory decision curves at 0.05 and 0.10 only as nonvalidated thresholds. ## Falsification and interpretation

Run code/date position permutation and shifted-position controls; outcome-label/date permutation; within-current-risk and interval-stratum permutation of prior panels preserving current values, missingness, interval and selection; complete-case versus fold-local missingness sensitivity; raw-prior versus raw-change sensitivity; repeat-weight and interval-regime sensitivity with positivity diagnostics; and early-through-2012 fit followed by no-refit 2013 application when support permits.

Support requires certified field/outcome semantics, valid endpoint mode, adequate event and cell support, no integrity failures, and a prespecified lower confidence bound showing higher M1b capture@K and PPV@K than M1a at the same K. The gain must survive prior-panel permutation, selection weighting, interval regimes, and the 2013 no-refit test. If M2 is used for explanation, its interactions must be stable and calibrated; a small discrimination gain alone is insufficient.

Adverse evidence is no gain in adequately supported held-out capture or PPV, gain confined to spacing, disappearance under permutation or selection controls, pathway mix that sacrifices one pathway without improving pooled yield, or calibration harm that outweighs the capacity benefit. Inconclusive evidence includes failed semantic/timing/ascertainment gates, sparse cells, poor overlap or effective sample size, unstable folds, conflicting code/date definitions, or disagreement between recorded-event and gated prospective modes.

Computationally checkable claims are source/header/key integrity, cohort and endpoint construction conditional on certified mappings, fixed-K accounting, held-out predictions, metrics, confidence intervals, calibration, permutations, and whether conclusions match those artifacts. Clinical adjudication or new data are required for true disease onset, phenotype severity, specimen timing, clinical usefulness, action thresholds, causal benefit of repeat testing/review, treatment effects, mechanisms, and external transport. The result cannot establish that a review policy improves patient outcomes.

## Alternatives, demonstrations, and deferred branches

The transparent M1a/M1b contrast is primary because it identifies the information increment attributable to prior values while holding capacity, current values, and repeat spacing fixed. M2 is retained because it can reveal clinically interpretable discordance structure that additive models lose; it is deferred from primary inference if unstable or poorly calibrated. A mechanistic organ-score model is deferred because field labels, units, assay quality, and specimen dates are unverified. A disease-history transformer adaptation is deferred because this export is a sparse field-position array, not a verified dated disease sequence. Genetics/PRS is deferred because genomics is derived metadata rather than a verified sequence/variant bundle or validated disease-specific score. Imaging, sleep accelerometry, infection staging, CHIP, cancer stage, and raw fat-distribution branches remain expert ideas requiring unavailable or unverified modalities/chronology.
