# Selective suppression of a pathogenic fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state

## Seed and question
Develops expert seed D2-T2 (`[prior hypothesis]`). The unresolved question is whether a candidate intervention can reduce a harmful fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state, rather than merely shifting the overall transcriptomic profile.

## Clinical importance
Fibroblasts are a major target of fibrotic disease, but their pathogenic program may be induced, amplified or restrained by macrophage signals. A target that is responsive only in a particular macrophage context could be a more selective intervention point than a target whose expression merely differs between disease and control. Resolving this question can prioritize candidates for perturbation and reduce false prioritization from a global expression contrast.

## Evidence and closest work
The available data include E-MTAB-8316 (four FLS donor clusters, six nested monocyte donors, twelve condition samples) and GSE95588 (coculture HT-Seq counts, including r1/r2 derived from earlier GSE57723 and therefore not independent validation). E-MTAB-8316 has an explicit FLS/monocyte context design; GSE95588 provides a complementary coculture assay. The closest inspected work is the D2-T1 branch on context-dependent fibroblast response to macrophage conditioning, which establishes the donor-aware baseline and mixed-model alternative design.

## Hypothesis
A candidate intervention that reduces a harmful fibroblast program while preserving a protective macrophage-linked state will show a selective transcriptomic signature that is not explained by a global shift in the overall expression profile.

## Data bindings
- E-MTAB-8316: four FLS donor clusters, six nested monocyte donors, twelve condition samples.
- GSE95588: coculture HT-Seq counts, including r1/r2 derived from earlier GSE57723.
- Donor-aware baseline: matched contrasts within donor clusters.
- Mixed-model alternative: donor and condition fixed effects, with a random effect for donor.

## Analysis
1. Define a donor-aware baseline using matched contrasts within donor clusters.
2. Fit a mixed-model alternative with donor and condition fixed effects, and a random effect for donor.
3. Compare the two approaches to identify a selective transcriptomic signature.
4. Validate the signature in GSE95588, accounting for the non-independence of r1/r2.

## Falsification criteria
- If the selective signature is not observed in the donor-aware baseline, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the selective signature is not observed in the mixed-model alternative, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the selective signature is not validated in GSE95588, the hypothesis is not supported.

## Outcomes
- Supportive: a selective transcriptomic signature is observed in both the donor-aware baseline and the mixed-model alternative, and is validated in GSE95588.
- Adverse: no selective transcriptomic signature is observed, or the signature is not validated in GSE95588.
- Inconclusive: the signature is observed in one approach but not the other, or the validation is inconclusive.

## Limitations
- The data are transcriptomic and do not establish direct target action; perturbation validation is required for causal claims.
- The non-independence of r1/r2 in GSE95588 must be accounted for in the validation.
- The donor-aware baseline and mixed-model alternative are computational approaches and do not establish a causal mechanism.
