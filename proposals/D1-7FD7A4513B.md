# Episode 3 evolution: a day-42, censoring-aware test of recorded assay state versus care process after first dated exact-code TACE

Status: proposed repair; no predictive model has been fitted and no clinical conclusion is claimed. The source audits below are feasibility observations. This child changes the primary estimand and the state/process channel definitions because the parent’s complete-180-day binary cohort could condition on future observation, and its state channel could absorb measurement opportunity.

## Decision-relevant question and claim boundary

Among patients with a first **dated** locally recorded exact-code TACE who reach a day-42 landmark without another exact-code TACE, does the recorded laboratory trajectory through day 42 improve prediction of a later exact-code TACE during days 43–180 beyond pre-index severity/history and the post-index observation process?

The decision is not whether to give, withhold, or repeat TACE. It is whether this limited EHR signal is strong and robust enough to justify an expert-imaging/measurement-validation study of a day-42 risk stratification workflow, or whether the direction should be abandoned in favor of a care-process explanation.

The strongest available evidence supports only that some pre-TACE biomarkers have prognostic associations in retrospective TACE cohorts [K1], and that HCC treatment response is ordinarily adjudicated with imaging concepts such as viable enhancement/necrosis and mRECIST/RECICL [K2]. A consequential rival is that repeat treatment and laboratory density reflect planned retreatment, current severity, access, or surveillance intensity. HCC surveillance signals can also be heterogeneous and lagging rather than direct measures of tumor response [K3].

The falsifiable claim is narrower: at a day-42 decision landmark, exact-label numeric assay observations and their within-patient changes add held-out predictive information for a later **operational repeat-TACE event** after accounting for baseline/history and measured observation opportunity. A supportive result does not establish biological response, progression, treatment failure, TACE benefit, or mechanism.

## Why the parent endpoint needed repair

The parent’s known-status binary comparison required obs_end >= index+180. Because obs_end is only known using future records, this selects on post-index follow-up and can be affected by the same severity or care process being studied. It is useful as a descriptive complete-case diagnostic, but not the primary next-decision population.

The repaired primary population is defined at day 42: require obs_end >= index+42 and no later exact-code TACE in the interval from index through day 42. Both facts are available at the intended day-42 decision. A patient with an early repeat is not a negative for the later-repeat question; report early repeats separately and exclude them from this landmark estimand. Patients not observable through day 42 are outside this day-42 decision population, not biological negatives.

For each eligible landmark patient, follow from day 43 to the earlier of an exact-code repeat TACE or administrative/recorded censoring at obs_end, through day 180. A patient with no event and obs_end >= day 180 is a complete negative. A patient with no event and obs_end between days 42 and 180 is right-censored, never a negative. No death, outside-care, transplant, or treatment-intent assumption is made. The primary analysis is a discrete-time landmark hazard/risk analysis with inverse-probability-of-censoring weights estimated inside each training fold; a complete-180-day binary analysis is a sensitivity restricted to known-status patients, not the main estimand.

Use elapsed timestamps and the fixed interval 43 <= delta_days < 181, with feature cutoff at index+42 days. Do not use date rounding that can move a 42-day-23-hour record into the risk window or a 181-day record into it.

## Assay identity and state/process separation

The labs source is `[internal dataset path]` with columns patient master index, encounter number, test, qualitative result, quantitative result, specimen type, test time. A full scan found these exact labels: alpha-fetoprotein (190,022 rows; 42,304 patients), albumin (238,293; 42,490), prealbumin (184,917; 38,444), total bilirubin (238,553; 42,490), direct bilirubin (238,334; 42,491), alanine aminotransferase (308,671; 43,271), platelets (74,121; 19,152), platelet count (253,588; 42,932). The counts are source-audit facts, not cohort/model results.

There is no units or reference-range column. Specimen type is blank in nearly all audited target rows. Therefore do not convert units, use clinical thresholds, pool labels, or call the values liver function/tumour burden. Keep albumin separate from prealbumin, total bilirubin separate from direct bilirubin, and platelets separate from platelet count. Use only ordinary numeric quantitative results in the primary numeric channel. Preserve explicit inequality/censoring flags and qualitative-result flags as separate recorded-result features; never replace values such as >60500 with an exact number. Standardization and any numeric imputation are fitted within training folds and exact assay labels remain separate. The solver must audit label-by-calendar value distributions before fitting; material scale drift makes the state result inconclusive.

The parent’s state-only model included missingness indicators, which can make measurement opportunity look like physiology. The repaired disjoint ablation is:

- M0: pre-index demographics, department, encounter/admission history, linked pre-index diagnosis dictionary counts, pre-index procedure counts, and pre-index exact-assay summaries. All counts use only timestamps before index and exact-duplicate collapse.
- M_state: M0 plus response-window (index+0 through index+42) observed numeric assay values, assay-specific changes from a valid pre-index baseline in [-365,0) to a response value in (0,42], and result/censoring flags. Missing values are fold-imputed without a missingness indicator in this channel.
- M_process: M0 plus response-window observation opportunity: dated encounter/examination/order/medication counts, gaps, assay request counts by exact label, and measurement missingness/availability indicators. It contains no assay values or result flags.
- M_both: M0 plus the union of the disjoint M_state and M_process channels.

A paired change requires both valid time-bounded observations; unpaired patients remain in the landmark population through the explicit process channel and deterministic state imputation. This is still observational: a numeric state signal can be selected by who gets tested, and the decomposition does not identify a biological mechanism.

Every feature must be materialized using only information available by index+42. Exclude all post-index procedure tokens, including early TACE, from predictors. Early TACE is instead an exclusion from this landmark estimand. Do not use obs_end, repeat-event flags, future visit counts, future labels, file order, or outcome ascertainment fields as predictors.

## Split, models, and falsification

Use one frozen patient-level split with no patient in both partitions. The primary split is a calendar-block holdout: earliest 80% of eligible index dates for development and latest 20% for untouched test, with the boundary frozen before outcome evaluation. Within development, use five grouped folds for all preprocessing, codebook decisions, imputation, hyperparameters, and censoring models. A stratified random patient split is a prespecified secondary transport check, not a substitute for the temporal test. Report index-date and outcome/censoring composition in each partition.

The simple baseline is a fold-fitted elastic-net pooled-logistic discrete-time hazard model for days 43–180, with the same M0 inputs, fixed landmark rows/intervals, IPCW training weights, held-out weighted log loss, weighted Brier at day 180, calibration slope/intercept, and bootstrap uncertainty. The learned alternative is a compact GRU (or equivalent gated sequence) over the same patient histories from -365 through +42, carrying event category, exact assay label, numeric value only after audit, result/censor flags, time since index/prior event, and a state/process channel tag; it outputs the same discrete-time hazard and uses the same split, censoring target, metrics and four ablations S0/S_state/S_process/S_both. Token caps, truncation, parameter count, and three fixed seeds are frozen before test evaluation.

The learned alternative is scientifically useful because it can retain irregular timing, transient trajectories and interactions that window summaries lose. It is secondary because the transparent hazard model makes the estimand, censoring weights and channel attribution easier to audit. If the sequence fit exceeds the approved envelope, complete the transparent models and controls and label the learned comparison incomplete; do not change the question. A mechanistic state-space model is deferred because imaging, intent, adjudicated burden, units and reference ranges are unavailable.

Falsification controls, applied using the same folds/split and reported before any favorable interpretation, are:

1. Permute response-window timestamps within patient while preserving each patient’s assay/result multiset and counts.
2. Permute process marks across development patients within index-calendar and event-count strata, without moving assay values.
3. Shuffle same-assay response values across patients within each development fold while preserving measurement timing/count structure.
4. Compare against a capacity-matched process-only model.
5. Fit an outcome-label permutation negative control in development and evaluate on untouched true-label test data as a leakage/sanity check.

A state increment that disappears after process controls, is reproduced by process/timing permutations, or changes materially under exact-label/code/observation sensitivities is evidence for workflow/measurement explanations, not biological state.

## Outcomes, uncertainty, and interpretation

Primary held-out outcomes are IPCW log loss and IPCW Brier at day 180 for M_state versus M0 and M_process; calibration is co-primary for decision usefulness. AUROC/AUPRC are secondary and not sufficient evidence of clinical value. Use patient-level bootstrap intervals that respect the frozen test predictions; for the sequence use fixed seed ranges rather than selecting the best seed. Report censoring support and effective sample size.

Supportive results require: at least 50 observed repeat events as a prespecified precision screen; completed codebook and all-source observation audits; a state increment over M0 and process in held-out weighted loss/Brier with uncertainty excluding no improvement; acceptable calibration; stable direction across the temporal and random split checks, exact-code sensitivity, undated-history sensitivity, and observation definitions; and no comparable gain from the permutation controls. Even then, the conclusion is only that recorded assay information has incremental predictive value for an operational repeat-TACE event at this landmark. The next action is expert imaging/intent linkage, not a treatment rule.

Adverse results include no state increment after process adjustment, process-only/permuted features matching the increment, poor calibration, or instability under coding/censoring sensitivity. With adequate precision, this supports abandoning a state-triggered validation direction and prioritizing care-process measurement. Inconclusive results include fewer than 50 events, too few day-42 landmark patients, unstable assay identity/scale, severe or unsupported censoring weights, wide intervals, or an incomplete learned fit. An imprecise null is not biological refutation.

No output establishes that a patient responded to TACE, has progression, needs repeat treatment, benefits causally from TACE, or has a particular mechanism. Stronger claims require expert review and imaging-derived viable enhancement/necrosis (mRECIST/RECICL/LI-RADS), treatment intent and scheduling, clinically validated laboratory units/reference ranges, death/outside-care ascertainment, and ideally an independent temporal/external cohort.
