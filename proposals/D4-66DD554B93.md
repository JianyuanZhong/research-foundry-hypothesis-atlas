# UKB two-panel metabolic change and route-specific transition to a second cardiometabolic domain

## Audit repair and scientific deliverable

This successor preserves the selected parents' verified UK Biobank two-panel binding, one-domain-at-landmark population, delayed entry, five-year 1-to-2 question, and conditional repeat-panel estimand. It repairs a real comparison gap: the prior M0 was a pooled current-level Cox model for any second domain, while M1 could gain both from biomarker change and from decomposing the endpoint into route-specific hazards. The prior text also did not explicitly require a death hazard for individual competing-risk predictions. The repaired primary comparison gives M0 and M1 the same route-specific second-domain and death-hazard architecture; M1's only primary information addition is the dated two-panel change vector.

The solver must produce a read-only source/header and overlap audit; frozen codebook and identical-suffix first-event construction; flow through repeat-panel eligibility, the one-domain landmark and washout; measurement-selection and positivity summaries; route/event-support tables; fitted M0/M1 coefficients with 95% intervals; locked five-year route-specific and aggregate cumulative-incidence predictions; calibration, Brier, discrimination, RMST and decision-curve outputs; and washout, gap, selection-weighting, competing-death, negative-control and within-state permutation falsifications. Completion is these computed outputs with uncertainty and a supportive, adverse or inconclusive interpretation, not readiness or a successful fit.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by current evidence is that baseline metabolic measurements are associated with later coded diabetes, cardiovascular or chronic-kidney disease, and that a subset has two dated laboratory panels. The available evidence does not establish whether within-person change in HbA1c, triglycerides or HDL adds prognostic information after latest levels, existing disease identity, repeat-panel selection and competing death are accounted for.

Primary hypothesis:

> In UKB participants aged 40–69 with exactly one coded diabetes, cardiovascular disease or chronic kidney disease domain at a prespecified post-repeat-panel landmark, annualized worsening in HbA1c, triglycerides and/or HDL adds prognostic information for the first transition to a second domain beyond latest levels and prespecified covariates, with possibly different associations across existing-domain/new-domain routes.

This is a prognostic association and internal prediction hypothesis. It is not a causal claim about changing a biomarker, a treatment effect, a chart-adjudicated diagnosis, or benefit from acting on a risk score. Acute appendicitis is a negative-control outcome and is not expected to share the cardiometabolic change signal.

## Phenotype and temporal boundaries

Freeze the parser before model development. Normalize code case and whitespace, retain ICD-9 decimal detail for audit, and pair each code only with the date at the identical suffix. A code-only value, date-only value, invalid date, blank/nonfinite value or unrecognized code is audit-only and cannot create an event. For each domain use the earliest valid paired date across all positions:

- diabetes: ICD-10 E10–E14; ICD-9 250;
- cardiovascular disease: ICD-10 I20–I25, I50, I60–I69; ICD-9 410–414, 428, 430–438;
- chronic kidney disease: ICD-10 N18 or N19; ICD-9 585 or 586;
- negative control: ICD-10 K35; ICD-9 540.

These are first recorded administrative codes, not confirmed onset. Death is parsed separately and is never a disease domain. If a disease code and death share a date, death wins in the primary competing-risk convention; a reverse tie rule is a sensitivity.

Define d0 as main 53-0.0 and d1 as main 53-1.0, cross-checked against assessment dates after ISO normalization. Include age 40–69 at recruitment, valid sex, finite field-valid primary markers at both panels, d1>d0, and gap 365–3,650 days. Exclude date-order contradictions among available 53-0.0/1.0/2.0/3.0. Calculate annualized change as (x at panel 1 minus x at panel 0) divided by (d1-d0)/365.25; reverse HDL so positive means worsening. Standardize only with fit-partition means and standard deviations.

Construct domain history through d1. Exclude two or more domains on or before d1, and exclude death or a new second-domain onset in (d1,o], where o=d1+365 days. The primary risk set is exactly one domain at o, retaining whether diabetes, CVD or CKD was first. Events/death at o are excluded and follow-up starts strictly after o. Use 0-, 2- and 5-year washout sensitivities and a 182–5,475-day panel-gap sensitivity. Follow to the first new domain, death, the fixed global administrative boundary of the frozen outcome snapshot, or five years after o. Death is absorbing and competing. Report full-five-year opportunity and censoring by the snapshot boundary.

This is intentionally a landmark estimand among repeat-tested, alive participants who remain in one coded domain through o. It does not estimate the full UKB population effect and does not remove survivor, measurement-indication or administrative-phenotype selection.

## Repeat-panel selection and covariates

The primary estimand is conditional on a valid two-panel core-marker set and eligible one-domain landmark. Among participants with valid panel-0 core markers, define S=1 only if panel-1 HbA1c, TG, HDL and d1 pass the same rules. Model S using only pre-panel information: panel-0 markers, age, sex, BMI, smoking, BP, baseline domain state/history through d0, and whether alive and without a second domain before d1. Report availability and positivity by age, sex, baseline domain and panel-0 risk strata. If positivity is adequate, use stabilized inverse-probability-of-S weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles for a sensitivity targeting the panel-0-eligible population under this measured selection model. This does not repair unmeasured indication, treatment, adherence, socioeconomic selection, assay units or outcome misclassification.

For both primary models, use latest valid assessment covariates no later than d1: BMI, averaged BP, smoking, center, age, sex, panel gap, existing-domain identity and explicit missingness indicators. Continuous covariates use fit-partition medians and categorical covariates fit-partition modes; no outcome-informed imputation or test-set tuning is allowed.

## Matched competing-risk models

Both models use exactly the same one-domain-at-o participants, covariates, origin, endpoint definitions, participant split, preprocessing, weights and locked test set. Both explicitly fit a death cause-specific hazard, so predicted five-year cumulative incidence is identifiable rather than inferred from an event-only Cox model.

Let d be the existing domain and b the absent new domain. There are up to six d-to-b second-domain routes plus death.

M0 is the current-level baseline: cause-specific Cox hazards for every supported d-to-b route and for death, using latest HbA1c, TG and HDL with fit-frozen restricted cubic splines, existing-domain identity, age, sex, latest BMI/BP/smoking/center, panel gap and missingness indicators. It contains no panel-change terms. Combine route-specific hazards by numerical integration/Aalen–Johansen to predict any second-domain five-year cumulative incidence. If a route has inadequate event support or separation under the locked support rule, the route-specific primary comparison is inconclusive; it must not silently collapse to a different estimand.

M1 has the identical route/death architecture and all M0 inputs, adding the three dated changes to the d-to-b hazards. A common change effect with prespecified existing-domain interactions is primary; fully route-specific change coefficients are reported only when the training/validation event-support rule permits them. To isolate incremental second-domain information, changes do not enter the primary death hazard; a change-in-death analysis is secondary and cannot establish the primary claim. M1 route predictions are aggregated to the same any-second-domain outcome as M0. Thus any primary performance difference tests dated change, not a hidden change in death modeling or endpoint route representation. Route coefficients and the joint change null are reported separately from aggregate prediction.

A secondary zero-domain-to-one analysis may use its own risk set and is never pooled into the primary 1-to-2 comparison.

## Split, uncertainty and evaluation

Use deterministic participant-level hash buckets: 0–59 fit, 60–69 model/threshold decisions, 70–79 locked evaluation, 80–99 reserved. Never split rows from one eid. Freeze codebook, transforms, splines, weights, route support decisions and thresholds before locked evaluation.

Report route and aggregate cause-specific coefficients, 95% intervals, five-year cause-specific and competing-risk cumulative incidence, time-dependent/integrated Brier score, discrimination, calibration intercept/slope/plots, RMST and paired 200-resample participant-bootstrap intervals for M1 minus M0. Preserve seeds, bootstrap indices, warnings and convergence. Evaluate at fixed 5%, 10% and 20% predicted five-year review-risk operating points; these are not validated policy cutoffs. The action is referral for additional metabolic/cardiovascular/kidney risk review, with no claim that referral improves outcomes. Report threshold crossings, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, number needed to review, review fraction and decision-curve net benefit.

Supportive evidence requires the joint change association to be directionally consistent with worsening and its uncertainty compatible with a non-null incremental effect; M1 to improve locked aggregate Brier, calibration or net benefit with a 95% interval favoring improvement; no material calibration harm by sex, age or existing-domain identity; and direction/performance to persist across washout, gap, repeat-weighting and competing-death sensitivities. Route heterogeneity is supportive only when route support is adequate and the relevant interval excludes zero. The appendicitis signal must be null or materially weaker, and within-state/sex/gap-bin permutations must not reproduce the result.

Adverse evidence is a null/reverse change association, no locked improvement or worsened calibration/net benefit, disappearance under participant splitting/washout/selection weighting, comparable appendicitis association, or permutation signal. This falsifies the incremental prognostic claim in this selected cohort, not biological harmlessness or causality. Inconclusive evidence includes inadequate route/event support, separation, poor positivity, code/date disagreement, too few complete panels/events, snapshot truncation, severe subgroup calibration failure, or intervals spanning meaningful benefit and no benefit. Policy value, threshold transport and clinical benefit remain inconclusive even if computation is supportive.

Computationally checkable claims include paths/hashes/headers, joins, code/date parsing, counts, timings, splits, route/death fits, predictions, uncertainty, calibration, Brier, decision curves, permutations and sensitivities. Chart adjudication, assay dictionaries, treatment/indication data, causal effects, external transport and intervention benefit require clinical review, additional data or another study.

## Alternative method and compute choice

The simple baseline is the route-matched current-level competing-risk Cox system M0. The selected substantive alternative is the route-matched transition model M1 because it tests whether dated within-person change adds information and can reveal which existing/new-domain routes carry that information; the baseline comparison no longer confounds this with route decomposition or death prediction.

A compact recurrent/temporal-attention model remains a matched, deferred alternative: exactly the two panel values and dates, latest covariates, gap, existing-domain state, same one-domain 1-to-2 target, competing-death rules, participant split, locked metrics and participant-bootstrap uncertainty. It could reveal nonlinear direction-by-domain interactions that M0/M1 splines lose, but two verified assay panels provide little sequence structure and extra capacity may amplify selective repeat measurement. No GPU/runtime was measured. Revisit only after three verified dated assay panels or a bounded preregistered diagnostic shows stable incremental value over M1; this is a scientific deferral, not a neural-network prohibition.

The planned primary fit is CPU-first tabular survival/multistate work under the solver envelope of at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM and 28,800 seconds. A rough unmeasured planning estimate is under 8 hours for route/death fits and 200 bootstrap evaluations on CPU; actual time, memory and event support are unverified. A future learned diagnostic would require an allocated GPU job (one A100, explicit cuda:0) and is not part of this proposal's completion. No discovery GPU probe is needed.
