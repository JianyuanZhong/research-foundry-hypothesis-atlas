# Outcome-blind support, calendar transport, and observation-selection audit for frozen P-beyond-B repeat-TACE HCC validation

## Unresolved question and substantive advance

In the frozen adult HCC repeat-TACE complete-triplet population, does the contemporaneous pre-repeat assay value `P` improve prediction of the subsequent same-encounter assay value `Y` beyond remote baseline `B` and the locked decision-time core when evaluated strictly forward in calendar time? The frozen estimand, event reconstruction, exact clocks, and non-overlapping annual test blocks are unchanged. The remaining material weakness is not model specification: later calendar arms are sparse, and the prior support gate was evaluated on complete observed triplets, so outcome observation could silently redefine the apparent transport population.

This successor adds an executable, outcome-blind support and selection audit before interpreting the existing held-out RMSE results. For every selected repeat event and each assay, it constructs a risk-set funnel using only event timing and availability of `B` and `P`: selected event -> `B` available -> `P` available with strict clock ordering -> outcome-blind `B/P` eligible. Only after this table is frozen is observed `Y` recorded separately. It reports calendar overlap from pre-`Y` covariates, transport-weight effective sample size, same-arm nearest-neighbor extrapolation, and outcome-observation fractions by arm and calendar block. These diagnostics are contextual gates and do not alter the frozen complete-triplet P-beyond-B estimand or add a treatment estimand.

The strongest claim supported by the predecessor is limited: in the adequately supported 2022 forward block, adding `P` improved held-out albumin and bilirubin prediction; later point estimates are sparse and cannot establish reproducible transport. The unresolved claim is whether those gains transport to future calendar mixtures with adequate outcome-blind support and nonselective outcome observation. A favorable complete-triplet estimate without support and observation diagnostics is insufficient evidence for that claim.

The systemic-record/comparator label remains a prespecified covariate and composition diagnostic only. No exchangeability, treatment effect, treatment response, toxicity, safety, utility, actionability, or patient-benefit claim is made. Conditioning on repeat TACE and an observed complete triplet remains acknowledged selection conditioning.

For each assay, the existing locked models remain:

```

       + TACE2-to-repeat days + P-lead hours
M1: M0 + P
```

Training-only age median imputation, age-missingness indicator, sex coding, variance filtering, centering/scaling, inner leave-one-patient-out alpha choice over `[0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100]`, and ridge fitting are unchanged. The primary estimand remains assay-specific standardized held-out RMSE gain, `[RMSE(M0)−RMSE(M1)]/SD_test(Y)`, with paired arm-stratified patient-frequency bootstrap intervals conditional on fixed pre-origin fits. MAE, bias, arm-specific errors, drift, and influence remain secondary diagnostics. No support diagnostic changes the fitted rows, endpoint, model, or estimand.

## Outcome-blind support and selection diagnostics

For each assay and origin, first create an event-level risk-set table from the complete reconstructed event population. Before reading or selecting `Y`, define:

1. `has_B`: valid `B` exists under the frozen B clock.
2. `has_P`: valid `P` exists under the frozen P clock.
3. `BP_eligible`: `has_B`, `has_P`, strict `B_time < P_time < event_time`, positive P lead, and no future outcome information.
4. `Y_observed`: valid post-event assay exists under the frozen Y window.
5. `complete`: `BP_eligible` and `Y_observed`; this is exactly the frozen analysis population, not a newly defined estimand.

Report the funnel overall and separately for systemic and comparator records in every training and future block. The outcome-blind support gate requires at least 20 BP-eligible episodes total and at least 10 in each descriptive arm in both pre-origin training and future test. The complete-triplet analysis gate is reported separately with the same thresholds and remains the gate for interpreting RMSE. A block failing either arm-support requirement is descriptive regardless of a favorable point estimate or interval.

For calendar transport, use only pre-`Y` variables (`B`, `P`, age, sex, P-lead hours, TACE2-to-repeat days, and the prespecified arm label) to fit a standardized logistic calendar-membership model among BP-eligible training and test episodes. Report future-membership propensity range relative to the training range, fraction of future episodes outside the training range, normalized training transport-weight effective sample size, maximum normalized weight, and arm-specific ESS. These are diagnostics, not weights used for the primary estimator. Also report same-arm nearest-neighbor distances in the standardized pre-`Y` feature space, using the 95th percentile leave-one-training-neighbor distance as an outcome-blind extrapolation threshold.

For observation/selection, report complete-Y observation fraction among BP-eligible episodes with Wilson 95% intervals overall and by arm. Fit a training-only, pre-origin logistic model for `Y_observed` from the same pre-`Y` variables and apply it to future BP-eligible episodes. Report predicted probability range, observed fraction, Brier score, and inverse-observation-probability ESS among observed cases solely as a selection diagnostic. Do not reweight the frozen complete-triplet RMSE, impute Y, or claim missing-at-random. Also report standardized mean differences between future episodes with and without observed Y for B, P, P-lead, timing, and age when estimable.

Pre-specified interpretation thresholds are descriptive warnings rather than hidden post hoc exclusion rules: flag calendar support when any future propensity lies outside the training range, any arm-specific transport ESS is below 10, or more than 5% of same-arm future episodes exceed the training nearest-neighbor 95th percentile; flag observation selection when future Y-observation is below 80%, its Wilson interval is clearly lower than the pre-origin rate, observation ESS is below 10, or any pre-Y observed-versus-missing SMD has absolute value at least 0.5. These flags never turn a sparse block into evidence or remove it from the frozen analysis.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence is restricted to a block that passes reconstruction, clocks, outcome-blind BP support, complete-triplet support, and the locked RMSE criteria, with no material adverse arm diagnostic or selection warning. Under this executed audit, the 2022 block remains supportive for incremental short-horizon prediction in that selected calendar mixture only. It does not support reproducible multi-era transport.

An adverse result is a gated interval at or below zero, repeated M1 degradation in adequately supported blocks, or a material adverse arm diagnostic under adequate support. This would challenge incremental prediction in the tested selected population, not prove that B alone is sufficient or establish a mechanism. A selection adverse result is a large outcome-observation collapse, low selection ESS, or large pre-Y observed-versus-missing imbalance; it invalidates transport interpretation even if the complete-triplet RMSE is favorable.

An inconclusive result includes sparse arms, failed outcome-blind support, low Y observation, influential episodes, calendar extrapolation, denominator or clock drift, or missing assay units. The executed findings make reproducible transport inconclusive: only 2022 is arm-supported, 2023 and 2024 have severe outcome-selection loss, and 2024 systemic complete-Y support is one episode.
