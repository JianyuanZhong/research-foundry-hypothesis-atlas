# Provisional hypothesis: duration of hypotension and subsequent AKI in eICU

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in eICU with a recorded nadir blood pressure below 65 mmHg, a longer cumulative duration of hypotension during the first 24 ICU hours is associated with a higher risk of new or worsening acute kidney injury in the next 48 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The relationship is expected to be stronger than the association using nadir pressure alone, but this is a hypothesis, not an established result.

## Why it matters

A minimum blood pressure is a single, potentially transient measurement and may not distinguish a brief dip from sustained circulatory compromise. If duration adds clinically meaningful information, it could improve early risk stratification and motivate prospective evaluation of resuscitation and monitoring strategies. It would not establish that changing blood pressure causes AKI or that a threshold is a treatment target.

## Falsifiable test

Restrict to adults with a documented ICU admission and at least one blood pressure below 65 mmHg in the first 24 hours. Define hypotension burden as the cumulative time below 65 mmHg using prespecified interpolation rules and sensitivity analyses. Define AKI as a new KDIGO-compatible creatinine rise or urine-output criterion in the following 48 hours where creatinine timing permits. Compare a simple nadir-only model with a duration-adjusted model, stratified by hospital, with uncertainty intervals and held-out-hospital evaluation. The hypothesis is supported if duration adds reproducible information and the association is directionally consistent across hospitals; it is adverse if duration adds no information or reverses after prespecified adjustment; it is inconclusive if creatinine coverage, timing, or sampling artifacts prevent a reliable comparison.
