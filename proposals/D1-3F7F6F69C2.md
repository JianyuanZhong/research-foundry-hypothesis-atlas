# Hypotension Duration and AKI: A Falsifiable eICU Study

## Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

The unresolved question is whether, at the same minimum MAP, longer duration of hypotension (MAP < 65 mmHg) predicts subsequent AKI (KDIGO stage >= 2) in eICU patients. This matters because hypotension is a common ICU finding, and the duration of hypotension may be a more informative risk factor than minimum MAP alone. If confirmed, this could inform monitoring and resuscitation strategies.

The closest relevant prior work inspected includes:
- [K1] Lehman et al. (2010) "Hypotension as a Risk Factor for Acute Kidney Injury in ICU Patients." This study used the MIMIC II database to show that both severity and duration of hypotension are associated with AKI risk. The risk of AKI increased with each additional hour MAP was below 70, 60, or 50 mmHg.
- [K2] Shang et al. (2026) "Electronic Phenotype for Detection, Staging, and Subtyping of Acute Kidney Injury." This study developed and validated a pragmatic electronic phenotype for AKI diagnosis, staging, and subtyping using EHR data. The AKI e-phenotype had a positive predictive value of 95.4% and was reproducible in diverse hospital datasets.
- [K3] Fage et al. (2026) "Renal Resistive Index Responsiveness to a Mean Arterial Pressure Test As a Guide for Mean Arterial Pressure Target in the Early Phase of Septic Shock." This study investigated the physiologic impact of a higher MAP on renal function in early septic shock. Among RRI responders, the higher MAP target was not associated with improved renal outcomes.

## Study Population and Data Bindings

**Population:** Adult eICU patients with continuous MAP recordings and at least one serum creatinine measurement.

**Data bindings (eICU):**
- Vitals: eicu_crd_vital (patient_id, timestamp, mean_arterial_pressure)
- Labs: eicu_crd_lab (patient_id, timestamp, item_id, value) - serum creatinine
- Medications: eicu_crd_medication (patient_id, timestamp, medication_name) - vasopressors
- Demographics: eicu_crd_patient (patient_id, age, gender)
- Outcomes: eicu_crd_disposition (patient_id, discharge_status)

## Hypothesis

At the same minimum MAP, patients with longer duration of hypotension (MAP < 65 mmHg) have higher risk of subsequent AKI (KDIGO stage >= 2).

## Experiment Design

**Temporal boundaries:**
- Exposure window: First 24 hours of ICU stay
- Outcome window: 48 hours after the exposure window

**Variables:**
- Primary predictor: Hypotension duration (hours MAP < 65 mmHg)
- Covariates: Minimum MAP, age, gender, vasopressor use
- Outcome: AKI (KDIGO stage >= 2) based on serum creatinine

**Analysis:**
1. Simple baseline: Logistic regression with hypotension duration as the primary predictor, adjusted for minimum MAP, age, gender, and vasopressor use
2. Learned alternative: Gradient boosting with time-series features (MAP trajectory, vasopressor exposure)

**Falsification criteria:**
- If hypotension duration is not significantly associated with AKI after adjustment for minimum MAP, the hypothesis is not supported
- If the association is not consistent across hospitals, the hypothesis is not generalizable

## Method Comparison

- **Simple baseline:** Time-weighted hypotension burden (hours MAP < 65) with logistic regression
- **Learned alternative:** Gradient boosting with time-series features (MAP trajectory, vasopressor exposure)

The learned alternative could reveal non-linear relationships and interactions that the simple baseline would miss. However, the simple baseline is more interpretable and easier to validate.
