# Scientific Proposal: Post-Infection Proteomic Recovery Trajectories Predict Long-term Organ Outcomes

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question**: Do distinct temporal proteomic recovery trajectories following hospitalization for infection predict long-term cardiovascular and renal outcomes, and do these trajectories differ based on the organ system most severely affected during the acute infection?

**Secondary Question**: Can machine learning models identify proteomic recovery "phenotypes" that stratify patients by risk for long-term organ dysfunction, outperforming traditional clinical risk scores?

## Clinical Importance

### 1. Early Identification of High-Risk Patients
- Current post-infection care relies heavily on clinical scores (SOFA, APACHE) that may not capture individual recovery trajectories
- Identifying patients with aberrant proteomic recovery could enable:
  - Early intervention strategies to prevent long-term organ damage
  - Targeted monitoring and follow-up
  - Personalized rehabilitation programs

### 2. Understanding Recovery Mechanisms
- Distinct proteomic recovery trajectories may reflect different biological pathways:
  - **Pro-inflammatory phenotype**: Sustained elevation of inflammatory markers
  - **Pro-coagulant phenotype**: Persistent coagulation activation
  - **Immunomodulatory phenotype**: Dysregulated immune response
  - **Metabolic phenotype**: Metabolic dysfunction during recovery

### 3. Bridging Acute and Chronic Care
- Most clinical research focuses on either acute infection management OR chronic disease prevention
- This study bridges the gap by examining the transition from acute infection to long-term organ outcomes

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

### Closest Inspected Works

1. **Expert seed UKB-01** ("Infection onset versus progression to severe disease"):
   - Examines pre-illness proteomic profiles predicting infection onset vs. organ damage
   - Does NOT address recovery trajectories after infection
   - Does NOT examine long-term outcomes beyond the index hospitalization

2. **Expert seed MIMIC-01** ("Asynchronous organ recovery"):
   - Examines timing of organ-specific recovery after critical illness
   - Focuses on clinical variables (lab values, imaging)
   - Does NOT incorporate proteomic biomarkers

3. **Expert seed UKB-06** ("Cardiovascular risk after infection"):
   - Examines pre-illness proteomics predicting post-infection cardiovascular risk
   - Does NOT examine recovery trajectories
   - Does NOT examine organ-specific recovery patterns

### Gap Addressed
The existing literature and expert seeds address:
- Pre-illness risk factors for infection
- Pre-illness predictors of infection severity
- Post-infection long-term risk for specific outcomes

**What remains unexamined**:
- **Temporal recovery trajectories** of proteomic profiles after infection
- How recovery trajectories differ by **organ system involvement** during acute infection
- Whether proteomic recovery phenotypes predict **long-term organ-specific outcomes**
- Whether machine learning can identify recovery trajectories better than traditional clinical scores

### Substantive Advance
This proposal explicitly:
1. Examines **temporal dynamics** of proteomic recovery (not just baseline or final states)
2. Tests whether recovery trajectories differ by **organ system** most affected during infection
3. Predicts **long-term outcomes** (cardiovascular, renal, mortality)
4. Compares proteomic recovery phenotypes against traditional clinical risk scores
5. Uses machine learning to identify optimal recovery trajectory features

## Study Design

### Phase 1: Data Preparation and Recovery Trajectory Definition

#### 1.1 Identify Study Population
- Filter participants with infection hospitalization
- Filter participants with proteomic measurements
- Filter participants with ≥5 years follow-up
- Filter participants without prior infection at baseline

#### 1.2 Outcome Definition
- **Index hospitalization**: First hospitalization for infection (any severity)
- **Organ involvement**: Identify organ system(s) most severely affected during index hospitalization
- **Long-term outcomes** (≥5 years post-infection):
  - Cardiovascular events (MI, stroke, heart failure)
  - Renal events (CKD progression, dialysis)
  - All-cause mortality
  - Composite of major adverse events

#### 1.3 Recovery Trajectory Definition
- **Time points**: Baseline (pre-infection), 7 days, 30 days, 90 days, 365 days post-infection
- **Proteomic features**: All proteomic columns (3-0.0 through 1883-3.0)
- **Trajectory**: Time series of proteomic profiles at each time point
- **Trajectory features**:
  - Rate of change (slope) between time points
  - Acceleration (change in slope)
  - Variability (standard deviation of changes)
  - Area under curve (AUC)
  - Specific protein trajectories

### Phase 2: Statistical Analysis

#### 2.1 Baseline Approach: Traditional Clinical Risk Scores
- Use established clinical scores (e.g., Framingham, RECKON)
- Test whether proteomic baseline adds value

#### 2.2 Advanced Approach: Machine Learning with Recovery Trajectories
- Define trajectory features (slope, acceleration, variability)
- Train Random Forest, XGBoost, or LSTM models
- Compare performance against traditional clinical scores
- Use SHAP values to identify important features

#### 2.3 Organ-Specific Analysis
- Stratify by organ system most affected during acute infection
- Test interaction effects
- Identify organ-specific recovery signatures

### Phase 3: Sensitivity Analyses

#### 3.1 Different Time Windows
- Short-term recovery (0-90 days)
- Medium-term recovery (0-365 days)
- Long-term recovery (0-1825 days)

#### 3.2 Different Proteomic Panels
- All proteins
- Top proteins only
- Pathway-restricted proteins

#### 3.3 Different Machine Learning Methods
- Tree-based: Random Forest, XGBoost
- Neural networks: LSTM, Transformer
- Compare performance

## Falsification Criteria

### Hypothesis Supported
- Distinct recovery phenotypes predict long-term outcomes
- Recovery trajectories differ by organ system
- Machine learning outperforms traditional scores

### Hypothesis Refuted
- No predictive value beyond traditional scores
- No organ-specificity in recovery patterns
- Results disappear after confounding adjustment

### Inconclusive Results
- Intermediate predictive value
- Mixed organ-specificity
- Partial biological plausibility
