# Residual multiorgan instability at ICU discharge and near-term in-hospital deterioration

Status: repaired Harbor proposal; observational prognostic experiment, not a treatment-effect study.

Parents: `[prior hypothesis]` (residual instability before ICU discharge), `[prior hypothesis]` (discordant AKI recovery), `[prior hypothesis]` (persistent sedation), `[prior hypothesis]` (diuretic timing).

## Clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

Among patients discharged alive from an ICU during a hospital admission, does the pattern of residual physiology in the last 24 hours before ICU discharge identify patients at high risk of first ICU readmission or hospital death in the next 48 hours, beyond the latest observed values and routine discharge context?

Primary hypothesis: a pre-discharge phenotype combining persistent oxygen/ventilatory support, hemodynamic variability or vasoactive support, and discordant kidney recovery (creatinine trajectory not accompanied by urine output) has higher 48-hour cumulative incidence of the first adverse event than patients with similar terminal values but stable trajectories.

The substantive question is incremental clinical information, not whether a more complex model predicts slightly better. The learned model is useful only if the trajectory/discordance block improves calibrated competing-risk predictions and the improvement disappears when temporal order is destroyed or that block is removed.

## Evidence-supported versus untested claims

The available local evidence supports only that MIMIC-IV 3.1 contains timestamped ICU stays, ICU charted physiology, laboratory values, inputs, outputs, procedures, hospital admission/discharge/death fields, and an item dictionary that can bind the variables below. The expert seed identifies residual instability and competing-risk concerns; it does not establish the hypothesis. The parent AKI seed explicitly cautions that measured GFR/cystatin C is unavailable. No result from this proposed experiment has been computed.

The experiment can support an association/prediction claim about observed same-hospital outcomes among ICU survivors. It cannot establish that instability causes readmission or death, that lighter sedation or earlier diuresis would improve outcomes, that a creatinine fall represents renal recovery, or that the phenotype is valid outside this MIMIC snapshot. Clinical adjudication would be required to distinguish treatment limitation, planned ICU return, procedure-related readmission, indication for sedation/vasopressors, true kidney recovery, and clinically appropriate discharge.

## Population, index, and temporal boundaries

Use the MIMIC-IV 3.1 snapshot and one index ICU stay per hospital admission: the last ICU stay ending before hospital discharge is eligible if its `outtime` is observed and occurs while the patient is alive.

Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `subject_id, hadm_id`. Exclude an index stay if `admissions.deathtime` is non-null and is at or before `icustays.outtime`; do not use later ICU stays to define the index population. Patients may contribute at most one index stay per `hadm_id`; if multiple admissions are present, retain distinct admissions but split by `subject_id`, never randomly by stay.

Time zero is the index ICU `outtime), not the first appearance of a candidate feature and not a discharge order. Build features only from records with event time strictly before `outtime), primarily `[outtime - 24 hours, outtime)`. A value with `storetime` after `outtime` is not eligible even if its `charttime` is earlier; use the event/measurement time for clinical availability and record `storetime` only for a leakage audit. No post-`outtime` value, subsequent ICU stay, discharge disposition, or outcome field may enter predictors.

Outcome follow-up is `[outtime, min(outtime + 48 hours, admissions.dischtime)]`, ending earlier at hospital discharge. Define the first event:

1. ICU readmission: a later `icu/icustays` row with the same `subject_id, hadm_id` and `intime > outtime` before the follow-up end.
2. Hospital death: `hosp/admissions.deathtime` in the follow-up interval and before any qualifying ICU readmission.
3. Censoring: hospital discharge alive before 48 hours without either event, or the 48-hour administrative endpoint.

Readmission and death are competing first events. Report separate cause-specific cumulative incidences and the composite, with death before readmission never counted as a readmission. Do not call a post-ICU event a causal “failure of discharge.” Planned versus unplanned ICU return cannot be reliably adjudicated from these tables; this is an explicit limitation and sensitivity analysis should report all returns.

The 48-hour window is intentional: same-admission readmission and death are observable without pretending that MIMIC provides post-hospital readmission surveillance. A secondary descriptive analysis may extend to 7 days subject to the same hospital-discharge censoring, but it is not the primary claim.

Kidney discordance is operational, not a diagnosis. For each stay, calculate within the final 24 hours: latest creatinine, slope from the first to last valid creatinine when at least two measurements are available, latest 6-hour urine-output rate, 24-hour urine-output rate, and whether a creatinine decrease co-occurs with low/unchanged urine output. Use prespecified continuous values and missingness indicators in the primary model; display a descriptive binary discordance phenotype only after determining thresholds from a training fold (no outcome-informed cutoffs). There is no cystatin C, measured GFR, renal blood-flow, or reliable indication for diuresis in these bindings.

The residual instability block contains oxygen burden (latest and 24-hour maximum FiO2/O2 flow, support-procedure overlap), hemodynamic burden (latest and minimum MAP, HR variability, vasoactive overlap), kidney discordance, and measurement density. The latter is reported separately because selective testing and charting can create an apparent signal.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods answer the same 48-hour competing-risk prediction question and use the same patient-level split.

Transparent baseline: two cause-specific penalized Cox models (readmission and death) or an equivalent discrete-time cause-specific logistic hazard model with only prespecified terminal/summary variables: age/sex, index ICU length of stay, admission type, discharge care-unit context, latest HR/MAP/SpO2/FiO2/creatinine, latest urine-output rate, current vasoactive/ventilatory support, and indicator variables for whether each value was observed. No trajectory slope, within-window variability, or measurement counts enter the baseline except the required missingness indicators. Use the training fold only for imputation, scaling, and any regularization. Generate 48-hour cumulative incidences with the competing-risk survival calculation.

Scientifically substantive learned alternative: CPU HistGradientBoosting discrete-time competing-risk hazards, one event-specific classifier per 6-hour bin (0–6, 6–12, 12–24, 24–48 hours), using the same baseline variables plus the full prespecified 6-hour sequence of physiology/support summaries: latest, median, minimum/maximum, slope, variability, number of valid observations, oxygen-support overlap, vasoactive exposure overlap, urine-output rates, creatinine change, and cross-organ discordance indicators. No post-time-zero value is allowed. The alternative is learned because it estimates nonlinear interactions and time-order patterns; it is scientifically informative because it tests whether asynchronous recovery carries information lost by terminal values. Use shallow bounded trees, fixed seeds, no outcome-driven feature search, and class-weighting only within training data if required.

Preselect the baseline as the decision comparator because it is auditable and plausibly implementable at the bedside. Retain the learned alternative because it tests the specific scientific uncertainty—trajectory/asynchrony versus terminal snapshot—not merely model size. A neural sequence model is deferred: it could represent irregular event ordering, but the available question can be answered with 6-hour summaries, and a larger model would add engineering/overfitting risk without adding a distinct clinical estimand. This is a deferral, not a blanket rejection of neural methods.

Split by `subject_id` using the catalog’s participant-level SHA-256 partition convention; use only accessible discovery buckets 0–79 and an 80/20 patient-level train/test split within them, with 5-fold patient-level cross-validation inside training for tuning. The reserved buckets 80–99 are not available and are not an external validation set. No stay from a test subject can occur in training. Freeze feature definitions, event labels, seeds, and hyperparameters before test evaluation.

## Analysis and uncertainty

Primary estimands are the 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU readmission and first hospital death, plus their composite, conditional on being alive at index ICU outtime and under the observed hospitalization/censoring process. Report absolute risk calibration (calibration intercept/slope and observed-versus-predicted cumulative incidence), integrated Brier score for the competing-risk outcome, cause-specific discrimination with confidence intervals, and decision curves over prespecified 5%, 10%, and 20% risk thresholds. The primary comparison is paired test-subject difference in Brier score and calibration, with 95% subject-level bootstrap confidence intervals stratified by event type; uncertainty is not replaced by a single AUC.

Pre-specified ablations:

- baseline plus terminal values versus baseline plus trajectory summaries;
- trajectory summaries with cross-organ discordance removed;
- learned features with temporal order permuted within patient while preserving each feature’s marginal distribution and missingness;
- learned features with measurement-density variables removed;
- complete-case analysis versus missingness-indicator analysis.

Support for the hypothesis requires: (a) adequate event and feature coverage documented before model fitting; (b) reproducible improvement in calibration/Brier score for the learned model over the baseline; (c) the improvement materially attenuates in the temporal-order permutation and discordance-removal ablations; and (d) the direction is not explained solely by measurement density or discharge destination. A positive association in a descriptive discordance analysis alone is insufficient.

The claim is falsified for this dataset/estimand if the baseline and learned model have indistinguishable test-set calibration and Brier uncertainty, if trajectory/order ablations preserve the claimed gain, or if any apparent gain disappears after removing chart-density proxies. An adverse result—terminal values outperform trajectories—would be clinically useful by arguing against adding a complex trajectory display for this decision. An inconclusive result—low event support, wide intervals, missingness dominating, or unstable results across folds—means the question remains unresolved, not that residual instability is safe.

## Leakage, immortal-time, and competing-risk safeguards

- All predictors satisfy `charttime/starttime/endtime < outtime`; `storetime` is checked for delayed availability and never used to rescue a late chart.
- The index is defined before outcome follow-up. No patient must survive an exposure window to enter: every eligible alive ICU discharge enters at `outtime`, including those with sparse measurements, represented with missingness flags.
- The model never uses later ICU-stay rows, discharge destination, hospital-expire flag as a predictor, or variables derived from the future event.
- Readmission and death are mutually exclusive first events; death is not censored as if it could later readmit. Hospital discharge before 48 hours is explicit censoring.
- Planned/unplanned return, treatment limitation, and clinical indication are not inferred from a code. If a QA script silently treats a missing urine record as zero, invalidates timestamps, or reports a causal effect, the submission fails verification.

## Actual scientific deliverable

The deliverable is a frozen, newly fitted pair of competing-risk prediction systems and their test-set estimates: (1) baseline and learned 48-hour cumulative-incidence predictions; (2) calibration/Brier/discrimination and bootstrap uncertainty; (3) trajectory, discordance, temporal-order, and measurement-density ablations; (4) cohort-flow and feature-coverage tables; and (5) a conclusion that is explicitly supportive, adverse, or inconclusive under the criteria above. The final report must link every conclusion to computed output files and state which claims remain clinical-adjudication or external-validation questions.

## Compute and feasibility

Parsing the selected CSV.gz members from the 10.55-GB archive is the main cost. Budget up to 2 CPU cores, 4 GiB RAM, and 1,800 seconds for feature extraction/support audit; store only derived patient/stay-level feature tables and model outputs in the workspace. Fit the baseline and bounded HistGradientBoosting hazards within 1,800 additional CPU seconds, with checkpoints for feature tables and fold predictions. No GPU is required; CPU choice follows the bounded tabular question and auditability, not a prohibition on learned models. If event counts or coverage fail the predeclared minimum (at least 100 first readmissions and 100 first deaths in the accessible cohort, or fewer with an explicit precision audit), stop before test claims and report a feasibility failure.

## Limitations and next study

The data lack measured GFR/cystatin C, blood gas-derived oxygenation in a universally observed form, microcirculation, post-discharge readmission, treatment intent, sedation indication, goals-of-care, and clinician adjudication of planned returns. The phenotype therefore means “observed residual support/trajectory pattern,” not organ recovery, instability mechanism, or modifiable risk. Positive prognostic results motivate temporal validation in another ICU cohort and expert review of return/readmission labels; they do not justify changing discharge policy, sedation, fluids, or diuretics without a prospective study.

## Branch decisions

The residual-instability branch is selected because it has a fixed, observable decision time and directly measurable competing outcomes. The discordant-AKI branch is incorporated as one prespecified feature block but cannot claim renal recovery. Persistent sedation is not selected as the primary question because sedation scores and drug administration are selectively charted and indication is not observed; it remains a sensitivity/context feature only. Diuretic timing is deferred as a causal question: medication timing is observable, but confounding by indication and time-varying treatment decisions cannot be repaired by this prognostic design. A causal diuretic or sedation claim would require a separate target-trial design, richer indication data, and clinical review.
