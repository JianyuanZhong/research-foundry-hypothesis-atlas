## Unresolved clinical question and substantive advance

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that metabolic measurements and longitudinal disease histories are clinically associated with future disease burden, and that time-aware models can represent disease ordering and competing events. I read the locally available natural-history demonstration, which reports that longitudinal disease history can predict future disease rates and emphasizes calibration, temporal dependence and bias; I read the Bayesian demonstration, which emphasizes an explicit longitudinal likelihood and selection correction. These demonstrations do not establish the HbA1c hypothesis in this UKB export. The cancer demonstration’s main article and STAR Methods remain unavailable; only its supplement was inspected, so no claim about its complete method is imported.

The UKB expert seed asks whether deterioration in metabolic measures adds information beyond their current level. The unresolved, decision-relevant claim is narrower:

> Among UKB participants who have a valid paired repeat HbA1c, remain alive and free of inpatient-coded type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease and heart failure at the repeat landmark, does prior HbA1c history distinguish 5-year risk of developing a second distinct disease family even when current HbA1c is the same?

This advances a static association study by testing whether history dependence is clinically material after the current HbA1c is known. It also explicitly separates this question from the different claim that increasing HbA1c at a fixed baseline is harmful. The result could inform whether a repeat HbA1c should be interpreted in context of a prior value when discussing cardiometabolic multimorbidity. It cannot establish that ordering repeat HbA1c or acting on it improves outcomes.

Primary hypothesis: at the same current HbA1c, a 0.5 percentage-point lower prior HbA1c (therefore a larger observed rise) is associated with a higher 5-year risk of the second distinct disease family among type 2 diabetes, CKD and heart failure. The hypothesis is two-sided for falsification: a clinically material opposite contrast would refute the expected direction while supporting history dependence; a precise null would refute the added-history claim.

## Population, exposure and temporal boundaries

Use one row per participant and horizontal one-to-one `eid` joins. Never append UKB tables as additional observations.

- Baseline assessment is instance 0 with date `53-0.0`. Require valid baseline age `21022-0.0`, sex `31-0.0`, baseline HbA1c `30750-0.0`, and prespecified baseline covariates.
- Repeat assessment is instance 1 with date `53-1.0`; require `53-1.0 > 53-0.0` and valid repeat HbA1c `30750-1.0`. Require an auditable field-level verification that biological-sample instances 0 and 1 correspond to those assessment dates. Field-instance indices are not assumed to be elapsed time.
- The landmark is the repeat assessment date. No outcome follow-up before that date is included. Participants who develop a component disease or die between baseline and repeat are selection losses, not eligible follow-up.
- Exclude any participant with an aligned qualifying component diagnosis or death on or before the repeat date. The target population is therefore the repeat-observed, disease-free landmark population, not all UKB participants.
- Exposure variables are current HbA1c (C), baseline HbA1c (B), and change (C-B), after field-specific unit verification and documented conversion. The primary contrast is history at the same current value: (B=C-0.5) versus (B=C), standardized over observed current HbA1c and baseline covariates and restricted to common empirical support. The secondary contrast is deterioration at fixed baseline: (C=B+0.5) versus (C=B). Do not call the primary contrast a causal effect of deterioration.
- Follow from the repeat date to the second-family event, death, or administrative end 2023-03-31. Death before the second-family event is a competing event. A prespecified one-year post-landmark washout and a baseline-to-repeat interval restriction of 1–6 years are reverse-causation sensitivities.

## Outcome construction

In `health_outcomes`, pair every `41270-j` inpatient ICD-10 code with `41280-j` from the same index (j). Do not match codes to dates after sorting or across indices.

Define the earliest valid post-landmark date for each disease family:

- type 2 diabetes: ICD-10 E11–E14;
- chronic kidney disease: N18.x;
- heart failure: I50.x.

The primary event is the second distinct family: the later of the first post-landmark dates of the first two distinct families. Distinct families occurring on the same date count as a second-family event. A code without its aligned date, or an invalid/unresolved date, is an ascertainment failure rather than evidence of no disease.

Secondary outcomes are first occurrence of each component, first any component, all-cause death, and an expanded second-family endpoint adding coronary heart disease I20–I25 and stroke I60–I69. Secondary outcomes do not replace the primary endpoint if it is infeasible. Report family identity, same-date ties, competing deaths and administrative censoring.

## Analysis, baselines and uncertainty

First emit an auditable flow report containing source row counts, `eid` uniqueness and overlap agreement, valid baseline/repeat dates, biomarker missingness and nonnumeric/negative/out-of-range counts, repeat interval distribution, date-mapping verification, pre-landmark disease/death exclusions, aligned code/date failures, family order and ties, follow-up, events, competing deaths, and a comparison of repeat-observed versus no-valid-repeat baseline participants.

Primary model: cause-specific hazards for the second-family event with death as a competing event, using a prespecified flexible joint surface in current (C) and baseline (B) HbA1c plus age spline, sex, BMI, systolic/diastolic BP, and verified baseline renal covariates. Obtain standardized 5-year cumulative incidence with Aalen–Johansen; report absolute risk difference, risk ratio, cause-specific hazard ratio, cumulative-incidence curves and participant-bootstrap 95% intervals. Fine–Gray is sensitivity only.

Fit nested models in the same landmark cohort:

- M0: current HbA1c (C) plus baseline covariates.
- M1: current HbA1c (C), baseline/history surface (B,C), plus baseline covariates.
- M2: baseline (B), change (C-B), plus baseline covariates, using identical complexity.

Do not fit unrestricted current, baseline and change terms together because (C=B+(C-B)) is algebraically redundant. The primary standardized history contrast is generated only within observed common support; the fixed-baseline deterioration contrast is separate. Report calibration slope/intercept, Brier score and optimism-corrected incremental performance over M0. AUC or small machine-learning gains are descriptive, not evidence of clinical utility. A regularized spline or shallow gradient-boosting model may be a bounded sensitivity on the same cohort, with all tuning inside resampling partitions. Multiple imputation, if needed, is inside bootstrap partitions. Repeat-selection inverse-probability weighting is a sensitivity, with prespecified truncation, positivity and effective sample size.

## Falsification and interpretation

Set a research materiality threshold before fitting: an absolute 5-year risk difference of 2 percentage points. This is not a validated treatment threshold.

Support for the expected positive history contrast requires every hard data gate to pass; at least 100 primary second-family events overall and 10 per displayed contrast stratum; common-support coverage; a positive history contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero and point estimate at least 2 points; broadly consistent direction after washout, interval restriction, competing-risk and repeat-weighted analyses; calibrated M1; and no equally large association for the fixed negative-control inpatient appendicitis endpoint K35.x, whose event count must be reported.

A positive history contrast supports an inpatient-code-defined prognostic association in the selected landmark population. It does not show that HbA1c testing or management caused benefit. A material opposite contrast is adverse to the expected direction but supports history dependence. A null with a narrow interval excluding the 2-point materiality threshold supports current-level sufficiency for this endpoint in this selected population. A current-level association with no history increment supports static risk association but falsifies the added-history claim. Disappearance after washout suggests reverse causation; disappearance after repeat weighting suggests measured selection.

Results are inconclusive if a hard field/date/code/death gate fails; the primary endpoint has fewer than 100 events, fewer than 10 events in a displayed stratum, or sparse negative-control events; support is extrapolative; weights are unstable; or uncertainty spans trivial and clinically material effects. A secondary endpoint cannot rescue an infeasible primary hypothesis.

Computation can establish field availability, filtering, temporal order, inpatient-code-defined disease-family events, disease order, competing-risk estimates, calibration, prediction comparisons and prespecified gate status. It cannot establish outpatient disease, adjudicated diagnoses, disease severity, treatment effects, causal benefit, representativeness, measured GFR, or clinical utility. Those claims require validated dictionaries and dates, outpatient laboratory/medication data, clinical adjudication, external validation and a prospective testing-and-management study.
