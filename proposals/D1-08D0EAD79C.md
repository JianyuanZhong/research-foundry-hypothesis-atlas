## Clinical question, evidence boundary, and advance

After resuscitation, clinicians commonly face an invasively ventilated patient with marked positive balance who is no longer receiving vasopressors. Initiating a loop diuretic may accelerate a favorable hospital transition, but may also precipitate hypotension, electrolyte disturbance, renal deterioration, or renewed organ support. Fluid accumulation is associated with poor outcomes, but that association does not establish benefit from active removal.

The strongest claim supported by the evidence inspected for this episode is narrower. McMullan et al., *Critical Care Explorations* 2026;8:e1404, DOI `10.1097/CCE.0000000000001404`, frozen full-text source `[source checksum]`, reports no detected between-arm differences in lactate, AKIRisk, urinary cystatin-C, or vascular-injury biomarkers in a modest secondary analysis of the randomized RADAR-2 conservative-fluid/active-deresuscitation strategy. The study was hypothesis-generating and clinically important effects remained possible. It evaluates a bundle, not isolated loop initiation at this landmark, and does not establish patient benefit or renal safety.

Boyle et al., *Am J Respir Crit Care Med* 2026, DOI `10.1093/ajrccm/aamag238`, frozen full-text source `[source checksum]`, explains why death and discharge are competing events and why state-based reporting avoids collapsing unlike failure modes. It does not validate administrative discharge as functional recovery. Wang et al., *NPJ Digital Medicine* 2026, DOI `10.1038/s41746-026-02563-z`, frozen XML `[source checksum]`, distinguishes a conceptual target trial from the trial realizable in healthcare-generated EHR data. It supports explicit data-constrained estimands; it does not establish exchangeability.

The unresolved falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In the measured-overlap population of invasively ventilated adults at ICU hour 48 who have recovered from vasopressor-treated shock, have cumulative balance at least +50 mL/kg, retained urine output, and potassium at least 3.0 mmol/L, assignment to initiate an actually delivered ICU IV loop within the next 24 hours increases alive index-hospital discharge by day 28 to a recorded non-hospice, non-acute-hospital destination (ANHATD28) by more than 5 percentage points versus no loop by any route during that window, without a definitively adverse alive-durable-liberation signal, excess recorded day-28 mortality, or excess observed post-landmark RRT use by day 7.

A supportive adjusted result would justify external validation and inform the design of a randomized trial. It would not justify bedside loop initiation. The substantive advance is a treatment- and outcome-coherent estimate for a specific decision, with discharge destinations, death, respiratory observation, and RRT provenance kept separate rather than treating fluid removal or leaving hospital as recovery.

## Falsification and interpretation

Magnitude status:

- **Supported** only if the lower 95% confidence limit for ANHATD28 RD exceeds +0.05.
- **Falsified at the margin** if the upper limit is at most +0.05.
- **Unresolved** otherwise.

Clinical branch:

- **Supportive:** magnitude supported; upper 95% limit of `RD_M28,U < +0.03`; upper 95% limit of post-landmark-RRT `RD_RRT,U < +0.05`; upper 95% limit of `RD_ALDL,U > 0`; destination estimates do not reverse under the event/ESS rule; and every overlap, balance, temporal, missingness, negative-control, reconciliation, and sensitivity gate passes. This supports only validation and trial prioritization.
- **Adverse:** upper 95% limit of ANHATD28 RD is at most 0; or upper 95% limit of `RD_ALDL,U` is at most 0; or lower 95% limit of `RD_M28,L` is at least +0.03; or lower 95% limit of post-landmark-RRT `RD_RRT,L` is at least +0.05 under both date brackets.
- **Inconclusive:** every other result, including a precise +2-point ANHATD effect, plausible but nondefinitive harm, discrepant incident versus all-post-landmark RRT, sparse overlap, modest residual confounding sufficient to reverse the estimate, or conflicting sensitivity analyses.

Thus an effect may falsify the >5-point hypothesis without proving harm. Nonsignificance is not equivalence; absent recorded RRT is not renal safety.
