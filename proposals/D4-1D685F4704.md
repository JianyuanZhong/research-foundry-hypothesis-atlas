## Question, supported evidence, and untested claim

The clinically consequential unresolved question is whether discordance of one filtration-marker estimate at baseline is associated with crossing the q=60 threshold on the other marker at the first repeat visit 2–8 years later, among participants who actually return for that qualifying first-repeat assessment. This can inform whether a discordant protocol biomarker merits external validation as a monitoring or reclassification signal; it cannot by itself justify CKD diagnosis, measured-GFR decline, treatment, referral, or benefit.

The frozen evidence supports only the identity and units of fields 30700 and 30720 as documented by the inspected official field material, the field-53 visit-date semantics, the stated CKD-EPI calculation as a protocol-derived quantity, and the availability of the exact UKB snapshot and joins. The experiment tests only an observable first-repeat protocol-biomarker threshold-status association in selected repeat attenders. It does not test latent GFR, chronicity, causal exposure effects, assay traceability beyond the available field evidence, population risk among baseline nonattenders, clinical utility, or transportability.

`eGFRcr = 142 * min(Scr/k,1)^a * max(Scr/k,1)^(-1.200) * 0.9938^Age * (1.012 if female)`,

## Primary estimand: selected-first-repeat-attender incident risk difference

For direction `d in {A,B}`, define the primary analysis population

`S_d = T_d intersect {R=1}`.

This is the population of baseline-eligible participants who supplied a qualifying first-repeat assessment. The primary estimand is the model-standardized selected-attender risk difference

`RD_d^S = (1/|S_d|) sum_{i in S_d} [m_d(1,Z_{di}) - m_d(0,Z_{di})]`,

where

`m_d(e,z)=Pr(Y_d=1 | E_d=e,Z_d=z,R=1,T_d)`.

The two values `m_d(1,z)` and `m_d(0,z)` are descriptive model-standardization values, not counterfactual causal risks. Standardization is over the empirical covariate distribution of `S_d`, and both predictions are evaluated only for rows in `S_d`; there is no prediction or averaging over `T_d\S_d`. Thus `RD_d^S` is identified from the observed selected-repeat-attender records subject to ordinary model/support conditions, and it makes no claim about baseline nonattenders.

The unmodeled selected-attender crude risk difference, the exposure prevalence, and all four exposure-by-outcome cells are reported as descriptive checks. If the primary model or any support gate fails, the direction is inconclusive rather than transported or rescued by weighting.

## Exact covariate and model basis

No interactions are included anywhere in the primary model. The basis is frozen as follows. For each direction, let `x_d` be baseline target-marker eGFR (`eGFRcr_0` for A and `eGFRcys_0` for B), `age0=21003-0.0`, `sex=31-0.0`, and `years=(53-1.0-53-0.0)/365.25`. Fit among `S_d`:

`logit Pr(Y_d=1 | E_d,x_d,age0,sex,years,R=1,T_d)`
`= alpha_d + beta_d E_d + gamma_1 x_d + gamma_2 age0 + gamma_3 sex + gamma_4 years`.

The primary model therefore contains exactly the intercept, exposure indicator, four listed main effects, and no spline terms, products, nonlinear terms, or unspecified interactions. Continuous variables are used in their original units; do not center, winsorize, transform, select, or regularize them after seeing outcomes. The estimand is the g-computation risk difference from this logistic model, not `exp(beta_d)` and not an odds-ratio claim. Report standardized risk under E=0, standardized risk under E=1, `RD_d^S`, and a two-sided 95% interval.

The outcome model is fit separately by direction. The primary model must have positive exposure support, full design rank, no complete/quasi-complete separation, and observed outcome variation within each exposure arm. A predeclared sensitivity may omit `sex` only if field-31 certification fails, but it is not a substitute for silently changing the primary model; if the required equation cannot be certified, mark that direction uncertified/inconclusive.

## Multiplicity and falsification

The only confirmatory family is the two selected-attender direction-specific RDs. Generate the two predeclared two-sided p-values using the same fixed model/bootstrap procedure, sort `p_(1)<=p_(2)`, set adjusted values `min(1,2p_(1))` and `min(1,p_(2))`, and apply the sequential family rule: reject the first only if `p_(1)<=.025`; reject the second only if `p_(2)<=.05`. Crude risks, attendance diagnostics, calibration, cell counts, model diagnostics, and any sensitivity are not additional confirmatory hypotheses.

Support for a direction requires estimability, a prespecified positive RD, the corresponding multiplicity-adjusted test meeting the family rule, a simultaneous interval excluding zero, and stable agreement with the crude selected-attender risk difference under the fixed model. Because the target is selected attenders, no agreement with an attendance-weighted estimate is required or permitted as a primary support criterion. A reversed or null result, loss after the fixed baseline target-marker adjustment, sparse cells, separation, unstable bootstrap, or failure of field/equation certification is adverse or inconclusive for that selected-attender protocol-biomarker association. A positive result in one direction does not establish reciprocity, renal specificity, latent renal decline, or a population-level risk difference.

The strongest supportive conclusion would be that, among UKB participants meeting the direction-specific baseline non-event restriction and returning for a qualifying first repeat within 2–8 years, baseline discordance is associated with first-repeat crossing of the other protocol-derived q=60 threshold after the fixed main-effect adjustment. Adverse evidence weakens or falsifies that selected-attender association. Inconclusive evidence means no claim, especially when support or model gates fail. Claims about baseline nonattenders require a separate design with observed follow-up/outcomes, validated censoring/attendance information, or an explicitly assumption-indexed study; claims about CKD, measured GFR, treatment benefit, and clinical action require external clinical adjudication and another study.
