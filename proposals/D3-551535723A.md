# JAK Inhibition Redirects LPS-Driven Monocyte Inflammation from IFN/STAT to NF-κB/IL-6 in RA: A Pathway-Specific, Not Global, Effect

## Domain: Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses
## Claim stage: Hypothesis (perturbation-based, computational)
## Version: v1 (episode 5)
## Seed: New direction (E-MTAB-8316, not previously used in this campaign)

---

## 1. Scientific Opening

JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib) are approved for rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and block JAK1/JAK2/JAK3-mediated cytokine signaling. The canonical expectation is that JAK inhibition suppresses the IFN/STAT transcriptional program. However, a critical unresolved question is whether JAK inhibition in RA monocytes is a **pathway-specific redirect** (suppressing IFN/STAT while enhancing JAK-independent NF-κB/IL-6 signaling) or a **global suppression** of the LPS-driven inflammatory response.

This distinction has direct clinical consequences: if JAK inhibition redirects inflammation to NF-κB-dependent pathways, this could explain (a) why some RA patients develop secondary loss of response to JAK inhibitors, (b) why combination therapy with NF-κB inhibitors might be beneficial, and (c) why the clinical benefit of JAK inhibitors may be limited in patients with high baseline NF-κB activity.

**Established evidence (not re-tested here):**
- Alivernini et al. [K1] (2020, Nat Med) defined distinct synovial tissue macrophage (STM) subsets in RA, showing MerTKpos subpopulations with remission-associated signatures and HBEGF+ inflammatory macrophages driving FLS invasiveness. Data deposited as E-MTAB-8322, E-MTAB-8873, E-MTAB-8316.
- Kuo et al. [K2] (2019, Sci Transl Med) showed that HBEGF+ inflammatory macrophages in RA synovium promote FLS invasiveness via EGFR, and that most RA medications target these macrophages but can redirect them into non-resolving states.
- Philippon et al. [K3] (2026, Arthritis Res Ther) demonstrated in a 3D RA synovial tissue model that tofacitinib (JAK1/3 inhibitor) selectively reduces JAK-dependent signaling while an IKKβ inhibitor suppresses NF-κB-dependent mediators, establishing the separability of these two pathways in the RA synovial microenvironment.

**Unresolved question:** Does JAK inhibition in LPS-stimulated RA monocytes produce a pathway-specific redirect (IFN/STAT suppressed, NF-κB/IL-6 enhanced) or a global suppression of the inflammatory response? Is this redirect consistent across donors, and can it be quantified as a "redirect index" that might predict clinical response to JAK inhibitors?

## 2. Hypotheses

**H1 (Redirect hypothesis):** JAK inhibition (UNC106238) in LPS-stimulated RA monocytes produces a pathway-specific redirect: IFN/STAT signature genes are suppressed while NF-κB/IL-6 signature genes are enhanced, producing a positive "redirect index" (mean NF-κB log2FC − mean IFN log2FC) in ≥5/6 specimen pairs.

**H2 (Global suppression hypothesis):** JAK inhibition produces a global reduction in LPS-driven inflammatory gene expression, with no pathway-specific redirect. The redirect index would be near zero or negative.

**H3 (Donor-specific hypothesis):** The redirect is not consistent across donors; it is present in some donors but absent in others, suggesting that the redirect depends on donor-specific baseline signaling states.

## 4. Analysis Plan

**Baseline (executed in discovery probe):**
- Paired log2 fold change: log2((UNC+1)/(none+1)) for each of 6 specimen pairs
- Mean and median across specimens
- Pathway-specific means: IFN signature (4 genes), NF-κB signature (5 genes)
- Redirect index: mean(NF-κB log2FC) − mean(IFN log2FC) per specimen
- Consistency: fraction of genes with same direction in all 6 specimens
- Total transcriptional activity: sum of log2(count+1) per specimen
- Correlation between baseline expression and UNC effect

**Alternative (planned for solver):**
- Per-gene paired t-test across 6 specimens (with Bonferroni correction)
- Pathway enrichment: hypergeometric test for IFN/STAT vs NF-κB/IL-6 gene sets among DE genes
- Redirect index distribution across specimens with 95% CI
- Donor-level analysis: aggregate specimens within donor, test for donor-specific effects
- Cross-dataset validation: check if the same IFN/NF-κB genes show consistent direction in E-MTAB-8322 (active vs remission macrophages)

**What the alternative adds over the baseline:**
- Statistical rigor: per-gene p-values and FDR correction
- Pathway-level enrichment: formal test that the redirect is not driven by a few genes
- Donor-level generalization: tests H3 (donor-specific hypothesis)
- Cross-dataset validation: tests whether the redirect is specific to UNC106238 or a general property of JAK inhibition in RA myeloid cells

## 5. Falsification Criteria

**H1 (Redirect) is supported if:**
- Redirect index > 1.0 in ≥5/6 specimens
- IFN signature mean log2FC < −0.5 (suppressed)
- NF-κB signature mean log2FC > +1.0 (enhanced)
- ≥3 IFN genes consistently DOWN in all 6 specimens
- ≥3 NF-κB genes consistently UP in ≥5/6 specimens

**H1 is falsified (H2 supported) if:**
- Redirect index < 0.5 in ≥4/6 specimens
- Total transcriptional activity reduced by >20% in ≥4/6 specimens
- No pathway-specific pattern: IFN and NF-κB genes show similar direction

**H3 (Donor-specific) is supported if:**
- Redirect index > 1.0 in ≥2 donors but < 0.5 in ≥1 donor
- The exception specimen (SA049/1463, redirect = −0.59) is explained by donor-specific baseline

**Inconclusive if:**
- Redirect index is between 0.5 and 1.0 in most specimens
- Pathway-specific effects are present but not consistent across specimens

## 6. Discovery Probe Results (executed this episode)

- 1,629 genes with |log2FC| > 1 (828 UP, 801 DOWN)
- IFN signature: IFIT1 mean log2FC = −2.076, MX1 = −1.289, ISG15 = −0.974, OAS1 = −0.857
- NF-κB signature: IL1B = +2.530, CXCL8 = +2.341, PTGS2 = +2.315, IL6 = +1.036, TNF = +0.397
- Redirect index: +4.13, −0.59, +1.73, +4.10, +4.68, +4.09 (5/6 positive)
- Total transcriptional activity: reduced in 5/6 specimens (not >20%)
- No correlation between baseline expression and UNC effect (r = −0.010)
- **Preliminary finding: H1 (Redirect) is tentatively supported, with one exception (SA049/1463)**

## 7. Limitations

- Small n: 4 donors, 6 specimen pairs (limited statistical power for donor-level analysis)
- Single compound: UNC106238 is a research JAK inhibitor; results may not generalize to approved JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib)
- In vitro LPS stimulation: does not capture the full complexity of the synovial microenvironment
- Monocytes, not tissue macrophages: E-MTAB-8316 uses isolated monocytes, not synovial tissue macrophages
- No clinical outcome data: cannot directly test whether the redirect predicts clinical response
- Cross-sectional: no time-course data to distinguish acute from sustained effects
