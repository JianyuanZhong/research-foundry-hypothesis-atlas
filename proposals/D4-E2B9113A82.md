The clinically relevant decision is whether a fixed-capacity service can use a grip-augmented cystatin-C allocation rule when the UACR component is actually observed, rather than relying on a model fallback for missing/uncertain urine information. The latest parent asks whether adding grip after whole-body FFM produces reproducible equation phenotypes and kidney-coded enrichment, while the record-density child tests a recording-opportunity threat. The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> At the same 466 cystatin-C slots in the exact 2,334-person visit-1 holdout, the nested AFG1 policy (routine variables + observed/fallback UACR + whole-body bioimpedance FFM + grip) has a materially positive fixed-denominator increment over AF1 (the same policy without grip) in the observed-UACR stratum, and that increment is not reversed in the prespecified fallback stratum.

Support would justify a prospective study that measures UACR and compares grip augmentation in an explicitly observed-result workflow. Adversity would reject the claim that the grip increment is robust across UACR-information states. Mixed or sparse evidence would be inconclusive, not evidence for fallback deployment.

The primary interpretation is conjunctive:

- **UACR-state supportive:** all gates pass; Q_E18,V=1 has sharp lower bound at least m and simultaneous lower limit strictly above 0; Q_E18,V=0 has sharp lower bound at least 0 and simultaneous lower limit not below 0; and the inherited nested biochemical/N18 and record-density qualifications that are required by the parent hierarchy are supportive. - **UACR-state adverse:** all gates pass; the observed-UACR contrast has simultaneous upper limit below 0, or the fallback contrast has simultaneous upper limit below -m, while the other required inherited gates pass. This falsifies the corresponding robustness claim; it does not prove AF1 superiority, UACR fallback harm, no benefit, or clinical equivalence.
- **UACR-state mixed:** both strata are estimable and one is supportive while the other is adverse or materially discordant. This rejects a single uniform deployment claim and requires a stratified prospective study.
- **UACR-state inconclusive:** any gate failure, missing artifact, sparse stratum/event count, nonpositive or unstable variance, bounds crossing the decision region, only one informative stratum, or noncomputability. Gate failure is never adverse.

A supportive result still cannot establish that urine was collected before allocation, that fallback would be needed in practice, or that a clinician should order cystatin C.
