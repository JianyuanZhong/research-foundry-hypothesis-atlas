# Discordant AKI recovery: separating true renal recovery from creatinine artifacts

## Question

Among ICU patients with KDIGO stage 2-3 acute kidney injury, does creatinine recovery without concurrent recovery in urine output or fluid balance identify a discordant-recovery phenotype with worse subsequent outcomes than concordant recovery?

## Why it matters

Creatinine is the standard marker for AKI recovery, but it can be affected by muscle mass, fluid shifts, and measurement timing. Urine output and fluid balance provide complementary information about renal perfusion and function. If discordant recovery (creatinine improves but urine output/fluid status does not) carries higher mortality or longer ICU stay, clinicians should not rely on creatinine alone to declare recovery. This could change how recovery is assessed and when supportive care is escalated.

## Evidence base

[K1] Haines et al. (2023) demonstrated that eGFR creatinine systematically overestimated kidney function after prolonged critical illness, with a posterior mean difference of 33 ml/min/1.73m² at ICU discharge. Cystatin C better estimated true kidney function because it was unaffected by muscle loss. This supports the biological plausibility that creatinine-based recovery can be misleading.

[K2] KDIGO guidelines define AKI stages by creatinine and urine output criteria, but do not specify how to assess recovery when these markers diverge. This creates a clinical gap that the proposed study addresses.

[K3] A recent MIMIC-IV study of AKI trajectories found that patients with persistent oliguria had worse outcomes than those with creatinine-only AKI, supporting the clinical relevance of urine output as an independent prognostic marker.

## Data bindings (MIMIC-IV)

- **Population**: ICU patients with KDIGO stage 2-3 AKI (creatinine ≥2× baseline or urine output <0.5 ml/kg/hr for ≥6 hours).
- **Source tables**: `icu/icustays` (ICU admission/discharge), `icu/chartevents` (creatinine, urine output), `icu/ingredientevents` (fluid input), `icu/outputevents` (fluid output), `hosp/admissions` (demographics, discharge status).
- **Time fields**: `chartevents.charttime` for creatinine and urine output; `ingredientevents.starttime`/`endtime` for fluid input; `outputevents.starttime` for fluid output.
- **Recovery definition**: Creatinine recovery = creatinine falls to <1.5× baseline within 72 hours of AKI peak. Urine output recovery = urine output ≥0.5 ml/kg/hr for ≥12 hours. Fluid balance recovery = net fluid balance turns negative or stabilizes.
- **Discordant recovery**: Creatinine recovery without concurrent urine output or fluid balance recovery.
- **Outcomes**: 28-day mortality, ICU length of stay, hospital length of stay.

## Analysis plan

1. **Baseline comparison**: Compare discordant versus concordant recovery groups for 28-day mortality and ICU length of stay, with adjustment for age, sex, SOFA score, and fluid shifts.
2. **Recurrent-event model**: Fit a recurrent-event model that accounts for the temporal sequence of creatinine, urine output, and fluid balance measurements, to test whether the discordant-recovery signal persists when the order of recovery is considered.
3. **Sensitivity analysis**: Exclude patients with extreme fluid shifts (net balance >5 L/day) or missing measurements, and test whether the discordant-recovery signal is robust.

## Falsification criteria

- If discordant recovery does not differ from concordant recovery in 28-day mortality or ICU length of stay after adjustment, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the discordant-recovery signal disappears after excluding patients with extreme fluid shifts, the signal may be driven by measurement artifacts rather than true renal discordance.

## Method comparison

- **Baseline**: Simple logistic regression comparing discordant versus concordant recovery groups, with adjustment for severity and fluid shifts.
- **Alternative**: Recurrent-event model that accounts for the temporal sequence of measurements, which could reveal whether the discordant-recovery signal is driven by the order of recovery rather than the final state.

## Limitations

- MIMIC-IV does not include cystatin C or measured GFR, so true renal function cannot be directly assessed.
- Fluid balance is an imperfect proxy for renal perfusion and can be affected by non-renal factors.
- The study is observational and cannot establish causality.
