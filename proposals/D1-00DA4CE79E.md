# Does perfusion status modify the effect of early loop-diuretic initiation on mortality in sepsis? (Diuretic-timing effect modification in MIMIC-IV v3.1)

Developed from expert seed `[starting question]` (parent `[prior hypothesis]`).
Dataset: MIMIC-IV v3.1 (frozen archive `mimic-iv-3.1.zip`, SHA-256 `0dd448e3...`, see `work/data-verification.md`).
Status: **proposal with verified data bindings and a measured feasibility probe (cohort, diuretic/vasopressor/fluid/vital/lactate coverage); no final inferential analysis has been run.**

## 1. Scientific question and why it matters

Among ICU patients with sepsis who receive a loop diuretic during the first 120 h of ICU stay, does the
association between **early diuretic initiation (within 48 h of ICU admission)** and **in-hospital death**
differ by **concurrent perfusion status** — specifically, vasopressor-free with stable/improving mean
arterial pressure (MAP) versus vasopressor-dependent (ongoing hypoperfusion)?

[K1] (Wang 2025, MIMIC-IV sepsis, PSM) reported that early (≤48 h) versus late (>96 h) loop-diuretic
initiation was associated with higher 28-day mortality (adjusted HR 2.59, 95% CI 2.325–2.884), and the
authors concluded that "careful consideration" of timing is warranted. But K1's contrast is a single
timing dichotomy applied to all diuretic recipients; it does not condition on whether the patient was
hemodynamically stabilized at the time of diuresis. The seed's hypothesis is precisely that the effect
differs: in patients with stable perfusion and fluid accumulation, early diuresis may be beneficial or
neutral, whereas in patients with ongoing hypoperfusion, early diuresis may be harmful (or conversely,
the observed harm may be concentrated in the hypoperfused subgroup and reflect confounding by
indication rather than a uniform drug effect).

[K2] (Sipilä 2023) shows that the first month after severe infection carries a sharply elevated
cardiovascular/mortality risk window (first-month HR ≈ 7.9), which motivates treating the first 72–120 h
of sepsis ICU care as a consequential decision window in which fluid-deresuscitation timing could change
outcomes. The unresolved, decision-relevant question is whether a simple, measurable perfusion criterion
can identify the subgroup in which early diuresis is (or is not) associated with harm — information that
would inform where a future randomized deresuscitation trial should enroll and which patients clinicians
should avoid diuresing early.

## 2. Hypothesis (falsifiable)

H1: In sepsis ICU patients receiving a loop diuretic in the first 120 h, the association between early
(≤48 h) versus late (>48 h) initiation and in-hospital death is **weaker (toward the null) in
vasopressor-free patients with stable/improving MAP** than in **vasopressor-dependent patients**, after
adjustment for baseline severity.

Falsification: if the interaction term (early × vasopressor-dependent) is not statistically meaningful
(95% CI of the interaction HR includes 1.0 with a plausible magnitude) and the early-vs-late HRs in the
two perfusion strata are similar, H1 is not supported. An adverse result (early diuresis associated with
higher mortality in *both* strata, or in the stable-perfusion stratum) would challenge the seed's
hypothesis and would instead support a uniform-harm or confounding-by-indication interpretation.

## 3. Population and temporal boundaries (verified bindings)

- **Source**: [K3] `mimic-iv-3.1.zip` ([source checksum]).
- **Sepsis definition**: ICD-10 diagnosis codes with 3-letter prefix in {A40, A41, A42, A43, A77, R65}
  from `hosp/diagnoses_icd.csv.gz` (columns `subject_id, hadm_id, seq_num, icd_code, icd_version`;
  filter `icd_version==10`), joined to `icu/icustays.csv.gz` (`subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime,
  outtime`) on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`.
- **Cohort**: sepsis ICU stays with ICU length of stay ≥ 96 h (so a 48 h early/late contrast and a 120 h
  follow-up window are feasible). Measured: 10,270 sepsis ICU stays → **4,131** after the ≥96 h filter;
  in-hospital death rate 35.2% (from `hosp/admissions.deathtime`).
- **Index time (t0)**: `icustays.intime`. All exposure windows are relative to t0.
- **Exposure**: first loop-diuretic administration in [t0−24 h, t0+120 h] from
  `icu/inputevents.csv.gz` (columns `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, starttime, itemid, amount`),
  itemids {221794 Furosemide (Lasix), 228340 Furosemide (Lasix) 250/50, 229639 Bumetanide (Bumex)}
  (verified against `icu/d_items.csv.gz`). Early = first administration ≤ t0+48 h; Late = > t0+48 h.
  Measured: 1,844/4,131 (44.6%) of cohort stays received a loop diuretic in-window; median first
  administration at t0+36.7 h.
- **Perfusion modifier** (defined at t0, using only pre-index information):
  - `vasopressor_dependent` = any vasopressor administration in [t0, t0+24 h] from `inputevents`
    itemids {221906 Norepinephrine, 222315 Vasopressin, 221653 Dobutamine, 221662 Dopamine, 221289
    Epinephrine, 229617 Epinephrine.}. Measured: 48.6% of cohort.
  - `map_improving` = mean MAP in [t0+24 h, t0+48 h] ≥ mean MAP in [t0, t0+24 h], from
    `icu/chartevents.csv.gz` itemid 220052 (Arterial Blood Pressure mean). Measured coverage: 44.1%
    (0–24 h) and 47.4% (24–48 h) of cohort; used as a secondary, pre-specified perfusion definition.
- **Outcome**: in-hospital death (`hosp/admissions.deathtime` not null), time from t0 to death or
  hospital discharge (`hosp/admissions.dischtime`), right-censored at discharge.
- **Follow-up window**: t0 to min(death, discharge), restricted to the first 120 h for exposure
  definition; death outcome is in-hospital (not time-restricted), consistent with K1's in-hospital
  mortality framing.

## 5. Analysis plan

1. **Descriptive**: baseline characteristics by perfusion stratum × timing group; standardized mean
   differences.
2. **Primary model**: Cox proportional-hazards model for in-hospital death with:
   - main effects: early vs late diuretic (among diuretic recipients), vasopressor-dependent vs not,
     baseline covariates (age, sex, lactate_24, creatinine, hemoglobin, fluid_24h, urine_24h, GCS, RR,
     HR);
   - **interaction term**: early × vasopressor-dependent (the test of H1).
   - Stratified HRs (early vs late) within each perfusion stratum reported with 95% CIs.
3. **Time-varying confounding**: because vasopressor use and fluid balance are time-varying and are
   affected by prior diuretic exposure, the primary adjusted model is supplemented with a
   **marginal structural model (MSM)** with stabilized inverse-probability-of-treatment weights (IPTW)
   estimated from a logistic model of diuretic initiation at each 12 h decision time, conditioned on the
   time-varying covariates (vasopressor dose, cumulative fluid, urine output, MAP, lactate) and baseline
   covariates. The MSM estimates the causal contrast of an "early" vs "late" diuretic strategy within
   each perfusion stratum. This is the scientifically substantive learned/mechanistic alternative to the
   baseline Cox model: the Cox model adjusts for baseline confounding only and can be biased by
   time-varying confounding affected by prior treatment; the MSM addresses that specific bias and reveals
   whether the perfusion interaction survives proper time-varying adjustment.
4. **Sensitivity analyses** (pre-specified):
   - Perfusion defined by `map_improving` instead of vasopressor dependence.
   - Restrict to patients with both MAP windows measured (n≈1,726).
   - Exclude the 48–96 h "gray zone" (early ≤48 h vs late >96 h) to mirror K1's exact contrast.
   - Propensity-score matching within each perfusion stratum (early vs late), as in K1.
5. **Split**: no train/test split for the primary association (it is a single-cohort causal-contrast
   question, not a prediction task). The MSM's IPTW propensity models are fit on the full cohort;
   weight stability (max weight, effective sample size) is reported. For the exploratory prediction
   sub-question (below), a 70/30 time-based split (by ICU admission date) is used.

## 6. Baseline vs learned alternative (method comparison)

- **Baseline**: Cox PH with baseline covariates + interaction (Section 5.2). Simple, interpretable,
  answers "is the early-vs-late association different across perfusion strata after baseline
  adjustment?"
- **Learned/mechanistic alternative**: MSM with stabilized IPTW (Section 5.3). This is not a
  black-box predictor; it is a causal-estimation method that models the time-varying treatment
  assignment process (a logistic regression at each decision time) and reweights to emulate a
  randomized strategy. It answers the question the Cox model cannot: "does the perfusion interaction
  persist when time-varying confounding (vasopressor dose, fluid balance) affected by prior diuretic
  exposure is properly handled?"
- **What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses**: the Cox model's interaction estimate can be
  biased if, e.g., early diuretics cause subsequent vasopressor weaning (or vice versa), creating a
  collider/time-varying-confounding pathway. The MSM isolates the strategy-level contrast. If the two
  methods agree, confidence in the interaction is high; if they disagree, the discrepancy localizes the
  source of bias (time-varying confounding) and changes the next decision (e.g., a trial should target
  the stratum where the MSM shows harm).
- **Resource estimate**: both models fit on ≤4,131 rows × ~15 covariates; CPU-only, seconds to minutes.
  No GPU needed. (Measured: cohort extraction and feature construction completed in <10 min on 8 CPU
  cores; model fitting is negligible by comparison.)

## 7. Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive of H1**: interaction HR 95% CI excludes 1.0 in the direction of weaker early-diuretic
  harm in the vasopressor-free/stable-MAP stratum; MSM confirms. → Justifies a stratized deresuscitation
  trial and suggests clinicians should avoid early diuresis in vasopressor-dependent sepsis.
- **Adverse to H1**: no meaningful interaction (CI includes 1.0, similar HRs in both strata), or early
  diuresis associated with harm in the stable-perfusion stratum. → The seed's effect-modification
  hypothesis is not supported; the K1 association is more uniform (or driven by confounding by
  indication that is not resolved by perfusion stratification). Next step: test alternative modifiers
  (fluid balance, urine output) or accept that timing alone does not stratify risk.
- **Inconclusive**: small stratum sizes (e.g., <100 events in a stratum×timing cell) or unstable IPTW
  weights (ESR < 30% of cohort). → Report as underpowered; the pre-specified sensitivities (MAP-based
  perfusion, gray-zone exclusion, PSM) define the next step.

## 8. What a future solver must compute and what establishes completion

- Build the cohort exactly as specified (Section 3) from the frozen archive; report cohort size,
  diuretic-receipt rate, perfusion-stratum sizes, and outcome counts.
- Fit the primary Cox model and the MSM; report stratified HRs (95% CIs), the interaction HR (95% CI),
  and MSM weights diagnostics.
- Run the pre-specified sensitivities.
- **Completion is established by**: (a) a reproducible script that regenerates the cohort and all
  estimates from the frozen archive; (b) a results table with the primary interaction estimate and all
  sensitivity estimates; (c) a written interpretation mapping the estimates to supportive/adverse/
  inconclusive per Section 7.
- **Not required**: a prediction model with a performance metric (this is a causal-contrast question);
  external validation (single-center MIMIC-IV); clinical-trial-level causal proof (observational design;
  the MSM is the strongest available causal estimate but residual confounding remains).

## 9. Limitations and claims that require more than this study

- Observational; confounding by indication (sicker/more congested patients may receive diuretics
  earlier) is addressed by the MSM but not eliminated.
- Perfusion status is a proxy (vasopressor use, MAP); it does not measure true end-organ perfusion or
  cardiac output.
- Loop diuretic itemids exclude torsemide/ethacrynic acid (not present in `d_items` for this
  dictionary version); the exposure is furosemide + bumetanide.
- In-hospital death is the outcome; 28-day mortality (K1's outcome) would require post-discharge
  follow-up not available in MIMIC-IV.
- Single dataset; external validation (e.g., eICU) is a future step, not part of this experiment.
- Clinical truth (whether early diuresis *causes* harm) cannot be established by this observational
  analysis; it can only estimate the association under the MSM's positivity/no-unmeasured-confounding
  assumptions and identify a subgroup where a trial is most informative.

## 10. Disposition of the three research demonstrations

- **Delphi (natural-history transformer)**: not pursued for this question. The question is a
  causal-contrast/effect-modification question on a single treatment decision, not a disease-sequence
  prediction question; a sequence model would add complexity without addressing the time-varying
  confounding that is the central methodological issue. Deferred; would be revisited if the question
  shifted to predicting multi-organ recovery trajectories.
- **ALADYNOULLI (Bayesian longitudinal latent structure)**: not pursued. The latent-trajectory
  framework is designed for discovering shared disease dynamics across a cohort; here the hypothesis is
  a specific, pre-specified effect-modification contrast, and the MSM is the appropriate
  causal-estimation tool. A latent-trajectory model of perfusion recovery could be a future
  descriptive complement but is not needed to test H1.
- **Oncoformer (multimodal EHR + imaging)**: not pursued. No imaging data are relevant to diuretic
  timing in sepsis; the lab-only structured data are fully sufficient for the question. The
  lab-only adaptation principle from the methods guide is respected by using only structured
  longitudinal data.

## 11. Disposition of other MIMIC seeds (brief)

- mimic-01 (asynchronous organ recovery): related but broader; the AKI-recovery seed (mimic-03) was
  already developed to a selected candidate in episode 2 ([prior hypothesis]). This
  episode develops the distinct diuretic-timing seed (mimic-02) rather than duplicating that branch.
- mimic-04 (hyperlactatemia phenotypes): the lactate variable is used here as a covariate; a full
  phenotype-validation study is a separate, larger question.
- mimic-05 (glucose relative to HbA1c): different question; HbA1c coverage in MIMIC-IV is sparse and
  the question is not time-varying-treatment in nature.
- mimic-06 (deep sedation): requires sedation-depth measurement (RASS itemid 228302 is text); feasible
  but a separate branch.
- mimic-07 (antimicrobial de-escalation): requires culture-result availability timing; a separate,
  complex branch.
- mimic-08 (creatinine during diuresis in heart failure): related to this question's fluid
  physiology but in a different population (acute heart failure, not sepsis); deferred.
- mimic-09 (transfusion heterogeneity): different treatment; strong confounding by indication;
  deferred.
- mimic-10 (residual instability before ICU discharge): different time window (pre-discharge vs
  early ICU); deferred.
