# Cell-compartment-specific MERTK target value in rheumatoid synovium

**Status: hypothesis and design only.** No new Harbor analysis or wet-lab experiment has been executed. The scientific hypothesis is that selective MERTK loss has opposite functional consequences by synovial cell compartment: it can reduce pathogenic inflammatory/invasive behavior in RA fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS), while impairing efferocytosis/resolution and stromal-supportive functions of remission-associated synovial macrophages. If true, the consequential target decision is to prioritize a fibroblast-restricted preclinical strategy over systemic/broad MerTK inhibition. It is not a claim of clinical efficacy, safety, or a ready drug-delivery method.

## Evidence boundary, opening, and rival

The strongest existing claim is cell-context-specific, not a single target direction. Alivernini et al. report MerTK-positive synovial tissue macrophage states enriched in drug-free remission, production of resolving lipid mediators, and macrophage-induced FLS repair in vitro [K1]. Li et al. report increased GAS6/MERTK in RA samples and that GAS6 or MERTK siRNA attenuated several pathogenic RA-FLS phenotypes [K2]. These studies use different cohorts, assays, and cell contexts; the latter is a hypothesis-generating study. Neither establishes that selective MERTK inhibition causes a favorable net effect across both cell types, that GAS6 acts exclusively through MERTK, or that pharmacologic exposure is target-specific. Richard et al. show that stromal/matrix remodeling can itself shape immune-cell spatial access and remission-associated features, adding a consequential niche-level rival to simple cell-autonomous interpretation [K3].

**Unresolved claim to test:** with engagement-verified, orthogonal MERTK loss, does FLS MERTK loss reduce FLS inflammatory/invasive behavior while macrophage MERTK loss impairs efferocytosis/resolution, and can a compartment-restricted intervention separate those effects? The strongest rival is that apparent opposite roles arise from cell state, donor selection, culture/assay context, GAS6 effects through AXL or other pathways, off-target siRNA/inhibitor effects, or viability changes—not MERTK-specific compartment biology. AXL/TYRO3 compensation and differences in receptor/ligand abundance remain alternatives, not established mechanisms.

**Predictions and interpretation:** support requires target-engaged MERTK loss to reproducibly reduce prespecified FLS pathogenic function and independently worsen a macrophage resolution function in the predicted contexts, with WT but not kinase-dead MERTK rescue reversing each target-dependent effect and acceptable viability. FLS-only benefit with preserved macrophage functions would support further FLS-restricted delivery work, not systemic treatment. No FLS benefit, or macrophage harm at exposures needed for FLS effect, weakens the proposed therapeutic window. Compound-only changes, unequal engagement, toxicity, failed rescue, low donor precision, or unvalidated assays are inconclusive or favor artifacts; no automatic verifier can adjudicate biological specificity or clinical margins.

## Harbor-computable archival check

The Harbor task is a planned, descriptive, donor-aware check of whether the existing RNA datasets contain the two *separate* transcript associations that motivate the hypothesis: (A) MERTK/GAS6 expression and receptor co-detection in remission versus treatment-naive/resistant synovial macrophage samples, and (B) MERTK/GAS6 availability plus a fixed FLS inflammatory-program score in FLS output samples. The cross-study signs are displayed side by side but are never pooled into a cell-type interaction estimate: cohorts, assay scales, donor namespaces, and endpoints differ. The check can prioritize or defer a prospective perturbation; it cannot test MERTK causality or the therapeutic window.

No GPU is needed for these sparse descriptive computations; no GPU feasibility claim is being made for unavailable wet-lab work.

**Supportive archival pattern:** correct metadata/feature joins, target transcripts observed with stable donor-level direction for the macrophage remission association and compatible FLS-panel associations across sources. This only supports that the context is measurable and merits direct perturbation; it is not proof of opposite target function. **Adverse:** usable, well-measured data consistently fail to reproduce either predeclared expression association or show a strong opposite pattern; this weakens the transcript-level rationale and can justify deferring expensive target-specific work, but does not falsify protein-level or functional biology. **Inconclusive:** dropout/feature absence (especially TYRO3), source mapping disagreement, repeated donors, 4-cluster FLS structure, uncertain dose, shallow targeted-panel coverage, wide donor intervals, or disagreement across non-comparable studies. Such uncertainty is not a null result.

### Prospective mechanistic test and what it adds

In primary human RA synovial FLS and sorted synovial macrophages from each donor where feasible, randomize matched aliquots to non-targeting control, at least two independent MERTK genetic-loss reagents, guide/siRNA-resistant WT rescue, kinase-dead rescue, and orthogonal pharmacologic inhibition with exposure and target-engagement verification. Stratify or balance active versus remission/low-activity tissue, but do not confuse cross-sectional disease state with treatment. Verify MERTK and AXL/TYRO3 protein and proximal signaling; include vehicle, AXL/TYRO3-specific controls, viability, and wash/carry-over controls. Measure FLS inflammatory/invasive outputs (IL-6/CXCL8 secretion, migration/invasion, MMP3) separately from macrophage efferocytosis, pro-resolving mediator production, and macrophage-supported FLS repair/matrix deposition. The biological unit is donor, with paired conditions analyzed in donor-blocked models and held-out donors only for any exploratory fitted predictor; target engagement and functional assay QC gate interpretation. Expert adjudication must set assay validity and meaningful benefit/harm margins prospectively. A supportive result would justify cell-restricted delivery and in-vivo testing; broad macrophage harm, absent FLS benefit, or lack of genetic/rescue concordance stops or revises MERTK targeting. Clinical benefit, joint pharmacokinetics, delivery selectivity, and safety require a separate in-vivo/translational study and clinical expert review.

## Method alternatives and deferred branch

For the computable RNA question, donor-level fixed summaries are adequate: the archive has only 7 remission/6 naive/6 resistant macrophage donors, sparse single-cell transcripts, four FLS clusters in E-MTAB-8316, and no harmonized cell-state labels. A latent-cell-state or ligand–receptor model would not recover causal direction or missing protein/functional data and is deferred. The substantive alternative is the prospective, donor-blocked MERTK loss/rescue factorial experiment on the same cell-compartment uncertainty; it reveals function and target specificity but is not executable from the four configured transcript datasets. We select the descriptive Harbor audit only as the bounded computational deliverable and require the perturbation before target-prioritization conclusions. The initial TAM-receptor-redundancy branch was explored in worker notes: RNA co-detection in E-MTAB-8322 can test transcript availability but cannot show compensation; it is retained as a competing mechanism, not promoted to a separate hypothesis. The three clinical-research demonstration papers are historical EHR/multimodal examples and not topic/method choices for this molecular target domain; the domain guide correctly treats them as nonbinding. The expert-seed baseline is D2-T2; D2-T1's broader intervention-response premise remains covered by the inherited MerTK context and supplies no independent evidence.

## Domain-specific disposition

The three clinical-research demonstrations concern EHR disease trajectories, routine cancer EHR/imaging, and longitudinal EHR/genetics. They are not pursued as topic/method templates here because this is a molecular therapeutic-target island with read-only synovial multiomic/assay data and no longitudinal patient-care tables or imaging. This is a domain fit decision, not a claim that their methods are unsuitable generally.
