# Directional macrophage-to-fibroblast signaling in RA synovium

## Scientific question and hypothesis

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), macrophages and synovial fibroblast-like cells (FLS) are repeatedly described as interacting, but a cross-sectional expression association cannot tell whether a signal is produced by macrophages, received by FLS, or merely reflects shared disease, treatment, or donor state. The unresolved claim is whether macrophage-conditioned communication has a reproducible, directional FLS response that is attenuated when the macrophage signal is pharmacologically reduced.

Working hypothesis: among paired E-MTAB-8873 FLS samples, MerTK/CD206-positive macrophage coculture will induce a reproducible FLS response relative to FLS-only and MerTK/CD206-negative controls; the response will be attenuated by the macrophage MerTK-inhibitor contrast. A donor-aware model will test whether this pattern is specific to positive-macrophage/inhibitor contrasts rather than a general treatment or donor effect. GSE95588 will be a bounded exploratory cross-assay check, not independent validation.

The strongest claim currently supported is narrower: the available data contain explicit FLS-only, positive/negative macrophage coculture, LPS, and inhibitor labels, and the panel measures candidate FLS response genes. No effect has been estimated in this episode. The experiment will test whether those labels support a directional response; it will not establish a unique molecular mechanism, clinical causality, or therapeutic benefit.

## Why this matters

If a reproducible macrophage-to-FLS response is found, it would justify targeted perturbation and mechanistic follow-up of a specific communication circuit. If the inhibitor does not attenuate the response, or if the pattern is equally present in negative-macrophage/LPS controls, the data would favor shared inflammatory treatment, macrophage phenotype, or measurement explanations and would redirect work toward a different mediator or assay. This distinction is clinically consequential because macrophage-targeted strategies and FLS-targeted strategies imply different intervention logic.

## Population, units, and temporal boundaries

Primary population: the 33 recorded FLS pseudobulk samples in E-MTAB-8873, from adult female RA donors SA256A, SA257A, SA258R, SA263R, and SA266A (ages 50, 50, 63, 70, and 69 as recorded). The unit of analysis is a sample/condition contrast nested within donor; donors are reused across conditions, so samples are not independent biological replicates. The design metadata explicitly marks `donor_status: requires_contributing_donor_model`.

The primary contrast is within each FLS donor/replicate context: FLS-only versus MerTK/CD206-positive macrophage coculture, with MerTK/CD206-negative coculture as a composition/condition control. Where available, the paired positive-coculture versus positive-coculture-plus-MerTK-inhibitor contrast is the prespecified perturbation check. LPS-pre-treated samples are a secondary stressor control, not a substitute for the inhibitor contrast. No temporal ordering is inferred from the cross-sectional sample labels; “pre-LPS” and “pre-inhibitor” describe macrophage preparation, not a longitudinal FLS time course.

## Variables and estimands

Outcome: log2(1 + expression) for each measured feature, with feature-instance labels retained. Primary response is a prespecified FLS activation vector: `COL1A1` (both source instances), `COL1A2`, `MMP1`, `MMP9`, `IL6`, `CXCL8`, `IL1B`, `IL1RN`, `STAT3`, `TNF`, `MERTK`, and `AXL`; the full 445-feature matrix is retained for an exploratory multivariate screen. No gene is treated as absent merely because it is not on the targeted panel.

Primary estimand: the donor-weighted within-context difference in FLS response between positive-macrophage coculture and FLS-only, with the positive-versus-positive-inhibitor difference as the perturbation estimand. A secondary estimand compares positive versus negative macrophage coculture at the same donor/replicate context. The model includes donor/replicate random effects or a contributing-donor covariance structure, condition fixed effects, and treatment fixed effects; it does not use an ordinary independent-donor t-test. The analysis reports effect size, uncertainty, and the number of valid paired contrasts, not a p-value as a standalone conclusion.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Baseline: a donor-aware paired linear mixed model (or hierarchical shrinkage model when contrasts are sparse) for each feature and the prespecified response vector. It estimates the direct condition and inhibitor contrasts, handles reused donors, and provides a transparent falsification check using negative-macrophage and LPS controls. This is the primary method because the question is a small, structured perturbation comparison and interpretability is essential.

Substantive alternative: a mechanistic communication-state model with two latent states—macrophage signal state and FLS response state—estimated from the same paired features and labels. The macrophage state is represented by measured macrophage-associated markers available in the panel (for example MERTK/AXL and inflammatory markers), while the FLS state is represented by the prespecified activation vector. The model compares (i) a shared-state-only model, (ii) a macrophage-to-FLS directional model, and (iii) a bidirectional model, using donor-aware leave-one-donor-out prediction and permutation of condition labels within donor. It can reveal whether the FLS response is predictable from macrophage-condition state beyond shared donor/treatment state, and whether the inhibitor specifically breaks that directional link. It is not a claim that latent states are biological ground truth.

The baseline loses information about coordinated multivariate state and can flag individual genes without distinguishing shared-state from directional signaling. The alternative retains joint feature structure and tests the scientific uncertainty directly, but is more sensitive to panel selection and small sample size. The alternative is deferred if the paired contrasts are too sparse or if its held-out donor prediction is no better than the baseline; it is not selected for a small predictive gain alone.

## Analysis and falsification

1. Validate the one-to-one matrix/design join, preserve feature-instance labels, and report missingness, donor/condition counts, and the exact paired contrasts used.
2. Fit the baseline on the primary E-MTAB-8873 contrasts with donor-aware uncertainty. Use a fixed, prespecified response vector and report all features, not only significant ones.
3. Run the negative-macrophage and LPS controls. A directional claim requires a positive-coculture effect that is not equally reproduced by the negative control and is attenuated by the inhibitor contrast.
4. Fit the mechanistic alternative with leave-one-donor-out evaluation. Compare held-out directional prediction against the shared-state baseline and against within-donor label permutations. Report calibration/uncertainty and feature contribution; do not interpret a small score improvement as mechanism.
5. Use GSE95588 only to test whether the direction of the same source-preserved features is concordant across its labeled condition columns. Do not call this independent validation because donor mapping is unresolved and r1/r2 reuse GSE57723.

Supportive result: a reproducible positive-macrophage FLS response, attenuated by the inhibitor, with uncertainty excluding a clinically/biologically meaningful null under the prespecified threshold, and better held-out donor prediction for the directional model than shared-state and permuted controls. This supports a directional communication hypothesis in this assay, not a unique pathway or clinical effect.

Adverse result: no positive-specific response, equal response in negative/LPS controls, no inhibitor attenuation, or failure of the directional model to beat the shared-state baseline. This would weaken the directional claim and favor shared inflammatory treatment, macrophage phenotype, donor/replicate, or targeted-panel measurement explanations.

Inconclusive result: too few valid paired contrasts, wide uncertainty, inconsistent donor directions, or inability to separate inhibitor specificity from nonspecific treatment. This is a useful negative/informative outcome and should trigger a larger paired assay or targeted mediator measurement rather than a post hoc claim.

## What remains unavailable

The data do not provide direct macrophage transcriptomics for every E-MTAB-8873 condition, a verified inhibitor identity/specificity assay, protein/secreted-ligand measurements, cell-surface validation, clinical outcomes, or a longitudinal patient trajectory. GSE95588 lacks resolved donor identity and independent annotation. Thus the experiment can test a reproducible directional expression pattern and a bounded perturbation association; it cannot establish direct ligand-receptor action, causality in vivo, or treatment benefit. Those claims require additional paired macrophage/FLS assays, inhibitor specificity controls, protein/secretome measurement, and expert clinical adjudication.
