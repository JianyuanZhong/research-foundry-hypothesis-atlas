# MerTK cell-context therapeutic-window hypothesis

**Status:** untested hypothesis and proposed study; no computational result or wet-lab intervention has been executed. The clinically important question is whether MERTK inhibition can suppress inflammatory macrophage-to-FLS signaling while sparing resolution-associated macrophage functions, or whether historical inhibitor signals reflect off-target exposure, toxicity, wash/carry-over, donor composition, or measurement. A positive future result could justify a cell-restricted delivery study, not broad systemic inhibition or clinical use. A precise absent benefit or broad repair/efferocytosis harm would weaken the target rationale; inadequate exposure/engagement or wide intervals would be inconclusive.

## Evidence and distinguishing predictions

The strongest supported evidence is published association between MerTK-positive synovial tissue macrophage states and RA remission/stromal repair context [K1], alongside MERTK's broader homeostatic and immune-regulatory roles [K2]. Matrix/niche remodeling is itself consequential for immune patterning and resolution [K3]. These observations do not establish target causality or therapeutic selectivity. The strongest rival is compound off-target action or unverified exposure (including the annotated 250 µM UNC106238), cytotoxicity/carry-over, or treatment/donor/state artifacts rather than MERTK-specific function. The discriminating prediction is whether orthogonal MERTK loss reproduces drug-associated functional changes and guide-resistant WT—but not kinase-dead—rescue reverses them while function differs by cell context. A drug-only phenotype without genetic/rescue concordance supports compound effects. Even concordant ex-vivo findings would not establish in-vivo selectivity, benefit, or clinical safety.

## Prospective test of the unresolved claim

After Stage 0 confirms UNC106238 identity, free concentration, stability, exposure and washout, validate MERTK engagement, AXL/TYRO3 off-target readouts and viability. Stop the drug arm if no engaged, non-toxic exposure is found; proceed only with genetics. Cross independent RA myeloid donors with FLS donors. Target 24 donors (12 active RA, 12 sustained remission; design target requiring clinical adjudication, material feasibility and blinded variance/power update), balanced/crossed FLS pairs; experimental unit is myeloid donor, wells nested. Randomize/blind matched vehicle, qualified dose range/second chemotype, two independent MERTK guides or reagents, non-targeting controls, WT rescue and kinase-dead rescue. Compare LPS-activated monocyte-derived macrophages with remission-associated sorted synovial macrophages under basal and defined inflammatory challenge; dose myeloid cells, wash, and include FLS-only dosing and residual-drug-spike controls. Record exact assignment, donor, time, dose, batch and cell state.

Collect engagement/exposure, viability and functional outputs at prespecified 0, 1, 6, 24 and 48 h. Primary benefit: 24-h FLS inflammatory protein composite (IL-6, IL-8/CXCL8, MMP3; components also shown). Primary harms: macrophage efferocytosis of labeled apoptotic cells at 60–90 min, viability-normalized with extracellular signal quenched; and 48-h FLS matrix repair (closure plus collagen/fibronectin deposition or contraction, and viability). Estimate donor-average within-donor perturbation effects and context interaction using prespecified hierarchical models with myeloid/FLS donor effects and batch blocks; binomial denominator model for proportions; donor-cluster bootstrap, all donor points, 95% uncertainty intervals, QC and sensitivity. Clinician/lab experts set SESOI and harm/equivalence margins before unblinding. Support requires engaged drug/genetic loss concordance, WT but not kinase-dead rescue and context-specific benefit/harm beyond agreed bounds. Adverse adequate-precision absence of benefit or broad harm weakens prioritization. Failed engagement/rescue, toxicity, poor yield or margins crossed by wide intervals are inconclusive. Follow-up requires independent donors and later delivery/in-vivo/clinical work.

## Method comparison and deliverable

The Harbor baseline is the transparent paired six-experiment score analysis above. A same-input secondary alternative is a low-dimensional hierarchical shrinkage model of the E-MTAB-8316 gene-response vector, predeclared inflammatory/ECM modules and donor-pair deviations/covariance, evaluated by leave-one-pair-out prediction and interval/directional calibration (never splitting genes within a pair). It could show coordinated response, donor heterogeneity and module covariance lost by two fixed scores, but six pairs/reused FLS donors make it unstable and it cannot establish target specificity; defer unless QC passes and fit is identifiable. Estimated Harbor CPU budget is 4 cores/8–16 GiB/<30 min for baseline or alternative; estimates unmeasured, GPU unjustified.

For the prospective study, compare paired donor mixed-effects estimates (primary baseline) with a nonlinear hierarchical exposure–target-engagement–function model estimating context-specific Emax/EC50, donor effects and joint benefit/harm. Inputs include free drug, engagement, AXL/TYRO3, genetic loss/rescue and functional endpoints; hold out entire donors, compare uncertainty/coverage and predictive calibration, but base mechanism conclusions on engagement and rescue—not small prediction gains. This model may distinguish exposure-toxic from target-engagement-linked responses and quantify functional window. Defer if engagement/exposure assays, sample size, identifiability or held-out calibration fail. Unmeasured planning estimates: baseline CPU 4 cores/8–16 GiB/<1 h; mechanistic model CPU 8 cores/16–32 GiB/1–4 h; no GPU justified. No models were fitted.
