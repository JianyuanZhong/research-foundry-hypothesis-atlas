## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

After a person has a current blood-biomarker panel, is the earlier panel worth retaining for risk triage when the current-only risk estimate is ambiguous, and does that incremental value remain valid when the assessment calendar era changes? This matters because a historical result could change who is referred for review or prevention research, but an apparent benefit could instead reflect the fact that people return for repeat assessment, the spacing between assessments, or an era-specific measurement/ascertainment process.

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among participants with a valid paired panel and a verified five-year assessment landmark, adding prior-panel values to current values plus repeat-spacing metadata improves held-out prediction of a later qualifying recorded target more in the prespecified middle two quintiles of current-only risk than in the lower or upper tails, and this middle-band prior-value increment is retained, within a prespecified practical margin, when models fitted only through 2012 are evaluated without refitting in 2013.

The estimand is predictive information, not the causal effect of ordering a repeat panel. Let M0 contain current position-1 values, approved age, approved sex and T1 calendar year; M1a add verified assessment spacing L; and M1b add prior position-0 values. For participant-level held-out loss, define
`d_g = BS(M1b)-BS(M1a)`
in current-only risk band g. Negative values favor prior values. The primary interaction is
`D_mid-tail = d_middle - (d_lower+d_upper)/2`.
The temporal estimand applies early-fitted M0/M1a/M1b to the late era and is
`tau_mid = D_mid-tail,late(no-refit)-D_mid-tail,early`.
The principal transport claim requires the confidence interval for `tau_mid` to lie within a prespecified practical margin of [-0.002, 0.002] Brier units, with no material calibration deterioration and no reversal of the middle-band direction. The 55–64 age group and age-aligned contrasts are secondary descriptive sensitivities, not substitutes for the middle-risk estimand.

A result cannot establish that repeat testing, monitoring, treatment, a threshold, or referral improves patient outcomes. It can test whether prior values contain conditional prognostic information for recorded future categories and whether that information appears robust to a defined calendar-era shift.

## Evidence boundary and substantive advance

The strongest local evidence supports availability of paired field-instance columns, two assessment-date fields, demographic fields, position-matched diagnosis code/date arrays, and death-date fields. It does not establish biomarker identities, units, allowable clinical ranges, missing-value meanings, specimen collection time, disease onset, code-domain mapping, individual observation end, or complete five-year ascertainment. Field-instance suffixes are not elapsed time, and parseable numbers are not clinical semantics.

Existing parent evidence supports a careful random-split, selection-aware decomposition: M0 to M1a measures repeat-spacing information and M1a to M1b isolates prior values conditional on current values and spacing. The temporal parent supports adding an early-fit/late-test stress test, but its primary emphasis is age modification. This crossover makes the clinically more direct current-risk ambiguity subgroup the transported estimand. A positive result would identify a reproducible prognostic-information target for an external validation or prospective selective-monitoring study; a null or era-specific result would warn against carrying the finding into a new workflow. This is a substantive validity question, not a small model leaderboard comparison.

## Selection control and no-refit transport

Create a flow from the population anchor through T0-complete, valid paired observation, complete panel, timing window, prior-record exclusion, early/late era, current-risk band and outcome categories.

Among T0-complete participants, define S=1 as valid paired T1 plus complete panel. Fit a regularized repeat-observation propensity using only pre-T1 T0 panel values, age, sex and T0 calendar year. Fit it in the early training data, apply it to late T0-complete people, truncate stabilized inverse-probability weights at the early 1st/99th percentiles, and report overlap, maximum weight, standardized differences and effective sample size. This is a measured-selection transport sensitivity under a missing-at-random assumption, not causal correction and not evidence that returning for assessment causes benefit.

For each early training fold, fit the current-only target/death-risk model, derive 20th and 80th percentile cut points from training-only cross-fitted M0 target-risk predictions, and freeze them. Apply the frozen cut points to late predictions; neither late outcomes nor M1b predictions define bands. The primary interaction is the middle two quintiles versus the lower/upper tails; report exact counts and require at least 200 eligible participants and 30 target events in every interaction cell. If early-to-late overlap is absent, or late effective sample size is below 200 or below 50% of the unweighted cell size, the transport claim is inconclusive.

## Models, alternatives and evaluation

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on `eid) within early data. All imputation, scaling, regularization, propensity fitting, tuning and early calibration are training-fold-only. No `eid) may occur in multiple folds.

Transparent nested models:

- **M0:** current position-1 panel, approved age, approved sex and T1 calendar year.
- **M1a:** M0 plus verified L, measuring information in repeat spacing.
- **M1b:** M1a plus all eight literal position-0 panel values, the primary prior-value increment.
- **M1c:** M1a plus eight raw changes (T1-T0) as a representation sensitivity; annualized changes are allowed only if timing/units justify them.
- **A1:** M1b plus prespecified current/prior discordance contrasts and current-risk-band interaction, with shrinkage; it is interpretive, not a replacement for the nested M1b-vs-M1a estimand.

Use regularized multinomial probability models for recorded categories. If all timing and ascertainment gates pass, use a common regularized discrete-time competing-risk hazard model and cumulative-incidence predictions as a separately labelled sensitivity.

Matched learned alternative:

- **M2:** a small gradient-boosted multinomial model (or cause-specific hazards only when gated), with exactly M1b inputs and a prespecified depth/regularization grid, fit within each early training fold and frozen for late evaluation. A matched M2a removes prior fields but retains all other inputs.

M2 may reveal threshold, nonlinear current/prior discordance and interval interactions that additive M1b averages away. It is scientifically useful only when those shapes are stable across folds, calibrated, and reproduced in late no-refit data; it cannot replace the transparent nested estimand for a small score gain. The transparent model is primary because it identifies whether the information comes from prior values rather than spacing. A mechanistic organ-score model is deferred: field identities, units, assay quality and specimen timing are not verified, so biological organ assignment would be fabricated.

Report early cross-fitted and late no-refit `d_lower`, `d_middle`, `d_upper`, `D_mid-tail), and `tau_mid), all raw and repeat-selection-weighted sensitivities, multiclass Brier and log loss, reliability/resolution decomposition, calibration intercept/slope, target/death observed-versus-predicted summaries, fixed descriptive risk movement at 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10% and 15%, and optional exploratory net benefit. Thresholds are not validated clinical utilities. Use paired participant bootstrap intervals preserving era, folds and interaction strata; the late bootstrap holds early-fitted models/weights fixed and is labelled test-sample uncertainty. A permitted sensitivity refits the early pipeline in prespecified early bootstrap samples; if not run, state that model-training uncertainty is not included.

## Falsification and interpretation

Retain exact paths, source/schema hashes, headers, row counts, key uniqueness, join coverage, overlap agreement, gate counts, parsing failures, interval/horizon checks, fold manifest, predictions and weight diagnostics. Run:

- endpoint-label permutation within folds and eras;
- prior-panel permutation within era, current-risk band, interval, missingness and selection strata while preserving current values and L;
- deliberately broken code/date position pairing, which must be detected before any accepted result;
- nested M0 versus M1a versus M1b comparison;
- complete-case versus prespecified fold-local missingness handling;
- raw versus change representation only under verified timing/units;
- early/late era-label permutation control;
- calibration, resolution, fold-instability and early-to-late covariate-overlap checks.

Supportive evidence requires passed timing, biomarker and outcome gates; at least 100 target events overall and minimum late-cell support; no integrity or leakage failures; a negative (improving) M1b-M1a Brier contrast in the middle band with a 95% upper bound below zero; a middle-tail advantage not contradicted by weighting/calibration; and a late no-refit `tau_mid` inside [-0.002,0.002] with no direction reversal. Stronger support requires stable nonlinear shape and selection-weighted results.

Adverse evidence is no or worsening prior-value increment, a gain attributable to M1a spacing rather than M1b prior values, disappearance under selection weighting or permutation, calibration degradation, an early-to-late reversal, or drift outside the practical margin. This weakens the case for retaining prior values for this recorded-risk task; it does not prove no clinical value in another population.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed semantic/timing/ascertainment gates, fewer than 100 target events, sparse cells, poor overlap/ESS, unstable weights, endpoint ambiguity, broken pairing, fold instability, or materially conflicting missingness/endpoint results. Inconclusive is not evidence against the hypothesis.

Computationally checkable claims are source integrity, flow counts, era and band assignments, model fits, held-out predictions, score/calibration arithmetic, overlap/ESS, uncertainty and permutation behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for biomarker meaning, specimen timing, disease onset, complete ascertainment, utilities/action thresholds, causal repeat-testing effects, treatment benefit, mechanism, external transport beyond measured support and prospective policy evaluation.
