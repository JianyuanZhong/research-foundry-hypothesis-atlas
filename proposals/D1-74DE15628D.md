# Episode 72 child: does repeat-lactate queue value survive generic measurement intensity?

## Decision question and substantive advance

The parent established a rigorous question about optional repeat-lactate review under a fixed capacity and across hospitals with different repeat-lactate availability. Its strongest unresolved weakness is that lactate availability, result age, and numeric value may be proxies for a broader phenomenon: some patients are watched, sampled, and documented more intensively. A lactate block can appear useful simply because it marks that workflow.

This child tests the sharper claim:

> In the complete baseline/L24 population, does adding the optional repeat-lactate process and numeric result improve a within-hospital fixed-capacity mortality-review queue beyond an outcome-blind generic measurement-intensity block built from non-lactate laboratory and vital-observation density?

The advance is an explicit nested comparator for low-cost surveillance/data-density signals. A positive result would show that the lactate block carries ranking information not reproduced by generic monitoring intensity. A null or adverse result would be clinically useful: it would redirect attention from a lactate-specific queue toward a general surveillance signal, and would argue against presenting repeat lactate as uniquely informative.

This is a prognostic re-ranking experiment, not an intervention or treatment-effect study.

## Evidence boundary

The inspected full-text XML for Baysan et al. (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750, PMCID PMC9444407) reports a recalibrated APACHE IV C-statistic of 0.62 in its MIMIC validation cohort and 0.64 after adding 24-hour lactate. That supports modest incremental prognostic information from a late lactate in that cohort. It does not support a fixed-workload queue gain, a lactate-ordering policy, generic-attention independence, transportability to EICU hospitals, or patient benefit.

The configured research-ambition README was read for the three demonstrations and their availability limitations. They are not treated as evidence for the present clinical claim; in particular, unavailable main-paper or supplementary methods are not inferred. The available EICU structured records cannot adjudicate whether a pressor was administered, whether a result was displayed, whether a clinician reviewed it, or whether testing changed care.

## Claims being separated

The following claims must not be conflated.

1. A repeat-lactate block can improve an observed risk ranking in this EICU snapshot.
2. That improvement remains after adjustment for generic, outcome-blind measurement intensity.
3. The numerical repeat-lactate value contributes beyond both generic intensity and repeat-lactate availability/timing.
4. The result is stable across hospitals with different lactate availability.
5. Ordering or reviewing repeat lactate improves patient outcomes.

Only claims 1–4 are computationally testable here. Claim 5 requires an intervention study with ordering, display, treatment, harms, workload, and patient-important outcomes.

## Models and exact feature blocks

All models use the same people, outcome, folds, preprocessing family, and fixed L24 snapshot. No hospital identifier is a predictor.

### Core clinical-state block C

C contains:

- age and age squared;
- Female and Male indicators, omitting Other/Unknown;
- t0/1440 and its square;
- baseline collection lag (t0-B_offset)/360, ln(B), and ln(B)^2;
- for each fixed non-lactate marker below, the latest valid numeric value in [t0,L24];
- for heart rate, respiration, and SpO2, last, median, minimum, and maximum accepted values in [L24-360,L24].

C deliberately does not contain missingness flags, measurement ages, observation counts, or lab-result counts. Missing continuous C values are imputed using the training-fold median after scaling; the missingness mechanism belongs to the explicit generic-process block below.

The fixed non-lactate numeric marker keys are:

potassium|mmol/L, sodium|mmol/L, chloride|mmol/L, BUN|mg/dL, glucose|mg/dL, creatinine|mg/dL, bicarbonate|mmol/L, calcium|mg/dL, Hgb|g/dL, platelets x 1000|K/mcL, WBC x 1000|K/mcL, albumin|g/dL, total bilirubin|mg/dL, paO2|mm Hg, paCO2|mm Hg, and pH with missing or blank labmeasurenamesystem.

For these numeric markers, use finite coercion, exact analyte/unit/window/range rules, revision deadlines, greatest-final-revision selection, conflict invalidation, and duplicate collapse. The admissible ranges must be frozen in the compiler manifest from the parent implementation; no outcome-based range or feature selection is allowed.

For vitalPeriodic, accept heart rate 20–250/min, respiration 2–80/min, and sao2 40–100%. At each patient/timestamp, invalidate contradictory valid values and collapse identical ties to the smallest vitalperiodicid. Counts are resolved timestamps, not export rows. These are five-minute summary observations, not waveforms and not clinician action records.

### Generic measurement-intensity block G

G is constructed before outcomes are read by the feature builder and contains no measurement values.

For all lab rows belonging to a primary-cohort patient:

- exclude any row whose trimmed, case-folded labname is lactate;
- require finite labresultoffset in [t0,L24] and finite labresultrevisedoffset <= L24;
- regard a test event as the unique tuple (patientunitstayid, normalized labname, normalized labmeasurenamesystem, labresultoffset); retain the smallest labid for exact duplicates;
- do not require a finite numeric result for event-density features, because a recorded non-lactate test/result event is the process signal. Such rows cannot enter C’s numeric values unless they satisfy the exact numeric marker rules.

For each of four half-open bins, [0,360), [360,720), [720,1080), and [1080,1440] minutes relative to t0, create:

- lab_draw_count_b = log1p(number of distinct labresultoffset values);
- lab_analyte_event_count_b = log1p(number of distinct normalized analyte/unit/offset tuples);
- lab_breadth_b = number of distinct normalized analyte/unit pairs.

Also create the full-window number of distinct non-lactate analyte/unit pairs and the normalized time from L24 to the latest finalized non-lactate lab event, capped at 1,440 minutes; use an all-missing indicator when none exists.

For each of the same four bins and each of heartrate, respiration, and sao2, create log1p of the number of distinct resolved observationoffset timestamps with an accepted value. Create log1p of the number of distinct timestamps with at least one accepted signal and the capped terminal gap from L24 to the last such timestamp.

Finally, for each of the 16 fixed non-lactate markers, add:

- marker_available;
- measurement age (L24 - latest collection offset)/720, when available;
- a missing-age indicator.

G therefore represents generic test density, analyte breadth, vital-monitor density, and reporting/measurement recency. It is not named or interpreted as verified clinician attention: EICU has no reliable clinician-order, display, or review timestamp. G must not use lactate rows, lactate values, discharge fields, hospital identifiers, post-L24 data, or outcome-derived selection.

### Lactate process P and numeric value V blocks

P contains only:

- F_available;
- follow-up measurement age (L24-F_offset)/720 when F exists;
- an explicit missing-age indicator.

V contains:

- ln(F) and ln(F)^2, when F exists;
- training-fold median imputation for missing numeric F.

The models are:

- C: core clinical state;
- CG: C + generic measurement intensity;
- CGP: C + G + lactate availability/timing;
- CGPV: C + G + P + repeat-lactate numeric value;
- CPV: C + P + V, without G, retained as the pre-comparator apparent lactate sensitivity arm.

The primary comparison is CGPV versus CG. The process-versus-value decomposition is CGP versus CG and CGPV versus CGP. CPV versus C is descriptive only: it must not be compared as if it had the same nuisance-process adjustment as the primary models.

This model hierarchy intentionally demotes the parent’s unadjusted “lactate process versus baseline” decomposition. It is still useful for continuity, but it cannot answer whether the signal is specific to lactate rather than generic surveillance.

## Fitting, ranking estimand, and calibration

Use hospital leave-one-out prediction on the fixed primary hospital set. For each held-out hospital, fit unweighted L2 logistic regression (lbfgs, C=1, max_iter=10000, tol=1e-8, seed 20260972) on all other hospitals.

Within each outer training set, winsorize continuous predictors at training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize using training means and population SDs, and mean-impute missing continuous predictors after scaling to zero. Do not scale indicators and do not drop a feature for a fold. All preprocessing, including imputation, is fit within the outer training data.

For model m, let s_im be its held-out raw linear predictor. In hospital h, let k_h=ceil(0.10 n_h), and define Q_m,h as the k_h patients with the largest s_im, breaking ties by ascending patientunitstayid. The primary capture is:

C_m,10 = [sum_h sum_(i in Q_m,h) D_i] / [sum_h sum_i D_i].

The primary generic-adjusted lactate contrast is Delta_L|G = C_CGPV,10 - C_CG,10. Report percentage points and the bootstrap 95% interval. Secondary contrasts are Delta_G = C_CG,10 - C_C,10, Delta_P|G = C_CGP,10 - C_CG,10, Delta_V|GP = C_CGPV,10 - C_CGP,10, and Delta_L|C = C_CPV,10 - C_C,10. Also report the number of deaths gained and lost among patients whose queue membership changes.

A 20% queue uses k_h=ceil(0.20 n_h) and is secondary; it cannot rescue a failed 10% result. The denominator is all deaths in the complete primary cohort, not only patients with F.

Nested Platt calibration is not part of the primary rank estimand. A strictly increasing calibration map leaves each within-hospital ordering unchanged, so requiring calibration to pass before accepting a ranking result would test a different property and could fail the stated queue question for a mathematically irrelevant reason. Report raw-score discrimination/ranking outputs and calibration-in-the-large/slope as secondary probability diagnostics. If probability calibration is desired, fit the parent’s inner hospital-out-of-fold two-parameter Platt map using training hospitals only, apply it to the held-out hospital, and report its slope; do not use calibrated probabilities to form the primary queue. A non-positive fitted slope is a calibration/model warning and is reported, not silently used to reverse the raw-score queue. Threshold DCA and fixed risk-threshold claims are dropped from the primary experiment because they are not relevant to a within-hospital fixed-quota ranking decision.

## Hospital availability and robustness

Define hospital F availability before fitting and without mortality as the fraction of complete baseline/L24 patients with F_available=1. Freeze high availability as hospitals at or above the population median and low availability as hospitals below it. Report Delta_L|G, Delta_P|G, and Delta_V|GP within each stratum, plus each hospital’s paired contrast.

The primary support label requires:

- all computability gates pass;
- the pooled Delta_L|G 95% lower bound is at least +3 percentage points;
- the numeric contrast Delta_V|GP has a 95% lower bound above zero;
- neither F-availability stratum has an upper confidence bound below zero for Delta_L|G;
- at least 70% of finite leave-one-hospital-out deletions within each F-availability stratum have positive Delta_L|G;
- the 20% Delta_L|G is not opposite in direction.

The +3-point threshold is a prespecified decision-relevance anchor for modeled queue capture, not a validated utility threshold. The value-contrast criterion is deliberately separate: a positive full contrast with a nonpositive V contrast is process-specific residual information, not evidence that the numeric lactate itself adds value.

As a secondary, outcome-blind robustness analysis, freeze the hospital median of the patient-level G intensity score (defined as the standardized sum of the four lab-draw and four vital-any log counts) and report the same contrasts in low- and high-intensity hospitals. This analysis is descriptive and cannot replace the prespecified F-availability strata, because the intensity score is partly the comparator under test.

Use copied-hospital bootstrap uncertainty: sample eligible hospitals with replacement, retain copied originals as held-out groups and exclude each copied original from its corresponding training data, then refit preprocessing, models, raw predictions, and queues in each replicate. Use 2,000 replicates with seed 20260972; require >=95% finite converged pooled replicates. Report percentile 95% intervals for pooled and stratum-specific captures, all nested contrasts, calibration diagnostics, and deletion results. Stratum intervals additionally require adequate sampled representation of that stratum; otherwise report them as unavailable rather than silently pooling.

## Falsification and interpretation states

Apply source, flow, feature, fold, and convergence gates before interpreting an effect.

- Not computable: source/header/hash mismatch; changed cohort flow; missing columns or keys; incorrect one-person selection, revision/conflict/tie rule, window, or availability deadline; any leakage; failed hospital gates; incomplete held-out predictions; nonfinite fit; or fewer than 2,000 valid pooled bootstrap refits. Do not substitute random splits, pooled folds, raw unheld-out probabilities, the measured-overlap cohort, altered windows, or post hoc strata.
- Specific lactate support: all gates pass; the pooled Delta_L|G lower bound is >=+3 points; Delta_V|GP lower bound is >0; availability strata are not wholly negative; deletion stability and 20% direction pass. This supports robust added re-ranking information from the lactate block beyond the defined measurement-density comparator.
- Process-specific lactate support: pooled Delta_L|G meets +3 and robustness passes, but Delta_V|GP does not have a positive lower bound. The result supports residual information in optional lactate availability/timing, not a unique numeric-lactate value or a policy to order tests.
- Generic-surveillance explanation: Delta_G is material while Delta_L|G is adverse, near zero, or below +3. The apparent unadjusted lactate gain is compatible with generic measurement intensity; it does not establish that G is causal clinician attention.
- Hospital-process dependence: pooled Delta_L|G is positive but one availability stratum is wholly negative or deletion stability fails. The result is not robust across observed EICU lactate processes.
- Opposite: the pooled Delta_L|G upper bound is below zero, or both F-availability strata are wholly negative. This opposes the locked optional-lactate re-ranking claim for this snapshot and quota, not the use of lactate in every clinical setting.
- Adverse to materiality: the interval is nonnegative but its upper bound is below +3, with other gates adequate. The result does not meet the prespecified materiality claim.
- Inconclusive: all other computable outcomes, including intervals spanning the margin, unstable decomposition, discordant queue sizes, or insufficient stratum bootstrap support. Do not interpret a favorable point estimate as support.

Even “specific lactate support” remains noncausal. It does not show that clinicians paid attention, that repeat testing was ordered for the same reason in every hospital, that the result was visible in time, that lactate measures occult hypoperfusion, that review changes treatment, or that mortality, safety, workload, cost, or equity improves.

## What remains uncertain

G is a deliberately observable proxy for measurement intensity, not a validated measure of attention. VitalPeriodic may reflect automatic monitor capture rather than a clinician decision, and a lab event may reflect a standing protocol rather than review. Conversely, excluding all lactate rows from G does not remove every pathway by which clinicians respond to lactate. The structured data lack order time, display time, clinician review, assay quality, dose/indication, treatment assignment, competing risks, cause/time of death, utility weights, and patient preferences.

A genuinely independent health system, synchronized order/result/display data, adjudicated clinical review and assay semantics, and stakeholder-specified queue harms are needed to establish transportability or usefulness. A prospective impact study—ideally randomized at the ordering or review-policy level—is required before claiming patient benefit.
