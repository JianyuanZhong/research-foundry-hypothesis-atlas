# Admission-anchored competing observed states of documented HCC pathway change

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly freeze an adult first-observed HCC-coded admission cohort, exact order/procedure dictionaries, the two-window documented-pathway states, and a terminal-state analysis in which every eligible admission remains in the primary population. It must fit a matched transparent baseline (B0), a same-input early-laboratory model (B1), and an interpretable irregular longitudinal alternative (B2); produce locked test predictions and calibrated uncertainty; and emit transition/state audits, missing-documentation bounds, falsification analyses, and a claim-to-output table.

This is a substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. The parent made pathway change observable but its primary R analysis still required a valid 24–72-hour window, continued local observation through 72 hours, and an assigned later state. That can select on a downstream care/documentation process. This child repairs the estimand: there is no primary restriction to still-inpatient status at 72 hours, no requirement that P1 be assigned, and no post-window order-density adjustment. Early discharge, a qualifying procedure before 72 hours, absent later qualifying documentation, stable documentation, clearly documented pathway change, and ambiguous documentation are mutually exclusive observed endpoint states.

## Unresolved question, supported evidence, and hypothesis

The available local evidence supports the existence of dated encounters, HCC-like diagnosis strings, numeric laboratory records, non-drug orders, and procedure strings. It does not establish pathology-confirmed HCC, stage, tumor burden, resectability, treatment intent, recommendation, scheduling, completion, appropriateness, benefit, harm, or complete outside-hospital capture. An order is a documented record, not intent; a procedure string is a recorded event, not proof of the reason or clinical success.

The unresolved question is:

> Among adults with a first observed HCC-coded inpatient admission and no valid-start liver-directed procedure in the first 24 hours, does numeric hepatic/renal laboratory information observed in [0,24) increase the held-out, calibrated probability of a clearly documented pathway change across the subsequent [24,72) order window, after accounting for admission/pre-admission information and the initial documented order state, when early discharge, early procedure, absent later documentation, and ambiguous documentation remain competing observed states?

The primary hypothesis is a recorded-workflow association: worsening or coupled hepatic/renal laboratory movement is associated with a higher probability of the clearly classifiable state C1, and B1 has a prespecified incremental absolute-risk contrast for C1 over B0. This is not a claim that physiology caused a clinical decision or that the pathway change was appropriate.

Define the primary terminal state Y at 72 hours as follows, in priority order:

- D0: discharge before 72 hours with no qualifying procedure before discharge. This is an observed early-discharge state, not treatment failure.
- H0: the first valid-start qualifying E/A/S/X procedure begins in [24,72) before D0. The procedure family is retained as a substate. H0 prevents later order-state labels from being interpreted as the first observed transition.
- N1: no qualifying E/A/S order is opened in [24,72), among records not assigned D0 or H0. This means no later qualifying documentation in the local order source; it is not stable care, absent intent, or no clinical change.
- S1: P0 is one clear family E/A/S and P1 is the same family.
- C1: P0 is one clear family E/A/S and P1 is a different single family or the later state is M (multiple/unassignable). This is the primary clearly documented pathway-change state.
- U1: P0 is M or N and P1 is qualifying or M, or another record cannot support a directional comparison. This preserves ambiguous documentation as observed uncertainty rather than calling it change.

For records without D0/H0, P0 is derived from qualifying orders opened in [0,24) and P1 from qualifying orders opened in [24,72). P0 and P1 are E, A, S when exactly one family is represented; M when two or more families or a potentially therapeutic unassignable string is represented; N when no qualifying family is represented. A P0=N/M record with no P1 remains N1, not a stable or negative clinical plan. This state partition is exhaustive for eligible records after invalid times are reported. It does not condition on continued hospitalization, complete later documentation, P1 assignment, or a later procedure.

The primary estimand is the standardized locked-test contrast
[
\Delta_{C1}=N^{-1}\sum_i[\hat p_{B1,i}(C1)-\hat p_{B0,i}(C1)]
]
over every eligible locked-test admission, with the full multinomial state vector reported. The prespecified relevance threshold is ΔC1 >= 0.01 absolute probability points and a two-sided patient-bootstrap interval excluding zero. Secondary contrasts are the B1-B0 changes in S1, N1, D0, H0 and U1 probabilities and the clear-initial-state contrast in the P0=E/A/S subset; these do not replace the all-admission primary estimand. The model comparison is predictive evidence about recorded states, not a test of clinical utility.

A supportive result requires adequate locked support for C1 and the other competing states, a calibrated B1, ΔC1 meeting the relevance/uncertainty gate, and attenuation under temporal/state-label falsifications. A stronger supportive result is a stable, calibrated B2 improvement beyond B1 with the same ΔC1 direction. An adverse result is a null or opposite ΔC1, poor calibration, dependence on one service/string/era, or persistence after lab-time/state-label permutation. An inconclusive result includes sparse C1/H0 states, dominant U1/N1, missing or invalid native times, dictionary instability, model nonconvergence, or intervals too wide to distinguish the relevance threshold. Any result is limited to the recorded-state estimand.

## Baseline, alternative, split and locked evaluation

B0 is a transparent multinomial terminal-state model for Y in {D0,H0,N1,S1,C1,U1}. It uses only information available no later than hour 24: age, sex, admitting `encounter department`, admission-era descriptors, diagnosis-type descriptors, pre-admission encounter/procedure summaries, pre-admission exact-assay nearest value/recency/count/missingness, non-pathway first-day order/documentation counts, and P0 (including N/M). It contains no discharge field, P1, later order density, later procedure, or post-24 lab. B1 adds the exact [0,24) assay summaries above and uses exactly the same eligible records, preprocessing, split, target and terminal-state definition. The primary incremental question is B1 versus B0, not B2 versus B0.

B2 is an interpretable irregular longitudinal coupled-state model with two low-dimensional latent coordinates labelled hepatic-reserve and renal-stress only as modelling constructs. It uses the exact assay observations/timestamps, assay-specific offsets/noise (not pooled units), shrinkage, irregular observation updates and an observation-intensity component restricted to pre-24-hour sampling, together with every B0 covariate and a terminal-state head for the same six Y states. It can expose nonlinear timing, coupled movement and uncertainty from irregular sampling that first/last summaries lose. It must not use [24,72) order density or any P1-derived feature. Latent names are not diagnoses.

Assign every record by `Patient Master Index` to fitting buckets 0–59, selection buckets 60–69, and locked test buckets 70–79 under catalog policy `participant-sha256-80-20/v1`; buckets 80–99 are inaccessible and are not external validation. No patient crosses partitions. Fit dictionaries, assay semantics, preprocessing, model parameters and B2 specification using fitting data; use selection data only for prespecified tuning/selection; freeze before one locked test evaluation. Do not tune on test state counts.

Report locked multiclass Brier score, log loss, per-state calibration intercept/slope and observed-versus-predicted probabilities, plus the primary ΔC1, all state contrasts and patient-clustered bootstrap intervals (at least 500 resamples; 1,000 preferred). Report P0/P1 transition tables, D0/H0 timing and family counts, N1/U1 rates, order-status distributions, source capture and missing-time audits, and internal descriptions by P0, service and admission era. The primary estimand is not replaced by a complete-case or P0-clear analysis.

For the secondary [72,144) recorded outcome, among all primary-cohort members without a qualifying procedure before 72 hours, report first E/A/S/X procedure or D72 discharge before such a procedure, with administrative censoring at 144 hours and native procedure start times. This secondary is not a primary endpoint and is not used to define C1. Report procedure-family alignment with P0/P1 descriptively only; no alignment is called intent or appropriateness.

## Falsification, missing-documentation and robustness tests

The solver must predeclare and report:

1. native-time leakage proof showing all primary lab timestamps are <24 hours and no post-24 input enters B0/B1/B2;
2. lab timestamp permutation within patient/assay, preserving values, counts and missingness, and a separate within-assay value permutation; temporal physiology-specific signal should attenuate;
3. P1/Y state-label permutation within service/era strata while preserving state frequencies and the pre-24 covariates; a genuine predictive relationship should disappear;
4. removal and addition of pre-24 observation-count, missingness, gap and density terms; post-24 documentation density must never be added as an adjustment;
5. duplicate and same-assay/same-time rules, exact dictionary perturbation, order-status/cancellation sensitivity, and leave-one-assay/order-family/procedure-family-out analyses;
6. midnight procedure-start and missing-procedure-time bounds, plus sensitivity excluding H0 records; these test time resolution and early-procedure selection rather than silently changing the primary estimand;
7. a negative-control routine/central-line/venipuncture workflow endpoint built from the same orders source, clearly labelled as a workflow falsification and not as a clinical outcome;
8. complete-case versus missing-as-observed-state reporting, and extreme bounds that reassign N1 to S1 or C1 in the clear-P0 subset. These bound an unobserved true change; they do not convert missing documentation into a clinical event;
9. stratified calibration/contrast by P0=E/A/S versus M/N, admitting service and calendar era, with no claim of external transportability.

Persistence after timestamp/state-label permutation, strong dependence on one string/service/era, or disappearance when pre-24 observation capture is represented falsifies a physiology-specific interpretation. Sensitivity excluding H0 or restricting to P0-clear is a secondary estimand, never evidence that the primary all-admission result is unbiased.

## Clinical evidence limits and method choice

Clinical review is required for pathology confirmation, stage, burden, vascular invasion, resectability, performance status, intent, recommendation, scheduling, reason for order change, reason for discharge, reason for absent documentation, laboratory units/reference ranges, complications, death/transplant, and outside-hospital or cross-system care. The local lexical HCC detector and order/procedure dictionaries are not validated clinical adjudication. Clinical documents and pathology cannot be time-aligned for this protocol; imaging text lacks validated stage/indication labels; images, waveforms and reliable vitals are unavailable. This study cannot establish a causal effect, treatment benefit/harm, recurrence, progression, organ failure, mortality, appropriateness, clinician intent, or clinical utility. Prospective chart adjudication, linkage to outside care, external validation and an implementation/causal study would be needed for those claims.

The demonstrations were used as method context, not as a topic or reproduction requirement. The available natural-history demonstration supports dated structured-history modelling and held-out calibration; its UK Biobank/Danish data and original external-validation setting are unavailable. The Bayesian longitudinal demonstration supports an interpretable latent-trajectory adaptation, but its genetic inputs are unavailable; B2 is explicitly an EHR-only adaptation. The cancer demonstration's accessible supplement supports modality-ablation thinking, while its main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable and HCC images are absent; no claim is made to have inspected the unavailable text.

B0/B1 are CPU-first chunked CSV aggregation and multinomial fits: estimated 20–60 minutes with 8–16 CPUs and 64–128 GiB RAM, unmeasured in this discovery episode. B2 is estimated at 2–8 hours CPU with 8–16 CPUs and 64–256 GiB RAM, or 1 allocated A100 for batched matrix operations if the implementation uses CUDA; these are planning estimates, not measured full-study runtimes. The future solver envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, 8 GPUs and 28800 seconds; the discovery science budget is 7200 seconds. A GPU is optional and earns no scientific preference; any allocated probe must use cuda:0. No full solver fit or solver/proposer training was run here.

The selected alternative is B2 because the question concerns irregular, differently sampled laboratory trajectories and missingness, where first/last summaries can discard timing and coupling information. A GRU/temporal-attention model is deferred: it may model ordering interactions, but it would add representation complexity before establishing that B2 captures a reproducible recorded-state signal; revisit it only if locked residuals show structured timing error. Text/image/multimodal models are deferred because temporal labels, images and validated clinical semantics are unavailable. A validated liver score is deferred because units, reference ranges and adjudicated disease state are absent. These are evidence-based deferrals, not blanket method restrictions.

## Interpretation gate and actual completion outputs

The scientific deliverable is complete only when the solver has newly produced: (1) frozen cohort/state/dictionary and row/join/time audits; (2) B0, B1 and B2 fitted on the declared training/selection process; (3) locked patient-held-out predictions for all terminal states and the secondary 72–144-hour outcomes; (4) ΔC1, state probabilities, calibration, Brier/log-loss and bootstrap uncertainty; (5) missing-documentation bounds and all falsification outputs; and (6) a claim-to-output table.

Computationally checkable claims are whether these states, models, splits, predictions, uncertainty intervals and falsification analyses were computed as specified, and whether the locked results meet the gates. A supportive locked ΔC1 means early recorded laboratory information is associated with prediction of a clearly classifiable documented pathway change in this local snapshot after the specified baseline adjustment. It does not show that physiology caused reassessment, that a change was needed or appropriate, or that acting on a prediction improves outcomes. Those stronger conclusions require clinical adjudication, missing evidence, external validation, and a separate prospective or causal study.
