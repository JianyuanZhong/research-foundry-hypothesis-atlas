# Transition subtype under matched low final burden at first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and fit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study testing whether the *kind of recorded respiratory transition* carries information beyond final observed burden and the documentation process. Completion is established only by locked patient-level test outputs and audits, not by readiness checks:

- `run_manifest.json`: catalog/source/snapshot/schema hashes, archive members, item-label checks, software, split seeds, locked thresholds/ties/topology/q grid and output hashes;
- `cohort_audit.parquet`: one earliest eligible ICU stay per subject, joins, exclusions, window/coverage flags and primary versus audit strata;
- `hourly_features.parquet`: 12 one-hour rows in [outtime-12h,outtime), physiology, support evidence, source masks and process variables;
- `transition_ledger.parquet`: every qualifying chart/interval/procedure row, validity, channel, half-window state, contradictions and subtype;
- `outcome_topology.parquet`: every candidate later ICU return, transfer bridge, timing/tie status and lower/upper endpoint;
- `cif_contrasts.parquet`, `test_predictions.parquet`, `calibration_uncertainty.parquet`, `falsification.parquet` and `interpretation.md`, each linking claims to estimates, intervals, counts and limitations.

The actual new work is fitting the prespecified competing-risk estimand and the matched observation-aware alternative, then estimating whether subtype contrasts remain in the common low-burden population. No association is claimed here.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The expert residual-instability seed and prior board work support only feasibility: MIMIC contains dated ICU outtimes, later ICU stays, transfer intervals, charted physiology, infusion intervals and respiratory procedure markers. A procedure marker is not continuous ventilation, absence of a row is not proof that support was absent, and ICU return is not always clinically adjudicated deterioration. Existing evidence and the local audit therefore support at most that recorded transitions could be associated with outcomes; they do not establish the association, causality, readiness or intent.

The unresolved clinical claim is whether two patients with similarly low final recorded respiratory/hemodynamic burden have different early failure pathways when one has evidence compatible with recent invasive liberation and the other has only an oxygen-flow/FiO2 transition, after accounting for observation and discharge-process differences.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> In adults at the first valid ICU outtime with low final recorded respiratory and MAP burden and adequate coverage, an invasive-liberation-compatible recent respiratory transition has a higher 48-hour ICU-escalation-compatible cumulative incidence than an oxygen-only recent respiratory transition; recent vasoactive-only cessation has a different, potentially more death-dominant competing-risk signature. The respiratory subtype contrast is expected to persist after adjustment for observation intensity, support intensity and observable discharge process.

This is a retrospective prognostic association hypothesis, not an effect of extubation, oxygen weaning or discharge. The primary reference is durable recorded-off; the key matched contrast is invasive-compatible versus oxygen-only. Joint respiratory-plus-vasoactive withdrawal is prespecified as a separate exploratory stratum, not merged to increase power.

Mutually exclusive exposure ledger, assigned only in [outtime-12h,outtime):

1. durable recorded-off: no positive respiratory or vasoactive evidence in either six-hour half and adequate domain coverage;
2. oxygen-only recent transition: qualifying O2-flow (>2 L/min) or FiO2 (>0.21) evidence in the preceding half, no qualifying O2/FiO2 positive evidence in the final half, no PEEP or invasive/NIV marker in the locked transition window, and no vasoactive-positive interval;
3. invasive-liberation-compatible recent transition: extubation marker (227194) in the locked boundary window, preceding PEEP or FiO2 evidence, no final-half PEEP/FiO2 positive evidence, and no vasoactive-positive interval. This label means compatible evidence only, never continuous ventilation or successful extubation;
4. vasoactive-only cessation: a valid listed vasoactive interval in the preceding half, none in the final half, and no respiratory transition;
5. joint withdrawal: a respiratory subtype (2 or 3) and vasoactive cessation, assigned before the two single-domain groups;
6. active, discordant, unknown or inadequate: retained and reported, never silently recoded as off.

The primary low-burden stratum requires final-half respiratory positive evidence in fewer than one third of one-hour bins and final-half MAP (arterial 220052 or NIBP 220181) observed in fewer than one third low-MAP bins under the locked parent convention, with at least two respiratory and two MAP observations in each half. A full-cohort adjusted analysis and alternative threshold/window grids are secondary. If cells are sparse or overlap is poor, the matched hypothesis is inconclusive rather than merged.

For horizon h in {6,24,48} hours after outtime, define L_h as the cumulative incidence of a later ICU stay preceded by a documented non-ICU transfer bridge, and U_h as any later ICU return. The unobserved true escalation T_h is reported only as L_h <= T_h <= U_h. For exposure contrast A-B, report the compatible interval [L_A-U_B, U_A-L_B], never a midpoint. In-hospital death before return and alive hospital discharge before return are competing first events. Apply a one-minute tie rule, then 0- and 5-minute sensitivities.

Falsification occurs if the direction fails in worst-case bounds, disappears in the low-burden restriction or process adjustment, reverses across horizons, depends on one channel/procedure/tie/topology rule, survives transfer-topology permutation or appears at a pre-outtime pseudo-landmark. A signal in U without L is adverse to the stronger transition interpretation. Wide intervals, high unknown/discordant prevalence, poor positivity, unstable q/topology frontiers or high latent-state entropy are inconclusive.

## Clinical importance and advance

ICU discharge is a high-consequence transition under constrained beds and uneven ward surveillance. A respiratory-transition subtype that remains informative among similarly low final burden could motivate targeted reassessment questions; a null after process adjustment would warn clinicians and modelers not to treat sparse records as physiologic recovery. Separating a possible invasive-liberation pathway from low-flow oxygen reduction also avoids a clinically misleading pooled “support off” label. The advance is a matched, competing-pathway measurement test, not a small predictive-score improvement.

Supportive results require adequate events/overlap, a directionally stable worst-case bound, uncertainty excluding the prespecified null where a directional claim is made, agreement on observable outcomes between methods, and loss of signal under transfer permutation/storetime controls. Adverse results include null/reversal, attenuation to observation-only estimates, dependence on one channel or procedure, persistence after topology permutation, or failure of calibration. Inconclusive results include sparse cells, broad compatible intervals, unstable sensitivity frontiers, high unknown prevalence or non-identifiability. Even supportive results establish only MIMIC-record association; discharge policy, monitoring benefit, preventability, intent and causality require clinician adjudication, external validation and prospective/causal study.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Use fixed subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits with seeds 17, 29 and 43. Fit all thresholds/imputation/model parameters on training/validation only.

The transparent baseline is a pooled one-hour discrete-time cause-specific competing-hazard model for L, U, death and alive discharge, plus an aggregate-return model. Include exposure subtype, final/preceding physiology, support intensity/duration, source-specific masks, row counts, pre-index transfer process proxies, admission context, age, gender, anchor-year group and ICU LOS. Report nested physiology/support-only, observation-only, process-only and full models. Standardize held-out CIFs to the primary low-burden population and report calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier, overlap/effective sample size and subject-bootstrap 95% intervals.

The substantive alternative is a factorized observation-aware hidden semi-Markov model with respiratory states (invasive-compatible, PEEP/NIV-supported, oxygen-only, off, unknown) and hemodynamic states (vasoactive, off, unknown), duration-dependent transitions over eight six-hour intervals, emissions from the same ledger and a separate observation-mask/intensity process. Post-landmark location states are ICU, documented non-ICU and unknown. Fit only on training/validation data, lock test posterior states/CIFs, and reject any latent escalation estimate outside [L,U]. It can reveal ordered duration, discordance and record-generation patterns lost by half-window summaries; it cannot recover true ventilation, intent or unobserved ward care. The baseline remains primary if states are not identifiable or do not replicate across seeds.

A GRU/TCN is deferred because it adds flexible sequence capacity without resolving the specific evidence-tier/topology uncertainty. Causal treatment/discharge-policy estimation, validated readiness/intent NLP, continuous waveforms, MIMIC-CXR images, external validation and outside-hospital readmissions are unavailable or require another study.

## Uncertainty, evidence and interpretation limits

Report counts, positivity, unknown/discordant rates, bound widths, calibration, Brier scores, latent entropy and split stability. Falsifications include within-stay order shuffling, mask-only/mask-removed/physiology-only/process-only models, storetime substitution, procedure removal, channel-specific analyses, q in {0,.10,.25,.50,.75,1}, 6/12/24-hour windows, bridge/topology permutations and +6h transfer shifts, pseudo-landmark before outtime, discharge-as-censoring, careunit/admission-type strata and all tie tolerances.

The local research-ambition demonstrations are methodological context: the natural-history paper supports dated-sequence adaptation but its UKB/Danish cohort is not reproduced; the Bayesian paper motivates latent longitudinal structure but verified genetic inputs are absent and this is an EHR-only adaptation; the cancer paper's main text and full STAR Methods were unavailable, and its chest-X-ray modality is absent, so only a lab/EHR idea is deferred. The relevant MIMIC seeds were read: asynchronous organ recovery and residual instability are conceptually related; diuretic timing, AKI/creatinine, lactate, glucose, sedation, antimicrobial de-escalation and transfusion require different exposures or unavailable adjudication and are not silently substituted. The imported eICU/UKB seeds were assessed as valid source proposals for their own islands but are not eligible MIMIC parents.

Measured discovery facts are the configured 7,200-second discovery allowance, solver envelope (<=16 CPUs, 262144 MiB RAM, <=8 A100 80GB GPUs, <=28800 seconds), exact catalog/schema/archive availability, and the bounded archive member/item-label audit. Full extraction, bootstrap and HSMM runtime are unmeasured estimates: baseline is expected CPU-suitable; the HSMM can begin CPU and request one allocated A100 only if profiling warrants it. No GPU is mandatory and no discovery result is a clinical result.

MIMIC lacks raw waveforms, continuous support between observations, clinician intent/goals of care/treatment limitations, discharge rationale, ward monitoring, adjudicated deterioration, validated transfer semantics and external outcomes. Computation can establish data construction, recorded-support association, calibration, uncertainty and sensitivity consistency. It cannot establish causality, bedside readiness, preventability, planned/unplanned return, clinical utility or policy benefit.
