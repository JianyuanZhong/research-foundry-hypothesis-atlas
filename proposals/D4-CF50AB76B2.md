# Source-robust, calibrated CBC profile contrast for CRC versus other primary cancers

Status: substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. This is a planned Harbor experiment. No participant-level cohort, fit, event count, calibration result, or hypothesis result was computed in this branch.

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The parent demonstrated a well-specified opening but left its most clinically important interpretation unresolved: a relative CBC coefficient or an absolute-risk label can look useful while representing only generic cancer burden, ascertainment, or a selected pattern of repeat observation. A clinically consequential next question is therefore not whether one CBC profile “predicts cancer” in the abstract, but whether a fixed CBC comparison yields a calibrated, source-reproducible difference in which cancer type is subsequently observed, after a delayed risk-set entry that reduces proximity to assessment.

The inspected evidence bounds this question. Virdee et al. [K1] found heterogeneous and limited blood-test trend evidence across test/cancer combinations, including no reported association for some trends with combined haematological cancer. Sala et al. [K2] reported a retrospective CRC-only CBC-trajectory signal up to 24 months before diagnosis, but described it as investigational pending independent validation. Hampton et al. [K3] showed that CRC risk models may report discrimination while omitting calibration and having limited validation, and that population and adoption claims require stronger evidence. These works support the plausibility of a CBC signal and the need for calibration and validation; none tests the UKB fixed-profile, cancer-type, source-robust contrast.

The strongest claim currently supported by the inspected evidence is descriptive: CBC levels or trends can be associated with later cancer-related outcomes in selected or heterogeneous studies. The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among UKB adults with a complete instance-0 CBC and no source-observed qualifying cancer or death at assessment, two fixed, development-defined CBC profiles have a calibrated population-standardized absolute competing-risk contrast for delayed colorectal cancer (CRC; C18–C20) versus all other primary malignant cancers that is concordant in registry and inpatient ascertainment and is not explained by early proximity, calendar era, or selection into a future repeat CBC observation.

This is a source-specific observational risk contrast. It is not a treatment effect, mechanism, diagnostic probability for an individual, cancer-onset date, stage prediction, screening threshold, referral rule, or net-benefit claim.

The advance over the parent is a change in the primary clinical estimand and comparator, not a change in data binding: CRC-versus-all-other-primary-cancer specificity is primary; CRC-versus-haematologic cancer is retained as a prespecified biologically motivated secondary comparator; source concordance and calibration are requirements for the interpretation; and a fixed profile comparison is explicitly preferred to thresholding.

## Clinical decision estimand: what is and is not being estimated

Let g_C be a fixed “CRC-like” profile and g_H a fixed “haematologic-like” contrast, both defined only from the development split:

- g_C: platelet = development mean + 1 SD; haemoglobin = development mean − 1 SD; WBC and RDW = development means.
- g_H: platelet = development mean − 1 SD; haemoglobin = development mean + 1 SD; WBC and RDW = development means.

These are standardized hypothetical profiles for comparing model-implied risks. They are not treatment assignments, observed phenotypes, or recommendations to move a patient’s CBC.

For source s in {registry, inpatient}, class c in {CRC, H, Other}, and the common target population P, estimate F_s,c(g; 2–5), the model-standardized cumulative incidence of the first source-observed event of class c between T0+2 and T0+5 years, with other cancer classes and death as competing events. The clock begins only after the delayed-entry exclusions below. Define:

- Δ_s,c = F_s,c(g_C; 2–5) − F_s,c(g_H; 2–5)
- T_s,Other = Δ_s,CRC − Δ_s,Other — the primary CRC-specific profile contrast
- T_s,H = Δ_s,CRC − Δ_s,H — secondary CRC-versus-haematologic contrast
- B_s = F_s,All(g_C; 2–5) − F_s,All(g_H; 2–5), where All = CRC + H + Other
- Source discrepancy: T_registry,Other − T_inpatient,Other

Report all six component risks needed for the primary and secondary displays, each Δ, T, B, the source discrepancy, and 95% bootstrap intervals in percentage points. Also report 0–90 days and 90 days–2 years, not only 2–5 years. A positive delayed T_s,Other with a small or comparable Δ_s,Other is evidence against CRC specificity even if Δ_s,CRC is positive.

The fixed-profile estimand is preferable to a threshold or net-benefit analysis here. It directly asks whether the observed CBC contrast separates calibrated population risks, while thresholds would require symptoms, test indication, prevalence in the tested population, referral harms, treatment capacity, and patient preferences. No threshold, minimum clinically important difference, decision curve, or net benefit will be claimed. A +1 percentage-point interval boundary may be reported only as a descriptive scale marker; it is not a clinical cutoff. The clinical decision implication is limited to whether an indication-rich validation and utility study is warranted.

## Transparent baseline and substantive learned alternative

The transparent baseline is four source-specific cause-specific Cox models for CRC, H, Other and death, using standardized instance-0 WBC, haemoglobin, RDW, platelets, age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP and T0 calendar year. Fit complete cases after the cohort screen, report the missingness flow, use robust variance, inspect proportional-hazard diagnostics, and convert cause-specific hazards to standardized cumulative incidence under g_C and g_H with competing risks. Aalen–Johansen estimates are a model-free descriptive check of observed incidence, not a substitute for profile standardization.

The substantive same-input alternative is a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model with the same people, source-specific labels, exclusions, dates, covariates, profile definitions, split, and outcomes. Use a first 90-day interval followed by yearly intervals to T0+5; fit registry and inpatient models independently; use separate hazards for CRC, H, Other and death. Development-only tuning is allowed. This model reveals nonlinear CBC ranges, covariate interactions, and time-varying source/class hazards that the additive proportional-hazards baseline can hide. It cannot recover symptoms, CBC order indication, laboratory order timestamps, pathology, stage, treatment, healthcare utilization, transfusion context, or biological onset.

Both models must produce the same profile-specific outputs: F_s,c(g_C), F_s,c(g_H), Δ_s,c, T_s,Other, T_s,H, B_s, source discrepancy, calibration intercept and slope, calibration plots, 2-year and 5-year cumulative-incidence Brier and log scores, and secondary discrimination. Use 500 participant-bootstrap intervals, preserving temporal split and source strata. If the locked holdout contains fewer than 50 H events, H-specific model comparison is inconclusive; do not change the split or endpoint. The primary CRC-versus-Other comparison still requires its own event-support report.

The baseline is primary for presentation because its hazards and absolute-risk decomposition are auditable. ## Falsification and source-robustness checks

Prespecify the following before holdout scoring:

1. Timing: report 0–90 days, 90 days–2 years, and delayed 2–5 years. A signal confined to 0–90 days, or disappearing after that band is removed, supports proximity/ascertainment rather than a stable delayed association.
2. Comparator specificity: report CRC, H and Other Δ values, T_s,Other, T_s,H, and B_s. If Δ_s,Other is comparable to Δ_s,CRC, or B_s is large while T_s,Other is near zero, interpret the result as generic cancer burden/capture, not CRC specificity.
3. Source bridge: fit identical models on registry and inpatient series in the common source-clean population. For same-class paired events report sample size, date-displacement summaries, fractions crossing 90 days, 2 years and 5 years, reciprocal capture, and capture by CBC-score decile and WBC/CRP strata. If fewer than 50 paired events exist for a class, source-displacement interpretation is inconclusive. A same-direction contrast with large source discrepancy is adverse to source robustness, not evidence that either source is biologically correct.
4. Era stability: prespecify calendar-era bins from development data and report source-specific T values by era. A contrast present in only one era is adverse to temporal robustness.
5. Repeat-observation selection: report T_s,Other in R=1 and R=0. Fit a development-only R model from T0 CBC and baseline covariates; use stabilized inverse-probability weighting as a stress test; report weight tails and positivity. Poor positivity is inconclusive and must not be repaired by favorable trimming.
6. Pair integrity: rerun after excluding every invalid or incomplete same-index date/code pair and report changed denominators. Never cross array indices.
7. Label permutation: within development only, permute CRC and H labels while preserving event time, censoring and source; the cancer-type contrast should collapse toward zero. This checks implementation/specificity, not biology.
8. Absolute/relative consistency: report the parent relative summary as secondary only and compare its direction with T values and component absolute risks. A coefficient or rank improvement without calibrated absolute separation is adverse to a decision-facing interpretation.

## Interpretation rules

Supportive results require all of the following for the primary claim: registry and inpatient delayed T_s,Other have the same direction with intervals excluding zero; Δ_s,CRC is not merely a generic burden signal because Δ_s,Other is materially smaller and T_s,Other is positive in both sources; calibration is acceptable by prespecified development-only criteria and held-out Brier/log scores are reported; the result is not confined to 0–90 days, one era, or R=1; source discrepancy and weighting/positivity are compatible with the source-robustness claim; and the label permutation collapses. Agreement would justify an external, indication-rich validation study, not a referral or screening rule.

Adverse results include a delayed reversal, source reversal, large source discrepancy, comparable Other and CRC profile contrasts, large B with small T_s,Other, early-only association, era confinement, repeat-stratum confinement, invalid pairing sensitivity, or a learned-only ranking gain without calibrated absolute-risk separation. These weaken a stable CRC-specific interpretation but do not refute a generic CBC/cancer association.

Inconclusive results include fewer than 50 held-out events for the comparison being interpreted, fewer than 50 paired source events for a bridge claim, intervals spanning both zero and the descriptive +1-point scale marker, poor positivity, nonconvergence, invalid date/code parsing, or a wide null that cannot distinguish no meaningful contrast from the descriptive scale marker. An imprecise null is not refutation.

Computationally checkable claims are binding and namespace integrity, cohort flow, event counts, event windows, profile values, component risks, Δ/T/B values, calibration/Brier/log outputs, source-displacement and capture summaries, era and repeat-selection diagnostics, permutation behavior, bootstrap intervals, and whether the final conclusion follows the rules above. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for symptom status, reason for CBC, lab order and timing, cancer onset validity, pathology, stage, treatment, transfusion, healthcare utilization, causal mechanism, transportability, clinical utility, or intervention effects. Without those data, “higher absolute risk” remains a population-standardized descriptive label.
