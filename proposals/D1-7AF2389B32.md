# Episode 6 successor: a bounded, executable test of repeated instability beyond ICU charting intensity

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`, assessed valid and highest-ranked assessed-valid MIMIC candidate (mu 32.42, sigma 6.67, one comparison).

This is a substantive implementation-and-estimand repair, not a new clinical question. It preserves the parent's primary claim: whether repeated, final-day vital abnormality carries prognostic information beyond a last observation and the intensity of observation. It adds a fail-closed, single-pass data contract, separates descriptive cohort strata from predictors, tightens event/partition feasibility, and makes the computed-output-to-conclusion contract explicit. The experiment remains observational and prognostic.

## Unresolved clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

ICU-to-ward transfer changes the available monitoring and rescue environment. A patient can have an acceptable last vital sign while experiencing repeated instability earlier in the final ICU day. Yet repeated measurements are also more common when clinicians are worried or illness is severe. Thus the unresolved question is not simply whether abnormal observations correlate with ICU return; it is whether a repeated physiologic pattern adds information after a realistic last-value snapshot and explicit chart-observation intensity have been accounted for.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only provenance and measurable-data availability: the frozen MIMIC-IV 3.1 core snapshot contains linked admissions, patients, ICU stays, timestamped ICU chart events, the ICU item dictionary, transfers, and in-hospital death/discharge fields. Direct inspection of the archive headers and dictionary confirms the required columns and item definitions. Neither the expert seed, the earlier candidates, nor the public literature establishes that recurrent instability is independent of charting density, predicts a competing ICU-return/death endpoint in this cohort, or identifies a modifiable discharge policy.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among adults whose first eligible ICU stay in a hospital admission ends while that admission continues, a higher proportion of covered four-hour blocks containing at least one abnormal valid vital observation in the final 24 hours before ICU `outtime` is associated with higher seven-day cumulative incidence of first later ICU return and death before ICU return, and adding this repeated-physiology burden to a last-observation model plus prespecified measurement-density features improves locked-test calibration and Brier score.

The primary incremental contrast is `B_phys - B_density`, defined below. Charted oxygen intensity is a separate secondary contrast (`B_full - B_phys`) and cannot rescue a null primary physiology result. The estimand is prognostic association and incremental prediction at ICU `outtime`; it is not a causal effect of discharge timing, oxygen therapy, surveillance, or treatment.

A current Europe PMC search found a 2026 MIMIC post-valve-replacement benchmark using longitudinal EHR sequences for ICU readmission (Blattmann et al., DOI 10.3390/diagnostics16030447; acquired frozen source `[source checksum]`). That work supports the endpoint's relevance and the need for leakage control, but its sequential-model comparison does not test the narrower physiology-versus-ascertainment question here. It is not evidence for this hypothesis.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Construct the cohort and select the primary index before reading any chart exposure rows or future outcome rows.

1. Parse all timestamp fields with an explicit timezone-naive datetime convention. Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, and `hosp/patients` on `subject_id). Record unmatched keys and impossible chronology.
2. Candidate index stays must have `anchor_age >= 18`, nonmissing `intime), `outtime), and `admissions.dischtime`, `outtime < dischtime`, ICU duration at least one hour, and `deathtime) missing or strictly later than `outtime`. Retain anchor age 91 as the MIMIC >89 category; do not reinterpret it as an exact age.
3. Within each `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, sort candidate stays by `intime`, then numeric `stay_id`, and select exactly one earliest candidate as the primary index. This selection is made without chart-event or future-outcome information. A later eligible stay is an outcome candidate, not another primary opportunity. Retain all eligible non-index stays for chronology auditing and a prespecified all-eligible sensitivity.
4. Set `t0 = index outtime`. The final exposure window is exactly the half-open interval `[t0 - 24 hours, t0)), divided into six consecutive four-hour blocks. Rows at `charttime == t0) or later are excluded. Rows from a different `stay_id), even with matching subject/admission keys, are excluded. `storetime) is retained only for audit.
5. A primary analytic record requires at least four of six blocks to have at least one valid vital-domain observation. All eligible indexes, including records failing this gate, remain in the manifest and are compared by outcome, care unit, anchor-year group, and chart density. The threshold is not changed after seeing events.

For bounded computation, implement this as a two-stage, projection-only pipeline: first read the small admissions/patients/icustays/transfers members and build the frozen index/outcome manifest; then make one sequential pass through `icu/chartevents.csv.gz` inside the ZIP, retaining only the fixed item IDs and rows whose `stay_id) is a selected index and whose `charttime) lies in its pre-`t0) window. Do not materialize the full chartevents table or perform one archive pass per item. The reference implementation must use bounded memory, declare CPU/memory/time resources within `inputs.json` (`science_seconds=7200`), write only aggregate/audit/model outputs, and fail closed if the one-pass projection or output manifest cannot be completed. This is a feasibility contract, not a claim that the scan has already succeeded.

For every retained chart row, preserve `value, valueuom, warning, charttime, storetime, itemid) in an internal audit only; derived outputs must not contain raw clinical rows or notes.

Use `valuenum) for numeric features. Fixed validity rules, applied before any summary:

- HR: 20--250.
- RR: 4--80.
- SpO2: 50--100.
- Each MAP: 20--200 mmHg.
- Temperature: 30--43 °C; Fahrenheit is converted as `(F - 32) / 1.8).
- Oxygen flows: nonnegative.
- FiO2: accept 0.21--1.00 as a fraction or values in (1,100] as percentages divided by 100; normalized values outside 0.21--1.00 are invalid.

Invalid values are counted by item and block and excluded from derived numeric features, never silently repaired. A block is vital-covered if it contains at least one valid HR, MAP, RR, SpO2, or temperature observation. A valid vital is abnormal when HR <50 or >100, RR <8 or >24, SpO2 <92%, MAP <65 mmHg, temperature <36 °C or >=38 °C. Arterial and non-invasive MAP remain separate; combined MAP is the minimum valid MAP within a block.

Primary repeated-physiology exposure:

`abnormal_block_fraction = abnormal_vital_block_count / covered_vital_block_count`.

Save its numerator and denominator, total valid/invalid rows, per-domain counts, and per-domain abnormal indicators. The primary gate guarantees a nonzero denominator. Also retain the six block-level abnormal/covered indicator vector for audit and a prespecified sensitivity using abnormal valid-observation fraction.

For each numeric domain, the last-value snapshot is the median of valid observations at the latest `charttime); ties at that timestamp are resolved by the median, so no nonexistent row ID is assumed. Missing domains remain missing with an observed indicator. Save separate arterial, non-invasive and combined MAP values. Oxygen features are the maximum valid flow over the three flow IDs and maximum normalized FiO2, plus item-specific maxima and observed indicators. Device and code-status text contribute only observed/not-observed indicators and frequency tables of raw values in a restricted audit; they must never be auto-interpreted as a validated support mode or treatment-limitation decision.

## Outcomes and estimands

For each primary index define `tau = min(t0 + 7 days, admissions.dischtime)`; events at `tau) are included. A candidate ICU return is a later ICU stay with the same `subject_id, hadm_id), a different `stay_id), and `intime >= t0). Select the earliest candidate by `intime), then numeric `stay_id). A stay with `intime < t0) is an overlap/chronology anomaly, not a return.

- Cause 1: first qualifying ICU return at or before `tau), provided it is strictly earlier than qualifying death.
- Cause 2: `admissions.deathtime > t0) and `deathtime <= tau), strictly earlier than the first qualifying ICU return.
- If return and death have exactly equal timestamps, label the index tie/indeterminate and exclude it from the primary competing-risk fit; report sensitivities assigning all ties to return and all to death.
- No cause by `tau) is censored at `tau). Death after a return is not cause 2.
- The primary composite is cause 1 or cause 2. Prespecified descriptive sensitivities use 48-hour and 12-hour horizons and follow-up through hospital `dischtime).

Report Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence for each cause and the composite. The primary incremental estimand is the paired locked-test difference in seven-day competing-risk Brier score and calibration slope/intercept for `B_phys) versus `B_density), with hazard-ratio associations per development-cohort SD for burden. These are within-snapshot associations/predictions. They are not bedside thresholds, causal effects, or validated discharge-readiness measures.

## Baselines and analysis

Use the same primary records, labels, preprocessing, subject partition, and locked test set for every nested model:

- `B0): gender, anchor_age, admission_type, admission_location, insurance, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU LOS, hours from `admittime) to `t0), and number of earlier ICU stays in the admission ending by `t0), with missing indicators. `anchor_year_group) is a descriptive stratum and a prespecified categorical covariate only if included consistently in every model; it is not a time split.
- `B_last): `B0) plus last valid HR, separate/combined MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, oxygen flow and FiO2, source/observed indicators and missing indicators.
- `B_density): `B_last) plus six-block vital coverage count, total valid vital rows, per-domain valid-row counts, per-domain observed indicators, oxygen observation indicators, and fixed `log1p) counts.
- `B_phys): `B_density) plus abnormal-block count/fraction and per-domain abnormal indicators. No repeated-burden variable is added to `B_density).
- `B_full): `B_phys) plus oxygen intensity features. This is a secondary bundled-support model; oxygen cannot be used to claim a physiology effect.

Fit two ridge-penalized cause-specific Cox models (return; death-before-return), choosing penalty only by inner development cross-validation. Competing-cause cumulative incidence is derived from the fitted cause-specific hazards; non-target causes are not treated as ordinary censoring in the reported Aalen-Johansen estimates. Use subject-level SHA-256 partitions (60% development, 20% validation, 20% locked test), keeping every admission and stay of a subject together. Development alone determines imputation, one-hot levels, scaling, penalty and any coverage model. Do not use outcome-derived cut points, automated feature search, post-`t0) fields or future charting.

Use 1,000 fixed-seed subject-bootstrap resamples of the locked test set for paired 95% intervals of Brier, calibration and absolute-risk differences, and a subject-clustered bootstrap or robust estimator for coefficients. Require both a total-cohort gate (>=1,000 primary indexes, >=100 composite events, >=60% of all eligible indexes with >=4/6 coverage) and a locked-test gate (>=20 composite events and >=10 of each cause when cause-specific inference is reported). A failed gate is reported as infeasible/sparse, with no threshold relaxation or model substitution. If a cause gate fails, report descriptive Aalen-Johansen estimates and uncertainty only for that cause.

## Falsification, integrity and ascertainment checks

The reference solver must output a machine-readable manifest and a conclusion ledger linking each conclusion to output names/rows. The verifier should check computation, not hypothesis confirmation.

Fail closed on any of the following: a feature row with `charttime >= t0); a chart row from another stay; use of `storetime) for eligibility; duplicate primary index; impossible event chronology; prohibited predictor; subject leakage across partitions; preprocessing fit on validation/test; or an event assigned inconsistently with the tie rules.

Required checks:

1. Repeat the complete pipeline with the exposure window shifted earlier to `[t0-48h, t0-24h)), holding indexes and outcomes fixed. Similar/stronger effects weaken the claim that the signal is discharge-proximal residual instability.
2. Fit density-only and earlier-window-density models. If measurement density explains the result and adding `B_phys) does not improve `B_density), interpret the signal as ascertainment/general acuity, not independent physiology.
3. Permute outcome labels within locked subject partitions while preserving feature coverage; held-out performance must return to chance within bootstrap uncertainty. Failure invalidates the computation, not the clinical hypothesis.
4. Repeat with arterial and non-invasive MAP separately, abnormal observation fraction, two-hour return-gap restriction, tie assignments, 48-hour and 12-hour horizons, and all-eligible-stay sensitivity with subject clustering. Large divergence is selection/definition dependence.
5. Audit code-status and device raw-value frequencies without classifying goals of care or support mode.
6. Compare burden, coverage and density across outcome, careunit, anchor-year group and partition; report missingness, invalid-value rates and unmatched joins.

Supportive results require: (a) a prespecified positive or monotonic burden association in the locked analysis, (b) improved seven-day calibration and/or Brier for `B_phys) versus `B_density) with a paired interval excluding trivial/no improvement, (c) absolute-risk separation that is not merely a tiny AUROC change, (d) direction that is not materially erased by density, earlier-window, MAP-source, tie and return-gap checks, and (e) a successful label-permutation check. Call `B_full) an additional charted-oxygen signal only if it improves over `B_phys) under the same locked evaluation; do not interpret this as delivered oxygen physiology.

Adverse results are a null/negative burden association, no incremental calibration/Brier benefit, or reversal after density, earlier-window, competing-risk, source or selection checks. They would argue that repeated charted instability adds no reliable information beyond last values and observation intensity, or is mainly a general-acuity/documentation proxy. They do not establish that transfer is safe.

Inconclusive results include failed event/coverage/partition gates, sparse or unstable intervals, contradictory sensitivity results, inability to distinguish density from physiology, or failed permutation computation. A failed permutation check is a computational invalidity. No result supports a clinical policy without external validation and prospective evaluation.

## Evidence boundaries and next study

Computationally checkable claims are source/member/schema availability, exact counts, item validity, cohort/index construction, timestamp ordering, outcome labels, model estimates, uncertainty, calibration, missingness, chart-density dependence and falsification outputs. The data cannot adjudicate:

- true delivered FiO2, work of breathing, bedside instability, functional/cognitive readiness, staffing, clinician discharge intent or ward capacity;
- goals of care or treatment limitation from code-status text;
- adjudicated reason for ICU return;
- whether delaying discharge or changing monitoring/oxygen treatment changes outcome;
- causal treatment effects, external validity, transportability, safe thresholds or prospective clinical utility.

Those stronger claims require clinician review of raw records and bedside context, richer prospective physiology and treatment data, external validation in another ICU system, and a prospective surveillance or target-trial study.
