# Provisional hypothesis: visceral fat versus muscle fat infiltration as divergent risk phenotypes

Status: provisional and not selected. This is a design hypothesis, not an observed result.

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with body-composition MRI and repeated functional measurements, at similar total body fat, higher visceral fat will be more specifically associated with subsequent metabolic disease, whereas higher posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration will be more specifically associated with subsequent functional decline or falls. The proposed advance is a test of whether fat distribution identifies distinct downstream clinical trajectories rather than merely predicting a generic health-risk score.

## Unresolved question and rival explanations

Does fat distribution separate metabolic from musculoskeletal/functional risk, or are both associations driven by age, adiposity, physical inactivity, pre-existing disease, reverse causation, or selective attendance at repeat assessment? A clinically important supportive result would show a reproducible, temporally ordered divergence after accounting for total fat and baseline function; it would not establish a causal mechanism.

## Falsifiable test

1. Restrict to participants without prevalent metabolic disease or functional limitation at the first eligible assessment and with valid visceral fat, muscle-fat-infiltration, total-fat, grip, and follow-up data.
2. Use the first assessment as time zero; define subsequent incident metabolic outcomes from diagnosis fields and subsequent decline/falls from repeated functional fields, with explicit lag and censoring.
3. Compare a prespecified composition model (visceral fat and MFI, with total fat held fixed) against a simple total-fat/age/sex baseline. Evaluate calibrated risk and cross-validated discrimination with participant-level splits; report uncertainty and calibration, not only p-values.
4. Test whether the two exposure-outcome contrasts are directionally distinct and robust to excluding early events, restricting to repeat-measurement participants, and adjusting for activity/medication when available.
5. Supportive: both contrasts remain directionally distinct in held-out data with credible uncertainty and do not disappear after lag/selection checks. Adverse: contrasts are absent, reverse, or fully explained by baseline severity/attendance. Inconclusive: sparse events, poor calibration, or wide intervals do not refute the hypothesis.
