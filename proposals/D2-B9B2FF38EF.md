# Is remission-associated MERTK mostly a macrophage-state mixture signal?

**Episode 11 evolution of `[prior hypothesis]`; proposal only.** No analysis/model fit has been run and no biological result is claimed. This is a bounded reanalysis of published source data, not independent replication.

## Question, supported evidence, and clinical relevance

**Falsifiable hypothesis:** In the cross-sectional rheumatoid arthritis (RA) synovial-tissue macrophage samples available in E-MTAB-8322, the remission-versus-treatment-naive difference in aggregate MERTK expression is primarily attributable to a difference in the abundance of MERTK-high macrophage states, rather than a broadly higher MERTK level within comparable states. The rival predicts that remission-associated MERTK differences remain within states after standardizing state proportions.

**Strongest claim already supported:** Alivernini et al. report macrophage-state heterogeneity, remission-associated MerTK-positive subsets, in-vitro fibroblast-repair activity and an association between low MerTK-positive STM proportion in remission and later flare after treatment cessation [K1]. Independent synovial work supports myeloid-state heterogeneity [K2], and matrix remodeling/spatial immune ingress provide a plausible niche and recruitment explanation for apparent cell proportions [K3]. These published observations motivate the question; no result below is newly computed.

**Unresolved claim to test:** For the 7 remission and 6 treatment-naive RA donors in the source dataset, how much of the aggregate MERTK contrast is attributable descriptively to state proportions versus state-conditional MERTK expression, with donors—not cells, barcodes, or sequencing runs—as the inferential units? Published findings are not independent validation of this decomposition. The most serious rival is that differential digestion/freezing, cell survival/recovery, sampling, library depth, treatment selection, age/sex imbalance or state-label instability creates the observed mixture. Cross-sectional groups cannot separate these explanations or establish a transition.

Resolving the decomposition would change which prospective mechanism to prioritize: state/niche abundance and spatial recruitment versus within-state regulation. It would not itself nominate treatment, show macrophage protection, prove MERTK causality, predict clinical outcome, or establish that modulating MERTK is beneficial. A future causal decision needs new donor-matched functional experiments and clinical/expert interpretation.

## Computable experiment and methods

**Time/population boundary:** Cross-sectional E-MTAB-8322 samples recorded as RA remission versus treatment-naive RA, sampled at their single biopsy/assay time. No temporal ordering, paired transition or post-cessation outcome is inferred. The donor is the independent unit (7 versus 6). Before fitting, resolve exact sample/donor/run mapping and count alignment; freeze QC, state-label rules and any expert-defined material-effect thresholds. Report age, sex, run/library, cell-yield/depth, and processing metadata by group; do not overfit adjusted covariates with 13 donors. Treat them as descriptive/sensitivity covariates, not proof of exchangeability.

**Outcome and estimand:** Estimate donor-level macrophage state proportions and donor-state MERTK pseudobulk expression from raw counts. Define states using macrophage features excluding MERTK (including its aliases) and response labels; verify state markers and alignment without using either outcome gene or group label. Decompose remission-minus-naive aggregate MERTK into (i) a composition component using a common/reference state-conditional MERTK profile and differing state proportions and (ii) a within-state component using differing state-conditional MERTK profiles at a common/reference proportion. Predeclare a symmetric/Shapley decomposition so parts sum to total contrast and report the reference convention. Report all components on the same normalized/log-expression scale, donor-level values, donor support and uncertainty. The estimand is descriptive, not causal.

**Baseline (primary):** From the 13 raw cell matrices, perform documented per-cell QC with frozen minimum coverage/doublet rules, pseudobulk raw counts by donor and MERTK-independent state, compute donor-state proportions and state-conditional MERTK, then estimate the decomposition. Use donor bootstrap and donor-level permutation/leave-one-donor-out as uncertainty and influence summaries; never use cell-level p-values. Repeat across predeclared defensible annotation resolutions, QC/depth matching, and donor/run/yield sensitivities. Report if too few cells/donors support a state rather than collapsing groups post hoc. Because there is no cell recovery denominator or gold-standard state labels, do not present observed recovered-cell proportions as absolute in-vivo tissue abundance.

**Learned/mechanistic alternative (same estimand, secondary and gated):** Fit a low-dimensional probabilistic latent-state mixture from the exact same MERTK-excluded cell-by-gene input, donor-balanced so high-yield donors do not dominate and without using response labels. Outer leave-one-donor-out folds hold out all cells/runs from one donor; fit signatures/mixture on remaining donors, align states using MERTK-excluded features, and estimate soft state membership for the held-out donor. Compute the identical composition and within-state components using soft proportions and conditional MERTK. Evaluate held-out state assignment/reconstruction and cross-fold state alignment, and compare component estimates/uncertainty to the fixed-label baseline using a full donor-resampled refit. The purpose is to expose assignment uncertainty that hard-label pseudobulk loses, not improve remission prediction. Defer or omit this alternative if state alignment is unstable, folds leak donors, groups are used to learn states, or the 13-donor sample cannot identify latent components; no GPU is selected. Alternative resource estimate is unmeasured planning only: CPU 4–8 cores, 16–32 GiB, likely <1–4 hours for QC, folds and donor bootstrap, depending on retained cells/features. The baseline is expected CPU 2–4 cores, 8–16 GiB, minutes to hours; no timing probe was run. A100 hardware exists but allocation is not needed absent a measured matrix bottleneck.

**Why this comparison matters:** The baseline yields transparent, donor-level components but assumes hard state labels and can conceal uncertainty in borderline cells. The soft latent alternative can reveal whether a seemingly dominant composition component is an artifact of label assignment. With only 13 independent donors, however, a flexible model may manufacture unstable states and precision from cell count; it cannot compensate for missing clinical or recovery data. Keep it as a sensitivity comparator, not the scientific winner based on model complexity or small predictive gains.

## Outcomes, falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive of composition-dominant hypothesis:** a nontrivial composition component exceeds the within-state component by the prespecified expert-adjudicated material margin; donor-level uncertainty supports that ordering; direction and ordering remain stable under donor deletion, label resolution, QC/depth and yield/run sensitivities, and the latent alternative (if estimable) does not reverse it. This would support prioritizing state/niche abundance or spatial-recruitment follow-up in this cohort, not causal recruitment, MERTK protection, or therapeutic benefit.
- **Adverse to composition-dominant hypothesis:** within-state MERTK is the larger, robust component, or the composition component is near zero/opposite with intervals sufficiently narrow relative to a material margin and insensitive to plausible labels/recovery. This redirects next work toward within-state regulation; it does not prove that MERTK is causal or clinically actionable.
- **Inconclusive/mixed:** wide donor-level intervals, near-zero total contrast (unstable component ratios), opposing components, few state-supporting donors/cells, failed cell/gene alignment, unstable learned states, or conclusions that track yield/depth/processing indicate insufficient resolution or a mixed model. Do not call nonsignificance equivalence or claim falsification from an imprecise null.

The numeric minimum meaningful MERTK contrast/state-fraction difference is not established by available files; a rheumatology/immunology/statistics panel must adjudicate and freeze these margins before outcome computation. If no defensible margin is obtained, report component estimates and uncertainty without a categorical “dominant” claim. Automatic verification can check file joins, MERTK feature identity, donor aggregation, formulae, split isolation, output linkage and consistency of supportive/adverse/inconclusive language with computed intervals. It cannot adjudicate state biology, recovery bias, causal mechanisms, functional protection, or clinical benefit. Conclusions beyond descriptive decomposition need source/assay expert review; target-specific causality requires a separate prospective donor-crossed MERTK loss/rescue and target-engagement study with distinct inflammatory, efferocytosis/repair and viability endpoints. Clinical recommendations require further translational studies.
