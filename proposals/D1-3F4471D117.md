# Selection-robust late instability after live ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable and substantive successor

The future Harbor solver must newly fit and audit a selection-aware, transition-aware prognostic experiment. Completion requires:

1. A reproducible first eligible live-ICU-discharge cohort, plus the broader valid ICU-exit frame used to estimate live-discharge selection. The flow table separates invalid timing, missing joins, coverage insufficiency, and unknown outcome ascertainment.
2. A fixed twelve-bin late-window physiology representation and a separate observation/testing representation, with row-level provenance, clinical-time checks, unit failures, and prohibited-field assertions.
3. Training-only live-discharge selection models applied in selection-score strata and inverse-selection-weighted sensitivity analyses, with overlap, clipping, and effective sample size (ESS).
4. Paired held-out predictions from a transparent order-blind aggregate baseline and a substantive chronological masked GRU, using the same patients, target, source whitelist, split, uncertainty procedure, and underlying pre-outtime inputs.
5. The primary competing-risk transition distribution over ICU readmission (R), timed in-hospital death (D), and alive hospital discharge before 48 hours (S), with no transition by 48 hours (N) retained as administrative event-free follow-up and missing death timing (U) treated as unknown.
6. Paired Brier/CIF/calibration contrasts, selection-stratum interactions, process-only and chronology-shuffle falsifications, unknown-label bounds, and subject-level bootstrap uncertainty.
7. A conclusion audit linking every scientific statement to a computed output and distinguishing prognostic robustness from discharge appropriateness, intervention benefit, preventability, utility, and external validity.

This is a substantive child of the assessed-valid transition-aware proposal. It preserves its live-outtime landmark, exact MIMIC-IV bindings, competing-risk discipline, process-only controls, and ascertainment handling. The new scientific advance is to make live-discharge conditioning itself measurable: the solver estimates whether the late-instability signal is stable across a pre-outtime live-discharge selection score and after inverse-selection weighting, rather than merely adjusting for charting intensity inside the selected cohort. This is a robustness/transport diagnostic, not a causal correction and not evidence that a patient should have remained in the ICU. No fitted result is claimed here.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

At the moment a patient leaves the ICU alive, does the preceding 12-hour physiologic trajectory identify near-term ICU rescue or timed death in a way that is robust to who becomes a live ICU discharge and to the discharge/observation process? Or does the apparent signal mainly reflect selection into live discharge, selective recording, or the opportunity to observe a subsequent ICU return?

This matters because a post-ICU review system would need a signal that survives the ordinary selection imposed by ICU transfer decisions and remains informative across discharge-process conditions. A model that only recognizes a particular discharge pathway, or that predicts early hospital discharge, is not evidence of residual physiologic risk. The study can inform whether a prospective review/monitoring evaluation is justified and what data it should adjudicate; it cannot establish that delaying transfer or adding monitoring improves outcomes.

The strongest available evidence is only that this snapshot contains timestamped ICU boundaries, admissions, transfers, structured bedside observations, interval inputs, laboratory testing, and admission death/discharge fields. The available data do not establish measurement invariance, transport robustness across live-discharge selection, a latent physiologic state, discharge appropriateness, or benefit from a review policy.

### Primary falsifiable hypothesis

Among first eligible live ICU discharges, a model using the ordered twelve-hour pre-outtime history will improve held-out prediction of the first 48-hour observable transition distribution over an order-blind model using the same pre-outtime information summarized without time order. The improvement in adverse R-or-D cumulative incidence must:

- remain directionally consistent across prespecified live-discharge selection-score quintiles;
- persist under process-only inverse-selection weighting and, where overlap permits, the full selection-weighted sensitivity;
- improve calibrated R/D risk rather than only S (alive discharge) or N;
- exceed a process-only history model; and
- attenuate when chronological bins are shuffled.

The unresolved claim is this robustness and incremental chronology claim. It is not a claim that late instability caused an event, that discharge was inappropriate, or that review/monitoring would improve an outcome.

The primary model estimand is the paired held-out difference in transition-aware integrated Brier score over the first 48 hourly bins:
Delta_Brier_GRU = Brier(Gchrono) - Brier(Bagg).
Negative values favor the chronological model. Report cause-specific cumulative incidence functions (CIF_R, CIF_D, CIF_S), CIF_A = CIF_R + CIF_D, and P_N at 12, 24, and 48 hours, with calibration intercept/slope and calibration plots. The primary clinical interpretation requires improvement in calibrated CIF_A and/or its R and D components; improvement only in S or P_N supports a discharge-process association, not residual-risk robustness.

The selection estimand is a standardized contrast of the same held-out predicted R/D risk and observed LII association across the distribution of valid ICU exits. Let L=1 denote an eligible live ICU discharge and let Q be a training-only probability of L=1 among the broader valid ICU-exit frame. Report unweighted estimates, estimates by Q quintile with a LII-by-Q interaction, process-selection inverse-probability weights based on context and prewindow observation process, and a full selection sensitivity adding prewindow physiologic summaries. These quantities describe sensitivity to conditioning on live discharge. They do not identify the risk under a different discharge decision, because non-live-exit records do not supply a counterfactual post-discharge risk. Poor overlap or unstable weights makes the selection-robust result inconclusive, not positive.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The source unit is an ICU stay linked to one hospital admission.

1. Read every icu/icustays row with non-null subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, and outtime. Reject outtime <= intime.
2. Inner join hosp/admissions on (subject_id, hadm_id) and require non-null admittime and dischtime. Reject outtime < admittime.
3. Define the primary live-discharge cohort at t0 = outtime when outtime < dischtime and (deathtime is null or deathtime > outtime). Select the earliest such stay per (subject_id, hadm_id) ordered by outtime. Report all eligible live discharges as a clustered secondary analysis; do not pool repeated stays as independent observations.
4. Define the selection reference frame as all valid ICU stays after steps 1–2, including ICU exits associated with death before or at outtime. Define L=1 using the live-discharge rule and L=0 otherwise. The reference frame is used only to fit selection models; it is not assigned a post-exit R/D label.
5. A coverage-qualified row has at least four of twelve bins with at least two valid core bedside domains. Never silently replace this with the target cohort. Report target-cohort counts and process distributions for rows excluded by coverage and ascertainment.
6. Use a deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split, with no subject in more than one partition. All preprocessing, selection models, weights, model choice, calibration, clipping rules, thresholds, and hyperparameters are fit or selected using training/validation data only. Do not align subjects by calendar date because MIMIC timestamps are shifted by subject.

The feature window is exactly [t0-12 hours, t0], divided into twelve bins [t0-12h,t0-11h), ..., [t0-1h,t0]. Use charttime for charted measurements and starttime/endtime for intervals. Never use storetime for ordering, inclusion, missingness, or duration. No clinical-time row after t0 enters a feature matrix.

## Ascertainable 48-hour outcome

Within (t0, min(t0+48h, dischtime)], determine the first transition:

- R: first later icu/icustays row with the same subject_id and hadm_id, a different stay_id, and intime > t0.
- D: hosp/admissions.deathtime > t0, deathtime <= t0+48h, and no earlier R. If R and D tie, assign D first; the R-or-D composite is unchanged.
- S: dischtime < t0+48h, no earlier R or D, and deathtime is null or deathtime > dischtime with hospital_expire_flag=0. S is an alive hospital-discharge observation boundary, not safe recovery or a negative outcome.
- N: no R, D, or S by 48 hours while the record remains under observation. N is administrative event-free follow-up, not a fourth clinical event.
- U: missing deathtime with hospital_expire_flag=1, or a contradictory record preventing a timed classification. Retain U in flow and bounds analyses; exclude it from complete-ascertainment metrics and never label it N or S.

A known R or D resolves the first state even if another field is incomplete. A later ICU stay after hospital discharge is not R in this same-admission estimand. A non-null death outside the 48-hour window is not D for the primary horizon. Report the transition vector (CIF_R,CIF_D,CIF_S,P_N) and CIF_A, not a binary outcome that treats S as an ordinary negative.

For the primary complete-ascertainment analysis, report R/D/S/N counts and the exact eligible denominator. For all eligible live discharges, use a training-only ascertainment model based on pre-t0 context, coverage, measurement counts, and death-field availability; report stabilized inverse-ascertainment weighting, overlap, clipping, and ESS. Also report adverse/non-adverse U bounds by assigning U to A=(R or D) and to non-A in separate analyses, without presenting either as truth. The older binary R-or-D-with-discharge-censoring endpoint is a secondary sensitivity and must show how many S rows were removed.

## Measurement representation and process separation

Core physiologic domains are HR, MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, FiO2, O2 flow, and PEEP. Use numeric valuenum and valueuom, retain source itemid, and reject fixed impossible ranges: HR 20–250 bpm; MAP 20–200 mmHg; RR 2–80/min; SpO2 50–100%; temperature 30–43 C; O2 flow 0–100 L/min; PEEP 0–40 cmH2O. Convert FiO2 values above 1.5 by dividing by 100, then retain 0.21–1.00. Record rejected values and unit failures.

Within each bin and domain, deduplicate at each charttime by the median of valid numeric values. For MAP use the highest-priority available source in this order: arterial 220052, non-invasive 220181, ART 225312; then median across the selected source. A bin is valid for LII when at least two of eight domains are observed. Do not carry forward or treat missing as normal. Use fixed abnormal thresholds: HR <50 or >120; MAP <65; RR <8 or >24; SpO2 <92; temperature <36 or >=38.5 C; FiO2 >0.40; O2 flow >=4 L/min; PEEP >5. Define instability_b = abnormal observed domains / observed domains and LII as the equal-bin mean across valid bins, with a minimum of four valid bins. Report sensitivity at 4, 6, and 8 valid bins and at least four observed domains per bin.

For every bin save numeric values, abnormal flags, the eight domain masks, observed-domain count, valid-bin flag, chart-row count, distinct clinical timestamp count, and item-source conflicts. Separately save lab-testing indicators/counts, Foley charting, and clipped vasoactive interval presence/minutes. These process fields are not physiologic absence and are never relabeled as physiology.

## Selection-process analysis

Fit two selection models on the broader valid ICU-exit frame using only data available no later than each row's own outtime:

- Q_process: P(L=1 | C, prior-window observation/process), where C is anchor_age, gender, anchor_year_group, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU duration, admission_type, admission_location, earlier ICU-stay count, prior-window measurement counts/masks, and clinical-time coverage. It contains no late numeric physiology.
- Q_full: Q_process plus prewindow aggregate physiology summaries and terminal-bin physiology/masks. This is a sensitivity, not the primary adjustment, because it conditions on variables close to the selection event.

Fit penalized logistic models, with category handling and calibration chosen in training/validation only. For live discharges, use stabilized inverse-selection weights proportional to P(L=1) divided by the fitted probability, clip only at a prespecified training-derived percentile, and report unweighted and weighted distributions, weight percentiles, maximum weight, overlap, and ESS. Do not extrapolate beyond common support. L=0 rows do not receive an outcome prediction; their role is to define the observed selection mechanism.

Report held-out risk and LII contrast in Q quintiles, including counts and event support. Use hosp/transfers only for post hoc transition-pathway descriptors: whether a non-ICU transfer begins within 6 hours after t0, the first receiving careunit, time from ICU outtime to transfer, and the presence/time of a subsequent same-admission ICU stay. Use hosp/admissions.discharge_location, dischtime-t0, and outtime hour only as descriptive process strata or robustness tables, never as predictors, weights, or evidence of appropriateness. These downstream fields are intentionally excluded from risk matrices. Transfer-pathway analysis is descriptive because transfer and disposition are consequences of the ICU exit decision.

A process-only falsification fits the same risk target using masks, counts, test indicators, Foley charting, and vasoactive presence/minutes, with no physiologic values. If process-only predictions match the aggregate physiology model, documentation is a sufficient explanation. If the physiology model retains calibrated R/D increment over process-only predictions after Q_process weighting, that is evidence consistent with (not proof of) observed physiologic information beyond the documented process.

## Matched baseline and chronological alternative

Both models use exactly the same source whitelist, target, subject split, no-post-t0 rule, selection/ascertainment sensitivity, and held-out metrics.

Bagg, the transparent order-blind baseline: penalized discrete-time cause-specific hazard regression with three heads for R, D, and S; N is the no-transition state from the at-risk recursion. Inputs are C, terminal-bin physiology and masks, terminal process fields, and order-blind summaries over all twelve bins: mean, median, minimum, maximum, SD, IQR, abnormal-bin fraction, valid-bin count, domain observation/missingness counts, lab-testing indicators, Foley charting, and clipped vasoactive presence/minutes. No bin identity, slopes, reversal counts, sequence position, discharge fields, notes, dod, or post-outtime field is allowed.

Gchrono, the substantive chronological alternative: a small masked GRU with the same C and the same twelve bins, each containing the eight numeric domains, masks, abnormal flags, observed-domain count, and timed process indicators. It has hidden size 32 or 64, three cause-specific hazard heads, training-only early stopping/dropout selection, and five fixed seeds. It can reveal persistence, recovery, reversal, and coupled physiologic-process order that Bagg intentionally discards. It is a prognostic representation, not a causal model. The primary chronology contrast is Gchrono versus Bagg; a preterminal bin-order shuffle must be run with the same trained architecture and evaluation rules.

A third process-only model is required as a mechanism control. It receives C and the order-blind or chronological process masks/counts/testing/interval fields but no numeric physiology. It is not allowed to become a competing clinical claim.

For all models, derive hourly hazards into CIF_R, CIF_D, CIF_S, and P_N. Evaluate held-out transition-aware Brier score, cause-specific calibration at 12/24/48h, calibration intercept/slope, R/D AUROC and AUPRC as secondary metrics, and threshold operating characteristics at prespecified sensitivity/PPV values. Threshold tables describe operating performance only; they are not net benefit, utility, or a monitoring recommendation. Use paired subject bootstrap intervals for model differences and selection-stratum contrasts, with the completed replicate count reported.

## Falsification, interpretation and clinical limits

Supportive evidence requires a prespecified held-out Gchrono-vs-Bagg Brier improvement with a 95% interval excluding zero; no important R/D calibration degradation; directionally consistent calibrated CIF_A improvement in Q_process-weighted and ascertainment/coverage sensitivities with adequate overlap; a Gchrono increment over process-only history; and attenuation after chronology shuffle. A stable LII/risk contrast across Q quintiles strengthens selection robustness. These results support only reproducible prognostic information in this MIMIC snapshot under stated assumptions.

Adverse evidence includes no chronological increment; calibration worsening; a gain confined to S, N, one Q stratum, transfer pathway, or measurement intensity; disappearance under Q_process weighting; Q_full erasure of the contrast; process-only matching physiology; chronology-shuffle resistance; or label-permutation failure. Such results favor terminal state, selective documentation, or discharge-process selection over a robust residual-physiology interpretation.

Inconclusive evidence includes low R/D counts, high U fraction, sparse valid bins, poor Q overlap or ESS, unstable clipping, malformed timing, unit failures, contradictory endpoint records, subgroup non-support, or wide/discordant uncertainty. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.

Computationally checkable claims include archive/member/schema provenance; joins and split integrity; timing/coverage/unit filters; R/D/S/N/U flow; Q definitions and weights; feature whitelist; model configurations; held-out predictions; CIF/Brier/calibration; selection-stratum and transfer descriptors; bootstrap intervals; and falsification outputs.

Unavailable or non-identifiable claims include clinician discharge intent, appropriateness of ICU transfer, goals of care and treatment limitations, bed/staffing pressure, ward surveillance, reasons for testing, whether charted oxygen equals delivered physiology, whether an unknown death time represents a particular course, preventability, causal treatment effects, net benefit, external validity, and whether a review/monitoring intervention changes outcomes. Those require clinical adjudication, better outcome ascertainment in an external cohort, and preferably prospective or interventional evaluation. Even a result surviving all computational sensitivities cannot say that a patient was discharged too early or that monitoring would reduce readmission/death.

## Alternative selection record and resources

The actual scientific deliverable is the newly fitted selection model(s), the competing-risk Bagg and Gchrono models, process-only control, held-out predictions, selection-stratum/weight diagnostics, uncertainty, falsifications, and conclusion audit. A favorable result is not completion; null and adverse results are scientifically valid outcomes.

The transparent order-blind baseline is selected as primary because its aggregate summaries and cause-specific hazards make it possible to see whether the selection-weighted risk contrast is driven by terminal state, coverage, or trajectory burden. The chronological GRU is selected as the substantive alternative because only it can test whether persistence, recovery, reversal, and coupled order carry information lost by aggregate summaries. Both use the same raw input whitelist and estimand. A mechanistic state-space model was deferred: no validated delivered-oxygen, clinician-assessed recovery, or treatment-intent state is available, so its latent states would be weakly identified and would add assumptions without a distinct observable target. A transformer was deferred because twelve bins are short and would add capacity/tuning without a new clinical estimand. A causal discharge-policy model was deferred because discharge intent, ward surveillance, staffing/bed pressure, treatment limitations, and intervention assignment are unavailable. Revisit the mechanistic alternative if clinician-adjudicated states or richer interventions become available; revisit a longer-context transformer only if a robust chronological signal remains and a longer validated window is scientifically justified.

Future solver envelope: up to 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB memory, 8 GPUs, 28800 seconds. The 12-bin aggregation, selection logistic models, hazard regression, bootstrap summaries, and small GRU are expected to fit within this envelope; these are planning estimates, not measured results. CPU is adequate for tabular and primary fits. One allocated A100-SXM4-80GB may be used for repeated GRU seeds or bootstrap inference if profiling shows CPU time threatens the envelope; ordinary shell CUDA absence is not evidence against GPU feasibility. Discovery allowance is 7200 seconds, separate from the solver envelope. Full archive scans and planned bootstrap count are unmeasured; report actual counts and execution-limited uncertainty rather than silently shrinking the experiment. No discovery fit was run.
