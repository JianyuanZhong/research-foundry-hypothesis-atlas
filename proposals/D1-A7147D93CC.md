# Candidate: Which vasopressor should be reduced first in early recovery from dual-pressor septic shock?

## Clinical decision, supported evidence, and unresolved claim

**Decision.** During the first 72 hours of an ICU stay, an adult with presumed septic shock has received concurrent norepinephrine and fixed-dose vasopressin for at least 6 hours and has reached a reproducible readiness-to-wean state. Should the team first reduce norepinephrine while holding vasopressin stable, or first reduce vasopressin while holding norepinephrine stable?

**Strongest claim supported by inspected evidence.** The full open-access 2026 Alshreef et al. article (PMC12825015; DOI 10.5414/CP204891) documents genuine practice variation but not a causal answer: in 100 mechanically ventilated patients, 47 had vasopressin liberated first and 53 norepinephrine first; baseline SOFA differed, adjusted ventilation-duration estimates were very imprecise, and MAP did not differ. A current August 2026 review abstract (DOI 10.1016/j.jcrc.2026.155729; full text was not available and is not claimed as inspected) reports 13 comparative studies (>2,500 patients), a striking randomized-versus-observational directional conflict, and three unresolved confounds: sequence, abrupt versus tapered withdrawal, and corticosteroid use. Thus the defensible existing claim is that equipoise and practice variation exist and prior results are design-sensitive—not that either sequence is safer.

**Unresolved causal claim to test.** Among patients meeting the readiness state below, a **norepinephrine-first gradual-reduction strategy**, compared with a **vasopressin-first gradual-reduction strategy**, reduces protocol-independent 6-hour hemodynamic failure by at least 5 absolute percentage points, without increasing 28-day mortality by more than 3 points.

This is falsifiable and deliberately separates a physiologic benefit claim from a mortality-safety claim. The 5-point benefit and 3-point safety margins are prespecified clinical thresholds, not claims supported by prior data.

**Why it matters and substantive advance.** Avoidable hypotension can worsen organ perfusion and provoke re-escalation, while unnecessary catecholamine exposure can cause arrhythmia and myocardial stress. The advance over prior chart reviews is to align eligibility, assignment, and follow-up at a pre-withdrawal time zero; compare gradual reduction of either agent so sequence is not automatically confounded with abrupt vasopressin cessation; measure hypotension from blood-pressure observations rather than defining it solely by clinician rescue; explicitly adjust for corticosteroids; and report a causal risk difference with overlap/weight diagnostics. It is not another mortality predictor.

## Target trial protocol

### Population and temporal boundaries

Unit is the first qualifying ICU stay per `subject_id`.

Inclusion:

1. Age >=18 at hospital admission.
2. First ICU stay in the hospitalization.
3. Presumed infection established before time zero: at least one microbiology specimen collected from -24 hours through t0 plus at least two `event_txt='Administered'` doses of a prespecified, versioned systemic-antibacterial dictionary from -24 hours through t0. Primary phenotype requires a blood culture; sensitivity permits any sterile-site culture.
4. Concurrent positive-rate norepinephrine and vasopressin infusion coverage for the preceding 6 continuous hours, allowing documentation gaps <=15 minutes. Vasopressin must be 1.8-2.4 units/hour and norepinephrine 0.03-0.20 mcg/kg/min at the landmark.
5. First hourly landmark no later than ICU `intime+72h` at which: two valid MAP observations in the prior 2 hours are >=65 mmHg; the most recent MAP is >=65; neither pressor increased >20% over the prior 2 hours; no epinephrine, dopamine, or phenylephrine infusion is active; and the patient is alive and in the ICU.

Exclusion before t0: pregnancy cannot be reliably established and is therefore not an automatic exclusion; age <18; prior qualifying landmark; vasopressin rate suggesting an indication other than vasodilatory-shock dosing (>2.4 units/hour); extracorporeal support or active cardiac surgery/post-cardiotomy shock; chronic dialysis or any renal-replacement therapy before t0; missing both arterial and noninvasive MAP in the readiness window; impossible/unknown infusion units. Current-admission discharge diagnoses are not used to establish time-zero eligibility.

**Time zero** is the first qualifying readiness landmark. Eligibility, assignment, and follow-up all begin there. Follow-up is 6 hours for the primary outcome, 28 days for clinical safety, and until hospital discharge for in-hospital secondary outcomes.

### Strategies

Each eligible patient is cloned to both strategies and given a 2-hour grace period.

- **Norepinephrine-first gradual reduction:** by t0+2h, reduce norepinephrine rate by >=50% from t0 (or to zero) while keeping vasopressin within +/-20% of its t0 rate; do not increase total norepinephrine-equivalent dose above t0 during the grace period.
- **Vasopressin-first gradual reduction:** by t0+2h, reduce vasopressin rate by >=50% from t0 (or to zero) while keeping norepinephrine within +/-20% of its t0 rate; do not increase total norepinephrine-equivalent dose above t0 during the grace period.

Censor a clone at first protocol deviation. Death during grace is primary-outcome failure, not exclusion. If neither strategy is followed, both clones are censored; if both appear followed because both drugs are reduced, both are censored at the first simultaneous deviation. Abrupt cessation (from >=80% of baseline to zero within <=15 minutes) is excluded from the primary gradual-strategy contrast and analyzed separately in the prespecified sequence-by-method sensitivity analysis. Production code must reconstruct infusion coverage from all segments and must not equate a row `endtime` with true discontinuation.

### Outcomes and estimands

Primary outcome is **6-hour protocol-independent hemodynamic failure**: death by t0+6h, any valid MAP <55 mmHg, or at least two MAP values <65 mmHg 15-60 minutes apart. Arterial MAP takes precedence within a 5-minute tie; otherwise noninvasive MAP is used. Values outside 20-200 mmHg are invalid. Clinician dose escalation is not part of this outcome.

Primary estimand is the per-protocol marginal risk difference and risk ratio for 6-hour failure, norepinephrine-first minus vasopressin-first, in the target population eligible at t0.

Key safety estimand is the 28-day all-cause mortality risk difference, with +3 percentage points as the noninferiority margin. Secondary outcomes: severe MAP failure (<55); rescue increase in norepinephrine-equivalent dose >=0.05 mcg/kg/min sustained >=30 minutes; first-drug restart after >=1 hour off; vasopressor-free alive days through day 28; ICU-free alive days through day 28; new renal-replacement therapy through day 7; and in-hospital death. Pressor-free days assign zero to deaths before day 28.

## Baseline variables and analysis

Baseline adjustment includes demographics/calendar period/unit; source of admission; prior comorbidities; preceding duration and trajectories of both pressors; MAP mean/minimum/slope and measurement intensity; heart rate, temperature, oxygenation, ventilation; latest values and slopes of the listed laboratories; urine output; cumulative crystalloid/colloid; hydrocortisone timing/dose; sedative exposure; and prior organ support. Time-varying adherence models update MAP, both pressor doses, fluids, hydrocortisone, lactate, and urine output during grace/follow-up.

Primary analysis uses clone-censor-weighting: clone at t0, censor on deviation, estimate stabilized inverse-probability-of-censoring weights with pooled logistic models, truncate at prespecified 1st/99th percentiles, and fit weighted pooled-logistic outcome models standardized to the eligible cohort. Patient-clustered bootstrap (1,000 samples) supplies 95% CIs. A doubly robust longitudinal TMLE is the robustness analysis, not a vehicle for data-driven subgroup claims.

Baselines are (1) crude as-treated risk difference, clearly labeled biased; (2) single-time t0 propensity overlap weighting; and (3) the primary longitudinal clone-censor estimate. Diagnostics required before interpretation: CONSORT-like flow; missingness by arm; pre/post-weight SMDs (<0.10 target); propensity and adherence overlap by care unit/year; effective sample size; weight maxima/tails; observed-versus-weighted event rates; dose-trajectory plots; and manual audit of 100 reconstructed episodes. Failure of positivity (either strategy <50 effective patients in a major unit/era or ESS <25% of nominal) precludes an overall causal claim.

Prespecified sensitivity analyses: readiness windows of 1 and 3 hours; MAP failure as time-weighted area below 65 and any MAP <60; grace periods 1 and 3 hours; no culture restriction; Sepsis-3/current-admission ICD sensitivity; complete-case versus multiple imputation; exclusion of cardiac ICUs; hydrocortisone strata; arterial-MAP-only; and a sequence-by-withdrawal-method analysis including abrupt cessation only if all four cells have ESS >=100. Pre-t0 “pseudo-outcome” hypotension in the preceding 6 hours is a falsification diagnostic: a material weighted association with future strategy indicates residual confounding or misaligned t0.

## Empirical feasibility already checked

A read-only streaming pass over the actual ZIP counted 459,800 norepinephrine and 37,163 vasopressin input rows. Rate units were almost entirely `mcg/kg/min` and `units/hour`. There were 5,032 stays with any direct overlap and 1,745 with a longest directly overlapping row pair >=6 hours. A deliberately crude row-end screen found 1,086 norepinephrine-first, 283 vasopressin-first, and 376 ends within one hour. Critically, 976/1,086 and 199/283 nominally “stopped” drugs restarted within 24 hours. Therefore these are feasibility bounds, not the analytic cohort or outcomes; they empirically show both variation and why production reconstruction is mandatory. The actual screen and counts are in `pressor_wean_feasibility.json`; `profile_pressor_wean.py` records the sampling/filtering logic. No source rows were modified.

The final cohort can be smaller after infection/readiness/gradual-reduction criteria. If either arm has <200 adherent patients, primary estimation is declared feasibility-limited and no causal effectiveness conclusion is allowed.

## Interpretation and falsification

- **Supportive:** adjusted 6-hour RD <= -5 points, its 95% CI excludes 0, mortality RD upper 95% bound <=+3 points, diagnostics pass, and direction is robust to arterial-only and grace-window analyses. Conclusion: the data support a lower short-term hemodynamic-failure risk for norepinephrine-first gradual reduction in this MIMIC target population, with no mortality increase larger than the prespecified margin detected.
- **Adverse:** adjusted 6-hour RD >=+5 points with CI excluding 0, or mortality RD lower CI exceeds +3 points. Conclusion: the hypothesized strategy is contradicted and may be harmful; do not recommend it.
- **Inconclusive:** benefit CI crosses both 0 and -5, mortality safety is not established, estimates reverse under credible definitions, positivity/weight diagnostics fail, the pseudo-outcome remains associated, or adjudication PPV is <80%. Conclusion: MIMIC cannot resolve the decision, regardless of point-estimate direction.
- A smaller statistically detectable difference (<5 points) is not the prespecified clinically important benefit. A null does not prove equivalence.

Computationally checkable claims are cohort flow, exposure reconstruction, covariate balance, positivity, MAP outcomes, mortality/organ-support endpoints, effect estimates, CIs, and whether thresholds are met. Septic-shock phenotype validity, clinician intent/readiness, whether transient MAP failure is patient-important, and practice recommendations require blinded clinical adjudication and expert review. A multicenter prospective factorial trial is required before establishing sequence and withdrawal method as policy; MIMIC cannot settle transportability or unmeasured bedside assessments.

## Scientific-value comparison with the existing MRSA candidate

This proposal is more independent and addresses a broader hemodynamic practice with unusually explicit conflict between randomized and observational evidence. It also offers a methodological advance by separating sequence from withdrawal method and clinician-triggered rescue from measured hypotension. Its weaknesses are substantial: the decision state and intent are harder to observe, vasopressin-first gradual taper may have limited positivity, and the primary endpoint is a physiologic surrogate; an observational result cannot overrule discordant randomized trials.

The MRSA de-escalation candidate has a cleaner externally timestamped decision, a directly actionable stewardship intervention, and renal-harm avoidance, although recent randomized evidence already supports shorter therapy and leaves mainly harder-outcome uncertainty. On present evidence, neither should win merely for novelty. MRSA is stronger for immediate actionability and identification; this vasopressor proposal is stronger for unresolved equipoise and potential mechanistic clarification. Final preference should depend on the production cohort's gradual-taper positivity and adjudicated phenotype PPV, not model complexity.
