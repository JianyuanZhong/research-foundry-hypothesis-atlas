# Observation-aware physiologic recovery at the first ICU discharge

## Candidate objective and scientific deliverable

This successor tests whether a recorded recovery pattern before the first ICU discharge carries clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond how intensively the patient was measured and how the discharge process was documented.

The future solver must newly construct one adult first-ICU-discharge cohort; produce a leakage-safe 12-hour physiology/support/observation tensor; fit a transparent competing-risk baseline and an observation-aware factorized hidden semi-Markov alternative using the same underlying inputs and patient-level splits; and return locked test-set estimates. Completion requires:

1. a cohort, join, time-ordering, coverage, and event-count audit;
2. hourly final-12-hour physiology, support-documentation, and observation-process features;
3. 6-, 24-, and 48-hour cumulative-incidence estimates for first ICU return, in-hospital death, and alive hospital discharge as mutually exclusive competing events;
4. adjusted absolute CIF contrasts for recent respiratory withdrawal, recent vasoactive cessation, and their combination, with uncertainty;
5. baseline-versus-alternative calibration, IPCW Brier scores, state/observation diagnostics, and patient-bootstrap intervals;
6. predeclared observation, source-channel, timing, window, pseudo-landmark, and topology falsifications; and
7. an interpretation file that maps every scientific statement to a computed output row and labels the finding supportive, adverse, or inconclusive.

No clinical result is asserted by this proposal. The scientific deliverable is the newly fitted prognostic estimand and its uncertainty/sensitivity outputs, not a readiness check, a model score, or a claimed discharge policy.

## Unresolved claim and strongest supported claim

The expert seed [starting question] proposes that persistent oxygen requirements or fluctuating vital signs before ICU discharge are associated with ICU return or in-hospital death. The available MIMIC-IV snapshot supports a narrower, auditable question: among adults at the first ICU outtime, do domain-specific recorded support-withdrawal patterns and recent physiology predict the competing transition to ICU return, death, or alive hospital discharge when observation intensity and observable discharge-process proxies are modelled separately?

Existing prediction-oriented work can show that final-hours physiology or missingness improves risk discrimination. A targeted public search also surfaced prior MIMIC-II ICU-readmission studies using near-discharge physiology and studies treating measurement frequency as an informative EHR process. Those are related evidence, not a proof of this hypothesis; their full methods were not used as a reproduction target here. The unresolved claim is stronger and different:

> A recent recorded withdrawal of respiratory support and/or vasoactive support represents a domain-specific recovery trajectory whose association with early ICU return or death persists after adjustment for the observation process and observable discharge-process context. If the contrast collapses to the observation/process-only estimate, the apparent recovery signal is more consistent with selective measurement or documentation than with a distinct recorded recovery state.

This is prognostic, not causal. It does not claim that stopping support causes or prevents an event, or that changing monitoring would change outcomes.

The primary estimand is a standardized absolute cumulative-incidence contrast at 6, 24, and 48 hours after outtime for each mutually exclusive first event:

- ICU return: a later ICU stay in the same hospital admission;
- in-hospital death before any counted return; and
- alive hospital discharge before either of the above.

The primary comparison uses four recorded states when adequate coverage exists: durable recorded-off in both domains; recent respiratory withdrawal only; recent vasoactive cessation only; and recent withdrawal in both domains. Active/residual, discordant, unobserved, and inadequate-coverage patterns remain visible in audits and secondary analyses and are not silently forced into a recovered category.

The strongest evidence available before fitting is only that the source tables contain dated ICU observations, support-event records, transfers, admissions, and outcomes sufficient to construct the stated recorded-data estimand. It does not support an association, a treatment effect, a planned/unplanned return label, a treatment-limitation label, or bedside readiness.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

The first ICU-to-ward transition is consequential: a patient may leave critical care with less monitoring while still vulnerable to early deterioration. A result that respiratory withdrawal remains informative after observation adjustment could motivate a prospective study of targeted respiratory reassessment; a result dominated by vasoactive cessation could prioritize circulatory-transition review. A null after process adjustment would also be clinically useful, warning that apparent stable-off-support states are artifacts of sparse charting or discharge documentation.

The substantive advance over the parent is an explicit decision-relevant decomposition:

1. physiology/support documentation only;
2. observation intensity and missingness only;
3. discharge-process proxies only; and
4. the fully adjusted combination.

The question is whether the recovery contrast survives the decomposition, not whether a complex model produces a small predictive gain. The latent alternative is retained only if it identifies reproducible state/order information that changes the scientific interpretation, not merely if it improves discrimination.

No local data can adjudicate bedside readiness, clinician intent, goals of care, treatment limitations, ward monitoring, preventability, discharge appropriateness, or planned versus unplanned ICU return. Those claims require expert review and prospective or externally validated studies.

## Recorded physiology, support, and observation process

These streams must be materialized separately. A missing chart is not coded as stable physiology.

Physiology features are one-hour medians or robust summaries from HR, arterial MAP, NIBP MAP, RR, SpO2, FiO2, O2 flow, PEEP, and temperature, with source channels retained. Include last value, median, within-window slope, range, and final-minus-preceding-half change where observed. Convert Fahrenheit to Celsius only with an auditable unit rule.

Recorded support documentation is separate. In each hour, mark a respiratory-support record when any of the following is documented: O2 flow >2 L/min, FiO2 >0.21, PEEP >0, an active intubation-to-extubation interval, or an active non-invasive-ventilation interval. Mark a vasoactive-support record when a valid overlap interval exists for any listed vasoactive item. The solver must report these thresholds and run threshold sensitivities; they are operational recorded-support definitions, not proof of continuous bedside support. Use a low final-MAP indicator (<65 mmHg median) and a low-respiratory-burden indicator (<1/3 of available final-half respiratory support observations) as descriptive physiology/support summaries, not clinical diagnoses.

Define the primary recorded states using the 12 hourly masks:

- recent respiratory withdrawal: respiratory support documented in the preceding six-hour half, no respiratory-support record in the final half, and at least two valid respiratory-observation bins in each half;
- recent vasoactive cessation: analogous definition for vasoactive intervals, with at least two valid MAP bins in each half;
- durable recorded-off: no qualifying support in the final half and no qualifying support in the preceding half, with adequate domain coverage;
- active/residual: any qualifying support in the final half;
- unobserved/discordant: inadequate coverage or contradictory source evidence.

The primary contrast is limited to four adequate-coverage combinations; all other states are retained in flow counts and secondary estimates. A separate recorded-support-free buffer (time from the last qualifying support record to outtime) is continuous/descriptive and cannot be interpreted as continuous absence of support.

Observation-process features use only pre-outtime data: number and fraction of observed bins by domain, maximum gap, row counts, distinct item/source channels, final- and preceding-half coverage, number of support interval records, contradictory-time indicators, and pre-index transfer count/last careunit. Do not use post-landmark discharge location, future return topology, or discharge-note text as predictors. admission_type, admission_location, first_careunit, ICU LOS, age, gender, and anchor-year group are process/context covariates, not latent physiology.

## Baseline and scientifically informative alternative

Use subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits, fixed seeds 17, 29, and 43, so no subject appears in multiple partitions. Item mapping, unit conversion, thresholds, imputation, state restrictions, and all fitting are training/validation-only. The test set is opened once for locked estimates.

Both methods receive the same cohort, static covariates, and underlying 12-hour by channel matrix containing physiology values, support-documentation indicators, and observation masks. The baseline uses a prespecified fixed summary map of this same matrix; the alternative uses its hourly ordering. This tests a substantive question about temporal state structure, while ensuring neither method receives a different clinical data source.

Simple baseline: cause-specific pooled discrete-time hazards at 1, 6, 24, and 48 hours for ICU return, death, and alive discharge, with the four recorded-state indicators, physiology/support summaries, observation-process fields, and admission/context covariates. Fit nested versions: physiology/support only; observation-only; process/context-only; and full adjustment. Use one-hot categorical handling and training-set imputation only. Estimate standardized CIFs and contrasts from the full model; report cause-specific hazard ratios only as secondary descriptive parameters because absolute CIFs are the clinical estimand.

Substantive alternative: a factorized observation-aware hidden semi-Markov model with separate respiratory and hemodynamic latent state chains over the twelve hourly bins. Emission models use the same physiologic/support channels; a separate observation model estimates channel-specific measurement probability/intensity from the same available history; transition and dwell distributions encode ordered withdrawal and persistence. The event head estimates post-landmark cause-specific hazards conditional on posterior state uncertainty and context. Fit emissions, transitions, observation process, and event hazards on training data; choose state restrictions and regularization on validation; lock posterior state probabilities and CIFs on test data. The model’s added scientific information is whether ordered duration and missingness patterns distinguish a latent recovery trajectory from a sparse-record pattern that summary regression cannot distinguish. If states have high posterior entropy, fail split stability, or add no stable domain-specific information, the latent model is scientifically inconclusive and the transparent baseline remains the reportable result.

Evaluate both with discrimination only as secondary. Primary evaluation is calibration intercept/slope, time-specific IPCW Brier score, absolute CIF contrasts, interaction contrasts, and bootstrap 95% intervals. Report effective sample size, positivity/overlap, event counts, calibration plots, and uncertainty in latent state assignment. Use patient bootstrap on the locked test predictions for uncertainty; do not treat repeated seeds as independent patients.

## Falsification and interpretation gates

Predeclare the following tests:

- shuffle the twelve hourly time labels within subject while preserving values, masks, and support totals;
- compare full inputs with mask-removed, mask-only, physiology/support-only, and support-removed versions;
- replace charttime with storetime as a negative timing control;
- replace interval-overlap support with last-value support coding;
- remove procedure-event corroboration;
- separate arterial MAP from NIBP MAP and FiO2/O2-flow/PEEP channels;
- vary 6-hour versus 12-hour windows, 1/3 burden threshold, buffer definition, and support thresholds;
- use a pseudo-landmark six hours before outtime with a newly defined no-leakage outcome window;
- stratify by first careunit and admission type;
- compare aggregate return with topology-confirmed and topology-unconfirmed return;
- compare competing-discharge analysis with discharge-as-censoring as a sensitivity, never as the primary estimand; and
- audit the effect of excluding missing or contradictory timestamps rather than silently repairing them.

Supportive evidence requires a prespecified domain or interaction contrast with a clinically interpretable direction, adequate overlap, calibrated predictions, stable patient-bootstrap intervals, and persistence across the observation/process, source-channel, and timing falsifications. It supports only retrospective prognostic association with recorded recovery patterns.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed contrast, collapse toward the observation/process-only estimate, strong dependence on masks/storetime/last-value coding/topology, non-positivity, or a latent state that cannot be identified. This would argue against interpreting the recorded recovery pattern as a distinct physiologic prognostic signal.

Inconclusive evidence is sparse cells/events, wide or unstable intervals, missing transfer topology, poor calibration, high latent-state entropy, or contradictory results across reasonable windows. Inconclusive results must not be converted into a policy recommendation.

Even supportive results cannot establish causality, bedside readiness, monitoring benefit, preventability, appropriateness of discharge, treatment limitation, or planned/unplanned status. Those require clinician adjudication, richer ward monitoring, external validation, and ideally a prospective or causal study.

## Alternatives considered and compute

- Chosen baseline: transparent and CPU-suitable; it estimates the primary absolute CIF contrasts and exposes whether adjustment for observation/process changes the claim.
