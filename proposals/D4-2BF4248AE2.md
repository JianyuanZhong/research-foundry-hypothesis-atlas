# Provisional hypothesis: body-fat distribution separates metabolic from functional/fall risk trajectories

## Status

Provisional, not selected and not an observed result. This draft is grounded in the persistent UK Biobank candidate branch and the expert seed `[prior hypothesis]` ([starting question]). ## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with body-composition MRI and repeated functional measurements, at similar total body fat, higher visceral fat will be more specifically associated with subsequent metabolic disease, whereas higher posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) will be more specifically associated with subsequent functional decline or falls.

The clinical question is whether fat distribution identifies distinct downstream trajectories rather than merely predicting a generic health-risk score. A positive result could justify targeted body-composition assessment; a null or reversed result would argue against separating these phenotypes for risk stratification.

## Falsifiable test and interpretation

Compare a simple total-adiposity baseline with a prespecified composition model containing visceral fat and posterior-thigh MFI, using held-out evaluation and uncertainty estimates. The leading claim is supported only if the composition model improves the relevant prespecified trajectory contrasts beyond total adiposity, with directionally specific effects and adequate calibration/overlap. An adverse result is a null, reversed, or non-specific association after temporal and missingness checks. An inconclusive result is insufficient event support, unstable uncertainty, or inability to distinguish composition from observation/severity artifacts.

This design cannot establish causality or mechanism. Treatment effects, clinical adjudication of falls/disease, and external validation would require additional data or expert review.

## Method alternatives

The simple baseline is a prespecified total-adiposity model. The substantive alternative is a composition model testing visceral fat and MFI as distinct predictors, with trajectory or competing-risk outcomes as appropriate. A more flexible learned alternative (for example, a regularized multistate or sequence model) is deferred unless the bounded feasibility check shows sufficient event support and demonstrates information beyond the composition model; complexity alone is not a scientific objective.
