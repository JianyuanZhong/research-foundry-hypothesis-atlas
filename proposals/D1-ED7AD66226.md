## 1. The scientific opening

Monitoring intensity in the ICU is not assigned, it is *chosen*. A drop in testing is
simultaneously:

- **H-signal**: evidence that the treating team judged the patient to be improving
  (de-escalation as a correct decision);
- **H-cause**: a mechanism for *delayed recognition* of a deterioration that was in fact
  developing (afferent-limb failure by omission);
- **H-limit**: a marker of a decision to limit therapy, to transfer, or of imminent discharge;
- **H-artefact**: a hospital data-interface interruption, or the patient physically being out of
  the unit (procedure, imaging), with no clinical decision at all.

The seed asserted an association ("abrupt reduction in testing while unstable predicts
deterioration"). The first thing I did was measure the marginal association, and it goes the
**other way**:

> Among the 71,218 eICU-CRD stays with unit LOS >= 48 h and at least one post-24 h inter-draw
> interval, stays containing at least one inter-draw interval > 30 h had ICU mortality
> **0.0199** (n = 16,306) versus **0.0789** (n = 54,912) in stays with no such interval.

So in these data, less testing marks *better* outcomes by a factor of four. The strongest claim
that existing evidence plus this measurement supports is **H-signal plus H-limit**: testing
intensity is a read-out of clinician judgement and prognosis, consistent with [K1], which showed
across 272 test types that the *presence and timing* of a laboratory order predicted 3-year
survival better than the result value in 118/174 tests. A proposal that merely reports an
association between reduced testing and bad outcomes is therefore either wrong in sign or
recovering confounding by indication.

**The unresolved claim worth testing is narrower and directional:** *does the harm component
(H-cause) become detectable precisely where H-signal is implausible — i.e. in patients whose
measured physiology is **not** improving at the moment testing is de-escalated?* H-signal and
H-cause make different observable predictions:

| | prediction for non-improving patients | prediction for improving patients |
|---|---|---|
| H-signal (de-escalation tracks unmeasured reassurance) | gap is at most neutral in both strata; any excess risk is uniform and explained by state | gap is protective/neutral |
| H-cause (gap delays recognition) | gap carries **extra** 24 h deterioration risk | little or no extra risk |
| H-limit | excess risk concentrated in stays with a documented care limitation before the gap | — |
| H-artefact | excess risk concentrated where *all* documentation streams stop, and clusters in a few hospitals | — |

The design is built around separating these four, not around demonstrating an association.

## 2. What answering this would change

If H-cause is detectable in the non-improving stratum and survives the H-limit and H-artefact
screens, the clinically actionable object is not "test more" but a **stratification rule**: do not
extend the testing interval while a patient's measured instability index is not falling. That rule
is exactly the missing ingredient in current de-implementation work: the ongoing stepped-wedge
cluster-randomised WICH-ICU trial [K3] randomises *reduced* routine laboratory testing with 30-day
mortality as the primary outcome, but applies it to ICU populations without a physiology-based
exclusion. An observational effect-modification result cannot establish causality; it can supply
the pre-specified subgroup/safety rule that such a trial needs. If H-cause is **not** detectable,
that is also useful: it is observational support for the safety premise of [K3] in a 208-hospital
US population, and it closes the seed's direction.

## 4. Design

**Population.** Adult (`age` not in {'', values < 18}; `age = '> 89'` retained and coded 90) ICU
stays at hospitals with >= 200 stays in the database (160 hospitals); one hospitalisation per
`uniquepid` (earliest `patienthealthsystemstayid`); within it, stays with `unitvisitnumber = 1`;
`unitstaytype` = admit; at least two core-panel draw events before the landmark. Excluded:
stays with zero core draws (8,035 stays database-wide), and stays at hospitals failing the
feed-integrity screen (T4/F4).

**Time origin and landmark grid (immortal time).** Landmarks at `t` in {1440, 2160, 2880, 3600,
4320} minutes (24, 36, 48, 60, 72 h after unit admission). A stay contributes a landmark row at `t`
iff `unitdischargeoffset > t` (still in the unit) and >= 2 core draws occurred in `[0, t)`.
Exposure, stratifier and all covariates are functions of `[0, t)` only. Risk time starts at `t`.
No criterion uses information after `t`, and in particular **LOS >= 48 h is never an eligibility
criterion** (that was the immortal-time error latent in the seed). Robust SEs cluster on
`hospitalid`; stay-level repeated landmarks handled by the model (Section 5).

**Exposure at `t`, three levels** (pre-specified from the measured interval distribution,
p50 = 13.5 h post-hour-24):

- `E0` (reference): time since last core draw <= 14 h — testing on the daily/twice-daily schedule.
- `E1`: time since last core draw > 14 h **and** monitoring continued — >= 6 distinct hour-blocks
  in `[t-12h, t)` containing a non-null `heartrate` or `sao2` in `vitalPeriodic` (equivalently
  `noninvasivesystolic` in `vitalAperiodic`). This is "**testing de-escalated while the patient was
  still being watched in the unit**" — the only level that can bear on H-cause.
- `E2`: time since last core draw > 14 h **and** monitoring sparse (< 6 monitored hour-blocks).
  This is the H-artefact / out-of-unit / globally-reduced-observation level, kept as a *contrast*
  rather than discarded, because the audit shows it is large (long gaps have 0.306 vs 0.559
  monitoring continuity).

Sensitivity thresholds: 10 h, 18 h, 24 h, 30 h; and a continuous log(time-since-last-draw) spline.

**Primary stratifier: measured physiologic trajectory.** Pre-specified instability index
`I(w)` over a 12 h window `w`, computed as the count of abnormal domains among: heart rate
(< 50 or > 110), mean arterial pressure (< 65), respiratory rate (> 24 or < 8), SpO2 (< 92),
temperature (< 36.0 or > 38.3) — each taken as the worst 10th/90th-percentile value of the
`vitalPeriodic` 5-minute medians in `w` — plus lab domains from the last value in `w` (creatinine
>= 1.5x admission value; bicarbonate < 20; Hgb < 8) plus treatment domains (vasopressor infusion
active; invasive ventilation active). `I` ranges 0-10.
Trajectory `D(t) = I([t-12h, t)) - I([t-24h, t-12h))`.
- **Improving**: `D <= -1`.
- **Non-improving**: `D >= 0`.
(Stays with insufficient vitals to compute `D` form a third, reported-separately stratum.)
This operationalises the seed's undefined word "unstable" as a *trajectory*, which is what the
rival explanations actually disagree about, and keeps it strictly pre-landmark.

**Outcomes, window `(t, t+24h]`** (secondary window `(t, t+72h]`):
- **Primary (all 160 hospitals, uniformly ascertained): ICU death** —
  `unitdischargestatus = 'Expired'` and `unitdischargeoffset <= t + 1440`.
- **Secondary composite deterioration (restricted to hospitals with >= 20% stay-level
  `infusionDrug` coverage, measured median 0.34): ICU death, or new vasopressor initiation
  (`infusionDrug.infusionoffset` in window with no vasopressor record in `[t-12h, t)`; corroborated
  by `medication.drugstartoffset` + `routeadmin`), or new invasive ventilation
  (`respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset` in window, no active vent at `t`).
- **Competing event: alive discharge from the unit** (`unitdischargeoffset <= t + 1440` and status
  Alive). This is modelled as a competing risk, not as administrative censoring — the seed's
  unaddressed structural problem ("testing stops because the patient is leaving").
- Secondary, hospitalisation-level via `patienthealthsystemstayid`: in-hospital death and
  same-hospitalisation ICU readmission.

**Estimand.** Not a marginal association (measured above to run the wrong way) but the
**effect modification**: the ratio of the E1-vs-E0 24 h cause-specific deterioration risk ratio in
the non-improving stratum to that in the improving stratum. Reported with cause-specific hazard
ratios (primary) and subdistribution/absolute-risk differences (secondary).

**Confounders (all from `[0, t)`).** Age, gender, ethnicity, `unittype`, `unitadmitsource`,
`apacheadmissiondx` collapsed to APACHE groups, APACHE IVa `acutephysiologyscore` / `apachescore` /
`predictedicumortality`, landmark index `t`, `I(t)` level, `D(t)`, number of core draws in
`[0, 24h)`, number of core draws in `[t-24h, t)`, vasopressor/vent status at `t`, an elective-surgery
flag (`apachePredVar.electivesurgery`), and hospital-level `numbedscategory`, `teachingstatus`,
`region`, plus the leave-one-out hospital baseline testing intensity below.

**Hospital-practice contrast (not an instrument).** For each stay, hospital baseline testing
intensity = median core-panel draws per patient-day in hours 0-24 among *all other* patients at the
same `hospitalid` (leave-one-out). Measured range 1.0-8.5 draws/day across 138 hospitals. We test
whether the E1 effect in the non-improving stratum differs between low- and high-intensity
hospitals. **This is explicitly not a valid instrument**: hospital testing intensity is correlated
with case mix, teleICU configuration, bed count, teaching status and documentation completeness
(the latter measured here: 31% of hospitals have < 5% `infusionDrug` coverage), and the exclusion
restriction is not credible. It is reported as a descriptive practice contrast whose purpose is to
show whether the exposure is tracking institutional norm or patient state — a *weaker* but
*differently biased* comparison than the patient-level one. No LATE/IV estimate will be reported.

## 5. Baseline and substantive alternative on the same question

**Baseline — pooled landmark logistic / cause-specific discrete-time model.**
Rows = (stay, landmark). Expected size: 200,859 stays, 66.2% reach 24 h and 38.7% reach 48 h, so
roughly 0.45-0.6 M landmark rows after cohort restrictions, ~45 columns. Outcome = 24 h
deterioration; alive discharge handled by a competing cause-specific fit plus an Aalen-Johansen
subdistribution estimate. Interaction `E1 x non-improving` is the pre-specified primary term.
Cluster-robust (hospital) SEs; stay-level random intercept as a sensitivity fit.
Budget: CPU only, 8 cores, ~16 GB, < 15 min for all fits. Device reasoning: a GLM on < 1 M x 45
dense design is memory-light and converges in a few IRLS passes; GPU gives nothing.

**Substantive alternative — joint discrete-time model of the *testing process* and the
*deterioration hazard* with correlated stay-level random effects.**
Discretise each stay into 6 h bins `k` from hour 12 to ICU discharge. Two linked submodels:
1. *observation/testing process*: `P(draw in bin k) = logit^-1(alpha0_k + alpha' X_ik + a_i)`
2. *deterioration hazard*: `P(Y_ik = 1 | alive, in unit) = logit^-1(beta0_k + beta' X_ik +
   gamma f(time since last draw) + delta (f x non-improving) + b_i)`
with `(a_i, b_i) ~ N(0, Sigma)` and correlation `rho`. A third competing sub-hazard for alive
discharge shares `a_i` (because discharge planning and testing de-escalation are the same decision
process). Inputs: ~2.5-4 M person-bin rows built from `lab`, `vitalPeriodic`, `infusionDrug`,
`respiratoryCare`, `carePlanGeneral`.

*What the alternative recovers that the baseline loses.* The baseline conditions on measured state
at `t` and treats the testing decision as exogenous given covariates; it therefore cannot
distinguish "clinicians stopped testing because of unmeasured reassurance" (H-signal) from "the gap
delayed recognition" (H-cause) — the two produce the same conditional association. The joint model
estimates `rho`, the correlation between the latent propensity to be tested *less* and the latent
hazard of deterioration. `rho < 0` is a direct, quantified statement that de-escalation marks
unmeasured improvement, i.e. the baseline's within-stratum estimate is biased toward safety;
`rho ~ 0` materially strengthens the causal reading of `delta`. It also returns the *shape* of
`f(time since last draw)` free of the arbitrary 14 h cut, and it uses every bin of every stay
rather than five landmarks.
A second alternative on the same question, to be run in parallel: a **marginal structural
Cox/pooled-logistic model** with stabilised IPTW for the time-varying testing decision and IPCW for
alive discharge, targeting the risk under the hypothetical regime "never allow the interval to
exceed 24 h while `D >= 0`", with an E-value for unmeasured confounding. The MSM answers a policy
question the frailty model does not; the frailty model answers an identification question the MSM
assumes away. Both are reported.

*Budget and device reasoning (estimates; not verified by a submitted job — the Lead holds the
compute slot).* Feature construction dominates: one streaming pass over `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz`
(1,784,209,686 bytes gz). Measured anchors in this episode: a full `awk` pass over `lab.csv.gz`
(516 MB gz, 15.3 M projected rows) took 26 s; a full pass over `vitalAperiodic.csv.gz` (240 MB gz)
with per-(stay,hour) dedup took 29 s; `vitalPeriodic` decompresses 2 GB in 11.7 s. So one
projection pass over `vitalPeriodic` should be ~4-8 min on one core; the full feature build with
window aggregation, 16 cores / 32 GB, budget 45 min. Joint bivariate-frailty GLMM: 2 latent
dimensions, ~3 M person-bin rows; adaptive Gauss-Hermite or Laplace on 16 CPU cores ~1-3 h for one
fit. The sensitivity programme (a `rho` profile plus ~200 cluster-bootstrap replicates) is ~10-20x
one fit, which is the only part where a single A100 is worth requesting: a torch/minibatch
Monte-Carlo marginal-likelihood implementation should bring one fit to ~10-20 min, making the
bootstrap feasible inside the planning envelope (`solver_planning_limits`: 16 CPUs, 8 GPUs,
256 GB, 28,800 s). GPU is requested for *throughput of repeated low-dimensional fits*, not because
the model is large; if a bounded probe shows one CPU fit under 30 min, drop the GPU. Total estimated
solver cost: ~1 h feature build + ~0.3 h baseline + ~3-6 h alternative incl. sensitivity.

*Alternatives considered and not chosen.* (i) A deep sequence model (LSTM/transformer) on the raw
event stream: would likely predict deterioration better, but the question is about separating
explanations, and a black-box predictor cannot deliver `rho` or a regime-specific risk — recorded
as deferred, revisitable only if the question becomes predictive. (ii) An instrumental-variable
analysis using hospital testing intensity: rejected above on exclusion-restriction grounds, kept as
a descriptive contrast. (iii) Within-patient self-controlled (case-crossover) design across repeated
gaps: attractive for removing stay-level confounding, but exposure and outcome are strongly
time-trended within a stay (testing intensity falls monotonically while discharge hazard rises), so
the design is not valid here; recorded as rejected with that reason.

## 6. Pre-specified falsification criteria

- **F1 (primary).** H-cause is supported only if, in the non-improving stratum, the E1-vs-E0 24 h
  deterioration risk ratio is >= 1.25 with 95% CI lower bound > 1.05, **and** the ratio of risk
  ratios (non-improving / improving) is >= 1.3 with 95% CI excluding 1. If the interaction CI
  includes 1 and the point estimate is < 1.15, the monitoring-gap-harm claim is **not supported**
  at the precision achieved, and we say so.
- **F2 (end of life).** Re-run excluding every landmark for which a restrictive
  `carePlanGeneral` Care Limitation entry (any `cplitemvalue` other than 'Full therapy') or a
  `carePlanEOL.cpleoldiscussionoffset` exists at `cplitemoffset < t`. If the E1 effect loses
  significance and drops below RR 1.1, the signal is attributed to limitation of therapy, not to
  monitoring.
- **F3 (artefact / absence).** If the E2 association is substantially larger than the E1
  association (ratio of RRs > 1.5), the exposure is reading absence-from-unit or feed interruption,
  not testing de-escalation, and the H-cause interpretation is withdrawn.
- **F4 (feed-integrity screen).** Flag hospitals whose case-mix-adjusted long-gap rate is an
  extreme outlier (measured raw range 0.003-0.331; threshold: > Q3 + 3 IQR) **and** in which
  >= 5% of stays contain a >= 12 h window with no `lab`, no `nurseCharting` and no `vitalPeriodic`
  record. Exclude them and re-run F1. If F1 then fails, the original signal was an IT artefact.
- **F5 (negative control exposure).** A lengthened interval for tests not used for short-horizon
  ICU decisions (`total protein` 432,993 rows; `alkaline phos.` 429,899; `MCHC` 1,104,854) must
  **not** predict 24 h deterioration once core-panel interval is adjusted for. If it does, the
  exposure is a proxy for general documentation/process intensity, and the specific
  monitoring-information claim fails.
- **F6 (reverse causation).** Landmarks whose last draw was within 6 h before the deterioration
  event are excluded from the primary analysis (a draw stopped because the patient was already
  crashing, or a crash immediately after a draw). A "still in unit at t+6h" variant is reported
  separately and labelled as itself introducing mild selection.
- **F7 (dose-response).** H-cause predicts monotonic risk in time-since-last-draw within the
  non-improving stratum. A non-monotone or U-shaped curve is evidence against it.

## 7. What each outcome would and would not establish

- **Supportive** (F1 met; F2-F6 pass): the 24 h deterioration hazard is higher when testing was
  de-escalated in patients whose measured physiology was not improving, in a 160-hospital US ICU
  population, after accounting for care limitation, out-of-unit/feed artefacts, competing discharge
  and reverse causation. This would be a *risk-stratified hypothesis* with a concrete decision rule.
  It would **not** establish that restoring testing prevents deaths; that requires a randomised
  de-implementation/escalation design such as [K3], and clinical adjudication of whether the
  deterioration was in fact unrecognised (chart review of `note.csv.gz` on a sampled subset, which
  this study would provide but not complete).
- **Adverse** (no interaction, or E1 protective in both strata; or F2/F3/F4/F5 triggered): the seed's
  direction is rejected in eICU-CRD and the de-escalation signal is best read as clinician judgement
  plus end-of-life/artefact structure. This is a useful negative result: it is observational support
  for the safety premise being tested randomly in [K3], and a warning (reinforcing [K1]) that
  "testing intensity" in EHR data is a prognosis proxy rather than a manipulable exposure.
- **Inconclusive** (wide CIs, dominated by the non-improving x E1 cell being small — note only
  3.93% of post-24 h intervals exceed 30 h, and only 30.6% of long gaps have continued monitoring,
  so the E1 x non-improving cell may be of order 10^3-10^4 landmarks): report the *measurement*
  finding as the primary deliverable — that in eICU-CRD long laboratory gaps substantially co-occur
  with sparse bedside documentation (0.306 vs 0.559) and cluster by hospital (0.003-0.331) — which
  bounds every future study using eICU testing intensity as an exposure. An imprecise null will be
  reported as imprecise, not as refutation.

Claims that are **computationally checkable**: all coverage numbers, the interval distributions, the
interaction estimate and its CIs, the F2-F7 screens, `rho`, the MSM regime risk. Claims that
**require clinical adjudication or other data**: whether a given gap was a deliberate de-escalation
decision; whether a deterioration was *unrecognised* as opposed to recognised and accepted; whether
a 12 h all-table silence was a feed outage or a transfer out of the unit (eICU has no calendar
timestamps to align outages across patients, and no interface/audit log is published).

## 8. Threats

- **T1 Reverse causation.** Testing stops because the patient is already dying/crashing, or
  because the patient is improving. Handled by: F6 6 h lag exclusion, the trajectory stratification,
  F7 dose-response, and the joint model's `rho`. Not fully removable.
- **T2 Immortal time.** The seed's structure ("stays with a long gap" conditions on surviving long
  enough to have one). Handled by the landmark grid: eligibility uses only `unitdischargeoffset > t`
  and pre-`t` history, risk time starts at `t`, LOS is never an inclusion criterion.
- **T3 Discharge/transfer censoring = competing risk.** 93,121 stays discharge to Floor, 26,945 to
  SDU, 6,208 to another ICU, 4,164 to another hospital; discharge is the *competing* reason testing
  stops. Treated as a competing event with a shared random effect, plus IPCW in the MSM; a
  "transfer-to-other-ICU/other-hospital" sensitivity analysis treats those as censoring instead.
- **T4 Interface / data-feed interruption.** eICU `lab` is interfaced from hospital systems and
  completeness varies by hospital [K2, Table 8 / "Data completion" section]; a gap can be an IT
  artefact. Screens: E1/E2 split on monitoring continuity, F4 hospital-level feed screen, F5
  negative-control labs. **Limit:** without absolute timestamps, concurrent patients at one hospital
  cannot be aligned in wall-clock time, so a true outage test is only possible in the weaker
  "clusters by hospital and by all-table silence" form. Stated, not solved.
- **T5 End-of-life care.** 28,608 stays carry a restrictive Care Limitation entry (19.9% of LOS
  >= 48 h stays), median earliest entry 6.7 h. F2 excludes them before the landmark. The explicit
  'No blood draws' order is too rare (137 rows) to be usable.
- **T6 Confounding by indication / unmeasured reassurance.** The core identification problem;
  addressed by stratification, `rho`, and an E-value, not eliminated.
- **T7 Documentation heterogeneity as effect modifier.** Measured: 31% of hospitals with >= 200
  stays have < 5% `infusionDrug` coverage; the composite escalation outcome is therefore restricted
  to adequately documenting hospitals, with ICU death as the all-hospital primary.
- **T8 Multiplicity / peeking.** F1 is the single pre-specified primary test with one interaction
  term, one window (24 h) and one threshold (14 h); everything else is labelled sensitivity or
  exploratory. Hospital-level splits are reported with their nominal and multiplicity-adjusted CIs.
  Threshold and window choices are fixed from the audit in Section 3 *before* any outcome model.
