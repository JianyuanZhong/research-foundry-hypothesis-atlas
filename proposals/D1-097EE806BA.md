# When creatinine falls after AKI, which reason it fell for: a mass-balance separation of dilution, lost creatinine generation and restored clearance, and whether the mechanism predicts dialysis and death

Branch B repair of expert seed 03 ("Discordant recovery after acute kidney injury",
[prior hypothesis]). MIMIC-IV 3.1 only.

**[PLANNED]** = to be computed by the study, **[UNRESOLVED]** = acknowledged gap.

---

## 1. The unresolved question and why it matters clinically

A falling serum creatinine is the operational signal that an ICU patient is recovering from
acute kidney injury. That single signal drives consequential decisions: whether to stop or
not start renal replacement therapy, whether to re-dose renally cleared drugs upward,
whether nephrology follows the patient after discharge, and — in trials — whether the
patient counts as a treatment success. "Major adverse kidney events" and "renal recovery"
endpoints are built on it.

But serum creatinine is a concentration, and concentration has a denominator. A critically
ill patient who receives large volumes of fluid expands the volume in which creatinine is
distributed. The concentration can fall while the mass of retained creatinine, and hence
the underlying filtration failure, is unchanged. If this happens at clinically material
scale during the *recovery* phase, then some patients are being declared recovered by an
arithmetic artifact, and the decisions above are being made on a number that does not mean
what it is taken to mean.

**The specific unresolved claim this experiment tests:** at a prespecified landmark during
AKI, correcting serum creatinine for cumulative fluid-driven volume expansion reclassifies
a material fraction of patients out of the "recovered" class, and the reclassified patients
carry substantially higher subsequent risk of RRT initiation, persistent AKI and death than
the patients who remain classified as recovered. Equivalently: the apparent fall in
creatinine under large positive fluid balance is substantially dilution, not returning
filtration.

**Clinical consequence if true:** a bedside correction computable from data already charted
(admission weight and the fluid ledger) would identify a group currently mislabelled as
recovering, who should not have RRT withdrawn, should not have renal drug doses escalated,
and should be followed. **If false**, that is also worth knowing and is currently assumed
rather than demonstrated: it would mean that in the recovery phase the volume correction
adds noise rather than signal, and that effort should move to generation-aware or
excretion-based measures instead.

## 2. What the evidence already supports versus what is untested

**Strongest already-supported claim (do not re-test).** Fluid accumulation biases serum
creatinine downward by a quantifiable amount, and correcting for it detects AKI earlier.
Macedo et al. [K1], inspected in full text, computed in 253 PICARD patients
`adjusted creatinine = sCr x [(admission weight x 0.6 + Σ daily cumulative fluid balance (L)) / (admission weight x 0.6)]`
and observed median cumulative balance rising 2.7 L (day 2) to 6.5 L (day 7), median
adjusted-minus-crude difference 0.09 to 0.65 mg/dL, and ~13.6% underestimation by day 7,
with 64/253 (25%) of AKI diagnoses recognised late by crude creatinine. Independently,
Doi et al. [R-Doi] showed in nephrectomised mice that sepsis dramatically reduces creatinine
*production* without change in body weight, haematocrit or extracellular fluid volume —
so a falling creatinine has at least two volume-independent causes. ADQI 16 [R-ADQI] states
that definitions and management strategies for acute kidney disease and renal recovery are
"not currently available".

**What is NOT established, and is the target here.** [K1]'s cohort was deliberately selected
for 3–7 days of *consecutive rising* creatinine with no fluctuation, **before** dialysis —
i.e. the AKI *development* phase. Its outcome was earlier *detection*, and its late-recognition
group received more dialysis but showed **no mortality difference**. Nothing in [K1]
addresses the falling-creatinine *recovery* phase, reclassification of recovery status,
separation of dilution from reduced generation in the same patients, or competing-risk
outcomes after a landmark. [R-Doi] establishes the generation rival in animals but gives no
human estimate and no way to partition the two effects in a patient. [R-ADQI] establishes that
the recovery definition is an open consensus problem, not that any particular correction
fixes it. That gap — recovery-phase reclassification, with the generation rival actively
separated rather than assumed away — is this proposal.

## 2b. Prior art that bounds this claim (added by the Lead after an independent audit)

An independent literature audit run in this episode (`work/litA/novelty-audit.md`,
PubMed query log in `work/litA/search-log.md`) surfaced two works that bound this
proposal and that an honest version of it must answer, not omit.

**Pickering, Ralib and Endre 2013 [K2]** already combined creatinine kinetics with volume
kinetics, accounting for fluid type, infusion rate and urine output. In simulation, 6 h of
fluid at 1 L/h lowered creatinine by 11-19% (crystalloid) or 36-43% (colloid), delaying AKI
diagnosis by 2-9 h independently of any change in true GFR. Applied to 49 post-cardiac-arrest
patients, they concluded that **fluid dilution alone could not explain the rapid creatinine
fall in 39 of 49 patients, and that additional loss of creatinine production was required**;
six patients with near-flat creatinine were inferred to have lost about half their GFR, with
urinary cystatin C and NGAL as an independent arbiter. So the dilution-versus-generation
decomposition is not a new idea, and the available small-cohort human evidence points away
from dilution being the dominant cause.

**Törnblom et al. 2021 [K3]** reported that fluid-balance-adjusted creatinine added little
of clinical value for diagnosing AKI in a general adult ICU — a direct negative result
against the clinical usefulness of the correction in exactly the population studied here
(FINNAKI, n=2044, 7279 creatinine observations; adjustment reclassified **19 patients, about
1%**, absolute difference 0.9% [95% CI 0.3-1.6%], no mortality signal; the authors called the
physiological rationale questionable).

**A quantitative tension this proposal must own.** My own bounded probe
(`work/branchB/reclass_count.py`) found **19.7% [OBSERVED]** of observed-recovered patients
reclassified, roughly twenty times [K3]'s 1%. These are not the same quantity — [K3] asks
whether adjustment creates *new AKI diagnoses* at presentation, while this asks whether a
*creatinine fall to below a recovery threshold* survives correction, and the probe uses the
approximate day-level balance declared in §10 — but the gap is large enough that one credible
outcome of this study is that the 19.7% shrinks toward [K3]'s 1% once the exact all-output
balance is used. Criterion F1 (<5% reclassified) is therefore a live possibility, not a
formality, and that pre-specified null is reportable.

Three further inspected works (abstracts, receipts in `work/litA/refs/`) sharpen the bound
and must be stated rather than omitted:

- **Kuhnert et al. 2024** (doi:10.1111/ctr.15457, `refs/B04`) already recomputed
  **transient versus persistent AKI** under both crude and fluid-adjusted creatinine
  (n=131 lung transplant) and found persistence prognostic *regardless* of the adjustment.
  So even the recovery-classification framing has a published precedent with a null for the
  correction — in a single-surgical-population cohort, with a transient/persistent dichotomy
  rather than a mechanism decomposition, and without any generation anchor.
- **Prowle et al. 2014** reported ~30% creatinine falls independent of AKI and 135% more
  discharge CKD diagnoses after correction; **Stads et al. 2018** found the opposite
  prognostic direction (*low* adjusted creatinine predicted death). These two cannot both be
  the whole story, and neither separated dilution from generation.

**What this does to the proposal.** It removes "apply a dilution correction and show it
matters" as a defensible contribution, and it makes the unresolved question sharper rather
than absent:

1. **Phase.** [K1], [K2] and [K3] all study the *development/detection* phase. Whether the
   same decomposition holds while creatinine is *falling* — the phase in which recovery is
   declared, RRT is withdrawn and drug doses are escalated — is untested. The two phases are
   not symmetric: during recovery, volume is often being removed rather than added, muscle
   wasting has had days to accumulate, and the clinical decision is the opposite one.
2. **Scale and arbiter.** [K2]'s human decomposition rests on 49 post-cardiac-arrest patients
   with a biomarker arbiter that MIMIC-IV does not contain (no cystatin C; confirmed). This
   proposal substitutes a different, weaker but far more abundant arbiter — **measured
   urinary creatinine excretion** (itemid 51082, 237,799 rows) as a volume-independent
   generation anchor — in thousands of general ICU patients, and reports the subset without
   it separately rather than pooling.
3. **Consequence rather than detection.** Neither [K2] nor [K3] asks the question that
   matters for the decision: *given that creatinine fell, does it matter prognostically which
   reason it fell for?* The primary comparison here is therefore not "corrected versus
   observed creatinine" but **dilution-dominant versus generation-dominant versus true-
   clearance-dominant creatinine fall, and their subsequent risk of RRT and death under
   competing risks.**

**Revised primary claim.** Among ICU patients whose creatinine falls after AKI, the
mechanism of the fall — volume expansion, reduced creatinine generation, or restored
clearance — is separable with routinely collected data, and the three groups carry
materially different subsequent risk of RRT, persistent AKI and death. The original
dilution-only claim is retained as the first of three mutually exclusive mechanisms and can
now be refuted by the data as [K2] predicts, which is a genuinely informative outcome rather
than a failed study. Falsification criteria F1-F7 in section 9 are unchanged and already
cover that case: **F4 explicitly declares the generation rival the winner** if the median
volume share of the creatinine fall is below 0.25, and [K2] gives concrete prior reason to
expect it. If F4 fires, the conclusion is that recovery assessment needs a generation-aware
measure, not a fluid correction — and that conclusion would be established at roughly a
hundred times [K2]'s human sample size, in a general ICU population, in the recovery phase.

**Honest limit on this novelty statement.** It is a claim about the works actually inspected
in `work/litA/refs/` (34 frozen sources), not an exhaustive search. [K2] and [K3] were
inspected as abstracts only; a full-text read of [K2] could show that part of the phase
argument above is already addressed, which would narrow the contribution further.

## 3. Leading explanation and the rivals it must be separated from

**Leading claim (L):** during the recovery phase of AKI, a fall in observed serum creatinine
occurring under large cumulative positive fluid balance is substantially dilution; the
retained creatinine *mass* has not fallen proportionately, and filtration has not recovered.

**Rival R1 — reduced creatinine generation.** Sepsis and muscle wasting lower creatinine
production [R-Doi]. Observed creatinine falls, total creatinine mass genuinely falls, volume
correction does NOT fix it, and the patient's filtration is still impaired. This rival
predicts the *same* observed-creatinine trajectory as L, which is exactly why a volume
correction alone cannot settle the question.

**Rival R2 — severity confounding.** Sicker patients receive more fluid. Any association
between "reclassified by the correction" and bad outcomes could simply be the fluid dose
acting as a severity marker, with the creatinine arithmetic incidental.

**Rival R3 — treatment artifacts.** Loop diuretics raise urine output and lower creatinine
by improving clearance; RRT lowers creatinine by extracorporeal removal and simultaneously
removes volume. Both change numerator and denominator at once.

**Rival R4 — measurement and timing.** Creatinine draw frequency is informative (sicker
patients are drawn more often); urine output charting is incomplete; MIMIC records no
insensible losses, so every fluid balance is biased positive; admission weight is charted
mostly in pounds **[OBSERVED]** and is itself imprecise.

**Rival R5 — competing risks.** Death and ICU/hospital discharge remove patients from the
risk set for RRT and persistent AKI, in a way correlated with the exposure.

**Rival R6 — immortal time.** Any landmark defined using information that is only available
later (e.g. "patients who later recovered") manufactures survival.

### The observations that separate them

The design rests on one quantity the literature does not use in the recovery phase:
**creatinine mass**.

Let `V(t) = TBW0 + CumBal(t)` with `TBW0 = f x weight_kg`, `f = 0.6` (as in [K1]) and
`CumBal` the cumulative net fluid balance in litres. Define
`M(t) = Cr_obs(t) x V(t)` (mg/dL x L; a mass up to a unit constant).

| Observable | L (dilution) | R1 (reduced generation) | R2 (severity only) | R3 (treatment) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `M(t)` while `Cr_obs` falls | approximately **flat** | **falls** | no constraint | falls (removal) |
| Urinary creatinine excretion rate (51082 x urine volume / time) | **unchanged or rising** | **falls** | no constraint | rises (diuresis) |
| `Cr_obs` after documented net fluid *removal* (ultrafiltration 226457 / 224191, or a negative-balance day) | **rebounds upward** | does not rebound | no constraint | ambiguous during RRT |
| Dose-response of reclassification in `CumBal/TBW0` | **monotone, steep** | absent or flat | present but should be removed by severity adjustment | confined to treated patients |
| Non-muscle-generated dilution markers (Hgb 51222, Hct 51221, total protein 50976) | fall **in step** with the correction factor | do not track | no constraint | variable |
| Negative-control stratum: AKI patients with near-zero cumulative balance | correction changes **nothing** | creatinine still falls | outcomes still bad | n/a |

Four of these are *within-patient* comparisons, which is what makes R2 attackable: severity
does not change between the hour before and the hour after an ultrafiltration episode,
but volume does.

**Where they remain indistinguishable (stated, not papered over):** a patient who is
simultaneously fluid-overloaded and septic has both mechanisms operating; `M(t)` partially
flat and partially falling is the generic case, and the partition depends on `f` and on the
completeness of the fluid ledger. Haemoglobin and protein have their own non-dilution
dynamics (bleeding, transfusion, capillary leak), so they bound rather than identify.
No analysis here can establish true GFR, because **[OBSERVED] MIMIC-IV 3.1 contains no
cystatin C lab item** (1,651 catalogued items inspected) and measured creatinine clearance
(itemid 51080) has only 717 rows.

## 5. Temporal boundaries — no future information

- `t = 0` is ICU `intime`.
- **Landmark `t_L` = intime + 72 h.** Fixed in advance, identical for everyone, and
  chosen because it is the earliest time at which a meaningful cumulative balance has
  accrued (**[OBSERVED]** median 5.45 L by day 3 under the declared approximation) while
  retaining a large cohort.
- Everything used to classify a patient — baseline, AKI onset, peak creatinine, cumulative
  balance, weight, diuretics, RRT, severity covariates — comes from `[0, t_L]` only.
- Follow-up for outcomes starts **strictly after** `t_L` and runs to 90 days or the end of
  observation. This structure is what removes R6: nobody is required to survive past `t_L`
  to be classified, and nothing after `t_L` enters the classifier.
- Sensitivity: landmarks at 48 h and 7 d, prespecified, reported together, not
  selected post hoc.

## 6. Exact definitions and formulas

**KDIGO AKI staging (creatinine).** Baseline `b` = lowest serum creatinine in
`[intime − 7 d, intime + 6 h]`. Stage 1: peak ≥ 1.5b or rise ≥ 0.3 mg/dL in 48 h.
Stage 2: peak ≥ 2.0b. Stage 3: peak ≥ 3.0b, or peak ≥ 4.0 mg/dL, or RRT.

**KDIGO AKI staging (urine output).** Hourly urine from `icu/outputevents` urine itemids
(226559, 226560, 226561, 226563, 226564, 226565, 226567, 226557, 226558, 226627, 226631,
227489) divided by weight. Stage 1: < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6–12 h. Stage 2: < 0.5 for ≥ 12 h.
Stage 3: < 0.3 for ≥ 24 h or anuria ≥ 12 h. Urine-based staging is reported but, because
charting is incomplete (**[OBSERVED]** urine present for 90,366 of 94,444 stays, with
gaps within stays), it is a secondary criterion and an explicit sensitivity analysis, not
a primary inclusion gate.

**Cumulative fluid balance.** `CumBal(t) = Σ intake − Σ output` over `[0, t]`, with
intake = `icu/inputevents.amount` restricted to `amountuom in ('ml','L')` (L x 1000;
**[OBSERVED]** 5,906,295 mL rows and 72 L rows, with `mg`/`dose`/`units` rows excluded as
drug mass), and output = **all** `icu/outputevents` itemids plus chartevents ultrafiltrate
(226457) and hourly patient fluid removal (224191). Insensible losses are not recorded
**[UNRESOLVED]**, so `CumBal` is biased positive; this bias is quantified in §9.

**Dilution correction (Macedo form, [K1], verbatim from inspected full text).**

```
adjusted creatinine = sCr x correction_factor
correction_factor   = (weight_kg x f + CumBal_L) / (weight_kg x f)
```

with `f = 0.6` as the primary analysis exactly as in [K1], and a **prespecified sensitivity
range f ∈ {0.45, 0.50, 0.55, 0.60, 0.65}** covering sex, obesity and oedema effects on the
total-body-water fraction. `weight_kg` is admission weight from chartevents 226512 (kg) or
226531 (lbs x 0.45359237) **[OBSERVED, load-bearing: pounds is the more common unit,
143,674 vs 87,394 rows]**.

**Creatinine mass.** `M(t) = Cr_obs(t) x (f x weight_kg + CumBal_L(t))`. The dilution claim
predicts `M` approximately conserved while `Cr_obs` falls.

**Generation anchor.** Where a urine creatinine (51082) and a time-adjacent urine volume
exist, excretion rate `E = UCr x Uvol / Δt`; and with a paired serum value,
measured clearance `CrCl = UCr x Uvol / (SCr x Δt)`. **[OBSERVED]** 237,799 urine
creatinine rows; a timed-collection subcohort from 24 hr Creatinine (51067, 10,828 rows).

**Recovery status at the landmark (the exposure).**
- *Observed recovery*: `Cr_obs(t_L) ≤ 1.5 x b` (mean of values within ± 12 h of `t_L`).
- *Corrected recovery*: `Cr_adj(t_L) ≤ 1.5 x b`.
- Four-level exposure: recovered-by-both; **reclassified** (observed-recovered,
  corrected-not-recovered); recovered-by-neither; corrected-only (expected rare, a
  negative-balance artefact).
**[OBSERVED]** at 72 h with `f = 0.6`: 3,617 observed-recovered, 2,943 corrected-recovered,
**712 reclassified (19.7% of observed-recovered)**, 38 in the reverse direction, out of
5,846 analysable patients, with 5,120 in positive balance and median balance 0.109 of TBW
(p90 0.363) — under the declared calendar-day, urine-only-output approximation of §10.

Measured from `t_L` forward, to 90 days:

1. **RRT initiation after the landmark** — first `icu/procedureevents` 225441, 225802 or
   225805 with `starttime > t_L`. **[OBSERVED]** 4,472 ICU stays have delivered RRT.

2. **Persistent AKI at day 7 and at day 14** — serum creatinine ≥ 1.5 x baseline, or RRT,

3. **Death** — `hosp/admissions.deathtime` / `hospital_expire_flag` and `patients.dod`,

4. **Secondary: creatinine rebound** — `Cr_obs` returning to ≥ 1.5 x baseline within 72 h
   after the landmark. This is the most mechanism-specific outcome: under pure dilution,
   a reclassified patient whose fluid is subsequently removed should rebound.

Competing risks of death and of hospital discharge are handled explicitly (§8), not by
censoring.

## 8. Analysis

**A. Reclassification description.** Cross-tabulate observed vs corrected recovery at
`t_L`; report the reclassified fraction with a bootstrap CI across `f ∈ {0.45..0.65}`.
This step is descriptive and is already feasibility-confirmed **[OBSERVED]**.

**B. Baseline risk model (the required simple comparator).** Fine–Gray competing-risk
regression for post-landmark RRT with death as the competing event, and Cox for death.
Exposure = the four-level recovery status. Covariates measured in `[0, t_L]` only: age,
sex, AKI stage, baseline creatinine, SOFA components available in MIMIC, vasopressor
exposure, mechanical ventilation, sepsis, loop-diuretic exposure (**[OBSERVED]** 507,250
prescription rows, 55,751 subjects), total fluid intake, and **creatinine draw count** as
an explicit measurement-intensity control for R4. Pre-landmark-RRT patients analysed
separately. **[PLANNED]**

**C. Attack on R2 (severity).** (i) Add cumulative *intake* and a severity score to the
model so the reclassification indicator must carry risk beyond fluid dose and illness
severity; (ii) a within-stratum analysis matched on cumulative balance decile, so that
reclassified and non-reclassified patients received *the same* fluid and differ only in
the creatinine arithmetic. If reclassification carries no risk within balance strata, R2
is the better explanation of any crude association. **[PLANNED]**

**D. Attack on R1 (generation) — the discriminating analysis.** In patients with urine
creatinine (51082): regress the change in `M(t)` over `[0, t_L]` on the change in urinary
creatinine excretion rate. Partition the observed creatinine fall into a volume term and a
residual mass term:
`Δln Cr_obs = −Δln V + Δln M`. Report the distribution of the volume share
`−Δln V / Δln Cr_obs` across patients, and the fraction with `M` conserved
(|Δln M| < 0.1) versus `M` falling (Δln M < −0.2) with stable excretion. **[PLANNED]**

**E. Attack on R3 (treatment).** Stratify by diuretic exposure and by pre-landmark RRT;
within-patient reversibility analysis around documented ultrafiltration episodes
(chartevents 226457, 224191) and around negative-balance days, comparing `Cr_obs` before
and after with the patient as their own control. **[PLANNED]**

**F. Negative control.** The stratum with |CumBal| < 1 L at `t_L` (**[OBSERVED]** 726 of
5,846 are in non-positive balance, so this stratum is populated). Here the correction is
near-identity; reclassification must be near zero. Any apparent "reclassification effect"
in this stratum is an artefact of the pipeline. **[PLANNED]**

**G. Falsification-marker test.** Compare the correction factor against the simultaneous
fractional change in haemoglobin (51222), haematocrit (51221) and total protein (50976) —
markers that dilute but are not generated by muscle. Under L these should fall in rough
proportion to `1/correction_factor` in transfusion-free, bleeding-free windows. **[PLANNED]**

Uncertainty: bootstrap at the patient level; prespecified `f` sensitivity; all landmark
sensitivities (48 h, 72 h, 7 d) reported jointly. Exploratory analyses are labelled as such;
confirmation of any threshold chosen by inspection is deferred to a held-out split by
subject-id hash.

## 9. Prespecified falsification criteria, with numbers

The proposal is **falsified or materially weakened** if any of the following holds.

- **F1 (no reclassification).** Fewer than 5% of observed-recovered patients are
  reclassified at `f = 0.6`. Then the correction is clinically irrelevant regardless of
  risk. *Current feasibility estimate 19.7% [OBSERVED], so F1 is unlikely but is a real
  possibility once the exact, all-output balance replaces the §10 approximation.*
- **F2 (no risk separation).** The Fine–Gray subdistribution hazard ratio for post-landmark
  RRT in the reclassified group versus the recovered-by-both group has an upper 95% bound
  below 1.3, i.e. the reclassified group is not materially higher-risk.
- **F3 (severity explains it).** The reclassification association disappears within
  cumulative-balance deciles: HR point estimate moves to within 0.1 of 1.0, or the
  balance-matched estimate's CI includes 1.0 while the crude one does not. Then R2 wins.
- **F4 (generation explains it).** In the urine-creatinine subcohort, the median volume
  share of the creatinine fall is < 0.25, and/or more than half of patients show `M` falling
  (Δln M < −0.2) with stable excretion. Then R1 is the dominant mechanism and the volume
  correction is the wrong fix.
- **F5 (no reversibility).** Around documented ultrafiltration/negative-balance episodes,
  the median `Cr_obs` rebound is < 10% of the predicted dilution effect. The dilution
  account requires reversibility; its absence is strong adverse evidence.
- **F6 (negative control fails).** Reclassification rate in the |CumBal| < 1 L stratum
  exceeds 2%, indicating a pipeline artefact rather than a dilution effect.
- **F7 (correction is not robust to `f`).** The reclassified fraction or the risk estimate
  changes direction across `f ∈ {0.45, 0.65}`. Then the result is an artefact of an
  unmeasured volume-of-distribution assumption, and no clinical use follows.

## 11. What each result would mean

**Supportive** (F1–F7 all survive; reclassified fraction ≥ 10%; Fine–Gray HR for RRT with
lower bound > 1.3; volume share of the creatinine fall ≥ 0.5; reversibility present;
negative control clean): there is a reproducible, mechanistically coherent class of ICU
patients whose apparent AKI recovery is substantially dilution, who are higher risk, and
who are identifiable from already-charted data. This would justify (a) reporting corrected
creatinine alongside observed creatinine in AKI recovery definitions, and (b) a prospective
study with cystatin C or measured clearance as the reference. It would **not** establish
that acting on the corrected value improves outcomes — that requires an interventional
study, because the correction's value depends on what clinicians do differently.

**Adverse to the dilution mechanism** (F4 or F5 triggers): the recovery-phase creatinine
fall is driven more by reduced generation or by genuine clearance recovery than by dilution.
This is a genuinely useful result, and it is the outcome [K2] predicts from 49 post-arrest
patients: it would mean that extending [K1]'s development-phase correction to the recovery
phase is not justified — consistent with [K3]'s negative diagnostic finding — and would
redirect effort to excretion-based measures (urinary creatinine excretion rate), which this
work would have characterised at scale in humans, in the recovery phase, for the first time,
complementing [R-Doi]'s animal finding and extending [K2] beyond a single-aetiology cohort
of 49. Under the revised primary claim in §2b this outcome is adverse to mechanism R0
(dilution) but still answers the study's question, provided the prognostic comparison in F2
is reported for the generation-dominant stratum as prespecified.

**Severity-explained** (F3 triggers): the correction is a severity marker in disguise. The
honest conclusion is that corrected creatinine has prognostic but not mechanistic content,
and should not be used to justify continuing RRT in an individual.

**Inconclusive** (wide CIs, or results flipping across `f`, or heavy missingness in the
urine-creatinine subcohort): report as inconclusive with the achieved precision, and state
the missing evidence (cystatin C, insensible losses, muscle mass) rather than picking the
favourable landmark or `f`. An imprecise null does not refute the dilution claim.

## 12. What would require additional data or clinical adjudication

- **True filtration recovery** cannot be established here: no cystatin C, no measured GFR,
  and only 717 measured-clearance rows **[OBSERVED]**. Any claim about GFR requires a
  prospective cohort with a filtration marker.
- **Reduced creatinine generation** can be bounded by urinary excretion but not explained:
  muscle mass, protein intake and nitrogen balance are absent.
- **Whether a patient "really" recovered** at the bedside is a clinical judgement; chart
  review by a nephrologist on a sample of reclassified patients is needed to confirm that
  the reclassification is clinically recognisable and not a data artefact.
- **Whether acting on the corrected value helps** is interventional and outside any
  observational design.
- **Post-discharge dialysis and nephrology follow-up** are not observable in MIMIC, so
  90-day renal outcomes are incomplete.

## 13. Method comparison

A simple landmark competing-risk model versus a per-patient mechanistic mass-balance model
of creatinine kinetics (generation, expanding volume of distribution, clearance) are
developed as comparable alternatives on this same question, with identifiability concerns,
splits, evaluation and compute budget, in `work/branchB/method-comparison.md`.

**Novelty statement (revised).** Established: dilution lowers measured creatinine and
correcting for it detects AKI earlier [K1]; the correction adds little diagnostic value in
a general adult ICU [K3]; a combined creatinine-volume-kinetic decomposition in 49
post-cardiac-arrest patients attributes most of the fall to lost generation rather than
