# Provisional hypothesis: discordant HbA1c rise and weight trajectory predict incident diabetes

## Status

This is a provisional, evidence-bounded draft for episode 5. It is not a result and is not selected. The hypothesis is grounded in the prior episode's feasibility restart: the pancreatic-cancer direction failed its zero-event gate, while this metabolic endpoint is common and clinically interpretable. Catalog/table verification, event-count audit, and literature inspection remain incomplete.

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with two valid assessment-center visits, no diabetes or cancer before the second visit, a comparable rise in HbA1c is associated with higher subsequent incident-diabetes risk when accompanied by weight loss than when accompanied by weight gain or stability.

The clinical question is whether the same worsening glycemic state should be interpreted differently when body weight is falling, because that joint pattern may identify people needing earlier metabolic follow-up. This is a prognostic association, not a claim that weight loss causes diabetes, that it identifies occult cancer, or that treatment should be changed without clinical review.

## Evidence and uncertainty

The prior branch provides a concrete feasibility observation: the earlier pancreatic-cancer endpoint had inadequate event support, motivating a common incident-diabetes endpoint. It does not provide evidence that the proposed discordance exists. The available UKB fields can support paired HbA1c, glucose, weight/BMI, assessment dates, diagnosis dates, cancer dates, and treatment context, but exact source paths, table names, archive members, participant-key documentation, overlap, missingness, and event counts have not yet been verified.

The strongest plausible rival is that weight loss marks pre-existing illness, frailty, treatment, or observation intensity rather than a distinct pathway. Interval between visits, selection into repeat assessment, and prevalent disease are additional rivals. A positive association would not identify a mechanism.

## Falsifiable experiment

Population: adults with two valid assessment dates and paired HbA1c, glucose, weight, and BMI; exclude diabetes, cancer, and prespecified competing diagnoses before the second visit. Time origin is the second valid assessment date. Follow-up is restricted to dates after origin, with censoring for death, loss to follow-up, or registry end. Primary outcome is incident diabetes from available diagnosis fields and dates, with a sensitivity analysis separating inpatient from broader ascertainment. Fix 2-year and 5-year windows after the catalog/event audit and before modeling.

Exposure: weight-loss versus weight-gain/stability at comparable HbA1c rise, using continuous and prespecified categorical contrasts. Do not use post-origin outcomes to define exposure.

Baseline: prespecified discrete-time survival or Cox model with HbA1c change, weight-change direction/magnitude, assessment interval, age, sex, baseline BMI, smoking, and available treatment indicators, including an HbA1c-by-weight-change interaction. Use a participant-level split fixed before fitting and uncertainty by clustered bootstrap or repeated splits.

Substantive alternative: a joint repeated-measurement trajectory model for HbA1c and weight, estimating slope/curvature and their joint state, then relating that state to incident diabetes with the same population, origin, outcome, split, and uncertainty evaluation. It could reveal whether persistence, timing, and curvature add information beyond a single between-visit difference. Defer it if repeated measurements are sparse, event support is inadequate, or the model is not identifiable. CPU is adequate for the baseline and likely adequate for a bounded trajectory analysis; no GPU is assumed.

Supportive result: the weight-loss contrast remains positive after interval and treatment handling, uncertainty excludes a clinically negligible effect, and the trajectory model is directionally consistent beyond the baseline. This supports prognostic stratification only.

Adverse result: no excess risk, or disappearance after excluding early events, handling treatment, or requiring adequate follow-up. This weakens or refutes the proposed contrast.

Inconclusive result: wide uncertainty, sparse repeat overlap, inadequate events, or instability across windows/splits. This justifies revision or abandonment rather than favorable subgroup selection.

## Exact data required and missing evidence

Required UKB fields, pending catalog verification: 53 assessment date; 30750 HbA1c; 30740 glucose; 21002 weight; 21001 BMI; 20003 treatment/medication; 41270 ICD10 diagnoses; 41280 first inpatient diagnosis date; 40005 cancer diagnosis date; 40006 cancer type. Use only the documented UKB/ukb672073 namespace and its documented participant key; do not join Olink/dta or ukb671626 merely because IDs match.

Missing: exact source paths/table names/archive members and column mappings; repeat-measurement overlap; missingness and interval distributions; incident-diabetes event counts; precise ascertainment and prevalent-disease exclusions; and exactly three inspected key works with frozen receipts. Clinical adjudication of diabetes onset, treatment intent, unintentional weight loss, and preclinical disease is unavailable from these fields and would require expert review or another study.

## Method comparison and deliverable

The scientific deliverable is a newly fitted baseline interaction model and, if identifiable, a trajectory alternative, with estimates, uncertainty, split performance, event counts, and sensitivity analyses. The baseline is the interpretable falsification test; the trajectory model tests whether the signal is durable rather than a noisy endpoint difference. This is not a prediction contest. The alternative is deferred if the data cannot support it.

No conclusion is claimed until the catalog audit and bounded feasibility checks are completed.
