# Does persistent oliguria at the time of creatinine recovery identify high-risk "creatinine-recovered" AKI in sepsis? (MIMIC-IV v3.1)

Developed from expert seed `[starting question]` (asynchronous organ recovery; parent `[prior hypothesis]`).
Dataset: MIMIC-IV v3.1 (frozen archive `mimic-iv-3.1.zip`, [source checksum]; see `work/data-verification.md` for measured bindings).
Status: **proposal with verified data bindings and a measured feasibility probe (cohort, AKI/recovery landmarks, UO coverage, unadjusted mortality gap); the prespecified inferential analysis has NOT been run.**

## 1. Scientific question and why it matters

In sepsis, AKI is defined by KDIGO using either the creatinine arm or the urine-output (UO) arm, and the two arms capture different patients and carry different prognoses [K1][K3]. Creatinine-based AKI "recovery" (return of serum creatinine toward baseline) is a recognized prognostic landmark: recovered AKI carries substantially lower mortality than non-recovered AKI [K2]. In routine practice, however, clinicians treat a falling creatinine as evidence that the kidney has recovered — and may de-escalate renal-protective measures (fluid optimization, vasopressor weaning, avoidance of nephrotoxins, RRT planning) accordingly.

The unresolved question: **among sepsis ICU patients whose creatinine has recovered (KDIGO creatinine-based AKI that later reverses), does PERSISTENT OLIGURIA at the time of creatinine recovery — urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 h within the 24 h after the creatinine-recovery landmark — independently predict in-hospital death above a severity-adjusted baseline?**

If yes, creatinine-based recovery is a misleading reassurance in a clinically meaningful subgroup, and the UO arm of KDIGO should be checked before declaring renal recovery. If no, creatinine recovery is a sufficient functional readout in sepsis and the discordance is an artifact of documentation practice.

## 2. Closest prior work and the gap

- [K1] Teixeira 2013 (Crit Care, N=132 AKI, renal-SOFA definition): mean daily urine volume independently predicts 28-day mortality (adj. HR 0.47 per L/day) after adjustment for severity, sepsis and diuretic use. **Does not** condition on creatinine recovery, does not use a time-anchored oliguria definition, and is not MIMIC-based.
- [K2] Worden 2023 (Liver Transpl, N=322 cirrhosis): creatinine-based AKI recovery (ADQI consensus) is a strong prognostic landmark (adj. sub-HR 2.07 for non-recovery). **Does not** test the UO arm or the creatinine/UO discordance window, and is restricted to cirrhosis.
- [K3] Gao 2024 (Ren Fail, MIMIC-IV N=12,196 sepsis-AKI): reduced urine output is a top SHAP predictor of in-hospital mortality in a predictive model. **Does not** condition on creatinine recovery, does not define the discordance window, and is a prediction study, not a hypothesis test.

Gap: no inspected study has tested, in a sepsis/shock ICU population, whether oliguria measured **at the creatinine-recovery landmark** independently predicts death after severity adjustment. That is the claim this experiment tests.

## 3. Rival explanations (prespecified)

- (a) **Severity confounding:** oliguric patients are sicker (shock, higher vasopressor dose, ventilated). Addressed by adjustment for age, AKI stage (peak creatinine ratio), vasopressor use and cumulative dose in the 24 h post-recovery window, mechanical ventilation, and UO-coverage (number of UO entries in the window).
- (b) **Documentation/recording artifact:** UO is recorded mainly when a catheter is in place, and catheterized patients are sicker. Addressed by (i) restricting the primary analysis to stays with ≥1 UO entry in the window (n=4,092 measured), (ii) a pre-specified sensitivity analysis comparing oliguric vs non-oliguric patients **with equal UO coverage** (both ≥6 entries in the window), and (iii) reporting the coverage×oliguria interaction.
- (c) **Biomarker lag:** creatinine is a lagging readout and UO is the true functional state, so "discordance" partly reflects measurement timing rather than a distinct phenotype. Addressed by reporting the time from AKI peak to recovery and by a sensitivity analysis requiring ≥48 h between peak and recovery (ensuring the creatinine signal had time to catch up).

## 4. Population and temporal boundaries (measured)

- **Source:** MIMIC-IV v3.1 archive (see header). All joins on `subject_id`/`hadm_id`/`stay_id`; time fields `intime`/`outtime` (icustays), `charttime` (labevents/outputevents), `deathtime` (admissions).
- **Sepsis:** any `diagnoses_icd.icd_code` whose `d_icd_diagnoses.long_title` matches `Sepsis|Septicemia|Septic shock` (87 codes, measured) → 22,507 admissions → 17,240 ICU stays.
- **Restriction:** ICU `los` ≥ 3 days (8,446 stays) so that AKI onset, recovery and a 24 h post-recovery window can all occur in-stay.
- **Creatinine-based AKI:** baseline = median creatinine in first 24 h of ICU stay (labevents itemids 50912/50822, valuenum 0.2–15.0); AKI if peak ≥ 1.5× baseline or ≥ baseline + 0.3 mg/dL.
- **Recovery landmark `t_rec`:** first creatinine after the peak ≤ max(1.3× baseline, baseline + 0.3). Measured: 4,979 recovered AKI stays; **4,676** with ≥24 h between `t_rec` and `outtime` (analysis cohort).
- **Exposure window:** `t_rec` → `t_rec + 24 h`. Primary analysis restricted to stays with ≥1 UO entry in the window (measured n=4,092; 87.5% coverage).
- **Exposure:** persistent oliguria = exists a ≥6 h sub-window (span ≥5 h, anchored at a UO event) in the exposure window with mean UO < 0.5 mL/kg/h. Weight = median `inputevents.patientweight` (30–250 kg) else 75 kg. Measured: 1,534/4,092 (37.5%) oliguric.
- **Outcome:** in-hospital death (`admissions.deathtime` not null), measured 33.3% overall in the cohort.
- **UO itemids (outputevents, verified in d_items):** 226559 Foley, 226560 Void, 226561 Condom Cath, 226563 Suprapubic, 226564 R Nephrostomy, 226565 L Nephrostomy, 226567 Straight Cath, 226566 Urine and GU Irrigant Out, 226627 OR Urine, 226631 PACU Urine, 227489 GU Irrigant/Urine Volume Out (all mL).
- **Vasopressor covariate:** inputevents itemids 221906 (norepinephrine), 221289 (epinephrine), 222315 (vasopressin), 221662 (dopamine) — use in window and cumulative amount.

## 5. Prespecified analysis

1. **Primary (baseline) model:** Cox proportional-hazards model, time origin `t_rec`, censoring at `outtime` or death, for in-hospital death. Exposure: persistent oliguria (yes/no). Covariates: age, sex, AKI stage (peak/baseline ratio), vasopressor use in window, cumulative vasopressor amount in window, mechanical ventilation in window (procedureevents), number of UO entries in window (coverage), septic-shock ICD code (R65.2x) at admission. Report HR with 95% CI.
2. **Alternative (trajectory) model:** same covariates plus the 24 h post-recovery UO trajectory summarized by (i) total UO volume in the window (mL/kg) and (ii) the minimum 6 h mean UO (mL/kg/h), to test whether the binary oliguria flag is the right summary or whether the trajectory itself carries the signal. Compare models by likelihood-ratio test and by the change in the oliguria HR after adding the trajectory terms.
3. **Pre-specified sensitivities:**
   - (S1) Equal-coverage restriction: both groups require ≥6 UO entries in the window (tests rival b).
   - (S2) Peak-to-recovery ≥48 h (tests rival c).
   - (S3) Septic-shock-only subgroup (R65.2x codes).
   - (S4) Oliguria defined as mean UO <0.5 mL/kg/h over the full 24 h window (no 6 h sub-window requirement).
   - (S5) UO data from Foley itemid only (226559) — the most reliably documented source.
4. **Falsification thresholds (pre-specified):**
   - **Supportive:** primary adjusted HR for persistent oliguria ≥ 1.30 with 95% CI excluding 1.0, preserved in S1 and S2.
   - **Adverse:** adjusted HR ≤ 1.10 or CI including 1.0, or the HR attenuates to ≤1.10 in S1 (equal coverage) — indicating the association is a documentation artifact.
   - **Inconclusive:** HR between 1.10 and 1.30 with CI including 1.0, or the oliguric group has <200 events in the primary model.
5. **Interpretation mapping:** supportive results justify a follow-up study with 28-day mortality and external validation (eICU); they do not by themselves establish causality or a treatment effect. Adverse results support treating creatinine recovery as sufficient in sepsis. Inconclusive results defer the question and point to the sensitivity that failed.

## 8. Limitations

- Observational; confounding by indication cannot be fully excluded (S1 addresses the strongest documented form).
- UO documentation is selective (catheter-dependent); 12.5% of the cohort has no UO data in the window and is excluded from the primary analysis.
- In-hospital, not 28-day, mortality; single-center; no external validation.
- Sepsis defined by ICD codes; diagnostic accuracy is imperfect (septic-shock subgroup in S3).
- Clinical causality and any treatment implication require adjudication and further study; this experiment tests prognostic independence, not mechanism.
