# Scientific Proposal: Protein Signatures Distinguishing Infection Onset from Infection-Associated Organ Damage

## Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis**: Among UK Biobank participants without prior infection at baseline, pre-illness proteomic profiles that predict hospitalization for infection (any severity) differ from those that predict infection-associated organ damage (severe infection outcomes). Specifically, we test whether certain proteins are consistently associated with infection onset across all severities, while others are specifically associated with severe infection outcomes only.

**Formal Statement**: The set of proteins with significant associations (p < 0.05) with infection hospitalization in participants without prior organ damage is partially but not wholly overlapping with the set of proteins with significant associations with infection hospitalization in participants without prior infection. The proportion of proteins associated with both outcomes (infection onset and infection-associated organ damage) is less than 50%, suggesting distinct biological pathways.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Prevention vs. Treatment Target Identification**: If proteins differ between infection onset and organ damage, then:
   - Proteins uniquely associated with infection onset could be targets for infection prevention strategies
   - Proteins uniquely associated with organ damage could be targets for severe infection treatment
   - Proteins associated with both could be targets for broad-spectrum interventions

2. **Risk Stratification**: Current clinical practice treats infections similarly regardless of severity risk factors. This study could identify:
   - High-risk individuals who may benefit from more aggressive monitoring or prophylactic treatment
   - Low-risk individuals who may benefit from conservative management

3. **Mechanistic Understanding**: Distinguishing infection initiation from infection severity provides insights into:
   - Host immune response pathways
   - Microbiome-host interactions
   - Comorbidities that predispose to severe infection

## Study Design

### Phase 1: Data Preparation
1. **Identify participants without prior infection**: Filter from population table
2. **Extract proteomic measurements**: Join main table with population table on `eid`
3. **Identify infection hospitalizations**: Filter health_outcomes table for infection-related columns
4. **Identify infection with organ damage**: Filter health_outcomes table for infection with organ damage columns
5. **Define follow-up period**: Start from assessment date, end at infection hospitalization or study end

### Phase 2: Statistical Analysis

**Baseline Approach**: Logistic regression with protein measurements as continuous predictors.

**Model 1 (Infection Onset)**:
- Outcome: Hospitalization for infection (any severity)
- Predictors: All proteomic columns, covariates (age, sex, BMI, etc.)
- Test: Individual protein coefficients

**Model 2 (Infection with Organ Damage)**:
- Outcome: Hospitalization for infection with organ damage
- Predictors: All proteomic columns, covariates (age, sex, BMI, etc.)
- Test: Individual protein coefficients

**Overlap Test**:
- Compare sets of proteins with p < 0.05 in each model
- Calculate proportion of proteins associated with both outcomes
- Test if proportion < 0.50 (hypothesis)

### Phase 3: Interpretation
- Identify proteins uniquely associated with infection onset
- Identify proteins uniquely associated with organ damage
- Identify proteins associated with both
- Assess clinical relevance of protein signatures

## Falsification Criteria

**Hypothesis Supported**: If ≥50% of proteins associated with infection onset are also associated with infection-associated organ damage, suggesting shared biological pathways.

**Hypothesis Refuted**: If <50% of proteins associated with infection onset are also associated with infection-associated organ damage, suggesting distinct biological pathways.

**Adverse Result**: If no proteins show significant associations with either outcome, suggesting the data may not support this research question.

## Baseline Methods

**Baseline**: Logistic regression with individual protein coefficients.

**Limitations of Baseline**:
- Does not account for protein correlations (multicollinearity)
- Does not provide integrated protein signature scores
- May miss interaction effects between proteins

## Substantive Alternative Approach

**Alternative**: Machine learning approach with protein signature features.

**Method**:
1. Create protein signature scores using dimensionality reduction (e.g., PCA, WGCNA)
2. Train classification/regression models with signature scores as features
3. Compare model performance between infection onset and organ damage outcomes
4. Identify important features (proteins) using model interpretability methods

**Information Revealed**:
- Captures protein correlations and interactions
- Provides integrated risk scores
- May reveal non-linear relationships

**Selection Rationale**: The alternative could reveal whether protein correlations play a larger role in predicting infection severity than individual proteins alone. This addresses a key limitation of the baseline approach.
