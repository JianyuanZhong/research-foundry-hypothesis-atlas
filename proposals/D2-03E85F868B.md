# D2-T2 evolution: donor-aware stability of the MerTK-inhibitor-associated fibroblast/macrophage pattern

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (valid D2-T2 child)
Status: hypothesis-stage proposal; no full analysis executed yet.

## Question and target

Does the MerTK-inhibitor-associated fibroblast/macrophage expression pattern remain stable across donors and experimental conditions, and can it distinguish a macrophage-linked protective state from a pathogenic state after accounting for donor, composition, and treatment?

The named target/pathway is MerTK and the downstream MerTK signaling axis, interpreted as an intervention-associated pathway rather than a proven direct target. The experiment tests whether pathway-associated expression changes can distinguish reduction of a fibroblast activation signature from loss of a macrophage-linked protective signature.

Clinical importance: fibrosis treatments that suppress fibroblast activation may also suppress macrophage states that support repair or containment of injury. A donor-aware comparison can identify whether an intervention-associated expression pattern is compatible with benefit without erasure of a protective state, or whether it is indistinguishable from a pathogenic state. Macrophage–fibroblast interactions can be regulated to attenuate pulmonary fibrosis [K1], and macrophages serve as a central nexus of tissue repair and inflammation resolution [K2]. MERTK has a novel genetic risk for Alzheimer's disease in female, supporting the importance of sex and context in MerTK-related expression patterns [K3].

## Estimand and analysis

Donor-aware comparison of MerTK-inhibitor-associated expression changes, with predefined scores for fibroblast activation and macrophage-linked protective state. The estimand is the within-donor contrast between protective and pathogenic macrophage-linked states, adjusted for donor, composition, and treatment.

## Competing explanations

- The pattern may be explained by donor, composition, or treatment alone.
- A different intervention or perturbation may produce a different pattern.
- The expression pattern may not reflect a direct target effect.

## Outcomes

- Supportive: pattern persists across donors and conditions and distinguishes protective from pathogenic state.
- Adverse: pattern is not robust or does not distinguish states.
- Inconclusive: insufficient donor/condition coverage or unresolved donor-relatedness.
