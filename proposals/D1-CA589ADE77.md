# Proposal: early post-TACE biomarker trajectories under documented surveillance

## Unresolved clinical question and hypothesis

After TACE, clinicians must decide whether a patient needs early reassessment and whether additional liver-directed treatment is warranted. AFP and hepatic-function tests are clinically available but their interpretation is confounded by irregular testing and by who receives follow-up imaging. The selected parent asked whether early AFP change plus hepatic-marker recovery predicts a later recorded repeat TACE. This successor asks the stronger, unresolved question: does that signal persist after explicitly modeling documented imaging and measurement opportunity, or is it an artifact of surveillance intensity?

The primary hypothesis is:

> In adults with structured HCC and a first recorded TACE-like procedure, an early-response phenotype—at least a 50% fall in log1p AFP from the last numeric pre-index value in days -30 to -1 to the first numeric post-index value in days 7-45, with no deterioration in at least two of albumin, total bilirubin, INR, and platelet count—has lower adjusted odds of a recorded repeat TACE-like procedure during days 46-365, and the association is not materially modified by documented liver-imaging intensity in days 7-45.

The key falsification test is the prespecified phenotype-by-imaging-intensity interaction. A substantial interaction, or attenuation to the null after adding testing opportunity, would show that the apparent early-response signal is surveillance-dependent. The estimand is prognostic association, not the causal effect of changing AFP, ordering imaging, or administering TACE. Repeat TACE and other procedures remain recorded actions, not recurrence, radiologic response, or appropriate-care outcomes.

Current evidence supports only that tumor-marker kinetics, imaging response, and liver reserve are clinically relevant inputs to HCC decisions, while imaging response alone is insufficient and integrated criteria need prospective validation (Yuza and Pawlik, 2026, PMCID PMC13406758; frozen source [source checksum]). It does not establish this local association.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use one index per patient: the earliest procedure episode satisfying the TACE-like rule, with usable procedure start time. Require age at least 18 and nonmissing sex at the index encounter. Require a structured diagnosis name containing hepatocellular carcinoma on an encounter from 180 days before through 7 days after index. Restrict index time to 2025-01-01 or earlier, so a nominal 365-day window ends within the local snapshot. Collapse same-patient procedure rows within 24 hours into one episode while retaining matched names and source.

Primary TACE-like names match literal TACE, Transarterial Chemoembolization, or both Hepatic Artery and Embolization. A literal-name sensitivity uses only TACE or Transarterial Chemoembolization. The primary outcome is any later TACE-like episode starting after day 45 and on or before day 365. A secondary escalation outcome is any later episode matching Resection, Ablation, Radiofrequency, Microwave, or the TACE rule. These outcomes do not establish tumor progression.

The primary complete-pair cohort requires numeric AFP in both the pre-window (-30,-1) and early post-window (7,45); use the last pre-index and first post-index values. Use the latest valid marker-specific values in each broad window for albumin, total bilirubin, INR, and platelet count, preserve qualitative inequality flags, and retain missingness. An all-eligible analysis uses missingness indicators and does not treat absence of testing as response.

Define documented liver-imaging intensity from deduplicated examinations whose Examination name matches:
Liver elastography|Liver ultrasound|Liver contrast-enhanced ultrasound|Upper abdomen|Whole abdomen|Liver, spleen, and portal vein|Hepatic artery CTA|Portal vein CTV|Hepatic vein CTV|PET/CT|MRCP|Contrast-enhanced.
Use counts and any/none indicators in days 7-45 as the primary observation-process covariate. Counts in days 46-180 are a post-landmark descriptive/sensitivity analysis and cannot be a day-45 predictor. Deduplicate exact patient/encounter/examination/start/diagnostic-text rows and report quality counts. Presence of an examination is not interpreted as completed imaging, adherence, or response.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The baseline is penalized logistic regression for repeat TACE, using pre-index demographics, index procedure family, prior 180-day structured diagnosis/procedure history, pre-index AFP and hepatic markers, missingness, early AFP/marker phenotype, and day 7-45 imaging/testing intensity plus its prespecified interaction. It is the transparent adjusted association and calibration benchmark.

The substantive alternative is a joint informative-observation state-space/discrete-time hazard model. It estimates two patient-level proxy states from irregular observations in days -30 to 45: tumor-activity proxy driven mainly by log1p AFP, and hepatic-reserve proxy driven by albumin, bilirubin, INR, and platelets. Random intercepts/slopes represent asynchronous trajectories. An observation submodel models whether and when labs and liver examinations are recorded from prior state/history. A day 46-365 discrete-time hazard relates posterior states, slopes, observation intensity, baseline covariates, and treatment family to repeat TACE. The states are proxies, not true tumor burden or reserve.

The alternative reveals whether trajectory timing and informative testing explain the action association; a snapshot loses that distinction. A dated sequence transformer was considered but deferred because the complete-pair cohort is approximately 1,456 before exact eligibility, outcomes are action proxies, report labels are unvalidated, and no external cohort exists. This is a validation/data limitation, not a neural-network or GPU prohibition.

## Analysis, uncertainty, and falsification

Use a patient-level temporal split: 2010-2022 fit, 2023 tuning/threshold selection, and 2024 through 2025-01-01 locked test, with one index per patient and explicit split-membership hashes. Grouped random split is sensitivity only.

Report adjusted phenotype odds ratio, phenotype-by-imaging interaction odds ratio, and locked-test alternative-minus-baseline log-score and Brier differences, with patient-bootstrap or robust-sandwich 95% intervals. Report calibration intercept/slope, Brier, integrated log score, AUROC and PR-AUC; discrimination is supporting evidence, not clinical utility. Prespecify a negligible improvement margin before fitting. A decision curve is optional and validation-only.

Supportive evidence requires a consistent phenotype direction with interval excluding the null, no clinically meaningful phenotype-by-imaging interaction, persistence after measurement-opportunity adjustment, joint-model improvement beyond the negligible margin, and directional stability under literal-name TACE, 90- and 180-day windows, missingness-stratified or inverse-probability analysis, and duplicate exclusions. This supports only local prognostic association robust to measured surveillance.

Adverse evidence is null/reversed association, meaningful interaction, attenuation after observation adjustment, calibration deterioration, or instability across sensitivity analyses. It falsifies the standalone early-response claim or marks it as surveillance-dependent. Inconclusive evidence is sparse paired measurements/events, wide intervals, assay censoring/unit ambiguity, severe imaging sparsity, poor dates, or evidence that repeat TACE mainly reflects access/eligibility. The correct conclusion is insufficient evidence.

Clinical adjudication or another study is required for mRECIST response, viable tumor, BCLC stage, treatment intent, decompensation, mortality, outside-care events, and clinical appropriateness. Examination text flags for portal thrombus, metastasis, growth, or response may be reported as exploratory counts only; clinician review is required before they become labels. QA can verify computation and conclusion-to-output linkage, but cannot establish clinical truth.
