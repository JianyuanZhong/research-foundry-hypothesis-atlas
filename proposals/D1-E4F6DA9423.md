# Observation-era robustness of fixed-capacity lactate-by-perfusion prioritization

## Status, parent and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive successor to the assigned valid parent [prior hypothesis]. It preserves the parent's first eligible live ICU-exit selection, one-hour availability buffer, 12-hour dual clinical/store-time feature window, persistent-lactate by measured-perfusion phenotype, R/D/S/N/U competing endpoint, coverage and support gates, matched B1/Bdisc comparison, fixed 5%/10%/20% queues, uncertainty, falsifications and noncausal claim limits.

The new uncertainty is whether the phenotype and its fixed-capacity queue gain are stable under a prespecified observation-era shift within MIMIC-IV. The new scientific deliverable is an era-transport analysis, not a cosmetic subgroup table:

> Among adults at the first eligible live ICU exit, does adding the prespecified lactate-by-measured-perfusion phenotype to an order-blind history model retain a clinically meaningful fixed-10% review-queue gain when a model developed in earlier MIMIC observation eras is applied without outcome refitting to a later era?

The solver must newly fit the parent models and an era-blind temporal-transport version, produce held-out predictions in an internal historical test and a later-era target test, quantify observation-process change, and report whether the phenotype interaction and queue gain survive that shift. It must produce either a gate-passing result package or a complete row-level gate-failure audit. No fitted result is claimed here.

Completion requires:

1. the unchanged parent live-exit cohort flow, first-transition labels, phenotype and endpoint-independent coverage audit;
2. an exact era assignment and no-leakage manifest;
3. unchanged parent full-split B0/B1/Bmask/Bdisc/M2 outputs and fixed-capacity tables;
4. the new historical-development/late-era transport fits, with B1, Bdisc and Bmask using identical era-blind predictors, training-only preprocessing, and no later-era outcome access;
5. era-specific 24/48-hour competing-risk CIFs, theta_RD/theta_R/theta_D, calibration and fixed 5%/10%/20% queue tables, with paired Bdisc-minus-B1 contrasts;
6. measurement-process distributions and selection/overlap diagnostics by era;
7. subject-clustered uncertainty, prespecified process and outcome-permutation falsifications, and a conclusion ledger.

A failed era gate does not authorize pooling, changing the era cut, relaxing coverage, replacing the first exit, or selecting a favorable workload. The parent estimand remains reportable if its own gates pass, but the new robustness claim is then inconclusive.

## Unresolved question, strongest evidence and clinical importance

The expert seed proposes that persistent hyperlactatemia may have different meanings when perfusion is improving versus worsening, while warning that repeated measurements are selectively obtained and these proxies cannot diagnose microcirculatory dysfunction. The parent makes that question operational and adds a fixed-capacity review benchmark, but has no empirical result.

The strongest available evidence before fitting is design and data availability:

- the configured MIMIC-IV 3.1 snapshot contains ICU boundaries, admissions, repeated lactate and MAP observations, vasoactive intervals, organ laboratories, transfers and death/discharge fields;
- the local archive audit found all required members and dictionary rows, and a bounded join found 76,253 adult first eligible live exits before the strict measurement gate across all five anchor_year_group values;
- public MIMIC documentation states that dates are shifted independently by patient and that anchor_year_group was added to support analyses of changes in medical practice over time.

This establishes neither the phenotype, its prevalence, its event support, nor its predictive or clinical value. No result is treated as established evidence.

The unresolved clinical question is whether a retrospective risk-prioritization signal is robust to a plausible change in the way ICU observations are recorded. If the gain disappears in a later observation regime, a model trained on the older regime should not be presented as a stable basis for review prioritization. If it persists with calibration and process controls, that would justify a prospective silent-mode evaluation in a similarly selected population. This remains a policy-independent association and prioritization benchmark: it cannot establish that review, monitoring, treatment, escalation or discharge changes outcomes.

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is:

- Let G10e = Cap10e(Bdisc) - Cap10e(B1), where Cap is the proportion of known first R/D transitions among exactly the top 10% of the held-out target-era queue and S competes.
- In both the legacy and contemporary regimes, G10e is at least 0.05 absolute event proportion, with a subject-bootstrap 95% interval wholly above 0.05.
- The contemporary-minus-legacy difference G10C - G10L is no less than -0.05, with a 95% interval wholly above -0.05.

This is a non-inferiority-style robustness criterion, not a claim that eras are exchangeable. Failure of either regime's meaningful-gain criterion or of the -0.05 shift criterion is adverse evidence against a robust clinically meaningful queue gain. If event support or precision fails, the result is inconclusive rather than negative.

The parent phenotype hypothesis is retained as a secondary, era-specific question: the 48-hour standardized difference-in-differences theta_RDe should be negative in both legacy and contemporary regimes, with the same R/D component direction where event support permits. A result can support association without supporting queue value, or queue value without supporting the proposed lactate/perfusion interpretation; these are kept separate.

## Models and the new transport estimand

The parent full-split analysis is run unchanged: subject-level 70/15/15 split stratified only by primary cell, fixed endpoint label and original five-level anchor_year_group; all preprocessing and model selection use train/validation only. B0 is the latest-state/context cause-specific discrete-time model. B1 is the order-blind history baseline using the same 12 one-hour-bin summaries, organ/process fields and context but no phenotype interaction. Bmask retains context, missingness, counts, test presence and observation intensity while removing physiologic values. Bdisc adds the frozen P/NL and I/W terms, early/late values, changes, interaction, organ values and availability indicators. M2 is a small masked GRU over the same bins, masks, process indicators, context and frozen phenotype flags. All models predict competing R/D/S hazards and are evaluated on the identical parent test set and queues.

The new primary transport analysis is era-blind by design. It preserves every parent physiologic, process, context, endpoint and queue field except anchor_year_group, which is the regime-defining variable and is therefore excluded from predictors so that transport is not achieved by an era indicator. B1 and Bdisc use exactly the same remaining source whitelist, outcome target, preprocessing, model-selection rules and split logic; Bmask is the process-only control. The transport development population is L+B. Within it, split subjects deterministically 70/15/15, stratified by primary cell, endpoint label and original five-level group. Fit on historical train, use historical validation only for the predeclared regularization/early-stopping rule, and freeze the fitted model before any C outcome is read. Score the untouched historical test and every eligible C subject. No C outcome, calibration, threshold, queue size or feature frequency may influence fitting or tuning.

For the historical comparison, use the untouched historical test. For contemporary transport, use the strict-gated C target with known R/D/S/N labels and a separate all-eligible C audit retaining U. In each target, set kw=ceil(w Ntest,target) for w in {0.05,0.10,0.20}; ties are ordered by descending predicted 48-hour R/D CIF, then subject_id,hadm_id,stay_id. Define Capw as A=R/D events inside the queue divided by queue size and report event sensitivity, PPV, false negatives, R and D capture separately, S/U counts and a random-allocation benchmark. The new primary contrasts are G10L, G10C, and G10C-G10L. U favorable/adverse bounds are reported but never used to relabel U. The bridge regime receives the same descriptive tables if its gate passes, without replacing L or C.

For theta_RDe, theta_Re and theta_De, compute 48-hour Aalen-Johansen CIFs within each era for P/NL crossed with I/W, with S competing, and use the held-out era distribution for standardization. An era-specific interaction interpretation requires at least 40 subjects per primary cell, at least 10 known R/D events per cell, at least eight test subjects and three known R/D events per test cell, and U <=20% in each cell. If those conditions fail, report estimates descriptively but call the era-specific physiology/shift conclusion inconclusive. The queue analysis has a separate gate: each L and C target must have at least 90 strict known-label subjects, at least 10 known A events, a queue of at least 10 subjects and U <=10%; otherwise its shift estimand is inconclusive.

## Uncertainty, observation-process diagnostics and falsification

Use subject-level bootstrap resampling within the fixed target partitions, preserving admission clustering and using identical resamples for paired B1/Bdisc/Bmask predictions. For the transport difference, bootstrap L and C targets independently and combine the paired within-target contrasts. Report 95% intervals, exact bootstrap failure counts and effective sample sizes. Report calibration intercept, slope, observed/expected ratio, Brier score and 24/48-hour CIF calibration by target era. A calibration slope outside [0.80,1.25] or absolute intercept above 0.10 is material transport degradation; this blocks a calibrated risk-estimation claim but does not erase a ranking result.

Before examining outcomes, compare by L/B/C: lactate/MAP/pressor availability and counts, distinct MAP timestamps, positive pressor minutes, store-time lag, rejected/delayed units, Foley charting, phenotype-cell occupancy, U fraction, first-careunit and transfer-careunit distributions. Report standardized differences and missingness/process-only queue performance. These are descriptions of the observed record-generating process, not adjustments for confounding.

The fixed falsification set is:

- verify archive member, source and table schema hashes, dictionary links/units, all join keys and selected item IDs;
- audit first-exit ordering, era assignment, same-subject split integrity, no future clinical/recording times and exclusion reasons;
- repeat MAP duplicate collapse, pressor exact-repeat collapse and interval union audit;
- permute early versus late bins while preserving per-subject counts and availability;
- permute outcomes within era and split, expecting queue gains and theta contrasts to disappear;
- compare Bmask and a process-intensity-only model against B1/Bdisc; a comparable Bmask gain blocks phenotype attribution;
- repeat with MAP-only, pressor-only, arterial-MAP-only, Foley-only and lactate threshold 4.0 sensitivities;
- compare dual-time with chart-time-only features, 24-hour with 48-hour horizons, and 4/6/8-bin aggregation;
- report primary-cell, first-careunit and measurement-intensity strata descriptively, without changing the primary era cut;
- perform outcome-free selection-overlap q diagnostics separately by target era using subject-blocked cross-fitting, with q based only on baseline/context, transfer careunit, 12-hour counts/masks and process intensity available by each control time; never call q causal correction;
- audit exact queue sizes, deterministic ties, random benchmark and R/D-specific capture;
- report complete-ascertainment and favorable/adverse U bounds.

Support for the new robustness claim requires both L and C G10 intervals wholly above 0.05 and the C-minus-L interval wholly above -0.05, with no material calibration degradation and no comparable Bmask gain. Support for robust physiology interpretation additionally requires supported theta_RD^L and theta_RD^C in the prespecified negative direction with coherent R/D components. Adverse evidence is a contemporary gain <=0, attenuation beyond 0.05, calibration degradation, process-only replication, outcome-permutation resistance, reversed interaction, or failure under fixed timing/coverage/endpoint audits. Inconclusive evidence includes any target gate failure, sparse components, U above limits, unresolved units/joins, inadequate overlap/ESS, bootstrap instability or intervals too wide to distinguish thresholds.

A positive result supports only that the phenotype-augmented score retained observed first R/D-transition capture in a later MIMIC observation-era proxy under a fixed retrospective queue benchmark. It does not establish microcirculatory dysfunction, lactate etiology, clinician intent, treatment response, preventability, monitoring benefit, utility, net benefit, harms, ward surveillance, true calendar transportability, another hospital's performance or a causal intervention effect. Clinical adjudication, richer prospective measurement, an external institution or a silent-mode prospective study would be required for those claims.

## Method alternatives, compute and revisit record

B1 is the simple, transparent order-blind baseline and identifies the incremental contribution of the frozen phenotype. Bdisc is the selected transparent scientific alternative because its era-specific contrasts, cell estimates and process controls are auditable. M2 is the learned alternative: it receives the same 12 one-hour-bin lactate/MAP/pressor/organ/process records, masks, admission context and frozen phenotype flags, predicts the same discrete-time competing hazards, is trained only on historical L+B subjects with a deterministic subject split, and is evaluated on the same historical and contemporary targets with subject-bootstrap calibration, CIF and queue uncertainty. It can reveal nonlinear or order-dependent coupled trajectories that B1/Bdisc lose; it cannot establish biological mechanism.

A two-component diagonal-covariance trajectory mixture on the same training-only change vector is deferred if the GRU fails a pre-fit implementation/stability audit. A full transformer is deferred because its added capacity does not answer a distinct clinical uncertainty. A treatment-effect or fluid-response model is deferred because the source lacks clinician intent, fluid responsiveness, valid counterfactual assignment and adequate time-varying confounding control. These branches are not selected after test outcomes.

The future solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, up to 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds; discovery limits are 7,200 science seconds with concurrency 2. The bounded archive/dictionary/cohort audit was measured and took approximately seven seconds for the small joined eligibility check; strict feature coverage, event support, full event scans, model stability, bootstrap time and GPU necessity remain unmeasured. B0/B1/Bmask/Bdisc are expected to fit on CPU within the solver envelope. M2 may use one explicitly allocated A100 with cuda:0, but a GPU is not required by the scientific design and CPU feasibility must be measured. The discovery audit does not authorize a full fit.

The actual scientific outputs establishing completion are newly fitted parent and era-transport predictions, 24/48-hour competing-risk CIFs, era-specific interaction contrasts, fixed-capacity queue tables and uncertainty, process-shift/overlap manifests, falsification outputs and a conclusion ledger—or a complete fixed-gate audit showing which estimand is not estimable.
