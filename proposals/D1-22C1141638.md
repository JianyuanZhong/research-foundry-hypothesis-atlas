# Measurement-aware recovery phenotypes after ICU discharge

Parent: [prior hypothesis]
Dataset: MIMIC-IV v3.1, snapshot [source checksum]

## Unresolved question, strongest support, and clinical importance

The leading proposal showed how recovery, persistence, oscillation, and support de-escalation in the final ICU day could be tested against a last-state-plus-charting-process baseline. Its remaining clinically important uncertainty is ascertainment: requiring six observed hourly bins may select patients who are monitored more intensively, and a positive result in that selected subset may not apply to ordinary adult ICU discharges. The unresolved question is whether the recovery signal is present in the full eligible transition population after missingness and measurement intensity are represented explicitly.

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that residual oxygen/hemodynamic abnormalities and repeated structured observations are plausible warning signals and are available in this MIMIC snapshot. The local evidence and references do not establish an effect size, that recorded ICU returns are unplanned deterioration, that a discharge or monitoring change would improve outcomes, or that a recovery summary is a validated physiologic score.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among adults discharged alive from an index ICU stay, a prespecified final-24-hour recovery/persistence/oscillation/support-transition summary improves locked patient-held-out 48-hour cause-specific risk estimation for first subsequent ICU admission, without material death-calibration harm, in the full eligible cohort when missing domains and measurement intensity are retained. The incremental gain must be consistent across prespecified observation-density strata, not confined to the highly observed subgroup.

This is a substantive advance over the parent: it estimates whether the proposed information survives the parent’s observability selection, rather than treating the six-bin observable cohort as the primary population. A positive result would support prospective validation of a measurement-aware risk stratum. A negative or heterogeneous result would show that the apparent recovery signal depends on who gets measured, which is itself clinically consequential for deployment and ward surveillance.

## Outcomes and estimand

Start follow-up at ICU `outtime`. Primary horizon is 48 hours; secondary horizon is seven days. Use eight six-hour hazard bins for 48 hours and 28 for seven days. End follow-up at the earliest of horizon, first event, hospital `dischtime`, or unresolved event timing.

Cause 1 is the first later `icu/icustays` row for the same (`subject_id, hadm_id`) with `intime > index outtime` and `intime <= outtime+horizon`; event time is that ICU `intime`. Cause 2 is death before a later ICU admission: `hosp/admissions.deathtime > outtime), within horizon, and earlier than the first later ICU `intime`. Death after readmission is described separately. If no event occurs, censor at alive hospital `dischtime` after `outtime) and before horizon. If `hospital_expire_flag=1` with missing `deathtime`, mark timing unresolved and exclude from the primary timed analysis; report a sensitivity assigning death at `dischtime` only when no later ICU stay is recorded.

The estimand is observed-process cause-specific cumulative incidence of first subsequent recorded ICU admission and first death by 48 hours and seven days in the full defined population, with death competing for ICU admission. It is not a causal discharge, monitoring, treatment, or readmission-intent effect.

## Baselines, alternative, fitting, and analysis

Fit all models on identical full-cohort subject-level splits.

- B0: L2-regularized pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific hazards with interval indicators, age, gender, admission type/location, first/last ICU careunit, ICU duration, last valid physiologic values, support, recency, counts, masks and gaps. This is the simple last-state-plus-charting-process comparator. It loses ordered recovery relations and is therefore scientifically appropriate as the null for added trajectory information.

- B1: B0 plus the fixed recovery/persistence/oscillation/abnormal-burden/support-transition features. This is the primary newly fitted object. It can reveal whether interpretable temporal relations add information in low- and high-observation patients while preserving explicit missingness. The primary contrast is paired locked-test 48-hour ICU-admission Brier score and CIF calibration for B1 versus B0, overall and by observation stratum, with a prespecified B1-by-stratum interaction.

- B2: a one-layer 32-unit GRU-D-style encoder over the same four ordered bins, with values, support, masks, counts and delta-times plus baseline covariates; two competing-risk hazard heads; dropout 0.1; maximum 40 epochs; validation-loss early stopping with fixed patience. B2 is the substantive learned alternative, not a replacement for the interpretable test. Its contrast is B2 versus B1 on the same full cohort and splits. B2 may reveal nonlinear interactions or useful missingness/ordering structure that B1 loses, but a gain is scientifically meaningful only if it survives comparison to B1 and order/value falsification.

Use deterministic patient-level 70/15/15 splits with five fixed hash seeds; no `subject_id` or `hadm_id` crosses splits. Fit all imputation, standardization, decay, availability, and any observation-density model on training subjects only. Report paired 48-hour and seven-day cause-specific Brier scores, CIF calibration intercept/slope, integrated Brier, AUROC/AUPRC, and frozen-threshold decision curves. Use 2,000 subject-level paired bootstrap replicates where event support permits, preserving the full cohort and reporting all seed results, event counts, stratum counts, and uncertainty intervals. A 0.005 absolute Brier reduction is an interpretation benchmark only, not a clinical-utility threshold.

For an ascertainment sensitivity, fit a training-only model of membership in the parent’s six-bin observable subset using pre-landmark demographic, careunit, ICU-duration and chart-process variables, form stabilized inverse-observation weights with predeclared truncation [0.1, 10], and compare weighted restricted-cohort B1 predictions with full-cohort B1 predictions. This is a standardization/transport diagnostic, not a causal correction for unmeasured clinical severity. If the observation model is poorly calibrated or positivity fails, report that limitation rather than claiming transport.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required checks are: zero post-landmark timestamp violations; one row per index hospitalization; first-event ordering; exclusion ledger; rejected-value counts; observation-density flow; and split integrity. Required ablations are last-bin-only, value permutation with masks/counts preserved, within-patient order permutation where at least two bins exist, removal of mask/delta channels, process-only null, and outcome-label permutation within event-rate strata. Repeat the parent’s six-bin observable-cohort analysis as a locked secondary sensitivity and audit post-landmark transfer destination using `mimic-iv-3.1/hosp/transfers.csv.gz` (table `hosp/transfers`: `subject_id, hadm_id, transfer_id, eventtype, careunit, intime, outtime`), never as a primary predictor.

Supportive evidence requires B1 to improve B0 in the full cohort with uncertainty compatible with a nonzero gain, acceptable death calibration, no material reversal across observation strata, and loss of the gain under value/order permutation. Stronger support for transport requires agreement between full-cohort and weighted restricted-cohort contrasts and stable feature families across seeds. B2 additionally requires a gain over B1 and destruction under ordering/value perturbation.

Adverse results include no B1 increment with adequate events; gain only in the four-bin stratum; death-calibration harm; unstable feature groups; failure to lose the gain under permutation; label-permutation discrimination; or a B2 gain that disappears against B1. These refute the specified full-cohort incremental/transport claim, not every possible form of recovery information.

Inconclusive results include sparse events in low-observation strata, nonpositive observation-model support, broad intervals spanning no effect, unresolved death timing, invalid timestamps, poor linkage, split instability, or convergence failure within budget. A failure of transport from the observable subset is evidence of ascertainment limitation, not proof that physiology lacks prognostic value.

Computationally checkable claims are cohort construction, joins, timing audits, feature construction, missingness/observation strata, model fits, locked predictions, CIFs, calibration, bootstrap uncertainty, ablations, and convergence. Clinical adjudication remains necessary for planned versus unplanned ICU return, treatment limitation, clinician intent, delivered support fidelity, functional readiness, and actionability. Raw waveforms, images, bedside functional assessments, and prospective decision impact are unavailable; positive results require clinical review, external/prospective validation, and a decision-impact study before deployment.
