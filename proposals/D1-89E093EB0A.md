# Discharge-proximal hepatic recovery, observation intensity, and 7-day return after TACE in HCC

## Status and hypothesis

This episode-5 candidate is a substantive successor to [prior hypothesis]. It is an observational prognostic study, not a treatment-effect study or discharge-policy evaluation.

Primary new hypothesis: among adults with HCC who have a first observed TACE episode, remain in the encounter for at least 96 hours, and are discharged, the change in core laboratory values from the early post-TACE window to the final 24 hours before discharge adds held-out information about a subsequent inpatient admission in days 0–7 after discharge beyond baseline reserve, early post-TACE values/change, prior utilization, and recorded observation intensity before discharge.

Secondary, clinically specific hypothesis: the incremental discharge-recovery signal is larger for a subsequent admission carrying a prespecified liver-related diagnosis code than for a subsequent admission without that code, conditional on the same baseline, early-trajectory, and observation-process information.

These are hypotheses about coded outcomes. A coded admission is not adjudicated hepatic decompensation. The parent’s broader 30-day all-cause early-trajectory estimand remains an inherited analysis, so the small discharge-complete subgroup cannot silently replace the general question. The estimands are incremental prognostic associations and out-of-fold prediction changes, not causal effects of TACE, discharge timing, laboratory monitoring, delayed discharge, or any intervention.

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and advance

The strongest claim supported by inspected external evidence is narrower. Sanai et al., Scientific Reports 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-54466-4, PMCID PMC13457603, frozen source ID [source checksum], was read through its frozen Europe PMC full-text XML. Its abstract reports a four-center cohort of TACE/TARE patients, defines hepatic decompensation as ascites, variceal bleeding, or portosystemic encephalopathy, and reports baseline ALBI and treatment type as predictors of post-locoregional-treatment decompensation. This supports plausibility that reserve and post-treatment complications matter.

It does not show that routinely recorded, time-ordered local laboratory recovery adds prognostic information beyond baseline and early post-TACE values, identify a discharge-time state, or support changing discharge care. The local diagnosis codes have no independent event time.

The unresolved claim is: after accounting for baseline reserve, early injury/recovery, and how intensively the patient was observed before discharge, does the final 24-hour laboratory state improve prediction of a 0–7-day subsequent inpatient admission? A reproducible signal would motivate a prospective study of post-TACE monitoring and follow-up; a null result would caution against a trajectory-only escalation rule.

The substantive advance over the parent is a clean separation of early change, persistence/recovery at the discharge boundary, and the observation process that can produce spurious associations. A process-only comparator and a pre-index placebo trajectory test whether the apparent result is specific to TACE-linked recovery rather than generic recording intensity.

## Models and estimands

Inherited broad analysis: in all participants with paired core baseline/early values, compare B0 containing age, sex, department, three baseline medians, prior 365-day encounter count, and prior recorded-code indicators for cirrhosis, portal-vein thrombosis, ascites, diabetes, and prior liver surgery, against B1 adding the three early-minus-baseline changes. Build prior indicators by date-aligned encounter joins; diagnosis time comes only from joined encounter time. The inherited outcome is any subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days.

New nested analysis: compare D0 (B0 plus early values or a fixed baseline-plus-early block), D1 (D0 plus discharge changes), P (D0 plus process features), and D1P (D1 plus process features). The primary new contrast is D1 versus D0 for 0–7-day all-cause admission. Process contrasts are P versus D0 and D1P versus P. The temporal-specificity contrast is D1 versus D0 for days 8–30 among those without an early event. The liver-coded contrast is secondary.

Use penalized logistic regression as primary and a shallow gradient-boosted model only as a bounded functional-form sensitivity with identical feature blocks. Compare global likelihood-ratio contribution, adjusted odds ratios per training-fold IQR, representative-pattern absolute risk differences, out-of-fold Brier change, AUROC/AUPRC, and calibration intercept/slope. Use patient bootstrap intervals. Net benefit/decision curves, if reported, are descriptive prediction summaries and not clinical utility.

The parent feasibility audit found 404 core-complete nested participants and 65 all-cause 30-day events before discovery filtering. Harbor must recompute counts after buckets 0–79. Do not fit or interpret an unstable nested model with fewer than 20 0–7-day all-cause events or fewer than 20 liver-coded events. Penalization is not a rescue for inadequate events. If 0–7-day events are sparse but 30-day events adequate, report 30-day nested results as secondary and call the new 0–7-day hypothesis inconclusive.

## Validation and falsification

Use participant-level five-fold cross-validation within buckets 0–79, with all imputation, missingness handling, standardization, feature selection, and tuning inside training folds. Add chronological validation: 2010–2022 derivation and 2023–2025-12-02 testing with participant separation. Inaccessible buckets are not external validation.

Required checks are complete-case versus missingness-indicator and inverse-observation-weighting analyses; minimum-observation and maximum-gap restrictions; narrow versus broad procedure dictionary and 手术来源 sensitivity; calendar/department selection tables; duplicate/impossible-date/join-multiplication assertions; within-participant laboratory timestamp shuffle and reversal; explicit no-post-discharge leakage audit; and 0–7 versus 8–30-day timing stratified descriptively by length of stay. Also fit a pre-index placebo trajectory using two non-overlapping pre-TACE lab windows of comparable width. A large placebo gain indicates generic measurement or secular trend rather than TACE-linked recovery.

Supportive inherited result requires the B1 block to have global LR p < 0.05, optimism-corrected out-of-fold Brier improvement with a 95% bootstrap interval excluding zero, no chronological reversal, and persistence after density/gap and timestamp-shuffle checks. Otherwise call it adverse/falsified or inconclusive according to whether uncertainty is adequate.

Supportive new result requires the nested event gate, D1 out-of-fold Brier improvement over D0 with a 95% interval excluding zero and a prespecified incremental test, no chronological reversal, persistence in D1P versus P after the process comparator, stability under observation restrictions, attenuation under timestamp shuffling, and no equally strong 8–30-day signal. If D1 is explained by process-only information, the new biological interpretation is adverse even if raw prediction improves. A liver-coded result is supportive only if its event gate passes and its coded contrast is more stable/larger than the non-liver contrast; it remains a coded association, never adjudicated decompensation.

Call results inconclusive if the dictionary is not reproducible, assay semantics/units cannot be validated, time or admissions cannot be reconstructed, selection is strongly calendar/department dependent, events are inadequate, or uncertainty spans negligible and clinically important effects. Supportive results justify a prospective study with reliable units/provenance, procedure details, intent, adjudicated decompensation, and a defined monitoring/discharge pathway. Adverse results argue against a routine trajectory-only trigger. None establishes treatment harm/benefit, a safe discharge threshold, unplanned admission, recurrence, mortality benefit, or clinical utility.
