# Identity-robust availability and preference guarantees for preoperative HCC documentary-M2 review

## Clinical question, supported evidence, and targeted advance

The decision is whether a fixed, scarce multidisciplinary review capacity should be preferentially assigned to adults approaching a first liver resection when preoperative CT/MRI report semantics may concentrate patients whose eventual resection encounter documents high-grade microvascular invasion (documentary M2). The action is allocation of review positions. It is not a recommendation about resection extent, transplantation, locoregional or systemic therapy, surveillance, or any treatment.

The strongest claim currently supported by inspected evidence is narrower. The frozen HCC snapshot contains patient/encounter-linked operations with start/end fields, examination rows with acquisition-like start times and eventual CT/MRI narrative fields, untimed same-encounter pathology narratives, and dated medication/order records. A no-sampling source audit found 419,996 examination rows, 392,854 nonblank whole-accession keys, 72 blank-accession rows, and 16,534 keys represented by multiple rows; 16,527 of those multirow keys had nonidentical component payloads. Thus whole-accession identity and patient-level multiplicity are real computational issues. The data do not establish report authoring, finalization, release, version, retrievability, readability, or clinician viewing before a decision. They also lack pathology specimen/time/slide/block identifiers and sampling protocols, images, a referral roster, measured MDT capacity, clinical actions, recurrence, survival, harms, costs, and patient benefit.

The assigned parent has a clinically consequential estimand defect. Its nondominated set consists of *attained* masks indexed partly by an ordered patient-coverage curve. Coverage does not identify which patients or reports are visible. In an exhaustive four-patient fixture, six masks all had the same curve ((0,0,1,1)), while the semantic top-2 captured 0, 1, or 2 documentary-positive patients. An aggregate archive audit could therefore match the coverage curve of a favorable parent cell while the actual report identities produce the adverse result. Coordinatewise best results across years or cutoffs can likewise be unattainable under the required shared mask.

The unresolved primary hypothesis is:

> At the locked 10% capacity and 24-hour cutoff, there exists a nonzero patient-coverage lower contract (L) such that, for **every** coupled source world and whole-accession identity mask compatible with (L), every global radiology-by-pathology reader pair and every anchored whole-patient deletion, the locked semantic ranking captures more documentary-M2 cases than each locked comparator and capacity-matched random review by strictly more than five cases per 100 offered positions in both 2020 and 2021, and its minimum capture fraction has a nonempty strict stakeholder-preference interval.

The 12-, 48-, and 72-hour cutoffs and 5% and 20% capacities remain locked, non-rescuing stability analyses. The substantive repair is an identity-robust lower-orthant frontier: each reported contract is backed by the worst result over all source-world/mask identities that meet the contract. An external audit may bridge by either (a) identifying exact accession versions and masks or (b) supplying a simultaneous lower coverage curve that lands inside a robustly passing lower orthant. Mere proximity to an optimistic attained-mask curve cannot support action compatibility.

A 2026 systematic review of AI models after HCC resection (Neves and Soares, DOI 10.3390/cancers18132028; frozen full-text XML source [source checksum]) found predominantly retrospective, single-centre work, external validation in 6/36 studies, inconsistent calibration/decision-curve reporting, and mostly high risk of bias. That context supports strict transport and utility boundaries; it does not support this ranking, endpoint, or availability hypothesis.
