# Early laboratory state and delayed liver-directed procedural transition in HCC

## Scientific deliverable and hypothesis

The deliverable is a newly fitted, locked prognostic comparison that estimates whether the first 48 hours of dated laboratory information identifies a later recorded liver-directed procedural transition during the same admission, among patients without a liver-directed procedure in the first 48 hours. The solver must produce the frozen cohort and procedure dictionary, leakage and multiplicity audits, an admission baseline, a transparent early-summary model, an irregular latent-state model, held-out predictions and uncertainty, and a claim-to-output interpretation table. This is not a request to reproduce any demonstration or to report a readiness result as a scientific conclusion.

The hypothesis is:

> Among adults with a first observed HCC-coded inpatient admission who have no recorded liver-directed procedure in hours 0–48, a coupled early laboratory trajectory through hour 48 is associated with, and improves calibrated prediction of, a new recorded liver-directed procedure beginning in hours 48–120, beyond admission/pre-admission information and transparent first/last laboratory summaries.

The clinical question is whether early evidence of a changing physiologic or care trajectory can identify patients who will need a liver-directed intervention after the initial admission phase. This could inform prospective multidisciplinary review, diagnostic/therapeutic planning, and allocation of procedural capacity. The endpoint is deliberately called a **recorded procedural transition**, not unplanned escalation, treatment failure, tumor progression, or benefit from treatment: the available data do not record intent, indication, scheduling, imaging stage, or counterfactual need.

The narrower 48–120-hour window is primary because it represents a potentially actionable transition shortly after the admission/initial-assessment period while limiting contamination from late admission logistics. A 48–168-hour window is a prespecified secondary sensitivity.

## Evidence versus unresolved claim

The strongest evidence currently available is data availability plus bounded read-only audits, not a treatment conclusion. The HCC snapshot contains 105,044 encounters for 43,815 patients, dated admission/discharge fields, diagnosis-linked HCC lexical proxies, dated numeric/qualitative laboratory records, and dated procedure records. Under a provisional lexical rule (diagnosis name containing `肝细胞癌` or `肝癌`), adult, complete-discharge, first observed HCC-coded admissions from 2011-01-01 through before 2026-01-01 yielded 42,692 patients; 7,563 had an observed stay of at least 14 days. A managed read-only procedure audit found 29,086 with no provisional liver-directed procedure in hours 0–48, of whom 10,918 had a provisional liver-directed procedure in hours 48–120 and 14,195 in hours 48–168. These are feasibility counts generated by the audit files in this workspace; they are not locked study results and must be regenerated after the exact dictionary, duplicate handling, partitioning, and exclusion flow are frozen.

The data also show why the unresolved question is clinically nontrivial. A broad procedure-name scan in the no-early-procedure cohort found liver resection, embolization/TACE-family procedures, ablation, and related interventions, but a procedure row does not say whether the action was planned at admission, triggered by deterioration, performed for diagnosis, or recorded because of local workflow. A positive result would therefore support only a reproducible association with a later local recorded procedure. It would not establish that laboratory change caused the procedure, that the procedure was clinically necessary, that cancer burden progressed, or that acting on a score improves care.

A recent accessible review of acute-on-chronic liver failure describes dynamic instability, organ cross-talk, early recognition, repeated reassessment, and prompt treatment as clinically important concepts (Soldera, 2026, PMCID PMC13205394; acquired source hash `[source checksum]`). That review is background rationale, not validation of this HCC cohort or endpoint. The three demonstration references motivate dated sequence and latent longitudinal modelling, but do not establish this hypothesis. The Oncoformer main article and complete STAR Methods were unavailable locally; only its supplement was inspected, so no full-paper reproduction claim is made.

## Population, landmark, outcome and native-time rules

Use only configured catalog participant buckets 0–79: fitting buckets 0–59, selection buckets 60–69, and locked internal test buckets 70–79. Buckets 80–99 are not accessible and no external-validation claim is allowed.

1. Read encounters and diagnoses, normalize only whitespace and encoding, and freeze the HCC cohort rule before fitting. An eligible row requires:
   - adult age `年龄 >= 18`;
   - nonmissing, parseable native `入院时间` and `出院时间`;
   - `出院时间 >= 入院时间`;
   - `入院时间 >= 2011-01-01 00:00:00`;
   - `出院时间 < 2026-01-01 00:00:00`;
   - a diagnosis row joined on the exact identity pair whose `诊断名称` matches the frozen HCC lexical rule.
   
   This is a first observed HCC-coded admission, not a validated first HCC diagnosis. Report diagnosis multiplicity, diagnosis type, unmatched keys, invalid-time rows, and earlier HCC-coded rows with missing/ongoing discharge. Do not silently replace an invalid earliest candidate with a later admission without reporting that decision.

2. Select the earliest eligible HCC-coded encounter per `患者主索引`, sorting by native `入院时间` and then `就诊号`. Retain `就诊科室`, age, sex, calendar era, and admission time-of-day as admission descriptors. Audit duplicate encounter keys and overlapping admissions.

3. Define the primary index cohort as patients with no recorded member of the frozen liver-directed procedure dictionary whose native `开始时间` satisfies:
   `0 <= 开始时间 - 入院时间 < 48 hours`.
   A missing or invalid procedure start is not evidence of no procedure; report it separately and exclude it from the primary transition ascertainment unless a prespecified missing-time sensitivity is feasible.

4. Define the primary endpoint as:
   `delayed_liver_procedure_48_120 = 1` if at least one dictionary-matching procedure has valid native `开始时间` in
   `48 <= 开始时间 - 入院时间 < 120 hours`, and 0 otherwise.
   
   The procedure must be linked to the index encounter by the exact pair (`患者主索引`, `就诊号`). A procedure after native discharge is not eligible. The secondary endpoint uses `48 <= elapsed hours < 168`. Report first eligible procedure time, procedure family, number of rows, same-timestamp duplicates, and whether discharge precedes the endpoint window. Do not use procedure `结束时间` to define onset; retain it only for data-quality audit.

5. The prediction landmark is `入院时间 + 48 hours`. Primary predictors must be observable by this landmark:
   - admission/pre-admission model inputs use no post-admission information;
   - early-laboratory inputs use only `检验时间 >= 入院时间` and `检验时间 <= 入院时间 + 48 hours`;
   - no primary predictor may use a procedure, order, medication, examination, document, pathology, diagnosis row, discharge field, or length-of-stay information after the landmark;
   - no-early-procedure eligibility is defined from procedure records in hours 0–48 and is a cohort rule, not a predictor;
   - values at exactly the landmark are retained and audited;
   - same-timestamp duplicates are retained for the raw audit, then handled by a frozen assay/time aggregation rule in the modelling dataset.

6. Pre-admission features use only dated laboratory rows in `[入院时间 - 90 days, 入院时间)` and prior encounter/procedure counts in the same interval. Patients with no early numeric laboratory record remain in the primary cohort; missingness and measurement availability are features and are reported.

## Baseline, learned/mechanistic alternative and what it reveals

### B0: transparent admission/pre-admission baseline

Fit a training-only standardized elastic-net logistic regression for the primary 48–120-hour transition. Inputs are age, sex, admitting department family, calendar era, admission time-of-day, diagnosis-type indicators, frozen pre-admission HCC/liver-disease lexical proxies, prior encounter and inpatient counts, prior liver-directed procedure counts in the 90-day lookback, and per-exact-assay pre-admission nearest value, recency, count, missingness, and slope when at least two dated values exist. No early post-admission laboratory, procedure, order, medication, examination, document, pathology, discharge, or length-of-stay information enters B0. Preprocessing, imputation, assay dictionaries, dictionary expansion, scaling, and penalty selection are fit only in buckets 0–59 and selected in buckets 60–69.

### B1: transparent early-summary comparator

B1 is B0 plus, for each frozen exact assay, first numeric value, last numeric value, difference, elapsed time between first and last, number of observations, early-window missingness, and the number of distinct valid timestamps. B1 uses the same no-early-procedure cohort, target, participant split, and outcome. It is required so a richer model cannot claim scientific advance against an artificially weak baseline.

### Selected substantive alternative: irregular joint latent transition model

Fit a low-dimensional continuous-time observation/state model on exactly the B0/B1 cohort and split. A latent hepatic-reserve state and a renal-stress state evolve over hours 0–48 with assay-specific observation offsets/noise, patient random intercepts and slopes, shrinkage, irregular elapsed time, and explicit observation-process terms. The model receives every valid dated numeric token from the frozen channels, assay identity, elapsed time, qualitative-result/missingness indicators, and measurement density. A regularized logistic head predicts the same 48–120-hour transition from estimated state level at 48 hours, recent slope, coupled-state contrast, and posterior uncertainty, with B0 covariates retained. State dimension, process noise, shrinkage and outcome regularization are selected without using the locked test.

The scientific comparison is not merely predictive competition. B1 asks whether an interpretable first/last/difference summary is enough. The latent model asks whether irregular timing, coupled movement, shrinkage, and uncertainty reveal a clinically meaningful “changing reserve versus stable reserve” pattern that B1 loses, or instead reveal that apparent deterioration is explained by measurement intensity. State labels are model-derived summaries, not validated hepatic or renal diagnoses, especially because lab units/reference ranges are absent.

A one-layer GRU/temporal-attention model over identical early lab tokens is recorded as a deferred alternative. Revisit only if the latent model converges and locked residual analyses show reproducible nonlinear timing interactions that matter scientifically; model complexity or GPU availability alone is not a reason to add it. A text model is deferred because documents have no native time and pathology lacks native time, local text extraction is not validated, and expert labels are unavailable. The alternatives not chosen are therefore preserved with evidence-based reasons rather than categorically excluded.

## Analysis, uncertainty and falsification

Report a cohort flow from all encounters through diagnosis match, adult eligibility, complete native times, first-patient selection, no-early-procedure restriction, valid laboratory availability, and final split. Report all exclusions, invalid timestamps, overlapping admissions, key multiplicities, procedure dictionary counts, and exact assay counts.

On the locked patient-level test set report AUROC, PR-AUC, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, calibration plots, observed/predicted risk bins, and paired patient-bootstrap 95% intervals (at least 500 resamples) for B1–B0 and latent–B1 differences. Report event prevalence, number needed to review at prespecified risk thresholds, and decision curves only as exploratory planning summaries, not as a validated procedure policy. Report time-to-first-procedure descriptively; the binary 48–120-hour transition remains primary.

Prespecified falsification and sensitivity checks are:

- Add and remove measurement count, missingness, timestamp gap, and observation-process terms. If latent-model gain disappears after this adjustment, interpret it as surveillance/selection signal rather than organ-state evidence.
- Permute laboratory timestamps within patient and exact assay while preserving values and assay identity. A timing-specific claim should attenuate; unchanged performance implies value/density signal rather than trajectory timing.
- Use pre-admission laboratory timestamps as a negative timing control and verify no early token lies before admission or after hour 48.
- Repeat after a frozen same-assay/same-timestamp aggregation rule and after excluding duplicate rows.
- Repeat in the 48–168-hour endpoint, by admitting-department family, calendar era, assay availability, measurement density, and exact procedure family. These are descriptive heterogeneity checks, not causal interactions.
- Report the early-procedure exclusion sensitivity: all qualifying admissions with early procedure retained, with early procedure as a descriptive stratum, and a competing descriptive endpoint of any liver-directed procedure from admission through 168 hours. These analyses must not be interpreted as unbiased treatment comparisons.
- Repeat with the 14-day stay endpoint as a secondary bridge to the incumbent, but do not allow it to replace the new procedural-transition estimand.
- Use a negative-control procedure dictionary consisting of central-line/venipuncture terms. A genuine liver-directed transition signal should not be reproduced identically for this workflow endpoint; if it is, care-intensity confounding is implicated.
- Include post-landmark procedure/order features only in an explicitly labelled leakage demonstration, never in the primary analysis.

## Interpretation criteria

Supportive evidence requires all of the following:

1. Directionally coherent prespecified associations between an early state/trajectory pattern and the recorded transition.
2. B1 improves appropriately over B0, and the latent model improves over B1 in held-out calibration plus at least one prespecified discrimination or risk-stratification metric.
3. A prespecified absolute Brier improvement of at least 0.002 for latent versus B1, or a comparably meaningful calibrated risk-stratification improvement whose uncertainty is reported.
4. Paired uncertainty that does not leave the clinically relevant direction implausibly broad, with acceptable calibration by intercept/slope and plots.
5. The substantive gain persists after measurement-intensity adjustment, timestamp permutation behaves as expected, and the direction is not confined to one department/era or one poorly defined procedure string.
6. The same conclusion is not reproduced by the negative-control procedure endpoint.

Support would justify external/prospective validation of an early review or procedural-planning marker. It would not justify a treatment recommendation.

Adverse evidence includes a null or opposite trajectory association, no meaningful latent-versus-B1 gain, poor calibration, instability across department/era/assay availability, disappearance after observation-process adjustment, persistence under timestamp permutation, or identical performance for the negative-control workflow endpoint. A result driven only by procedure-name artifacts, prior scheduling signals, or one local department is adverse to a general physiologic interpretation.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 100 primary transition events after the frozen exclusions, inability to freeze a clinically coherent exact procedure dictionary, no repeatable dated assay support, substantial invalid/overlapping times, nonconvergence, incomplete discharge ascertainment, a dominant missing-time procedure fraction, or uncertainty too wide to distinguish meaningful benefit from harm. Inconclusive means the data cannot resolve the hypothesis.

## Clinical limits and unavailable evidence

The computation can establish at most a reproducible local prognostic association between early recorded laboratory information and a later local recorded liver-directed procedure in the defined admission and time window. It cannot establish:

- validated HCC diagnosis, radiographic stage, tumor burden, progression, response, or resectability;
- that a procedure was unplanned, necessary, beneficial, or caused by laboratory deterioration;
- hepatic failure, acute kidney injury, sepsis, portal hypertension, treatment toxicity, mortality, transplant eligibility, readmission, or outside-hospital events;
- treatment intent, clinician rationale, scheduling status, ICU status, transfers, complete hospital capture, or counterfactual outcomes;
- causal effect or clinical utility of acting on a score.

Those stronger claims require expert adjudication of diagnosis and procedure indication, pathology/imaging review with valid timestamps, laboratory units/reference ranges, treatment intent and scheduling data, mortality/transplant linkage, complete multi-hospital capture, external validation, and a prospective clinical/operational or causal study. The pathology and examination payloads could help such a future study only after timestamp restoration and expert labels.

## Compute and solver-ready outputs

The discovery audits were measured with read-only source scans using 4 CPUs and 16 GiB: the encounter/diagnosis/provisional cohort scan was approximately 10.6 seconds in the managed job, the procedure-category scan approximately 9.6 seconds, and the transition-cohort scan approximately 9.8 seconds. These measurements do not estimate the full laboratory feature build or model fitting.

The future solver planning envelope in `inputs.json` is up to 16 CPUs, 256 GiB RAM, 8 GPU slots available, and 28,800 seconds. CPU is appropriate and preferred for B0/B1, sparse elastic-net fitting, exact-assay aggregation, bootstrap evaluation, and the low-dimensional latent-state fit; this is a measured feasibility choice for the data scans, while full solver duration and repeated-fit memory are unverified. A deferred neural sequence model could request one allocated A100-SXM4-80GB and use `cuda:0` inside that allocation, but GPU is neither required nor a scientific merit criterion. No GPU job was needed for the selected experiment.

The solver must newly produce source/schema hashes; frozen cohort/HCC/procedure/assay dictionaries; cohort-flow and invalid-time audits; patient-level split assignments; B0/B1 preprocessing and coefficients; latent loadings, state/slope contrasts, posterior uncertainty and convergence diagnostics; locked predictions; AUROC, PR-AUC, Brier, calibration and paired-bootstrap outputs; endpoint, department, era, assay, density, duplicate, timestamp-permutation, negative-control and early-procedure sensitivities; and a claim-to-output table separating computationally checkable claims from those requiring clinical adjudication or another study.

This proposal preserves the incumbent’s evidence-bounded discipline while changing the population restriction, endpoint, and clinical decision context. It is a substantive successor, not a wording-only revision.
