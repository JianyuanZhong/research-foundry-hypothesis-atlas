# Proposal: Which vasopressor should be stopped first after dual-agent shock stabilizes?

## Actionable question and hypothesis

For adults in the ICU receiving overlapping norepinephrine and vasopressin whose shock has reached a prespecified low-dose, hemodynamically stable weaning state, emulate the decision to discontinue vasopressin first versus discontinue norepinephrine first. The falsifiable hypothesis is that **norepinephrine-first discontinuation reduces 6-hour hemodynamic failure without increasing 28-day mortality or kidney-replacement therapy**, relative to vasopressin-first discontinuation.

This is a bedside de-escalation decision, not antimicrobial stewardship. Both strategies are common and immediately actionable. A 2026 single-center retrospective cohort of 100 mechanically ventilated septic-shock patients reported associations favoring vasopressin-first for ventilation and mortality, while summarizing earlier evidence as favoring norepinephrine-first for clinically significant hypotension and showing no established mortality/length-of-stay difference (Alshreef et al., DOI 10.5414/CP204891; full text inspected from frozen Europe PMC source `[source checksum]`). These discordant, observational findings leave the causal weaning decision unresolved.

### Evidence/claim separation

* **Strongest supported claim now:** vasopressor discontinuation order is associated with differing post-wean hypotension in prior observational literature, but existing results are inconsistent for patient-centered outcomes and vulnerable to selection, indication, and time-varying severity confounding. MIMIC records both drugs with minute-level intervals and subsequent physiology, so a substantially larger replication with a treatment-decision-aligned estimand is feasible.
* **Unresolved claim tested here:** among patients genuinely eligible for either low-dose discontinuation strategy at a stable decision time, assigning norepinephrine-first rather than vasopressin-first would lower 6-hour hemodynamic failure. MIMIC can estimate an observational target-trial contrast, not prove assignment effects or dictate practice.

## Exact Harbor experiment

### Target trial

* **Population:** first qualifying weaning episode in the first ICU stay of an adult (estimated age at admission >=18 from `patients.anchor_age + year(admittime)-anchor_year`) with at least 6 continuous hours of concurrent IV norepinephrine and vasopressin. Require an acute-care admission, both infusions active 30 minutes before time zero, and at time zero: vasopressin <=2.4 units/hour (0.04 U/min), norepinephrine <=0.20 mcg/kg/min, no epinephrine/dopamine/phenylephrine infusion in the preceding 2 hours, median MAP >=65 mmHg and no MAP <55 in the preceding 2 hours, at least 3 MAP observations in those 2 hours, non-rising lactate (last <= prior, when two exist), and no death/discharge at time zero. Primary cohort does not require a retrospective septic-shock diagnosis because that diagnosis is not known at the decision point; characterize infection/sepsis codes and perform a prespecified sepsis-likely subgroup rather than conditioning the primary design on future discharge coding.
* **Time zero / observed treatment assignment:** the end of the first drug's final active segment, after both eligibility thresholds are met. `norepinephrine-first` means norepinephrine has no active interval from 5 minutes through 6 hours after time zero while vasopressin remains active for at least 90 of the next 120 minutes; `vasopressin-first` is symmetric. Exclude simultaneous stops (within 5 minutes), both-drug cessation, ambiguous charting gaps, and episodes in which the stopped agent restarts within the first 30-minute grace period. The primary per-protocol strategy additionally treats restart after grace, comparator cessation before 2 hours, or addition of another vasopressor as deviation; handle by clone-censor-weighting as below. A simpler intention-to-treat analogue by observed first stop is a sensitivity analysis.
* **Follow-up:** physiologic outcome from time zero through 6 hours; safety outcomes through 48 hours; death through 28 days or hospital discharge (MIMIC has only in-hospital event time plus `patients.dod`, so report 28-day all-cause death only where ascertainable and separately in-hospital death).
* **Primary outcome:** 6-hour hemodynamic failure, a composite of (a) sustained MAP <60 mmHg for two recorded measurements 15-120 minutes apart, (b) stopped-drug restart after the 30-minute grace period, (c) initiation of epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine, or (d) >=0.05 mcg/kg/min increase in norepinephrine rate from the rate at time zero (when norepinephrine remains). Report every component; this makes the endpoint resistant to clinician treatment masking hypotension.
* **Key secondary outcomes:** MAP area below 65 over 6 hours (linear interpolation only across gaps <=60 minutes; otherwise missing); alive and free of all vasopressors through hour 24; 24-hour lactate change; new invasive ventilation/continued invasive ventilation at 24 hours; new RRT by 48 hours; stage-2/3 creatinine/urine-output AKI by 48 hours where baseline and coverage permit; ICU/hospital length of stay among survivors; 28-day and in-hospital mortality. Mortality is safety/context, not the primary endpoint because residual confounding and limited sample size make survival effects especially fragile.
* **Estimand:** primary 6-hour per-protocol risk difference and risk ratio for assignment to norepinephrine-first versus vasopressin-first among eligible decision times. Also estimate intention-to-treat-analogue risk difference by observed initial stop. Use nonparametric bootstrap by `subject_id` for 95% confidence intervals.

### Analysis and confounding control

1. Reconstruct continuous infusions by `stay_id`, `itemid`, `starttime`, `endtime`, rates and `statusdescription`; collapse contiguous segments separated by <=5 minutes, preserve pauses, reject impossible units/rates, and publish attrition and arm counts. Do not use `storetime` to define clinical chronology.
2. Measure all confounders strictly before time zero: age, sex, race, care unit, admission type, ICU/hospital time, baseline/trajectory summaries in 0-2 and 2-6 hours for MAP, heart rate, lactate, urine output, creatinine, pH, oxygenation and ventilation; duration/dose/AUC and recent slopes of both vasopressors; preceding fluids and urine balance; ventilation status; RRT; comorbidity burden from diagnoses recorded for the admission (used only as baseline proxies, acknowledging discharge-time coding); prior ICU/hospitalizations; calendar/anchor-year group and hour/weekend; missingness and measurement frequency.
3. Fit a cross-fitted gradient-boosted propensity model and outcome model; estimate overlap-weighted augmented inverse-probability-weighted risk contrasts. Enforce positivity (propensity 0.05-0.95 for primary trimmed analysis), report standardized mean differences before/after weighting, effective sample size, weight tails, arm-specific outcome counts, and missingness. A parsimonious logistic propensity model is the required baseline; unadjusted and exact/coarsened matching on MAP, both doses, dual-agent duration, lactate, mechanical ventilation and care unit are sensitivity baselines.
4. For the sustained 2-hour strategy, create two clones per eligible decision time, censor each clone on deviation, and estimate inverse-probability-of-adherence weights in 15-minute bins from evolving MAP, doses, fluids, urine, lactate and ventilation. If this model is unstable (ESS <30% of eligible episodes or >1% truncated weights at the prespecified 99th percentile), downgrade the per-protocol result to inconclusive and privilege the simpler observed-first-stop analysis as descriptive.
5. Missing lactate does not exclude if other stability criteria are met; include missingness indicators and repeat in those with two lactates. Multiple imputation applies to covariates, never treatment/outcome times. Cluster all resampling and standard errors by patient.

## Direct feasibility audit

A complete streamed scan of `inputevents.csv.gz` (no row sampling) found 459,800 norepinephrine rows in 15,951 ICU stays and 37,163 vasopressin rows in 5,626 stays; 5,233 stays had both and 5,032 had temporal overlap. A broad last-end comparison yielded 2,345 vasopressin-first, 1,767 norepinephrine-first and 920 tied stays. A stricter preliminary computable screen requiring >=6 aggregate overlapping hours, one drug absent for the next 6 hours and the other covering >=1.5 of the next 2 hours yielded **1,994 episodes: 1,117 vasopressin-first and 877 norepinephrine-first**. These are upper bounds before low-dose/MAP/lactate/adult/first-stay exclusions, not the final analytic cohort. ## Falsification, interpretation, and limits

* **Supportive result:** adjusted norepinephrine-first 6-hour failure risk is lower by at least 5 absolute percentage points, its 95% CI excludes 0, components point coherently, balance/positivity pass, and there is no signal of >=3-point higher 28-day mortality or materially higher RRT. This supports prioritizing a randomized/prospective protocol test; it does not establish causal superiority.
* **Adverse/contrary result:** vasopressin-first has materially lower failure, or norepinephrine-first lowers transient hypotension but increases rescue vasopressors, RRT, or mortality. This falsifies the directional hypothesis and argues against assuming catecholamine-first removal is safer.
* **Inconclusive:** CI spans clinically important benefit and harm (primary margin +/-5 points), positivity/ESS fails, fewer than 200 eligible episodes or 40 primary events per arm remain, covariate balance cannot reach absolute SMD <0.10, results reverse across reasonable gap/grace/MAP definitions, or treatment-linked monitoring differs enough to undermine endpoint ascertainment.
* **Negative controls / diagnostics:** (1) future MAP measured 12-18 hours after both agents are off among survivors is a temporal negative-control outcome; a strong association suggests residual severity/selection. (2) Shift the pseudo-index 6 hours before actual discontinuation while both drugs continue; an apparent strategy effect on pre-decision MAP/lactate trends falsifies exchangeability. (3) Test whether post-index MAP measurement frequency differs by arm; repeat the primary endpoint using treatment-rescue components only and in arterial-line patients. (4) Compare weekday/hour and care-unit preference; leave-one-care-unit and anchor-year-group-out analyses assess practice-pattern confounding.
* **Unavailable or adjudication-dependent:** MIMIC lacks clinician intent, protocol assignment, bedside assessment of fluid responsiveness/perfusion, echocardiography as a consistently structured modality, complete outpatient death ascertainment, and reliable adjudication of septic versus cardiogenic/vasoplegic shock at each minute. Infusion chart boundaries can reflect bag changes/documentation rather than true stopping; the grace/gap rules require clinician/pharmacist review on a blinded sample of timelines. Automated computation can verify cohort construction, time ordering, outcome counts, weights, estimates and whether reported numerical conclusions match outputs. It cannot verify that the patient was truly eligible for either strategy, establish causal exchangeability, or turn discharge coding into clinical adjudication. A causal practice recommendation requires prospective expert review and preferably randomization.
