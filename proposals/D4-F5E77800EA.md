## 1. Scientific opening, strongest supported claim, and the exact unresolved claim

### 1.1 The opening

Plasma-proteome "organ clocks" have moved from a method to an interpretation in under three
years. The construct is: take the subset of plasma proteins whose transcripts are enriched in one
organ, train a chronological-age predictor on that subset alone, and read the residual
(predicted minus chronological age) as an **organ age gap** — a measurement of how old *that organ*
is. [K1] introduced this with SomaScan in 5,676 participants and reported that ~20% of people are
"accelerated agers" in exactly one organ, 1.7% in several, and that a high heart gap carries a
250% higher heart-failure risk. [K2] reproduced the construct on the platform we have (Olink
Explore 3072) in 43,616 UK Biobank participants with external validation in CKB and NHS. The
clinical reading that is now being promoted is **organ-targeted**: a panel that flags *which*
organ is failing, implying organ-directed surveillance or intervention.

The opening is that neither inspected paper measured organ specificity. They measured two
different things and let specificity be inferred:

- **[K1]** reports matched (diagonal) associations and a figure of pairwise organ-gap
  correlations. It also reports, in the same paper, three facts that point the other way:
  age-estimation accuracy correlates with the *number* of input proteins; 226 of 559 (40%)
  organ-gap-to-clinical-biochemistry associations were significant, i.e. gaps are broadly
  associated with general chemistry rather than selectively; and a "control" age gap correlated
  r = 0.98 with the organ-shared "organismal" gap. Its flagship brain claim (brain gap -> AD) **did
  not replicate** in Stanford-ADRC and had to be rescued with a trait-supervised model. [verified,
  full text]
- **[K2]** reports mean pairwise correlation among organismal + organ gaps of r = 0.16 in UKB
  (0.19 CKB, 0.10 NHS) and calls this "partial independence". It fits one Cox model per
  gap-outcome pair, never mutually adjusts the ten gaps, never contrasts matched against
  non-matched organ-disease pairs, and never benchmarks the curated panels against size-matched
  random protein sets. Its own strongest cross-organ result is off-diagonal: the brain gap is the
  best predictor of all-cause mortality and is also associated with CKD and depression. It also
  concedes that several organ clocks correlate with chronological age at only r = 0.3-0.5.
  [verified, full text]
- **[K3]**, working in the same resource (11 ProtBAGs, 2,448 proteins, 43,498 UKB participants),
  states in its abstract that organ specificity of proteins, age-bias correction, training size
  and training-set pathology all affect generalisability and interpretability, and that
  **incorporating features across organs improves** prediction of systemic disease categories and
  mortality. [verified, abstract only — no full text available]

Low gap-to-gap correlation (K2's evidence) and organ-specific *risk information* are different
quantities. Residuals of ten predictors trained on disjoint protein sets can be weakly correlated
with each other while all of their disease signal flows through one shared systemic axis, because
age-regression residualisation removes most of the shared *level* and the remaining organ-specific
variance can be mostly assay noise. Nothing in K1, K2 or K3 excludes this.

### 1.2 The strongest claim current evidence supports

1. Plasma-proteome age predictors built from organ-enriched protein subsets produce age gaps that
   are **prospectively associated with incident disease and mortality**, and this replicates across
   platforms (SomaScan [K1], Olink [K2]) and populations (UKB, CKB, NHS [K2]).
2. Matched associations in the expected direction exist for some organs (heart gap -> heart
   failure [K1]; multiple organs -> their diseases [K2]).
3. These gaps are **weakly to moderately correlated with each other** (mean r ~ 0.16 in UKB [K2]),
   so they are not literally the same variable.
4. Cross-organ feature sets predict systemic outcomes better than single-organ ones [K3].

That is a defensible claim about **predictive utility of proteomic aging summaries**. It is not a
claim about organ localisation.

### 1.3 The exact unresolved claim this experiment tests

> **Unresolved claim (the organ-specificity claim).** The disease associations of an organ age gap
> are *organ-specific*: a gap labelled for organ *o* is associated with incident disease of organ
> *o* substantially more than with incident disease of other organs; this matched association is
> not merely a projection of one shared systemic axis; and the curated organ-enriched panel carries
> information that a size-matched random protein panel does not.

### 1.4 The rival explanation and where the two differ observably

**Leading explanation (H1, organ-level construct).** There is organ-level aging biology; the
organ-enriched panel samples it; the gap is a noisy measurement of that organ's state.

**Rival (H0, shared axis + a few leak proteins).** Nearly all apparent organ specificity is produced
by (a) **one shared systemic axis** — inflammation/illness burden, renal clearance of all plasma
proteins, adiposity, frailty — on which every panel loads because every panel contains
inflammation- and clearance-sensitive members, plus (b) **a handful of genuinely organ-restricted
single proteins** (in our frozen Olink annotation the heart panel is **8** proteins and two of
them, NPPB and NTproBNP, assay the same natriuretic-peptide axis; the kidney panel is **7**,
including UMOD, NPHS2 and REN) that carry real organ information by themselves and need no
"clock" at all.

Differing observable predictions:

| Observation | H1 (organ construct) | H0 (shared axis + leak proteins) |
|---|---|---|
| Off-diagonal vs diagonal HRs | diagonal clearly larger | comparable |
| Matched HR after orthogonalising the gap against the common factor / mutually adjusting all gaps | largely retained | collapses toward null |
| Size-matched **random** protein-set clocks | clearly weaker matched associations than curated panels | about as good |
| Single best organ-restricted protein vs the whole organ clock | clock clearly better | single protein as good or better |

The last row is the sharpest discriminator in a 7- or 8-protein panel and we include it as a
prespecified secondary.

### 1.5 This is a construct-validity question, and the decision it informs

This is explicitly a **measurement / construct-validity** study, not a mechanism study and not a
prognostic-accuracy competition. We are asking whether the *organ label* on a proteomic age gap
carries the information the label claims. An association surviving adjustment would not identify a
mechanism, and we do not claim it would.

**Decision informed.** Whether to develop **organ-targeted proteomic screening panels** — small
assays marketed to tell a clinician which organ to investigate. If specificity is largely absent
(H0), the correct product is a single well-calibrated systemic-risk proteomic score (cheaper, one
panel, one threshold) plus the few genuinely organ-restricted analytes (NT-proBNP, UMOD, cystatin
C-like markers) measured directly, and organ-labelled multi-protein clocks are a misleading
interface that will drive organ-directed workups on a systemic signal. If specificity is present
(H1), per-organ panels are justified and the next question becomes which organs clear a useful
discrimination threshold. Either outcome changes what gets built. A null here is as decision-useful
as a positive.

### 1.6 Novelty statement (bounded, not exhaustive)

Closest work actually inspected: [K1] (construct origin, SomaScan), [K2] (same platform, same
cohort family, larger n, external validation), [K3] (same resource, abstract-level critique).
None of the three estimates a diagonal-vs-off-diagonal specificity contrast, a size-matched
random-panel null, or a common-factor orthogonalisation. Two targeted Europe PMC searches returned
**HITS 0**: `("organ aging" OR "organ-specific aging") AND ("random protein" OR "randomly selected
proteins") AND proteomic`, and `ABSTRACT:"organ age gap" AND ABSTRACT:"specificity"` [verified].
This is a novelty *signal*, not an exhaustive search; a random-set null may exist in a
supplementary file we did not inspect.

### 2.4 Temporal boundaries — and exactly what the missing draw date costs

**The constraint.** Field 53 (assessment/draw date) is **not in this namespace** [verified]. So:
(i) chronological age is available only as an **integer** at instance 0; (ii) the blood draw date
is known only to lie within UKB baseline recruitment, 2006-03..2010-10; (iii) there is no
participant-level time origin.

**What this costs a "clock".** Three real costs, each handled explicitly:

1. *Age granularity.* Integer age inflates irreducible MAE by ~0.29 years (SD of a uniform
   rounding error) and attenuates age-protein correlations slightly. Since **every** model — organ,
   organismal, random — is trained against the same coarse target, this is a **common** handicap
   and does not bias the diagonal-vs-off-diagonal contrast, which is the estimand. We report
   absolute MAEs as not comparable to [K1]/[K2] values.
2. *No individual follow-up origin.* We therefore use a **common calendar landmark L = 2011-01-01**,
   after the last possible baseline draw. All participants enter at L; follow-up time is calendar
   time from L. Up to ~4.8 years of variable draw-to-landmark lag is absorbed into the landmark and
   is **unmodelled**; we state this as a limitation, not a solved problem, and we add a sensitivity
   analysis adjusting for recruitment-era proxies available in-namespace (none are strong; see 6).
3. *Prevalent/incident boundary.* **Prevalent** = earliest `41280` first-diagnosis date for the
   outcome's code set **strictly before L**; such participants are **excluded from that outcome's
   risk set**. **Incident** = earliest such date **on or after L**. **Administrative censoring at
   2022-10-31**, the observed maximum `41280` date [verified]. Follow-up is therefore a fixed
   11.83-year window for everyone, which is a property of this design, not a defect to hide.

**Critical parsing correction [verified by me].** `41280` values are SAS `DDMMMYYYY` strings.
The Lead's `work/diag/diag1.py` split pre/post-2011 with `if s >= "2011-01-01"`, a *lexicographic*
comparison, which splits on **day of month** (days 20-31 sort "after" any ISO string, 01-19
"before"), not on calendar time. Predicted artifact ratio 19/12 = 1.58 vs observed heart-failure
2,382:1,469 = 1.62 — confirmed. **Only the pre+post sums in `diag1.json` are usable.** This
proposal uses an explicit parser,
`re.compile(r"^(\d{2})([A-Z]{3})(\d{4})$")` with a month-name map, and the solver must do the same.

## 3. The prespecified specificity estimand and the two discriminating nulls

### 3.1 Gap construction (common to every model class)

For each panel P (10 organ panels, 1 organismal panel, R random panels), on the **training split
only**: fit `age ~ f(proteins in P)`, predict in held-out participants, then compute
`gap = predicted_age - lowess(predicted_age ~ chronological_age, frac = 2/3)` evaluated at the
participant's chronological age — the age-bias correction used by [K1] — and **z-score within
split**. This removes the mean age trend so a gap cannot be a disguised age variable, and matches
[K1] so our numbers are comparable to the construct as published. [K3]'s abstract flags age-bias
correction as a determinant of interpretability, which is exactly why we fix it in advance.

### 3.2 The HR matrix and the single specificity index

Fit, for each organ gap g (10) x each qualifying outcome d, a Cox proportional-hazards model on
calendar time from L to first diagnosis or 2022-10-31:

```
h(t) = h0(t) exp( b_gd * gap_g + c1*age + c2*sex + c3*age*sex )
```

Covariates are limited to what this namespace has: integer baseline age, string sex (recoded),
their interaction. (Townsend, smoking, ethnicity and centre — used by [K2] — are **not available
here**; see 6.) This gives a 10 x D matrix of log-HRs per SD of gap, `B = {b_gd}`.

**Prespecified specificity index.** Let `D(g)` be the outcomes anchored to organ g and `O(g)` the
rest. Define per organ

```
S_g = mean_{d in D(g)} b_gd  -  mean_{d in O(g)} b_gd
```

and the primary scalar

```
S = mean over the 10 organs of S_g          [log-HR units per SD of gap]
```

Secondary, scale-free form: `S* = mean_g [ mean_{D(g)} b_gd / mean_{O(g)} |b_gd| ]`, reported
because `S` can be dominated by organs with large absolute effects. Both are computed on
**held-out participants only**. CIs by **nonparametric bootstrap over participants, 1,000
replicates**, resampling participants and recomputing the full matrix (gaps are refit inside each
bootstrap replicate using the frozen training-split models; the age-bias lowess and z-scoring are
recomputed per replicate). A row-permutation reference distribution (permuting the organ labels of
the gaps) gives the chance level of `S` given the correlation structure.

Prespecified reporting: the full `B` matrix with CIs, the diagonal mean, the off-diagonal mean,
`S`, `S*`, and the per-organ `S_g` with the organ's panel size alongside, since H0 predicts `S_g`
tracks "has a leak protein" rather than panel size.

### 3.3 Discriminating null 1 — size-matched random protein-set clocks

For each organ g with panel size `n_g`, draw **R = 200** random protein sets of exactly `n_g`
proteins from the **2,331 non-enriched** Olink proteins (and, as a stricter variant, from all 2,923
minus the 424, i.e. allowing non-focal-enriched proteins), refit the identical pipeline, and
compute for each random set the same quantities: age-prediction MAE/r, matched log-HR against
organ g's outcomes, and `S_g`.

This yields a **null distribution of `S_g` and of the matched log-HR under random panels of the
same size**. Prespecified statistic: the **empirical percentile of the curated panel within its own
size-matched random null**, per organ and pooled. Because [K1] itself reports that accuracy scales
with panel size, size matching is essential — comparing a 7-protein heart clock against a
240-protein organismal clock confounds specificity with capacity.

### 3.4 Discriminating null 2 — shared-axis orthogonalisation and mutual adjustment

Three nested versions, all prespecified:

1. **Common-factor orthogonalisation.** Estimate the first principal component `F1` of the
   standardised 424 panel proteins **in the training split**, project it into held-out
   participants, and replace each gap by its residual from regression on `F1` (and, in a variant,
   on `F1..F3`). Recompute the whole matrix and `S`.
2. **Mutual adjustment.** One Cox model per outcome containing **all 10 gaps simultaneously** plus
   age/sex. H0 predicts the matched coefficient collapses while one or two gaps (the ones loading
   most on the shared axis) absorb the signal.
3. **Organismal-gap adjustment.** Add the organismal gap (trained on the 2,331 non-enriched
   proteins) as a covariate to each matched model. This is the direct analogue of [K1]'s
   r = 0.98 "control gap" observation, promoted from a correlation remark to a test.

Prespecified secondary that separates H0's two components: **single-protein benchmark.** For each
organ, the best single panel protein (selected in the training split by matched-outcome
association) is entered as a competing predictor. If a 7-protein heart clock adds nothing over
NPPB alone, the "clock" framing is unsupported even if the matched HR is large.

### 3.5 Measured sizing of the shared axis — descriptive, and it already sharpens the design

`diagB.py` returned, in the complete-panel population on the 424 standardised panel proteins
(median-imputed, descriptive only, **no test performed**) [all verified]:

- **Panel PC1 explains 8.06% of variance**; PC2 4.69%, PC3 3.90%; **PC1-5 cumulatively 22.1%**.
- Per-organ PC1 variance explained: pancreas 0.300, artery 0.288, lung 0.216, heart 0.218,
  kidney 0.198, muscle 0.186, liver 0.158, immune 0.148, intestine 0.133, **brain 0.099**.
- Pairwise |correlation| among the 10 organ PC1s: **mean 0.240, median 0.237, max 0.498**
  (artery-brain). Full 10x10 matrix in `diagB.json`.
- Each organ PC1's |correlation| with the **global panel PC1**: **immune 0.883**, liver 0.736,
  kidney 0.576, intestine 0.521, artery 0.493, lung 0.468, muscle 0.359, pancreas 0.273,
  brain 0.244, **heart 0.115**.

**How this changes the design (and it does, in three ways).**

1. **The shared axis is real but not monolithic.** 8% on PC1 means there is no single dominant
   component swallowing the panel, so a crude "regress everything on PC1" null would be a weak
   test. We therefore prespecify the `F1..F3` variant and the **mutual-adjustment** version of
   null 2 as co-primary with the `F1` version, rather than treating `F1` orthogonalisation alone as
   the shared-axis test. This is a design decision taken *before* any outcome model is fitted.
2. **The shared axis is loaded very unevenly across organs.** Immune (0.883) and liver (0.736) are
   nearly collinear with the global axis, while heart (0.115) and brain (0.244) are nearly
   orthogonal to it. H0 therefore makes a **sharper, pre-registerable prediction than we could
   state before this measurement**: the organs whose matched associations should survive
   orthogonalisation are precisely heart and brain (which have their own axis), and immune/liver
   should collapse. If instead immune and liver survive and heart/brain collapse, H0 in this form is
   wrong and something other than a shared systemic axis is operating. This cell-level prediction
   is frozen here.
3. **Organ PC1s are more correlated with each other (mean |r| 0.240) than [K2]'s organ age gaps
   are (mean r 0.16).** The difference is consistent with age-regression plus bias correction
   removing part of the shared structure — i.e. **K2's low gap correlation is partly a product of
   the residualisation step, not independent evidence of organ independence.** That is the specific
   inferential gap this proposal closes, and it is now quantified rather than asserted.

None of this is evidence for or against H1: these are pre-clock covariance descriptives with no
outcome involved. They size the rival and fix the null specification.

## 4. Baseline and substantive alternative, at equal detail, on the same question

Both arms answer the **same** question (does the organ label carry organ-specific risk
information?) and share population, landmark, outcomes, splits and the estimand `S`.

### 4.1 Splits and leakage control (identical for both arms)

Participant-disjoint, hashed on `n_eid`: **train 60% / calibration 15% / test 25%** of the
complete-panel population (n = 41,927 verified, so approximately 25,156 / 6,289 / 10,482; with
the thinnest qualifying diagonal outcome at 233 incident events overall, ~58 land in test, which
is why per-cell HRs are descriptive and the pooled index `S` is the primary inference). All protein scaling, imputation, lowess age-bias curves, PC1/factor
estimation, hyperparameter selection and single-protein selection are fit on **train** only and
applied frozen to calibration/test. All reported HRs, `S`, `S*` and null percentiles are computed
on **test** only. Random panels use the same splits and the same frozen preprocessing. Bootstrap
resamples participants within test. No participant contributes to both a clock's training and its
own gap. Missing protein values are median-imputed **using train medians**, with a
complete-case-per-model sensitivity analysis; imputation never crosses the complete/reduced panel
boundary.

### 4.2 BASELINE — per-organ penalised linear age regression (elastic net) + Cox

- **Inputs:** the 424 panel proteins split into 10 organ blocks; 2,331 non-enriched proteins for the
  organismal clock; 200 size-matched random sets per organ.
- **Fitting target:** integer `n_21003_0_0`, squared-error loss.
- **Model:** elastic net (`alpha` grid 0.1..0.9, `lambda` by 10-fold CV inside train), standardised
  inputs. Chosen because it is the published-comparable linear clock, is stable with 7-protein
  panels where a boosted tree would overfit, and makes the random-panel comparison a clean
  capacity-matched contrast.
- **Then:** gaps per 3.1, matrix and `S` per 3.2, nulls per 3.3-3.4.
- **What it loses:** it *assumes* the HPA annotation is the correct decomposition. If the true
  shared axis is a linear combination that cuts across organs (likely, since inflammation and
  renal clearance affect every panel), the elastic net cannot separate shared from organ-specific
  variance; it can only regress the gap on an externally estimated `F1` after the fact. It also
  cannot discover an organ-specific component that the annotation missed, and cannot tell us
  whether the *annotation* or the *construct* is at fault for a null result.
- **Resource budget:** ~2,200 model fits (10 organs + 1 organismal + 2,000 random sets) x ~41k x
  <=108 features. **Measured basis:** reading the 424 panel columns for all 53,014 rows plus
  `to_numeric` coercion is the dominant I/O and was the body of the bounded diagnostic (same
  parquet, same columns). **Unverified estimate:** ~45-90 min on 16 CPUs, < 32 GB RAM. CPU only;
  no GPU benefit for penalised linear fits of this size.

### 4.3 SUBSTANTIVE ALTERNATIVE — structured shared+specific latent factor model

- **Question it answers:** the same one, but it **estimates** the shared/organ-specific
  decomposition from the data instead of assuming the annotation is it.
- **Inputs:** the standardised 424 panel proteins (same complete-panel population, same splits),
  with the organ block assignment used only as a **structural prior**, not as a hard partition.
- **Model.** An autoencoder with an explicitly partitioned bottleneck:
  `z = [z_shared (k_s = 4..8 dims, receives all 424 proteins), z_1..z_10 (k_o = 2 dims each,
  organ-specific, each receiving only its own organ block)]`; decoder reconstructs all 424
  proteins. Losses: reconstruction MSE + `lambda_age * ` age-prediction head on `z` +
  `lambda_orth * ` soft orthogonality penalty `||corr(z_shared, z_o)||_F^2` forcing organ factors to
  carry only variance **not** explained by the shared factors + L1 on encoder weights for
  interpretability. A linear ablation (sparse PCA / probabilistic shared-plus-specific factor model
  with the same orthogonality constraint) is fit first as the identifiability check; the
  autoencoder is only justified if the linear version shows nonlinear residual structure.
- **Fitting target:** joint — reconstruction of the proteome plus chronological age — so the
  shared axis is defined by *proteome covariance*, not by what happens to predict age. This matters:
  defining the shared axis by PC1 (baseline null 2) risks removing genuine organ signal if an organ
  dominates PC1; the constrained model defines it as what is *common across organ blocks*.
- **Then:** organ-specific age gaps are computed from `z_o` alone (age head restricted to `z_o`),
  and the identical matrix, `S`, `S*`, bootstrap and random-panel null are computed on test.
- **What it recovers that the baseline loses:** (i) the magnitude of genuinely organ-specific
  proteomic variance **after** the shared axis is removed by construction rather than by post-hoc
  regression — this is the quantity H0 and H1 actually disagree about; (ii) whether a null `S`
  reflects *no organ-specific biology* or *an inadequate annotation*, because the learned `z_o`
  can retain matched associations even if the curated clock does not; (iii) whether the shared axis
  is one dimension or several (K1's r = 0.98 control-gap observation implies ~one; if `k_s > 1` is
  needed, "the shared axis" is itself a simplification); (iv) nonlinear shared structure (e.g.
  renal-clearance effects saturating) that a linear residualisation leaves in the gaps.
- **Uncertainty evaluation:** 10 random restarts x 5 split seeds; report the across-restart
  spread of `S` alongside the bootstrap CI, since latent-factor models have rotation/initialisation
  variability that a single CI hides. Factor stability measured by across-restart canonical
  correlation of `z_shared`.
- **Resource budget.** 424 inputs x ~24k train rows is small. **Unverified estimate:** ~3-6 min
  per fit on one A100; 50 fits (restarts x seeds x `k_s` grid) ~ 3-5 h, plus the random-panel null
  **not** repeated for the autoencoder (prespecified: the random-panel null is run on the baseline
  only, because 2,000 autoencoder fits would not fit the envelope — stated as a scope limit, not
  hidden). Request `{"cpus": 8, "memory_mb": 65536, "gpus": 1}`. Both arms together remain inside
  the solver envelope (cpus <= 16, gpus <= 8, memory <= 256 GB, <= 28,800 s), with the CPU baseline
  and GPU alternative run as separate jobs.

### 4.4 Selection

**Primary = the BASELINE (4.2).** Reason: the claim under test is a claim about the *published
construct*, which is a curated-panel linear clock. Falsifying or supporting it requires measuring
*that* object; a better learned decomposition would answer a different question (is there any
organ-specific signal?) and could not refute the published claim. The baseline also carries the
random-panel null, which is the single most discriminating test and is only affordable with cheap
fits.

**The alternative (4.3) is retained as an explicit secondary**, run on the same splits, and is the
designated follow-up. **Evidence that would justify promoting it to primary** — revised after the
measurement in 3.5, which showed panel PC1 explains only **8.06%** of variance so a
"shared-axis-dominates-the-covariance" criterion is already answered in the negative:

- (a) the baseline yields `S` indistinguishable from zero **while** the organ blocks demonstrably
  carry non-shared structure — operationalised as: at least 5 of 10 organ PC1s have |r| < 0.5 with
  the global PC1 (**measured: 6 of 10 do — muscle 0.359, pancreas 0.273, brain 0.244, heart 0.115,
  artery 0.493, lung 0.468**) and per-organ PC1 variance explained >= 0.10 (**measured: 10 of 10**).
  That combination would mean organ-specific proteomic variance exists but the *annotation-plus-age-
  regression* pipeline fails to convert it into organ-specific risk information — a learned
  decomposition is then the only way to tell whether the construct or the pipeline is at fault.
  **This condition on the covariance side is already met**, so promotion hinges solely on the
  baseline `S` result.
- (b) curated panels sit inside their size-matched random-panel null distribution, which would mean
  the HPA annotation adds nothing and a learned decomposition is the only remaining route to an
  organ-specific construct.

Both are recorded now so that promotion is not a post-hoc rescue. If instead the baseline yields a
clear positive `S` surviving all nulls, the alternative becomes a refinement question (how much
*more* organ-specific variance is recoverable) rather than a test of the claim, and we would defer
it.

**Rejected alternatives, recorded with reasons.** (i) LightGBM+Boruta per organ as in [K2] —
rejected as primary because tree ensembles on 7-8-protein panels invite overfitting and make the
size-matched random null non-comparable across organs; retained as a prespecified sensitivity fit
for the three largest panels (brain, immune, liver) to confirm the conclusion is not an
elastic-net artifact. (ii) Mendelian randomisation of organ gaps — impossible, no genotypes in this
namespace. (iii) Mortality as the unifying outcome, as in [K1]/[K2] — impossible, fields 40000/40001
absent; this is a real loss discussed in 5.3.

## 5. Falsification criteria with numeric thresholds

All thresholds are on **test**-set estimates, bootstrap 95% CIs, log-HR per SD of gap.

### 5.1 Supportive of H1 (organ specificity is real) — all three must hold

1. **Diagonal dominance:** `S > 0.10` with 95% CI excluding 0, and `S` above the 95th percentile of
   the organ-label-permutation distribution. (0.10 log-HR = HR 1.105 per SD of *excess* matched
   over off-diagonal association; a smaller gap would not support an organ-targeted product.)
2. **Survives the shared axis:** after `F1` orthogonalisation **and** under mutual adjustment of all
   10 gaps, `S` retains **>= 50%** of its unadjusted value with CI excluding 0; and at least
   **4 of 10 organs** keep a matched log-HR with CI excluding 0.
3. **Beats the random null:** the curated panel's `S_g` exceeds the **95th percentile** of its
   size-matched random null in **>= 6 of 10 organs**, and the pooled curated-vs-random comparison
   has a one-sided permutation p < 0.05.

**Interpretation if supportive:** the organ label carries information beyond a shared systemic axis
and beyond panel size. This would justify developing per-organ panels for the specific organs that
clear criterion 2 — and only those. It would **not** establish that the gap measures organ aging
rather than organ *damage* or prodromal disease (see 6.4), nor that intervening on an organ gap
changes outcomes.

### 5.2 Adverse to H1 / supportive of H0 — any of these

1. **No diagonal dominance:** `S <= 0.05` or its CI includes 0 while diagonal HRs are individually
   large. Reading: the gaps are prognostic but not organ-localising.
2. **Shared-axis collapse:** `S` falls below **30%** of its unadjusted value after `F1`
   orthogonalisation or mutual adjustment, or fewer than 2 of 10 organs keep a matched CI excluding
   0. Reading: one systemic axis accounts for the apparent specificity.
3. **Random-set equivalence:** curated `S_g` falls below the **median** of its size-matched random
   null in **>= 5 of 10 organs**. Reading: the HPA annotation contributes nothing; "organ clock"
   is a size-matched random clock with a label.
4. **Single-protein equivalence:** in **>= 3 organs**, the best single panel protein attains **>= 90%**
   of the clock's matched log-HR and the clock adds no significant increment when both are
   entered. Reading: the real content is a few organ-restricted analytes; measure them directly.

**Interpretation if adverse:** organ-targeted proteomic screening panels are not supported by this
evidence; the defensible products are one systemic proteomic risk score plus direct assay of the
handful of organ-restricted analytes. This is a **measurement** conclusion — it would not show that
organ-level aging biology does not exist, only that these panels do not measure it in plasma at
this sample size with these outcomes.

### 5.3 Inconclusive — prespecified

- `S` CI width > 0.20 log-HR. Then the study is underpowered for the index and we report the matrix
  with per-organ conclusions only, with no verdict on `S`. (The companion condition we had planned
  — fewer than 7 of 10 organs retaining a >= 200-event outcome — is **already resolved**: all 10
  organs retain >= 2 qualifying outcomes, see 2.7, so this route to inconclusiveness is closed.)
- `S` between 0.05 and 0.10 with CI excluding 0: specificity detectable but **too small to support
  an organ-targeted product**; reported as a quantified bound, explicitly not as a vindication.
- Baseline and alternative disagree (e.g. curated `S` null but learned `z_o` matched associations
  survive): reported as evidence that the **annotation**, not the construct, is the binding
  constraint — a genuinely informative intermediate outcome and the designated next study.

### 5.4 Conclusions that would require data we do not have

- **Mortality and multi-organ "accelerated ager" claims** (central to [K1] and [K2]): need death
  registry fields 40000/40001, **absent** in this namespace. We cannot test them and do not.
- **Organ-level ground truth** (does a high kidney gap mean a worse kidney?): needs eGFR /
  creatinine / cystatin C (biochemistry absent), or organ MRI/imaging-derived phenotypes (absent).
  Without it, "organ-specific" can only be assessed through *organ-anchored disease*, a proxy.
- **Renal-clearance confounding** (6.3): would need eGFR; we can only bound it with in-namespace
  proxies and prevalent-CKD exclusion.
- **Adiposity/smoking/deprivation adjustment** as in [K2]: BMI, smoking and Townsend are absent;
  our models adjust only age and sex. This is a material difference from [K2] and is stated
  wherever our HRs are compared with theirs.
- **Causal or mechanistic claims** (does organ aging drive the disease?): would need genotypes for
  MR (absent), repeat proteomics for within-person trajectories (instance-0 only), or intervention.
- **Clinical adjudication:** whether a high organ gap should change management, and whether
  hospital ICD-10 codes identify the intended clinical entity (e.g. G31 for "other degenerative
  brain disease", K57 diverticular disease, M62 muscle disorders) requires expert chart review that
  no automatic verifier can supply. **Computationally checkable** here: the matrix, `S`, the null
  distributions, the split discipline, the event counts. **Not checkable:** whether the outcome code
  sets are the right clinical constructs, and whether the decision implication is correct.

## 6. Serious alternative explanations and how each is handled

**6.1 UKB-PPP selection.** The UKB Pharma Proteomics Project cohort is a ~46-54k subset that
includes a **consortium-selected** component (~6,000-6,500 participants chosen by the member
companies for disease enrichment) alongside a randomised component. [K2] describes its n=46,785 as
"a subset of randomly selected, representative UKB participants" — a cohort-documentation claim we
**cannot verify inside this namespace** (no selection-indicator field, no batch field) [verified
limitation]. If disease-enriched participants are over-represented, *all* organ gaps are inflated
toward illness burden, which **favours H0** and would make a positive specificity result
conservative but a null result partly attributable to selection. Handling: report it as an
unresolved bound; prespecified sensitivity analysis excluding participants with high baseline
ICD-10 code counts (>= p90 = 37 codes [verified-by-Lead]) as a crude illness-burden proxy; state
explicitly that we cannot reconstruct the selection indicator.

**6.2 Batch, plate and LOD effects.** No plate/batch field is available in this namespace
[verified]. Values are already normalised (median per-protein mean 0.0235, median SD 0.538
[verified]). The structured missingness (median Jaccard 0.99 among high-missing participants
[verified-by-Lead]) is a **reduced assay panel**, not LOD censoring; treating it as MAR would
induce artificial shared structure, which is why the primary population is complete-panel only and
the reduced-panel group is a separate subgroup. Residual LOD left-censoring within retained
proteins would compress variance and attenuate all gaps roughly equally, biasing `S` toward 0.
Handling: sensitivity analysis restricted to the 1,459 proteins with < 10% missing [verified]; a
prespecified check that per-protein missingness is uncorrelated with panel membership (otherwise
small panels are differentially degraded, which would masquerade as low specificity for heart and
kidney).

**6.3 Renal clearance.** Plasma concentration of most proteins depends on glomerular filtration, so
reduced eGFR raises many analytes simultaneously — a *mechanical* shared axis that would produce
exactly H0's pattern and would also make the kidney panel look specific for CKD for the wrong
reason. We have **no eGFR or creatinine** in this namespace [verified]. Handling: (i) prevalent CKD
(N18) and AKI (N17) before L are excluded from all primary analyses as a sensitivity arm;
(ii) `F1` orthogonalisation removes the dominant shared axis whatever its cause, so the
post-orthogonalisation `S` is interpretable even if `F1` is largely clearance; (iii) we state
plainly that we **cannot separate** "shared axis = inflammation" from "shared axis = clearance" —
this is a named limitation, not a solved problem, and the kidney-diagonal result in particular must
be read with it.

**6.4 Reverse causation and prodromal disease.** A gap measured in 2006-2010 may reflect
*undiagnosed existing* disease rather than aging. [K2] itself notes its brain clock captures
changes preceding but not following dementia onset. Handling: prevalent exclusion at L uses the
full `41280` history back to 1995 [verified], not just baseline self-report; prespecified
**landmark-shifted analysis** excluding events in the first 2 and first 5 years after L; if the
diagonal HRs are driven by early events, the conclusion is about prodromal detection, not aging —
a different and weaker product claim, which we will state as such.

**6.5 Age coarseness and the missing draw date.** Integer age plus a 4.8-year draw window inside
one landmark adds noise to the fitting target and to the exposure-to-landmark interval. It
attenuates all clocks equally (so `S` is conservatively biased) but it does make our absolute
age-prediction accuracy **not comparable** to [K1]/[K2]. Handling: we never claim comparable MAE;
`S` is always a within-study contrast; sensitivity analysis stratified by baseline age tertile to
check the lowess bias correction is not leaving an age-dependent residual.

**6.6 Hospital-only outcome ascertainment.** `41270`/`41280` capture **in-patient** diagnoses only;
no primary care, no cancer registry, no death certificate [verified]. Consequences: outcomes with
mostly outpatient management (early CKD, mild COPD, T2D, Parkinson) are under-ascertained and
**skewed toward severity**, which inflates the shared axis's apparent importance (sicker people are
hospitalised for everything) and therefore biases *against* specificity. Outcomes anchored to
organs differ in their hospitalisation propensity, so the diagonal is not uniformly ascertained.
Handling: prespecified sensitivity using ICD-9 (`41271`/`41281`) and OPCS-4 procedure codes
(`41272`/`41282`) to broaden case definitions; report per-outcome incident counts so readers can
see which diagonal cells are thin; and state that an organ with poorly ascertained outcomes cannot
be scored for specificity.

**6.7 Unobserved death as informative censoring.** Death fields are **absent** [verified]. Deaths
are therefore treated as ordinary administrative censoring at 2022-10-31 — wrong, and in a known
direction: people with high gaps die earlier, lose follow-up time, and have fewer observed
diagnoses, attenuating **all** gap-outcome HRs. Because high-mortality gaps are exactly the ones
loading on the shared axis ([K2]: brain gap is the strongest mortality predictor), this attenuation
is **not uniform across cells** and could distort `S` in either direction. Handling: we cannot fix
it; we bound it with a prespecified sensitivity analysis truncating follow-up at 5 years (where
cumulative mortality in a 57-year-old cohort is small), and we report the 5-year and 11.8-year `S`
side by side. If they disagree materially, the verdict is inconclusive on this ground.

**6.8 The annotation itself.** HPA consensus **RNA** enrichment is a proxy for the tissue origin of
a **plasma protein**; secretion, stability and clearance break the link. 37 Olink composite assay
names cannot be mapped cleanly (handled by a 7-entry alias table plus a drop-all sensitivity
analysis), 0 adipose proteins forced dropping an organ that [K1] includes, and heart/kidney panels
have only 7 members each [all verified]. A null `S` is therefore ambiguous between "no organ
specificity" and "this annotation is the wrong decomposition" — which is precisely why the learned
shared+specific model (4.3) is retained with explicit promotion criteria.

**6.9 Multiplicity.** 10 gaps x ~23 outcomes = up to 230 cells plus nulls. The **primary** inference
is the single prespecified scalar `S` with one bootstrap CI and one permutation reference, so the
headline test is not multiplicity-inflated. Cell-level HRs are reported with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR
across the matrix and are explicitly **descriptive**.
