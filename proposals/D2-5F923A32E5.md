# Context-dependent fibroblast response to macrophage conditioning

## Seed and question
Develops expert seed D2-T1 (`[prior hypothesis]`). The unresolved question is whether a fibroblast transcript response to a defined intervention is context-dependent on the macrophage phenotype present in the coculture, rather than being a uniform fibroblast response.

## Clinical importance
Fibroblasts are a major target of fibrotic disease, but their pathogenic program may be induced, amplified or restrained by macrophage signals. A target that is responsive only in a particular macrophage context could be a more selective intervention point than a target whose expression merely differs between disease and control. Resolving this question can prioritize candidates for perturbation and reduce false prioritization from a global expression contrast.

## Evidence and closest work
The available data include E-MTAB-8316 (four FLS donor clusters, six nested monocyte donors, twelve condition samples) and GSE95588 (coculture HT-Seq counts, including r1/r2 derived from earlier GSE57723 and therefore not independent validation). E-MTAB-8316 has an explicit FLS/monocyte context design; GSE95588 provides a transcriptomic coculture contrast but its nested donor structure limits claims of independent replication.

The closest inspected work is the macrophage–fibroblast crosstalk literature summarized in the frozen Europe PMC search and the selected Frontiers full text, which supports the biological plausibility that macrophage state can alter fibroblast behavior [K1]. The selected OpenAlex work provides a broader context for context-dependent transcript responses and the need to separate donor-specific variation from condition effects [K2]. The selected Europe PMC full-text source supports the interpretation that macrophage conditioning can induce fibroblast phenotypic changes, but does not establish that a particular intervention target acts directly [K3]. These works motivate the hypothesis; they do not establish the proposed context-dependent intervention response.

## Hypothesis
In the available E-MTAB-8316 and GSE95588 coculture data, the fibroblast transcript response associated with macrophage conditioning will be larger, or directionally different, in fibroblast–macrophage contexts enriched for a pro-fibrotic macrophage phenotype than in contexts enriched for a less pro-fibrotic phenotype, after accounting for donor and technical variation.

## Distinguish hypothesis from supported evidence
The supported evidence is that macrophage–fibroblast interaction is biologically plausible and that the datasets contain condition and donor structure suitable for a context-specific contrast. The unresolved claim is that the intervention-associated fibroblast response is specifically modulated by macrophage phenotype in a way that would improve target prioritization. The available data can test a transcriptomic association, not direct target causality.

## Data bindings
- E-MTAB-8316: use the four FLS donor clusters and six nested monocyte donors as the donor-aware structure; condition labels define the macrophage context; sample identifiers and condition metadata are the join keys.
- GSE95588: use HT-Seq counts for the coculture transcript response; retain the r1/r2 provenance and do not treat them as independent validation.
- Do not join patient/donor identifiers across datasets unless a documented crosswalk exists.

## Baseline and alternative
Baseline: donor-aware matched contrasts comparing fibroblast transcript response across macrophage contexts, with donor fixed effects and condition controls where present.
Alternative: a mixed-model analysis with donor and condition random effects, or a permutation test preserving donor structure, to test whether the context-specific contrast remains after accounting for nested donor variation. The alternative can reveal whether the apparent context effect is driven by a small number of donors or by a reproducible pattern.

## Falsification and outcomes
Supportive: the context-specific contrast is directionally consistent and statistically distinguishable after donor-aware adjustment, with the effect not explained by a single donor cluster.
Adverse: the response is uniform across macrophage contexts after donor-aware adjustment, or the direction reverses in the mixed-model analysis.
Inconclusive: the contrast is unstable, underpowered, or dominated by donor-specific variation; this would not refute the hypothesis but would justify a larger donor-aware study.

## Limitations and next steps
The data are transcriptomic and do not establish direct target action. Essential clinical adjudication would require perturbation experiments or independent cohort validation. The next test is a donor-aware context-specific contrast with a mixed-model alternative, followed by perturbation validation if the association is robust.
