# Provisional hypothesis: directional macrophage-to-fibroblast signaling in synovial coculture

## Proposed relationship

In the available synovial fibroblast (FLS) and macrophage coculture datasets, a macrophage-derived inflammatory state may induce a reproducible, condition-specific transcriptional response in FLS that is distinguishable from FLS-only controls and from macrophage composition or donor effects. This is a hypothesis, not an executed finding.

## Grounding and observed evidence

The dataset guide specifies E-MTAB-8873 as a targeted 445-feature assay with 12 condition samples, including FLS-only, macrophage-positive, macrophage-negative, pre-LPS, and inhibitor-associated conditions, with repeated context measurements and reused/multiple donors. Its exact source is the E-MTAB-8873 prepared count matrices and design JSON listed in datasets/disease_mechanisms/README.md. The guide also warns that pseudobulk does not make samples independent, that the panel is targeted, and that ordinary donor t-tests are disabled.

The closest seed question is D3-M2 (“directional cell communication”), which asks whether macrophage–fibroblast or endothelial–fibroblast mechanisms can be distinguished by paired/condition comparisons. This seed is literature-informed and untested; it does not establish a causal mechanism.

## Unresolved claim and falsifiable test

The unresolved claim is that macrophage-to-FLS communication produces a condition-specific FLS transcriptional signature beyond FLS-only and macrophage-negative controls. A first computable test will:

1. Reconcile E-MTAB-8873 feature identifiers and condition/sample metadata before naming any gene.
2. Average repeated measurements within donor/condition and preserve donor dependence.
3. Compare macrophage-positive versus FLS-only and macrophage-negative conditions using a donor-aware, permutation-based or hierarchical model, with pre-LPS/inhibitor conditions as mechanistic context rather than independent causal proof.
4. Require a prespecified direction and effect-size threshold, with negative control features/conditions and a donor/condition permutation null.

Supportive evidence would be a reproducible condition-specific signature with donor-aware uncertainty and separation from controls. Adverse evidence would be no separation, reversal, or failure to survive donor/condition permutations. Inconclusive evidence would include low feature detectability, unresolved sample-to-donor mapping, or uncertainty too large to distinguish the proposed effect from technical or composition artifacts.

## Missing evidence and limits

No result has been computed in this draft. The assay cannot measure unlisted genes, and targeted expression cannot establish direct target action, ligand-receptor causality, or clinical transition. Reused donors and repeated samples require dependence-aware analysis. Clinical phenotype, treatment history, age, and independent external validation are not assumed available. A future test would need verified metadata mappings and, for causal interpretation, perturbation or independent experimental evidence.

## Method comparison

A simple donor-aware paired contrast is the baseline. A substantive alternative is a hierarchical/regularized model that borrows information across the 445 measured features while retaining donor and condition random effects; it could reveal coordinated pathway-level structure that a single-feature contrast loses. The alternative is deferred unless the baseline establishes adequate feature-level signal and metadata integrity. Both use the same inputs and outcome; neither is a claim of mechanistic proof.
