# Observation-validity stress test of early hepatic reserve after first TACE

## Question, hypothesis and substantive advance

In adults with HCC undergoing a first qualifying TACE, does an early within-person change in routine liver-function assays from T0-14:-1 to T0+1:+7 provide reproducible prognostic information for a new coded hepatic-decompensation encounter on T0+8:+90 beyond pre-TACE state/history and the observation process that generated the tests?

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is that an adverse multi-assay trajectory (lower albumin and platelets and/or higher total bilirubin and INR) is associated with the broad coded endpoint and improves held-out proper scoring over a current-state/history plus care-process baseline. The trajectory association and score improvement must remain directionally similar under stabilized observation/follow-up weighting and endpoint-specificity checks.

This is a prognostic-information claim about an operational local-record endpoint. It is not a claim that TACE causes decompensation, that a lexical diagnosis is adjudicated liver failure, that missing follow-up means no event, or that a model defines a treatment threshold.

The assigned parent already addressed sparse identification by replacing a joint latent observation model with an anchored assay-specific reserve model. This child makes the remaining clinically consequential repair: it tests whether the broad diagnosis signal survives severe-term and concordance stress tests. The broad coded endpoint remains the primary estimand to preserve power and the parent's question; the new tiers determine whether a supportive broad result can reasonably be interpreted as hepatic decompensation rather than documentation intensity.

## Established evidence versus unresolved claim

Available local evidence establishes dated TACE procedures, encounter dates, repeated laboratory rows, diagnosis labels, treatment-context rows, and a way to construct patient-held-out splits. The HCC catalog and row audit do not establish a diagnosis timestamp, adjudicated hepatic failure, complete death capture, reliable medication administration, lab units, or a complete external population.

A public TACE study was acquired/read from Europe PMC (Li et al., doi 10.2147/JHC.S499436; source ID recorded in `work/episode5_hcc_repair_support.md`). Its accessible abstract reports a 210-patient TACE cohort in which baseline INR and albumin were clinical predictors of post-TACE liver failure and a clinical/radiomics model reported AUC 0.878. That supports the plausibility and clinical importance of reserve markers; it does not establish incremental value of an irregular early post-TACE trajectory in this EHR, nor validate this local coded endpoint. The imaging, clinical adjudication and validation context of that study are not available here, so this is a bounded adaptation, not a reproduction.

The strongest claim this experiment can test is: conditional on the stated landmark and ascertainment assumptions, the early trajectory adds prognostic information to a current-state/history and observation-process model, and that information is not confined to a low-specificity coding artifact.

The experiment cannot establish causal TACE toxicity, true hepatic failure, mortality, monitoring benefit, clinical utility, transport to buckets 80-99, or external validity. Those require adjudication/linkage and another cohort or prospective study.

## Population and temporal definitions

Use only discovery buckets 0-79 under SHA-256("ehr-hypothesis-discovery-v1" + NUL + "hcc" + NUL + patient key) mod 100. Buckets 80-99 are inaccessible and are not external validation. All splitting and bootstrap resampling is by `Patient Master Index`.

1. Read `procedures` from `[internal dataset path]`. A qualifying procedure has `Start Time` present and `Surgery` equal to TACE case-insensitively or containing `chemoembolization`. Link it to `encounters` by `(Patient Master Index, Encounter Number)`, deduplicate same-patient/same-encounter rows, and set T0 to the earliest qualifying `Start Time) per patient. T0 is procedure start, not diagnosis or discharge time.

2. Read `encounters` from `[internal dataset path]`. Require `Age` >=18 at the index encounter and an HCC label in `diagnoses` from `[internal dataset path]`, where `Diagnosis Name` contains `hepatocellular carcinoma` or `liver cancer` at the index encounter or at an encounter with `Admission Time` in T0-180:-1. Because diagnoses has no temporal field, `Admission Time` is a record-time proxy. Preserve `Diagnosis Type` strata.

3. Exclude any decompensation term (`ascites`, `liver failure`, `hepatic failure`, `decompensation`, `hepatic encephalopathy`, `hepatic coma`) on an encounter with `admission time` in T0-180:-1. Exclude a liver resection/ablation procedure whose `procedure` contains `resection`, `ablation`, `radiofrequency`, or `microwave` and whose `start time` is T0-7:+7. These are index-window exclusions, not lifetime exclusions.

4. Read numeric observations from `labs`, `[internal dataset path]`, using exact `Test` names and numeric `Quantitative result`; use `Test time` for all windows and never infer units. Primary assays are exactly `Albumin`, `Total bilirubin`, `International normalized ratio`, `Platelet count`; context assays are `Creatinine`, `Sodium`, `Alpha-fetoprotein`. Pre-window is T0-14 through T0-1 and post-window is T0+1 through T0+7. Same-day observations are excluded.

5. The landmark requires at least one numeric primary-assay observation in each window. For each exact assay/window record median, count, median time, numeric parse failures and missingness. A complete four-assay cohort is sensitivity only. Transformations are training-fold only.

6. The primary outcome joins diagnoses to encounters on `(patient master index, encounter number)). A positive is the first broad decompensation term on `admission time` T0+8:+90 with no matching term in T0-180:-1; event time is the linked `admission time` proxy. A negative requires no event through day 90 and an encounter with `admission time` or `discharge time` at least 90 days after T0. A patient with an event is positive even without later follow-up. No-event/no-anchor is unknown: exclude it from the primary binary analysis and use it in follow-up-weighted/pattern-mixture sensitivity. There is no supported death endpoint, so no death composite is claimed.

## Endpoint-validity tiers

The broad coded endpoint above is primary and unchanged from the parent. Refit the same models and split for these prespecified sensitivity outcomes, without selecting the primary result based on them:

- Severe-term tier: event `Diagnosis Name` contains one of `Liver failure`, `Hepatic failure`, `Hepatic encephalopathy`, `Hepatic coma`. Report `Ascites` and `Decompensation` separately rather than treating them as equivalent.
- Repeat-confirmed tier: a broad decompensation term occurs on at least two distinct `Encounter Number` encounters separated by at least one day in T0+8:+90. The first event remains anchored to `Admission Time`.
- Care-concordant tier: a broad event is accompanied, on its event encounter or a patient-linked encounter from event day through +7 days, by a medication `medication` containing `spironolactone`, `furosemide`, `torasemide`, `lactulose` or `rifaximin`, with `start time` in that interval. Use `start time`/`end time` only as treatment-context evidence; do not infer administration, dose or indication. If exact strings are absent, report this tier unavailable rather than changing the map post hoc.

Counts, diagnosis types and term distributions must be reported separately. A broad-only signal with absent or contradictory specificity tiers is adverse to the clinical interpretation and may represent documentation intensity. Sparse tiers can be inconclusive rather than null.

## Exact source bindings and leakage controls

Required source schemas are those in the HCC guide: `encounters` has `patient master index`, `encounter number`, `age`, `sex`, `encounter time`, `admission time`, `discharge time`, `encounter department`; `procedures) has `patient master index`, `encounter number`, `surgery`, `start time`, `end time`, `surgery source`; `diagnoses) has `patient master index`, `encounter number`, `diagnosis name`, `diagnosis type`; `labs) has `patient master index`, `encounter number`, `test`, `qualitative result`, `quantitative result`, `specimen type`, `test time`; and `medications) has `patient master index`, `encounter number`, `medication`, `single-dose medication amount`, `single-dose medication amount unit`, `frequency`, `start time`, `end time`, `route of administration`, `drug type`.

Aggregate every one-to-many table before joining. Direct identifiers in encounters are not predictors or outputs. No post-prediction diagnosis, follow-up anchor, post-day-90 row, or future treatment can enter the day-7 feature or weight model. Patient-level splits prevent within-patient leakage.

Examinations and pathology are bound in the supporting record but not primary predictors: examinations has narrative `Examination Findings`/`Examination Diagnosis` and `Start Time`, but no validated local stage/response extraction; pathology has narrative fields and no catalogued timestamp. Clinical documents have no usable temporal columns; vitals, transfers and front_page are identifier-only.

## Baseline and scientifically substantive alternative

B0 is a rare-event penalized logistic model using pre-window exact-assay medians/missingness, age, sex, prior encounter count, prior coded liver/decompensation history, diagnosis-type stratum and pre-index medication/treatment-context indicators. B1 adds post-window assay availability, counts and median timing. B1 is the primary comparator because it holds the care process constant while testing whether the trajectory adds value. Imputation, robust scaling, regularization and rare-event correction are train/tune only.

The selected A1 alternative is an anchored one-factor assay-specific random-effects reserve model. Robust-standardize each exact assay using training median/IQR; fit irregular observations at actual `test time` with assay-specific intercepts and residual variances; estimate patient pre-reserve and post-change/slope; anchor albumin loading to +1 and constrain bilirubin/INR adverse and platelets opposite; retain loadings, residuals, posterior/empirical-Bayes change uncertainty and discordant recovery. Add reserve change and uncertainty to exactly B1. A1 retains the information that assays are irregular, on different scales, measured with error, and may recover discordantly; B1 cannot express this scientific structure.

Also fit A2, a clinically legible signed reserve-change score: training-fold robust assay changes are signed so lower albumin/platelets and higher bilirubin/INR are adverse; fixed or train/tune-estimated weights are declared before test evaluation. A2 tests whether the result survives a constrained mechanistic representation but loses assay-specific uncertainty and discordance. It is a sensitivity comparator, not the primary selected method.

A full joint observation/latent model and a black-box sequence/image model are deferred because the parent audit found sparse endpoints and the local image/pathology inputs are unavailable or unvalidated. This is an identifiability and clinical-information decision, not a ban on neural methods or GPU use.

## Analysis and uncertainty

Use deterministic patient-level 60/20/20 train/tune/test folds within buckets 0-79. Fit B0, B1, A1, A2, assay availability weights and follow-up weights only on train/tune. Availability models may use pre-index assay history/count/timing, prior encounter history, age/sex, index department and pre-index treatment context. Follow-up models use T0-known covariates. A sensitivity model may add history observed up to each assay decision, but no future diagnosis/anchor or latent outcome. Stabilize/truncate weights at training-fold prespecified percentiles and report positivity/effective sample size.

Primary estimands are A1 adverse-IQR reserve-change odds ratio, paired test log-loss and Brier-score changes versus B1, calibration intercept/slope and reliability. Report A2 and all endpoint tiers as sensitivity. AUROC/AUPRC are descriptive. Decision-curve net benefit at 5%, 10%, 20% is exploratory and not a threshold. Use 200 patient-level bootstrap replicates for uncertainty, preserving all rows for a patient; if test events are too sparse, report intervals/descriptive outputs and no confirmatory interpretation.

Falsification checks are:

- post-window process-only counts/timing model;
- remove post-window counts/timing from B1/A1;
- within-fold permutation of post-window lab patient assignments preserving assay/window/count;
- any-primary-assay versus strict all-four-assay coverage;
- unweighted versus stabilized-weighted results and unknown-follow-up pattern-mixture event-odds multipliers;
- diagnosis-type, admission-time versus discharge-time proxy, severe-term, repeat-confirmed and care-concordant tiers;
- simulated irregular observations with the observed assay/window pattern to test A1 sign/scale and parameter recovery.

A confirmatory supportive interpretation requires at least 30 broad events overall and 8 test events, positive/negative support in the relevant endpoint strata, stable weights and positivity, successful recovery, adverse-direction association, improvement in both proper scores over B1 without material calibration harm, no comparable process-only gain, and no material reversal under endpoint tiers. The source audit motivating the parent reported only 21 events in the observable primary landmark and 12 with complete four-assay coverage; if the episode-5 audit does not exceed the thresholds, the valid result is bounded feasibility/inconclusive.

Supportive results mean incremental prognostic information for this coded outcome under observation assumptions, strengthened if specificity tiers agree. Adverse results include null/reversal, no score improvement, calibration harm, process-only replication, permutation gain, unstable weighting, or broad-only signal lost on specificity tiers; these falsify the incremental coded-endpoint claim in this snapshot, not liver-reserve biology. Inconclusive results include event/support thresholds not met, sparse tier counts, failed recovery, poor positivity, wide intervals, or proxy disagreement. No clinical recommendation follows any category.

## Completion deliverable and evidence limits

Newly fit outputs must include cohort flow and unknown statuses; exact assay inventory and parseability; deterministic split hashes; B0/B1/A1/A2 fitted parameters and training-only transforms; reserve loadings, uncertainty and simulated recovery; weighted/unweighted predictions; effective sample size and positivity; held-out log-loss/Brier/calibration with patient-bootstrap intervals; broad/severe/repeat/care-concordant and diagnosis-type outputs; all falsification outputs; and a claim-to-output table marking supportive, adverse or inconclusive.

Computationally checkable claims are parsing, joins, temporal eligibility, fitted parameters, recovery diagnostics, held-out scores, calibration, weighting and falsification results. Whether a diagnosis is genuine hepatic failure, a drug was administered for decompensation, a death was missed, or a result changes care requires blinded clinical adjudication, linkage, expert review or another study. The discovery split is not external validation. All source CSVs remain read-only; only ordinary derived files are written to the workspace.
