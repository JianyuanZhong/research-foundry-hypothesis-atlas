# HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B validation with an event-level administrative observability audit around Y

## Targeted two-parent successor and unchanged scientific question

This is a targeted two-parent successor of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. The first parent is the assessed valid leader and supplies the mature differential-Y, assay-separated prognostic and missing-Y design. The second is the assessed repairable source-availability design and supplies the discharge/transfer/downstream-record bridge. The repair here is deliberately narrow: make administrative observability an executable **event-level, source-specific at-risk audit**, rather than a snapshot-only coverage label, while preserving the entire mature repeat-TACE P-beyond-B experiment.

The primary falsifiable question remains:

> Among selected adult HCC repeat-TACE episodes with remote baseline assay B and contemporaneous pre-repeat assay P, does P improve held-out prediction of the subsequent same-assay laboratory value Y beyond B and the locked pre-event core?

The added operational question is:

> Among the same outcome-blind BP-eligible episodes, which source-specific records are administratively observable through the Y and downstream horizons, and does Y recording differ by documented selected-encounter discharge state or later **recorded** system activity without treating absent rows as evidence of no care, death, transfer, completed testing, treatment delivery, or patient-important outcome?

This can establish only a reproducible source-record availability and recording pattern. It cannot establish that a patient was discharged to a destination, died, transferred, remained in care, completed an assay, received a procedure or medication, or experienced a clinical outcome. No empirical HCC number or new clinical conclusion is claimed by this proposal.

## Strongest supported claim, unresolved claim, and clinical importance

The inherited full-source work supports feasibility of a selected, short-horizon, same-assay prognostic comparison and an outcome-blind observation audit. It does not establish robust incremental prediction, missing-at-random observation, stable calendar transport, assay clinical interpretation, or benefit. The inherited reconstruction is known to contain severe later Y loss and sparse support in later calendar blocks; this is a design constraint, not a new result from this invocation.

The unresolved claim is narrower and clinically consequential: whether an apparent P-beyond-B prognostic association is being evaluated in a record process whose administrative visibility can be described at the event and source level. Discharge timestamps, encounter capture, laboratory rows, examination rows, orders, medication rows, procedures, and document presence may explain where the source record is observable, but they are not interchangeable and are not patient outcomes. A valid event-level audit prevents two dangerous errors: interpreting an absent downstream row as a negative care event, and interpreting a nominal source maximum as complete individual follow-up. A positive, adverse, or null audit result is therefore informative about the evidence boundary, not proof of care quality or clinical benefit.

## Event-level administrative observability contract

The audit is performed after the frozen BP rows are constructed. Discharge states are outcome-blind and are computed without inspecting target Y values, target-assay rows in the Y window, or downstream rows after the Y deadline. Downstream recording flags are attached only after Y selection/attachment is complete, and are never predictors, imputation variables, censoring variables, weights, bound inputs, or bootstrap inputs.

### 1. Documented discharge states

For each BP-eligible event, let `D = event_time + 72 hours`. For the selected event encounter only, parse `出院时间` as a finite timestamp. It is valid only if it is not earlier than valid `入院时间` when both exist. Assign exactly one mutually exclusive state:

* `discharge_before_event`: valid discharge `< event_time`;
* `discharge_in_Y_window`: valid discharge `>= event_time` and `<= D`;
* `discharge_after_Y_window`: valid discharge `> D`;
* `discharge_missing_or_invalid`: absent, malformed, or pre-admission discharge.

The exact-event boundary belongs to `discharge_in_Y_window`; the exact `D` boundary also belongs to that state. These are administrative encounter-close descriptors only. They do not imply discharge disposition, death, transfer, alive status, inpatient status, inability to obtain Y, or complete health-system follow-up. They do not exclude or censor BP rows, and must not alter source-horizon status.

There is no configured death field or death table. Emit `death_status = unavailable` globally and by origin/block; never emit a death-negative category. The identifier-only transfers table can produce only `nominal_transfer_key_present` versus `transfer_payload_unavailable` after a deduplicated key existence check. It must never produce a timed transfer, destination, disposition, death, or transfer outcome.

### 3. Event-level at-risk and administrative coverage, with no false negatives

For every BP row, assay, origin/block, arm, source, and window, retain separate fields:

* `source_has_valid_time_field`;
* `source_snapshot_max_time`;
* `source_global_horizon_reaches_window`, a necessary file-level check that the source maximum reaches the relevant window end;
* `patient_source_max_time`, computed only from valid same-patient timestamps in that source (or from a valid linked encounter timestamp for encounter-timed documents);
* `patient_source_horizon_reaches_window`, true only when that same-patient source timestamp reaches the relevant window end;
* `administrative_observability_status`.

The relevant endpoint is `D` for Y-window observability and `event_time + 30 calendar days` for D2 observability. A source with no temporal field (transfers; documents as a native source) is `timestamp_unavailable`; it cannot be made observable by an identifier or narrative text. If the source snapshot maximum is earlier than the event-specific endpoint, label `administratively_incomplete`. If the global maximum reaches the endpoint but no valid same-patient timestamp reaches it, label `unknown_horizon`, not administratively complete and not no-care. Only a valid same-patient timestamp reaching the endpoint yields `administratively_observable` for that source/window. If a source has a valid timestamp field but a parser or required key is invalid, fail closed as `unsupported`, rather than treating absence as zero.

This hierarchy is essential. A global snapshot maximum is only a necessary coverage check. A patient-level timestamp anchor is a conservative administrative observability condition, not proof of external-care capture or complete follow-up. A missing source row means `no_record_observed_under_definition` only when the source/window is administratively observable under the stated same-patient horizon condition. When status is `unknown_horizon` or `administratively_incomplete`, the corresponding absent flag is `not_observable`, and the cell is reported `inconclusive/administratively_incomplete` or `inconclusive/unknown_horizon`, never as a zero-care finding. For sources whose timestamp rows are absent by construction, report unavailable rather than infer a negative.

For selected encounter discharge, do not use the discharge timestamp as a substitute for patient-level source horizon. Discharge states are descriptive strata only. Do not combine discharge and transfer identifiers into a synthetic disposition or use discharge to censor, reweight, impute, or declare a source covered.

## Preservation of the missing-Y prognostic experiment

For every assay and origin, preserve the inherited training-only range and fixed-prediction partial-identification analysis. Set `L_train=min(Y_train)` and `U_train=max(Y_train)` from finite observed pre-origin Y only. Empty or degenerate range, future Y outside the range, nonfinite predictions, or failed support marks the relevant result unsupported/inconclusive as specified; never widen the range using future or full-source values.

For each all-BP row, let `p0_i` and `p1_i` be frozen predictions and define observed contrast `d_i=(Y_i-p0_i)^2-(Y_i-p1_i)^2`. For missing rows, evaluate `d_i(y)=2*y*(p1_i-p0_i)+p0_i^2-p1_i^2` at `L_train` and `U_train`. Use the exact all-BP denominator N:

```
Delta_lo = (sum_observed d_i + sum_missing min(d_i(L), d_i(U))) / N
Delta_hi = (sum_observed d_i + sum_missing max(d_i(L), d_i(U))) / N
```

Report all-missing-at-L, all-missing-at-U, least-favorable assignments, model-specific SSE/MSE endpoint components, and the conservative non-primary RMSE-difference enclosure. Category-specific intervals are selection diagnostics, never an aggregate effect; the only overall envelope is computed directly from all BP rows. Preserve common-displacement stress testing and the inherited fixed-prediction patient-frequency bootstrap, including declared seed, arm stratification, frozen fits, original test SD denominator, and non-estimability rules. The observability audit cannot fill missing Y, change the envelope, alter the denominator, or rescue a failed primary result.

## Exact verifier precedence and branch semantics

The verifier must implement the following mutually exclusive precedence, evaluated in order:

1. **`unsupported`** if any source/schema/header/hash assertion fails; a required clock or join assertion fails; required parser semantics change; an unavailable death/transfer/disposition field is inferred from text or identifier-only data; or a future Y is outside the training-only range. This branch overrides every numerical or recording result.
2. **`inconclusive`** (with typed reason such as `inconclusive_support`, `inconclusive_sparse`, `inconclusive_nonestimable`, `inconclusive/administratively_incomplete`, or `inconclusive/unknown_horizon`) if support gates fail, any required administrative source horizon is incomplete/unknown for the claimed source-level absence, cells are sparse, predictions/range/SD/bootstrap are non-estimable, or a source-specific contrast lacks the minimum at-risk denominator. It is never converted to no-care or a negative result.
3. **`adverse_primary`** if, despite adequate computational and support gates, the frozen primary observed-case gain is nonpositive, repeated M1 degradation occurs, or the global all-BP missing-Y interval is wholly nonpositive. Downstream recording or discharge patterns cannot rescue a nonpositive primary prognostic result.
4. **`selection_sensitive`** if the primary observed-case gain is favorable but the global all-BP envelope crosses zero, or if observation/discharge gradients, source disagreements, assay differences, calendar/arm heterogeneity, or negative-control warnings materially threaten interpretation. This is not support for P, care, or benefit.
5. **`supported_recording_description`** only when a finite, reproducible, gate-passing source-record vector has complete administrative horizons for the claimed source/window and the primary prognostic prerequisites above do not trigger a stronger branch. The label describes recorded availability only; it never means completed care, clinical outcome, utility, actionability, or benefit.

A primary positive observed gain plus a crossing all-BP envelope must remain `selection_sensitive`, regardless of favorable discharge or recorded-care fractions. A nonpositive primary gain remains `adverse_primary`, regardless of favorable source strata. Any invalid source or illicit semantic inference remains `unsupported`, regardless of all other outputs. A positive recording audit cannot change the prognostic estimand or label.
