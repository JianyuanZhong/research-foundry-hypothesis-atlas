# D3-M3: Data-Driven Pathway Activity Inference in RA Synovial Tissue

## Scientific Opening

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue exhibits dysregulated signaling pathways (NF-κB, JAK-STAT, MAPK) that drive inflammation and joint destruction. Standard pathway analysis assumes fixed pathway membership (e.g., a gene is either in the NF-κB pathway or not) and then measures the aggregate expression of member genes. However, pathway membership is context-dependent: a gene may participate in multiple pathways, and the functional relevance of a pathway may depend on the cell type, disease state, or microenvironment.

The D3-M3 expert seed asks: "Can pathway-level activity be inferred from gene expression without assuming a fixed pathway membership?" This is clinically consequential because if fixed-pathway enrichment misses context-specific pathway activity, then pathway-based interventions (e.g., JAK inhibitors) may be misdirected. Resolving this question changes the next mechanistic experiment: if data-driven inference reveals context-specific pathway activity not captured by fixed membership, then pathway-targeted therapies should be re-evaluated in the specific disease context.

## Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: In RA synovial tissue, data-driven pathway activity inference (using co-expression networks or pathway topology) reveals context-specific pathway activity that is not captured by fixed-pathway enrichment, and this context-specific activity is associated with disease severity or treatment response.

## Competing Explanations

1. **Fixed-pathway enrichment is sufficient**: The aggregate expression of fixed-pathway member genes adequately captures pathway activity, and data-driven inference adds no meaningful information.
2. **Context-specific pathway activity exists**: Pathway membership is context-dependent, and data-driven inference reveals activity patterns that fixed-pathway enrichment misses.
3. **Both are needed**: Fixed-pathway enrichment captures the core pathway activity, while data-driven inference captures context-specific modulation.

## Data Bindings

## Experiment Design

### Baseline: Fixed-Pathway Enrichment
- Use standard gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) or over-representation analysis with fixed pathway definitions (e.g., KEGG, Reactome).
- Measure aggregate expression of pathway member genes in each sample.
- Compare pathway activity between RA and OA, or between treatment responders and non-responders.

### Alternative: Data-Driven Pathway Inference
- Use co-expression network analysis (e.g., WGCNA) or pathway topology-based inference (e.g., PARADIGM, VIPER) to infer pathway activity from gene expression.
- Allow pathway membership to be data-driven: genes are assigned to pathways based on their co-expression patterns or topological position in the network.
- Compare the inferred pathway activity with fixed-pathway enrichment.

### Falsifiable Test
- If data-driven inference reveals context-specific pathway activity that is not captured by fixed-pathway enrichment, and this activity is associated with disease severity or treatment response, the hypothesis is supported.
- If fixed-pathway enrichment captures the same activity patterns as data-driven inference, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If data-driven inference reveals activity patterns that are not associated with clinical outcomes, the hypothesis is inconclusive.

## Method Comparison

| Aspect | Fixed-Pathway Enrichment | Data-Driven Pathway Inference |
|--------|--------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Pathway membership | Fixed (KEGG, Reactome) | Data-driven (co-expression, topology) |
| Computational cost | Low | Moderate (network inference) |
| Interpretability | High (known pathways) | Lower (inferred pathways) |
| Context-specificity | Low (fixed membership) | High (data-driven membership) |
| Clinical utility | Established | Emerging |

## Limitations

- The available data does not include direct pathway activity measurements (e.g., phosphoprotein assays), so pathway activity is inferred from gene expression.
- Data-driven pathway inference may be sensitive to the choice of network inference method and parameters.
- The association between inferred pathway activity and clinical outcomes is correlational, not causal.
- Additional data (e.g., phosphoprotein assays, perturbation experiments) would be needed to establish causality.
