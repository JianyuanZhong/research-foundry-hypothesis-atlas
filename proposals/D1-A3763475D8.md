# Is the "night-time ICU discharge" penalty an artefact of a mis-read clock?

Recovering hospital-local time in eICU-CRD and re-estimating the harm of out-of-hours ICU discharge

Dataset island: **eICU** (eICU Collaborative Research Database v2.0 snapshot
`[source checksum]`).
Scientific parent: expert seed `[starting question]` (board id `[prior hypothesis]`),
targeted repair. Transition context only (not ancestry): seed `[starting question]`.

---

## 1. The scientific opening

Discharging a patient from the ICU at night is one of the few timing decisions an intensivist
makes repeatedly and can actually change. A large body of work reports that out-of-hours ICU
discharge is associated with higher in-hospital mortality and ICU readmission [K1], and
guidelines and quality programmes have taken this seriously enough to discourage night transfers.
Two explanations have never been separated: (a) the night *environment* (thinner ward staffing,
handover, delayed response) causes harm; (b) night discharge is a *marker* of the patient and
the system — patients pushed out at night are those discharged while still unstable, or under bed
pressure. Nearly all of the observational evidence adjusts for severity at ICU **admission**, not
for the patient's physiological state in the hours **before** discharge, which is the variable
that actually distinguishes these explanations [K1].

While binding the seed to data I found a third possibility that no part of that literature, and
no part of the seed, anticipates: **the clock in the database may not be the clock in the ward.**

In eICU-CRD only one wall-clock field exists per stay (`patient.unitadmittime24`); every other
event is an integer minute offset from ICU admission, so any event's clock time is
`tod = (unitadmittime24 + offset) mod 1440`. Reading `patient.csv.gz` directly (200,859 stays,
208 hospitals) gives an ICU **admission** peak at 22:00-01:00 and a trough at 10:00-11:00, and an
ICU **discharge** distribution with 42% of all discharges in the stored window 19:00-23:00 and
64% in 19:00-06:59. No US ICU discharges two thirds of its patients between 7 pm and 7 am.
Full numbers, code and job provenance: `work/diagnostic-evidence.md`.

## 2. What the evidence already supports, and what remains unresolved

**Strongest claim the executed evidence already supports** (bounded diagnostic, this episode;
job `[research job]`, 39.1 M lab rows scanned):

The stored time-of-day field in eICU-CRD is *not* hospital-local clock time, and the deviation is
a near-constant, region-ordered offset consistent with a single reference clock.
- Routine scheduled panels (Hgb, WBC, platelets, creatinine, potassium; 0.72-0.98 M results each)
  show a strong single daily peak (resultant length R = 0.46-0.65) at **10:00-10:30 stored time**.
  Routine US ICU morning phlebotomy happens at ~04:00-05:00 local.
- Streams with no scheduled rhythm behave as negative controls: bedside glucose (2.19 M results,
  R = 0.07) and blood gases (R = 0.12) show essentially no daily peak, so the 10:00 peak is a real
  scheduled-care rhythm and not an artefact of the offset reconstruction.
- Per-hospital phase (194/206 hospitals with usable rhythm) spans only ~4.4 h (p5-p95
  8.73-13.10 h), **not** the ~uniform 24 h scatter a random per-site de-identification shift would
  produce, and it is ordered by `hospital.region`: Northeast median 10.06 h, South 9.64 h,
  Midwest 10.49 h, **West 12.21 h**; Kruskal-Wallis H = 105.2, p = 1.2e-22; Northeast vs West
  Mann-Whitney U = 0.0 (complete separation), p = 7.4e-08. `region` is external to the phase
  estimate, so this is a genuine out-of-sample prediction of the single-reference-clock
  explanation and is incompatible with both rivals.

**Unresolved claim this study tests** (three nested parts):

- **H1 (confirmatory, measurement).** Each hospital's offset `delta_h` is identifiable with stated
  uncertainty from several independent care-rhythm streams jointly, validated on a held-out stream
  and against `region`; the offsets cluster near a small number of values ~1 h apart spanning
  ~3-4 h. (The diagnostic above supports this; the study must establish it with uncertainty,
  held-out validation and negative controls before anything is built on it.)
- **H2 (does it matter).** The estimated effect of night-time ICU discharge changes materially
  when exposure is defined on recovered local time rather than on the stored field, and the
  risk-by-hour curve **aligns across hospitals on recovered local hour, not on stored hour**.
- **H3 (the seed's clinical question).** Under correctly phased exposure, the excess risk of
  night-time ICU discharge is concentrated among patients with residual physiological instability
  in the 8 h before ICU discharge, and is small or absent among patients who were physiologically
  stable at discharge.

These are falsifiable in different directions: H2 can fail (raw and corrected agree — reassuring
for the existing literature), H3 can fail (the penalty is uniform across stability strata, which
would favour an environmental rather than a triage mechanism), and H1 can fail (offsets not
identifiable in enough hospitals), each of which is an informative result.

## 3. Why this matters clinically, and what each outcome would change

- If **H2 is supported**: every eICU-based study that defined an off-hours exposure on
  `unitadmittime24`/`unitdischargetime24` — and eICU-CRD is among the most used multicentre ICU
  databases in the world — has been labelling late afternoon and evening local time as "night".
  This is measured, not asserted: in the eligible cohort of **114,958** index ICU stays discharged
  alive to a ward/step-down, a 22:00-06:59 window applied to the stored field versus to recovered
  local time classifies **38.0% of stays discordantly**; the stored field calls **39.5%** of
  discharges "night" while recovered local time calls **9.0%** of them night. The corrected
  prevalence is in the range reported for out-of-hours ICU discharge in the clinical literature;
  39.5% is not — an independent plausibility check that does not use `region`. Raw and corrected
  exposures are therefore not noisy versions of one another. The correction is cheap, reusable,
  and publishable as a derived per-hospital offset table other investigators can apply.
- If **H3 is supported**: the actionable quantity is not the clock but the patient. "Do not
  discharge at night" would be the wrong rule; "do not discharge an unstable patient, and the
  night is when that happens" would be the right one, with a concrete pre-discharge stability
  check. This directly addresses the decision the seed names.
- If **H3 is refuted** (penalty present and similar in stable patients under corrected local
  time), the environmental explanation is strengthened for exactly the local hours where ward
  staffing is thinnest — which raw-time analyses could not have identified — and the target
  becomes post-discharge monitoring rather than patient selection.
- **Negative/inconclusive results are informative**: agreement between raw and corrected exposure
  would validate a decade of eICU off-hours analyses; non-identifiability of phase in many
  hospitals would bound what this database can support about any time-of-day exposure.

## 4. Closest inspected work and the remaining gap

See `work/branch-lit/key-references.json` and `work/branch-lit/notes.md` for the three key works,
the inspected source bytes and their hashes, and the honest inspection limits (abstract-only
where that is all that was retrievable). In brief: [K1] is a systematic review and meta-analysis
of 18 cohorts and 1,191,178 patients (data 1994-2014) finding out-of-hours ICU discharge
associated with in-hospital mortality (RR 1.39, 95% CI 1.24-1.57) and ICU readmission (RR 1.30,
95% CI 1.19-1.42), and it states that whether this reflects "patient differences, differences in
care, or a combination remains unclear". Two features of it define our gap precisely. Its quality
assessment frames confounder adjustment in terms of *admission* severity, while it separately
notes that out-of-hours patients are sicker on their last ICU day and cites a cohort in which only
44.1% of night discharges were judged "fully ready" for discharge versus 86.3% by day — so the
pre-discharge-state adjustment our clinical arm supplies is a gap the review itself names and
could not pool. And none of its 18 cohorts faces our measurement problem, because each was a
single-timezone study using real local clock time; the review therefore bounds what the *literature*
says but cannot tell an eICU user whether their own exposure variable means the same thing.
[K2] establishes that care-process variables in EHRs
carry information about the health system rather than only the patient, which is the general
phenomenon of which a mis-phased clock is a particularly literal instance; [K3] is the eICU-CRD
resource/documentation evidence on how the timestamps were de-identified, which bounds what can
be asserted about the mechanism of the offset.

A specific and consequential **documented silence** was established by inspection, not assumed.
The resource paper states only that de-identification "include[s] the removal of all protected
health information (PHI), such as personal numbers (e.g. phone, social security), addresses,
dates, and ages over 89"; it says nothing about time of day or time zone. The official v2.0
documentation defines `unitAdmitTime24` as "time in 24 hour format of when the unit admit event
occurred e.g.: '12:45', '15:30', '3:45'" and states that "[t]he time stamp of all events are
stored as offsets from the time of ICU admission, in minutes", again with no time-zone statement;
release note #69 records that all other `time`/`time24`/`year` columns were removed as
"redundant" because they "can be easily rederived if needed using offsets and joining to the
patient table" — i.e. the documentation positively encourages the reconstruction
`tod = unitAdmitTime24 + offset` without ever saying which clock it is on. A user therefore has
no warning. Frozen inspected bytes: `work/branch-lit/` and
`work/lead-refs/K3-eicu-crd-pollard2018-excerpt.txt`.

The silence has already propagated into published practice, which is why this is a live problem
rather than a pedantic one. One inspected example (frozen at
`work/branch-lit/extra-eicu-nocturnal-diurnal-hr-index-2024.txt`) is a peer-reviewed eICU-CRD
study of 32,412 patients that defines night as 22:00-06:00 **on exactly this reconstructed clock**
and interprets the resulting contrast as circadian biology, with no mention of time zone. If H1
holds, that window is not night in any patient's body or ward, and the same applies to any eICU
analysis of time-of-day exposure. The purpose of this study is to establish, one way or the other,
whether that whole class of results is interpretable.

No inspected work recovers hospital-local clock phase in a de-identified multicentre ICU database
or re-estimates an off-hours effect against it. Europe PMC title/abstract searches run in this
episode returned no methodological study of clock-time validity or time-zone normalisation in
eICU-CRD or a comparable multicentre ICU database; that is reported as "not found in these
searches", which is not proof of absence, and this is not an exhaustive novelty search.

Power bound on the discriminating test, measured: 25 of the 194 hospitals with a usable phase
estimate (12.9%) have a missing `hospital.region`, so they contribute to the phase model but not
to the region test. The region test therefore rests on 169 hospitals.

## 6. Experiment

### 6.1 Arm A — recovering local clock phase

*Baseline (simple).* Per hospital, build 30-min histograms of the reconstructed `tod` of the
routine scheduled panel draws; estimate `delta_h` as the circular mean minus an assumed local
anchor hour; report the sensitivity of everything downstream to the assumed anchor hour over
03:00-06:00.

*Substantive alternative (fitted).* A hierarchical latent-phase Poisson model: for hospital `h`,
stream `s`, 30-min bin `b`, counts `y_{hsb} ~ Poisson(exp(a_{hs} + f_s(b - delta_h)))` where `f_s`
is a shared population circadian shape per stream in a low-order Fourier basis, `a_{hs}` absorbs
hospital-stream volume, and `delta_h` is a circular hospital offset with a weakly informative
prior; identifiability fixed by constraining the volume-weighted mean `delta_h` to 0, so only
*relative* offsets are claimed. Fit by maximum likelihood (Adam or L-BFGS on the circular
parameterisation), multiple restarts against local optima. Streams: routine panels, scheduled
oral medication administration (`frequency` matching QDAY/Q6H/Q8H/Q12H), care-plan charting,
treatment orders. What the alternative recovers that the baseline loses: a per-hospital
uncertainty interval on `delta_h`; separation of phase from amplitude/volume so that case-mix
differences do not masquerade as phase; joint use of several streams so a single stream's local
practice idiosyncrasy does not drive the estimate; and the ability to detect multi-modal or
non-integer offsets, which would falsify a pure time-zone explanation.

*The decisive extension: a daylight-saving mixture test (M-DST).* This is the single strongest
discriminator available and the baseline cannot perform it at all. eICU-CRD v2.0 covers 2014-2015.
If stored times are on one reference clock while local clocks observe DST, then each
DST-observing hospital's events are necessarily a mixture of **two phases exactly one hour apart**,
with mixture weight equal to the DST fraction of the sampling year — 2014 (Mar 9 - Nov 2) and 2015
(Mar 8 - Nov 1) both give 238/365 = **0.652**. So we fit

    lambda_{hsb} = exp(a_{hs}) * [ pi_h * exp(f_s(t_b - delta_h)) + (1-pi_h) * exp(f_s(t_b - delta_h - 1)) ]

with `pi_h` estimated per hospital. The pre-specified predictions are quantitative and sharp:
pooled `pi_hat` should concentrate near 0.65, and the **non-DST US states (Arizona, Hawaii) should
appear as a `pi_hat ~ 0 or 1` subgroup inside `region='West'`**. Nothing about a random per-site
de-identification shift predicts a 1 h bimodality, still less one with weight 0.65 and a non-DST
subgroup in the correct region. A fixed-anchor circular mean silently collapses such a mixture to
its mean — returning a value no clock ever showed — which is precisely why the fitted alternative
is scientifically necessary here rather than decorative. **Criterion**: supportive if
`AIC(M-DST) - AIC(M-free) < -20` with pooled `pi_hat` in [0.55, 0.75]; adverse if the AIC
improvement is <5 or `pi_hat` falls outside [0.4, 0.85]; inconclusive between.

*Validation not reusing fitting information.*
(i) **Held-out event stream**: physician/progress notes (`note.noteenteredoffset`) are excluded
from fitting entirely; after the fit the corrected pooled note phase must fall in local 08:00-14:00
(attending rounds) **and** the between-hospital SD of the corrected note phase must be at least
1.5x smaller than the uncorrected SD — the correction must actually *align* an independent stream,
not merely shift it.
(ii) **A labelled local-clock ground truth, discovered in this episode.** Scanning all 7,301,853
rows of `medication.csv.gz`, 57,853 rows (0.79%) state the intended local administration hour
explicitly inside `frequency`: `DAILY (0900)` 38,542; `QHS (2100)` 10,757; `DAILY (1700)` 4,881;
`DAILY (0600)` 1,860; `DAILY (0900) Routine` 1,813 — four distinct labelled hours. They come from
only **3 hospitals: 420 (51,420 rows), 424 (3,978), 421 (2,455)**, each with >=2 distinct labelled
hours. For those three sites `delta_label = circmean(reconstructed tod) - stated local hour` is a
ground-truth offset that requires **no physiological anchor assumption whatsoever**, and having >=2
labelled hours per site provides an internal consistency check: the implied offsets from different
labelled hours must agree within 30 min, or `drugstartoffset` is not administration time and the
anchor is void. These rows are excluded from the fitting stream. **Criterion**: pass if
`|delta_hat_h - delta_label_h| <= 45 min` for all three; fail if any exceeds 90 min. Honest limit:
n = 3 hospitals, plausibly one EHR configuration — this validates the *estimator*, not the
*population* of offsets, and cannot calibrate the other 205 sites.
(iii) **Negative controls**, now four rather than one: bedside glucose and blood gases must stay
near-arrhythmic (already measured at R=0.07 and R=0.12); blood cultures
(`microLab.culturetakenoffset`) must show R<0.15 and must not be phase-locked to `delta_h`
(circular correlation |rho|<0.2) — if they are, the "phase" is a nightly batch/upload rhythm and
**the whole Arm A interpretation is void**; a within-hospital permutation of `unitadmittime24` must
collapse the refitted `delta_h` to between-hospital SD <15 min; and troponin must show
`R < 0.5 x R(routine panels)`, since equal amplitude would indicate specimens are timestamped at
analyser run time rather than draw time, shifting the meaning of the anchor.

*Pre-specification status — stated honestly, because it limits what Arm A can claim.* The
region-ordering result in `work/diagnostic-evidence.md` §3 was computed by me during this episode
**before** the plan was written, so it is motivating evidence that has already been seen, **not** a
pre-specified confirmatory test, and it is reported as such. The confirmatory discriminators are
the ones nobody has looked at: M-DST (A4), the labelled-hour validation (ii), the held-out note
stream (i), and the quantitative slope of `delta_h` on each region's composition-weighted expected
UTC offset (slope 1 predicted by the reference-clock explanation, 0 by the uniform-shift rival).
A proposal that presented the already-seen region p-value as its pre-specified test would be
overstating its own evidence.

*Pre-specified criteria.* **Supportive of a reference-clock offset**: >=80% of region-labelled
hospitals lie within +/-20 min of one of <=5 circular cluster centres whose total spread is <=3.5 h;
the Jonckheere-Terpstra trend across the a priori order Northeast < South < Midwest < West has
p<0.01 (descriptive, see above); the West-minus-Northeast contrast is in [+1.5, +3.5] h with a CI
excluding 0; M-DST is supportive by the criterion above; and all four negative controls are clean.
**Adverse**: `delta_h` not distinguishable from uniform (Rayleigh p>0.05) with <=60% of hospitals
near any <=5 centres and no DST improvement — i.e. a random per-site shift, in which case the
stored field is uninterpretable as any clock and Arm B is reported under raw time with that
explicitly stated. Separately adverse to the *mechanism* but not to the *correction*: clustering at
<=5 centres with the right spread but no region ordering and slope ~0, which we pre-commit to
reporting as *"systematic but non-geographic"* — a vendor or feed timestamp convention — which
still justifies phase correction while refuting the time-zone explanation. **Inconclusive**: <60%
of hospitals identifiable, median SE(`delta_h`) >45 min, the held-out note stream passing while the
labelled-hour check fails, or the UTC-offset slope intermediate between 0 and 1.

### 6.2 Arm B — the clinical estimand

*Population.* All ICU stays in eICU-CRD v2.0 with: adult age (`age` is a string; '> 89' recoded
to 90); `unitdischargestatus` = 'Alive'; `unitdischargelocation` in {Floor (93,121),
Acute Care/Floor (20,279), Step-Down Unit (SDU) (26,945), Telemetry (10,995)} — i.e. discharge to
a lower level of care inside the same hospital, excluding Home, Other Hospital, Other ICU, Death,
SNF/rehab; ICU length of stay >=8 h so a pre-discharge window exists; hospital with an
identifiable `delta_h`; at least one vital-sign observation in the final 8 h. Index ICU stay =
the first ICU stay of a `patienthealthsystemstayid` (later stays are outcomes, not exposures).
Treatment limitation is handled as a **covariate, not an exclusion**, and this reverses the
intuitive choice for a measured reason (section 5): only 11.35% of eligible stays carry a
restrictive `carePlanGeneral` value, 80.20% are documented `Full therapy` only, and 8.45% have no
Care-Limitation row at all, with per-hospital group documentation ranging from 6.8% to 100% and the
restrictive rate from 0.0% to 43.1%. Absence of a restrictive entry therefore does **not** mean
full code, so a "full-therapy-only" primary cohort would select on site documentation habit rather
than on patient intent, and would do so differentially across the 208 hospitals. The primary
analysis instead retains all eligible stays with limitation status entered as a three-level
covariate (restrictive / `Full therapy` documented / no documentation) and as a pre-specified
effect modifier, with hospital fixed effects absorbing the site-level documentation propensity. The
restricted full-therapy cohort becomes the sensitivity analysis. A direct diagnostic is also
pre-specified, since it bears on the interpretation either way: the local-hour distribution of
`cplitemoffset` for Care-Limitation entries, tested for a circadian pattern — if limitation
documentation is itself strongly hour-dependent, that constrains any mortality interpretation and
is reported as a measurement finding in its own right.

*Temporal boundaries.* Time origin = `unitdischargeoffset` of the index ICU stay. Covariates use
only data with offset <= that origin (final 8 h window for physiology, whole stay for severity
and comorbidity). Follow-up to `hospitaldischargeoffset`.

*Exposure.* Local discharge hour = `(unitdischargetime24 - delta_h) mod 24`. Primary binary:
night = local 22:00-06:59. Secondary: continuous circular hour via a periodic spline. Comparator
specification: the identical window applied to the raw stored field.

*Outcomes.* (1) In-hospital death after ICU discharge (`hospitaldischargestatus` = 'Expired');
measured baseline risk 4.13% in the 151,026-stay eligible set. (2) **Unplanned** ICU readmission
within the same `patienthealthsystemstayid`. **This definition was substantively corrected by a
measurement made in this episode and the naive version must not be used.** "Any later unit stay in
the same hospitalisation" captures 27,445/151,026 = 18.17%, but the gap distribution between index
ICU discharge and the next unit admission (computed on the absolute within-hospitalisation timeline
`T = -hospitaladmitoffset + offset`) is: `<=0 min` 18,364; `1-59` 449; `60-239` 280; `240-1439`
1,897; `1440-4319` 2,788; `>=4320` 3,667. **Two thirds of the naive "readmissions" have a zero
minute gap and are contiguous unit transfers** (unit relabelling, MICU-to-SICU moves), not
discharge failures. Requiring gap >= 240 min gives 8,352 (5.53%), of whose next stays 7,744 (92.7%)
are independently coded `unitstaytype='readmit'` — convergent validation from a field not used in
the definition. Further excluding next stays arriving from `unitadmitsource` in {Operating Room
(1,159), Recovery Room (352), PACU (72)}, which are *planned* post-operative ICU returns, gives
**6,769 unplanned readmissions (4.48%)** — the primary readmission estimand. Pre-specified
secondary windows: gap in [240 min, 72 h], and any gap >= 60 min (5.72%). The crude 18%-style
figure in `work/diagnostic-evidence.md` §6 is superseded by this and was already labelled there as
deliberately crude and not the proposal's outcome. (3) Composite of (1) or (2), expected ~8.0%.
Death before readmission is handled as a competing risk for
outcome (2).

*Confounders measured before discharge.* Age, sex, ethnicity, unit type, admission source,
`apacheadmissiondx` group, APACHE IV/IVa `acutephysiologyscore` and `predictedhospitalmortality`,
ICU length of stay, last-8-h summaries (min/max/last/variability of heart rate, systolic and mean
arterial pressure, SpO2, respiratory rate, temperature; last GCS), last-24-h labs (creatinine,
WBC, haemoglobin, platelets, sodium, lactate where present), ongoing organ support at the time of
discharge (vasopressor infusion running, mechanical ventilation, dialysis), and hospital identity.

*Estimators.* Baseline: hospital-stratified (fixed-effect) logistic regression, plus conditional
logistic within hospital, so all comparisons are within-hospital and hospital-level confounding
(including case-mix, region and phase) is differenced out. Substantive alternative: augmented
inverse-probability weighting with gradient-boosted propensity and outcome models (within-hospital
propensity for night discharge), reporting a risk difference; plus a hospital random-effects
model with a periodic spline on local hour. What the flexible version recovers that a binary
indicator loses: the *shape* of risk over the 24 h cycle — whether risk rises at shift change,
in the small hours, or monotonically with lateness — which is the thing that distinguishes an
environmental from a triage mechanism and which a dichotomy destroys.

*The alignment test (the sharpest discrimination, and robust to the unknown absolute offset).*
Fit two otherwise identical models, risk ~ periodic spline(stored hour) + hospital fixed effects
versus risk ~ periodic spline(local hour) + hospital fixed effects, with equal degrees of freedom,
and compare cross-validated log-likelihood with hospitals held out. If discharge-hour risk is a
real circadian/staffing phenomenon, hospitals' risk curves must align on *local* hour; if the
stored field were already local, they must align on stored hour; if the apparent effect is
triage confounding, neither alignment should survive adjustment for pre-discharge instability.
Pre-specified: local-hour model better by >=10 AIC units and a positive cross-validated
log-likelihood difference whose bootstrap 95% interval excludes 0.

*The seed's hypothesis as a pre-specified interaction.* A residual-instability index computed
from the final 8 h (pre-specified components and weights fixed before outcome analysis; an
ordered 3-level stratum: stable / borderline / unstable). Primary interaction test: night x
instability on the additive (risk-difference) scale, reported with its CI, plus stratum-specific
effects. This is pre-specified, not a subgroup search.

*Measured cohort and precision.* The eligibility criteria above were executed against
`patient.csv.gz` in this episode: **114,958** index ICU stays qualify (599 of them in hospitals
with no usable phase estimate). Corrected-local-time night exposure applies to 9.0% (10,256
stays) versus 39.5% (45,173) under the stored field. Crude in-hospital mortality after ICU
discharge in the cohort is 3.7-5.5% depending on exposure arm (see
`work/diagnostic-evidence.md` §6 — recorded there as an explicitly exploratory, unadjusted peek,
not as the study result). With ~10^5 stays, ~4% events and ~9% exposure prevalence the main
effect is resolvable to roughly a few tenths of a percentage point in absolute risk; the
three-level instability interaction is materially less precise, and the exact pre-specified
minimum detectable difference is computed in `work/branch-design/analysis-plan.md`. An
interaction that is merely imprecise must be reported as inconclusive, not as a null.

*Pre-registration hygiene.* The exploratory peek above has been recorded in writing rather than
hidden. It is unadjusted, ignores hospital structure, and uses a deliberately crude readmission
definition that is **not** the proposal's outcome. The confirmatory analysis stands as specified;
the solver must report the pre-specified estimators and must not retune the exposure window,
instability index or outcome definition after seeing adjusted results.

### 6.2b Power, computed from the measured cohort rather than assumed

Analytic calculations on the observed cohort sizes and observed baseline risks (not simulations).
N = 151,026 eligible stays across 208 hospitals; a conservative analysable N of 130,000 is
bracketed after the index-stay and LOS filters. Two-sided alpha 0.05, 80% power (z = 2.802),
`SE(RD) = sqrt(p0(1-p0)(1/n_e + 1/n_u))`. At the corrected-clock night fraction of 0.12
(n_e = 18,123, n_u = 132,903):

| outcome | measured baseline risk | MDE (absolute RD) | equivalent OR | after 1.2x clustering inflation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| in-hospital death after ICU discharge | 4.13% | 0.44 pp | 1.107 | 0.53 pp / OR 1.134 |
| unplanned ICU readmission | 4.48% | 0.46 pp | 1.107 | 0.55 pp / OR 1.129 |
| composite | ~8.0% | 0.60 pp | 1.082 | 0.72 pp / OR 1.099 |

Two consequences matter for honest interpretation. First, the **raw-clock analysis is nominally
*more* precise** (night fraction 0.41 → MDE 0.29 pp, OR 1.070) purely because the mis-defined
exposure is more balanced: a precision gain bought entirely with misclassification, which is a
compact illustration of why a narrower confidence interval is not a better estimate. Second, the
key comparison — the ratio `OR_corrected / OR_raw` — has `SE(log OR)` of 0.0398 corrected and
0.0263 raw; at a plausible correlation of 0.3 between the two estimates (same patients, overlapping
exposure definitions), `SE(log ratio) ≈ 0.041`, so the ratio resolves to about **±8%**, or **±10%**
after clustering inflation. The pre-specified 15% materiality threshold for H2 is therefore
detectable — the study can actually answer its own central measurement question.

The interaction is the weakest link and this is stated in advance. Assuming 25% of the cohort is
UNSTABLE (to be computed, not assumed, in the final report), with baseline mortality 8% unstable
versus 2.8% stable, `SE` of the risk-difference contrast is 0.455 pp, so the **minimum detectable
RD-scale interaction is 1.28 pp**. If the seed's true effect modification is below ~1 pp the study
will be *inconclusive about the seed's central claim even while being conclusive about the
measurement question*. That asymmetry is declared now rather than discovered afterwards.

### 6.3 Falsification summary

| result | reading |
|---|---|
| H1 supported, H2 supported (OR changes materially, local-hour alignment wins) | The off-hours exposure in this database has been misclassified; corrected estimates and the released `delta_h` table supersede raw-time analyses |
| H1 supported, H2 not supported (estimates agree within the pre-specified margin) | Reassuring: existing eICU off-hours findings are robust to the clock; report as a validated negative |
| H1 supported, H3 supported | Risk is carried by residual instability; the clinical lever is a pre-discharge stability check, not the clock |
| H1 supported, H3 refuted (uniform across strata) | Favours an environmental/staffing mechanism at true local night; motivates a monitoring-intensity study we cannot do here (no staffing or occupancy data) |
| H1 adverse (offsets uniform / no region ordering) | The field may be local; Arm B proceeds on raw time and the measurement claim is retracted |
| Wide intervals throughout | Inconclusive; state the precision achieved and do not convert it into a null |

### 6.4 What is computationally checkable versus what is not

Checkable by the solver: all phase estimates and their validation; cohort construction and
coverage; every effect estimate, interaction and the alignment test; calibration and competing-risk
handling. **Not** checkable here and requiring other evidence: that the reference clock is exactly
UTC (needs the data provider or site metadata); that any excess risk is *caused* by night-time
ward conditions (needs staffing/occupancy/rapid-response data, or a trial of discharge timing);
clinical adjudication of whether an ICU readmission was avoidable; and whether a pre-discharge
stability rule would improve outcomes if deployed (needs prospective evaluation).

## 7. Alternative explanations considered

1. *The anchor assumption is wrong* (routine draws are not at 04:00-05:00 local). Addressed by
   making the primary clinical test depend only on *relative* offsets (the alignment test and the
   within-hospital fixed-effect design), and by reporting sensitivity over anchor hours 03:00-06:00.
2. *Random per-site de-identification shift.* Predicts uniform scatter of `delta_h` and no region
   ordering; the diagnostic observed a 4.4 h span and p=1.2e-22 region ordering.
3. *Genuinely nocturnal local activity.* Predicts no region ordering and would place routine panels
   at a plausible local hour; both fail.
3b. *A single uniform shift applied to every hospital, plus a real regional difference in local
   draw practice.* This is the strongest surviving rival to the reference-clock explanation and the
   region-ordering test alone does **not** exclude it: a uniform additive shift preserves whatever
   ordering exists in local practice. It is separated quantitatively rather than ordinally. Under
   the reference-clock explanation the region contrasts must match the *known* UTC offsets of those
   regions in magnitude: Pacific minus Eastern = 3 h, Central minus Eastern = 1 h, Mountain minus
   Eastern = 2 h. The observed West-minus-Northeast median contrast is 2.15 h and West-minus-South
   2.57 h, which is close to the 3 h and ~2 h predicted by census-region time-zone composition
   (census regions are not time zones: the South spans Eastern and Central, the West spans Pacific
   and Mountain, so attenuation toward the composition-weighted mean is expected and the predicted
   contrasts are bounds, not point values). Under the rival, the magnitudes have no reason to
   coincide with time-zone differences. The pre-specified test is a regression of `delta_h` on the
   state-composition-weighted expected UTC offset of each census region, with slope 1 predicted by
   the reference-clock explanation and slope 0 by the rival; a fitted slope near 1 with intercept
   near the anchor hour supports H1, a slope indistinguishable from 0 refutes it, and an
   intermediate slope is inconclusive and would be reported as such. Residual limitation: the
   mapping from census region to expected UTC offset is itself approximate because hospital states
   are not released, so this test bounds rather than identifies the mechanism.
4. *Confounding by indication / triage* for Arm B. The main rival to an environmental effect;
   addressed by pre-discharge physiology adjustment, the pre-specified instability interaction, and
   within-hospital comparison. Residual confounding by unmeasured clinician judgement remains and
   is stated as a limit, not solved.
5. *Reverse causation / sickest-kept-longest.* Time origin fixed at discharge; only pre-discharge
   information used.
6. *Differential documentation.* Treatment-limitation documentation and discharge-location coding
   may vary by hour and hospital. Handled **without** exclusion, per §6.2: limitation status enters
   as a three-level covariate (restrictive / `Full therapy` documented / no documentation) and as a
   pre-specified effect modifier, hospital fixed effects absorb site-level documentation propensity,
   the restricted full-therapy cohort is a sensitivity analysis only, and the circadian diagnostic
   on `carePlanGeneral.cplitemoffset` tests directly whether this documentation is itself
   hour-dependent. This is the one threat on this list that the design cannot fully neutralise: if
   that diagnostic shows strongly hour-dependent limitation documentation, the Arm B mortality
   contrast is partly confounded by a care-process artefact in a way adjustment cannot repair, and
   the honest report is a bounded Arm A measurement result with an explicitly compromised Arm B.
7. *Competing risk.* Death precluding readmission handled explicitly.
8. *Phase-estimation error.* Non-differential with respect to outcome, so it biases the corrected
   estimate toward the raw-time estimate and toward the null — meaning H2 is conservative:
   observing a difference is harder than not observing one.

## 8. Method selection record (baseline vs learned alternative)

Both arms specify a simple baseline and a substantive fitted alternative on the *same* question,
at matching specificity (sections 6.1 and 6.2). Device reasoning: the latent-phase model has
~208 circular parameters, a handful of shared Fourier shapes and <=6 streams x 48 bins per
hospital — a few thousand parameters on aggregate counts, seconds to minutes on CPU; the outcome
models are tabular on ~10^5 rows, which is also CPU work. GPU is therefore **not** requested
because the workload does not need it, not because neural methods are excluded. One alternative is
explicitly **deferred, not rejected**: a sequence model over the raw pre-discharge vital-sign
trajectory (vitalPeriodic is ~1.8 GB compressed at ~5-minute resolution) as a richer
residual-instability representation than hand-built summaries; a GPU allocation would be
appropriate for that. It is deferred because the scientific question here is whether the clock
and the instability stratum change the estimate, and a learned instability representation would
confound that comparison with a representation-quality comparison. It should be revisited if the
pre-specified hand-built instability index shows poor discrimination (e.g. c-statistic <0.65 for
the composite outcome) — in that case the interaction test would be underpowered by measurement
error rather than by biology.

## 9. Disposition of the three expert research demonstrations

- *Delphi / generative transformers for disease natural history* (Nature 2025): not adopted. The
  bounded adaptation would be a learned sequence representation of ICU trajectories; the missing
  dependency here is not compute but relevance — eICU has no calendar time, no post-discharge
  follow-up and a single hospitalisation per patient, so learned long-horizon disease-history
  ordering has nothing to order. Recorded as the deferred alternative in section 8 in its one
  genuinely applicable form (within-stay vital-sign sequences).
- *ALADYNOULLI / Bayesian longitudinal latent structure* (Nature 2026): partially adopted in
  spirit. The hierarchical latent-phase model in 6.1 is a small, honest instance of fitting a
  latent longitudinal structure with shared shapes and site-specific parameters. The paper's
  genetic arm has no counterpart in this snapshot (no genotypes), which is stated rather than
  worked around.
- *Oncoformer / multimodal longitudinal clinical data* (Cell 2026): not adopted. The required
  imaging modality is absent from the eICU snapshot, and the accessible local material is the
  supplement only; no claim is made about its unavailable main text or STAR Methods.
