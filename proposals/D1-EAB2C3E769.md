# Hospital heterogeneity in persistence of the documented hypotension–creatinine signal

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly construct an auditable eICU six-hour landmark cohort and 30-minute event ledger, fit two matched process models, and produce uncertainty-quantified estimates of one new primary quantity:

> the hospital 90th-minus-10th percentile spread in the standardized 48-hour cumulative incidence of a distinct later recorded creatinine confirmation after a first qualifying recorded rise.

This is a hospital-practice/observation estimand. It asks whether a “persistent” recorded signal is comparable across ICUs or depends materially on local repeat-testing and unit-exit processes. Completion requires the hospital-specific standardized confirmation CIFs, the primary spread and interval, its decomposition into repeat-test opportunity, confirmation after a repeat test, and death/alive/unknown-status unit exit, plus the parent’s paired any-rise/confirmed-rise documented-timing contrast, held-out-hospital transport and falsification sensitivities. No result has been fitted and no clinical conclusion is claimed in discovery.

The advance over the parent is not another predictive model: it makes portability of the persistence endpoint itself a prespecified estimand. A large spread would tell clinicians and future trialists that a recorded confirmation is not a site-neutral signal without surveillance-process calibration. A small spread after process adjustment would support use of the recorded endpoint as a more transportable observational marker. Neither result establishes biological AKI, treatment benefit/harm, or a pressor effect.

## Evidence-supported claim and unresolved question

The evidence inspected supports data availability and observation semantics only. The frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains ICU-relative MAP observations, ICU-keyed lab results and revision offsets, named infusion rows, patient ICU disposition and hospital identifiers. The direct schema files show the exact fields below. A bounded read-only probe ([research job]) confirmed the raw headers, the presence of creatinine rows and mg/dL values in lab measurement-system/interface fields, Alive/Expired/blank unit discharge statuses, and named pressor strings. The probe was not a prevalence estimate and did not fit a model.

The official eICU patient documentation defines unitdischargeoffset as minutes from the specific unit admission to unit discharge; all event offsets are ICU-relative and unit admission is offset 0. It documents unitdischargestatus as Alive, Expired, or NULL. Thus a NULL status means condition at departure is undocumented; it is not evidence of an ongoing stay and must not be recoded as alive. The primary analysis treats unit discharge as the end of observability, uses status Expired for recorded unit death, status Alive for alive exit, and treats non-expired NULL status as an unknown-status administrative censor. A sensitivity uses unitdischargelocation=Death when status is missing, but does not silently resolve disagreements.

The unresolved clinical-process claim is:

> After standardizing patient severity, low-MAP history, first-rise characteristics, and prior testing opportunity, hospitals differ materially in the probability that a first recorded creatinine rise receives a distinct later qualifying recorded confirmation within 48 hours, and part of the apparent spread is explained by repeat-testing and unit-exit practice.

Primary hypothesis: the development-hospital 90th-minus-10th percentile spread H90-10 in that standardized confirmation CIF is at least 5 percentage points, with a two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval whose lower bound is above zero, and remains at least half of the unadjusted spread after the repeat-test and competing-exit processes are represented. The 5-point threshold is a prespecified clinical-scale criterion for a site-comparability concern, not a claim that any treatment decision follows from it.

This is not the claim that hospitals differ in true AKI, creatinine biology, or pressor efficacy. A first rise and its confirmation are observed documentation states. The strongest available evidence cannot distinguish renal injury, hemoconcentration, assay variation, or undocumented testing from the recorded process.

## Population and ICU observation boundary

Use eICU snapshot [source checksum]. The unit is patientunitstayid; repeated ICU/unit stays remain separate. uniquepid is used only for a clustered sensitivity and connected-component split check, never to concatenate stays.

Include:

- adults with patient.age >=18, parsing > 89 as 90 and retaining an age-censored flag;
- at least two valid raw vitalPeriodic.systemicmean observations in minutes 0–360, restricted to 20–200 mmHg;
- a valid baseline creatinine at or before the first raw low MAP;
- a first raw periodic systemicmean <65 in [0,360];
- no qualifying baseline-plus-0.3 mg/dL creatinine rise through minute 360; and
- unitdischargeoffset >360.

The last condition means the stay is observable beyond the landmark. It is not a claim that it remains in the ICU for 48 hours. Let U = unitdischargeoffset, E = min(2880, U). Source observations at offset t are eligible only while t < U; if U > 2880, an event at exactly 2880 is eligible, while if U <= 2880, discharge at U takes precedence over a same-minute measurement. No result after unit discharge is used. At U, status Expired is a death competing event, status Alive is an alive unit-exit competing event, and NULL is unknown-status censoring. unitdischargelocation is retained as destination context and is not used to infer survival except in the prespecified location sensitivity.

This interpretation follows the documented meaning of unit discharge, not hospital discharge. Transfer to floor, telemetry, another ICU, another hospital or another location ends this unit’s observation. hospitaldischargeoffset is not substituted for the ICU boundary. A patient may have later unit stays, but those are not appended to the index stay.

## Landmark variables and recorded timing

### MAP ledger

Use raw vitalPeriodic observations for the primary ledger. Deduplicate exact vitalperiodicid; if multiple valid rows remain at a stay/time, use their median and report the count. Construct the 0–360 minute ledger with no interpolation as a sensitivity and primary trapezoidal area-under-65 burden with only gaps <=30 minutes bridged. Report raw and bridged minutes, gap and coverage, minimum MAP, number and duration of low-MAP runs/episodes, recovery time and slope. The first low and all pressor timing categories use raw observation offsets, not interpolated values. vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean is a separate non-invasive sensitivity and is never pooled with periodic MAP.

The primary burden is the trapezoidal integral of max(65 - MAP, 0) over the 0–360 ledger, in mmHg-min, with the stated gap rule. The scientific comparison is standardized P25 versus P75 burden within the observed support; the burden is not a treatment dose or a MAP target.

### Baseline creatinine and units

Use case-folded lab.labname=creatinine, numeric labresult in [0,60], and explicit unit verification from the actual available fields labmeasurenamesystem and labmeasurenameinterface. The primary rule requires both nonblank fields to normalize to mg/dL; conflicting or non-mg/dL rows are excluded. A sensitivity accepts one explicit mg/dL field when the other is blank, without converting mmol/L. This corrects the common shorthand “unit fields”: the table has measurement-system/interface fields, not separately named unit columns.

The baseline is the earliest qualifying creatinine in [0,60] at the smallest labresultoffset, not the minimum value. Deduplicate exact labid; if multiple valid rows remain at the selected offset, use their median and report count. A rise is the first eligible post-landmark result at least 0.3 mg/dL above this baseline. Exclude urinary creatinine and invalid numeric/unit rows. The early-injury exclusion uses all eligible qualifying creatinine results through offset 360, including the baseline; no post-landmark result is allowed into the baseline or exclusion.

### Documented pressor timing

In infusionDrug, define the first target-pressor record as the smallest raw infusionoffset in [0,360] whose case-insensitive drugname matches norepinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine or epinephrine. Categories are:

1. pre-low/concurrent: record offset <= first raw low-MAP offset;
2. prompt-after-low: > first low and <=30 minutes;
3. delayed-after-low: >30 through 360 minutes; and
4. no target-pressor record by 360.

Same-minute MAP and infusion record is pre-low/concurrent. drugrate, infusionrate, drugamount, volumeoffluid and patientweight are audit fields. A row is a documented record at an offset, not validated administration, start, dose, indication, intent or complete exposure. A positive-rate and a narrower agent-name map are sensitivities; no pressor category is interpreted causally.

## Recorded confirmation states

Follow from >360 until E, subject to the strict t < U rule. A qualifying result is a valid primary-unit creatinine row after the landmark and before unit discharge. A first rise occurs at the first qualifying result r with value >= baseline+0.3.

A confirmed recorded rise requires a second qualifying creatinine result s with:

- a distinct labid from the first-rise row;
- s >= r + 360 minutes;
- value >= baseline+0.3 mg/dL; and
- s <= 2880 and s < U when the unit discharges by that boundary.

A same-minute row, exact duplicate, revised version of the same labid, or result less than six hours after the first rise cannot confirm. The primary event clock is labresultoffset; rerun with labresultrevisedoffset where present. A first rise that never confirms remains non-confirmed; it is not treated as latent injury. Confirmation is a persistence-of-record endpoint, not KDIGO AKI.

For process accounting, represent:

- no qualifying test;
- qualifying test without a rise;
- first recorded rise;
- repeat qualifying test after first rise;
- confirmed recorded rise;
- recorded unit death;
- recorded alive unit exit; and
- unknown-status unit discharge censoring.

The six-hour confirmation delay means no confirmation can occur before r + 360. The model must preserve that structural delay and must not count a discharge-boundary row as a lab event.

## Primary and retained estimands

### New primary hospital-practice estimand

Let R_h be the first-rise risk set in development hospital h: cohort stays with a first qualifying rise before the observation boundary. For each supported hospital, estimate q_h, the 48-hour CIF of a confirmed recorded rise after that first rise, with recorded unit death and alive unit exit as competing events and unknown-status discharge as censoring. The horizon is min(r + 2880, U), with no event after U.

Standardize each q_h to the same development risk-set distribution of:

- age, sex, ethnicity, unit/admission source and unit type;
- baseline creatinine, first-rise value and delta;
- first-low MAP, burden, coverage, low-MAP runs and recovery;
- time since the previous qualifying creatinine and number of qualifying tests before the first rise;
- documented pressor timing;
- admission acutephysiologyscore, apachescore and predictedicumortality; and
- hospital bed category, teaching status and region as context, without using hospital identity in standardization.

For hospitals with at least 50 first-rise risk-set stays and 10 confirmed events, define:

H90-10 = Q90(q_h) - Q10(q_h).

The quantiles are across supported development hospitals; the rule and support threshold are frozen before outcome inspection. Also report the raw hospital spread and process components: the 48-hour CIF of a repeat qualifying creatinine after first rise and the confirmation probability among repeat-test transitions, each with death/alive/unknown exit handling. Those components explain the primary spread; they are not additional primary hypotheses. If fewer than 10 supported hospitals remain, H90-10 is inconclusive.

The paired process question is whether the spread is mainly an opportunity phenomenon. Let H_repeat and H_confirm_given_test be the analogous hospital spreads from the repeat-test and repeat-test-to-confirmation transitions. The solver must report these alongside H90-10, not multiply unstable ratios. “At least half remains after process accounting” means the standardized confirmation spread estimated by the full ordered model is >=0.5 times the transparent model’s primary spread, with adequate support; a fractional statement is not interpreted if the baseline interval includes zero.

### Parent estimand retained as secondary

Retain the parent’s standardized 48-hour CIF burden contrast for any observed rise and for confirmed recorded rise by documented pressor timing. For endpoint e in {any, confirmed}, let G_h,c,e be the hospital-standardized CIF difference for MAP-burden P75 versus P25 in timing category (c). Report:

[
D_e = G_prompt,e - G_delayed,e
]

and no-record separately; pool delayed/no only if overlap and support are adequate. This remains an observational timing association, not a pressor effect. It tests whether the parent’s signal survives the distinct-later-result endpoint but is not the new hospital-spread estimand.

## Matched methods

### Transparent baseline: additive competing-process model

Fit a regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic model on identical 30-minute intervals and identical cohort/split for both endpoints. Include separate processes for repeat test, confirmation, unit death, alive exit and unknown-status censoring. Use prespecified linear/spline time terms and common covariate slopes, with hospital random intercepts by transition and a hospital-by-transition elapsed-time slope where support permits. The baseline is deliberately interpretable; it does not use latent physiology or an unobserved treatment variable.

Inputs frozen at the relevant landmark are exactly the variables in the standardization list above, plus interval time and the applicable observed process history. Post-rise lab values are outcomes, not predictors of confirmation. Nested fits are:

- B0: case-mix and common elapsed-time effects, no hospital heterogeneity;
- B1 (primary transparent fit): B0 plus transition-specific hospital random intercepts;
- B2: B1 plus hospital-context fixed effects;
- B3: B1 plus prespecified repeat-testing opportunity terms;
- B4: B1 with no hospital-by-time slope, a stability/null-heterogeneity sensitivity.

Derive q_h, H90-10, the parent D_e, process CIFs and standardized predictions from the same fitted model. B0/B4 are falsification and stability references, not competing primary answers.

### Substantive alternative: ordered piecewise-exponential multi-state model

Fit an ordered piecewise-exponential multi-state model on the same risk sets, inputs, hospital split, standardization distribution and uncertainty procedure. Use separate states and intensities for first-rise detection, repeat-test opportunity, confirmation, unit death, alive unit exit and unknown-status censoring. The first-rise-to-repeat-test and repeat-test-to-confirmation transitions are distinct; the six-hour delay is structural. Use transition-specific hospital random effects with shrinkage and fixed hospital context only where estimable.

This alternative can show whether between-hospital variation is carried by clinicians’ opportunity to repeat the test, by the value after a repeat test, by timing of the repeat, or by competing ICU exit. That information is substantively lost in the additive endpoint-only baseline. Agreement between models on a large H90-10 supports a robust recorded-process heterogeneity finding. A large spread only in repeat-test opportunity points to surveillance practice; a spread after repeat testing remains comparable points to unresolved measurement, case-mix, or local ascertainment differences. A stable any-rise timing contrast but unstable confirmation contrast indicates endpoint sensitivity.

No learned sequence model is required initially. It would be reconsidered only if both prespecified models leave reproducible shape-specific held-out miscalibration after process accounting and a learned model can estimate the same hospital spread and process decomposition. This deferral is recorded in [method-selection-hospital-heterogeneity.md](support/method-selection-hospital-heterogeneity.md), with exact inputs, targets, split, evaluation, resource estimate and rationale. It is not a rejection of neural methods or structured data. A causal pressor branch remains deferred because the available infusion records do not validate administration/start, dose, indication, intent or complete capture; a biological-AKI branch requires unavailable urine output, renal reserve, adjudication and treatment context.

## Split, uncertainty, transport and support

Assign hospitals to 80% development and 20% held-out sets by a deterministic hash of hospitalid, before cohort summaries, imputation, fitting or threshold decisions. If any uniquepid spans hospitals, assign its connected hospital component together and report coverage loss. No patient, hospital or connected component crosses the split. A second fixed hash is a sensitivity, not a post hoc choice.

Fit and tune only in development hospitals. Estimate H90-10 among supported development hospitals. Apply the frozen model to held-out hospitals without refitting, local recalibration or tuning; report calibration, Brier score, observed-versus-predicted confirmation and repeat-test CIF curves, plus the direction and size of held-out aggregate process discrepancies. Held-out hospital-specific descriptive CIFs may be shown with uncertainty but are not used to improve the model. Transport is inconclusive if there are fewer than 10 held-out hospitals, insufficient supported risk sets, missing timing/overlap, or no confirmation support.

Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates for H90-10, process spreads, paired D_e, model differences and calibration summaries; use a uniquepid-clustered sensitivity. Report hospital-specific support, effective sample sizes, convergence, random-effect shrinkage, overlap, observed and predicted event counts, and intervals. Do not report a hospital spread from an unregularized proportion in a hospital below the support threshold.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence for the new hypothesis requires a supported H90-10 point estimate >=5 percentage points, a two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval excluding zero, the same qualitative ordering in both matched models, and a nontrivial remaining spread after repeat-test and exit processes are represented. It is still only evidence of heterogeneity in the recorded confirmation process.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed spread, a lower interval compatible with no material difference, disappearance after the explicit test-opportunity process, or instability across lab-clock, confirmation-gap, unit-boundary, MAP-channel, unit-status and agent-map sensitivities. A large raw spread that collapses after repeat-test modeling supports a local surveillance explanation rather than a persistent endpoint difference. A stable any-rise association with no confirmed-rise association is evidence against treating the original single-rise signal as persistent.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 10 supported hospitals, sparse first-rise or confirmation events, poor timing overlap, extreme inverse-opportunity weights, failed convergence, unstable bootstrap intervals, pooled/equal-hospital disagreement, strong missing-unit dependence, boundary sensitivity, or held-out calibration failure. Inconclusive is not evidence for or against clinical benefit.

Computationally checkable claims are the source hashes/headers, joins, age parsing, unit verification, deduplication, raw offsets, ledger construction, unit-discharge censoring, state transitions, deterministic split, fits, standardized predictions, intervals, support counts, calibration and sensitivity reproducibility. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a recorded creatinine result represents biological kidney injury, KDIGO AKI, hemoconcentration, assay/revision artifact, renal reserve, urine-output change, or a response to an administered pressor. Expert review is also required before using any result to change repeat-testing policy or treatment. No result can establish causal harm/benefit, pressor equivalence, a MAP target, or a biological renal null.
