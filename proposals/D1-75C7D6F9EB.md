# Candidate: Early checkpoint–antiangiogenic intensification with first TACE

## Decision and unresolved claim

The clinical decision is whether an adult with newly observed, liver-confined, TACE-treated hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) should begin a checkpoint inhibitor plus an antiangiogenic/TKI during the first 30 days, or initially receive TACE alone.

**Strongest existing evidence claim.** A 2026 open review of EMERALD-1 and LEAP-012 reports randomized phase 3 progression-free-survival benefit for TACE-based targeted immunotherapy in selected, TACE-eligible intermediate-stage populations (LEAP-012 HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.41–0.77; EMERALD-1 HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.52–0.95), while mature LEAP-012 overall survival was not statistically significant (HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.52–1.18). The review emphasizes Child–Pugh A trial selection, heterogeneity, subgroup selection, and dynamic liver-function monitoring. This evidence is from the inspected full XML of Chen et al., 2026, DOI 10.1136/bmjgast-2025-002154, frozen source `[source checksum]`. The EMERALD-1 publisher landing page was acquired as source `[source checksum]`, but its article text was unavailable and is not claimed as read.

**Unresolved test claim.** In routine-care adults meeting computable liver-confined eligibility, starting both a confirmed checkpoint inhibitor and antiangiogenic/TKI within 30 days of first observed TACE causes at least a 20% reduction in the number of EHR-recorded repeat TACE sessions through day 365, compared with initially receiving TACE alone, without increasing 30–90-day biochemical liver injury by more than 5 percentage points.

This advances beyond trial PFS by testing a concrete routine-care benefit–harm decision across Chinese real-world regimens: whether intensification substitutes for repeated invasive embolization without trading that benefit for hepatic injury. It does **not** test mortality, true radiographic PFS, or class-wide oncologic efficacy.

## Data feasibility already observed

Direct scans of snapshot `[source checksum]` found 105,044 encounters for 43,815 patients (2010-07-27 to 2026-01-01), 20,064 patients with a TACE-like procedure, and 10,124 patients whose first observed TACE fell between 2019-10-01 and 2024-12-31. Medication-name confirmation identified 1,169 with a checkpoint inhibitor within −7/+30 days, 883 with both a checkpoint inhibitor and an antiangiogenic/TKI in that window, and 7,499 with neither. There were 4,102 patients with another distinct TACE date during days 31–365. Among the 10,124, paired baseline (days −14 to 0) and follow-up (days 30–90) measurements existed for albumin in 2,980, total bilirubin in 2,979, INR in 3,046, and AFP in 2,343. These are feasibility counts, not results, and all final attrition must be recomputed and reported.

## Target-trial emulation

### Eligibility and time boundaries

- One row per adult patient (`age >=18`) at the first observed TACE session in the entire snapshot, with same-day duplicate/component procedure rows collapsed.
- Index date from `procedures.Start Time`, between 2019-10-01 (first observed immune-treatment era) and 2024-12-31 (365-day potential source coverage).
- HCC required from `diagnoses.diagnosis name` linked by patient and encounter at or before index; reject uncertain-only HCC strings.
- At least one contrast CT/MRI/ultrasound liver report during days −60 to 0 and baseline albumin, total bilirubin, INR, platelet count, creatinine, and AFP during days −14 to 0.
- Exclude prior observed TACE at any time; liver resection, ablation, or transplant during days −180 to −1; checkpoint inhibitor or antiangiogenic/TKI during days −180 to −1; and computable pre-index macrovascular invasion or extrahepatic metastasis in reports/diagnoses.
- The imaging eligibility lexicon and numeric tumor-burden parser are frozen before outcome analysis. A stratified random sample of 200 reports (including all rare positive categories if fewer) must be independently clinically adjudicated. If positive predictive value for exclusion concepts is <0.90 or sensitivity on enriched review is <0.80, the primary analysis becomes “computable apparent liver-confined HCC,” and no true-stage conclusion is allowed.

### Strategies and assignment

- **A, early combination:** initiate at least one confirmed checkpoint drug and at least one confirmed antiangiogenic/TKI on days 0–30.
- **B, initial TACE alone:** initiate neither class on days 0–30.
- Checkpoint lexicon includes Chinese generic substrings `tislelizumab|camrelizumab|sintilimab|pembrolizumab|nivolumab|atezolizumab|durvalumab|toripalimab`. Antiangiogenic/TKI lexicon includes `bevacizumab|lenvatinib|sorafenib|regorafenib|apatinib|donafenib`. Explicit `placebo` rows are excluded. Drug-level results are descriptive unless adequately powered.
- Use cloning, censoring, and inverse-probability weighting from day 0 across the 30-day grace period. Clone each eligible patient to both strategies, censor a clone when observed treatment becomes incompatible, and estimate treatment/censor weights from information available up to each day. This prevents guaranteed-survival/immortal-time credit for later initiation. A day-31 landmark analysis is sensitivity only.
- Follow-up is day 0 through day 365, end of source coverage, or last recorded encounter. Lack of a record is not death.

### Outcomes and falsification threshold

**Co-primary benefit:** count of distinct repeat TACE session dates on days 31–365. Collapse all TACE component labels on the same patient/calendar date to one session. The benefit target is weighted rate ratio (RR) ≤0.80.

**Co-primary harm:** biochemical liver injury on days 30–90: either total bilirubin at least 50% above the last pre-index value or albumin at least 20% below it, using the same exact assay name and quantitative field, with physiologically impossible values removed by a blinded rule. Relative changes are unit-invariant because the source has no separate lab-unit column. Estimate risk difference (RD); non-inferiority margin is +5 percentage points. Report the two components separately. Observation of follow-up labs is modeled with inverse-probability-of-observation weights; the paired-measurement subset remains a selection-limited estimand.

Support requires RR point estimate ≤0.80 with 95% CI excluding 1.00 **and** liver-injury RD upper 95% confidence limit <+5 points. Evidence is adverse if the repeat-TACE RR lower confidence limit is ≥1.00, or liver-injury RD lower confidence limit exceeds +5 points. Every other result is inconclusive, including failure of overlap, effective sample size <50 in either weighted arm, >1% extreme-weight truncation changing either conclusion, or imaging-adjudication failure. This rule forbids declaring benefit from one favorable component.

Secondary outcomes are day-365 conversion to a resection/ablation/transplant procedure; documented new hepatic decompensation (ascites/paracentesis, variceal bleed, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatorenal syndrome); AFP percent change; total HCC-directed procedure days; and admissions. These are explanatory and multiplicity-controlled with Benjamini–Hochberg q=0.05, not substitutes for failed co-primary outcomes.

## Variables, confounding, and analysis

Baseline covariates use only days −180 to 0: age, sex, calendar month/year, encounter department, prior encounter/admission burden, HBV/HCV and cirrhosis diagnoses, baseline decompensation, diabetes/renal/cardiovascular diagnoses, exact last baseline laboratory values and missingness, AFP, and report-derived lesion count, maximum diameter, bilobar disease, macrovascular invasion, and extrahepatic spread. Pathology-derived differentiation and microvascular invasion are used only when the pathology encounter precedes index; otherwise they are post-index leakage.

Estimate stabilized daily treatment/censor weights with flexible pooled logistic models; truncate at prespecified 1st/99th percentiles and report weight distributions, standardized differences, positivity by calendar year, and weighted effective sample sizes. Estimate repeat-session RR with weighted negative-binomial or Poisson-with-robust-variance model after checking dispersion; estimate harm RD with weighted binomial standardization. Bootstrap patients (not rows) for 95% intervals. Prespecified sensitivity analyses: overlap weighting; 2021+ cohort; active comparator A versus TACE+antiangiogenic/TKI without checkpoint drug; landmark day 31; exact-drug exclusions for trial/placebo-like strings; complete paired labs; alternative 15%/30% albumin and 25%/100% bilirubin thresholds; and E-values/quantitative bias analysis for an unmeasured confounder.

Falsification diagnostics are (1) weighted balance of all prespecified covariates, (2) association of assigned strategy with pre-index repeat-procedure and admission counts, which should be null after weighting, (3) negative-control outcome of non-hepatic diagnostic testing during days 31–90, and (4) calendar-year-stratified estimates. A non-null negative control or severe year interaction downgrades causal language even if co-primary thresholds pass.

## Interpretation boundaries and verifier obligations

A supportive result supports only an association compatible with a routine-care reduction in **recorded** repeat TACE burden under measured-confounding assumptions and no excess selected paired-lab injury. It motivates prospective/pragmatic validation. It does not establish survival, radiographic response, cost-effectiveness, or a drug-specific class effect.

An adverse result argues against routine early class-level intensification under this benefit–harm criterion; it does not prove every regimen ineffective. An inconclusive result leaves the decision unresolved.

Computationally checkable claims include cohort counts, temporal ordering, grace-period cloning/censoring, lexicon hits, deduplication, attrition, covariate balance, weights, effect estimates/intervals, thresholds, sensitivity analyses, and whether prose conclusions obey the decision rule. The verifier must explicitly reject: “beneficial” when RR=0.70 but harm RD=+8 points; “harmful” from a wide RD crossing zero and +5; “no effect” when the RR interval spans clinically important benefit and harm; or any mortality/PFS statement from these outputs. It must accept appropriately bounded supportive, adverse, and inconclusive interpretations regardless of hypothesis confirmation.

Clinical adjudication is required for true HCC stage, tumor burden, macrovascular invasion, extrahepatic spread, decompensation validity, curative intent, and whether repeat TACE represents failure rather than planned staged treatment. Missing death, out-of-system care, ECOG status, raw images, validated mRECIST response, costs, and exact lab units prevent automatic establishment of clinical truth. A randomized or prospective multicenter study with mortality, raw imaging, adjudicated response, performance status, toxicity grading, and complete follow-up is required for stronger treatment recommendations.
