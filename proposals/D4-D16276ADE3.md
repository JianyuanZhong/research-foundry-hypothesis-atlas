# Provisional hypothesis: fat distribution separates metabolic from functional/fall risk

Status: provisional design hypothesis, not a computed result and not selected.

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with a valid second-assessment body-composition MRI and subsequent follow-up, at similar total body fat, higher visceral fat will be more specifically associated with subsequent metabolic disease, whereas higher posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) will be more specifically associated with subsequent functional decline or falls. The substantive advance is to test whether fat distribution identifies distinct downstream clinical trajectories rather than merely predicting a generic health-risk score.

The simple baseline is a penalized logistic/Cox model using age, sex, ethnicity, body-fat percentage, baseline grip, and available medication/activity indicators. The substantive alternative adds visceral fat and mean left/right MFI, with separate outcome models and a prespecified exposure-by-outcome interaction. ## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results require directionally distinct visceral-fat/metabolic and MFI/functional contrasts that persist after lag, selection, and baseline-function checks, with uncertainty and held-out calibration reported. Adverse results are absent/reversed contrasts or no information beyond total adiposity and demographics. Sparse events, unstable estimates, poor calibration, or large missingness/selection differences are inconclusive and do not refute the hypothesis. Even supportive associations would not establish mechanism, causality, or treatment benefit; clinical adjudication of falls/fractures and additional longitudinal or interventional evidence would be required.
