## Remaining weakness and substantive repair

The parent correctly moved from a six-bin observable subset to the full eligible adult ICU-discharge population and retained missingness and observation density. Its remaining validity weakness is attribution of the incremental signal. Its process-aware B0 includes chart density, masks, gaps, careunit and admission features, but it does not make a deliberately value-free care-process model a central null, and it does not make the competing-event/deployment interpretation sufficiently explicit. A recovery feature can appear useful because it marks where a patient is in an ICU-to-ward workflow, which service is documenting the patient, or how close a transfer is to a handoff. Conversely, a process channel can look useful because it is a proxy for residual severity. Neither pattern alone establishes a physiologic recovery phenotype or a beneficial discharge intervention.

This child therefore asks a narrower and more discriminating question:

At the ICU outtime landmark, does the ordered final-24-hour physiology/support trajectory add reproducible information beyond both (i) a strong last-state-plus-observation baseline and (ii) a value-free care-process model containing careunit, service, transfer, timing, and measurement-intensity information; and does any incremental signal survive explicit competing-event definitions and clinically interpretable threshold contrasts?

The strongest claim supported before fitting remains only that repeated structured measurements, residual oxygen/hemodynamic abnormalities, and changing ICU/ward workflow are available and plausibly prognostic. No available evidence establishes that the trajectory is a validated physiologic construct, that a later recorded ICU stay was unplanned deterioration, that a discharge decision caused it, or that changing monitoring or discharge policy would improve outcomes.

The primary hypothesis is falsifiable: in the full defined adult ICU-discharge cohort, the prespecified recovery/persistence/oscillation/support-transition features (B1) improve locked 48-hour cause-specific estimation of first subsequent recorded ICU admission, with acceptable death calibration, over a process-aware last-state baseline (B0), and the B1 increment remains present when compared with a value-free care-process null (P0), across prespecified observation-density strata. The deployment-relevant secondary hypothesis is that B1 changes threshold classifications and net-benefit contrasts beyond B0 at prespecified risk thresholds, but this will be interpreted only as an observed-risk stratification result, not as evidence that retaining a patient in ICU or increasing ward monitoring improves outcomes.

A result confined to P0, or a B1 gain that disappears after the process-only contrast, would support an ascertainment/workflow explanation rather than the proposed incremental recovery claim. A B1 gain that survives P0 but is not stable across observation strata, careunit strata, competing-event definitions, or thresholds would support only a limited association and not a deployable rule.

## Outcomes and competing-event definitions

Read the full icustays and admissions rows for the hospitalization and later ICU rows. Start follow-up at index outtime. Primary horizon is 48 hours, secondary horizon seven days. Use eight six-hour bins for 48 hours and 28 six-hour bins for seven days.

Define the first subsequent recorded ICU admission as the earliest later icu/icustays.intime > index outtime for the same subject_id, hadm_id, within the horizon and before death. Define death as admissions.deathtime > outtime within the horizon and before any later ICU admission. If a later ICU admission and death are tied at the same timestamp, mark the event ordering unresolved and exclude it from the primary timed competing-risk analysis, reporting the count and a sensitivity rule. If hospital_expire_flag=1 but deathtime is missing, timing is unresolved: exclude from the primary timed analysis and report a sensitivity assigning death at dischtime only when no later ICU stay is recorded.

Censor at the earliest of the horizon, the first event, hospital dischtime, or unresolved event timing. Keep death after a later ICU admission as a descriptive post-readmission outcome rather than changing the first-event estimand. Report three linked but distinct estimands:

1. Cause-specific cumulative incidence of first subsequent recorded ICU admission, with death a competing event.
2. Cause-specific cumulative incidence of first death before later ICU admission, with ICU admission a competing event.
3. A composite first recorded transition failure outcome, the first of ICU admission or death, used only for the threshold/deployment contrast.

The scientific claim is observed-process prognostic information for these recorded events. It is not a causal discharge, retention, surveillance, treatment, readmission-intent, or patient-benefit effect.

## Comparable models and method exploration

All models use identical full-cohort patient-level splits, outcomes, horizons, and preprocessing. Use deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits under five fixed hash seeds; no subject_id or hadm_id crosses splits. Fit imputation, scaling, decay, observation-density and any category encoding on training subjects only.

### B0: strong simple baseline

Fit L2-regularized pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific hazards for ICU admission and death, with interval indicators and:

- age, gender, admission type/location, ICU duration, first/last ICU careunit;
- last valid physiologic values and support state;
- recency, counts, masks and gaps for each domain;
- explicitly missing process channels.

This is the clinically interpretable last state plus observation process null. It is simple enough to inspect but strong enough that a B1 gain cannot be attributed merely to recency, missingness, or last careunit.

### P0: value-free care-process null

Fit the same hazard architecture using no valuenum, support intensity, trajectory value, or physiologic threshold. Inputs are demographics/admission metadata, ICU duration, first/last careunit, pre-landmark transfer/service features, event counts, domain measurement masks, recencies, gaps, and generic procedure/charting intensity. This asks how much of the observed risk signal is reproducible from workflow and observability alone.

The planned comparisons are:

- P0 versus B0: whether last-state physiology adds to workflow/measurement information;
- B1 versus B0: the primary recovery increment;
- B1 versus P0: whether recovery summaries contain information beyond the process-only null;
- P0 versus a value-permuted P0: whether any process-only signal is an artifact of labels/splits.

### B1: primary interpretable recovery model

Fit B0 plus the fixed recovery/persistence/oscillation/abnormal-burden/support-transition features. Keep care-process features in the model so this is an incremental test, not a confounded physiology-only comparison. The primary contrast is B1 minus B0 and B1 minus P0 on locked test predictions.

A secondary physiology-isolated contrast removes transfer/service and observation-intensity variables while retaining basic demographics and landmark-safe last values/recovery features. This is descriptive only: it shows what the physiology block can do without process covariates, while B1 is the primary adjusted comparison.

### B2: substantive learned alternative

Fit a one-layer 32-unit GRU-D-style encoder over the same four ordered bins, with values, support states, masks, counts, delta-times, and baseline/process covariates; two competing-risk hazard heads; dropout 0.1; maximum 40 epochs; validation-loss early stopping with fixed patience. Use identical splits and targets as B0/B1.

B2 can reveal nonlinear interactions between recovery order, intermittent observation and support transitions that fixed summaries lose. It is scientifically useful only if B2 improves B1 reproducibly, its gains disappear under within-patient order/value perturbations, and its calibration is not worse. A B2 gain over B0 alone is not evidence that a neural representation discovered physiology.

All models report 48-hour and seven-day cause-specific Brier scores, integrated Brier score, CIF calibration intercept/slope, AUROC/AUPRC, and five-seed variability. Use 2,000 subject-level paired bootstrap replicates where event support allows; otherwise report the prespecified reason, effective replicate count, and uncertainty limitation. Do not treat an absolute Brier reduction of 0.005 as a clinical-utility threshold.

## Clinically interpretable deployment contrasts

These are prospective-policy-motivated summaries of observed risk, not policy-effect estimates.

For each locked model, report predictions at 48 hours for:

- composite first transition failure (ICU admission or death);
- ICU admission CIF with death as a competing event;
- death CIF before ICU admission.

At prespecified thresholds 1%, 2%, 5%, and 10%, report per 1,000 index transitions:

- fraction flagged;
- observed composite-event count among flagged and unflagged;
- sensitivity and false-positive fraction for the composite;
- the number and outcome composition of transitions reclassified by B1 versus B0;
- net benefit using a predeclared threshold-to-harm weight for the composite, with the threshold convention stated in the output;
- for the ICU-admission target, a competing-risk decision curve based on the predicted ICU-admission CIF, treating death before ICU admission as a non-ICU-return event and displaying it separately rather than relabeling it as a successful discharge.

Use the same fixed thresholds for B0, P0, B1 and B2. Include treat-all and treat-none reference curves where the utility convention is defined. Report decision curves overall and by observation-density and last-careunit strata only when event support permits. A favorable net-benefit curve is evidence that the model changes observed-risk stratification under the chosen hypothetical utility, not evidence that an ICU-retention or ward-monitoring action would help. Any claim about actionability requires clinician-defined harms/benefits, intended-use review, calibration in a new site, prospective impact evaluation, and adjudication of plannedness and goals of care.

## Falsification, nulls, and robustness

Required computational audits:

- zero post-landmark predictor timestamps;
- exact one-row-per-index-hospitalization construction;
- split integrity and no identifier leakage;
- exclusion and unresolved-timing ledgers;
- first-event ordering and competing-event counts;
- observation-density flow;
- rejected-value and unit-conversion counts;
- transfer/service feature timing;
- post-landmark destination excluded from primary predictors.

Required perturbations:

1. last-bin-only model;
2. B0 process-only and value-free P0;
3. value permutation within observation-density/event-rate strata, retaining masks/counts and care-process variables;
4. within-patient bin-order permutation when at least two valid bins exist;
5. removal of masks/delta-time channels;
6. removal of careunit/transfer/service channels;
7. label permutation within event-rate strata;
8. parent six-bin observable cohort as locked secondary sensitivity;
9. alternative competing-event treatment: composite first event, cause-specific CIF, and a sensitivity assigning unresolved hospital death at dischtime under the stated restriction;
10. descriptive post-landmark transfer-destination audit, never a primary predictor.

Supportive results require all of the following: B1 improves B0 and P0 on the locked full-cohort 48-hour ICU-admission estimand with uncertainty compatible with a nonzero increment; the gain is not confined to the four-bin stratum; death calibration is not materially harmed; B1 survives care-process channel removal as an appropriate sensitivity; value/order permutation removes the trajectory increment; and deployment contrasts show stable direction across prespecified thresholds without relying on a single threshold. Stronger transport support additionally requires agreement with the parent's six-bin sensitivity and a well-calibrated observation model with adequate positivity, but this remains an internal transport diagnostic.

Adverse results include: P0 matches or exceeds B1; B1 adds no increment after B0; gain appears only among highly observed patients; gain disappears after careunit/transfer adjustment; death calibration worsens; the increment survives value/order permutation; label permutation discriminates; competing-event handling reverses the conclusion; or B2 improves B0 but not B1. These findings refute the specified incremental recovery claim, not every possible prognostic use of ICU-discharge data.

Inconclusive results include sparse events in process or observation strata, poor positivity, unresolved event timing, invalid timestamps, weak linkage, broad bootstrap intervals, seed instability, or B2 convergence failure within budget. Failure of the deployment contrast does not prove lack of physiologic information; it limits the evidence for a threshold-based use. A recorded ICU return remains an imperfect outcome because planned readmission, treatment limitation, transfer logistics and clinician intent are not adjudicated.

## Alternatives, selection, and deferred branches

The simple B0 is selected as the minimum adequate clinical comparator because it preserves last-state physiology and explicit ascertainment channels while remaining interpretable. P0 is required because it directly tests the main unresolved workflow explanation. B1 is the primary scientific deliverable because it estimates the clinically meaningful incremental information in interpretable recovery relations after both comparators. B2 is retained as a learned alternative with the same target/split and bounded CPU budget; its purpose is to test whether nonlinear ordered/missingness structure is lost by B1, not to win by small predictive improvement.

A physiology-only model is retained as a secondary decomposition, not as the primary claim, because removing process channels can induce ascertainment confounding. A transfer/careunit-only model is retained as P0 rather than promoted to the clinical hypothesis because workflow prediction is not physiological recovery. A text model using note/discharge is deferred: the note table is available, but validated extraction of intent, plannedness and functional readiness is unavailable and leakage risk is substantial. A causal policy analysis is deferred because treatment assignment, clinician intent, bedside readiness and action outcomes are not identified. Raw-waveform and image adaptations are unavailable in this configured snapshot. MIMIC seeds about AKI, diuresis, antimicrobial de-escalation and asynchronous organ recovery remain separate questions and are not imported into this repair.

The documented feasible implementation is CPU-based: chunked extraction, at most four CPUs, 16 GB RAM and 120 minutes for fitting/evaluation, within the configured 7,200 science seconds. B0/P0/B1 use sparse tabular design; B2 uses the bounded 32-unit encoder and compact checkpoints. No GPU is required, and no method is rejected because it uses structured data or a neural network.
