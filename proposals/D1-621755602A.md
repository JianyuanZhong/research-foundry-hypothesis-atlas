# Asynchronous Organ Recovery: A Multi-Organ Recovery Phenotype Predicts ICU Deterioration

## Scientific Question

After circulatory stabilization in critically ill patients, do patients with **asynchronous organ recovery** (i.e., persistent impairment in one or more organs after stabilization of others) have higher rates of subsequent deterioration compared to those with synchronous multi-organ recovery?

## Hypothesis Statement

**Primary Hypothesis:** In patients who have achieved hemodynamic stability (sustained mean arterial pressure [MAP] ≥ 65 mmHg for ≥6 hours without escalating vasopressor requirements) following sepsis or shock, those exhibiting **asynchronous organ recovery**—defined as persistent elevation in organ-specific markers (kidney: creatinine > baseline + 0.3 mg/dL for ≥12 hours; respiratory: PaO2/FiO2 < 300 for ≥12 hours; cardiovascular: lactate > 2 mmol/L for ≥6 hours) after initial stabilization—are at significantly higher risk of subsequent clinical deterioration (requiring escalation of organ support or death) compared to patients with synchronous multi-organ recovery.

**Expected Effect Size:** Hazard ratio for deterioration ≥1.5 (95% CI: 1.3-1.8) for asynchronous vs. synchronous recovery groups.

**Falsification Criteria:** If the hazard ratio for deterioration is <1.2 (95% CI includes 1.0), the hypothesis is falsified.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Early Warning System:** Current ICU practice relies heavily on single-timepoint SOFA (Sepsis-related Organ Failure Assessment) scores. Serial trajectory analysis of organ-specific recovery could identify high-risk patients earlier, before deterioration becomes apparent in static measurements.

2. **Treatment Guidance:** Different recovery patterns may suggest different underlying pathophysiology (e.g., progressive organ failure vs. delayed recovery), potentially informing treatment intensification or de-escalation decisions.

3. **Risk Stratification:** Could refine risk scores beyond baseline SOFA by capturing temporal dynamics that static assessments miss.

4. **Resource Allocation:** Identifying patients at high risk of deterioration could enable earlier intervention, reducing length of stay and mortality.

## Study Population and Main Data

### Population Definition
- **Inclusion:** Adults (≥18 years) with sepsis or shock in MIMIC-IV ICU stays
- **Exclusion:** Patients without at least 3 pre-stabilization organ measurements, or without post-stabilization follow-up data
- **Stabilization Criteria:** Sustained MAP ≥65 mmHg for ≥6 consecutive hours without escalating vasopressor requirements

### Data Tables and Variables

| Table | Key Variables | Purpose |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| `icu/icustays` | subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, outtime, los | ICU stay boundaries |
| `icu/inputevents` | subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, starttime, endtime, itemid, rate, rateuom | Organ support measurements (vasopressors, fluids) |
| `icu/outputevents` | subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, charttime, storetime, itemid, value, valueuom | Laboratory measurements (creatinine, lactate, PaO2/FiO2) |
| `icu/ingredientevents` | subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, starttime, endtime, itemid, amount, rate | Medication administration (vasopressors, antibiotics) |
| `hosp/patients` | subject_id, age, sex, race | Demographic covariates |

### Recovery Phenotype Definition

**Synchronous Recovery:** All organs show improvement (return toward baseline) within 12 hours of hemodynamic stabilization

**Asynchronous Recovery:** At least one organ shows persistent impairment (defined below) after stabilization of others

| Organ | Metric | Impairment Criterion |
|-------|--------|----------------------|
| Kidney | Creatinine | > baseline + 0.3 mg/dL for ≥12 hours |
| Respiratory | PaO2/FiO2 | < 300 for ≥12 hours |
| Cardiovascular | Lactate | > 2 mmol/L for ≥6 hours |

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Phenotype Definition and Validation
1. Identify all ICU stays with sepsis/shock diagnosis (ICD-9/10 codes)
2. Define hemodynamic stabilization using vasopressor requirements and MAP
3. Calculate organ-specific recovery trajectories
4. Classify as synchronous vs. asynchronous based on criteria above

### Phase 2: Prognostic Analysis
1. Define deterioration endpoints:
   - Escalation of organ support (e.g., new vasopressor, ventilator weaning failure)
   - Death within 7 days of stabilization
2. Calculate hazard ratios comparing asynchronous vs. synchronous groups
3. Adjust for covariates: age, sex, baseline organ function, comorbidities

### Phase 3: Baseline Comparison
Compare with simple categorical approach (high vs. low SOFA score) to demonstrate added value of temporal analysis

## Baseline and Alternative Approaches

### Baseline: Static SOFA Score
- **Method:** Use baseline SOFA score at ICU admission
- **Limitation:** Single timepoint cannot capture recovery dynamics
- **Information Lost:** Temporal patterns of organ-specific improvement/deterioration

### Alternative: Transformer-Based Trajectory Model
- **Method:** Train a transformer model (inspired by Shmatko et al. [K2]) on organ-specific trajectories
- **Expected Advantage:** Could capture complex, non-linear recovery patterns
- **Resource Cost:** Significant GPU time for training
- **Rationale for Testing Baseline First:** Simple trajectory analysis may be sufficient; complexity only justified if baseline fails

### Alternative: Machine Learning Classifier
- **Method:** Random forest or gradient boosting on trajectory features
- **Expected Advantage:** May capture non-linear interactions
- **Resource Cost:** Moderate computational resources
- **Rationale:** Could serve as intermediate step before full ML approach

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **falsified** if:
1. Hazard ratio for deterioration in asynchronous vs. synchronous group is <1.2 (95% CI includes 1.0)
2. After adjustment for baseline SOFA score, asynchrony no longer predicts deterioration
3. Simple categorical approach (high vs. low SOFA) performs as well as or better than temporal analysis

The hypothesis will be **supported** if:
1. Hazard ratio ≥1.5 (95% CI excludes 1.0)
2. Temporal analysis outperforms static SOFA in predicting deterioration
3. Asynchrony predicts deterioration independent of baseline severity

## Limitations and Unresolved Questions

### Data Limitations
1. **Missing Measurements:** Not all organ-specific measurements (e.g., PaO2/FiO2) are recorded for all patients
2. **Selection Bias:** ICU admissions are not representative of all critically ill patients
3. **Temporal Resolution:** Measurements are point-in-time, not continuous

### Clinical Limitations
1. **Causality:** Observational design cannot establish that asynchrony causes deterioration
2. **Intervention:** Does preventing asynchrony improve outcomes? (requires RCT)
3. **Generalizability:** May not apply to non-ICU settings or different populations

### Unresolved Questions
1. **Mechanism:** What biological mechanisms drive asynchrony and why does it predict deterioration?
2. **Intervention:** Can we identify patients at risk early enough to intervene?
3. **Thresholds:** Are the impairment criteria (e.g., creatinine > baseline + 0.3) optimal?
