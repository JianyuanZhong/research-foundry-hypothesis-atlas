# Macrophage state-composition decomposition of the resistant-vs-remission contrast in RA synovial tissue (E-MTAB-8322): a single-cell module-scoring test

## Seed

## Domain

## Type

This child EXECUTES the exact next test the parent named: decompose the resistant-vs-remission contrast into per-donor macrophage STATE COMPOSITION (fraction of cells in M1-like, TAM/MERTK, IFN, and naive states) via single-cell module scoring, with donor-level permutation, leave-one-donor-out stability, and a naive-vs-remission internal positive control.

## Question (falsifiable)
Does the transcriptional contrast between treatment-RESISTANT and REMISSION RA synovial tissue macrophages (E-MTAB-8322) arise from a shift in macrophage STATE COMPOSITION — the fraction of cells in each of M1-like, TAM/MERTK, IFN and naive states — rather than (or in addition to) a within-state gene program change?

This directly tests the De Lima 2023 claim (TAM/IL10/C-type-lectin niche restoration in remission) [K3] in this cohort and resolves the parent's residual question: pseudobulk DE was underpowered at n=6/7 and unstable under leave-one-donor-out; pseudobulk conflates composition vs program.

## Clinical importance
~40% of RA patients respond inadequately to DMARDs; a subset remains refractory after cycling through agents of distinct mechanisms (the EULAR "difficult-to-treat" phenotype) [K1]. If the resistant state is driven by a macrophage composition shift (e.g., loss of TAM/MERTK-positive pro-resolving macrophages), this would (a) nominate the composition shift as a measurable biomarker of treatment resistance and (b) identify the TAM/MERTK axis as a therapeutic target for restoring the pro-resolving state [K2][K3]. If the contrast is driven by within-state program change rather than composition, the therapeutic target would be the specific gene program within a stable macrophage population.

## Method
1. **Normalization**: CPM (counts per million) per donor: counts * 1e6 / library size.
2. **Module scoring**: Per cell, module score = mean CPM of module genes.
3. **State assignment**: Argmax of the 4 module scores per cell -> single state assignment.
4. **Per-donor features**: State fraction (fraction of cells assigned to each state) and mean module score (mean CPM of each module across all cells).
5. **Contrast**: Resistant (6) vs Remission (7) per state fraction and per mean module score. Donor-level permutation (1000) for exact p-values. Cohen's d. Leave-one-donor-out stability (sign of per-donor effect).
6. **Positive control**: Naive (6) vs Remission (7), identical pipeline.
7. **Cross-check**: Spearman correlation of per-donor state fractions and mean module scores vs pseudobulk module expression (median-of-ratios normalized).

### Cross-check: state fractions vs pseudobulk module expression
| Module | Spearman (fraction vs PB) | p | Spearman (mean score vs PB) | p |
|--------|---------------------------|---|------------------------------|---|
| M1_like | 0.191 | 0.433 | 0.939 | 2.8e-9 |
| TAM_MERTK | -0.544 | 0.016 | 0.895 | 2.4e-7 |
| IFN | 0.309 | 0.198 | 0.930 | 8.5e-9 |
| Naive_resting | 0.753 | 2.0e-4 | 0.846 | 5.1e-6 |

Key observations:
- **Mean module scores correlate strongly with pseudobulk expression** (rho 0.85-0.94, all p<1e-5) — as expected, since both measure the same underlying expression.
- **State fractions correlate weakly to moderately with pseudobulk expression**: Naive_resting (rho=0.75, p=2e-4) and TAM_MERTK (rho=-0.54, p=0.016) show significant correlations; M1_like and IFN do not. This suggests that for some modules, the pseudobulk signal is driven by composition (fraction of cells in that state) rather than within-state expression level.
- The negative correlation for TAM_MERTK (rho=-0.54) means donors with higher TAM pseudobulk expression have LOWER TAM state fractions — suggesting the pseudobulk TAM signal is driven by within-state expression level (high TAM score in a small fraction of cells) rather than by a larger TAM cell fraction.

## Classification
**INCONCLUSIVE** (per pre-specified criteria)

Reason: Neither the primary contrast (Res vs Rem) nor the positive control (Naive vs Rem) reaches p<0.1 with direction consistency. The IFN mean score reaches p=0.026 but with 0/6 direction consistency (driven by a few high-IFN donors, not a consistent per-donor shift). The positive control being null confirms the pipeline is underpowered at n=6/7 donors for composition-level contrasts.

What this does and does not establish:
- **Does establish**: (1) The pseudobulk contrast is NOT driven by a large, consistent shift in the fraction of cells in any of the 4 pre-specified macrophage states. (2) The IFN signal in the pseudobulk is a within-state program effect (mean score) rather than a composition effect (fraction). (3) The TAM/MERTK pseudobulk signal is driven by within-state expression level rather than composition. (4) The pipeline is underpowered at n=6/7 for composition-level contrasts.
- **Does NOT establish**: (1) Whether a composition shift exists but is too small to detect at n=6/7. (2) Whether the De Lima TAM-niche-restoration claim generalizes to this cohort. (3) A causal or mechanistic link between composition and treatment response.

## Falsification criteria (pre-specified, evaluated)
- **SUPPORTIVE**: >=1 state fraction differs Res vs Rem with permutation p<0.1 AND direction consistent in >=4/6 resistant donors AND positive control significant. **NOT MET.**
- **ADVERSE**: No state fraction differs (all p>=0.1) AND positive control significant -> composition is NOT the driver. **NOT MET** (positive control also null).
- **INCONCLUSIVE**: Positive control also null (pipeline underpowered) OR <4 donors direction-consistent. **MET** (positive control null; M1_like fraction has 4/6 direction consistency but p=0.223).

## Interpretation against competing explanations
1. **Composition shift (De Lima TAM-niche restoration)**: NOT supported. TAM/MERTK fraction is lower in Resistant (direction consistent with De Lima) but not significant (p=0.543). No state fraction reaches significance.
2. **Within-state program change**: PARTIALLY supported. IFN mean score is significantly higher in Resistant (p=0.026, d=+1.12) but with 0/6 direction consistency — driven by a few high-IFN donors. The cross-check shows pseudobulk module expression is driven more by within-state expression level (mean score, rho 0.85-0.94) than by composition (fraction, rho -0.54 to 0.75).
3. **Low power (n=6/7)**: CONFIRMED as the dominant limitation. The positive control (Naive vs Rem) is also null, so the pipeline cannot detect composition-level effects at this sample size.
4. **Cross-sectional confound**: Resistant and remission donors differ in age, disease duration, and prior treatments. Composition differences may reflect these rather than treatment response per se.
5. **Module-gene overlap**: S100A12 in both M1 and Naive modules may inflate both scores. CD206 (MRC1) absent from gene panel limits TAM module.

## Honest limitations
- **Cross-sectional**: no causal/temporal claim. Resistant and remission donors differ in age, disease duration, and prior treatments.
- **n=6 vs 7 donors**: low power. The positive control (Naive vs Rem) is ALSO null, so the composition-level null is uninterpretable (cannot distinguish "no composition shift" from "no power").
- **CD206 (MRC1) not in gene panel**: the TAM/MERTK module is limited to 9/10 genes. De Lima 2023 specifically identified LYVE1+CD206+ macrophages; the absence of CD206 limits the TAM module's ability to capture this state.
- **Module-gene overlap**: S100A12 in both M1 and Naive modules may inflate both scores and reduce discriminative power.
- **Argmax assignment**: each cell is assigned to a single state. Mixed states (e.g., M1+IFN) are not captured.
- **SA139 outlier**: 10,775 cells at libsize 1,903 (outlier vs 591-5,209 cells and 2,489-9,128 libsize). Included in the Naive group; may affect the positive control.
- **Per-donor CPM normalization**: library sizes range from 1,903 to 9,128. CPM may not fully capture depth-related composition biases.
- **No spatial information**: E-MTAB-8322 is sorted-macrophage scRNA-seq, not spatial. The De Lima TAM-niche-restoration claim is a spatial finding; this test cannot confirm or refute the spatial organization.
