TITLE
Assumption-indexed cross-marker persistence experiment for protocol renal biomarkers in UK Biobank repeat attenders

SUBSTANTIVE ADVANCE OVER THE PARENT
This child preserves the parent’s clinical question, frozen source join, 16,546-person complete repeat-attender cohort, actual-date interval, P1/P2 cross-marker ANCOVA, symmetric confirmation, negative controls, and attendance sensitivity. It makes three substantive repairs required for a valid execution. First, it removes the claim that a two-visit “calibrated no-decline” simulation is empirically identified. Two waves cannot separately identify latent kidney trajectory, stable non-GFR marker components, biological within-person variation, assay error, and their cross-marker/cross-visit correlations. The simulation is therefore recast as an assumption-indexed sensitivity analysis, never as an estimated null or proof of decline. Second, it separates an executable certified-core primary analysis from gated richer covariate sensitivities, so unavailable coding maps cannot silently make the primary experiment infeasible. Third, it specifies a single cross-fitted estimating procedure and matched negative-control samples, eliminating ambiguity about inference and specificity comparisons.

SCIENTIFIC QUESTION AND CLINICAL IMPORTANCE
Among participants with both protocol serum creatinine and cystatin-C at baseline and repeat assessment, does baseline cystatin-C unexpectedly high relative to creatinine predict subsequent repeat creatinine after conditioning on baseline creatinine and elapsed time? The reciprocal question asks whether unexpectedly high creatinine predicts repeat cystatin-C. This matters because discordant markers can affect CKD staging, drug dosing, monitoring, and referral, but a single discordant pair may reflect muscle mass, adiposity, smoking, thyroid or inflammatory states, medication effects, biological variation, or assay error. A longitudinal cross-marker association could justify external validation of discordance as a monitoring signal; it cannot establish measured GFR decline, CKD progression, causality, treatment benefit, ordering benefit, or transportability.

EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED AND UNTESTED CLAIMS
Chen et al. (Kidney Medicine 2024, PMCID PMC10986041, DOI 10.1016/j.xkme.2024.100796; frozen XML source ID [source checksum]) supports that eGFR discrepancies based on cystatin-C and creatinine are common in UK Biobank and have associations with demographic, lifestyle, body-composition, disease, and medication factors. That evidence supports biological ambiguity, not longitudinal deterioration. The untested claim here is narrower: baseline cross-marker discordance contains a component associated with the other protocol biomarker at a later assessment among eligible repeat attenders. The strongest possible result remains protocol-biomarker persistence, not true renal-function decline.

PRIMARY EXPOSURES AND OUTCOMES
After positivity and range checks, use log concentrations. Within each training fold, fit a restricted cubic spline of baseline log cystatin-C on baseline log creatinine, certified recruitment age, sex, age-by-sex interaction, and baseline center; standardize the residual by the training-fold residual SD. This is R_cys|cr. P1 outcome is repeat log creatinine, with baseline log creatinine as a direct comparator. The tested parameter is the association per one training-fold SD of R_cys|cr.

P2 is reciprocal: residualize baseline log creatinine on baseline log cystatin-C, recruitment age, sex, age-by-sex, and baseline center, then predict repeat log cystatin-C while conditioning on baseline log cystatin-C. P2 is confirmatory/secondary; directional agreement is required for a shared-signal interpretation. A shared standardized-marker model may be reported as descriptive only and cannot add information beyond both markers.

The P1 baseline covariate vector is fixed as: baseline log creatinine, recruitment age, sex, baseline center, repeat center/change indicator if center is certified, elapsed-time spline, and only the certified-core covariates whose identities and units are frozen before outcome inspection. The primary certified-core model therefore does not require BMI, waist, smoking, diabetes, blood pressure, albumin, CRP, HbA1c, urate, or medication codes. Each richer adjustment is a separately labeled sensitivity, not a prerequisite that can make the primary model appear executable. Date/season adjustment is included only after date identity is certified and is defined before analysis.

CROSS-FITTED INFERENCE
Use prespecified participant-level 10-fold cross-fitting, with folds assigned before fitting and balanced by sex, baseline center where feasible, and exposure decile. In each training fold, fit all residualization, spline bases, imputation, standardization, and attendance models. Generate out-of-fold residual exposure and out-of-fold predictions for the comparator and residual-added outcome models. For the primary inferential coefficient, stack the ten held-out rows and fit one prespecified estimating regression using the out-of-fold exposure and the fixed outcome/covariate specification, with fold indicators and heteroskedasticity-robust participant-level standard errors. Do not average fold coefficients. Report coefficient, robust 95% CI, partial R-squared, RMSE, and log predictive density. Prediction gains are calculated only from held-out predictions; bootstrap participants for prediction-metric intervals. The estimand is associational and restricted to repeat attenders.

IDENTIFIABILITY REPAIR AND MEASUREMENT-ERROR SENSITIVITY
The original proposed calibrated null cannot be estimated from two visits. In particular, the observed four-marker covariance does not identify latent stable level, person-specific slope, stable marker-specific non-GFR component, visit-specific biological variation, assay error, or correlated errors. Preserving empirical cross-lag covariance would also risk placing the hypothesized persistence directly inside the null. Therefore the experiment does not estimate a no-decline covariance null.

Instead, after the primary result is frozen, conduct an assumption-indexed sensitivity analysis. Treat assay CVs only as external inputs when an authoritative assay source is frozen; do not estimate them as individual-level reliabilities from these two waves. Specify a grid before looking at P1/P2 results over: (i) assay-error fractions constrained by external CVs and plausible biological-error fractions; (ii) cross-marker same-visit error correlation; (iii) cross-visit error correlation; and (iv) stable non-GFR component fractions. Under each grid point, simulate latent stable marker levels with no person-specific slope, generate marker-specific non-GFR components and visit errors according to that grid, preserve the observed baseline covariate, interval, center, and marginal distributions only where this does not preserve observed cross-lag persistence, and explicitly set latent cross-visit slope to zero. Do not preserve the empirical cross-lag covariance as a whole. Run the complete residualization and stacked P1/P2 pipeline in each replicate, with at least 10,000 replicates per prespecified grid point, and report the distribution of coefficients and prediction gains. This answers only whether the observed result is compatible with selected externally imposed error structures. No grid point is privileged after seeing results. If conclusions change across plausible grid points, label the experiment inconclusive; if all plausible points fail to reproduce the observed signal, describe the sensitivity as supportive of persistence but still not evidence of GFR decline. If external CVs or error assumptions cannot be justified, omit this sensitivity and retain the primary result without simulation-based claims.

REGRESSION-TO-THE-MEAN, PLACEBOS, AND CENTER CHECKS
Do not regress a same-marker change on a baseline difference made from the same noisy observations. Report same-marker change only as an explicitly labeled mathematical-coupling illustration. Use permutation placebo within baseline sex, age decile, center, baseline outcome-marker decile, and interval decile, with at least 1,000 permutations; nominal significance without separation from the placebo distribution is adverse. Test whether baseline discordance predicts elapsed interval after conditioning on baseline date and center; a material association flags selection/invitation structure. Repeat analyses among same-center visits and leave out each baseline center in turn. Center-specific instability is adverse.

FALSIFICATION AND DECISION RULES
Supportive means: P1 is positive with robust CI excluding zero and the prespecified smallest relevant effect of 0.05 SD in repeat log marker per SD exposure; held-out prediction gain is nontrivial; P2 agrees directionally; results persist across certified-core complete-case/imputation, interval, center, and attendance analyses; weights have acceptable positivity and effective sample size; matched negative controls are materially weaker; and no plausible assumption-indexed error grid reverses the interpretation. Even then, the conclusion is only longitudinal persistence of protocol-measured biomarker association in repeat attenders.

Adverse means P1 is null/negative with CI excluding the smallest relevant effect, P1 is indistinguishable from placebo or plausible error sensitivity, P2 conflicts, a negative control is as large or larger, attendance weighting materially changes the result, or center dependence dominates. Conclude that the study does not provide specific evidence of persistent cross-marker renal-biomarker signal.

Inconclusive means any required field identity/unit/coding certificate fails, source integrity fails, intervals or ranges are uncertifiable, intervals are too wide to distinguish the prespecified effect, weights lack positivity, results depend materially on imputation or matched-sample choice, or error-grid conclusions vary across plausible assumptions. Inconclusive is not equivalent to no association.

WHAT REMAINS UNAVAILABLE
The available two-wave phenotype data cannot establish measured GFR, CKD progression, pathology, clinical staging consequences, completeness of medication exposure, causal effects, benefit from cystatin-C ordering, or generalizability outside eligible repeat attenders. Those claims require authoritative laboratory metadata and clinical pathology review, repeated measured GFR or adjudicated CKD outcomes, additional waves or external reliability data, external validation, and potentially a prospective clinical-impact study.
