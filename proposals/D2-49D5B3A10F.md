This is a provisional, falsifiable research hypothesis for episode 9. It is not an executed result, a selected candidate, or evidence of direct target action, causality, protection, clinical efficacy, or patient benefit.

## Proposed relationship

In the available E-MTAB-8873 rheumatoid-arthritis macrophage–fibroblast assay, the transcriptional response associated with MerTK inhibition will differ between macrophage contexts (MerTK/CD206-positive versus MerTK/CD206-negative macrophages). The primary estimand is a donor-aware, condition-verified context-by-inhibitor interaction, not a generic mean-expression change. A secondary descriptive question is whether the interaction separates a fibroblast-linked harmful program from a macrophage-linked resolving/protective program.

## Supporting observations and claim boundary

The closest inspected evidence supports that MerTK-positive synovial macrophage subsets are associated with remission and can induce repair responses in synovial fibroblasts, that macrophage functional reprogramming can alter inflammatory mediator production in arthritis, and that macrophage–fibroblast repair circuits are context-, timing-, and niche-dependent. These observations make a context-dependent MerTK modulation hypothesis plausible.

The strongest supported claim is therefore biological plausibility of a context-dependent macrophage–stromal response. The unresolved claim is whether the inhibitor-associated transcriptional pattern in this assay is reproducible and context-specific after accounting for donor, condition, timing, composition, and measurement artifacts. The available data do not establish that UNC106238 directly acts on MerTK, that the response is causal, or that an in-vitro pattern transfers to patients.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive results would be a reproducible context-by-inhibitor interaction with adequate precision, directionally consistent with the prespecified repair/harm contrast, and robust to donor and condition controls. Adverse results would be no interaction, an interaction in the opposite direction, or a pattern explained by donor/condition/composition artifacts. Inconclusive results would include low precision, missing or ambiguous context labels, insufficient independent donors, or failure to separate technical from biological variation.

A supportive result would justify a follow-up perturbation or independent-cohort study; it would not establish direct MerTK target action or clinical benefit. An adverse result would weaken the proposed context-specific therapeutic rationale and favor a revised target or assay design. An inconclusive result would identify the missing evidence required for a definitive test.
