TARGET ESTIMANDS AND ORDER OF OPERATIONS
For direction d, the primary target population is T_d, the baseline target-marker-at-least-60 population, not merely the selected repeat attenders. The primary estimand is the standardized risk difference in repeat threshold status under the two observed baseline exposure levels, averaged over the covariate distribution in T_d:

RD_d = |T_d|^{-1} sum_{i in T_d} [m_d(1,Z_i)-m_d(0,Z_i)],
where m_d(e,z)=Pr(Y_d=1 | R=1,E_d=e,Z_d=z) is modeled among observed first-repeat outcomes and transported to T_d under the explicit conditional attendance assumption Y_d(e) independent of R given (E_d,Z_d) within T_d. This is a descriptive transport/standardization estimand, not a causal effect; “under exposure level” is a model-standardization notation only.

Z_d contains the continuous baseline target-marker eGFR, baseline age, sex and elapsed years, with predeclared restricted cubic splines for baseline target-marker and elapsed years. Since T_d is defined by target-marker >=60, knots and any spline basis must be computed in T_d and evaluated with a documented boundary rule; never extrapolate outside the observed target-marker support. Sex is not adjusted unless field-31 coding is certified.

Three analyses must be kept distinct. (1) The unweighted primary descriptive analysis estimates the selected-attender estimand among T_d intersect {R=1}, with standardization over that observed cohort. (2) The measured-attendance weighted analysis estimates the stated T_d target estimand: fit the attendance model only within T_d with denominator pi_d(Z,E)=Pr(R=1 | T_d,Z,E), stabilized by the marginal or predeclared numerator Pr(R=1 | T_d), and use w=1/pi or stabilized w among R=1. Exposure is included in the attendance model because attendance may depend on the discordant marker. (3) A prespecified .005 probability-floor sensitivity uses pi=max(pi,.005), with no post hoc truncation choice. For weighted outcome regression, fit the outcome model among R=1,T_d using w and predict both exposure counterfactual model values for every baseline member of T_d, then average predictions over all T_d. This prevents the common error of applying weights to repeat attenders while standardizing over a different selected population. Report the unweighted selected-attender estimate separately and never call it the T_d population estimate.

A useful diagnostic is the direct weighted empirical distribution among R=1,T_d versus the full T_d distribution for every Z component. Report stabilized-weight mean, SD, min/max, 1st/99th percentile cutoffs, effective sample size (sum w)^2/sum(w^2), and standardized mean differences. FALSIFICATION AND INTERPRETATION
Support requires an estimable direction, positive prespecified direction, Holm-adjusted p-value meeting the family rule, a simultaneous interval excluding zero, acceptable overlap/calibration/separation, and concordance of the target-population weighted result with the unweighted selected-attender and floor sensitivity in direction and clinically material magnitude. A result in only one direction is direction-specific and cannot support reciprocal renal specificity. Adverse evidence includes null/reversed contrast, loss after target-marker adjustment, strong floor/truncation dependence, poor calibration, or support failure; this falsifies the incremental protocol-biomarker reclassification claim for that direction but does not prove absence of renal biology. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse cells, separation, unsupported extrapolation, unstable weights/bootstrap, failed equation/unit/sex certification, or unresolved assay traceability. A statistically positive result still supports only conditional protocol-biomarker threshold-status association in T_d; it does not support CKD progression, true GFR decline, causality, clinical benefit, or a treatment decision.
