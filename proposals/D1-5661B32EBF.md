# Asynchronous kidney–respiratory recovery after circulatory stabilization

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future Harbor solver must newly construct and evaluate a leakage-controlled, organ-recovery phenotype at an early ICU landmark. Completion requires:

1. a reproducible eligible-stay, stabilization-landmark, measurement-coverage, and outcome-ascertainment flow;
2. a row-level, clinical-time-audited six-hour kidney/respiratory recovery representation;
3. held-out cause-specific estimates for a prespecified four-level phenotype and a matched terminal-state/context baseline;
4. a substantive same-input chronological model as a sensitivity, with uncertainty and calibration;
5. observation- and treatment-process controls, temporal and label falsifications, and sensitivity to creatinine/urine and respiratory-support definitions; and
6. a conclusion audit mapping every claim to computed outputs.

No fitted result is claimed in this proposal.

This is an evolution of assessed-valid [prior hypothesis]. That parent asks whether late history before live ICU discharge survives discharge-selection correction. The present child moves the estimand earlier, before discharge selection, and tests a clinically interpretable organ-recovery distinction that the parent cannot resolve: whether kidney and respiratory recovery are synchronous or discordant after observable circulatory stabilization.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

Evidence currently supports only that MIMIC-IV contains dated ICU physiology, support, laboratory and urine-output observations and that subsequent ICU/hospital events can be reconstructed. It does not establish that an observable recovery pattern is a distinct physiologic state, that it causes deterioration, or that changing treatment based on it improves outcomes.

The unresolved prognostic claim is:

> Among ICU stays reaching an observable circulatory-stabilization landmark, patients with kidney-lag or respiratory-lag recovery during the next six hours have a higher risk of subsequent 24-hour ICU rescue/escalation or timed death than patients with concordant recovery, beyond the latest state, admission/stay context, active support and observation intensity.

The primary estimand is the held-out difference in cause-specific 24-hour cumulative-incidence/Brier-calibrated risk between the four prespecified recovery phenotypes and a matched terminal-state/context model. A secondary estimand tests whether chronological organization of the same six-hour measurements adds information beyond order-blind summaries. This is prognostic association and incremental information, not a treatment effect.

A supportive result is a reproducible phenotype contrast, with uncertainty excluding the null in the prespecified direction, stable calibration, and no comparable signal in process-only or time-shuffled controls. An adverse result is no contrast, a reversed contrast, or a signal explained by observation/treatment process. An inconclusive result is sparse phenotype coverage, poor overlap, wide uncertainty, or instability across predeclared measurement definitions; it does not prove absence of biologic heterogeneity.

## Population, landmark and outcome

Use the read-only MIMIC-IV 3.1 ZIP at `[internal dataset path]`, archive members named below. Include ICU stays with valid `intime < outtime`, linked `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, and `stay_id`, and restrict to adults using `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18` (report the MIMIC top-coded-age limitation). Use the first eligible ICU stay per subject-admission to avoid correlated repeated stays; retain a stay only when at least six hours remain after landmark and outcome follow-up is observable.

For each stay, scan candidate landmark hours from ICU `intime+6h` through `outtime-30h`. Define the first landmark h for which the preceding six hours have at least four valid MAP observations in at least three distinct hours, median MAP >=65 mmHg, no overlap with an active vasoactive interval, and no death before h. MAP is `icu/chartevents.charttime/valuenum` for itemids 220052 (arterial mean), 220181 (non-invasive mean), with arterial prioritized and non-invasive sensitivity. Vasoactive exposure is `icu/inputevents.starttime/endtime/itemid` for dictionary-confirmed norepinephrine 221906, vasopressin 222315, epinephrine 221289/229617, phenylephrine 221749/229630/229631/229632, dopamine 221662 and dobutamine 221653. The landmark rule is an observable operational definition of stabilization, not bedside adjudication of perfusion.

Use h through h+6h only to define recovery phenotype. Follow from h+6h through h+30h, excluding any event at or before h+6h. The primary competing first event is (R) major ICU support escalation or (D) timed death before h+30h; otherwise censor at ICU outtime/h+30h, whichever comes first, and report alive ICU discharge as a competing observation boundary. Define R using new/restarted vasoactive input intervals after h+6h, or a new invasive-ventilation procedure/ventilator-support record after h+6h, with item identities read from `icu/d_items`/`icu/procedureevents` and fixed before fitting. Report separate vasopressor and ventilatory components; do not call R organ failure without clinical adjudication. Death time comes from `hosp/admissions.deathtime` when timed and linked; a hospital-expire flag without deathtime is unknown, not a timed negative, and receives a bounds sensitivity.

## Exact measurements and phenotype

Kidney recovery uses serum creatinine from `hosp/labevents` itemid 50912 (label verified in `hosp/d_labitems`) by `hadm_id/subject_id`, `charttime`, `valuenum`, and urine output from `icu/outputevents` `charttime/value/itemid`, restricted to the solver-verified urine-output item rows and units. Compute change from the latest valid pre-h baseline to the h–h+6h window and urine mL/kg/h when patient weight is available; otherwise retain unnormalized urine as a sensitivity, never silently impute weight. Require prespecified minimum observations and show complete-case/coverage flow.

Respiratory recovery uses dated `icu/chartevents` values for FiO2 itemid 223835, oxygen flow 223834, PEEP 220339, respiratory rate 220210, SpO2 220277 and ventilator mode 223849, linked to `icu/d_items` for labels/units. Blood-gas oxygen-related fields, when present, are a sensitivity and not required for the primary phenotype. Summarize support intensity and direction over h–h+6h, with the exact unit and text-to-category mapping frozen before fitting.

Define kidney improvement as non-worsening creatinine trajectory plus improving/adequate urine-output trajectory; respiratory improvement as decreasing FiO2/oxygen-flow/PEEP or liberation from ventilator support without worsening oxygenation. Use clinically readable, predeclared standardized thresholds based on training data only, with a continuous-slope sensitivity. The four phenotype categories are concordant recovery (both improve), kidney-lag (respiratory improves but kidney does not), respiratory-lag (kidney improves but respiratory does not), and dual-persistent/worsening (neither improves). If one organ lacks required coverage, classify as unknown and report it; do not force a phenotype. The primary analysis uses only eligible complete phenotypes, with inverse-coverage weighting and complete-case bounds.

## Models and fair comparison

B0, the simple baseline, is penalized logistic cause-specific risk modeling (or discrete-time competing-risk regression) using the same latest pre-h and h values, admission/ICU context, active support, landmark hour, and observation counts. It includes no post-h+6h information. The main interpretable model adds only the prespecified four-level recovery phenotype and its fixed contrasts to B0. Compare held-out cause-specific cumulative incidence, integrated Brier score, calibration slope/intercept, calibration plots, and paired bootstrap uncertainty; report AUROC/AUPRC only secondarily.

The substantive alternative is a masked chronological GRU or equivalent small temporal model receiving exactly the same six-hour bins, source whitelist, missingness masks, context vector and subject-level split as the aggregate model. It is useful scientifically because it can reveal whether timing, persistence and cross-organ coupling contain information that a four-level summary discards; it is not selected for complexity. Train only within training folds, tune once within training data, and evaluate on a fixed patient-held-out split, with an anchor-year and care-unit sensitivity. A mechanistic alternative (joint latent kidney/respiratory slopes with cause-specific hazards) is deferred unless the solver can fit it within the declared CPU budget; it would be useful for uncertainty about latent recovery but risks strong untestable assumptions.

The actual future solver deliverable is newly fitted phenotype contrasts, B0 and phenotype-model predictions, and the matched chronological-model predictions, with confidence/credible intervals, coverage/overlap diagnostics, and falsification outputs. Discovery has not fitted these models.

## Controls, falsification and limits

Record observation counts, lab ordering, charting density, ICU location, active support and time since admission. Fit a process-only model from masks/counts/order intensity; a large process-only signal or disappearance of the phenotype contrast after process adjustment weakens the physiologic interpretation. Shuffle timestamps within each stay while preserving values and masks; a retained result indicates order-insensitive or process-driven signal. Permute phenotype labels within training folds; any comparable gain is a pipeline failure. Repeat with arterial-only versus non-invasive MAP, creatinine-only versus urine-supported kidney definitions, and support-only versus blood-gas respiratory definitions. Require no post-landmark outcome or disposition fields in predictors.

Use subject-level bootstrap or repeated held-out splits, report effective sample size for coverage weights, positivity/overlap, missing-label bounds, and subgroup stability by care unit, anchor year, sex and age band where supported. Do not use discharge notes, radiology text, or future transfers as predictors. The parent’s all-at-risk live-exit selection analysis is retained as a separate sensitivity only; it cannot turn this earlier prognostic analysis into a causal discharge or treatment analysis.

MIMIC does not provide adjudicated bedside perfusion, fluid balance intent, treatment indication, reliable clinical severity adjudication, radiographic lung status, clinician reasoning, or external validation. The study cannot establish that a kidney or respiratory lag is preventable, that a particular fluid/vasopressor/ventilator action is beneficial, that ICU rescue was appropriate, or that a bedside intervention would improve net benefit. Those claims require expert chart adjudication, richer physiologic data, external cohorts, or a prospective/interventional study.
