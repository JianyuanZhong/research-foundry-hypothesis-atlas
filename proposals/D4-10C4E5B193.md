# UKB-12: CAD PRS × ApoB × CRP Interaction and Residual CAD Risk

## Unresolved clinical question
Current CAD risk prediction relies heavily on lipid levels (LDL-C, ApoB) and genetic risk scores (PRS). However, the relative contribution of genetic risk vs. measured lipids vs. inflammation (CRP) to residual CAD risk is not well characterized. The unresolved claim: among participants with high CAD PRS, those with low ApoB but elevated CRP have different CAD risk than those with low ApoB and low CRP, suggesting that inflammation captures risk not explained by lipids or genetics.

## Leading claim vs strongest rival
- **Leading claim**: High PRS + low ApoB + high CRP → higher CAD risk than high PRS + low ApoB + low CRP (inflammation captures residual risk beyond lipids and genetics). [K1] establishes PRS as a clinically meaningful stratifier; [K2] establishes ApoB as an independent lipid biomarker; [K3] establishes CRP as the best-established biomarker of residual inflammatory risk.
- **Strongest rival**: CRP is a proxy for age, BMI, or general health status. After adjusting for age, sex, BMI, and genetic PCs, the CRP effect should attenuate to null.

## Discriminating observation
In the high-PRS stratum, compare CAD event rates between low-ApoB/high-CRP and low-ApoB/low-CRP participants. If the leading claim is correct, the high-CRP group should have significantly higher CAD risk after adjusting for age, sex, BMI, and genetic PCs.

## Analysis plan
1. **Primary analysis**: Cox proportional hazards model for CAD events
   - Exposure: ApoB × CRP interaction (continuous, centered)
   - Stratified by high PRS (top 25%) vs. low PRS (bottom 75%)
   - Adjusted for: age, sex, BMI, genetic PCs 1-10
   - Compare: high-PRS/low-ApoB/high-CRP vs. high-PRS/low-ApoB/low-CRP
2. **Secondary analysis**: Gradient Boosting classifier for CAD risk
   - Features: PRS, ApoB, CRP, Lp(a), age, sex, BMI, genetic PCs
   - SHAP values to quantify contribution of each feature
   - Compare: SHAP importance of CRP vs. ApoB in high-PRS stratum
3. **Sensitivity analysis**:
   - Restrict to participants without prior CAD (need date-based exclusion)
   - Adjust for Lp(a) in subset with data
   - Use different PRS thresholds (top 10%, top 25%, top 50%)

## Falsification criteria
- **Supportive**: Significant ApoB × CRP interaction in high-PRS stratum (p<0.05), with high-CRP group having higher CAD risk than low-CRP group after adjustment.
- **Adverse**: No significant interaction, or CRP effect attenuates to null after adjusting for age, sex, BMI, and genetic PCs.
- **Inconclusive**: Interaction is significant but effect size is small (HR<1.2), or results are inconsistent across PRS thresholds.

## Limitations
1. Single timepoint measurements for ApoB, CRP, and Lp(a)
2. CAD outcome is based on ICD10 codes, not clinical adjudication
3. Cannot exclude prior CAD without date-based analysis
4. CRP is a non-specific marker of inflammation
5. UKB population is relatively healthy (volunteer bias)
