# Selection and censoring boundary for the eICU testing-process transportability signal

Status: proposed substantive branch-B child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. No model fit or hypothesis result is claimed.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The parent asks whether early laboratory-result presence, frequency, and timing add transportability-sensitive information beyond laboratory values. Its hospital-held-out process permutation breaks patient linkage, but it still leaves two consequential interpretations unresolved:

1. The six-hour landmark conditions on remaining in the ICU and observable long enough to accumulate results. ICU discharge, early death, or early transition to end-of-life care can select a subgroup whose documentation process is unusually informative.
2. A same-hospital permutation can preserve stable case mix and hospital recording density. It does not show that a process increment is dynamic deterioration rather than a stable severity/documentation mixture.

The strongest inspected evidence supports only bounded premises. Temporally ordered EHR data can predict mortality across centers [K1]. Information presence can be predictive and missingness patterns can threaten transportability, but the inspected Gao evidence is abstract-only [K2]. External mortality performance may retain discrimination while local adaptation changes calibration and requires harmonization [K3]. None establishes the eICU mechanism.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among adult first ICU stays eligible at six hours, the process-aware model's hospital-held-out transport penalty is not solely a consequence of measured landmark/discharge or early end-of-life selection. Specifically, the process increment should remain after (a) a prespecified >720-minute landmark sensitivity, (b) inverse-probability standardization for measured six-hour eligibility, and (c) restriction to stays without an early carePlanEOL record. A state-matched within-hospital process permutation should attenuate a genuine patient-state increment; if it does not, stable case mix or workflow/documentation remains a stronger explanation.

This is a descriptive transportability and selection-boundary hypothesis, not a causal claim that testing, documentation, or end-of-life care causes death.

## Clinical importance and decision boundary

If the process increment survives selection audits and is removed by state matching, it is plausible that repeated testing reflects dynamic recorded illness and can help a hospital-held-out early review score. If it disappears after the no-EOL or >720-minute boundary, apparent transportability may be driven by discharge/end-of-life selection and should not justify deployment. If it survives state matching and the null retains the increment, a site-specific documentation or stable case-mix explanation is more credible, supporting data-feed harmonization and recalibration rather than more testing.

The consequential modeled action is a senior review of monitoring adequacy and goals-of-care context when predicted ICU-discharge mortality is at least 10%; 5% and 20% are secondary thresholds. This is not authorization to alter treatment or end-of-life decisions. Report alert burden and decision-curve net benefit against treat-none and treat-all. A process model that adds net benefit only in the early-EOL stratum is not a validated decision aid: it may duplicate documentation and requires clinical adjudication.

`D_AUC = (AUC(VP-GBM, internal)-AUC(VP-GBM, external)) - (AUC(V-GBM, internal)-AUC(V-GBM, external))`.
Define the analogous contrasts for Brier error, calibration error, and net benefit, with signs oriented so positive means an increment or improvement as appropriate.

The selection audit has three prespecified boundaries:

- `S6 = 1(unitdischargeoffset >360)` is the primary landmark selection. On the full adult first-admission denominator, report `S6), early discharge/death status, and EOL6 availability. Do not pretend that a post-360 outcome is defined for `S6=0`.
- `S720 = 1(unitdischargeoffset >720)` is a stricter descriptive landmark sensitivity using the same [0,360] features and outcome. Compare `D_720) to `D_AUC); it is a selection boundary, not a new causal estimand.
- `EOL6) is an audit-only indicator: at least one finite `cpleolsaveoffset` or `cpleoldiscussionoffset` in [0,360]. Do not use `carePlanEOL` or `activeupondischarge` as predictors. Report `D_{mathrm{noEOL}}) among `EOL6=0` and `D_{mathrm{EOL}}) separately only if event and hospital counts support an interval. An EOL record is a documented care-plan event, not proof of a clinical decision time or death time.

For measured selection standardization, fit a training-fold-only logistic model for `S6) using admission fields `age`, `gender`, `ethnicity`, and `admissionweight), with categories and medians learned in training folds. Use stabilized inverse-probability weights for S6=1, truncate only at prespecified [0.05,0.95] propensity bounds, report the fraction truncated and effective sample size, and use hospital-cluster bootstrap. This is a sensitivity estimand standardized over measured admission covariates; it cannot recover outcomes after early ICU discharge or correct unmeasured severity.

## Features and the stable-case-mix control

Within each outer training partition, select the 30 analytes with the largest distinct eligible-patient coverage among analytes with at least 1% coverage, using training hospitals only. For each selected analyte, retain the latest valid value and its original offset in [0,360], with no carry-forward. Impute values by training-fold medians. Value inputs are age, gender, ethnicity, admissionweight, and the selected lab values. Process inputs are per-analyte presence, valid-row count, distinct-offset count, first/last result offset, and overall valid-row/distinct-offset counts. These are recording proxies, not confirmed orders.

The main models use the same feature definitions as the parent. NC-State-GBM changes only the null assignment of the complete process vector: within each hospital, sort by SHA-256(`"eicu-process-state-null-v1\\0"+patientunitstayid`) and cyclically assign the next vector within a coarse, training-defined state stratum. The state stratum is the quintile of a value-only risk score from V-GBM, calculated out-of-fold for training stays and by the fold-fitted value model for held-out hospitals, with admission demographic/weight bins as tie-breakers. If a cell has fewer than 10 stays, use the prespecified adjacent-quintile merge and report the merge. Keep the original values, outcome, and hospital; use no process or outcome in the assignment.

This control is stronger than an unrestricted same-hospital permutation for the stable-case-mix rival, but it is not a causal adjustment: a value-only score is an imperfect state summary, and within-cell documentation can remain correlated with unmeasured severity. Retain the parent's unrestricted NC-GBM and cyclic offset-shift TS-GBM as secondary controls when feasible; the state-matched audit is the new primary mechanistic boundary.

## Models, split, uncertainty, and alternative selection

V-Logit is the interpretable elastic-net logistic baseline using demographics and value features. V-GBM is the capacity-matched value-only histogram gradient booster. VP-GBM is the identical booster with original process features; P-GBM is process-only. NC-State-GBM uses the same learner and hyperparameters as VP-GBM but the state-matched process vector. TS-GBM shifts only process offsets by t-180 when t>=180, otherwise t+180, as the parent's timing placebo. Categories, medians, analyte selection, class policy, seeds, and hyperparameters are learned within outer training folds only.

The primary split holds out hospitals. Sort hospital IDs by SHA-256(`"eicu-transport-v1\\0"+hospitalid`) and greedily balance eligible stays into five contiguous folds. Within training hospitals, reserve a fixed 20% patient-stratified internal sample using SHA-256(`"eicu-internal-v1\\0"+patientunitstayid`). No patient-only split is called external validation. The same folds, outcomes, prediction rows, bootstrap cluster indices, and uncertainty metrics are used for every model and sensitivity boundary.

For model m and evaluation e, let `I(m,e)=AUC(m,e)-AUC(V-GBM,e)`; orient Brier and calibration contrasts so positive means better performance or smaller error. Report `D_AUC`, `D_720`, `D_noEOL`, and weighted `D_IPW`, plus direct held-out differences. Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples with identical indices across paired models. Report per-hospital estimates, prevalence, event counts, effective sample sizes, propensity truncation, and missing EOL strata.

Baseline versus learned alternative reasoning: V-Logit is scientifically useful because its coefficients and calibration show whether a process increment is directionally consistent and interpretable under the same outcome and hospital split. V-GBM versus VP-GBM is the capacity-matched learned alternative: nonlinear value-process interactions can reveal whether process dependence occurs only in state-dependent combinations that a linear baseline misses. NC-State-GBM, not a larger model, is selected because it directly tests the stable-case-mix/documentation rival. Inputs are the same 0-360 demographics, values, and process summaries; the target is ICU-discharge mortality; evaluation is held-out-hospital discrimination, calibration, and 10% review net benefit with cluster uncertainty. CPU planning estimate: 16 CPUs, 64-128 GiB RAM, 2-6 hours for extraction, five-fold fits, selection sensitivities, and bootstrap; one-fold readiness should measure actual cost. This fits the configured planning envelope of 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, and 28,800 seconds. GPU is not selected: the data are tabular, the GBM and bootstrap are CPU-feasible, and an A100 would not answer the selection mechanism. A temporal neural model is deferred because it would add representation complexity without improving this selection falsification; reconsider only if a bounded diagnostic shows summary features lose relevant temporal information. ## Interpretation and falsification

Support for a patient-state/dynamic-deterioration interpretation requires a positive, uncertainly precise primary process increment; attenuation of the process increment in NC-State-GBM relative to VP-GBM; and broadly similar D across no-EOL, >720-minute, and measured-selection-weighted boundaries. This remains descriptive evidence that process features carry patient-linked information, not proof of physiology or causality.

Support for selection/documentation is a positive primary D that materially attenuates after >720 restriction, no-EOL restriction, or IPW; concentration of net benefit or calibration change in EOL6 or near-landmark discharges; or retention of a similar process increment in NC-State-GBM. This establishes a boundary where process prediction is compatible with stable case mix or workflow/end-of-life documentation; it does not establish that documentation caused error.

Adverse to the process-aware claim is an upper confidence limit below zero for D with VP-GBM no better in calibration or 10% net benefit, provided technical checks and control penalties do not explain it. Mixed results arise when state matching and selection boundaries disagree. Results are inconclusive when intervals span clinically meaningful values, EOL6 is too sparse, propensity weights are unstable, hospital/event counts fail, analyte selection changes materially, or the label/audit discordance is substantial.

The verifier can check cohort construction, joins, offsets, fold isolation, null permutations, model parity, predictions, metrics, bootstrap intervals, and whether conclusions match those outputs. It cannot determine whether a result was clinically an order, an interface omission, an end-of-life decision, or a useful alert. Those claims require clinician adjudication, order/specimen/interface data, a death timestamp, and prospective silent deployment/impact evaluation. No result supports changing monitoring, treatment, or goals-of-care practice without those studies.
