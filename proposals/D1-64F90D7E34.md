# Does early measurement opportunity explain transfer-source risk after a valid minute-60 landmark?

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct and analyze a leakage-safe, minute-60 landmark cohort of adult first ICU admissions in eICU 2.0, then estimate whether the observed 48-hour recorded ICU-unit mortality contrast between `Other Hospital` and `Direct Admit` is explained by early measurement opportunity, by measured early physiology, or by a residual source-associated trajectory signal.

Completion requires:

1. a frozen source/header/schema/hash audit and cohort-flow ledger;
2. an explicit pre-landmark exit ledger for every eligible stay;
3. a minute-60 risk-set ledger proving that all primary predictors precede the post-landmark risk interval;
4. an observation-opportunity/value ledger for 0–60 minutes with result-time rules;
5. nested transparent B0/B1/B2 competing-risk fits;
6. a matched early-trajectory/observation-process alternative on the identical risk set, inputs, hospital split and outcome;
7. standardized post-landmark cumulative incidences and Other Hospital minus Direct Admit contrasts with hospital-clustered uncertainty;
8. hospital-held-out transport, calibration/proper-score checks and prespecified falsifications;
9. a conclusion-to-output map distinguishing computational findings from clinical claims.

Discovery has fitted no model and establishes no clinical association or causal result.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The expert seed asks whether risk differences between interhospital-transfer and directly admitted patients become smaller after accounting for arrival organ injury. The parent adaptation sharpened that question to whether a source contrast is attenuated by early measurement opportunity and recorded values. Its remaining temporal flaw is substantive: a 0–60-minute feature block was being used with an outcome beginning at minute 0. A patient who died or left the ICU during that feature window could contribute an apparently informative missingness/opportunity pattern to a later outcome analysis, creating reverse-observation and immortal-time problems.

The repaired question is:

> Among patients known to remain under ICU-unit observation through minute 60, does the post-minute-60 recorded unit-death risk contrast by arrival source shrink after accounting for what was measured in the first 60 minutes, and does a time-ordered model localize any remaining contrast to worsening/recovery physiology rather than to the measurement process?

This matters because transfer-source labels may trigger different urgency and surveillance. If the portable contrast is largely an ascertainment signal, structured arrival reassessment could reduce avoidable information asymmetry. If it persists after a valid landmark and transport evaluation, source may identify residual unmeasured route, referral or severity differences that require prospective data. Neither result alone justifies a transfer policy.

The strongest supported claim before fitting is limited to data availability and a bounded feasibility audit: the frozen snapshot contains source labels, ICU-relative disposition, hospital identifiers, time-stamped vital/lab rows, and ICU-stay join keys. The source audit does not support a mortality association, a transfer effect, an organ-injury diagnosis, or a treatment-delay conclusion.

## Hypothesis and estimand

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: in the minute-60 landmark risk set, the standardized Other Hospital minus Direct Admit cumulative incidence of recorded ICU-unit death from minute 60 through minute 2,880 is positive in B0; adding 0–60-minute measurement opportunity (B1) attenuates the contrast; adding valid 0–60-minute values and missingness (B2) attenuates it further. The attenuation must be assessed only among patients observable at minute 60, so it is not an estimate of the effect of surviving or remaining in the ICU.

Define the primary contrast at 2,880 minutes as:

`Delta_k = CIF_death,OtherHospital^k(60,2880) - CIF_death,DirectAdmit^k(60,2880)`

under model k in {B0, B1, B2}. Report the opportunity step `Delta_0-Delta_1`, the value step `Delta_1-Delta_2`, and total attenuation `Delta_0-Delta_2`, with absolute and relative versions. Standardization averages each model's predicted competing-risk probabilities over the landmark cohort under each source label; it is descriptive/prognostic, not causal mediation.

Use a clinically consequential prespecified margin of 2 percentage points in absolute CIF difference and a 50% attenuation target. A supportive primary result requires the direction-preserving B0 contrast, a B2 attenuation of at least 50% of the B0 absolute contrast with uncertainty compatible with that criterion, and the same qualitative decomposition in held-out hospitals. The margin is an interpretation gate, not a claim that 2 percentage points is a validated treatment threshold.

This estimand deliberately excludes early deaths and alive exits from the primary post-landmark risk set. They are not discarded silently: their counts, source distribution, timing and disposition are reported as a pre-landmark competing-exit audit. A secondary descriptive analysis reports the full eligible cohort's 0–2,880-minute outcomes, clearly labeled as a different estimand and never combined with the landmark estimate.

## Population, landmark and outcomes

The unit is one ICU stay keyed by `patientunitstayid`. Include a patient row from `patient` if:

- parsed age is at least 18; parse `> 89` as 90 and retain an age-censored flag;
- `unitvisitnumber=1` and `unitstaytype=admit`;
- `hospitalid` is nonmissing;
- `hospitaladmitsource` is exactly `Other Hospital` or `Direct Admit`.

Keep all ICU stays sharing `uniquepid` in one data split. Do not substitute `unitadmitsource` for the primary exposure; use it only for a source-concordance sensitivity. Do not infer the actual transfer route, distance, transport time or referring facility from the label.

Time zero is ICU admission. All offsets are raw ICU-relative minutes. The primary landmark risk set R60 contains eligible stays with a valid numeric `unitdischargeoffset >= 60` and `unitdischargestatus` in `Alive` or `Expired`. Thus every primary subject is known to remain in the ICU unit through minute 60, regardless of whether the eventual disposition is an event or an administrative censor.

The audited eligible population contains 12,688 stays: 2,917 Other Hospital and 9,771 Direct Admit. There are 205 valid pre-landmark competing exits: 3 deaths and 26 alive exits among Other Hospital, and 14 deaths and 162 alive exits among Direct Admit. There are 12,483 stays with offset at least 60; one has unknown/blank unit-discharge status and is excluded from the primary R60 set and reported separately. The audited primary R60 counts are therefore 2,888 Other Hospital and 9,594 Direct Admit, 12,482 total. These are discovery counts, not fitted results; the solver must reproduce them from the frozen files.

For the primary post-landmark outcome, follow each R60 stay from 60 minutes until the earliest of 2,880 minutes or unit discharge:

- Event 1: `unitdischargestatus=Expired` and `60 <= unitdischargeoffset < 2880`.
- Event 2: `unitdischargestatus=Alive` and `60 <= unitdischargeoffset < 2880`, a competing end of ICU-unit observation.
- Administrative censoring: no qualifying unit exit before 2,880, including a unit discharge at or after 2,880.
- Unknown censoring: invalid/missing status or offset, with an explicit count and sensitivity.

Use ICU-unit disposition only. Do not use hospital-discharge status, hospital mortality, location as a proxy for death, or events after unit discharge as primary outcomes. Print all observed disposition/status values before freezing the event map. A discharge status says how the recorded ICU-unit stay ended; it does not establish adjudicated cause of death or complete vital status after unit exit.

A secondary event-specific report gives alive-exit CIF and the competing-state total. No claim about overall hospital mortality, post-ICU mortality, or clinical deterioration is made.

## Reverse-observation protection

The 0–60-minute feature ledger is built only after R60 eligibility is fixed. It uses observation/result offsets in [0,60] inclusive and never uses rows after the landmark. For every domain and variable, retain:

- whether at least one valid record existed;
- number of valid records;
- number of distinct observation/result timestamps;
- first valid value and its offset;
- last valid value and its offset;
- missingness/invalid-unit indicators;
- source-table row counts before and after filtering.

A feature can be used only if its recorded offset is no later than the feature-window boundary. Invalid negative offsets, sentinel values such as -1, nonnumeric results and invalid units are missing. For labs, the primary clock is `labresultoffset`; `labresultrevisedoffset` is used only in a prespecified timing sensitivity and never retroactively makes a post-landmark result pre-landmark.

This protects temporal ordering but does not make observation opportunity exogenous. Clinicians may measure more often because a patient is deteriorating, and transfer status may affect monitoring. Therefore B1 is not a causal mediator model. It estimates how much source-specific prognostic separation is removed by recorded opportunity in a fixed landmark population. The solver must show the fraction of each group's subjects with any pre-landmark opportunity and compare opportunity distributions, rather than interpreting missingness as normal physiology.

## Matched transparent models: B0/B1/B2

Use the same R60 subjects, horizon, outcome ledger, covariates, standardization, hospital split and uncertainty procedure for all models.

B0 is a transparent pooled piecewise-exponential cause-specific hazard model using 30-minute post-landmark bins through 2,880 minutes. Covariates are source label, age, age-censored flag, gender, ethnicity, unittype, hospital context, and prespecified post-landmark time-bin indicators. Include no 0–60 values or opportunities in B0. Use a source-by-hospital-context interaction only if prespecified and supported.

B1 adds only 0–60 opportunity/burden features: per supported vital/lab domain and analyte, indicator of any valid record, count, distinct timestamp count, and coverage/density summaries. B1 does not include measured values.

B2 adds valid 0–60 measured values and missingness indicators to B1. Use bounded transformations and development-only imputation/standardization. Do not use outcome-derived thresholds. Do not collapse value and opportunity into a single missing-value code.

Report model coefficients only as supporting artifacts; the primary outputs are standardized competing-risk CIFs, `Delta_0, Delta_1, Delta_2`, stepwise attenuation, cluster uncertainty, held-out performance and calibration. A predictive improvement without a stable decomposition is not a substantive success.

## Matched observation-process / early-trajectory alternative

The substantive alternative is a joint, low-dimensional observation-process and early-state model, denoted J, fit on the identical R60 cohort and split. Its purpose is scientific localization, not a contest against B2.

Partition 0–60 minutes into fixed 15-minute bins for observation indicators and 30-minute bins for physiologic states. In each bin, construct history-only features from the exact vital/lab fields above: last valid value and offset, change from the prior bin when available, number of records, and domain-specific observation indicators. An observation indicator in bin b may depend on source, baseline variables, prior observed history and prior state, but not on a value first recorded in the same bin. A measurement is never allowed to predict an outcome interval preceding its result offset.

Define development-frozen states from available physiology, with an explicit missing/unknown state rather than normal imputation: stable/observed, abnormal-but-not-worsening, worsening/persistent instability, and recovery/partially recovered. Thresholds and analyte dictionaries are frozen in development hospitals using clinical-range rules, not selected on held-out outcomes. The model has:

1. a measurement submodel for the probability/intensity of observing each domain in each bin;
2. a state-transition submodel allowing persistence, worsening and recovery;
3. a post-landmark competing-risk submodel for recorded unit death and alive unit exit.

Compare two prespecified J variants:

- J_obs: source may affect the observation intensities, while source has no direct post-landmark death/exit edge beyond baseline and state history;
- J_full: add a source term to state transitions and post-landmark competing hazards.

J_obs tests whether a source contrast can be represented as different ascertainment/surveillance. The residual J_full minus J_obs is evidence of a source-associated prognostic component not represented by recorded opportunity and state history; it is not proof of route biology or causal transfer harm. Both variants must be compared with the same B0/B1/B2 inputs and outcome, using held-out hospital CIF calibration, integrated Brier score or another proper competing-risk score, and uncertainty on standardized contrasts. Report state occupancy, transition probabilities and observation-intensity ratios, not just a single AUC.

This alternative adds information B2 loses: temporal ordering, persistence, recovery and the separation of “was measured” from “what value was recorded.” It does not solve informative observation mathematically. If support is too sparse to identify transitions or if state definitions are dominated by missingness, J is reported as infeasible/inconclusive and B0/B1/B2 remain the primary computable decomposition. Do not replace J with a neural sequence model merely to increase complexity.

## Splitting, overlap, transport and uncertainty

Use a fixed hospital-held-out split of approximately 20% of hospitals, stratified by hospital size and availability of both source arms. Keep all `uniquepid`-linked stays in one fold. Freeze dictionaries, transformations, imputations, state thresholds, model hyperparameters and calibration on development hospitals using grouped inner validation by hospital.

The landmark audit found 178 hospitals with at least one R60 stay and 110 with both arms. Report these counts, arm sizes and per-hospital support before fitting. The primary transport result is the held-out-hospital standardized decomposition. Report hospitals with only one source arm separately rather than silently extrapolating a within-hospital contrast. Add a sensitivity restricted to hospitals with at least 20 R60 patients per arm, a leave-one-large-hospital-out analysis, and a `uniquepid`-clustered uncertainty sensitivity.

Use a hospital-block bootstrap or hospital-clustered sandwich intervals for CIF contrasts and attenuation; preserve the split in resampling. If a source arm has inadequate events or state transitions, apply a frozen sparse-support gate and report the estimand as inconclusive rather than changing the horizon or merging outcomes.

## Falsification and sensitivity criteria

Required checks are:

- source-label permutation within hospital, repeated with preserved arm sizes;
- source-label permutation within hospital and measurement-density strata;
- restriction to hospitals with adequate two-arm overlap;
- leave-one-large-hospital-out;
- primary `hospitaladmitsource` versus `unitadmitsource` concordance;
- result-offset versus revised-offset laboratory ordering;
- alternative first-valid-value and same-time tie rules;
- APACHE-free versus APACHE-secondary models;
- low-, medium- and high-measurement-density strata;
- explicit accounting for pre-landmark deaths, alive exits and unknown status;
- separate death and alive-exit CIFs;
- a negative-control documentation event selected from a structured post-index field only if its timing is available and it is not a physiology proxy.

A strong source contrast after within-hospital source permutation, a result confined to dense-measurement hospitals, a direction reversal under trivial tie rules, or a contrast created primarily by post-event records falsifies the intended ascertainment/physiology interpretation. A model that uses same-bin observations to predict earlier outcomes fails temporal validation.

## Supportive, adverse and inconclusive results

Supportive evidence is a positive B0 post-landmark contrast, meaningful opportunity/value attenuation under B1/B2, directionally consistent held-out-hospital decomposition, and J showing that residual source separation is concentrated in interpretable worsening/persistence or recovery states rather than an unexplained direct source edge. It supports a prospective hypothesis that structured, time-stamped arrival reassessment could improve risk information.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversal in B0, no attenuation after opportunity/value adjustment, a residual contrast that is not transportable, or a J result in which source separation remains unexplained and observation intensity does not differ. This rejects the corresponding “early recorded information explains the portable source contrast” interpretation; it does not demonstrate that transfer is harmless or equivalent.

Inconclusive evidence includes inadequate two-arm hospital overlap, sparse post-landmark deaths or states, wide cluster intervals crossing the clinical margin, unstable analyte dictionaries, materially different result/revision timing, unresolved unknown disposition, or failure of J identifiability. Inconclusive results require a data-quality report, not a positive conclusion.

## Evidence limits

Computationally checkable claims include source and schema hashes, headers, row counts, source-value-unit ledgers, cohort flow, landmark eligibility, event ordering, join integrity, hospital overlap, split integrity, fitted parameters, predicted CIFs, attenuation contrasts, uncertainty, calibration, proper scores, state occupancy and falsification outputs.

The available eICU data do not establish actual transfer route, transport duration, pre-arrival treatment, referral rationale, time-varying treatment administration or indication, adjudicated AKI/organ injury, physiological waveform-level state, extubation, bedside utility, patient preference, overall mortality or causal treatment/transfer effects. `vitalPeriodic` is a five-minute summary stream, not raw waveform data. Public narrative note sections are removed; retained structured note fields do not support validated comprehensive clinical-text extraction. Hospital `region`, teaching status and bed-size category are context variables, not hospital quality measures.

Clinical adjudication, expert review and another prospective or externally linked study would be required to claim that a source label causes risk, that a specific route or delay explains a residual signal, that an intervention improves outcomes, or that a threshold is safe and useful. An automatic verifier can check the computation and whether conclusions follow from reported outputs; it cannot establish those unavailable clinical claims.

## Alternatives, compute and selection

The transparent B0/B1/B2 family is selected as the primary estimand because it is auditable and directly decomposes source-only, opportunity-only and value-containing information on a fixed landmark population. J is retained as the substantive mechanistic alternative because temporal states and observation intensities answer whether the same apparent signal is ascertainment, persistence or recovery; it is not selected for a predictive score.

A learned irregular-sequence model is deferred. It could encode ordering and missingness flexibly, but eICU’s sparse, site-dependent observation process and lack of raw waveforms make a black-box sequence representation less informative than an auditable J model for this question. Revisit it only if J has adequate state support, stable held-out transport, and a preregistered model can use the identical history-only inputs, split, competing outcomes and calibration/proper-score evaluation. This is a scientific deferral, not a neural-network or GPU prohibition.

The full future solver envelope is the configured 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, up to 8 A100 GPUs and 28,800 seconds; discovery science is separately limited to 7,200 seconds. CPU-first is appropriate for chunked gzip extraction, ledgers and low-dimensional B0/B1/B2/J fits. A GPU is optional for a later learned-sequence sensitivity only after the scientific gate; no GPU feasibility claim is needed for the selected design. These estimates are planning estimates except for the measured source/header audit and the measured landmark counts above. The prior full multi-source scan interruption at its 300-second ceiling is not evidence of absent data; the solver must declare outputs/checkpoints and report actual runtime.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted landmark competing-risk contrasts and the matched J observation/state decomposition, with transport and uncertainty artifacts. No fitted result, clinical benefit or causal conclusion is claimed here.
