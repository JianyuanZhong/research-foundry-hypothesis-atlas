# Count-reconciled paired-change study after TACE2, with repeated trajectories downgraded to descriptive feasibility

## Targeted repair and clinical question

This child preserves the assigned common-time-zero study: adults with coded HCC and an earliest adjacent pair of strict qualifying TACE patient-day episodes 14–180 days apart; TACE2 is time zero; prior matched systemic medication records are excluded; institutional observation through day 14 is required; and the exposure is only a systemic HCC-directed medication record during TACE2 days 1–14 versus an ambiguity-excluded no-systemic-record pathway. It does not treat a record as administration or a later third TACE as a baseline comparator.

The unresolved clinical question remains whether the immediate systemic-record pathway is associated with a different day-30–90 within-person hepatic-reserve change rather than merely a different baseline liver state, calendar/agent channeling, or differential laboratory surveillance. The strongest currently supported claim is narrower: the exact complete-source trajectory audit demonstrates paired-assay feasibility at the prespecified threshold, inadequate exposed repeated-bin coverage for trajectory modeling, abundant marginal availability of candidate non-hepatic controls, and an unadjusted direction that is adverse to the prespecified favorable hypothesis. It does not estimate an adjusted pathway effect.

The hypothesis to be tested is that, after locked pre-TACE2 confounding adjustment and observation-process sensitivity, the immediate systemic-record pathway has less albumin decline without greater total-bilirubin increase than the no-systemic-record pathway. The preliminary descriptive audit instead shows larger mean albumin decline and larger mean bilirubin increase in the systemic-record arm. That observation is an adverse directional warning, not evidence that medication was administered or caused harm.

## Actual assay audit and implications

The completed audit scanned all laboratory records for its reconstructed cohort and retained 2,273,443 numeric, timestamped laboratory rows; it was not a row sample. For exact `Albumin`, paired baseline (days -30 to -1) and day-30–90 follow-up were available for 100/320 systemic14 and 399/1,518 no-systemic14 patients. Their unadjusted mean changes (follow-up minus baseline) were -2.080 and -1.253, respectively, an unadjusted arm difference of approximately -0.827 on the unvalidated recorded scale. Median changes were -2.0 and -1.0; median baseline values were 39.5 and 40.0; selected follow-up days had medians 57.5 and 59.0; and both arms had a median of two eligible follow-up measurements.

For exact `Total Bilirubin`, the same arm counts were paired: 100 and 399. Unadjusted mean changes were +10.063 and +6.723, respectively, an unadjusted arm difference of approximately +3.340 on the unvalidated recorded scale. Median changes were +4.2 and +2.7; median baselines were 10.9 and 12.3; selected follow-up-day medians were 57.5 and 59.0; and both arms had a median of two eligible follow-up measurements. Thus both unadjusted mean-change directions are adverse to the prespecified favorable hypothesis. These figures are preliminary descriptions from the unreconciled 320/1,518 cohort. They are not adjusted for baseline severity, calendar/agent selection, or observation, have no confidence intervals here, use units that are not validated in the extract, and cannot support medication-harm, toxicity, causality, or clinical-significance claims.

Repeated-bin feasibility fails the proposal's own gate. Using fixed days 30–44, 45–59, 60–74, and 75–90 bins, only 47 systemic14 versus 157 comparator patients had at least two observed bins for albumin, and only 7 versus 24 had at least three; total bilirubin had the same counts. Because the smaller arm has fewer than the prespecified 50 patients with at least two bins, no mixed-effects, spline, random-slope, or pathway-by-time trajectory model is permitted as confirmatory or secondary inference. Report only arm/bin observation counts and descriptive patient-level slopes, clearly labeled as selected-observation summaries. The audit's median slopes may be tabulated for reproducibility but cannot rescue trajectory inference. Do not weaken the gate, pool assays, interpolate missing bins, or choose another window after viewing outcomes.

## Interpretation branches

* Supportive: after count reconciliation, both adjusted assays directionally show less worsening in the systemic-record pathway, overlap/ESS and observation gates pass, complete-case and observation-weighted directions agree, and negative-control, pseudo-change, ontology, calendar, and timing checks do not materially contradict the finding. This supports only a robust recorded-pathway association.
* Adverse: reconciled, adjusted and adequately weighted estimates retain greater albumin decline and/or bilirubin increase with sensitivity agreement. This argues against assuming a liver-preserving association. It still cannot show medication harm or toxicity because indication, baseline severity, administration, dose, intent, outside care, and post-TACE events remain incompletely observed.
* Precise null: reconciled confidence intervals are narrow enough under prespecified scale-specific margins to exclude a material recorded-scale contrast while every measurement, overlap, observation, ontology, and falsification gate passes. Absent validated units/thresholds, this is not clinical equivalence or safety.
* Falsified: count reconciliation shows the pathway cannot be implemented as written; generic or bevacizumab ambiguity cannot be separated; strong pre-index/negative-control patterns appear; or overlap, calendar, ontology, or observation diagnostics invalidate the claimed robust post-index pathway contrast.
* Inconclusive: counts remain unreconciled; exposed paired n falls below 100; repeated trajectories remain below their gate; intervals are wide; weights are unstable; adjusted and observation-weighted directions conflict; timing is not comparable; or adjudication needed for the claimed endpoint is unavailable. The current trajectory analysis is already descriptive/infeasible under its >=50-exposed gate, regardless of later primary paired-change results.

## Computational and clinical claim limits

The experiment can verify source filtering, discordant cohort assignments, timestamps, exact-assay paired changes, descriptive bin coverage, missingness, overlap/ESS, weighting, and negative-control/pseudo-change outputs. An automatic verifier must require every reported number and conclusion branch to link to these computed artifacts, and must reject a medication-harm or clinical-benefit conclusion even if the adjusted direction is adverse or supportive. It cannot validate medication administration/adherence/intent, laboratory units/reference ranges, Child-Pugh or performance status, radiographic response/progression, TACE refractoriness, decompensation/toxicity attribution, outside-care completeness, mortality, or survival. Those stronger claims require medication-administration evidence, clinician imaging/intent adjudication, validated units and clinical outcomes, and vital-status/out-of-system linkage in another study.
