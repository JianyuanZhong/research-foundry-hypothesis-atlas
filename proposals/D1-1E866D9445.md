# Post-TACE AFP Kinetics as a Dynamic Predictor of HCC Recurrence at 12 Months (v2 — targeted repair)

**Parent:** [prior hypothesis] (v1, selected for compilation in episode 3)
**Dataset:** HCC (single-institution, 43,815 patients, 2010–2026)
**Draft ID:** hcc-ep4-lead

## 0. What changed in v2 and why (repair rationale)

Episode 4 probes resolved the two unresolved claims flagged in v1 and exposed one design defect:

1. **Cohort size was over-estimated in v1 (corrected).** v1 estimated 2,000–5,000 patients after all filters. Actual probe results: 3,206 patients have both pre-TACE AFP (≤14d) and post-TACE AFP (28–56d); only **767** have ≥365 days of follow-up. A 12-month recurrence endpoint requires ≥365d observation, so the primary cohort is ~767 (range 600–900 after remaining exclusions), not 2,000–5,000. Power implications are now explicit (Section 8).
2. **The v1 primary endpoint (free-text recurrence diagnosis) is not directly timestamped.** The diagnoses table has no time column; v1 imputed recurrence timing from encounter dates, which v1 itself flagged as a limitation. v2 replaces the primary endpoint with **repeat TACE at 30–365 days after index TACE**, which is directly timestamped (`开始时间` in the procedures table) and observed in 7,016/19,610 (36%) of TACE patients. The free-text recurrence diagnosis is retained as a secondary/sensitivity endpoint.
3. **No defensible death signal exists in this EHR (verified, documented).** Probes of 出入转 (only 患者主索引+就诊号, no status), 病历文书 (0 coded death mentions in 出院情况/出院诊断; 27 free-text "death" mentions in 诊疗经过, all narrative risk disclaimers), and diagnoses (death terms are disease names like 梗死) confirm there is no coded mortality. Last-encounter-gap imputation is **not** defensible: 76% of TACE patients (15,129/19,610) have their last visit <365d after index TACE, so a "death" class would be dominated by loss-to-follow-up. **Consequence: mortality is out of scope for this study.** The sibling candidate ([prior hypothesis], systemic therapy timing → 12-month survival) remains on the board with this blocker documented; it cannot be repaired without external mortality data.

These are substantive design changes (endpoint, cohort, estimand), not documentation-only edits. The scientific question, exposure, and hypothesis are unchanged.

## 1. Scientific Question and Hypthesis

**Question:** Does the dynamic change in alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) from pre-TACE to 4–8 weeks post-TACE predict HCC recurrence within 12 months, independent of baseline AFP level, liver function, and tumor burden?

**Hypothesis:** In patients with HCC treated with TACE, a greater proportional decline in AFP (≥50% reduction from pre-TACE baseline) within 4–8 weeks post-treatment is associated with significantly lower 12-month recurrence risk (HR < 0.70) after adjustment for baseline AFP, liver function (albumin, total bilirubin), and tumor burden. The dynamic AFP response captures information about treatment efficacy and residual tumor biology not available from the baseline AFP measurement alone.

**Falsification:** If the adjusted HR for AFP decline ≥50% vs. <50% is ≥0.85 (95% CI includes 1.0) AND the incremental C-index improvement over a baseline-only model is <0.02 AND the categorical response model shows no significant trend (p > 0.10), the hypothesis is not supported.

## 2. Clinical Importance

- HCC recurrence after TACE occurs in 60–80% of patients within 2 years, driving mortality [K1].
- Current prognostic models for post-TACE recurrence use **baseline** (pre-treatment) variables as static predictors [K1, K3]. The dynamic response of AFP to treatment is a biologically distinct signal: it reflects both tumor necrosis (successful treatment) and residual/progressive disease (treatment failure).
- The Palihati 2026 review explicitly identifies "dynamic serum biomarkers such as AFP kinetics" as an emerging but under-studied dimension for treatment response prediction [K2].
- Clinically, identifying patients with poor AFP response at 4–8 weeks post-TACE could trigger earlier intensification of surveillance (e.g., 6-week vs. 12-week imaging) or earlier consideration of alternative therapies (ablation, systemic therapy).

## 4. Population Definition

### Inclusion Criteria
1. Adult patient (age ≥ 18) with HCC diagnosis (诊断名称 contains `肝细胞癌` or `肝癌` or `HCC`)
2. Received TACE (procedure name contains `栓塞` or `TACE` in procedures table)
3. Has pre-TACE AFP (`甲胎蛋白`, numeric-parseable, within 14 days before index TACE `开始时间`)
4. Has post-TACE AFP (`甲胎蛋白`, numeric-parseable, between 28 and 56 days after index TACE `开始时间`)
5. Has ≥365 days of follow-up (last encounter `就诊时间` ≥365 days after index TACE `开始时间`)

### Exclusion Criteria
1. Prior HCC treatment (surgery, ablation, or prior TACE) before the index TACE
2. Concurrent or prior systemic therapy (medications table: `索拉非尼`, `仑伐替尼`, `帕博利珠`, `阿替利珠`, `信迪利`, etc.)
3. Hepatic resection or liver transplantation before or within 30 days of index TACE
4. AFP₀ > 1000 ng/mL (assay saturation, different biological behavior)
5. Missing baseline liver function (albumin AND total bilirubin both unavailable within 7 days of TACE)

### Verified Cohort Size (ep4 probe, not an estimate)
- TACE patients with parseable index date: 19,610
- With pre-TACE AFP (≤14d): 15,626
- With post-TACE AFP (28–56d): 3,737
- With BOTH: **3,206**
- Of those, with ≥365d follow-up: **767** ← primary cohort
- After remaining exclusions (prior treatment, systemic therapy, AFP₀>1000, missing liver function): expected **600–900**
- Event rate (repeat TACE 30–365d): 36% in all TACE patients; expected ~250–320 events in the primary cohort

## 5. Outcome Definition (v2)

**Primary outcome: repeat TACE at 30–365 days after index TACE** (time-to-event, directly timestamped).
- Event: first TACE procedure with `开始时间` in (index+30d, index+365d]
- Censoring: last encounter `就诊时间` if before the event and before 365d
- Time scale: days from index TACE `开始时间`

**Secondary outcome: free-text recurrence diagnosis within 12 months** (v1 composite, sensitivity only):
- Explicit recurrence diagnosis (patterns above) OR new HCC diagnosis >30d post-TACE
- Timing imputed from encounter `就诊时间` (limitation: no diagnosis timestamp)

**Death:** out of scope (Section 3, documented absence).

## 6. Exposure Definition (AFP Kinetics) — unchanged from v1

**Index TACE date:** `开始时间` of the first qualifying TACE procedure.

**Pre-TACE AFP (AFP₀):** most recent `甲胎蛋白` within 14 days before index TACE; numeric-parseable.

**Post-TACE AFP (AFP₁):** most recent `甲胎蛋白` between 28 and 56 days after index TACE; numeric-parseable; if multiple in window, use the LAST.

**Derived variables:**
1. AFP decline ratio: `AFP₁ / AFP₀`
2. AFP decline percentage: `(AFP₀ - AFP₁) / AFP₀ × 100`
3. AFP response category (primary):
   - "Complete response": AFP₁ < 20
   - "Partial response": AFP₁ ≥ 20 AND decline ≥ 50%
   - "No response": decline < 50%
   - "Progression": AFP₁ > AFP₀
4. Log-transformed AFP₁: `log(AFP₁ + 1)`

**Sensitivity analyses:** alternative windows (21–42d, 42–84d); median split of decline ratio; exclude AFP₀ < 20.

## 7. Covariates — unchanged from v1

**Baseline (pre-TACE, within 7 days):** age, sex, log(AFP₀), albumin, total bilirubin, direct bilirubin, ALT/AST (if available), GGT (if available), platelet count (if available).

**Tumor burden proxy:** number of TACE procedures within 90 days of index TACE; number of TACE procedures in the 12 months before index TACE.

**Treatment context:** department of TACE (`介入手术室` vs. other); concurrent RFA (`射频消融` within 14 days of TACE).

## 8. Analysis Plan (v2, power-corrected)

### Primary Analysis
**Model 1 (Baseline-only Cox):**
```
Cox PH: time-to-repeat-TACE ~ AFP₀ + albumin + total_bilirubin + age + sex + tumor_burden
```
**Model 2 (Dynamic AFP Cox):**
```
Cox PH: time-to-repeat-TACE ~ AFP₀ + AFP_decline_ratio + albumin + total_bilirubin + age + sex + tumor_burden
```
**Model 3 (Categorical response Cox):**
```
Cox PH: time-to-repeat-TACE ~ AFP_response_category + AFP₀ + albumin + total_bilirubin + age + sex + tumor_burden
```

### Power (v2 correction)
With n ≈ 767 and expected event rate ~36% (~276 events), the study has 80% power at α=0.05 to detect:
- HR ≈ 0.75 for the ≥50%-decline vs. <50% contrast (assuming 50/50 group split)
- HR ≈ 0.80 with a 60/40 split
The v1 hypothesis threshold (HR < 0.70) is at the edge of detectable power; the falsification threshold (HR ≥ 0.85) is comfortably detectable. If the primary cohort is <600, the study is underpowered for HR < 0.75 and the claim scope must be narrowed to the falsification test only.

### Discrimination
- C-index (Harrell's) for each model, 95% CI (200 bootstrap resamples)
- **Key comparison:** ΔC-index = C-index(Model 2) − C-index(Model 1)
- NRI and IDI for Model 2 vs. Model 1

### Calibration
- Calibration plots (predicted vs. observed) at 12 months for Model 1 and Model 2

### Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Secondary endpoint:** free-text recurrence diagnosis (v1 composite) — expect lower event count, higher misclassification
2. **Alternative AFP windows:** 21–42 days and 42–84 days
3. **Exclude low-baseline AFP:** AFP₀ < 20
4. **Time-varying AFP:** if ≥3 AFP measurements available, fit a simple linear slope (AFP change per week) in the first 8 weeks
5. **Assay stability:** stratify by calendar year (2010–2017 vs. 2018–2026) to test the single-platform assumption
6. **Relaxed follow-up:** 180d follow-up requirement (enlarges cohort to ~1,500–2,500; endpoint becomes repeat TACE 30–180d) — descriptive only

### Statistical Testing
- Primary: adjusted HR for AFP decline ≥50% vs. <50% (Model 3)
- Secondary: continuous AFP decline ratio (per 0.1 decrease in ratio)
- Interaction: AFP decline × baseline AFP
- Two-sided α = 0.05, no multiplicity adjustment (exploratory)

### Software
- Python 3.11: pandas, scipy, lifelines (Cox PH), scikit-learn (C-index, NRI)
- CPU only; expected runtime < 30 minutes for cohort construction + model fitting
- Memory < 8 GB (2.2 GB labs file, chunked reads)

## 9. Falsification Criteria (v2)

**NOT SUPPORTED** if:
1. Adjusted HR for AFP decline ≥50% vs. <50% ≥ 0.85 (95% CI includes 1.0)
2. AND ΔC-index (Model 2 vs. Model 1) < 0.02
3. AND categorical response model shows no significant trend (p > 0.10)

**SUPPORTED** if:
1. Adjusted HR < 0.70 (95% CI excludes 1.0)
2. AND ΔC-index ≥ 0.03
3. AND significant dose-response trend (p < 0.05)

**INCONCLUSIVE:** HR 0.70–0.85, or ΔC-index 0.02–0.03, or significant trend but HR not < 0.70. With n ≈ 767, an inconclusive result cannot be distinguished from underpowering for HR 0.70–0.80; this is a limitation, not a failure.

## 10. Limitations (v2)

1. **Repeat TACE as recurrence proxy:** repeat TACE captures recurrences/progression that are clinically actionable and treated with TACE; it misses recurrences managed with other modalities (ablation, surgery, systemic therapy) or detected but not treated. This biases toward under-ascertainment of the full recurrence burden, but the proxy is directly timestamped and clinically meaningful. The free-text diagnosis endpoint (sensitivity) partially addresses this.
2. **No death signal (verified):** mortality and competing-risk analyses are out of scope. Last-encounter-gap imputation was rejected (76% of TACE patients have last visit <365d after index TACE).
3. **Cohort size:** n ≈ 767 is adequate for the falsification test but underpowered for the full support threshold (HR < 0.70). A negative or inconclusive result must be interpreted in light of this.
4. **No unit column for labs:** cross-assay pooling unsupported; single-platform assumption tested by calendar-year stratification.
5. **Single institution, 15-year span (2010–2026):** treatment practices and assay platforms may have changed over time.
6. **Tumor burden proxy:** number of TACE sessions, not actual tumor size/number (imaging not available).
7. **Selection bias:** patients who receive follow-up AFP at 4–8 weeks may differ from those who do not (informative censoring).
8. **No HBV status:** etiology cannot be stratified.
9. **Free-text diagnosis for secondary endpoint:** no timestamp; misclassification risk.

## 11. Method Alternatives

### Alternative A: Machine Learning (XGBoost)
- XGBoost with SHAP, as in [K3]. Same covariates + AFP kinetics features; binary repeat-TACE at 12 months; time-based 60/20/20 split; AUC + calibration + SHAP.
- **Why not primary:** with n ≈ 767, ML models risk overfitting relative to the ~10-covariate Cox model; the primary scientific question (does AFP kinetics add value beyond baseline AFP?) is a direct, interpretable Cox comparison.
- **When to prefer:** if Cox shows strong non-proportional hazards or clearly non-linear AFP decline effect.

### Alternative B: Landmark Analysis with Time-Varying Cox
- Treat post-TACE AFP as time-varying covariate; uses all AFP measurements.
- **Why not primary:** more complex; informative censoring; may be overpowered by measurement frequency rather than biological signal.
- **When to prefer:** if the 4–8 week window shows weak signal but a longer-term trend is informative.

### Preferred: Cox Regression (Model 1 vs. Model 2)
1. The primary question is whether AFP kinetics **adds predictive value beyond baseline AFP** — a direct, interpretable Cox comparison.
2. n ≈ 767 is adequate for Cox with ~10 covariates but marginal for stable XGBoost with 20+ features.
3. The HR for AFP decline is directly clinically interpretable.
4. ΔC-index directly quantifies incremental predictive value.
5. CPU-only, fast, transparent.

**Decision rule:** if Cox shows significant non-proportional hazards (Schoenfeld residuals p < 0.05) or strongly non-linear AFP decline effect, proceed to XGBoost as secondary analysis.

## 13. What Would Change Understanding

**If supported:** Establishes that post-TACE AFP kinetics is an independent, clinically actionable predictor of 12-month recurrence beyond baseline AFP and liver function. Provides a simple, inexpensive (routine blood test) biomarker for risk stratification; informs timing of follow-up imaging; guides treatment intensification decisions; adds a dynamic dimension to current static prognostic models.

**If not supported:** Suggests the AFP response to TACE is largely determined by baseline tumor biology and liver function, and the dynamic change does not add independent predictive value. Redirects attention to other dynamic biomarkers (AFP-L3 ratio, PIVKA-II, inflammatory markers); supports baseline AFP + liver function as sufficient for risk stratification.

**If inconclusive:** May indicate the 4–8 week window is suboptimal, or the effect is modified by baseline AFP (interaction), or the cohort is underpowered (n ≈ 767). Would motivate a larger multi-institutional study or a different time window.
