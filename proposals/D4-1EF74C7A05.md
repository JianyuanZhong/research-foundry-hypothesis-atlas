# Baseline-only decision value of continuous cystatin-C discordance after a borderline log-loss signal

## Lineage and substantive advance

This is a prospective child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent's UK Biobank snapshot, one-to-one joins, selected-cohort boundary, inclusive 2–8-year outcome window, outcome definition, baseline biomarker fields, ten repeated outcome-stratified five-fold cross-fitting, fixed L2 logistic regression family, aggregate-only publication boundary, and all noncausal/evidence limitations.

The parent established a reproducible, strongly negative held-out log-loss interaction for `Z = log(eGFRcys_0/eGFRcr_0)` near baseline creatinine-eGFR 60–75, but did not establish fixed-capacity concentration: the global 10% event difference was only +1.0 (98% interval -6.401 to 8.5), the borderline global contribution was -2.4, and elapsed repeat time was included in the parent model even though it is future-known at baseline. The unresolved decision question is therefore:

> **Within the same selected evidence boundary, does adding baseline-known continuous cystatin-C/creatinine discordance produce positive, threshold-specific decision-curve net benefit for a predeclared action, beyond a baseline-only creatinine model, at clinically interpretable risk thresholds or fixed capacities?**

The substantive advance is not another loss or localization test. It tests whether the localized probabilistic signal changes an explicit flagging policy. The action is deliberately limited to *offering or prioritizing confirmatory renal-function reassessment*, not treatment, diagnosis, or a claim that the action benefits patients. Net benefit is a decision-analytic estimand under an explicitly hypothetical harm-to-benefit ratio, not observed clinical utility.

The strongest claim already supported is protocol-bounded: in these selected complete first-repeat attenders, adding baseline `Z` improved held-out log loss on average and the loss increment was concentrated in the baseline eGFRcr 60–<75 stratum, while the inherited global 10% concentration gate failed. The untested claim is that this information yields positive and practically nontrivial decision value when used without future-known elapsed time. This experiment cannot establish CKD, measured GFR, a causal effect of cystatin-C testing, patient benefit, or transportability.

## Primary decision-curve estimands

The primary action is `A=1`: flag a participant for an additional confirmatory renal-function reassessment/referral discussion during the outcome horizon. Since the data contain neither actions nor harms, this is a hypothetical policy label, not an observed intervention.

For a predicted risk `p` and a predeclared threshold `t`, set `A_m(t)=I(p_m >= t)` separately for `m in {M0,Mc}`. Use the conventional threshold odds `w(t)=t/(1-t)` and define participant-average net benefit for model `m` as

`NB_m(t) = mean[A_m(t) Y - w(t) A_m(t) (1-Y)]`.

Also report the equivalent number of net true-positive events per 1,000 participants, `1000*NB`, and the paired incremental estimand `DeltaNB(t)=NB_Mc(t)-NB_M0(t)`. Report sensitivity, flagging proportion, PPV, false-positive proportion, and event capture descriptively; these are not substitutes for NB.

The primary threshold is `t=0.02` (2% predicted repeat-threshold risk), chosen before fitting because it is an interpretable low-risk flagging threshold close to the inherited cohort event prevalence (309/16,372 = 1.888%). Ordered secondary thresholds are exactly `t=0.01, 0.05, 0.10`; no other threshold may be promoted to a confirmatory result. For context, plot/report treat-none `NB=0` and treat-all `NB=prevalence - (1-prevalence)w(t)` using the observed selected-cohort prevalence, while clearly labeling both as descriptive decision-analytic references. Because no harm or benefit was measured, the odds weights are hypothetical and sensitivity to `w` must not be interpreted as patient utility.

The primary policy estimand is the paired `DeltaNB(0.02)` averaged over the ten held-out repeats. The primary practical margin is +0.005 net benefit, equivalent to five additional net true-positive events per 1,000 under the stated hypothetical weight. This margin is an operational decision-analysis margin, not a clinically validated minimal important difference. Secondary thresholds use a simultaneous 98% family interval across the four thresholds and may not override a failed primary gate.

To connect directly to the parent's failed capacity result, also calculate the fixed-capacity policy contrast at exactly 5%, 10%, and 20% flagged capacity. Within each held-out fold, select the top predicted risks with deterministic descending probability and `eid` tie-break, using floor-plus-largest-remainder quotas so the global target is exactly 5%, 10%, or 20% of S_A in each repeat. Report `DeltaEvents_C = sum[Y*(A_Mc-A_M0)]`, event capture, and paired 98% intervals. The 10% result is the inherited capacity bridge, not the primary estimand; it must not be reframed as positive merely because a threshold curve crosses zero. A fixed-capacity supportive claim requires a positive lower interval and at least +5 events at 10%; the other capacities are descriptive secondary checks.

## Uncertainty, multiplicity, and falsification

Use the parent's conditional participant bootstrap: 4,000 paired resamples with replacement, seed `520241`, no refitting and no fold reconstruction. For each resample, recompute all ten-repeat `NB` and `DeltaNB` values at the four thresholds and the three capacities from the stored held-out predictions/selections. Construct one max-|T| simultaneous 98% family over the four threshold-specific `DeltaNB` values and the three capacity event contrasts, with percentile intervals additionally reported. The estimand is conditional on the fixed repeated splits and fitted predictions; this is not an external-validation interval. Require finite predictions, exactly ten complete repeats, exact quotas, and exact bootstrap arithmetic.

Predeclare the following falsifications and diagnostics, none of which can rescue a failed primary gate.

1. **Orientation falsification:** rerun the policy scoring after replacing `Z` by `-Z` without changing any other design. A large apparent benefit that persists under arbitrary orientation is evidence of a coding/selection defect, not support.
2. **Permutation falsification:** within the frozen baseline strata (`border`/`far`), permute Y and reconstruct outcome-stratified folds under each permuted label vector, then refit both models with the same baseline-only pipeline. Use 200 permutations, seed `520242`, and compare the observed primary `DeltaNB(0.02)` and `DeltaLL` to the empirical refit null. This is a falsification diagnostic, not a proof of clinical value; use a randomized-plus-one favorable tail of at most 0.02 for the observed direction and require no repeat-reproducible directional anomaly under the parent's statistic-specific median/1st–99th-percentile rule.
3. **No-discordance control:** rerun Mc after independently permuting Z within each baseline eGFRcr stratum before every training/test split, preserving its marginal distribution but breaking participant-level discordance. It should not reproduce a primary improvement; if it does, classify the result as implementation/selection suspect.
4. **Capacity bridge:** the fixed 10% contrast must be reported even if threshold NB is favorable. A positive primary NB gain with a negative or highly uncertain 10% capacity contrast is threshold-dependent information only, not evidence of general triage concentration.
5. **Calibration/support audit:** report calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope from held-out predictions, Brier score, flag counts and event counts at every threshold. A threshold with fewer than 20 flagged participants or fewer than 10 captured events in a repeat is unsupported and cannot be certified; the overall experiment remains reportable as inconclusive if the primary threshold lacks support.

## Decision rules and interpretation

Classify the frozen package using the primary threshold first, then the prespecified family and capacity bridge.

- **Supportive baseline policy signal:** all of the following must hold: (i) complete source/cohort/model/fold/bootstrap audits; (ii) primary `DeltaNB(0.02)` point estimate is at least +0.005 and its simultaneous 98% lower bound is above 0; (iii) the permutation/orientation/no-discordance falsifications do not indicate a defect; (iv) the primary threshold meets support counts; and (v) the 10% fixed-capacity contrast is not adverse, defined as its simultaneous 98% upper bound below -5 events. This supports only a protocol-bounded, baseline-only incremental decision-analytic signal under the hypothetical flagging harm ratio.
- **Adverse result:** the primary `DeltaNB(0.02)` simultaneous 98% upper bound is below 0, or the point estimate is at most -0.005 with a lower bound below 0, with complete audits and no implementation defect. This falsifies the claim that baseline Z improves this prespecified policy in this selected cohort; it does not falsify biological relevance or every other use of cystatin C.
- **Inconclusive:** any failure of source semantics, cohort anchors, convergence, threshold support, bootstrap validity, or falsification integrity; a primary interval crossing zero without a practically meaningful point estimate; a positive threshold result that fails the 10% bridge; or conflicting threshold/capacity results. Non-rejection is never equivalence, and a positive NB under a hypothetical ratio is not clinical benefit.

If supportive, adverse, or inconclusive, report the full curve and all predeclared thresholds/capacities, not only the most favorable point. Claims about the appropriate action, harm/benefit ratio, measured GFR, CKD, renal decline, causal testing benefit, clinician behavior, cost, patient outcomes, missing-not-at-random selection, nonattenders, or transportability require expert adjudication, an intervention/utility study, authoritative field/assay documentation where needed, and external prospective validation. In particular, the selected complete repeat-attender population and future-dependent selection prohibit population-level deployment claims.

## Literature and evidence limits

Decision-curve analysis is motivated by the standard threshold-probability net-benefit framework commonly attributed to Vickers and Elkin (DOI `10.1177/0272989X06295361`). The frozen public acquisition encountered an access challenge page rather than article text, so this proposal does not claim to have inspected that paper's full methods. A current Europe PMC search result for a 2026 commentary (source cache ID `[source checksum]`) explicitly emphasizes that decision thresholds, calibration, absolute impact, and external validation need clarification in clinical prediction work; it is contextual motivation, not evidence about this UKB cohort. The downloaded natural-history demonstration was inspected for its emphasis on calibration and risk trajectories, but it does not supply renal clinical utility evidence for this experiment. None of these references supplies a validated threshold or harm ratio here.
