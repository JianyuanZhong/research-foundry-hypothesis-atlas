# Remote assay history beyond contemporaneous P in the frozen repeat-TACE HCC cohort

## Decision question and substantive advance

At the time of a recorded repeat TACE, does a prespecified remote same-assay history add reproducible information about the next observed same-assay laboratory result after the contemporaneous pre-repeat assay value `P` and a small, frozen set of information available at that time are known?

This is the clinically relevant reverse-nested question. A clinician who has a current assay value should not need an older value unless that history carries incremental information. The experiment therefore compares a current-state reference model with a current-state-plus-remote-history model. It does **not** test a treatment effect, toxicity, recovery, response, safety, benefit, or a change in TACE policy. The endpoint is the next *observed laboratory result*, an observation-process endpoint. A supportive result would justify only a subsequent study of whether history improves calibrated prediction of an adjudicated clinical outcome; it would not justify changing treatment.

The strongest claim currently supported by the evidence is availability and direction-blind feasibility: the frozen construction has 319 systemic-record and 1,491 TACE2-only patients, 159 and 526 first eligible repeat events, and expected complete albumin triplets of 40/118 and total-bilirubin triplets of 41/119 (systemic/comparator). The completed audit also shows that the rich longitudinal history proposed by the higher-ranked parent is sparse: most complete triplets have at most one usable remote observation, only 18 patients per arm have at least two historical observations for the relevant assay, and the history-vector/interaction design can be rank deficient in the exposed triplets. The event-linked pseudo-current feasibility check is likewise negative, with only 2 systemic and 4 comparator candidates per assay. None of those audits examined outcome direction or association.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is narrower and prespecified: among the selected first-repeat-event patients with a complete triplet, adding one remote pre-`P` anchor `B` to the model containing `P` improves out-of-fold prediction of `Y` under the locked design. The primary history representation is deliberately parsimonious and identifiable. A richer repeated-history representation is a gated secondary feasibility analysis only; it cannot replace the primary or be promoted after seeing results.

For each assay and selected event define, without outcome-dependent selection:

- `B` (remote anchor): the latest valid, uncensored, concordant numeric result on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1, excluding day 0. Retain its timestamp `B_time`.

- `Y` (next observed result): a valid, uncensored, concordant numeric result in that same encounter during `(event_time, event_time + 72 hours]`, selected as the nearest to nominal `event_time + 24 hours`; an earlier timestamp resolves an exact-distance tie. Retain its timestamp for audit only.

## Reverse-nested history definition and sparsity repair

For every assay-specific complete triplet, construct history strictly before `P` and never use the selected `P` encounter. The primary history window is `[TACE2_event_time, event_time − 72 hours)`. This leaves a locked 72-hour separation before the repeat event and excludes near-event observations that could duplicate current state. Exclude every history row in the selected `P` encounter, every row at or after `P_time`, every row at or after the repeat event, and any row created by a duplicate patient join. Use exact assay labels only.

The **primary history representation is one variable, `B`**, the remote anchor already defined above. It is included exactly once in the model. Do not add a slope, recency, observation count, `has_any_history`, `P−B`, `(P−B)/time`, specimen dummy, department expansion, or broad historical laboratory vector to the primary estimand. `P−B` is algebraically equivalent to adding `B` conditional on `P`, not an independent history discovery.

This is a substantive repair rather than a loss of the scientific question. The completed direction-blind feasibility audit found remote observations in the locked window for 37/40 albumin systemic, 108/118 albumin comparator, 38/41 bilirubin systemic, and 108/119 bilirubin comparator cases, but most exposed cases had exactly one remote observation; only 18 per arm had at least two historical observations for the assay. The proposed rich slope/count/recency vector was therefore sparse and potentially rank deficient in the 40/41 systemic triplets. The primary B-only feature is identifiable whenever the audited triplet is complete and avoids changing the estimand through an outcome-adjacent requirement for repeated history. Emit counts of any remote history, at least two distinct historical timestamps, and history-pattern availability descriptively, without using those counts to select patients or claim a result.

A **gated secondary repeated-history feasibility analysis** may construct a locked, assay-specific slope only if at least two distinct, uncensored, concordant historical timestamps occur for the patient. It may use the same history window and a predeclared representation `{B, N_H, S_H, R_H}` only as a descriptive/secondary sensitivity, with no variable or model selected after outcomes are read. Before fitting it, report the complete design rank, per-fold rank, feature missingness, and arm/fold support. If any training fold is rank deficient, if fewer than five patients per arm support a held-out fold, or if the repeated-history matrix is materially sparse, do not fit it and report the completed negative feasibility finding. The secondary can never overturn or replace a primary B-only result, and no clinical or pathway conclusion may rely on it. No repeated-history extension is required for a valid primary analysis.

## Frozen covariates and nested estimand

Use one compact, pre-outcome covariate core, chosen to be computable before `P` and `Y` and identical in both models:

`P`, pathway arm (`A`: systemic-record versus TACE2-only), age, sex, TACE2-to-repeat interval in calendar days, and event-minus-`P` lead time in hours.

`P` occurs exactly once. Do not add a covariate because of outcome association, observed balance, or a favorable result. Do not include `Y`, post-repeat information, post-event medications, any panel-derived selection variable, or variables that encode complete-triplet availability. If age or sex is missing, apply the locked training-only imputation rule and retain a missingness indicator only if it is part of the predeclared core; otherwise report non-estimability rather than silently changing the columns. The core is deliberately smaller than the higher-ranked parent's broad `Z` because the systemic arm has approximately 40 observations and broad history/covariate blocks risk non-identifiability.

For each assay, fit the same complete-triplet patients and frozen patient-level folds in both nested models:

```text
M0: Y ~ P + A + age + sex + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_lead_hours
M1: Y ~ P + B + A + age + sex + TACE2_to_repeat_days + P_lead_hours
```

Use ordinary ridge linear regression with a locked penalty grid `{0, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}` and an unpenalized intercept. Standardize continuous predictors and impute only within each training fold. Select the penalty by inner leave-one-out MSE within the outer training fold; never use held-out `Y`. If a redundant column makes preprocessing non-estimable, apply the deterministic rank rule (remove only the predeclared redundant column, with `P` retained); if the six-column core cannot be estimated in a fold, the assay comparison is computationally inconclusive. OLS with HC3 intervals is a transparency sensitivity, not the primary estimand. No machine-learning model replaces this interpretable nested comparison.

The primary estimand is the paired cross-fitted change in prediction performance of `M1` versus `M0`, pooled within assay over the complete-triplet patients. Define the primary direction as

`Delta_RMSE_std = RMSE_std(M0) - RMSE_std(M1)`,

where `RMSE_std` divides native RMSE by the held-out complete-triplet outcome standard deviation defined by the locked pooled evaluation set. Report paired `Delta_MAE_std`, cross-fitted `Delta_R2`, prediction variance, calibration intercept and slope, fold-specific performance, selected penalties, and support separately by arm. Native units are not clinically interpretable because the source has no laboratory units or reference ranges.

## Fold construction and uncertainty

Before inspecting outcome values for model choices, assign each assay's complete-triplet patients to five fixed outer folds separately within pathway arm by sorting a privacy-preserving SHA-256 hash of the patient key, assay, repetition index, and fixed string, then round-robin. Use one primary assignment; report, without selection, 20 fixed hash repetitions as a partition-sensitivity analysis. The expected arm counts for the primary assignment are systemic 8 per fold for albumin and 9,8,8,8,8 for bilirubin, with comparator counts 24,24,24,23,23, but these are audit expectations and must be recomputed.

Each outer fold fits imputation, scaling, rank handling, inner penalty selection, and coefficients on training patients only, then generates held-out predictions for both models. Require at least five held-out patients in each arm per fold and nonzero held-out outcome variance. If support or variance fails, do not merge folds, alter the split, or use a more flexible model; mark that assay comparison computationally inconclusive.

For the primary 95% interval, use 2,000 fixed-seed, stratified patient-level bootstrap replicates of paired held-out predictions, preserving patient pairing and the complete cross-fitting assignment. Report the estimable-replicate fraction and percentile or predeclared studentized interval consistently. A 200-replicate full-pipeline refit bootstrap is a sensitivity only. If fewer than 95% of primary bootstrap replicates are estimable, the uncertainty gate fails. Report fold-level estimates, arm-level support, and the complete uncertainty interval; do not call a positive point estimate supportive without the prescribed interval and stability checks.

## Negative controls, feasibility findings, and falsification

The completed direction-blind audit is retained as a negative feasibility result, not discarded because it makes the richer hypothesis difficult:

- The rich history vector is not a primary estimand because repeated-history support is sparse and rank deficiency was observed or anticipated in the exposed 40/41 triplets.
- The event-linked pseudo-current check is not an interpretable negative control: it has only 2 systemic and 4 comparator candidates per assay, far below the prespecified support requirement. Report it as non-estimable and do not use it to block the generic remote-history prediction question; it does block any claim that the finding is specifically event-related.
- The audit found no additional albumin triplets and only one additional bilirubin triplet per arm from assay-specific rather than common timestamps. Preserve common-timestamp counts and do not select the more permissive clock after results.

Apply the following locked computational checks, without outcome-direction screening:

1. **Current-state dominance.** M0 must contain `P` and the full compact core. Any gain over a B-only model is descriptive and cannot support the reverse-nested claim.
2. **History-linkage destruction.** Within each outer training and held-out partition, permute paired `(B, B_time)` assignments among analysis patients by 500 fixed, predeclared permutations, leaving `P`, `Y`, and the covariates fixed. Fit/evaluate the placebo M1 with the identical pipeline. If observed gain is not distinguishable from the linkage-destroyed distribution, report no demonstrated patient-specific history increment; this is a predictive diagnostic, not a causal test.
3. **Temporal leakage audit.** Automatically assert that no history row belongs to the selected `P` encounter, is at/after `P_time`, is at/after the repeat event, or is duplicated through a many-to-many join. Re-run after excluding all same-timestamp duplicate groups and under concordant-only handling.
4. **Timing stability.** Repeat the complete nested comparison under the locked inherited pre/post selectors and common-timestamp pairing. Count empirically distinct estimable variants; identical selected rows are one variant, not independent corroboration. Do not select a favorable clock.
5. **Observation-selection sensitivity.** First report unweighted complete-triplet results. As a sensitivity only, model repeat-event and assay-observation availability from information available before the relevant observation, use cross-fitted overlap/inverse-probability weights, and report positivity, balance, arm-specific effective sample size, maximum and concentration of weights. Weighting diagnoses differential selection; it cannot recover unobserved `Y` or remove collider bias from conditioning on repeat TACE and measured panels. Material weighted/unweighted conflict or failed positivity is selection-sensitive/inconclusive.
6. **Influence and partition stability.** Report leave-one-patient-out changes, leverage, Cook's distance, and DFBETA for the B coefficient in the OLS transparency fit; report all 20 partition sensitivities. If the direction or branch changes after a predeclared influence threshold (Cook `4/n` or DFBETA `2/sqrt(n)`), or if a few patients dominate, classify as inconclusive.
7. **Pseudo-current boundary.** The event-linked pseudo-current analysis may be emitted only as non-estimable under the audited support. Do not manufacture pseudo-observations, widen its window, or turn its failure into a favorable or adverse outcome claim.
8. **Negative-control assay.** Use a non-target laboratory only if an exact label and adequate pre-event support are documented before outcome analysis. Otherwise state that no validated negative-control assay is available; do not invent one.

The old ordering `B` versus `B+P` may be reported as an ordinary-persistence benchmark, but it is not the primary question and cannot substitute for M0 versus M1. Do not calculate or interpret `Y−P`, `P−B`, change-on-change outcomes, or any outcome-direction discovery as a primary analysis.

## Deterministic branch precedence and interpretation

Apply branches separately for albumin and total bilirubin, in this order, before writing interpretation:

1. **Reconstruction failure:** any mismatch in source hashes/snapshot, full-scan cohort construction, 319/1,491 arms, 159/526 events, exact parser, duplicate policy, triplet clocks, or expected audit targets yields `inconclusive` and stops inference. Audit targets are not hard-coded; a mismatch must be explained.
2. **Computational instability:** failure of the minimal core's rank rule, fold support, nonzero outcome variance, training-only preprocessing, cross-fitting, or 95% bootstrap estimability yields `inconclusive`.
3. **Selection sensitivity:** failed positivity/ESS or material weighted-versus-unweighted conflict yields `selection-sensitive/inconclusive`, even if the unweighted point estimate is favorable.
4. **Adverse degradation:** if the primary 95% interval for `Delta_RMSE_std` lies wholly below zero, or M1 materially worsens calibration under the locked rule, report `adverse degradation`.
5. **Supportive:** only if all prior gates pass, the primary point estimate is at least the prespecified design margin `delta = 0.05`, its two-sided 95% interval excludes zero, `Delta_MAE_std` and `Delta_R2` are non-adverse, the history-linkage placebo does not reproduce the observed gain, timing and concordant-only sensitivities do not reverse it, and influence/partition diagnostics do not dominate it. `delta` is a design convention, not a clinical-benefit or minimal-important-difference threshold.
6. **Precise null:** if all computational gates pass and the upper 95% bound is below `delta`, report no detectable improvement of at least 0.05 standardized RMSE under this selected cohort and locked model. This is not equivalence, no-biology, or no-clinical-value evidence.
7. **Ordinary persistence/no demonstrated remote increment:** if M1 does not improve M0 while the secondary B-to-B+P ordering improves, or if the linkage-destruction placebo is comparable, report that contemporaneous persistence or non-specific structure explains the observed signal; do not call remote history supportive.
8. **Inconclusive:** broad intervals spanning meaningful degradation and `delta`, selector reversal, influence dependence, failed secondary feasibility, non-estimable required diagnostics, or any remaining ambiguity yields `inconclusive`.

A broad two-assay statement is prohibited unless both assay-specific primary branches are supportive with multiplicity handled by Holm-adjusted primary tests; no arm interaction or pathway-treatment claim is supportable at these sample sizes. With approximately 40/41 systemic triplets, any arm-specific history claim is exploratory and must not be used to infer treatment-pathway effects.

## Evidence limits and verifier boundary

HCC lacks laboratory units and reference ranges, adjudicated acute illness, imaging/tumor burden or response, performance status, complete Child–Pugh context, transfusion or albumin/fluid balance, repeat-TACE rationale, technical details or complications, reliable outside-care capture or mortality, and verified medication administration/adherence/dose. Diagnoses are lexical proxies without native timestamps. Conditioning on a recorded repeat TACE and complete observed panels is post-baseline selection and can induce collider bias through disease, access, clinician decisions, observation, and survival. The day-14 observation rule is not proof of survival.

An automatic verifier can check source hashes and row counts, exact joins, cohort/event/triplet counts, parser and censoring rules, duplicate handling, history-window exclusion, fixed folds, training-only preprocessing, ridge selection, nested predictions, metrics, bootstrap arithmetic, permutation consistency, timing variants, selection diagnostics, rank checks, and branch precedence. It cannot establish clinical importance, causal or treatment effects, actual administration, safety, mechanism, or whether a clinician would change a TACE decision. Such conclusions require units/reference ranges, expert-adjudicated clinical outcomes (for example decompensation or another clinically meaningful post-TACE endpoint), treatment rationale/exposure, and prospective or external validation of calibration and net benefit.
