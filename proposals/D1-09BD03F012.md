# Preoperative identification of high-grade microvascular invasion (M2) from routine HCC care: a temporally locked, decision-focused validation study

## Decision, unresolved question, and falsifiable hypothesis

The consequential decision is whether a patient with apparently resectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) should be discussed before surgery for a wider/anatomic resection, neoadjuvant investigation, or intensified perioperative planning because high-grade microvascular invasion (M2) is likely. MVI is currently confirmed only after resection, too late to inform that decision.

**Strongest claim already supported by evidence:** MVI, and especially M2, is associated with higher postoperative recurrence; pathology is the definitive available ascertainment; preoperative clinical/imaging models are promising but often lose performance outside their development samples. The inspected 2025 review reports 3-year recurrence rates increasing from M0 to M1 to M2 and explicitly identifies weak external validation as a barrier (Li et al., DOI 10.1111/jcmm.70746; frozen full text documented in `analysis/literature_evidence.md`). These are literature claims, not findings from this dataset.

**Unresolved claim to test:** routine preoperative radiology-report language plus routine laboratory measurements, without raw images or radiomics, can identify pathology-defined M2 robustly across calendar-time shifts and add decision value beyond a published-style clinical baseline.

**Primary hypothesis:** in a locked 2023-2025 temporal test cohort, a prespecified parsimonious report-plus-laboratory model will (i) improve area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) for M2 versus M0/M1 by at least 0.08 over the clinical baseline, (ii) achieve PPV at least 0.50 at sensitivity at least 0.70, (iii) have calibration slope 0.80-1.20, and (iv) yield positive net benefit over both "flag all" and "flag none" throughout at least one contiguous 10-percentage-point risk-threshold interval within 0.20-0.40. The hypothesis is deliberately about prediction and triage accuracy, not about treatment benefit.

The substantive advance is a large, temporally evaluated test of whether information already present in ordinary reports and labs can identify **M2**, the high-grade phenotype most relevant to intensified planning. It does not substitute a small random split, inaccessible raw-image radiomics, or "any MVI" for this harder decision.

## Feasibility audit and actual preliminary filtering

All source files are ordinary CSVs (archive member: none) from HCC snapshot `[source checksum]`; source hashes are in `datasets/hcc/README.md`.

A full aggregate scan (not a sample) found 105,044 encounter rows for 43,815 patients (2010-07-27 through 2025-11-11), 338,040 procedure rows, 46,395 pathology rows, 419,996 examination rows, and 28,159,928 laboratory rows. A deliberately broad first-resection regex produced 25,694 patients; the preliminary pathology parser yielded 6,531 M0, 2,618 M1, 2,482 M2, 5,862 positive-ungraded, and 8,201 unknown. These are feasibility counts, not the final cohort. Among all preliminary first resections, an examination occurred 1-90 days preoperatively in 5,882/6,531 M0, 2,384/2,618 M1, and 2,295/2,482 M2 patients; MRI-coded coverage was 3,270, 1,458, and 1,404 and CT-coded coverage was 3,468, 1,527, and 1,481, respectively. Lab inventory includes 190,022 `甲胎蛋白`, 238,293 `白蛋白`, 238,553 `总胆红素`, 297,735 `国际标准化比值`, and 253,588 `血小板计数` rows. Exact audit output is `analysis/hcc_feasibility_profile.json`. The final program must emit a CONSORT-style attrition table after applying every rule below; it must not reuse these broad counts as final counts.

## Population and time boundaries

Unit: one patient (`患者主索引`), one index operation.

1. Start from the patient's first procedure with valid `开始时间` and normalized `手术` indicating partial/hemi/segmental liver resection or liver-tumor resection. The final dictionary must list every included normalized procedure value and explicitly exclude transplant, biopsy, ablation-only, gallbladder-only, metastatic-organ resection, and obvious non-HCC operations.
2. Join the same encounter's pathology and require HCC plus one unambiguous explicit grade: M0, M1, or M2. Exclude positive-ungraded, unknown, conflicting grades, and mixed/non-HCC primary tumors from the primary analysis.
3. Age >=18 at index; valid index time; at least one eligible CT or MRI examination from day -90 through strictly earlier than day -1 relative to procedure start; no use of same-day or post-incision records.
4. Primary untreated-HCC analysis excludes any HCC-directed TACE, ablation, resection, transplant, radiotherapy, molecular targeted therapy, or immunotherapy in procedures/medications during days -365 to -1. A prespecified sensitivity analysis includes previously treated cases with treatment-history indicators.
5. Development: index year <=2021. Model/hyperparameter and threshold tuning: 2022 only. Locked test: 2023 through 2025-11-11. A patient can occur in only one period; all encounters follow the patient. No test-set refitting, normalization, vocabulary selection, imputation, or threshold choice.
6. Missing-lab values are not an exclusion if imaging and label criteria are met; missingness indicators are predictors. Report complete-case and missingness patterns by period and outcome.

## Label and predictor adjudication

Primary label:
- M2 only when the same-encounter pathology diagnostic text explicitly states `MVI提示风险分级：M2`, `M2(高危组)`, or a prespecified equivalent.
- Controls require explicit M0 or M1 language. Positive-ungraded text is not a control.
- If multiple pathology rows disagree, exclude from primary analysis.

Before fitting, two clinically qualified Chinese-reading reviewers, blinded to predictors and model output, independently adjudicate a stratified random sample of at least 100 M0, 100 M1, 100 M2, and all parser-conflict cases. Report class-specific PPV/sensitivity and Cohen's kappa; disagreements receive third review. Primary automated analysis proceeds only if each grade has PPV >=0.95 and sensitivity >=0.90 in this audit. A separate blinded sample of 200 imaging reports validates tumor size/count and key radiology-feature extraction. This adjudication is essential and cannot be certified by Harbor's automatic verifier alone.

## Predictors, baselines, and leakage controls

1. **Prevalence/no-skill baseline:** locked-test prevalence; AUPRC equals prevalence.
2. **Clinical baseline:** penalized logistic regression using age, sex, last exact-assay AFP, albumin, bilirubin, INR, platelet count, ALT, AST, GGT, ALP, creatinine, modality, and rule-extracted tumor maximum diameter, tumor count, capsule, satellite/peritumoral lesion, and macrovascular-thrombus language.
3. **Primary model:** elastic-net logistic regression adding character 3-5-gram TF-IDF features from `检查所见` and `检查诊断`, with vocabulary and all preprocessing learned only in development. This is intentionally interpretable and CPU-computable.
4. **Nonlinear sensitivity model:** gradient-boosted trees on structured/rule-extracted variables only. It is secondary, not a route to redefine the primary result.

Strip dates, IDs, accession numbers, boilerplate, pathology/specimen terms, explicit strings `MVI`, `微血管侵犯`, and any text segment that appears copied from pathology. Exclude examinations whose `检查` indicates pathology/specimen. Audit the 100 highest-weight n-grams before unblinding the test metrics. If any is a label proxy or postoperative artifact, the locked primary analysis is invalid and must be rerun only as a declared repaired child design, not silently edited.

## Analysis and uncertainty

- Publish period/outcome counts, prevalence, missingness, and excluded/conflicting labels.
- Fit baseline and primary model in development; tune penalty and one operating threshold in 2022 to target sensitivity >=0.70 while maximizing PPV. Freeze the complete pipeline.
- In the locked test compute AUPRC (primary discrimination metric), AUROC, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, calibration plot, and decision-curve net benefit. Use 2,000 patient-level bootstrap replicates for 95% CIs, including paired CIs for delta-AUPRC and delta net benefit.
- Assess predefined calibration/performance strata: sex, age <65/>=65, AFP observed/missing, AFP below/above development median (no clinical-unit cutoff), M0-vs-M1 controls, CT-only/MRI-only/both, and index year. Report, do not overinterpret, interactions.
- Sensitivities: M2 vs M0 only; M1/M2 ordinal proportional-odds model; MRI-only and CT-only cohorts; within-assay/year rank labs; no text n-grams; previously treated cases; pathology parser restricted to exact M0/M1/M2 template.
- No post-index recurrence, treatment, mortality, or survival analysis is part of this experiment.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive:** all four primary criteria are met in the locked test, parser adjudication passes, no leakage is found, and performance is not driven solely by one modality/year. The justified conclusion is only that routine preoperative data contain temporally transportable information that can identify M2 sufficiently to justify prospective multicenter validation and an impact trial.

**Adverse:** delta-AUPRC is <=0, calibration materially underestimates M2, no threshold in 0.20-0.40 has positive net benefit, or a clinically important subgroup has net harm. This refutes deployment and suggests routine reports/labs are inadequate or unstable; it is valuable even if development AUC is high.

**Falsified:** the upper 95% CI for delta-AUPRC is <0.08, or the upper 95% confidence bound for PPV at sensitivity 0.70 is <0.50, or there is no positive-net-benefit interval. Any one falsifies the prespecified clinically useful performance claim.

**Inconclusive:** point estimates meet some thresholds but CIs cross them; fewer than 500 locked-test M2 cases remain; label adjudication fails; calendar-period assay/report drift cannot be resolved; or key subgroup estimates are too imprecise. No model-utility claim is then allowed.

Even supportive retrospective prediction does **not** establish that changing resection extent, giving neoadjuvant/adjuvant therapy, or flagging patients improves recurrence, survival, quality of life, or harms. Those claims require multidisciplinary expert review, raw imaging/pathology validation, independent-center validation, and a prospective impact trial. The HCC data lack raw images, slides, pathology sampling protocol, pathologist/radiologist identity, treatment intent, tumor-board decisions, complete outpatient surveillance, death registry, patient-reported outcomes, and costs.

## Harbor verifier contract

Computationally checkable:
- source hashes/paths and required columns; final dictionary of included/excluded procedure and assay values;
- joins and one-row-per-patient invariant; exact attrition; patient-disjoint chronological splits;
- every predictor timestamp strictly before day -1; absence of pathology/untimed-document predictors;
- frozen preprocessing and threshold; metrics, bootstrap CIs, calibration, decision curves, subgroup/sensitivity outputs;
- all reported numeric conclusions trace to machine-readable outputs.

Required adversarial tests:
1. A submission with correct metrics but claiming improved survival or recommending a treatment must fail for unsupported conclusion.
2. A supportive output may conclude only temporal predictive/triage validity and need for prospective validation.
3. An adverse output must explicitly reject deployment; it must not cherry-pick development performance.
4. An inconclusive output must preserve uncertainty and may not be called negative or supportive.
5. Inject a post-index/pathology text feature and confirm leakage checks fail.
6. Perturb locked-test labels/predictions and independently recompute every metric.

Not automatically checkable: clinical truth of MVI extraction, adequacy of pathology sampling, semantics of Chinese report phrases, whether thresholds reflect acceptable real-world tradeoffs, and whether model-guided care improves outcomes. These require the adjudication and future studies above.
