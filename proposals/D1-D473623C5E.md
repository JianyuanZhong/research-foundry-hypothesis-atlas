# Does a within-patient drop in ICU lab-draw intensity require implausibly strong unmeasured prognosis confounding to be a between-hospital artifact? An E-value–bounded, decision-thresholded child of the eICU monitoring branch

Lead working proposal — eICU 2.0 island, **episode 25**. Operation: **evolve** of true parent
`[prior hypothesis]` (valid, delivery-complete, mu 33.92, generation 10; episode-22
evolve of `[prior hypothesis]`; seed origin `[starting question]` =
"Reduced testing and subsequent deterioration"). This is a **bounded evolution, not a rewrite**:
population, exposure, outcome, landmark, severity adjustment, the within-vs-between decomposition and
the random-slope/LOHO transport test are **frozen from the parent**. Status: **design owned by the
Lead for compilation**. No reference/solver solution was executed at discovery — this is design +
grounding + a bounded binding check, not a solved result.

The parent converted H1 (within-patient unmet monitoring need) vs R2 (between-hospital draw-practice
fingerprint) into a computable within-vs-between decomposition with pre-specified numeric rules. Its
*only* acknowledged, computable residual threat is **within-hospital unmeasured prognosis/anticipation
confounding** (its own note: "charting density is itself a process"; no organisational mechanism). This
child closes that gap by **converting an untestable caveat into two quantified, falsifiable outputs**:
(i) an **E-value / bias-factor bound** for the within-hospital effect, and (ii) a pre-specified
**decision threshold** for the clinical decision the branch informs. Nothing about the estimand changes.

## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICU patients are monitored by repeated lab draws, and clinicians decide how often to draw. [K1] Agniel
2018 showed across 272 lab tests in 669,452 patients that the *presence and timing* of a test order —
not only the value — carries survival signal (order presence significant in 233/272 tests; timing beat
the value in 118/174). Lab-draw intensity therefore encodes a **care process**, not just physiology.

The decision at stake is "**is less ICU laboratory testing harmful here, or is it low-value testing we
can safely reduce?**" This is exactly the decision now under randomized test in Swedish ICUs
([K2] WICH-ICU, a stepped-wedge cluster randomized trial of Choosing Wisely de-implementation
interventions; PMID 42778513 / DOI 10.1111/aas.70339 — metadata inspected this episode). The
branch's association (a within-patient panel-draw-rate drop precedes organ-support escalation) is
**not** decision-grade if it can be produced by clinicians withholding draws from patients they already
expect to deteriorate or recover. The unresolved question this child targets: **how fragile is the
within-hospital effect to unmeasured within-hospital confounding, and does its magnitude reach a
decision-relevant threshold?** [K3] VanderWeele & Ding 2017 supplies the E-value machinery that turns
"we cannot exclude unmeasured confounding" into a number that can be compared with plausible
confounder strengths.

## 2. What is already supported vs the unresolved claim

- **Already supported (not re-claimed here):** (a) test-order presence/timing carries survival signal
  independent of the value [K1]; (b) a severity-adjusted within-patient panel draw-rate change is
  associated with later escalation, non-monotone (parent branch probe); (c) sicker patients receive more
  monitoring (severity↔intensity coupling).
- **Unresolved claim the experiment will test:** the within-**hospital** component of the draw-rate
  effect is a *patient-level unmet-need signal* that survives (a) the between-hospital fingerprint
  decomposition, (b) explicit control of channel-disjoint instability, and (c) a **quantitative bound
  on unmeasured prognosis confounding** at/above a pre-specified strength — i.e. H1 wins only if the
  E-value for the effect exceeds the strength of confounding that a monitoring-behaviour process could
  plausibly generate. This is the claim the strongest rival (below) denies.

## 3. Leading claim H1 vs the strongest rivals, and the observation that separates them

- **H1 (leading, falsifiable):** the association is carried by the **within-hospital (hospital-mean-
  centered) exposure component** β_W < 0, with high within-hospital variance share S_W, small
  random-slope ICC, stable LOHO transport, and — **new in this child** — an **E-value ≥ 2.0** for the
  point estimate (an unmeasured confounder would need to roughly double the odds of *both* the
  draw-drop and escalation to explain it away).
- **R2 (between-hospital practice fingerprint, parent rival):** β_W ≈ 0 while β_B ≠ 0, low S_W, large
  between-hospital slope heterogeneity, poor LOHO transport.
- **R4 (reverse ordering / time-varying exposure; [K3] bounds the method):** deterioration inside the
  window causes the drop; addressed by strict temporal ordering + washout + hour-12 placebo landmark.
- **R5 (NEW — within-hospital prognosis/anticipation, the parent's last open caveat):** clinicians
  reduce draws *because they already judge the patient will deteriorate* (or recover), so the
  within-hospital coefficient is residual confounding by anticipated prognosis, not a monitoring
  failure. **The observation that separates H1 from R5:** (a) an **E-value bound** — if a plausible
  monitoring-behaviour confounder (e.g., an anticipation process acting on both draw intensity and
  escalation with RR ≈ 1.5–2) can fully explain the within-hospital OR, then H1 is not distinguishable
  from R5 (adverse); if the E-value materially exceeds that plausible range, R5 is not a sufficient
  explanation; and (b) a **pre-specified negative-control outcome** that monitoring cannot plausibly
  affect (see §4c) — the exposure coefficient on it must be null.

## 4. The substantive advance over the parent (three computable additions)

**(a) E-value / bias-factor bound.** For the within-hospital adjusted OR from the frozen multilevel
model, compute the E-value for the point estimate and the CI bound closest to the null
(EV = OR + sqrt(OR·(OR−1)), OR>1; reciprocals for OR<1) per [K3]. Report EV_point, EV_CI. This converts
the parent's "no organisational mechanism" caveat into a **quantitative robustness statement**.

**(b) Decision threshold.** Pre-specify that the branch is **decision-relevant** only if (i) β_W CI
excludes 0 in the harm direction and (ii) EV_point ≥ 2.0. The ongoing randomized WICH-ICU de-
implementation trial [K2] is the external arbiter of the same decision; the trial's numeric target
difference was **not** re-inspected this episode and is **not** asserted.

**(c) Negative-control outcome (falsification device).** Define a channel-disjoint outcome that a
monitoring process cannot plausibly cause within the design window: **presence of a pre-existing
chronic organ-insufficiency diagnosis recorded at ICU admission** (`diagnosis.diagnosisstring`, mapped
to a pre-specified chronic set, fixed before outcome modelling). β_W for the draw-rate exposure on this
negative-control outcome must be **statistically null**; a non-null coefficient indicates residual
confounding by severity/anticipation (adverse for H1).

## 5. Baseline vs substantive alternative (same question, same data)

- **Simple baseline (what a naive design uses):** fixed-effect cause-specific logistic for escalation
  with a **single uncentered linear `logR`** adjusted for hour-24 APACHE-IVa; reports one pooled OR.
  *Information it loses:* it confounds the within-hospital and between-hospital components and yields
  no bound on unmeasured confounding.
- **Substantive alternative (parent's estimand + this child's additions):** multilevel model with
  hospital-mean-centered within component β_W and hospital-mean between component β_B, hospital random
  intercept + random slope on `logR`, channel-disjoint SILENT/VITALS_FLAG stratum, + E-value bound +
  negative-control outcome + LOHO transport. *What it reveals that the baseline cannot:* whether the
  effect is a patient-level signal, how much between-hospital practice it contains, **and how strong an
  unmeasured within-hospital confounder would have to be to nullify it** — the quantity that decides
  between H1 and R5.
- **Selection reason for the alternative:** the branch's whole value hinges on H1-vs-R5; the baseline
  cannot address it. No GPU/neural model is scientifically motivated here (tabular multilevel GLM);
  estimate is CPU-only. Approx budget: one multilevel GLM on ~1.8k events / ~130k stays across ≤208
  hospitals + bootstrap for the CI-bound E-value; minutes of CPU, well inside the solver envelope.

## 7. Falsification criteria (pre-specified; supportive / adverse / inconclusive)

- **Supportive H1:** β_W CI excludes 0 (OR>1 for escalation per unit draw-drop), S_W ≥ 0.60, random-
  slope ICC ≤ 0.10, negative-control β_W CI includes 0, and **EV_point ≥ 2.0**.
- **Adverse (R2/R5):** β_W CI includes 0 with β_B CI excluding 0; OR S_W ≤ 0.40; OR ICC ≥ 0.20; OR
  EV_point ≤ 1.5 (a plausible anticipation confounding process explains the effect); OR the
  negative-control β_W CI excludes 0; OR β_W null after hospital-mean + charting-density adjustment.
- **Inconclusive:** S_W ∈ (0.40, 0.60) spanning thresholds; EV_point ∈ (1.5, 2.0); < 15 hospitals or
  < 100 primary events; negative-control estimate unstable.

## 8. What each outcome would and would not establish

- Supportive ⇒ a severity-adjusted within-hospital draw-drop signal that is robust to plausible
  unmeasured monitoring-behaviour confounding — **association/transport**, not proof of monitoring as
  the causal lever; still not a treatment effect.
- Adverse ⇒ the branch's signal is a practice/confounding fingerprint; the "less testing is harmful"
  reading loses its eICU support and the branch should be parked (the randomized WICH-ICU trial
  remains the arbiter). A valuable negative result.
- Inconclusive ⇒ eICU cannot separate H1 from R5 at this power; state the missing evidence (order-set/
  staffing/bed-capacity data absent in eICU) rather than substituting a stronger claim.

## 9. Honest limits (carried forward, unchanged)

- Readiness only — no solver/reference executed; E-value/negative-control outputs are **not** computed
  here. Exact panel `labname` and escalation `drugname`/vent strings remain a solver binding task.
- E-value methods assume the bias is a single unmeasured confounder; multiple small confounders are not
  fully captured — a known limitation [K3].
- The negative-control chronic-diagnosis set is a pre-specified design choice, not validated.
- LOHO is within-database transport, **not** external validation.
- Causal "unmet monitoring need", policy-grade claims, and the WICH-ICU target-difference comparison
  require stewardship/quality-registry data or trial results; explicitly outside this computable
  experiment.
