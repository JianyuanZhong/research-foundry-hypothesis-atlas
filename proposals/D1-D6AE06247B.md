# Episode 8 child: domain-specific patient-state versus documentation process

Status: proposed substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`, informed by the domain-decomposition context `[prior hypothesis]`. No cohort count, fitted parameter, prediction, metric, or hypothesis result is claimed.

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The parent established a useful transportability question: does a six-hour laboratory-recording process vector add hospital-held-out early ICU-discharge mortality information beyond recorded values? Its remaining ambiguity is clinically consequential. A process increment can be patient-linked information about unrepresented acuity; generic observation intensity; a stable hospital workflow/feed pattern; or landmark/end-of-life selection. The parent’s state-matched null and selection audits address this broadly, but a combined process block cannot say which recording domain drives the result.

The closest inspected evidence bounds the opening. Rajkomar et al. [K1] show that sequential EHR representations can predict mortality across centers, but do not isolate recording process. Gao et al. [K2] show that information-presence indicators can be predictive while missingness shifts threaten transportability, but their single-center CLABSI setting does not separate state from workflow. Hadler et al. [K3] show that external discrimination, calibration, and local EHR patterns can diverge, without identifying the source of that divergence. None establishes the eICU mechanism.

The unresolved claim is:

> In adults who remain in the ICU beyond six hours, conditional on the same admission variables and compact recorded laboratory/vital state, does either laboratory-result process or vital-observation process add a reproducible hospital-transportable increment for ICU-discharge mortality, and is that increment attenuated by a domain-specific state-matched null and robust to measured selection/end-of-life boundaries?

This is a descriptive transportability hypothesis, not a causal claim about ordering, monitoring, documentation, physiology, or mortality.

## What would change the next clinical decision

The modeled action is a prespecified scenario in which an early review of monitoring adequacy and goals-of-care context is considered at predicted ICU-discharge mortality thresholds of 5%, 10%, and 20%. These are not validated treatment or goals-of-care thresholds. If a domain-specific increment remains calibrated and improves scenario net benefit on held-out hospitals after state matching and selection audits, the next step is domain-specific feed/order/specimen adjudication and silent prospective review. If only laboratory process survives, prioritize laboratory harmonization; if only vital process survives, prioritize vital-feed/observation semantics. If both survive only in the combined process density or only in EOL/near-discharge strata, do not call either domain actionable: prioritize selection and documentation audit. If both attenuate under state matching, prefer the value/state model and do not add process features. No result authorizes changing monitoring, treatment, transfer, or end-of-life care.

## Rivals and falsifiable predictions

*Patient state:* process density proxies acuity not represented by compact values. Adding state and domain-specific state matching should attenuate the corresponding increment; residuals should be small and broadly stable across held-out hospitals.

*Generic observation density:* lab and vital process blocks are correlated manifestations of observation intensity. T+L and T+Q should have similar increments, and T+L+Q should add little beyond either block. Domain-specific nulls may attenuate both, but this design cannot identify which workflow generated shared density.

*Stable site workflow or feed behavior:* a domain increment remains after state matching, is heterogeneous or concentrated in a few hospitals, and/or survives a timing placebo. That supports a documentation/feed explanation, not a physiologic mechanism.

*Selection/EOL timing:* a process increment attenuates under the >720-minute risk-set boundary, no early-EOL restriction, or measured eligibility weighting; utility changes concentrate in EOL6 or near-landmark stays.

These tests cannot separate order status, specimen timing, device aggregation, interface outage, or clinician intent because those fields are unavailable.

For each analyte use latest valid value and original offset, no carry-forward, training-fold median imputation. Construct a compact vital state T from the fixed periodic/aperiodic payloads in bins [0,120), [120,240), [240,360], retaining first, last, change, and slope summaries with training-fold imputation. T also includes age, gender, ethnicity, and admissionweight. Report coverage and imputation by hospital/fold.

Define L (lab-result process): per selected analyte presence, finite-row count, distinct-offset count, first/last valid offset, observed-bin count, total valid rows and distinct offsets. Define Q (vital-observation process): per vital field/table presence, finite-row count, distinct observation-offset count, first/last offset, observed-bin count, and overall vital row/offset counts. These are recorded-row proxies, not orders, specimens, bedside observations, or measurement quality. Define B=L+Q for combined density. Do not condition cohort on complete L/Q.

## Matched baseline and substantive learned alternative

The interpretable baseline is elastic-net logistic regression for T, T+L, T+Q, and T+B, with training-only encoding, scaling, imputation, and fixed regularization. Save coefficients, odds-direction, block-level standardized contributions, calibration, and utility. Its value is to test whether a domain has stable directional information rather than only nonlinear interactions.

The capacity-matched learned alternative is histogram GBM with identical folds, target, preprocessing, seeds, feature blocks, and fixed parent hyperparameters for T, T+L, T+Q, and T+B, plus the state-null/timing variants. It can reveal state-by-domain thresholds/interactions that additive logit loses. A domain conclusion is credible only when held-out calibration and utility are coherent across methods; a GBM-only AUROC gain is not enough. This pairing tests the scientific uncertainty (additive domain signal versus state-dependent domain process), not a small leaderboard gain.

## Interpretation, falsification, and limits

Support requires a domain increment above a prespecified clinically negligible margin on held-out hospitals, with coherent calibration and at least one threshold’s scenario net benefit; attenuation under its state-matched null; no single-hospital/fold explanation; and broad agreement across S720, no-EOL, and measured-IPW analyses. If both domains improve similarly but their combination adds little, generic observation density is favored. If one remains after conditioning on the other and its null attenuates it, domain-specific patient-linked information is supported, still descriptively.

Adverse evidence is an upper interval below the negligible margin, no calibration/utility support, persistence under state-null with hospital concentration (documentation/feed), or disappearance under S720/no-EOL/IPW (selection). A null with wide uncertainty is inconclusive, not refutation. Also inconclusive: <5 viable hospitals, sparse EOL strata, unstable weights/ESS, coverage collapse, collinearity making conditional blocks unstable, feature-selection instability, or failed join/offset/leakage checks.

Computationally checkable: source hashes and columns, joins, cohort/landmark flags, fold isolation, domain membership, null assignment, model parity, predictions, metrics, bootstrap, and conclusion-to-output linkage. Requiring clinical adjudication: whether a lab row is an order/specimen/revision/interface artifact; whether vital rows are bedside/device aggregates; whether documentation reflects intent; whether alerts improve care. Additional order/specimen/device/interface timestamps, death time, clinician review, prospective silent deployment, and impact study are required for those claims.

Alternatives not chosen: raw-event neural sequence model (defer unless a bounded diagnostic shows fixed summaries erase ordering relevant to the prespecified contrast); hospital random-effects model (secondary heterogeneity only, because site effects would be absorbed rather than transported); causal monitoring/testing analysis (unavailable fields); and adding treatment/note/respiratory sources (would obscure the minimal lab-vital decomposition). Revisit only after a domain shows a coherent held-out increment, state-null attenuation, and selection robustness.
