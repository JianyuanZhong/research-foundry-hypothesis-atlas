## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

Existing evidence supports associations of baseline metabolic indices with later cardiometabolic disease and the use of administrative multistate models for disease progression. It does not establish whether change between two UKB assessment-linked metabolic panels adds route-specific prognostic information after panel-1 levels, established domain, and duration are accounted for. The local snapshot cannot establish exact specimen collection times or clinical onset. Public UKB repeat-assessment documentation and literature indicate that return is a selected process with roughly 20–21% response and health/geographic differences; therefore a complete-repeat-panel result cannot be generalized to the full recruited cohort.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UKB participants aged 40–69 at recruitment who have exactly one of diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or chronic kidney disease coded by paired administrative code/date fields at both assessment-linked panels, remain in the same one-domain state at panel 1, and have complete paired HbA1c, triglyceride, and HDL values, worsening assessment-linked change predicts the first subsequent transition to a second domain beyond panel-1 levels, existing-domain identity, disease duration, and prespecified covariates.

The primary estimand is conditional internal prognosis among people with an observed valid second assessment and complete paired assays. It is not a causal effect of changing a biomarker, a biological slope, a clinical-onset claim, a treatment recommendation, a monitoring benefit, or a result transportable to non-returners. The exact unresolved claim is incremental route-specific prognostic information, not baseline association.

## Matched baseline, alternative, and substantive comparison

All models share the population, routes, death hazard, time zero, censoring, split, preprocessing, weights, uncertainty and locked evaluation.

B0 is the simple current-level baseline: route-specific cause-specific Cox hazards for each existing-domain-to-new-domain transition plus explicit death. Inputs are panel-1 HbA1c, triglycerides, HDL, existing domain, duration, age, sex, gap, BMI, SBP, DBP, smoking and center, with fit-frozen splines for continuous variables. No changes enter B0.

M1 has the identical hazards and inputs and adds the three assessment-linked changes, with change-by-domain interactions only if prespecified and route support permits. It tests the primary incremental-information claim and can reveal marker-specific or route-specific history lost by B0.

M2 has the same architecture and inputs but adds a fixed fit-partition common-axis change: standardize HbA1c, triglycerides and reverse-HDL, set S_t=mean(z_HbA1c,t,z_TG,t,z_reverseHDL,t), and add deltaS=S1–S0 plus prespecified deltaS-by-domain terms. M2 tests whether coordinated deterioration is a more coherent explanation than freely estimated marker-specific changes. M1 can detect discordant marker signals M2 averages away; M2 can reveal a stable common burden that M1 obscures. Compare M1 and M2 scientifically, without selecting whichever has a small score advantage: M1-only support suggests marker-specific information, M2-only support suggests common-axis information, and support for both suggests complementary signals.

A two-panel recurrent/attention model is deferred because two observations contain little sequence structure and extra capacity cannot resolve assessment selection, date-proxy timing, or coded-onset uncertainty.
