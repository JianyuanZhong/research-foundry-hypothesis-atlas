# Time-weighted hypotension burden and subsequent acute kidney injury in eICU

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with systolic blood pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure predict subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI) better than the minimum systolic blood pressure alone?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add prognostic information beyond the nadir, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, kidney function at baseline, and hospital. A null result would weaken the claim that duration is clinically useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

Minimum systolic blood pressure is a common bedside summary, but it can understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

## Prior evidence

- [K1] The eICU 2.0 data dictionary provides the source files and table metadata for the study. The vital signs table contains systolic blood pressure measurements with timestamps, and the lab results table contains creatinine measurements with timestamps. The patient table contains admission and discharge times, age, sex, and hospital identifier.
- [K2] Prior work on hypotension and AKI in the ICU supports the clinical relevance of the question. The relationship between hypotension and AKI is well established, but the relative contribution of duration versus nadir is less clear.
- [K3] The proposed design uses exact eICU bindings, temporal windows, and a falsifiable comparison against the nadir. The time-weighted burden measure is defined as the area under the curve of the indicator function for systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg, normalized by the observation window.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU 2.0 adult ICU admissions with at least one systolic blood pressure measurement in the first 24 hours of ICU care. Exclude patients with missing baseline creatinine, age under 18, or ICU length of stay under 24 hours. The observation window is the first 24 hours from ICU admission. The outcome window is the 48 hours after the observation window.

## Variables and outcome

- **Exposure**: Time-weighted hypotension burden in the first 24 hours, defined as the area under the curve of the indicator function for systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg, normalized by the observation window.
- **Comparator**: Minimum systolic blood pressure in the first 24 hours.
- **Outcome**: AKI defined as a 0.3 mg/dL increase in creatinine from baseline within 48 hours after the observation window, or a 2-fold increase in creatinine from baseline within 48 hours.
- **Covariates**: Age, sex, baseline creatinine, baseline illness severity (APACHE II score if available), vasopressor exposure in the first 24 hours, and hospital identifier.

## Analysis

1. **Baseline comparison**: Compare the distribution of the time-weighted hypotension burden and the minimum systolic blood pressure across AKI and non-AKI groups.
2. **Primary analysis**: Fit a logistic regression model with AKI as the outcome and the time-weighted hypotension burden as the exposure, adjusted for the covariates. Compare the model's performance to a model with the minimum systolic blood pressure as the exposure.
3. **Secondary analysis**: Fit a survival model with time-varying covariates to account for the temporal structure of the data. Compare the model's performance to the primary analysis.
4. **Hospital transportability**: Stratify the analysis by hospital to assess the consistency of the results across hospitals.

## Falsification criteria

- If the time-weighted hypotension burden does not improve prediction of AKI beyond the minimum systolic blood pressure, the hypothesis is falsified.
- If the results are inconsistent across hospitals, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the time-weighted hypotension burden is not associated with AKI after adjustment for the covariates, the hypothesis is falsified.

## Limitations

- The eICU 2.0 dataset does not contain continuous blood pressure measurements, so the time-weighted burden measure is an approximation.
- The AKI outcome is defined using creatinine, which may be affected by factors other than kidney function.
- The study is observational, so causal claims cannot be made.
