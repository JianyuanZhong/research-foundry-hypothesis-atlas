# Persistent lactate with improving versus worsening perfusion at live ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly construct a prespecified phenotype-eligible cohort at live ICU discharge and estimate whether persistent hyperlactatemia has different prognostic meaning according to the direction of contemporaneous perfusion proxies. Completion requires:

1. an auditable cohort and measurement-coverage flow;
2. phenotype definitions and row-level clinical-time provenance;
3. fitted held-out cause-specific risk models for the inherited generic-history baseline and the lactate/perfusion phenotype;
4. a substantive learned joint-trajectory sensitivity on the same inputs;
5. uncertainty, missingness/testing-process, selection and endpoint sensitivities;
6. falsification outputs; and
7. a conclusion audit that distinguishes prognostic association from treatment response, causality, or discharge appropriateness.

No cohort result or fitted model is claimed here.

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent’s live-ICU-discharge transition outcome, exact MIMIC-IV bindings, process controls, live-exit selection stress, and fair baseline/history comparison. It adds a clinically interpretable uncertainty: whether repeated high lactate is less adverse when measured perfusion is improving than when it is worsening. The expert seed’s suggested fluid-response comparison is explicitly not estimated because this snapshot lacks fluid responsiveness, treatment intent, and adequate control of time-varying confounding.

## Unresolved question and evidence boundary

The strongest claim supported before the experiment is only that MIMIC-IV contains repeated lactate, vital-sign, vasoactive-interval, urine-output, and liver-function records that can define candidate observational trajectories. The seed and clinical literature do not establish that any proposed phenotype is present at a usable frequency, that the proxies measure microcirculation, or that fluid treatment has different effects.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among first eligible live ICU discharges with repeated lactate and adequate paired perfusion measurements, does persistent lactate with improving measured perfusion have lower 48-hour first ICU-rescue/timed-death risk than persistent lactate with worsening measured perfusion, beyond the parent’s generic late-history representation and observation process?

Primary hypothesis: the improving-perfusion/persistent-lactate phenotype will have a lower adjusted 48-hour R/D cumulative incidence than the worsening-perfusion/persistent-lactate phenotype, and a phenotype model will improve held-out calibrated R/D prediction beyond the generic history model. The direction is falsifiable: equality, reversal, or an increment explained by process-only features is adverse to the hypothesis.

This is an observational prognostic question. It cannot determine whether lactate is caused by hypoperfusion, whether perfusion is adequate, whether a clinician should give fluid, or whether changing treatment would change outcome.

## Population, time, and endpoint

Use one index per hospital admission: the first ICU stay in `icu/icustays` that ends in an observed live ICU exit (`outtime=t0)), after excluding missing/reversed `intime/outtime`, non-live exits as defined by admission/death reconciliation, and stays without the parent’s minimum valid late-window coverage. Keep an all-eligible flow before coverage exclusions. Restrict the primary phenotype analysis to stays with at least two valid lactate results in the 12-hour window, one in an early half ([-12h,-6h]) and one in a late half ([-6h,0h]), and valid paired perfusion summaries in both halves. The solver must report counts for progressively less restrictive coverage thresholds rather than silently changing the cohort.

The feature window is [t0-12h,t0], with six two-hour bins for the phenotype audit and the parent’s twelve one-hour bins for the matched prediction models. Clinical timestamps are inclusive at t0 only for measurements charted at or before the boundary; no post-outtime row may enter a feature. For each domain/half, use the prespecified median of valid numeric observations after unit/value checks. Do not impute absent measurements as normal.

Define persistent lactate using early and late lactate medians, with the primary cutpoint `>=2.0` in the recorded unit after verifying units and a sensitivity at `>=4.0`; persistence requires both halves above the cutpoint. Report unit strata and reject values with incompatible units or implausible/non-numeric support rather than converting without a documented rule.

Define perfusion change using three predeclared domains:

- MAP: median of arterial/non-invasive MAP items in `icu/chartevents` (220052, 220181, 225312), with a sensitivity restricted to arterial item 225312;
- vasoactive support: any overlap/minutes of prespecified vasoactive `icu/inputevents` intervals (221906, 222315, 221289, 229617, 221749, 229630, 229631, 229632, 221662, 221653, 221986), clipped to each half using valid `starttime/endtime`;
- urine output: total valid Foley volume from `icu/outputevents` item 226559, with missing remaining missing and a sensitivity using presence/absence and log-volume among observed values.

For each domain calculate late minus early change, standardized using training-partition scale. “Improving perfusion” is a prespecified joint rule: MAP non-decrease by at least 5 mmHg, no increase in vasoactive minutes (and, if a valid rate representation exists, no increase in documented support), and non-decreasing observed Foley output after normalization by half duration. “Worsening perfusion” is MAP decrease by at least 5 mmHg, or increased vasoactive minutes, or decreased output, with no more than one domain contradicting. Cases between these categories are discordant/indeterminate and are retained for a three-level descriptive and model sensitivity, not forced into a binary contrast. A continuous-change and leave-one-domain-out sensitivity tests whether the rule is driving the result.

The primary phenotype contrast is persistent-lactate/improving-perfusion versus persistent-lactate/worsening-perfusion. A secondary contrast compares persistent versus non-persistent lactate within the same perfusion category. Include early/late liver markers (ALT 50861, AST 50878, bilirubin 50885), creatinine 50912, bicarbonate 50882, hemoglobin 51222, platelets 51265, and WBC 51300 only when clinically timed and numerically supported; their presence/testing indicators are separate from their values.

Use the parent’s mutually exclusive first transitions in [t0,t0+48h]:

- R: a different ICU `stay_id` in the same `hadm_id` with later `intime>t0`;
- D: timed admission `deathtime` in the interval, ordered before R when both occur;
- S: alive hospital `dischtime` before 48 hours, an observation-boundary competing transition rather than a safe outcome;
- N: no R/D/S while ascertainable through 48 hours, administrative continuation;
- U: `hospital_expire_flag=1` with missing `deathtime`, unknown timed-death ascertainment, never silently relabeled N.

Report cause-specific hazards and 48-hour cumulative incidence for R, D, S and derived R/D. A 7-day analogue, complete-ascertainment analysis, and U adverse/non-adverse bounds are sensitivities. Do not use discharge notes, discharge location, `dod`, or any post-t0 field as prediction input.

## Models and method comparison

Freeze a deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split before outcome-based thresholding. Fit all scaling, cutpoints, missingness rules, feature selection, weights, calibration, and learned phenotype labels using training/validation only.

- B0: transparent penalized discrete-time cause-specific hazards for R, D and S, with admission/demographic/ICU context, terminal physiology/masks and terminal observation-process fields.
- B1: the parent’s matched order-blind twelve-bin history summaries (all core values, abnormal flags, valid counts, masks, tests, Foley and vasoactive process indicators) plus B0 context. This is the generic-history comparator.
- Bmask: the same history masks, counts, lab-testing, Foley and vasoactive process indicators with physiologic values removed. It tests whether any increment is observation process.
- Bdisc: B1 plus the prespecified persistent-lactate/perfusion category, early/late lactate and change, domain changes, liver-marker values and missingness indicators. The primary contrast is Bdisc versus B1, and the standardized phenotype coefficient/CIF contrast is reported separately.
- M2: a two-component diagonal-covariance Gaussian mixture fit on the standardized early-to-late change vector (lactate, MAP, vasoactive minutes, output, and liver markers) in phenotype-eligible training cases. Call a component improving/worsening only if its training means satisfy the prespecified directions; otherwise report posterior component probability without a clinical label. M2 is a learned sensitivity, not a substitute for the auditable rule.

The baseline loses cross-domain direction and interpretable interaction structure; Bdisc exposes whether the particular joint phenotype adds information. M2 can reveal nonlinear combinations and coupled changes that a hand rule loses, while its label stability and calibration are testable. A chronological GRU and transformer are deferred for this branch: the parent already tests chronology versus order-blind history, while the new scientific uncertainty is cross-domain lactate/perfusion discordance. A causal fluid-policy model is deferred because no valid fluid-responsiveness endpoint, clinician indication/intent, counterfactual dose, or sufficient time-varying confounding control is available. A GPU is optional, not a merit criterion; B0/B1/Bdisc/M2 are expected CPU-feasible, with the small M2 fit and any repeated GRU sensitivity profiled within the future envelope.

Report held-out transition-aware Brier scores for R, D, S, R/D and derived N; 48-hour CIF calibration intercept/slope; AUROC/AUPRC; paired Brier differences; standardized phenotype contrasts; and subject-bootstrap 95% intervals. Repeat with valid-bin thresholds 4/6/8, lactate 2/4, arterial-MAP-only, output-observed-only, continuous changes, care-unit strata, anchor-year groups, and parent live-exit selection overlap/weight sensitivities. Report phenotype prevalence, unit support, coverage, positivity and effective sample size.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required falsifications and audits:

1. Verify every clinical timestamp is at or before t0, every input interval is valid/clipped, all item labels/units/linksto are correct, and no prohibited field or future transfer enters features.
2. Compare Bdisc with Bmask and a process-only phenotype model. If the increment is reproduced without physiologic values, a physiology-specific interpretation fails.
3. Shuffle early/late assignment within subject while preserving counts and masks. The directional phenotype contrast and M2 interpretation should attenuate; persistence indicates a construction or leakage defect.
4. Permute outcomes within partitions and refit. Persistent improvement indicates leakage or an evaluation defect.
5. Repeat the parent’s q live-exit selection overlap/stabilized-weight stress, coverage weighting and U bounds. Reversal, low overlap/ESS, or calibration collapse indicates selection fragility.
6. Repeat the endpoint with R and D separately and include S explicitly. A gain confined to S or administrative continuation is not support for adverse perfusion biology.
7. Fit a negative-control six-hour live-ICU-exit model using the parent’s outcome-free risk-set construction. A much stronger phenotype signal for exit-process prediction than R/D supports selection/process explanation.
8. Recompute under the specified lactate and perfusion thresholds and report indeterminate cases; do not choose a cutoff after inspecting held-out outcomes.

Supportive evidence requires a nontrivial phenotype prevalence and adequate event/measurement support; a directionally lower adjusted R/D CIF for improving versus worsening persistent lactate; Bdisc’s held-out calibrated R/D improvement over B1 with uncertainty excluding a prespecified null; Bdisc exceeding Bmask for physiology-specific information; and stability across coverage, selection, unit, care-unit and endpoint sensitivities. M2 may corroborate the structure, but a learned gain alone does not validate the clinical label.

Adverse evidence is no phenotype support, reversal of the expected contrast, no Bdisc increment, Bdisc matching Bmask, strong dependence on measurement intensity, instability under q/coverage/endpoint sensitivity, or gains limited to S/administrative continuation. Label-permutation failure invalidates the corresponding computation.

Results are inconclusive with sparse repeated lactate, insufficient paired perfusion, high indeterminate/U fractions, incompatible units, low event counts, low q overlap/ESS, unstable calibration, wide intervals, or contradictory label flow. Inconclusive means the question was not resolved.

Computationally checkable claims are source/member/schema/item provenance, joins/timing, cohort/coverage flow, phenotype construction, split integrity, feature whitelist, fitted model specifications, predictions, CIF/Brier/calibration, uncertainty, selection stress and falsification outputs. Clinical adjudication is required for true microcirculatory dysfunction, lactate etiology, clinician intent, appropriateness of ICU discharge, treatment indication, preventability, monitoring benefit, net utility, and transportability. The study cannot establish a fluid-treatment effect or recommend a fluid strategy.
