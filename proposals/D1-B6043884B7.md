# Candidate: Dynamic ventilator driving-pressure burden during the first ICU day

## Hypothesis and unresolved claim

Among adults receiving invasive mechanical ventilation at ICU admission, a greater burden of high driving pressure (plateau pressure minus PEEP >=15 cmH2O) during the first 24 ICU hours is associated with higher hospital mortality and longer ventilation, independent of tidal-volume exposure and pre-exposure severity. The unresolved, clinically actionable claim is whether a dynamic threshold burden, rather than a single peak or tidal-volume proxy, identifies a reproducible subgroup for ventilator-setting trials, including whether its association differs between admission acute respiratory failure and other indications. This is an observational prognostic/decision-support hypothesis, not proof that lowering pressure improves outcomes.

## Design

Use the same first-ICU-stay adult ventilated population and time zero as the oxygen proposal: patient first stay per uniquepid, age >=18, ventilation documented by respiratoryCare.ventstartoffset in [-60,360] plus an overlapping record or apacheApsVar.vent=1, and survival in ICU to the 24-hour landmark. Exposure window [0,1440] minutes. From respiratoryCharting, select numeric Plateau Pressure, PEEP, and TV/kg IBW or Tidal Volume (set) records; pair plateau and PEEP within 15 minutes, form driving pressure only for valid pairs, and summarize hourly median driving pressure. Primary exposure is proportion of valid paired hours with driving pressure >=15; categories 0%, >0–25%, >25–50%, >50%, requiring >=2 paired hours. Secondary exposures are time-weighted burden, median, and maximum. Validate unit/range plausibility and report missingness and setting-observation density.

Primary outcome is apachePatientResult.actualhospitalmortality; secondary outcomes are ICU mortality, actual/unabridged ventilation days among survivors, and death or >7 ventilation days. Exact time-to-discharge analyses treat death as competing risk.

Adjust for only time-zero data in the primary model: demographics, admission source, unit type, hospital/ward, admission diagnosis, pre-zero vital/lab summaries, and baseline ventilation status. Add TV/kg IBW, tidal-volume burden, PEEP, FiO2 and respiratory rate in nested models to test whether driving-pressure signal is incremental; these are post-exposure mechanistic variables and should not be mistaken for confounders. APACHE variables are sensitivity-only because they summarize early physiology. Use hospital effects, robust clustering, standardization, and complete-case versus observation-density-weighted analyses.

Baselines are crude, baseline-adjusted, nested tidal-volume-only, and full respiratory-setting models. Prespecify an interaction by acute respiratory failure at/before zero. Falsify with a prespecified null/weak association after adjustment and by demonstrating that the signal is explained by recording density or APACHE severity; do not use a post-outcome negative-control shortcut.

Support requires >=2 percentage-point higher adjusted mortality risk for >50% versus 0–25%, CI excluding 0, dose response, and stability across observation weighting and nested models. Adverse is a protective dose response of >=2 points; inconclusive includes intervals spanning +/-2 points, no overlap, or instability. Even support cannot establish ventilator-induced injury: clinician-selected settings, disease severity, sedation, proning, mode, and treatment limitation are incompletely observed. Stronger claims require ventilator waveforms/modes, adjudicated ARDS, clinician rationale, 28-day outcomes, external validation, and an interventional trial.
