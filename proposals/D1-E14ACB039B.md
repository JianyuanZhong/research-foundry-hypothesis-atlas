# Repaired hypothesis: discordant renal recovery after AKI

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among adults with an ICU stay and a documented acute kidney injury (AKI), does a creatinine trajectory that appears to improve while urine output and/or fluid balance remain abnormal identify higher subsequent risk than concordant recovery? This matters because a creatinine-only recovery assessment may miss residual renal dysfunction or fluid accumulation and could misguide monitoring and renal-risk decisions.

The expert seed `[starting question]` proposes this question. It is an untested opening, not evidence for the association. The closest inspected evidence is that creatinine can overestimate kidney function after prolonged critical illness [K1], that urine-output criteria can identify AKI earlier than creatinine criteria in a selected cardiac-surgery setting [K2], and that KDIGO defines AKI using both creatinine and urine-output criteria [K3]. None establishes that discordant recovery after a fixed landmark predicts later outcomes.

## Hypothesis and rival explanation

Hypothesis: at a prespecified 48-hour post-AKI landmark, patients with creatinine recovery but persistent oliguria and/or positive fluid balance have higher risk of subsequent in-hospital death or a new kidney-replacement event than patients with concordant recovery, after adjustment for baseline illness severity and treatment context.

The strongest rival is that discordance is a marker of severity, observation intensity, diuretics, dialysis, or documentation rather than residual renal dysfunction. The experiment must therefore compare adjusted associations, repeat analyses with alternative AKI definitions and measurement windows, and explicitly treat a null/reversal as evidence against using this discordance as a risk marker. This is prognostic, not causal.

## Population and temporal design

Use one first qualifying ICU stay per patient in MIMIC-IV 3.1. Include adults (anchor_age >=18; ages >89 are represented as 91) with a valid `icu/icustays.intime`, a creatinine measurement in `hosp/labevents` during the ICU stay, and at least 48 hours of observation after the AKI landmark. Exclude stays with a missing or non-numeric creatinine at the landmark or with a creatinine measurement after the landmark that is used to define the exposure.

Define AKI onset as the earliest creatinine time satisfying either (a) an increase of >=0.3 mg/dL from the earliest prior creatinine within the preceding 48 hours, or (b) an increase to >=1.5 times the earliest prior creatinine within the preceding 7 days. If no prior creatinine exists, use the first creatinine >=1.5 mg/dL as a secondary prespecified proxy and report it separately. The primary AKI definition is the creatinine criterion; urine-output-only AKI is a sensitivity analysis because the ICU stay may begin after the urine-output event. Set the landmark to 48 hours after AKI onset. Require a creatinine measurement within +/-12 hours of the landmark and a urine-output record in the preceding 24 hours. Follow-up begins at the landmark and ends at hospital discharge, death, or 7 days, whichever occurs first.

## Exposure, outcome and variables

Creatinine recovery is defined as landmark creatinine <= baseline creatinine +0.3 mg/dL and <=1.2 times baseline, using the earliest qualifying prior creatinine as baseline. Urine-output recovery is >=0.5 mL/kg/hour over the 24-hour window before the landmark, using `icu/inputevents.patientweight` when available and otherwise a prespecified imputation/missingness analysis. Fluid-balance abnormality is positive net balance over the same 24-hour window: sum of `icu/inputevents.amount` for `Fluids/Intake` items minus sum of `icu/outputevents.value` for urine items, with unit conversion to mL. The primary discordance group is creatinine recovery plus either urine-output non-recovery or positive fluid balance. The concordant reference group has creatinine recovery, urine-output recovery, and non-positive fluid balance. A secondary exposure contrasts creatinine non-recovery with recovery while stratifying by urine/fluid status.

Primary outcome is in-hospital death after the landmark, from `hosp/admissions.deathtime` and `hospital_expire_flag`. Secondary outcome is a new kidney-replacement event after the landmark, identified from `icu/procedureevents` dialysis/CRRT procedure items (including item IDs 225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809 and 225810) or `hosp/procedures_icd` dialysis procedures; report item-level adjudication and exclude events already present before the landmark. Persistent/recurrent creatinine elevation is an exploratory endpoint, not a substitute for measured kidney function.

Covariates are age, gender, admission type/location, ICU care unit, landmark creatinine, baseline-to-landmark creatinine change, SOFA or APACHE components available from `icu/chartevents` and `hosp/labevents`, vasopressor exposure, diuretic exposure, dialysis before landmark, and missingness indicators. Treatment variables are time-varying in the alternative model and are not interpreted as causal mediators.

## Analysis and method comparison

Primary baseline: a prespecified multivariable logistic regression for death and complementary-log-log/Cox model for time to death or kidney-replacement event, with the discordance indicator, covariates, and robust uncertainty. Report adjusted risk difference and hazard ratio with 95% confidence intervals, calibration, and cohort/event counts.

Substantive alternative: a time-to-event recurrent-event or landmark survival model using the 48-hour creatinine, urine-output, and fluid-balance trajectories (hourly or 6-hour summaries), missingness indicators, and time-varying treatment covariates. Compare it with the snapshot baseline on the same patient split, outcomes, calibration, Brier score, and bootstrap confidence intervals. The alternative can reveal whether persistence and ordering of discordance carry information lost by a single threshold; it cannot establish mechanism or causality.

Use a patient-level split, with all measurements for a patient in one split; if event counts permit, use an internal temporal validation by admission year group. Report bootstrap or cluster-robust uncertainty. A result is supportive only if the adjusted discordance association is directionally positive, clinically meaningful, and robust to the prespecified sensitivity analyses. Adverse evidence is a null/reversed association or instability after adjustment. Inconclusive evidence is insufficient valid landmarks/events, wide intervals, or failure of measurement coverage.

## Deliverable and limitations

Completion requires cohort counts, exposure counts, event counts, model estimates, confidence intervals, calibration/Brier metrics, and a supportive/adverse/inconclusive interpretation.

MIMIC lacks measured GFR/cystatin C for most patients, so the endpoint is suspected discordance, not adjudicated renal recovery. Dialysis, diuretics, fluid documentation, and selective measurement may confound interpretation. Clinical adjudication of AKI, dialysis indication, and true kidney function would require expert review or additional data. No causal treatment recommendation follows from this retrospective association.

## Alternatives and demonstrations

The simple baseline is retained because it is transparent and clinically interpretable. The trajectory model is retained because it tests a substantive uncertainty—whether persistence/ordering matters—rather than merely chasing prediction. A neural sequence model is deferred: the question is a bounded clinical landmark comparison, and a trajectory model is sufficient unless the prespecified model fails to represent persistence. The three research-ambition demonstrations were considered: the generative disease-history method is not needed for this fixed landmark question; the Bayesian longitudinal latent model is a possible future extension but adds unverified assumptions without measured kidney function; the multimodal cancer model cannot be reproduced because images and its main methods are unavailable, and its lab-only adaptation does not answer this renal-recovery question.
