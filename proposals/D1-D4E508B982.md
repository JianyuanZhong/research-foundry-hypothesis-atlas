# Metabolic–perfusion discordance after support withdrawal at first ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly fit and audit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study. Its primary deliverable is not a clinical rule or a better predictive score. It is a held-out, uncertainty-quantified test of whether the meaning of a recent recorded support transition differs when lactate remains elevated despite stable or improving recorded MAP.

Completion requires:

1. `run_manifest.json` with source/catalog hashes, archive members, schema hashes, verified dictionary labels and item IDs, software versions, split seeds, locked exposure/coverage/tie/topology rules, resource use and output hashes;
2. `cohort_audit.parquet`, one row per subject's earliest valid ICU stay, with adult/first-stay/temporal-integrity flags, complete-window and respiratory/MAP/lactate coverage flags, exclusion reasons and structural versus primary populations;
3. `hourly_features.parquet`, twelve one-hour rows in `[outtime-12 hours,outtime)`, with physiology, support, lactate, fluid/urine proxies, observation masks and process fields; no post-outtime predictors;
4. `support_transition_audit.parquet`, the inherited mutually exclusive respiratory/vasoactive transition ledger, including positive evidence, observed non-positive values, uninformative absence, contradictions, valid intervals, procedure-marker limitations and q recodings;
5. `perfusion_lactate_audit.parquet`, every blood-lactate observation and MAP observation used, valid units, half-window summaries, measurement gaps, state assignment, unknown/discordant reasons and sensitivity recodings;
6. `transfer_outcome_audit.parquet`, all later ICU candidates through 48 hours, every candidate non-ICU bridge, topology ambiguity and lower-bound/upper-bound labels;
7. `bound_cif.parquet`, 6-, 24- and 48-hour cumulative incidences for documented-bridge return (lower bound L), any ICU return (upper bound U), in-hospital death and alive hospital discharge, with support-transition-by-lactate contrasts, bound widths and competing-event accounting;
8. locked subject-level test predictions from a transparent discrete-time cause-specific competing-hazard baseline and a matched observation-aware factorized hidden semi-Markov alternative, with calibration, IPCW Brier scores, overlap, effective sample size and subject-bootstrap uncertainty;
9. `falsification_outputs.parquet` and `interpretation_map.parquet`, linking every reported claim to computed output rows, event counts, uncertainty, sensitivity ranges and evidence limitations.

These newly fitted contrasts, state/observation estimates and audits establish completion. Discovery probes, packaging readiness and source feasibility do not establish a clinical result.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the selected parent and local source audit is only that MIMIC contains dated support markers, physiology, blood lactate, ICU outtimes, later ICU stays, hospital death/discharge and transfer records. The parent also supports a rigorous distinction between respiratory evidence tiers and between a documented non-ICU bridge and any later ICU return. None of those facts establishes that support withdrawal is safe, that lactate reflects microcirculatory failure, or that any subtype is causally responsible for an outcome.

The unresolved claim is whether a recent support transition has a different prognostic meaning when routine MAP appears stable or improving but lactate remains high. This is clinically important because an apparently normalized blood pressure can coexist with unresolved metabolic stress; a respiratory- or vasoactive-transition label alone may therefore conceal a death-dominant subgroup. Resolution could determine whether post-ICU risk stratification needs a metabolic/perfusion check in addition to support status, while a null result would argue against adding this marker to the record-based phenotype.

The prespecified primary hypothesis is:

> Among adults at a valid first ICU outtime with adequate respiratory, MAP and lactate observation, recent vasoactive-only cessation accompanied by persistent lactate elevation despite stable/improving MAP has a higher 48-hour in-hospital-death CIF than the same support-transition group with lactate clearance, and its death contrast is larger than the corresponding contrast among oxygen-only respiratory transitions.

A secondary pathway hypothesis is that the discordant phenotype is more death-dominant than return-dominant: its death contrast is positive while its documented-bridge/any-return contrast is not comparably positive. This is a contrast of recorded outcomes, not proof of occult shock, a biological mechanism, treatment harm, or a reason to delay discharge.

### Locked phenotype definitions

Start from the parent population and inherited support ledger. The primary population is one earliest valid ICU stay per subject, adult, valid `subject_id/hadm_id/stay_id`, `intime < outtime < admissions.dischtime`, non-null discharge time, no death at or before outtime, and a complete 12-hour pre-outtime window. Require at least two respiratory and two MAP observation bins in each six-hour half, as in the parent. For the lactate modifier analysis additionally require at least one valid blood-lactate observation in each half; patients with inadequate lactate coverage remain in the structural cohort and in a prespecified unknown analysis, never silently in the reference group.

For the six-hour preceding half and six-hour final half:

- Lactate uses `hosp/labevents` item IDs 50813 (Lactate), 52442 (Lactate) and 53154 (Lactate), checked against `hosp/d_labitems`. Keep finite numeric `valuenum` observations with a documented mmol/L-compatible `valueuom`; retain invalid units and nonnumeric rows in the audit. Summarize each half by the median and retain count, first/last charttime, gap and source fields.
- MAP uses `icu/chartevents` item IDs 220052 (Arterial Blood Pressure mean) and 220181 (Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean), checked against `icu/d_items`. Keep source-specific observations and do not treat absence of one source as a normal value.
- Define a lactate-recovery contrast only among patients with preceding-half median lactate at least 2 mmol/L and valid summaries in both halves. “Persistent/high” means final median at least 2 mmol/L and decline from preceding median less than 0.5 mmol/L. “Cleared” means final median below 2 mmol/L and decline at least 0.5 mmol/L. “Other/discordant” includes all remaining observed patterns. These cut points are analysis definitions, not diagnostic thresholds; locked sensitivity grids use lactate thresholds 2 and 4 mmol/L and decline thresholds 0, 0.5 and 1 mmol/L.
- Define stable/improving MAP as final-half median at least preceding-half median minus 5 mmHg. The primary modifier comparison is restricted to this state; MAP-worsening and insufficient-MAP states are reported separately. Sensitivities use tolerances 0 and 10 mmHg and mean instead of median.
- The primary contrast compares the parent’s mutually exclusive recent vasoactive-only cessation and oxygen-only recent respiratory transition categories, each split into lactate-persistent versus lactate-cleared stable/improving-MAP states. Joint withdrawal and invasive-liberation-compatible respiratory transition are secondary prespecified strata, not merged to rescue sparse counts. Positive vasoactive evidence is valid `icu/inputevents` interval evidence for norepinephrine 221906, epinephrine 221289/229617, dopamine 221662, phenylephrine 221749/229630/229631/229632 or vasopressin 222315. Respiratory evidence retains O2 flow 223834, FiO2 223835, PEEP 220339, intubation 224385, extubation 227194 and NIV 225794 with the parent’s conservative rules. Procedure rows remain transition corroboration, never continuous ventilation.
- Uninformative absence is handled under the inherited q grid `{0, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00}`; observed non-positive values are never recoded. Lactate and MAP observations are not imputed into the primary phenotype. Fluid/urine features are descriptive or adjustment covariates only: `icu/outputevents` items 226559 Foley, 226560 Void, 226561 Condom Cath, 226563 Suprapubic, 226564 R Nephrostomy, 226565 L Nephrostomy, 226566 Urine and GU Irrigant Out and 227489 GU Irrigant/Urine Volume Out, plus valid `icu/inputevents` intervals. They cannot establish congestion, measured GFR or microcirculatory status.

## Outcomes and estimands

Use index `icustays.outtime). Predictors are restricted to `[outtime-12 hours,outtime)`; clinical observation time is `charttime` for chart/lab/output observations and `starttime/endtime` for input/procedure intervals. `storetime` is never a clinical predictor and is used only in a negative-control analysis.

Follow-up is `[outtime,min(outtime+48 hours,admissions.dischtime))`. The observable first events are any later ICU return, in-hospital death before return, and alive hospital discharge before either. Apply the one-minute tie rule and repeat with 0- and 5-minute tolerances. For topology, L is a later ICU stay preceded by a qualifying documented non-ICU transfer bridge; U is any later ICU return. The latent ICU-level escalation event T is only partially identified, `L_h <= T_h <= U_h). For each group and horizon h report `L_h`, `U_h`, death and discharge CIFs. For contrast A-minus-B report the compatible interval `[L_A-U_B, U_A-L_B]`, never a midpoint as if identified. Death and alive discharge remain competing events.

For L, use all later `icu/icustays.intime` values and `hosp/transfers` rows with non-null `careunit`, interval start no earlier than index outtime minus five minutes and end no later than later ICU intime plus five minutes; require 30 minutes in the primary bridge and careunit distinct from the later ICU stay’s first/last careunit. Record all candidates. Sensitivities are 0/5-minute endpoint tolerances, 0/15/30/60-minute bridge duration, careunit-only versus interval logic, eventtype/discharge exclusion and ICU-unit matching. Do not treat topology as intent, plannedness, preventability or biological deterioration.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Both methods use identical cohort flags, lactate/MAP/support ledgers, q scenarios, topology rules, covariates and subject-level 70/15/15 splits (seeds 17, 29 and 43). No row-level split is permitted.

The transparent baseline is a pooled one-hour discrete-time cause-specific competing-hazard model for any return, death and alive discharge, with L reported as a bound rather than a separate biological outcome. Include support subtype, lactate state, stable/improving-MAP indicator and prespecified support-by-lactate interaction; preceding/final physiology, support intensity/duration, observation masks and row counts, process proxies, admission context, age, gender, anchor-year group and ICU LOS. Fit nested models: physiology/support-only, observation/process-only, and full interaction models. Standardize predictions descriptively to the common adequately observed population using a landmark coverage model; do not call this a causal correction. Return held-out CIFs, bound-aware contrasts, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier, overlap, effective sample size and bootstrap intervals.

The substantive alternative is an observation-aware factorized hidden semi-Markov model with support states (invasive-liberation-compatible, non-invasive/PEEP-supported, oxygen-only, off, unknown), perfusion/metabolic states (lactate-high, lactate-clearing, MAP-worsening, MAP-stable/high-lactate, recovered, unknown), duration-dependent transitions and an observation model for lactate/MAP/support masks, source channels and row counts. Add post-landmark location states ICU, documented non-ICU and unknown only for modeling the outcome process. Fit state, duration and regularization parameters on training/validation data, lock test outputs, and reject any latent transition estimate outside [L,U].

The baseline tests the observable interaction with maximal auditability. The semi-Markov alternative can reveal whether ordering, duration, intermittent lactate sampling and discordance across respiratory/hemodynamic/metabolic domains explain information lost by two-half summaries. This is scientifically informative because the question concerns recovery sequence and observation, not merely score improvement. It cannot identify true perfusion, true ventilation, clinician intent or causality, and it is subordinate to the nonparametric bounds. A generic GRU/transformer is deferred: it would add capacity without resolving the central lactate/MAP measurement and topology uncertainties. A causal target-trial emulation of vasoactive withdrawal is deferred because indication, treatment limits and clinician intent are incompletely observed.

## Uncertainty, falsification and interpretation

Use subject-level bootstrap 95% intervals for CIFs and compatible contrast intervals, preserving L/U logic; retain bootstrap draws and repeat seeds 17/29/43. Report all subtype/state event counts, unknown and discordant rates, lactate measurement gaps, overlap, effective sample size, calibration, IPCW Brier, bound widths, latent state entropy and split stability.

Predeclare timestamp/leakage audits; charttime-versus-storetime negative control; within-window order shuffling; support-only, physiology/MAP-only, lactate-only, observation-only and full ablations; removal of procedure corroboration; arterial versus non-invasive MAP; lactate channel/threshold/unit sensitivity; q, window, coverage and support rules; 0/5-minute tie and all bridge/topology grids; transfer-careunit permutation and +6-hour transfer shift; pre-landmark pseudo-return negative control; discharge-as-censoring sensitivity; first-careunit, admission-type, anchor-year and transfer-density strata; and latent-state stability/out-of-bound rejection. A prespecified “future lactate” negative control must never improve a valid pre-outtime model; any such improvement indicates leakage or selection error.

Supportive evidence requires the primary vasoactive-cessation persistent-versus-cleared lactate death contrast to be directionally stable across the declared lactate/MAP/q/topology grids, its uncertainty interval to exclude zero where a directional claim is made, adequate events/overlap and lactate coverage transport, no material storetime or single-channel dependence, loss of topology-specific signal after transfer permutation, and agreement of the alternative with the observable baseline. A positive U contrast with null/negative L, or an effect only in a sparse unknown-sensitive subgroup, is evidence against the stronger transition interpretation.

Adverse evidence includes reversal or collapse after observation/process adjustment, threshold or unit dependence, persistence after topology permutation, a signal only in storetime or one lactate/MAP channel, non-positivity, or a latent estimate outside [L,U]. Inconclusive evidence includes wide bound intervals, sparse lactate-covered support strata, high unknown/discordant prevalence, unstable split/bootstrap estimates, poor coverage transport or high latent-state entropy. Do not turn inconclusive intervals into a confirmed null.

## Clinical limits and future value

The study can establish computationally checkable facts: exact cohort construction, data coverage, reproducible recorded-state definitions, held-out association/forecasting estimates, competing-event CIFs, topology-compatible bounds, calibration, uncertainty and falsification behavior. It cannot establish that lactate represents microcirculatory dysfunction, that a support transition caused death/return, that a discharge was inappropriate, that a return was planned/unplanned or preventable, that ward monitoring would help, or that acting on a risk threshold improves outcomes.

Unavailable or insufficient evidence includes clinician intent and treatment limitations, validated discharge readiness, continuous ventilator/waveform data, reliable ward surveillance intensity, direct congestion/measured GFR, microcirculatory measures, outside-hospital readmission, external validation and prospective utility. Discharge location is post-index audit information and is not a predictor. These stronger claims require clinician adjudication, richer monitoring/intent data, external validation and preferably a prospective or causal study.
