# Interhospital transfer and in-hospital mortality in eICU: does arrival organ-injury burden explain the excess?

- **Parent:** [prior hypothesis] (expert seed eicu-07, "Risk among interhospital transfer patients")
- **Dataset:** eICU-CRD 2.0, snapshot `[source checksum]`
- **Status:** Complete proposal, ready for publication. Probe results marked **[measured]** (episode 10); all design sections are **prespecified, not yet executed**.

## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

Interhospital transfer (IHT) of critically ill patients is common (about 4% of ICU
admissions in large US cohorts; [measured] 4.3% in this snapshot) and the receiving
team must decide how much of a transferred patient's risk is due to the illness they
arrived with versus the transfer process itself (transport, pre-arrival treatment
delays, information loss). Existing evidence is small, single-system, and mostly
pre-2020, and it is **conflicting**:

- Barratt et al., Crit Care 2012 (ICNARC, England/Wales, 2008-2011) [K1]:
  propensity-matched comparison of 759 non-clinical ICU-to-ICU transfers vs 1,518
  matched controls; RR for ultimate acute-hospital mortality 1.01 (95% CI 0.87-1.16)
  — no excess after matching, but only non-clinical transfers in a single health
  system, with no stratification by arrival organ-injury burden.
- Toss Agegard et al., BMJ Open 2025 (Stockholm, 2020-2021) [K2]: 133/665 critical
  COVID-19 patients transferred for capacity reasons; adjusted 30-day HR 0.47
  (0.30-0.76) — LOWER mortality, i.e., selection of stable-enough patients.

No large US multi-center study has asked the mechanistic question the seed poses:
**after matching/adjustment on organ-injury burden at arrival, does the mortality
difference between transferred and directly admitted ICU patients become smaller?**
If the excess is explained by arrival injury, then arrival-based risk assessment
should reclassify transferred patients toward their directly-admitted counterparts;
if a residual excess persists, it points to the transfer process (delay, transport,
information loss) or unmeasured pre-arrival course, and argues for transfer-specific
risk adjustment in receiving-ICU triage. This is a consequential clinical decision:
whether receiving units should apply a transfer-specific risk penalty, or whether
standard arrival-severity scoring (APACHE/SOFA) already carries the relevant
information.

## 2. Leading claim vs strongest rival

- **Leading claim (selection):** the crude excess mortality of IHT patients is
  largely explained by heavier organ-injury burden at arrival; within strata of
  arrival injury the excess shrinks toward null.
- **Rival (process/residual):** a residual excess persists within arrival-injury
  strata, attributable to pre-arrival treatment delay, transport, or unmeasured
  pre-arrival deterioration (the pre-arrival course is not recorded in eICU — the
  seed's own stated limitation).
- **Artifact rivals:**
  (a) APACHE II is computed from the first 24 h of the **receiving** stay, so for
  transferred patients it partly reflects transport/early-receiving physiology
  (anticipatory/measurement confounding) — the same window problem applies to the
  SOFA-like first-24h-lab score, which is why it is a sensitivity measure, not the
  primary one;
  (b) transferred patients may be selected for transfer precisely because they are
  stable (Toss Agegard [K2]), which would bias the crude comparison toward null and
  make a residual excess more, not less, meaningful.

The two claims make different, testable predictions: the selection claim predicts
adjusted/stratified transfer ORs near 1 (prespecified threshold < 1.20 / < 1.50);
the residual claim predicts a persistent excess (adjusted OR >= 1.50 with CI
excluding 1.0, or >= 30% excess in >= 2 strata). An adjusted OR < 1.0 would
indicate the selection-of-stable pattern and must be reported as such, not as
"transfer is safe".

## 4. Prespecified design (not yet executed)

### Population
- IHT group: `patient.unitadmitsource` in {'Other Hospital', 'Other ICU'}.
- Comparator: `patient.unitadmitsource` in {'Emergency Department', 'Direct Admit'}.
- Require `apachePatientResult.apachescore` present (APACHE II, first 24 h of
  receiving stay) and `actualhospitalmortality` in {ALIVE, EXPIRED}.
- Expected n: T ~4,300, D ~85,500 ([measured] 4,303 / 85,505).

### Exposure
Interhospital transfer (binary, from `unitadmitsource`).

### Arrival organ-injury burden (the matching/adjustment target)
Primary: **APACHE II** (`apachescore`), used in two ways:
  (i) stratified analysis in prespecified strata (0-9, 10-19, 20-29, >=30);
  (ii) as a continuous covariate in the multivariable model.
Secondary (sensitivity): 5-component **SOFA-like** score from first-24h labs
(platelets, creatinine, bilirubin, pH, P/F from paired paO2/FiO2), requiring >=3
components, as an independent arrival-injury measure not derived from the APACHE
physiology set.

### Outcome
In-hospital mortality: `apachePatientResult.actualhospitalmortality == 'EXPIRED'`.

### Covariates
Age (`patient.age`), sex (`patient.gender`), APACHE II stratum (primary model),
hospital random effect (`patient.hospitalid`). For the SOFA-like sensitivity:
APACHE II continuous + SOFA-like continuous.

### Analysis
1. Crude OR (T vs D) with 95% CI.
2. Stratified ORs by APACHE II stratum (prespecified).
3. **Baseline model: logistic GLMM** with hospital random intercept,
   outcome ~ transfer + APACHE II (stratum or continuous) + age + sex.
   Key estimand: transfer OR within/after adjustment for arrival injury.
4. **Learned alternative: gradient-boosted trees (CPU)** on the same question —
   train on D + T with transfer as a feature; compare the transfer effect estimate
   (SHAP or coefficient of a linear probe on GBM leaf values) and hospital-holdout
   calibration. Purpose: test whether the residual transfer effect is
   linear/additive (GLMM) or concentrated in specific injury subgroups (GBM
   interaction).
5. **Hospital-holdout evaluation:** hold out 20% of hospitals (seeded), refit,
   report transfer OR and calibration slope on held-out hospitals.

### Falsification criteria (prespecified)
- **Supportive of selection claim:** adjusted transfer OR (GLMM) < 1.20 AND
  stratified ORs in the two largest strata (20-29, >=30) < 1.50 — the excess is
  largely explained by arrival injury.
- **Supportive of process/residual claim:** adjusted transfer OR >= 1.50 with CI
  excluding 1.0 in the GLMM, OR a >= 30% excess persisting in >= 2 strata.
- **Inconclusive:** adjusted OR 1.20-1.50, or stratum-specific estimates with wide
  CIs (n too small in low strata); then the study reports the range and defers.
- **Direction flip (adjusted OR < 1.0):** would indicate selection of stable
  patients (Toss Agegard pattern [K2]) and must be reported as such, not as
  "transfer is safe".

## 5. Honest limitations

- Pre-arrival course is invisible in eICU; APACHE II is computed from the first 24 h
  of the **receiving** stay, so it partly captures transport/early-receiving
  physiology for transferred patients (measurement/anticipatory confounding). The
  SOFA-like first-24h-lab score has the same window problem but is an independent
  measure.
- `unitadmitsource` is a single self-reported field; no transport-mode or
  pre-arrival-timing data.
- Comparator mixes ED and direct admits; within-hospital transfers excluded by
  design.
- APACHE II availability (89,808/110,853) induces selection; sensitivity analysis
  restricted to stays with both APACHE II and >= 3 SOFA components.
- **No causal claim:** this is an association study; the seed's own caveat that
  findings cannot be attributed to transfer itself or treatment delay is retained.
  Claims beyond association (e.g., that transport delay causes the excess) would
  require additional data (pre-arrival course, transport logs) and another study.

## 6. What the solver must fit/estimate and deliverables

- The GLMM (hospital random intercept) transfer effect with 95% CI, stratified ORs,
  and the GBM transfer-effect estimate with hospital-holdout calibration.
- Deliverables: analysis-set table (n, strata, covariate balance), model outputs,
  holdout evaluation, and a written conclusion mapped to the prespecified
  falsification criteria (supportive-selection / supportive-residual /
  inconclusive / direction-flip). Conclusions must be linked to computed outputs;
  the verifier should check correct computation and that reported conclusions follow
  from the results, not reward confirmation of the hypothesis.

## 8. Alternatives considered and not chosen

- **MIMIC-IV replication:** deferred; eICU is the configured dataset for this
  island and has the `unitadmitsource` field directly; MIMIC admission-source
  granularity would be a later replication, not the primary study.
- **Propensity-score matching** instead of stratification+GLMM: deferred as an
  unstable sensitivity at 4.3% treatment prevalence with hospital-level clustering;
  the GLMM+stratification design directly answers the seed's "match on organ
  injury" question with prespecified strata.
- **Transport-mode / pre-arrival-timing analysis:** not possible in eICU (fields
  absent).
