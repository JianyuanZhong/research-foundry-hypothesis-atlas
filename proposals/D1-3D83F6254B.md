## Targeted scientific repair

The parent is the strongest assessed design because it freezes the corrected discovery partition, selects each subject's earliest broad hour-48 opportunity before applying the remaining gates, compares source-symmetric ICU `inputevents` recorded-loop strategies, protects decision-time covariates from future storage, treats death and ICU exit symmetrically during the grace period, targets measured overlap, and reports partially identified rather than fabricated complete outcomes. I retain those features unchanged.

Its remaining consequential weakness is endpoint-clock coherence. DRR28 says an “accepted extubation” by day 5 must remain free of invasive ventilation under continuous linked-ICU observation until the fixed day-7 horizon, but it never binds accepted extubation to MIMIC's distinct planned and unplanned procedure items. Consequently, an accidental tube removal can qualify as recovery, while a planned extubation on day 1 must be observed for six days and the same event on day 5 for only two. Early ICU exit after an otherwise observed 48-hour success becomes unknown because of a treatment-dependent administrative clock. The favorable-disposition conjunction then mixes this respiratory event with a later hospital decision, obscuring which component changed.

This child replaces only the primary endpoint and its associated result branch with **recorded planned extubation by day 5 without invasive reintubation or death for 48 hours (PE48-D5)**. The respiratory outcome uses a common 48-hour post-extubation clock, restricts the initiating event to the source-labeled planned-extubation item, adds explicit reintubation and tracheostomy contradictions, and remains partially identified when ICU observation ends early. The parent's DRR28 becomes a key secondary outcome, reconstructed both exactly as written and with PE48-D5 as its respiratory component. Thus destination remains an important guard, but no longer determines whether the primary respiratory event occurred.

The falsifiable hypothesis is: among the parent's unchanged eligible adults and measured-overlap population, initiating a qualifying ICU-input-system recorded furosemide/bumetanide delivery before the 24-hour strategy closure increases the PE48-D5 risk by more than 5 percentage points versus avoiding that same recorded event, while conservative upper risk-difference limits for recorded death/hospice/acute-hospital discharge and observed RRT7 remain below +3 and +5 percentage points.

This is not a hypothesis that all-route loop treatment causes successful weaning, that fluid overload caused respiratory failure, or that every patient was ready for extubation.

## Existing evidence, unresolved claim, and clinical importance

The full XML of Clerk et al.'s 2024 ISCCM position statement on ventilator weaning was acquired and inspected (PMCID `PMC11369923`, DOI `10.5005/jp-journals-10071-24716`, frozen source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]). It defines conventional weaning success around no reinstitution of ventilatory support for 48 hours after extubation, includes reintubation/support or death within that window as failure, recommends avoiding positive fluid balance before a weaning trial, and describes diuretic use to achieve negative balance as controversial. The statement also discusses a seven-day WIND definition; that motivates a prespecified sensitivity, not the parent's variable-length interval from extubation to fixed day 7.

The strongest supported claim is therefore that fluid state is clinically relevant to weaning and that a fixed 48-hour post-extubation failure window is a recognized endpoint convention. Neither this statement nor the source audit establishes benefit from an isolated loop-initiation decision. The unresolved claim tested here is whether the *recorded strategy contrast* predicts a clinically meaningful increase in observed planned 48-hour extubation success without an adverse recorded renal or terminal-outcome signal.

That distinction matters at the bedside: the decision is whether to begin active pharmacologic fluid removal in a still-ventilated, post-shock patient. A planned extubation that survives the conventional failure window is closer to that decision than discharge destination, yet death, renal replacement, hospice/acute transfer, later reventilation, and destination must remain visible so a narrow respiratory signal cannot be promoted into overall benefit.

The local research-ambition README and all three availability statements were inspected. None of those demonstrations is cited as evidence for this clinical claim; in particular, the cancer article's main text and full STAR Methods remain unavailable and were not treated as read.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive recorded evidence** requires the lower one-sided 95% bootstrap limit for `DeltaL` to exceed +0.05; the conservative upper recorded death/hospice/acute-transfer RD limit to be below +0.03; the conservative upper observed-RRT7 RD limit to be below +0.05; no adverse ALDL7 bound or destination reversal; consistent direction for the any-extubation and 72-hour analyses; all source/partition/clock/unit/state/overlap/calibration/negative-control/bound checks to pass; B eMAR contamination at most 5%; candidate ambiguity at most 1%; late confirmation at most 1% without material differential lag; each arm ESS at least 75; and no more than 25% of compatible decisions closed by ICU exit before G. This supports only an association for the recorded strategies in measured overlap.

**Adverse recorded evidence** is declared if the upper one-sided 95% limit for `DeltaU` is at most 0; the lower recorded death/hospice/acute-transfer limit is at least +0.03; the lower observed-RRT7 limit is at least +0.05 under both date brackets; or the ALDL7 `DeltaU` limit is at most -0.05. These are adverse record outcomes, not proof of toxicity.

**Margin-falsified** means the upper one-sided 95% limit for `DeltaU` is at most +0.05. It rejects the prespecified clinically meaningful PE48-D5 margin, not all physiologic benefit.

**Inconclusive/infeasible** covers every other result, including bounds spanning the margin; precise effects between 0 and +0.05; eligible n below 200; fewer than 100 A satisfactions or B completions/compatible closures; fewer than 100 definite endpoint events; ESS below 75; more than 10% of person-hours outside treatment probability `[.01,.99]`; more than 1% ambiguity/contradiction; B eMAR contamination above 5%; late-confirmation or differential-lag failure; more than 25% E-first strategy closures; more than 40% primary endpoint intervals remaining `[0,1]` in either arm; material reversal under timing/status/NIV/any-extubation/72-hour/seven-day sensitivities; failed negative controls; or unreconciled state/bound arithmetic. Never rescue feasibility by including unplanned extubations, assuming ward liberation, changing the first opportunity, broadening exposure sources, or searching subgroups.

Supportive results would justify saying that, in the measured-overlap discovery cohort, the recorded-loop strategy was associated with at least a five-point increase in observed planned 48-hour extubation success without the prespecified recorded harm signals. Adverse results would identify record-defined respiratory, terminal or renal concern. Margin-falsified results would argue against a clinically meaningful improvement in this endpoint. Inconclusive results would mean that overlap, observation, event yield, uncertainty or sensitivity prevents resolution—not that the strategies are equivalent.
