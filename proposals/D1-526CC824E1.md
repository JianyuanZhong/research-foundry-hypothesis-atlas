# Locked exact-binomial validation of the EHR vasopressor-restart endpoint

## Child objective and evidence boundary

This is a substantive, targeted child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It retains the parent's completed symmetric restart-instability audit and every frozen adverse/noncausal finding. The repair is to make the prospective validation target, estimands, clocks, adjudication states, and exact operating characteristics executable rather than leaving precision and equivalence as an unclosed proposal.

The clinically important unresolved question is narrow and falsifiable: **does a six-hour EHR restart of the first-stopped vasopressor identify (a) genuinely resumed pump delivery and, separately, (b) a clinician-intended hemodynamic rescue, with adequate accuracy and symmetry between observed NE-first and VP-first stops?** Resolution determines whether this chart endpoint may be used in a later randomized stopping-order study. It does not compare stopping orders, infer pump truth from MIMIC, or estimate causal benefit.

The strongest supported claim is retrospective and negative: MIMIC can compute a restart-dominated endpoint, but the index is unstable under plausible symmetric transaction reconstruction. The audit found 137/137 usable boundaries, 59/59 raw restarts, 13/59 raw restarts invalidated by a shared-chain plus 20% dose-concordance/60-minute rule (R2), 16/59 by shared-chain/60-minute rule (R3), and a 17-case review-priority stratum with only four VP-first cases. These are frozen findings, not pump or intent validation. The prospective claim tested here is stronger and remains untested: a raw EHR restart is a sufficiently accurate, arm-symmetric marker of each separately defined reference standard.

## Phase 2: locked prospective verification experiment

### Population, consecutive enrollment, and index rules

At participating ICUs with synchronized pump export, screen consecutive adults from a predeclared start date through 48 months or the cap below. Require the same phenotype, without future information: first qualifying ICU stay; concurrent positive NE and VP for at least 6 hours; NE >0 to 0.20 mcg/kg/min and VP >0 to 0.04 U/min; neither dose increased in the prior 30 minutes; no epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine active in the prior 2 hours; alive and in ICU; and at least three valid MAP observations in three of the four prior 30-minute bins, including the final bin, with median MAP >=65 and minimum >=55. The episode enters when a clinician documents or executes a non-simultaneous first stop while the comparator remains active. Do not use a future restart, pump completeness, later outcome, or reference availability for eligibility.

The primary unit is the **first qualifying patient**: one first qualifying stop per patient, with the six-hour clock beginning at the mapped, adjudicated EHR stop boundary. Enroll the first 200 eligible restart-positive EHR candidates per observed arm consecutively for primary validation, with a hard cap of 250 eligible restart-positive candidates per arm. The first 200 are fixed by arrival/order, not chosen after outcomes; verify every selected candidate. If 200 cannot be obtained by the cap or enrollment period, primary validation is inconclusive. The cap is an operational feasibility boundary, not a favorable-result stopping rule. Enroll and retain every eligible apparent non-restart in the same calendar/site frame; verify a prespecified consecutive sample of 200 non-restarts per arm if available (or all if fewer), for sensitivity, specificity and NPV, but do not use non-restarts to replace missing restart candidates.

Recurrent episodes are not silently discarded. A recurrent episode is eligible only if its `t0` is at least 24 hours after the prior episode's t0, all prior six-hour windows are closed, and a new four-bin MAP baseline and six-hour no-restart reset are available; overlapping/within-reset episodes are linked to the first episode and excluded from the primary first-patient denominator. Verify recurrent episodes consecutively after the primary frame and report them as a separate episode-level secondary estimand with patient-clustered bootstrap or GEE. They cannot change the primary sample, gates, or stopping decisions.

### Locked clocks, source contract, and indeterminate state

Synchronize pump, monitor, EHR and adjudication clocks using recorded device offsets and predeclared calibration events. Represent each event time as a mapped interval `[t_minus,t_plus]`, including offset uncertainty and documented missing/downtime intervals. An EHR restart is in the six-hour window only when its entire relevant boundary is within the mapped window; an event whose classification changes under the clock interval, has an unbounded offset, or crosses an unobserved export interval is **indeterminate**, never rounded into a favorable class. For deterministic matching, pair each EHR restart to at most one pump episode and each pump episode to at most one EHR event, using earliest mapped start then smallest absolute mapped-time distance; ties are indeterminate. Preserve raw device and source identifiers.

For every stop and apparent restart retain pump/channel and drug-library identity, concentration, programmed and delivered rate, start/stop/pause/resume/bolus/alarm events, bag/syringe and line IDs, channel/pump replacement, downtime, workflow transition and handoff events; EHR item, start/end/store times, amount/rate/units, weight, order/link IDs, caregiver, category/status/open-bag/next-department fields and audit trail; timestamped intent and reason; high-frequency MAP and all vasoactives; death/ICU transfer and lactate/perfusion context; MCS device type, insertion/removal, active/standby, flow/support setting and source; and clock offsets/missing intervals. Missing intent is unknown, not negative. MIMIC has no pump-native delivery, raw waveforms, or verified intent/MCS absence, so these are prospective requirements.

### Two separate reference standards

The **pump-native delivery reference** is positive only when the pump trace shows a continuous off interval of at least 5 minutes (no delivered volume/rate in that interval, allowing only a documented bounded clock uncertainty) followed by stopped-agent delivery at a positive delivered rate for at least 1 minute, with one-to-one channel/drug identity linkage and no unresolved pump export gap. A bounded technical pilot may reveal that a device cannot resolve five minutes, but it may not change the locked primary threshold after arm-level results are opened; any technical inability is inconclusive. A true off interval and delivered restart without intent is pump-positive.

The **intent-qualified hemodynamic-rescue reference** is a separate positive subset of pump-positive events for which two blinded critical-care/pharmacy reviewers independently document that the delivered restart was intended as hemodynamic support/rescue after the stop. Explicit alternative reasons (titration, bag/channel/line transition, transport, procedure, handoff, documentation correction) are not rescue-positive; insufficient evidence is indeterminate. Reviewers see local packet-level physiology and workflow records but not arm-level results or EHR-positive counts. Disagreement goes to a blinded third reviewer. Report pump-native and intent-qualified results separately; never use physiology alone to manufacture intent.

Build a packet for every selected EHR restart and every selected non-restart, oversampling the inherited review-priority features only for a clearly labeled descriptive stratum. Oversampling never changes the consecutive primary denominator. Report two-reviewer agreement, disagreement causes, third-reviewer use, site/unit/system composition, and all indeterminates.

## Exact estimands, gates, and operating characteristics

For each reference standard `r` and observed-stop arm `a`, define the primary PPV as `P(reference-positive | EHR restart candidate)` among the fixed 200 first-patient candidates. Let `X_ar` be reference-positive, `Y_ar` reference-negative, and `I_ar` indeterminate; `X+Y+I=200`. The confirmatory estimand is all-candidate PPV, not PPV among cases that happened to be adjudicable. Primary confirmatory acceptance requires **I_ar=0 in all four arm-by-reference cells**. If any cell has an indeterminate, report the exact worst-case completion interval `[X/200,(X+I)/200]` and classify the confirmatory result inconclusive; do not silently delete or impute it. This makes missing reference evidence an explicit failure of verification, not a favorable endpoint definition.

For each cell calculate the two-sided exact Clopper–Pearson 95% interval, and use the Bonferroni simultaneous one-sided lower bound with per-cell alpha `0.0125` (four cells; family coverage at least 95%). With `n=200`, a cell must have at least `X=155` positives for its one-sided lower bound to reach 0.70 (bound 0.70148); the point estimate must also be at least 0.80. A cell with X<=154 fails that minimum even if its point estimate is high. The design therefore has transparent integer acceptance: 155–159 is possible only if point PPV >=0.80, hence in practice X>=160; X=160 has point 0.80 and one-sided lower bound above 0.70.

Arm symmetry is tested separately for each reference standard. Define `Delta_r = p_NE,r - p_VP,r`. Invert the exact unconditional two-binomial test by enumeration of `(x_NE,x_VP)` and maximization over nuisance `p_VP` (and the constrained null boundary for each one-sided direction); use alpha 0.05 for the two one-sided equivalence tests and state the algorithm and seed-independent acceptance table in the compiled code. Require the resulting exact 90% confidence set for `Delta_r` to lie strictly inside `(-0.10,0.10)`. This is not a normal approximation and is not pooled-arm validation. To preserve familywise 95% confidence across the two reference standards, each direction is evaluated at alpha 0.0125 in the locked implementation (four one-sided tests total), and both reference-specific exact equivalence intervals must pass. Observed absolute differences >0.10 are automatically adverse; differences <=0.10 without the exact interval gate are not acceptance.

These rules are deliberately stringent. Under independent `Binomial(200,p)` arms, the probability that one standard's two arm cells each meet the one-sided 0.70 lower-bound gate is approximately 0.00012 at p=0.70, 0.0544 at p=0.75, 0.6966 at p=0.80 after the |difference|<=0.10 restriction, 0.9933 at p=0.85, and 0.9993 at p=0.90. For both standards under a common independent p these approximate probabilities are 1.5e-8, 0.00296, 0.485, 0.9866, and 0.9987, respectively. These are planning characteristics, not observed evidence; the two reference outcomes are correlated in reality, so the joint figures are not claimed exact for the experiment. At p=0.80, a single cell's probability of meeting the lower-bound threshold is 0.8349. At p=0.60 it is 1.2e-7, and at p=0.70 it is 0.0111. The acceptance design thus has high sensitivity to clearly inadequate PPV and roughly 83.5% per-cell power at the planning value 0.80, with an explicit four-cell multiplicity burden.

For the consecutive non-restart sample, report exact confusion matrices against each standard and exact two-sided intervals for sensitivity, specificity, NPV, and indeterminate rate. These are secondary because the fixed 200 restart candidates determine primary PPV. No class-balanced convenience sample may be called prevalence-representative.

### Acceptance, futility, stopping, and interpretation

Before any arm-level outcomes are opened, lock the population, n=200 per arm, cap=250 per arm, five-minute/one-minute pump thresholds, intent rubric, mapped-clock algorithm, indeterminate policy, exact tests, alpha allocation, and interpretation. A blinded pilot of 30 packets per arm may test export linkage, clock bounds, reviewer agreement and feasibility; it may not estimate arm-level PPV for confirmatory acceptance, lower any threshold, or alter the fixed target.

Pause for data-quality remediation, without declaring efficacy, if after 30 packets per arm reviewer agreement (predeclared kappa or percent agreement) is below 0.80, clock intervals are unbounded in >5%, or pump export is absent/delayed in >10% overall or differs by >5 percentage points by arm/site. Stop for operational infeasibility if fewer than 25 eligible restart candidates accrue in either arm by the prespecified enrollment period or if the 250-candidate cap cannot yield 200 fixed candidates. Stop for futility after 50 adjudicated candidates in an arm if a locked sequential monitor's one-sided lower bound is below 0.60; this is a feasibility/futility signal, not a final endpoint claim. Any early stop is reported with its information time and cannot be converted into confirmatory support.

A **supportive** final result requires all four cells determinate, all four PPVs >=0.80 with simultaneous exact lower bounds >=0.70, both pump-native and intent-qualified exact arm-equivalence intervals inside ±0.10, indeterminate rate 0%, clock/export and reviewer-quality gates passed, and no prespecified concentration of errors by site, unit, system, calendar period or transition type. Pump-only support with intent-qualified failure means the EHR restart detects delivery but not rescue intent and is not a full clinical endpoint. An **adverse** result is any failed PPV lower/point gate, failed arm equivalence, material arm/site concentration, or failed reference-specific validation after complete ascertainment; it falsifies use of the raw restart as a sufficiently symmetric endpoint under the tested configuration. An **inconclusive** result is any indeterminate, unresolved clock/export interval, inadequate accrual, failed adjudication reliability, or incomplete source linkage; it leaves endpoint truth unresolved and requires better capture or another study. Do not lower thresholds, pool arms, relabel a paused transaction, or use recurrent cases to rescue a failed primary result.

## Falsification and claim boundary

Computationally checkable claims are the exact archive member and schema bindings, hashes, source-read-only status, no-sampling audit, cohort counts, key joins, dose normalization, time windows, symmetric rule application, instability counts, clock interval arithmetic, one-to-one matching, binomial arithmetic, exact confidence/equivalence inversion, indeterminate accounting, caps, stopping logic, and reported operating-characteristic calculations. The verifier must include adversarial fixtures in which correct computation nevertheless cannot support a favorable conclusion: one arm at 0.80 and the other at 0.70; one reference passing pump-only but failing intent; a 1% arm-concentrated clock gap; all indeterminates removed from denominators; recurrent episodes duplicated as independent; an apparent restart with no pump-off interval; and a restart after hypotension without adjudicated intent. The correct outputs for these fixtures are adverse or inconclusive, not supportive.

Clinical adjudication is required for true pump-off time, delivered flow, drug identity/channel linkage, rescue intent, MCS activity/absence, and whether five minutes or one minute is clinically meaningful. Another study is required for clinical utility, safety, equipoise, minimum important benefit, transport beyond participating pump/interface configurations, and any causal effect or treatment-order preference. MIMIC cannot establish those claims. Even a supportive validation only licenses a subsequent endpoint-using study; it does not prove benefit, safety, causality, or superiority of NE-first or VP-first discontinuation.
